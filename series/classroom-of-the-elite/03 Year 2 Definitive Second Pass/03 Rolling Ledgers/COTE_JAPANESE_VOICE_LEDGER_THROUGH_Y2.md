# Japanese Voice Ledger
## Canonical written-voice / narration reference through Year 2 / *Second List*

# 0. What this ledger is for

A character voice in *Classroom of the Elite* is not a bag of catchphrases.

The written Japanese repeatedly makes characterization audible through combinations of:

- first-person reference;
- sentence completeness;
- contractions;
- politeness;
- sentence-final particles;
- honorifics;
- name choice;
- pronoun avoidance;
- blunt versus cushioned requests;
- questions versus declarations;
- insult register;
- strategic euphemism;
- hesitation;
- self-correction;
- rhythm under stress;
- and the gap between spoken dialogue and interior narration.

The governing Year-2 method explicitly requires attention to:

- first-person self-reference;
- given-name use;
- surname versus first-name use;
- honorific reduction;
- politeness shifts;
- public/private speech;
- insult register;
- strategic euphemism;
- and Ayanokōji's internal comic register versus cold declarative register.

This ledger operationalizes that protocol.

Its central question is:

> **What remains recognizably this person's voice when role, intimacy, stress, secrecy, leadership, or self-understanding changes?**

The answer matters for interpretation and for later character reconstruction.

A strong model must be able to distinguish:

- a character **changing register** from a character becoming somebody else;
- **development** from simple politeness reduction;
- **public performance** from falsehood;
- **private bluntness** from “true self” essentialism;
- **stress fragmentation** from the normal baseline;
- **interior language** from spoken language;
- and **relationship-conditioned speech** from general personality.

# 1. Core Year-2 thesis — development usually changes permission, not linguistic identity

The Year-1 specialist established a durable principle:

> **Voice and image produce competing partial truths; no single register is the whole person.**

Year 2 strongly preserves that principle.

Most major characters do **not** demonstrate development by abandoning their established linguistic identity.

Horikita does not become colloquial, gushy, or slang-heavy in order to become more caring.

Kei does not stop using the socially fluent `あたし` register in order to become more authentic.

Kushida does not lose all cushioning, memory, or public charm merely because her public/private architecture collapses.

Ryūen does not become polite in order to become more politically sophisticated.

Sakayanagi does not lose elegance when love, grief, or institutional defeat become more legible.

Hiyori does not become loud in order to become consequential.

Sudō does not become linguistically refined in order to become self-regulating.

Ayanokōji does not cease using the same `オレ` because ordinary desire and strategic severity coexist more openly.

The stronger Year-2 pattern is:

> **development changes what a stable register is allowed to contain.**

A voice that once carried certainty may acquire uncertainty.

A voice that once carried friendliness as camouflage may carry self-interested cooperation without affection.

A voice that once carried blunt aggression may acquire self-reflection.

A voice that once carried inclusive care as total responsibility may learn to name jurisdictional limits.

A voice that once narrated desire as utility may sometimes state desire as desire.

This gives the ledger its primary change model:

`stable idiolect`
`+ expanded permissible speech acts`
`+ relationship-specific register`
`+ stress deformation`
`+ new self-knowledge`
`= development without voice replacement`

# 2. Voice evidence hierarchy

Use voice evidence in this order.

## 2.1 Highest-value evidence

1. repeated Japanese dialogue across multiple ordinary and high-stress scenes;
2. first-person interior narration;
3. explicit self-reference changes;
4. repeated address/name changes;
5. register shifts whose trigger is textually clear;
6. dialogue plus later behavior that confirms the utterance was not merely strategic noise.

## 2.2 Medium-value evidence

- one unusually diagnostic line;
- another character's observation of how someone speaks;
- a guidebook fiction scene;
- editorial description consistent with repeated novel evidence.

## 2.3 Low-value evidence unless corroborated

- one sentence-final particle;
- one honorific omission;
- one joke;
- a translated English catchphrase;
- a single angry outburst;
- an isolated stylized spelling.

## 2.4 Do not infer from written voice alone

Written register does not, by itself, establish:

- acoustic pitch;
- vocal fry;
- breathiness;
- loudness;
- actual speaking tempo;
- regional accent;
- timbre;
- seiyū performance;
- psychiatric diagnosis;
- sincerity;
- morality.

# 4. Ayanokōji Kiyotaka — `オレ`, register plurality, and the narrator who can sound ordinary and terrifying without changing speakers

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-AYANOKOJI`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 4.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

The Year-1 specialist established Ayanokōji as the corpus's most important case of register plurality.

His first-person narration can be:

- socially awkward;
- comic;
- sexually curious;
- bored;
- self-mocking;
- observant;
- legalistic;
- tactically compressed;
- clinically coercive;
- affectionate;
- emotionally uncertain.

His ordinary spoken presentation is much narrower:

- short;
- low-pressure;
- non-showy;
- often deliberately unremarkable.

The Year-1 conclusion remains binding:

> **flat public delivery is not evidence of an empty interior.**

His stable `オレ` does not tell the reader which mode is currently controlling action.

## 4.2 Year 2 does not replace the voice; it broadens self-permission

The major Year-2 development is not a pronoun shift.

It is the increasing coexistence of three language systems he can recognize as his own.

### Ordinary/comic `オレ`

This mode worries about:

- whether an invitation will be rejected;
- consumer purchases;
- food;
- embarrassment;
- friendship;
- dating;
- physical desire;
- social mistakes;
- unfamiliar ordinary experiences.

V08's bus and skiing material and V09.5's yogurt-maker sequence are especially important. The humor is not filler. It demonstrates that the narrator can inhabit a world in which inefficiency, ignorance, bodily failure, and disappointment do not have to be converted immediately into strategy.

### Analytical/strategic `オレ`

This mode:

- enumerates conditions;
- distinguishes probabilities;
- models another person's likely response;
- compresses emotion into decision variables;
- manages information;
- separates nominal from practical outcomes.

It remains extremely stable.

V12 is the most severe late-Year2 proof: he can make a direct command unnecessary by controlling enough of another person's information and relation to him.

### Emerging participant `オレ`

This is the most important developmental addition.

He sometimes states:

- what he wants;
- that another person may refuse;
- that a future is not his jurisdiction;
- that an ordinary day matters because he wants it;
- that another person's answer may be better precisely because he did not supply it.

V09.5 with Hiyori is the cleanest participant-mode local model.

V11 gives the strongest explicit stopping rule:

> `ここから先は、オレの立ち入る領域じゃない。`

The line is linguistically ordinary for him. Its significance lies in the **speech act**: the strategist names a limit on his own standing.

## 4.3 Public understatement remains a major voice invariant

Ayanokōji repeatedly minimizes his own authorship.

V07:

> `オレがしたことは本当に些細なことばかりだ。`

He later characterizes the Yagami operation as setting a stage rather than deciding the verdict.

This is not always a lie.

It is a recurrent **causal-understatement register**:

- describe the direct act narrowly;
- leave the environmental effect implicit;
- distinguish the target's agency from his own staging;
- thereby preserve a technically defensible description that can understate responsibility.

Simulation must therefore avoid making him boast about manipulation.

He is far more likely to:

- describe the mechanism;
- reduce his own role to one part of it;
- and let the listener infer scale.

## 4.4 Emotional uncertainty is usually analytic before it is confessional

Ayanokōji does not normally narrate:

> “I am deeply sad.”

He is more likely to:

- notice bodily response;
- compare present state with an expected state;
- test labels;
- reject one interpretation;
- keep another open.

V10's involuntary smile is exemplary.

The important fact is that the smile occurs **before he instructs himself to produce it**.

He treats the expression as evidence.

This is consistent with his voice: affect often becomes legible to him through observation of himself.

## 4.5 V12.5 creates a narratorial warning stronger than ordinary unreliability

The breakup chapter temporarily presents a romantic interior that appears to belong to Ayanokōji.

The spoken `別れよう` reveals that the preceding interior was Kei's hoped-for counterfactual.

This is not merely “Ayanokōji lied to the reader.”

It is a formal point-of-view trap.

The ledger therefore distinguishes:

- **Ayanokōji's selective narration**
- from
- **the novel temporarily misaligning perceived focal ownership**.

A reconstruction model must not treat every emotionally rich first-person-like passage near him as automatically his own interior without source-level focalization.

## 4.6 Volume 0 — child voice as retrospective genealogy

The child narrator is not simply the adult voice in miniature.

The retrospective source shows a world organized around:

- function;
- input;
- performance;
- test;
- observation.

Yuki's carrot question is a particularly diagnostic linguistic event.

Kiyotaka initially responds to preference through nutritional/function information before being pushed toward:

> what do **I** like?

This is a genealogy of the adult narrator's tendency to move from objective function toward subjective preference only after explicit prompting.

Volume 0 also blocks an “emotionless child” reading. Appetite, curiosity, dislike, and preference exist.

What is starved is the expectation that feeling has relational effect.

## 4.7 Simulation constraints

Do not write Ayanokōji as:

- a robot;
- an emotionless monosyllable machine;
- an omniscient villain monologuing his plans;
- a perpetually sarcastic teenager;
- a conventionally warm boyfriend;
- or a person whose every ordinary joke is secretly strategic.

Correct reconstruction requires switching among registers while preserving the same sparse outward baseline.

# 5. Horikita Suzune — stable polished femininity, expanding corrigibility

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-HORIKITA`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 5.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Horikita's voice is one of the most stable in the corpus.

The Year-1 specialist identifies:

- complete sentences;
- controlled feminine endings;
- `～わ`;
- `～よ`;
- `～かしら`;
- judgment-oriented diction;
- precise evaluation;
- limited need for social cushioning.

Development occurs without discarding this register.

By the Year-1 boundary, the same voice can contain:

- gratitude;
- request;
- uncertainty;
- self-revision.

## 5.2 Year 2 expands collaborative speech without making her linguistically soft

The most important Year-2 change is increased ability to speak in forms such as:

- “I need your judgment here”;
- “this is my decision”;
- “I may be wrong”;
- “take responsibility with me”;
- “I cannot do this alone.”

These are not literal reusable lines; they describe new **speech acts** inside the established register.

V01's cross-year negotiation shows her constructing an equal cooperative proposal rather than only issuing judgments.

V06 shows her entering a formal bounded agreement with Ryūen and accepting Kushida's openly self-interested terms.

V09 shows a student-council voice that must explain reasons others can own rather than merely command compliance.

## 5.3 Horikita/Kushida — adversarial speech remains adversarial

A major reconstruction error would be to treat cooperation as warmth.

The V06 compact explicitly preserves hostility.

Kushida says:

> `私は私のためだけに１年半戦ってクラスに貢献する。`

Horikita accepts the self-interested premise.

Their left-handed handshake makes the antagonism formal rather than erased.

Horikita's voice around Kushida should therefore preserve:

- clipped mutual skepticism;
- challenge;
- negotiated utility;
- occasional recognition;
- no counterfeit sweetness.

## 5.4 Horikita/Ibuki — antagonistic familiarity can lower ceremony

The school-trip snowball material and their broader Year-2 interaction show that Horikita can participate in a more bodily, childish, competitive social mode without losing her core syntax elsewhere.

This is **relationship-specific relaxation**, not a global rewrite.

With Ibuki she can tolerate:

- direct challenge;
- pettiness;
- banter;
- mutual irritation;
- play.

The same person remains formal in leadership.

## 5.5 Late Year 2 — care enters the same precise register

V12.5 gives one of Horikita's strongest care scenes.

She worries about Ayanokōji's heart after the accumulated expulsions and offers support if he is hurting.

The important voice result is that care does not arrive through a newly sentimental persona.

It arrives as:

- observation;
- concern;
- practical offer;
- bounded directness.

This is exactly how Year-2 development should sound for Horikita.

## 5.6 Simulation constraints

Do not write Horikita as:

- archaically aristocratic;
- tsundere catchphrase automation;
- permanently cold;
- suddenly slangy after development;
- secretly begging for Ayanokōji's approval in every exchange.

Her mature voice remains controlled. Development increases **corrigibility and care**, not verbal softness for its own sake.

# 6. Karuizawa Kei — `あたし`, social competence, intimacy, and stress fracture

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-KEI`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 6.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Kei's public `あたし` is not best understood as a fake shell covering a “real victim.”

It is an acquired social technology that became genuine competence.

Her baseline supports:

- quick informal response;
- affect display;
- status negotiation;
- complaint;
- teasing;
- social correction.

Fear can fragment the same voice.

Private vulnerability does not require a new pronoun.

## 6.2 Year 2 preserves `あたし` while intimacy widens the speech domain

Relationship development with Ayanokōji gives Kei more space to:

- make demands;
- ask for reassurance;
- complain about jealousy;
- negotiate ordinary time;
- teach him what kind of answer a girlfriend expects.

V06 is especially diagnostic.

When he answers her hypothetical expulsion question through increasingly complicated conditional logic, she rejects the optimization frame.

The language function is correction:

> stop solving the hypothetical; tell me you would protect me.

Her voice becomes educational without becoming formal.

## 6.3 Given-name intimacy remains important but not sufficient proof of reciprocity

The established private `清隆` / `恵` world remains significant.

Given-name use marks:

- private relational space;
- intimacy;
- changed role;
- a world distinct from public surnames.

It does not prove:

- equal information;
- healthy dependency;
- conventional love on both sides.

The Year-2 relationship is precisely the case where linguistic intimacy and informational asymmetry coexist.

## 6.4 V12.5 — spoken control versus interior collapse

Kei's breakup language is a major stress case.

Internally she wants to:

- cling;
- beg;
- bargain;
- preserve the bond.

Outwardly she performs the independence she thinks Ayanokōji expects.

This is not evidence that the socially fluent Kei was fake.

It is evidence that social self-control remains available even at severe emotional cost.

A simulation model should therefore support a split between:

- high private arousal;
- controlled public line selection.

## 6.5 Simulation constraints

Do not write Kei as:

- permanently shrill;
- only gyaru slang;
- instantly in tears under every threat;
- cured of dependency because she can function socially;
- or reverted to a wholly different “true” timid voice in private.

The same `あたし` can carry status, fear, intimacy, anger, care, and self-command.

# 7. Kushida Kikyō — public cushioning survives the collapse of secrecy

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-KUSHIDA`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 7.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Kushida is the canonical demonstration that register is an instrument of social power.

Her public language uses:

- cushioning;
- friendliness;
- remembered personal detail;
- inclusive engagement;
- affective responsiveness.

Her hostile/private register is sharper, contemptuous, and less buffered.

Year 1 already rejected the simplistic conclusion that one register is “fake” and the other is “real.”

Both are organized capacities.

## 7.2 V05 collapses the public/private architecture, not the underlying skill

Exposure destroys the old equilibrium.

What disappears is the ability to maintain:

> perfect public warmth / hidden hatred

as separate social worlds.

What remains:

- memory;
- rapid interpersonal calibration;
- public fluency;
- status awareness;
- capacity to perform institutional friendliness when useful.

Year 2 therefore does **not** support writing post-exposure Kushida as universally abrasive.

## 7.3 V06 supplies the new canonical self-authored declarative register

The line:

> `私は私のためだけに１年半戦ってクラスに貢献する。`

is one of the most important voice lines in Year 2.

It is:

- first-person emphatic;
- self-interested;
- declarative;
- anti-redemptive;
- politically clear.

The repetition of `私` is not a generic personality catchphrase.

In context it insists on motive ownership.

She will contribute.

She refuses to pretend the motive is affection.

## 7.4 Council/public competence becomes chosen performance

By V09, Kushida can use socially skilled public language inside the student council without restoring the old counterfeit innocence.

This is a key reconstruction rule:

> **performed politeness can remain authentic skill after the performance's innocence is gone.**

She can be helpful and self-interested simultaneously.

## 7.5 Simulation constraints

Do not write mature Year-2 Kushida as:

- redeemed sweetheart;
- permanently snarling “true self”;
- incapable of public charm;
- secretly affectionate toward Horikita because they cooperate.

The distinctive Year-2 voice is **functional coexistence without counterfeit reconciliation**.

# 8. Hirata Yōsuke — `僕`, inclusive care, and the ability to name a limit

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-HIRATA`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 8.1 Frozen baseline

Hirata's ordinary `僕` is:

- gentle;
- explanatory;
- inclusive;
- de-escalatory.

Year 1 showed that this register is both:

- ethical care;
- defensive role organization.

Under crisis it can break.

## 8.2 Year 2 makes the same gentle voice less jurisdictional

V05–V06 repeatedly force Hirata to live through decisions he would rather prevent.

The development is not that he stops caring.

It is that care becomes less equivalent to:

> I must personally preserve everyone.

His language increasingly supports:

- mediation;
- bounded disagreement;
- trust in another actor's domain;
- continued relation after an outcome he dislikes.

## 8.3 V12.5 — `越権行為` as mature dissent vocabulary

Hirata's characterization of the Maezono decision as near `越権行為` is important.

The normally conciliatory speaker can name overreach without becoming Ryūen-like or adversarial in style.

This is a mature extension of his voice:

> **gentleness can contain constitutional accusation.**

## 8.4 Simulation constraints

Do not make Hirata:

- endlessly agreeable;
- unable to judge others;
- a therapist cliché;
- or suddenly harsh when he disagrees.

His mature dissent should usually remain:

- reasoned;
- relationally aware;
- low-theater;
- explicit about impact.

# 9. Sudō Ken — blunt `俺`, reduced impulsive violence, increased metacognition

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-SUDO`  
**Confidence:** `A/B`

Sudō's voice remains blunt and masculine.

Development does not turn him into a polished intellectual speaker.

The Year-2 change is primarily in **what happens between impulse and utterance/action**.

He increasingly:

- asks for advice;
- identifies what he does not know;
- names goals realistically;
- notices other people's problems;
- accepts correction;
- transfers lessons forward.

V06's anger-regulation sequence is the strongest behavioral analogue.

Onodera supplies a delay technique.

Sudō can keep the same basic roughness while no longer allowing the first aggressive impulse to dictate the next act.

After romantic rejection, his speech does not collapse into entitlement.

This is important for reconstruction:

> **maturity reduces impulsive domination without erasing bluntness, competitiveness, athletic confidence, or embarrassment.**

# 10. Ryūen Kakeru — roughness, amusement, coercion, and increasingly sophisticated contract language

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-RYUEN`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 10.1 Frozen baseline

Ryūen's speech is:

- rough;
- invasive;
- amused;
- physically threatening;
- proprietary;
- rhetorically totalizing.

His language often tries to make a local dominance relation sound like a general law of people.

## 10.2 Development does not make him linguistically democratic

Year 2 gives Ryūen:

- more contracts;
- more recruitment;
- more delegation;
- more willingness to update models;
- more voluntary follower legitimacy.

His register remains recognizably Ryūen.

This matters.

A leader can become more sophisticated without adopting the language of consensus.

## 10.3 Contractual precision joins, rather than replaces, intimidation

The Horikita alliance and Katsuragi role show that Ryūen can operate through explicit bounded terms.

He does not need every agreement to be sentimental.

His most characteristic mature speech can combine:

- mocking confidence;
- hard condition-setting;
- respect for competence;
- willingness to honor a useful bargain.

## 10.4 Meaningful victory becomes sayable

By V12.5, Ryūen refuses to treat the official outcome against Sakayanagi as the victory he actually wanted.

This is a deep change in evaluative vocabulary.

Earlier Ryūen could treat a politically secured result as sufficient.

Late Year 2 can distinguish:

- official result;
- substantive defeat;
- future proving field.

The voice remains rough while the concept of victory becomes more demanding.

## 10.5 Simulation constraints

Do not write Ryūen as:

- constant profanity without analysis;
- a shouting thug;
- secretly soft in a way that erases coercion;
- politely reformed.

His humor and menace should coexist with real political cognition.

# 11. Ichinose Honami — inclusive warmth acquires declarative and adversarial force

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-ICHINOSE`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 11.1 Frozen baseline

Ichinose's voice combines:

- colloquial warmth;
- public-command fluency;
- questions that invite participation;
- apologetic cushioning;
- inclusive orientation.

Her Year-1 problem is not lack of language.

It is the risk that apology and accommodation become overused when she experiences moral shame or leadership failure.

## 11.2 Year 2 preserves warmth while expanding strategic opacity

The class does not become Ryūen-like in order to become more adversarial.

By V09, Ichinose's class can allow an opponent to believe sabotage is working.

The voice therefore gains an important political possibility:

> friendliness need not equal strategic transparency.

## 11.3 V12 shows stress collapse, not the “true dark voice”

Ayanokōji deliberately attacks the trust structure supporting Ichinose's cognition.

Her breakdown and apology-heavy return in V12.5 should not be treated as revealing a hidden weak essence.

It is a stress register produced by:

- betrayal;
- exhaustion;
- romantic injury;
- leadership failure;
- manipulated information.

## 11.4 V12.5 third answer — soft voice can carry counter-authorship

The terminal development is not “Ichinose becomes cold.”

It is:

> **inclusive relational language acquires the ability to impose a new answer.**

She rejects the binary Ayanokōji prepared.

She can claim reciprocal use, set a relational condition, and become the person whose answer forces his model to update.

This is stronger than merely speaking more harshly.

## 11.5 Simulation constraints

Do not write late Year-2 Ichinose as:

- naïve sunshine;
- permanently broken;
- “yandere/dark Ichinose” shorthand;
- or a Ryūen imitation.

Her mature force should remain recognizably relational.

# 12. Sakayanagi Arisu — refined politeness remains predatory even when vulnerability becomes real

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-SAKAYANAGI`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 12.1 Frozen baseline

Sakayanagi's speech is:

- controlled;
- refined;
- feminine;
- playful;
- often formally polite.

The content can be brutal.

Her linguistic signature is the refusal to make severity sound crude.

## 12.2 Year 2 increases personal stakes without replacing the register

Kamuro's expulsion, Yamamura's hurt, love for Ayanokōji, and eventual withdrawal make Sakayanagi more relationally legible.

She does not need a colloquial voice change to prove vulnerability.

The emotional development occurs inside:

- continued poise;
- deliberate wording;
- strategic framing.

## 12.3 V12 — inferred speech and relational authority

The phrase Sakayanagi reconstructs from Ayanokōji's clue field is:

> `ここで負けてくれ`

The important point for this ledger is not to turn that into an Ayanokōji catchphrase.

It is the opposite:

Sakayanagi understands an **unspoken relational message**.

Her voice and his become linked by inference.

This is a high-level address phenomenon:

> intimacy can reduce the need for explicit language while increasing the power of inferred language.

## 12.4 Simulation constraints

Do not write Sakayanagi as:

- excessively archaic princess speech;
- generic “ojou-sama” parody;
- casually slangy once vulnerable;
- or always emotionless.

Her severity and tenderness are usually strongest when delivered through control.

# 13. Kōenji Rokusuke — theatrical self-mythology, noncompliance, and own-answer rhetoric

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-KOENJI`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 13.1 Frozen baseline

Kōenji speaks theatrically.

His register assumes:

- his own exceptional status;
- the scene as performance;
- amused superiority;
- low obligation to ordinary consensus.

The voice is openly noncompliant.

## 13.2 Year 2 turns theatrical autonomy into explicit philosophy

The island bargain formalizes the difference between:

- capability;
- obligation.

V10 deepens his distinction between:

- genuine unsolicited benefit;
- strategic gift intended to purchase later compliance.

V12.5 develops an “own answer” philosophy: absorbing every existing answer can undermine the opportunity to discover something for oneself.

The theater therefore contains a serious epistemic principle.

## 13.3 Simulation constraints

Do not reduce Kōenji to:

- comic narcissist;
- random non sequiturs;
- helpful eccentric;
- or hidden altruist.

His speech should preserve the premise that **he decides the terms on which the world becomes relevant to him**.

# 14. Shiina Hiyori — quiet pressure, cultural vocabulary, and direct preference without command

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-HIYORI`  
**Confidence:** `A`

## 14.1 Frozen baseline

Hiyori's voice is quiet and low-pressure.

Her book conversations with Ayanokōji are important precisely because they do not require:

- crisis;
- leverage;
- office;
- rivalry theater.

She can sustain relation through shared cultural interest.

## 14.2 Quietness is not indecision

Year 2 increasingly shows Hiyori able to:

- diagnose Ryūen's class-development problem;
- distinguish leader victory from class growth;
- disclose personally meaningful information;
- state a desire to spend time together;
- imagine cross-class graduation/friendship.

V09.5's disclosure that her father is the author of the book she gave Ayanokōji is particularly diagnostic.

The importance lies in **voluntary personal information**.

She is not forced into confession by crisis.

## 14.3 Intimacy register remains permission-based

Ayanokōji/Hiyori works as a countermodel because an invitation can remain:

- wanted;
- uncertain;
- refuseable.

Hiyori's voice should therefore not be made coy merely because romantic possibility exists.

Its core remains:

- cultural specificity;
- understated directness;
- room for the other person.

## 14.4 Simulation constraints

Do not write Hiyori as:

- whispering every line;
- endlessly shy;
- passive;
- or cryptically wise in every scene.

Her insight is often concrete and relational.

# 15. Nagumo Miyabi — casual hierarchy, self-authentication, and bounded rivalry

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-NAGUMO`  
**Confidence:** `A/B`

## 15.1 Frozen baseline

The Year-1 specialist identifies Nagumo's casual `～ッス` toward Manabu.

This register simultaneously performs:

- junior status;
- social ease;
- teasing familiarity;
- confidence inside hierarchy.

Important constraint:

> `～ッス` is relationship/register evidence, not a universal suffix to paste onto every Nagumo sentence.

## 15.2 Year 2 exposes a different interior voice

V09's opening monologue gives him a self-authentication crisis.

The public ruler who can sound socially easy asks whether his `実力` was ever properly tested or whether he became a `裸の王様`.

The contrast matters:

- public casual dominance;
- private doubt about the evidentiary value of dominance.

## 15.3 V11 — rivalry becomes more bounded

The final exchange-camp proving field is self-financed and does not extract third-party pooled resources.

This supports a late voice that can still be provocative while being less institutionally possessive.

## 15.4 Simulation constraints

Do not make Nagumo:

- constant bro-slang;
- only smug;
- or suddenly humble.

He can recognize a genuine validation problem while retaining high confidence.

# 16. Nanase Tsubasa — `私` / `ボク` as explicit self-authored psychological technology

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-NANASE`  
**Confidence:** `A`

Nanase is the most important new Year-2 pronoun case.

## 16.1 V01 flags the distinction before explaining it

`Y2V01-E035` records a momentary movement from `ボク` to `私` during self-introduction.

At the V01 boundary, this is evidence.

It is not yet a diagnosis.

## 16.2 V03 explains the architecture locally

Nanase's prologue explicitly distinguishes:

- a fragile `私`;
- a `ボク` created to carry the justice/aggression she cannot sustain otherwise.

The key logic is:

> `『私』の脆い心では、その『正義』を支えることは出来ない。`
>
> `だから『ボク』がそれを支える。`

Ayanokōji later rejects the separate-supernatural-person explanation and interprets `ボク` as a trigger into an aggressively performed self.

The ledger therefore classifies the switch as:

`PERFORMED_IDENTITY_WITH_BEHAVIORAL_EFFECT`

not:

- simple lying;
- ordinary gendered pronoun preference;
- or sufficient evidence for a psychiatric diagnosis.

## 16.3 The performance is causally real

The `ボク` register changes what Nanase can do.

That is the literary point.

A performed identity can produce:

- different aggression;
- different bodily willingness;
- different self-authorization

without becoming a separate ontological person.

## 16.4 Simulation constraints

A reconstruction should never randomly alternate `私` and `ボク`.

The switch requires:

- psychological state;
- motive;
- self-authorization context.

# 17. Amasawa Ichika — playful senior-directed speech and the instability beneath the tease

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-AMASAWA`  
**Confidence:** `B`

Amasawa's Year-2 textual presence supports a recognizable contrast between:

- playful;
- provocative;
- socially invasive;
- senior-directed speech

and a later, more exposed dependency around Ayanokōji/Yagami/White Room loss.

Her surface ease should not be confused with emotional invulnerability.

V11's Ayanokōji-to-Nagumo message for her is important because it does not prescribe a new identity; it reopens a future.

The later voice model should preserve the possibility that teasing remains available even when the underlying relation to school has become more serious.

Do not infer acoustic seductiveness from textual teasing.

# 18. Yagami Takuya — polished public competence and catastrophic register collapse

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-YAGAMI`  
**Confidence:** `B`

Yagami is a strong public/private contrast case.

The public layer supports:

- polished competence;
- student-council usefulness;
- socially credible helpfulness;
- disciplined presentation.

V07 demonstrates that this layer can coexist with:

- fixation;
- covert violence;
- comparative grievance;
- White Room identity pressure.

When the convergence scene strips away control, the public register can no longer contain the underlying fixation.

The analytical mistake would be to say:

> the polite Yagami was fake, therefore only the breakdown voice was true.

The better model is:

- polished public speech was real operational competence;
- it was not a complete account of motive.

# 19. Hōsen Kazuomi — rough command as sovereignty

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-HOSEN`  
**Confidence:** `B`

Hōsen's language belongs to direct coercive sovereignty.

The stable tendencies are:

- imperatives;
- intimidation;
- physical threat;
- contempt for negotiated equality;
- direct valuation of strength.

His voice is useful as a contrast with Ryūen.

Both can be rough and coercive.

Ryūen increasingly uses:

- information;
- contract;
- delegation;
- political result.

Hōsen's Year-2 function remains more immediately embodied and dominating.

Do not collapse them into the same “delinquent” idiolect.

# 20. Kanzaki Ryūji — earnest dissent becoming constitutional speech

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-KANZAKI`  
**Confidence:** `B`

Kanzaki's important Year-2 voice development is not toward charisma.

It is toward **legitimate disagreement**.

V05 gives isolated dissent.

V07–V09 turn that dissent into an effort to create an internal second cognitive center in Ichinose's class.

His speech should therefore preserve:

- earnestness;
- dissatisfaction;
- strategic concern;
- lack of Ryūen-style theatrical domination.

He increasingly speaks as someone trying to make criticism compatible with belonging.

# 21. Hashimoto Masayoshi — social ease, performed uncertainty, and insurance language

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-HASHIMOTO`  
**Confidence:** `B`

Hashimoto's voice should be modeled around social adaptability.

He can sound:

- easy;
- curious;
- nonthreatening;
- open to multiple sides.

V10's monologue establishes the interior invariant:

> assume betrayal is possible; insure against being the victim.

V11 then shows he can **perform uncertainty** after a betrayal serious enough that he must already possess a survival model.

This gives a strong reconstruction rule:

> **his public uncertainty can be genuine in degree while also serving optionality.**

Do not make him a snake who hisses his intentions.

His danger is precisely that brokerage can sound socially ordinary.

# 22. Yamamura Miki — quiet existence becoming self-assertable

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-YAMAMURA`  
**Confidence:** `A/B`

V11 gives Yamamura one of the clearest first-person identity lines in late Year 2:

> `それでも───私は確かにココに存在している。`

Her voice is organized around social under-registration.

The development is not toward loudness.

It is toward:

- permission to state hurt;
- expectation of hesitation from someone who claims she matters;
- willingness to communicate rather than remain invisible.

This is a particularly important simulation constraint:

> **self-assertion does not require changing a quiet idiolect into a forceful one.**

# 23. Ibuki Mio — blunt antagonism as stable social relation

**VOICE_ID:** `VOICE-IBUKI`  
**Confidence:** `B`

Ibuki's speech remains:

- blunt;
- confrontational;
- minimally cushioned;
- quick to challenge.

Her Year-2 relation with Horikita demonstrates that this can become **familiar antagonism** rather than simple hostility.

The school-trip play is useful precisely because neither character has to verbalize sentimental friendship for the relationship to become richer.

Do not make Ibuki affectionate merely because she remains present.

# 24. Adult voices — office, grievance, and political authorship

This ledger does not attempt a full adult-cast idiolect dictionary, but three voices require boundary notes.

## 24.1 Chabashira Sae

Her teacher register remains institutional and controlled.

Year 2 increasingly permits:

- historical self-implication;
- vulnerability around her own failed class;
- differentiated care for current students.

The register does not need to become maternal to show development.

## 24.2 Hoshinomiya Chie

Her lighter social presentation must not be treated as evidence that the underlying grievance is light.

V12's monologue demonstrates adult fixation capable of converting friendly/teasing surfaces into cover for severe competitive intent.

A future specialist should audit her public playful register versus adversarial interior more completely.

## 24.3 Atsuomi Ayanokōji

Volume 0 gives sustained first-person political consciousness.

His rhetoric repeatedly converts:

- exploitation into realism;
- domination into competence;
- preference manipulation into superior political technique;
- human material into project resource.

The sushi/curry/gyūdon model is representative of his conceptual style:

> the stronger ruler does not simply satisfy declared desire; he controls the field in which a different outcome becomes acceptable.

His voice is useful as a **genealogical contrast** with Kiyotaka.

Do not make father and son sound identical merely because they share environmental-authoring structures.

# 25. Relationship-conditioned address and naming

Address is one of COTE's strongest relational signals, but it must remain contextual.

## 25.1 Given-name use is relational evidence, not proof of love

The Year-1 `清隆` / `恵` transition remains the clearest example.

It marks a private relational world.

Year 2 demonstrates why this cannot settle ethical questions.

Given-name intimacy can coexist with:

- dependency;
- hidden curriculum;
- major nondisclosure;
- predetermined separation.

## 25.2 Surname retention can coexist with deep relation

Ayanokōji/Horikita does not need a romanticized naming shift for the relationship to become central.

Their relation is built heavily through:

- repeated judgment;
- challenge;
- delegated authority;
- mutual recognition;
- later care.

The corpus should resist fandom-style overreading of one naming convention as a complete intimacy scale.

## 25.3 `senpai / kōhai` language is institutional and relational at once

Amasawa, Nanase, Yagami, Nagumo, and other cross-year actors use hierarchical address inside relationships that may contain:

- teasing;
- rivalry;
- manipulation;
- genuine admiration;
- threat.

Honorific form therefore tells us the social frame.

It does not settle the motive.

## 25.4 Titles can temporarily dominate personhood

`生徒会長`, class labels, teacher titles, and representative roles can replace personal address when institutional function matters.

Year 2 repeatedly tests whether the person can speak outside the office.

# 26. Public/private voice is not a true-self binary

The completed corpus supports four distinct public/private structures.

## 26.1 Deliberate concealment

**Ayanokōji**

Public underexpression conceals scale.

The interior is broader.

## 26.2 Socially acquired performance that became genuine competence

**Kei**

Public gyaru fluency is a real skill.

Private fear does not invalidate it.

## 26.3 Compartmentalized social mask whose underlying capacity survives exposure

**Kushida**

Public cushioning is strategic and real.

Private contempt is strategic and real.

After exposure, the capacities recombine.

## 26.4 Polished public competence concealing incompatible hidden motive

**Yagami**

The public register is operationally genuine while biographical/motivational truth remains hidden.

The universal rule is:

> **“performed” does not mean “unreal.”**

# 27. Stress-register taxonomy

Stress changes voice in diagnostically different ways.

## `FRAGMENTATION`

Speech becomes incomplete, repeated, hesitant, or physically interrupted.

Typical relevance:

- fear;
- grief;
- acute relational loss.

## `REGISTER_COLLAPSE`

A maintained public style can no longer contain the hidden state.

Primary case:

- Yagami at exposure.

## `MASK_PRESERVATION_UNDER_STRESS`

The speaker continues a socially functional outward register despite severe private distress.

Primary case:

- Kei at breakup.

## `COLD_COMPRESSION`

Stress reduces speech toward procedural/action language rather than emotional overflow.

Primary case:

- Ayanokōji in high-stakes operations.

## `DECLARATIVE_HARDENING`

A normally cushioning speaker becomes more direct without becoming linguistically different.

Primary cases:

- mature Ichinose;
- Kushida's self-owned compact;
- Hirata naming overreach.

Do not use one generic “angry voice” state.

# 31. Voice profiles for behavioral reconstruction

When generating novel situations later, use a two-stage procedure.

## Stage A — choose the **social state**

Examples:

- public class meeting;
- private one-on-one;
- intimate relationship;
- institutional negotiation;
- low-stakes leisure;
- acute threat;
- grief;
- leadership crisis.

## Stage B — choose the **voice mode permitted by that state**

Example:

### Horikita at a class meeting

Use:

- full sentences;
- explicit judgment;
- reason-giving;
- bounded authority.

Do not automatically use:

- teasing Ibuki register;
- vulnerable care register.

### Kei with Satō

Use:

- faster peer informality;
- social calibration;
- more horizontal disclosure.

Do not automatically use:

- romantic reassurance-seeking used with Ayanokōji.

### Ayanokōji under threat

Use:

- concise speech;
- wider interior analysis.

Do not speak the full analysis aloud.

This state-first procedure is more faithful than writing from generic personality adjectives.

# 32. Voice-confidence discipline

A voice claim should receive `A` when:

- repeated across multiple volumes;
- explicitly described by the Year-1 specialist;
- or supported by a highly diagnostic self-reference/register event.

`B` when:

- repeatedly observable but not yet exhaustively indexed;
- or based on fewer Year-2 scenes.

`C` when:

- tempting but dependent on one utterance;
- especially for secondary characters.

This ledger deliberately keeps several secondary voices at `B`.

It does not create unsupported catchphrases to make them feel more distinctive.

# 33. Claim transition ledger — Year 1 → Year 2

## PRESERVE

1. Ayanokōji's `オレ` narration contains multiple genuine registers.
2. Flat public delivery does not imply absent interior.
3. Horikita's development occurs inside a stable polished feminine register.
4. Kei's `あたし` social voice is authentic competence, not disposable mask.
5. Kushida's public cushioning is real social skill even where strategically used.
6. Hirata's `僕` is inclusive and relationally political.
7. Ryūen's roughness is compatible with sophisticated reasoning.
8. Sakayanagi's politeness can intensify rather than soften threat.
9. Kōenji's theatricality is part of his autonomy politics.
10. Nagumo's casualness can coexist with hierarchical power.
11. Address changes are relational evidence, not automatic romance proof.
12. Voice profiles are models, not total people.

## STRENGTHEN

1. **Stable register can contain genuine development.**
2. **Public performance can survive exposure because the capacity underlying it remains real.**
3. **Declared desire is an important voice marker of self-authorship.**
4. **Ayanokōji's strongest developmental shift is often modal/permission-based rather than lexical.**
5. **Relational knowledge can reduce the need for explicit speech while increasing inferred-language power.**
6. **Counter-authorship can be audible as the ability to state an answer not supplied by the stronger actor.**
7. **Stress must be modeled as register deformation rather than personality replacement.**
8. **Low-pressure scenes are indispensable for voice reconstruction because high-stakes dialogue over-selects strategic language.**

## REVISE

1. **“Kushida's true voice is the hostile one.”**  
   → Both public cushioning and hostile private language are organized capacities; Year 2 recombines them after exposure.

2. **“Kei's gyaru voice is fake.”**  
   → It is acquired but genuine social competence.

3. **“Ayanokōji gets warmer.”**  
   → Too coarse. He gains more participant-mode speech acts while strategic compression remains fully available.

4. **“Horikita softens.”**  
   → Better: she becomes more corrigible, collaborative, and capable of care inside a stable precise register.

5. **“Ichinose turns dark.”**  
   → Reject the binary. Her relational voice acquires adversarial opacity and declarative force without becoming a separate persona.

6. **“Nanase changes pronouns because she has two literal personalities.”**  
   → V03 locally frames `私` / `ボク` as a self-authored psychological trigger with real behavioral effect, not sufficient proof of separate ontological identity.

7. **“Nagumo talks casually because hierarchy is weak for him.”**  
   → His casualness can be an expression of confidence backed by hierarchy.

## DOWNGRADE

1. Isolated sentence-final particles as personality proof.
2. One honorific change as decisive relationship state.
3. English-translation slang as Japanese idiolect evidence.
4. Generic “cold/warm” axes.
5. “Polite = kind.”
6. “Rough = unintelligent.”
7. “Quiet = passive.”
8. “Friendly = sincere about motive.”
9. “Private = true; public = fake.”
10. “Stress outburst = baseline voice.”

## REJECT

1. Written Japanese voice can determine seiyū acoustics.
2. Ayanokōji should always speak in long analytical monologues.
3. Horikita must lose feminine formality to demonstrate closeness.
4. Kei needs a different first-person pronoun to reveal authenticity.
5. Post-exposure Kushida loses social fluency.
6. Ryūen's development should make him polite.
7. Sakayanagi's vulnerability should make her casual.
8. Hiyori's insight should make her oracular.
9. Kōenji's theatricality is merely comic filler.
10. Nanase's `ボク` can be randomly inserted for flavor.
11. “Dark Ichinose” is an adequate late-Year2 voice model.
12. Every relationship should be represented through progressive honorific reduction.

# 36. Canonical through-Year-2 voice synthesis

The completed Year-2 corpus does not show a cast gradually becoming linguistically homogeneous as they mature.

It shows the opposite.

Development becomes more convincing because it remains **idiolect-preserving**.

Horikita learns to ask, care, negotiate, and admit uncertainty without ceasing to sound like Horikita.

Kei becomes more horizontally capable without ceasing to speak through the `あたし` identity that helped her survive and organize a social world.

Kushida loses the ability to keep her public and private selves institutionally separate, then proves that the social competence attached to the public register did not disappear with the secret.

Hirata learns that care does not grant jurisdiction over every outcome and can name overreach without abandoning gentleness.

Sudō learns delay, self-reflection, and peer-generative responsibility without becoming verbally polished.

Ryūen learns contracts, recruitment, delegation, and a more demanding idea of victory without learning democratic diction.

Ichinose learns strategic opacity and counter-authored demand without becoming a separate “dark” persona.

Sakayanagi learns grief, love, and futurity without losing the refined language through which she has always exercised force.

Hiyori becomes more direct without becoming loud.

Kōenji becomes more philosophically legible without becoming less theatrical.

Nagumo's public ease remains compatible with private doubt about whether the field ever truly tested him.

Nanase demonstrates the reverse possibility: a deliberately constructed register shift can itself become a psychological technology that changes what the speaker can do.

And Ayanokōji remains the central formal experiment.

The same `オレ` can:

- worry about a yogurt maker;
- fear an ordinary invitation;
- notice a kiss;
- calculate another person's behavioral probability;
- understate his causal authorship;
- recognize a smile he did not choose;
- define a stopping rule;
- and later violate the spirit of that rule without issuing a direct command.

His development is therefore not audible as a replacement voice.

It is audible as **more things becoming sayable, thinkable, and personally attributable inside the same voice**.

That is the ledger's terminal principle:

> **A mature character voice is not a fixed set of verbal tics. It is a stable linguistic identity with a changing range of permitted acts: what the person can ask, admit, refuse, joke about, threaten, conceal, name, and allow another person to answer.**

For character reconstruction, the practical corollary is:

> **Preserve the idiolect first. Then model state, relationship, role, stress, and development as transformations inside it.**

That is the canonical Japanese written-voice boundary through Year 2 / *Second List*.
