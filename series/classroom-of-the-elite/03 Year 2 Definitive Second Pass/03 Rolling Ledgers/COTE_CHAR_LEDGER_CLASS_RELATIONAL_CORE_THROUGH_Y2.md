# Relational and class-infrastructure core
## Canonical character ledger through Year 2 / Second List

**Primary individual members:** Karuizawa Kei, Kushida Kikyō, Hirata Yōsuke, Sudō Ken, Shiina Hiyori, Kanzaki Ryūji, Hashimoto Masayoshi.

**Collective longitudinal structure:** the Ayanokōji Group rupture and successor social core — Hasebe Haruka, Miyake Akito, Yukimura Keisei, Sakura Airi — plus selected supporting nodes whose Year-2 function becomes analytically indispensable, especially Satō Maya, Ishizaki Daichi, and Onodera Kayano.

# 0. Ledger function

This ledger is the structured Year-2 boundary home for students whose importance is not adequately captured by the categories **protagonist**, **formal leader**, or **rival sovereign**.

They are the people through whom a class or relationship actually becomes livable:

- social coordinators;
- mediators;
- friends;
- dissenters;
- tutors and learners;
- informal diagnosticians;
- brokers;
- betrayed or expelled members;
- peer teachers;
- cross-class companions;
- and successor groups that continue after an earlier organizing relationship breaks.

The governing architecture correctly treats this as **relational and class infrastructure**, not a miscellaneous supporting-cast bucket. The central analytical question is not "which side character became more important?" It is:

> **What social capacities continue functioning when the person, secret, romance, leader, or group identity that originally organized them changes or disappears?**

This distinction prevents several recurrent errors.

First, **relationship state is not identical to character state**. Kei can become more socially autonomous while remaining emotionally dependent in romance. Kushida can become a functional class and council actor without becoming affectionate toward Horikita. Haruka can abandon indiscriminate sabotage without forgiving Airi's expulsion. Hiyori can diagnose Ryūen's developmental failure without possessing authority to correct him.

Second, **class function is not identical to formal office**. Hirata's legitimacy, Kei's social circulation, Sudō's peer-generative development, Kushida's interpersonal access, and Hiyori's quiet correction can be politically important even when none of them is sovereign.

Third, **survival after role change is diagnostically stronger than persistence inside the original role**. The Ayanokōji Group matters most when it survives only by ceasing to be the Ayanokōji Group. Satō matters because Kei's horizontal friendship remains available when the romantic dyad becomes destabilizing. Airi matters because expulsion removes institutional membership without exhausting her capacity to author a future.

Fourth, **social competence is morally non-self-interpreting**. Kushida and Hashimoto are both socially perceptive. One increasingly turns exposed capacity toward bounded cooperation; the other repeatedly converts relationships into insurance and optionality. Capacity is real in both cases. Its political and ethical use differs.

Fifth, `Second List` measurements are calibration rather than ontology. OAA can describe observable school performance, but it does not contain the relationship-specific intelligence through which Kei coordinates girls, Hiyori diagnoses a leader's succession problem, Hashimoto rapidly integrates strangers, or Airi changes Haruka from outside the school.

The final Year-2 relationship specialist will eventually provide the full multi-dyadic synthesis. This ledger instead asks, character by character and social structure by social structure:

- what the Year-1 boundary actually established;
- what Year 2 genuinely develops;
- what Year 2 merely reveals;
- which older inferences must be corrected;
- what role changes survive;
- what new dependencies appear;
- what remains unresolved;
- and what a future character model must not flatten.

# 1. Comparative Year-2 boundary thesis — from a "class core" to plural social infrastructure

The Year-1 class core could still be imagined too easily as a set of useful people orbiting stronger centers: Ayanokōji, Horikita, Ryūen, Ichinose, Sakayanagi, formal class competition.

Year 2 progressively makes that picture inadequate.

The strongest characters in this ledger become important precisely when they stop being reducible to the person who first activated, protected, evaluated, or organized them.

**Kei** is the clearest split case. Romance deepens vertical dependency on Ayanokōji at the same time her horizontal social structure becomes more independent. Satō becomes a best friend who knows the bullying past and can morally criticize Ayanokōji from outside the romantic dyad. Kei can continue studying, coordinate peers, preserve public function, seek counsel, and manage social fallout even when the romance is destabilized. At the Year-2 boundary, the correct statement is neither "Kei became independent" nor "Kei remained a parasite." It is:

> **behavioral and horizontal autonomy grow while emotional dependency and informational asymmetry remain severe.**

**Kushida** undergoes a more radical role conversion. The old public/private equilibrium collapses in V05. The socially perfect mask can no longer function through secret compartmentalization. Yet exposure does not destroy the underlying capacities. Memory, access, quick interpersonal calibration, social labor, and status-seeking remain. V06 gives her a self-authored reason to cooperate; V07 shows her acting independently for the class; V09 gives her a council role whose value she can appropriate as her own. The Year-2 endpoint is not redemption. It is **functional coexistence without counterfeit reconciliation**.

**Hirata** develops more quietly but in a politically important direction. His Year-1 universalism was both virtue and trauma defense: he wanted no one excluded because exclusion threatened to reproduce the social catastrophe that shaped him. Year 2 repeatedly confronts him with cases where universal rescue is impossible. He does not cease caring. He becomes better able to remain functional while another legitimate actor makes a painful decision, to mediate without claiming ownership of every outcome, and to name overreach when leadership crosses jurisdictional limits. His particular-attachment problem remains only partially answered.

**Sudō** supplies the cleanest positive model of development escaping its original motive. What begins as discipline partly organized around Horikita's approval becomes self-owned study, athletic pacing, anger regulation, social judgment, and eventually peer-generative teaching/help. By V09 his development persists after romantic rejection and begins reproducing itself in weaker classmates. He is therefore one of the corpus's strongest demonstrations that a developmental relationship succeeds when the learner no longer needs the original reward.

**Hiyori** moves from quiet correction into a high-value form of relational and political diagnosis. She can maintain cross-class friendship without treating competition as hatred, disclose something personally meaningful without making the disclosure transactional, recognize hidden motives in conversation, and identify the difference between a Ryūen victory and a class that actually became more capable. Yet diagnosis is not jurisdiction. `Y1H-026` remains open because she has not shown that her moral or developmental analysis can bind Ryūen's conduct.

**Kanzaki** makes dissent structural. Year 1 left him as a possible loyal opposition inside an unusually high-trust polity. V05 shows isolated dissent without enough legitimacy to change the class. V07 begins the reform coalition. V09 turns that coalition into a "second brain" containing disagreement even within the opposition itself. He does not become an anti-Ichinose sovereign. His success is narrower and more constitutional: **criticism can become part of belonging rather than evidence of disloyalty**. `Second List` simultaneously prevents triumphalism by showing that the reform faction has not yet solved the class's durable point-conversion problem.

**Hashimoto** is the negative control. He is highly socially capable, adaptive, observant, and courageous enough to act under uncertainty. His political diagnosis of Sakayanagi's fiduciary misalignment is not foolish. His weakness is that he lacks a relationship in which another person has standing to correct him before his model becomes irreversible action. Trust is translated into insurance, people into exit options, and cooperation into secured collateral. His Year-2 arc demonstrates that **social intelligence without answerable trust can produce extreme relational optionality but weak epistemic correction**.

The **Ayanokōji Group** then supplies the collective proof. Airi is expelled. Haruka becomes openly hostile. Akito follows Haruka from particular loyalty. Keisei remains. The group does not recover by restoring the hidden strategist to the center. It survives only when Haruka, Akito, and Keisei reconnect and Ayanokōji consciously refuses re-entry. Airi, from outside ANHS, becomes a counter-author whose new future changes what loyalty to her means for Haruka.

The strongest Year-2 class-relational thesis is therefore:

> **Social infrastructure becomes mature when a bond, skill, grievance, or group can survive role change without requiring its original organizer to remain sovereign.**

That standard does not demand independence from all dependence. COTE repeatedly rejects such a fantasy. It asks for something more realistic and more demanding:

- plural supports rather than one monopolized support;
- motives the actor can own;
- permission to remain angry or dissenting;
- relationships that survive new information;
- abilities that can be taught onward;
- and social structures that continue after the person who once seemed indispensable becomes unnecessary.

# 2. Karuizawa Kei

## 2.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

The frozen Year-1 corpus established Kei as a character whose **social power and private dependency are both real**.

Her ability to manage atmosphere, female-group status, rumor, access, and social circulation is not a façade in the sense of being unreal. It is competence. At the same time, the private model through which she understands survival is organized around `寄生虫` / parasite and `宿主` / host. The false relationship with Hirata initially supplies safety through social status. Ayanokōji later becomes the more powerful protector.

The rooftop sequence had already made a decisive correction to any totalizing host theory. Kei had an available exit through betrayal and did not take it. She accepted continued suffering rather than give Ryūen the answer that would expose Ayanokōji. That did not erase trauma dependency. It proved that survival optimization did not exhaust her capacity for commitment.

The Year-1 endpoint therefore contained two simultaneous possibilities:

1. romantic/physical protection could deepen an exclusive vertical dependency;
2. wider social ties and growing refusal capacity could let Kei participate in dependence without becoming wholly owned by it.

Year 2 answers both at once.

## 2.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

### Romance deepens dependency instead of simply curing it

Year 2 does not support the common simplification that dating Ayanokōji naturally transforms protection dependency into healthy reciprocity.

The relationship becomes more emotionally important. Ordinary dating, physical intimacy, jealousy, reassurance, domestic routine, gifts, and shared time are real. Kei is not pretending to care. Ayanokōji is not merely pretending that the relationship exists.

But the romance also concentrates emotional regulation in him.

By V08–V09 the project has enough evidence to distinguish **vertical dependency** from **horizontal autonomy**. Ayanokōji can become Kei's central psychological pillar while she remains socially competent outside him. V09 makes the distinction especially clear because conflict with him produces severe private distress without erasing her ability to study, appear in class, manage her public role, think about repair, and seek Satō's judgment.

The correct boundary claim is not "Kei functions, therefore she is independent." Function can coexist with major attachment distress.

### Satō becomes the decisive horizontal counterweight

V07 is the cleanest positive development in Kei's social architecture.

Ayanokōji decides the moment at which Kei will disclose almost all of her bullying history to Satō. That makes the disclosure environment ethically compromised by his control over timing. But Satō's answer is not his to own.

Satō does not abandon Kei after receiving socially dangerous information. The narration explicitly treats the friendship as moving from ordinary friendship to `親友`.

The significance is structural:

`romantic vertical dependence -> disclosure risk -> peer acceptance -> horizontal support that does not depend on the boyfriend`

That support later proves active rather than ceremonial. In V09, Kei consults Satō during conflict with Ayanokōji. Satō independently judges his behavior and tells Kei he is wrong. The romantic dyad therefore acquires an external moral witness who is loyal to Kei rather than to the developer's model of Kei.

### Kei can teach Ayanokōji an answer his optimization does not generate

V06 gives Kei one of her most important active contributions to Ayanokōji's ordinary-life development.

She asks whether, if she had low OAA, little social value, and no friends, he would have expelled her. He initially answers by building increasingly elaborate counterfactual conditions. Kei rejects the optimization frame. The answer she wants is simply that he would protect her.

Ayanokōji recognizes the lesson through the formulation that in such a relationship `そこに理屈なんかは不要` — reason need not be the basis.

The scene matters because Kei is not simply a subject being taught. She becomes the teacher of a non-instrumental relational grammar.

Yet V06 immediately preserves the asymmetry. When Kei asks him not to keep secrets, his internal inventory makes clear that she lacks decisive truth about his family, White Room origin, class-transfer project, wider relationships, and the meaning he assigns to romance.

### The "romance curriculum" is a revelation about Ayanokōji, not a negation of Kei's lived relationship

V09.5 is devastating precisely because it reveals that the two participants have not been inhabiting the same informational object.

Ayanokōji recognizes that he has completed many behaviors associated with romance without acquiring the emotional state he hoped the behaviors would produce. He understands Kei as the person through whom he has learned a romance curriculum, explicitly exploits the dependency that has grown, and can imagine trying another person if this relationship does not teach him love.

That is strong evidence against **current irreplaceability in his internal romantic architecture**.

It is not evidence that Kei's feelings are fake, that the shared experiences did not happen, or that every instance of care was insincere. The ethical problem is asymmetrical authorship: Kei participates in a romance while lacking the information that Ayanokōji is also treating the romance as a designed learning environment.

### Breakup proves behavioral capacity but not informed relational freedom

At the terminal fiction boundary, the relationship ends.

Kei attempts to perform the independence she believes Ayanokōji expects. She refuses a fuller explanation in acute distress, manages the public story, and retains social function. Privately, however, the loss is severe. V12.5 preserves bodily and habitual residue, including the ordinary domestic traces of the relationship.

Ayanokōji also protects her public standing by allowing the socially useful story that Kei dumped him. That is genuine care in effect. It does not repair the earlier disclosure deficit.

The final handoff answer to `Y1H-009` is therefore negative: **the romance ends without enough information for fully informed reciprocal choice**.

At the same time, `Second List` refuses to treat the breakup as instant emotional compartmentalization. Its relationship summary explicitly describes her as an ex-girlfriend who has not fully moved on.

The Year-2 endpoint is thus deliberately split:

> **Kei can act after separation before she has emotionally completed separation.**

# 3. Kushida Kikyō

## 3.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Year 1 established Kushida as a character whose social gift and destructive use of social intimacy must never be collapsed into a "fake versus real self" binary.

Her public accessibility is skilled labor:

- remembering personal details;
- listening even when conversation is unrewarding;
- shortening social distance;
- creating trust;
- maintaining broad network access;
- performing friendliness under emotional cost.

Her private hostility, recognition hunger, witness-elimination behavior, and desire to remove people who know the hidden self are also real.

The Year-1 questions were therefore not "which self is authentic?" They were:

- can public and private selves coexist without secrecy requiring witness destruction?
- can hatred coexist with legitimate cooperation?
- can Horikita's integration project become Kushida-authored rather than merely imposed?

Year 2 answers all three, but only after an ethically catastrophic route.

## 3.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

### V05 destroys the old equilibrium

The Unanimous Vote Examination makes secret compartmentalization unsustainable.

Ayanokōji exposes Kushida's middle-school history, non-aggression arrangements, betrayal, collaboration, and repeated attempts to remove him and Horikita. Kushida retaliates by weaponizing classmates' secrets. The "universally kind classmate whose hostile self must remain unknown" cannot be restored afterward.

This is not merely humiliation. It changes the structural problem.

Before V05:
`coexistence = two social registers protected by secrecy`

After V05:
`coexistence = one known person whose contradictory registers must share a public social field`

That is a far harder but potentially more legitimate form of coexistence.

### Horikita's preservation is independent but not consensual

Horikita's decision to preserve Kushida is one of Year 2's strongest demonstrations of independent Horikita judgment. It is not yet a positive Kushida development.

Kushida does not authorize the project. She wants expulsion rather than being converted into a salvaged class asset. Airi bears the irreversible cost after the class has locked itself into the expulsion route.

Any later success must therefore preserve the distinction:

> **the ongoing life Kushida later authors can become legitimate without retroactively legitimizing the imposed preservation that kept her in school.**

### V06 converts the ongoing project into self-owned cooperation

This is the decisive Kushida transition.

Horikita stops demanding gratitude or class-love and tells Kushida to use her rare capacities for **herself**. Kushida responds:

`私は私のためだけに１年半戦ってクラスに貢献する。`

The compact is intentionally anti-redemptive. Kushida will fight and contribute for herself, not because she has forgiven Horikita or fallen in love with the class. Their left-handed handshake preserves hostility as part of the agreement rather than lying about it.

This is one of the cleanest Year-2 examples of **legitimate cooperation without affective purification**.

### V07 proves the compact can generate independent action

Kushida investigates Haruka and Akito because their threatened departure/sabotage endangers the class future that now overlaps with her own chosen future.

She also ends the Yagami relationship on terms she owns and threatens reciprocal disclosure if he tries to use her.

The important point is that Horikita does not need to script every contribution. The preserved person's capacity has become operationally self-directed.

### V09 turns integration into an institutionally chosen role

Horikita's student-council recruitment is a second test.

Kushida initially proposes humiliation as the price of participation. Horikita is willing to kneel, but refuses to treat humiliation as a sufficient mechanism for purchasing a useful officer. She instead frames the council through things Kushida herself can value: recognition, influence, status, and a renewed Class-A route.

Kushida chooses the role because she can appropriate the reason.

That closes the old handoff question at the Year-2 boundary:

> **the Kushida project has become Kushida-authored in its continuing form.**

The qualification remains permanent: Kushida did not author the V05 decision that preserved her.

### `Second List` confirms capacity persistence after interface collapse

The guidebook is especially useful here because it separates capacity from moral interpretation.

Its Kushida profile maintains unusually high quick-thinking/social contribution and explicitly notes that the underlying social facility persists after public exposure changes her interface and motives. The formal file therefore corroborates what the novels already demonstrated: public-mask collapse did not delete the underlying ability.

This is a `GENUINE_DEVELOPMENT + CONFIRMED_CONTINUITY` pattern, not a revelation that the old public competence was fake.

## 3.3 Changes since frozen Year-1 boundary

### CONFIRMED_CONTINUITY

- exceptional memory and interpersonal access;
- strong recognition/status motive;
- capacity to weaponize private information;
- hostility toward Horikita remains real;
- social labor remains effortful rather than effortless moral sweetness.

### GENUINE_DEVELOPMENT

- hostile/private and public/cooperative registers function in the same known social environment after exposure;
- accepts a self-interested class compact rather than demanding either expulsion or restored secrecy;
- contributes independently when class survival aligns with her chosen future;
- accepts student-council responsibility for reasons she can own;
- becomes usable class infrastructure without counterfeit friendship.

### REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_HIDDEN_STATE

V05 makes hidden middle-school and Year-1 conduct public to classmates. For the **reader**, much of that material was already known; for the **class**, this is a major character-knowledge transition. The ledger must distinguish revelation to characters from new reader knowledge.

### RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION

Volume 0 does not materially rewrite Kushida's origin. Post-V00 environmental-authorship analysis does, however, sharpen the distinction between **being preserved by another person's project** and later appropriating that preserved possibility as one's own.

### CORRECTION_OF_PRIOR_INFERENCE

- **Reject:** "exposure destroys Kushida's usefulness." It changes the interface, not the underlying capacity.
- **Reject:** "cooperation proves reconciliation." The cooperation is explicitly compatible with continuing hatred.
- **Reject:** "Horikita saving her was consensual because Kushida later thrives." Later self-authorship does not retroactively authorize V05.
- **Revise:** "public Kushida was simply fake." The friendliness was performed, but the skill required to produce it was real.

### RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC_EXPRESSION

- **Horikita:** adversarial integration; hatred, recognition, utility, and negotiated cooperation coexist.
- **Ayanokōji:** mutual caution; he accepts her real nature more readily than most, but he also helped expose and politically reorder her.
- **classmates:** the social network becomes a field where people know more of her contradiction yet still rely on her abilities.
- **student council:** an institutional role that converts recognition hunger and social competence into a self-chosen public function.

### UNRESOLVED_CONTRADICTION

> **Kushida's social skill becomes safer for the class after exposure without becoming morally neutral, affectionate, or incapable of future coercive use.**

The achievement is not purification. It is making contradictory motives politically legible enough that cooperation no longer depends on a false total identity.


# 4. Hirata Yōsuke

## 4.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Hirata's Year-1 strength was broad social legitimacy.

He can:

- lower interpersonal temperature;
- speak to students across status divisions;
- coordinate ordinary class life;
- make anxious or marginal students feel included;
- provide public-facing stability;
- and absorb social friction that would otherwise escalate.

The same capacity is organized around an unresolved wound. Hirata's universalism is not merely "being nice." It is a response to earlier social collapse and the terror of being the person who watches exclusion happen again.

That produces a characteristic Year-1 danger:

> **If preserving everyone becomes the condition under which Hirata can remain morally intact, universal care may stop being care for actual people and become a defensive demand that no tragic distinction ever be made.**

The Class Vote crisis already cracked that structure. Hirata collapsed when the institution forced the class to name someone disposable. Ayanokōji's intervention helped him return, but the frozen handoff deliberately left open whether Hirata could choose particular attachments and bounded obligations without experiencing them as betrayal of everyone else.

## 4.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

Hirata's Year-2 development is quieter than Sudō's, Kushida's, or Kei's because it consists less in acquiring a new power than in **limiting the jurisdiction of an old virtue**.

### V01 preserves broad competence without making him sovereign

OAA-era legibility fits Hirata relatively well in one narrow sense: broad competence, social usefulness, and stable participation are visible to the institution.

But his actual importance remains relational rather than scalar. Horikita can distribute recruitment, social negotiation, and class coordination through Hirata precisely because she does not need to imitate his social style herself.

Hirata therefore functions as one of the first proofs that a stronger class does not need one leader to possess every useful interpersonal capacity.

### V05 is the decisive stress test — universal protection yields to responsibility

The Unanimous Vote Examination recreates the kind of structure most dangerous to Hirata.

His instinct remains to preserve everyone. But unlike the Year-1 Class Vote collapse, he eventually recognizes that his personal wish cannot legitimately impose a catastrophic class-point loss on everyone else once the class has entered an irreversible decision structure.

He remains distressed. He does not become utilitarian. He does not suddenly approve of sacrifice.

The development is narrower:

> **Hirata can remain morally opposed to an outcome while remaining functional inside a polity that must choose it.**

This is the difference between universal care as aspiration and universal rescue as personal jurisdiction.

### V06 redistributes mediation

V06 makes this constitutional maturation operational.

Hirata continues mediation work after Airi's expulsion but does not insist that Ayanokōji secretly solve every social fracture for him. Ayanokōji deliberately resists being the hidden help desk, and Hirata becomes one of the people through whom the class must learn to rely and be relied upon.

This does not mean Hirata becomes fully independent of Ayanokōji's judgment. He chooses to trust him despite the moral wound of V05. The important change is that trust does not make Ayanokōji the sole owner of Hirata's social labor.

### V10 — protection can be delegated without becoming moral abandonment

During the survival/elimination exam, Hirata does not try to personally absorb every protection decision or overturn Horikita's leadership because the distribution of danger pains him.

He accepts that the visible leader has bounded authority to allocate protection while he continues to function as social infrastructure.

Again, this is not evidence that he has stopped valuing universal safety. It is evidence that he can distinguish:

- **my moral preference**;
- **the leader's legitimate decision domain**;
- and **my continuing responsibility to people after the decision**.

### V12.5 — Hirata can name overreach

The Maezono aftermath gives Hirata an important answer-back function.

He identifies Ayanokōji's representative action as approaching `越権行為`: the representative's power under the exam rules does not automatically become sovereign moral jurisdiction to expel a classmate.

This is a significant Year-2 endpoint for Hirata because his earlier failure mode was often to convert care into an inability to choose. Here he does not need to own the decision to criticize the person who over-owned it.

He becomes a more constitutional moral actor:

> **care no longer requires controlling every outcome; it can also mean preserving standing to judge the outcome afterward.**

## 4.3 Changes since frozen Year-1 boundary

### CONFIRMED_CONTINUITY

- strong desire to prevent exclusion;
- unusually broad accessibility and social trust;
- aversion to class fracture;
- willingness to absorb emotional labor for others;
- tendency to treat interpersonal stability as a moral good in itself.

### GENUINE_DEVELOPMENT

- remains functional during unavoidable sacrifice rather than collapsing into total refusal;
- accepts bounded leadership by others without treating delegation as moral abandonment;
- performs mediation as one node in distributed class agency rather than as sole caretaker;
- can criticize leader overreach after the fact without demanding that the polity return to impossible unanimity;
- increasingly distinguishes care from jurisdiction.

### REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_HIDDEN_STATE

No major Year-2 revelation rewrites Hirata's known trauma structure. His development is primarily behavioral and constitutional rather than revelatory.

### RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION

The broader Year-2 authorship framework makes Hirata a useful positive counterpoint. He is powerful socially, but his better moments increasingly involve **not claiming ownership** of another person's answer. This should not be confused with passivity; restraint can itself be mature exercise of power.

### CORRECTION_OF_PRIOR_INFERENCE

- **Reject:** "Hirata is merely a pacifist who cannot tolerate conflict." He can remain inside conflict and continue functioning.
- **Revise:** "his kindness automatically makes him the healthiest relational actor." Universalism can itself become defensive and coercive if it denies the legitimacy of bounded decisions.
- **Revise:** "development means becoming willing to sacrifice people." His development is better described as tolerating that he cannot personally rescue everyone while retaining moral judgment.

### RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC_EXPRESSION

- **Horikita:** complementary public leadership; he increasingly accepts her authority without disappearing into it.
- **Ayanokōji:** trust mixed with knowledge that Ayanokōji may do what Hirata cannot; Year 2 raises rather than solves the ethical problem of delegating dirty work.
- **Kei:** former public-protection relationship demonstrates that social shield, romance performance, and genuine care can be distinct.
- **class as a whole:** Hirata's strongest domain; he remains one of the principal mechanisms through which a heterogeneous class stays socially inhabitable.

### UNRESOLVED_CONTRADICTION

`Y1H-034` remains `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED`.

Hirata has become better at allowing bounded, particular decisions to exist. The Year-2 boundary still does not provide a decisive test in which he chooses one particular attachment for himself and accepts that doing so may disappoint or exclude others.

## 4.4 Boundary modeling constraints

A future Hirata model should preserve the following:

- He will normally seek a low-harm, socially inclusive route first.
- He is highly sensitive to exclusion dynamics and signs that a class is turning one person into a scapegoat.
- He can accept another person's authority when he believes the process is legitimate.
- He may remain distressed after accepting a decision; acceptance is not emotional approval.
- He is more likely to challenge **jurisdictional overreach** than to seek personal dominance.
- His growth should not be written as cynicism. He does not become "realistic" by ceasing to care.
- Particular self-owned desire remains less developed than his capacity to care for the whole.

# 5. Sudō Ken

## 5.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Sudō entered the series as one of the clearest warnings against static merit judgment.

His early profile emphasized:

- exceptional athletic ability;
- low academic performance;
- impulsivity;
- anger;
- insecurity;
- high sensitivity to status and humiliation;
- and romantic fixation on Horikita as an important external motive.

Year 1 then made him the strongest **development-from-liability** case.

He improved because other people:

- held him accountable;
- protected him when appropriate;
- taught him;
- demanded effort;
- let him fail;
- and increasingly treated his strengths as something the class could use.

The frozen handoff question was whether that growth would remain permanently organized around Horikita's approval or become **self-owned and generative**.

Year 2 answers this strongly.

## 5.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

### Development survives the year boundary

V01 immediately establishes that Sudō's Year-1 gains are not temporary compliance.

OAA makes his physical excellence institutionally legible, but the more important fact is longitudinal: the school separately records change, and Sudō's improving conduct/academics are no longer well described by his initial reputation.

The point is not that the new file finally "discovers his essence." It is that the person has changed enough that the earlier file is stale.

### V05 — self-owned academic motive exists before the romantic structure ends

Before the Unanimous Vote Examination, Sudō has begun designing his own academic improvement and wants to raise the measurable result.

This matters because the progression has moved from:

`study because Horikita matters -> study because improved competence has become part of who he wants to be`

The motives overlap for a time. Development does not require a pure origin.

### V06 — peer learning replaces protagonist-centered development

V06 is Sudō's decisive distributed-agency volume.

When Ike and Shinohara fracture, Sudō:

- notices the problem;
- asks for limited advice;
- reasons about the social dynamics;
- and reaches the useful next action himself.

Ayanokōji explicitly refuses ownership:

`オレは何もしてない。おまえが自分で考えて自分で答えを出しただけだ`

followed by:

`そうやってクラスは回っていく`

The developmental meaning is unusually clean. The former "problem student" is now part of the infrastructure through which other problems are solved.

The sports festival then supplies an even stronger event.

Onodera teaches Sudō a simple technique for riding out the brief peak of anger. Under Hōsen's deliberate provocation, Sudō uses it, refuses the post-match fight, takes a punch without escalating, and recognizes Hōsen as an ugly mirror of his earlier self.

The immediate teacher is **Onodera**, not Ayanokōji or Horikita.

This is why the case belongs in a relational-core ledger:

> **mature development becomes peer-transmissible.**

### V09 — development survives romantic rejection

This is the strongest closure of the old motive question.

Sudō's pursuit of Horikita no longer supplies the same romantic future. Yet his study discipline does not collapse.

Instead, he begins helping academically weaker classmates. Development that once flowed toward him through stronger figures now flows outward through him.

The sequence can be written:

`externally motivated learner -> self-regulating learner -> peer learner -> peer developer`

That is one of Year 2's strongest `GENUINE_DEVELOPMENT` trajectories.

### V10 — mature development includes pacing, not only escalation

By V10 the danger is no longer "Sudō will not work hard enough." It is overtraining.

Ayanokōji's useful intervention is to advise sustainable pacing rather than intensification.

This is important because it corrects a simplistic developmental model in which more effort is always better. Sudō now possesses enough self-drive that the relevant skill is regulation.

### V12.5 — moral questioning becomes part of class citizenship

After Maezono's expulsion, Sudō asks whether a no-expulsion route to victory existed.

Ayanokōji's answer is that it was not impossible; it was less probable.

Sudō's question matters because the boy who once required the class to rescue him from his own behavior now asks the strategist to justify why another classmate's removal was necessary.

That is not proof of philosophical sophistication in the abstract. It is evidence of **moral membership**: he has acquired standing and willingness to question what the class does in the name of winning.

# 6. Shiina Hiyori

## 6.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Hiyori's Year-1 function was easy to underestimate because it did not resemble sovereign leadership.

She brought:

- quiet observation;
- literary affinity;
- low-pressure conversation;
- willingness to correct Ryūen;
- selective refusal;
- and a capacity to remain inside a coercive class without becoming cognitively identical to its leader.

The frozen handoff asked a precise question:

`Y1H-026` — **Can Hiyori morally redirect Ryūen?**

The question deliberately did not ask whether she can see his errors. She already could.

The test was whether her judgment could become a **binding change in his conduct or class constitution**.

Year 2 strengthens the diagnosis dramatically and leaves the jurisdictional test open.

## 6.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

### Ordinary cross-class relation becomes a political countermodel

Hiyori and Ayanokōji's book-centered relationship develops without crisis as its main engine.

V09.5 gives the clearest positive case.

Hiyori gives him a personally selected book, later reveals that the author is her father, and explains the disclosure through a simple relational motive: she wanted **him** to know.

This is important because the information is not:

- extracted;
- traded;
- used as protection currency;
- demanded as proof of loyalty;
- or converted into leverage.

Disclosure itself is the relational act.

Ayanokōji's later invitation works similarly. He tells Hiyori the desire is his: he wanted to invite her. He explicitly preserves her right to refuse.

The pair therefore supplies a useful local model of **declared desire + preserved refusal**.

That does not make the relationship perfectly symmetrical. It makes the purpose substantially more legible than in Ayanokōji's hidden-curriculum relationships.

### Competition does not own affection

Hiyori's Year-2 endpoint in V09.5 is one of the cleanest statements of relational plurality.

She wants them to graduate together despite remaining members of competing classes.

The point is not that class rivalry is fake. It remains institutionally real.

The point is:

> **competition can govern outcomes without monopolizing the meaning of the people competing.**

That proposition is central to this ledger. Social infrastructure can cross the borders created by class politics.

### V10 — Hiyori becomes a developmental diagnostician of her own polity

Ryūen wins the special exam.

Hiyori does not ask only whether the tactic was clever. She asks whether the victory produced durable `上積み` — developmental accumulation for the class.

Her diagnosis is constitutional:

- Ryūen's singular irregularity wins;
- that singularity can prevent classmates from developing their own winning capacity;
- a class can become stronger in outcome while more dependent in mechanism.

This is a sophisticated distinction between:

`leader performance` and `class development`.

It places Hiyori among the corpus's strongest non-sovereign political thinkers.

### V11 / `Second List` — the institution under-describes her

The exchange camp reveals fine-motor, craft, observational, and interpersonal capacities in contexts that OAA does not heavily reward.

`Second List` makes the under-description explicit. Its Hiyori profile combines a modest formal quick-thinking score with prose emphasizing quiet judgment, situational leadership among girls, stronger class commitment, and concern that excessive dependence on Ryūen may obstruct classmates' development.

The correct conclusion is not that OAA is "wrong." It is that Hiyori's most valuable cognition is often:

- situational;
- relational;
- low-visibility;
- exercised without formal command;
- and therefore only partly captured by institutional proxies.

### Diagnosis still does not equal jurisdiction

V11 provides the necessary negative control.

Ryūen responds to Tokitō's continued dissent with physical coercion. Hiyori has not made a rule, veto, or moral norm binding enough to prevent it.

The Year-2 endpoint is therefore:

> **Hiyori has become more important as the class's internal conscience and developmental diagnostician without yet becoming an institutional constraint on Ryūen.**

`Y1H-026` remains `STILL_OPEN`.

### RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION

Year 2 reframes Hiyori as more than "the gentle girl in Ryūen's class." Her gentleness is compatible with sharp institutional judgment. Low-intensity behavior should not be mistaken for low political cognition.

# 7. Kanzaki Ryūji

## 7.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Kanzaki ended Year 1 as the clearest potential **loyal opposition** inside Ichinose's solidaristic polity.

He could see a problem that many classmates could not or would not state:

- extraordinary trust can produce cohesion;
- cohesion can become pressure to agree;
- a humane refusal to sacrifice classmates can become strategic rigidity;
- and one beloved leader can become an epistemic bottleneck even when that leader is morally serious.

The Year-1 question was not whether Kanzaki could criticize Ichinose. He already could.

It was whether dissent could become:

- legitimate;
- socially survivable;
- distributed;
- and useful enough to strengthen rather than destroy the polity.

Year 2 answers that affirmatively at the level of **constitutional form**, while leaving competitive efficacy unfinished.

## 7.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

### V05 — correct diagnosis without enough legitimacy

The Unanimous Vote Examination gives Kanzaki an unusually harsh early test.

He alone votes YES on the expulsion-for-points question because he believes the class's automatic refusal to contemplate sacrifice reflects a dangerous normalcy bias and lack of competitive urgency.

His reasoning is not frivolous. The later Year-2 corpus confirms that Ichinose's class has a real conversion problem.

But V05 also shows why correct diagnosis does not grant sovereign authority.

Kanzaki cannot simply impose his strategic theory on a class whose members understand their no-expulsion solidarity as part of the purpose for which they seek Class A. Ichinose articulates that value directly: winning by destroying the class's constitutive reason for winning would empty the objective of meaning.

Kanzaki eventually cannot transform isolated dissent into a legitimate new constitution.

This is the frozen problem from which Year-2 reform grows.

### V07 — opposition becomes a project rather than a complaint

Kanzaki approaches Ayanokōji because he believes the class possesses enough individual ability to do better than its current trajectory.

Ayanokōji's intervention is ethically mixed. He helps identify the constitutional problem and gives Kanzaki/Himeno a method for finding other dissenting or quieter students, then declares that from that point onward the work is not his domain.

Whatever the hidden stage-setting, Kanzaki must still build the actual coalition.

Himeno is crucial because she demonstrates that apparent harmony contained preference suppression. Her dislike of compulsory togetherness had often been easier to hide than to express.

Kanzaki's problem therefore changes:

`I disagree with the class -> the class contains unrepresented differences -> opposition must create legitimate channels for those differences`

That is a constitutional upgrade.

### V09 — the reform cell becomes a "second brain"

V09 supplies the strongest answer.

The group around Kanzaki, Himeno, Watanabe, Amikura, and Hamaguchi begins functioning as more than a Kanzaki faction. Its goal is not to replace Ichinose with Kanzaki as another singular interpreter. It is to create **another cognitive center** around the leader.

The best evidence that the structure is plural is that the reform group itself contains correction:

- Hamaguchi can criticize Kanzaki's severity;
- Watanabe need not simply defer;
- Himeno's preferences remain distinct;
- members can share the diagnosis without sharing one personality.

This satisfies the core Year-1 test:

> **dissent becomes part of belonging rather than evidence that the dissenter must exit or seize sovereignty.**

`Y1H-021` is therefore `ANSWERED_BY_DEVELOPMENT`.

### Later Year 2 — loyal opposition does not become instant institutional success

The later class trajectory prevents overstatement.

Ichinose herself becomes much more strategically capable. Her class survives Ryūen's sabotage, performs sophisticated coordination, and reaches an extraordinary V12 individual/team strategic peak. Kanzaki's role therefore cannot be described as "the reformer who fixed Ichinose."

At the Year-2 boundary, `Second List` explicitly describes the reform faction as not yet fully realized and the class as still requiring greater distributed individual capacity.

That is important.

The positive answer is about **political form**:

- dissent exists;
- it has allies;
- it can speak without becoming treason;
- it creates another source of interpretation.

The open problem is **conversion**:

- does this plural structure produce enough durable strategic output across future conditions?

Year 2 does not complete that test.

## 7.3 Changes since frozen Year-1 boundary

### CONFIRMED_CONTINUITY

- strong Class-A motivation;
- willingness to challenge comforting consensus;
- comparative severity;
- concern that Ichinose's moral constitution may be strategically exploitable;
- loyalty to the class rather than simple hostility toward Ichinose.

### GENUINE_DEVELOPMENT

- moves from isolated dissenter to coalition builder;
- learns that reform requires social legitimacy, not only being correct;
- accepts criticism inside his own reform structure;
- becomes part of a distributed "second brain" rather than an alternative singular sovereign;
- proves loyal opposition can coexist with continued Ichinose leadership.

### REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_HIDDEN_STATE

Himeno and the reform coalition reveal that Ichinose-class harmony had contained more private difference than its surface suggested. This is partly a revelation about the **polity's hidden state**, not a sudden creation of dissent by Kanzaki.

### RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION

The Year-2 authorship framework clarifies why Kanzaki's success should not be credited solely to Ayanokōji's V07 intervention. Ayanokōji opens a route; the students must appropriate, revise, contest, and sustain it. The reform becomes meaningful where it stops being reducible to the outsider's plan.

### CORRECTION_OF_PRIOR_INFERENCE

- **Reject:** "Kanzaki is anti-Ichinose." His project is to make the class more capable while retaining the class's core identity.
- **Reject:** "because his V05 diagnosis had merit, he should have been obeyed." Correctness is not legitimacy.
- **Revise:** "loyal opposition means one designated critic." V09 develops a plural corrective structure.
- **Qualify:** "the reform solved the class." `Second List` explicitly keeps distributed competence and point conversion open.

### RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC_EXPRESSION

- **Ichinose:** criticism grounded in shared class purpose, not personal contempt.
- **Himeno:** essential co-creator of legitimate dissent; gives experiential form to the problem of harmony pressure.
- **Watanabe / Amikura / Hamaguchi:** turn opposition from a personality into a social structure.
- **Ayanokōji:** external catalyst whose involvement must be bounded so the reform can become class-authored.

### UNRESOLVED_CONTRADICTION

> **Kanzaki succeeds in making dissent structurally legitimate before the reform structure proves that it can consistently improve the class's institutional results.**

The polity has another brain. Whether it has enough distributed hands, initiative, and strategic reproduction remains a future test.

# 8. Hashimoto Masayoshi

## 8.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Hashimoto's Year-1 function was already that of an opportunistic broker inside Sakayanagi's centralized polity.

He watched:

- Ayanokōji;
- Ryūen;
- Sakayanagi;
- factional strength;
- private points;
- hidden ability;
- and the probability that any current patron would remain the best route to Class A.

His social intelligence made him useful precisely because he was not wholly absorbed into the sovereign's model.

The unresolved danger was whether that independence could become constructive correction or only **insurance politics**.

Year 2 gives the stronger negative answer.

## 8.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

### Social intelligence is real

Hashimoto should not be caricatured as a foolish traitor.

The exchange-camp material demonstrates rapid social integration with unfamiliar first-years. He can read status, generate conversation, probe without immediately exposing the probe, and make himself socially useful across groups.

Hiyori's V09.5 reaction to him is diagnostically useful: she quickly notices that his apparent question about Ayanokōji contains another question — what Hashimoto intends to **do** if the answer confirms Ayanokōji's importance.

That scene captures both sides of Hashimoto:

- socially sophisticated;
- purposefully acquisitive.

### V10 gives his political anthropology directly

Hashimoto's monologue removes the need to infer his deepest assumption.

He fundamentally distrusts people. He expects betrayal. The rational defensive rule is therefore:

> **betray first rather than wait to become the betrayed.**

Class-A graduation is the closest thing he has to a non-negotiable collective/institutional end. Leaders and relationships are evaluated partly by whether they preserve routes to that end.

This is why his betrayal of Sakayanagi is not simple greed.

He sees a genuine fiduciary problem.

Sakayanagi and Ryūen can care more about defeating worthy rivals than about the intrinsic value of Class-A graduation. Hashimoto contrasts this with the `熱意` he perceives in Horikita and Ichinose.

His diagnosis is analytically serious:

> **great governing ability is not identical to alignment with the governed group's declared end.**

### His remedy reproduces the defect in stronger form

Hashimoto supplies Ryūen with Sakayanagi's attack targets and protects himself with a large private-point escrow.

The escrow is perfect evidence of his relational logic. Even betrayal requires insurance against the partner in betrayal.

He wants Ayanokōji imported into the class. If Sakayanagi refuses, he imagines escalating against her and potentially taking control himself.

The constitutional contradiction is severe:

`diagnosis: singular sovereign insufficiently aligned with class goal`

`remedy: replace or subordinate that sovereign to an even more exceptional singular center`

Hashimoto sees leader-dependence and proposes **more concentrated leader-dependence**.

### V11 clarifies the missing structure — corrective trust

After the betrayal, Hashimoto presents himself to Ayanokōji as if uncertain about what to do.

Ayanokōji recognizes the performance: someone willing to plan this much betrayal does not arrive with no survival model.

The deeper weakness is then identifiable.

Hashimoto has almost no stable epistemic community in which another person possesses standing to tell him, before irreversible action:

- your model is wrong;
- your risk estimate is distorted;
- this relationship matters for reasons beyond optionality;
- or this action will destroy the very structure you claim to protect.

His strength and weakness are the same architecture.

Because he trusts no one, he is difficult to trap.

Because he trusts no one, correction arrives late.

### V12.5 — insurance infrastructure outlives the original faction

The transfer fund Hashimoto had accumulated eventually becomes part of the financing that allows Ayanokōji to enter the former Sakayanagi class. Other class members collectively consent to cover the remaining shortfall.

This is a striking Year-2 endpoint.

Hashimoto's financial/strategic preparation becomes useful to a polity after Sakayanagi's withdrawal. Yet the resulting rescue still imports another exceptional center rather than solving the succession problem from within.

Hashimoto's infrastructure survives; his underlying theory of salvation through the right singular person remains substantially intact.

Post-Volume-0 environmental-authorship analysis makes Hashimoto an instructive non-White-Room comparison. He also converts people into variables in a survival environment, but the corpus supplies no evidence of common institutional origin or conscious doctrinal relation. # 9. Ayanokōji Group rupture, Airi after ANHS, and the successor social core

## 9.1 Why the group belongs in a character ledger

The original Ayanokōji Group is not merely a friendship subplot.

It is one of the corpus's clearest tests of whether ordinary social ties survive contact with the concealed strategist.

The Year-1 group included:

- Ayanokōji Kiyotaka;
- Hasebe Haruka;
- Miyake Akito;
- Yukimura Keisei;
- Sakura Airi.

The group offered something structurally unusual for Ayanokōji:

- purposeless time;
- nicknames;
- routine companionship;
- low-stakes preference;
- and a social identity not reducible to class strategy.

That is why Airi's V05 expulsion is not just one student's removal. It attacks the proposition that ordinary friendship provides protected status outside the disposability calculus.

## 9.2 V05 — friendship does not exempt Airi from Ayanokōji's culling model

Airi has not betrayed the class.

Her current OAA and social protection are weak. Ayanokōji, needing a criterion after Horikita preserves Kushida, ranks current institutional value and identifies Airi as the most disposable choice.

He explicitly refuses to rely on speculative future development.

That decision is internally coherent under his criterion.

It is also a direct injury to the relationship architecture Year 1 had built.

Haruka understands this immediately. The hidden strategist who had spent ordinary time with Airi still uses intimate knowledge of her feelings to make the final vote possible.

The group learns that:

> **being a friend does not place a person outside Ayanokōji's terminal optimization.**

## 9.3 Haruka — grievance is not pathology

V06 and V07 are careful not to make Haruka's anger a defect the class must cure.

She correctly identifies morally relevant facts:

- Airi did not deserve betrayal;
- Ayanokōji used Airi's feelings;
- Horikita preserved Kushida by transferring the terminal cost elsewhere;
- class-point gain does not settle the moral meaning of the act.

Haruka initially moves toward destructive revenge and planned withdrawal with Akito. That is dangerous. It is also an intelligible attempt to remain loyal to a friend whose removal the class is already learning to treat as past business.

The healthier Year-2 development is not "Haruka admits the class was right."

It is:

> **Haruka revises what loyalty to Airi requires after Airi herself authors a future.**

## 9.4 Airi becomes a counter-author from outside the institution

V07's Airi/Shizuku material is one of the most important class-relational corrections in Year 2.

Outside ANHS, Airi:

- resumes a public identity;
- passes an audition;
- begins constructing a future;
- and addresses the friend still inside school through evidence that she is not socially or existentially annihilated.

Haruka realizes:

`退学した人は全員不幸になると決めつけてた。`

She had decided in advance that expulsion meant permanent unhappiness.

The correction applies to more than Haruka.

ANHS can decide:

- membership;
- class points;
- institutional rank;
- and who remains in the competition.

It cannot decide the total future of the removed person.

Airi therefore becomes a **counter-author from outside the institutional frame**.

Her post-expulsion growth does not retroactively justify the expulsion.

That distinction is non-negotiable.

## 9.5 Haruka's return does not require forgiveness

When Haruka returns and helps with the cultural festival, she explicitly says she has not forgiven everyone.

Horikita does not demand a false moral consensus.

This is a major class-infrastructure result:

> **a polity can continue with incompatible memories of the same injury.**

The class does not need Haruka to call V05 right.

It needs a way for Haruka to remain a classmate without being required to erase the moral judgment that makes her return difficult.

This is more mature than compulsory reconciliation.

## 9.6 Akito — particular loyalty over frictionless reintegration

Akito's role is often analytically underweighted because he does not produce the central speech.

His importance is relational.

When Haruka moves toward revenge and possible withdrawal, Akito follows because his attachment to her matters more than the convenience of immediate class unity.

He is not merely manipulated by Haruka. His loyalty is a choice about **particular obligation**.

Later, as Haruka changes course, he can remain part of the successor social structure without requiring the class to pretend the grievance never existed.

Akito therefore supplies a useful contrast to Hirata's unresolved universalism:

- Hirata worries about caring for everyone;
- Akito demonstrates that one particular person's pain can legitimately reorganize one's own action.

Neither principle is sufficient alone.

## 9.7 Keisei — continuity without centrality

Keisei carries continuity across the rupture.

He is not the cause of Airi's expulsion, not the primary avenger, and not the hidden strategist. That makes his presence important.

A successor social core needs members who can continue relation without needing to become the new sovereign of the relation.

Keisei's function after V07 is therefore less spectacular but structurally necessary: ordinary peer continuity.

## 9.8 The group survives only by ceasing to be the Ayanokōji Group

V07 gives the canonical endpoint.

Haruka, Akito, and Keisei begin reconnecting.

They look toward Ayanokōji.

He consciously refuses re-entry:

`5人だったグループは3人になってしまったが、以前よりも強い絆で結ばれていくことを願う。あの場所にオレの存在は必要ない。`

The five-person group has become three; he hopes their bond becomes stronger; his presence is not necessary there.

This is one of the most successful succession events in the Year-2 corpus because the social structure does **not** preserve itself by reinstalling the original center.

The answer to `Y1H-033` becomes:

> **yes, the bond can survive the concealed person — but only by becoming a different bond no longer organized around him.**

That is stronger than restoration.

Restoration would imply that the old structure was fundamentally adequate.

Reconstitution accepts that Airi's absence and Ayanokōji's role changed the social object permanently.

## 9.9 Collective delta classification

### CONFIRMED_CONTINUITY

- ordinary friendship had real value before the rupture;
- Haruka's attachment to Airi was particular and deep;
- Akito's loyalty is relationally particular;
- Keisei provides continuity;
- Ayanokōji values the group enough to want its survival.

### GENUINE_DEVELOPMENT

- Haruka can continue without forgiveness;
- Akito can support Haruka without permanently following her into destruction;
- Keisei participates in a successor group;
- Airi authors a future outside ANHS;
- the trio reconnects without reinstalling Ayanokōji;
- Ayanokōji recognizes that his presence is no longer required.

### REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_HIDDEN_STATE

V05 reveals to friends the practical limit of friendship's protected status inside Ayanokōji's strategy. The revelation is not that he never cared; it is that care did not override terminal optimization.

### RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION

Airi's later success forces revision of the meaning of expulsion without revising the historical legitimacy of the expulsion itself. The correct rule is:

`future success != retroactive authorization of imposed loss`

### CORRECTION_OF_PRIOR_INFERENCE

- **Reject:** "the Ayanokōji Group was fake because it broke." Its pain is evidence that the bond mattered.
- **Reject:** "survival means restoring the original group." The canonical success is successor formation.
- **Reject:** "Airi's post-school success proves she was correctly expelled." Institutional outcome and life worth are separate.
- **Reject:** "Haruka's recovery requires forgiveness." V07 explicitly preserves non-forgiveness.

### RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC_EXPRESSION

- **Haruka–Airi:** friendship strong enough for an expelled person to redirect the remaining friend's future from outside ANHS.
- **Haruka–Akito:** particular loyalty under grievance.
- **Haruka/Akito/Keisei:** successor triad.
- **Ayanokōji–group:** ordinary affection plus an irreversible limit on the protection friendship receives from his optimization.
- **Horikita–Haruka:** polity relation capable of continuing without agreement about the moral past.

### UNRESOLVED_CONTRADICTION

The successor structure is real at the Year-2 boundary, but its long-run Year-3 durability is outside scope.

The ledger can certify **reconstitution**, not future permanence.

# 10. Supporting social nodes

These characters do not require equal-length full profiles in this ledger, but Year-2 relational infrastructure cannot be modeled correctly without them.

## 10.1 Satō Maya — horizontal support and independent moral witness

Satō's Year-2 importance increases far beyond "former romantic candidate."

Her high-value functions are:

1. **dangerous-truth recipient** — she learns almost all of Kei's bullying history and remains;
2. **best-friend confirmation** — V07 explicitly promotes the bond to `親友`;
3. **care independent of romance** — Kei chooses time with Satō because Satō cared for her while she was ill;
4. **moral witness** — during Kei/Ayanokōji conflict, Satō judges Ayanokōji's conduct independently rather than serving his plan;
5. **exit-capacity support** — her existence makes Kei's social world less vertically monopolized.

Satō should therefore be modeled as a **horizontal autonomy node**, not merely a romantic triangle residue.

## 10.2 Ishizaki Daichi — rivalry without total antagonism

Ishizaki is important because he repeatedly demonstrates that cross-class rivalry need not own ordinary relation.

Across Year 2 he can be:

- loyal to Ryūen;
- socially friendly with Ayanokōji;
- part of leisure and travel interactions;
- connected to Hiyori's social field;
- and still committed to his own class.

His function is less philosophical than Hiyori's, but more socially ordinary.

Hiyori can articulate "we can be rivals and still want to graduate together." Ishizaki helps make that proposition **behaviorally normal**.

This matters because a White-Room-like total curriculum would tend to interpret every cross-group tie by its instrumental function. Ishizaki repeatedly supplies ties whose strategic and ordinary meanings coexist.

## 10.3 Onodera Kayano — peer pedagogy

Onodera earns a place in the ledger because one small interaction carries a large developmental implication.

She teaches Sudō the anger-delay technique that he successfully applies under Hōsen's provocation.

The important feature is **who teaches whom**.

A mature developmental system should not require all useful correction to descend from:

- Ayanokōji;
- Horikita;
- a formal teacher;
- or a designated genius.

Onodera demonstrates peer pedagogy.

The system becomes more robust when developed people can learn laterally from someone whose importance was not pre-certified by a hierarchy.

# 12. Role-change stress tests

Year 2 repeatedly demonstrates that the most useful measure of a relationship is what happens when its original role becomes unavailable.

## 12.1 Kei — host/false-romance history -> girlfriend -> ex-girlfriend

The role changes are not clean substitutions.

- Hirata's public protection role ends without making him irrelevant as a classmate.
- Ayanokōji becomes boyfriend without eliminating protector dependency.
- breakup ends formal romance before emotional residue disappears.
- Satō becomes more important as the exclusive romantic channel becomes less stable.

**Result:** role change is behaviorally survivable; emotional reorganization remains incomplete.

## 12.2 Kushida — trusted public mask -> exposed hostile classmate -> council actor

Exposure destroys the secrecy architecture but not the social ability.

**Result:** a social function can survive the death of the identity-performance regime that originally carried it.

## 12.3 Hirata — universal protector -> bounded stabilizer

Hirata increasingly permits legitimate decisions he would not personally choose and retains standing to criticize overreach afterward.

**Result:** relinquishing total rescue does not require relinquishing care.

## 12.4 Sudō — protected problem student -> self-owned learner -> peer developer

The original mentor/reward structure becomes unnecessary.

**Result:** strongest positive proof that development survives the original developer/motive.

## 12.5 Hiyori — quiet observer -> class diagnostician / cross-class friend

Her relational intelligence becomes more visible without turning into sovereign office.

**Result:** increased political importance need not take the form of increased domination.

## 12.6 Kanzaki — dissenter -> organized opposition

Criticism becomes distributed and correctable.

**Result:** loyal opposition is strongest where the opposition itself can be opposed.

## 12.7 Hashimoto — broker -> betrayer -> survival planner / transfer-finance residue

His role changes without changing the deeper prior that trust is dangerous.

**Result:** adaptability can be high while relational learning remains low.

## 12.8 Ayanokōji Group — five-person group -> rupture -> three-person successor group

The original name becomes analytically misleading after the rupture.

**Result:** survival occurs through reconstitution rather than restoration.

## 12.9 Airi — student -> expelled person -> Shizuku/public future

The institution loses jurisdiction over her trajectory.

**Result:** institutional exit can expand, rather than end, the set of possible identities.

# 14. `Second List` calibration — measurement, official framing, and the social person

`Second List` is especially useful for this ledger because it repeatedly demonstrates its own limits.

The guidebook gives OAA a formal definition and then supplies characters whose important social functions do not fit neatly inside the numbers.

The correct chain is:

`performed school behavior -> institutional observation -> recorded metric`

not:

`person -> total score`.

## 14.1 Kei — quick-thinking is real; dependency is not erased by competence

The guidebook's treatment of Kei preserves both sides.

Her social navigation among girls is real enough to appear in institutional assessment. Yet official retrospective framing also emphasizes that dependency, possessiveness, and jealousy intensified with romance and that the breakup does not instantly erase attachment.

The file therefore blocks two bad readings:

- high social competence does not prove emotional independence;
- emotional dependency does not prove low social competence.

## 14.2 Kushida — capacity is not morality

`Second List` gives Kushida strong quick-thinking/social contribution and explicitly separates extraordinary social facility from the destructive hidden conduct through which it had sometimes been used.

Known guidebook route:

- `Y2SL-E021` — extraordinary social facility versus hidden conduct;
- `Y2SL-E022` — OAA: B(72) academics, C+(60) physical, A-(82) quick-thinking, A(88) social, overall B(74);
- `Y2SL-E023` — social capacity persists after exposure changes interface and motives.

That is almost a direct formal statement of this ledger's Kushida thesis.

## 14.3 Hiyori — quiet judgment is institutionally under-described

Known route:

- `Y2SL-E032` — profile foregrounds observation, reading bond, dislike of conflict, stronger class commitment;
- `Y2SL-E033` — OAA: A(86) academics, D(28) physical, C-(42) quick-thinking, B(74) social, overall C(55);
- `Y2SL-E034` — prose notes situational leadership and concern that Ryūen-dependence may obstruct member development.

The discrepancy between the C- quick-thinking metric and the quality of her V10 institutional diagnosis is not proof that the metric is fraudulent.

It is proof that institutional proxies observe only some contexts.

## 14.4 Sudō — development makes old labels stale

Even without promoting one guidebook number into ontology, Sudō remains a canonical argument for longitudinal measurement.

The key analytical requirement is temporal:

> a file about Sudō must always ask **when** the observation occurred.

A measurement architecture that cannot represent development will misclassify him. The Year-2 system partly corrects that by maintaining changing observation, but the person can still develop beneath and between updates.

## 14.5 Hirata — legible competence still cannot encode trauma structure

Hirata is comparatively easy for an institution to read as "competent, social, reliable."

That description is not false.

It cannot, by itself, explain why forced exclusion can trigger disproportionate distress or why learning not to own every rescue is development.

Institutional legibility captures function more readily than **the private reason the function became necessary**.

## 14.6 Hashimoto — social generation does not imply trust

Exchange-camp behavior demonstrates that Hashimoto can rapidly integrate unfamiliar people.

A metric or observer could reasonably count this as social competence.

The character ledger must preserve a separate variable:

`ability to enter relationships != willingness to become answerable inside a relationship`

Hashimoto is high on the first and weak on the second.

# 15. Modeling constraints — prohibited simplifications

This section is binding for later character synthesis, speech/behavior reconstruction, and novel-situation simulation at the Year-2 boundary.

## 15.1 Kei

Do **not** model Kei as either "independent now" or "still only a parasite."

Required simultaneous state:

- socially capable;
- horizontally supported;
- behaviorally resilient;
- emotionally attached;
- information-disadvantaged;
- capable of refusal;
- and vulnerable to abandonment.

A novel-situation model should ask which dependency axis is being stressed.

## 15.2 Kushida

Do **not** model exposure as moral redemption or social neutralization.

Required state:

- classmates know substantially more;
- hostility can be overt;
- recognition motive persists;
- social skill remains elite;
- cooperation can be rational and legitimate without affection;
- secrets remain potentially coercive resources.

Her "public self" and "private self" should be treated as interacting registers, not two metaphysical persons.

## 15.3 Hirata

Do **not** reduce him to "nice pacifist."

Required state:

- highly competent mediator;
- trauma-sensitive to exclusion;
- increasingly capable of functioning through painful bounded decisions;
- willing to criticize overreach;
- still underdeveloped in particular self-preference relative to universal care.

## 15.4 Sudō

Do **not** freeze him at either the early delinquent stereotype or the purified "perfectly mature" endpoint.

Required state:

- direct;
- competitive;
- proud;
- much better regulated;
- self-motivated;
- teachable by peers;
- increasingly capable of teaching others;
- morally more engaged;
- still temperamentally intense.

## 15.5 Hiyori

Do **not** infer passivity from softness or low formal quick-thinking.

Required state:

- observant;
- relationally precise;
- capable of hidden-motive recognition;
- willing to state developmental criticism;
- low appetite for domination;
- not yet capable of making her correction binding on Ryūen.

## 15.6 Kanzaki

Do **not** model him as the "real leader Ichinose should have listened to."

Required state:

- serious dissent;
- real diagnosis of conversion weakness;
- limited initial legitimacy;
- coalition-building growth;
- increasingly plural opposition;
- continued need for Ichinose rather than simple replacement of her.

## 15.7 Hashimoto

Do **not** reduce him to greedy cowardice.

Required state:

- perceptive;
- socially skilled;
- high-risk tolerant;
- sincerely committed to Class-A outcome;
- structurally distrustful;
- insurance-seeking;
- prone to converting good diagnosis into unilateral, destabilizing action.

His major modeling deficit is **corrective trust**, not intelligence.

## 15.8 Ayanokōji Group successor structure

Do **not** write the original group as restored.

At the Year-2 boundary:

- Airi is outside ANHS;
- Ayanokōji does not re-enter the group;
- Haruka does not erase grievance;
- the meaningful survivor is the Haruka/Akito/Keisei successor bond.

The change of structure is the development.

## 15.9 Airi

Do **not** write post-expulsion success as proof that expulsion was good for her or ethically justified.

Required distinction:

`Airi authors a future after harm` is not the same proposition as `the stronger actors were right to impose the harm`.

## 15.10 Satō

Do **not** use Satō only as a romantic comparator.

By Year 2 she is one of the clearest proofs that Kei possesses a meaningful support structure not owned by Ayanokōji.

# 19. Boundary synthesis

Year 2's relational-core development can be summarized by a series of separations that **do not equal annihilation**.

Kei separates from the formal romance before emotional attachment fully separates.

Kushida separates social competence from the secret moral identity that once organized its use.

Hirata separates care from the need to own every rescue.

Sudō separates growth from the romantic approval that helped motivate its earliest form.

Hiyori separates class loyalty from cognitive submission to Ryūen.

Kanzaki separates dissent from disloyalty.

Hashimoto fails to separate independence from distrust — and thereby becomes the negative control.

Haruka separates continued class membership from forgiveness.

Akito separates particular loyalty from the demand for frictionless collective unity.

Airi separates institutional expulsion from total future failure.

The Ayanokōji Group separates social survival from restoration of its original center.

Satō separates support for Kei from deference to Kei's boyfriend.

Ishizaki and Hiyori separate rivalry from hatred.

Onodera separates pedagogical authority from formal hierarchy.

These are not variations of one simple "independence" arc.

They establish a stronger Year-2 principle:

> **Autonomy is not isolation. It is the capacity to remain in relationships, classes, rivalries, obligations, and dependencies without letting one role exhaust the person's available purposes or one stronger actor permanently own the answer.**

That formulation explains why some of Year 2's most successful developments remain dependent in ordinary senses.

Kei still needs people.

Sudō still learns from people.

Hiyori still belongs to Ryūen's class.

Kanzaki still follows a polity led by Ichinose.

Haruka still lives inside the class whose decision she condemns.

The achievement is not emancipation from relation.

It is **pluralization of relation**.

The class becomes stronger when:

- Kei has Satō as well as Ayanokōji;
- Kushida can cooperate without pretending love;
- Hirata can care without becoming sovereign;
- Sudō can receive and transmit development laterally;
- Hiyori can be loyal and critical;
- Kanzaki can organize dissent without replacing the leader;
- Haruka can remain without forgiving;
- Airi can matter from outside the institution;
- and a friendship group can continue after the person named by the group is no longer needed.

Hashimoto shows the converse.

A person can preserve many options and still be relationally trapped if every bond is interpreted as insurance and no one is trusted enough to correct the governing model.

The completed Year-2 character-core thesis is therefore:

> **The Year-2 relational core becomes strongest where social function survives role change without requiring the original organizer to remain sovereign. The mature alternative to dependency is not self-sufficiency; it is plural, answerable interdependence.**

That is the state this ledger hands forward to the full through-Year-2 relationship ledger and specialist synthesis.
