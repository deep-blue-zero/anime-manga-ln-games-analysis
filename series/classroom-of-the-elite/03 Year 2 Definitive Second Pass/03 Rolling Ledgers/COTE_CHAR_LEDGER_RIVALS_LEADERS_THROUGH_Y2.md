# Rival leaders and alternative elite models
## Canonical character ledger through Year 2 / Second List

**Primary members:** Ryūen Kakeru, Ichinose Honami, Sakayanagi Arisu, Kōenji Rokusuke, Nagumo Miyabi.

# 0. Ledger function

This ledger is a structured Year-2 boundary reference for the characters who function as the series' strongest alternative theories of leadership, ability, collective action, rivalry, or autonomy outside the Ayanokōji/Horikita dyad.

It is **not** the final Year-2 class-politics specialist synthesis. Class-wide institutional claims belong ultimately in `COTE_CLASS_POLITY_LEDGER_THROUGH_Y2.md` and `COTE_Y2_05_CLASS_POLITICS_LEADERSHIP_AND_CONSTITUTIONAL_DEVELOPMENT.md`. The purpose here is narrower:

- preserve each character's frozen Year-1 baseline;
- record what Year 2 genuinely changes in the character rather than merely revealing about them;
- separate individual leadership development from changes in the polity they lead or influence;
- distinguish sociological legitimacy, ethical legitimacy, strategic effectiveness, and institutional success;
- track whether followers can answer, constrain, or survive the leader;
- preserve the difference between exceptional ability and the willingness to make that ability collectively available;
- route `Second List` measurement/editorial evidence without treating OAA or official summaries as total character ontology;
- and give later synthesis/modeling work a compact set of high-confidence behavioral and political constraints.

The five characters should not be collapsed into a single ladder of "who is the best leader." Their Year-2 functions are structurally different:

- **Ryūen** asks whether an authoritarian ruler can acquire voluntary legitimacy without developing protected dissent or succession.
- **Ichinose** asks whether solidarity can learn adversarial competence without becoming a coercive polity, and whether a leader can become a counter-author rather than a developmental object.
- **Sakayanagi** asks whether aristocratic optimization can remain fiduciary when private desire becomes sovereign, and whether exceptional centralized judgment can reproduce itself after the sovereign leaves.
- **Kōenji** asks what obligations membership can legitimately impose on a person whose capacity is both exceptional and voluntarily unavailable, and whether refusing inherited answers can itself be a form of ability.
- **Nagumo** asks whether individual mobility can be genuine while the mobility mechanism remains centralized patronage, and whether rivalry can move from forced validation toward bounded relation.

# 1. Comparative Year-2 boundary thesis — from competing sovereigns to competing theories of independence

The frozen Year-1 political corpus ended with four incomplete constitutions and a series of handoff tests. Ryūen had learned that fear alone did not explain why people preserved him after defeat. Ichinose had proven the depth of solidaristic trust without yet proving that trust could resist adversarial exploitation. Sakayanagi had built the most efficient centralized polity while carrying a serious fiduciary risk in her private Ayanokōji rivalry. Kōenji had established that possessed talent is not automatically collective property. Nagumo had built a rhetoric of individual mobility without yet revealing whether the mechanism could exist outside personal patronage.

Year 2 does not flatten those differences. It makes them sharper.

Ryūen becomes more adaptive, more capable of contracts, recruitment, delegation, and meaningful rivalry. His followers increasingly have reasons to follow him that exceed fear. Yet the polity remains dependent upon him, and when Tokitō directly challenges his conduct, Ryūen still answers dissent through bodily coercion rather than a protected constitutional channel. His development is therefore real but non-redemptive: **greater legitimacy and greater sophistication coexist with persistent authoritarian jurisdiction**.

Ichinose undergoes the most dramatic leadership transformation. Her Year-1 solidarity survives. What changes is the assumption that solidarity must remain strategically transparent or defenseless. Her class learns to coordinate under observation, conceal resilience from Ryūen, and build an internal reform/"second brain" without replacing her with another sovereign. Ichinose herself stops treating desire only as shame, develops sharper adversarial boundaries, and ultimately gives the strongest Year-2 counter-authorship result: a third answer that Ayanokōji did not possess and *Second List* explicitly describes as exceeding his prediction. Her remaining problem is institutional conversion: **the polity becomes more capable without yet proving it can convert its capacities into enough durable competitive output**.

Sakayanagi becomes more relationally legible to herself at the same time her political constitution fails its hardest test. Kamuro's loss reveals that relational irreplaceability can exist before the optimizer's decision model has language for it. Yamamura forces recognition that service is not the same as being personally registered. Love for Ayanokōji becomes something Sakayanagi refuses to classify simply as weakness. Yet when the year-end choice arrives, she allows that private relation to override fiduciary duty to her class, voluntarily withdraws, imposes a 300-point penalty, and leaves a succession vacuum. Her personal self-authorship survives school defeat; **her polity does not reproduce the sovereign function that had made it excellent**.

Kōenji turns refusal itself into a coherent political and epistemic position. The island-exam bargain converts his relation to the class from undefined conflict into bounded obligation. Later he distinguishes genuine reciprocity from strategic gifts intended to purchase compliance. By the end of Year 2 he articulates an ability theory almost opposite to White Room maximal curriculum: he sometimes refuses to absorb every existing answer because preserving room to discover **his own answer** matters. That stance is powerful evidence against developmental capture, but it does not make him a moral ideal. **Autonomy, self-sovereignty, and generativity coexist with indifference to some weaker others and with a father's externally imposed condition on "true freedom."**

Nagumo receives the most important retrospective political clarification. His mobility regime genuinely creates opportunities that the old class system would deny. It also does so through centralized custody of points, obedience, ranking, revocability, and personal allocation. Year 2 therefore answers the Year-1 question by refusing the binary: **mobility and patronage are the same institutional mechanism, not competing descriptions of it**. His personal rivalry then moves in a more constructive direction. The final exchange-camp contest is self-financed and bounded, and Nagumo becomes capable of wanting future association with a rival rather than forcing the rival into his own political order.

The common Year-2 test is therefore no longer simply who can win.

> **The rival-leader corpus moves from competing sovereigns to competing theories of independence: can leadership create capable people without permanently owning the conditions under which those people remain capable?**

No character fully solves that problem. The differences among their failures are more analytically valuable than a scalar ranking.

# 2. Ryūen Kakeru

## 2.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

The Year-1 endpoint classified Ryūen as the clearest case of **centralized authoritarian mobilization**. He converted fear, retaliation, information monopoly, asymmetric tactics, and personal nerve into extraordinary coordination speed. The rooftop defeat then exposed a fact his own theory had underweighted: Ibuki, Ishizaki, Albert, and other followers could choose to preserve or continue following him even after the coercive sovereign had been decisively beaten.

The correct frozen conclusion was therefore already double:

- fear and coercion were genuine foundations of his rule;
- fear did not exhaust the relation between Ryūen and his followers.

Year 1 ended with increased **sociological legitimacy**, not democratic reform or moral absolution. Its handoff questions were correspondingly precise:

- can voluntary loyalty constrain the ruler rather than merely strengthen him?
- can Hiyori's correction become a binding moral limit?
- is Ryūen becoming a better leader, or merely a more sophisticated predator?

## 2.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

Ryūen ends Year 2 as a much stronger political actor than the Year-1 caricature "violent tyrant" can represent. He recruits expertise, signs bounded agreements, delegates, updates failed models, accepts that bodily dominance is not the only win condition, and increasingly values victories that prove something about his own developed capacity rather than simply any official result.

The strongest positive progression is:

`fear-based sovereign -> defeated but voluntarily preserved leader -> recruiter/delegator -> adaptive contract actor -> ruler seeking meaningful victory`

The strongest negative continuity is:

`coercive jurisdiction -> managed dissent -> still no protected follower veto -> leader-dependence remains developmental bottleneck`

These are simultaneous truths.

### Strategic adaptation becomes institutional rather than merely predatory

Y2V01 immediately preserves his Year-1 adaptive intelligence. In the cross-year pairing market he does not simply compete for the strongest students. He inflates price expectations, converts recruitment into information gathering, and uses the new OAA/partner economy as another field whose incentives can be altered.

Y2V02 makes the development more important by introducing Katsuragi. Ryūen is willing to spend enormous collective resources to acquire a form of institutional/defensive expertise his own polity lacks. This is not egalitarianism. It is nevertheless a meaningful move away from the assumption that all useful capacity should be generated by making current subordinates obey harder. Ryūen can now **import and preserve distinct expertise**.

The transfer also preserves a real consent boundary: Katsuragi must choose the move under school rules. Ryūen heavily authors the environment that makes the choice attractive, but the result is not literal purchase of an unwilling person.

### Leadership becomes more than physical sovereignty

Y2V04's Hōsen confrontation is one of the clearest corrections to the idea that Ryūen equates strength with winning a fight. Hōsen is physically superior in the immediate bodily contest. Ryūen nevertheless defines the political win condition differently: the emergency alarm keeps institutional intervention approaching, Ishizaki and Albert need not be sacrificed for his pride, Katsuragi can continue handling examination responsibilities, and Hōsen's apparent physical advantage does not grant him control over what the confrontation ultimately means.

Ryūen's Year-2 leadership is therefore increasingly **systemic** rather than merely bodily.

### Voluntary legitimacy becomes much harder to dismiss

V04.5 supplies the cleanest follower testimony. Komiya explicitly distinguishes the present Ryūen from the earlier ruler who treated classmates as disposable and says that the changed leader is someone he can now follow. Ryūen's personal visit to the injured Komiya gives the shift behavioral substance rather than leaving it as abstract praise.

This does not mean the polity has become consensual in every decision. It does mean the Year-1 post-rooftop loyalty was not a one-scene anomaly.

### Dissent becomes visible without becoming protected

Y2V05 is the strongest mixed case. During the Unanimous Vote Examination Ryūen uses false YES votes and allied coordination to identify the genuine dissenter. Tokitō can say, publicly, that Ryūen should step down. Ryūen threatens and humiliates him, but does not simply expel or permanently purge him when he has leverage to make life difficult. Katsuragi interprets the maneuver as preserving a route by which Tokitō can remain inside the polity.

This is best described as **managed dissent**.

It is development from a political order where disagreement was nearly synonymous with subordination or disposal. It is not a constitutional right to disagree.

Y2V11 supplies the negative control. Tokitō's continued moral opposition is answered through physical coercion and threatened pursuit. Mixed-year bystanders can interrupt the immediate incident, but Ryūen's own polity still lacks an internal rule that makes dissent safe enough to constrain the ruler.

Thus `Y1H-025` remains only `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED`, and `Y1H-026` remains `STILL_OPEN`.

### Rivalry becomes a self-authentication project

The late Year-2 shift is especially important. Ryūen has always valued strong opponents, but V12.5 shows that the meaning of victory itself has changed.

Sakayanagi defeats him on the merits of the year-end contest. The official outcome reverses only because she reconstructs Ayanokōji's preference and elects to lose. Ryūen explicitly refuses to treat that result as the victory he wanted. The distinction matters because his earlier political identity was close to "a win is a win if I can make it stick."

By the Year-2 boundary, he increasingly wants an outcome that **authenticates the person he has become**. Sakayanagi's disappearance therefore matters not only strategically but relationally: a rival whose existence gave his development a meaningful proving field is gone.

That does not make him non-instrumental. It makes the object of his instrumentality more demanding.

## 2.3 Changes since frozen Year-1 boundary

### CONFIRMED_CONTINUITY

- Coercion, threat, asymmetric information, and willingness to injure opponents remain available tools.
- Ryūen continues to treat competition as adversarial rather than consensual moral education.
- He remains unusually willing to exploit rule gaps, traitors, fear, and opponent miscalibration.
- Personal charisma and singular decision speed remain central to his polity.
- Hiyori remains a valued internal corrective voice without possessing demonstrated veto authority.

### GENUINE_DEVELOPMENT

- **Recruitment of complementary expertise:** Katsuragi is acquired because Ryūen recognizes capacity his existing political structure does not reproduce.
- **Delegation:** leadership increasingly survives temporary leader absence because Katsuragi and other followers can own bounded domains.
- **Contractual cooperation:** Horikita/Ryūen sports-festival and cultural-festival arrangements show he can operate through negotiated terms rather than domination alone.
- **Model updating:** after Ichinose's class defeats his V09 sabotage, he revises the threat model rather than preserving a falsified belief for pride.
- **Meaningful-victory standard:** V12.5 demonstrates that official victory is no longer sufficient if the opponent actually defeated him and then surrendered for another person's reason.
- **Voluntary follower legitimacy:** Komiya and the continuing Ishizaki/Albert/Katsuragi structure strengthen the conclusion that followers have positive reasons to remain.

### REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_HIDDEN_STATE

No Volume-0 revelation materially rewrites Ryūen's personal prehistory. The important Year-2 revelation is instead institutional/relational: continued follower loyalty makes it increasingly clear that the Year-1 fear model was always incomplete.

### RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION

The post-Volume-0 reader can compare Ryūen's environmental intimidation with broader Year-2 questions about authored choice, but the corpus does **not** establish that his method shares White Room genealogy or that he consciously operates from Atsuomi-like preference engineering. The useful comparison is structural, not biographical.

### CORRECTION_OF_PRIOR_INFERENCE

- **Reject:** "defeat reformed Ryūen into a benevolent leader." The Tokitō material directly blocks this.
- **Revise:** "followers remain because they fear him." Fear remains relevant, but V04.5 and later evidence establish genuine voluntary legitimacy.
- **Revise:** "Ryūen's strength is brute-force improvisation." Katsuragi recruitment, contracts, delegation, and strategic update demonstrate a broader institutional intelligence.

### RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC_EXPRESSION

- **Ayanokōji:** desired rival, benchmark, and increasingly part of Ryūen's own self-authentication project. Ayanokōji also wants Ryūen preserved as one of the four-class competitors, producing reciprocal strategic objectification.
- **Katsuragi:** complementary institutional expertise and one of the strongest proofs that Ryūen can value a subordinate precisely for being different from him.
- **Hiyori:** moral/strategic diagnostician whose influence is respected but not yet proven capable of binding the ruler.
- **Ishizaki / Albert / Komiya:** evidence that loyalty can survive defeat and become positively motivated rather than merely terror-based.
- **Tokitō:** stress test showing that dissent may exist socially without being constitutionally protected.
- **Sakayanagi:** rival whose loss matters because Ryūen wanted a victory with epistemic meaning, not only an official result.

### UNRESOLVED_CONTRADICTION

> **Ryūen becomes a more legitimate and adaptive leader by learning to preserve distinct capacities around him, but the more effective the class becomes under him, the more dangerous its dependence on his singular judgment becomes.**

The unresolved Year-2 question is not whether he can lead. It is whether the polity can become more capable **without making Ryūen even more irreplaceable**.

## 2.5 Measurement and downstream modeling constraints

`Second List` records Ryūen's March OAA as:

- academics C+ (59)
- physical B (71)
- quick-thinking B (70)
- social contribution E+ (18)
- overall C+ (60)

The official profile immediately warns against treating that file as the whole person: his leadership and strategic quality exceed what the axes represent (`Y2SL-E030-E031`). The low social-contribution score especially must not be confused with low **social causality**. He can produce enormous effects through intimidation, recruitment, incentive design, rivalry, and follower loyalty while remaining normatively poor by the school's prosocial axis.

### High-confidence modeling tendencies

- Defaults to adversarial interpretation sooner than Ichinose or Hiyori.
- Values nerve, useful dissent, and demonstrated competence more than politeness.
- Can revise quickly when evidence shows the opponent changed.
- Respects an actor more after meaningful resistance than after passive loyalty.
- Uses coercion readily when he believes it preserves sovereignty or exposes weakness.
- Increasingly distinguishes official outcome from meaningful victory.
- Can delegate when the subordinate possesses a genuinely differentiated skill.
- Is more relationally attached to rivals/followers than his own early fear theory admits.

### Common errors to avoid

- Do not model him as merely violent or unintelligent.
- Do not model his Year-2 development as moral redemption.
- Do not treat follower loyalty as proof coercion disappeared.
- Do not treat coercion as proof all loyalty is fake.
- Do not assume Hiyori currently possesses a moral veto; the corpus explicitly leaves that open.

# 3. Ichinose Honami

## 3.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

The Year-1 Ichinose endpoint was the strongest **solidaristic trust order** among the four classes. Her political assets were unusual cohesion, shared resources, personal accessibility, truth-surviving trust, and reluctance to sacrifice members. The weakness was a conversion problem rather than fraudulent kindness:

> norms designed for reciprocity were vulnerable to actors who did not reciprocate, and extraordinary social capital did not automatically become enough class points or adversarial leverage.

The Year-1 handoff therefore asked whether solidarity could defend itself **without ceasing to be solidarity**.

## 3.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

Ichinose gives the strongest positive answer among the rival leaders, but the answer is narrower than "her class is fixed."

The personal/leadership progression is:

`solidarity under shame -> solidarity as strategic capital -> crisis and leader-centered dependency -> self-authored prioritization -> adversarial opacity -> counter-authorship`

The polity progression is:

`high trust -> exploitable openness -> internal loyal opposition -> coordinated resilience -> distributed care -> still-incomplete point conversion`

### Solidarity becomes strategic before it becomes aggressive

Y2V01 already demonstrates that Ichinose's care has political force. She protects weak students in the cross-year pairing market, and that willingness to rescue creates trust and recruitment advantages that the more price-driven classes cannot reproduce in the same way.

Y2V02 makes the logic explicit in her negotiation with Sakayanagi. Sakayanagi correctly identifies `信頼` as an asset unique to Ichinose's polity. Ichinose does not simply accept the superior strategist's evaluation. She reads the deeper fear behind Sakayanagi's proposal—a durable lower-three-class alignment—pushes the rescue deposit upward, and begins planning the alliance. Solidarity is already becoming **strategically articulate**.

### Crisis reveals the danger of leader-centered solidarity

The island/confession sequence and V04.5 aftermath demonstrate that a highly relational polity can concentrate emotional cost in the leader. Ichinose's pain propagates through a class built around mutual concern. Nagumo can weaponize information precisely because the relationships are real.

The Year-2 answer is not to destroy the relational structure. It is to add cognitive plurality around it.

### Loyal opposition becomes a second brain

The Kanzaki/Himeno reform line is one of the most important constitutional developments in the series. Kanzaki begins from frustration that the class's harmony suppresses urgency and disagreement. Himeno gives the problem a social texture: silence can look like agreement when the cost of becoming the one who introduces friction is high.

By V09, the reform cell is no longer a one-man anti-Ichinose faction. Watanabe, Amikura, Hamaguchi, and others can join or contest the discussion, and the opposition itself contains opposition. The objective is not to replace Ichinose with Kanzaki. It is to create a **second cognitive center** capable of giving the leader information she does not already possess.

This is one of Year 2's strongest examples of leadership becoming more answerable without leadership disappearing.

### Adversarial competence arrives without moral inversion

V09 provides the locally sufficient answer to `Y1H-020`.

Ryūen attacks the class through a model built from its old vulnerabilities: visible distress should consume study time, relational density should magnify disruption, and the weakened leader should make the group strategically brittle.

The visible distress is real. The strategic conclusion is false.

Ichinose's classmates preserve study time behind the disruption, coordinate support, refuse bribery/coercion, and allow the opponent to observe enough fear that his stale model remains plausible. They defeat an adversarial pressure campaign **without adopting Ryūen's coercive constitution**.

The developmental conclusion is precise:

> **solidarity and adversarial competence are compatible.**

This is not the same as saying solidarity always wins.

### Desire becomes self-owned rather than only disabling

Ichinose's romantic desire is politically relevant because Year 1 and early Year 2 often make it a site of shame, asymmetry, or collapse. By V09 she no longer treats the existence of the desire as an instruction to retreat from class purpose. She can want Ayanokōji, want Class A, recognize Kei's position, and still reject the idea that personal desire makes Kei a legitimate expulsion target.

That distinction is one of the clearest signs that her development is not simply "becoming ruthless." She becomes more willing to compete while retaining reasons that remain illegitimate even if tactically useful.

### V12 violation becomes the precondition for V12.5 counter-authorship, not its justification

V12 is the strongest negative relational case. Ayanokōji protects Ichinose's polity from one form of exam harm while deliberately attacking Ichinose's reasoning through accumulated trust and timed disclosure. Her extraordinary competence in the year-end exam is precisely why he decides to lower the functioning of the cognitive system making her dangerous.

The fact that she later grows does **not** retroactively legitimate this intervention.

V12.5 matters because she does not simply execute one of the answers he had prepared. She recovers enough to generate a **third answer** outside his binary and forces him into a new relation of response. The developed person does not merely survive the developer; she makes the developer's model incomplete.

`Second List` strongly confirms that reading. Its late Check Point explicitly describes her action as exceeding Ayanokōji's prediction. The bonus fiction `あの頃から───` supplies the intermediate state: Ayanokōji refuses sole credit for restoring her, states that she stood back up by her own power, observes real growth rather than return to baseline, and ends by recognizing that she can produce new discoveries after he believed the analysis complete.

### Distributed care exists below the leader

The same bonus fiction gives an important class-level control. When Ichinose accidentally creates a compromising message situation and freezes, her female classmates collaboratively construct a cover story that protects another person's privacy and stabilizes the scene. The class can therefore exercise **distributed protective competence when the leader herself cannot author the answer**.

This is evidence against treating the polity as nothing but Ichinose's charisma.

It does not solve the point-conversion problem.

## 3.3 Changes since frozen Year-1 boundary

### CONFIRMED_CONTINUITY

- Membership protection and sacrifice aversion remain genuine governing values.
- Trust and relational memory remain her strongest political resources.
- She continues to treat people as members before treating them as disposable production units.
- Her class remains unusually cohesive even under failure.

### GENUINE_DEVELOPMENT

- **Strategic reading:** increasingly reads the incentive and fear structure behind rival proposals rather than only their surface terms.
- **Self-authored prioritization:** resigns from the student council to concentrate on the class without converting resignation into shame ritual.
- **Adversarial opacity:** class can allow an opponent to believe a pressure campaign is working while maintaining hidden resilience.
- **Ethical boundary under harder competition:** willingness to become more aggressive does not erase the distinction between class strategy and personal romantic retaliation.
- **Constitutional pluralization:** Kanzaki/Himeno reform becomes an internal second brain rather than pure factional revolt.
- **Counter-authorship:** V12.5 produces an answer Ayanokōji did not already possess.
- **Distributed protection:** `Second List` fiction demonstrates classmates can protect the leader and one another without waiting for Ichinose to command the solution.

### REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_HIDDEN_STATE

No Volume-0 revelation rewrites Ichinose's biography. The major Year-2 revelation is causal restraint: `Second List` fiction directly blocks the strongest "Ayanokōji fixed her" interpretation by having Ayanokōji attribute recovery to Ichinose's own power.

### RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION

The late counter-authorship result reframes earlier instability without falsifying it. The V08 crisis was real; V09's growth does not turn the original disappearance into a master plan; later strategic understanding is retrospective cognition, not proof of earlier manipulation.

### CORRECTION_OF_PRIOR_INFERENCE

- **Reject:** "solidarity is inherently strategically naive." V09 supplies a successful adversarial counterexample.
- **Reject:** "becoming competitive requires becoming Ryūen-like or Sakayanagi-like." The class preserves its membership norm while learning opacity and coordinated resistance.
- **Revise:** "Ichinose is only a leader-centered polity." Leader centrality remains a risk, but reform actors and distributed protective competence are now proven.
- **Reject:** "romance is the sole cause of her later growth." `Second List` treats romance as one catalyst inside a larger reconstruction of agency.

### RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC_EXPRESSION

- **Ayanokōji:** protector, destabilizer, developmental claimant, desired person, and finally an actor whose model she exceeds. This is the ledger's strongest case where relational asymmetry becomes counter-authored rather than merely endured.
- **Kanzaki:** necessary internal critic whose legitimacy grows when dissent can remain loyal rather than sovereign replacement.
- **Himeno:** evidence that suppressed difference exists inside warm cohesion and can become politically useful when given a channel.
- **Classmates:** unusually dense support network that can protect Ichinose and one another, not merely receive care from her.
- **Ryūen:** adversary whose stale model demonstrates the political value of the class learning to manage what opponents can infer.
- **Sakayanagi:** elite observer whose old model of Ichinose becomes a liability when Ichinose begins reading her back.

### UNRESOLVED_CONTRADICTION

> **Ichinose proves that solidarity can acquire adversarial intelligence and that a leader can counter-author the developmental model imposed on her, but neither result proves that the polity has solved its chronic conversion problem or distributed enough strategic initiative to survive every future pressure.**

`Second List` preserves exactly this distinction: extraordinary unity is real; the end-of-year point deficit and unrealized reform are also real (`Y2SL-E041-E042`).

# 4. Sakayanagi Arisu

## 4.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

The Year-1 Sakayanagi endpoint was **aristocratic optimization**: an unusually accurate sovereign who recognizes unequal ability, assigns people to roles, controls strategic information, and produces excellent institutional results from a deep talent pool.

The constitutional risk was built into the same strength. When the sovereign holds the best model, the polity can become efficient without developing independent model-builders, robust dissent, or succession. Her private rivalry with Ayanokōji added a fiduciary problem because classmates could bear costs generated by a contest they did not author.

The handoff questions were therefore:

- can superior judgment remain accountable to classmates' interests?
- can the class reproduce governance without the sovereign?
- can private Ayanokōji priority remain subordinate to fiduciary duty?

## 4.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

Sakayanagi ends Year 2 as a more relationally self-aware person and a failed sovereign successor-builder.

Her personal progression is:

`elite optimizer -> observer whose old models can be exceeded -> person discovering irreplaceable attachment -> lover/rival who owns a private choice -> person capable of futurity beyond ANHS`

Her polity progression is:

`highly centralized excellence -> loss of alternative elite centers -> internal betrayal -> private-priority override -> sovereign withdrawal -> succession vacuum -> imported strategic center`

The distinction between **person** and **constitution** is essential. Her personal development is real. The polity's succession failure is also real.

### Centralized excellence remains strategically formidable

Year 2 repeatedly confirms that Sakayanagi's model of ability is not empty elitist rhetoric. In the pairing market she can use prestige and resources selectively. On the island she deploys a distributed protective network capable of counter-authoring Tsubaki's coalition. In the Unanimous Vote exam she rapidly calculates that sacrificing a useful member for 100 class points is ordinarily a poor exchange once income, morale, distrust, and future optionality are considered.

Nothing in the corpus suggests that centralized optimization stopped working because it was strategically foolish.

Its problem is political reproduction and private priority.

### Loss of Katsuragi and Hashimoto instability weaken internal correction

Katsuragi's transfer to Ryūen removes an obvious alternative governing center. Hashimoto's later betrayal exposes a different failure: the class contains independent intelligence, but independence has not become an accountable corrective channel. Hashimoto treats survival through self-insurance rather than trusted internal correction, and his solution to dissatisfaction is external realignment rather than constitutional challenge.

The result is a polity with talent but limited reliable mechanisms for telling the sovereign that the sovereign's model is wrong before the error becomes costly.

### Old models begin failing against changing people

V09 makes this explicit through Ichinose. Sakayanagi approaches a person through an older model of emotional vulnerability and attempts to define the meaning of Ichinose's relation to Ayanokōji. Ichinose reads the motive back, rejects the assigned interpretation, and suggests that Sakayanagi's own attachment is affecting her judgment.

This is not Sakayanagi suddenly becoming unintelligent. It is Year 2's larger epistemic rule:

> **a model can be highly accurate and become obsolete because the person develops.**

### Kamuro exposes relational value omitted by optimization

V10 is the decisive personal correction. Kamuro is not the strongest institutional contributor among the vulnerable candidates. Sakayanagi therefore treats multiple losses as roughly substitutable and permits chance to decide.

Only after Kamuro is gone does she recognize the category her optimization failed to include: **Kamuro had become her friend**.

The lesson is not that friendship should always override strategy. It is that history-dependent relational value can become real before the optimizer has consciously represented it. The decision model can therefore be internally coherent and still omit a value the decision-maker herself actually possesses.

Yamamura sharpens the same problem in V11. A subordinate may continue serving and still experience the sovereign's effortless willingness to discard her as evidence that the service never made her personally visible. Recognition is not reducible to utilization.

### Love becomes self-authored power and fiduciary danger at once

V09.5 lets Sakayanagi recognize romantic love without immediately treating attachment as weakness. She wants the feeling to become something capable of disrupting Ayanokōji's expected world.

That is genuine self-authorship.

V12/V12.5 then make the cost clear. Sakayanagi is defeating Ryūen on the merits. Ayanokōji sends no literal instruction to lose. She reconstructs what he wants from the relational signal and independently chooses defeat because his preference matters more to her than the class's immediate interest.

Her agency is real. So is the fiduciary failure.

The later withdrawal completes the political result: -300 class points, leader absence, member despair, and no internally generated successor.

### Personal futurity survives institutional defeat

Sakayanagi's departure should not be treated only as political failure. Her father respects the withdrawal. She plans to apologize/reconnect with Kamuro, extends her relation with Yamamura beyond school, and asks Ayanokōji for a future meeting after graduation. The person creates a future that the Class-A institution no longer contains.

This is one of Year 2's strongest demonstrations that institutional defeat does not delete personhood.

The constitutional result remains negative:

> **aristocratic optimization did not reproduce the optimizing function after the sovereign chose a private priority and left.**

## 4.3 Changes since frozen Year-1 boundary

### CONFIRMED_CONTINUITY

# 5. Kōenji Rokusuke

## 5.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Year 1 established Kōenji as the cleanest distinction between **possessed ability** and **collectively available ability**. Horikita could recognize his strength without acquiring ownership of its use. His V11 principle was explicit: deciding whether his talent would be used for someone else's objective remained his right.

The open political question was therefore not whether he was capable. It was:

> **What obligation, if any, does membership legitimately create for a person who never consented to making all of his capacity common property?**

## 5.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

Kōenji's Year-2 development is not a conventional integration arc. He does not become a loyal class soldier. Instead, the corpus gradually builds a theory of **bounded obligation, voluntary legibility, and epistemic self-sovereignty** around him.

The progression is:

`unavailable excellence -> negotiated bounded obligation -> fulfilled self-authored contribution -> anti-purchase reciprocity -> refusal of developmental capture -> own-answer epistemology`

### The island bargain constitutionalizes his relation to the class

Y2V02 creates the first genuinely explicit contract between Kōenji's autonomy and class need. He demands broad freedom through graduation. Horikita does not answer by claiming the class owns his ability. She negotiates a conditional exchange:

- if he takes first place **alone**, the contribution will count as advance payment for future freedom;
- if he fails, further cooperation remains owed.

The arrangement matters because both parties understand the terms in advance, and neither side pretends the underlying interests are identical.

Kōenji then wins the island exam. Ayanokōji's later intervention against Nagumo affects the command environment and contributes to the final two-point margin, so the result should not be mythologized as causally isolated. But Kōenji's extraordinary self-authored performance remains genuine, and the explicit bargain reaches its performance condition.

### Horikita later treats the promise as bounded rather than infinitely expandable

The V05 and V10 materials are politically important because the class encounters new reasons it would be convenient to demand more from Kōenji. Horikita nevertheless refuses to treat prior contribution as a blank check for future ownership.

V10 supplies the most explicit philosophical rule. Kōenji distinguishes genuine benevolence or obligation he himself recognizes from a strategic "gift" offered in order to purchase future compliance. An actor cannot simply create unlimited jurisdiction by helping him for instrumental reasons and later presenting the bill.

That is the locally sufficient Year-2 answer to `Y1H-032`:

> **membership can create bounded obligations through explicit agreement, genuine reciprocity, or self-recognized debt; it does not convert the person's total capacity into permanent class property.**

### Refusal becomes a limit on developmental universalism

V11 makes Kōenji important beyond class-contract theory. Ayanokōji's Year-2 project increasingly assumes that people and classes can be developed toward stronger forms. Kōenji rejects the premise that he needs the developer at all.

He does not need Nagumo's proving field to validate himself. He does not treat OAA as a final evaluator. His governing criterion remains radically self-referential: what belongs to "me," what does not, and how he can adapt.

This is narcissistic. It is also a serious limit on the ethics of universal developmental authorship:

> **a system that celebrates plural growth can still become imperial if it treats refusal to be developed as pathology.**

### "Own answers" become an epistemic theory of ability

V12.5 gives the most important Kōenji statement in the Year-2 corpus. He says he deliberately avoids absorbing every conventional answer so that he can preserve room to discover **his own answers**.

The contrast with White Room maximal curriculum is structural. The White Room tries to eliminate uncertainty by saturating the learner with solutions and escalating performance thresholds. Kōenji protects some ignorance because inherited completeness can reduce the space in which generative discovery occurs.

This does not prove his pedagogy is universally superior. It supplies a missing dimension of `実力`:

> **knowing more existing answers is not identical to being more capable of authoring a new answer.**

Ichinose's third answer provides the political/relational analogue.

### His freedom remains externally conditioned

V12.5 also blocks romanticization of Kōenji as absolutely free. His father defines "true freedom" through a condition: preserve the current class as A through graduation. Atsuomi then attempts to use that condition as political leverage.

Kōenji's school autonomy is therefore strong but not metaphysically conditionless. He is a self-sovereign student inside a larger familial/economic structure that still claims authority over what counts as freedom.

# 6. Nagumo Miyabi

## 6.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

The Year-1 Nagumo endpoint was a political project rather than a completed verdict. He had expanded student-council influence toward examination design, promoted rhetoric of freer individual competition, and increasingly personalized access to status/mobility around himself.

The handoff questions were:

- can individual mobility exist without patronage?
- does mobility liberate strong students from collective failure or license abandonment of weaker peers?
- can a ruler who controls the proving field ever know whether his supremacy is genuinely tested?

## 6.2 Canonical Year-2 boundary state

Nagumo ends Year 2 with the clearest distinction between **institutional reform** and **personal development** in this ledger.

The institutional result is not redemptive:

> **his mobility system genuinely creates opportunity and genuinely centralizes that opportunity into patronage.**

The personal result is more constructive:

> **his rivalry gradually moves from forced self-authentication through political dominance toward bounded competition and future relation.**

### The island exposes centralized command as both power and brittleness

Y2V03-V04 make Nagumo's third-year order a shadow polity. He can mobilize groups, surround Kōenji, allocate resources, and convert a school year into a network centered on his instructions.

That power contains a single-point-of-failure problem. When Ayanokōji incapacitates Nagumo and the radio goes silent, the command structure stops functioning normally. Kōenji's eventual two-point victory cannot be separated from the interruption.

The lesson is not "Nagumo is weak." It is:

> **centralized sovereignty can produce extraordinary coordination while becoming systemically brittle around the sovereign node.**

### Mobility is real; patronage is not an accusation external to it

V04.5 and especially V09 reveal the institutional mechanism. Transfer opportunities and final Class-A routes are possible because Nagumo collects and controls extraordinary private-point resources. The same mechanism includes extraction, obedience, ranking, custody, revocability, and lottery-like allocation.

This answers `Y1H-030` by revelation:

> **No: the demonstrated mobility system does not exist independently of patronage. Patronage is how the mobility is produced.**

It simultaneously reframes `Y1H-031`:

> **mobility can liberate a high-performing or lucky student from the class into which they were assigned while the same structure places weaker or less favored students under stronger abandonment pressure.**

The two effects coexist.

### Counter-authorship arises from inside the system

Kiriyama is important because he is not an external democrat defeating Nagumo. He has benefited from and served inside Nagumo's order. In V09 he uses the authority, dependencies, and transfer arrangements generated by that order to create a public crisis that forces constraint on Nagumo's discretion.

Horikita's student-council response then moves the office toward auditable procedure rather than personal arbitration.

This is a major constitutional lesson:

> **a system becomes more answerable when dependents can use its own procedures to constrain the patron.**

Nagumo's system did not intentionally design that outcome as its telos. The counterforce emerges from inside it.

### Supremacy becomes an epistemic wound

V09's Nagumo monologue gives the character interiority at exactly the point needed to avoid a one-note reading. Being first in academics, athletics, popularity, and institutional power has not settled whether his `実力` is genuine. If sufficiently strong people refuse to contest him, he risks becoming a `裸の王様`—a naked emperor whose supremacy is partly an artifact of the field.

Ayanokōji therefore becomes more than a trophy. He is an external standard Nagumo cannot simply create by decree.

This explains, without excusing, why Nagumo keeps trying to manufacture a proving field.

### The final contest becomes cleaner than the polity

Y2V11 gives Nagumo a more mature version of competition. The exchange-camp wager is comparatively bounded and self-financed. He does not risk the pooled third-year fund or make dependents bear the cost of his desire for validation. Ayanokōji finishes 17-2; Nagumo pays.

The post-contest relation then becomes future-facing. Nagumo can imagine university, later activity, and future competition rather than only absorption, humiliation, or domination.

His reflection on Manabu and Ayanokōji adds a final distinction. He cannot force himself into the kind of memory they occupy. Political custody can compel behavior; it cannot guarantee **relational remembrance**.

Nagumo increasingly wants the latter.

## 6.3 Changes since frozen Year-1 boundary

## 6.5 Measurement and downstream modeling constraints

The guidebook's most important Nagumo contribution in this ledger is editorial rather than a load-bearing OAA number: his profile frames reform as declared meritocracy organized through charisma and control across classes (`Y2SL-E048`). - Seeks environments where hierarchy can become visible and convertible into reward.

- Wants recognition from people he cannot trivially dominate because easy submission cannot certify superiority.
- Can confuse creating opportunity with gaining rightful custody over the beneficiary.

### Common errors to avoid

- Do not reduce his reform to empty hypocrisy.
- Do not describe genuine opportunities as proof the regime is non-patronal.
- Do not treat his later sympathetic rivalry as retroactive absolution for coercive governance.
- Do not model the third-year polity as resilient simply because its reach was large; island command exposes central-node brittleness.

# 8. Ensemble changes since frozen Year-1 state

## CONFIRMED_CONTINUITY

- None of the five becomes a moral opposite of their Year-1 self.

- Rivalry remains a major source of self-knowledge, but the meaning of rivalry changes by character.

## GENUINE_DEVELOPMENT

- **Ryūen:** recruits complementary expertise, delegates, contracts, updates stale models, and wants victory with authenticating meaning.

- **Sakayanagi:** gains relational self-knowledge, recognizes omitted attachment, and creates person-level futurity beyond institutional defeat.
- **Kōenji:** moves from raw refusal to a theory of bounded obligation and generative "own answers."
- **Nagumo:** becomes more capable of recognizing that supremacy and remembrance cannot be manufactured solely through political custody; final rivalry is more bounded.

- **Kōenji / Second List:** official documentation confirms that mediocre-looking axes underrepresent voluntarily withheld capacity.

## RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION

- Year-2 counter-authorship gives a stronger criterion for judging each leader: not only "can they produce results?" but "can people around them produce answers the leader does not own?"
- Volume 0's authored-environment genealogy sharpens the reader's sensitivity to patronage, curriculum, and preference-sensitive control without turning every leader into a White Room analogue.
- `Second List` measurement evidence strengthens the distinction between institutional legibility and political/relational capacity.

## CORRECTION_OF_PRIOR_INFERENCE

- **Ryūen:** voluntary legitimacy is real; moral reform is incomplete.
- **Ichinose:** solidarity is not inherently incompatible with adversarial competence.
- **Sakayanagi:** deep talent and excellent optimization are not succession.
- **Kōenji:** bounded membership obligations can exist without collective ownership of all talent.
- **Nagumo:** meritocratic mobility can be both genuinely liberating for some students and structurally patronal at the same time.

## RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC_EXPRESSION

The five characters reveal different ways a rival can matter:

- Ryūen wants a rival capable of making victory meaningful.
- Ichinose becomes capable of making the stronger analyst revise his model.
- Sakayanagi's rivalry becomes inseparable from love and therefore capable of overriding polity interest.
- Kōenji refuses the premise that a rival or evaluator has jurisdiction to define his worth.
- Nagumo needs a rival because a sovereign cannot certify himself using only a field he controls.

## UNRESOLVED_CONTRADICTION

> **Year 2 repeatedly proves that strong leaders can make systems more capable while making those systems more dependent on the strong leader. The mature question is therefore not whether leadership works, but whether successful leadership produces independent capacity, protected counterforce, and legitimate refusal before the leader's absence makes those needs unavoidable.**

Ryūen and Sakayanagi provide the sharpest negative succession pressures. Ichinose provides the strongest positive counter-authorship result but an unfinished polity-level conversion result. Kōenji provides the strongest refusal of developmental capture but not a general theory of solidarity. Nagumo provides real mobility but demonstrates how easily opportunity can become custody.

# 11. Boundary synthesis

The rival-leader corpus begins Year 2 as a set of competing governing styles and ends as a set of competing answers to a harder question: **what remains when a person's strength can no longer be equated with control over everyone around them?**

Ryūen's answer is adaptation. He becomes more capable of contracts, delegation, recruitment, and self-authenticating rivalry while still failing to make dissent safe or succession robust.

Ichinose's answer is pluralized solidarity. Her class learns to hide resilience from adversaries and develop internal countervoice, while she herself becomes the clearest example of a developed person generating an answer the developer did not possess. The class still struggles to convert that human capacity into enough institutional output.

Sakayanagi's answer divides person from polity. The person becomes more capable of friendship, love, accountability, and future life outside the institution. The polity exposes the cost of optimizing around one exceptional sovereign: when private desire overrides fiduciary duty and the sovereign leaves, high individual ability does not spontaneously become succession.

Kōenji's answer is refusal. He accepts bounded obligations he can recognize as legitimate while rejecting both the class and the developer as owners of his whole capacity. By the boundary he makes generative ignorance—preserving room for one's own answers—part of the project's theory of `実力`.

Nagumo's answer is the most institutional. His mobility system demonstrates that a reform can be genuinely liberating in one dimension and dependency-producing in another. His personal arc then discovers what the system cannot grant him: meaningful recognition from an opponent who remains outside his custody.

The strongest common conclusion is therefore:

> **Year 2 does not identify the best sovereign. It progressively weakens sovereignty as the final measure of leadership. The more diagnostic question is whether a leader, rival, institution, or class can produce capable actors who retain the power to refuse, correct, outgrow, or survive the system that helped make them capable.**

On that standard, Ichinose supplies the strongest counter-authorship result, Kōenji the strongest refusal-of-capture result, Nagumo the clearest mobility/patronage paradox, Ryūen the strongest legitimacy-without-constitutional-restraint contradiction, and Sakayanagi the clearest negative succession case.

Those differences should remain visible in every later Year-2 specialist synthesis. They are not five ranks on one ladder. They are five incompatible theories of what power, ability, leadership, and independence are for.
