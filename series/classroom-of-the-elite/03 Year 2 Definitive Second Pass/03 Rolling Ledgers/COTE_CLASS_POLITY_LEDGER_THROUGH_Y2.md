# Class polity ledger
## Canonical student-polity state through Year 2 / *Second List*

# 0. Ledger function

This ledger treats the four second-year classes as **polities rather than score containers**.

A polity is a durable student community that must solve recurring constitutional problems:

- who may decide;
- who may know;
- who may dissent;
- who bears the cost of defeat;
- how scarce protection is allocated;
- whether ability can remain private;
- whether leadership is delegated or monopolized;
- whether weak members are developed, tolerated, protected, traded, or culled;
- whether a leader can be corrected;
- whether a class can act when its exceptional center is absent;
- and whether success survives succession.

The Year-1 ledger ended with four sharply different constitutions:

- `POLITY-HORIKITA` — **dynamic developmental pluralism under hidden environmental sovereignty**;
- `POLITY-RYUEN` — **authoritarian mobilization gaining voluntary sociological legitimacy without moral or constitutional reform**;
- `POLITY-ICHINOSE` — **solidaristic trust with weak adversarial security and weak conversion of social capital into points**;
- `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` — **aristocratic optimization with deep talent, strong allocation, and extreme sovereign concentration**.

Year 2 does not turn those four constitutions into one model of "good leadership."

It stress-tests whether each polity can reproduce its strengths **without permanently depending on the person who made those strengths usable**.

The central Year-2 polity question is therefore:

> **Can a class become capable enough to survive the removal, refusal, failure, or moral error of its strongest organizing actor?**

This is a stronger test than point gain.

A class can rise rapidly while becoming more dependent on a hidden or visible sovereign. A class can lose points while developing a more durable opposition structure. A class can remain highly cohesive while failing to convert cohesion into institutional victories. A class can contain elite individuals while collapsing after the leader leaves.

The governing distinction is:

> **leader development is not polity development.**

# 2. Executive thesis — Year 2 moves from leadership styles to succession constitutions

The strongest Year-2 polity progression is:

> **Year 1: four competing leadership systems → Year 2: four competing succession systems.**

Every class becomes a test of whether organized competence can outlive its center.

## `POLITY-HORIKITA`

The class develops the widest portfolio of **distributed usable capacity**:

- Horikita as visible strategic and institutional leader;
- Hirata as social mediator and moral critic;
- Kei as female social coordinator;
- Kushida as exposed but still exceptional interpersonal infrastructure;
- Sudō as self-regulating and increasingly generative peer;
- Kōenji as contractually bounded exceptional capacity;
- multiple classmates capable of operating without Ayanokōji supplying the immediate answer.

Yet the most powerful hidden security layer remains Ayanokōji until the final boundary.

His transfer therefore does not prove the polity independent.

It finally creates the first valid test.

## `POLITY-RYUEN`

Ryūen's class becomes **more sophisticated without becoming less centralized at the point of ultimate authority**.

It adds:

- Katsuragi's complementary expertise;
- negotiated contracts;
- stronger delegation;
- followers with positive reasons to remain;
- more complex strategic adaptation.

But `Second List` explicitly warns that increasing dependence on Ryūen may obstruct classmates' development, and the class still lacks protected dissent. The polity is better coordinated and more legitimate sociologically, but its succession constitution remains weak.

## `POLITY-ICHINOSE`

Ichinose's class develops the most important **pluralization of internal thought**.

The class retains:

- no-expulsion solidarity;
- extraordinary mutual support;
- high trust;
- broad social competence.

It adds:

- Kanzaki/Himeno opposition;
- a "second brain";
- strategic concealment and adversarial response;
- a leader capable of setting boundaries and counter-authoring Ayanokōji's development model.

But the reform structure remains incomplete. *Second List* explicitly says the class needs more individual capacity beyond dependence on Ichinose's strategy.

The polity's problem is no longer "too kind."

It is:

> **Can solidarity reproduce strategic initiative broadly enough that the leader does not have to remain the class's sole political nervous system?**

## `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN`

The most talented and centralized Year-1 polity suffers the clearest succession failure.

The failure is not lack of elite personnel.

It is constitutional.

The polity had relied on Sakayanagi to:

- interpret the field;
- allocate roles;
- coordinate talent;
- determine priority;
- settle internal meaning.

When private Ayanokōji priority overrides fiduciary duty and Sakayanagi withdraws:

- the class absorbs a severe point penalty;
- members despair;
- no internal successor emerges;
- the class accepts Ayanokōji as imported strategic center.

The class collectively consents to financing the transfer, which is real polity agency.

But collective consent to import a sovereign is not the same as successful internal succession.

# 3. Constitutional dimensions

Every polity is tracked across twelve dimensions.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Authority** | Who can make binding decisions? |
| **Legitimacy** | Why do members accept that authority? |
| **Information** | Is strategic knowledge distributed, compartmentalized, or monopolized? |
| **Dissent** | Can disagreement alter policy without retaliation or exclusion? |
| **Weak-member treatment** | Are weaker members developed, protected, ignored, exploited, or culled? |
| **Resource allocation** | Who controls private points, protection rights, contracts, and collective capital? |
| **Delegation** | Can domains be owned by people other than the leader? |
| **Generativity** | Do developed members teach, reproduce, or create new capacity? |
| **External diplomacy** | Can the polity cooperate across class lines without surrendering identity? |
| **Provenance / accountability** | Can members tell who authored a decision and contest the record? |
| **Succession** | Can the system continue after leader absence or removal? |
| **Concentration risk** | What indispensable person, hidden system, or norm could collapse the polity if removed? |

# 5. `POLITY-HORIKITA`
## From developmental pluralism under hidden sovereignty to distributed competence under a real separation test

# 5.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

The Year-1 polity was unusually heterogeneous.

Its strongest capacities were distributed across people who did not naturally cooperate:

- Horikita — academics, planning, increasingly legitimate visible leadership;
- Hirata — broad interpersonal legitimacy;
- Kushida — exceptional social access;
- Kei — female peer-network coordination;
- Sudō — physical capacity and rapid developmental trajectory;
- Ike — contextual/practical competence;
- Kōenji — elite but self-sovereign capacity;
- Ayanokōji — concealed synthesis, security, and contingency control.

The Year-1 constitutional problem was therefore not lack of talent.

It was **aggregation under opacity**.

The class increasingly looked pluralistic while decisive hidden interventions still came from Ayanokōji.

The danger was:

> **public leadership could mature while remaining dependent on a private sovereign nobody could audit.**

Year 2 brings that contradiction to its endpoint.

# 5.2 Y2V01–Y2V04 — public ability grows, but hidden security still controls the worst contingencies

The OAA era changes the information environment.

Students become more institutionally legible.

Ayanokōji deliberately reveals more ability.

Horikita's class gains stronger public evidence that hidden capacity exists inside the polity.

But greater visibility is not equivalent to constitutional transparency.

The first-year pairing crisis and Hōsen conflict still demonstrate that Ayanokōji can alter the feasible field outside Horikita's public chain of command.

The uninhabited-island sequence then supplies a more constructive split.

Kōenji makes extraordinary ability available under a bounded bargain rather than becoming a normal subordinate.

The polity gains from capacity it does not fully own.

That is constitutionally important:

> **collective benefit need not imply collective ownership of the person.**

Kōenji's later `Second List` profile reinforces the principle: his OAA under-describes true ability precisely because participation remains elective (`Y2SL-E024–E026`).

# 5.3 Y2V05 — the class becomes truly Horikita-governed and simultaneously morally compromised

The Unanimous Vote Examination is the most important constitutional rupture of the year.

Horikita rejects Ayanokōji's intended solution and preserves Kushida.

This proves that:

- Horikita is not merely his visible proxy;
- her judgment can bind the class;
- Ayanokōji's preferred answer can lose.

But the exam does not produce a clean democratic triumph.

The class still faces mandatory expulsion pressure.

Airi becomes the person sacrificed.

The governing class decision therefore contains multiple, non-collapsible facts:

1. Horikita's preservation of Kushida is independently authored.
2. Kushida does not consent to being preserved as Horikita's project.
3. Ayanokōji becomes decisive in identifying and executing Airi's expulsion.
4. Airi bears a cost produced by the class's collective inability to escape the rule structure plus the leaders' chosen prioritization.
5. Later Kushida contribution cannot retroactively authorize the original decision.
6. Later Airi success cannot retroactively prove the polity judged her total human worth correctly.

This is the point at which `POLITY-HORIKITA` becomes politically mature enough to own decisions and morally mature enough to accumulate **moral remainder**.

# 5.4 Y2V06 — training wheels and distributed agency

V06 gives the polity its clearest positive developmental model.

Ayanokōji increasingly declines to become first-line answer.

Sudō diagnoses the Ike/Shinohara problem, asks for bounded advice, and reaches his own conclusion.

Ayanokōji's formulation is explicit:

`オレは何もしてない。おまえが自分で考えて自分で答えを出しただけだ`

and:

`そうやってクラスは回っていく`

The polity-level significance is larger than Sudō.

It defines a constitution in which:

`problem -> local actor -> bounded consultation -> self-owned answer -> distributed class function`

rather than:

`problem -> hidden sovereign -> solved environment -> public proxy`

Ayanokōji's `補助輪` metaphor makes the succession criterion explicit.

Training wheels succeed only if they can be removed.

# 5.5 Y2V06–Y2V09 — peer-generative capacity appears

Sudō becomes especially important because development becomes transmissible.

Onodera teaches him an anger-regulation technique.

He uses it under Hōsen provocation.

Later his academic discipline survives romantic rejection and begins flowing toward weaker students.

This gives the polity an important new constitutional property:

> **development can travel laterally rather than only downward from leader to follower.**

Kushida undergoes a parallel transformation.

Her public/private equilibrium collapses in V05.

Yet social capacity survives exposure.

V06 creates an openly self-interested cooperation compact.

V07 shows her independently protecting a class future that now overlaps her own future.

V09 gives her an institutional role she can appropriate for reasons she owns.

Thus social infrastructure becomes **less dependent on a false identity**.

# 5.6 Horikita's leadership becomes more institutional and more correctable

Across the middle and late year Horikita increasingly:

- negotiates with rival leaders;
- builds bounded agreements;
- accepts expertise she does not personally possess;
- recruits people by offering reasons they can own;
- tolerates openly adversarial cooperation;
- moves into student-council presidency;
- allows Hirata, Sudō, Kushida, Kei, and others to occupy distinct domains.

This is not leader weakness.

It is a stronger polity because the leader does not need to simulate omniscience.

The Year-2 Horikita ledger's distinction is essential here:

> **independence of Horikita's judgment is established before independence of the polity from Ayanokōji is established.**

# 5.7 V12 — victory reveals an unresolved constitutional shadow

The year-end exam gives Ayanokōji a representative position from which he can secure the result.

Maezono is expelled through an authored sequence whose broader purpose is not disclosed to the class.

The aftermath in V12.5 makes the constitutional issue explicit.

Sudō asks whether a no-expulsion victory existed.

Ayanokōji judges it was not impossible, merely less probable.

Hirata describes the act as approaching `越権行為`.

That distinction is polity-defining:

> **representative authority is not unlimited sovereign jurisdiction over membership.**

The class has gained extraordinary competitive capacity.

It has not fully solved who may authorize irreversible sacrifice.

# 5.8 V12.5 — the class reaches A as the hidden security layer exits

The polity enters the A position and simultaneously loses Ayanokōji.

That timing matters.

If the ledger ended at the score, it would misread the year.

The proper Year-2 endpoint is not:

> Horikita's class succeeded.

It is:

> **Horikita's class has become sufficiently developed that the hidden-security layer can finally be removed, but the corpus has not yet observed whether the polity reproduces its performance after removal.**

`Second List` makes the same distinction:

- `Y2SL-E027` credits extraordinary Year-2 growth and promotion;
- `Y2SL-E028` identifies Kōenji's future contribution as important;
- `Y2SL-E081` retrospectively frames Ayanokōji's final-exam conduct as preparation for the class to continue after transfer;
- `Y2SL-E082` explicitly leaves post-transfer performance unresolved.

# 5.9 Constitutional strengths at Y2SL

- highest demonstrated diversification of usable capacity;
- leader increasingly capable of bounded delegation;
- legitimate internal contradiction;
- strong peer-level social infrastructure;
- self-authored motives increasingly replace dependency on one reward;
- bounded contracts with autonomous exceptional actors;
- ability to absorb Kushida's exposed contradiction without requiring counterfeit reconciliation;
- enough moral pluralism that Hirata and Sudō can challenge the meaning of victory.

# 5.10 Constitutional vulnerabilities at Y2SL

- post-Ayanokōji succession not yet observed;
- Kōenji remains contractually bounded rather than organically committed;
- Ayanokōji leaves behind incomplete provenance around major decisions;
- expulsion history creates distrust and moral residue;
- formal success may conceal how much terminal security still came from one departing actor;
- the polity's new A position may increase pressure to preserve status rather than continue developmental experimentation.

# 5.11 Year-2 boundary lock

> **`POLITY-HORIKITA` ends Year 2 as the strongest candidate for a genuinely distributed developmental constitution, but not yet as a proven self-sustaining polity. Its success is that the succession test has become possible, not that the succession test has already been passed.**

# 6. `POLITY-RYUEN`
## From fear-centered authoritarianism to adaptive authoritarian legitimacy without constitutionalized dissent

# 6.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Year 1 established:

- fear;
- retaliation;
- information monopoly;
- rapid offensive coordination;
- centralized decision;
- willingness to exploit formal gaps.

The rooftop defeat then destroyed the claim that fear **fully** explained follower loyalty.

Followers preserved Ryūen after his sovereignty had been visibly broken.

The Year-1 endpoint was therefore mixed:

> **sociological legitimacy rises without moral or constitutional reform.**

Year 2 strengthens both sides.

# 6.2 Y2V01–Y2V02 — market manipulation becomes institutional intelligence

Ryūen adapts quickly to the OAA/partner market.

His strategy is not merely "find strong students."

He understands price expectations, scarcity, and the information generated by bidding behavior.

Y2V02 then produces one of the most important constitutional changes: Katsuragi.

Ryūen spends heavily to recruit a student whose competence is **different from his own**.

This matters because authoritarian polities often fail by treating difference as disobedience.

Katsuragi is useful precisely because he supplies:

- defensive judgment;
- institutional caution;
- rule literacy;
- administrative competence.

The polity becomes more capable of preserving distinct expertise.

# 6.3 Delegation becomes real

Katsuragi is not merely decorative.

Ryūen can assign domains he does not personally execute.

Other members also retain differentiated functions:

- Ishizaki;
- Albert;
- Hiyori;
- Kaneda;
- Komiya;
- later operational participants.

The class therefore becomes less bottlenecked at the level of **task execution**.

But authority remains centralized at the level of **ultimate political judgment**.

This distinction is crucial:

`delegation != distributed sovereignty`

# 6.4 Y2V04–Y2V04.5 — voluntary legitimacy becomes stronger

The Hōsen confrontation shows Ryūen distinguishing physical dominance from political victory.

He is increasingly willing to use:

- institutional timing;
- alarms;
- external intervention;
- preservation of subordinates;
- delegated exam responsibilities.

Komiya then gives the clearest follower-side evidence.

He distinguishes present Ryūen from the earlier ruler who treated classmates as disposable and says the changed leader is someone he can follow.

This is real legitimacy.

It is not democracy.

# 6.5 Y2V05 — managed dissent is a partial constitutional innovation

During the Unanimous Vote Examination, Ryūen uses false YES votes and allied coordination to identify the true dissenter.

Tokitō can publicly argue that Ryūen should step down.

Ryūen threatens and humiliates him.

Yet he does not simply eliminate him.

Katsuragi reads the arrangement as preserving a route for Tokitō to remain inside the polity.

This is best classified as:

> **managed dissent.**

The polity can now contain disagreement without immediate purge.

But disagreement is still tolerated at the sovereign's discretion.

# 6.6 Contracts replace some coercion without replacing coercive sovereignty

Year 2 repeatedly shows Ryūen capable of:

- negotiated alliance;
- bounded exchange;
- temporary cooperation;
- external recruitment;
- credible contract.

The Horikita/Ryūen arrangements are especially important because the polity can cooperate with a rival without pretending friendship or surrendering identity.

This is genuine institutional development.

But the contract logic mostly governs **external relations**.

Internally, no equivalent protected rule prevents Ryūen from coercing dissenters.

# 6.7 V09 — model updating is a polity strength

Ryūen's sabotage against Ichinose's class fails.

The important response is not failure itself.

He updates.

The polity does not preserve a false theory merely to protect the leader's pride.

That adaptive capacity is one reason the class becomes competitively formidable.

# 6.8 V10–V11 — Hiyori identifies the hidden weakness

Hiyori's diagnosis is not that Ryūen cannot win.

It is that repeated leader-authored victories may prevent classmates from developing `上積み` — durable additional capacity.

This becomes one of the strongest polity-level criticisms in Year 2.

`Second List` later formalizes it:

- `Y2SL-E034`: Hiyori worries excessive Ryūen-dependence may obstruct classmate development;
- `Y2SL-E035`: class review credits stronger coordination and Katsuragi while warning of increasing dependence on Ryūen.

The problem is therefore not rhetorical.

It is the official future-task field of the polity.

# 6.9 V11 negative control — dissent remains unsafe

Tokitō's continuing opposition is answered through physical coercion and threatened pursuit.

Outside actors can interrupt.

The class itself still lacks an internal institution that gives dissent enough security to constrain the ruler.

Thus:

> **the polity has learned how to contain dissent socially before learning how to protect dissent constitutionally.**

# 6.10 Basic academic weakness limits sovereignty-by-strategy

`Y2SL-E036` adds a material structural problem.

The class's basic academic capacity remains weak in cooperative/written contexts.

This matters because Ryūen's extraordinary political intelligence can mask a broader portfolio weakness.

A polity that repeatedly needs the sovereign to invent compensating tactics may become more dependent precisely because the tactic works.

# 6.11 V12–V12.5 — meaningful victory becomes a higher standard, not a succession solution

Ryūen is beaten by Sakayanagi on the merits of the final contest.

Sakayanagi later throws the result after reconstructing Ayanokōji's preference.

Ryūen refuses to treat the official outcome as the victory he wanted.

This is significant leader development.

It raises the polity's theory of success beyond scoreboard opportunism.

But it does not solve succession.

Ryūen remains the class's strategic center.

# 6.12 Constitutional strengths at Y2SL

- strong rapid coordination;
- strategic adaptability;
- credible external bargaining;
- growing differentiation of subordinate expertise;
- real voluntary follower investment;
- improved preservation of members relative to early Year 1;
- leader willing to revise failed threat models;
- ability to recruit institutional competence absent from the original class.

# 6.13 Constitutional vulnerabilities at Y2SL

- no protected veto against Ryūen;
- continued bodily coercion;
- increasing leader dependence;
- weak basic academic layer;
- Hiyori's correction lacks binding force;
- succession remains unresolved;
- delegated expertise does not yet reproduce the leader's strategic function.

# 6.14 Year-2 boundary lock

> **`POLITY-RYUEN` ends Year 2 as a stronger, more legitimate, more differentiated authoritarian polity. Its problem is no longer whether followers possess agency. They do. Its unresolved problem is whether their agency can ever become strong enough to limit, replace, or survive the sovereign rather than merely make his rule more effective.**

# 7. `POLITY-ICHINOSE`
## From solidaristic trust under adversarial pressure to solidaristic pluralism with an unfinished second brain

# 7.1 Frozen Year-1 baseline

Year 1 established a polity with unusual social capital:

- strong trust;
- no-expulsion norm;
- collective point pooling;
- high membership protection;
- broad interpersonal infrastructure;
- leader legitimacy rooted in care and reliability.

The weakness was not that solidarity was fictitious.

It was that solidarity could be exploited by actors who did not reciprocate its assumptions.

The Year-1 question was:

> **Can the class become adversarially secure without remaking itself in Ryūen's image?**

Year 2 increasingly answers yes.

But the answer is institutionally incomplete.

# 7.2 Y2V05 — isolated dissent exposes the conformity problem

Kanzaki's dissent is strategically meaningful.

He recognizes the danger of `正常性バイアス` and the class's habit of treating agreement as moral safety.

But one dissenter cannot yet convert concern into policy.

The class's cohesion has a shadow:

> **when being a good member is strongly associated with affirming the group, dissent can become socially costly even without formal punishment.**

This is a softer constitutional problem than Ryūen's coercion.

It is still a real constraint.

# 7.3 Y2V07 — reform begins only when an outsider stops owning it

Ayanokōji helps connect Kanzaki and Himeno.

But the crucial analytical move is his stopping rule:

`ここから先は第三者であるオレが立ち入る領域じゃない。`

If the reform is to be constitutional rather than another hidden Ayanokōji subsystem, the class must own it.

This is a strong positive example of environmental authorship ending before political authorship is stolen.

# 7.4 Y2V09 — the polity develops a second brain

The reform structure becomes plural.

It includes:

- Kanzaki;
- Himeno;
- Watanabe;
- Amikura;
- Hamaguchi;
- internal disagreement among reform-minded members.

The importance is not that every reform proposal succeeds.

It is that:

> **criticism becomes compatible with belonging.**

The polity no longer needs to treat opposition as evidence of disloyalty.

# 7.5 Adversarial competence grows without culling becoming the norm

Y2V09 also shows the class defeating Ryūen's sabotage.

The class can:

- conceal resilience;
- coordinate under hostile observation;
- allow the opponent to misread the state of the system.

This is important because Year 1's solidarity sometimes relied on transparent good faith.

Year 2 develops **strategic opacity** without turning the class into a fear polity.

# 7.6 Ichinose's desire stops functioning only as constitutional liability

The polity's leader remains emotionally exposed.

But later Year 2 shows desire becoming:

- motivation;
- strategic information;
- boundary-setting energy.

The class does not become less solidaristic because Ichinose becomes more personally assertive.

This is a major correction to any model where "kindness" and "strength" are mutually exclusive.

# 7.7 V12 — the polity is protected while the leader is attacked

Ayanokōji's bargain during the year-end exam protects Ichinose's classmates from a traitor-expulsion risk and yields material advantage.

At the same time, he weaponizes trust against Ichinose personally.

This produces a difficult class-level result:

> **the polity can be protected by a method that violates the leader's relational autonomy.**

Collective benefit therefore cannot automatically legitimate the intervention.

# 7.8 V12.5 — solidarity nearly becomes passive consolation, then hardens

After V12, Ichinose returns shattered.

The class reflex is consolation.

# 9. Four-class ecology
## Ayanokōji's project is not itself a fifth class constitution

Late Year 2 makes Ayanokōji's four-class project explicit.

He wants all four polities to retain a plausible path to A.

This should not be mistaken for a neutral constitutional principle.

It is an **external developmental ecology** authored by a student who sits inside one polity and then transfers into another.

The four-class project has at least three defensible features:

1. it resists a stable dominant-class equilibrium;
2. it preserves rival systems long enough to develop independently;
3. it increases the probability that strong alternative answers emerge.

It also has three severe legitimacy problems:

1. the affected polities do not collectively authorize him to design the ecology;
2. individuals such as Maezono, Ichinose, Kei, and Sakayanagi can bear costs created by the experiment;
3. preserving formal class competition can become an excuse for treating polities as experimental subjects.

Thus:

> **plurality of polities is valuable without granting Ayanokōji ownership of the plurality.**

The strongest Year-2 result is that the classes increasingly become capable of answering the experiment back.

# 16. Cross-polity comparison VII — legitimacy

The corpus requires at least four kinds of legitimacy.

### Procedural legitimacy

Was the decision authorized by a recognized rule or office?

### Sociological legitimacy

Do members actually accept or follow the authority?

### Epistemic legitimacy

Does the authority possess enough reliable information to make the decision?

### Ethical legitimacy

Was the authority entitled to impose the cost on the affected person?

These often diverge.

Examples:

- Ryūen gains sociological legitimacy without ethical or procedural constraints on coercion.
- Ayanokōji can possess overwhelming epistemic capacity while lacking ethical jurisdiction over Maezono or Ichinose.
- Horikita can hold procedural leadership while still being wrong about a decision's moral cost.
- Sakayanagi can possess epistemic superiority while violating fiduciary responsibility.
- Ichinose can possess enormous sociological legitimacy while needing stronger distributed strategic capacity.

No polity has a monopoly on legitimacy because legitimacy is not one axis.

# 17. Cross-polity comparison VIII — provenance and hidden sovereignty

Year 2 increasingly treats **who authored the outcome** as a constitutional question.

`POLITY-HORIKITA` has the largest hidden-sovereignty problem because Ayanokōji can alter outcomes while allowing visible leadership to carry the public result.

The class becomes more legitimate as Horikita's real authorship increases.

But V12.5 still shows Ayanokōji deliberately managing how much truth the class receives about Maezono and his broader purpose.

`POLITY-RYUEN` has the opposite problem.

Authorship is usually legible.

The problem is not hidden sovereignty but excessive visible sovereignty.

`POLITY-ICHINOSE` develops toward plural authorship.

The reform coalition and Ichinose's third answer make it progressively harder to attribute the class's future to one external developer.

`POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` suffers from sovereign provenance so complete that when the sovereign exits, the class lacks a replacement interpretive center.

The Year-2 rule is:

> **A constitution is more auditable when members can tell who made the decision, why they had standing to make it, and how the decision can be challenged before the cost becomes irreversible.**

The transfer restores competitive capacity.

Class A is an institutional rank.

# 26. Canonical Year-2 polity synthesis

Year 2 does not reveal one superior class constitution.

It reveals **four different ways a polity can become dependent on excellence**.

`POLITY-HORIKITA` depends on a hidden security layer even as public capacity becomes increasingly distributed.

`POLITY-RYUEN` depends on an openly sovereign strategist even as delegation and voluntary legitimacy improve.

`POLITY-ICHINOSE` depends on extraordinary solidaristic leadership even as loyal opposition and adversarial competence become more plural.

`POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` depends on a sovereign interpreter so completely that the class cannot reproduce the function after her exit.

The year therefore changes the political question.

The question is no longer:

> **Which leader is strongest?**

It is:

> **What remains of the polity when the strongest person is wrong, absent, unwilling, expelled, transferred, defeated, or no longer sovereign?**

By that standard, the Year-2 evidence is deliberately uneven.

Horikita's class has the most promising distribution of usable agency, but its definitive succession test has only just begun.

Ryūen's class has become substantially more sophisticated, but its own members still cannot reliably bind the leader.

Ichinose's class has learned that solidarity can contain dissent and strategic hardness, but it has not yet reproduced enough independent strategic capacity to convert social strength consistently into institutional outcome.

Sakayanagi's class provides the negative control: elite talent is not a succession constitution.

The terminal Year-2 polity principle is therefore:

> **A mature class is not one whose strongest leader can solve every problem. It is one whose members can continue to produce, contest, transmit, and revise political judgment when the strongest leader no longer supplies the answer.**

And the hardest unresolved Year-3 test remains:

> **Can any of the four polities preserve exceptional ability without turning the exceptional person into the constitution itself?**
