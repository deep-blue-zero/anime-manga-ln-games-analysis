# Classroom of the Elite - Year 2 Volume 3
## Second-Pass Deep Reading
### Attribution, hidden authorship, performed identity, and responsibility inside an engineered field

> **Local analytical boundary:** this document uses the complete original Japanese Year 2 Volume 3, the frozen Year 1 V2 boundary, and the canonical endpoints of `COTE_Y2_V01_DEEP_READING.md` and `COTE_Y2_V02_DEEP_READING.md`. It deliberately does **not** use Y2V04 or later volumes, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptations, wikis, or remembered later revelations to identify the White Room student, identify the attacker who injured Komiya and Kinoshita, settle the island examination, or read later Year 2 designs backward into this volume.

---

# 1. Scope, provenance, and inherited state

`Y2V03` is the third canonical volume artifact in the Year 2 definitive second pass. The Japanese EPUB was read in full spine order, including the author afterword. The source passed EPUB/ZIP integrity testing and matches the Phase-0 source lock byte-for-byte. The analysis uses deterministic paragraph locators of the form `COTE:Y2V03:spineXX:paraYYYY`, plus a separate illustration namespace.

The local source contains **27 spine items**, **4,207 parsed non-empty paragraph records**, and an explicitly unfinished special examination. The author confirms in the afterword that this is the first time a special exam is divided across two or more books: `今巻で初めて特別試験が2冊以上に分かれる形になります。まずはその点をご了承ください。各地に散った生徒たちの状況も書きたいと思いつつ、あっと言う間にページ数が最大にまで膨れ上がってしまい、1冊で出来ることに限界があるなと痛感しています。`. That formal fact governs interpretation. V03 is not permitted to manufacture closure where the novel intentionally ends in the middle of the island exam.

The inherited Year 2 state is already specific.

V01 established **legibility**: OAA makes bounded models of students public and politically actionable while the bounty reveals that sovereign rule-making can remain hidden.

V02 established **jurisdiction**: once ability and risk can be grouped, insured, traded, transferred, protected, or cultivated, the question becomes who has the right to bind another person's future and whether refusal remains meaningful.

V03 moves one level deeper. The island creates a field in which outcomes are visible but authorship is often not. A student is injured, but the GPS record does not identify an attacker. An official history appears trustworthy, but Shiba later says it has been falsified. Kushida intends to film one event while another actor controls where she stands and what she sees. Nanase claims another identity, yet Ayanokōji reads that identity as a self-authored psychological instrument. Kei receives a sincere kiss that Ayanokōji also uses as intentional emotional regulation. Ike speaks for himself, but only after Ayanokōji deliberately withholds information that would lower the emotional stakes. Nanase is offered a hand, but the narration immediately reclassifies her as something that can be used and discarded.

The governing V03 question is therefore:

> **When the result is visible but the causal chain is hidden, layered, performed, or administratively rewritten, who authored the outcome - and on what evidence may responsibility be assigned?**

That is a problem of **attribution**.

# 2. Executive thesis - from jurisdiction to attribution

Year 2 Volume 3 is a novel about **hidden authorship**.

The island examination is physically expansive but epistemically restrictive. Students scatter across a large terrain, GPS devices expose coordinates only under specific conditions, search itself costs points, ranking information appears asymmetrically, equipment and weather interfere with observation, and the school mediates nearly every official fact. A student can know where someone was without knowing what she did. A witness can know what happened without being able to prove it. An administrator can know what the record should say and possess the capacity to make the record say something else.

The most important institutional episode is therefore not a contest result. It is Shiba's quiet statement that a history has been altered: `「履歴の方はこちらで改ざんしておいたので、足がつくことはないかと」`. That single line changes the epistemology of the exam. V01 asked whether a file captures the person. V02 asked who may use a file to bind another person. V03 asks whether the file itself can be trusted when the rule managers are hostile participants.

The Komiya/Kinoshita incident makes the problem concrete. Shinohara says a third party pushed them. Physical traces and an unseen observer make deliberate intervention plausible. But the GPS history offers no obvious suspect and the school trends toward treating Shinohara's statement as a face-saving story after an accident. The available evidence is not simply incomplete; different evidence layers point in different directions. Later, Tsukishiro and Shiba reveal that Chabashira has been searching histories and that they have already altered records to prevent a trace back to themselves. The political question is no longer merely whether a record is accurate. It is **who controls the provenance of accuracy**.

The same structure appears inside individual psychology.

Nanase's prologue explicitly describes a fragile `私` that cannot sustain a chosen justice and a `ボク` created to carry it. Her internal language is direct: `『私』の脆い心では、その『正義』を支えることは出来ない。` followed by `だから『ボク』がそれを支える。`. During the confrontation she later claims that `ボク` is Matsuo Eiichirō occupying her body. Ayanokōji considers the phenomenon seriously enough to test it, then rejects the supernatural or ontologically separate-person explanation. His conclusion is that she has forced another persona into being and uses `ボク` as a trigger: `「単純に心の中でもう1つの人格を強引に作り出しただけに過ぎない。『私』と『ボク』とをあえて使い分けているのも、自らに言い聞かせるためだ」`; `『ボク』をトリガーに攻撃的な自分へと変化させていただけ。`.

That is not evidence that Nanase is simply lying. The performance changes what she can do. Her speed, violence, and willingness to attack are subjectively and behaviorally real. V03 therefore gives the project an unusually precise case of **performed identity producing real capacity without becoming proof of separate ontological identity**.

Her backstory then revises the White Room suspicion attached to her. Matsuo Eiichirō was a real person she loved and admired. Atsuomi's retaliation against Matsuo's family produced a chain of ruin and suicide. Tsukishiro approached Nanase and supplied a causal story in which Ayanokōji's escape made that destruction necessary. Nanase admits that she knows almost nothing about the White Room itself. The local evidence therefore moves Nanase away from White Room origin and toward a different category: **grief recruited by an administrator into an expulsion project**.

Ayanokōji is not outside this theme. V03 supplies some of the clearest local evidence yet that his authorship of other people can work by controlling what they know.

Ike's crisis over Shinohara is allowed to become his own. Ayanokōji recognizes that neither he nor Sudō nor Nanase can substitute the decisive words. Ike finally says not a polished confession but the thing that actually matters: `「ある! 大ありだ! 俺はおまえを退学にさせたくない、だから助けるんだよ!」`. That moment is genuinely Ike-authored.

Yet Ayanokōji knows he has a high-probability rescue strategy and deliberately withholds it. His reasoning is explicit: hope would soften the determination required to keep fighting, so the group should maintain tension to the end. The sequence `オレはプランを口にしようかとも一瞬考えたが思い留まった。` -> `しかし同時に、篠原を救うための決意に緩みが生じる。それは得策とは言い難い。` -> `池たちには、最後まで強い緊張感を持って特別試験を戦ってもらうことが大切だ。` is almost a textbook statement of **development by information architecture**.

This complicates the promising V02 answer to `Y1H-001`. Ayanokōji can cultivate without manufacturing a wholly artificial crisis, but he still edits another person's epistemic environment to preserve the developmental pressure he wants.

His relationship with Kei shows the same doubleness in a gentler register. Their first kiss is not reducible to manipulation. His narration records sensory novelty and genuine desire: `オレ自身初めて知る異性の唇の感触は、想像よりもずっと柔らかいものだった。` and `本当なら長い間堪能していたいところではあったが、今は無人島試験の真っ最中。`. But he also consciously uses the kiss to displace her anxiety about Nanase: `七瀬と行動していることで頭の中が不安でいっぱいであるなら、それよりも強烈な記憶を植え付けてやるのが一番手っ取り早い。`. **Authentic desire and strategic emotional regulation coexist in the same act.** The text does not allow the analyst to purify the event into either romance or manipulation alone.

The ethical climax arrives after Nanase's defeat. Ayanokōji recognizes that he and his father share an underlying essence: `オレとあの男と本質は同じ。`. He identifies a decisive difference in whether one can extend a hand to a fool, and physically offers Nanase that hand. The illustration gives this gesture major editorial emphasis.

Then the narration immediately destabilizes the moral victory:

`実際に役立つか役立たないかは関係がない。`

`使い捨てる形になろうとも、役立たせるようにこちらが上手く使うことが肝要。`

The distinction from Atsuomi is therefore real but not clean. Ayanokōji can rescue a person whom his father would discard. He can also continue thinking of the rescued person as an instrument whose usefulness can be extracted even if she is ultimately disposable.

V03 is the first volume in this second pass where the phrase **developmental author** becomes impossible to treat as merely descriptive. It now demands an ethical audit:

> **Does extending the hand recognize the other person as an author, or merely preserve a potentially useful piece on the board?**

The novel does not yet answer.

Its compact progression is:

> **V01: who becomes legible?**  
> **V02: who gains jurisdiction over that legibility?**  
> **V03: who authored the visible outcome, and who controls the evidence by which authorship is assigned?**

# 3. Position in the Year 2 architecture

V03 is the first full operational test of the constitutional field built in V02, but it is only **Part I** of that test. The island examination remains unfinished. Its function is therefore not to tell us which political constitution finally wins. It shows how each constitution behaves when ordinary classroom visibility collapses.

Several Year 2 threads move simultaneously:

- `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD` escalates from bounded measurement to adversarial record control.
- `SURVEILLANCE` becomes spatial, costly, and manipulable through GPS.
- `ENVIRONMENTAL_AUTHORSHIP` becomes explicit through information withholding and psychological pacing.
- `HORIKITA_INDEPENDENCE` advances because Ayanokōji trusts her to judge her own limits rather than supervising her survival.
- `KOENJI_AUTONOMY` becomes publicly productive under a negotiated obligation.
- `RYUEN_FEAR_LEGITIMACY` gains the first strong operational example of another capable adult-like judgment constraining Ryūen from within his own polity.
- `ICHINOSE_SOLIDARITY` becomes logistics at scale while also becoming a hostage vulnerability.
- `WHITE_ROOM` becomes more epistemically disciplined because Nanase's origin is clarified without solving the actual White Room identity.
- `RELATIONSHIP_RECIPROCITY` gains real romantic intimacy while information asymmetry persists.
- `ORDINARY_LIFE_COUNTER_CURRICULUM` survives inside a survival exam through food, jealousy, romance, rivalry, embarrassment, and care.

Most importantly, V03 introduces a distinction that later Year 2 analysis must preserve:

> **authorship of an action is not necessarily authorship of the conditions that made the action likely.**

Ike authors his words. Ayanokōji authors part of the motivational environment. Nanase authors her vengeance persona. Tsukishiro authors the story that gives her vengeance an object. Kushida authors her decision to participate. Yagami authors the operation that positions her. The school authors the exam. Tsukishiro and Shiba can alter the record used to judge all of them.

The more actors the volume adds, the less adequate single-cause interpretation becomes.

# 4. Narrative architecture - ten movements of causal opacity

## 4.1 Nanase's monologue: justice as a psychological technology

The prologue is not merely foreshadowing for a fight. It gives the fight's causal grammar in advance.

Nanase describes grief that her ordinary self cannot convert into sustained aggression. She therefore reaches for `正義` - justice - as a legitimating frame. The crucial sequence is not "I discovered the truth and will punish the guilty." It is closer to:

> I need a justice strong enough to let me fight without collapsing under guilt; `私` cannot carry it, so `ボク` will.

This makes justice simultaneously sincere and instrumental. The belief matters to her, but she also uses it to make a prohibited action psychologically possible.

The prologue therefore prepares the volume's later distinction between **motive** and **truth**. A motive can be completely real even when the causal story supporting it is false or manipulated.

## 4.2 `Ten people, ten strategies`: public rules create plural rationalities

The pre-exam briefing emphasizes that the island system has no single optimal route. Equipment, movement, grouping, food, endurance, cards, private points, and risk appetite generate legitimately different strategies.

Tsukishiro's most important intervention is not a hidden rule here but a public ambiguity. He strongly prohibits serious misconduct, then says ordinary student scuffles will be accepted to some degree: `「それからもう1つ。無人島での滞在が長くなればなるほど、自然とフラストレーションは溜まるものです。食料不足や水不足によって、時には生徒同士で小競り合いに発展することもあるでしょう。それに関しては───私はある程度認める方針です」`. Staff discomfort matters. Majima objects. Tsukishiro refuses to narrow the statement.

That creates a gray zone in which violence can occur before the institution decides whether it was a punishable violation, an accident, or tolerated friction.

## 4.3 Opening deployment: delegated sovereignty

The island starts by testing whether V02's bargains can survive contact with reality.

Ayanokōji does not supervise Horikita. He believes that the present Horikita can recognize her own limit: `1年前と違い、今の堀北なら自分の限界のラインを見極めることも出来るはずだ。`. That is an important developmental act of **non-intervention**.

Kōenji's promise is also revealed to have no mechanical enforcement. Ayanokōji explicitly notes that the promise has no coercive force, but that breaking faith with a Horikita now recognized as a leader would make future class assistance harder to obtain. Contract is therefore enforced partly through **reputation and future reciprocity**, not ownership.

## 4.4 Nanase becomes a companion rather than a solved identity

Nanase requests to travel with Ayanokōji despite the scoring disadvantages of cross-year accompaniment. He accepts partly because an explicit companion is easier to observe than an unseen tail.

This is a recurring V03 principle: making surveillance visible can reduce one uncertainty while creating another. Nanase becomes physically close enough for Ayanokōji to study, but her motives remain layered.

Her behavior during travel is not easily reduced to acting. She assists weaker students, worries about injuries, and insists on searching for missing students before the school arrives. The line `協力してくれないのなら自分1人でも行くという強い覚悟の表れが見て取れる。` makes her determination legible before the backstory explains it.

## 4.5 Love and development: real intimacy inside curated pacing

The chapter titled `相手を好きになるということ` places emotional development beside the survival exam rather than outside it.

Kei and Ayanokōji's first kiss is the obvious landmark, but the deeper structure is one the Year 2 method must keep intact: **the relationship is both genuine and pedagogically curated by Ayanokōji**.

He wants the kiss. He enjoys it. He is learning something he did not know through embodied experience. At the same time, he is conscious that a strong romantic memory can regulate Kei's jealousy and that the relationship's pace affects her emotional development.

The scene therefore resists both cynical and sentimental reduction.

## 4.6 The invisible enemy: injury without attributable author

Komiya and Kinoshita's fall is the volume's central evidentiary puzzle.

Shinohara says another person pushed them. The physical environment supports the possibility of intervention. Nanase pursues an extraordinarily fast hidden figure and appears to recognize that person. Yet the local text does **not** name the figure, and the GPS history does not supply an obvious suspect.

The school's provisional judgment therefore tends toward accident or an exculpatory story invented to prevent withdrawal.

This is exactly the kind of case where later hindsight must be quarantined. V03 establishes a hidden actor problem; it does not authorize the analyst to fill in the blank.

## 4.7 Ryūen, Katsuragi, and Kōenji: strength becomes public but not self-explanatory

The mid-volume cluster around Ryūen and Kōenji tests several forms of capacity.

Katsuragi prevents Ryūen from pursuing a point at inefficient physical cost. Ayanokōji notes that Ishizaki or Kaneda could not have stopped him and that Katsuragi is already becoming important: `肩を竦めながら、龍園は薄く笑う。どこまでも限界まで強行するつもりだった龍園だが、葛城によって止められたか。これが石崎や金田であれば龍園を止めさせることは無理だったはず。早くも2年Bクラスの中で、葛城は重要な役割を果たしつつあるな。`. This is a concrete shift from "does Ryūen have loyal followers?" toward "can allied judgment actually constrain his action?"

Kōenji, meanwhile, converts a long-hidden stock of capacity into sustained public performance. Ayanokōji repeatedly revises his estimate upward. Their tug-of-war suggests roughly comparable arm force under the immediate conditions, but Ayanokōji intentionally yields for class-score reasons. The scene therefore does **not** settle total strength. It does demonstrate that Kōenji's previously unavailable ability has become socially usable because he chose to make it so under the V02 bargain.

## 4.8 First-year coalition: secrets become collateral

Tsubaki's gathering of first-year representatives expands the political field beyond the Tsukishiro operation.

Yagami is not simply a polite networking first-year. Tsubaki identifies that he has been moving secretly against Ayanokōji. Yagami then reveals Kushida's past under coercive pressure and later recruits Kushida into an operation against Ayanokōji by appealing to her own self-preservation goal.

The important V03 point is again categorical discipline:

- hidden plotter does not equal White Room student;
- unusual information does not equal White Room origin;
- coercion by first-year peers is not identical to Tsukishiro command;
- a shared target does not imply a shared institution.

## 4.9 `The revealed identity`: Nanase's identity becomes psychologically legible, not ontologically supernatural

Nanase engineers an isolated confrontation and openly states that Tsukishiro arranged her admission and mission.

During the fight, `私` gives way to `ボク`. Her physical style and aggression change. She claims that Matsuo Eiichirō is now acting through her body.

Ayanokōji refuses the claim without dismissing the phenomenon. His analysis is that Nanase has forced herself into a second performance because the actual Nanase is too nonviolent to sustain revenge.

The title `明かされる正体` - "the revealed identity" - is therefore deliberately slippery. What is revealed is not "Nanase is the White Room student." What is revealed is the psychological architecture by which Nanase turned grief into a fighting identity and how Tsukishiro gave that grief an institutional target.

## 4.10 `Seeds of unease`: the sovereign can rewrite the evidence

The final chapter splits the volume's causal problem in two.

Ichinose accidentally overhears Tsukishiro and Shiba. She learns that Chabashira has been investigating histories and that the hostile administrators can alter those histories. When discovered, she is threatened not only with violence but with the expulsion of an innocent C-class group if she tells anyone. Tsukishiro says that creating a rule-violation reason is trivial for the side that manages the rules: `「そんなこと出来るはずがないと思いますか? 不正の理由を作ることなど、ルールを管理する側にしてみれば造作もないことなんです。特にこの監視の目が行き届かない無人島の中では何が起こっても不思議ではない」`.

Nanase, meanwhile, explains how Tsukishiro recruited her grief and accepts that her anger was misdirected. Ayanokōji offers cooperation rather than expulsion from school.

The two endings mirror each other:

- Tsukishiro uses another person's attachments as leverage to silence truth.
- Ayanokōji uses another person's attachments and grief to recruit cooperation after extending mercy.

The moral difference is meaningful. The structural resemblance is not negligible.

# 5. The island exam as an epistemic institution

The V02 analysis emphasized the exam as a political economy of cards, grouping, insurance, and transferable risk. V03 shows what those rules become once bodies enter geography.

## 5.1 Information now has direct cost

GPS search is not free. Ranking visibility is delayed and asymmetric. Location, order, weather, battery, physical endurance, and opportunity cost all affect what a student can know.

Information becomes another resource category beside food, points, physical capacity, and time.

This matters because it destroys the fantasy of an omniscient strategist. Even Ayanokōji makes decisions under bounded evidence. The correct standard is not whether he "knows everything," but how he handles uncertainty, what he chooses to verify, and what assumptions he refuses to convert into fact.

## 5.2 Public rankings alter behavior before the exam ends

When lower-ranked groups become visible, some groups relax because they infer that their own position is safe. The information may be statistically useful, but it also changes the future behavior that the ranking is supposed to predict.

That is a classic reflexive measurement problem:

> **the metric does not merely describe the field; publication of the metric changes the field.**

The OAA problem has therefore migrated from personal profiles into live competition.

## 5.3 Embodied capacities that OAA underweights become decisive

The island's tasks reveal capacities not well represented by ordinary academics or headline athletic scores.

Ike's outdoor competence, Hondō's cultural knowledge, navigation, endurance, fishing, food management, improvised cooperation, and emotional steadiness become consequential. The exam does not abolish hierarchy; it changes which dimensions can become valuable.

This is one reason a second-pass ability model must remain multidimensional. A student who is a low academic performer can be locally generative in an environment designed differently.

## 5.4 The school is simultaneously referee, sensor network, insurer, and archive

Students depend on the institution to:

- record movement;
- validate tasks;
- manage health checks;
- decide withdrawal;
- calculate points;
- adjudicate misconduct;
- preserve histories;
- and determine whether an account is credible.

That concentration of functions is efficient when the institution is trustworthy.

V03 shows why it is dangerous when one administrator is an adversary.

# 6. Truth, proof, and the authored record

## 6.1 Witness testimony and machine record diverge

Shinohara's testimony is direct: a third party caused the fall.

The GPS record fails to identify that third party.

The school has good procedural reasons not to treat one distressed student's account as conclusive. But V03 also gives the reader reasons not to equate lack of machine confirmation with falsity.

The distinction is:

- **truth**: what actually happened;
- **testimony**: what a witness says happened;
- **proof**: what can be established under the institution's evidentiary rules;
- **record**: what the institution stores as the history of the event.

These can diverge.

## 6.2 The invisible attacker problem disciplines identity inference

Nanase appears to recognize the hidden figure she chases. The figure is extremely fast. Those are material clues.

They are not a license to name the person in a V03-local artifact.

The project's White Room protocol must therefore be applied to ordinary attack attribution as well:

> **unusual capacity + suspicious timing + privileged access is evidence; it is not identity proof.**

## 6.3 Chabashira's investigation is real institutional resistance

Chabashira does not simply accept the record. Later hostile-administrator dialogue reveals that she has been tracing histories.

That is evidence that adult counterweights are not fictional. Teachers can notice anomalies and investigate.

But the same scene shows the limit: Shiba can say that the history has been altered so it will not lead back to them.

This moves `Y1H-037` and `Y1H-040` together. Counterweights exist, but auditability fails when the administrator being audited controls the audit substrate.

## 6.4 Rule provenance becomes more important than rule text

V02 already showed that Tsukishiro could force an unusually dangerous exam through resistance.

V03 is worse. He claims the practical ability to fabricate reasons for disqualification because he occupies the rule-management side.

The institutional legitimacy problem is therefore no longer:

> Are the written rules fair?

It is:

> **Who can alter the evidence that decides whether the written rules were violated?**

That is a problem of sovereign provenance.

# 7. Ayanokōji Kiyotaka - hidden authorship and the offered hand

V03 is the clearest early Year 2 volume for separating Ayanokōji's four layers without pretending they have fused into one mature design.

## 7.1 Ordinary adolescent - school life is not erased by survival

He remains capable of ordinary pleasure, embarrassment, attraction, annoyance, curiosity, and friendship while under a credible expulsion threat.

The island is therefore not "real Ayanokōji returns and normality disappears." Ordinary life and strategic survival coexist.

The first kiss with Kei is the strongest evidence. He does not narrate it solely as experiment. He discovers an unfamiliar bodily sensation and wants the contact to last. That is ordinary adolescent experience occurring inside a relationship he also analyzes.

## 7.2 Relational layer - he increasingly lets other people act

He trusts Horikita to judge her own limit.

He credits Horikita rather than himself for Sudō's fundamental development: `「言っておきますけどオレじゃないですからね」` followed by `1つのキーではあったかも知れないが、本質的に須藤を大きく変えたのは堀北だ。`.

He entrusts Ike's group to Sudō because he now judges Sudō the calmest decision-maker present: `「須藤、池たちを頼む。この中で一番冷静な判断が出来るのはおまえだ」`.

These are not trivial compliments. They show Ayanokōji recognizing capacities that can operate without him.

## 7.3 Strategic layer - uncertainty is managed rather than abolished

He monitors Nanase, suspects an observer, asks Chabashira for quiet record checking, coordinates rescue contingencies, withholds some information from first-years, and calibrates how much of his own ability becomes visible.

Crucially, he does not convert every suspicion into identity certainty. That restraint is part of his competence.

## 7.4 Environmental-author layer - information withholding becomes developmental intervention

The Ike/Shinohara sequence is the volume's clearest example.

Ayanokōji has a rescue plan. He chooses not to tell them because he believes certainty would weaken their commitment. He is not inventing Shinohara's danger. He is **editing access to hope** so the real danger retains maximum motivational force.

This is development without fabricated crisis, but not development without manipulation.

The distinction matters for `Y1H-001`.

Ayanokōji has moved beyond the most extreme Year 1 model in which he could manufacture suffering as curriculum. Yet he still believes superior understanding can justify controlling the informational environment in which another person becomes capable of action.

## 7.5 The hand to Nanase - moral difference and structural resemblance

After Nanase explains her grief, Ayanokōji thinks that he and his father are the same in essence. The claimed decisive difference is the capacity to extend a hand rather than discard the foolish or failed.

This is morally meaningful.

Atsuomi retaliated against Matsuo's family to preserve control. Ayanokōji refuses to punish Nanase for having been manipulated into attacking him. He gives her a way to remain at school and redirect her agency against the person who exploited her.

But the narration after the gesture prevents self-congratulation. Usefulness is not necessary; even if she becomes disposable, what matters is using her well.

The strongest V03 judgment is therefore:

> **Ayanokōji is developing an ethic of rescue without yet abandoning an ontology of instrumentality.**

That is more precise than either "he is becoming compassionate" or "he is just like his father."

## 7.6 The V03 threshold

The old first-pass synthesis described Year 2 broadly as the controlled child becoming the controller.

V03 finally makes a narrower version of that thesis locally defensible:

> **Ayanokōji is no longer only controlling his own exposure. He is demonstrably authoring selected motivational and relational environments around other people.**

What V03 still does **not** establish is a mature school-wide experimental design. That belongs to later evidence if later evidence supports it.

# 8. Nanase Tsubasa - grief, justice, performed identity, and revised allegiance

Nanase receives the volume's largest interpretive correction.

## 8.1 Her hostility is sincere

The correct response to discovering Tsukishiro manipulation is not to retroactively label all of Nanase's earlier care fake.

Her grief is real. Her anger is real. Her concern for weaker students is also real. The contradiction is the character.

She can sincerely believe that Ayanokōji must be stopped and sincerely ask why anyone would need a reason to help an injured person.

## 8.2 `Watashi` and `ボク` are functional identities

Nanase's pronoun split is not decorative voice acting.

`Watashi` marks the self that cannot sustain violence. `Boku` is the self she builds to authorize vengeance.

The performed self becomes physically useful. It changes style, aggression, and confidence.

The important analytical rule is:

> **psychological performance can produce real behavioral difference without proving separate personhood.**

This matters beyond Nanase because COTE repeatedly concerns performed social selves. The series does not require a persona to be "fake" in order for it to be constructed.

## 8.3 Matsuo Eiichirō is not a White Room identity clue

At the exact V03 boundary, Matsuo is the key to Nanase's grief, not a code proving White Room origin.

Ayanokōji explicitly finds it strange that a White Room student would invoke Matsuo. Nanase later admits she knows little about the White Room.

The correct identity transition is therefore:

- **Tsukishiro-linked operative:** confirmed;
- **personal grievance:** confirmed and explained;
- **White Room origin:** downgraded in plausibility, still not logically impossible only because the wider mystery remains open;
- **the actual White Room student:** unresolved.

## 8.4 Her causal model collapses without her grief disappearing

Nanase acknowledges that Ayanokōji was not the true moral author of Matsuo's destruction. She needed an object onto which she could direct unbearable anger.

That admission is psychologically mature because it distinguishes:

> the person who triggered a chain in a causal story

from

> the person who chose the wrongful acts inside that chain.

Atsuomi and Tsukishiro become more legible as authors of coercion; Ayanokōji becomes less defensible as a revenge target.

## 8.5 Allegiance changes because belief changes

Nanase's movement from hostile operative to proposed collaborator is not a hidden-all-along reveal.

It is a real transition produced by:

- defeat;
- collapse of the `ボク` self-suggestion;
- causal reappraisal;
- Ayanokōji's refusal to punish her;
- and her existing desire to prevent further victims.

This should be classified as **DEVELOPMENT / REVERSAL**, not `REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_STATE`.

# 9. Kei Karuizawa - first kiss, genuine desire, and curated intimacy

## 9.1 The kiss is canonically ordinary before it is analytically suspicious

The first interpretive obligation is not to erase the romance.

Ayanokōji experiences the texture of a girl's lips for the first time. He wants the kiss to continue. Kei's jealousy is recognizable girlfriend anxiety rather than simply a trauma symptom. Their private meeting and physical closeness belong to the ordinary school life he values.

## 9.2 The kiss is also used

The next obligation is not to erase strategy.

Ayanokōji consciously recognizes that a powerful new romantic memory is an efficient way to displace Kei's fear about Nanase.

The act therefore carries at least three simultaneous functions:

1. genuine mutual intimacy;
2. Ayanokōji's own experiential learning about romance;
3. deliberate emotional regulation of Kei.

The three functions can coexist without one invalidating the others.

## 9.3 Dependency and autonomy remain mixed

Kei is not merely waiting to be rescued. She is performing a security function for Ayanokōji, tracking contacts who may be suspicious and communicating information.

But she does not possess enough information to evaluate the full risk surrounding him or herself.

Thus `Y1H-008` becomes more strongly "both," while `Y1H-009` remains open with increasingly serious negative evidence about disclosure sufficiency.

## 9.4 Romantic development is not proof of relational equality

The relationship is more mutual at the level of desire than it was at the beginning of Year 1.

It remains sharply asymmetric at the level of information and developmental pacing.

That distinction should remain stable in every later synthesis.

# 10. Ike, Shinohara, and Komiya - the right not to be substituted

The Shinohara crisis is one of V03's most humane counterpoints to hidden authorship.

Komiya, injured and himself romantically interested in Shinohara, tells Ike simply: `「男を見せろ」`.

That moment refuses possessive rivalry. Komiya's desire does not become jurisdiction over Shinohara. He recognizes that Ike is the person who must now act.

Ayanokōji reaches the same conclusion from another direction: neither he, Sudō, nor Nanase can produce the words Ike needs to say. The relationship cannot be outsourced to a more competent speaker.

Ike's line is clumsy by romantic-comedy standards but ethically stronger for being his own:

`「ある! 大ありだ! 俺はおまえを退学にさせたくない、だから助けるんだよ!」`

It does not promise ownership or demand reciprocation. It states why **he** will act.

That makes the scene an important answer to the project's authorship problem:

> **Some human outcomes lose their meaning if a superior actor substitutes for the person who must choose and speak.**

Ayanokōji understands this at the level of action even while manipulating the informational environment around it.


# 11. Sudō Ken - development becomes delegated responsibility

Sudō's V03 role is easy to miss because he is not the center of a major fight. Longitudinally, that is exactly why it matters.

Ayanokōji tells Hoshinomiya that Horikita, not he, fundamentally changed Sudō. Later, when Ike's group needs a reliable decision-maker, Ayanokōji selects Sudō as the calmest person present.

The movement from volatile weak-link to delegated protector is not merely higher test performance. It is **social usability of developed character**.

Sudō's growth now benefits people who are not the original object of his romantic motivation. That is evidence that the development is becoming more self-sustaining and generative.

This also provides a counterexample to the fear that every successful Ayanokōji-adjacent development must remain dependent on Ayanokōji. Sudō is increasingly able to carry responsibility without the developer remaining in the room.

# 12. Kōenji Rokusuke - possessed ability becomes socially available by choice

## 12.1 The island finally gives Kōenji an environment worth entering

Kōenji has always demonstrated a gap between possessed ability and collective availability.

V03 does not show him suddenly becoming capable. It shows existing capacity becoming **publicly usable** under a self-chosen contract.

That distinction is essential. His island performance should be classified primarily as `REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_STATE` and `RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC_EXPRESSION`, not development in raw ability.

## 12.2 Reputation gives the Horikita bargain teeth without giving it coercive force

The V02 agreement cannot literally force Kōenji to cooperate. V03 says so directly. Yet breaking the agreement would damage his ability to demand help from a class whose leader is increasingly recognized.

The obligation therefore becomes relational:

> Horikita cannot own his capacity, but Kōenji cannot demand collective assistance while treating every promise to the collective as meaningless.

This is the strongest current partial answer to `Y1H-032`.

## 12.3 Tug-of-war is not a definitive power ranking

Ayanokōji judges their arm force roughly equal in the immediate contest: `この状況から察するに、オレと高円寺の腕の力はほぼ互角なようだ。`. He also emphasizes Kōenji's exceptional body: `痛いほどに縄が指に食い込み、腕の力が互角であるという事実について改めて考える。高円寺という男の身体能力はやはり桁違いだ。高校生の中では群を抜く須藤やアルベルトでも、この男の前では遥かに見劣りする。超高校生級という肩書すら生ぬるいだろう。`.

But Ayanokōji intentionally loses because allowing the currently high-ranked classmate to collect the points is strategically better.

The scene therefore proves neither "Kōenji is stronger than Ayanokōji" nor the reverse.

It proves:

- Kōenji's displayed physical power is extraordinary;
- Ayanokōji's estimate of him must be revised upward;
- and competition outcomes can be authored by incentives rather than raw ability alone.

That last point belongs directly to the V03 attribution thesis.

## 12.4 The body becomes editorial spectacle

The illustration of Kōenji pouring water over an exaggeratedly muscular body is not subtle. It editorially foregrounds embodied exceptionalism, self-regard, and physical abundance.

The visual layer makes his capacity legible before the narrative fully explains its limits.

# 13. Ryūen, Katsuragi, and Sakayanagi - constraint, coalition, and reciprocal bargaining

## 13.1 Katsuragi is already changing how Ryūen's polity makes decisions

When Ryūen is prepared to overextend for a marginal point, Katsuragi stops him: `「俺が止めた」`.

Ayanokōji's commentary is explicit that Ishizaki or Kaneda would not have been able to make Ryūen stop. Katsuragi can.

This is the strongest local movement yet for `Y1H-025`:

> **voluntary allied judgment can begin constraining Ryūen's coercive political style without requiring Ryūen to cease being the leader.**

It does not prove constitutionalized restraint. Katsuragi has influence, not a veto codified by rule. But the polity is no longer simply one man's force radiating outward.

## 13.2 Ryūen still speaks in utility language, but utility can motivate preservation

When Komiya and Kinoshita are endangered, Ryūen quickly agrees to a rescue strategy. His language remains harsh and instrumental; the injured classmates are still useful.

That should not be romanticized into tenderness.

It should also not be dismissed as meaningless. A leader who once treated weakness as something readily exploitable is now willing to coordinate resources to preserve members because they remain part of the class's future capacity.

The ethical question remains whether a person whose usefulness drops to zero retains equivalent protection.

## 13.3 Sakayanagi's cooperation remains contractual rather than charitable

Sakayanagi does not provide help for free merely because a rival asks.

She accepts symmetrical reciprocal terms. This is consistent with her polity: centralized judgment, explicit value, reliable bargains.

The second-year rescue structure therefore emerges not from moral convergence among Horikita, Ryūen, Sakayanagi, and Ayanokōji, but from **overlapping interests made contractible**.

That is politically important. Cooperation need not require shared ideology.

# 14. Ichinose Honami - solidarity as logistics, initiative, and hostage vulnerability

V02 showed Ichinose's solidarity becoming strategically articulate. V03 tests whether that solidarity can operate materially on an island.

## 14.1 Care becomes logistical infrastructure

Her class does not express solidarity only through reassuring dialogue. Students create food and support nodes, invest in cooking equipment and supplies, and share resources beyond the narrowest group interest.

That is **solidarity as logistics**.

The political value is substantial. A polity whose members can reliably feed, orient, and support one another lowers the cost of uncertainty.

## 14.2 Ichinose chooses risk rather than merely absorbing it

When her watch fails and she loses the normal GPS layer, Ichinose still attempts to contribute to the Sakayanagi alliance rather than simply retreating into safety.

This is relevant to the persistent criticism that her political style is purely passive or reactive. V03 gives direct evidence of agency under uncertainty.

## 14.3 The same solidarity becomes a hostage surface

Tsukishiro identifies the vulnerability instantly.

He does not need to threaten Ichinose alone. He can threaten an innocent group from her class and rely on her refusal to sacrifice classmates for information.

This is one of the most important corrections to any simplistic "trust is power" thesis.

> **A strong relational bond is simultaneously a coordination asset and a coercive attack surface.**

Ichinose's ethics create political capital under ordinary conditions and political vulnerability under an adversary willing to punish unrelated third parties.

## 14.4 The volume does not yet decide what she will do with the knowledge

The local boundary ends before Ichinose's final choice about disclosure can be adjudicated.

Her silence under threat should therefore not be pre-labeled cowardice, prudence, betrayal, or strategic delay.

The source has not yet given the answer.

# 15. Kushida, Yagami, Amasawa, Tsubaki, and the politics of secret collateral

The first-year field becomes much more political in V03, but not more ontologically clear.

## 15.1 Tsubaki reads concealed action without proving institutional origin

Tsubaki recognizes that Yagami has been secretly moving against Ayanokōji and forces him into greater disclosure.

That is strong evidence of political perception.

It is not White Room evidence.

The project should resist the same error OAA encourages: turning an accurate profile of one capacity into a total identity claim.

## 15.2 Yagami emerges as a covert political operator

Yagami's polite public presentation now coexists with:

- secret contact with Kushida;
- knowledge of first-year pressure networks;
- an explicit desire to remove Ayanokōji;
- a plan to use Kushida as a recording asset;
- and willingness to target other first-years who possess Kushida's secret.

The correct V03 classification is **hidden plotter / coercive information broker**.

The volume still does not authorize "therefore White Room student."

## 15.3 Kushida's secret becomes transferable leverage

Kushida's deepest vulnerability is no longer merely that one classmate knows her past. Her past becomes information other actors can trade, threaten to expose, or use to recruit her into operations.

This is the political maturation of the Year 1 public/private-self problem.

A secret identity is not only psychologically costly. It can become **collateral**.

## 15.4 Amasawa possesses abnormal informational reach

Amasawa intercepts Kushida before she can capture the confrontation on video. She knows details of Kushida's past and other contacts that Kushida did not personally disclose to her.

She states that Ayanokōji is a `特別な人` - special person - to her: `「うんうん、開き直った方が良いと思う。だけどさ、大ありなんだよね。だって綾小路先輩はあたしにとって特別な人なんだから」`.

When Kushida asks whether she likes him, Amasawa refuses ordinary romantic vocabulary and reaches for something stronger than love.

The important classification is **privileged knowledge + extraordinary attachment + physical capacity**.

That is powerful identity/allegiance evidence.

It is still not, at this volume boundary, permission to write "Amasawa is the White Room student" as settled fact unless the source itself makes that identification explicit. V03 does not do so in the relevant scenes.

## 15.5 Physical domination does not defeat Kushida's motive

Amasawa overwhelms Kushida physically and strips away her public voice.

Kushida nevertheless does not psychologically surrender her goal of removing people who know too much.

That is `CONTINUITY`, not resolution. The question of whether her public and private selves can coexist remains open.

# 17. Tsukishiro and Shiba - arbitrary sovereignty over evidence

## 17.1 Shiba is not an institutional counterweight

The V02 project still allowed the possibility that some adults around Tsukishiro might serve as constraints.

V03 establishes Shiba as a collaborator willing to alter histories and contemplate severe physical violence against a student.

His rubber gloves make the threat bodily and practical rather than abstract.

## 17.2 Tsukishiro's restraint is strategic, not rights-based

Tsukishiro prevents immediate violence against Ichinose, but he does not do so because she possesses an inviolable right against harm.

He replaces direct violence with hostage coercion: if she speaks, an innocent C-class group can be removed by an administratively manufactured reason.

The result is colder than a simple assault.

It makes rule management itself the weapon.

## 17.3 The hostile administrator can exploit institutional trust

Students must rely on official records and rule adjudication to survive the exam.

Tsukishiro's power is therefore greatest not when he breaks the system openly, but when he can make abuse **look like ordinary system output**.

That is why falsification matters more than raw violence for the series' institutional analysis.

## 17.4 Chabashira and Majima remain morally relevant but structurally weak

Both adults show resistance: Majima objects to permissive violence; Chabashira investigates suspicious histories.

Their failure to control Tsukishiro should not erase the distinction between colluding and resisting adults.

But the practical conclusion is severe:

> **a conscientious subordinate cannot reliably audit a hostile sovereign who controls the record layer.**

# 18. Japanese voice, register, and the language of authorship

## 18.1 `Seigi` - justice as self-authorization

Nanase's prologue repeatedly uses `正義` not as an objective narrator label but as the framework a person adopts so she can continue fighting.

The wording `だから自らを『正義』の旗の下に正当性を主張して戦おうとする。` followed by `自らの正義がある限り、その信念を抱くことで戦い続けられる。` makes justice function as a psychological authorization mechanism.

This does not mean justice is fake. It means moral language can become part of the machinery through which action becomes possible.

## 18.2 `Watashi / ボク` - grammatical self-positioning as behavioral trigger

The pronoun switch is central evidence. Nanase says `ボク`; attack style and aggression intensify. Ayanokōji later interprets the distinction as deliberate self-suggestion.

Japanese self-reference therefore does more than mark gendered or stylistic voice here. It becomes a **switch between authored modes of agency**.

## 18.3 `Shiru kenri` - a right to know that originates in the speaker's desire to confess

After the confrontation, Nanase begins with `senpai ni wa 知る権利` - "you have the right to know" - then corrects herself toward "I want you to know": `「そうですね、先輩には知る権利───いえ、私が知ってもらいたいと思っています」`.

That self-correction is ethically significant.

"You have a right" frames disclosure as the recipient's entitlement.

"I want you to know" reclaims disclosure as the speaker's act.

The shift turns confession from compliance into authorship.

## 18.4 `Tsukai suteru` - rescue does not yet eliminate disposability

Ayanokōji's phrase about using Nanase even if she becomes something disposable is one of the volume's most important lexical warnings.

The term keeps the White Room comparison alive inside his supposed moral distinction from his father.

He can extend a hand and still preserve an instrumental vocabulary.

## 18.5 `Shinrai` - trust is both social capital and coercive surface

Trust appears across several scales:

- route leadership under dangerous visibility;
- class cooperation;
- first-year claims about reliable informants;
- Nanase's observation of Ayanokōji's relationships;
- Ichinose's class cohesion.

V03's contribution is to show that trust is not inherently protective. The stronger the trust network, the more an adversary may be able to threaten one node to control another.

## 18.6 `Uso` and the asymmetry of lying

The volume repeatedly stages accusations of lies: Shinohara may be lying; Nanase may be performing; exam scores may be false; administrators can alter histories; Kushida performs one face while acting through another.

Yet the most dangerous falsehood is not an individual's verbal lie. It is **institutionally authored evidence** that other actors must treat as neutral fact.

# 20. Formal and literary strengths

## 20.1 The unfinished exam is used productively rather than apologetically

The afterword confirms that the special exam exceeds a single volume. Instead of treating V03 as half a story, the novel gives this half a coherent epistemic movement: students enter the field believing they know the rules, then progressively lose confidence that visible outcomes reveal causal truth.

## 20.2 Geography converts information into drama

The island is not merely a large battle map.

Distance, terrain, rain, device limits, missed designated areas, search cost, and isolation determine what can be known and what can be proven.

The physical setting is therefore part of the epistemology.

## 20.3 The volume repeatedly pairs sincere acts with strategic functions

The strongest examples are:

- Kei's first kiss: desire + emotional regulation;
- Nanase's help: genuine care + hostile mission;
- Ayanokōji's rescue of Nanase: mercy + recruitment;
- Ike's declaration: self-authored feeling inside an engineered motivational environment;
- Ichinose's solidarity: care + exploitable hostage surface.

This prevents easy moral sorting.

## 20.4 Pronoun performance becomes plot mechanism

Nanase's `私 / ボク` divide is formally elegant because a linguistic marker becomes a psychological action technology. The reader can hear identity management happen in grammar.

## 20.5 The record-falsification reveal retroactively changes how earlier uncertainty is read

Once the text establishes that hostile administrators can alter histories, earlier apparently neutral absences in the record become less reassuring.

The key is not to overcorrect. The reveal does not prove every inconvenient record false. It changes the **prior probability** the analyst should assign to institutional neutrality.

# 21. Critical reservations and counterreadings

## 21.1 Do not over-medicalize Nanase

The text itself considers and rejects a simple DID-style explanation. The safest reading is not a clinical diagnosis but a narratively explicit self-induced persona used to authorize aggression.

Psychological realism should be discussed without pretending the novel provides a psychiatric assessment.

## 21.2 Do not turn every Ayanokōji intervention into proof of a grand school-wide experiment

V03 clearly establishes local environmental authorship.

It still does not establish the mature total design described by later first-pass synthesis. The analyst should resist reading future architecture backward.

## 21.3 Do not treat the kiss as "actually manipulation"

The strategic function is explicit.

So is the desire.

A reading that declares one layer the truth and the other a mask is weaker than the text.

## 21.4 Do not treat Nanase's downgrade as solving the White Room mystery

Removing one suspect category does not identify the remaining actor.

Amasawa, Yagami, Tsubaki, and other first-years acquire suspicious evidence of different kinds. The correct status remains unresolved unless the volume names the identity.

## 21.5 Do not identify the Komiya/Kinoshita attacker from later memory

V03 deliberately withholds the name. The local artifact must preserve that ignorance.

## 21.6 Do not equate Katsuragi's influence with democratic constraint

He can stop Ryūen in one operational decision. That is significant.

It is not yet an institution in which followers possess stable rights against the leader.

## 21.7 Do not romanticize Ayanokōji's "hand" without reading the next sentences

The volume itself supplies the counterevidence. Compassion and instrumentality coexist.

## 21.8 Do not interpret Tsukishiro's willingness to spare Ichinose as legal restraint

He substitutes hostage coercion for immediate violence and explicitly asserts the ability to manufacture rule violations. The restraint is tactical.

# 29. Final synthesis

Year 2 Volume 3 begins with a girl constructing a justice strong enough to let her fight and ends with administrators discussing how to construct a record strong enough to make wrongdoing untraceable.

Those two acts belong to the same thematic family.

Both concern the production of a framework that makes action possible.

Nanase cannot sustain vengeance as `私`, so she authors `ボク`.

Tsukishiro cannot openly expel Ayanokōji under ordinary legitimacy, so he authors exceptional conditions, covert incentives, and eventually the possibility of fabricated institutional cause.

Ayanokōji cannot simply tell Ike what to feel, so he authors an information environment in which Ike's own feeling must remain urgent.

Yagami cannot attack openly without consequence, so he authors Kushida's position as a recording asset.

Kushida cannot tolerate people holding her secret, so she accepts new alliances whose purpose is to erase witnesses.

Kōenji will not give the class ordinary jurisdiction over him, so Horikita authors a contract under which he can choose to make extraordinary capacity public.

Katsuragi cannot constitutionally overrule Ryūen, but his recognized competence becomes enough to alter Ryūen's decision.

Ichinose's class authors a logistics network from trust; Tsukishiro authors a hostage threat from the same trust.

The volume's political world is therefore not divided into people who manipulate and people who do not.

It is divided by **how environmental authorship treats the other person's remaining agency**.

Horikita's bargain with Kōenji is visible, bounded, and answerable.

Katsuragi's constraint on Ryūen is conversational and can be accepted or rejected.

Ike's words to Shinohara cannot be delegated because the meaning depends on his being the speaker.

Ayanokōji's hidden rescue plan is not answerable because Ike does not know the information has been withheld for his development.

Tsukishiro's threats are radically unanswerable because he controls the rule and record system through which objection would have to travel.

That gradient is more useful than a binary manipulation/non-manipulation vocabulary.

V03 also makes identity itself an attribution problem.

Nanase's `ボク` is real enough to change behavior, but not real in the ontological sense she claims during the fight. Matsuo Eiichirō is the object through whom grief is organized, not the proof of a supernatural occupation or White Room origin. Her prior hostility was not fake merely because the causal belief supporting it was wrong.

Likewise, Amasawa's extraordinary knowledge is real evidence without being a complete identity file. Yagami's covert operation is real evidence without proving institutional origin. The hidden attacker is a real causal problem without a locally named solution.

The analyst must therefore practice the discipline the novel demands from its characters:

> **attribute only what the available evidence can bear.**

This is why the Komiya/Kinoshita incident is structurally central. Shinohara's testimony, physical traces, GPS absence, Nanase's pursuit, school procedure, Chabashira's investigation, and later record falsification are all different evidence layers. None can be collapsed into the others.

The event demonstrates the mature version of the V01 principle:

> **The file is evidence about the event. The file is not the event.**

And V03 adds a darker corollary:

> **When the authority that owns the file is also a participant in the conflict, the file may become an authored weapon.**

Ayanokōji's personal arc in the volume is the ethical analogue of that institutional problem.

He increasingly understands development. He can see when not to intervene. He credits Horikita for Sudō rather than claiming ownership. He recognizes that Ike must speak for himself. He allows Nanase's own revised understanding to become the basis of cooperation rather than forcing submission through violence.

Those are real gains.

But he also withholds hope to maintain productive fear. He uses a desired kiss to regulate Kei's emotional state. He offers Nanase mercy and immediately thinks about how to use her even if she becomes disposable.

The emerging problem is no longer whether Ayanokōji can help people grow.

He plainly can.

It is whether his superior capacity to model another person's development can become **answerable to the person whose life is being modeled**.

That is where V03 connects most strongly to the frozen Year 1 thesis.

Year 1 asked whether understanding grants jurisdiction.

V01 asked who becomes legible.

V02 asked who may bind what legibility reveals.

V03 now asks:

> **Who gets to write the causal story explaining why a person acted, changed, failed, loved, fought, or disappeared - and can the subject answer back when that story becomes the basis for power?**

The exam is not finished.

The White Room student is not identified.

The hidden attacker is not identified.

Ichinose's response is not resolved.

Kōenji's final result is unknown.

Tsukishiro's final plan has not yet unfolded.

Those are not deficiencies in the analysis. They are the correct prospective state at the end of a volume whose author explicitly says the examination continues into another book.

The second pass should therefore preserve uncertainty as evidence of form rather than treat it as missing information to be repaired by hindsight.
