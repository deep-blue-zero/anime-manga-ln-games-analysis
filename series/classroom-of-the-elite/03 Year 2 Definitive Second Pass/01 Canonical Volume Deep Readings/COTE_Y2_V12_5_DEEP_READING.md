## Year 2 Volume 12.5 — Second-Pass Deep Reading
### Separation as proof, counter-authorship at the boundary, and the difference between surviving a developer and becoming independent of him

# 1. Scope, provenance, and year-boundary discipline

Volume 12.5 is not merely an after-story to the year-end special examination. It is the volume in which Year 2 converts its long-running **succession/separation problem** from stated intention into irreversible institutional action. V06 supplied the `補助輪` metaphor: Ayanokōji understood himself as training wheels that should eventually come off Horikita's class. V10–V12 increasingly made the four-class ecology explicit as his self-chosen educational project. V11 introduced a provisional stopping rule—`ここから先は、オレの立ち入る領域じゃない`—for intervention that recognizes another person without claiming ownership over the answer. V12 then showed how easily formal choice can still become an authored control surface when Ayanokōji manages information, timing, stakes, and relational dependence.

V12.5 asks the question that all of those earlier findings were preparing:

> **When the developer finally leaves, do the people he developed continue because he successfully authored their independence—or do they become independent only when they can reinterpret, resist, or exceed the lesson he intended?**

The difference is not semantic. If Kei behaves exactly as Ayanokōji hoped after he breaks the relationship, that behavior may demonstrate acquired competence while also demonstrating how deeply his expectations have become internalized. If Horikita continues after he transfers classes, that future may demonstrate succession while still beginning from a separation she did not meaningfully consent to because he never told her it was coming. By contrast, when Ichinose rejects the two outcomes Ayanokōji has prepared and produces a “third answer” that **he himself does not possess**, development becomes counter-authored in a much stronger sense.

# 2. Executive thesis — separation is not proof; counter-authorship is

The strongest conceptual progression is:

> **V12 — relational jurisdiction / authorship without command → V12.5 — separation as proof / counter-authorship at the boundary.**

The compact thesis is:

> **Year 2 ends by converting Ayanokōji's long-planned succession experiment into irreversible separation. Yet separation alone does not prove autonomy. Kei performs the independence he was trying to teach while collapsing after he leaves; Horikita asks him to remain precisely as he silently exits; Sakayanagi turns institutional defeat into an out-of-school future; and Ichinose gives the strongest answer of all by rejecting the binary he prepared and producing a third path he himself could not predict. V12.5 therefore makes counter-authorship—not mere survival after the developer—the strongest local test of development. Ayanokōji's transfer removes the training wheels, but because he has authored the board, concealed the departure, and entered the weakest former class as its new strategic center, the ethical problem is not solved. He relinquishes one position of control by assuming another.**

This yields a terminal Year-2 distinction among three forms of “successful development”:

1. **Authored compliance after development** — the target can perform the desired behavior, but the developer's expected answer still organizes the choice.
2. **Succession competence** — the target or institution can continue functioning after the developer leaves, but may still be responding within a field whose terms the developer authored.
3. **Counter-authorship** — the developed actor can generate an answer the developer did not supply, did not predict, or cannot legitimately claim as the realization of his own plan.

The third is the strongest evidence of independence because it makes the developer **answerable to the developed person**, rather than merely making the developed person capable of carrying out the developer's hoped-for future.

V12.5 gives all three forms. Kei is the most painful example of the first. The former Sakayanagi class is initially a negative succession case: it cannot self-reproduce Sakayanagi's leadership and moves toward a leader vacuum until Ayanokōji offers another central organizing intelligence. Horikita reaches the threshold of the second but cannot yet be judged on post-separation performance within the V12.5 boundary. Ichinose supplies the clearest third case.

The year-boundary answer is therefore:

> **Development survives the developer most convincingly where it becomes counter-authored.**

# 4. `舞台の裏側` — ANHS is politically permeable, not a sealed experiment

The opening chapter recontextualizes the year-end special examination from the outside. Hoshinomiya had known the **originally scheduled** exam, which would have left teachers some room to intervene. The school abruptly substituted the unusual hidden-role examination because an external political observer arrived (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine11:para0008-para0018`).

The visitor is Prime Minister Kijima. His presence is not decorative. The school's examination architecture itself is responsive to elite political observation. ANHS therefore cannot be modeled as a laboratory sealed from national politics; it is an educational institution whose governance, prestige, observation regime, and performance can be altered by actors outside the student-class system (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine11:para0031-para0067`).

Kijima's interest in post-expulsion follow-up is also important for the long-running `EXPULSION_DISPOSABILITY` thread. The school can expel someone, but the political observer explicitly asks what happens afterward. That question echoes the Airi and Kamuro findings: institutional removal is not ontological deletion. The person's future remains analytically relevant even when the institution's competitive ledger no longer contains them.

The chapter also establishes an April meeting involving Kōenji's father, foreshadowing—within V12.5 itself—the later parent/child chapter. The year boundary is thus framed from the start by **multiple authors of educational environments**: the school, national political leadership, business/family power, Atsuomi's White Room network, and students such as Ayanokōji who increasingly design developmental conditions at a local level.

For Hoshinomiya, however, the external view initially deepens despair. She sees Ayanokōji as Chabashira's unbeatable `ジョーカー`. That framing matters because her adult grievance has already appropriated students into her own unresolved past. V12.5's later Hoshinomiya material will not erase the rivalry, but it will change the permissible means through which she pursues it.

# 5. `それぞれの答え合わせ` — accountability after Maezono

The immediate Horikita-class aftermath is one of the most important corrections to any triumphalist reading of V12. The class has effectively reached A, yet the room cannot celebrate because Maezono is in the middle of expulsion processing (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine12:para0021-para0035`). The rules technically permit a 20-million-point rescue, but the class cannot assemble that sum in time. Procedure makes the outcome final without making it morally uncontested.

Ayanokōji then controls the **public narrative of causality**. He explains Maezono's information leaks through Hashimoto, but he does not give the class a complete account of his broader purpose. More revealingly, his interior narration states that he deliberately wants a mixed reception: enough explanation for the class to function, while leaving some students with continuing distrust because that distrust may become useful later. This is a compact demonstration of `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD` as environmental authorship. He is not merely deciding what is true. He is deciding **how much of the truth each audience should possess and what political distribution of belief will best serve future plans** (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine12:para0070-para0150`).

Sudō asks the question V12 itself left open: was there a path to victory without expelling anyone? Ayanokōji's own assessment is that a no-expulsion victory was **not impossible**; it was simply less probable. That admission materially strengthens the V12 conclusion that Maezono's removal was disproportionate. The choice was not forced by physical impossibility. It was chosen because it improved the reliability of the larger plan.

Hirata names the constitutional problem more clearly than almost anyone else: the representative's decision comes close to `越権行為`. A class representative does not automatically possess sovereign authority to sacrifice a classmate merely because the exam permits that action. This is exactly the distinction the Year-1/Y2 architecture has tried to preserve between **ability to act** and **legitimate jurisdiction**.

The class also notices a troubling pattern. Yamauchi, Airi, and now Maezono were all expelled through events in which Ayanokōji played a decisive role. Kushida publicly defends him as someone willing to do the dirty work others avoid; privately, she correctly suspects that Maezono's expulsion served a purpose larger than the simple probability of winning. Her own cooperation remains self-interested: she wants a stable place in which she will not become the next target. That is not hypocrisy. It is precisely the adversarial/self-authored cooperation V06 made legitimate. Kushida can contribute without converting gratitude, friendship, or moral approval into fictitious premises.

The Ichinose-class “answer checking” is harsher. Ichinose returns physically and psychologically shattered and apologizes; the class's reflex is immediate consolation. Himeno recognizes the old conformity pressure but still cannot make herself intervene at the decisive moment. Kanzaki has moved from loyal opposition into exhaustion. Hoshinomiya then explodes into the vacuum, refusing the possibility that Ichinose should resign and shifting toward the fantasy that **the teacher** will find an external way to rescue the class.

The irony is severe: Ichinose's class is morally cohesive enough to console its leader, yet precisely that cohesion has historically made dissent difficult. V12.5 will not solve that contradiction by turning the class into a culling polity. It will instead make Ichinose's leadership more adversarial and more explicit about uncertainty.

# 6. `また今度` — Ryūen loses the fight he wanted, Sakayanagi chooses the defeat she owns

V12 ended with the paradox `龍園 翔 は負けた。／そして坂柳 有栖も負けた。` V12.5 explains why both statements matter.

Ryūen explicitly refuses to treat the official result as the kind of victory he wanted. He knows he lost the **merit contest**. The final official reversal occurred because Sakayanagi reconstructed Ayanokōji's hidden preference and chose to satisfy it. For Ryūen, that outcome is corrosive because his Year-2 development had increasingly moved from “win by any means” toward the desire to prove that the person he had become could defeat a strong opponent on meaningful terms (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine13:para0036-para0098`).

Sakayanagi confirms the V12 interpretation without surrendering her own agency. Ayanokōji wanted Ryūen left in school as the Year-3 opponent. She understood this and complied. She does not claim mind control; instead, she argues that if either she or Ryūen could be “controlled,” their own insufficiency made that possible. That is an unusually mature allocation of responsibility: Ayanokōji authored the relational signal, Sakayanagi authored her response, and Ryūen must live with an official win that does not erase his substantive defeat.

Sakayanagi's description of Ayanokōji is similarly important. She does not romanticize him as a saint. His root motivation remains self-oriented; he can hurt, manipulate, sacrifice, or expel people when doing so serves the purpose he has chosen. Yet she also thinks ANHS has changed him. The White Room child who treated removed peers as effectively finished is no longer identical with the student who seeks ordinary relationships, out-of-school promises, and a future he might one day want to change with his own hands.

Her final strategic act toward Ryūen is therefore relational rather than institutional: she intends to help create a **weakness** in Ayanokōji that may make him beatable. Even rivalry has become developmental and relational. Ryūen is no longer simply a predator who wants the strongest opponent alive; he experiences the loss of Sakayanagi as the disappearance of a rival whose existence had become part of his own horizon.

`また今度`—“next time”—is thus more than a farewell phrase. Sakayanagi's institutional future ends, but her personhood is not exhausted by ANHS membership. She intends to visit Kamuro, apologize, and continue living outside the competitive system. The story finally gives one of its most elite-identified characters a future that is not reducible to class rank.

# 7. `空席の王座` — aristocratic optimization fails the succession test

The former Sakayanagi class provides the cleanest institutional answer to one of the longest-running handoff questions.

Sakayanagi's voluntary withdrawal carries a **-300 class-point penalty**. The class falls to roughly 800 points and loses not only its sovereign but the political assumption that the sovereign will always be there to decide. Morishita says the quiet part directly: if the class now complains that it cannot function without Sakayanagi, that complaint proves the extent to which it had been **dependent on her** (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine14:para0060-para0170`).

The problem is more severe than losing 300 points. It is succession failure. Sakayanagi's political design optimized decision quality by centralizing judgment in an exceptional person. Once that person's private priority overrides fiduciary duty and she leaves, the polity lacks an internally generated successor. Morishita estimates only about a ten-percent chance of recovering Class A by their own strength and begins considering a private-point strategy that could rescue only a small number of individuals. Collective ambition threatens to dissolve into managed abandonment.

This makes `Y1H-029` locally answerable in the negative:

> **Aristocratic optimization did not survive sovereign private priority.**

The class does not simply lose because Sakayanagi made one bad tactical decision. It reveals that the constitution had not adequately separated **the leader's excellence** from **the polity's capacity to reproduce governance after the leader**.

Yamamura and Morishita nonetheless demonstrate that the polity is not empty. Yamamura's grief reflects genuine attachment after V11's recognition repair. Morishita's eccentric attention to kabuto-ebi, her refusal to perform conventional mourning, and her willingness to think probabilistically about the class all reinforce V09.5's relational-plurality insight: people exceed the political roles the leader assigned them.

But latent plurality is not yet institutional succession. That difference explains why Ayanokōji's later arrival will be both rescue and indictment. The class agrees to finance his transfer because it still wants to fight; that consent is real. Yet needing a new singular strategic center to replace the old singular strategic center confirms the weakness that Sakayanagi's departure exposed.

# 8. `先回り` — answerable staging through witnesses

V12.5's Hoshinomiya intervention is one of the strongest positive contrasts to V12's secret relational jurisdiction.

Ayanokōji does not simply confront Hoshinomiya in private and rely on superior psychological control. He deliberately constructs a **witness architecture**. Chabashira, Mashima, and Sakagami are brought into the evidentiary field under separate pretexts and listen in real time. Hoshinomiya is allowed to articulate how far she is willing to go; neutral adults hear it; and Ayanokōji distinguishes testimony from sufficient proof rather than pretending one conversation gives him omniscient authority (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine15:para0180-para0380`).

This matters because the intervention becomes **auditable**. If he later lies about what happened, the account does not belong to him alone. The scene therefore advances `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD` beyond mere exposure: provenance can itself function as a constraint on the stronger actor.

Hoshinomiya's motive also becomes clearer. She tells herself that she is fighting for Ichinose's class, but her deepest fixation is preventing Chabashira from escaping their old wound by graduating from Class A. She wants Sae to remain guilty. She is willing to risk her own teaching career if doing so lets her preserve that emotional jurisdiction. Present students become instruments in a decades-old adult relationship.

Ayanokōji's immediate objective is strikingly conservative: **do not remove Hoshinomiya**. Losing the homeroom teacher at that moment could further destabilize Ichinose's class. He therefore uses the witnesses primarily as deterrence and containment, not as a justification to expel or destroy the adult wrongdoer.

Later, Hoshinomiya says Ayanokōji persuaded her to retract the illegal path. The exact persuasive mechanism is intentionally not shown. The canonical reading must preserve that gap. What is established is the boundary outcome: she still wants to defeat Chabashira, but she now commits to doing so **fairly**, because the `ジョーカー` is no longer in Chabashira's hand (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine22:para0018-para0049`).

This is not moral purification. Rivalry remains. What changes is jurisdiction: she stops claiming the right to cheat current students' futures in order to settle an adult grievance.

# 9. `幻想` — Kei, romance as curriculum, and the failure of behavioral autonomy as a sufficient test

The breakup chapter is one of the most important relationship documents in the entire Year-2 corpus because it finally adjudicates not merely whether Kei becomes more independent, but **what kind of independence Ayanokōji has been trying to produce**.

Before the breakup, his interior narration confirms that separation was part of the relationship's architecture from the beginning. He describes the relationship as a mutual curriculum: Kei would be moved away from parasitic dependency toward greater independence, while he would learn romance, love, and eventually breakup. The post-breakup possibilities—truancy, withdrawal, successful recovery—are also objects of curiosity. The relationship is genuine lived experience and experiment at the same time.

Yet the narration also contains a crucial crack in prediction. Before the planned ending, Ayanokōji **hopes his forecast will fail**. He hopes some uncalculated emotion might stop him, that he might embrace Kei rather than complete the schedule. That wish matters for `AYANOKOJI_ORDINARY_LIFE` and `AYANOKOJI_FREEDOM`: he increasingly desires to be surprised by himself. But wanting an uncontrollable feeling is not the same as having surrendered control of the situation.

The ordinary date is real. Movies, popcorn, handholding, karaoke, shared routines, and the familiarity of being together are not fake simply because one participant privately assigns them curricular functions. The later rice-bowl scene confirms that the relationship has entered his embodied ordinary life even after it ends.

## 9.1 The point-of-view trap

The chapter then executes a deliberate narratorial deception. A long interior sequence appears to describe sudden Ayanokōji regret: racing heartbeat, inability to say the breakup words, the realization that he loves the person in front of him. At `COTE:Y2V12.5:spine16:para0280`, the spoken `別れよう` breaks the apparent alignment. The subsequent lines reveal that the preceding romantic interior was **Kei's hoped-for counterfactual**, her `妄想` and `願望`, not Ayanokōji's reversal (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine16:para0240-para0293`).

This is a major `AYANOKOJI_NARRATION` event. The text temporarily makes the reader inhabit the emotional answer Kei wants, then denies it. The effect is not only shock. It dramatizes the central epistemic asymmetry of the relationship: Kei has spent the relationship trying to infer an interior she was never given enough direct information to know.

## 9.2 Kei performs independence because she thinks he expects it

Kei's own narration is painfully precise. She realizes that he likely never loved her in the way she loved him. She understands the relationship as “half for me, half for him,” but also recognizes that Ayanokōji tends to treat his own design as absolute. She therefore reconstructs the breakup as something scheduled from the start (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine16:para0304-para0323`).

Then comes the most important autonomy problem. Kei desperately wants to cling, beg, and promise anything. She does **not** do so because she believes resistance would betray **his expectation** that she become capable of standing alone (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine16:para0324-para0334`). When he offers an explanation, she refuses it.

That refusal cannot be treated as proof of informed reciprocal choice. The informational asymmetry is the very reason the question exists. Kei declines explanation while cognitively overwhelmed and while trying to perform the developmental answer she thinks Ayanokōji wants from her. Her behavior is agentic, but the norm governing that behavior has been deeply authored by the person leaving her.

This is the negative Year-2 answer to `Y1H-009`:

> **No: the romance ends without Kei receiving enough information for genuinely reciprocal choice.**

Her agency is not erased. She successfully controls the public presentation, refuses to degrade herself in front of him, and later has a genuine friendship network around her. But the chapter closes with the internal collapse that behavioral observation cannot see. She wonders whether she performed independence well enough and, once alone, begs the absent Kiyotaka for help (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine16:para0370-para0393`).

The resulting principle is central to the entire second pass:

> **Behavioral independence is not emotional independence. A person can perform the developer's desired endpoint while remaining internally organized around the developer's judgment.**

Ayanokōji's later public choice to accept the story that **Kei dumped him** is genuinely protective of her social standing. The relationship therefore does not reduce to abuse or falsity. Care, instrumental design, social protection, ordinary memory, and severe informational asymmetry coexist.

# 10. `卒業後も` — Sakayanagi exits the institution without surrendering futurity

Sakayanagi's formal departure is one of the clearest positive cases of **life beyond institutional defeat** in Year 2.

Her father could plausibly try to use institutional authority to block the withdrawal. He refuses. He respects her decision and the school's rules (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine17:para0016-para0019`). That makes the exit genuinely hers even though Ayanokōji's V12 relational signal contributed to the official defeat that activated the prior pact.

Ayanokōji also refuses false guilt. Sakayanagi accepts that the final withdrawal decision belongs to her (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine17:para0069-para0081`). This allocation is important: causal influence does not require fictionalizing her into a puppet, while her agency does not absolve him of having designed the influence.

Her first farewell request then turns outward. She asks Ayanokōji to promise that they will meet again after he leaves school. She knows about the White Room environment but insists:

> `今ここに立っているあなたの人生は他でもないあなたが決めることです`

His reply remains aspirational rather than triumphant. He says perhaps someday he will be able to think that way, and privately recognizes that the promise could become useful if he ever genuinely wants to change his future with his own hands (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine17:para0097-para0118`).

This is a major strengthening of `AYANOKOJI_FREEDOM`. School life has generated a possible **outside-school relational future** that Atsuomi did not need to assign. But it cannot close the freedom question because the next chapter immediately reasserts Atsuomi's postgraduation plan and coercive capacity.

Sakayanagi's second request concerns Hashimoto. She cannot cleanly decide whether to forgive him and delegates future judgment to Ayanokōji (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine17:para0122-para0129`). That delegation is itself an interesting residue of sovereign politics: even while leaving, she transfers some unfinished jurisdiction to another exceptional judge.

Ayanokōji then deletes the compromising photograph he had retained as counter-leverage. The leverage is no longer operationally necessary (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine17:para0133-para0139`). The deletion is ethically preferable to retention, but it does not retroactively make the original creation of the leverage legitimate.

The farewell with Yamamura completes V11's recognition arc: Yamamura cries for perhaps her first genuine friend, and the relationship extends outside ANHS through the promise to meet again (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine17:para0157-para0159`). Institutional separation does not destroy relational continuity.

## 10.1 A troubling counterpoint: the transfer plan begins with false attribution

Immediately afterward, Ayanokōji tells Morishita and Yamamura that Sakayanagi left “important advice” for the former A class. His narration then states directly that this is false: the plan is **his own**, not Sakayanagi's (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine17:para0163-para0178`). Morishita correctly detects the provenance problem and asks why such advice would be routed through him.

This is a crucial terminal `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD` result. The class-transfer project that will supposedly produce more autonomous competition begins through **misattributed authority**. Ayanokōji uses Sakayanagi's residual legitimacy to make his own plan more acceptable to a polity in crisis.

The year therefore does not end with a clean ethical conversion from manipulation to transparent development. The stronger actor is learning stopping rules and generating real counter-authors while continuing to exploit provenance when it helps him assemble the desired board.

# 11. `親と子、子と親` — three generations of conditional freedom

This chapter expands environmental authorship beyond ANHS more radically than any ordinary school chapter.

## 11.1 Atsuomi's postgraduation curriculum remains live

Kiyotaka tells Atsuomi that emotionally their relation does not resemble an ordinary father/son bond. Atsuomi nevertheless has a very specific postgraduation future prepared: Kiyotaka is to become the next White Room leader and produce a generation that surpasses him (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine18:para0174-para0185`).

Atsuomi promises roughly one more year of noninterference with school life, but the surrounding threat structure makes clear that this is **permission inside continuing coercive jurisdiction**, not emancipation. If Kiyotaka genuinely rebels after graduation, Atsuomi retains the power and willingness to impose consequences far beyond ordinary family conflict (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine18:para0194-para0205`).

Yagami is being re-educated; Atsuomi describes excessive emotion as something to be removed. Amasawa may remain in school but is still expected to return to institutional comparison afterward. Kiyotaka's concern for both as something like younger siblings (`弟分`) is therefore meaningful precisely because the White Room still classifies them primarily through developmental utility (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine18:para0211-para0238`).

Atsuomi can even treat Kiyotaka's apparent humanization as useful capital if it remains controllable. This is Volume 0's political genealogy returning in present tense: the institution can absorb the language of development so long as the product remains deployable.

Kiyotaka responds by telling Atsuomi something he has not made fully visible to the school: he does **not** intend simply to graduate from the top class. He intends to transfer downward and preserve uncertainty because an unknown outcome is itself valuable to him (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine18:para0244-para0251`). That school purpose is self-chosen in a strong local sense, even though his postgraduation future remains contested.

## 11.2 Kōenji — generative ignorance as a competing theory of intelligence

Kōenji's confrontation provides one of the best philosophical counterpoints to White Room maximal education. He recognizes that Ayanokōji's Maezono/class-victory explanations are partly pretexts for pursuing what he personally wants and anticipates the coming class separation (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine18:para0313-para0350`).

More importantly, Kōenji says he deliberately avoids absorbing every conventional answer so that he can preserve the possibility of finding **his own answers** (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine18:para0364-para0372`). This is almost the inverse of White Room epistemology. The White Room tries to eliminate ignorance by filling the student with maximal prior solutions. Kōenji protects some ignorance because over-complete inheritance may crowd out generative discovery.

That does not make Kōenji morally superior. His worldview remains harsh toward the weak, and his self-regard is extreme. But as an ability theory he supplies an important distinction:

> **Knowing more existing answers is not identical to being more capable of authoring a new answer.**

Ichinose's third answer later demonstrates the same principle in relational/political form.

## 11.3 Kōenji's father and Atsuomi — freedom as a conditional reward

The meeting among Kijima, Kōenji's father, and Atsuomi makes the generational symmetry explicit. Kōenji's father defines “true freedom” for his son through a condition: preserve the current class as A through graduation. Atsuomi then proposes a wager in which Kiyotaka's ability to prevent that outcome becomes leverage for future political access (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine18:para0460-para0554`).

The irony is stark. Kōenji presents himself as the freest student in the school, yet his father still defines the condition under which that freedom will be recognized. Kiyotaka has spent two years seeking a self-authored life, yet Atsuomi treats that life as a useful trial period and postgraduation asset. Kiyotaka, in turn, has built a four-class environment whose participants are meant to develop inside conditions he selected.

The terminal Year-2 comparison is therefore:

> **Three generations of “freedom” are authored through somebody else's conditions: Atsuomi over Kiyotaka, Kōenji's father over Kōenji, and Kiyotaka over the school ecology.**

The ethical question is not whether conditions can ever exist—no social life is conditionless. It is whether the subject can reject the condition, reinterpret it, produce another purpose, and remain a person whose future is not owned by the author.

Tsukishiro's observation that Kiyotaka uses women's feelings in ways resembling Atsuomi is admissible testimony, and Atsuomi's “use anything to survive” response is revealing. But the reader-level retrospective discipline remains: resemblance of technique does not establish identical telos, self-conscious imitation, or identical capacity for future change.

# 12. `お祝い` — ordinary-life residue and the weakness he still will not disclose

The morning after Kei's exit contains one of the quietest pieces of evidence that the relationship mattered materially to Ayanokōji's ordinary life. He begins preparing a second bowl of rice through **muscle memory** before remembering that there is no longer another person in the room (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine19:para0002-para0019`).

This does not prove the romantic love Kei wanted. It proves something subtler: repetition has written another person into his embodied routine. Ordinary-life counter-curriculum can leave traces even when Ayanokōji's explicit emotional taxonomy judges the curriculum unsuccessful.

He also permits the public story that Kei dumped him because this protects her social position. Again, the relationship produces real care even alongside the severe disclosure failure documented in the breakup chapter.

Horikita then gives `Y1H-035` perhaps its strongest negative test. She is not asking for strategic information. She worries about **his heart** after Airi, Maezono, and the other burdens he has carried. She explicitly offers to support him if he is hurting (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine19:para0105-para0124`).

His interior answer is that he does not experience the pain she imagines; he evaluates whom to preserve and whom to erase mechanically. Outwardly, however, he tells her that if it ever becomes painful he will say so. This is not a revelation of weakness. It is another deferral.

Thus `Y1H-035` remains open. V12.5 gives him an unusually clear opportunity to place himself under another person's protective authority, and he does not take it.

Horikita's own interior state changes. A playful “lover candidate” exchange is followed by a bodily response she cannot classify: accelerated heart rate and an unfamiliar emotional disturbance (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine19:para0131-para0175`). The source does **not** locally require a definitive romance label. What is certain is epistemic novelty. Horikita encounters a response to Ayanokōji that her existing categories do not explain.

That uncertainty should be preserved, especially because the next major step is separation rather than confession.

# 13. Ishizaki and Hiyori — the ordinary future Ayanokōji does not choose

Before the central Ichinose encounter, V12.5 shows Ayanokōji a remarkably plausible ordinary-life alternative.

Ishizaki wants him in Ryūen's class not only because of ability but because he considers him a friend. Hiyori's invitation is similarly relational rather than purely strategic. Ayanokōji recognizes that if his only objective were to spend the final year **enjoying school in the ordinary way he originally wanted**, Ryūen's class might be the most enjoyable destination (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0107-para0187`).

This is a major `AYANOKOJI_ORDINARY_LIFE` result. The desired life is no longer abstract. It is literally available in front of him through people who want his company.

He declines it.

That refusal should not be read as proof that ordinary life was false. It shows that his later self-chosen developmental experiment has become a competing value strong enough to override the easiest fulfillment of the earlier dream. Freedom can involve choosing the more difficult purpose rather than simply maximizing comfort.

Hiyori's interaction also remains characteristically low-jurisdiction. Her relational invitation does not need coercion, threat, or grand explanation. The scene carries romantic embarrassment but does not amount to a definitive romantic confession; the canonical claim is narrower: Hiyori wants him in the class and her desire is legible as personal as well as political.

# 14. `約束の夜` — `介錯` becomes a defeated binary

The Ichinose chapter is the conceptual summit of V12.5 and arguably the strongest single payoff to the second-pass authorship framework.

Ayanokōji begins from a position of enormous informational and developmental asymmetry. Ichinose has been isolated since V12. He is waiting to see what remains. He defines `介錯` in its dual register—killing and attending/supporting—and offers a terminal political solution: he can join her class as its new leader and guarantee a path to A, but **Ichinose must withdraw** (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0285-para0418`).

He then reveals something more disturbing. The earlier advice that Ichinose should “remain herself” also helped preserve her forty-person class as a possible future takeover target. The environment he appeared merely to support was being kept in a strategically usable state.

He makes the terms harsher still: if he takes the class, he will not guarantee the survival of all thirty-nine other students. Even people close to Ichinose may be expelled if he judges them unnecessary (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0430-para0435`).

The apparent binary is therefore:

1. accept his rescue, withdraw, and allow him to cull if necessary; or
2. reject him, remain leader, and convert betrayal/hatred into fuel for resistance.

Ayanokōji predicts the second at about **99 percent** because Ichinose cannot abandon her classmates. But crucially, he does not actually want either answer. He wants a **third answer he himself does not possess** (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0436-para0474`).

## 14.1 Ichinose defeats the developmental binary

Ichinose recognizes the trap almost immediately. She says neither “withdraw” nor “do not withdraw” contains the true answer. She wants to lose no one; she knows her own power alone is insufficient; but she does not simply demand that Ayanokōji transfer in while she remains. The narration states that she articulates **a method for maintaining the four-class balance through her own thought** and reaches the one-percent outcome Ayanokōji wanted but could not supply (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0480-para0504`).

The source intentionally does **not** spell out the exact strategy in direct expository terms. The canonical reading must not invent it. What matters locally is structural:

- the answer is generated by Ichinose;
- it preserves the refusal to sacrifice her classmates;
- it addresses the four-class equilibrium problem;
- and Ayanokōji himself recognizes that she has acquired the leadership element she previously lacked.

This is the strongest counter-authorship event of Year 2.

Ayanokōji designed the dilemma. He cultivated contradictory emotions. He expected to observe the result. But the outcome most strongly proving development is precisely the one he **cannot claim to have authored as content**, because he did not have it.

That transforms `介錯`. The Year-1 question `Y1H-024` asked what “kaishaku” would mean if Ichinose's class collapsed. V12.5 shows that the premise has become inadequate. `介錯` is no longer simply mercy-killing, rescue, or authoritative judgment over whether her leadership should live or die. It becomes a **developmental jurisdiction claim that Ichinose defeats by answering outside the judge's binary**.

The correct transition is therefore `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED → REFRAMED`, not a simplistic “Ayanokōji saved her.”

## 14.2 Ichinose counter-authors the encounter itself

The scene then reverses authorship a second time. Ayanokōji assumed he had forced the decisive encounter by coming to her room. Ichinose reveals that she predicted **he** would come. Her unanswered messages, unlocked door, prepared room, and prepared appearance were part of her own expectation structure (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0513-para0527`).

The observer discovers he has also been observed.

This is exactly the kind of reciprocity the series often withholds from him. Ichinose cannot match his total informational advantage, but she can model his curiosity and developmental compulsion well enough to make his “spontaneous” arrival part of **her** plan.

She then explicitly criticizes his ethics. Even if hurting, breaking, and repairing people eventually produces rescue, doing so unilaterally is not thereby right. She understands that Kei and others have been made to dance on his palm (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0530-para0543`).

Her answer is not purity or withdrawal. It is reciprocal jurisdiction:

> `綾小路くんが私を利用するように、私もあなたを利用する。その権利はあるよね?`

Ayanokōji admits he lacks the right to deny that proposition (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0549-para0553`).

The balance is still unequal. Ichinose remains intensely attached; important truths remain undisclosed; Ayanokōji possesses far greater historical and institutional knowledge. “Mutual use” does not magically equalize power. But for the first time a target of his developmental authorship **names the method, refuses its one-sided legitimacy, and claims reciprocal causal rights over him**.

The subsequent scene is framed as a mutually chosen intimate encounter and as an “absolute contract” of needing and being needed rather than as the formal beginning of a conventional romantic relationship (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0560-para0596`). Because the characters are minors, this document does not reproduce or elaborate the scene's physical detail. Analytically, the important point is relational: Ichinose does not accept the role of passive subject after the V12 violation. She insists on leaving a mark in Ayanokōji's interior by **her own will**.

That is counter-authorship, though not symmetry.

# 15. Ichinose's class after the third answer — solidarity hardens without becoming culling

Ichinose's reappearance before classmates is visibly different. She is recovered enough to lead, but she does not return to the older form of unconditional reassurance.

She insists that she would not withdraw simply to escape the class's current situation. Only an extraordinary counterfactual—if everyone else could be guaranteed Class A through her withdrawal—could make self-expulsion acceptable (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0714-para0729`). Her commitment to collective survival therefore remains strong.

She publicly admits her love for Ayanokōji and, more importantly for class politics, reveals that his true capability is far beyond the class's earlier assumptions (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0790-para0809`). This is a significant change in `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD`: rather than protecting her own image by concealing the humiliating asymmetry of V12, she exposes strategically relevant information to the polity she leads.

Her promise also becomes more accountable. She does **not** promise that she will certainly deliver everyone into Class A. She promises to restore the class to a position where it can **contest** for A (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0851-para0869`). The difference is constitutional. Hope is retained without converting aspiration into a false guarantee.

Kanzaki then discovers that the warmer Ichinose is not simply the same leader restored. She knows he asked Horikita to throw the exam. Her gentle invitation to “ask me when worried” carries unmistakable political force (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine20:para0884-para0908`).

It would be reductive to call this merely a “dark Ichinose.” Her solidarity has not become a culling constitution. What changes is that care can now coexist with deterrence, strategic opacity, and the capacity to confront disloyal action rather than simply absorb it.

V09 answered whether solidarity could gain adversarial competence without losing itself. V12.5 adds the year-boundary formulation:

> **Solidarity becomes politically durable when it can impose boundaries without defining expendability as its first answer.**

# 16. `目標` — Horikita asks the training wheels to remain as they disappear

The final Horikita-class celebration is the symbolic culmination of the V06 succession metaphor.

Kei is absent. Her friends' colder attitude toward Ayanokōji is interpreted by him as a **good sign**: Kei has horizontal relationships organized independently enough that loyalty to her can damage their relationship with him (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine21:para0003-para0007`). Hirata likewise commits himself to caring for Kei and the broader class, then offers Ayanokōji help if **he** ever becomes lost (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine21:para0020-para0029`). The care network is no longer vertically centered on Ayanokōji.

Horikita wants to believe Maezono's expulsion was simply “correct.” Ayanokōji refuses that moral simplification. He tells her that she does not need to force herself into a clean judgment; people should struggle with irreducibly dirty answers and keep trying to approach something better (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine21:para0031-para0043`). That is one of his more responsible pedagogical statements precisely because it does **not** claim moral omniscience.

The class then articulates Year-3 goals. Ike, notably, has converted Maezono's expulsion into a reason to take his own future more seriously. Horikita's goal is to trust both herself and her classmates and graduate from A (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine21:para0054-para0080`).

When she asks Ayanokōji for his own goal, he postpones the answer. She tells him that his presence is genuinely supportive, wants to become a classmate he recognizes, and asks him to remain beside her and watch over the class (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine21:para0083-para0096`).

But he is already gone.

Horikita looks for him and discovers that he has silently left the celebration. She experiences an inexplicable loneliness/pain, and he never returns (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine21:para0097-para0105`).

This is V06's `補助輪` metaphor enacted in narrative form. Yet it is **not clean succession**.

Horikita chooses a future in which Ayanokōji remains nearby at exactly the moment he is already executing the opposite future. The separation is therefore developmentally intentional but not informed. V12.5 cannot establish that her leadership “survives without hidden Ayanokōji security,” because the post-transfer year lies outside the source boundary. What it establishes is that the security layer has finally been withdrawn.

The year ends the relationship state with a paradox:

> **Horikita is more capable of standing without him than ever before, but she is not given the truth that would let her author the meaning of his departure before it happens.**

# 17. `新たなる幕開け` — the class transfer is collectively consented rescue and renewed concentration

The final chapter first closes the Hoshinomiya containment arc. She retracts the plan to use illegal means, attributes that change to Ayanokōji's persuasion, and insists that she will still try to defeat Chabashira—but now through fair competition. The exact persuasion remains off-page and must not be invented (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine22:para0018-para0049`).

Then the institutional separation becomes literal.

A staff member tells Chabashira that one of her students has just completed a class transfer. Ayanokōji enters the former Sakayanagi class and introduces himself as a transfer student who spent **20 million private points** (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine22:para0056-para0079`).

The financing is analytically crucial. The transfer uses:

- Ayanokōji's own private points;
- the transfer fund Hashimoto had accumulated;
- and an equal contribution of the remaining shortfall from class members who **all agree** to provide it.

This is therefore not a unilateral takeover of an unwilling polity. The receiving class materially consents to the rescue. Its consent is understandable: Sakayanagi's exit has left a leader vacuum, the class has fallen sharply, and Ayanokōji offers a credible path back into competition.

But consent does not erase the institutional critique. The former Sakayanagi class failed to produce an internal successor and survives by importing another exceptional strategic center. Ayanokōji's own narration also continues the language of utility: a class full of high-ability students will be useful for controlling multiple boards (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine22:para0080`).

He then declares the four-class preparation effectively complete. The arrangement is not perfectly ideal, but the final year can now begin with all four classes still in the contest (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine22:para0081-para0084`).

The ethical ambiguity is the point:

> **Ayanokōji removes himself as one class's central hidden security layer by becoming another class's overt strategic center.**

He relinquishes one position of authorship by assuming another.

Separation therefore cannot itself prove that he has learned to relinquish control. The stronger proof will come only if the people and polities around him can create outcomes he did not prescribe and if he can allow those outcomes to stand.

# 19. Development versus ethical legitimacy — V12.5 refuses to let success settle morality

A core methodological gain of the V2 reread is the strict separation of three questions:

1. **Did development occur?**
2. **Did the target exercise agency?**
3. **Was the developmental arrangement ethically legitimate?**

V12.5 repeatedly answers these differently.

| Case | Development | Agency | Ethical legitimacy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kei breakup | real growth in social self-control and broader ties | real | compromised by predetermined timetable and disclosure insufficiency |
| Ichinose third answer | major leadership development | exceptionally strong | Ayanokōji's preceding experimental manipulation remains ethically compromised; her counter-authorship improves reciprocity rather than retroactively legitimizing him |
| Sakayanagi withdrawal | relational/self-authorship development | strong | self-withdrawal is voluntary, but private desire imposed severe class externalities |
| Former A-class transfer | restores competitive possibility | collective consent is real | rescue also reconcentrates strategic authorship; provenance was initially manipulated through false Sakayanagi attribution |
| Hoshinomiya containment | adult behavior changes | meaningful re-choice | witness architecture is relatively answerable; exact hidden persuasion remains unknown |
| Horikita separation | succession test finally enacted | Horikita has no informed choice over timing | ethically compromised by nondisclosure even if future development proves successful |
| Kōenji | strong self-authored epistemic stance | strong | his own freedom philosophy remains indifferent to some weaker others; autonomy is not identical to virtue |

This table prevents a recurrent analytical failure: treating a successful outcome as retroactive proof that the stronger actor was right to engineer it.

The Year-2 corpus now supports a stronger rule:

> **Developmental efficacy is evidence about capability, not moral title.**

Ayanokōji can be extraordinarily good at producing growth without therefore acquiring jurisdiction to decide what growth another person should undergo, what information they should lack, or when separation should occur.

# 20. Ayanokōji four-layer update at the Year-2 boundary

## 20.1 Ordinary adolescent layer

This layer is no longer speculative. V12.5 contains ordinary relational residues and desires that have no need to be reduced to strategy:

- preparing the second rice bowl out of habit after Kei is gone;
- recognizing Ryūen's class as the place where an ordinary enjoyable final year would be easiest;
- participating in farewell/celebration rituals;
- imagining an out-of-school reunion with Sakayanagi;
- purposeless, playful conversation with Horikita;
- and the accumulated experience of friendship, dating, embarrassment, food, travel, games, and failure from earlier volumes.

The problem is not whether ordinary life affected him. It plainly did. The problem is whether ordinary desire can become strong enough to override his environmental-author layer when the two conflict.

## 20.2 Relational layer

The relational layer is simultaneously more developed and more dangerous.

Ayanokōji can:

- protect Kei's social standing after breakup;
- understand Sakayanagi's future as more than institutional utility;
- care about Yagami/Amasawa in a sibling-like register;
- recognize that Ryūen class friendship would be enjoyable;
- offer Ichinose genuine attention while also experimenting on her;
- accept reciprocal use from Ichinose;
- and experience Horikita as someone with whom purposeless interaction has value.

Yet relationship is also the medium through which his deepest authorship works. The better he knows a person's needs, the less he needs overt command.

## 20.3 White Room / strategic layer

The White Room layer remains extraordinarily stable. He can still classify, cull, lie about provenance, model dependency, and think across multiple classes while maintaining ordinary social presentation. Atsuomi's postgraduation plan also keeps the White Room politically present even after the direct Year-2 retrieval threat has receded.

Kōenji's “own answers” philosophy and Ichinose's third answer expose a potential limitation of this layer: maximal prior modeling can be outperformed, at least locally, by generative answers that the model did not contain.

## 20.4 Environmental-author layer

This is now fully self-conscious. Ayanokōji wants four classes to remain competitively viable, has intentionally preserved and altered their developmental conditions, and executes a class transfer to continue the project from a new position.

The layer has also acquired a paradoxical aspiration: **he wants an answer he cannot predict**. That aspiration is ethically significant because it points beyond total authorship.

But it is not yet surrender. He still designs the experiment in which the unpredictable answer is requested.

The Year-2 boundary therefore leaves the layer with its final contradiction:

> **He increasingly wants people to exceed his authorship, while continuing to claim the right to construct the conditions under which they must exceed it.**

# 21. Ability and generativity — “own answers” become the missing dimension of `実力`

The Year-2 reread began by separating possessed, displayed, measured, socially usable, developmental, political, and generative ability. V12.5 makes **generative ability** impossible to treat as a secondary category.

Kōenji explicitly values preserving the capacity to find his own answers rather than absorbing every existing answer. Ichinose produces a solution to the four-class/leadership dilemma that Ayanokōji does not possess. Sakayanagi imagines an out-of-school future not reducible to the Class-A system. Kei's challenge is partly the opposite: she is strong enough to enact the expected answer, but not yet clearly free of the expectation that defines what “strong enough” should look like.

The resulting `実力` formulation is:

> **The highest form of ability is not merely solving a difficult problem. It is producing a legitimate new answer when the available problem statement was authored by somebody else.**

That is why counter-authorship is both an ethical and ability concept. It protects personhood while also identifying a kind of intelligence that centralized measurement and training systems systematically underestimate.

# 22. Ordinary-life counter-curriculum — not an escape from institutions, but a source of alternative ends

By the Year-2 boundary, ordinary life should no longer be described merely as a “break” from strategy.

Ordinary experiences have generated ends the governing institutions did not fully assign:

- Kei's independent friendships;
- Sakayanagi's wish to reunite after school;
- Yamamura's first meaningful friendship;
- Hiyori and Ishizaki wanting Ayanokōji around because they like him;
- Kiyotaka's desire for purposeless conversation;
- Horikita's bodily response she cannot fit into strategic categories;
- the physical habit of making food for another person;
- and the possibility that a post-ANHS future might contain relationships chosen for themselves.

The counter-curriculum is therefore not anti-institutional in the simple sense. ANHS itself stages many ordinary experiences. The crucial property is **semantic openness**: the experience can mean something the institution did not prescribe.

This is where ANHS remains a meaningful local alternative to the White Room despite political contamination and severe competitive rules. The school can assign class competition; it cannot completely dictate what skiing, books, romance, cooking, friendship, heartbreak, or a farewell will become to the person who experiences them.

# 24. Truth, proof, record, and provenance

V12.5 contains four different evidence regimes:

1. **Selective public narrative** — Ayanokōji tells Horikita's class enough about Maezono to stabilize the polity while intentionally preserving some distrust.
2. **Audited witness architecture** — Hoshinomiya's plan is heard by multiple adults, reducing Ayanokōji's monopoly over the record.
3. **False attribution** — Ayanokōji presents his own transfer plan as Sakayanagi's final advice to borrow her residual authority.
4. **Counter-observation** — Ichinose correctly models Ayanokōji's own compulsion to observe her and prepares for his arrival.

These regimes are not morally equivalent. The witness architecture is the strongest because the actor with superior strategic ability does not exclusively own the evidence. False attribution is the weakest because provenance is deliberately corrupted to smooth acceptance.

The broader Year-2 principle is:

> **A claim becomes more answerable when the evidence required to challenge it is not controlled by the same actor whose power depends on the claim.**

This is the institutional counterpart to relational disclosure. Both ask whether the weaker participant possesses enough independent evidence to reinterpret the stronger actor rather than merely accept the supplied account.

# 27. Revision ledger — what V12.5 strengthens, revises, and rejects

## PRESERVE

- **Ordinary life is a genuine counter-curriculum.** The second rice bowl, friendships, farewells, future reunion, and purposeless conversation all preserve the claim that ordinary experience creates values not reducible to competition.
- **Development and ownership must remain separate.** Ayanokōji's efficacy does not grant moral jurisdiction.
- **White Room origin/allegiance/purpose remain distinct.** Atsuomi's current plans do not make every Kiyotaka school purpose a hidden White Room purpose.

## STRENGTHEN

- **Succession/separation is Ayanokōji's central self-chosen educational project.** The class transfer makes the design institutional fact.
- **Generative ability matters independently of stored ability.** Kōenji's epistemic philosophy and Ichinose's third answer strengthen the distinction.
- **Relational stakes survive institutional exit.** Sakayanagi/Kamuro/Yamamura, Kei's embodied residue, and future reunion all demonstrate this.
- **Truth/provenance is part of political legitimacy.** Hoshinomiya witness architecture versus false Sakayanagi attribution gives a direct contrast.

## REVISE

- **“Development survives the developer” is too weak as the highest test.** Revise to: *development most strongly demonstrates independence when it becomes counter-authored and can answer the developer back.*
- **“Kei becomes independent after breakup” is too coarse.** Revise to: *Kei demonstrates behavioral self-control and horizontal social resources while emotional dependence remains acute; her performance of independence is itself partly organized by Ayanokōji's expectation.*
- **“Ayanokōji leaves Horikita so the class is independent” is premature.** Revise to: *the separation test begins; local evidence does not yet show post-transfer class performance, and Horikita was denied informed participation in the timing.*
- **“Ichinose is rescued by Ayanokōji” is inadequate.** Revise to: *Ayanokōji creates a developmental crisis; Ichinose's strongest recovery occurs by defeating his binary and claiming reciprocal jurisdiction.*
- **“Sakayanagi's class is resilient because of elite personnel” is downgraded.** The polity fails internal succession and needs outside centralization.

## DOWNGRADE

- Any claim that **formal consent alone establishes legitimate authorship**. V12.5 repeatedly shows that consent can coexist with severe prior asymmetry, authored dependency, or lack of alternatives.
- Any claim that **an institutionally removed person is narratively finished**. Sakayanagi's out-of-school future makes the opposite explicit.

## REJECT

- **“Successful development retroactively validates the method.”** V12.5 decisively rejects this inference. Kei, Ichinose, and the class-transfer ecology all separate developmental result from ethical title.
- **“The Year-2 endpoint resolves Ayanokōji's authorship problem.”** It does not. He changes seats on the board while continuing to design the board.

## OPEN

- Whether Ayanokōji can allow an unwanted counter-authored outcome to stand when he could still correct it.
- Whether ordinary relational desire can acquire enough weight to override his environmental-author project.
- Whether he can voluntarily reveal helplessness and let another person become his protector rather than his subject/partner/opponent.
- Whether his postgraduation future can become meaningfully self-authored against Atsuomi's coercive political plan.

# 28. Final Year-2 fiction-boundary thesis

At the end of Year 2, Ayanokōji has achieved something the White Room's strongest ideology could not easily model.

He has helped produce people who are not merely stronger but **differently authored**:

- Sudō can continue developing after Horikita's approval ceases to be the motive.
- Kushida can cooperate for herself while preserving hostility.
- the post-Ayanokōji Group can survive by ceasing to organize itself around him.
- Ichinose can answer a developmental dilemma with a solution he did not possess.
- Sakayanagi can carry rivalry and relationship outside the school institution.
- Kōenji protects his own answer-generating epistemology against curricular saturation.
- Horikita's class has enough distributed ability that Ayanokōji is willing to remove himself as terminal security.

That is genuine development.

But the White Room genealogy has not disappeared. Ayanokōji still chooses environments, withholds decisive information, manipulates provenance, experiments through relationship, and treats people/classes as variables in a four-class design. His transfer is not abdication. It is a relocation of authorship.

The Year-2 governing question asked whether a person who escaped being another institution's curriculum could become an environmental author without reproducing ownership.

The terminal sequential answer is:

> **Partially. Ayanokōji has learned that development requires room for answers he does not possess, and he increasingly values those answers. He can sometimes recognize a stopping boundary, sometimes accept reciprocal use, and sometimes deliberately withdraw so others must act. But he still claims too much authority over the timing, information, and conditions of that independence. Year 2 therefore does not resolve the authorship problem; it refines the legitimacy test. The strongest proof of freedom is not that the developed person survives his departure. It is that they can produce an answer that belongs to them strongly enough that he must respond to it rather than merely claim it as the success of his design.**

That is why Ichinose's third answer, not the class transfer itself, is the strongest local symbol of the Year-2 endpoint.

The transfer is Ayanokōji's proof that he is willing to remove the training wheels.

The third answer is proof that a student he tried to develop can **teach the developer something he did not already know**.
