# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ ２年生編10』
## Year 2 Volume 10 — Second-Pass Deep Reading
### Protection as valuation, relational stakes, and the politics of irreplaceable loss

> **Analytical boundary.** This document uses the complete Japanese Year 2 Volume 10 together with the frozen Year 1 V2 corpus, canonical Y2V01–Y2V09.5, and Volume 0 only in its architecture-mandated reader-retrospective position. Y2V11–12.5, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptations, wikis, and remembered later outcomes are excluded from V10-local adjudication.

# 1. Scope, provenance, and reading discipline

The Japanese replacement EPUB is structurally healthy and matches the Phase-0 source lock exactly: **9,871,375 bytes**, SHA-256 `310bb57a23fe737f7dae1f5b91c7acb37b195658cfc6071c91a96c8a94fbfe7e`. The audited OPF contains **24 spine items**. Deterministic extraction produces **4,132 non-empty text records** under `COTE:Y2V10:spineXX:paraYYYY`, with locator-text SHA-256 `764bbb8c5a45df7962a8d529bd2c7113221f527f28c20d6f65a230de00328d88`.

The local spoiler boundary matters unusually strongly here. V10 creates several situations whose long-term meaning later volumes may clarify: the A-class civil war, Sakayanagi's response to Kamuro's departure, Ayanokōji's declared plan to raise every class toward maximum potential, his relationship with Kei, Ichinose's changing ethical constitution, and the approaching end-of-year phase. This reading freezes what V10 itself permits rather than importing the destination.

Volume 0 remains admissible only as **reader knowledge**. It permits the reader to notice structural resemblance between Atsuomi's preference-engineering grammar and Kiyotaka's environmental authorship. It does not establish that Kiyotaka consciously imitates his father, remembers the sushi/curry formulation while acting, or shares Atsuomi's political purpose.

V10 also contains one of the clearest reasons for preserving that distinction. Kiyotaka's methods are becoming increasingly explicit to himself, yet V10 simultaneously gives evidence of reactions that are *not* experienced as calculated outputs: an involuntary smile with Horikita, the blank sketchbook he cannot fill through learned technique alone, and his recognition that Sakayanagi formed an attachment she herself failed to model. The volume therefore cannot be reduced to either "everything is planned" or "he is simply becoming normal." Both authored and unbidden processes coexist.

# 2. Executive thesis — desire acquires stakes

V09.5 established **desire as counter-authorship**. People inside ANHS can want things the institution, leader, rival, or developmental author did not assign: friendship, ordinary adulthood, private disclosure, romantic access, reciprocal obligation, quiet time, recognition, even the right to make yogurt badly. Those desires matter because they show that authored environments do not exhaust the purposes generated inside them.

V10 asks what follows once those desires create attachments.

The answer is **stakes**.

If another person becomes meaningful, that person can be protected, endangered, betrayed, lost, or discovered too late to have been irreplaceable. V10's special examination literalizes this problem. Leaders receive finite protection capacity. They must decide who to shield, who can be exposed, which external class may be injured, and—if their class finishes last—which already eliminated member must leave the school. Protection is therefore not just a tactical mechanic. It becomes a forced statement about **valuation under scarcity**.

The volume repeatedly separates four forms of value:

1. **institutional value** — what the class or OAA can measure;
2. **strategic value** — what a leader believes helps the class win;
3. **contractual value** — what one person is owed because of an explicit agreement;
4. **relational value** — what a particular person has come to mean through history, often without anyone consciously assigning that meaning.

The tragedy of Sakayanagi's V10 decision is that she treats the fourth category as if it did not exist until the special exam makes the mistake irreversible. Kamuro is not the strongest student in Class A. Sakayanagi therefore insists that Kamuro, Yamamura, and the other vulnerable dropouts may be treated as equivalent contributions and lets chance decide. Only after Kamuro is gone does she become able to say what her optimization model had excluded: **Kamuro had become her friend.**

That realization produces the volume's compact proposition:

> **A person can become irreplaceable before the optimizer has language for the irreplaceability.**

This is the relational counterpart to the project's old OAA principle. The file may contain evidence about the person, but the file is not the person; similarly, a leader's contribution model may contain evidence about a classmate, but the contribution model does not exhaust what that classmate has become to the leader.

V10 gives the same problem several political forms.

- Horikita learns that Ibuki's possible expulsion now produces hesitation where the earlier Horikita would have reasoned cleanly. Friendship has altered the cost function.
- Horikita nevertheless handles Kōenji through the *actual scope of their contract* rather than inventing unlimited duties because the class needs him.
- Kōenji reveals a moral distinction between genuine unsolicited benevolence and strategic attempts to purchase future compliance.
- Ichinose preserves every member of her own class while accepting that victory may require other classes to suffer casualties.
- Ryūen wins by exploiting a traitor inside Sakayanagi's class, but Hiyori immediately asks whether a victory generated by Ryūen's personal irregularity actually develops the class that depends on him.
- Hashimoto betrays Class A because he believes its leader lacks sufficient commitment to the class's own governing purpose, yet his proposed remedy is to make the class maximally dependent upon Ayanokōji.
- Ayanokōji tells Sakayanagi that his goal is to draw out the **maximum potential of every class**, making his cross-class developmental jurisdiction more explicit than ever.

The volume therefore asks not merely, "Whom do you protect?"

It asks:

> **Who has the right to decide what is worth protecting, and what happens when the decision-maker discovers that their own actual attachments exceed the model through which they made the decision?**

V10's answer is not anti-strategy. Horikita's contractual reasoning, Ichinose's coordination, and even Ryūen's tactical innovation are treated seriously as competence. The critique is aimed at **totalization**: the moment a strategic model claims to contain the whole person, loss exposes what the model omitted.

# 3. Position in the Year 2 architecture

The current conceptual sequence is:

> **V01 — legibility**  
> **V02 — jurisdiction**  
> **V03 — attribution**  
> **V04 — sovereignty / counter-authorship**  
> **V04.5 — embeddedness**  
> **V05 — responsibility**  
> **V06 — distributed agency**  
> **V07 — staged agency**  
> **V08 — relational legibility / moral friction**  
> **V00 — retrospective genealogy of environmental authorship**  
> **V09 — unmodeled agency / constitutional counter-authorship**  
> **V09.5 — desire as counter-authorship / relational plurality**  
> **V10 — protection as valuation / relational stakes**

The move from V09.5 to V10 is especially tight. V09.5 asks whether people can form desires not assigned by the surrounding system. V10 asks what those desires cost once they become attachments and must survive scarcity.

This is why V10's special exam is thematically stronger than a generic "knowledge quiz with expulsion." The categories include academics, entertainment, life knowledge, and subculture; the leader allocates finite protection; a student with three misses becomes eliminated; and a last-place class with any eliminated members must expel one. The institution places **plural knowledge**, **finite care**, and **disposability** inside one mechanism.

The volume then surrounds that mechanism with two scenes about things a curriculum does not fully produce.

Before the exam, Ayanokōji opens the sketchbook he bought after entering school. The White Room taught him techniques including copying, but not the process of producing something from his own thought. He cannot begin.

Later, while talking with Horikita, he smiles without intending to perform a smile. He recognizes the expression as an unplanned emotional output and compares it to a drop of color falling on the blank sketchbook.

The structural claim is precise:

> **Technique can be trained; generativity and spontaneous meaning cannot simply be commanded into existence on schedule.**

That distinction becomes the appropriate frame for Kamuro as well. Sakayanagi can design a hierarchy, rank contribution, and command followers. She cannot simply decide in advance who will become a friend.

# 4. Narrative architecture — nine movements from valuation to loss

## 4.1 Hashimoto Masayoshi's monologue — betrayal as a theory of self-insurance

V10 opens by giving Hashimoto his own account before the special exam provides evidence of what he will do.

He says that he fundamentally distrusts people. His governing expectation is that people betray, and therefore the rational defense is to betray first rather than wait to become the victim. He is willing to do dirty things for the one outcome he actually treats as non-negotiable: graduating from Class A. His horizon includes Ryūen, Sakayanagi, and Ayanokōji not primarily as objects of loyalty but as possible vehicles or threats to that goal (`COTE:Y2V10:spine10:para0002–0031`).

This matters later because the class betrayal cannot be reduced to a sudden bribe. Hashimoto's conduct is the practical expression of an already articulated political anthropology:

> **Trust is a vulnerability unless it is converted into insurance.**

His later escrow arrangement with Ryūen follows exactly that logic. He wants to betray Class A, but he does not trust Ryūen enough to accept an unsecured bargain. The private-point deposit functions as collateral against Ryūen using the operation to make Hashimoto expendable. Hashimoto's world is one in which even cooperation requires a mechanism for surviving the partner.

That makes him an important negative comparison with Kōenji later in the volume. Hashimoto assumes genuine obligation is unsafe and therefore seeks contractual or strategic insurance. Kōenji insists that *genuine* obligation is precisely what cannot be manufactured by offering aid in order to purchase future compliance.

## 4.2 Third-term opening — an approaching ending and the limits of trained competence

At the beginning of the third term, Ayanokōji becomes unusually conscious of time. He senses the end of his present school life approaching and appears to begin valuing scenes partly because they will end (`COTE:Y2V10:spine11:para0005–0016`). This is not yet nostalgia in a fully reflective adult sense. It is an early sign that temporality itself has become emotionally legible.

The relationship with Kei remains double. Ayanokōji recognizes that romance has given him practical social and communication experience that the White Room did not provide. Yet his language continues to include observational and dissective elements. He still treats ordinary life partly as a curriculum whose outcomes can be inspected.

Sudō supplies a healthier model of developmental pacing. His study and sports ambitions are now sufficiently self-authored that the new danger is overtraining rather than external motivation. Ayanokōji's advice is no longer "push harder" but effectively *急がば回れ*: sustainable development may require rest, pacing, and respect for the body's limits (`COTE:Y2V10:spine11:para0052–0057`). This is a small but important revision of developmental authorship. The strongest intervention is sometimes the one that lowers pressure.

The career interview material reintroduces a harsher constraint. The school has already contacted guardians and organized future-path conversations. Ayanokōji judges that his own future cannot presently be decided by `自由意思` alone (`COTE:Y2V10:spine11:para0070–0083`). Volume 0 makes the reader understand why that sentence carries more political weight than most classmates could perceive. But V10 itself establishes the crucial point: school-life freedom exists inside a future whose external jurisdiction remains contested.

The most important scene in this movement is the sketchbook.

Ayanokōji takes out the unused sketchbook and colored pencils he bought near the beginning of school. The White Room taught numerous artistic techniques, including `模写`, copying. What it did not include was `自分で考えて創作する過程`—the process of thinking and creating for oneself (`COTE:Y2V10:spine11:para0423–0444`).

He looks at the blank page and closes the pencils without drawing.

This is not evidence that he is literally incapable of creativity. It is evidence that **possession of technical competence does not automatically produce self-authored expression**. The blank page becomes V10's strongest image for the difference between trained ability and generative agency.

## 4.3 The survival/elimination special exam — protection becomes a political act

The exam's twenty-turn attack/defense structure forces all four classes into a rotating quadrilateral (`COTE:Y2V10:spine12:para0029–0041`). Attackers select a knowledge category, difficulty, and five targets. Defenders protect five students. A protected student counts as correct. Three wrong answers cause elimination; every eliminated member costs the class one point. If the last-place class has at least one eliminated member, its leader must select one of them for expulsion.

The knowledge categories are deliberately broad. Chabashira warns that a person may be filtered out—`淘汰`—even with high academic competence if they lack social or worldly knowledge (`COTE:Y2V10:spine12:para0042–0060`). This extends the project's long argument that `実力` is plural. The school has moved from measuring academic/physical legibility to constructing a game where cultural breadth, ordinary experience, and leader judgment alter survival.

The exam also makes privacy and politics collide. Each student can exclude several categories, but the leader ultimately controls attack and protection. A single protected slot therefore has at least three meanings at once:

- probability management;
- public confidence in a student's value;
- and possible life-or-death insulation against the later expulsion mechanism.

Kei immediately understands protection relationally. She wishes Ayanokōji were leader because she believes he would absolutely protect her. Ayanokōji does not validate the fantasy as an unconditional promise. Instead he explains why Horikita has class-political reasons to treat Kei as valuable, including Kei's position among the girls and her relationship to him (`COTE:Y2V10:spine12:para0301–0322`).

The scene makes the distinction between **being loved**, **being strategically valuable**, and **being protected** impossible to ignore. Kei wants those categories to collapse into one another. The exam refuses to guarantee that they do.

## 4.4 Morishita Ai — dormant capacity becomes active under institutional danger

Morishita's scrutiny of Kei continues V09.5's theme of unmodeled actors. She notices the mismatch between Kei's strong public social persona and the possibility of a much more vulnerable interior (`COTE:Y2V10:spine12:para0323–0368`). Ayanokōji intervenes before that inquiry penetrates too far, using the ordinary-language shield that romance is not reducible to rational explanation.

The statement is partially true. It is also protective misdirection.

Morishita explains why she is becoming more active now. In a class dominated by Sakayanagi and other capable actors, there was little reason for her to expend effort. Crisis changes the marginal value of her own agency. This is an important ability-taxonomy case: **possessed capacity may remain socially dormant until the environment makes its expression worthwhile.**

Her emergence matters later because Class A's crisis cannot be described only as Sakayanagi versus Hashimoto. Other members are beginning to act as independent interpreters precisely because the sovereign center no longer guarantees safety.

## 4.5 Kōenji and Mii-chan — genuine obligation cannot be manufactured into leverage

The anonymous food-delivery thread finally resolves when Mii-chan chooses to know who helped her. The source is Kōenji (`COTE:Y2V10:spine13:para0418–0425`).

His explanation is unusually important for the project's protection/ownership ledger. Kōenji says that he dislikes hypocrisy but does not slight `義`. He experienced Mii-chan's earlier action as `偽りなき恩`, unfeigned benevolence, and therefore repaid it naturally (`COTE:Y2V10:spine13:para0426–0444`). The exact initiating act remains deliberately small enough that Kōenji does not even dignify it as a strategic transaction.

Ayanokōji then tests the principle. Could someone help Kōenji now in order to purchase future class cooperation?

Kōenji rejects the premise (`COTE:Y2V10:spine13:para0462–0477`). Once aid is offered *for the purpose of purchasing compliance*, it ceases to possess the moral quality that made the original obligation binding to him.

This gives V10 one of its cleanest ethical distinctions:

> **Reciprocity is not ownership. An act can generate obligation without giving the benefactor jurisdiction over the beneficiary's future.**

Kōenji also repeats that self-protection belongs to himself. Ayanokōji briefly considers the class logic under which Kōenji could be surplus and therefore a culling candidate if Ayanokōji were leader. Kōenji detects the danger and makes clear that anyone trying to remove him must accept the consequences. Ayanokōji withdraws from the hypothetical because Horikita—not he—is the leader.

The point is not that Kōenji owes nothing to collective life. It is that the obligations he recognizes are **bounded**: explicit agreements and genuinely received benevolence, not unlimited membership claims silently authored by whoever currently needs his power.

## 4.6 Iké's strategy — leadership becomes capable of being corrected from below

Iké proposes an apparently absurd mass-sick-leave strategy: deliberately begin with thirty-two eliminated students so that only five active students plus the leader need to be protected, guaranteeing a stable score ceiling. The plan is ultimately rejected because its predictability allows the other classes to coordinate above that ceiling. But Horikita's initial reaction is more important than the outcome.

She expected little from Iké. When she understands the plan, she explicitly apologizes for having underestimated him and recognizes the idea as genuinely good (`COTE:Y2V10:spine14:para0138–0141`).

This is distributed agency in miniature. A leader's model of a weak member is falsified by the member producing an answer the leader did not possess. Horikita does not protect her status by pretending she had already seen it. She updates publicly.

That matters because V10 repeatedly tests whether leadership can distinguish **rank** from **contribution under a particular problem**. Iké's OAA profile does not predict the marginal strategic value of his idea. Horikita's capacity to admit that fact is itself leadership evidence.

## 4.7 Advice about Ibuki — relational legibility becomes costly

Ayanokōji's pre-exam advice to Horikita is one of the volume's cleanest continuations of V08.

He moves from the abstract possibility that another class must lose someone to the concrete possibility that the casualty could be Ibuki (`COTE:Y2V10:spine14:para0295–0349`). Horikita eventually says she would still choose her own class's victory, but she hesitates long enough for the problem to become visible.

A year earlier, the decision would have required much less emotional processing. Now Horikita knows Ibuki as a person. Their rivalry has become a form of friendship whether either woman likes the label or not.

Ayanokōji's advice is not "be willing to expel Ibuki." He tells Horikita to maintain awareness that external sacrifice can become part of class conflict and to find a leadership style that is actually hers rather than copying Ryūen, Sakayanagi, or Ichinose.

The exchange therefore reveals two things at once.

First, relational legibility creates moral friction. Knowing Ibuki makes the same strategic action costlier.

Second, Ayanokōji still deliberately introduces developmental inputs into Horikita's field. He explicitly notes that the awareness is beginning because he placed it there. Her future answer may be hers, but the question was staged by him.

## 4.8 The involuntary smile — a drop of color on the blank page

Immediately after discussing matters Horikita still cannot know about Ayanokōji's past, the two accidentally say the same thing and tell each other not to imitate the other (`COTE:Y2V10:spine14:para0390–0412`).

Ayanokōji smiles.

The analytical importance lies in his own surprise. He did not choose the expression as social performance. He identifies it as an `意図しない感情の表現`, an unintended expression of emotion. Horikita says she may never have seen that face in almost two years.

Ayanokōji then connects it to the blank sketchbook: something like a drop of color has finally fallen onto the empty page.

This should not be inflated into a definitive romantic declaration. The text itself says he does not know why the expression happened before Horikita rather than Kei, Hirata, or someone else.

Its stronger and safer importance is developmental:

> **Ayanokōji produces something affectively generative without first knowing the correct output.**

That is precisely what the sketchbook scene established as difficult. He can perform smiles when socially useful. V10 gives him a smile that appears before conscious authorship.

The difference between **performing an emotion competently** and **discovering an emotion through its spontaneous expression** is one of the volume's strongest contributions to the Ayanokōji model.

## 4.9 The quadrilateral exam — four constitutions reveal what they protect

The attack order places Horikita, Ichinose, Ryūen, and Sakayanagi inside one interdependent circuit. No class can fully control its own outcome. Each leader must attack one rival while being attacked by another, and the order reverses after the halfway point.

The design turns class constitution into behavior.

**Horikita** experiments, updates, accepts imperfect information, and retains credibility without false guarantees.

**Ichinose** makes zero internal dropouts a hard priority while accepting that external classes may suffer casualties.

**Ryūen** uses asymmetric information, betrayal, and score-control to reverse a weak first half.

**Sakayanagi** remains individually the strongest reader of target selection but proves institutionally vulnerable to a member whose political purpose has diverged from hers.

The exam is therefore not finally about who knows the most trivia. It is about which constitution can survive the fact that knowledge, loyalty, protection, and purpose are distributed among people rather than concentrated in the leader alone.

# 5. Ayanokōji Kiyotaka — from trained competence toward unplanned affect, while developmental jurisdiction expands

V10 contains one of the sharpest internal contradictions in Ayanokōji's Year-2 development.

On one side, he becomes more capable of experiencing outcomes he does not fully author. On the other, he becomes more explicit that he intends to intervene across **all four classes** in order to draw out their maximum potential.

These are not mutually exclusive. They are the problem.

## 5.1 The blank sketchbook and the limit of curriculum

The White Room taught him how to reproduce forms. It did not teach him how to decide what should exist on a blank page.

This distinction should be routed to `GENERATIVE_ABILITY`, not merely `AYANOKOJI_ORDINARY_LIFE`.

Generative ability is not the same as having a high technical ceiling. It is the capacity to produce a new answer whose value cannot be exhausted by correct replication of an existing model. The sketchbook scene shows that Kiyotaka's technical education may be enormous while a particular kind of self-authored creation remains underdeveloped.

That makes the later smile formal evidence rather than a cute interlude. The smile is the first thing in the volume that resembles a mark appearing on the page without his first deciding what the mark should communicate.

## 5.2 Affective spontaneity does not equal ethical transformation

It would be a mistake to infer from the smile that Ayanokōji has abandoned strategic treatment of people.

The opposite appears in the same volume.

He analyzes Kōenji as a possible surplus piece. He continues to model Kei through relationship utility and dependency. He deliberately gives Horikita a question designed to alter how she thinks about class sacrifice. At the end, he tells Sakayanagi openly that his objective is to bring every class toward its maximum potential and that he will interfere with Ryūen, Ichinose, Sakayanagi, and others when he judges intervention useful (`COTE:Y2V10:spine18:para0141–0148`).

Thus the correct local formulation is:

> **V10 strengthens Ayanokōji's capacity for spontaneous affect at the same time that it strengthens his self-assigned developmental jurisdiction.**

Humanization and manipulation are not opposite tracks. They are developing together.

## 5.3 He increasingly values development that falsifies his model

Ayanokōji is interested in Horikita precisely because she can exceed his expectations. He says her overall capacity would not necessarily make her a once-in-history genius in the wider world, but within the bounded environment of ANHS she possesses potential to produce outcomes beyond his model (`COTE:Y2V10:spine14:para0350–0357`).

This continues V09's unmodeled-agency thesis. What V10 adds is that he now sees his own role as helping create conditions under which those deviations can occur.

That is more ethically promising than insisting that the developed person reproduce his answer. It is also more ethically dangerous than simple tutoring because the environment itself becomes his object of design.

## 5.4 Sakayanagi's loss becomes developmental material

The ending provides the darkest version of this contradiction.

Ayanokōji correctly identifies that Kamuro's loss has affected Sakayanagi more deeply than she can initially admit. He helps her name the relationship as friendship. That intervention is emotionally perceptive and arguably beneficial.

Immediately afterward, his narration interprets Kamuro's expulsion as having become `好材料`, favorable material, because the loss can stimulate Sakayanagi's growth and because class points compressed without his having to control the field directly (`COTE:Y2V10:spine18:para0177–0187`).

This is a crucial ethical warning.

Ayanokōji did not cause Kamuro's expulsion. He is not morally responsible for the lottery result in the same way he was for Airi's constraint environment in V05. But his immediate conversion of another person's irreversible removal into **developmental material** reveals that his experimental orientation remains powerful even when the event is genuinely tragic.

The ability to help someone derive growth from loss is not itself wrong. The danger appears when **the value of the growth begins to dominate the moral reality of the person who was lost**.

## 5.5 The end-state purpose becomes more explicit

Ayanokōji closes the volume by preparing actions concerning Kei, Ichinose, and the class, with the stated horizon of becoming an existence that remains in other people's memories (`COTE:Y2V10:spine18:para0186–0191`).

This materially advances the legacy thread.

His purpose is no longer only "learn ordinary life" or "graduate." He now wants to leave a durable trace in other people's developmental histories.

That trace can be benevolent, painful, or both.

The unresolved question is whether wanting to remain in others' memories can become a **legacy offered to them**, or whether it remains another project whose form he determines in advance.

---# 6. Horikita Suzune — protection without possession

Horikita's V10 leadership is defined less by winning than by the way she assigns protection while accepting that protection has limits.

She finishes third. The result matters, but it is not a collapse. More important is that her class does not require Ayanokōji to take command when the score turns dangerous. She supplies the attack/defense decisions, absorbs new information, accepts help from below, and keeps the class coherent when she can no longer promise victory.

## 6.1 The leader can be corrected by the weak member

Iké's proposal is an explicit test of whether Horikita's hierarchy of expected competence has become rigid. It has not. She initially discounts him; he produces a clever strategy; she apologizes and updates.

That is a small constitutional virtue with large implications. A leader who cannot be falsified by a low-status member eventually converts competence into rank essentialism. Horikita instead allows a member's contribution to change her model of the member.

## 6.2 Friendship alters judgment without automatically owning judgment

The Ibuki thought experiment demonstrates that Horikita is no longer emotionally external to the people around her. She hesitates.

The hesitation is not leadership failure. It is evidence that relational knowledge now enters the decision.

The harder question is what she does with it.

Ayanokōji does not tell her to suppress friendship. He tells her not to pretend the possibility of cross-class sacrifice has disappeared. Horikita therefore enters the exam with a more complex political burden: she must govern while knowing that the people on the other side are no longer abstract rivals.

This is exactly the moral friction V08 predicted.

## 6.3 Kōenji's contract is interpreted narrowly rather than colonized

Horikita's handling of Kōenji is one of the strongest positive governance cases in the volume.

She does not assume that the promise to guarantee his freedom and prevent his expulsion obligates her to spend a permanent protection slot on him. She lets him accumulate misses because his demonstrated competence makes self-protection plausible. More cleverly, she recognizes that if he were actually eliminated, his existing protection point would prevent his expulsion if the class finished last. Sakayanagi's attempt to force Horikita into wasting resources therefore fails (`COTE:Y2V10:spine16:para0345–0356`).

The political importance is the boundedness of the obligation.

> **A contract creates a duty to satisfy the contract, not a license to rewrite the other party's future around every later collective need.**

Horikita neither abandons Kōenji nor claims him.

This is strong enough to answer the inherited question about what membership may legitimately require from him: it requires what he has actually agreed to, and genuine reciprocal obligations he himself recognizes—not unlimited submission derived from the fact of sharing a class.

## 6.4 Crisis rhetoric becomes truthful rather than omniscient

When Horikita's class drops close to last place, she refuses to manufacture certainty (`COTE:Y2V10:spine16:para1032–1045`). She tells the class the situation is bad, acknowledges that she cannot guarantee victory, and asks them to trust her anyway.

The narration emphasizes that what reaches the class is the truth underneath the words.

This is a marked contrast with forms of authority that secure compliance by hiding the actual distribution of risk. Horikita does not have full information. Her legitimacy therefore cannot come from omniscience. It comes from **answerability under acknowledged uncertainty**.

The class trusts a leader who is visibly inside the same risk rather than a leader who pretends the risk has vanished.

## 6.5 V10 does not yet eliminate Ayanokōji's hidden-security role

The positive evidence should not be overstated. Ayanokōji gives Horikita the pre-exam moral/strategic intervention about Ibuki. During the exam he notices that Sakayanagi's targeting implies an information leak and alerts Horikita. He remains a hidden analytical reserve.

Accordingly `Y1H-012` remains `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED` rather than closing here.

The stronger development is that **his reserve no longer substitutes for her leadership**. She can receive a piece of information or a question without becoming merely the visible hand executing his complete plan.

# 7. Kōenji Rokusuke — obligation without purchase

Kōenji's V10 material finally clarifies the ethical structure beneath conduct that can otherwise look like simple narcissistic exemption.

He is not a communitarian. He does not accept that membership automatically creates an unlimited claim on his labor. He also is not incapable of obligation.

His obligation grammar is selective.

## 7.1 `偽りなき恩` and `義`

The Mii-chan reveal establishes that Kōenji distinguishes genuine benevolence from strategic exchange. A small act performed without an ulterior demand can generate `義`, a felt obligation or duty of reciprocity. He responds to that on his own terms.

This is important because it explains why the same person who ignores class pressure can quietly deliver food for months.

The apparent contradiction dissolves once the source of the claim is distinguished:

- **collective demand:** "You owe us because you are useful and share our class" — weak legitimacy to Kōenji;
- **explicit agreement:** "You promised this in return for that" — binding within scope;
- **unfeigned benevolence:** "Someone helped without purchasing you" — can create self-authored reciprocity.

## 7.2 Why strategic kindness fails

Ayanokōji's hypothetical attempt to turn kindness into leverage is rejected because the intention changes the act. If the benefactor helps *in order to create an obligation*, then the benevolence is no longer the same moral object.

The scene therefore prevents an easy instrumentalization of reciprocity.

> **Care cannot be converted into legitimate ownership merely because the beneficiary later feels gratitude.**

This is directly relevant to Ayanokōji's developmental ethics. A developer cannot point to past help and infer unrestricted future jurisdiction.

## 7.3 Kōenji is not integrated by becoming normal

Nothing in V10 suggests Kōenji has been domesticated into ordinary class loyalty. He explicitly says he does not expect to change through graduation or afterward. That continuity matters.

Horikita's success is therefore not "fixing" him. It is building a political arrangement that can include a radically autonomous member without pretending his motives have become identical to everyone else's.

This closes `Y1H-032` locally by development: the class now has a tested, bounded model of legitimate requirement rather than an unresolved demand for total participation.

# 8. Ichinose Honami — solidarity becomes strategically dangerous without ceasing to be solidarity

V09 answered the question of whether Ichinose's class could gain adversarial competence without abandoning its solidaristic constitution. V10 moves beyond proof of competence into a more difficult moral transformation.

Ichinose makes **zero internal dropouts** a hard constraint. But she no longer treats that principle as requiring universal non-harm.

## 8.1 "Protect everyone" becomes class-bounded rather than universal

During the first half, Ichinose accepts that defeating Ryūen's class may create eliminated students there. She feels the moral cost, but she no longer treats discomfort as a veto (`COTE:Y2V10:spine16:para0930–0937`).

The change is significant.

Earlier Ichinose often behaved as though preserving her own moral purity and preserving everyone could be kept together. V10 forces the separation:

> **She may refuse to sacrifice her own classmates while accepting responsibility for competitive harm to another class.**

That is not simple moral decline. It is the emergence of political partiality.

Every class leader already acts from some partial commitment. Ichinose's distinctive development is that she keeps the internal no-sacrifice promise while becoming willing to fight externally.

## 8.2 The Ryūen agreement is trust as inference, not naïveté

Ichinose approaches Ryūen early. She explains her no-dropout requirement and initially offers target information. Ryūen rejects the offered payment but agrees to avoid eliminating her students if she can survive Horikita's first half, on the condition that she later accept points from him when necessary (`COTE:Y2V10:spine16:para0942–0999`).

She agrees quickly.

The text explicitly refuses the interpretation that she does so merely because she is gullible. She infers from the timing of Ryūen's response and the structure of his interests that he already possesses a reason to want coordination.

Her trust has become **analytical**.

## 8.3 The Kei sequence contains both temptation and restraint

This sequence is easy to flatten incorrectly.

Ichinose notices that Kei could potentially be pushed toward elimination and that a last-place Horikita class might then create some chance of expulsion. For several paragraphs she seriously entertains the personal possibility. She recognizes the thought as ugly, tells herself that she must act for the class rather than private desire, and also thinks with disturbing confidence that Ayanokōji will not reject her and that she may eventually overwrite the existing relationship (`COTE:Y2V10:spine16:para1001–1008`).

Then she **rejects the personal tactic**.

She continues targeting Kei because the repeated targeting is a deliberately legible signal to Horikita. After the exam, the plan is explained: Horikita realizes that the repeated choice of a socially central, obviously protectable student is a message, contacts Ichinose, and receives later target information that helps Horikita's class avoid last place (`COTE:Y2V10:spine17:para0193–0211`).

Thus both facts are canonical:

1. Ichinose is now capable of imagining politically exploiting Kei's vulnerability for romantic gain.
2. She restrains that desire and uses the same outward action for a class-political alliance instead.

This is much more interesting than either "Ichinose turns evil" or "the targeting was innocent all along."

> **Moral development is visible because the worse option becomes imaginable and is nevertheless refused.**

## 8.4 Direct class conflict does not destroy personal trust

V10 finally supplies the direct test required by `Y1H-022`.

Ichinose's class and Horikita's class are actual opponents within the exam. Ichinose attacks Horikita's students. Horikita attempts to score against Ichinose. Neither relationship collapses into personal betrayal. Instead, once the larger field changes, Ichinose and Horikita coordinate against Sakayanagi in a way that both understand as compatible with their own class interests.

This is enough for `Y1H-022 -> ANSWERED_BY_DEVELOPMENT`.

The answer is not that class conflict is harmless. It is that **personal trust can survive direct institutional opposition when both parties distinguish rivalry from total enmity**.

## 8.5 Ichinose now authors her class rather than merely embodying its old morality

Ayanokōji's post-V09 judgment becomes even more important here. The reform movement no longer necessarily requires him to intervene as hidden corrective because Ichinose herself is changing the class's political repertoire.

She preserves the class's governing promise—do not discard our own people—while changing how the class bargains, deceives, attacks, and cooperates.

This is constitutional development rather than simple personality hardening.

# 9. Ryūen Kakeru and Hiyori Shiina — victory can conceal developmental stagnation

Ryūen wins V10's special exam. The victory is real and tactically impressive.

It is also the volume's clearest example of why **outcome** and **developmental quality** must be separated.

## 9.1 Ryūen's asymmetric victory

Ryūen accepts Hashimoto's proposal because he has no moral objection to profitable betrayal. He uses the leaked target choices to protect perfectly through the entire second half and coordinates score flow with Ichinose. The final reversal—from a weak first half to 69 points and first place—is a genuine strategic achievement (`COTE:Y2V10:spine17:para0181–0188`).

His philosophy remains consistent: losing intermediate battles is acceptable if the eventual sequence leads to victory. He recognizes Ayanokōji's superiority without translating recognition into surrender. Sakayanagi is the nearer obstacle; Ayanokōji remains the later desired target.

This is the V06 reframe continuing. Better leadership and better predation are still developing together.

## 9.2 Hiyori's dissent is about generative capacity

Immediately after the victory, Hiyori does not celebrate. She worries that the method produced no durable `上積み`, no developmental accumulation for the class (`COTE:Y2V10:spine17:para0237–0268`).

Her criticism is not that tricks are immoral or that Ryūen should have lost nobly. It is institutional:

> **Did this victory make the class itself more capable of winning next time?**

Her answer is doubtful.

Ryūen's personal irregularity becomes both the class's necessary piece and its obstructive piece. The class is strong because it has Ryūen. It can also become weak because so much adaptive intelligence remains concentrated in him.

This is precisely the succession problem previously seen in Sakayanagi's class, expressed through a very different constitution.

## 9.3 Hiyori now diagnoses; she does not yet govern the correction

The scene materially strengthens `Y1H-026` but does not close it. Hiyori sees the moral/developmental problem more clearly than many followers. She has not yet demonstrated that she can redirect Ryūen's conduct or institutionalize an alternative.

Therefore `Y1H-026` remains `STILL_OPEN`.

The important advance is that **internal moral intelligence exists**. Ryūen's class is no longer cognitively identical to Ryūen.

# 10. Sakayanagi Arisu, Kamuro Masumi, and Hashimoto Masayoshi — the optimizer discovers a friend after the model has already killed the choice

This is V10's deepest character movement.

Sakayanagi enters the exam as the strongest individual reader of the four leaders. Ayanokōji later says that in the direct reading of attacks, defenses, and opponents, she did not lose because of inferior strategic ability. She loses because a polity is not reducible to its sovereign's intelligence.

## 10.1 Hashimoto's betrayal reveals the constitutional weakness of Class A

Hashimoto supplies Ryūen with Sakayanagi's attack targets through a borrowed other-class phone hidden in the classroom. He protects himself through a large private-point escrow; the actual information fee is only 500,000 points (`COTE:Y2V10:spine17:para0339–0375`).

The motive is not ordinary greed.

Hashimoto believes Sakayanagi's class lacks a credible route to Class-A graduation because Sakayanagi does not treat Class A itself as the supreme purpose. He wants Ayanokōji imported into the class. If Sakayanagi refuses, he is prepared to continue betraying her, ultimately imagining expelling her and taking control himself (`COTE:Y2V10:spine17:para0389–0462`).

His diagnosis has real textual support. Sakayanagi explicitly says that she and Ryūen care far more about defeating worthy rivals than about the intrinsic value of graduating from Class A. Hashimoto contrasts that with Horikita and Ichinose, whose classes possess what he calls genuine `熱意` toward the institutional objective.

But Hashimoto's remedy is constitutionally perverse.

He diagnoses leader-centered weakness and proposes **greater dependency on a singular person**. He worries that Sakayanagi cannot secure the class's future, then wants Ayanokōji installed as the ultimate guarantee.

His politics are therefore:

> **fear of dependence solved through a search for a more powerful dependency.**

## 10.2 Sakayanagi's individual excellence cannot prevent institutional betrayal

Sakayanagi's response is not naïve. She identifies Hashimoto, reconstructs the escrow logic, infers the borrowed-phone method, and even explains why an immediate search would probably have failed.

The failure occurred earlier, at the constitutional level. Her system had not produced a form in which a member who believed the class's governing purpose was wrong possessed a legitimate, consequential avenue for opposition.

Hashimoto therefore chooses treason.

That does not make treason legitimate. It makes the absence of legitimate opposition politically relevant.

## 10.3 The lottery is "fair" precisely where Sakayanagi's self-knowledge is false

After the loss, Sakayanagi has five eliminated students from whom she must choose an expellee: Kamuro, Yamamura, Sugio, Toba, and Machida. She judges the candidates broadly equivalent in contribution and uses a random draw (`COTE:Y2V10:spine17:para0103–0131`).

The lottery appears impartial.

Yet impartiality is not necessarily truth.

When only Kamuro and Yamamura remain, Yamamura freezes. Sakayanagi momentarily tries to treat Yamamura's inability to draw as refusal and thereby spare Kamuro. Kamuro sees what is happening and rejects the rescue. She physically pulls Yamamura forward so both can draw simultaneously, knowingly restoring her own 50% risk (`COTE:Y2V10:spine17:para0141–0167`).

Kamuro receives the losing paper.

Her act reveals something Sakayanagi's contribution model did not contain: **Kamuro herself values Yamamura enough not to accept a technically convenient escape purchased by the other's panic.**

The lottery's deepest function is therefore epistemic. It does not merely choose an expellee. It exposes hidden relational values at the moment they can no longer change the outcome.

## 10.4 Kamuro's exit refuses victimhood

Kamuro does not demand rescue, does not spend the farewell accusing Sakayanagi, and quickly identifies another school she can enter if she passes its examination (`COTE:Y2V10:spine18:para0038–0052`).

As with Airi, expulsion from ANHS is not narrated as ontological death.

Kamuro also continues caring for Sakayanagi in the ordinary bodily sense—returning when Sakayanagi falls, helping her up, picking up the cane, and telling her to find someone else because Kamuro will no longer be there (`COTE:Y2V10:spine18:para0053–0064`).

This is relational evidence stronger than a sentimental speech precisely because neither woman is comfortable speaking sentimentally.

Kamuro's final explicit request is not "remember me" or "avenge me for my sake." She asks Sakayanagi to ensure that the traitor who harmed the class eventually follows the same road, and asks Ayanokōji to witness whether Sakayanagi keeps the promise (`COTE:Y2V10:spine18:para0075–0086`).

The request binds friendship, class loyalty, anger, and ordinary Kamuro bluntness without simplifying any of them.

## 10.5 Sakayanagi's awakening is revelation of a relationship already formed

Ayanokōji waits for Sakayanagi after Kamuro departs and pushes against her insistence that Kamuro was functionally replaceable.

Sakayanagi initially repeats her elite model: Kamuro was not exceptionally capable, not obedient, and therefore should not materially affect the class or leader.

Ayanokōji identifies the contradiction. If Kamuro were truly equivalent to anyone else, Sakayanagi would not be altered by the result.

Eventually Sakayanagi says it plainly:

> `どうやら私の中で真澄さんは、いつの間にか友人になってしまっていたようです。`

Kamuro had apparently become her friend before Sakayanagi consciously acknowledged the category (`COTE:Y2V10:spine18:para0157–0169`).

This is a **REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_STATE** inside a developmental relationship. The friendship did not begin at the moment of confession. The loss makes the already-existing relation legible.

That distinction should be preserved carefully. V10 does not say Sakayanagi suddenly learns to care because Kamuro is expelled. It says the expulsion destroys the self-description under which Sakayanagi had been caring without naming the care.

## 10.6 Relational value can rationally matter to leadership without becoming favoritism-as-default

Ayanokōji tells Sakayanagi that, given what Kamuro actually meant to her, preserving Kamuro would have been strategically rational for **Sakayanagi's own future strength** even if ordinary leadership ethics would criticize obvious favoritism (`COTE:Y2V10:spine18:para0126–0137`).

This is a difficult claim.

It should not be generalized into "leaders should always save friends." The point is narrower: a leader's emotional constitution is itself part of institutional capacity. If losing a specific person will damage the leader more than losing an apparently equivalent member, the relationship is strategically relevant information.

Sakayanagi's error was not possessing an attachment. It was pretending the attachment did not exist and therefore making a supposedly neutral decision from a false model of herself.

> **Impartial procedure can become irrational when it is built on dishonest self-knowledge.**

## 10.7 `Y1H-029` remains partial, but the negative evidence becomes much stronger

V10 supplies severe evidence that Sakayanagi's aristocratic optimization does not gracefully survive sovereign private priorities, hidden attachments, and internal dissent. Yet the class is not destroyed and Sakayanagi has now gained information capable of changing her leadership.

The year-long question therefore remains `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED` rather than closing. V10 is both a failure and the beginning of a possible constitutional correction.

# 11. Kei Karuizawa, Kushida Kikyō, Morishita Ai, and the social politics surrounding protection

V10's protection system makes several secondary relationships analytically important.

## 11.1 Kei's fear shows the difference between horizontal autonomy and vertical reliance

Kei has real independent social support. Satō immediately places herself physically between Kei and Ichinose after the exam when she believes Kei has been targeted maliciously (`COTE:Y2V10:spine17:para0193–0197`). That horizontal autonomy remains real.

But Kei also wishes Ayanokōji were exam leader because she imagines his protection as absolute. This confirms the V08/V09.5 answer: horizontal independence can coexist with vertical psychological dependency.

The exam turns that dependency into a visible political expectation: **to be loved should mean to be protected**.

Ayanokōji's refusal to answer through romantic absolutes keeps `Y1H-009` open. Kei still does not possess the information required to evaluate the larger architecture of his relationship plans.

## 11.2 Kushida becomes informational infrastructure without becoming purified

Kushida's integration continues to produce class value. Her social knowledge helps Horikita interpret other classes and the people inside them. She also participates in the strange Horikita–Ibuki domestic/social triangle that all three resist naming as friendship.

This matters because the V05 question was never whether Kushida could become morally spotless. It was whether public competence and private hostility could coexist without the class requiring a fake purified self.

By V10 the answer remains yes.

## 11.3 Morishita is a warning against leader-centric models of Class A

Morishita's growing activity is evidence that Sakayanagi's class contains more latent agency than the sovereign model made visible. Her intervention does not save the exam, but it demonstrates that Class A's future need not be reducible to Sakayanagi versus Hashimoto.

The crisis is creating new political subjects.

That is one reason Hashimoto's claim that "the class has no winning route" should not be accepted as omniscient fact. It is his diagnosis at V10, formed from a leader-centered model that may itself underestimate dormant members.

# 12. The special exam as institutional theory

V10's exam deserves to be read as a political machine.

It turns four abstract questions into enforced decisions:

1. **Who counts as worth protecting?**
2. **What kinds of knowledge count as ability?**
3. **How much authority may one leader hold over another member's survival?**
4. **What does a class owe its own members when winning requires harming outsiders?**

## 12.1 Protection is scarce by design

Because only five students can be protected per defense turn, the school prevents leaders from avoiding valuation. Every protection decision creates an opportunity cost.

The same person can be protected for different reasons:

- because they are weak;
- because they are valuable;
- because they are politically central;
- because they are contractually owed protection;
- because the leader personally cares about them;
- because protecting them manipulates the attacker.

The mechanic therefore refuses a simple equation between protection and love.

## 12.2 Elimination is not expulsion, but it creates an expulsion pool

A student who reaches three misses becomes `脱落`, eliminated from the remainder of the exam. That status itself is not expulsion. Only the last-place class is forced to select an expellee from among its eliminated members.

This two-step structure matters analytically.

First, the exam creates **legible vulnerability**.

Then it forces political authority to act upon that vulnerability.

The institution therefore does not directly choose Kamuro. It creates the pool and transfers terminal selection to Sakayanagi.

This is a classic ANHS distribution of responsibility: the school authors the coercive environment while the students author the particular injury inside it.

## 12.3 Broad knowledge tests ordinary life as ability

The use of entertainment, life, and subculture categories extends `JITSURYOKU` beyond formal academics. Ayanokōji himself recognizes vulnerability because White Room excellence does not guarantee current popular-cultural knowledge.

The exam therefore gives institutional form to a point the ordinary-life ledger has been developing for many volumes:

> **A person optimized for formal performance can still be weak in socially embedded knowledge.**

ANHS does not merely reward that knowledge sentimentally. It lets the knowledge affect survival.

## 12.4 The leader's final expulsion authority is extremely strong

If the class finishes last, the leader—not a full-class vote—chooses the expelled member from the eliminated pool. V10 thereby creates a sharp contrast with V05's unanimous-vote exam.

V05 dispersed responsibility across the whole class and made consensus coercive.

V10 concentrates terminal responsibility in one visible leader.

The comparison is important:

- **V05:** everyone must participate in authoring the sacrifice;
- **V10:** the institution identifies a vulnerable subset and requires the sovereign to choose among them.

Neither design eliminates coercion. They distribute authorship differently.

# 13. Protection, ownership, and answerability

V10 substantially advances the project's protection/ownership distinction.

Protection is ethically ambiguous because it can mean:

- care;
- control;
- contract performance;
- strategic investment;
- reputational management;
- or possession.

The volume's strongest positive cases are the ones where protection does **not** erase the protected person's separate authorship.

Kōenji's contract is bounded.

Mii-chan's benevolence does not purchase him.

Ichinose protects her classmates while permitting them to retain ordinary relations outside her control.

Kamuro refuses Sakayanagi's attempted rescue and restores Yamamura's equal chance.

Horikita cannot simply decide that friendship with Ibuki gives her jurisdiction over Ibuki's choices.

By contrast, Ayanokōji's relational experiments remain ethically dangerous because he often possesses information about future endpoints that the other person does not. His capacity to protect can therefore coexist with ownership pressure.

The volume suggests a useful criterion:

> **Protection is least proprietary when the protected person can still refuse, reinterpret the relationship, leave, and create obligations the protector did not assign.**

Kamuro's exit is an unusually strong example. She is the person who leaves, but she still authors the meaning of the departure through the choice to restore Yamamura's chance, reject self-pity, plan a new school route, and leave Sakayanagi with one specific demand.

# 14. Japanese vocabulary and conceptual pressure points

## 14.1 `淘汰` — selection / elimination

Chabashira's use of `淘汰` for students who lack broad social/world knowledge connects V10 back to the culling vocabulary of V05 and the White Room. The important difference is not that ANHS never filters. It plainly does. The question is whether the filtered person is reduced to the institution's verdict.

Kamuro again supplies the counterexample: expulsion ends her ANHS trajectory, not her future.

## 14.2 `プロテクト` / `守る` — protect

The exam literalizes protection as a consumable allocation. This makes the word politically visible. To protect one person is to leave another exposed.

The series therefore prevents `守る` from functioning as uncomplicated moral vocabulary. Protection needs an object, a cost, a source of authority, and an exit condition.

## 14.3 `友人` — friend

Sakayanagi's confession that Kamuro had `いつの間にか友人になってしまっていた` is linguistically important. The phrase suggests a state that came into being without a formal moment of decision and became consciously legible only afterward.

The relation precedes the label.

That is exactly why this scene belongs under `REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_STATE` as well as development.

## 14.4 `裏切り` — betrayal

Hashimoto repeatedly names his own action through betrayal vocabulary rather than euphemizing it as negotiation. His justification does not deny the category. It contests whether betrayal can be instrumentally legitimate when the existing class trajectory is judged doomed.

Sakayanagi's answer is constitutional: he crossed a line that makes common fate impossible.

## 14.5 `熱意` — commitment / heat

Hashimoto's most penetrating political criticism is that Ryūen and Sakayanagi possess power without treating A-class graduation as a sacred objective, while Horikita and Ichinose possess less overwhelming power but more `熱意` toward that institutional purpose.

The word distinguishes **ability to govern** from **fiduciary alignment with the governed group's stated goal**.

## 14.6 `自由意思` — free will / one's own will

Ayanokōji's career reflection that some aspects of his future cannot presently be changed by `自由意思` sharpens the post-Volume-0 freedom problem. The school has produced genuine new desires and purposes inside him, but external structures still constrain the range over which those purposes can act.

## 14.7 `意図しない感情の表現` — unintended emotional expression

Ayanokōji's description of the smile should be retained nearly as a technical term in the emotion ledger. The scene distinguishes chosen performance from spontaneous output using his own metacognitive vocabulary.

# 16. Formal strengths and critical reservations

## 16.1 Strength: the exam mechanic and character theme are unusually well integrated

V10's strongest craft achievement is that the special exam does not merely create suspense. Its mechanics *are* the thematic question. Finite protection, broad knowledge, elimination, and leader-selected expulsion turn abstract debates about value and responsibility into repeated decisions.

The leader cannot hide behind general benevolence. A slot must be assigned.

## 16.2 Strength: Kamuro's expulsion revises Sakayanagi without rewriting her past

The reveal that Kamuro had become a friend works because the prior relationship was always combative, practical, and emotionally undernamed. V10 does not suddenly transform Sakayanagi into a sentimental character. It reveals that her own model of herself was incomplete.

The distinction between **new emotion** and **new access to existing emotion** is handled particularly well.

## 16.3 Strength: the blank sketchbook and smile form a concise symbolic pair

The sketchbook could have remained a generic "White Room child lacks creativity" symbol. The later smile gives it a more precise function. The problem is not lack of all creativity or all emotion. It is the difference between reproducing a known form and producing something whose meaning is not fully specified beforehand.

The drop-of-color callback makes that difference legible without requiring a long explicit psychological lecture.

## 16.4 Strength: Ichinose's Kei temptation is neither erased nor consummated

The later explanation that repeated Kei targeting was a signal to Horikita does not cancel the private moment in which Ichinose considers exploiting Kei's vulnerability. The volume allows the darker desire to become thinkable and then shows self-restraint.

That is stronger characterization than either moral purification or sudden villainization.

## 16.5 Reservation: Hashimoto's betrayal mechanism depends on unusually permissive device conditions

The hidden borrowed phone is clever and Sakayanagi's reconstruction is internally coherent, but the plot demands a relatively permissive exam environment around devices and classroom search. The novel anticipates some objections by explaining why a rushed phone sweep would not necessarily solve the problem, yet the mechanism remains more contrived than the political motive.

The motive—the mismatch between Hashimoto's Class-A fixation and Sakayanagi's rival-centered priorities—is the more durable part of the subplot.

## 16.6 Reservation: Ayanokōji's diagnostic authority remains extremely broad

The text frequently lets Ayanokōji explain what other characters' losses mean. In V10 this is productive with Sakayanagi, but it also risks giving his interpretation excessive narratorial gravity.

The method should therefore preserve a distinction:

- **fact:** Sakayanagi eventually says Kamuro had become her friend;
- **strongly supported inference:** Ayanokōji correctly noticed her unacknowledged grief;
- **not automatically fact:** every developmental conclusion Ayanokōji draws from the event will prove correct.

His plan to maximize all four classes' potential remains his project, not an omniscient statement of what each class actually needs.

# 17. Historical first-pass revision audit

The available first-pass summary treated mid-to-late Year 2 broadly as the school class system maturing around Ayanokōji, with Horikita, Ichinose, Ryūen, Sakayanagi, Nagumo, and Hashimoto becoming sharper, more desperate, or more dangerous, and Ayanokōji increasingly shaping conditions under which major players evolve or break. That broad direction is **PRESERVED**, but V10's Japanese-primary reread makes several important revisions.

### PRESERVE — Ayanokōji as environmental force

The old broad model is strongly supported. V10 makes the point unusually explicit when he says his purpose is to draw out the maximum potential of every class and that he will interfere with Sakayanagi, Ryūen, Ichinose, and others when necessary.

### STRENGTHEN — Horikita class consolidation

The first-pass category "class consolidation" is too broad. V10 shows the mechanisms: accepting low-status strategic input, narrow contract interpretation with Kōenji, truthful crisis rhetoric, and the ability to receive Ayanokōji's advice without becoming his puppet.

### REVISE — Ichinose hardening

A simple "Ichinose becomes more ruthless" reading is inadequate. V10 shows **bounded partiality**. She accepts harm to rival classes, briefly entertains a personal attack on Kei, restrains that temptation, and uses the outward targeting pattern as an inter-class signal to help Horikita while protecting her own class.

### STRENGTHEN — Hashimoto's maneuvering

His betrayal is not merely opportunistic scheming for money. It is rooted in an explicit constitutional diagnosis: Sakayanagi lacks fiduciary commitment to Class-A graduation as an intrinsic goal. His remedy remains self-interested and dependency-producing, but the disagreement is political rather than only venal.

### REVISE — Sakayanagi's vulnerability

Her weakness is not simply "she cares too much about Ayanokōji." V10 reveals at least three distinct vulnerabilities:

# 26. What V10 changes about the governing Year-2 question

The Year-2 governing question remains:

> **What happens when a person who escaped being another institution's curriculum becomes capable of designing the environments in which other people develop—and can that new authorship remain compatible with the freedom he originally sought for himself?**

V10 adds a new condition:

> **The environmental author does not merely have to permit other people to choose. He has to accept that their choices may create attachments whose value becomes real before he, they, or the institution can model it.**

That changes the ethical problem of authorship.

If people are only bundles of capacities, a sufficiently accurate developer may imagine that every loss can be compensated by substituting another capacity.

V10 demonstrates the failure of that assumption.

Kamuro cannot be replaced *as Kamuro* even if Class A can replace her functional contribution.

Ibuki's possible loss means something different to Horikita now than it would have a year earlier.

Mii-chan's small act creates a form of obligation in Kōenji that cannot be reproduced by a strategically identical gift with a different intention.

Ayanokōji's spontaneous smile cannot be reproduced merely by performing the facial movement correctly.

These are all forms of **history-dependent value**.

A person becomes what they mean within a particular sequence of interactions. That meaning is not necessarily transferable even when the function is.

The challenge to Ayanokōji is therefore sharper than "learn empathy."

It is:

> **Can he allow history-dependent value to constrain his optimization even when the optimized system would prefer substitution?**

V10 does not answer yes.

His response to Sakayanagi is still partly experimental. He immediately sees developmental advantage in Kamuro's loss. He plans interventions across all classes. He continues to think in terms of maximum potential.

But the volume also gives him the blank page and the unplanned smile. Something has begun to occur inside him that is not fully equivalent to a chosen developmental output.

The ethical future now depends on whether he treats that unplanned color as **information to exploit** or as **a claim on him that deserves answerability**.

# 27. Final synthesis

Year 2 Volume 10 is a volume about **what becomes visible when protection cannot be universal**.

Its exam forces every class to reveal how it values people.

Horikita reveals a constitution increasingly capable of bounded contracts, correction from below, truthful uncertainty, and relational friction without paralysis.

Ichinose reveals that solidarity can become politically dangerous without disappearing: she preserves every member of her own class while accepting rival casualties, bargaining with Ryūen, resisting a private temptation to weaponize Kei, and coordinating across direct class conflict.

Ryūen reveals the power and weakness of irregular personal sovereignty. He wins brilliantly, but Hiyori immediately sees that the victory may have developed **Ryūen** more than it developed **Ryūen's class**.

Sakayanagi reveals the cost of believing that correct evaluation of contribution is equivalent to correct knowledge of people. She is strategically excellent and still loses because her polity contains an actor whose purpose diverges from hers. Then, forced to choose among vulnerable followers, she uses impartial chance because she believes the relevant people are equivalent. Kamuro's departure proves the self-description false.

Hashimoto reveals another danger. He is correct that a leader's private purpose can diverge from the polity's stated purpose. Yet his answer to insufficient fiduciary alignment is to seek a more overwhelming sovereign. He wants Ayanokōji because dependence on the strongest person feels safer than dependence on a leader whose priorities he distrusts.

Kōenji supplies the cleanest alternative logic. Obligation can exist without ownership. Genuine benevolence may create reciprocal duty precisely because it was not offered as a purchase. An explicit contract can bind precisely because its scope is knowable. The class can coexist with him without claiming unlimited jurisdiction.

And Ayanokōji stands across all of these cases.

He understands protection, vulnerability, hidden attachment, and developmental potential better than almost anyone around him. He can see why Sakayanagi should have saved Kamuro even before she can admit what Kamuro meant. He can help Horikita notice the moral friction friendship introduces into competition. He can see the developmental weakness in Ryūen's victory and the strategic transformation in Ichinose.

Yet his vision creates its own danger. He increasingly treats the entire year group as a developmental field and openly assigns himself the right to intervene so that each class reaches its maximum potential.

V10 therefore gives him two images.

The first is the **blank sketchbook**: all the technique in the world does not tell him what he should create.

The second is the **involuntary smile**: something meaningful appears before he has specified what it should be.

Between those images lies the year's deepest unresolved possibility.

> **Perhaps freedom is not the absence of environment. Perhaps it is the capacity for an environment to produce meanings its author did not assign—and for the author to become answerable to those meanings rather than immediately converting them back into material for the next design.**

V10 cannot prove Ayanokōji will make that transition.

It does prove that the world around him is already doing so.
