# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ ２年生編12』
## Year 2 Volume 12 — Second-Pass Deep Reading
### Relational jurisdiction, authorship without command, and the violence of making another person choose what you want

> **Analytical boundary.** This document uses the complete Japanese Year 2 Volume 12 together with the frozen Year 1 V2 corpus, canonical Y2V01–Y2V11, and Volume 0 only in its architecture-mandated reader-retrospective position. Y2V12.5, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptations, wikis, external summaries, and remembered later outcomes are excluded from V12-local adjudication. In particular, the administrative aftermath of the Sakayanagi–Ryūen withdrawal pact and the Ayanokōji–Kei relationship after this volume remain outside the local boundary.

# 1. Scope, provenance, and reading discipline

Volume 12 is the most severe stress test yet of the ethical vocabulary developed across the Year-2 second pass. V11 proposed a provisional stopping rule for legitimate environmental authorship: recognition may justify attention, access, or bounded assistance, but superior knowledge does not grant ownership over another person's answer. V12 asks what happens when an author does not issue a command at all. Instead, he arranges information, timing, trust, incentives, and emotional dependencies so precisely that the other person produces the desired act from inside their own motive structure.

That problem requires unusually strict distinctions among **command**, **stage**, **relational jurisdiction**, and **self-authored choice**. The relevant acts in V12 are not equivalent. Maezono is maneuvered into a false denial and expelled. Ichinose knowingly enters one bargain but is kept ignorant of the bargain's actual function, then has accumulated trust weaponized against her cognition. Sakayanagi receives no direct instruction to throw the exam; she reconstructs Ayanokōji's preference from a deliberately designed clue chain and then independently chooses to satisfy it. Amasawa, by contrast, recognizes that Ayanokōji is partly trying to tether her to school and can answer that intervention with substantially greater informational symmetry. The ethical analysis therefore cannot flatten all four cases into either “free choice” or “mind control.”

# 2. Executive thesis — formal freedom can become a medium of control

The strongest progression from V11 is:

> **V11 — recognition without ownership / bounded intervention → V12 — relational jurisdiction / authorship without command.**

V12's governing question is:

> **When direct commands become unnecessary because another person's trust, love, fear, loyalty, or desire is predictable enough to generate the preferred action, does preserving formal freedom reduce the author's responsibility—or merely make authorship harder to see?**

The volume answers through three escalating cases.

First, Maezono is treated as an available institutional object. Ayanokōji knows that her information leak does not ordinarily merit expulsion and explicitly thinks that she could probably be corrected. He nevertheless designs the traitor bargain so that Ichinose will select Maezono, reassures Maezono that he will not identify her, induces the denial that activates the expulsion rule, and then classifies the person as a convenient `道具`—a tool close at hand. Here formal procedure is used to manufacture the terminal condition. The student technically speaks the decisive lie; the author controls the informational environment in which that lie becomes predictable.

Second, Ichinose is protected at the class level while violated at the personal level. Ayanokōji's bargain preserves her classmates from traitor-expulsion risk and even gives her class 50 points, yet the same arrangement makes her unknowingly complicit in Maezono's expulsion. He then attacks the non-exam foundation of her judgment: her trust in him. He reveals—or strategically frames as revelation—his role in the first-year shoplifting exposure and declares that every prior intervention had an ulterior purpose. His stated objective is physiological and cognitive: lower the functioning of the reasoning system that makes Ichinose nearly unbeatable in this examination. Protection of the polity and instrumental destruction of the person occur simultaneously.

Third, Sakayanagi is not commanded at all. Ryūen is losing on merit. Both Ryūen and Sakayanagi understand that Sakayanagi has the match effectively won. Ayanokōji has prearranged an encoded route: if Ryūen reaches defeat, he may use the traitor right on Hashimoto and tell Sakayanagi that Hashimoto carries Ayanokōji's “message.” There is no literal message. Sakayanagi must infer it from the absurdity of the situation, her knowledge of Ayanokōji, his four-class ecology, and his preference for Ryūen's further growth. She reaches the sentence herself: `ここで負けてくれ`—lose here. She then chooses to do so.

This is V12's most difficult ethical configuration. Sakayanagi's cognition is highly autonomous; no threat compels her; she can win by simply doing what her class interest requires. Yet the desire of the person she loves has become sufficiently authoritative that his inferred preference functions as a relational veto. Ayanokōji does not need to order her. He needs only to know that she will understand him.

The compact thesis is therefore:

> **V12 converts freedom itself into part of the control surface. Its darkest interventions work not by removing choice, but by knowing another person's choice architecture so well that their own agency can be recruited to complete an outcome the hidden author prefers.**

The necessary qualification is equally important:

> **Agency under authored conditions remains agency. Responsibility is therefore distributed, not erased: Sakayanagi really chooses; Ichinose really agrees to the bargain she understands; Maezono really lies. But the more predictable those choices are because of information asymmetry or relational dependence, the less credible it becomes for the environment's designer to disown causal responsibility.**

# 3. Position in the Year-2 architecture

The Year-2 sequence now reads:

1. **V01 — legibility:** institutions make people measurable and therefore targetable.
2. **V02 — jurisdiction:** measurement becomes a struggle over who may bind another person's future.
3. **V03 — attribution:** hidden authorship becomes difficult to identify under engineered opacity.
4. **V04 — sovereignty / counter-authorship:** rival plans collide and distributed counter-authorship defeats singular control.
5. **V04.5 — embeddedness:** relationships make life meaningful and simultaneously create leverage.
6. **V05 — responsibility:** collective procedure creates injury for which someone must become answerable.
7. **V06 — distributed agency:** developed actors begin solving problems without the original developer.
8. **V07 — staged agency:** environments are designed so autonomous actors complete outcomes from their own motives.
9. **V08 — relational legibility / moral friction:** knowing rivals as people makes competition both more informative and more costly.
10. **Volume 0 — retrospective genealogy:** preference-sensitive environmental authorship receives a political and developmental prehistory.
11. **V09 — unmodeled agency / constitutional counter-authorship:** developed actors produce answers the model did not already contain.
12. **V09.5 — desire as counter-authorship:** ordinary wants generate ends not assigned by formal systems.
13. **V10 — protection as valuation / relational stakes:** scarcity forces actors to reveal what and whom they actually value.
14. **V11 — recognition without ownership / bounded intervention:** legitimate assistance requires knowing where one's standing ends.
15. **V12 — relational jurisdiction / authorship without command:** hidden authorship learns to operate through another person's own attachment, trust, or predicted decision.

V12 is not simply a reversal of V11. The stopping rule discovered in V11 remains conceptually real. What changes is that the text now demonstrates Ayanokōji can know the boundary and still choose methods that avoid crossing it **overtly** while producing equivalent control. The relevant ethical failure is subtler than direct coercion: he can preserve a target's formal act of choosing while controlling the informational and relational conditions that make one choice overwhelmingly likely.

This is why V12 is a crucial bridge into the Year-2 endpoint. The question is no longer whether Ayanokōji has developed humane relational capacities. He has. Nor is it whether he remains capable of ruthless manipulation. He plainly does. The harder question is whether the humane capacities can become **constraints** on the strategic capacities rather than additional information that makes strategy more accurate.

# 4. Narrative architecture — eleven movements toward indirect jurisdiction

## 4.1 Hoshinomiya's monologue — trauma becomes active sabotage

Chie Hoshinomiya opens the volume by identifying Sae Chabashira as both best friend and rival and then revealing the persistence of a grievance that has outlived their student years. Her decision to become a teacher is partly organized around preventing Sae from realizing the Class-A dream that their own generation lost. She is willing to become unfit as a teacher, lose her job, or take Sae down with her if necessary (`COTE:Y2V12:spine11:para0010-para0034`; `Y2V12-E001–E010`).

The prologue therefore makes adult memory an environmental force. V05 allowed Chabashira to recognize that she had been using current students to repair a frozen past; V12 gives the negative mirror. Hoshinomiya knowingly treats present students' futures as instruments in a private historical grievance.

## 4.2 The year-end exam — representative authority without operational control

The school announces a role-detection examination in which representatives watch mixed-class discussions but cannot instruct participants. The representative has one rule set, participants have another incomplete rule set, and neither side enjoys total knowledge of the other's incentives. Victory depends less on conventional OAA measures than on familiarity with personality, behavior, microexpression, social ties, and the ability to reason under asymmetric information (`COTE:Y2V12:spine12:para0001-para0274`; `COTE:Y2V12:spine14:para0001-para0449`; `Y2V12-E011–E020`, `Y2V12-E051–E065`).

The institutional design therefore turns Year 2's philosophical vocabulary into game mechanics: **partial knowledge, hidden roles, delegated action, and costly inference**.

## 4.3 Ayanokōji's ecology is threatened by autonomous leaders

The Sakayanagi–Ryūen withdrawal pact threatens the four-class contest Ayanokōji has consciously been preparing. He explicitly thinks that historical school dynamics tend toward one dominant class or a two-class duel and that he intended to overturn that structure by preserving all four classes as plausible Class-A competitors into Year 3. Yet he initially insists that a third party should not interfere in the leaders' private agreement (`COTE:Y2V12:spine12:para0148-para0164`; `Y2V12-E021–E024`).

That position becomes one of the volume's most important contradictions. V12 will show that he ultimately does interfere—just indirectly enough that Sakayanagi must perform the terminal act herself.

## 4.4 Kei — separation is being authored before she can choose it

Kei accurately senses increased distance. Ayanokōji asks permission to meet each class leader, and she interprets the request as evidence of trustworthiness. Internally, however, he has already moved from maintaining hope to preparing to extinguish it: `希望を絶つための準備段階` (`COTE:Y2V12:spine12:para0165-para0235`; `Y2V12-E025–E030`).

The scene is therefore almost a laboratory example of disclosure insufficiency. The visible act—asking permission—looks reciprocal. The hidden temporal architecture is not.

## 4.5 Horikita — leadership is asked to accept its own succession test

Horikita asks Ayanokōji to serve as a representative. He accepts only as the `大将`, while placing her in the middle position. If he must enter and win, he demands a Kōenji-like right not to cooperate with class affairs afterward; if he enters and loses, he eventually offers the inverse promise—cooperation until graduation. Horikita accepts because class victory outranks her need to appear as the strongest visible leader (`COTE:Y2V12:spine13:para0133-para0267`; `COTE:Y2V12:spine14:para0383-para0386`; `Y2V12-E031–E040`).

The arrangement is a succession stress test disguised as exam strategy. Ayanokōji wants Horikita's class to win without requiring him. If she fails and he must act, he receives the practical freedom needed for separation.

---## 4.6 Haruka and Amasawa — refusal of reintegration versus legible tethering

Haruka has begun to smile again because Airi's life outside ANHS has forced her to revise the idea that expulsion permanently destroyed her friend. She begins to move toward inviting Ayanokōji back into the successor social circle. He deliberately signals Amasawa to interrupt and internally dismisses further conversation as `価値のないこと`—something not worth additional time (`COTE:Y2V12:spine13:para0271-para0324`; `Y2V12-E041–E044`).

This is not simple social incompetence. He recognizes the possibility of reintegration and declines it. The successor Ayanokōji Group survives precisely because he no longer occupies its center, and V12 shows him choosing to preserve that separation even when a relational route back may be opening.

Amasawa then produces the opposite pattern. She says she has not solved her post-Yagami future but will remain in school and trust that something better may exist. Ayanokōji assigns her an investigation into Nanase's true aim; internally he admits the information is not urgently needed and that the task partly serves to bind Amasawa to school. Amasawa sees through the intervention and thanks him anyway (`COTE:Y2V12:spine13:para0328-para0352`; `Y2V12-E045–E047`).

This is still authored environment, but the manipulation is substantially more legible to the person being influenced. That informational symmetry changes its moral character.

## 4.7 The pre-exam leader circuit — Ayanokōji's claimed neutrality already has an answer

Ayanokōji meets or contacts Ryūen, Ichinose, Kanzaki, Sakayanagi, Hashimoto, Yamamura, and Matsushita. These scenes look like observation and information collection, but they also prepare the terminal manipulations.

Ryūen vows to fight Sakayanagi directly and fairly enough to demonstrate his growth. Ichinose is reminded of the one-year promise while being lied to about Ayanokōji's representative status. Sakayanagi reveals that Kamuro's loss has changed how she approaches classmates: she now seeks individual conversations, admits regret at not knowing Kamuro more deeply, and says Ayanokōji has changed her. Ayanokōji then recognizes that despite his posture of neutral observation, he already knows which of Sakayanagi and Ryūen he wants to survive (`COTE:Y2V12:spine13:para0390-para0829`; `Y2V12-E048–E050`).

The volume therefore establishes preference **before** the encoded intervention. The final message is not a spontaneous rescue. It is execution of an answer already privately chosen.

## 4.8 Hirata and Horikita — different limits of relational competence

Hirata's strengths in empathy, mediation, and social care do not automatically translate into superior hidden-role inference. He loses to Hamaguchi after cautious play and a misread. Horikita subsequently defeats Hamaguchi and a demoralized Kanzaki but is completely dismantled by Ichinose (`COTE:Y2V12:spine15:para0001-para0242`; `COTE:Y2V12:spine17:para0229-para0477`; `Y2V12-E066–E075`, `Y2V12-E086–E098`).

The contrast matters because the examination distinguishes **social breadth** from **social depth**. Horikita has become far more capable of reading people than the Year-1 version of herself, yet Ichinose's accumulated everyday intimacy with classmates becomes a form of knowledge that cannot be quickly copied by strategic intelligence.

## 4.9 Horikita's defeat — failure becomes relationally visible

Ichinose reduces Horikita from ten life to zero without losing a single life herself. Ayanokōji judges the result as genuine proof that Horikita is far behind Ichinose in this particular examination rather than a matter of bad luck. Horikita returns shattered, admits she wanted to win not only for the class but so that Ayanokōji would recognize and praise her, and rejects empty consolation (`COTE:Y2V12:spine17:para0473-para0542`; `Y2V12-E099–E105`).

Ayanokōji responds by embracing her, telling her she does not have to remain strong alone, and directing her toward classmates as future supports. The scene matters for succession precisely because his comfort points **away from himself**: `おまえには仲間がいる`. Yet he immediately follows with total confidence that he will prevent the class from losing (`COTE:Y2V12:spine17:para0543-para0561`; `Y2V12-E106–E110`).

Horikita therefore witnesses not Ayanokōji's failure but the opposite structure: her own limits are exposed and then covered by his certainty. `Y1H-016` cannot close here.

## 4.10 Maezono — procedure becomes culling technology

Ayanokōji and Ichinose agree to neutralize the traitor mechanism. Ichinose improves the proposal by suggesting that both traitors be left alive so each class gains 50 class points. Ayanokōji accepts. He then uses the reciprocal selection process to make Maezono his class's traitor, creates a private confrontation, reassures her that he will not identify her, induces her to deny the role, and then identifies her anyway (`COTE:Y2V12:spine18:para0036-para0258`; `Y2V12-E111–E127`).

His narration removes any plausible “necessary punishment” defense. Maezono's leaks are serious but, by his own account, do not ordinarily merit expulsion and could probably be corrected. He expels her because doing so is useful. The phrase `手近にあった前園という道具` is among the harshest formulations of personhood in the Year-2 corpus.

## 4.11 Ichinose — trust becomes an attack surface

Maezono's expulsion is only the opening. Ayanokōji explicitly reasons that ordinary exam feints cannot destabilize Ichinose because she is too strong within the examination's cognitive frame. He therefore attacks from outside that frame. He tells her that he, not Sakayanagi, placed the shoplifting-exposure letter; explains the strategic benefit of forcing the crisis when he could then rescue her; and declares that all his later interventions contained ulterior motives (`COTE:Y2V12:spine18:para0259-para0349`; `Y2V12-E128–E140`).

The narration then clinically describes her stress physiology—rising heart rate and blood pressure, narrowed visual field, and reduced prefrontal function—and treats the resulting collapse as the mechanism of victory.

This is not merely “Ayanokōji beats Ichinose.” He converts **relationship history into an epistemic weapon**.

## 4.12 Ryūen and Sakayanagi — merit decides one battle, relationship decides the official one

Ryūen genuinely fights Sakayanagi on the terrain he said he would. He uses Hiyori to stabilize Tokitō rather than simply expelling or suppressing him, and the text itself identifies the method as using the environment to produce another person's growth. He also outreads Sakayanagi at one key point. But the overall contest is clear: Sakayanagi remains stronger. Ryūen falls to one life against her five, admits that she has beaten him, and accepts that his straight fight has failed (`COTE:Y2V12:spine19:para0250-para0436`; `Y2V12-E141–E164`).

Only then does the preloaded Ayanokōji route activate. Ryūen tells Sakayanagi that if she uses Hashimoto's group he will assign Hashimoto the traitor role; the “message” is supposedly contained there. Sakayanagi knows the rational choice is to ignore this and finish the match, but curiosity about Ayanokōji draws her into the final dialogue (`COTE:Y2V12:spine19:para0437-para0519`; `Y2V12-E165–E170`).

In that dialogue, Sakayanagi first comes to understand Hashimoto rather than merely classify him. She recognizes the fear and prior injury behind his betrayal habit and even considers giving him another chance. Then she realizes that this cannot be the message. The message has no propositional content because the situation itself is the proposition. Ayanokōji wants Ryūen to remain. He is no longer waiting for Sakayanagi as the opponent whose growth he most wants to see. She infers `ここで負けてくれ`, prevents Hashimoto from completing a confession, refuses to identify him as traitor, loses five life, and ends the exam (`COTE:Y2V12:spine20:para0118-para0281`; `Y2V12-E171–E180`).

The closing paradox is exact:

> `龍園 翔 は負けた。`
>
> `そして坂柳 有栖も負けた。`

Ryūen loses the contest of demonstrated ability. Sakayanagi then chooses the official defeat.

# 5. The special exam as institutional theory — asymmetric knowledge is the actual medium

V12's examination is one of the franchise's clearest institutionalizations of **epistemic asymmetry**.

Representatives possess rules participants do not. Participants possess direct bodily access to each other that representatives lack. Representatives cannot order behavior during the discussion, but they must infer hidden roles from conduct. The school then adds a traitor role whose danger depends on a further information split: the representative can know enough to accuse, but the participant does not know with certainty whether honesty or deception will best serve the class. A lie can produce personal expulsion if the representative correctly identifies it.

This design makes the examination a direct test of Year 2's central distinction between **knowledge** and **jurisdiction**. Knowing another person better improves prediction, but it also gives the representative power to impose consequences the participant cannot fully model.

The school even prevents simple translation of OAA into victory. Academic and physical rankings matter little compared with:

- knowledge of classmates' speech habits;
- familiarity with their emotional leakage;
- ability to detect performance versus sincerity;
- awareness of cross-class relationships;
- comfort under ambiguity;
- and willingness to act under incomplete evidence.

Ichinose's dominance over Horikita demonstrates the point. Ichinose is not simply “higher ability.” She has spent two years accumulating relational data through ordinary intimacy. Her class constitution—often treated as strategically soft—produces an examination-specific form of **high-resolution social knowledge**. She recognizes role-holders because she knows what her friends look like when they are lying, withholding, anxious, or performing.

The exam therefore offers a concrete answer to the `JITSURYOKU` thread:

> **Ability includes access to forms of knowledge that cannot be institutionally measured in advance because they exist in the history between particular people.**

This also explains why Hirata's result should not be flattened into “Hirata is bad at people.” His care leadership and universal orientation operate differently from Ichinose's class-wide memory of individual micro-signals. Social ability is not one scalar.

The traitor mechanic adds the darker lesson. An institution can create a rule in which **trust becomes terminal evidence**. Maezono's expulsion occurs because Ayanokōji knows both her likely answer and the procedural consequence of that answer. The rule does not remove agency. It gives superior predictive knowledge a lethal jurisdictional edge.

# 6. Chabashira and Hoshinomiya — adult pedagogy splits into service and appropriation

V12 gives the cleanest adult ethical comparison in the Year-2 corpus.

Chabashira begins from self-critique. Her prior idea of professionalism was to remain at a “fair” distance from students. She now recognizes that distance partly functioned as escape: if a teacher can support student development without substituting the teacher's own goal for theirs, refusing to engage can itself mean abandoning legitimate pedagogical responsibility.

Hoshinomiya represents the opposite corruption. She does not merely care too much. She appropriates student futures into an unresolved adult grievance. Her prologue makes the motive explicit, and her later approach to Ayanokōji removes any remaining ambiguity. She asks him to throw the exam, offers future privileged information, implies intimate or sexual inducement, and admits that Ichinose's own students are too morally decent to cooperate with this sabotage (`COTE:Y2V12:spine14:para0397-para0449`; `Y2V12-E061–E065`).

The distinction is not intervention versus nonintervention. It is **whose purpose the intervention serves**.

A legitimate teacher may:

- create opportunities for students to learn;
- provide information or support they cannot reasonably access alone;
- help them recover after failure;
- and challenge avoidance that prevents development.

A corrupt teacher converts institutional authority into an instrument for the teacher's unowned past.

This produces a useful pedagogical rule:

> **Support becomes domination when the adult's unresolved need substitutes itself for the student's right to define what success is for.**

The Chabashira/Hoshinomiya mirror also matters for Ayanokōji. Chabashira's development shows that an author can recognize prior overreach and reorient toward student-owned ends. Hoshinomiya shows what happens when trauma instead becomes a permanent license to manipulate. V12 places Ayanokōji between these poles. He understands stopping rules in principle, but his own experiment can still authorize forms of manipulation that subordinate another person's ends to his preferred developmental ecology.

# 7. Ayanokōji Kiyotaka — from environmental author to relational sovereign

## 7.1 The four-class ecology is now explicit political design

V12 confirms that the four-class balance is not merely an analyst's pattern imposed on scattered interventions. Ayanokōji explicitly wanted all four classes to retain plausible Class-A paths into Year 3 and says he intended to overturn the school's familiar drift toward one dominant class or a two-class contest.

This makes his role unmistakably political. He is not simply “helping people grow.” He is attempting to design a **competitive constitution**.

The benefit of that design is real. A four-class ecology can prevent premature monopoly, generate more developmental pressure, preserve multiple leadership styles, and keep alternative political constitutions alive. The danger is equally real: once he treats the four-class ecology as a desired system outcome, other people's decisions can become variables that must be corrected when they threaten it.

## 7.2 V11's stopping rule fails under pressure

At the V11 endpoint, the strongest positive formulation was that legitimate intervention must know where to stop. V12 does not erase that insight. It exposes its fragility.

Ayanokōji initially says the Sakayanagi–Ryūen pact is not something a third party should interrupt. Yet he already knows which leader he wants to survive and creates a contingency that will communicate that preference only if Ryūen loses. He does not cancel the pact. He does not order Sakayanagi. He creates a **relationally legible signal** and lets her decide.

This is precisely why the intervention is ethically difficult. He can plausibly say that Sakayanagi remained free. She did. But he also deliberately selected a communication mode that would work only because he knew the weight she gives his judgment.

V12 therefore REVISES the optimistic form of the V11 stopping rule:

> **Stopping before the final act is insufficient if one's earlier design has made the other person's final act predictably serve one's preference.**

A stronger stopping rule must ask not only “Did I leave them the last choice?” but also:

> **“Did I preserve a meaningful possibility that their answer could diverge from mine without relational punishment, informational disadvantage, or engineered collapse?”**

## 7.3 He protects systems while sacrificing persons

Ayanokōji repeatedly distinguishes institutional preservation from personal preservation.

He protects Horikita's class victory. He allows Ichinose's class to gain 50 points and avoids expelling one of its members. He preserves Ryūen as the opponent whose further growth he wants. Yet these system-level protections coexist with severe person-level harm:

- Maezono is expelled because she is useful as a disposable instrument;
- Ichinose's trust is deliberately shattered to disable her cognition;
- Sakayanagi's love is used as a channel through which his preference can override her class duty.

This makes “protection” a morally incomplete category. An actor can protect the **continuity of a developmental system** while violating the autonomy of the people through whom that system is sustained.

## 7.4 Truth becomes another manipulable resource

V12 is especially severe about disclosure because Ayanokōji does not rely only on lies. He uses truth, partial truth, unverifiable truth, and strategically framed truth as different tools.

With Ichinose, the point is not simply whether each sentence about the shoplifting incident is factually accurate. The scene itself says that `真実と不実`, truth and falsehood, are now cognitively inseparable for her. He chooses the timing and framing so that she cannot audit the information while remaining capable of competing.

This yields a major `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD` principle:

> **Truth delivered under conditions designed to destroy the recipient's capacity to evaluate it is not the same thing as disclosure for informed choice.**

Ayanokōji's V12 disclosures are therefore often epistemically richer but ethically less reciprocal than silence.

## 7.5 Post-Volume-0 recontextualization: preference engineering reaches relational maturity

Volume 0 allows the reader to see a structural resemblance to Atsuomi's “sushi/curry” politics: the most durable control is achieved when the target experiences the preferred action as their own reasoned choice.

V12 is the most mature school-era version of that grammar so far. Sakayanagi is not told what to do; she reconstructs what the valued other wants and converts that preference into her own action. Ichinose's dependence on Ayanokōji is not externally imposed at the moment of the exam; it has accumulated through a long history of support and trust and can therefore be reversed into vulnerability.

But the difference from Atsuomi remains essential. Kiyotaka's ends are not reducible to political reproduction, and the school environment has produced desires in him that Atsuomi did not assign. The analogy is structural, not total.

# 8. Horikita Suzune — growth is real enough that failure can become hers

Horikita's V12 defeat is not a narrative cancellation of her development. It is possible **because** her development has become substantial enough that she can now own a meaningful failure.

Earlier Horikita often failed while operating with incomplete social competence and hidden Ayanokōji correction. Here she makes real representative choices, defeats two opponents, and enters the Ichinose match with developed strategic judgment. The resulting 0–10 defeat is therefore painful in a new way: she cannot dismiss it as the failure of a naïve earlier self.

Her confession that she wanted Ayanokōji's praise is equally important. Horikita has long sought recognition—from Manabu first, then increasingly from her class and from Ayanokōji. V12 makes that desire explicit without reducing her leadership to it. She wanted class victory **and** personal recognition. The motives coexist.

Ayanokōji's embrace is one of his most relationally direct acts. He does not merely diagnose her. He gives physical comfort, recognizes that she has been carrying pressure alone, and tells her to depend on the class. That instruction is succession-oriented: the correct future support system is not “call me when you fail” but “remember that you have comrades.”

Yet his nondisclosure makes the tenderness politically unstable. He knows the class relationship is close to ending. Horikita does not. The person comforting her for future trials is also silently preparing to remove himself from the structure through which she currently interprets that support.

This is why V12 still cannot answer `Y1H-004` or `Y1H-016`. Horikita receives genuine care without the information needed to interpret its temporal horizon, and she witnesses Ayanokōji's certainty rather than his failure.

# 9. Ichinose Honami — relational ability becomes both sovereign competence and exploitable dependence

Ichinose's V12 performance vindicates much of the Year-2 analysis of her class constitution.

Her intimacy with classmates is not merely sentimental cohesion. It has become an **operational epistemic asset**. She can identify role-holders from minute deviations in people she knows well. She reads Kanzaki's despair without being briefed. She recognizes the nature of the exam quickly enough to believe she can defeat Ayanokōji himself.

This is a direct answer to the old accusation that solidarity and competitive competence must be opposites. V12 shows solidarity producing a specialized competence no rival can immediately imitate.

But the same relational openness creates a vulnerability at a different scale. Ichinose's strongest source of psychological security is Ayanokōji. She explicitly says that facing him makes her nervous and simultaneously gives her the feeling of being supported. That contradiction is the opening through which he attacks.

The attack is effective because trust has become embodied. He does not need to convince her through a carefully argued case. He needs to make the trusted person suddenly incompatible with the model through which she has stabilized herself. Once he does, her cognitive performance collapses.

This is why the scene should not be summarized as “love makes Ichinose weak.” Her love and trust were also major sources of Year-2 strength. The correct formulation is more exact:

> **Dependence becomes an attack surface when one person possesses disproportionate power over the truth conditions of the relationship.**

The class-level bargain also creates a cruel constitutional irony. Ichinose behaves responsibly toward both classes' ordinary members. She proposes a mutually beneficial way to preserve traitors and earn 50 points. Ayanokōji accepts that cooperative institution and uses its trust assumptions as the mechanism of Maezono's elimination.

The cooperative constitution is not inherently naïve. It is vulnerable because one participant possesses a hidden objective the other cannot price.

---# 10. Sakayanagi Arisu — recognition matures into relational self-abdication

V10 and V11 forced Sakayanagi to recognize that people she had treated as pieces could become historically irreplaceable. Kamuro's expulsion gave her the concept too late. Yamamura gave her a second chance to practice recognition before loss. V12 shows genuine development from those experiences: Sakayanagi now meets classmates individually, wants to know them rather than merely know about them, and even begins reconsidering Hashimoto after understanding the fear structure behind his betrayals.

This is significant development. It also makes the final choice more tragic rather than less.

Before Ayanokōji's encoded message becomes legible, Sakayanagi is prepared to win. She thinks of Yamamura, her class, Kamuro's memory, and the possibility of reintegrating Hashimoto. She knows that class duty requires victory. The text makes the merit result clear: Ryūen is at one life, Sakayanagi at five, Ryūen has already conceded that he lost the straight contest, and Sakayanagi believes victory requires only one more push.

Then she understands Ayanokōji's preference.

The phrase `ここで負けてくれ` is not spoken to her by Ayanokōji. It is the proposition she reconstructs. That distinction is analytically central. Sakayanagi is not deprived of cognition; her exceptional cognition is exactly what makes the indirect message possible. Ayanokōji uses the trait that most defines her—her ability to see the hidden structure—to deliver a message no one else could receive.

Her ensuing defeat is therefore a case of **authentic agency serving another person's authored preference**.

This should not be mislabeled as straightforward coercion. She can refuse. No formal punishment follows refusal. She explicitly sees the class-centered reasons to continue. But the relational stakes are immense: she understands the message as evidence that the person she most wants to fight and be recognized by does not want her to remain as his future opponent. The inferred preference alters the meaning of victory itself. Winning would preserve her class position but confirm that the future she most desired is unavailable.

V12 thus exposes a severe form of relational jurisdiction:

> **A person can become sovereign over another person's choice without possessing formal authority if their approval, desire, or recognition has become sufficiently constitutive of the other's ends.**

Sakayanagi's Year-2 development does not fail because she becomes more relational. It fails politically because one relationship remains disproportionately sovereign over the rest.

This intensifies `Y1H-029`. Aristocratic optimization has gained empathy, but the sovereign private priority can still override the fiduciary obligation to the class. The earlier `Y1H-028` answer—that she cannot reliably keep fiduciary duty above Ayanokōji priority at this boundary—is not reversed; V12 makes it more consequential.

# 11. Ryūen Kakeru — development is proven precisely because he loses on merit

Ryūen's V12 result should not be read as another clever Ryūen comeback.

His own stated aim is to fight Sakayanagi directly enough that Ayanokōji can see how much he has grown. He does. He deploys more sophisticated institutional reasoning than the Year-1 tyrant, anticipates sabotage, distributes roles, and uses Hiyori not as a blunt enforcer but as a relational stabilizer for Tokitō. When Sakayanagi recognizes what he has done, she explicitly senses a faint trace of Ayanokōji's environmental method: using what already exists in the environment to create another person's developmental opportunity.

That is real growth.

The cleanest evidence comes from failure. Ryūen does not need to pretend he won. Once Sakayanagi has established the decisive edge, he admits `この勝負……俺の負けだ`. He recognizes her superior play, particularly her breadth of vision, low error rate, and ability to convert his information into her own advantage.

This matters for `RYUEN_FEAR_LEGITIMACY`. Earlier Ryūen frequently made domination inseparable from his political identity. V12 demonstrates a different form of strength: he can lose a direct strategic contest without psychologically needing to falsify the result.

Hiyori's role remains more complicated. She prevents Tokitō from turning Sakayanagi's provocation into violence and tells him he still has a place in the class. But Ryūen had pre-positioned Hiyori partly because he predicted precisely that she could stabilize Tokitō. Hiyori therefore exerts genuine moral influence on Tokitō while simultaneously functioning inside Ryūen's authored design.

This does **not** yet close `Y1H-026`. V12 shows Hiyori helping humanize the operation and helping Tokitō internalize restraint. It does not show Hiyori causing Ryūen to renounce coercive governance or accept a moral limit he otherwise would reject.

The official survival of Ryūen's side at the end of V12 must also be described carefully. On the evidence available inside this volume, he **loses on merit** and then benefits from Sakayanagi's deliberate surrender under Ayanokōji's encoded preference. V12.5 is excluded, so the later administrative execution of the private withdrawal pact cannot be imported here.

# 12. Hashimoto Masayoshi — betrayal is re-read as survival before it is forgiven

Hashimoto's final conversation with Sakayanagi is one of V12's quieter but most consequential relational scenes.

Sakayanagi has every strategic reason to treat him as a finished traitor. She also promised Kamuro that she would punish him. Yet the conversation shifts from classification to explanation. She identifies the behavioral residue of earlier persecution, his tendency to retreat to private spaces when afraid, and the survival logic that made preemptive betrayal feel safer than depending on another person's loyalty.

Hashimoto reacts with anger because recognition initially feels like appropriation: someone who has not lived his history should not claim easy understanding. But the act of being seen nevertheless reaches him. He begins crying before he understands why.

The scene does not excuse betrayal. Sakayanagi explicitly conditions any possible reintegration on his future conduct. What changes is the ontology: Hashimoto is no longer merely “a treacherous piece.” His strategy has a developmental history.

This would normally have produced a restorative possibility. Sakayanagi imagines bringing him back into the class and rebuilding. Ayanokōji's encoded message interrupts precisely at that moment.

There is therefore a second irony. V11's recognition-without-ownership ethic is emerging **inside Sakayanagi** just as her own relationship with Ayanokōji causes her to abandon the institutional future in which she could practice it.

# 13. Maezono — artificial selection returns in its most explicit person-as-tool form

V05 introduced `人為淘汰`—artificial selection—as a grammar that ANHS can reproduce under scarcity. V12 makes the culling branch of Ayanokōji's authorship even less abstract.

Three facts must remain together:

1. Maezono has actually violated class trust by leaking information.
2. Ayanokōji believes the behavior is correctable and explicitly says it is not ordinarily expulsion-worthy.
3. He chooses expulsion anyway because the current exam provides a useful mechanism and her removal serves several objectives at once.

This is why the “tool” language is so important. V12 is not asking the reader to decide whether Maezono is innocent. It is asking whether culpability grants another actor **unlimited sanctioning jurisdiction**.

Ayanokōji's answer in practice is yes: once the person is useful as a sacrifice, he combines genuine wrongdoing with a punishment selected for his own hidden purposes.

The scene also revises the relation between proof and sanction established in V05. There, Kushida's guilt did not logically entail expulsion; Horikita exercised independent judgment about sanction. Here, Ayanokōji uses a valid procedural rule to create an expulsion outcome whose severity he himself knows exceeds the ordinary offense.

The governing rule becomes:

> **Procedural validity does not cleanse authored disproportionality.**

This is a direct `PROTECTION_OWNERSHIP` and `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD` escalation.

# 14. Kei Karuizawa — visible permission inside an invisible separation plan

# 15. Haruka, Airi, and the refusal to restore the old center

V07 established that the Ayanokōji Group survives by ceasing to be the Ayanokōji Group. V12 adds an important active choice by Ayanokōji: even when Haruka appears ready to open a path toward re-entry, he prevents the invitation from reaching completion.

The narration's `価値のないこと` language is intentionally cold. Ayanokōji does not need the relationship for his current design, and the successor group has already reorganized without him. But it is also a negative case for relational valuation. A person may have historical meaning without current strategic value, and V12 still shows Ayanokōji capable of treating such a conversation as an inefficient expenditure of time.

# 16. Amasawa Ichika — a comparatively legitimate form of authored support

Amasawa offers the volume's most useful internal control case.

Ayanokōji gives her an investigative task he does not urgently need because activity and responsibility may help anchor her to school after Yagami. This is environmental authorship. Yet Amasawa recognizes the motive, names it, and can decide whether to accept the tether.

The information asymmetry is therefore much lower than in the Kei, Ichinose, Maezono, or Sakayanagi cases.

This suggests a practical criterion for legitimate developmental intervention:

> **An intervention becomes more compatible with autonomy when the person can identify what the intervener is trying to do, understand the relevant costs, refuse without catastrophic penalty, and still redefine the task's meaning for themselves.**

Amasawa's future remains open. V12 does not prove that the task creates a self-authored life. It does show that manipulation need not be hidden in order to be effective.

# 17. Hiyori Shiina — moral stabilization inside someone else's strategy

Hiyori's intervention with Tokitō is one of the volume's clearest demonstrations of quiet agency.

Sakayanagi's class deliberately provokes Tokitō in the mixed discussion. He moves toward physical violence. Hiyori calmly takes his raised fist, refuses to be intimidated, tells him that he is not the kind of person who would hit her, and insists that he has a place in the class now and in the future (`COTE:Y2V12:spine19:para0254-para0291`; `Y2V12-E151–E156`).

The intervention works because it does not threaten him into obedience. It gives him a more desirable identity to inhabit. Ryūen had anticipated this possibility and placed Hiyori in the group partly because her presence could stop Tokitō from self-destructing.

This creates a fascinating nested authorship:

- Ryūen designs the environment;
- Hiyori performs the moral intervention;
- Tokitō chooses to lower the fist;
- and the class gains a more stable dissenter rather than simply removing one.

This is development compared with V11's direct punitive treatment of Tokitō. But the moral credit cannot be assigned solely to Ryūen. The stabilizing content is Hiyori's: recognition, belonging, and refusal to treat dissent as disposability.

`Y1H-026` remains open because the question is whether Hiyori can morally redirect **Ryūen himself**, not whether she can rescue people inside Ryūen's system. V12 strengthens the possibility without demonstrating the closure condition.

# 18. Relational jurisdiction — a new analytical category

V12 requires a category more precise than “manipulation.”

## 18.1 Command

An actor explicitly orders or threatens another person. The causal responsibility is visible. Ryūen's older violence-based governance often operates here.

## 18.2 Stage

An actor designs incentives, information, timing, witnesses, or opportunities so that other people generate the outcome. V07's Yagami operation is the clearest earlier example.

## 18.3 Relational jurisdiction

A valued person's anticipated approval, disappointment, recognition, love, or withdrawal becomes an internal reason powerful enough to alter the target's choice. The influential actor may not need formal authority or explicit command.

V12 makes this third form central.

Sakayanagi decodes an unstated preference because Ayanokōji's view of her matters more than the class-centered future immediately available. Ichinose's trust in Ayanokōji is strong enough that his reinterpretation of their shared history can collapse the cognitive platform on which she competes. Kei's desire to be loved allows Ayanokōji to control the pace of hope while disclosing far less about the relationship's intended endpoint.

Relational jurisdiction is not inherently abusive. Parents, friends, lovers, teachers, and teammates inevitably influence one another because their judgments matter. The ethical problem is **asymmetry plus strategic use**.

A useful test is:

| Question | More reciprocal form | More dominating form |
|---|---|---|
| Is the influence legible? | Target knows what the other wants and why | Preference is hidden, encoded, or strategically timed |
| Can the target refuse? | Refusal preserves relationship/standing | Refusal threatens belonging, love, safety, or future access |
| Is material information shared? | Both can model the relevant future | One controls the transition timetable or causal history |
| Is the target's motive respected? | Influence invites a choice | Influence is calibrated to predictably produce the preferred choice |
| Can meaning diverge? | Target can reinterpret the intervention | Target's desire is used as an instrument of the author's project |

Amasawa is relatively close to the reciprocal side. Maezono is near the dominating extreme. Ichinose and Sakayanagi are difficult middle cases because their agency remains substantial while the hidden author deliberately recruits their relationships to him.

The exam role literalizes the gap between social trust and institutional role. A participant can be “traitor” as assigned game function without being a traitor in the broad moral sense, while Maezono's preexisting information leak gives the role a second layer of real class betrayal.

## 19.2 `道具` — tool

Ayanokōji's description of Maezono as a convenient tool is not metaphorically innocent. It marks the point where a person with correctable wrongdoing is subordinated to another actor's multi-objective optimization.

Calling Maezono `不良品` deliberately echoes the series' long history of classifying low-performing or problematic students as defective. In the Kei material, this phrase names temporal authorship. He is not simply uncertain about the relationship. He is moving into a phase whose purpose is to prepare the end while she remains uninformed.

## 19.5 `完封` — shutout

Ayanokōji describes Ichinose's defeat of Horikita as a complete shutout. The term matters because it prevents later victory from retroactively reducing Horikita's loss to bad luck.

## 19.6 `ここで負けてくれ` — lose here

This is the most important non-spoken “message” in the volume. Its authorship is distributed: Ayanokōji creates the route; Sakayanagi reconstructs the proposition; Sakayanagi then chooses to enact it.

## 19.7 `勝者と敗者` — winner and loser

The final contradiction—Ryūen lost and Sakayanagi also lost—distinguishes merit result from official outcome. The volume refuses to let formal victory erase who actually demonstrated superior ability.

# 21. Formal strengths and critical reservations

## 21.1 Strength — the exam mechanics and the volume's ethics are the same structure

V12's best formal achievement is that the special exam is not decorative complexity. Its rules instantiate the novel's philosophical problem: actors decide under partial knowledge while other actors possess asymmetric information capable of imposing consequences. The traitor mechanic is especially strong because the same rule supports cooperation, temptation, deception, culling, and relational trust.

## 21.2 Strength — Horikita's loss preserves rather than cancels development

A weaker structure would need Horikita to defeat Ichinose to “prove” growth. V12 instead allows a developed Horikita to encounter a form of competence she does not possess and lose badly. That makes development nonteleological: becoming better does not mean becoming superior to everyone in every domain.

## 21.3 Strength — Ryūen's clean loss is more developmental than another trick victory

Ryūen's admission that Sakayanagi beat him is evidence of a self capable of processing defeat without rewriting reality. The later official result does not negate that merit outcome, because the narration explicitly preserves both losses.

## 21.4 Strength — Sakayanagi's surrender is ethically difficult because it is voluntary

The novel would be simpler if Ayanokōji blackmailed Sakayanagi. Instead he uses a message that exists only if she can reconstruct it. The scene therefore forces a distinction between autonomy and independence from influence. Sakayanagi's choice is hers, yet its motive is relationally colonized by the value she assigns Ayanokōji's desire.

## 21.5 Strength — care and cruelty coexist in Ayanokōji without being collapsed into one hidden “true self”

The man who embraces Horikita and tells her to rely on friends is the same man who shortly afterward describes Maezono as a tool and dismantles Ichinose through relationship history. V12 does not require one set of scenes to reveal the other as fake. The more disturbing interpretation is that both capacities are real.

## 21.6 Reservation — Ayanokōji's interpretive dominance risks overdetermining other characters

The Ichinose sequence in particular gives Ayanokōji near-clinical certainty about how stress will alter cognition. The scene is thematically coherent, but his explanatory voice can make another character's interior appear fully captured by his model. Later analysis should remain alert to evidence that Ichinose interprets the event differently once immediate shock has passed; V12 itself cannot import that later answer.

## 21.7 Reservation — Sakayanagi's relational priority creates a sharp fiduciary plausibility problem

The text intentionally recognizes the problem by making her think about her classmates and class duty before choosing defeat. That makes the decision legible, but also underscores how severe it is. The class bears enormous unchosen consequences from a preference rooted in one private relationship.

## 21.8 Reservation — the exam's hidden-rule elegance can obscure sanction proportionality

Maezono's expulsion is procedurally valid inside the game. The novel's own narration fortunately states that her underlying offense is not ordinarily expulsion-worthy. That admission is essential; without it, the game mechanic could make punishment look self-justifying merely because a rule permits it.

# 23. Historical first-pass revision

The older broad Year-2 synthesis characterized the late second year as Ayanokōji deliberately shaping conditions in which Horikita, Ichinose, Ryūen, Sakayanagi, and others could evolve, break, or be tested. The V2 Japanese-primary pass **STRENGTHENS** that observation but **REVISES** its level of precision.

The key V12 distinction is not simply “he shapes everyone.” It is that he uses several distinct modes of authorship:

- succession design with Horikita;
- legible tethering with Amasawa;
- culling through procedure with Maezono;
- psychological disablement through trust with Ichinose;
- and encoded relational preference with Sakayanagi.

The old “experimental force” formulation is therefore too unitary. V12 demonstrates that the ethical character of environmental authorship depends on **information symmetry, refusal cost, sanction proportionality, relational leverage, and whether the target can generate an answer that the author did not preselect**.

# 26. Cumulative Year-2 thread state after V12

V12 does not require a new top-level Year-1 handoff category, but it materially changes the **shape** of several Year-2 longitudinal threads. The distinction matters. A state ledger is not a scoreboard in which every important volume must move a question from one bucket to another. Sometimes a volume supplies the strongest evidence yet for an already-established answer, deepens an unresolved contradiction, or creates a more precise vocabulary for a problem whose closure condition has not yet been met.

## 26.1 `ENVIRONMENTAL_AUTHORSHIP`: from control to recruitment of agency

The Year-2 sequence has progressively complicated what it means to author another person's environment.

- **V02–V03:** actors compete over jurisdiction and hidden attribution.
- **V04:** rival authorships collide; successful control becomes distributed counter-authorship rather than one-person omnipotence.
- **V05:** authorship acquires responsibility because it can cultivate, succeed, or cull.
- **V06:** the developer begins designing conditions under which competence can survive his absence.
- **V07:** direct command gives way to staged agency.
- **V08–V09:** richer relational knowledge allows actors to produce answers that exceed models.
- **V09.5:** desire itself becomes a source of counter-authorship.
- **V10:** attachments create relational stakes that optimization cannot fully price.
- **V11:** recognition introduces a possible stopping rule: knowing another person better need not imply jurisdiction over what they should choose.
- **V12:** that stopping rule is stress-tested and repeatedly violated through **relational jurisdiction**.

The important V12 advance is that environmental authorship no longer requires command, explicit threat, or even a manufactured external stage. If an author knows another person's values deeply enough, the other person's *own* intelligence, love, loyalty, fear, or desire may become the mechanism that completes the preferred outcome.

That produces a stricter causal principle:

> **Agency in the target does not negate authorship in the environment designer. The two can be simultaneously real.**

This is why Sakayanagi's final decision cannot be reduced either to “Ayanokōji forced her” or to “she freely chose, so he is uninvolved.” The text supports neither simplification. She understands, interprets, and chooses. He creates the communicative route precisely because he can predict what her attachment and intelligence will do with it.

## 26.2 `SUCCESSION_SEPARATION`: Horikita is being prepared for absence, not merely promoted

The V12 representative arrangement makes the succession project more explicit. Ayanokōji accepts the terminal representative role while expressing a preference that he never need to act. Horikita occupies the middle position and is expected to solve as much of the examination as possible before the hidden security layer becomes necessary.

The contract he demands if he must intervene and win is revealing: he seeks Kōenji-like freedom from class obligations afterward. This makes his participation a **transitional guarantee** rather than a promise of permanent guardianship.

Horikita's own conduct supports the development side of this project. She accepts the structure because class victory matters more than her personal status. She defeats Ichinose's earlier representatives efficiently, then suffers a genuine and devastating mismatch against Ichinose herself. What follows is equally important: Ayanokōji does not tell Horikita to become more dependent on him. He tells her:

> `おまえには仲間がいる。`

The direction of support is outward, toward the class.

Yet V12 also preserves the contradiction. When Horikita reaches the limit of her present competence, Ayanokōji remains the terminal security layer and guarantees the result. The succession architecture therefore remains transitional rather than complete.

## 26.3 `HORIKITA_INDEPENDENCE` and `HORIKITA_LEADERSHIP`: failure becomes developmental evidence

V12 is one of the strongest Horikita-development volumes precisely because she loses badly.

Ichinose's 10–0 sequence is not framed as bad luck. Ayanokōji's evaluation is that Horikita is genuinely inferior to Ichinose for this particular examination, which rewards accumulated interpersonal knowledge and fine-grained social inference. Horikita's defeat therefore protects the series from a simplistic developmental ladder in which leadership growth means becoming universally superior.

Her response is significant. She returns to the class, acknowledges failure, apologizes, and admits that she wanted not only class victory but Ayanokōji's recognition. This combines political responsibility with personal desire rather than pretending the latter does not exist.

The embrace that follows is not a reversal of the succession project. It is a moment in which support and separation coexist. Ayanokōji can provide immediate care while still directing her long-term dependence away from him.

## 26.4 `ICHINOSE_SOLIDARITY` / `ICHINOSE_SELF_WORTH`: relational mastery becomes vulnerability to relational jurisdiction

V12 first gives Ichinose perhaps her strongest institutional demonstration yet. She turns years of attention to classmates into extraordinary hidden-role inference, protects her own members, and overwhelms Horikita without sacrificing the solidaristic structure of her class.

This is a mature form of **generative ability**. Her strength is not simply friendliness. She has transformed accumulated care into politically usable knowledge.

That same architecture creates the vulnerability Ayanokōji attacks.

He does not defeat Ichinose by proving that her class method is strategically worthless. He targets the person whose trust in him has become one of the organizing supports of her psychological world. The resulting asymmetry is therefore especially severe:

> **Ichinose's relational competence protects her class from strangers and rivals, but it cannot protect her from the person to whom she has voluntarily granted unusual epistemic and emotional authority.**

This is why the V12 sequence should not be summarized as “love makes Ichinose weak.” Her love coexists with immense competence. The vulnerability arises from a specific trust relationship combined with radical information asymmetry.

## 26.5 `RYUEN_FEAR_LEGITIMACY`: developmental leadership and personal wager remain unresolved

Ryūen's performance in V12 provides unusually clean evidence of development. He wants to confront Sakayanagi directly rather than escape through cheap circumvention; he accepts the reality of a merit defeat; and his environment now contains actors such as Hiyori who can preserve people he once might simply have discarded.

Hiyori's intervention with Tokitō is particularly revealing. Ryūen anticipated the possibility of Tokitō destabilizing and placed Hiyori where her temperament could interrupt that trajectory. That is environmental authorship in a developmental rather than purely punitive mode.

But the private withdrawal pact remains a constitutional defect. Ryūen can personally consent to wager his school membership. His class did not collectively consent to the succession shock and class-point consequences that his voluntary withdrawal would impose on them. Personal courage and collective jurisdiction are not the same thing.

## 26.6 `SAKAYANAGI_RIVALRY_GENIUS`: relational development is real, but private sovereignty survives it

Kamuro's loss changed Sakayanagi. V12 makes that development behavioral rather than rhetorical: she begins meeting classmates individually, wants to know them, revisits what she failed to understand about Kamuro, and becomes capable of seeing Hashimoto's fear and history rather than reducing him to the category “traitor.”

The tears Hashimoto cannot control are evidence that this recognition matters.

Yet Sakayanagi's final choice demonstrates that relational development has not displaced Ayanokōji as her highest private priority. The problem is not that she becomes irrational or stupid. She understands the fiduciary argument for winning. She understands her classmates' interests. She understands the pact's consequences. She can even imagine repair with Hashimoto.

She chooses the inferred Ayanokōji preference anyway.

Thus V12 strengthens a difficult conclusion:

> **A person can become more humane in how she sees others while remaining politically unfit to subordinate a sovereign private attachment to the interests of the institution she leads.**

## 26.7 `KEI_DEPENDENCY_AUTONOMY`: ordinary consent language conceals exceptional nondisclosure

Kei's scene is constructed around the language of trust. Ayanokōji asks permission to meet the four leaders; Kei reads that gesture as evidence that the relationship has matured enough for him to seek her consent rather than simply disappear.

But the reader has access to a radically different layer. He is already preparing to extinguish her hope and deliberately withholds the timetable that would make her consent meaningful.

This is not evidence that every affectionate act between them is fake. It is evidence that **procedural gestures of consent cannot compensate for missing information about the decision that matters most**.

The V12 boundary must therefore remain strict. The relationship's next administrative/emotional state is outside the admissible source. At V12, `Y1H-009` stays open with strongly negative present evidence.

## 26.8 `EXPULSION_DISPOSABILITY`: Maezono converts procedure into disproportionate culling

V05 established that culling can use a sophisticated model of personality, social network, and likely future contribution rather than OAA alone. V12 shows an even more troubling form.

Maezono has committed a real wrong by leaking information through Hashimoto's network. Ayanokōji nevertheless recognizes that the offense is ordinarily correctable and not inherently expulsion-worthy. He uses the examination's traitor mechanic because it is available, convenient, and useful to his larger plan.

His own language is deliberately dehumanizing: Maezono becomes a nearby `道具`, later a `不良品` in the psychological attack on Ichinose.

The distinction between **proof of wrongdoing** and **legitimate sanction** therefore returns in a harsher form. The procedure validly permits expulsion. That does not make the chosen sanction proportionate.

## 26.9 `PROTECTION_OWNERSHIP`: protecting a class can mean violating a person

V12 supplies perhaps the cleanest evidence yet that “protection” has multiple objects.

Ayanokōji protects Horikita's class outcome. He also avoids sacrificing Ichinose's classmates to produce his preferred psychological result. But the method involves destroying Ichinose's epistemic confidence in a relationship she experienced as a source of safety.

The protection question must therefore always specify:

- protection of whom;
- from what;
- at whose cost;
- and with what jurisdiction.

A strategy can protect a **polity** while violating a **person** inside another polity.

## 26.10 `ANHS_EXPERIMENT` / `WHITE_ROOM`: plurality does not automatically equal freedom

Post-Volume-0 reader knowledge makes V12 especially severe. ANHS is genuinely more plural than the White Room. Ichinose, Ryūen, Sakayanagi, Horikita, Hiyori, Hashimoto, Kanzaki, and others develop their own ends and can surprise one another. The institution contains friendships, ordinary life, rival constitutions, and futures not determined by one central curriculum.

But V12 shows that plurality can itself become material for a more sophisticated author. Ayanokōji does not need to erase other people's desires. He can preserve them, study them, and sometimes **recruit them**.

That is why the proof that ANHS is a genuine alternative cannot be merely “people have choices.” The stronger proof would require that people can make consequential choices against the author's preferred outcome **while possessing enough information to know what they are refusing**.

# 32. Governing Year-2 question update

The Year-2 method asks:

> **What happens when a person who escaped being another institution's curriculum becomes capable of designing the environments in which other people develop—and can that new authorship remain compatible with the freedom he originally sought for himself?**

V12 supplies the strongest provisional answer so far, but not the final Year-2 answer.

Ayanokōji's authorship has clearly become **generative**. He is not merely solving exams or concealing ability. He has helped create conditions in which:

- Horikita can govern and fail without collapsing;
- Sudō's development has survived its original motive;
- Kushida can cooperate for self-authored reasons;
- Ryūen's class contains specialized counterweights and developmental functions;
- Ichinose has transformed solidarity into formidable adversarial competence;
- Sakayanagi has become more capable of seeing classmates as persons;
- Amasawa can imagine a school future after Yagami;
- and the four classes remain more competitive than the school's historical tendency toward early collapse would predict.

If the question were only **“Can he develop other people?”**, Year 2 has already answered yes.

If the question were only **“Can those people become capable of acting without him?”**, the answer is also increasingly yes.

V12 reveals the harder question:

> **Can he tolerate their independent agency when it produces an answer he does not want?**

The Sakayanagi–Ryūen pact is the decisive test. V11 gave him a local stopping rule: there are domains he should not enter. V12 presents an outcome generated by two autonomous actors that threatens his preferred four-class ecology. He does not simply accept the unwanted answer. He creates a contingency that preserves formal choice while making his preference legible to the person most likely to obey it voluntarily.

That is neither ordinary dictatorship nor clean pluralism.

It is a form of **meta-authorship** in which independent actors remain independent enough to generate motives, but the better-informed author selectively intervenes when their motives threaten the experiment he values.

Post-Volume-0, the resemblance to Atsuomi's preference-engineering grammar is structurally unavoidable. Atsuomi's ideal form of power is to alter circumstances until the subject's own preference produces the desired result. V12 does not establish conscious imitation or identical purpose. Ayanokōji values plural development, ordinary experience, rivalry, friendship, and the possibility of being defeated in ways Atsuomi's political project did not. But their methods can converge at the level of **causal grammar**:

> do not force the subject if the subject's own reasons can be made to carry the outcome.

The distinction between them therefore cannot rest on whether the target experiences choice. It must rest on harder constraints:

- Was the purpose genuinely shared?
- Was decisive information available?
- Could the participant refuse without disproportionate relational or institutional cost?
- Did the author accept outcomes he disliked?
- Was the intervention proportionate?
- Could the participant reinterpret the relationship and still remain safe?
- Did development survive in a direction the developer did not prefer?

At the V12 boundary, Ayanokōji passes some of these tests in some relationships and fails them spectacularly in others.

That asymmetry is the current answer.

# 33. Final synthesis

V12 is the volume in which *Classroom of the Elite* most clearly demonstrates why **freedom cannot be measured solely by the presence of choice**.

Almost nobody important in this volume is a puppet.

Horikita chooses to take responsibility. Ichinose chooses to trust. Maezono chooses to deny. Ryūen chooses to fight directly and later to activate a route prepared for defeat. Hiyori chooses to stop Tokitō. Hashimoto chooses betrayal. Sakayanagi chooses to infer a message she could ignore and then chooses the official loss she believes Ayanokōji wants.

Those choices are real.

And yet the volume is saturated with authorship.

The institution distributes different rule sets to representatives and participants. Hoshinomiya tries to purchase a student outcome through adult authority and future information. Ayanokōji arranges his representative role so Horikita develops under a hidden guarantee. He gives Amasawa a task partly because continued purpose may keep her connected to school. He refuses Haruka's possible reintegration because he has already judged the successor friendship healthier without him. He uses a traitor mechanic to expel Maezono even though he knows the underlying misconduct is normally correctable. He weaponizes years of Ichinose's trust by disclosing truths at exactly the moment those truths will destroy her ability to evaluate him. And when Sakayanagi is about to produce a rational outcome he does not want, he does not issue an order. He creates a route through which she can discover what he wants and then lets her own exceptional intelligence and attachment do the rest.

That final operation is the volume's governing image of power.

Ryūen loses the direct contest. He says so. Sakayanagi is stronger on the merits of the examination. The official outcome is nevertheless reversed because Sakayanagi willingly accepts the defeat that preserves Ryūen.

The closing contradiction—Ryūen lost, and Sakayanagi also lost—is not merely a clever plot twist. It identifies two different jurisdictions of victory.

Ryūen loses the **merit contest**.

Sakayanagi loses the **institutional result**.

Ayanokōji wins the **environmental preference** without taking the field.

Sakayanagi still authors the final choice.

No single sentence such as “Ayanokōji manipulated her” or “Sakayanagi chose freely” is adequate. V12 requires both truths at once.

That is why **relational jurisdiction** is the appropriate concept. A valued person's anticipated preference can become part of another person's internal reason structure. In ordinary life this is inevitable and often benign: people make choices partly because friends, lovers, teachers, or rivals matter to them. The ethical danger appears when one party has superior information, intentionally activates that attachment, and can predict the response while remaining insulated from reciprocal exposure.

Sakayanagi is the most sophisticated example because her intelligence does not protect her. It becomes part of the mechanism. She is able to infer the message precisely because she understands Ayanokōji unusually well.

Ichinose provides the inverse case. Her relational intelligence makes her nearly unbeatable in an examination based on knowing people. She destroys Horikita 10–0 because years of care have accumulated into extraordinary social knowledge. But the person she trusts most possesses information about their relationship that she does not. Her greatest strength cannot defend against an asymmetry inside the relationship that grants the attacker epistemic jurisdiction over the history she thought they shared.

Horikita occupies the more hopeful middle.

She fails. She cries. She admits wanting recognition. She accepts comfort. None of those facts erase her political development. Indeed, they make that development more credible. Her class has increasingly become capable of functioning without constant hidden direction, and Ayanokōji's response to her defeat points explicitly toward other people rather than toward himself: **you have comrades**.

That is succession rather than ownership—at least in the immediate scene.

But even Horikita remains outside the most important decision concerning their shared future: Ayanokōji intends to leave her class and has not told her.

V12 therefore refuses the easy redemptive answer. Ayanokōji has learned some boundaries. He can recognize when another relationship is not his to define. He can genuinely care. He can value purposeless conversation. He can want Horikita's class to survive him. He can give Amasawa a transparent enough task that she understands what he is doing and thanks him anyway.

He can also call Maezono a tool, destroy Ichinose through trust, prepare Kei's loss without disclosure, and recruit Sakayanagi's love to preserve a four-class experiment she never consented to serve.

The same person does all of these things.

The resulting Year-2 portrait is no longer adequately described by “cold strategist becoming more human.” Humanity is not a scalar that rises as manipulation falls. V12 shows something more difficult: **ordinary desire, genuine care, developmental generosity, curiosity, attachment, and domination can grow together**.

That is also why Volume 0 remains essential without becoming deterministically explanatory. The reader now knows the political genealogy of preference-sensitive environmental engineering. Ayanokōji did not invent its grammar in school. Yet ANHS has given him purposes and relationships that exceed the White Room/Atsuomi framework. The question is whether those new purposes will eventually transform the grammar itself, or merely give a more humane man better reasons to use it.

The four-class ecology is the largest-scale version of the same problem.

Ayanokōji wants all four classes to enter Year 3 as viable competitors. That goal has genuine developmental value. It resists the school's historical tendency toward early political collapse and creates a richer environment in which more students can grow. But V12 reveals the ownership claim hidden inside the benevolent objective: when independent leaders use their freedom to threaten the ecology, he reserves the right to intervene in who survives.

This is precisely where good developmental outcomes stop being sufficient ethical justification.

The question is not whether the garden flourishes.

The question is whether the gardener can accept a plant growing in a direction he did not choose.

At the V12 boundary, the answer remains mixed.

Ayanokōji has learned how to produce successors.

He has learned that defeated people can have futures outside the institution.

He has learned that ordinary experience contains knowledge optimization cannot preinstall.

He has learned that another person's relationship may be outside his jurisdiction.

But when the stakes reach the architecture he personally values, he still acts as though the final environment is his to preserve.

That is V12's decisive contradiction.

> **Ayanokōji increasingly wants people capable of choosing against him, while continuing to design conditions that make their most consequential choices serve a future he has already preferred.**

Year 2 is therefore approaching its endpoint not with the problem of whether he can author people, but with the problem of whether he can ever **relinquish authorship at the moment relinquishment actually costs him something**.

V12.5 must inherit that problem intact.
