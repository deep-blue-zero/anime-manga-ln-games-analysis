# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ 0』
## Volume 0 — Retrospective Deep Reading
### The political genealogy of authorship, the limits of manufacture, and freedom inside an inherited design

> **Retrospective boundary:** this document reads the complete original Japanese Volume 0 only at the position mandated by the governing architecture: after the V01–V08 sequential record has been frozen and before V09. Volume 0 may establish past facts and change the meaning of earlier evidence, but it may not be used to pretend that those facts were available to the reader or cast at earlier local boundaries. Every major transition is therefore recorded as `PRESERVE`, `STRENGTHEN`, `REVISE`, `DOWNGRADE`, `REJECT`, or `OPEN`. The artifact excludes Y2V09+, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptations, wikis, and external summaries.

# 1. Scope, provenance, and the pre-Volume-0 epistemic lock

`V00` is the architecture-mandated retrospective interruption between `Y2V08` and `Y2V09`. Its source chronology begins decades before Advanced Nurturing High School and reaches the year before Kiyotaka's admission, but its analytical authority begins **only now**. The Japanese EPUB was read completely in audited spine order through the afterword before targeted retrieval. It exactly matches the Phase-0 source lock: **9,460,508 bytes**, SHA-256 `ec30387a4de96870b53e88a4a2ca5d28ac27455e5ffb15936f4895852fcdb209`. The deterministic prose layer contains **4,292 records** in the namespace `COTE:V00:spineXX:paraYYYY`; the normalized locator text hashes to `6e55085bee7608a4fd4d4874c6cb6dac1a531e0d9e182ccbf19fc41bf011017e`.

The pre-Volume-0 record is not a disposable draft. V01–V08 established a sequence of local truths under the information actually available at each boundary:

> **legibility → jurisdiction → attribution → sovereignty / counter-authorship → embeddedness → responsibility → distributed agency → staged agency → relational legibility / moral friction**

At the end of V08, the project had deliberately frozen several uncertainties:

- whether Tsukishiro genuinely intended Kiyotaka's expulsion, or whether expulsion was only one layer of a more complex operation;
- whether Nanase's deeper function was hostile, protective, mixed, or independent;
- how much of the Matsuo/Eiichirō account was true;
- who made Kiyotaka's ANHS admission administratively possible;
- how Atsuomi, the White Room, Kijima, and ANHS were politically related;
- whether Kiyotaka was primarily manufactured, innately exceptional, or produced by an interaction that neither category alone could explain.

Volume 0 answers some of those questions, substantially reframes others, and leaves several deliberately open. The governing discipline is not to maximize closure. It is to preserve the distinction between:

1. **a newly established fact about the past;**
2. **a fact that an earlier reader could reasonably have inferred but could not verify;**
3. **a genuine correction of an earlier interpretation;**
4. **a sharper causal explanation that leaves the earlier local reading rational;**
5. **and a mystery the prequel itself does not answer.**

A source-text anomaly also requires explicit control. `COTE:V00:spine61:para0018` contains a missing initial cohort number and a dropout percentage internally inconsistent with the separately stated fourth-generation total of seventy-four children and later survivor counts. This artifact preserves the malformed passage as source evidence but does **not** silently reconstruct the missing number or use its exact percentage as a quantitative conclusion.

# 2. Executive thesis — Volume 0 as the genealogy of authored contingency

Volume 0 reveals that the White Room is not fundamentally an educational institution that was later corrupted by politics. It is a **political project that uses education as its medium**.

Naoe commissions the human-development plan as part of a struggle against Kijima, whose political capital includes the government-backed Advanced Nurturing High School. The original design combines infant education, concealed child procurement, elite financing, and ideological conditioning so that future high-performing adults can enter public life as obedient members of the Naoe faction. Atsuomi then operationalizes, expands, and gradually captures the project. He turns abandoned children, unacknowledged children, wealthy patrons' inconvenient offspring, disgraced researchers, criminal networks, philanthropic language, and scientific ambition into one vertically controlled developmental machine. `COTE:V00:spine12:para0214–0238`; `spine12:para0312–0351`.

The project's deepest principle appears before the White Room is named. Atsuomi defines the exceptional politician not as someone who gives a client the sushi requested, but as someone who can provide curry or gyūdon and still induce maximum satisfaction. `COTE:V00:spine12:para0253–0268`. This is a theory of **preference engineering**. The superior ruler does not merely respond to desire; he authors the field in which another person experiences the imposed outcome as acceptable or desirable.

That principle becomes the White Room's governing ontology. Children are not simply taught. Their available desires, relationships, emotional repertoires, performance thresholds, and possible futures are designed. White becomes a brand of cleanliness for an institution whose administrators knowingly use illegality, secrecy, and children as commodities. `COTE:V00:spine13:para0009–0016`; `spine17:para0028–0032`; `spine20:para0016–0022`.

Kiyotaka is born inside this political machine, but Volume 0 rejects two opposite simplifications about him.

He is **not born complete**. Suzukake states that when Kiyotaka first encounters a new academic or physical discipline, his initial performance is often ordinary or unremarkable. What distinguishes him is the speed with which he absorbs foundations, converts them into his own capacity, and then generalizes them into novel situations. `COTE:V00:spine61:para0014–0018`.

He is also **not reproducibly manufactured from an interchangeable blank child**. The White Room can intentionally produce highly capable people—Suzukake estimates something like the human upper ten percent—but the institution cannot reliably create the extreme upper fraction represented by Kiyotaka. He calls Kiyotaka a mutation, an exception, and ultimately concludes that neither innate potential nor the White Room alone would have produced this result. Both were necessary; the result remains non-repeatable. `COTE:V00:spine61:para0020–0033`.

The correct retrospective formula is therefore:

> **Kiyotaka is an unreproducible interaction between extraordinary adaptive potential and an inhuman environment that gave that potential almost unlimited material while stripping away ordinary developmental limits and relationships.**

This changes how his emotional life should be understood. Volume 0 does not support the proposition that he literally possesses no affect. As a child he likes the taste of sweets, experiences curiosity, dislikes Atsuomi, notices Yuki, feels the difference between preferences, and wants to know the outer world. What the White Room teaches very early is that crying, smiling, anger, grief, and appeals to attachment do not alter adult response. Emotion is not metaphysically absent; spontaneous expression is made **causally useless**, ordinary relational vocabulary is starved, and internal states are subordinated to performance. `COTE:V00:spine31:para0003–0053`; `spine37:para0106–0111`.

Yuki and Shirō are indispensable because they show that common environment does not produce common self. Yuki spontaneously smiles without being taught and seeks relationship. Shirō chooses deliberate dropout because he wants freedom, friends, and a real world beyond the facility. Kiyotaka chooses to remain because his curiosity still finds the White Room educationally productive. `COTE:V00:spine33:para0023–0064`; `spine35:para0001–0008`; `spine46:para0023–0047`; `spine48:para0001–0034`. The institution creates strong regularities, but it does not fully author desire.

Volume 0 also makes Kiyotaka's later authorship of other people less accidental. Atsuomi's preference-engineering theory, the White Room's manipulation of thresholds, and the child's own decision to preserve weaker peers as additional data all establish an early grammar of environmental control. When six-year-old Kiyotaka deliberately scores ninety rather than one hundred, he does not do so out of solidarity. He wants weaker children to remain in the field because their continued presence produces experience he has not yet extracted. `COTE:V00:spine37:para0055–0108`. V05's culling, V06's training-wheels metaphor, and V07's staged agency are not imported backward as completed childhood plans; they are retrospectively linked to a deeply learned way of treating human environments as analyzable and adjustable systems.

The prequel's most consequential political revelation concerns ANHS. Atsuomi and Tsukishiro have already arranged an untraceable background and discuss a future plan aimed at Kiyotaka's personality formation and at testing how far he can go. Atsuomi also wants intelligence on Kijima and ANHS. The narration then states that one year later Kiyotaka will decide to enroll. `COTE:V00:spine68:para0022–0047`.

This requires a precise conclusion:

> **Kiyotaka's opportunity to enter ANHS was anticipated and partly architected by Atsuomi and Tsukishiro. The decision to use that opportunity, the purposes Kiyotaka later forms inside the school, and the counter-authored relationships the school produces are not therefore reducible to paternal control.**

Authorship of an opening is not authorship of every future choice.

Volume 0 consequently transforms the series' freedom problem. ANHS is no longer simply an external refuge beyond Atsuomi's design. It is a politically entangled institution entered through an opportunity his enemies and father had already begun to manipulate. But this does not make freedom unreal. It makes freedom **relational and second-order**: the capacity to generate purposes, attachments, refusals, and institutions that exceed the intent of those who created the initial environment.

The compact retrospective thesis is:

> **Volume 0 is the genealogy of authored contingency. It shows political actors designing institutions, curricula, identities, and opportunities while repeatedly failing to determine what their designs will finally mean. The White Room can manufacture capacity within limits; it cannot reproduce Kiyotaka, fully suppress Yuki's smile, prevent Shirō's desire for freedom, settle Arisu's theory of genius, or guarantee that an ANHS opportunity arranged for one purpose will not generate a different life.**

# 3. Architectural position — retrospective revelation without retrospective omniscience

The volume's story chronology could tempt an analyst to move it to the beginning of the corpus. Doing so would destroy one of its central literary functions.

Before Volume 0, the reader encounters the White Room through Kiyotaka's guarded narration, Atsuomi's confrontation, Sakayanagi's childhood memory, Tsukishiro's adversarial claims, Amasawa and Yagami's behavior, Nanase's recruited grief, and the institutional effects of a hidden adult struggle. The resulting uncertainty is not an analytical failure. It is the form through which the series asks the reader to judge evidence supplied by interested actors.

Volume 0 changes source position. It gives sustained access to:

- Atsuomi's first-person political consciousness;
- the founding and financing of the White Room;
- Kiyotaka's childhood first-person narration;
- the internal observations of researchers;
- and the private arrangement among Atsuomi, Tsukishiro, and Kamogawa immediately before the ANHS period.

That increased access is authoritative about the scenes shown. It is not omniscience about every later operation.

Three limits remain.

First, Atsuomi is a highly self-interested narrator. His descriptions of poverty, ambition, political hierarchy, and his own methods are valuable primary evidence, but his moral framing is not neutral. He repeatedly converts exploitation into realism and calls domination competent leadership. The artifact therefore distinguishes what he **does**, what he **believes**, and what the analysis judges.

Second, Volume 0 ends one year before Kiyotaka's admission and omits the “blank year” between the prequel and Y1V01. The afterword explicitly says such planning material exists but was not published here. `COTE:V00:spine69:para0004`. The exact mechanics by which Kiyotaka learns of ANHS, chooses it, and passes through administrative procedures are therefore only partly shown.

Third, the volume does not narrate Tsukishiro's later chairmanship campaign, Nanase's full assignment, the Matsuo/Eiichirō chain, or every step of the second-year operation. V08's hypothesis that Tsukishiro never truly intended straightforward expulsion becomes much stronger, but the prequel does not supply a complete later mission document.

The proper architectural result is therefore not “Volume 0 explains everything.” It is:

> **Volume 0 gives the political and developmental genealogy required to revise earlier models, while preserving the distinction between founded institution, later operation, and character-level choice.**

The Year 2 sequence now acquires a retrospective insertion:

> **V01–V08: increasingly complex forms of legibility and authorship → V00: genealogy of authorship / authored contingency → V09 onward: consequences under newly available prehistory.**

# 4. Narrative architecture — eight movements from ambition to designed escape

## 4.1 Atsuomi's monologue — the have-not turns ambition into identity

The prologue defines Atsuomi through absence. He claims no wealth, education, physical exceptionality, unusual patience, or natural diligence. What he possesses is expanding ambition: the determination to rise to the country's summit. `COTE:V00:spine09:para0002–0022`.

His first electoral defeat teaches him that clean policy slogans do not create political power. Organization, faction, patronage, enemies, and willingness to perform unwanted work do. He joins the ruling Citizens Party, wins at twenty-seven, and becomes Naoe's hidden blade through bribery, intelligence work, sexual procurement, and violence. `spine09:para0023–0051`.

The narrative establishes a character who experiences politics as an environment to be reverse-engineered. Moral norms are not internal limits; they are variables in an ascent problem.

## 4.2 Naoe's commission — education becomes factional infrastructure

Naoe introduces the human-development project while discussing Kijima, the rising clean-image politician associated with Advanced Nurturing High School. Education matters because it produces public legitimacy and future power. `spine12:para0208–0238`.

The plan is radical but politically intelligible: begin at infancy, use children whose existence can be concealed, finance the system through elite patrons, condition successful adults toward Naoe's faction, and place them in the political field. `spine12:para0312–0351`.

The White Room is therefore born as a rival answer to `高育`: not simply “better school,” but a hidden pipeline for politically usable human capital.

## 4.3 Atsuomi's operational capture — brand, money, people, and crime

Naoe gives the commission but refuses direct financial support. Atsuomi must build the institution through networks. He recruits Narumori Sakayanagi's advice, Mika's social intelligence, Tsukishiro's covert services, criminal brokers, compromised doctors, and rival researchers. `spine12:para0383–0389`; `spine14:para0051–0073`; `spine18:para0032–0068`; `spine19:para0003–0087`.

He packages child exploitation as rescue, calls the institution White Room to project purity, and turns secrecy into prestige. The institution is not only a laboratory; it is a brand, patronage market, disposal service, and political asset.

## 4.4 Transactional parenthood and the fourth generation — the founder supplies his own child

Atsuomi's relationship with Mika is an extension of political logistics. She agrees to pregnancy and birth as paid work. He pays fifty million yen and treats his biological child as the strongest fundraising signal that he trusts the project enough to risk his own blood. `spine26:para0044–0062`; `spine27:para0003–0020`; `spine11:para0013–0028`.

He then chooses the impossible `βカリキュラム` for the fourth generation. Researchers warn that it exceeds expected human limits. Atsuomi replies that even universal failure, including his son's death, is acceptable if it reveals the limit. `spine30:para0031–0059`.

The fourth generation begins with seventy-four children. Kiyotaka is not protected by parenthood. He is intensified by it.

## 4.5 The child's first-person world — affect becomes useless, not nonexistent

The narration shifts from institutional founders to the child raised inside the design. Kiyotaka's first remembered object is the white ceiling. He cries from instinctive human need and learns that no one comes. Adults feed, test, treat, and monitor him without worry, delight, or comforting response. `spine31:para0003–0024`.

The chapter then makes a crucial correction to the “emotionless child” image. He wants the rare gummy, likes its taste, uses other children's responses as data, notices the test's hidden structure, and considers unannounced alternatives. `spine31:para0025–0080`. Affect, appetite, curiosity, and strategic inference coexist. What disappears is the expectation that feeling can summon care.

## 4.6 Yuki, Shirō, and Arisu — the design encounters counter-authored meaning

Yuki produces a smile no one taught her. Shirō desires freedom and chooses dropout. Arisu, observing from outside, turns Kiyotaka into a living test of heredity and education. `spine33:para0023–0064`; `spine35:para0001–0008`; `spine46:para0023–0047`; `spine48:para0001–0034`; `spine40:para0049–0078`; `spine42:para0001–0032`.

These are not side anecdotes. They are three failures of total authorship:

- spontaneous affect appears inside affect-suppressing design;
- desire for freedom appears inside total institutional enclosure;
- and an external child converts the White Room's own showcase into an argument against its philosophy.

## 4.7 Political rupture and scientific limit — the machine outlives its sponsor

Naoe's faction loses strategic position to Kijima. Naoe orders the project shelved and Atsuomi marginalized because Atsuomi's accumulated money, criminal ties, and independent influence have become dangerous. `spine52:para0067–0173`.

Atsuomi refuses to surrender. He preserves the White Room through elite networks and criminal capacity, survives political exclusion, and waits for Naoe's decline. The founder's agent becomes an autonomous political center. `spine62:para0002–0045`.

Meanwhile the researchers reach a limit. Later generations can be made highly capable, but Kiyotaka is non-reproducible. The White Room succeeds as an elite-production institution and fails as a reliable Kiyotaka factory. `spine61:para0009–0042`.

## 4.8 Epilogue — the escape opportunity is itself designed

At fourteen, Kiyotaka enters the outside world during the White Room's temporary suspension. He meets the traumatized Yuki, refuses connection, observes the world, and conceals his developing intentions from Atsuomi. `spine63:para0002–0083`; `spine65:para0001–0022`; `spine67:para0001–0006`.

Atsuomi then debates whether to use him as a White Room educator or political instrument. Emotional and social deficiency becomes the problem to solve through a changed environment. Tsukishiro confirms that an untraceable history has been arranged and accepts a future plan. One year later Kiyotaka decides to enter ANHS. `spine68:para0003–0047`.

The prequel thus ends not with pure escape but with **designed possibility**. The institution Kiyotaka uses for freedom is already part of an adult political field. The outcome remains contingent because the designers cannot pre-author what school relationships will become.

# 5. Atsuomi Ayanokōji — preference engineering as political and paternal philosophy

Atsuomi is not simply an ambitious villain with no ideas. Volume 0 gives him a coherent, deeply anti-liberal theory of competent power.

His critique of the political system contains recognizable truths. Hereditary politicians possess advantages unavailable to outsiders. Faction, sponsorship, reputation, and organizational power can matter more than policy merit. Aging elites protect position. Clean slogans without institutions accomplish little. `COTE:V00:spine09:para0023–0047`.

The problem is what he concludes from those truths.

For Atsuomi, because existing politics is already structured by hidden power, the rational answer is not to build more answerable institutions. It is to become better at hidden power than everyone else. Bribery, sexual procurement, surveillance, criminal violence, narrative control, patron manipulation, and child experimentation become acceptable technologies of ascent.

The sushi/curry analogy expresses the governing principle. An ordinary good politician satisfies an articulated desire. The exceptional politician changes the available outcome and manages perception so effectively that the governed accept it. `spine12:para0253–0268`.

This is **environmental authorship before pedagogy**. The leader does not ask what people choose under conditions they can contest. He decides the destination and engineers satisfaction around it.

The same structure governs Atsuomi's relationship to nearly everyone:

- Naoe is a staircase to be surpassed;
- Kamogawa is a loyal subordinate to be cultivated and retained;
- Mika is a socially intelligent operative and reproductive partner hired for a political purpose;
- wealthy parents are sources of money, children, and influence;
- researchers are capacity containers whose disgrace makes them governable;
- Kiyotaka is a fundraising symbol, experimental subject, future educator, possible political weapon, and potential successor;
- Tsukishiro is a useful but untrustworthy broker whose transactional nature must be managed.

Atsuomi's treatment of Kiyotaka is therefore not best described as ordinary parental cruelty. It is the refusal of parenthood as a special moral relation. Biological connection matters only because it creates credibility, future power, and strategic options. He holds the infant for the camera because paternal sacrifice sells. He selects the harshest curriculum partly because using his own son shields him from accusations of protecting elites. He later worries that making Kiyotaka a politician will appear like second-generation nepotism—not because that would wrong the child, but because it would damage public interpretation. `spine11:para0013–0028`; `spine30:para0040–0059`; `spine68:para0018–0027`.

There is nevertheless a structural resemblance between father and son that should not be exaggerated into identity.

Both:

- read social environments as systems of incentives;
- conceal intentions from stronger observers;
- treat other people's predictable responses as usable causal material;
- prefer indirect control when direct command is inefficient;
- distinguish public narrative from private objective;
- and are capable of valuing competence independently of conventional status.

But Volume 0 also supplies differences.

Atsuomi's ambition is terminal: domination and national supremacy are ends in themselves. Kiyotaka's childhood curiosity is epistemic. He remains in the White Room because there is still something to learn. Later at ANHS, ordinary life, friendship, romance, rival recognition, and the possibility of an answer he did not predetermine become ends that complicate pure control. The son's grammar is inherited and institutionalized; his purposes are not simply the father's purposes.

Atsuomi's political ideal therefore needs a `REVISE`, not a total dismissal. He does possess a critique of stagnant hereditary politics and a desire to produce capable leadership. Those beliefs do not exonerate him. They make the White Room more dangerous because its coercion is organized around a real diagnosis and a plausible public good rather than arbitrary sadism.

# 6. The White Room's political genealogy — rival state projects, not isolated laboratories

## 6.1 Naoe, Kijima, and `高育`

The founding scene places the White Room beside Advanced Nurturing High School from the beginning. Kijima's association with `高育` strengthens his public image because child education is inseparable from national development. Naoe admires the political effectiveness of the idea while treating Kijima as an enemy. `COTE:V00:spine12:para0208–0238`.

The human-development plan is therefore a rival political technology. It promises a more radical pipeline: begin at birth, eliminate ordinary family and school variation, produce elite adults, and bind them to the faction that created them.

This requires revision of two common models.

First, ANHS and the White Room are not historically unrelated opposites. They emerge inside the same national political contest over how education can produce legitimacy, capability, and future leadership.

Second, their shared genealogy does not make them institutionally identical.

The White Room is secret, illegally sourced, non-consensual, vertically governed, family-severing, and prepared to destroy children who cannot meet escalating thresholds. ANHS is coercive and competitive in serious ways, but it permits plural social worlds, legal personhood, changing affiliations, public records, peer counter-authorship, ordinary adolescence, and meaningful institutional exit through graduation or even expulsion. V05 showed structural kinship through culling; V08 showed meaningful difference through relational contact and embodied failure. Volume 0 sharpens both sides.

## 6.2 Naoe as initiator, Atsuomi as captor

Naoe is the project's political originator. Atsuomi is not merely a manager following a finished blueprint.

The original proposal is close to blank outside infant education. Atsuomi creates financing, branding, staffing, procurement, research competition, patron relations, secrecy, criminal enforcement, and a developmental ideology. He turns the commission into an institution that can outlive Naoe's sponsorship. `spine13:para0003–0017`; `spine17:para0013–0048`; `spine19:para0064–0087`; `spine22:para0020–0047`.

When Naoe later orders the project shelved, Atsuomi discovers the same lesson Kiyotaka will later apply to class and relationship structures: a structure survives its original author only if resources, loyalties, and capacities have been distributed beyond the author's direct control.

Atsuomi has already built such a structure. The White Room's investors, researchers, criminal contacts, and prestige network give him enough independent capital to resist political death. `spine52:para0141–0173`; `spine62:para0007–0045`.

The result is not democratic succession. It is one hidden sovereign escaping another.

## 6.3 Tsukishiro as broker rather than simple subordinate

Tsukishiro enters as Naoe's “anything-man,” capable of security, intelligence, cross-party access, and likely lethal action. He monitors the project and assists its chosen political operator. `spine12:para0401–0424`; `spine19:para0012–0068`.

His relation to Atsuomi is transactional from the start. He investigates Atsuomi, tests him, prices assistance, and retains broad networks. Atsuomi recognizes that Tsukishiro rides likely winners and cannot be trusted, but continues using him because his capabilities are difficult to replace. `spine19:para0071–0138`; `spine68:para0032–0045`.

This prehistory revises any model in which Tsukishiro is merely a remote-controlled extension of Atsuomi. Their interests overlap, but each treats the other as a dangerous instrument.

# 7. Political economy of the White Room — rescue rhetoric, child commodities, and elite patronage

## 7.1 Philanthropic language functions as camouflage

Atsuomi understands that the project cannot be publicly described as using children. It must be framed as saving abandoned infants and providing education unavailable to them. `COTE:V00:spine13:para0009–0016`.

The rhetoric is not wholly fictional. Some children would otherwise face abandonment, institutional neglect, or death. The White Room does provide food, medicine, instruction, and future capacity.

But rescue language conceals the transfer of jurisdiction. Children are removed from ordinary social protection and placed inside a system whose administrators treat them as experimental material, political capital, and future factional assets.

The moral danger lies partly in the mixture of benefit and domination. A project that did nothing valuable would be easier to reject. The White Room can plausibly claim rescue because it genuinely converts some desperate conditions into survival and elite ability—while denying the rescued person authorship over the terms and purpose of that rescue.

## 7.2 Child acquisition makes the commodity logic explicit

The source routes children through several channels:

- infants abandoned immediately after birth;
- mothers in poverty or concealment;
- unrecognized children of wealthy or public figures;
- children supplied through bought institutional connections;
- children acquired through criminal brokers;
- and elite parents using the institution as an experiment, disposal service, or prestige investment.

`COTE:V00:spine12:para0328–0350`; `spine20:para0010–0027`; `spine30:para0055–0059`; `spine55:para0041–0091`.

Atsuomi himself calls the children products or samples in his governing logic. Their bodies carry different kinds of value:

- biological material for research;
- proof for investors;
- hidden liabilities removed from elite households;
- possible future officials;
- and evidence that the founder's method works.

The White Room therefore joins scientific, political, reproductive, and financial markets in one institution.

## 7.3 Elite funding changes what the institution is for

The project initially needs money simply to exist. Atsuomi then turns secrecy and scarcity into status. He circulates claims of exceptional results before full proof, increases demand, and allows patrons to imagine first-mover advantage if government recognition later arrives. `COTE:V00:spine22:para0020–0038`.

The money does more than fund a fixed curriculum. Patron interests alter cohort composition and institutional uses. Hidden children can disappear socially. Siblings can be split as comparative experiments. Wealthy parents can outsource unwanted offspring. Children with names and powerful families receive different post-dropout handling from nameless children.

This is not a pure meritocracy. The institution attempts to standardize education after intake, but the political meaning of a child's body remains stratified by origin.

## 7.4 `ホワイトルーム` is deliberate ethical laundering

Atsuomi chooses the name because white conveys cleanliness and incorruptibility. `COTE:V00:spine17:para0028–0032`.

The irony is architectural rather than incidental. White is the first color Kiyotaka remembers. The visual blankness presents itself as absence of contamination, but the institution is saturated with political purpose, money, secrecy, violence, and adult desire.

White does not mean neutral.

It means:

> **the institution has successfully hidden the hands that authored the room.**

# 8. Research governance and the curriculum — competition, fear, revision, and limit

## 8.1 The White Room is itself an experiment in institutional selection

The first cohort is divided randomly among researchers with incompatible theories. Their groups compete under surveillance. `COTE:V00:spine21:para0003–0025`.

This design is scientifically and politically revealing. Atsuomi does not need consensus about child development. He needs a contest whose results allow him to select a method.

Suzukake's model wins because punishment, fear, and affect suppression produce stronger short-term capacity. Atsuomi elevates him because outcome governs legitimacy. The institution selects not the most humane pedagogy but the one that produces the most politically saleable result.

## 8.2 The fourth generation is a limit test, not a normal cohort

The fourth generation receives the `βカリキュラム`, a level even its designer regards as beyond expected human sustainability. `COTE:V00:spine30:para0031–0046`.

Atsuomi's justification is acceleration. Gradual evidence takes too long. A cohort may be destroyed if destruction reveals the limit. He chooses the cohort containing his own child so that investors cannot accuse him of reserving safety for himself.

This is an equality of exposure without equality of dignity. All children are equally disposable before the experiment.

The decision also clarifies Kiyotaka's later relation to thresholds. He is not merely trained to pass hard tests. He grows inside a system where the threshold itself moves in response to his performance and where stronger output can make the environment more destructive for everyone else.

His deliberate ninety-point score is an early attempt to reverse that relation: he manipulates the displayed output in order to influence the curriculum's next move. `spine37:para0055–0108`.

## 8.3 The institution learns—but learning is not moral reform

Later generations add communication after staff observe that children seek it spontaneously. Suzukake admits that affect and interaction reduce pure efficiency but improve the overall viability and social usability of the graduates. `COTE:V00:spine61:para0009–0012`.

This is real institutional adaptation. It should not be misread as repentance.

The governing purpose remains capability production. Communication is introduced because a totally affect-suppressed product has limitations, not because children acquire an acknowledged right to relationship.

The distinction matters beyond the White Room. An institution can become less brutal because brutality is counterproductive while remaining fundamentally unanswerable to those it governs.

## 8.4 Success and failure are both partial

The White Room works.

Volume 0 does not support a comforting claim that coercive education inevitably fails. It can intentionally produce unusually capable adults. Suzukake estimates that upper-ten-percent performance is achievable. `spine61:para0020–0030`.

It also fails relative to its grandest claim. Kiyotaka cannot be reproduced. Curriculum difficulty beyond a certain level destroys cohorts. Educator capacity and child potential both impose ceilings. The institution can expand many people's abilities without erasing individual variation.

This makes the ethical problem harder, not easier.

A useless abusive institution would offer no tragic tradeoff. The White Room's horror lies partly in the fact that it produces real capacity by treating people as developmental property.

# 9. Nature, nurture, selection, and the non-reproducibility of Kiyotaka

The series' heredity/environment question is often reduced to a fan debate: “Was Kiyotaka born a genius, or did the White Room make him?” Volume 0 refuses the binary.

## 9.1 He is not initially perfect

Suzukake's retrospective record says Kiyotaka's first encounter with a subject often produces ordinary or unremarkable results. That applies to academic study and combat disciplines. `COTE:V00:spine61:para0014–0016`.

Shirō's account confirms the pattern in martial arts. Shirō can win the first encounter or two; once Kiyotaka has learned, the relation reverses permanently. `spine46:para0018–0021`.

The distinctive capacity is therefore not static prior mastery. It is an extraordinary learning function:

- high-fidelity observation;
- rapid absorption;
- memory;
- transfer across domains;
- adaptive generalization;
- and refusal to settle at an inherited ceiling.

## 9.2 He is not an interchangeable product

The researchers cannot explain why the same institution produces Kiyotaka and not another equivalent child. Atsuomi wants stable supply; the data do not provide it. `COTE:V00:spine51:para0041–0044`; `spine61:para0014–0033`.

Kiyotaka's parents complicate rather than solve heredity. Atsuomi selects Mika partly because her background appears ordinary enough to support the political claim that an unexceptional child can be polished into greatness. The source does not establish a genetic study capable of ruling out exceptional inherited potential, mutation, recombination, or unknown ancestry.

The responsible conclusion is narrower:

> **Volume 0 makes simple parental-status inference unreliable. It does not scientifically prove that heredity is absent.**

## 9.3 Selection and education are intertwined

The White Room does not merely educate. It repeatedly removes those who fail. The final “product” is therefore shaped by:

- intake variation;
- curriculum;
- medical survival;
- motivation;
- threshold policy;
- and continuous selection.

Calling Kiyotaka “the White Room's masterpiece” can obscure this. He is the only fourth-generation child who survives an impossible environment; survival itself selects for capacities the institution did not create from nothing.

At the same time, the environment supplies breadth and intensity no ordinary childhood could offer. Suzukake explicitly says that without the White Room Kiyotaka would probably have remained merely very capable. `spine61:para0033`.

The strongest formula is interactive:

> **potential × environment × selection × self-directed adaptation.**

No single factor owns the result.

## 9.4 The malformed dropout sentence must remain an anomaly

`COTE:V00:spine61:para0018` is internally damaged. It omits the original fourth-generation count after `当初` and gives a percentage inconsistent with the stated seventy-four-child starting cohort and later survivor counts. The artifact therefore uses the separately clear facts—seventy-four initial children and five remaining around the eighth year—without repairing the percentage from memory or external editions.

This is a small but important provenance rule: even primary sources can contain textual defects, and analytical confidence should follow the quality of the specific passage rather than the prestige of the source category.

# 10. Kiyotaka's childhood psychology — memory, affect, curiosity, and strategic concealment

## 10.1 First memory is a lesson in causal futility

Kiyotaka remembers the white ceiling before his own fingers. He cries, seeks human presence, and learns that no one responds. `COTE:V00:spine31:para0003–0019`.

The formative lesson is not “emotion does not exist.” It is:

> **emotion does not alter the environment.**

The facility supplies nutrition and medical treatment while withholding the ordinary reciprocal signals through which an infant learns that another person can be moved by need. Care becomes maintenance without recognition.

This helps explain the adult Kiyotaka's asymmetry between accurate cognitive understanding and thin spontaneous moral response. He can model fear, attachment, dependence, shame, and grief with exceptional precision. His earliest world trained him to regard such states as information rather than claims upon another person.

## 10.2 Desire and preference survive

The gummy scene establishes ordinary appetite. Kiyotaka wants the sweet and remembers liking it. `spine31:para0028–0053`.

Yuki's question about carrots forces him to consider preference as something other than nutrition. He does not have a ready answer, but the category begins to exist. `spine33:para0023–0046`.

He also develops dislike toward Atsuomi after their dispute about the ninety-point test. `spine37:para0106–0111`.

These are not proof of rich ordinary emotional life. They are enough to reject literal affective nullity.

## 10.3 Extraordinary memory does not equal complete autobiographical life

Kiyotaka retains early sensory and procedural detail with extraordinary fidelity. He knows Atsuomi is his father because he remembers adult conversation from age four. `spine51:para0010–0016`.

He can also intentionally discard information judged unnecessary, although some traces remain recoverable, as the Yuki reunion shows. `spine63:para0023–0027`.

This produces a paradox:

- he possesses more searchable factual memory than ordinary people;
- but his autobiographical world contains fewer socially meaningful categories.

Memory is dense; life narrative is thin.

## 10.4 Curiosity predates ANHS

Kiyotaka remains in the White Room partly because he wants to know how far he can develop. He studies the gummy test's hidden structure, seeks information from weaker peers, and rejects Shirō's escape because the facility still contains unexhausted knowledge. `spine31:para0055–0080`; `spine37:para0085–0096`; `spine48:para0012–0018`.

ANHS does not create curiosity from nothing. It changes the **object** of curiosity. The White Room supplies endless formal difficulty; the outside world supplies contingency, ordinary experience, reciprocal persons, and outcomes that cannot be reduced to curriculum.

## 10.5 Strategic concealment is pre-school behavior

At fourteen, Kiyotaka understands that Atsuomi interprets his quiet observation of the outside world as emotional emptiness. He deliberately permits that misunderstanding because appearing controlled is strategically useful while he sharpens his fangs. `COTE:V00:spine65:para0015–0022`; `spine67:para0001–0004`.

This corrects any claim that the ordinary-student persona and hidden-capacity strategy originate at ANHS. School provides a new interface and new purposes for concealment; the capacity and policy already exist.

## 10.6 Simulated affect and genuine affect are not mutually exclusive

Researchers observe that Kiyotaka can use lies and perform emotional forms when required. `spine51:para0036–0038`.

That fact should not be used to classify every later affection as simulation. Volume 0 itself shows appetite, curiosity, dislike, attention, and differing degrees of concern. The more accurate model is:

> **Kiyotaka can simulate social expression with unusual control while also possessing genuine internal states whose spontaneous expression and relational development were severely impoverished.**

The ANHS question is therefore not whether “real emotion” begins from zero. It is whether real states can become reciprocal commitments strong enough to constrain optimization.


# 11. Yuki and Shirō — common curriculum, divergent desire, and the failure of total environmental authorship

Yuki and Shirō are not side examples attached to Kiyotaka’s childhood. They are the volume’s strongest internal counterevidence to the White Room’s claim that sufficiently total environmental control can determine the person.

All three children share the fourth-generation field. They receive the same severe curriculum, the same surveillance, the same threat of irreversible exclusion, and nearly the same absence of family, ordinary friendship, and outside reference. Their capacities and survival lengths differ, but the more important divergence concerns what they come to **want**.

## 11.1 Yuki creates preference inside a world organized around necessity

At five, the children know one another’s measured abilities but do not think of one another as companions. Kiyotaka describes them as scenery: neither help nor obstacle, therefore socially unnecessary. Food is nutritional input, not preference. Conversation has no assigned function. `COTE:V00:spine33:para0012–0022`.

Yuki interrupts that world by saying she does not like carrots and asking Kiyotaka whether he does. The question forces him to move from biochemical description to first-person preference. He initially retrieves the nutrients and functions of β-carotene; after observing Yuki and Shirō, he finally asks what *he* likes and concludes that he does not dislike carrots. `COTE:V00:spine33:para0023–0046`.

The scene is small, but its analytical weight is large. Yuki does not teach Kiyotaka a fact. She introduces a category of self-relation:

> **What does this object mean to me, apart from what it objectively does?**

Preference is therefore not absent because the curriculum has prohibited the word. It is underdeveloped because the environment has removed most situations in which subjective difference would matter.

## 11.2 Her smile demonstrates spontaneous human surplus

When Kiyotaka catches Yuki after she stumbles, she produces an expression neither child has been taught to perform. Kiyotaka anatomizes the muscles involved and then identifies the expression from stored knowledge as a smile. Yuki herself is surprised by it. `COTE:V00:spine33:para0047–0064`; `spine35:para0001–0008`.

The narration calls the smile something humans may possess from birth or even before birth. That is not a scientifically sufficient proof of a universal innate essence. It is, however, clear fictional evidence against the institution’s strongest deterministic fantasy. The White Room can deprive children of modeled warmth and still fail to prevent unassigned expression from emerging.

Yuki’s smile is thus not merely “emotion.” It is **surplus over curriculum**: a human act not commissioned by the institution and not reducible to the performance metric currently governing the room.

## 11.3 Her expulsion exposes the difference between measurement and justice

Yuki later misses a written threshold by five points after acute illness associated with unusually early menstruation, fever, and stress. The White Room initially treats the result as final proof that her ceiling has been reached. Kiyotaka first accepts that logic and refuses her plea for help. He tells her that present failure demonstrates the failure to prepare earlier. `COTE:V00:spine43:para0021–0074`.

He then notices evidence that the test result does not cleanly measure the capacity the institution claims to measure. He violates the rule against unauthorized speech, withstands physical punishment, and forces the adults to recognize an unanticipated medical condition. `COTE:V00:spine45:para0031–0056`.

This should neither be romanticized into ordinary childhood compassion nor dismissed as pure calculation. The text supports at least three simultaneous propositions:

1. Kiyotaka is genuinely attentive to Yuki in a way he is not attentive to anonymous scenery;
2. he is offended by a causal and evidentiary error—the institution is treating a contaminated observation as a valid measurement;
3. his intervention remains framed through correct classification and institutional procedure rather than a general claim that Yuki deserves care independent of performance.

The scene is an early version of the series’ truth/proof problem. A score can be real and still be an invalid basis for the conclusion drawn from it.

Yuki never returns. The institution therefore preserves its practical judgment even after its immediate rationale is challenged. The correction of one measurement does not overcome a sovereignty that controls the next opportunity.

## 11.4 Shirō authors exit rather than victory

After Yuki disappears, only Kiyotaka and Shirō remain. Shirō is one of the few children who can initially defeat Kiyotaka in new combat disciplines. Kiyotaka repeatedly overtakes him after one or two exposures. `COTE:V00:spine46:para0013–0022`.

Yet Shirō’s decisive act is not an attempt to win the internal ranking. He decides to make himself fail so he can leave. He wants freedom, friends, and a nonvirtual world. He has studied the institution’s treatment of dropouts closely enough to conclude that deliberate exit will not simply mean execution. `COTE:V00:spine46:para0023–0035`.

This is environmental intelligence used against the environment’s preferred objective.

The White Room has trained Shirō to observe systems, infer consequences, endure difficulty, and act strategically. He uses those capacities to reject the institution’s definition of success.

Kiyotaka, at this boundary, gives a different answer. He does not remain because he loves captivity or because Atsuomi has persuaded him of a political mission. He remains because the facility still contains difficult knowledge and he wants to discover his own ceiling. `COTE:V00:spine46:para0036–0047`; `spine48:para0012–0018`.

This is self-authorship, but not yet freedom in a rich relational sense. The objective has become internally endorsed while the available value vocabulary remains institutionally narrow.

## 11.5 Shared formation does not produce shared allegiance

Shirō invites Kiyotaka to leave with him. Kiyotaka refuses, defeats him one final time, and accepts that Shirō has his own answer. `COTE:V00:spine48:para0001–0034`.

The contrast establishes a principle that later White Room identity analysis must preserve:

> **Shared origin establishes neither identical desire nor identical allegiance.**

That principle retrospectively strengthens the separation already maintained among Amasawa, Yagami, Tsukishiro-linked actors, Atsuomi-aligned actors, and independently exceptional students. The White Room is a powerful formative environment. It is not a machine that outputs one political will.

## 11.6 Yuki’s later reunion reveals the cost of unilateral protective judgment

At fourteen, Kiyotaka encounters Yuki outside the facility. She remains psychologically damaged and reaches toward him as the one person around whom attachment had formed. He notices that the encounter carries a reality absent from simulated knowledge, yet refuses prolonged contact and concludes that his continued presence would worsen her condition. `COTE:V00:spine63:para0003–0047`; `spine63:para0048–0083`; `spine65:para0001–0009`.

His decision may be partly correct. The text does not permit the analyst to know that renewed contact would heal her. But the ethical structure is already familiar from later Year 2:

- he possesses more information and greater composure;
- he predicts the other person’s developmental response;
- he selects the environment he judges best;
- he does not make the protected person a participant in that judgment.

The reunion is therefore a prototype of **protective jurisdiction without answerability**.

Yuki and Shirō together expose both the failure and the partial success of the White Room. The institution produces extraordinary capacities and profound damage. It does not fully control which desires survive, which values emerge, or which exits its own training makes possible.

# 12. Mika, Narumori, Arisu, and the unequal distribution of protected childhood

Volume 0 places several children and parents near the White Room while distributing recognition, protection, and future ownership radically unequally.

## 12.1 Mika — biological parenthood as contract and proof

Mika is not a hidden source of lost maternal care. She is a former hostess and information asset whose pregnancy is negotiated as paid work. Atsuomi selects her partly because her ordinary educational and social background strengthens the public claim that environment, not inherited elite status, produced the result. `COTE:V00:spine26:para0027–0063`.

She treats gestation and birth as contracted labor and leaves without a maternal transformation. She chooses the name `清隆` after a favored host; Atsuomi accepts because the name’s private emotional origin has no importance to his project. `COTE:V00:spine27:para0003–0047`; `spine29:para0029–0043`.

Tsukishiro later raises the possibility of removing her. Atsuomi keeps her alive because a verifiable biological mother protects the experiment’s provenance and future credibility. `COTE:V00:spine29:para0048–0061`.

Family is therefore converted into evidentiary infrastructure:

- the mother’s ordinary background supports the environmental thesis;
- the father’s public sacrifice supports fundraising and legitimacy;
- the child’s performance supports the institution;
- and biological records protect the story told about the result.

Kiyotaka is recognized as a son where sonship is politically useful and denied the protection ordinarily associated with the relation.

## 12.2 Narumori — moral resistance without sufficient jurisdiction

Narumori Sakayanagi supports the stated goals of rescue and education and becomes a finance bridge. Atsuomi deliberately withholds the project’s political and coercive depth. Narumori later objects to emotion-erasing cruelty and regards normalization of the institution as the beginning of unhappiness. `COTE:V00:spine25:para0010–0037`; `spine42:para0022–0029`.

His presence proves that adult counterweights existed. His limited effect proves that moral objection without access to the full record, control over funding, or authority over curriculum is insufficient. He can protect his own daughter and disapprove of the institution; he cannot make the children inside it rights-bearing participants.

## 12.3 Arisu identifies the experiment’s attribution problem before meeting Kiyotaka

Young Arisu is fascinated by the project of artificially creating genius, but immediately asks whether a successful child would prove educational causation. A survivor might simply have possessed exceptional genes from the beginning. `COTE:V00:spine40:para0063–0078`; `spine42:para0001–0012`.

Her question anticipates the method governing this entire second pass:

> **Did the person change because of the environment, or did the environment reveal and select a prior capacity?**

Volume 0 never gives Arisu a clean victory. Kiyotaka’s ordinary first performance in new domains and extraordinary subsequent adaptation demonstrate genuine environmental effect. His unusual memory and nonreproducibility demonstrate that prior individual capacity matters. Arisu’s hereditarian thesis is therefore too strong—but the causal problem she identifies is real.

## 12.4 Kiyotaka becomes evidence, rival, and philosophical challenge

Arisu notices Kiyotaka completing the tasks with unusual calm and asks whether his lineage explains him. Narumori notes that neither parent supplies an obvious elite-performance pedigree. Arisu then treats him as an especially valuable test case for the experiment. `COTE:V00:spine42:para0014–0020`.

Her later chess interest begins here. She announces that she will prove genius is determined at birth rather than by education and immediately says she wants to learn chess. `COTE:V00:spine42:para0029–0032`.

This corrects any account in which her rivalry begins merely because ANHS places two exceptional students in proximity. The school supplies the arena; the governing question predates enrollment.

The rivalry is simultaneously:

- personal fascination;
- a contest over comparative ability;
- and an attempt to settle what Kiyotaka **is evidence of**.

## 12.5 Narumori supplies the protected-child counterfactual

The illustration places Arisu on her father’s lap while Kiyotaka is observed through glass. The contrast is not reducible to affection versus cruelty, because Narumori is also a political and educational elite who participates in institutional life. But he grants Arisu forms of personhood the White Room denies its children:

- family recognition;
- physical protection;
- conversational reciprocity;
- the right to form her own theory;
- and a future not defined solely as research output.

Narumori calls Kiyotaka pitiable because his first world was the facility’s white ceiling rather than a parent. He recognizes that surviving and failing children alike are being used as samples for a project whose beneficiaries may live decades later. `COTE:V00:spine42:para0020–0026`.

When Arisu asks whether he dislikes the facility, he says he cannot sincerely support a future in which this form of upbringing becomes normal. `COTE:V00:spine42:para0023–0028`.

That response matters because it distinguishes two kinds of educational ambition:

1. creating conditions in which a child can develop capacities while remaining a person with claims against the institution;
2. treating the child as an input whose value is exhausted by what the project learns.

## 12.6 The scene complicates the ANHS/White Room opposition without erasing it

Narumori’s father is associated with ANHS, Narumori has relationships across factional lines, and Atsuomi uses the Sakayanagi connection to finance and legitimate the hidden project. The institutions therefore share political genealogy.

Yet Volume 0 also gives Narumori a genuine moral boundary. He will not place Arisu inside the White Room simply because its curriculum could increase capability. He fears the social normalization of the institution even if it produces superior performers.

This does not prove ANHS morally pure. Later school evidence demonstrates coercion, disposability, hidden administration, and severe rank pressure. It does establish that related political origins do not eliminate meaningful institutional differences.

## 12.7 Arisu is both counterevidence and product of protection

Arisu’s intelligence develops outside the White Room under elite family support, physical limitation, education, and social recognition. She is therefore direct counterevidence to any White Room monopoly on excellence.

But she is not a neutral control condition. She possesses unusual lineage, resources, family attention, and opportunities. Her existence cannot prove that environment is irrelevant any more than Kiyotaka can prove heredity is irrelevant.

The paired image and dialogue are strongest when read as a deliberately unresolved comparison:

> **Two extraordinary children are formed under radically different relations of care, constraint, and institutional ownership. Their later rivalry tests not only whose ability is greater, but what kinds of human development those environments make possible.**

# 13. ANHS admission provenance — authored opportunity and self-authored decision

Y2V08 correctly froze the provenance of Ayanokōji’s admission as unresolved. Volume 0 now supplies strong but incomplete retrospective evidence.

## 13.1 What Volume 0 directly establishes

By the final car scene:

- Atsuomi is deciding whether Kiyotaka should become a White Room educator or political actor;
- he regards Kiyotaka’s affective/personality deficits as a problem requiring intervention;
- Tsukishiro has completed arrangements for a background whose verification will not expose the truth;
- Atsuomi explains another future plan concerning Kiyotaka;
- Tsukishiro accepts and anticipates that additional pieces will be required;
- Atsuomi wants to identify Kijima/ANHS weaknesses as a second objective;
- one year later, Kiyotaka decides to enroll in ANHS.

`COTE:V00:spine68:para0005–0047`.

## 13.2 What it does not establish

The source does not show the missing year. It does not specify:

- who first presented ANHS as an available destination;
- whether Kiyotaka knew Atsuomi had prepared the route;
- whether the Matsuo story belongs to the plan, opposes it, or sits partly outside it;
- whether Tsukishiro’s later pressure was meant to retrieve, test, harden, observe, or genuinely expel him;
- which institutional actors inside ANHS knew any part of the arrangement.

The afterword itself notes that an unpublished “blank year” exists in planning material, covering the interval between Volume 0 and Y1V01. That paratext explains why Volume 0 deliberately stops before the causal chain becomes complete. `COTE:V00:spine69:para0004`.

## 13.3 The correct retrospective formulation

The admission should now be understood as **multi-authored**:

> Atsuomi and Tsukishiro prepared an external field involving Kiyotaka and ANHS; Kiyotaka later chose to enter that field; the exact relation between the offered route and his own knowledge remains unshown.

This reframes `Y1H-007`. “Self-chosen purpose or controlled curriculum?” is too binary. A person can choose a purpose inside an opportunity another actor created for reasons the chooser does not know.

The ethical question becomes:

> **How much self-authorship is possible when one chooses among doors whose existence, location, and hidden surveillance were arranged by someone else?**

# 14. Ishigami, Kanzaki, and Amasawa — political prehistory without identity collapse

## 14.1 Ishigami Kyō — external political formation, not White Room proof

Young Ishigami intervenes in a conflict involving his friend Fuji. Rather than simply fight, he manipulates the social visibility of the encounter: he places himself where adults will see apparent victimization, makes the aggressors’ position untenable, and protects the person he considers important. Atsuomi recognizes both intelligence and political instinct. `COTE:V00:spine56:para0085–0203`.

This strongly confirms the V08 picture of Ishigami as an Atsuomi-linked political protégé. It also explains why his methods can resemble Ayanokōji’s staged agency without requiring White Room origin. He learns inside elite family, school, and political environments rather than the sealed project.

Volume 0 therefore **downgrades** any temptation to infer White Room provenance merely from exceptional competence or Atsuomi connection. It does not logically prove that Ishigami could never have later entered some related program; it simply supplies a sufficient and textually grounded alternative provenance.

## 14.2 Kanzaki Ryūji — the childhood problem of unused ability

Young Kanzaki observes the same conflict and remains outside it. His reasoning is not cowardice in the ordinary sense. He judges the event unrelated to him and sees no reason to incur risk. Atsuomi later tells him that lacking power can justify retreat, but possessing power and refusing to use it is foolish. `spine57:para0008–0018`; `spine59:para0002–0011`.

This recontextualizes Kanzaki’s later evolution into loyal opposition. The political question has existed since childhood:

> When does capability create a duty to intervene in a collective problem?

Y2V07 answers developmentally: Kanzaki begins acting because Ichinose’s class is no longer external to him. Volume 0 supplies prehistory; it does not erase that development.

## 14.3 Amasawa Ichika — strong inference must remain inference

Atsuomi speaks with Tianzawa about a first daughter already inside the White Room: a test-tube child, fifth-generation No. 2, placed under near-total institutional control. `spine55:para0052–0089`.

Given the name, cohort, sex, later White Room identity, and family context, the natural retrospective inference is that this child is Amasawa Ichika. But Volume 0 does not directly name her in this scene.

The evidence classification is therefore:

- institutional/family provenance: strong;
- exact child identity: `UA` / high-probability inference, not direct textual fact.

This is a useful methodological test. Retrospective confidence must not erase the difference between explicit naming and convergent identification.

# 15. White Room and ANHS — related experiments, different degrees of counter-authorship

Volume 0 makes any simple “White Room versus ANHS” binary less defensible and more interesting.

## 15.1 Shared political field

Both institutions emerge from national elite-formation politics. ANHS is Kijima’s visible government project. The White Room is initially Naoe’s covert counterproject and later Atsuomi’s private base. Their histories intersect through factional conflict, Narumori, Tsukishiro, and the desire to control future human capital.

Thus ANHS is not an untouched outside world that accidentally receives a White Room child. It is already part of the political struggle that created his origin.

## 15.2 Different relation to consent and exit

The White Room acquires children before meaningful consent and conceals the institution from ordinary accountability. Exit is failure, often managed through disappearance or controlled aftercare.

ANHS students do not design the institution, and expulsion is coercive. But they enter with recognized personhood, form lateral relationships, dispute leaders, move among groups, and can build futures after exit. Airi’s V07 future becomes particularly important after Volume 0: ANHS can judge her unfit for its game, but it cannot erase her from the denominator of human possibility.

## 15.3 Different treatment of plurality

The White Room eventually adds communication because children seek it, but treats the resulting slowdown in ability growth as a cost. Human relation is a curriculum variable.

ANHS also authors contact—V08 makes that explicit—but participants can reinterpret it. Sudō can change motive. Yamamura can experience visibility. Kushida can choose adversarial cooperation. Ryūen can be constrained outside his constituency. The institution does not fully control what those contacts mean.

## 15.4 Different record regimes

Both institutions measure. Both can misrecognize. Both turn people into data.

The White Room’s distinctive corruption is control of the denominator and nonpublic rule environment. It can erase failed entrants and represent the surviving sample as the original whole.

ANHS’s official records can also be manipulated, as Year 1 and Year 2 establish, but competing witnesses, teachers, classes, devices, and procedures sometimes generate rival provenance. The system contains more independent authors of the record.

## 15.5 The correct alternative standard

The question `Y1H-042` should therefore be reframed.

ANHS need not prove that it has no political relation to the White Room. Volume 0 shows that standard is unavailable.

A genuine alternative would require:

- persons who remain more than project inputs;
- meaningful lateral relations;
- the possibility of dissent;
- development that survives the developer;
- exit that does not erase future personhood;
- independent record sources;
- and the ability to surprise, refuse, or defeat the environment’s author.

The distinction is not **authored versus unauthored environment**. No education is unauthored.

It is:

> **environmental authorship that preserves counter-authorship versus environmental authorship that seeks to monopolize the person’s meaning.**

# 16. Truth, proof, records, and the politics of the denominator

Volume 0 is one of the series’ most important texts about evidence.

## 16.1 Public rescue, private acquisition

The proposed website would use phrases such as protecting life, anonymous acceptance, poverty consultation, and foster care. The public language is not wholly disconnected from real social need. Some children might otherwise die. That partial truth is what makes the front effective. `COTE:V00:spine20:para0010–0018`.

The deception lies in the omitted destination and purpose. Parents are not giving informed consent to beta curricula, political asset production, concealed mortality, or permanent institutional possession.

## 16.2 The denominator is a political object

If an institution can decide that failed children were never part of the public sample, “success rate” becomes authored narrative rather than empirical fact.

This changes `Y1H-040`. The prior question asked whether institutional records can become independently auditable. Volume 0 strengthens the answer:

> Auditability requires independent authority over entry criteria, denominator, failure definition, aftercare, and publication—not merely access to records written by the institution being audited.

## 16.3 Biological identity becomes proof infrastructure

Mika is kept alive partly because her biological relation protects the experiment’s future credibility. DNA, parentage, background, and the apparent ordinariness of the parents become evidence that the curriculum produced the child. `spine11:para0006–0009`; `spine29:para0048–0061`.

Yet the same institution resists data that would undermine the conclusion. When scientists call Kiyotaka a natural genius, Atsuomi objects because the category threatens the project’s purpose. `spine40:para0022–0028`.

The institution therefore wants proof, but only within a conclusion-compatible frame.

## 16.4 Kiyotaka learns the same terrain

Kiyotaka grows up inside a world where:

- displayed ability changes how the environment treats him;
- hidden ability preserves options;
- official tests reveal only what they are designed to measure;
- instructors can be modeled;
- records can become targets;
- and the observer’s theory shapes what counts as evidence.

His later manipulation of official and social legibility is not merely a skill acquired at ANHS. Volume 0 reveals its childhood epistemology.

# 17. Ethics — rescue, experimentation, paternal authority, and the ownership of another person’s future

## 17.1 A beneficial possibility does not legitimate the institution

The White Room may genuinely save some infants from death or abandonment. It may genuinely produce unusual competence. Those benefits are morally relevant.

They do not answer:

- whether children may be acquired through fraud or crime;
- whether lethal curricula are proportionate;
- whether failure may be erased;
- whether political loyalty may be manufactured from social vulnerability;
- whether parents understand the actual intervention;
- whether a child’s future belongs to the sponsor who funded it.

The project repeatedly treats achieved capacity as retroactive authorization. Volume 0 refuses that inference by making the cost visible.

## 17.2 Atsuomi’s “equal risk” is false equality

Atsuomi places his own son into beta and treats this as proof that he is not exempting himself. But equal exposure chosen by the sovereign is not equal consent.

Kiyotaka cannot authorize the risk. Atsuomi bears no equivalent bodily exposure. The decision grants the father moral theater while transferring danger to the child.

## 17.3 Research and political purpose contaminate one another

Specialists may genuinely seek knowledge. Atsuomi may genuinely seek national competitiveness. Donors may genuinely desire educational innovation.

But the institution lacks fiduciary priority toward the child. When child welfare conflicts with data, proof, political value, or future capacity, the child loses.

That is the decisive ethical defect.

## 17.4 Paternal authority becomes project ownership

Atsuomi does not appeal to intimate parental responsibility. He treats biological relation as a property-like claim over experimental use. Kiyotaka’s body, education, identity, and future career all become objects of planning.

The later Year-2 question of developmental jurisdiction therefore has a direct genealogy. Ayanokōji’s tendency to assign purposes to Kei, Ichinose, Horikita’s class, or an entire class ecology is not identical to his father’s politics, but Volume 0 shows what the method becomes when no reciprocal limit exists.

## 17.5 Origin does not abolish responsibility

Kiyotaka’s formation explains:

- why bodily cost can become a variable;
- why he conceals vulnerability;
- why he models people through utility and development;
- why he finds hidden stages natural;
- why ordinary relational norms must be learned explicitly.

Explanation is not exoneration. Year 2 increasingly gives him alternatives. Once he can recognize autonomy, care, consent, and counter-authorship, continued manipulation becomes ethically answerable to knowledge he now possesses.

# 18. Japanese conceptual vocabulary and register

## `格差`

The opening sequence names multiple inequalities before Atsuomi reduces them to a personal ascent problem. `格差` is structural description; his answer is competitive mobility rather than distributive justice. `COTE:V00:spine09:para0002–0009`.

## `野心`

`野心` is Atsuomi’s positive self-foundation. It is not momentary ambition but an expanding surplus that substitutes for family, education, and inherited capital. `spine09:para0018–0022`.

## `極めて優秀な政治家`

The superlative politician redirects preference. The phrase supplies the explicit political theory behind hidden environmental authorship. `spine12:para0240–0272`.

## `商品`

Atsuomi calls children `商品` while discussing acquisition and risk. The noun is not metaphorical decoration; it reveals the institution’s transactional ontology. `spine20:para0022`.

## `成功例` / `失敗例`

These terms convert children into institutional outcomes. Their force depends upon who controls the denominator and what happens to the person behind the classification.

## `βカリキュラム`

Beta is not simply “harder.” It marks a curriculum beyond the specialists’ estimated human limit, used to test whether an outlier exists. `spine30:para0031–0057`.

## `力を持っていながら、それを使わないのは愚か者のすることだ`

Atsuomi speaks this maxim first to Kiyotaka and later to Kanzaki. It treats capacity as carrying an obligation to intervene—but leaves the purpose and beneficiary of intervention unspecified. `spine37:para0042`; `spine59:para0008`.

## `天才`

The term becomes contested evidence. Specialists use it to name Kiyotaka; Atsuomi resists because it threatens the environmental-production thesis; Arisu uses the heredity problem against the institution; Suzukake ends with a mixed account. `spine40:para0022–0028`; `spine42:para0001–0012`; `spine61:para0020–0036`.

## `上位10%` / `上位0.01%`

Suzukake’s distinction gives the White Room a bounded achievement. It can manufacture high competence more reliably than singular genius. `spine61:para0030`.

## `牙を研ぐ`

Kiyotaka’s concealed “sharpening of fangs” marks active counter-authorship under apparent obedience. `spine67:para0001–0004`.

## `入学を決める`

The final wording matters: Kiyotaka “decides” to enter ANHS. It preserves agency even as the surrounding political infrastructure has been arranged. `spine68:para0047`.

# 20. Formal and literary strengths

## 20.1 Political prehistory enlarges rather than excuses the school story

The volume could have reduced itself to a catalogue of Kiyotaka’s childhood feats. Instead it devotes extensive space to factional politics, fundraising, procurement, institutional design, and adult compromise. The result is a stronger causal world: the White Room exists because people built it for reasons.

## 20.2 The narration changes scale effectively

Atsuomi’s sections think through organizations, resources, leverage, and political time. Kiyotaka’s childhood sections think through stimuli, tests, bodies, memory, and immediate inference. The shift lets the same institution be seen as project and lived environment.

## 20.3 The volume refuses a simple nature/nurture answer

Kiyotaka’s extraordinary memory, ordinary observed parental lineage, massive cohort attrition, adaptive learning, and Suzukake’s mixed judgment prevent a single-cause solution. This preserves the series’ larger interest in `実力`: capacity is possessed, developed, displayed, measured, and socially made usable through different processes.

## 20.4 The best reveals answer one question while opening another

Volume 0 confirms the political relation among Atsuomi, Tsukishiro, Kijima, Narumori, and ANHS. It deliberately withholds the final operational plan. It confirms background preparation but not the blank year. It confirms the White Room’s generational structure but not every later allegiance.

The result is retrospective clarification without total closure.

## 20.5 Yuki and Shiro prevent Kiyotaka from monopolizing childhood meaning

Both characters reveal something the institution cannot express through rank. Yuki reveals the cost of relation under a system that punishes threshold failure. Shiro reveals that leaving can be success when the institution’s goal is not one’s own.

## 20.6 The final line preserves agency grammatically

After extensive evidence of adult planning, the narration says Kiyotaka will **decide** to enroll. That verb prevents the political prehistory from becoming fatalism.

# 21. Critical reservations and counterreadings

## 21.1 Do not convert political complexity into moral ambiguity without limit

Atsuomi has real grievances and coherent reform beliefs. That does not make child acquisition, lethal experimentation, denominator erasure, or political loyalty manufacture ethically balanced propositions. Complexity explains the actor; it does not neutralize the wrong.

## 21.2 Do not overclaim scientific validity

Volume 0 is fiction, and the White Room’s own records are compromised. Its nature/nurture claims cannot be treated as empirical developmental science. The responsible literary claim is about what the institution believes, what the narrative establishes within its world, and what contradictions its own evidence reveals.

## 21.3 Do not infer compassion from every intervention

Kiyotaka’s report of Yuki’s condition, his refusal at the clinic, and his preservation of weaker peers as data can produce beneficial consequences. The narration often supplies instrumental reasons. Benefit and care must not be conflated.

## 21.4 Do not infer total emotional absence from institutional assessment

Observers call his affect nearly zero. Volume 0 also shows dislike, curiosity, preference formation, strategic concealment, and attention to others. The strongest formulation is severe impairment and suppression, not metaphysical absence.

## 21.5 Do not make Ishigami White Room-born by association

V00 supplies an external elite/political developmental path. Atsuomi connection and unusual ability are not provenance proof.

## 21.6 Do not call the Tianzawa child explicitly Ichika without marking inference

The evidence is highly convergent, but the scene does not name the child.

## 21.7 Do not close Tsukishiro’s intent

The final plan is withheld. Volume 0 makes a broader strategic reading more plausible, not certain.

## 21.8 Do not let Volume 0 erase development

Kiyotaka’s childhood shows roots of concealment, analysis, and instrumentalism. Year 1 and Year 2 still contain genuine changes: chosen ordinary life, relationships, desire for counter-authored outcomes, willingness to distribute agency, and recognition of futures beyond institutional utility.

Prehistory explains the starting state. It does not turn every later development into revelation.

# 30. Retrospective endpoint and unresolved questions

Volume 0 changes current reader knowledge but leaves several questions deliberately open.

## 30.1 Admission and the blank year

1. What exact sequence connects the prepared identity/history to Kiyotaka's enrollment decision?
2. What did Matsuo and Eiichirō actually do, know, choose, or suffer?
3. Did Atsuomi permit a controlled escape, create an apparently uncontrolled escape, or prepare an environment he expected Kiyotaka to choose?
4. Which parts of the later admission story were true, incomplete, instrumental, or fabricated?

## 30.2 Tsukishiro and Nanase

5. What was Tsukishiro's exact task during the Year 2 operation?
6. How much genuine expulsion risk was authorized as part of the test?
7. Was Nanase assigned partly as protection, observation, provocation, or several functions?
8. Did any actor's allegiance or objective change during execution?

## 30.3 Kiyotaka's interior development

9. Which later emotions are newly developed, which were previously unreadable, and which are strategically concealed?
10. Can he transform inherited environmental authorship into answerable, reversible development?
11. Can he disclose weakness rather than only allowing others to see selected strength or history?
12. Will he eventually reject the person-as-piece ontology when doing so imposes a real cost on his own project?

## 30.4 White Room scientific future

13. Can later generations produce high capability with substantially less deprivation?
14. Does communication curriculum create generative social ability or merely functional performance?
15. Can Kiyotaka as educator raise others without reproducing the institution's jurisdiction?
16. What becomes of failed children outside the few elite families capable of recovery support?

## 30.5 ANHS as alternative

17. Is ANHS plurality structurally protected or contingent on current actors?
18. Can its records, rules, and hidden interventions become independently auditable?
19. Does ordinary life remain counter-curriculum once institutions deliberately stage it?
20. Can Kiyotaka's later class-separation experiment produce an answer he does not own?

The correct post-Volume-0 endpoint is not complete explanatory closure. It is a better partition of what is known, what is strongly inferred, and what the published source still withholds.

# 31. Final synthesis — the child who exceeds the institution that authored his conditions

Volume 0 begins with a man who believes history belongs to whoever can author the conditions of choice.

Atsuomi learns this lesson through political defeat. Voters do not reward slogans without organization. Parties do not reward competence without allegiance. Patrons do not preserve subordinates once their use changes. He responds by building an institution in which he can control organization, allegiance, population, expertise, record, and future placement.

The White Room is that response made architectural.

It is born because ANHS gives Kijima political power. Naoe wants a rival achievement and a future faction. Abandoned children provide the moral story; hidden elite children provide capital and connections; isolated researchers provide controllable expertise; white walls provide purity; edited records provide success; conditioning provides future loyalty.

The institution is therefore political before it is pedagogical.

That fact does not make its educational results unreal.

It can intensively train people. It can raise ordinary ceilings. It can produce disciplined, adaptive, high-performing adults. Its own senior researcher estimates that it can intentionally produce people in the upper ten percent. To dismiss all of this as fake would reproduce the same analytical error the series repeatedly warns against: a measurement can be partial without being meaningless.

The White Room's failure lies somewhere more precise.

It cannot reproduce Kiyotaka.

Its greatest success depends upon a capacity it did not manufacture and cannot reliably locate in another child. Kiyotaka begins new subjects imperfectly, then absorbs, adapts, and transfers at a rate the institution cannot explain. Without the White Room he would not become the same person. Without his singular capacity the White Room would not produce him. He is neither pure product nor untouched natural genius.

He is the conjunction that falsifies every exclusive theory.

This is why Arisu's childhood challenge and Suzukake's final record belong together. Arisu correctly senses that curriculum alone cannot explain him. The White Room correctly demonstrates that environment transformed what the capacity became. Both are wrong when they claim exclusive jurisdiction over the explanation.

The institution's deeper failure concerns personhood.

Its curriculum treats relation as noise until children generate relation anyway. Yuki smiles. Shirō wants freedom and friends. Children seek communication. Researchers later incorporate communication into the curriculum and can then count the resulting social capacity as institutional achievement.

But the temporal order remains visible:

> **life exceeds design; design notices the excess; design attempts to appropriate it.**

Kiyotaka's own childhood is marked by the same struggle.

He learns preference through a carrot question. He learns a smile through Yuki. He learns that another exceptional child can choose freedom through Shirō. He learns to preserve weaker peers as objects of study. He learns that a test includes the examiner. He learns that displayed ability and possessed ability can be separated. He learns that allowing the observer to believe in total control may be safer than openly resisting.

These are not disconnected anecdotes. They form the prehistory of the later protagonist.

The school-age Ayanokōji who hides his power, studies people, creates developmental stages, and lets others experience choices as their own did not invent those capacities at ANHS. They were cultivated under a political-pedagogical system that understood information and desire as instruments.

Yet later development remains real.

Childhood Kiyotaka rejects Shirō's freedom. The Year 1/Year 2 Kiyotaka comes to value ordinary school life. Childhood Kiyotaka treats removed peers as dead to the system. The later Kiyotaka recognizes Airi's future outside ANHS, accepts successor relations without himself, and wants Horikita's class to become capable of defeating him. Childhood Kiyotaka cannot smile. Later ordinary relations produce embarrassment, affection, attachment, curiosity, and forms of care that are not exhausted by strategic utility even when instrumental grammar remains.

Volume 0 therefore does not reveal that the later person was always present unchanged.

It reveals the baseline from which change must be measured.

The admission ending makes that distinction politically urgent. Atsuomi recognizes Kiyotaka's affective incompleteness and prepares an alternative environment. Tsukishiro arranges an identity/history and accepts a future testing role. One year later Kiyotaka chooses ANHS.

The doorway is authored.

What happens after he passes through it is not wholly authored.

Atsuomi may intend political evaluation, emotional repair, succession, or proof. He cannot assign in advance what Kei, Horikita, Sudō, Ryūen, Ichinose, Sakayanagi, Hirata, Airi, the successor Ayanokōji Group, travel, food, snow, failure, or ordinary friendship will mean to Kiyotaka. The school itself can stage encounters without owning every relation they produce.

This is the strongest post-Volume-0 formulation of freedom available at the present boundary:

> **Freedom need not require an uncaused beginning. It requires the capacity to generate purposes, relations, refusals, and meanings that the authors of one's conditions cannot legitimately claim as their property.**

That standard also exposes the unfinished ethical problem.

Kiyotaka has begun exceeding Atsuomi's design. He still reproduces part of Atsuomi's jurisdictional grammar toward others. He can care genuinely while secretly deciding when another person should break. He can promote independence while controlling the field that makes it necessary. He can accept separation while choosing its timing alone.

Volume 0 explains this inheritance. It does not absolve it.

The White Room's final question is not whether it made Kiyotaka strong.

It did.

The question is whether a person made strong under unanswerable authorship can become capable of strength that does not require owning another person's future.

That question now governs the return to Year 2 Volume 9.
