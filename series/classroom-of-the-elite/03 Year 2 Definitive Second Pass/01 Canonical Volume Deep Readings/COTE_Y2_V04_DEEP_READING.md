# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ ２年生編４』
## Year 2 Volume 4 — Second-Pass Deep Reading
### Counter-authorship, parallel sovereignties, and the problem of making an outcome stick

> **Local analytical boundary:** this document uses the complete original Japanese Year 2 Volume 4, the frozen Year 1 V2 boundary, and the canonical endpoints of `COTE_Y2_V01_DEEP_READING.md` through `COTE_Y2_V03_DEEP_READING.md`. It deliberately does **not** use Y2V04.5 or later volumes, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptations, wikis, or remembered later revelations to identify still-hidden actors, settle later allegiances, or reinterpret the island examination from hindsight.

---

# 1. Scope, provenance, and inherited analytical state

`Y2V04` is the fourth canonical volume artifact in the Year 2 definitive second pass and the closing half of the second-year island examination. The Japanese EPUB was read completely in spine order, including the afterword, before targeted retrieval. The source passed the project’s Phase-0 integrity lock and is fingerprinted above. The deterministic textual locator namespace is `COTE:Y2V04:spineXX:paraYYYY`; visual evidence is separately routed to audited internal image files so that prose and illustration claims remain distinguishable.

The inherited Year 2 progression entering this volume is already structured:

- **Y2V01 — legibility:** bounded information about people becomes public infrastructure, while sovereign rule-making remains selectively hidden.
- **Y2V02 — jurisdiction:** once ability and risk become actionable, the question becomes who may bind another person’s future and under what right of refusal.
- **Y2V03 — attribution:** when visible outcomes can be detached from their causal chains by GPS blind spots, performed identity, strategic withholding, or falsified records, the problem becomes who actually authored what happened.

V04 does not abandon any of those problems. It asks what happens after several competing authors have been identified and their plans collide.

The island now contains multiple overlapping command systems:

- Nagumo’s third-year network;
- Kōenji’s radically individual autonomous strategy;
- Tsubaki’s first-year coalition;
- Hōsen’s opportunistic violence;
- Yagami and Utomiya’s covert counter-operation against Tsubaki;
- Sakayanagi’s distributed second-year protective network;
- Ryūen’s mercenary and class-governance machinery;
- Horikita and Ibuki’s improvised two-person cooperation;
- Tsukishiro and Shiba’s administrative extraction plan;
- Amasawa’s partially independent White Room-derived agency;
- Ichinose’s unilateral warning;
- Kiryūin’s autonomous intervention;
- and Ayanokōji’s own layered countermeasures.

This changes the governing question from simple causation to **sovereignty**:

> **When several actors can shape the same environment, whose authored conditions actually become binding—and what makes an outcome survive contact with other authors?**

The volume’s answer is not “the strongest person wins.” Physical, informational, social, bureaucratic, and institutional strength repeatedly produce different winners depending on which level of the contest becomes decisive.

# 2. Executive thesis — from attribution to sovereignty

Year 2 Volume 4 is a novel about **counter-authorship**.

V03 demonstrated that knowing who was present, who moved where, or who possessed a particular tool did not automatically establish causal responsibility. V04 goes further. Even when authorship becomes visible, no author controls the field alone. Every serious plan is exposed to another actor who can reinterpret, reroute, absorb, or invalidate it.

The volume therefore distinguishes **having a plan** from **possessing sovereignty**.

Tsubaki has a sophisticated plan. She creates a first-year coalition, identifies isolated upperclass targets, intentionally permits some groups to be caught, studies Ayanokōji’s aversion to public exposure, and uses the visible failure of one layer to open a cleaner route for the true physical attacker. Her design is not amateurish. Yet it exists inside a field she does not control. Sakayanagi counter-authors the pursuit network. Ryūen intercepts Hōsen. Yagami and Utomiya covertly work toward Tsubaki’s failure while presenting themselves as participants. Tsubaki’s authorship is real and still not sovereign.

Nagumo appears closer to sovereignty. The third-year class system has become a **shadow polity** in which many students cooperate with him because ordinary class mobility has effectively closed and he alone can offer alternative routes. He can surround Kōenji, allocate free groups, suppress rivals, and deliberately stage the point race. Yet that very concentration creates a single point of failure. When Ayanokōji incapacitates him and Nagumo’s radio falls silent, the third-year command architecture stops functioning. The final two-point gap between Kōenji and Nagumo cannot be separated from that interruption. Ayanokōji did not intend to choose the final ranking or the identities of the expelled third-years, but by coercively removing the sovereign node he changed the whole system.

This is one of the volume’s most important ethical findings:

> **Environmental authorship can produce consequences beyond the author’s intended object.**

Ayanokōji acts against Nagumo partly to stop him from walking into Tsukishiro’s danger and partly to preserve his own low visibility. The immediate act is bounded. Its effects are not. Nagumo loses the command capacity that was restraining Kōenji; Kōenji wins by two points; Ayanokōji himself recognizes that the bottom-of-table outcome may also have changed. Once one person operates on a sufficiently central node, “I only meant to solve this local problem” becomes an inadequate account of responsibility.

Ryūen supplies a different theory of sovereignty. Hōsen is physically stronger in their confrontation, but Ryūen refuses the premise that bodily victory defines the contest. He permits the emergency alarm to continue, uses institutional arrival as leverage, protects Ishizaki and Albert from unnecessary exposure, delegates the actual examination to Katsuragi, and explains why Albert’s superior raw strength does not make him leader. Leadership is not the body that can win one fight. It is the capacity to organize people, define the relevant win condition, and make the group continue functioning when the leader is absent or injured.

Horikita’s smaller confrontation with Amasawa and Ibuki offers the most normatively promising counterexample. Horikita cannot defeat Amasawa alone. Rather than covertly authoring Ibuki’s behavior, she creates an explicit cooperative structure: link groups so one incapacitated partner cannot expel the other, recognize Ibuki’s superior martial intuition, demand coordination, and tell her openly that she needs to break out of total isolation if she wants to become a better rival. The developmental intervention is still asymmetrical—Horikita is diagnosing Ibuki—but the diagnosis is **spoken, contestable, and tied to a mutually intelligible immediate goal**. It is much closer to answerable developmental authorship than Ayanokōji’s hidden curricula.

Amasawa’s White Room identity is finally locally established, but that revelation destroys the simplest model of the institution. She knows curriculum-specific details that only a White Room student should know, and she explains that the fifth generation allowed limited communication because the earlier radical individualist model caused serious failures. Her expressiveness is therefore partly a **curricular difference**, not evidence that White Room upbringing leaves all students socially identical. More importantly, Amasawa rejects the institution’s strongest ideological claim. Her own existence—an exceptionally selected child born through assisted reproduction—and her observation of persistent differences in intelligence, personality, and spirit lead her to doubt that environment can equalize all human beings. She wants freedom outside the closed system and admires Ayanokōji rather than inheriting the resentment expected of a later generation.

White Room origin therefore ceases to function as an allegiance label.

That distinction becomes even more important because V04 reveals another covert layer around Yagami and Utomiya. Yagami publicly assists Tsubaki with tablet and penalty strategy. At the end of the chapter, Utomiya secretly reports to him that Tsubaki’s plan failed “as you wanted.” This directly proves a covert relation and a counter-operation against Tsubaki. It does **not**, at the V04 boundary, prove every later proposition one might know about Yagami’s origin. The local artifact must preserve the narrower claim: Yagami is a hidden counter-author inside the first-year coalition.

Tsukishiro is the volume’s most powerful would-be sovereign. He and Shiba have administrative authority, altered conditions, physical competence, access to extraction support, and the ability to suppress or falsify records. The I2 beach is meant to convert the school examination into a private retrieval zone. Yet Tsukishiro loses because Ayanokōji does not try to outfight administrative sovereignty by physical ability alone. He **counter-authors the institution**.

Using Sakayanagi’s borrowed transceiver and established rescue procedure, he arranges a report that second-year students with broken watches are down at I2. The claim is sufficiently grounded that GPS verification would support it. Mashima and Chabashira therefore have institutional reasons to arrive independent of Ayanokōji’s private story. The intervention converts ordinary school procedure into an adversarial witness mechanism. Tsukishiro’s altered environment cannot survive the transition from private control to inspectable institutional presence.

This is the constructive counterpart to V03’s record-falsification problem:

> **Independent provenance becomes possible when the target can route a fact through a procedure the hostile record-author does not fully control.**

The victory remains incomplete. Tsukishiro declares that “nothing happened,” and Ayanokōji accepts pragmatic containment rather than demanding a fully truthful public record. The coercive episode is interrupted without becoming transparently adjudicated. Adult counterweights can therefore stop an immediate act more easily than they can force the institution to narrate the act truthfully.

The volume’s most explicit moral contrast with the White Room comes during this confrontation. Ayanokōji says that school has given him things he wants to do and enjoy and that outside the White Room he has encountered many excellent human beings the White Room could never produce. The claim is not that ANHS is pure. The same examination has enabled covert violence, patronage, administrative capture, and expulsion. His claim is narrower and more powerful:

> **plurality produces forms of human excellence that a closed curriculum cannot author in advance.**

Ichinose’s decision to warn him, Kiryūin’s unsolicited intervention, Horikita and Ibuki’s self-created cooperation, Sakayanagi’s network, Ryūen’s political reinterpretation of violence, and Kōenji’s refusal to become predictable are all examples of this surplus.

The compact thesis is therefore:

> **V04 turns attribution into sovereignty. It asks not merely who caused an event, but whose authored environment becomes binding when rival plans collide. Its answer repeatedly favors distributed counter-authorship over single-author control: a plan survives when it can recruit autonomous actors, resilient procedures, and alternative sources of judgment rather than depending upon one person’s total command.**

That answer is ethically hopeful without being clean. Ayanokōji himself is becoming exceptionally good at building such environments. The question remains whether he will use that ability to preserve other people’s authorship—or to become the most sophisticated sovereign in the school.

# 3. Position in the Year 2 architecture

V04 closes the island-examination movement without closing the White Room problem.

The afterword explicitly describes this volume as the latter half of the island exam and states that the Tsukishiro confrontation reaches a temporary endpoint while the White Room story continues. That publication boundary matters. It permits several local conclusions:

- Amasawa can be classified as White Room-confirmed.
- Nanase’s grievance and recruitment can be separated from White Room origin.
- Tsukishiro’s immediate brute-force extraction attempt fails.
- Kōenji fulfills the explicit V02 bargain with Horikita.
- Nagumo’s third-year system is exposed as both extraordinarily powerful and structurally centralized.
- Ryūen’s post-Year-1 leadership gains stronger evidence of voluntary legitimacy and delegation.
- Tsubaki’s first-year operation fails.
- Yagami and Utomiya are directly shown to possess a hidden line of coordination against Tsubaki.
- no second-year is expelled from the island exam.

It does **not** permit the analyst to use later volumes to settle:

- the complete identity, origin, or motive of every hidden first-year actor;
- the longer-term meaning of Amasawa’s “traitor” status;
- Tsukishiro’s ultimate political role or sincerity;
- later Nagumo/Ayanokōji conflict;
- or later changes in any class constitution.

The island arc should therefore be treated as a completed **political experiment** with unresolved actors still inside it.

Its four-volume local progression can now be stated with confidence:

> **V01: who becomes visible?**  
> **V02: who may bind whom?**  
> **V03: who authored the event?**  
> **V04: whose authorship becomes sovereign when several authors collide?**

# 4. Narrative architecture — nine collisions of authorship

## 4.1 Amasawa’s monologue — the experiment produces a critic of its own premise

The opening monologue immediately breaks the assumption that White Room origin determines one ideological type.

Amasawa describes herself as a child created through assisted reproduction, never knowing her parents, and raised in an environment whose explicit purpose was to test the proposition that human limits are determined by environment rather than heredity. The irony is structural: the institution that wants to demonstrate the supremacy of environment is also willing to work with unusually advantaged biological material.

Amasawa notices the contradiction from inside.

She concludes that equal curriculum does not erase individual differences in intelligence, personality, or spirit. She therefore becomes more than another data point. She is a subject capable of forming a theory **about the experiment conducting her life**.

Her desire for freedom matters for the same reason. The White Room can control curriculum and information; it cannot prevent the learner from assigning value to an unplanned outside world.

## 4.2 Amasawa’s identification — evidence becomes sufficient without becoming total

When Amasawa confronts Ayanokōji and Nanase, she supplies curriculum-specific details from childhood that create a locally sufficient identification route. Ayanokōji no longer has to infer origin from exceptional ability, handwriting, handedness, or strange access. The evidentiary category changes from suspicion to confirmation.

But the scene immediately preserves the distinction between **identity** and **allegiance**.

Amasawa says she is not his enemy in the expected sense. She admires him and wanted to evaluate him personally. Her fifth-generation social fluency is partly explained by a curricular revision: later cohorts were allowed a minimum level of communication after the earlier radically individualist environment produced failures. White Room “personality” is therefore not a stable essential type.

The same scene refuses to solve the Komiya/Kinoshita attack merely because Amasawa was there. A second footprint set and the prepared club indicate another actor. Amasawa may even have intervened against that actor. Presence becomes proven while causation remains open.

## 4.3 Nagumo’s hidden polity — parallel government emerges inside the school

Ayanokōji’s stored GPS snapshots reveal that Nagumo has reorganized the third year into something more politically significant than a collection of classes.

Students from lower classes support him because their normal route to Class A is effectively closed. Nagumo can offer forms of mobility, reward, and recognition unavailable through ordinary class competition. He therefore becomes a **parallel sovereign**: his personal network mediates opportunity more strongly than the formal class structure for many students.

The network is enormously effective. It can deploy free groups, surround Kōenji, shape the point race, and conceal Nagumo’s true maximum output.

Its weakness is constitutional. The system depends disproportionately upon Nagumo as an active command node.

## 4.4 Kōenji versus the third-year network — capacity becomes generative through refusal

Kōenji’s achievement is not simply physical superiority.

The third-years attempt several forms of control:

- political bargain;
- intimidation;
- path obstruction;
- task interference;
- physical harassment;
- false-injury tactics.

Kōenji refuses to accept their framing. He evades rather than strikes, maintains movement, exhausts pursuers, and forces them to spend bodies and attention simply to keep contact with him.

His ability is therefore **generative** in a specific sense. He changes the problem faced by the network. Nagumo cannot merely ask, “How many points can Kōenji score?” He must ask, “How much of the year must I organize around preventing him from scoring?”

The V02 Horikita/Kōenji contract is essential background. His contribution is now socially usable without becoming class property. He is not cooperating because vague solidarity has overridden autonomy. He is fulfilling a bounded bargain he accepted.

## 4.5 Tsubaki’s coalition — visible failure as a designed intermediate state

Tsubaki’s first-year plan initially appears to be a broad encirclement operation. Its deeper structure is more sophisticated.

She wants to protect weak first-year groups from occupying the expulsion slots. She targets isolated upperclass students and explicitly recognizes that violence requires crossing a social threshold: if Hōsen acts first, others may follow once the norm is broken. She is willing to accept mastermind responsibility and even her own expulsion.

When Sakayanagi’s five second-year groups pin the first-year attackers, Tsubaki reveals that the capture itself was useful. The five visible groups test Ayanokōji’s preference for avoiding attention and draw protective groups away, creating a cleaner corridor for the real physical confrontation.

The plan therefore turns apparent defeat into information.

Yet Tsubaki is herself being used as an object of counter-authorship.

## 4.6 Yagami and Utomiya — the coalition contains its own counter-operation

Yagami publicly helps Tsubaki with tablet and penalty strategy. Utomiya publicly acts as one of Tsubaki’s core collaborators. The closing private radio exchange reveals a second structure: Utomiya reports to Yagami that Tsubaki’s plan failed “as you wanted,” and Yagami dismisses the plan as crude and doomed.

This is the cleanest local V04 evidence that the first-year operation contains a hidden counter-author.

It also disciplines later inference.

The scene proves:

- Yagami and Utomiya have a covert communication line;
- Yagami wanted Tsubaki’s operation to fail;
- at least some public assistance was compatible with private sabotage or steering;
- first-year class affiliation does not map cleanly onto operational allegiance.

It does **not** by itself prove every proposition about Yagami’s background that a later volume might establish.

## 4.7 Ryūen versus Hōsen — changing the win condition

Hōsen treats the encounter as a physical contest. Ryūen changes the object.

Even while taking severe punishment, Ryūen allows the emergency signal to continue. The prospect of institutional arrival changes what Hōsen can gain. Ishizaki and Albert then enter as collective force. Ryūen has also protected them from becoming the immediate injury-bearing frontline and entrusted the examination itself to Katsuragi.

The result demonstrates a mature political distinction:

> **winning a fight and winning a conflict are different achievements.**

Ryūen explicitly explains why Albert’s superior physical strength does not make him leader. Albert is loyal to the class and follows Ryūen because he judges Ryūen to be the better route to collective success. Voluntary loyalty does not erase Ryūen’s coercive history, but it is no longer analytically responsible to describe the polity as fear alone.

## 4.8 Horikita, Ibuki, and Amasawa — open developmental authorship

Horikita’s route to I2 is self-authored. An unsigned note warns of `K・A / 退学 / I2`; she infers danger to Ayanokōji and chooses to investigate even though doing so costs her examination efficiency.

Amasawa blocks her and attacks the legitimacy of her concern by emphasizing how little she actually knows about Ayanokōji.

Horikita does not deny the epistemic asymmetry.

Her answer is developmental rather than possessive: she wants to continue learning who he is through graduation, as classmate and friend, and become someone capable of helping him. She refuses to let Amasawa substitute her judgment for Ayanokōji’s own answer.

Ibuki then returns rather than accepting an easy victory over a distracted Horikita. Horikita links their groups for expulsion insurance and explicitly recruits Ibuki’s martial judgment. Her developmental language—break the shell, cooperate, grow into a rival—is direct and contestable. Ibuki can resist, argue, and still participate.

Their apparent exhaustion of Amasawa then receives its own attribution correction: Amasawa was already badly injured before the fight. What looked like a newly discovered stamina limitation was partly a hidden prior state.

## 4.9 I2 — private administrative sovereignty versus institutional counter-procedure

Tsukishiro’s design tries to make I2 a zone where official examination structure and private White Room retrieval become indistinguishable.

Ayanokōji refuses both premises available to him:

- he does not simply submit to administrative power;
- and he does not treat the encounter as a pure two-on-one combat puzzle.

The arrival of Kiryūin is autonomous and unplanned by him. Her presence already breaks Tsukishiro’s attempt to isolate the event. Ayanokōji then adds his own countermeasure: a rescue report routed through Sakayanagi and normal school procedure, grounded in real missing/broken watch signals. Mashima and Chabashira become independent institutional witnesses.

The trap fails because it cannot survive a change in **who is entitled to observe it**.

This closes the island examination on a principle stronger than personal superiority:

> **a private sovereign can be defeated by changing the evidentiary and procedural audience.**

# 5. The island examination as a conflict among sovereignties

## 5.1 Formal rules never fully determine the real game

The island examination has a dense public ruleset:

- designated-area movement;
- task competition;
- GPS searches;
- group composition;
- penalties for missed areas;
- retirement conditions;
- watches as location/safety infrastructure;
- point accumulation;
- and expulsion at the bottom.

V04 demonstrates that none of these rules is “just mechanics.” Each creates a political affordance.

GPS can coordinate allies and also expose them. Breaking a watch can erase a student from normal tracking while creating a rescue signal. Penalties can be used to force movement. Low supervision can enable violence. Group linking can become expulsion insurance. Emergency alarms can change a fight’s institutional meaning. Rain compensation can increase final-day volatility. A ruleset becomes political when actors can author second-order uses for its components.

## 5.2 Information is valuable because it changes which sovereignty is visible

Ayanokōji’s ten saved GPS snapshots are powerful not because they contain a secret message, but because longitudinal comparison reveals a structure no single image would show: Nagumo’s support network.

Tsubaki uses GPS differently. She watches Hōsen and tracks upperclass movement while trying to infer the invisible commander behind the second-year defense.

Sakayanagi performs another form of map authorship. From visible positions and terrain she simulates likely routes and identifies stationary first-years as possible command nodes.

The same technical infrastructure therefore supports several political styles:

- Ayanokōji: hidden longitudinal inference;
- Tsubaki: active operational command;
- Sakayanagi: distributed predictive coordination;
- Nagumo: centralized mass deployment.

## 5.3 The final ranking is causally overdetermined

Kōenji wins with 327 points. Nagumo finishes second with 325.

The raw table is factual.

Its causal meaning is not simple.

Kōenji’s extraordinary performance is real. Nagumo’s deliberate staging is real. Third-year interference against Kōenji is real. Ayanokōji’s incapacitation of Nagumo is real. Nagumo’s radio silence is real. The final-day score emerges from all of them.

The correct inference is therefore not:

> Kōenji proved he would always defeat Nagumo under neutral conditions.

Nor is it:

> Kōenji only won because Ayanokōji interfered.

The stronger statement is:

> **The result is an authored institutional fact whose causal history includes several independent and adversarial contributions.**

That distinction is precisely what the V03 attribution framework was built to preserve.

## 5.4 Expulsion becomes an unintended externality of command failure

Fifteen third-years are ultimately expelled from the bottom five groups. No second-year is expelled.

Ayanokōji explicitly considers that his interruption of Nagumo may have changed not only first/second place but the bottom of the table.

This matters because it widens the ethics of intervention.

A person who disables a central political node may affect participants he never directly considered.

The island exam therefore supplies a systems-level version of the project’s recurring principle:

> **effectiveness does not exhaust responsibility.**

Ayanokōji may have had defensible reasons to stop Nagumo. Those reasons do not make downstream consequences analytically disappear.

# 6. Ayanokōji Kiyotaka — counter-author without total control

V04 is one of the clearest early Year 2 demonstrations that Ayanokōji’s strength lies not merely in superior direct performance but in the ability to **change the layer on which a conflict is decided**.

## 6.1 Ordinary-adolescent layer — bodily need and chosen school life remain real

The volume repeatedly resists the idea that White Room competence abolishes embodiment.

Ayanokōji experiences real hunger and fatigue. He notes that deliberate starvation was not part of his earlier training. Food, water, sleep, weather, and movement impose constraints even upon an exceptional body.

More importantly, his confrontation with Tsukishiro produces one of his clearest positive statements about school life so far. He has found things he wants to do and enjoy. He has met excellent people the White Room could not produce.

That statement strengthens `AYANOKOJI_ORDINARY_LIFE` because school is no longer merely the environment he refuses to lose. It has become an evidentiary basis for a competing theory of human development.

## 6.2 Relational layer — aid can preserve dignity rather than purchase ownership

The Ibuki water exchange is deceptively important.

Ayanokōji recognizes that she is dehydrated and that direct charity would likely be rejected or converted into an unwanted debt relationship. He therefore proposes an ostensibly equal exchange: food for water. Ibuki later discovers that the water was not truly surplus.

His answer is that the transaction can still be treated as equal because he is not demanding gratitude or leverage.

This is not perfect transparency. He conceals the actual generosity.

But the concealment functions differently from his most coercive hidden curricula. The immediate goal is to make urgently needed aid **acceptable on the recipient’s own dignity terms**, not to engineer her later dependence or behavior.

The ethical distinction should be preserved:

> concealed benevolence can still be asymmetrical, but asymmetry becomes less possessive when it removes rather than creates future claims.

## 6.3 Strategic layer — concealment becomes dynamic rather than static

By day nine Ayanokōji deliberately targets a position just below the publicly displayed top ten. He is no longer simply “hiding ability.” He is managing the timing of legibility so that a late surge remains possible without inviting early countermeasures.

This is a more advanced version of the V01 mathematics reveal.

The question is no longer whether to be visible or invisible.

It is:

> **which visibility state produces the largest future option set?**

That is environmental authorship applied to self-presentation.

## 6.4 Developmental-author layer — V04 shows both restraint and hidden curation

His treatment of several characters occupies different ethical positions.

With Ibuki, he preserves dignity through an unequal exchange represented as equal.

With Nanase, he gets her to protect herself by explaining that reckless self-sacrifice could harm him. The method uses her orientation toward him instrumentally, but for a protective end.

With Sakayanagi, he openly asks for assistance and reveals enough about the White Room/bounty situation to permit a meaningful strategic response. This is unusually high-information collaboration by his standards.

With Ichinose, however, the old hidden-curriculum problem returns sharply. After she confesses, he deliberately does not tell her that Kei exists because he believes the revelation might damage her ability to finish the examination. He postpones a relational truth in order to preserve a desired functional state.

The action may be tactically compassionate.

It is also a clear assertion of jurisdiction over **when another person is allowed to receive information relevant to her own emotional choice**.

`Y1H-004` therefore receives strong negative evidence even if it remains open.

## 6.5 Nagumo — protective coercion with system-wide consequences

Ayanokōji wants Nagumo away from the Tsukishiro confrontation and does not want his own hidden status converted into public spectacle.

Nagumo refuses to disengage.

Ayanokōji incapacitates him with a single strike.

The immediate competence display is less analytically important than the network effect. Nagumo is not merely one student. He is the command node of a third-year shadow polity. Removing him therefore changes the behavior of many people at once.

This is environmental authorship by **node intervention**.

It is also a warning about concentrated sovereignty. The more effective a leader becomes at coordinating an entire system personally, the more consequential any intervention against that leader becomes.

## 6.6 Tsukishiro — Ayanokōji wins by changing procedure, not merely combat

Ayanokōji’s most significant V04 strategy is the rescue protocol.

He recognizes that a private beach under hostile administration is not a favorable jurisdiction even if he can physically survive there. He therefore creates an institutionally legible reason for ordinary school authorities to enter the scene.

This deserves to be classified as **generative strategic ability**.

He does not merely find the best move inside Tsukishiro’s game. He changes the game from:

> can you defeat two hostile adults and an extraction team in isolation?

into:

> can Tsukishiro preserve a clandestine extraction operation once independent school personnel have a procedural reason to arrive?

Tsukishiro cannot.

That is sovereignty defeated by counter-jurisdiction.

# 7. Amasawa Ichika — White Room identity without White Room determinism

## 7.1 Identity becomes direct evidence

V04 is the first local boundary at which Amasawa’s White Room origin can be treated as established rather than suspected.

The evidence is not “she is too strong to be normal.” It is curriculum-specific autobiographical knowledge that aligns with Ayanokōji’s own experience.

This is methodologically important. The project’s identity protocol works only if it permits eventual confirmation when evidence quality changes.

## 7.2 Her biology exposes an internal ideological contradiction

Amasawa’s assisted-reproduction background makes her a particularly revealing experimental subject.

The White Room wants to prove that environment rather than inherited advantage determines human limits. Yet selecting or creating unusually advantaged biological subjects weakens the purity of that claim.

Amasawa herself notices that equal treatment does not erase differences.

The literary significance is larger than a scientific objection. She demonstrates that the subject of an experiment can become capable of judging the experiment’s ontology.

## 7.3 Generational curriculum matters

Amasawa explains that the fifth generation was allowed minimum communication because earlier radical individualism produced breakdowns.

That means White Room cohorts are not interchangeable.

If a later-generation student is socially fluent, playful, or relationally expressive, those traits cannot be used straightforwardly to argue “this person cannot be White Room.” The institution itself modified the curriculum.

This is a clean `RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION` of earlier suspicion cues without using later-volume hindsight.

## 7.4 Admiration is not obedience

Amasawa calls Ayanokōji special and admires him.

Tsukishiro later calls her a traitor because she was selected to help retrieve him but never truly intended to fulfill the assigned role.

The text therefore directly separates:

- institutional origin;
- assigned mission;
- personal attachment;
- operational choice.

White Room origin becomes a provenance field, not a political party.

## 7.5 “Failure” reveals the institution’s ownership claim

Tsukishiro says Amasawa is now disqualified or failed and has no place to return.

Even if the exact reliability of everything he says remains unresolved, the category itself is revealing. The White Room treats refusal of assigned purpose as a failure state.

Amasawa’s ability has not vanished.

Her institutional value has changed because she will not function as instructed.

The “failed student” can therefore be excellent by every ordinary measure and still fail an institution whose real criterion is obedience to purpose.

# 8. Horikita Suzune and Ibuki Mio — explicit cooperation as developmental countermodel

## 8.1 Horikita follows a warning she cannot verify

The anonymous `正午 / K・A / 退学 / I2` note presents a classic attribution problem.

Horikita cannot verify the author or full meaning. She nevertheless identifies a plausible threat to Ayanokōji and chooses to investigate at personal examination cost.

That decision is not evidence that she now understands his hidden world.

It is evidence that her relationship to uncertainty has changed.

She can act under incomplete knowledge without pretending the knowledge is complete.

## 8.2 Amasawa’s challenge makes Horikita articulate what she actually wants

Amasawa attacks the legitimacy of Horikita’s concern by pointing out that she knows very little about Ayanokōji.

Horikita concedes the factual point.

Her response is not:

> I know him better than you.

It is approximately:

> **I want to keep learning who he is, and I want to become capable of helping him.**

This is an important relational distinction. The desire for knowledge becomes a future-oriented relationship project rather than a claim of current possession.

## 8.3 Ibuki refuses an unearned victory

Ibuki returns even though Horikita’s distraction could make their rivalry easier to exploit.

Her reasoning is particularistic and pride-driven: only she should defeat Horikita, and she does not want the result cheapened by Horikita abandoning points to help someone else.

That is not generalized benevolence.

It is nevertheless a form of rival recognition strong enough to generate aid.

## 8.4 Group linking converts rivalry into mutual insurance

Horikita’s proposal to link groups means one person’s incapacitation will not automatically eliminate the other.

The mechanism matters because it changes the cooperation from sentiment into structure.

They do not have to become friends in order to become mutually protective partners.

This is a small but valuable example of institutional design making cooperation possible between people who remain adversarial.

## 8.5 Horikita’s developmental speech is answerable

Horikita tells Ibuki that her martial instincts are valuable but that total isolation limits her. She asks for coordination and frames it as necessary if Ibuki wants to become a better rival.

This is developmental authorship.

It is also ethically different from Ayanokōji’s hidden environmental design because:

- the diagnosis is spoken;
- the goal is intelligible to the recipient;
- the recipient argues back;
- the immediate risk is shared;
- and cooperation does not require Ibuki to become Horikita’s dependent.

The scene should become an important comparative evidence point for later specialist work on **answerable cultivation**.

# 9. Nagumo Miyabi and the third-year shadow constitution

## 9.1 Nagumo has created mobility outside ordinary class competition

The third-years no longer behave like four independent classes contesting the same ladder.

Students from lower classes support Nagumo because he controls alternative opportunities and because the ordinary route to Class A is increasingly unrealistic.

This is politically double-edged.

Nagumo can liberate individual talent from class fate.

He can do so because access to mobility depends heavily upon **his personal recognition and resources**.

`Y1H-030` therefore remains only partially answered. The meritocratic impulse is institutionalized enough to be real, but patronage remains central.

## 9.2 Nagumo’s strategic ability is stronger than Ayanokōji initially credited

Nagumo accurately infers Ayanokōji’s hidden-ranking logic. He deliberately keeps another third-year group around the tenth-place threshold as a barrier against late surges. He claims he could have scored far higher and has deliberately staged the contest for interest.

Ayanokōji revises his own estimate upward.

This is important because the volume should not be read as simple humiliation of an overrated antagonist. Nagumo’s system is genuinely formidable.

## 9.3 Personal sovereignty is also a single point of failure

The network’s weakness is not lack of scale.

It is excessive centrality.

Once Nagumo is unconscious and no longer answering the radio, command stops. The third-year machinery loses the capacity to keep converting distributed bodies into one coordinated will.

The political lesson is familiar outside this series:

> a system can become more powerful and less resilient at the same time when coordination is concentrated in one sovereign node.

V04 makes that abstraction visible through a two-point result.

# 10. Kōenji Rokusuke — autonomy becomes contractually social without becoming owned

Kōenji wins the island examination alone with 327 points.

The raw achievement is extraordinary.

The second-pass classification must nevertheless remain precise.

The volume does not establish a sudden developmental leap in raw ability. It reveals the scale of capacity he already possessed and makes that capacity socially consequential because a prior political bargain has changed his willingness to deploy it.

## 10.1 V02’s bargain reaches performance closure

Horikita promised future non-interference if Kōenji took first place alone.

Kōenji accepted.

V04 records fulfillment.

This is stronger than vague statements that he “finally helps the class.” He does not submit to class ownership. He completes a contract under which the class receives an extraordinary one-time contribution and he receives freedom through graduation.

`Y1H-032` therefore becomes much more sharply partially answered:

> **In this specific polity, collective obligation becomes legitimate enough for Kōenji when it is explicit, bounded, reciprocally priced, and terminable.**

That is not yet a universal answer to what class membership always requires.

## 10.2 Restraint is part of the ability display

Against third-year harassment, Kōenji repeatedly avoids striking back even when physically provoked.

That matters because physically defeating pursuers would be an easier spectacle but a worse institutional strategy.

His capacity includes control over **which ability not to display**.

## 10.3 Kōenji remains epistemically resistant

The third-years cannot reliably predict his fatigue, recovery, motivation, or threshold. The safest analysis is not to turn the novel’s suggestions about his recovery into biomedical certainty.

What is locally established is functional:

- he sustains an exceptional solo pace;
- ordinary suppression methods are expensive;
- he remains difficult to model from visible prior performance;
- he fulfills a chosen bargain without accepting broader subordination.

This keeps him one of Year 2’s strongest counterexamples to the assumption that accurate observation naturally produces jurisdiction.

# 11. Ryūen Kakeru, Hōsen Kazuomi, Katsuragi Kōhei, and Albert — leadership beyond the fight

The Hōsen confrontation is one of V04’s clearest demonstrations that the series’ title concept `実力` cannot be reduced to a physical ranking.

## 11.1 Hōsen possesses extraordinary embodied force

The prose does not soften Hōsen’s physical superiority. He can dominate the direct exchange and inflict serious punishment. His decision to discard radio coordination once he has located Ayanokōji also fits his political psychology: plans are useful until they obstruct the personal contest he actually wants.

This means his failure should not be explained by denying his strength.

It should be explained by asking what **kind of strength** becomes decisive.

## 11.2 Ryūen refuses Hōsen’s definition of victory

Ryūen’s central move is conceptual.

Hōsen behaves as though the relevant question is:

> Who can physically beat whom?

Ryūen changes it to:

> Who will still possess usable institutional standing when the encounter ends?

Allowing the emergency signal to run means that a “victory” producing disciplinary removal can become a political defeat. The scene therefore reproduces in miniature the entire volume’s sovereignty thesis: the actor who defines the level of adjudication may defeat the actor who dominates the immediately visible level.

## 11.3 Albert’s loyalty is explicit evidence against fear-only governance

Hōsen asks why Albert, who may be physically stronger, follows Ryūen.

Ryūen’s answer emphasizes Albert’s class loyalty and judgment that Ryūen is the better route to collective success.

This does not erase Year 1 coercion.

It does establish that the polity now contains something coercion alone cannot explain: **voluntary evaluative allegiance**.

The followers are not simply bodies Ryūen frightens into motion.

## 11.4 Katsuragi makes the polity more resilient to leader absence

V03 established that Katsuragi can operationally constrain Ryūen. V04 adds delegation. Ryūen can spend his body and attention on Hōsen because he has entrusted the actual examination to Katsuragi.

That is a meaningful institutional development.

A polity becomes less purely personal when a leader can be temporarily absent and critical functions continue through another competent actor.

`Y1H-027` therefore receives stronger evidence for “better leader,” though the predator side remains unresolved. Ryūen has improved how power is distributed without yet establishing a principled ethical boundary on what power may do.

## 11.5 The mercenary contract with Sakayanagi preserves political plurality

The results chapter confirms that Sakayanagi hired Ryūen for a future favor. The favor she wants is personal: a serious one-on-one contest with Ayanokōji.

This is another example of cross-polity cooperation that does not require ideological merger.

Ryūen remains Ryūen. Sakayanagi remains Sakayanagi. Their temporary alignment is contractual rather than assimilative.

That matters because V04’s strongest systems are not necessarily harmonious. They are capable of coordinating **without erasing difference**.

# 12. Tsubaki Sakurako, Utomiya Riku, Yagami Takuya, and first-year political opacity

## 12.1 Tsubaki is a serious political thinker, not merely a decoy

V04 gives Tsubaki a coherent theory of coalition action.

She understands:

- expulsion risk as a year-group problem;
- the need to preserve weak groups;
- the difference between strategist and fighter;
- violence as a threshold whose normalization changes future conduct;
- the possibility that the school may hesitate to punish a large number of first-years at once;
- and the need for one actor to accept formal mastermind liability.

She is therefore doing more than following a hidden superior’s instructions.

The plan is genuinely hers at one layer.

## 12.2 Her willingness to self-expel is both commitment and political danger

Tsubaki had originally considered pairing with Ayanokōji, deliberately scoring zero, and using her own expulsion to transfer bounty value toward Utomiya/class C.

That willingness complicates the normal incentive model.

A student prepared to treat her own membership as expendable cannot be controlled through ordinary expulsion deterrence.

The same commitment also makes her dangerous to herself. Political authorship becomes self-annihilating when the author treats personal continuation as merely another resource.

## 12.3 Utomiya’s visible loyalty and hidden line are not the same thing

Utomiya appears protective of Tsubaki and class C. He also privately reports to Yagami.

The correct local reading is not that one layer must be false.

He may genuinely care about Tsubaki while participating in another hidden project. Year 2 repeatedly demonstrates that allegiance is **layered rather than binary**.

## 12.4 Yagami’s support is revealed as counter-authorship

Yagami’s public contribution to Tsubaki’s plan includes useful penalty/tablet strategy. The private exchange establishes that he wanted the operation to fail.

The important analytical category is therefore **steered assistance**:

> an actor can improve one part of another person’s plan while authoring the larger conditions under which the plan loses.

This is a sophisticated form of political action because it preserves the target’s sense of authorship.

It also resembles a recurring danger in Ayanokōji’s own developmental method: the person making the choice may not know that another actor has curated the field of available outcomes.

# 13. Ichinose Honami — solidarity crosses the class-enemy boundary

Ichinose’s warning is one of the most consequential relational choices in the volume because it directly violates the clean logic of inter-class competition.

## 13.1 Tsukishiro attacks solidarity through hostage leverage

Tsukishiro threatens Ichinose’s classmates if she warns Ayanokōji.

The tactic is precisely fitted to her political constitution. A threat aimed only at her would be weaker. Her class is the lever because care is the structure through which she organizes choice.

This confirms the V03 concern that concentrated solidarity can become a hostage vulnerability.

## 13.2 Ichinose nevertheless chooses to warn him

She travels for hours to reach Ayanokōji and informs him of Tsukishiro/Shiba’s plan.

Ayanokōji explicitly points out that, as a class enemy, she could rationally let him disappear.

She rejects the premise.

That is politically significant. Ichinose’s morality does not stop operating when competitive structure says another person is an enemy.

The act therefore supplies positive evidence that her solidarity is not merely in-group cohesion.

## 13.3 The confession makes the motive plural rather than impure

Ichinose confesses that she loves him.

This does not cancel the ethical content of the warning.

Her motive can simultaneously include:

- personal love;
- refusal to permit unjust harm;
- relationship loyalty;
- and fear for her classmates.

The analytically weak move would be to say either “she did it only because she is good” or “she did it only because she is in love.” The scene is more difficult precisely because private attachment and public morality reinforce each other.

## 13.4 Ayanokōji’s response preserves function by withholding truth

Ayanokōji recognizes the confession as a major branch point. He does not give an immediate romantic answer and, more importantly, deliberately withholds Kei’s existence because he judges that the truth may break Ichinose’s fighting spirit before the exam ends.

This is one of V04’s clearest negative disclosure cases.

The action may reduce immediate harm and help Ichinose complete the examination.

It also denies her information relevant to interpreting her own confession and his relational availability.

This is developmental authorship through **timed truth**.

## 13.5 Handoff implications

`Y1H-020` remains partially answered: Ichinose shows initiative, logistics, moral courage, and willingness to act under adversarial pressure without abandoning care.

`Y1H-022` remains open because this is not the required direct class-versus-class trust collision.

`Y1H-004` receives stronger negative evidence because Ayanokōji again curates relational knowledge around desired performance.

# 14. Sakayanagi Arisu — distributed protection without ideological annexation

Sakayanagi’s role in V04 is easy to understate because she does not dominate the action physically.

Her importance lies in network precision.

## 14.1 Ayanokōji explicitly recruits her as an ally

Ayanokōji tells Sakayanagi enough about Nanase, Amasawa, the bounty, and the first-year threat to request protection.

He reflects that he had previously avoided treating her as an ally because they belong to competing classes.

The relationship has now accumulated enough history that this boundary can be revised.

That is a relational development: class opposition no longer exhausts the possible political relationship.

## 14.2 Her A-class/Ichinose alliance becomes a defensive sensor network

Sakayanagi’s ability to coordinate five groups and infer likely first-year command nodes depends upon resources Horikita’s class does not possess at the same scale:

- high-information communication;
- class-wide discipline;
- alliance reach;
- predictive confidence;
- and the ability to delegate without losing strategic coherence.

This is centralized intelligence translated into distributed action.

## 14.3 The protection is reciprocal rather than free

Sakayanagi frames her assistance as another favor owed.

That matters because it prevents the relationship from becoming invisible obligation. Both parties know that aid has future political cost.

The eventual result chapter makes the desired repayment explicit through her Ryūen contract: she wants a serious Ayanokōji match and is willing to wait because she understands his current desire for a more peaceful school life.

The evidence is personal, but no decisive fiduciary conflict occurs in V04. `Y1H-028` and `029` therefore remain open.

# 15. Kiryūin Fūka — autonomous excellence as proof against single-author systems

Kiryūin is structurally important because her intervention is **not authored by Ayanokōji**.

## 15.1 OAA does not contain her most useful ability

Earlier in the volume she reads the Nagumo/Kōenji trajectory with striking intuition. Ayanokōji notes that such insight is not cleanly captured by the visible OAA categories.

This repeats the Year 2 principle that institutional scores can contain real information while missing the generative property that matters most in a new environment.

## 15.2 She makes herself temporarily invisible to normal GPS

At I2 Kiryūin arrives after intentionally breaking her watch, making ordinary tracking less effective. She observes the confrontation and chooses to help.

Her intervention is not based on:

- class obligation;
- Nagumo’s command;
- Ayanokōji’s request;
- a bounty;
- or a hidden adult mission.

She frames assistance to a junior as natural enough for a senior and also acts from curiosity and personal judgment.

## 15.3 She is exactly the kind of person Ayanokōji says the White Room cannot guarantee

Kiryūin’s excellence is not valuable because she reproduces an assigned answer.

It is valuable because she generates an answer nobody in Tsukishiro’s plan fully authored.

She therefore becomes a concrete example of the plurality Ayanokōji defends in his argument about school.

# 16. Tsukishiro and Shiba — administrative power at the limit of private control

## 16.1 I2 is designed as a jurisdictional conversion zone

Ayanokōji recognizes that the location and GPS mechanics are not incidental. The beach is a place where ordinary observation is weak and where the school examination can be converted into a private retrieval operation.

Tsukishiro’s goal is not to “win the exam.”

It is to make Ayanokōji’s school membership cease to matter.

This is sovereign power in its strongest form: the attempt to decide which institution has final jurisdiction over the person.

## 16.2 Tsukishiro’s theory of excellence differs from the school’s plurality

Tsukishiro treats ordinary school competition as trivial relative to the White Room’s highest product.

His model privileges broad output across situations and the capacity to stand above and lead many.

Ayanokōji’s answer is not that this ability is fake.

He says the outside world contains excellent people the White Room could never create.

The disagreement is therefore about **whether one optimized developmental regime can define the space of human excellence**.

## 16.3 Combat itself becomes an information contest

Tsukishiro and Shiba are genuinely dangerous. Tsukishiro also deliberately feeds misleading information through performance, allowing Ayanokōji to model one combat ratio before revealing greater speed.

This repeats the OAA problem at the bodily level:

> observed performance can be strategically authored evidence.

Ayanokōji survives partly because he notices anomalies rather than trusting the first model.

## 16.4 Independent procedure defeats private coercion

The rescue call is the political climax.

Ayanokōji uses the school’s own safety infrastructure to generate an arrival Tsukishiro cannot quietly absorb. The report is not a fabricated emergency detached from reality; broken/missing watches and the students’ actual location make verification supportive.

This is why the countermeasure is stronger than simply “call a teacher.”

It is routed through a procedure with independent institutional reasons to act.

## 16.5 Counterweights are effective without becoming sovereign

Mashima and Chabashira’s arrival stops the private confrontation from continuing cleanly.

Yet Tsukishiro can still insist that “nothing happened,” and the adults cannot simply adjudicate him out of authority on the spot.

The result is mixed:

- adult/institutional counterweights can interrupt;
- they can create witness presence;
- they can make hidden manipulation harder to sustain;
- they cannot necessarily force a truthful public history or immediate accountability.

`Y1H-037` therefore remains partially answered rather than resolved.

# 17. Truth, proof, record, and adversarial provenance

V04 provides the first positive procedural model for the auditability problem raised in V01–V03.

## 17.1 The project’s record problem before V04

Year 1 showed that official records can fail to preserve what happened.

V01 showed that some decisive rules may be hidden from ordinary participants.

V02 reframed auditability around provenance and jurisdiction.

V03 showed administrators actively capable of falsifying histories.

The obvious question became:

> If the authority producing the record is hostile, what kind of evidence could matter?

## 17.2 V04’s answer is independent procedural entry

Ayanokōji does not produce a perfect archive of the I2 event.

He produces something more modest and immediately useful: a reason for additional institutional actors to arrive that is not dependent upon Tsukishiro voluntarily inviting scrutiny.

This suggests a stronger audit principle:

> **A record becomes more trustworthy when no single interested actor controls observation, preservation, and adjudication simultaneously.**

The rescue procedure separates at least part of those roles.

## 17.3 Yet public truth remains compromised

The ending refuses a triumphalist reading.

Tsukishiro’s “nothing happened” framing survives as the practical public outcome. Ayanokōji accepts containment rather than forcing exposure.

That means V04 does not answer `Y1H-040` with “yes, the school is now auditable.”

It strengthens the reframe:

> **adversarial provenance can stop an event from disappearing completely even when official narration remains compromised.**

# 18. Protection, ownership, and the ethics of intervention

V04 contains several forms of protection that should not be morally collapsed.

## 18.1 Ibuki — concealed generosity without future claim

Ayanokōji’s water trade is asymmetrical in knowledge but low in ownership. He conceals his sacrifice so Ibuki can accept aid while retaining dignity and without acquiring a debt he intends to collect.

This is ethically healthier than protection that creates dependency.

## 18.2 Nanase — protection through instrumentalized attachment

Ayanokōji persuades Nanase to avoid reckless self-sacrifice partly by making her imagine harm to him.

The end is protective.

The method uses her emotional orientation as leverage.

This is more ethically mixed.

## 18.3 Horikita and Ibuki — protection through explicit reciprocal structure

Their linked groups create mutual insurance. Both know the mechanism, both benefit, and both remain capable of disagreement.

This is the strongest answerable protection model in the volume.

## 18.4 Sakayanagi — protection through acknowledged political debt

Sakayanagi’s assistance is not disguised as altruism. She accepts a favor structure that both parties recognize.

This preserves agency better than invisible obligation, though the relational power between them remains unusual.

## 18.5 Ichinose — protection that risks one polity for another person

Ichinose’s warning protects Ayanokōji while exposing her classmates to the threat Tsukishiro attached to them.

The act is courageous and morally intelligible.

It also raises a fiduciary question: when may a leader or central figure place the group at risk for a person outside it?

V04 does not settle that question.

## 18.6 Ayanokōji and Nagumo — protection through coercive incapacity

Ayanokōji believes Nagumo is entering danger and incapacitates him when persuasion fails.

Even if the protective motive is accepted, the action abolishes Nagumo’s immediate right to decide his own risk.

The system-wide consequences make the ethical cost larger.

Protection and ownership are therefore separated most cleanly by asking:

- Was the danger understood?
- Could the protected person refuse?
- Did the protector create future leverage?
- Were third parties made to bear consequences?
- Could a less coercive route have worked?

V04 supplies different answers in each case rather than one rule.

# 19. Japanese conceptual vocabulary and register

The Japanese text supplies several terms whose local force should remain visible in later synthesis.

## 19.1 `実力` — sovereignty requires more than raw output

The island exam is saturated with visible performance, but V04 repeatedly separates physical or numerical strength from politically usable strength.

Hōsen can dominate a fight and lose the conflict.

Albert can be stronger than Ryūen and still judge Ryūen the better leader.

Nagumo can possess enormous network power and still be vulnerable to central-node failure.

Kōenji can have exceptional possessed ability that becomes class-useful only through contract.

`実力` therefore expands again: the important question is not merely what a person can do, but what kind of **order** the ability can sustain.

## 19.2 `使い捨て` — disposable-use grammar remains active in Ayanokōji

The V03 Nanase material carried the sharpest direct use of this vocabulary, and V04 does not erase it. His protective behavior can be genuine while his strategic narration continues to treat people in functional terms.

This is why moral development cannot be inferred simply from helping behavior.

## 19.3 `裏切り者` / traitor — White Room value is mission-relative

Tsukishiro’s treatment of Amasawa as a traitor is conceptually revealing.

Her ability remains intact. Her institutional classification changes because she refuses assigned function.

The label therefore encodes an ownership claim over **purpose**, not merely conduct.

## 19.4 `失敗` / failure — institutional failure is not human incapacity

Amasawa can be treated as a failed/disqualified White Room output while remaining extraordinarily capable by school standards.

The term should therefore always be source-attributed when used analytically.

“White Room failure” means failure against the institution’s objective function, not proof of low human ability.

## 19.5 Horikita’s language of knowing Ayanokōji

Her response to Amasawa is notable because she does not claim complete `理解` or privileged ownership of his hidden self. She frames knowledge as something still to be acquired through continued relationship.

The linguistic posture is epistemically modest:

> not “I know who he really is,” but “I intend to keep learning.”

That matters to `RELATIONSHIP_RECIPROCITY` because it leaves conceptual room for Ayanokōji to answer rather than treating observation as entitlement.

## 19.6 The repeated grammar of “nothing happened”

Tsukishiro’s final minimization is not a neutral factual sentence. It is an exercise of record power.

The statement attempts to transform a witnessed event into an institutionally non-event.

The gap between occurrence and narratable occurrence remains central to the project’s truth/proof thread.

# 21. Formal and literary strengths

## 21.1 The volume turns hidden plotting into a study of competing authorship

The greatest formal success of V04 is that it does not merely reveal more conspirators.

It layers plans so that each revelation changes the causal status of the one before it:

1. Amasawa was present at the Komiya/Kinoshita scene.
2. Presence does not prove attack.
3. Another footprint suggests another actor.
4. Tsubaki appears to organize the first-year coalition.
5. Sakayanagi appears to defeat that coalition.
6. Tsubaki reveals the apparent defeat was part of a deeper trap.
7. Hōsen uses the trap for his own personal objective.
8. Ryūen intercepts Hōsen.
9. Yagami and Utomiya reveal that Tsubaki herself was being counter-authored.
10. Tsukishiro’s adult plan sits above the student conflict.
11. Ayanokōji and autonomous allies counter-author Tsukishiro’s jurisdiction.

The effect is not arbitrary “twist stacking” when read carefully. Every layer asks the same question at a different scale:

> **Does observing a person’s plan mean that person controls the outcome?**

The answer is repeatedly no.

## 21.2 The island exam is a systems novel inside a school thriller

The exam’s scale allows the narrative to depict:

- centralization and resilience;
- patronage and formal institutions;
- network coordination;
- information asymmetry;
- externalities;
- coalition formation;
- bounded contracts;
- emergency procedure;
- and the difference between tactical and political victory.

This is why V04’s most important scenes should not be reduced to power-scaling contests.

Hōsen versus Ryūen is about the level on which victory is defined.

Nagumo versus Kōenji is about centralized network power versus radically autonomous capacity.

Ayanokōji versus Tsukishiro is about changing jurisdiction and witnesses.

The physical action carries political structure.

## 21.3 The book repeatedly corrects the reader’s causal inference

Several local interpretations are deliberately made plausible and then qualified:

- Amasawa’s presence looks like attack evidence, then a second footprint appears.
- Amasawa looks exhausted by Horikita/Ibuki, then prior injury changes the inference.
- Tsubaki’s five groups appear defeated, then the defeat is revealed as bait.
- Yagami looks like a helper, then his private line to Utomiya reveals counter-authorship.
- Nagumo’s second place looks like a clean ranking, then Ayanokōji identifies his own interference as causally relevant.
- Tsukishiro’s private control looks administratively absolute, then ordinary rescue procedure creates an audience he cannot fully exclude.

The form trains the reader in the exact epistemic caution the analytical method demands.

## 21.4 Ordinary and strategic scenes remain interleaved

The water exchange with Ibuki, Shiranami rescue, Hiyori’s health concern, camping with mixed-class students, hunger, food production, and small rival banter are not decorative interruptions.

They show what political systems are for.

Ayanokōji’s defense of school life would be hollow if the book contained only examinations and conspiracies. The ordinary scenes establish the human experiences that make continued membership valuable.

## 21.5 The White Room reveal expands rather than closes Amasawa

A weaker revelation structure would make Amasawa simpler once her origin is known.

V04 does the opposite.

Knowing she is White Room-raised creates new questions:

- why does she admire rather than resent Ayanokōji?
- what did fifth-generation curricular revision do?
- what does her biological selection imply for White Room ideology?
- what does “traitor” mean to an institution that claims to produce autonomous excellence?
- what becomes of someone declared failed after refusing the mission?

Identity confirmation therefore increases personhood rather than reducing her to a category.

# 22. Critical reservations and counterreadings

## 22.1 Do not treat “sovereignty” as a claim that every actor is politically equal

The volume contains enormous asymmetries.

Tsukishiro has adult administrative power. Ayanokōji has extraordinary training. Nagumo has a year-wide network. Tsubaki commands a smaller first-year operation. Ibuki has almost no institutional reach.

Calling them competing authors does not erase unequal capacity. It identifies a shared analytical function: each can shape some part of another person’s environment.

## 22.2 Do not turn Kōenji’s victory into a neutral ability ranking

The final score is real and his performance is exceptional.

The causal history includes third-year interference, Nagumo’s deliberate staging, Ayanokōji’s incapacitation of Nagumo, and different group structures.

The result should not be converted into a context-free claim that one character is definitively “stronger” in every relevant sense.

## 22.3 Do not turn Nagumo’s loss into proof that his system was weak

The opposite is closer to the evidence.

His system is powerful enough that disabling him changes the whole exam ecology. The weakness is **centralization**, not insignificance.

## 22.4 Do not moralize Ryūen’s growth into rehabilitation

Ryūen shows better delegation, more voluntary legitimacy, strategic restraint toward his own followers, and a richer theory of leadership.

He remains willing to use violence, deception, intimidation, and mercenary bargaining.

“Better leader” and “ethically safe leader” remain different propositions.

## 22.5 Do not interpret Ayanokōji’s rescue protocol as proof of transparent institutional justice

The protocol works because it creates additional observers and procedural pressure.

The final public story is still suppressed.

V04 demonstrates **counterweight**, not full accountability.

## 22.6 Do not overdiagnose Amasawa’s heredity/environment observations as scientific refutation

Her experience provides an internal critique of White Room ideology.

The novel does not supply experimental controls sufficient for literal scientific conclusions about nature and nurture.

The literary claim is stronger than the empirical one: the institution’s own subjects recognize persistent difference that its ideology wishes to subordinate.

## 22.7 Do not convert Ichinose’s confession into a total explanation of her warning

Love matters.

So do moral judgment, accumulated trust, and refusal to permit covert violence. Reducing the action to romance would erase political character; denying romantic motive would erase the scene itself.

## 22.8 Do not read Horikita’s desire to know Ayanokōji as proof of present intimacy symmetry

Her language is valuable because it acknowledges incompleteness.

At the end of V04 Ayanokōji still withholds the actual Tsukishiro confrontation from her. Their care has grown faster than their information symmetry.

## 22.9 Do not promote Yagami’s local covert behavior into a later identity conclusion

V04 proves that Yagami is secretly coordinating with Utomiya and wants Tsubaki’s plan to fail.

That is already significant.

The volume-local artifact must stop there unless another V04 passage independently proves more.

## 22.10 Do not call Tsukishiro “defeated” in the total sense

His immediate I2 extraction attempt fails and the afterword describes this phase of the confrontation as temporarily ending.

Ayanokōji himself doubts that Tsukishiro’s depths or purposes are fully explained. The White Room story remains open by explicit publication design.

## 28.1 The new V04 thread: counter-authorship

V04 reveals a cross-cutting property that should be carried forward even though it does not require a new permanent controlled thread ID yet:

> **counter-authorship = the capacity of another actor to revise, redirect, or invalidate an environment someone else designed.**

Examples at this boundary:

- Sakayanagi counter-authors Tsubaki’s first-year pursuit.
- Tsubaki counter-authors the apparent Sakayanagi victory by making it bait.
- Yagami/Utomiya counter-author Tsubaki from inside her coalition.
- Ryuen counter-authors Hosen’s physical contest by changing the institutional win condition.
- Koenji counter-authors Nagumo’s suppression by refusing its behavioral assumptions.
- Horikita/Ibuki counter-author Amasawa’s roadblock through explicit mutual structure.
- Kiryuuin counter-authors Tsukishiro simply by independently entering a scene meant to be isolated.
- Ayanokoji counter-authors Tsukishiro’s jurisdiction by routing the event into rescue procedure.

This concept may later deserve a specialist matrix under `ENVIRONMENTAL_AUTHORSHIP`, but V04 alone does not justify proliferating infrastructure prematurely.

# 30. Final synthesis

Year 2 Volume 4 begins with a child produced inside the White Room explaining why the White Room’s own theory of human production does not satisfy her.

It ends with Ayanokoji surviving the institution’s attempt to reclaim him because the school contains people and procedures that cannot be fully authored by one hand.

That symmetry is the volume’s deepest achievement.

Amasawa’s prologue gives the ideological proposition in miniature. The White Room wants to demonstrate that controlled environment can determine human excellence strongly enough to overcome heredity and ordinary social contingency. Yet Amasawa—the experimental subject—develops an interpretation the experiment did not need her to possess. She sees persistent difference. She wants freedom. She admires the student she was expected to treat as benchmark or obstacle. Her White Room identity can finally be proven, but proof of identity makes institutional determinism less convincing rather than more.

The rest of the novel scales that problem outward.

Every major political actor attempts to create an environment in which someone else’s behavior becomes predictable.

Tsubaki believes she can convert first-year preservation, bounty incentives, GPS pressure, and Hosen’s violence into a controlled corridor around Ayanokoji.

Sakayanagi believes she can convert alliance data and distributed groups into a protective perimeter.

Yagami covertly permits Tsubaki to believe she is authoring the operation while arranging for her plan to fail.

Nagumo believes the third year can become an extension of a single sovereign network.

The third years believe bodies, paths, and tasks can be organized to contain Koenji.

Hosen believes exceptional physical power can define victory.

Ryuen believes political consequence can redefine victory after the fight begins.

Tsukishiro believes administrative control can turn an official examination into a private extraction zone.

Ayanokoji believes procedure, witnesses, time, and other people’s independent incentives can make that zone impossible to preserve.

None of these beliefs is entirely wrong.

That is the point.

V04 is not a story in which foolish planners lose to the one genius who sees everything. It is a story in which **several intelligent authors possess partial jurisdiction over the same field**.

Tsubaki really is clever. Her plan really does penetrate the first defensive layer. Sakayanagi really is an elite coordinator. Nagumo really has constructed a formidable school-year polity. Hosen really is physically dangerous. Ryuen really can convert follower loyalty into political resilience. Tsukishiro and Shiba really can overpower ordinary institutional assumptions. Ayanokoji really can counter them.

The volume’s mature political question is therefore not:

> Who is strongest?

It is:

> **Whose structure continues functioning when another competent actor refuses its premise?**

That criterion immediately changes how several characters should be judged.

Nagumo’s polity is extraordinarily powerful and structurally fragile. It has scale but depends too heavily on its sovereign node. A single incapacitated person can silence a year-wide radio architecture. Kōenji’s radical autonomy is structurally opposite: difficult to coordinate, but equally difficult to decapitate because almost nothing he does requires another person to keep the system alive. Horikita’s emerging model seeks a middle position. Her cooperation with Ibuki is explicit, bounded, and reciprocal enough that neither party disappears into the other’s will.

Ryuen’s polity is also moving toward that middle position. The importance of Katsuragi is not that Ryuen has acquired another smart subordinate. Katsuragi can alter Ryuen’s conduct and carry examination responsibility while Ryuen is elsewhere. Albert’s loyalty matters because it is described as judgment about collective good rather than simple fear. A ruler whose followers can possess reasons is becoming different from a ruler who only possesses followers.

That development should not be romanticized.

Ryuen still uses violence and intimidation. The political improvement is **structural before it is moral**.

The same distinction applies to Ayanokoji.

V04 displays some of his most legitimate interventions so far. The Ibuki water exchange is asymmetrical but deliberately non-possessive. He creates no debt and conceals generosity in order to let her receive help without surrendering dignity. His request to Sakayanagi includes enough threat information to make her a genuine strategic participant rather than a blind instrument. His rescue protocol against Tsukishiro creates independent witnesses through an existing safety procedure rather than asking one private ally to fabricate a story.

But the volume also displays the old problem at greater scale.

He decides when Ichinose is allowed to know that Kei exists because he wants to preserve her ability to function through the exam. He disables Nagumo because he judges Nagumo’s chosen risk unacceptable and because Nagumo threatens his low-visibility life. The resulting radio silence changes the final ranking and may change which third-years are expelled.

This is environmental authorship entering systems territory.

Ayanokoji no longer needs to touch every person whose outcome he affects.

That makes the old Year 1 ethical question more urgent, not less.

A hidden educator can manipulate one student.

A hidden systems author can manipulate the **conditions under which many students act**, including people he never directly evaluated.

V04 is still too early to call this the mature four-class experiment of the historical first-pass synthesis. His strategic purpose remains primarily defensive: stay in school, prevent forced retrieval, preserve low visibility where possible, protect selected people, survive the examination.

Yet the capacity is now undeniable.

He can author environments at multiple scales.

The crucial unresolved issue is what purpose he will eventually assign that capacity.

Horikita offers the volume’s clearest ethical contrast.

Amasawa tells her, correctly, that she knows almost nothing about Ayanokoji’s hidden reality. Horikita does not respond by claiming secret intimacy. She responds by making knowledge a future relational project. She wants to keep learning who he is and become someone able to help him.

This is a profound difference from the White Room’s epistemology.

The White Room’s file says:

> we know the subject well enough to define what he is for.

Horikita’s position is closer to:

> I do not know enough yet; the relationship must continue if I am going to understand.

The distinction is not sentimental. It is political.

One model converts knowledge into jurisdiction.

The other allows incomplete knowledge to place a limit upon jurisdiction.

Her interaction with Ibuki makes that principle practical. Horikita diagnoses a limitation—Ibuki’s isolation—but says so openly, acknowledges Ibuki’s superior martial judgment, links their risks, and invites cooperation around a shared immediate objective. Ibuki can contest the interpretation. She can refuse parts of it. She can remain a rival.

That is developmental authorship without total authorship.

The I2 confrontation then places this alternative beside its opposite.

Tsukishiro’s authority is impressive because he can act simultaneously as administrator, examiner, record-controller, combatant, and agent of another institution. His strongest advantage is not physical skill. It is **jurisdictional compression**: the same political apparatus can define the environment, control observation, produce the explanation, and decide what becomes official.

Ayanokoji’s countermeasure works because it decomposes that compression.

Kiryuuin arrives for reasons of her own.

Sakayanagi helps because of a relationship and favor structure Tsukishiro does not control.

A rescue report creates procedural obligations.

Mashima and Chabashira arrive under institutional logic rather than White Room logic.

The beach can no longer remain a privately narrated space.

The victory is therefore not “Ayanokoji is stronger than Tsukishiro.”

It is:

> **Tsukishiro cannot remain sovereign once observation and procedure escape his monopoly.**

That is the first constructive answer Year 2 has offered to the project’s auditability problem.

It is still incomplete. The final institutional narration remains “nothing happened.” Truth can enter the scene without becoming a truthful public record. Counterweights can stop coercion without producing accountability.

But V04 has identified the mechanism through which resistance becomes possible:

> no interested authority should control the fact, the witness, the record, and the judgment at the same time.

This principle links the political and developmental sides of the volume.

A person is safer when no single actor owns the entire meaning of that person.

A system is safer when no single actor owns the entire meaning of an event.

That is why Ayanokoji’s defense of ordinary school plurality matters so much.

He has met people the White Room could never produce—not because the White Room is technically incapable of teaching strength, intelligence, or discipline, but because **plural environments generate people whose purposes are not fully contained by the curriculum that shaped them**.

Kiryuuin helps because she decides to.

Ichinose warns him even though class logic says he is an enemy.

Ibuki protects Horikita while continuing to call her a rival.

Sakayanagi helps a competitor through an acknowledged future debt.

Ryuen redefines a fight around institutional survival.

Koenji fulfills a collective bargain precisely because the bargain preserves his independence.

Amasawa rejects the mission attached to her own White Room excellence.

These are not examples of social harmony.

They are examples of **counter-authorship**.

People remain capable of producing answers the surrounding sovereign did not fully assign.

The White Room’s deepest failure is therefore not that it cannot produce extraordinary ability.

V04 provides no basis for that comforting claim.

Its failure is that extraordinary ability does not grant it final ownership over the purpose of the person it produced.

Amasawa can become a traitor.

Ayanokoji can want school.

A child can read the experiment back.

The island exam closes with the same possibility operating at every scale:

> **A designed environment is never identical to a designed human outcome as long as another actor can still answer back.**

That is the strongest local meaning of sovereignty in Year 2 Volume 4.
