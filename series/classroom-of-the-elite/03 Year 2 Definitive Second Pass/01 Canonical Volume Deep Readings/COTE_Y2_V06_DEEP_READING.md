# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ ２年生編6』
## Year 2 Volume 6 — Second-Pass Deep Reading
### Distributed agency, succession, and the developer who wants to be defeated

> **Local analytical boundary:** this document uses the complete original Japanese Year 2 Volume 6, the frozen Year 1 V2 corpus, and canonical Y2V01–Y2V05 as inherited state. It deliberately does **not** use Y2V07 or later material, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptations, wikis, or remembered later outcomes to settle local claims. The historical full-Year-2 first pass is consulted only as an explicit revision target. Where V06 leaves a caller, motive, consequence, or future plan unresolved, this document leaves it unresolved.

---

# 1. Scope, provenance, and inherited analytical state

`Y2V06` is the seventh canonical sequential artifact in the definitive Year 2 second pass. The Japanese EPUB was read completely in spine order through the afterword before targeted retrieval. The source exactly matches the Phase-0 lock: **16,440,065 bytes**, SHA-256 `b4daa5f1998a3d1e05a783371233b5bd6c9a3d7b6624971a227653b2f0ae436b`. The deterministic prose layer contains **4,281 paragraph records** in the namespace `COTE:Y2V06:spineXX:paraYYYY`. Narrative illustrations are tracked separately from prose; small gaiji glyph images are excluded from the visual evidence layer.

The inherited Year-2 conceptual progression is:

> **V01 — legibility → V02 — jurisdiction → V03 — attribution → V04 — sovereignty / counter-authorship → V04.5 — embeddedness → V05 — responsibility.**

V05 ended with the class materially strengthened and morally fractured. Horikita had consequentially disagreed with Ayanokōji by preserving Kushida. Ayanokōji then converted that independent judgment into a new sacrificial selection procedure and drove Airi’s expulsion. The class gained 100 points, but Haruka lost the friend around whom much of her ordinary school life had become organized; the Ayanokōji Group learned that friendship did not exempt a member from Ayanokōji’s disposability calculus; Hirata learned to remain functional through a decision he regarded as painful; and Chabashira began separating her students’ present Class-A purpose from her own old failure.

V05 therefore forced a refinement of environmental authorship:

> **cultivation → succession → culling.**

V06 does not erase the culling branch. It asks what happens next.

The immediate temptation is to call this merely a “fallout” or “recovery” volume. That is true at the plot level but analytically incomplete. V06 repeatedly refuses a model in which one superior leader repairs every wound. Instead, the class begins functioning through distinct people acting from motives that do not belong to one central author:

- Horikita accepts that leadership requires other people and that error is not disqualification;
- Kushida returns on explicitly self-interested terms rather than as a converted friend;
- Hirata chooses to trust Ayanokōji while doing mediation work Ayanokōji refuses to own;
- Kei repairs social relations and teaches Ayanokōji a non-utilitarian answer he did not generate himself;
- Sudō diagnoses problems, seeks advice, and later self-regulates under provocation;
- Onodera becomes Sudō’s immediate teacher in the moment that matters;
- Mii-chan decides to return after receiving a trigger rather than being dragged back;
- Ryūen and Katsuragi enter a bounded contract with Horikita rather than absorbing her under their polity;
- Haruka refuses moral reconciliation even while ceasing indiscriminate sabotage;
- Akito follows Haruka because particular loyalty matters more to him than frictionless class reintegration.

Ayanokōji does not simply tolerate this distribution. He begins deliberately **withdrawing himself from first-line solution** so that other people must become capable of solving the class’s problems. Then, near the end of the volume, he makes the direction explicit: he thinks of himself as Horikita’s `補助輪`—training wheels—intends eventually to leave her class, and wants the class he has helped strengthen to confront him as an enemy. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0321–0337`.

The governing question is therefore:

> **Can a developer create a system that no longer needs him without claiming ownership of the independence that results?**

That question makes **distributed agency** the strongest V06 concept.

# 2. Executive thesis — responsibility becomes distributed agency

Year 2 Volume 6 is about **living after a collective sacrifice by redistributing the capacity to act**.

The volume begins by refusing moral closure. Ayanokōji explicitly judges Horikita’s decision to preserve Kushida as irrational, inconsistent with his own optimum, and—by his model—a mistake. He still supported it because he wanted to see what would happen if Horikita followed a path he himself would never choose. `COTE:Y2V06:spine12:para0035–0063`.

This is the first major V06 contradiction.

On one side, Ayanokōji is learning to let another person’s judgment remain consequential even when it frustrates his own model. That is real development. The pre-ANHS Ayanokōji, he says, would not have chosen to entrust himself to an “irrational failure.” `spine12:para0038–0039`.

On the other side, he justifies the tolerance partly as observation. He wants to see Horikita’s `成長`, `方向性`, and `結果`; he wants the chemical reaction produced by preserving Kushida. `spine12:para0057–0062`. Another person’s independence is therefore both **respected** and **converted into experimental value**.

V06 spends the rest of the volume testing whether independence can escape that frame.

Horikita and Kushida provide the strongest affirmative case. Horikita eventually stops trying to make Kushida cooperate “for the class” or “for Horikita.” She tells her to use her rare capacity for **herself**. Kushida answers with a contract that is almost aggressively anti-redemptive:

> `私は私のためだけに１年半戦ってクラスに貢献する。`

She will fight and contribute for the remaining year and a half only for herself—not for Horikita or her classmates. Horikita accepts this without complaint because she wants results rather than moral affection. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para0470–0525`.

Their left-handed handshake makes the political meaning explicit. Both know that a left-hand handshake signifies hostility. The compact therefore does not pretend conflict has vanished. `spine14:para0560–0585`.

This resolves an important Year-1 problem prospectively:

> **hatred can coexist with legitimate cooperation when the terms do not require false affection, and an imposed preservation project can become self-authored at the point where the preserved person chooses her own reason to continue.**

The distinction is essential. V05’s preservation of Kushida was not Kushida-authored. She wanted expulsion rather than Horikita’s imposed rescue. V06 does not rewrite that. What changes is the **ongoing project**: Kushida now chooses to remain, contribute, preserve secrets conditionally, and pursue Class A because those actions serve her own interests. `spine14:para1018–1055`.

Distributed agency also becomes the operational architecture of Horikita’s class. Hirata and Kei initially continue treating Ayanokōji as the hidden help desk for difficult social problems. Ayanokōji deliberately resists that pattern. His principle is that the class must learn both `頼ること` and `頼られること`—to rely on others and to become someone others can rely upon. Horikita needs experience succeeding and failing as the visible leader; Hirata, Kei, Sudō, and others must become problem-solvers rather than mere conduits to Ayanokōji. `COTE:Y2V06:spine13:para0001–0033`; `para0180–0210`.

Sudō then becomes the cleanest proof that the succession model can work. Faced with Ike and Shinohara’s fracture, he diagnoses the problem, asks for limited advice, and reaches the useful next step himself. Ayanokōji refuses credit:

> `オレは何もしてない。おまえが自分で考えて自分で答えを出しただけだ`

and concludes:

> `そうやってクラスは回っていく`.

`spine13:para0400–0508`.

The sports festival converts this distributed model into a live institutional test. Ayanokōji is physically absent. Horikita has independently authored an alliance with Ryūen; Hirata, Kei, and Kushida perform social functions; Sudō and Onodera become the dominant athletic pair; Kushida recruits a cross-class volleyball team through relationships no other classmate can reproduce; and the class finishes first. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0001–0295`; `spine16:para0380–0555`; `spine17:para0285–0292`.

The test is not pure. Ayanokōji secretly influences the environment by arranging his own absence, telling Chairman Sakayanagi enough to make Arisu likely to stay away, and using that absence as part of his experiment. The victory therefore does **not** prove Horikita’s polity has become independent of him. It does prove that agency is no longer concentrated in his direct commands.

The Ryūen alliance is especially important because Horikita owns it. When Ryūen asks who conceived the partnership, Horikita truthfully says she did. Ryūen accepts partly because the proposal is hers rather than Ayanokōji’s. Katsuragi notices her change. The agreement is then put into a written teacher-observed contract rather than resting on sentimental trust. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0150–0210`; `para0240–0310`.

This is visible political authorship.

Sudō’s sports-festival climax gives distributed development an even cleaner form. Hōsen repeatedly tries to provoke him. Onodera teaches Sudō a simple delay technique: the peak of anger is brief; pause, breathe, count. Sudō applies it under live pressure, wins the match, refuses Hōsen’s post-match fight, receives a punch without escalating, and recognizes Hōsen as an ugly mirror of his own former conduct. `COTE:Y2V06:spine16:para0640–0756`.

The important author is no longer Ayanokōji or Horikita. **Onodera becomes Sudō’s immediate teacher.** The student who was once developed by stronger actors can now learn peer-to-peer and carry his own restraint into a high-stakes situation. This is one of the strongest `GENERATIVE_ABILITY` examples in the project so far.

Yet the volume repeatedly preserves the moral residue V05 created.

Haruka returns to school but does not forgive. She tells Horikita directly that Ayanokōji used Airi’s feelings to corner her in a way ordinary people could not. Horikita refuses a false apology and says that, with her current knowledge, she would still preserve Kushida and expel Airi; her mistake was making an irresponsible promise before she knew which path she would take. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para1190–1285`.

At the very end, Haruka says she will not indefinitely sabotage the whole class, but asks Akito to help with a single act of revenge. She then withdraws the invitation verbally while continuing forward; Akito silently follows. `COTE:Y2V06:spine18:para0005–0029`.

This is not reconciliation. It is **bounded dissent with unresolved grievance**.

Ayanokōji himself makes the ethical danger sharper. He explicitly tells Haruka that Airi’s removal can function as a warning to weak students: the fear of becoming next may motivate greater contribution. He recognizes the resemblance to Ryūen’s older fear politics. The culling branch of environmental authorship has therefore not disappeared; it has become a disciplinary signal. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para1260–1311`.

Kei supplies the volume’s strongest counter-curriculum. In an ordinary domestic conversation, she asks whether Ayanokōji would have expelled her if she had low OAA, low social status, and no friends. He initially responds with increasingly elaborate counterfactual logic. Kei corrects him: the answer she wants is simply that he would protect her anyway. He concludes that she has taught him that in such a relationship **reason is unnecessary**—`そこに理屈なんかは不要`. `COTE:Y2V06:spine13:para0147–0168`.

The moment matters because it teaches an answer selection logic cannot derive.

But V06 immediately refuses to treat intimacy as reciprocity achieved. During later shopping, Kei says she has no secrets left and asks him not to hide things. Ayanokōji mentally inventories his family, the White Room, his school plan, his friendships, and his romantic feelings and concludes that he has effectively told Kei **none of the truth**. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0395–0415`.

Real intimacy and radical disclosure asymmetry remain simultaneous.

The most consequential transformation occurs in Ayanokōji’s self-authored project. In conversation with Sakayanagi, he finally states why he has begun helping the class seriously. Sakayanagi wants to defeat him in order to answer her own question about genius. He wants, in his own way, to prove that White Room education is **not perfect**. When she asks whether he intends to build the strongest class himself, he nods yes. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0138–0165`.

His theme for the sports festival is `静観`: observe how far the others can fight without him.

Then the closing Horikita conversation makes succession literal. She tells him he has become indispensable. He privately compares himself to training wheels. Training wheels are initially necessary; eventually they must come off. He intends to leave Horikita’s class after watching a little more growth. He will become its enemy. He believes he can defeat it—but wants that belief to be wrong. `spine17:para0318–0337`.

The V06 paradox can therefore be stated precisely:

> **Ayanokōji is trying to create people and a class capable of escaping his necessity. That is a genuine movement toward succession. But he still treats their independence as part of a personally authored experiment whose final desired output is his own unexpected defeat.**

The developer now wants the learner to surpass the curriculum.

The unresolved ethical question is whether he can accept that surpassing at the point where it ceases to belong to his design.

The compact V06 thesis is:

> **Responsibility becomes distributed agency. A class cannot recover from V05 by pretending its wound never occurred or by allowing one superior actor to repair everyone. It begins to recover when distinct people act from motives the central author does not own. Ayanokōji deliberately encourages that distribution because he wants a class that can survive his absence and ultimately defeat him; V06 therefore turns succession into his clearest self-chosen educational project while simultaneously exposing the danger that he may claim authorship over independence itself.**

# 3. Position in the Year 2 architecture — from responsibility to distributed agency

The Year-2 sequence now reads:

> **legibility → jurisdiction → attribution → sovereignty → embeddedness → responsibility → distributed agency**

These are not replacements for one another. Each new concept exposes a problem created by the previous one.

- **Legibility** made capacity politically actionable.
- **Jurisdiction** asked who may bind another person’s future.
- **Attribution** asked who actually authored an outcome under opacity.
- **Sovereignty** tested competing command structures.
- **Embeddedness** showed that meaningful relationships create both human value and vulnerability.
- **Responsibility** forced embedded people to participate in collective injury.
- **Distributed agency** asks whether a damaged polity can continue without solving every contradiction through a single sovereign actor.

The formal architecture of V06 supports this transition. The volume begins with Akito’s private monologue, moves through multiple repair attempts, shifts heavily into Horikita’s perspective during the sports festival, returns to Ayanokōji only for the hidden architectural reveal, and ends with Haruka and Akito outside the repaired public narrative. No single perspective owns the whole recovery.

That polycentric structure is the point.

# 4. Narrative architecture — eight movements from wound to succession

## 4.1 `三宅明人の独白` — particular loyalty refuses class abstraction

Akito’s opening monologue establishes the moral residue before any institutional strategy appears. He considers himself ordinary and historically passive, but realizes that school and friendship have changed what he is capable of choosing. If Haruka leaves, he cannot simply watch her go because his emotions will not permit it. His loyalty is neither class universalism nor strategic optimization. It is particular attachment. `COTE:Y2V06:spine11:para0001–0045`.

The prologue therefore prevents the volume from making “class recovery” the only relevant good. A polity may recover while particular people remain unwilling to surrender a wound.

## 4.2 `勝利の代償` — the class gains points and loses innocence

Ayanokōji begins by analyzing Horikita’s V05 choice as a mistake by his own model, then chooses to watch its consequences. The OAA roster has already removed Airi’s name `まるで最初からそんな生徒は存在しなかったかのように`—as though she had never existed. Institutional data processes the membership change faster than relationships can. `COTE:Y2V06:spine12:para0035–0065`.

The sports-festival rules then introduce a different allocation problem: individual performance generates personal reward while class outcomes socialize aggregate performance. The limited class-transfer ticket gives exceptional individuals a temporary exit option without making mobility costless. `spine12:para0213–0295`.

The chapter also preserves Airi’s own post-decision agency. The school discovers a small private-point expenditure made after her expulsion was fixed but before the formal process completed. The exact use is not disclosed to Ayanokōji or the reader in V06; he is told only that he is a relevant party and decides to cover the amount. Chabashira asks whether he orchestrated it. He says no: Airi reached the choice herself. `spine12:para0580–0608`.

This is important precisely because the detail remains withheld. The source supports **self-authored action after selection**, not a later-V06 claim about what Airi’s outside life becomes.

## 4.3 `避けては通れない道` — the hidden solver tries to become backup

Hirata and Kei still bring difficult class problems to Ayanokōji because that behavior has been trained by prior success. He recognizes that a dependency structure he helped create cannot simply be wished away. The next developmental task is to redirect responsibility outward: Horikita must be relied upon, while Hirata, Kei, Sudō, and others must learn to solve problems without converting every uncertainty into a request for Ayanokōji. `COTE:Y2V06:spine13:para0001–0033`; `para0180–0210`.

The domestic Kei sequence runs in parallel. Ordinary cooking, touch, teasing, and conversation form a space where relational competence is learned through interaction. Kei then produces the most concise challenge to Ayanokōji’s selection grammar: she wants protection to be stated without a utility proof. `spine13:para0040–0168`.

Hirata later chooses trust while acknowledging uncertainty, and Sudō begins making useful leadership judgments on his own. The chapter’s title applies not only to V05 fallout but to succession itself: the path the class cannot avoid is learning to act without the hidden solver.

## 4.4 `それでもやるしか` — integration without reconciliation

Horikita faces the consequences of her independent decision from inside her own perspective. Chabashira’s new professional posture matters immediately: she tells Horikita not to fear error and says she does not want to be an adult incapable of permitting children to fail. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para0055–0095`.

Horikita then reaches the Kushida breakthrough by abandoning the demand for moral conversion. The left-handed hostile handshake is the visual and linguistic emblem of the new arrangement. `spine14:para0430–0618`.

Mii-chan provides a different repair model. She has withdrawn from school after the Kushida exposure and Airi’s expulsion. Anonymous food bags appear at her door, but V06 does **not** establish who leaves them. She eventually goes to Ayanokōji herself. He gives her a deliberately sharp truth: classmates care about her, but continued absence also harms the class and especially Hirata, who will blame himself. He judges that she is already trying to stand and can tolerate the pressure. She returns. `spine14:para0625–0855`.

The intervention supplies a trigger rather than replacing the target’s decision.

The chapter later returns to Haruka. Horikita refuses an apology that would imply she now believes the Kushida decision was wrong. Haruka refuses the idea that successful performance can make Airi’s removal acceptable. Both positions remain live. `spine14:para1160–1311`.

## 4.5 `協定` — independent political authorship becomes contractual

Horikita independently proposes cooperation with Ryūen and Katsuragi to defeat Sakayanagi’s class. Ryūen tests whether the proposal is really hers and whether Ayanokōji is the hidden author. Horikita refuses to remove Ayanokōji from the negotiation merely because Ryūen demands it; she treats having him present as one available negotiating asset. Yet when asked who devised the plan, she truthfully claims it herself. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0001–0210`.

The resulting agreement is written and teacher-observed. Horikita accepts the risk of trusting a person she does not regard as trustworthy because aligned incentives and enforceable terms can support cooperation without moral intimacy. `spine15:para0180–0295`.

The same chapter makes Kei’s disclosure asymmetry explicit and shows Ryūen’s leadership changing. Ayanokōji describes Ryūen as learning to control both sides of the coin: formerly he used “evil” as his weapon; now he is learning how to deploy “good” and legitimate procedure as well. `spine15:para0490–0605`.

That is not redemption. It is political evolution.

## 4.6 `２度目の体育祭` — distributed competence becomes visible

Horikita becomes the dominant perspective while Ayanokōji is absent. The class runs through planning, event selection, cooperation, and rapid reassessment without a live hidden commander. `COTE:Y2V06:spine16:para0001–0756`.

Kushida’s contribution becomes empirically visible: she can recruit and coordinate students across ordinary class boundaries in ways Horikita and Ibuki cannot. Sudō and Onodera become the strongest performance pair. Horikita and Ryūen’s alliance holds. The class takes first place. `spine16:para0380–0555`; `spine17:para0285–0292`.

Sudō’s restraint against Hōsen is the chapter’s cleanest developmental proof because the immediate causal chain is peer-based. Onodera teaches the technique; Sudō uses it; Sudō owns the decision not to fight. `spine16:para0640–0756`.

## 4.7 `客人` — the hidden curriculum becomes an explicit school experiment

Ayanokōji’s absence is not passivity. He has arranged security because of possible White Room-linked visitors and has intentionally created conditions in which Sakayanagi is likely to stay away from the festival. Sakayanagi knowingly accepts the possible 150-point class loss to visit him. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0050–0080`; `para0138–0155`.

Their conversation contains the volume’s central project reveal: Ayanokōji is trying to prove White Room education imperfect by building the strongest class. The festival theme is `静観`—observe what everyone can do without him. `spine17:para0138–0166`.

Immediately afterward, however, he uses Sakayanagi’s knowledge of that plan to justify manufacturing private counter-leverage. The scene is deliberately ambiguous about the exact off-page photographs. The text establishes coercive physical positioning, a camera, an inability to escape, a subsequent “shared secret,” approximately ten seconds in his bed, and selection/deletion of photographs. It does **not** establish sex, nudity, or any further act, and this analysis does not invent one. `spine17:para0169–0235`.

The juxtaposition is ethically central: the same person who wants other people to become independent will use intimate counter-leverage when a peer gets too close to his undisclosed design.

An unknown same-age male then warns Ayanokōji not to believe that removing Tsukishiro and the White Room student will restore peace. The caller identifies himself as neither enemy nor ally. Sakayanagi experiences a vague sense of very old familiarity, perhaps five to ten years. The man’s identity remains unresolved in V06. `spine17:para0240–0283`.

## 4.8 `秋の訪れ` — recovery remains incomplete by design

The volume closes outside the victorious class narrative. Haruka is attending school and ceases the fantasy of destroying everyone indefinitely, but she does not forgive Ayanokōji, Horikita, or the classmates who voted Airi out. She asks Akito about one act of revenge, withdraws the request verbally, and walks on. Akito silently follows. `COTE:Y2V06:spine18:para0001–0029`.

The moral residue survives the institutional recovery.

# 5. Horikita Suzune — from independent judgment to distributed leadership

## 5.1 V06 does not retroactively declare her V05 choice correct

The most important methodological restraint is simple: V06 gives evidence that Kushida is becoming useful, but it does not convert the V05 decision into a costless or universally legitimate choice.

Ayanokōji begins by calling the decision irrational and wrong by his model. Haruka remains harmed. The Ayanokōji Group remains fractured. Horikita herself later says that V05 taught her through **both success and failure**. `COTE:Y2V06:spine12:para0035–0063`; `spine17:para0299–0318`.

The correct transition is therefore:

> **Horikita’s independent judgment is validated as genuinely hers; its outcome becomes increasingly defensible instrumentally; its distributive and relational costs remain unsettled.**

## 5.2 Chabashira gives Horikita permission to be a developing leader rather than an already-perfect sovereign

Chabashira’s line that she does not want to become an adult unable to recognize and forgive children’s mistakes is one of V06’s quiet institutional corrections. Horikita does not accept the premise that her Kushida decision is already a failure, but she receives something more important: permission to lead without treating every error as disqualification. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para0065–0095`.

The teacher has moved from demanding results for her own old wound toward supporting another person’s developmental responsibility.

## 5.3 Horikita learns that preserving Kushida requires relinquishing ownership of Kushida’s motive

The V05 version of the project was still Horikita-centric: *I will preserve you because I believe you are valuable.* V06 improves the legitimacy of the ongoing project by shifting its motive to Kushida.

Horikita’s crucial insight is that Kushida does not need to become cooperative **for Horikita**. She can pursue Class A for herself. She can preserve her own social value because doing so helps her. She can remain hostile. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para0470–0525`.

That is a major advance in the project’s freedom framework. Legitimate development does not require that the learner adopt the developer’s emotional story.

## 5.4 She accepts open hostility as politically usable

The left-handed handshake refuses the language of reconciliation. Kushida says she hates Horikita; Horikita accepts the terms. They can pinch each other’s cheeks, insult each other, and still produce a functioning compact. `spine14:para0560–0620`.

V06 therefore distinguishes:

- forgiveness;
- friendship;
- trust;
- incentive compatibility;
- and legitimate cooperation.

They are not synonyms.

## 5.5 Horikita’s anti-gag-order decision is a constitutional improvement

After Kushida’s public exposure, Horikita does not attempt to enforce universal silence about her past through coercive promises. She recognizes that classmates know the truth and must decide whether circulating it serves their interests. A forced promise not to speak would not create genuine legitimacy. `COTE:Y2V06:spine12:para0320–0360`.

This is consistent with the wider Year-2 distinction between **truth, possession of truth, and legitimate authority over disclosure**.

## 5.6 The Ryūen alliance is her first major externally recognized independent political authorship

The sports-festival alliance matters more than its tactical cleverness. Ryūen explicitly wants to know whether Ayanokōji is the author. Horikita says no: it is her proposal. Ryūen’s acceptance is partly recognition of that independence. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0150–0210`.

Her leadership has therefore crossed an important threshold:

> **another class leader can now treat Horikita as the political author of a consequential inter-class arrangement rather than merely Ayanokōji’s visible proxy.**

`Y1H-012` still does not close, because Ayanokōji continues to alter the surrounding field invisibly. But the burden of proof has shifted substantially.

## 5.7 Her closing self-description is plural rather than sovereign

After the festival, Horikita says she has learned from V05, including from her own reckless promise. She also explicitly refuses the fantasy that she must become Ryūen, Sakayanagi, or Ichinose. She will move forward as herself, supported by Hirata, Kei, Sudō, Onodera, Kushida, Kōenji, and others. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0299–0320`.

This is a constitutional transformation from **leader as person who contains the class** to **leader as node within distributed competence**.

# 6. Kushida Kikyō — from exposed contradiction to self-authored adversarial cooperation

## 6.1 Exposure destroys the old equilibrium but not her underlying capacities

V05 ended the secrecy regime in which Kushida’s public social labor and private hatred could coexist only by eliminating witnesses. V06 does not restore that regime. The class now knows enough that the old universal-good-girl identity cannot function internally in the same way.

What survives is capacity:

- social memory;
- cross-class access;
- conversational agility;
- trust production;
- athletic and academic competence;
- and the ability to recruit people who would not respond similarly to Horikita or Ibuki.

The sports-festival volleyball sequence provides direct functional proof. `COTE:Y2V06:spine16:para0390–0525`.

## 6.2 The integration succeeds because Horikita stops demanding moral purification

Horikita does not require Kushida to love the class, forgive her, or become a nicer person. She asks her to preserve and deploy capacities because Kushida herself benefits from Class-A success.

Kushida’s answer is equally clear: she fights `私のためだけ`—only for herself. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para0470–0525`.

The arrangement therefore rests on **aligned self-interest**, not on a claim that the hidden self was fake or that the public self was wholly false.

## 6.3 Public and private Kushida begin to coexist in one social field

Kushida can now display irritation and hostility around classmates who know her history while continuing to use polished sociability externally. She tells the class that she will not expose secrets unless someone attacks her first; this promise is explicitly for her own value and Class-A future. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para1015–1055`.

This is the first strong local answer to `Y1H-017`:

> **the two selves can coexist once coexistence no longer depends on nobody knowing they are both real.**

The result is not psychological integration in a clinical sense. It is political and social coexistence.

## 6.4 Hatred becomes a legitimate boundary rather than an automatic war condition

The left-handed handshake is unusually precise symbolism. The relationship remains adversarial, but hostility itself is no longer enough to invalidate cooperation.

This gives `Y1H-018` a strong local answer: **yes, hatred can coexist with legitimate cooperation when both parties know the hostility and the arrangement leaves motives transparent rather than demanding counterfeit trust.**

## 6.5 The project becomes Kushida-authored only after preservation

The distinction between two stages must remain explicit:

1. **Preservation:** V05 Horikita imposes continued membership against Kushida’s expressed wish.
2. **Integration:** V06 Kushida chooses her own reason to remain and contribute.

This is why `Y1H-019` can now be answered without retroactively sanitizing V05. The ongoing project becomes Kushida-authored; the original preservation decision was not.

# 7. Ayanokōji Kiyotaka — the author who deliberately builds his own dispensability

## 7.1 Ordinary-life layer — Kei teaches an answer without a utility proof

The domestic sequences matter because they produce knowledge that is not reducible to strategic calculation. Ayanokōji cooks; Kei creates conversational rhythm; physical affection becomes ordinary. `COTE:Y2V06:spine13:para0040–0112`.

When Kei asks the low-status counterfactual, Ayanokōji keeps solving the wrong problem. She wants not a model of why present Kei is valuable but a statement of unconditional relational priority. His conclusion—`そこに理屈なんかは不要`—is one of the clearest examples yet of ordinary life functioning as counter-curriculum. `spine13:para0147–0168`.

## 7.2 Relational layer — genuine intimacy coexists with severe disclosure insufficiency

The same relationship remains radically asymmetric in information. Kei tells him she has disclosed everything and asks the same of him. His internal list—family, White Room, school plan, social and romantic truths—leads to the admission that he has told her almost none of what matters. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0395–0415`.

V06 therefore strengthens both halves of the Kei thesis:

> **real intimacy is growing; informed reciprocity is not.**

## 7.3 Strategic layer — he continues to manufacture hidden conditions

His “absence” from the sports festival is itself authored. He uses Chairman Sakayanagi’s protection, anticipates Arisu’s response, and treats her resulting absence as useful environmental change. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0050–0080`; `para0151–0166`.

He is not simply observing an untouched field.

## 7.4 Environmental-author layer — delegation is now an explicit anti-dependency program

Ayanokōji deliberately stops being first-line solver. His reasoning is developmental: Horikita must experience success and failure, while other class members must learn to rely on one another. `COTE:Y2V06:spine13:para0001–0033`; `para0180–0210`.

This is stronger than earlier hidden cultivation because the intended endpoint is **his own dispensability**.

## 7.5 Environmental-author layer — the White Room counter-experiment becomes self-chosen purpose

Sakayanagi asks directly why he now cares about building the class. Ayanokōji answers directly: he wants to prove White Room education imperfect by his own method, and confirms that he intends to build the strongest class. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0138–0165`.

This is a major `AYANOKOJI_FREEDOM` and `ANHS_EXPERIMENT` development. The project is not assigned by Atsuomi, Tsukishiro, Chabashira, Horikita, or the school. It is something Ayanokōji chooses because he wants an answer to his own formation.

The motive is nevertheless relationally inherited from the White Room. Self-authorship is real without being ex nihilo.

## 7.6 Separation is no longer hypothetical

The closing monologue is the clearest succession statement so far. Horikita calls him indispensable; he privately calls himself training wheels; he plans to leave; he expects to become her enemy; he wants to be defeated even though his own model says he will win. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0318–0337`.

This gives `Y1H-002` a strong local answer:

> **Ayanokōji can accept—and actively desire—development that leads away from his support and toward opposition to him.**

The remaining problem is no longer whether he wants independence. It is whether he can stop treating the desired independent answer as an output of his own experiment.

## 7.7 The culling branch remains active

V06 never permits succession to become a moral absolution. In the Haruka confrontation, Ayanokōji openly recognizes the disciplinary use of Airi’s expulsion. Lower-performing students may improve because they now know membership is conditional. The logic resembles Ryūen’s fear governance. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para1260–1311`.

He is therefore simultaneously:

- building independent agents;
- producing fear through selective exclusion;
- and studying both effects.

## 7.8 The Sakayanagi counter-leverage scene is the volume’s sharpest ownership contradiction

The coercive “special lesson” is analytically important because it cannot be collapsed into romance, harmless teasing, or proven sexual misconduct. The text gives enough evidence for a narrower and more serious claim: Ayanokōji uses physical asymmetry, an isolated room, a camera, and a manufactured shared secret to prevent Sakayanagi from weaponizing knowledge of his plan. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0169–0235`.

The important contradiction is:

> **he wants a future in which capable others can surprise and defeat him, but he still reserves the right to manufacture counter-leverage against a peer who acquires too much knowledge of the experiment.**

Freedom for the learner remains unevenly distributed.

# 8. Haruka, Akito, Keisei, and the Ayanokōji Group — moral disclosure destroys the old low-pressure space

## 8.1 Haruka’s anger is not a misunderstanding

Haruka understands why the class chose someone. Her objection is not ignorance of the rule. It is that the class had promised the betrayer would be removed and then transferred the sacrifice to Airi. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para1190–1285`.

She also identifies Ayanokōji’s most disturbing role accurately: he used Airi’s feelings and Airi’s trust to make the final veto collapse.

## 8.2 Horikita refuses false reconciliation

Horikita does not say what Haruka wants to hear. She says that, given the same knowledge, she would preserve Kushida again. What she would change is the careless promise that created an expectation she could not later honor. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para1230–1280`; `spine17:para0299–0318`.

This is responsible accountability precisely because it does not counterfeit remorse for the underlying policy.

## 8.3 Keisei converts fear into contribution

Keisei remains damaged by the event but also recognizes that low present contribution can become survival risk. His response is not cheerful acceptance; it is an increase in effort under newly credible membership insecurity. V06 therefore provides an important caution:

> **behavioral improvement after culling may be development, fear-conditioned compliance, or both.**

The outcome cannot be classified as humane merely because contribution rises.

## 8.4 Akito chooses particular loyalty

Akito’s willingness to follow Haruka is not institutionally optimal. It is morally revealing because it demonstrates that friendship can create obligations the class cannot fully absorb into performance logic. `COTE:Y2V06:spine11:para0001–0045`; `spine18:para0001–0029`.

## 8.5 The group is not restored

V06 does not recreate the Ayanokōji Group as the old ordinary friendship space. Haruka remains distant from Keisei; the grievance persists; Akito organizes around Haruka rather than around group repair.

`Y1H-033` therefore remains **REFRAMED** rather than closed. The question is no longer whether friends can survive hidden ability. It is whether a low-pressure friendship can survive discovery that one member will impose a political theory of disposability on another.

# 9. Hirata Yōsuke — care leadership after the impossibility of universal rescue

Hirata continues to feel the V05 vote physically and morally. His question—whether he has become stronger or merely broken—captures the ambiguity of adaptation after traumatic responsibility. Yet he stays active. `COTE:Y2V06:spine13:para0300–0385`.

His trust in Ayanokōji is also explicitly chosen rather than blind. He does not claim perfect knowledge; he says he wants to believe him. That formulation contains a reciprocal demand: *I choose trust, therefore do not make my choice foolish by betraying it*. It is a more mature relational structure than dependency presented as certainty.

Hirata then participates in repairing the Shinohara fracture while Ayanokōji recedes to backup. His care is increasingly compatible with conflict, partial repair, and differentiated responsibility. He does not need everyone to feel whole before the class can function.

This is development without emotional sterilization.

# 10. Sudō Ken and Onodera Kayano — self-owned development becomes generative

Sudō is one of the cleanest control cases in the Year-2 method because his development increasingly survives the people who originally helped produce it.

Earlier Sudō improved because of Horikita, class pressure, Ayanokōji’s interventions, and basketball discipline. V06 adds two important steps.

First, he begins solving political-social problems. When Ike and Shinohara fracture, Sudō notices, seeks limited advice, and arrives at involving Hirata rather than waiting for Ayanokōji to manage the relationship. Ayanokōji explicitly refuses authorship of the answer. `COTE:Y2V06:spine13:para0400–0508`.

Second, Onodera becomes his immediate developmental peer during the festival. Hōsen tries to trigger exactly the old Sudō pattern: anger, escalation, physical pride. Onodera gives him a simple embodied technique. Sudō uses it under pressure, wins, refuses the post-match fight, and recognizes his former self in Hōsen. `COTE:Y2V06:spine16:para0640–0756`.

This is not merely emotional suppression. The text emphasizes reflection: Sudō sees how ugly his old behavior looked from the outside.

The chain is now:

> **externally motivated improvement → self-discipline → socially usable competence → peer-receptive self-regulation → generative class capacity.**

Onodera matters because she demonstrates that development no longer flows only downward from recognized “developers.” The class is beginning to teach itself.

# 11. Kei Karuizawa — ordinary life teaches the limit of selection logic

V06 gives Kei one of her strongest ordinary-life functions in the series.

Her domesticity with Ayanokōji is not merely romantic fanservice. She creates an environment in which he learns that a relational answer can be correct because of **who the person is to you**, not because a utility model proves continued membership efficient. `COTE:Y2V06:spine13:para0147–0168`.

That does not mean he has acquired unconditional relational ethics generally. The lesson is local and relationship-specific.

Kei also continues functioning socially beyond the romance: she attempts to repair Shinohara relations, gives Mii-chan encouragement rather than jealousy, and has her own friendship network. This supports `KEI_DEPENDENCY_AUTONOMY` without closing it.

But the disclosure asymmetry is now undeniable. Ayanokōji himself inventories the truths he withholds. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0395–0415`.

The relationship therefore remains structurally double:

> **Kei can teach Ayanokōji something the White Room could not while still being denied the information necessary to evaluate the full relationship he has placed her inside.**

# 12. Mii-chan — repair by trigger rather than capture

Mii-chan’s withdrawal provides a useful contrast with Ayanokōji’s harsher developmental methods.

1. she is genuinely struggling with guilt, exposure, and her feelings toward Hirata;
2. other people care about her;

Ayanokōji’s intervention is intentionally sharp. He tells her that continued absence burdens the class and that Hirata is likely to blame himself. But he also monitors whether the pressure is too strong and judges that she has already moved toward return. `spine14:para0760–0855`.

This is an intervention with a more visible answerability structure than the V03 Ike case. Mii-chan knows she is being challenged; the causal stakes are explained; she can reject the advice; and the action of returning remains hers.

# 13. The sports festival — merit, mobility, and socialized outcomes

## 13.1 Nagumo’s reform makes individual ability institutionally convertible

The new festival is explicitly tied to Nagumo’s vision of a school that weighs individual merit more heavily. Every participant begins with five points, must enter five different events, can spend points to enter additional events up to ten, and combines individual choices with class aggregation. `COTE:Y2V06:spine12:para0213–0249`.

The class reward is strongly socialized: first place receives +150 class points; fourth loses 150. `spine12:para0250–0256`.

Individual rewards are much more portable: first place by year/gender receives either two million private points or a limited-duration class-transfer ticket. `spine12:para0261–0285`.

The design therefore turns athletic performance into three different currencies:

- personal score;
- private economic capital;
- potential political exit.

## 13.2 Mobility is real but relationally costly

The class-transfer ticket is not a fantasy. It is a real, school-recognized exit option. Yet its time limit and uncertainty mean the recipient must judge future class trajectories; leaving also means abandoning accumulated relationships. `spine12:para0276–0285`.

At the endpoint Sudō and Onodera both choose private points rather than transfer. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0289–0292`.

This is meaningful evidence for the project’s mobility thread, but not enough to settle whether institutional mobility is liberatory in general. The strongest recipients in this case already possess reasons to remain.

## 13.3 Class size becomes inherited structural inequality

Horikita’s class enters with 38 students and therefore a 20-point baseline deficit relative to Ichinose’s forty-person class if everyone merely completes minimum participation. `COTE:Y2V06:spine12:para0245–0248`.

Expulsion therefore has a continuing institutional cost beyond the removed person’s absence. Membership policy changes future scoring architecture.

# 14. Horikita–Ryūen alliance — trust is not required for contractual cooperation

The alliance is one of V06’s strongest examples of political adulthood.

Horikita does not claim Ryūen is trustworthy. Ryūen does not claim Horikita is a friend. Katsuragi does not rely on goodwill. The parties identify aligned incentives, negotiate the distribution of gains, and formalize the terms with school staff. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0001–0310`.

Horikita explicitly treats accepting the risk of an untrustworthy counterparty as part of her concession. That is different from blind trust. Political cooperation can be legitimate when expectations are inspectable and enforceable.

The alliance also demonstrates that the class is becoming institutionally legible to outsiders as **Horikita’s polity**, not merely as the place where an unseen Ayanokōji happens to operate.

# 15. Ryūen Kakeru and Katsuragi Kōhei — better leader and more sophisticated predator

The earlier handoff question asked whether Ryūen was becoming a better leader or merely a safer predator. V06 shows that the binary itself is wrong.

Ayanokōji observes that Ryūen is not merely “changing”; he is trying to **evolve**. Where he previously used `悪` as a weapon, he is learning to control `善` as well—the front and reverse sides of the same coin. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0490–0605`.

That means:

- he can use contracts where violence would be inefficient;
- he can preserve dissent where destroying it would waste capacity;
- he can let Katsuragi operate as a competent counterweight;
- he can exploit procedure itself as a coercive instrument.

During the festival, his conflict with A-class students demonstrates the last point. He manipulates obstruction, delay, apology, and official process rather than simply attacking. The predator has learned institutional grammar.

This is genuine leadership development **and** strategic predation.

`Y1H-027` should therefore be `REFRAMED`, not “answered” by choosing one side.

# 16. Sakayanagi Arisu — sovereign private priority exposes the succession weakness of aristocratic optimization

## 16.1 V06 gives the first clean fiduciary-priority test

Sakayanagi intends to participate in the sports festival as her class’s coordinating leader. Ayanokōji has Chairman Sakayanagi inform her of the real reason for his own absence, correctly anticipating that she may choose to remain away and visit him. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0050–0080`.

She does.

The class then finishes fourth. `spine17:para0285–0292`.

This is sufficient local evidence to answer `Y1H-028` negatively:

> **when Ayanokōji becomes a direct private priority, Sakayanagi can place that priority above maximizing her fiduciary duty to the class.**

The result is primarily `REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_STATE`: the volume reveals the hierarchy under an actual conflict rather than clearly establishing that a new hierarchy has just developed.

## 16.2 Her class is the clean negative succession case

Sakayanagi herself identifies the structural problem: excellent limbs are not enough when the head is absent. Ayanokōji generalizes the same issue. If the leader solves everything, the leader’s absence leaves capable people unable to coordinate. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0080–0110`.

This is the mirror image of Ayanokōji’s project with Horikita.

- Horikita’s class is being trained to function without the hidden developer.
- Sakayanagi’s class remains optimized around the sovereign coordinator.

The sports-festival result does not prove the aristocratic model always fails, but it exposes a serious succession vulnerability. `Y1H-029 -> PARTIALLY_ANSWERED`.

## 16.3 Recognition and coercion coexist in the private meeting

Sakayanagi reads Ayanokōji exceptionally well. She infers that Kei’s trust may be part of an experiment or rescue project, and she approaches the logic of his class plan. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0220–0235`.

Perfect recognition, however, creates leverage. Ayanokōji responds by manufacturing counter-leverage. V06 therefore adds a darker form of relational reciprocity:

> **the person who understands him most accurately may receive less freedom precisely because accurate understanding threatens his secrecy.**

# 20. Japanese conceptual vocabulary and voice

## 20.1 `静観` — observation as both restraint and experiment

Ayanokōji names the sports-festival theme `静観`, “watching quietly / observing without intervening.” `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0163–0166`.

The word carries two simultaneous meanings:

1. **restraint:** he does not directly command the festival;
2. **experiment:** he is measuring what others can do without him.

The distinction is central to V06. Nonintervention is not automatically non-authorship.

## 20.2 `補助輪` — training wheels as a succession metaphor

Ayanokōji’s private metaphor for himself is `補助輪`. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0321–0326`.

Training wheels are valuable precisely because their success makes them removable. The metaphor is therefore one of the clearest anti-dependency statements in the entire project.

It also preserves hierarchy: the learner is still being conceptualized through a developmental model the observer understands better than she does.

## 20.3 `私のためだけ` — self-interest as a legitimate cooperative motive

Kushida’s emphatic claim that she will contribute `私のためだけ` rejects the demand for moral conversion. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para0500–0525`.

V06 treats aligned self-interest as politically sufficient when it is explicit and stable.

## 20.4 `左手での握手には敵対` — hostility made explicit rather than concealed

The left-handed handshake literalizes the refusal of counterfeit reconciliation. `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para0560–0585`.

A relationship can become more legitimate by admitting hostility instead of performing friendship.

## 20.5 `頼ること、頼られること` — distributed leadership as reciprocal dependency

The class’s problem is not dependency per se. It is **monocentric dependency**. V06 shifts toward reciprocal reliance: different people should both rely and be relied upon.

That is a more plural theory of leadership than sovereign competence.

## 20.6 `進化` rather than simple `変化` for Ryūen

Ayanokōji’s distinction matters. Ryūen is not passively changing; he is trying to evolve. He is learning to use both `悪` and `善` as available political instruments. `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0490–0605`.

The language supports a morally ambiguous competence reading rather than redemption.

## 20.7 `秘密には秘密。脅しには脅し` — reciprocity corrupted into counter-leverage

Ayanokōji’s phrase to Sakayanagi—secret for secret, threat for threat—shows a different model of reciprocity. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0188–0195`.

Instead of reciprocal disclosure producing equality, asymmetric power is used to manufacture mutually dangerous secrecy.

## 20.8 `不確定であってほしい` — the desire for an answer outside prediction

At the end, Ayanokōji says he wants the future he predicts as certain to become uncertain. `COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0330–0337`.

This may be the most important emotional-intellectual phrase in V06. He wants reality to defeat his model.

# 22. Formal and literary strengths

## 22.1 The volume turns “recovery” into a polycentric structure

V06 could have been written as Horikita repairing the class or Ayanokōji solving the consequences of V05. Instead it distributes repair across many actors. That formal choice enacts the theme.

## 22.2 The sports festival is a proof environment rather than merely a competition

The festival tests whether the class can operate with Ayanokōji absent. Because his absence is partly authored, it is not a clean laboratory. The impurity is analytically productive: it demonstrates exactly how difficult it is for a developmental author to test independence without influencing the conditions of the test.

## 22.3 The Horikita perspective makes distributed agency visible from inside leadership

The festival’s Horikita focalization prevents Ayanokōji from narratively claiming every successful move. We experience her scheduling, observation, alliance management, dependence on specialized classmates, and uncertainty directly.

## 22.4 The volume pairs two failed/successful succession models

Horikita’s class becomes more distributed while Sakayanagi’s class exposes dependence on the sovereign coordinator. The comparison turns succession from an abstract leadership virtue into competitive institutional capacity.

## 22.5 The final training-wheel monologue retroactively organizes the volume

Only near the end does Ayanokōji reveal that withdrawal is part of a larger separation plan. The earlier delegation scenes then acquire a second meaning: they were not merely convenient nonintervention but rehearsal for a future in which he intends not to belong to the class.

## 22.6 The final Haruka scene prevents victory from swallowing moral residue

# 35. Final synthesis — the class begins to move when the author stops being the only source of motion

V06 begins in the moral shadow of a decision that cannot be undone.

Airi is gone. The school’s digital roster has already incorporated that fact more cleanly than the class can. Haruka is absent. Kushida is absent. Mii-chan is absent. Hirata remains functional but wounded. Kei wonders what Ayanokōji’s selection logic would mean for her. Horikita owns a decision whose instrumental defense is not yet proven. Ayanokōji believes that decision was irrational and nevertheless helped impose its cost on someone else.

A lesser aftermath story could resolve this by restoring harmony.

V06 does not.

Instead, it changes the unit of analysis.

The question becomes not “Has the class healed?” but **“Who can act now?”**

At the start of the year, Ayanokōji’s superiority repeatedly made him the person who could see farther, diagnose more accurately, and convert hidden conditions into outcomes. Even when Horikita visibly led, the risk was that her polity depended on an invisible sovereign security layer. V05 made that dependence morally costly: Horikita could make the independent decision, but Ayanokōji still became the person who executed the substitute sacrifice.

V06 begins dismantling that architecture.

Hirata must mediate without being told the exact solution. Kei must repair her own social fracture. Sudō must notice a classmate’s relationship problem and decide whom to involve. Horikita must solve the Kushida problem she created. Kushida must decide whether any future remains worth choosing. Mii-chan must walk to Ayanokōji’s room herself. Onodera must teach Sudō a tool neither Horikita nor Ayanokōji supplied in that moment. Ryūen and Horikita must negotiate under conditions where neither trusts the other. Haruka must decide whether her grief justifies endless collective harm. Akito must decide which loyalty he values when the class and a wounded friend pull in different directions.

These actions are not morally identical.

That is exactly why they matter.

**Distributed agency is not distributed virtue.**

A polity becomes more autonomous when it contains real motives the leader does not own—even selfish motives, hostile motives, romantic motives, grief, pride, resentment, and private loyalty. Kushida’s compact is valuable not because she becomes nice but because her continuing participation becomes hers. Haruka’s dissent matters not because revenge is necessarily justified but because a “recovered class” is not allowed to define her wound out of existence. Sudō’s restraint matters because the lesson survives outside the original developer. Horikita’s alliance matters because Ryūen recognizes her authorship rather than accepting an Ayanokōji proxy.

This also explains why Sakayanagi’s class becomes the volume’s negative control. Her people may be capable, but capability that cannot coordinate without the sovereign leader is not succession. The class’s fourth-place finish after her privately chosen absence makes centralized excellence look institutionally brittle.

Ayanokōji sees the lesson.

He has already begun designing against it.

The sports festival is his `静観` experiment. He wants to know how far the others can go without him. When they succeed, he does not interpret that as evidence that his relevance should expand. He imagines himself as training wheels. Training wheels have a successful endpoint only if they become unnecessary.

That is one of the most important developments in the series so far.

A person raised inside an institution of total educational authorship is now constructing an educational project whose desired output is **his own dispensability**.

He goes further. He wants the class to become strong enough to oppose him. He expects to win, but wants the expected answer to be wrong. He wants reality to produce a result his model cannot guarantee.

This is not simple humility.

It is closer to an epistemological rebellion against the White Room.

The White Room claims that environment can make development predictable enough to manufacture excellence. Ayanokōji’s counter-experiment increasingly asks whether a plural social environment can produce a collective whose growth escapes the strongest product of designed education. Horikita, Kushida, Sudō, Kei, Hirata, Onodera, Ryūen, Haruka, and the others matter not because they approximate the same model but because their different motives create outcomes no single curriculum fully contains.

That gives ANHS its strongest positive case yet as a White Room alternative.

But V06 also supplies the objection.

Ayanokōji is still the one choosing the experiment.

He secretly decides to leave. He secretly arranges conditions. He withholds the plan from Horikita. He withholds decisive truths from Kei. He converts Sakayanagi’s knowledge into a reason to manufacture counter-leverage. He recognizes that Airi’s expulsion can motivate the weak through fear. He studies the outcomes of Horikita’s “wrong” choice because they interest him.

The danger is therefore not that he fails to value autonomy.

The danger is that he values autonomy **as an authored result**.

V06’s mature formulation is consequently neither “Ayanokōji becomes kinder” nor “Ayanokōji becomes a manipulative mastermind.” Both are too flat. He is learning forms of relationship the White Room did not teach him; he is allowing consequential disagreement; he is deliberately building people who can act without him; and he is still using hidden asymmetry, culling residue, and counter-leverage when those tools serve the design.

The decisive unresolved problem becomes:

> **Can he allow the people he develops to become authors of a future that is not merely the final successful outcome of his own curriculum?**

The most compact Year-2 progression after V06 is therefore:

> **legibility made people measurable; jurisdiction made them bindable; attribution made authorship contestable; sovereignty made control plural; embeddedness made relationships meaningful; responsibility made collective choice morally costly; distributed agency begins to make the developer dispensable.**

Whether dispensability becomes freedom—or merely the final stage of Ayanokōji’s experiment—remains open.
