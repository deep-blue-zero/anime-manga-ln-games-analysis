# Classroom of the Elite - Year 2 Volume 9.5
## Second-Pass Deep Reading
### Desire as counter-authorship, relational plurality, and the problem of enjoying human manipulation

> **Analytical boundary:** this document uses the complete original Japanese Year 2 Volume 9.5, the frozen Year 1 V2 corpus, canonical Y2V01-Y2V09, and Volume 0 only in its architecture-mandated retrospective reader position. Volume 0 may change what the reader can recognize about the genealogy of Ayanokoji's methods; it does not grant V09.5 characters knowledge they do not possess. Y2V10-12.5, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptations, wikis, and remembered later outcomes are excluded from V09.5-local adjudication.

# 1. Scope, provenance, and post-Volume-0 reading discipline

V09.5 is a decimal volume in the strongest sense of the project's method: it does not suspend the Year 2 argument in order to provide disposable holiday material. It relocates the argument into ordinary time, where the characters' private wants, friendships, romantic attachments, boredom, consumer choices, family backgrounds, and cross-class relationships become evidence about freedom and authorship.

The source was read completely in audited EPUB spine order, including the afterword and illustration layer. The Japanese replacement file is the Phase-0-authoritative byte stream: **9,575,530 bytes**, SHA-256 `8a4cadb422d030e60dca2b3b65b5e65321be3c4cdb13b102f0acdce45ed6620b`. The deterministic reading layer contains **4,167 non-empty paragraph records** in the namespace `COTE:Y2V09_5:spineXX:paraYYYY`; its normalized locator-text hash is `2438d4280c400612d2dc0c61b5a34d9f1b24b9c8beff781c32cd243ba919879b`.

Because Volume 0 has already been read in the prescribed retrospective position, V09.5 operates with two simultaneous epistemic layers:

1. **V09.5-local character knowledge.** Ayanokoji, Sakayanagi, Kei, Hiyori, Kiryuuin, Ryuen, Hashimoto, and the others know only what their scenes establish.
2. **Post-V00 reader knowledge.** The reader now possesses the political genealogy of environmental authorship, Atsuomi's preference-engineering logic, and a much fuller picture of Kiyotaka's childhood formation.

The second layer may recontextualize form, but it may not invent motive. When Ayanokoji manipulates Satou, intensifies Kei's dependency, or thinks about the pleasure of controlling larger groups, the reader can now recognize uncomfortable structural affinities with Atsuomi's grammar of preference engineering. The text still does **not** say that Kiyotaka consciously imitates his father, adopts his political project, or narrates these actions through an explicit remembered doctrine.

This distinction is especially important in V09.5 because the volume juxtaposes some of Ayanokoji's most coercive relational thinking with some of his cleanest self-owned ordinary desires. The same boy who treats a girlfriend as experimental material also wants a yogurt maker simply because he wants one, worries about whether a friend might reject an invitation, and later tells Hiyori that he invited her because *he* wanted to invite her while explicitly recognizing her right to refuse.

# 2. Executive thesis - desire becomes counter-authorship

The governing concept of V09.5 is **desire as counter-authorship**.

V09 argued that developed actors increasingly exceed the models that were supposed to contain them. V09.5 asks where those unmodeled answers come from. Its most persistent answer is surprisingly modest: they often begin as wants that the dominant system did not assign.

Ayanokoji wants an ordinary holiday rather than another optimized result. He wants to make yogurt even when cost-performance analysis tells him the purchase is irrational. Kiryuuin wants a future detached from her family name and status. Ichinose wants one photograph with Ayanokoji badly enough to surround that wish with a day of socially plausible camouflage. Sakayanagi discovers romantic desire and immediately recruits it into her wish to disrupt the future he thinks he controls. Kei chooses time with Satou because Satou cared for her while she was ill. Yamamura wants not to be disposable. Hiyori wants Ayanokoji to know that her father is the author of the book she gave him. At the end of the volume, Hiyori wants to graduate together even if their classes remain rivals.

None of these desires automatically produces moral goodness. Ryuen exploits Yamamura's fear. Sakayanagi's love can become a desire to break the person she loves. Ichinose's desire uses camouflage and enters an already asymmetric romantic field. Ayanokoji's curiosity becomes an appetite for manipulation. Desire matters here not because it is morally pure, but because it establishes **purpose owned by a person rather than exhausted by an institution's objective**.

That produces V09.5's central ethical distinction:

> **An authored environment becomes plural when participants can want something its author did not assign. It becomes domination when another person's desire is converted into experimental material without sufficient disclosure, refusal, or exit.**

This is why the volume's two strongest Ayanokoji scenes point in opposite directions.

With Kei, he reveals that the relationship has not moved his heart in the way he hoped, that he used her as an object through which to learn romance, that he deliberately exploited her dependency, and that if she cannot teach him love he can try another person. The reconciliation does not repair the ethical asymmetry; it deliberately pushes her dependency to what he calls its peak.

With Hiyori, he states his own desire as his own. He does not say that she secretly needs the outing. He does not manufacture a crisis that forces her to choose him. He recognizes that she has the right to refuse. His purpose is to share a day that she will enjoy. This is not proof that he has escaped environmental authorship. It is evidence that he can sometimes relate through **declared desire without converting the other's desire into a hidden curriculum**.

The volume therefore makes the Year 2 question more precise. The issue is no longer simply whether Ayanokoji can develop other people without owning them. It is whether he can become one participant among other participants - a person who wants, asks, risks refusal, and receives answers - rather than remaining the invisible author who turns everyone else's wants into data.

# 3. Position in the Year 2 architecture

The sequential conceptual path now reads:

> **V01 - legibility**  
> **V02 - jurisdiction**  
> **V03 - attribution**  
> **V04 - sovereignty / counter-authorship**  
> **V04.5 - embeddedness**  
> **V05 - responsibility**  
> **V06 - distributed agency**  
> **V07 - staged agency**  
> **V08 - relational legibility / moral friction**  
> **V00 - retrospective genealogy of environmental authorship**  
> **V09 - unmodeled agency / constitutional counter-authorship**  
> **V09.5 - desire as counter-authorship / relational plurality**

The progression from V09 to V09.5 is important. V09 showed actors becoming harder to predict. V09.5 asks what prediction leaves out. OAA can show Morishita's broad competence but cannot explain why she independently chooses to investigate Ayanokoji. Class position can explain why Hiyori should care about Ryuen's survival but cannot exhaust why she wants Ayanokoji to know something private about her father. Strategic analysis can explain why Ichinose might camouflage a photograph but not why she values that particular photograph. Cost accounting can explain why a yogurt maker is inefficient but not why the inefficiency is part of its meaning.

The volume is therefore a sustained argument against totalizing instrumental explanation. Strategic causation remains real. Institutional incentives remain real. Dependency remains real. But people repeatedly produce **surplus purpose**: motives and meanings not reducible to the system that hosts them.

This surplus is not outside causality. It has biographies, relationships, memories, habits, social pressures, and opportunity structures. What makes it counter-authorship is not metaphysical uncaused freedom. It is that the actor can appropriate a purpose as *hers or his*, act from it, revise it, and sometimes say no to the purpose another person assigned.

# 4. Narrative architecture - from precious ordinary time to chosen cross-class relation

V09.5 is organized as a winter-break sequence whose apparent looseness is analytically useful. The large formal exam temporarily recedes. Without an explicit examination dictating what counts as success, character purposes become easier to distinguish from institutional purposes.

The prologue, `kakegae no nai nichijou`, establishes the frame: Ayanokoji does not need something extraordinary to happen. Ordinary student holidays, friends, a girlfriend, seniors, juniors, and encounters are enough. He knows the remaining time is shrinking, calls what remains an extension period, and thinks of ordinary days as irreplaceable. The language of scarcity remains, but the valued object is no longer a score. It is lived time.

From there the volume moves through seven overlapping structures:

1. **Loneliness and social uncertainty.** Ayanokoji can manipulate complex systems but still does not know how to casually invite a male friend without fearing rejection or pity.
2. **Non-optimized desire.** The yogurt-maker sequence turns irrational consumer longing into evidence of ordinary self-authorship.
3. **Inherited status versus chosen future.** Kiryuuin rejects the obvious advantages of family and Class A because freedom itself is the good she wants.
4. **Desire under camouflage.** Ichinose's photograph project uses sophisticated social cover without ceasing to be a personal wish.
5. **Legibility becoming constitutional fear.** Horikita's classmates no longer ask only whether Ayanokoji is strong; some ask what kind of authority a coldly competent leader might exercise over weaker students.
6. **Love as disruptive power.** Sakayanagi refuses the proposition that attachment must be weakness and wants her love to become a force capable of disrupting Ayanokoji's expected world.
7. **Cross-class relation as alternative institutional meaning.** Hiyori ends the volume by saying that competitive classes do not have to hate one another and that people can still want to graduate together.

That last movement is the volume's most generous political idea. ANHS structures students as rivals, but the rivalry does not exhaust the relation. Friendship, attraction, curiosity, shared hobbies, gratitude, and memory create ties that the class system neither fully authors nor fully cancels.

# 5. Ayanokoji - ordinary desire and the persistence of the experimental gaze

V09.5 gives one of the richest demonstrations yet that Ayanokoji's development is not a linear movement from instrumentality toward normality. Ordinary desire expands while the experimental gaze becomes more explicit.

## 5.1 Ordinary life is no longer merely camouflage

The opening makes his valuation of school life direct. Friends, romance, senior-junior ties, and ordinary encounters are named as things that make the remaining time valuable. The significance is not that the White Room child has suddenly become a conventional teenager. It is that ordinary life has become a good whose value he can state without translating it into strategic utility.

The friendship sequence then preserves how incomplete this development is. He considers calling a male friend, imagines being rejected, worries that Hirata might accept only out of kindness, and concludes that solitude is easier. He can stage an environment involving dozens of actors, yet a casual invitation exposes him to a form of uncertainty he finds difficult: the other person may simply say no, and the answer cannot be made meaningful by calling it an experiment.

## 5.2 The yogurt maker is not trivial evidence

The yogurt-maker sequence is almost comically overdeveloped, which is precisely why it matters. Ayanokoji begins calculating cost recovery, then catches himself. If he optimizes hard enough, the correct decision is not to buy it. He stops the analysis and identifies the real reason: he wants the object and wants to use it.

The later disappointment when the sale item is gone matters as much as the desire. He does not convert failure into a hidden victory. He is simply disappointed and wants Kiryuuin to understand the disappointment. Kiryuuin laughs because the behavior is recognizably ordinary adolescent behavior.

This is `AYANOKOJI_ORDINARY_LIFE` at its cleanest. A desire does not need to maximize value to be legitimate. Failure does not need to become data to justify the attempt.

## 5.3 He is increasingly visible to himself as a manipulator

The volume's darkest self-revelation arrives after his phone call with Satou. He recognizes that using speech to manipulate another person belongs to the category of things he finds fun. Truth or falsehood is secondary. Even being deceived or manipulated by someone else attracts him because it teaches him something about people. He imagines that manipulating larger, unfamiliar groups might be even more enjoyable.

This should not be euphemized as mere strategic curiosity. The text identifies pleasure.

At the same time, pleasure in manipulation is not identical to Atsuomi's political telos. Post-Volume-0, the reader can recognize a structural genealogy: both father and son are interested in environments where preferences and actions can be shaped indirectly. But Kiyotaka's stated attraction here is experiential and epistemic - to know humans, to be taught by them, to experience unpredictable response - rather than a declared program of political control.

That difference matters, but it does not remove the ethical problem. Enjoying a person's unmodeled answer is compatible with respecting the person only if the person remains more than the experiment.

# 6. Kei - reconciliation as dependency peak, not romantic resolution

V09.5 is the strongest negative evidence yet for the proposition that Kei has become irreplaceable to Ayanokoji in his current romantic architecture.

The key sequence begins before they meet. He asks what dating and love are. He inventories what he has learned at ANHS: conversation, friendship, shopping, play, food, and a range of emotions. With Kei he has experienced what he understands to be the model behaviors of a romantic couple, including physical intimacy. He then reaches a devastating distinction: completing the behaviors associated with romance is not the same as acquiring the feeling the behaviors are supposed to express.

His internal answer is that his heart has not moved from before the relationship to the present. He suspects that he advanced through romantic stages before the desire to advance arose, using Kei as the object through which he would learn the curriculum of romance. The problem is therefore structurally similar to a recurring Year 2 distinction: **possessing the form is not possessing the underlying state**.

He does not regret the relationship. Kei has taught him a great deal. But he now treats the approaching separation as another possible lesson: if he hesitates to release her, perhaps that reluctance will reveal love. If Kei cannot teach him what he seeks, he explicitly thinks that he can try another person.

This is why `Y1H-011` moves from `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED` to `ANSWERED_BY_REVELATION` at this boundary. The answer is not that Kei can never become emotionally irreplaceable in any possible future. The answer is narrower and locally sufficient: **the hidden current state is that Ayanokoji does not experience her as irreplaceable and explicitly imagines substitution within the romance-learning project.**

The reconciliation intensifies the ethical problem rather than solving it. His apology is factually appropriate: the initiating wrong was his, Kei's insecurity was reasonable, and he gives her a gift accurately chosen from observed preferences. Yet internally he identifies the successful repair as having driven her dependency to a complete peak. He believes he could now compel extreme behavior. He deliberately does not separate immediately because, in his model, that would not solve the dependency problem. He promises stability through her March birthday while privately preserving the later experimental question.

The contrast between visible care and hidden purpose is therefore maximal. Kei receives reassurance, protection, a desired gift, physical affection, and a promise of near-term continuity. What she does not receive is disclosure sufficient to understand why the conflict was engineered, how her dependency is being measured, or that her boyfriend is evaluating whether another person might serve the same learning function later.

The volume therefore strengthens the negative answer to `RELATIONSHIP_RECIPROCITY` and `TRUTH_PROOF_RECORD` without reducing every tender act to fraud. The care works. The gift matters to Kei. Ayanokoji does accept responsibility for the pain he caused. The ethical problem is that these real goods are nested inside an unshared architecture whose purpose Kei cannot meaningfully accept or refuse.

# 7. Kei and Satou - horizontal autonomy grows while vertical dependency worsens

One of the most important corrections to a simple dependency narrative is that Kei's broader social life continues to become healthier at the same time her romantic dependency intensifies.

When Kei recovers from influenza, she prioritizes time with Satou because Satou cared for her while she was ill. Ayanokoji regards that choice as correct: if Kei neglected the friend who supported her in order to prioritize him immediately, the friendship would not deserve to be called genuine.

The handholding between Kei and Satou is deliberately stripped of romantic overinterpretation. They hold hands because they are best friends and want to. For Kei, who entered ANHS through fear, status performance, and dependence on stronger protectors, this is a major form of ordinary horizontal security. Satou is not useful because she can defeat an enemy or manage Kei's social status. She is valuable as a chosen person.

Ayanokoji then tries to convert that healthy tie into infrastructure for a future he may create. In his phone call with Satou, he encourages her to protect Kei and internally describes the resulting belief as an invisible contract. If he later discards Kei, Satou will be more likely to remain beside her. If Satou were expelled, she could not even verify whether he kept the reciprocal promise he offers.

The scene therefore contains both generative and appropriative authorship:

- Kei and Satou have independently produced a real friendship.
- Ayanokoji correctly values that friendship as protective horizontal capacity.
- He then tries to pre-author how that capacity will function after a possible future separation.

This is a recurring Year 2 danger. Development that genuinely survives the developer can still be appropriated by the developer as part of a larger design.

# 8. Hiyori - desire without ownership as a positive relational countermodel

Hiyori provides V09.5's cleanest local alternative to the hidden-curriculum relationship.

The relationship is built around quiet reciprocity rather than emergency. She selects a personally meaningful book, checks that Ayanokoji has not already read the author, purchases a copy, and carries it until she can give it to him. The gift initially looks like ordinary literary affinity. Later she reveals that the author is her father and that she has never told anyone at school. The important sentence is not merely the biographical fact. It is that she wanted *Ayanokoji* to know it.

Ayanokoji responds to the earlier gift by asking what he can do for her. By the final chapter he acts: he invites Hiyori out.

The scene is unusually explicit about relational jurisdiction. He worries that the invitation could feel like pity or pressure. When she asks why he invited her, he does not tell her that she secretly needs socialization. He says that he wanted to invite her. He frames the desire as his own and recognizes that Hiyori possesses the right to refuse. His goal is to spend a day that she will enjoy.

This is not an absence of influence. Every invitation changes another person's options. He chooses the time and initiates the outing. What makes the scene ethically different is **disclosure of authorship plus preserved refusal**. The purpose is not smuggled into Hiyori as though it were already hers.

The outing also demonstrates why OAA cannot exhaust `JITSURYOKU`. When Hashimoto later probes Ayanokoji's role, Hiyori immediately identifies the question behind the question: what does Hashimoto want to *do* if he confirms Ayanokoji's importance? Her intelligence becomes visible through interaction rather than administrative score.

The volume closes with Hiyori asking that they graduate together even though their classes compete. The line is not a rejection of the class system; she remains worried about Ryuen's class prospects. It is a refusal to allow the institution's rivalry to monopolize the meaning of every cross-class relationship.

> **Competition can govern outcomes without owning affection.**

That proposition is one of ANHS's strongest locally demonstrated alternatives to the White Room logic of total curriculum.

# 9. Sakayanagi - love becomes deliberate counter-authorship

Sakayanagi's V09.5 material is a decisive complication of both her leadership and her relationship to Ayanokoji.

The volume first humanizes the supposedly remote sovereign through mundane social acts. She helps Sanada think about a present for the first-year student he is dating. She brings Ayanokoji cake because he missed Christmas. She uses light preference steering to discourage him from buying dessert earlier so that the cake will have a place. This is manipulation in a trivial, legible, prosocial register. It matters because the series does not make all steering morally equivalent.

Their later conversation returns to the White Room and Ayanokoji's expected post-school future. He still assumes that he will eventually be returned and used as an instructor for later generations, but V09.5 adds a new hesitation: until recently he had no meaningful doubt about that endpoint; now the apparent irrationality of allowing him three years at ANHS and the unverifiable status of the White Room's restart create a small epistemic opening.

Ayanokoji then criticizes Sakayanagi's class governance. Her class is strong, but he sees weak lateral relationship construction. She can direct and manipulate people, but a class of capable individuals still needs bonds and autonomous will. Sakayanagi resists because attachment can produce hesitation when a person must be discarded. Her pet metaphor is revealing: care creates a cost to culling, and she has treated that cost as weakness.

The confession directly answers that premise. She tells Ayanokoji that her feeling is romantic and does not request an immediate answer. He asks whether she can turn the feeling into strength rather than weakness. She treats the question as trivial.

Her private monologue then converts love into explicit counter-authorship. She still wants to defeat him as the 'born genius' against the 'made genius', but she now also wants to disrupt him, make him troubled, force him into territory beyond calculation, draw out emotion, break him, and love him. She believes that a plan of his has already been going wrong since the island exam, but V09.5 does not identify the exact fact. That uncertainty must remain frozen.

The key is that Sakayanagi no longer wants merely to prove herself inside Ayanokoji's evaluative field. She wants to **change the evaluator**.

That is counter-authorship at the level of intimacy. Love becomes a desire not just to be recognized by the stronger actor, but to alter the conditions under which he recognizes anyone at all.

# 10. Kiryuuin - freedom as self-authored inefficiency

Kiryuuin's holiday conversation gives V09.5 one of its cleanest statements of freedom.

She has family status and resources that could make an elite path easy. She could also pursue the obvious ANHS good of Class A graduation. Instead she wants an ordinary adult life detached from the Kiryuuin name and the status it could purchase. The point is not asceticism. She wants to know that the life is hers.

Her failed attempt to investigate repeating a year is equally revealing. Private points are enormously powerful, but not omnipotent. The school permits certain conversions - class transfer, protection from some forms of expulsion, later educational or employment assistance - while excluding repetition of the school year. This is a useful institutional reminder: `POINTS_POLITICAL_ECONOMY` is a jurisdictional system with edges, not a universal market in which every rule can be purchased.

Kiryuuin also admits a residue of curiosity about the person she might have become if she had fully pursued competition. Self-authorship does not remove counterfactual longing. Choosing freedom means accepting that another version of the self will remain unrealized.

Ayanokoji recognizes the affinity with his own entry ideology, but his closing thought is more important than the comparison. He refuses to overinvest in imagined future reunions and returns to the present: he is living this time freely now, and that is enough.

Post-Volume-0, the line acquires additional weight. The opportunity to enter an outside environment may have been authored by Atsuomi, but the value Ayanokoji now places on cooking, shopping, friendship, reading, romance, and wasting time cannot be reduced to the original political purpose of that doorway.

# 11. Ichinose and Shiranami - desire, camouflage, and boundaries

V09.5 continues Ichinose's V09 transformation by showing desire as both socially intelligent and ethically bounded - though not uncomplicated.

Shiranami's scene is important before Ichinose herself appears. Ayanokoji remembers that Shiranami has romantic feelings for Ichinose and corrects his own initial heteronormative phrasing: the relevant fact is simply that one human being loves another human being. This is a small but explicit statement of the series' contemporary social framing.

When Shiranami asks about Ayanokoji and Ichinose, he answers carefully. There is no romantic relationship of the kind she fears **right now**, but he refuses to promise the future. He also withholds the details of Ichinose's new mental state because he does not want outside influence to disturb an outcome he is still observing. His advice to Shiranami is emotionally constructive - Ichinose's love for someone else does not reduce Shiranami's value, while violating Ichinose's wishes could damage the relationship she still has - but the advice is delivered inside Ayanokoji's continuing role as curator of what information may safely enter Ichinose's environment.

Ichinose's Christmas-photo plan then demonstrates desire under camouflage. She spends the day taking photographs with many people, allowing a photograph with Ayanokoji to exist inside a plausible social pattern. The labor is disproportionate if the objective is merely 'obtain one image.' That is the point: the photograph matters to her enough to justify the labor.

When the real wish becomes explicit, she takes both an ordinary photograph and a more intimate one. Ayanokoji permits her to keep the intimate image and accepts the risk of how it might later be used because he consented to its creation. The scene therefore has more disclosure symmetry than many of his hidden experiments, even though the surrounding romantic situation remains ethically unstable.

Nanase's later revelation that Housen apparently likes Ichinose adds a comic parallel: even one of Year 2's most overtly violent actors can become strategically awkward when ordinary desire asks him to request a photograph.

V09.5 therefore treats romance not as an escape from politics but as another source of motives that politics cannot fully simplify.

# 12. Horikita's class - Ayanokoji's legibility becomes constitutional fear

The informal eight-student meeting about Ayanokoji is one of the most important `OAA_LEGIBILITY` scenes since V01.

Early Year 2 asked what happens when students become measurable. V09.5 asks what happens when the class realizes that a person has been **mis-measured**.

Maezono's group assembles fragments: unexpectedly strong academic results, Sudou's knowledge of unusual physical ability, prior events, and the increasingly visible gap between Ayanokoji's public OAA and what he can apparently do. The discussion does not settle exactly what he is. Instead it produces political expectations.

Some students want him to contribute more. Matsushita protects a legitimate counterclaim: if he stayed hidden, there may be reasons, and the class should not simply force exposure. That is already a meaningful shift from V01's assumption that hidden ability is merely unused capital belonging to the class.

But the Sakura/Kushida memory produces a darker concern. Maezono remembers how coldly Ayanokoji could pursue an expulsion and describes the impression as machine-like. Lower-status students can imagine a future in which a more powerful Ayanokoji becomes the real leader and treats weak OAA performers as expendable.

Matsushita's own interior demonstrates the class divide created by the same possibility. As a high-performing, cooperative student, she can imagine Ayanokoji leadership as stabilizing: competence plus predictable rules may protect the upper tier. Students nearer the bottom experience the same imagined constitution as a culling threat.

This is why the scene is more important than another reveal of hidden ability.

> **Legibility has become a constitutional question: if this person is as capable as we think, what authority should that capability entitle him to exercise over us?**

That question is one of V09.5's strongest warnings against equating demonstrated competence with legitimate jurisdiction.

# 13. Hashimoto, Kamuro, Kito, Morishita - Sakayanagi's class is becoming plural

Sakayanagi's class is often described through the grammar of sovereignty, but V09.5 increasingly exposes lateral and bottom-up agency inside it.

Hashimoto records the Horikita-class conversation and treats Ayanokoji as the likely hidden strategic core. His loyalty is explicitly instrumental: his objective is Class A graduation. If the existing sovereign does not maximize that probability, he is willing to think about switching allegiance, importing talent, or rearranging the class around a different center.

Kamuro remains suspicious of both Hashimoto and Sakayanagi. She agrees to approach Ayanokoji only under conditions she chooses. Kito displays a minimum-courtesy ethic toward even opponents. Sanada maintains an ordinary romantic life and asks Sakayanagi for help. Yamamura has an intelligence role but also her own fear of disposability. Morishita independently decides to investigate Horikita's class and explicitly says that Sakayanagi did not order the contact.

Morishita is particularly useful because Ayanokoji initially uses OAA exactly as the method prescribes: as a fast administrative summary. It tells him that she is broadly competent. Interaction then supplies the information OAA cannot: eccentric social style, independent initiative, threat perception, and willingness to investigate without leader instruction.

By the final Hiyori outing, Hashimoto's group can improvise, joke, disagree, veto one another, and alter plans. Kamuro's refusal to cooperate with Hashimoto's excess actually changes what the group does. Hiyori can enter the social field without becoming a Sakayanagi proxy.

This does not prove that Sakayanagi's leader-dependence problem is solved. It does show that her class contains more independent political life than a pure monarchic model captures.

The tension with Sakayanagi's own pet metaphor is deliberate: the class she fears becoming attached to is already becoming a collection of people whose purposes exceed their sovereign's assignments.

# 14. Ryuen and Katsuragi - predation acquires internal friction

Ryuen's V09.5 scenes continue the V06 reframe: better leadership and better predation develop together.

Katsuragi increasingly operates as an internal competence Ryuen deliberately purchased because Ryuen knows there are methods he does not naturally supply. He tutors underused students, analyzes the third-term warning cautiously, resists irrationally choosing Sakayanagi before the exam structure is known, and repeatedly functions as a brake against unnecessary escalation.

This is not democratization. Ryuen remains the dominant decision-maker and still enjoys fear, bluff, and asymmetric information. But the fact that Katsuragi can disagree, slow him, and supply alternative methods gives the class an internal counterforce that early Ryuen leadership lacked.

The Yamamura sequence shows both sides. Ryuen's counter-tail is clever: instead of continuously tailing Ayanokoji, he leaves and returns later, allowing Yamamura's attention to settle before catching up. Once Yamamura is exposed, he immediately sees the leverage created by her fear of being discarded by Sakayanagi. His offer to keep the failure secret is not benevolent rescue; it creates dependency on his discretion.

Katsuragi pushes against coercive escalation and tells Yamamura to leave. Ryuen still retains the informational advantage, but the class now contains a person who can name when extracting additional leverage is strategically unnecessary or morally excessive.

`Y1H-025` and `Y1H-027` therefore strengthen without closing. Voluntary internal competence can constrain Ryuen at the margins, but no durable follower veto yet binds his sovereign decisions.

# 15. Yamamura - fear of disposability and the right to remain a person after failure

Yamamura gives V09.5 one of its clearest small-scale versions of the volume's core problem.

Her stealth has become the ability through which she thinks she justifies her place in Sakayanagi's class. When the tailing operation fails, the failure is therefore not merely tactical. She experiences it as evidence that the one thing making her useful may have disappeared.

Ryuen exploits exactly that gap. If she hides the failure, perhaps he will keep it secret and she can preserve her role. The hope is weak, but it is psychologically powerful because the alternative feels like disposability.

Ayanokoji tells her that concealment is the worst choice. Ryuen can weaponize the hidden failure whenever convenient. More importantly, he says directly that he does not want her expelled. When she asks why, his answer is relational rather than meritocratic: they were in the same school-trip group; that can be enough of a relationship.

The line is important, but it should not be overclaimed. Earlier in the same sequence Ayanokoji thinks that he has something in mind about making amends for accidentally exposing her, so Yamamura is not a perfectly clean case of 'strategically useless person valued for no possible reason.' `Y1H-003` therefore remains partial.

What does change is Yamamura's own authorship. She sends the truthful report immediately because she knows that waiting will allow fear to pull her back toward concealment. She deliberately closes her own escape route.

This is a small but strong example of freedom under pressure:

> **Self-authorship can consist of choosing the truthful constraint before another actor converts fear into a private contract.**

# 16. OAA, Class A privilege, and the limits of institutional guarantees

V09.5 contains two useful institutional clarifications that belong together.

First, Ayanokoji explicitly appreciates OAA when he needs fast orientation to Morishita. The system does what a good file should do: provide a compact account of visible academic, physical, cognitive, and social performance. The subsequent conversation immediately demonstrates the limit. OAA cannot tell him that she independently chose to investigate him or how her unusual social reasoning works.

The Year 1 principle therefore survives intact:

> **The file is evidence about the person. The file is not the person.**

Second, the volume clarifies what Class A's post-graduation privilege can and cannot author. Institutional intervention can help force admission under some conditions, pay tuition for a student who independently earned admission but lacks money, or preserve a later employment intervention. It cannot make a student competent enough to graduate from a university after placement, and it cannot make every institution accept every arbitrary exception.

This is a useful miniature of `ANHS_EXPERIMENT` itself. Opportunity structures matter enormously. They can alter access, resources, and risk. They do not completely author the capacity required to live inside the opportunity once it is granted.

The same point appears in Kiryuuin's failed repetition idea. Private points are powerful, but the institution refuses to sell every imaginable outcome. Scarcity, authority, and capability remain partially irreducible to one another.

# 17. Truth, disclosure, and relational provenance

V09.5 repeatedly distinguishes truthful content from truthful relationship.

Ayanokoji's conversation with Shiranami contains accurate and constructive statements, but he is still curating information about Ichinose because he wants to observe her new state with minimal outside disturbance. The advice can be good while the information architecture remains asymmetric.

His phone call with Satou sharpens the distinction further. He internally states that whether his promises are true or false is secondary to making Satou believe them. That is a direct case of **truth as instrument rather than shared provenance**.

With Kei, the visible apology is valid but radically incomplete. He acknowledges the wrong she can see while withholding the experiment she cannot see.

With Yamamura, by contrast, he recommends immediate truthful reporting precisely because hidden failure would create a coercive dependency on Ryuen. Truth here restores jurisdiction to the formal relationship with Sakayanagi instead of allowing a rival to privatize the secret.

Hiyori supplies the most intimate positive example. She volunteers a previously undisclosed fact about her father because she wants Ayanokoji to know. The information is not extracted, weaponized, or exchanged for protection. Disclosure itself is the relational act.

These scenes suggest a useful distinction for the rolling truth/proof ledger:

- **instrumental truth:** accurate information used to produce an effect;
- **curated truth:** accurate information selectively withheld or timed by an actor claiming authority over the recipient's developmental environment;
- **answerable truth:** information offered in a relationship where the recipient can respond, reinterpret, or refuse without hidden penalty;
- **self-disclosure:** information whose principal purpose is to let another person know the speaker more fully.

V09.5 contains all four.

# 18. Protection, ownership, refusal, and the ethics of the invitation

The volume's relational scenes can be arranged along a simple jurisdictional axis: **does the other person know what is being asked, and can that person refuse?**

Ryuen's offer to Yamamura preserves the surface form of choice but exploits a fear he intends to own. Ayanokoji's hidden experiment with Kei deprives her of the information needed to consent to the actual purpose of the conflict. His invisible contract with Satou tries to pre-author a future obligation she does not know she is entering in the same terms.

The Hiyori invitation is different. Ayanokoji owns the desire as his own and acknowledges her refusal right. This is not ethically perfect simply because he says she can say no, but it satisfies more of the project's relationship criteria: purpose disclosure, exit, reciprocity, and ordinary shared life.

Kiryuuin's chosen future operates on the same principle at a larger scale. She does not need her family to agree that an ordinary life is the optimal use of her capabilities. The value of the choice is partly that it is hers.

Sakayanagi's confession also preserves refusal in a striking way. She states her desire without demanding an immediate answer. She wants to change Ayanokoji eventually, but she does not force the present confession into a contract.

These differences matter because Year 2 has made 'protection' and 'development' too broad to function as moral categories on their own. The more useful test is:

> **Who selected the purpose, who knows the purpose, who can refuse, who bears the cost, and can the answer change the author rather than merely complete the author's model?**

# 19. Post-Volume-0 retrospective annotations - the genealogy is visible, not determinative

Volume 0 changes the reader's experience of V09.5 most strongly in three places.

## 19.1 Manipulation as pleasure

Atsuomi's political grammar treated the strongest form of influence as preference-sensitive environment design: do not simply force the target; create the circumstances in which the desired choice becomes subjectively available or attractive. V09.5 now gives Kiyotaka a self-revelation that he finds manipulating people enjoyable.

The resemblance is significant because it connects technique to appetite. Yet the goals remain distinct in the evidence currently available. Atsuomi's project is explicitly political and reproductive: power, elite production, institutional continuity, and control over developmental systems. Kiyotaka's V09.5 narration centers on learning human beings, being surprised, being deceived, and experiencing larger unknown systems.

The correct classification is **RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION**, not 'Ayanokoji is becoming Atsuomi.'

## 19.2 Ordinary self-authored desire

Volume 0 established how little space the White Room gave ordinary purposeless experience. V09.5 repeatedly presents desires that would have been meaningless inside that curriculum: choosing a kitchen tool because it seems fun, browsing physical media, worrying about a casual invitation, wanting a friend to enjoy a day, cooking because one wants to cook.

These are not politically grand acts of rebellion. That is why they matter. A curriculum optimized around externally assigned excellence has difficulty representing an activity whose justification is simply 'I wanted to.'

## 19.3 Sakayanagi's 'made genius' model becomes less complete

Volume 0 gives the reader much better grounds to see Kiyotaka as the interaction of unusual potential and an extreme environment, not a blank product manufactured at will. Sakayanagi still frames their rivalry as born genius versus made genius. V09.5 does not give her Volume-0-level knowledge and therefore does not correct her in-scene. Her formulation must remain **character belief**, now more visibly incomplete to the reader.

This is exactly the kind of epistemic separation the retrospective method is designed to preserve.

# 22. Formal strengths of V09.5

## 22.1 The decimal-volume form is thematically productive

By removing a single dominant special exam, the novel allows multiple local purposes to coexist. A holiday shopping failure can sit beside White Room speculation, a romantic confession beside class intelligence work, and a bookstore outing beside transfer-market politics. The form itself therefore models relational plurality.

## 22.2 Comedy carries philosophical evidence

The yogurt maker is funny, but the joke does analytical work. Ayanokoji's inability to optimize away a trivial want is stronger evidence of ordinary self-authorship than a grand speech about freedom would be, because the desire has no heroic function to excuse it.

## 22.3 Interior narration is unusually diagnostic

The Kei and Satou passages are among the clearest examples in Year 2 of narration removing interpretive ambiguity. The text does not require the reader to infer that Ayanokoji sees relationships experimentally; he names the experiment, dependency, manipulation, and substitutability himself.

## 22.4 Cross-class ordinary life thickens the political world

Ryuen, Katsuragi, Kiryuuin, Sakayanagi, Hiyori, Yamamura, Hashimoto, Kamuro, Kito, Morishita, Ichinose, Shiranami, Nanase, and Housen appear not only as class pieces but as people with private relations and interests. This makes later conflict morally and causally denser. Relational legibility from V08 is no longer a school-trip exception; it is becoming ordinary infrastructure.

# 23. Reservations and counterreadings

## 23.1 Do not romanticize every ordinary desire as liberation

Wanting something is not automatically self-authorship in the strong ethical sense. Ichinose's photo plan occurs inside an asymmetric romantic field. Sakayanagi's love contains a desire to break the loved person. Ryuen's desire for victory remains coercive. The relevant question is whether a desire can be owned, revised, refused, and answered - not whether it feels sincere.

## 23.2 Do not turn Hiyori into proof that Ayanokoji has solved reciprocity

The Hiyori outing is a positive counterexample to hidden curriculum, not a global transformation of his relational ethics. In the same volume he manipulates Satou and Kei with extreme informational asymmetry. Relationship-specific expression remains essential.

## 23.3 Do not overstate Yamamura as strategically useless

Ayanokoji's concern for Yamamura is relationally meaningful, but he also thinks about making amends and remains interested in her future position. The scene strengthens the possibility that ordinary relation matters independently of immediate utility; it does not close `Y1H-003`.

## 23.4 Do not identify Sakayanagi's 'island exam' disruption before the text does

Her monologue says Ayanokoji's plan has been going wrong since the summer island exam, but V09.5 does not disclose the precise fact she means. Later knowledge must not be back-projected.

## 23.5 Do not convert Ayanokoji's manipulation pleasure into a clinical diagnosis

The text gives a behavioral and phenomenological fact: he finds manipulation enjoyable and wants broader human experience. It does not license an external psychiatric label. The project should stay with narrative ethics, developmental history, and demonstrated cognition.

# 32. Final synthesis

Year 2 began by asking what happens when people become legible.

V09.5 arrives at a different question:

> **What remains of a person after the strongest models have explained her incentives, measured her abilities, mapped her dependencies, and predicted her likely response?**

The volume's answer is not mystical. What remains is often a desire.

Kiryuuin wants an ordinary future even though elite optimization points elsewhere. Ichinose wants one photograph and builds an elaborate social route to obtain it. Sakayanagi discovers that love does not have to cancel rivalry and wants to use it to disrupt the person she loves. Kei wants Satou as a best friend even while becoming more dependent on Ayanokoji. Yamamura wants not to disappear when one useful ability fails. Hiyori wants one particular person to know who her father is and wants rivals to graduate together. Ayanokoji wants to make yogurt.

These wants are not all wise. They are not all harmless. But they demonstrate that human beings continually generate purposes that exceed the institutional objective assigned to them.

That is the hopeful side of counter-authorship.

The dangerous side is Ayanokoji's discovery that the same surplus can be pleasurable to manipulate. He likes learning people by moving them, likes being moved or deceived in return, and imagines larger unknown groups as richer fields of experience. His curiosity is genuinely open to surprise, which differentiates him from a closed deterministic planner. Yet curiosity does not itself grant moral permission to make another person's dependency the medium of the lesson.

V09.5 therefore gives the Year 2 project its sharpest relational criterion so far:

> **Freedom is not proven merely because a person makes a choice inside an authored environment. It is better evidenced when the person's desire is legible as her own, when the governing purpose is disclosed, when refusal remains meaningful, and when the answer can change the author rather than simply confirm the experiment.**

Ayanokoji can already create both kinds of relationship.

With Kei, he remains the hidden curricular author.

With Hiyori, for one ordinary winter day, he is much closer to being simply another person who wants something, asks, and risks hearing no.

The distance between those two modes is now one of the central ethical distances Year 2 has left to travel.
