# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ ２年生編１』
## Year 2 Volume 1 — Second-Pass Deep Reading
### Legibility, markets, reciprocal leadership, and the hidden rule beneath the visible exam

> **Local analytical boundary:** this document uses the complete original Japanese Year 2 Volume 1 and the frozen Year 1 V2 boundary only. It deliberately does **not** use later Year 2 volumes, Volume 0, *Second List*, Year 3, adaptations, wikis, or remembered later revelations to identify the White Room student or settle questions the volume itself leaves open.

---

# 1. Scope, source, provenance, and inherited baseline

This is the canonical second-pass reading of `Y2V01`. The source EPUB was read completely in spine order before targeted retrieval. The audit produced a deterministic paragraph namespace of the form `COTE:Y2V01:spineXX:paraYYYY`, a separate illustration inventory, and normalized text only after the source-byte hash was recorded.

The volume begins from a Year 1 endpoint in which several distinctions are already established and must not be rediscovered as though they were new:

- **ability is multidimensional and environmentally expressed;**
- **displayed ability is not identical to possessed ability;**
- **measurement may be useful without exhausting the person measured;**
- **development and coercion can coexist, so beneficial outcomes do not legitimate the method that produced them;**
- **Horikita has developed toward distributed, corrigible leadership but still operates above an incompletely visible Ayanokōji security layer;**
- **Ayanokōji has begun choosing ordinary school life as a positive end, while his ability to author other people's environments remains ethically unresolved;**
- **the class polities have distinct political constitutions that cannot be reduced to their current A/B/C/D rank letters;**
- **institutional record power matters because the authority that decides what becomes official can alter the practical truth of an event.**

`Y2V01` does not replace those propositions. It changes their scale. Year 1 repeatedly asked what happens when decisive capacities are hidden from institutions and peers. Year 2 opens by making selected capacities dramatically more visible—and immediately demonstrates that greater visibility is not the same thing as greater transparency, fairness, or freedom.

The volume's governing second-pass question is therefore:

> **What becomes politically possible when a school turns partial models of people into public infrastructure, while its most consequential intervention remains deliberately hidden?**

That question joins the OAA system, the cross-year pairing exam, the first-year bargaining market, the covert bounty on Ayanokōji, Horikita's attempt to create a reciprocal inter-year alliance, Hōsen's coercive sovereignty, and Ayanokōji's decision to make one part of his own capacity publicly legible.

# 2. Executive thesis

Year 2 Volume 1 is a novel about **legibility becoming a political resource**.

The school introduces OAA—`Over All Ability`—and presents it as a system that makes student capacity visible across class and year boundaries. For the first time, every student can look up every other student and see a compact profile of academic ability, physical ability, adaptive/social intelligence, social contribution, and an aggregate score. The system is not simply fraudulent. It records real things. Hirata's broad competence is accurately legible. Sudō's exceptional physical capacity is unmistakable. Ayanokōji himself concedes that his mediocre-looking profile is reasonable given what he has actually chosen to display. Chabashira explicitly says `学校も万能ではない`—the school is not omnipotent—and the system preserves separate year-by-year records so that later development can become visible rather than being swallowed by the past.

But the same infrastructure turns people into **searchable strategic objects**.

A score can now determine:

- who is recruited;
- who is ignored;
- who can charge a premium;
- who appears safe as a partner;
- who is treated as a liability;
- which class can build a network fastest;
- where an opponent should spend money;
- and whose visible weakness can be politically exploited.

OAA therefore reduces one information asymmetry while producing a market around the information it exposes.

The cross-year exam makes that market concrete. Second-years need first-year partners to survive. The penalties are asymmetric: a second-year pair under the threshold means expulsion, while the first-year ordinarily risks only a temporary private-point loss. This transforms first-year ability into bargaining power. Sakayanagi uses brand and money. Ryūen deliberately inflates the market and uses recruitment as intelligence collection. Ichinose protects weak students and converts care into trust. Horikita attempts something more institutionally explicit: a class-to-class agreement with no price hierarchy, no academic exclusion, and a stated aim of building `対等な協力関係`—an equal cooperative relationship.

That is genuine Horikita development. She no longer treats the weakest students merely as defects to be corrected. Her first objective is preventing expulsion. She distributes work with Hirata, recognizes different kinds of weakness, refuses to imitate Ichinose's relational style, and constructs a political strategy fitted to her own class's resources. Ayanokōji tells her directly that she is becoming capable of competing as a leader with Sakayanagi, Ryūen, and Ichinose.

The volume nevertheless refuses an easy declaration of independence. Horikita's equal-contract strategy cannot itself force Hōsen to bargain. Hōsen possesses information she does not: a hidden twenty-million-point bounty on Ayanokōji. His apparent extortion is partly camouflage for a separate attack. Horikita makes a sound political judgment inside the world she can see, but the decisive enforcement that finally makes equal cooperation possible comes from Ayanokōji's hidden intervention. `Y1H-012` therefore remains open. Horikita has become more autonomous; the class has not yet demonstrated that its visible constitution can survive without hidden sovereignty.

Ayanokōji's own movement is equally double-edged. He increasingly chooses school life for himself. He refuses Sakayanagi's offer to curate a safe partner because `自分の運命は自分で決める`. He states that continuing school life is his highest priority. At the end of the exam he deliberately scores 100 in mathematics, not because his academic ability suddenly developed, but because **his policy toward the visibility of that ability changes**. The 100 is `REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_STATE` with respect to capacity and `DEVELOPMENT` with respect to public self-authorship.

Yet his old conditioning remains visible in the body. During Hōsen's knife trap, Ayanokōji converts his own hand into evidence by allowing the blade to pass through his palm. It is strategically brilliant because it destroys the planted-weapon narrative. It is also a stark example of a person whose inherited risk calculus can instrumentalize his own body with almost no ordinary hesitation. The volume's movement toward self-authorship therefore does not equal uncomplicated humanization.

The deepest structural irony is the relation between **OAA and the bounty**:

> **The school begins Year 2 by making ordinary student ability more visible than ever, while administrative power makes the rule that matters most to Ayanokōji almost completely invisible.**

The result is not “measurement is bad.” It is more exact:

> **Increased measurement can improve coordination while still leaving sovereignty opaque. Transparency about the governed does not imply transparency about the governors.**

That is the political problem with which Year 2 begins.

# 3. Position in the Year 2 architecture

`Y2V01` performs five opening functions.

First, it converts the end-of-Year-1 White Room threat from a largely external pressure into an **embedded school problem**. The prologue establishes that a White Room student has entered ANHS and has been briefed on Ayanokōji and the entire second-year cohort. The student's identity remains withheld.

Second, it changes the school's information architecture. OAA is not a one-exam gimmick. It is an institutional layer intended to follow students across Year 2, preserve separate annual records, and make those records visible to other students.

Third, it expands politics from inter-class competition to **inter-year politics**. First-years are not merely younger versions of the old cast. They arrive under a changed institutional curriculum: lower initial class points, more explicit instruction about the point system, new OAA legibility, and reported encouragement from Nagumo toward a freer and more aggressively meritocratic school culture.

Fourth, the volume tests whether Year 1 character development persists when the environment changes. Sudō's growth survives the year boundary. Horikita's leadership scales outward. Ryūen's defeat-derived adaptation persists. Ichinose's solidaristic method continues to generate trust. Kōenji remains resistant to collective ownership.

Fifth, it introduces the key Year 2 epistemic danger: **competence is not identity evidence**. Because the reader is told that a White Room student exists, almost every unusually capable first-year becomes suspicious. The novel itself repeatedly invites and frustrates this inference. The second-pass method must therefore resist the very profiling error that OAA dramatizes: a visible capability profile is evidence about a person, not proof of the person's institutional origin.

The correct local endpoint is not “which first-year is the White Room student?” It is:

> **The White Room student exists; Tsukishiro has an expulsion project; several first-years possess unusual knowledge, motives, or ability; and Y2V01 does not provide sufficient evidence to collapse those categories into one identity.**

# 4. Narrative architecture — from public measurement to hidden sovereignty

The volume's construction is unusually disciplined for such a cast-heavy restart.

## 4.1 `暗躍` — the hidden actor precedes the visible system

The prologue begins not with the class but with a secret briefing. Tsukishiro presents detailed data on Ayanokōji and the 156 other second-years to an unidentified White Room student and stresses that Ayanokōji must be expelled and returned to the White Room in April. The mission is to remain covert because exposure could damage `先生`—Atsuomi's political position.

The White Room student's resentment is not adequately described as simple jealousy. Tsukishiro invokes the institutional hierarchy of `最高傑作`, while the student's own memory contains the repeated command:

> `綾小路清隆を越える存在になれ`

The distinction matters. The institution has already organized the student's self-conception around Ayanokōji as benchmark. Even before the student exercises personal agency, the White Room has authored the rivalry's vocabulary.

The prologue therefore gives the volume an asymmetry the class cannot see: before OAA publicly profiles everyone, **someone has already received a private dossier on everyone**.

## 4.2 `実力とは` — the school asks what ability can be made visible

The school year begins with ordinary continuity—friends, class points, promotion failure, classroom routine—and then introduces OAA. This placement matters. OAA is framed as modernization rather than emergency. It is an ordinary institution meant to normalize the public comparison of students.

The chapter title `実力とは` is therefore not answered by a power ranking. The chapter immediately shows that “ability” depends on what is counted, how categories are weighted, what is observable, and what remains latent.

## 4.3 `新たなるステージ` / first-year contact — profiles become a market

The pairing exam weaponizes the new visibility. Students do not merely inspect OAA out of curiosity; they use it to decide who is worth approaching. A numerical profile acquires price.

The first-year cohort simultaneously frustrates the model. Hōsen's political control is not reducible to an OAA score. Nanase's composure and unusual attention to Ayanokōji do not identify her motive. Amasawa's academic and social performance does not explain what she is doing with the knife. Tsubaki and Utomiya can appear suspicious because the reader is primed to treat any anomalous approach as infiltration.

## 4.4 `一夏の試験` / `ＤクラスとＤクラス` — class constitutions meet

The middle movement compares political methods under the same incentive structure.

- Sakayanagi converts prestige and capital into selective recruitment.
- Ryūen converts bidding into pressure, intelligence, and future network construction.
- Ichinose converts rescue into trust.
- Horikita attempts reciprocal class-to-class institution building.
- Hōsen converts asymmetric risk and information into coercive rent extraction.

The volume thereby turns “finding a partner” into a comparative constitutional experiment.

## 4.5 `退学の足音` — the visible exam contains another exam

The knife plot reveals that the public pairing examination is not the whole environment. A hidden twenty-million-point incentive exists for a small subset of first-years. The visible exam's rules prohibit deliberate sabotage; the covert bounty rewards a result—Ayanokōji's expulsion—that can encourage precisely the sort of adversarial behavior the public rule ostensibly restrains.

The political problem is not simply hypocrisy. It is **layered jurisdiction**. Different students are operating under different rule sets inside what appears to be one examination.

## 4.6 `深まる謎` — victory produces more uncertainty, not less

The class survives. Horikita's minimum goals are achieved. Ayanokōji exposes his mathematics ability. Yet the White Room identity remains unknown, and Tsukishiro claims that the embedded student ignored the expulsion plan he had prepared.

The volume ends by refusing the detective payoff it seems to promise. That refusal is methodologically important. The correct response to incomplete identity evidence is continued uncertainty, not retrospective guessing.

The author's afterword reinforces the transition rather than settling it: Kinugasa describes the cast as showing visible signs of growth as they enter the second year and emphasizes how much is now moving at once. That paratextual observation supports the volume's year-boundary function but does not adjudicate specific character motives.

# 5. OAA — measurement, legibility, and the politics of the file

OAA is the most important institutional innovation in the volume because it gives concrete form to a theme Year 1 had already developed implicitly: **the difference between a person and an official model of that person**.

## 5.1 What OAA actually measures

Ayanokōji's first-year profile is:

- academic ability `C (51)`;
- physical ability `C+ (60)`;
- adaptive/social ability `D+ (37)`;
- social contribution `C+ (60)`;
- overall ability `C (51)`.

The four categories are explicitly constructed from observed institutional data. Academic ability derives mainly from written tests. Physical ability comes from physical education, club performance, and special examinations. `機転思考力` incorporates social position, number of friends, communication, improvisation, and adaptation. `社会貢献性` includes classroom conduct, lateness and absence, problem behavior, school service, morality, manners, and the way the student is seen by others.

The aggregate is mathematically weighted; social contribution counts at half strength.

This is not a neutral discovery of a preexisting essence. It is a **model with design choices**.

## 5.2 Ayanokōji's profile is wrong about capacity but not necessarily wrong about evidence

The key narratorial response is not indignation. Ayanokōji says that if he is evaluated according to what he normally shows, the numbers seem reasonable.

That distinction should control the entire Year 2 treatment of OAA:

> **A measurement can accurately summarize displayed evidence while badly underestimating possessed capacity.**

Ayanokōji's OAA profile is therefore not best called “false.” It is a truthful summary of a curated interface.

This is a more interesting problem than simple bad measurement. The subject can strategically manipulate the data-generating environment. OAA introduces institutional surveillance, but Ayanokōji has spent Year 1 practicing **adversarial observability**: he controls which outputs the institution receives.

## 5.3 OAA preserves history instead of silently overwriting it

Year 2 receives a separate evaluation page. First-year results remain visible as first-year results rather than being retroactively replaced.

That formal choice closely resembles the analytical architecture governing this project. Sudō's low first-year academic score remains historically true as a record of what he had demonstrated, even while his current ability has already moved beyond it. Horikita estimates him around `D–D+` by the beginning of Year 2, while OAA still displays `E+ (20)` from the completed first year.

This makes Sudō the volume's clearest example of **development outrunning the file**.

## 5.4 “The app is equal” is true in one narrow sense

Chabashira says:

> `このアプリは平等だ。`

Her claim is not meaningless. A student does not receive a higher score simply because the student's class currently has a higher rank. The categories are applied across the cohort. That is a real reduction in one kind of status privilege.

But equal application is not the same as complete fairness.

The system still contains:

- chosen categories;
- chosen weights;
- unequal opportunities to display capacity;
- strategic concealment;
- contextual differences;
- disability accommodation problems;
- reputation effects;
- and political consequences that arise only after scores become public.

Sakayanagi's physical score exposes the problem vividly. Because she cannot participate normally in physical activity, the school assigns her the year group's minimum physical value rather than removing the category from the aggregate. This is a deliberate accommodation, but it demonstrates that a scalar system must decide how to translate **inapplicability** into a number.

That decision is not simply “the truth about Sakayanagi's body.” It is an institutional convention required to keep the aggregate comparable.

## 5.5 `学校も万能ではない` is the system's most important safeguard

Chabashira explicitly says that students dissatisfied with their evaluation should demonstrate more and that `学校も万能ではない`.

This sentence sharply limits a totalizing reading of OAA. The institution admits model error.

Yet the answer it offers is still political: **become more legible to the evaluator**. A student with hidden or context-specific capacity must produce evidence in a form the school can see. The system can be corrigible without ceasing to privilege visibility.

The central question thus becomes:

> **Who bears the burden of correcting a mistaken model—the institution that modeled the person, or the person who must now perform for the institution?**

`Y2V01` does not settle that question.

## 5.6 Visibility changes incentives

OAA is explicitly expected to motivate improvement because students know their records are visible. This is plausible. It can help a weak student identify deficiencies, let a class locate complementary strengths, and reward genuine development.

The counterpressure is equally real. Public ratings create:

- stigma around low scores;
- reputational feedback loops;
- incentives to optimize what is measured;
- incentives to conceal what could lower a rating;
- pressure to become institutionally visible;
- and markets in which a person is selected because a profile makes them economically attractive.

The pairing exam arrives immediately so that the novel can show this effect rather than theorize it abstractly.

## 5.7 OAA does not abolish class politics

Horikita reacts, `まるで個人戦ね`—“it is almost like an individual competition.” Chabashira answers that this is both right and wrong: OAA derives from Nagumo's plan to make individual ability matter more, but class remains the basic institutional unit.

The result is not replacement but **superposition**:

- students remain collectively liable for class outcomes;
- individual ability becomes more portable and visible;
- cross-year relationships become politically important;
- and exceptional individuals can be recruited into networks not perfectly aligned with class identity.

This is precisely the institutional terrain on which Nagumo's Year 2 project can operate.

# 6. The first Year 2 special examination — asymmetric risk produces a market

The cross-year examination is effective because its rules do more than test academics. They manufacture a bargaining ecology.

## 6.1 Core mechanism

Each second-year pairs with a first-year. Five subjects produce a combined maximum of 1,000 points. Class ranking depends on the paired results associated with each second-year class, while individual rewards also exist.

The crucial threshold is 500 combined points. At or below it:

- the second-year is expelled;
- the first-year loses the private-point deposits that would otherwise be received for three months.

The school adds an anti-sabotage clause: deliberate underperformance or coercion to underperform can itself cause expulsion.

## 6.2 The asymmetry matters more than the threshold

The second-year risks school membership. The first-year ordinarily risks money.

That inequality creates bargaining leverage. A high-performing first-year can charge for safety because the second-year's loss from failure is far greater. A low-performing first-year can become unwanted because the first-year's own downside is comparatively limited.

The school therefore manufactures a market by assigning **different prices to the same pair outcome**.

The first-years are not immoral merely because they recognize their leverage. The institutional design has made the leverage economically rational.

## 6.3 The anti-sabotage rule works only if actors value their own membership

Ayanokōji notices the structural exploit immediately. Even if he earns a perfect 500, a partner who deliberately earns zero can still take him under the threshold. The public anti-sabotage clause should deter an ordinary first-year because that first-year would also be expelled.

But a White Room operative may value Ayanokōji's expulsion more than continued enrollment.

The rule therefore contains a hidden behavioral assumption:

> **all players value the penalty in roughly the way the institution expects.**

Once an actor enters with an external mission, the deterrence model breaks.

This is an important institutional principle for later Year 2 analysis: formal incentives govern behavior only to the extent that the institution has correctly modeled what participants value.

## 6.4 The exam makes OAA actionable

Students now search for partners through public performance profiles. A first-year with high academics becomes scarce capital. A weak second-year becomes an urgent rescue case. The market does not arise despite OAA; OAA reduces the search cost needed for the market to form.

This is one of the volume's strongest causal links:

> **measurement → legibility → targeting → bargaining power → political network formation.**

## 6.5 The school's predicted scores are themselves strategic information

At the end, the weakest D-class pair still totals 579, and students such as Ike and Satō can exceed 250. Ayanokōji concludes that the school's initial predicted-score table deliberately understated likely performance.

This is not equivalent to falsifying OAA. It shows that the institution itself sometimes uses **managed expectation** inside an exam.

The school is simultaneously:

- evaluator;
- information provider;
- rule author;
- and strategic designer of the participants' perception of risk.

The exam is therefore a lesson in institutional authorship as much as student ability.

# 7. Horikita Suzune — from developmental leadership to reciprocal political design

Horikita's `Y2V01` development is not best summarized as “she becomes more confident.” The meaningful shift is **constitutional**: she increasingly treats leadership as the design of relationships in which different capacities can remain useful without being reduced to rank.

## 7.1 The first objective is survival of the weakest, not maximization of class reward

Her strategic priorities are explicit:

1. produce no expulsion from the class;
2. avoid an unsustainable money war;
3. finish third or better in the class ranking.

This ordering matters. A pure point-maximizer would concentrate high-performing first-years around already strong second-years and accept that some weaker classmates become difficult to save. Horikita instead reserves strong partners for rescue cases and accepts that this may reduce the class's ceiling.

This is a direct continuation of her Year 1 move away from scalar disposability. It is also more politically mature because she does not confuse compassion with the absence of standards. Third place remains a target. She is trying to reconcile collective ambition with an anti-expulsion floor.

**Delta:** `DEVELOPMENT`, continuing a Year 1 trajectory rather than revealing a previously hidden trait.

## 7.2 Her leadership is now distributed

Horikita immediately works through Hirata rather than attempting to personally own every relationship. She understands that pairing has at least two separable problems:

- academic risk;
- social/negotiation difficulty.

The strongest academic student is not automatically the best negotiator. A student who can teach is not automatically the person with the social network needed to secure partners. Hirata's relational accessibility therefore becomes a distinct leadership resource.

This is precisely the kind of ecological ability Horikita had to learn during Year 1. What is new is that the lesson has become routine enough to structure her opening plan rather than arriving only after failure.

## 7.3 Sudō's outdated OAA score becomes evidence that Horikita has learned to see trajectories

OAA lists Sudō's first-year academic ability as `E+ (20)`. Horikita estimates his present state around `D–D+` and notes his extensive study during spring break.

Early Horikita treated current weakness as a stable property. Here she explicitly distinguishes:

- the historical record;
- her present estimate;
- and the likely future trajectory.

She is becoming a leader of **developmental time**, not merely a classifier of current value.

## 7.4 Trust in Ayanokōji is stated more plainly

When discussing who should remain flexible during pairing, Horikita tells Ayanokōji:

> `自由に動けるクラスメイトの中では、信頼しているわよ`

The wording is bounded. She does not suddenly claim complete interpersonal understanding. She says that among classmates able to move freely, he is someone she trusts.

That is important because Year 1 ended with a paradox: Horikita increasingly relies on Ayanokōji's judgment while knowing that her model of him is incomplete. `Y2V01` does not solve the epistemic problem. It does show that practical trust has become explicit enough to support political delegation.

**Delta:** `DEVELOPMENT` in relational acknowledgement; `UNRESOLVED` in information symmetry.

## 7.5 She does not imitate Ichinose

Horikita observes Ichinose's trust-first outreach and calls it a `眩しい方法`—a dazzling method that looks simple but is difficult to reproduce. The mature move is that Horikita does **not** conclude she must become Ichinose.

Instead, she asks what analogous political resource her own class can generate.

The distinction is crucial to her self-authorship. Growth no longer means becoming the person she previously idealized. It means constructing a form of leadership adequate to her own temperament and institutional position.

## 7.6 The D-to-D proposal is an attempt to create answerable inter-year asymmetry

Horikita's plan for first-year Class D is unusually explicit:

- fifteen pairings are to be settled together;
- academic rank from E to A is not a condition;
- no private points are exchanged;
- weak students on either side are protected;
- the relationship should continue beyond this exam;
- neither year should acquire a creditor/debtor status over the other.

Her most important phrase is:

> `ポイントで上下関係を作らずに対等な契約を結びたい。`

She is trying to prevent present asymmetry from hardening into future jurisdiction.

That is more than kindness. It is institutional design.

Horikita understands that first-years currently possess greater bargaining leverage because second-years face expulsion. But she also understands that future exams may reverse the dependency. A purely transactional contract now could leave Class D unable to buy help later. Her answer is to create reciprocal trust before the direction of need changes.

The time horizon has expanded from:

> “How do we survive this exam?”

into:

> **“What relationship should exist between these groups when either one may need the other?”**

That is one of the clearest Year 1 → Year 2 leadership developments in the volume.

## 7.7 Ayanokōji's recognition is narratively significant but not sovereign certification

Ayanokōji tells Horikita that Sakayanagi, Ryūen, Ichinose, and Horikita are now all constructing future relationships by different means and that she is becoming capable of competing with them as a leader:

> `おまえはもうその３人と張り合えるだけのリーダーになりつつある`

This is strong textual evidence of development, but it should not be treated as an omniscient authorial verdict merely because Ayanokōji says it. His judgment is unusually informed, not infallible.

The surrounding evidence independently supports him: Horikita defines priorities, delegates, differentiates political methods, builds an inter-year strategy, resists extortion, and refuses a tempting shortcut that would destabilize the first-year class.

## 7.8 Nanase's “leadership coup” test demonstrates Horikita's fiduciary reasoning

Nanase proposes a way around Hōsen: she could become the first-year D leader herself and form the alliance without him.

This would help Horikita immediately.

Horikita rejects the shortcut because it risks splitting the first-year class and because a relationship built around a factional seizure of authority would undermine the future cooperation she actually wants. She insists that Hōsen cannot simply be treated as an obstacle outside the polity; the class must somehow incorporate or constrain the actor who already possesses power.

This is significant because Horikita is choosing **the first-year class's institutional future over her own short-term bargaining advantage**.

It is an early fiduciary test, and she passes it.

## 7.9 But `Y1H-012` remains open

The strongest counterargument to declaring Horikita independently sovereign is the volume's climax.

Her political judgment toward first-year D is reasonable. Her insistence on equality is normatively coherent. Her refusal to pay Hōsen's extortionate price is defensible. Yet none of those choices alone compels Hōsen to cooperate.

The final enforcement comes from Ayanokōji. By understanding the knife plot, allowing the blade to enter his palm, and acquiring decisive leverage over Hōsen, he changes the bargaining environment underneath Horikita's visible negotiation.

The resulting alliance is therefore both:

- **Horikita's political design**, and
- **Ayanokōji's hidden security intervention**.

The correct Year 1 handoff update is not `ANSWERED_BY_DEVELOPMENT` for `Y1H-012`. It is `STILL_OPEN`, with stronger evidence that Horikita can independently author the political goal but not yet evidence that the visible constitution can reliably enforce that goal without hidden sovereignty.

## 7.10 `Y1H-013` also remains open

Horikita and Ayanokōji can disagree and negotiate more naturally than before, but the volume does not provide a clean case in which she sustains a consequential outcome against his contrary judgment and bears the result independently.

What `Y2V01` does establish is a precondition: Ayanokōji increasingly tells her more of his reasoning, and she increasingly acts from her own political model rather than waiting for his instructions.

That is development. It is not yet proof of full separation.

# 8. The class-polity comparison — four strategies for converting first-year uncertainty into political capital

The first exam works as a compact comparison of the four second-year class constitutions.

The important object is not current rank. It is **how each polity converts uncertainty, money, trust, coercion, and talent into durable coordination**.

## 8.1 `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` — prestige, capital, and selective portfolio construction

Sakayanagi begins from the strongest material position. A-class prestige itself attracts capable first-years, and the class can supplement that prestige with private points.

Her recruitment logic is more sophisticated than simply buying the highest academic scores. The novel shows her interest in students whose OAA profiles may contain useful nonacademic strengths. This is consistent with her Year 1 theory of talent allocation: the sovereign leader's function is not merely to rank but to recognize and place.

The exam also exposes a limit. Sakayanagi constructs an intelligent theory of Ryūen's bidding strategy—correctly recognizing that he is forcing A class to spend more—but initially interprets his objective partly through the assumption that he remains primarily focused on the present competitive ranking.

Ryūen's actual horizon is wider.

The moment matters because it keeps Sakayanagi's high cognitive ability from becoming narrative omniscience. A powerful model can still be incomplete.

### Current-state delta

`CONTINUITY` with sharpening. Her polity remains centrally interpreted and allocated through Sakayanagi's judgment. `Y1H-028` and `Y1H-029` receive no decisive local answer.

## 8.2 `POLITY-RYUEN` — from buying scores to pricing people and mapping the market

Ryūen's bidding initially looks reckless. His class has less aggregate academic ability than A class, yet he publicly drives up prices for high-performing first-years.

Sakayanagi correctly identifies one function: forcing A class into a money game and making it spend resources that would otherwise remain advantages.

But the more interesting function is **reconnaissance**.

By making large offers visible, Ryūen can learn:

- who responds to money;
- who delays;
- who can be intimidated;
- who is willing to negotiate across class lines;
- who recognizes that current prestige may not predict future rank;
- and which first-years might become useful contacts during the next two years.

The market itself becomes an intelligence-gathering device.

This is a meaningful development from the most myopic version of Year 1 Ryūen. He is increasingly willing to sacrifice local efficiency for long-horizon position.

It would be a mistake, however, to call this ethical reform.

His method is still comfortable with pressure, manipulation, and converting human relations into strategic advantage. The strongest justified conclusion is:

> **Ryūen is becoming a more politically sophisticated ruler, not yet demonstrably a morally better one.**

`Y1H-027` therefore remains `STILL_OPEN`.

## 8.3 `POLITY-ICHINOSE` — solidarity as infrastructure rather than sentiment

Ichinose's class responds by taking in weaker first-years rather than treating them as liabilities. Horikita recognizes the method immediately: Ichinose first builds `信頼関係`, then allows cooperation to emerge from it.

This is not “naïve kindness.” It generates concrete advantages:

- weak students acquire safe partners;
- first-years learn that Ichinose is reliable;
- information and social access flow toward her;
- and future cooperation becomes cheaper because the relationship does not have to be purchased from zero each time.

The volume therefore strengthens the Year 1 reading of Ichinose's solidarity as **institutional capital**.

Yet it does not test the hardest Year 1 question. Ichinose's method is effective here because the environment rewards broad rescue and because no severe betrayal is required from her own class. `Y1H-020`—whether solidarity can acquire adversarial resilience without losing its normative constitution—remains open.

## 8.4 `POLITY-HORIKITA` — bounded reciprocity and class-to-class institution building

Horikita cannot outspend Sakayanagi or Ryūen and cannot reproduce Ichinose's broad charisma. She therefore narrows the field.

Instead of building a school-wide personal network, she attempts a **polity-to-polity agreement** with first-year D.

The model is distinct:

- explicit rather than diffuse;
- class-specific rather than universal;
- anti-hierarchical in price structure;
- future-oriented;
- and founded on reciprocal vulnerability.

Horikita's leadership thus becomes more legible as a separate constitution rather than a weaker version of another leader's style.

## 8.5 Hōsen's first-year D — authoritarian individualism as a contradiction

Nanase describes Hōsen's foundation as `徹底した個人主義`, and immediately adds that he **forces** it upon the class.

That combination is revealing.

Hōsen's “individualism” is not liberal individual autonomy. The members do not possess broad freedom to construct their own associations. Hōsen centrally controls the terms on which they may commit, sets price floors, and uses fear and violence as enforcement.

It is better described as:

> **authoritarian non-solidarity: centralized rule that forbids collective mutual obligation except when the ruler authorizes it.**

This is why Nanase fears more than the immediate three-month point penalty. She fears that the exam will establish a social order in which weaker classmates are permanently marked as people who failed to secure partners and in which the class never develops collective responsibility.

The conflict between Horikita and Hōsen is therefore not merely negotiation over price. It is a conflict between two class constitutions:

- Horikita: differentiated people should be coordinated under reciprocal obligations;
- Hōsen: actors should bargain from power under a sovereign who monopolizes coercion.

The knife conspiracy later reveals that Hōsen's apparent economic position is also strategic camouflage, but the constitutional contrast remains real.

# 9. New first-year cohort — identity, allegiance, and the discipline of not knowing

The prologue makes the volume a trap for the analyst.

The reader knows one White Room student is embedded among the first-years. The natural impulse is to turn every strange behavior into identity proof. `Y2V01` repeatedly punishes that impulse.

The governing local rule must therefore be:

> **unusual ability, unusual knowledge, unusual interest in Ayanokōji, and participation in the twenty-million bounty are separate evidentiary categories.**

## 9.1 Hōsen Kazuomi — coercive sovereign, tactically intelligent, not reducible to “muscle”

Hōsen's body dominates his entrance. The prose repeatedly marks his size, violence, and street-fighting experience. Sudō—himself the only second-year with OAA physical `A+`—is outmatched.

That contrast is analytically useful. OAA physical ability is not a universal combat score. Athletic performance, fighting technique, violence tolerance, size, and street experience overlap but are not identical capacities.

Hōsen is also strategically capable.

He understands the penalty asymmetry, imposes a high price floor on first-year D negotiations, and uses the apparent possibility of cooperation to keep second-year D engaged long enough for the knife plan. The final conspiracy requires:

- a weapon traceable to Ayanokōji;
- a scenario in which violence appears plausible;
- controlled witnesses;
- physical pressure on Horikita and Sudō;
- and a narrative in which Ayanokōji appears to retaliate with the knife.

This is not the plan of a thoughtless brute.

The second-pass correction is therefore:

> **Hōsen's violence is part of his political method, not evidence that he lacks strategy.**

At the same time, his plan improves materially through Amasawa's participation. Tactical intelligence should not be inflated into omniscience.

## 9.2 Nanase Tsubasa — principled vocabulary, class loyalty, and complicity

Nanase is the most important first-year in the volume because she exposes how moral vocabulary and unethical participation can coexist.

She is polite, composed, and openly resistant to Hōsen's violence. She tells him that violence will not make her submit. She articulates a sophisticated critique of his forced individualism and worries about the class's long-term capacity for solidarity.

She also participates in the plan to expel Ayanokōji.

Her stated reasons include:

- the twenty-million-point advantage for her class;
- fear that another class might gain that advantage;
- and a personal judgment that Ayanokōji may be `邪悪で薄汚い` and unworthy of the school.

The tension should not be flattened into “therefore her earlier concern was fake.” The stronger reading is that Nanase possesses genuine collective concern but permits that concern to justify severe means.

This makes her ethically more interesting, not less coherent.

### The `ボク → 私` slip

Nanase introduces herself with:

> `ボクは、じゃなかった───私は七瀬翼と言います。`

At this boundary it is evidence of a register/self-reference discontinuity. It is **not** sufficient evidence of White Room origin, a fabricated identity, or any particular later explanation.

It belongs in the identity/allegiance ledger as an unresolved linguistic fact.

### Her test of Horikita

Nanase's proposal to replace Hōsen as leader is later revealed as a test of whether Horikita wants genuine class cooperation or merely an advantageous faction. Horikita's refusal increases Nanase's willingness to trust her.

Nanase is therefore not only an object of suspicion. She is herself evaluating political legitimacy.

## 9.3 Amasawa Ichika — playful ordinary competence and deliberate evidentiary engineering

Amasawa's introduction uses domestic comedy to conceal a much harder strategic function.

She requires Ayanokōji to cook tom yum goong before agreeing to help with pairing. The scene appears to be an ordinary-life skill test and does genuinely function that way: Ayanokōji has limited cooking experience, Horikita assists him remotely, and the episode expands the texture of his life beyond exams.

But the shopping sequence contains the planted-object infrastructure for the later attack. Amasawa selects an expensive sheathed petty knife immediately despite apparently entering the store for the first time. She later returns to Ayanokōji's room under the pretext of searching for a hair tie and removes the knife without leaving the evidentiary trail the attackers want to avoid.

The volume establishes participation in the Hōsen plan.

It does **not** establish White Room origin.

The twenty-million bounty supplies a sufficient local alternative motive. Amasawa's competence and odd precision therefore raise suspicion but do not close identity.

## 9.4 Yagami Takuya — social access is not institutional origin

Yagami arrives through Kushida's middle-school network and helps connect first-years to second-year D. He is academically strong and socially effective. His history with Kushida creates an additional information channel unavailable to most first-years.

These facts make him relevant to the identity ledger because he possesses access and competence.

They do not make him a White Room student.

The same discipline applies: **privileged social information can have an ordinary provenance.**

## 9.5 Tsubaki Sakurako and Utomiya Riku — suspicion generated by the reader's prior

Tsubaki and Utomiya approach Ayanokōji about pairing. Tsubaki says she dislikes creating debts and wants a relationship without lingering obligation.

Because the reader knows an infiltrator exists, the scene easily becomes suspicious. Ayanokōji himself must evaluate it as a possible approach.

But when he explains that he already has a prospective partner, they withdraw rather than force the situation.

Locally, the evidence supports caution and uncertainty—not identity attribution.

This is a useful metatextual demonstration of Bayesian distortion: once the prior probability of infiltration is raised, ordinary behavior can begin to look diagnostic even when it is not.

## 9.6 Identity/allegiance state at the endpoint

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hōsen Kazuomi | Y1D coercive leader | exceptional fighting ability; direct Ayanokōji attack | yes | none | non-WR origin cannot be absolutely proven from local text, but ordinary middle-school history is strong counterevidence; do not collapse into WR |
| Nanase Tsubasa | Y1D mediator / dissenter | unusual Ayanokōji focus; `ボク→私`; composure; personal hostility | yes | none | privileged knowledge + personal motive; unresolved institutional linkage |

# 10. Ayanokōji Kiyotaka — self-authored visibility without release from strategic conditioning

Year 2 requires a four-layer model of Ayanokōji:

1. ordinary adolescent life;
2. relational life;
3. White Room / strategic conditioning;
4. environmental authorship.

`Y2V01` contains evidence in all four layers, and its central character development comes from how their boundaries begin to move.

## 10.1 Ordinary life remains a positive end

The volume deliberately spends time on apparently low-stakes material:

- the Ayanokōji Group noticing his absence during vacation;
- Akito saying he would be lonely without him;
- Ayanokōji feeling awkward but pleased at being called a friend;
- grocery shopping;
- learning to cook;
- Kei studying in his room;
- teasing, jealousy, and domestic mess;
- library interactions;
- the normal embarrassment of being conspicuous among classmates.

These scenes are not filler around the “real” White Room plot.

Year 1 established ordinary school life as a chosen counter-curriculum. Year 2 immediately tests whether that value survives when external pressure becomes more direct.

Ayanokōji's response is explicit:

> `学校生活を続けることが、今のオレにとって最優先すべきこと。`

This is stronger than a desire to avoid returning to the White Room in the abstract. The object being defended has positive content: **this school life**.

`Y1H-041` therefore receives meaningful supporting evidence, but not a final answer. Ordinary life remains valuable under pressure; whether it remains institutionally sustainable is still unknown.

## 10.2 Friendship is increasingly accepted without being fully instrumentalized

When a friend tells Ayanokōji he would be missed, his response is narratorial rather than strategic:

> `友達にそう言われると少しむず痒くなる。だけど悪い気はしないな。`

The sentence is small but important. No tactical use follows. He registers the category “friend” as emotionally meaningful and slightly embarrassing.

This is `CONTINUITY` from late Year 1 with modest `DEVELOPMENT` in comfort and explicitness, not revelation of a hidden emotionless core becoming suddenly human.

## 10.3 Refusing Sakayanagi's safe route is a self-authorship statement

Sakayanagi understands the danger of a hostile first-year partner and offers to help Ayanokōji secure a candidate she considers safe.

He refuses:

> `おまえの実力は認めている。だが自分の運命は自分で決める。`

The sentence matters because its object is **jurisdiction** rather than trust. He does not say Sakayanagi is incompetent. He explicitly recognizes her ability and still refuses to delegate the choice.

This is one of the clearest positive Year 2 continuations of Ayanokōji's freedom project:

> **another person's superior information or ability does not automatically grant that person authorship of his future.**

The irony is that he has not yet shown that he consistently grants the same principle to people whose development he authors.

`Y1H-007` therefore remains the sharper long-term problem: self-authorship is real; reciprocal legitimacy remains unproven.

## 10.4 His policy toward concealment changes

At the Year 1 boundary Ayanokōji has already revealed more ability than at entrance, but concealment remains a default risk-management strategy.

In `Y2V01`, he explicitly reconsiders that policy under Tsukishiro pressure. He decides that if remaining at school requires it, he will stop holding back to the same degree.

The mathematics result is the concrete execution of that decision.

He earns 100 when:

- Horikita earns 87;
- Keisei earns 84;
- no other classmate exceeds 90;
- the final portion includes material such as measure theory and Lebesgue integration outside normal first-year high-school scope.

He deliberately does **not** reduce his score to preserve the old interface. His narration says `あえて手を抜かなかった` and frames the resulting visibility as a preemptive move against future Tsukishiro interference.

This requires two separate delta labels:

### Capacity
`REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_STATE`

Nothing in the volume suggests he learned advanced mathematics between Year 1 and this exam. His own comment that he had studied such material from childhood confirms prior possession.

### Visibility policy
`DEVELOPMENT`

He changes what he is willing to let the school and classmates know.

Collapsing these into “Ayanokōji became academically stronger” would be precisely the kind of file/person confusion the method is designed to avoid.

## 10.5 The 100 is self-authorship, but it is not uncomplicated authenticity

It would also be too celebratory to call the score simple self-expression.

Ayanokōji exposes only one controlled slice of himself:

- one subject receives a perfect score;
- the other subjects remain around 70;
- the revelation is timed to a strategic need;
- he anticipates the attention it will produce;
- and he chooses the exact form of disclosure.

The act is therefore **authored visibility**, not transparent self-revelation.

That distinction matters for `Y1H-004` and `Y1H-035`. He is more willing to disclose capacity. He is not yet demonstrably willing to disclose vulnerability or enough of his motive/history for others to make fully informed choices about him.

## 10.6 His improvised Year 1 persona becomes explicit

While considering the White Room student's preparation, Ayanokōji reflects on his own entrance behavior. He says that he quickly decided to `演じる` and constructed a character, then found sustained performance troublesome and returned closer to his ordinary way of speaking/behaving.

This is useful retrospective evidence inside Y2V01 itself, but it should be classified carefully.

It does not mean every Year 1 social awkwardness was fake. The stronger formulation is:

> **Ayanokōji consciously attempted an ordinary-student persona, but the persona did not erase the real difficulty of becoming socially ordinary.**

Performance and genuine adaptation coexist.

## 10.7 Environmental authorship remains his default strategic grammar

Ayanokōji repeatedly learns by structuring situations:

- he lets Horikita negotiate rather than replacing her immediately;
- he tests first-years through constrained information;
- he uses pairing choices to narrow candidate sets;
- he allows Nanase access to limited information and observes what she does with it;
- he manages what Kei learns about his ability;
- he uses the Horikita mathematics wager to change both his own visibility and her student-council trajectory;
- he reconstructs the knife plan through the provenance of an object he was induced to buy.

This is not new capacity. It is the Year 1 developmental-author pattern operating at a wider scale.

The volume contains no basis for claiming that the environmental-author layer has become more accountable simply because its goals are defensive.

## 10.8 The knife wound — strategic genius and bodily instrumentalization

The climax is easy to romanticize because it is an extreme display of control.

Hōsen's trap depends on a planted knife that can be linked to Ayanokōji. If Hōsen appears stabbed by Ayanokōji in anger, the evidence could support expulsion.

Ayanokōji realizes the orientation of Hōsen's grip and the purpose of the weapon. Rather than simply avoiding it, he places his own palm into the blade:

> `自らの手のひらに突きさすようにして。`

Hōsen later recognizes the extremity of the act: `自分から刺されに来る`.

Strategically, the move is elegant. It reverses evidentiary authorship. The weapon that was supposed to create a false record against Ayanokōji now produces physical evidence inconsistent with the intended story and gives Ayanokōji leverage over Hōsen.

Psychologically, the scene is more disturbing.

Ayanokōji treats severe bodily injury as a usable cost with minimal ordinary hesitation. This is an inherited White Room pattern: the body is an instrument inside a solved optimization problem.

The scene should therefore produce **two simultaneous readings**:

- **competence:** extraordinary situational analysis and pain/risk control;
- **constraint:** an abnormal ease with converting his own body into a strategic resource.

Calling the act simply “cool” loses half the character evidence.

## 10.9 Defensive coercion is still coercion

After gaining leverage, Ayanokōji forces terms that solve several problems at once:

- Hōsen becomes his partner;
- first-year D enters the reciprocal arrangement Horikita wanted;
- the immediate expulsion plot collapses;
- and the violent incident can be contained.

The intervention is far easier to justify than many Year 1 manipulations because the threat is active, severe, and intentionally directed at him and his classmates. Less coercive alternatives are limited in the moment.

Still, the analytical method should not erase the structure:

> **Ayanokōji acquires decisive leverage and uses it to determine another actor's available choices.**

“Defensive” explains why the intervention may be proportionate. It does not make the asymmetry disappear.

## 10.10 Year 2 opening state

The best local formulation is:

> **Ayanokōji begins Year 2 more willing to author his own public identity because ordinary school life has become valuable enough to defend openly, but he still protects that self-authorship through a strategic grammar that treats information, environments, opponents, and even his own body as controllable variables.**

That is development without release from conditioning.

# 11. Relationships — intimacy grows inside persistent information asymmetry

## 11.1 Ayanokōji and Kei — domesticity, teaching, partial disclosure

The secret romance enters Year 2 not through a grand declaration but through ordinary coordination.

Kei visits Ayanokōji's room to study. He treats her academic weakness as a developmental problem rather than a moral failing. Because bullying disrupted her middle-school education, he recognizes that some gaps reflect interrupted opportunity rather than incapacity. His method is notably gentler than the coercive developmental interventions associated with him elsewhere: diagnose the current level, provide manageable instruction, and avoid needless scolding.

This is one of the volume's strongest examples relevant to `Y1H-001`:

> **Ayanokōji can cultivate someone without manufacturing a crisis.**

The question should not yet be marked fully answered because one healthy tutoring case does not establish a stable general practice. But it moves from pure openness toward `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED`, with evidence for a low-coercion mode of development.

Kei, meanwhile, seeks reassurance that he cares because she is his girlfriend. The dynamic contains ordinary teenage insecurity as well as the deeper Year 1 dependency problem.

## 11.2 Information symmetry improves slightly but remains far from sufficient

Ayanokōji gives Kei a more explicit estimate of his academic capability than most classmates possess. Her awareness that his visible performance is curated therefore increases.

But she still does not know the scale or provenance of the White Room threat, the twenty-million bounty, the knife incident, or the full logic by which he evaluates their relationship.

`Y1H-009` remains `STILL_OPEN`.

The volume offers **more disclosure, not sufficient disclosure**.

## 11.3 Kei and Satō — independent friendship survives painful truth

The most important Kei scene is not with Ayanokōji.

Satō confronts her because she suspects Kei and Ayanokōji are dating. Kei initially tries to preserve social cover, then admits:

> `清隆と付き合ってる`

Satō is hurt. She has already confessed to Ayanokōji and still likes him. She slaps Kei once, but the scene does not end in social destruction. The two speak through the betrayal Satō feels, Kei acknowledges that she was already becoming attracted while Satō had been consulting her, and the relationship survives.

This is meaningful evidence for `Y1H-010`.

Kei's social world is not shown collapsing into exclusive dependence on Ayanokōji. A friendship can absorb conflict produced by the romance and remain independently valuable.

The question is therefore best moved to `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED`: Year 2 Volume 1 supplies affirmative evidence that independent friendship persists, but it is too early to claim her broader judgment and social organization are no longer protector-centered.

## 11.4 Jealousy is not the same thing as relational equality

Kei's comic reaction to Amasawa's hair in Ayanokōji's room uses girlfriend “rights” language and suspicion. The scene confirms emotional investment and ordinary romantic possessiveness.

It does not resolve the structural asymmetry in information or power.

The method must resist treating visible jealousy as proof that the relationship has become equal simply because it looks conventionally romantic.

## 11.5 Ayanokōji and Horikita — more explanatory reciprocity, still unequal knowledge

Horikita repeatedly notices that Ayanokōji is telling her more than an earlier version of him would have. He explains more strategic reasoning, lets her own plan develop, and engages her as a political actor rather than merely an instrument whose behavior must be steered from offstage.

That is real relational development.

Yet the volume also demonstrates the remaining gulf:

- Horikita does not know the covert bounty until Nanase reveals it;
- she does not know the full White Room context;
- Ayanokōji can recognize the knife trap on a level she cannot;
- his hidden intervention still supplies decisive enforcement.

The relationship therefore becomes more reciprocal in **discussion** before it becomes equal in **information**.

## 11.6 Hiyori — ordinary continuity amid political escalation

The library connection with Hiyori continues quietly. She reserves a place and interacts with Ayanokōji without the strategic pressure surrounding most other encounters.

Its significance is disproportionate to plot impact: it is one of the places where a relationship remains valuable precisely because it is not immediately converted into political utility.

This supports the ordinary-life counter-curriculum thread.

# 12. Sudō Ken — development becomes increasingly self-sustaining

Sudō provides the cleanest genuine-development case in `Y2V01`.

## 12.1 The OAA record is already behind him

His first-year academic OAA is `E+ (20)`. Horikita's present estimate is around `D–D+`. He studied extensively during spring break and increasingly handles material without constant direct supervision.

This is not revelation of hidden academic ability. It is **development**.

The distinction matters because the same volume contains Ayanokōji's opposite case: a visible score changes because concealed capacity is revealed.

Sudō and Ayanokōji therefore form a useful paired contrast:

- **Sudō:** person changes faster than record;
- **Ayanokōji:** record changes because person chooses to reveal what was already there.

## 12.2 Motivation begins externally but is not purely external anymore

Sudō is candid that Horikita's praise remains a powerful motivator. He does not suddenly become a disinterested scholar.

But he also says:

> `問題が解けると結構楽しい`

That is a small but significant internalization. The reward structure is no longer only “Horikita will approve.” Solving itself has begun to produce satisfaction.

This does not mean his growth is fully independent of her. It means the motivational chain is becoming more complex.

## 12.3 His romantic reasoning is markedly more mature

Sudō asks whether confessing now would realistically produce a positive answer. He can recognize that it probably would not.

His plan is therefore to spend the remaining school years improving:

- academics;
- emotional regulation;
- usefulness to the class;
- and the qualities he believes make him a more credible partner.

The important point is not that self-improvement for romance is automatically healthy. It is that he is increasingly capable of **delayed gratification and prospective self-assessment**.

He can also imagine continuing the growth even if the desired romantic outcome fails, because abandoning it would make the effort itself feel hollow and would disappoint the standard he has begun to adopt.

## 12.4 Violence remains a live constraint

Hōsen violently attacking Horikita still activates Sudō's old reactive pattern. His growth is not complete.

Yet he can sometimes stop when she tells him to stop, and his general behavioral regulation is visibly stronger than at entrance.

The correct delta is therefore:

> `DEVELOPMENT` — substantial, incomplete, and increasingly self-reinforcing.

This is exactly the sort of trajectory an annual OAA snapshot risks obscuring if the historical score is treated as present essence.

# 13. Kōenji — autonomy remains resistant to class ownership

Kōenji rejects Horikita's attempt to allocate his partner with the tautological answer:

> `私が私であるからさ`

The line compresses his political philosophy. He does not justify autonomy by claiming it maximizes class utility. He treats self-ownership as prior to the class's demand.

Horikita, importantly, does not respond by treating him as permanently disposable. She still wants a future in which his ability becomes socially usable.

The tension from `Y1H-032` therefore persists:

- the class has legitimate collective risks;
- Kōenji possesses extraordinary private capacity;
- membership creates some expectations;
- but the exact boundary between membership obligation and collective ownership is unresolved.

His later willingness to pair when Nanase personally asks is also a warning against overgeneralization. “Kōenji refuses class coordination” is too coarse. He chooses when and through whom he will cooperate.

That is `RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC_EXPRESSION`, not a contradiction in character.

# 14. Student council, Nagumo, and the altered institutional curriculum

Year 2 does not simply add new students to the old school. The institution itself has changed.

## 14.1 OAA makes Nagumo's ideology infrastructural

Chabashira identifies OAA as an implementation of Nagumo's proposal to make individual ability more directly meaningful.

This is a major scale change. Year 1 Nagumo could exercise power through student-council authority and personal networks. OAA embeds part of his ideology in **school-wide information infrastructure**.

The political significance is larger than one app:

> **the definition of merit becomes something an office-holder can help encode into the environment through which all students see one another.**

`Y1H-030` remains open because OAA may increase individual mobility while still making students dependent on institutional ratings and the networks that can capitalize on them.

## 14.2 First-years enter with more information and fewer initial class points

Nanase explains that her cohort begins with 800 class points rather than the 1,000 the previous cohort initially experienced, and first-years appear to receive more explicit instruction about how conduct affects class points.

This is not a minor continuity detail.

The school has revised the curriculum after the previous year's experience. A new cohort begins under a different epistemic environment.

Thus “Class D at entrance” is not a perfectly controlled experiment across years. The institution learns too.

## 14.3 Reported relaxation of behavioral constraints requires source caution

Nanase says selected first-year representatives were told by Nagumo that the school would become more meritocratic and freer, including greater tolerance for minor fighting.

This is relevant to Hōsen's willingness to test boundaries.

But the evidence is **attributed testimony**, not a directly observed written regulation. The correct source treatment is:

- strong evidence that Nanase was given or reports such guidance;
- meaningful evidence of Nagumo's ideological influence;
- not yet an exhaustive statement of formal disciplinary law.

This distinction becomes important whenever student-council rhetoric is mistaken for complete institutional statute.

# 15. Tsukishiro, the hidden bounty, and administrative sovereignty

The most politically important revelation in the volume is not that Ayanokōji earns a perfect mathematics score. It is that the school environment contains a **second incentive architecture invisible to most participants**.

## 15.1 The twenty-million bounty

Nanase states that a small number of first-years have been given a special task under which expelling Ayanokōji yields twenty million private points.

The amount is not incidental. Twenty million points is the established price associated with buying a transfer to another class. The bounty therefore translates one student's removal into a prize capable of transforming another student's institutional future.

Ayanokōji becomes literally priced as an obstacle.

## 15.2 The hidden rule creates unequal epistemic citizenship

Horikita objects not merely because the bounty targets her classmate but because the participants are not operating under the same known rules.

That objection is justified.

The public exam says deliberate sabotage can be punished. The hidden incentive encourages a subset of students to seek Ayanokōji's expulsion through whatever means they can make survive institutional scrutiny.

The problem is therefore:

> **some students have access to a rule that changes the value of the same action for them.**

This is a deeper asymmetry than OAA ranking because it concerns knowledge of the governing game itself.

## 15.3 Public transparency about students coexists with opacity about governors

The structural irony can now be stated fully.

OAA makes student records searchable across the school. Ayanokōji's friends, rivals, and strangers can inspect a standardized representation of him.

But Chabashira apparently does not know about the twenty-million bounty. Ordinary participants cannot inspect the administrative process that created it. The person being evaluated becomes transparent downward while the authority creating exceptional rules remains opaque upward.

This is **surveillance without reciprocal transparency**.

`Y1H-039`, `Y1H-040`, and `Y1H-042` are therefore complicated rather than answered:

- ANHS still contains plural actors who can help Ayanokōji;
- teachers such as Chabashira and Mashima can provide institutional counterweight;
- but administrative capture can author decisive incentives beyond their knowledge.

The school remains meaningfully different from the White Room. The difference is not yet secure enough to treat it as a fully reliable alternative.

## 15.4 Tsukishiro confirms the existence of an expulsion plan but remains adversarial testimony

At the end Ayanokōji confronts Tsukishiro. Tsukishiro admits that he had prepared a plan intended to make Ayanokōji's expulsion certain, then says:

> `しかし、あの子はそれを無視した`

This is important evidence for `Y1H-036`.

The core Year 1 uncertainty—whether Tsukishiro's White Room account was merely bluff—is narrowed because `Y2V01` independently opens with the White Room student receiving a mission briefing. The existence of an embedded White Room student is no longer merely Tsukishiro's later adversarial claim.

But Tsukishiro's finer statements remain suspect. He says the student ignored his prepared route and implies a degree of independence from both himself and perhaps Atsuomi.

Ayanokōji does not simply believe him. He retains substantial probability that Tsukishiro is lying or manipulating him.

The appropriate handoff transition is therefore:

> **`Y1H-036` — `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED`: the existence of a White Room operative and active expulsion project is independently confirmed; the reliability of Tsukishiro's detailed account of command, allegiance, and the student's deviation remains unresolved.**

That is exactly the distinction the identity/allegiance protocol exists to preserve.

# 16. Ethics — equality, coercion, protection, and the right to author another person's risk

## 16.1 OAA is not inherently dehumanizing, but its uses can become jurisdictional

The volume gives OAA genuine benefits:

- easier coordination;
- recognition of broad competencies;
- developmental feedback;
- cross-year discoverability;
- a persistent record that can show improvement.

A categorical rejection of measurement would therefore be too simple.

The ethical risk begins when a partial measure becomes a license to decide the whole person's future. A low score can mark a student as economically undesirable. A high score can make a first-year a commodity in a bidding war. The model becomes political because other people are allowed to act on it.

The correct ethical standard is not “never measure.” It is:

> **keep the model bounded, corrigible, contextual, and answerable to the person whose opportunities it affects.**

## 16.2 Horikita's reciprocal contract is normatively stronger because it anticipates reversal

Horikita's insistence on no creditor hierarchy is not altruism detached from strategy. It is strategic precisely because she anticipates that power changes direction.

A relationship that is fair only while one party is stronger is not a stable reciprocal institution.

Her proposal therefore has a genuinely constitutional dimension:

> **design the relationship now so that future vulnerability does not require submission.**

## 16.3 Hōsen exploits real institutional asymmetry; the school shares causal responsibility

Hōsen is responsible for violence, extortion, and attempted evidentiary fabrication.

But the school created the environment in which first-years can rationally extract payment from second-years by assigning dramatically unequal penalties.

Recognizing institutional causation does not absolve Hōsen. It prevents the opposite error: pretending his behavior emerges in a vacuum.

## 16.4 Nanase demonstrates how collective welfare can rationalize abuse

Nanase's justification is recognizably consequentialist: twenty million points could materially transform her class, and allowing another class to obtain them may be dangerous.

The weakness is that Ayanokōji's status as a person becomes subordinate to a class-level benefit he did not consent to provide.

Her concern for classmates is real. The means nevertheless treat an outsider as disposable.

The scene therefore anticipates a recurring Year 2 question:

> **When does responsibility to one's own polity cease to justify imposing catastrophic costs on someone outside it?**

## 16.5 Ayanokōji's response is comparatively proportionate but still raises bodily-autonomy questions

His knife reversal is directed against an active attack and prevents expulsion, protects Horikita and Sudō, and exposes the conspiracy. This gives it a far stronger necessity/proportionality case than many of his developmental manipulations.

The ethically strange part is his relation to **himself**.

He can consent to risk his own body. Yet the ease with which he turns his hand into evidence raises a psychological question different from third-party consent:

> **Does a person trained to treat his body as an instrument possess ordinary access to self-protective preference, or does conditioning distort what “voluntary” self-risk means?**

The volume does not answer it. It makes the question difficult to avoid.

# 17. Japanese narration, voice, and register

Several Japanese-language details carry analytical weight beyond plot information.

## 17.1 `実力` versus measured output

The chapter title `実力とは` asks what “real ability” is just as OAA introduces a formal attempt to quantify it.

The title does not make OAA the answer. The surrounding scenes immediately fracture `実力` into:

- what is possessed;
- what is displayed;
- what is measured;
- what others can use;
- and what an institution rewards.

The title term should therefore continue to be treated as contested vocabulary, not a synonym for power level.

## 17.2 `平等` is narrower than `公平`

Chabashira's phrase `このアプリは平等だ` uses `平等`—equal treatment/equality—not an exhaustive claim that every outcome is normatively fair under every context.

That lexical distinction matters. The system is equal in the sense that class rank does not directly alter the rating rule. Questions of disability, observability, context, and downstream opportunity remain.

## 17.3 `対等` is Horikita's political keyword

Horikita repeatedly uses `対等` for the desired D-to-D relationship.

This is stronger than “friendly.” `対等` concerns standing: neither side above nor below the other.

The repeated term reveals that her objection to Hōsen is not just his price. It is the jurisdictional hierarchy the price would establish.

## 17.4 `信頼関係` is used across competing political models

Trust is not exclusive to Ichinose.

The volume repeatedly uses `信頼関係` while distinguishing different ways of producing it:

- Ichinose through rescue and accessibility;
- Horikita through explicit reciprocal contract;
- Sakayanagi and Ryūen through prestige, money, and future usefulness;
- Nanase as a normative argument against Hōsen's forced individualism.

“Trust” is therefore not automatically virtuous. Its political meaning depends on how the relationship is created and what happens when interests diverge.

## 17.5 Nanase's self-reference shift is evidence, not solution

`ボクは、じゃなかった───私は` is conspicuous because Japanese first-person choice is socially and personally meaningful.

At this endpoint the safest reading is exactly what the text gives us: a moment in which one self-reference is corrected into another.

Any stronger explanation is outside the local evidence boundary.

## 17.6 Ayanokōji's internal register still moves between dry adolescent observation and cold optimization

His narration can feel mildly embarrassed by being called a friend, puzzled by cooking, amused by social situations, and concerned about ordinary awkwardness.

The same voice can then calculate the knife plot, evaluate the cost of injury, and treat attention from a perfect mathematics score as a controllable strategic variable.

The alternation matters more than either register alone.

Ayanokōji is not best modeled as one “true cold voice” underneath a fake ordinary voice. Year 2 begins with multiple real modes whose integration remains incomplete.

# 18. Illustration and embodied characterization

The EPUB contains twenty-two internal image assets, including chapter/ornamental material and a smaller set of full narrative illustrations. The illustrations were reviewed as visual paratext. They establish composition, bodily emphasis, and editorially selected moments; they do not independently prove interior motive.

## 18.1 Horikita, Sudō, and Kushida — comedy around relational tension

The illustration around Horikita's “resolution” conversation places Horikita vertically above a dramatically startled Sudō and Kushida. Horikita's expression is comparatively flat while the two foreground figures react with exaggerated surprise.

The composition visually reinforces a recurring social asymmetry: Horikita can deliver a severe or ambiguous statement with minimal affect while others experience the relational shock more visibly.

It is comic staging, not evidence that she lacks emotion.

## 18.2 Hōsen and Horikita — scale becomes political information

The first major Hōsen/Horikita image emphasizes physical scale. Hōsen occupies a large portion of the foreground with a relaxed, imposing posture; Horikita stands behind him, smaller in frame but composed.

The image makes bodily asymmetry immediately legible before the later confrontation literalizes it.

Crucially, the prose does not allow that physical dominance to settle political legitimacy. The volume repeatedly separates capacity to impose force from right to govern.

## 18.3 Nanase's smiling introduction — visual accessibility against epistemic uncertainty

Nanase receives a bright, open portrait with closed-eye smile and hand near her chest. The image makes her visually approachable at the exact stage where the narrative begins making her difficult to classify.

This is editorially useful because it refuses an easy “ominous infiltrator” visual code. Her later complicity cannot be read backward as though the portrait were a lie; rather, approachable social presentation and dangerous choices coexist.

## 18.4 Ayanokōji and Kei studying — the romance is given domestic visual space

The study illustration places Ayanokōji and Kei close together over ordinary school materials. There is no examination spectacle, violence, or public performance. The visual emphasis is domestic and mundane.

That choice supports the ordinary-life reading of the relationship. The romance is not represented only through declarations or jealousy; it occupies shared time and physical space organized around studying.

Again, domesticity does not erase information asymmetry. The image establishes closeness, not equality of knowledge.

## 18.5 Nanase in casual clothing — the first-year cast is allowed existence outside school-role diagrams

A later illustration presents Nanase in casual summer clothing in a more dynamic, open pose. This contributes to an important Year 2 visual principle: first-years are not introduced only as OAA profiles, class functions, or White Room candidates.

The publication gives them bodies, clothing, affect, and ordinary presence that exceed the analytical suspicion imposed on them by the plot.

That visual fact supports the methodological warning against reducing new characters to “candidate infiltrator” labels.

## 18.6 Illustration policy going forward

The V2 project should continue separating:

- **what the prose establishes;**
- **what the image literally shows;**
- **what editorial selection emphasizes;**
- **what remains interpretive.**

In a series preoccupied with dossiers, scores, and observation, the illustration itself is another partial file about a person—not transparent access to the whole person.

# 19. Formal and literary strengths

## 19.1 The volume makes an exposition-heavy restart thematically productive

A new school year requires large amounts of information:

- new rules;
- new students;
- new class points;
- OAA definitions;
- an examination structure;
- White Room threat;
- new inter-year relationships.

The novel avoids making all of this inert exposition by turning information itself into the object of conflict. OAA is exposition about students that changes student behavior. The exam rules are exposition whose asymmetry creates a market. The hidden bounty is withheld exposition that explains why ordinary negotiation fails.

The volume is therefore about **who possesses which exposition**.

## 19.2 Public and hidden systems mirror each other

The strongest formal construction is the pairing of:

- OAA, which publicly exposes partial student data;
- the bounty, which privately exposes an exceptional rule to a select few.

This produces a structural reversal:

> the governed become more visible while governance becomes selectively invisible.

The thematic argument is embedded in the distribution of information rather than delivered as a speech.

## 19.3 The White Room mystery is strongest when the book refuses premature solution

The prologue guarantees that the reader knows more than the cast in one respect: a White Room student exists.

But the volume then proliferates plausible candidates and alternative explanations. The result is not only a mystery mechanism. It forces the reader to experience the danger of **profiling from partial data** at the same moment OAA institutionalizes that practice.

This parallel is one of the volume's most interesting formal effects.

## 19.4 Ordinary-life scenes are multifunctional without becoming reducible to clues

The cooking episode is both:

- a comic ordinary-life scene;
- evidence of Ayanokōji learning an unfamiliar domestic skill;
- a Horikita/Ayanokōji coordination exercise;
- an Amasawa characterization scene;
- and the origin of the planted knife.

The later strategic function does not erase the ordinary meaning. The scene works because the same event belongs to several layers of life.

## 19.5 The ending changes the meaning of “visibility” without resolving identity

Ayanokōji's perfect mathematics score is a public revelation placed just before the private Tsukishiro confrontation.

The order is effective:

1. Ayanokōji chooses to make one part of himself more visible;
2. the class reacts to the new file;
3. he confronts the actor responsible for a hidden rule;
4. the actual White Room identity remains unknown.

Year 2 thus begins with **more information and more uncertainty at the same time**.

# 20. Critical reservations and counterreadings

A strong second pass should preserve several counterarguments.

## 20.1 Do not turn OAA into a simplistic surveillance-state allegory

OAA has real educational utility. Students can find partners, locate complementary strengths, recognize improvement, and discover peers outside their existing networks. Chabashira explicitly acknowledges fallibility and year-specific updating.

The criticism is not that quantification necessarily equals oppression. It is that public quantification changes power relations and can be overextended beyond its evidentiary limits.

## 20.2 Do not declare Horikita independent too early

Her political judgment is substantially more mature and independently authored.

But the Hōsen problem still requires hidden Ayanokōji leverage for final enforcement. A Year 2 synthesis that cites this volume as proof she no longer depends on shadow sovereignty would overstate the evidence.

## 20.3 Do not confuse Ayanokōji's visibility change with emotional transparency

Scoring 100 is a major public act. It is still a controlled strategic disclosure.

The volume does not show him giving classmates broad access to his history, motives, vulnerability, or the danger surrounding him.

## 20.4 Do not turn Ryūen's strategic maturity into ethical rehabilitation

He is more patient, more market-aware, and more capable of long-horizon planning.

Nothing in this volume proves that he has adopted a principled limitation on coercion.

## 20.5 Do not reduce Hōsen to intelligence because the brute stereotype is wrong

Correcting “Hōsen is stupid” should not produce the opposite exaggeration “Hōsen is a master strategist equal to Ayanokōji.” His plan is dangerous and sophisticated enough to matter; it also depends on collaborators, hidden bounty information, and a planted-evidence route that Ayanokōji defeats.

## 20.6 Do not treat Nanase's class concern as proof of moral innocence

Her concern is credible. She nevertheless collaborates in an expulsion plot using violence and fabricated evidence.

Sincere ends and wrongful means can coexist.

## 20.7 Do not identify the White Room student through hindsight

This is the most important interpretive limit.

At the `Y2V01` boundary, Ayanokōji himself concludes that none of the observed first-years provides decisive evidence of White Room origin. The local artifact must preserve that uncertainty even if a later reader knows more.

# 27. Final synthesis

Year 2 Volume 1 begins with two acts of classification.

In the first, Tsukishiro opens a secret file containing extraordinarily detailed information about Ayanokōji and the second-year cohort and gives it to an unnamed White Room student.

In the second, ANHS opens OAA to the student body and gives students a simplified file on one another.

The two systems differ radically in secrecy, depth, legitimacy, and purpose. Their juxtaposition nevertheless establishes the volume's governing problem:

> **To know a person in administratively useful terms is to acquire power over the situations in which that person must act.**

OAA is the benign and productive version of that power. It helps people find partners. It lets improvement become visible. It makes class rank irrelevant to the formal rating. It preserves a historical record rather than pretending one score is eternal. Chabashira even warns that the school can be wrong.

The pairing examination turns that useful information into a market.

Once the second-years face expulsion and first-years face a much smaller immediate penalty, academic ratings become prices. High-scoring first-years gain bargaining leverage. Weak students risk being stranded. Sakayanagi can purchase excellence. Ryūen can distort the market. Ichinose can convert trust into access. Horikita can attempt to build a reciprocal institution outside the price hierarchy.

The school has not merely measured ability.

It has changed what ability **does**.

That is the first major Year 2 expansion of `実力`. Possession, display, measurement, social usability, political use, and developmental trajectory are all different objects.

Ayanokōji is the clearest demonstration. OAA's mediocre profile is not simply “wrong.” It is reasonably accurate about the interface he deliberately offered Year 1. His mathematics 100 does not make him more capable. It changes which part of the capability can enter the official and social record.

His action is therefore best understood as self-authorship through controlled legibility.

The phrase matters because it prevents two opposite mistakes.

One mistake is to treat every reveal as proof that earlier Ayanokōji was fake. He was performing normality, but his awkward pleasure at friendship, domestic ignorance, growing school attachment, and relationship with Kei are not thereby erased.

The opposite mistake is to treat increased visibility as increased relational transparency. The 100 tells classmates something true about him. It does not tell them why he hid it, where he learned it, who is trying to remove him, or why a first-year conspiracy exists around his expulsion.

He becomes **more visible while remaining deeply unknown**.

Horikita supplies the volume's most important countermodel of authorship.

She also studies people, categorizes needs, allocates scarce resources, and creates an environment intended to produce a particular outcome. Developmental authorship is therefore not unique to Ayanokōji.

The difference lies in **answerability**.

Her proposed alliance tries to eliminate a point-created hierarchy. It incorporates students across the academic distribution. It depends upon explicit negotiation between classes. It treats trust as something reciprocal parties can build rather than a hidden condition she can engineer alone.

That does not make her perfectly autonomous or perfectly legitimate. Hōsen can reject the entire framework. Nanase can test her. Ayanokōji can see layers she cannot. The final agreement becomes possible only after concealed leverage changes Hōsen's incentives.

But the political development is real.

Horikita is increasingly trying to create structures in which other actors remain participants.

Hōsen supplies the opposite model. His `個人主義` is not freedom. Nanase's crucial qualification is that he **forces** it. Students are placed into a system where refusing the leader does not create collective independence; it creates isolated vulnerability. Hōsen's politics are therefore authoritarian precisely because he calls upon individuals to survive alone inside conditions he controls.

Ayanokōji defeats that system through a form of sovereignty of his own.

The knife scene is the volume's most spectacular act of proof production. He reads the setup, allows injury, converts fingerprints and bodily state into superior evidence, and obtains leverage sufficient to compel an agreement.

It is tempting to read the scene purely as mastery.

The second pass should resist that pleasure long enough to notice the cost.

Ayanokōji's body is simultaneously:

- his own;
- a weapon;
- a record;
- and an expendable input to a proof strategy.

The child produced by the White Room has escaped the institution but still carries an internal calculus in which pain can be subordinated instantly to outcome.

Self-authorship does not mean the inherited author has disappeared.

The White Room mystery closes the volume on the same epistemic problem with which OAA began it.

The reader knows there is a White Room student.

The reader sees many exceptional or suspicious first-years.

Those facts do not jointly identify anyone.

Amasawa's knife work proves unusual planning and bounty participation. Nanase's knowledge proves access to a hidden rule and personal hostility. Hōsen's violence proves formidable embodied ability and political coercion. Tsubaki and Utomiya prove independent negotiation. Yagami presents strong social and academic competence.

None of those profiles is identical to institutional origin.

The method must therefore practice the principle the novel is dramatizing:

> **A file can be accurate about what it contains and still be radically insufficient to identify the whole person.**

That is why Year 2 begins not by superseding the Year 1 thesis but by operationalizing it.

Year 1 concluded that understanding does not grant jurisdiction.

Year 2 Volume 1 adds:

> **Visibility does not grant total knowledge, measurement does not grant ownership, and transparency about subjects does not make sovereign power transparent in return.**

The public school app tells everyone more.

The hidden bounty tells almost everyone nothing.

Between those two information systems stand students trying to decide which forms of knowledge should become cooperation, price, leverage, trust, suspicion, or control.

That is the real beginning of Year 2.
