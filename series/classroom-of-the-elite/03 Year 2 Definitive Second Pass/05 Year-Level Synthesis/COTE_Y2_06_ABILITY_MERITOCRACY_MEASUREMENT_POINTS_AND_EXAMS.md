# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 2 — Ability, Meritocracy, Measurement, Points, and Examinations
### From public legibility to task-value inversion: what the school can measure, what rules can price, and what no score is entitled to own

> **Canonical specialist synthesis at the complete Year-2 / Second List boundary.**
>
> This document is the primary Year-2 home for `実力`, ability ontology, OAA, public legibility, rating change, class/private/exam-local point systems, protection and mobility assets, special-examination conversion systems, task-dependent value, meritocratic ideology, score interpretation, and the boundary between measured performance and person-level capacity. It synthesizes the frozen Year-1 specialist, the canonical through-Year-2 ability and examination ledgers, the immutable source-local readings, the completed `Y2_01–Y2_05` specialist layer, and the Year-2 *Second List* boundary audit. It does not replace those source-local authorities and imports no Year-3 narrative evidence.

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

The series title invites one of the most persistent analytical errors in the corpus.

A school organized around `実力` appears to promise that sufficiently rigorous testing will eventually reveal who is actually strong.

Year 2 makes that proposition harder, not easier.

The school gains a public rating system.

Students become more numerically legible.

Their ratings become searchable across classes and years.

Special examinations become more elaborate.

Private points can insure failure, purchase cooperation, and finance mobility.

Class points translate competitive performance into institutional rank.

Protection assets distribute immunity unevenly.

A transfer ticket can convert personal performance into escape from class fate.

One exam rewards the strongest academics.

Another makes a lower academic rating *more* valuable when the student has developed beneath the old label.

A later exam turns knowledge of another person's habits, microexpressions, loyalties, and likely lies into formal competitive capital.

By the end of Year 2, the problem is no longer whether ANHS can measure students.

It plainly can.

The problem is:

> **What exactly did the institution measure, under what observation regime, what did a particular rule make that measurement worth, and what legitimate conclusion may be drawn from the result?**

This specialist applies twelve strict controls.

## 0.1 `実力` is not one hidden universal stat

`実力` concerns actual or effective ability rather than prestige, title, pedigree, or reputation.

That does not make it context-free.

Actual ability can mean different things depending on whether the task demands:

- academic knowledge;
- physical endurance;
- tactical adaptation;
- social memory;
- negotiation;
- information security;
- teaching;
- coordination;
- refusal;
- leadership;
- or the ability to make other people more capable.

The analysis therefore rejects the idea that every student possesses one true scalar score underneath the school's imperfect approximations.

## 0.2 Capacity, expression, observation, rating, and task value are different variables

The specialist keeps five layers separate:

`possessed capacity`

`→ expressed / available performance`

`→ institutionally observed evidence`

`→ recorded rating`

`→ task-specific value under a rule`

A change at one layer does not automatically imply change at every other layer.

Ayanokōji can reveal old capacity without acquiring it during the year.

Sudō can genuinely develop while an older rating remains stale.

Kōenji can possess immense capacity while refusing to make it available.

V09 can increase the marginal value of a low rating without making the student intrinsically superior.

## 0.3 Every measurement has an object, date, observer, and consequence

A score can be accurate about:

- a written test;
- a public month of conduct;
- one physical event;
- one observation window;
- one set of selected axes;
- or performance under one special-exam rule.

The error occurs when the object silently expands from:

> performance under these conditions

into:

> the person's complete and permanent worth.

## 0.4 Measurement error and measurement-object mismatch are not the same thing

Sometimes the file is wrong.

Sometimes the file is right about the thing it actually measured and wrong only because an observer asks it to answer a different question.

Ayanokōji's early OAA profile is not random institutional incompetence. It is a reasonable model of the surface he made available.

Ryūen's social-contribution score can be low while his social causality is enormous because prosocial contribution and coercive strategic influence are different objects.

Sakayanagi's physical limitation can depress an aggregate without implying low political intelligence.

A model can be accurate and still be badly used.

## 0.5 Public measurement is reflexive

Once ratings become public, the sequence is no longer simply:

`person → measurement`.

It becomes:

`person → measurement → social response → changed opportunity/risk → changed behavior → later measurement`.

OAA therefore participates in the environment it claims to describe.

## 0.6 Examinations are authored conversion systems

An examination chooses:

- the operative unit;
- the rewarded capacities;
- the price of weakness;
- the value of cooperation;
- the availability of rescue;
- the information structure;
- the visibility of authorship;
- and the terminal consequences.

It therefore converts selected capacity into institutional outcome.

A result is real inside that conversion system.

It is not a timeless proof of human hierarchy.

## 0.7 Point currencies must never be collapsed

This specialist distinguishes:

- **class points**;
- **private points**;
- **exam-local points**;
- **OAA values**;
- **Protection Points / protection assets**;
- **cards and insurance assets**;
- **transfer tickets**;
- **other mobility options**.

The word “points” is not analytically sufficient.

The currency must be named before the number.

## 0.8 Price is not moral value

A rescue price, transfer price, or point reward tells us what the institution or market requires to alter an outcome.

It does not tell us what a person's life, belonging, relationship, or future is morally worth.

## 0.9 Score is not causal authorship

A winning score may be clean while the route to it includes:

- hidden intervention;
- opponent interruption;
- deception;
- administrative misconduct;
- delegation;
- social coordination;
- or strategic choices that the result table does not encode.

The score is evidence of outcome.

It is not automatically evidence of who authored the outcome.

## 0.10 Individual merit is not polity development

A class can win because one exceptional person solves the decisive problem.

That does not prove distributed competence or succession resilience.

`Y2_05` remains the primary home for that constitutional distinction.

## 0.11 Development and revelation can coexist

A hidden prodigy can genuinely develop.

An old low-rated student can outgrow the file.

A rating increase can mix:

- previously hidden capacity;
- changed participation;
- changed observation;
- and real new development.

The analysis must not force one cause where the source supports several.

## 0.12 Year-3 lock

This document ends at `Y2SL`.

Any post-transfer evidence belongs to Year 3 and is inadmissible here.

# 1. Executive thesis — Year 2 turns merit from a ranking problem into a conversion-and-jurisdiction problem

Year 1 established that ability is real, plural, context-sensitive, developable, and incompletely represented by institutional files.

Year 2 operationalizes those conclusions.

The school now publishes a live model of students and repeatedly constructs temporary worlds that decide what those modeled capacities are worth.

The deepest progression is:

> **legibility → market value → insurance → sacrifice → mobility → contribution capital → developmental-value inversion → protection scarcity → relational knowledge as formal ability.**

The crucial Year-2 finding is that four questions must be kept separate:

1. **What can this person do?**
2. **What has the institution observed this person doing?**
3. **What will this person actually make available here?**
4. **What does this particular rule make that capacity worth?**

Ayanokōji separates possession from expression.

Kōenji separates possession from availability.

Sudō separates old rating from present capacity.

Horikita separates personal ability from distributed/generative ability.

Ryūen separates prosocial contribution from social causality.

Ichinose separates everyday relational density from task environments that finally make that density formally valuable.

Sakayanagi separates physical limitation from broad general capability.

Hiyori separates quiet, relationship-specific judgment from broad social-performance proxies.

Kei separates academic profile from group-atmosphere coordination.

Kushida separates high social capacity from benign moral use.

The exam sequence then shows that even a correct measurement has no fixed practical meaning.

In V01, a low academic profile can create vulnerability.

In V09, the same kind of low profile can become a multiplier if development has outpaced the file.

Thus the specialist's central formula is:

> **measurement does not equal merit; merit does not equal task value; task value does not equal personhood; and institutional consequence does not become legitimate merely because it is numerically precise.**

The strongest Year-2 meritocratic question is therefore not:

> Who is best?

It is:

> **Which differences should count, for what purpose, under whose rule, with what possibility of development, refusal, correction, and appeal?**

# 2. Frozen Year-1 baseline and Year-2 escalation

The Year-1 specialist remains immutable historical authority at `Y1FF`.

Year 2 preserves its central propositions while making several of them materially harder.

## 2.1 Preserved Year-1 propositions

Year 2 does not overturn these conclusions:

1. ability is real and unequal;
2. `実力` is not one universal hidden stat;
3. every measurement has a bounded object and date;
4. possessed capacity differs from expressed performance;
5. development can make a once-correct model stale;
6. academic, physical, social, strategic, political, and generative capacities are non-identical;
7. contribution is not personhood;
8. Kōenji proves capacity is not automatically collective property;
9. Sudō proves developmental uncertainty matters;
10. record integrity is necessary for meaningful merit claims;
11. an honest file can remain incomplete;
12. ordinary life reveals capacities formal testing underweights.

## 2.2 What Year 2 adds

Year 2 strengthens the framework in six major ways.

### Publicity

The file becomes searchable and therefore socially causal.

### Reflexivity

A score changes opportunities, bargaining power, expectations, and risk, helping create later performance.

### Availability

The analysis must distinguish a student's capacity from willingness to supply it.

### Task-value inversion

The same rating can imply opposite marginal value under different exam rules.

### Generativity

Ability increasingly appears in other people's improved performance rather than the original actor's own score.

### Counter-authorship

The strongest developmental evidence is not merely better predicted performance but the capacity to produce an answer a prior model did not own.

## 2.3 Year 2 therefore rejects two opposite simplifications

The first simplification is institutional absolutism:

> the school has better data, therefore it now knows what students really are.

The second is anti-measurement relativism:

> metrics fail to capture everything, therefore scores mean nothing.

Both are wrong.

A mathematics score means something.

A physical result means something.

OAA means something.

A class-point total means something.

The analytical discipline is to keep each meaning bounded.

# 3. Japanese conceptual vocabulary and analytical translation discipline

## 3.1 `実力 / jitsuryoku`

`実力` points toward actual, effective, or demonstrated ability rather than prestige or appearance.

The corpus repeatedly resists treating “actual” as context-free.

A person's `実力` is expressed through an environment, a goal, a willingness to act, and an observer's access to the performance.

For this project, avoid translating the analytical concept into an imagined “true power level.”

Prefer:

> **actual capacity for a specified purpose under specified conditions.**

## 3.2 `能力 / nōryoku`

`能力` is the broader capacity term.

It is useful where ability can exist prior to demonstration.

Ayanokōji and Kōenji make the distinction especially important.

## 3.3 `学力 / gakuryoku`

Academic ability is among the most straightforward domains to standardize.

Year 2 nevertheless proves:

`academic ability ≠ total ability`

`academic rating ≠ current task value`

`academic knowledge ≠ teaching ability`

`academic rating ≠ developmental potential`

`academic capacity ≠ willingness to contribute`.

V09 makes these distinctions mechanical rather than merely philosophical.

## 3.4 `身体能力 / shintai nōryoku`

Physical capacity is genuinely relevant where tasks require speed, endurance, coordination, strength, or bodily resilience.

Its relevance is domain-dependent.

Sakayanagi's disability is therefore both a real limitation and a warning against aggregate overreach.

## 3.5 `機転思考力`

The live OAA axis roughly captures adaptive / quick-thinking / socially responsive performance.

It is not identical to:

- abstract intelligence;
- strategy;
- empathy;
- leadership;
- relational judgment;
- or moral insight.

Hiyori is particularly useful as a boundary case.

## 3.6 `社会貢献性`

Social contribution concerns institutionally observed conduct, contribution, cooperation, and school-facing social behavior.

It is not a general social-power statistic.

Ryūen may be low in prosocial contribution and extraordinarily high in social causality.

Kushida may score strongly on visible social performance while using that capacity destructively.

## 3.7 `総合 / overall`

An aggregate is mathematically meaningful for the model and weighting chosen.

It is not proof that every component should be commensurable for every purpose.

The aggregate is a model output, not ontology.

## 3.8 `実力主義` and `実力至上主義`

The corpus does not support either a simple endorsement or a simple rejection of meritocratic ordering.

The relevant question is what counts as merit and whether the institution preserves:

- task fit;
- developmental updating;
- refusal;
- record integrity;
- proportional consequence;
- and correction.

A system can recognize real ability differences while still misuse them politically.

# 4. A Year-2 ontology of ability

The Year-2 corpus now supports a more precise vocabulary than generic statements such as “X is smart” or “Y is strong.”

## 4.1 `POSSESSED_CAPACITY`

Evidence supports underlying capability regardless of whether the student currently displays it.

Ayanokōji and Kōenji are major cases.

## 4.2 `DEVELOPMENTAL_CHANGE`

The person genuinely becomes more capable over time.

Sudō is the cleanest academic/behavioral example.

## 4.3 `EXPRESSION_CHANGE`

The ability may be old, but the person changes how much of it becomes visible.

Ayanokōji's V01 mathematics reveal is canonical.

## 4.4 `AVAILABILITY_CHANGE`

The person changes willingness to make capacity usable by another actor, class, or institution.

Kōenji is the strongest case.

## 4.5 `OBSERVATION_CHANGE`

The institution obtains better evidence without proving intrinsic personal change.

## 4.6 `RATING_CHANGE`

The official model changes.

Rating change by itself does not identify which upstream cause changed.

## 4.7 `TASK_VALUE_CHANGE`

The same capacity becomes more or less valuable because the environment changes.

V01/V09 is the canonical pair.

## 4.8 `ECOLOGICAL_ABILITY`

A capacity becomes useful because a specific environment makes it relevant.

The island exams are foundational examples.

## 4.9 `COORDINATION_CHANGE`

A person or polity becomes better able to combine several capacities into collective action.

## 4.10 `GENERATIVE_OUTPUT`

A person's ability increases another person's ability.

Teaching, coaching, recruitment, and developmental leadership belong here.

## 4.11 `CONSTITUTIONAL_CAPACITY`

A polity can distribute, correct, and reproduce judgment beyond one exceptional actor.

This is the bridge to `Y2_05`.

## 4.12 `RELATIONAL_SPECIFICITY`

Competence may exist only in relation to a particular person, group, or history.

V12 makes this exam-relevant.

## 4.13 `MODEL_REVISION`

A previous estimate becomes inadequate because new evidence, changed expression, development, or task structure changes the inference.

## 4.14 `COUNTER_AUTHORSHIP`

The modeled person produces an answer the model/developer did not possess in advance and thereby forces revision.

This is stronger than generic surprise.

It is developmental evidence that the model is no longer sufficient to own the person's future response.

# 5. Live OAA — a public model of institutionally legible performance

Y2V01 turns the Year-1 file problem into live infrastructure.

`Y2V01-E007–E016` establish the primary source route.

OAA exposes profiles across classes and years and records selected dimensions such as:

- academic performance;
- physical ability;
- quick-thinking/adaptive-social performance;
- social contribution;
- an aggregate overall rating.

The key analytical point is not that the model is false.

Ayanokōji himself can regard his initial numbers as reasonable given what he has made visible.

The correct object is:

> **institutionally legible performance under the school's observation regime.**

That is narrower than latent ceiling.

## 5.1 Ayanokōji's opening profile

At the beginning of Year 2:

| Axis | Opening value |
|---|---:|
| Academic | `C (51)` |
| Physical | `C+ (60)` |
| Quick-thinking / adaptive-social | `D+ (37)` |
| Social contribution | `C+ (60)` |
| Overall | `C (51)` |

These numbers describe a surface shaped partly by deliberate underexpression.

They are evidence about the school's data.

They are not evidence that his latent capacity is actually C-level.

## 5.2 Publicity turns OAA into intervention

Once visible, the file can be used to:

- recruit;
- avoid;
- price;
- insure;
- protect;
- target;
- classify;
- bargain;
- and infer risk.

Measurement therefore alters the environment around the student.

This is why OAA is not merely documentary.

It is performative infrastructure.

## 5.3 Annual preservation is analytically valuable

The system's preservation of temporal states matters.

A prior rating can remain accurate as historical evidence while becoming stale as a current predictor.

That is better than silently rewriting the past.

The correct model is longitudinal, not replacement-based.

# 6. Ayanokōji — revelation and development in the same rating trajectory

Ayanokōji provides the best case for why rating change cannot be equated with personal growth.

## 6.1 Mathematics 100 — expression policy changes

At the end of Y2V01, Ayanokōji deliberately scores 100 in mathematics (`Y2V01-E058–E059`), including material beyond normal curriculum expectations.

The strongest classification is:

- preexisting capacity: high;
- expression: deliberately increased;
- observation: sharply improved;
- public reputation: changed;
- rating: later changes accordingly.

This is not a sudden acquisition of mathematics ability.

## 6.2 *Second List* supplies the cleanest OAA sequence

`Y2SL-E086` preserves:

| Snapshot | Academic | Physical | Quick-thinking | Social | Overall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| April | C 51 | C+ 60 | D+ 37 | C+ 60 | C 51 |
| May | A- 81 | B- 61 | D+ 40 | B 68 | B- 62 |
| March | A 87 | B 73 | C 54 | B 70 | B 71 |

The naive interpretation is rapid across-the-board development.

The opposite naive interpretation is that all change is merely revelation.

The source supports neither reduction.

The better model is:

`latent capacity`

`+ changed willingness to display`

`+ new institutional observation`

`+ genuine social/ordinary-life development`

`= changed OAA profile`.

## 6.3 Public self-authorship becomes part of the ability story

The important development is not only what he can do.

It is that he increasingly chooses what kind of student the institution is permitted to know.

A deliberate visibility policy is itself socially and politically consequential.

This links ability measurement to the second-order self-authorship argument in `Y2_02`.

## 6.4 Reconstruction implication

Do not derive Ayanokōji behavior from his OAA vector.

His OAA is partly evidence about his display policy.

A faithful model must separate:

- capability;
- default willingness to reveal;
- relationship-specific disclosure;
- and current strategic reason to remain legible or illegible.

# 7. Kōenji — the right to possess capacity without making it collective property

Kōenji is the strongest challenge to merit systems that silently equate capability with duty.

The school can increasingly recognize that he is exceptional.

The class can increasingly recognize that he is exceptional.

Neither fact means the class automatically owns his performance.

The relevant states are:

1. unknown capacity;
2. known capacity;
3. voluntarily available capacity;
4. contractually obligated capacity.

The island bargain matters because collective access to his ability is negotiated through bounded obligation rather than presumed from membership.

This produces a crucial meritocratic principle:

> **A system may correctly identify ability and still lack legitimate jurisdiction to demand its use.**

OAA can become more accurate about Kōenji while remaining incomplete as a predictor of class contribution because willingness is not a mere noise term.

It is part of the political fact.

This is why `Y2_03` and `Y2_05` treat him as a constitutional problem rather than simply an underperforming elite student.

# 8. Sudō — development makes old measurements stale

Sudō is the cleanest counterexample to static merit selection.

## 8.1 A once-valid rating can become a bad current predictor

In Y2V01, Horikita already judges Sudō's current academics above his historical OAA state (`Y2V01-E023`).

The institution's problem is not necessarily that it observed him badly.

The person changed faster than the file.

This yields a fundamental rule:

> **measurement confidence should decay when the measured person is developing rapidly.**

## 8.2 Development survives the original incentive

Sudō's early discipline is partly tied to Horikita.

Year 2 becomes more diagnostic because study and self-regulation continue after the romantic reward structure changes.

That strengthens the interpretation from externally sustained effort toward self-owned development.

## 8.3 Generativity is the stronger endpoint

Later Sudō does more than perform better.

He helps other weak students improve.

The most important ability is now partly visible in *their* output.

This is generative ability:

> **capacity whose causal product is increased capacity elsewhere.**

Individual scalar ratings systematically underrepresent this unless the institution models network effects.

# 9. Horikita, Hirata, and distributed ability

A major Year-2 correction is that leadership ability cannot be measured solely in the leader's own performance.

Horikita increasingly becomes capable of:

- identifying differentiated strengths;
- assigning bounded roles;
- soliciting expert input;
- creating contracts;
- allowing disagreement;
- correcting models;
- and making other people's competence politically usable.

Hirata similarly demonstrates adaptive coaching and social stabilization whose outputs often appear in other people.

The measurement problem is causal attribution.

If a weaker student performs better because someone taught, stabilized, recruited, or coordinated them, where should the generative contribution appear?

A system focused on personal outputs risks under-crediting the person who produced the network condition.

But the opposite overcorrection is also dangerous.

The leader should not be credited with ownership of every developed subordinate.

The correct distinction is:

> **causal contribution to another person's development does not convert the resulting capacity into the developer's property.**

This is the ability-side version of counter-authorship.

# 10. Ryūen, Kushida, Kei, and the failure of one-dimensional “social ability”

Year 2 supplies several reasons not to collapse social competence into one axis.

## 10.1 Ryūen — causality without prosocial contribution

Ryūen can have enormous ability to:

- intimidate;
- coordinate;
- bargain;
- recruit;
- delegate;
- model opponents;
- maintain follower loyalty;
- create adversarial information environments.

That does not imply high prosocial contribution.

A low contribution score therefore need not indicate low social power.

## 10.2 Kushida — capacity without moral self-interpretation

Kushida's memory, access, fluency, social reach, and ability to manage impressions are genuine capacities.

Their moral use is not encoded in the capacity itself.

A high social rating cannot function as a morality meter.

## 10.3 Kei — coordination outside academic hierarchy

Kei's academic profile cannot explain her influence over female-group atmosphere, information flow, and social coordination.

Her case shows that meaningful infrastructure can remain weakly represented in conventional academic/physical models.

## 10.4 Social capacity must be decomposed

At minimum separate:

- prosocial contribution;
- popularity;
- access;
- information brokerage;
- legitimacy;
- coercive influence;
- persuasion;
- coordination;
- relational memory;
- empathy;
- moral judgment.

A single “social stat” is too coarse for reconstruction.

# 11. Ichinose and Hiyori — task-fit and relationship-specific cognition

## 11.1 Ichinose — relational density becomes formal competitive capital

Ichinose's dense social knowledge can appear strategically soft when the task rewards secrecy, coercion, or atomized optimization.

V12 changes the task.

The hidden-role examination rewards:

- personality knowledge;
- microbehavioral familiarity;
- relational history;
- inference about likely lies and motives.

The exam therefore converts a capacity cultivated through relationship density into direct competitive power.

The correct conclusion is not that Ichinose has universally superior “social intelligence.”

It is:

> **the rule finally selects a domain unusually fitted to her accumulated relational competence.**

## 11.2 Hiyori — quiet judgment under-described by broad proxies

Hiyori's most important intelligence is often relationship-specific, observational, and low-temperature.

A broad quick-thinking or visible social-performance axis can under-describe that kind of cognition.

The measurement lesson is that a useful broad proxy can remain insufficient for fine-grained behavioral reconstruction.

# 12. Sakayanagi — disability, aggregation, and irrelevant cross-domain inference

Sakayanagi's physical disability creates a genuine limitation in embodied tasks.

A fair system may need to represent that limitation.

The problem emerges when an aggregate invites an observer to treat a domain-specific deficit as a general reduction in capacity.

Her strategic and political ability plainly remains high.

This produces two separate questions:

1. Is the physical score accurate for physical performance?
2. Is physical performance relevant to the purpose for which the aggregate is being used?

A composite can be mathematically correct and normatively or inferentially misapplied.

This matters far beyond Sakayanagi.

Every aggregate embeds a theory about which axes deserve commensuration and how much each should count.

# 13. Measurement-failure taxonomy

The Year-2 corpus supports a stable failure vocabulary.

## 13.1 `UNDEREXPRESSION`

Latent ability exceeds displayed output.

Primary case: Ayanokōji.

## 13.2 `VOLUNTARY_UNAVAILABILITY`

Ability is known but withheld from the collective task.

Primary case: Kōenji.

## 13.3 `DEVELOPMENTAL_STALENESS`

The file accurately described an earlier state but no longer predicts the developed person.

Primary case: Sudō.

## 13.4 `AXIS_UNDERDESCRIPTION`

A broad category does not capture an important subtype of competence.

Primary cases: Hiyori, Ryūen.

## 13.5 `MORAL_NONIDENTITY`

A capacity measure is mistaken for a moral-character measure.

Primary case: Kushida.

## 13.6 `AGGREGATE_DOMAIN_MISMATCH`

A valid component becomes misleading when weighted into a general conclusion for a purpose where it is less relevant.

Primary case: Sakayanagi.

## 13.7 `RELATIONAL_SPECIFICITY`

A capacity exists in a particular relational history and is obscured by generic social measures.

Primary case: V12 / Ichinose.

## 13.8 `GENERATIVE_INVISIBILITY`

A person's causal output appears in other people's improved performance.

Primary cases: Horikita, Hirata, Sudō.

## 13.9 `TASK_VALUE_INVERSION`

The same rating acquires a different marginal value because the exam changes the payoff rule.

Primary pair: V01 versus V09.

## 13.10 `RECORD_PROVENANCE_FAILURE`

The official result or underlying trace is compromised or cannot establish authorship.

Primary Year-1 anchor: Tsukishiro's chess interference.

Year-2 island evidence strengthens the category through telemetry and falsification problems.

## 13.11 `MODEL_FEEDBACK`

Public measurement changes how others treat the subject and therefore changes later opportunities and outcomes.

Primary case: OAA partner market.

## 13.12 `IDENTITY_OVERINFERENCE`

Observed competence is used to infer hidden biography or institutional origin beyond what competence alone proves.

# 29. Meritocracy — ten conditions for a defensible system

Year 2 does not support the claim that meritocracy is inherently illegitimate.

It supports a stricter claim:

> **a system that attaches unequal futures to measured difference assumes obligations proportional to the consequence it imposes.**

A defensible merit system would require at least:

## 29.1 Bounded object

State exactly what is being measured.

## 29.2 Temporal provenance

Preserve when and under what conditions the evidence was obtained.

## 29.3 Development sensitivity

Permit a person to outgrow the file.

## 29.4 Participation awareness

Distinguish inability from refusal, concealment, or strategic withholding.

## 29.5 Task-fit discipline

Do not universalize a score beyond the task it predicts.

## 29.6 Multi-domain structure

Academics, physical performance, strategy, social influence, judgment, teaching, and generativity are not interchangeable.

## 29.7 Record integrity

The official output must be trustworthy.

## 29.8 Model-use restraint

A profile should improve coordination without becoming title to the person's future.

## 29.9 Proportionate consequence

A bounded performance deficit should not silently become a total personhood judgment.

## 29.10 Counterforce and corrigibility

The measured person and other observers must be able to challenge the model before irreversible consequence.

ANHS satisfies some of these conditions and repeatedly violates others.

That tension—not a simple “merit good” or “merit bad” answer—is the analytical substance of `実力至上主義` at Y2SL.

# 30. Meritocratic ideologies inside the Year-2 corpus

The school does not contain one merit theory.

Several coexist.

## 30.1 Scalar merit

Higher measured performance deserves higher standing.

This is attractive in standardized academic contexts and weakest where task fit or hidden capacity dominates.

## 30.2 Market merit

Ability is worth what others will pay when rules make it scarce.

V01 is the canonical case.

## 30.3 Developmental merit

The strongest educational result is not selecting the already best but increasing capacity.

Sudō and V09 support this model.

## 30.4 Portfolio merit

Different capacities should be combined across a team or class rather than collapsed into one person-level scalar.

Island exams, festivals, and Horikita's polity strengthen this.

## 30.5 Generative merit

A high-value person may be the actor who makes other people better rather than the actor with the best direct score.

Horikita, Hirata, and Sudō increasingly support this.

## 30.6 Mobility merit

Exceptional individual performance can justify greater freedom to change institutional position.

Y2V06 makes this explicit through the transfer-ticket option.

## 30.7 Political merit

A capacity becomes important because it enables leadership, protection, bargaining, or institutional survival.

This overlaps with but is not identical to formal OAA.

## 30.8 Counter-authorship as the strongest educational merit

At the most demanding level, education succeeds when the developed person can generate purposes and answers that no longer belong to the developer.

This cannot be reduced to higher scores.

It is evidence that ability has become self-authored agency.

# 36. Reconstruction and simulation constraints

This specialist has direct consequences for character modeling.

## 36.1 Never use OAA as a personality vector

OAA describes selected observed capacities.

It does not directly encode:

- motivation;
- ethics;
- attachment;
- disclosure policy;
- risk appetite;
- humor;
- or willingness to help.

## 36.2 Separate capability from behavioral policy

Ayanokōji can solve a problem and choose not to.

Kōenji can contribute and refuse.

Sakayanagi can understand a strategic optimum and prioritize a private relational aim instead.

Ability predicts possible action, not selected action.

## 36.3 Model task fit before ranking

Before asking who is “better,” specify:

- the task;
- the unit;
- the information state;
- the stakes;
- the permitted resources;
- and whether cooperation is available.

## 36.4 Preserve generativity

For Horikita, Hirata, Sudō, and other developers, simulate the capacity to make others perform better.

Do not collapse leadership into direct personal dominance.

## 36.5 Confidence should decay with developmental velocity

A model of a rapidly changing student should become less confident when extrapolating stale evidence.

Sudō is the canonical case.

## 36.6 Preserve voluntary unavailability

Do not assume Kōenji performs at ceiling because the scenario would benefit the class.

Availability requires its own evidence.

## 36.7 Preserve relationship-specific cognition

Ichinose or Hiyori may be highly accurate about specific people without implying universal omniscience.

## 36.8 Counter-authorship is a feature, not error

A faithful model must allow developed characters to produce an answer the model did not predict.

A simulator that eliminates all future surprise would contradict the corpus's strongest developmental claim.

# 41. Canonical Year-2 synthesis

Year 2 begins by making students more visible.

It ends by showing why visibility cannot settle what they are.

OAA is a genuine institutional achievement.

It gives students and administrators a common language for selected dimensions of performance.

It preserves temporal snapshots.

It improves coordination.

It exposes changes that a purely narrative reputation system might miss.

It also immediately changes the social world around the people it measures.

A first-year's academic rating becomes a price.

A second-year's weak profile becomes a survival risk.

A deliberately mediocre student can rewrite his public identity with one perfect mathematics score.

A gifted student can remain institutionally inconvenient because he refuses to make his gift available.

A once-weak student can outgrow the file.

A leader can become more capable not by personally performing every task but by making other people more capable.

A low prosocial score can coexist with enormous social power.

A high social score can coexist with ethically destructive use of social skill.

A physical limitation can depress an aggregate without defining general intelligence.

A quiet observer can possess relationship-specific judgment broader proxies understate.

Then the exams repeatedly reprice the same people.

V01 makes public academic legibility a bargaining market.

The island converts ability into portfolio design, insurance, tradable advantages, surveillance traces, and group risk.

V05 shows that a perfectly counted vote can still radically under-describe who authored a sacrifice and why.

V06 allows individual excellence to feed the class or purchase mobility away from it.

V07 turns reputation, logistics, hospitality, and public performance into economic capacity.

V09 deliberately makes old weakness labels valuable when the student has developed beneath them.

V10 broadens knowledge and protection enough to reveal why scalar academic hierarchy is insufficient.

V11 demonstrates that lower stakes may produce better information about ordinary adaptability.

V12 makes personal history and relational inference formal competitive resources.

No single sequence better explains why the series title should not be read as a promise that the school will discover one final hierarchy of human worth.

The school does something more interesting and more dangerous.

It repeatedly authors environments in which different capacities become consequential.

It then converts those capacities into:

- points;
- rank;
- bargaining power;
- protection;
- mobility;
- expulsion risk;
- and social interpretation.

The correct meritocratic question therefore changes.

It is not:

> **Who has the most `実力`?**

It is:

> **What capacity is relevant here, what evidence supports that claim, how current is the evidence, did the person choose to make the capacity available, what did the rule convert it into, who bears the consequence, and what right does any observer have to generalize from that result?**

Year 2's strongest answer is neither anti-measurement nor pro-ranking.

It is **bounded merit realism**.

People genuinely differ.

Measurements can genuinely improve knowledge.

Tests can genuinely reveal capability.

Development can genuinely be tracked.

But every legitimate inference must preserve the remainder the measurement did not capture.

Every public model must remain corrigible.

Every old rating must be allowed to become stale.

Every capacity must remain distinguishable from willingness to serve the institution that identified it.

Every task result must remain bounded to the temporary world that made that task valuable.

And every educational system must tolerate the possibility that the person it developed will eventually produce an answer the model did not own.

The canonical Year-2 endpoint is therefore:

> **Ability is real. Measurement is useful. Merit is contextual. Public metrics are causal. Examinations are authored conversion systems. Development makes files provisional. And no score—however accurate—becomes a legitimate theory of the whole person merely because an institution can attach consequences to it.**
