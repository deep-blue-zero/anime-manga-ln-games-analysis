# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 2 — Relationships, Dependency, Friendship, Romance, and Recognition
### Reciprocity under superior modeling: from answerable asymmetry to counter-authorship

> **Canonical specialist synthesis at the complete Year-2 / Second List boundary.**
>
> This document is the primary Year-2 home for relationship systems: dependency, friendship, romance, recognition, protection, trust, rivalry, information asymmetry, refusal, separation, role survival, and counter-authorship. It does not replace the immutable source-local volume readings or the canonical through-Year-2 ledgers. It synthesizes them at `Y2SL`, preserves Volume 0's mandated retrospective position, and imports no Year-3 narrative evidence.

---

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

The frozen Year-1 relationship specialist ended with a principle strong enough to survive the second year:

> **Reciprocity is the capacity to answer back under conditions of difference.**

Year 2 does not reject that principle. It makes it harder.

By the end of Year 1, the central relational problem was already larger than the opposition between sincerity and manipulation. Affection and utility could coexist. Protection could be real while becoming proprietary. Dependency could be adaptive rather than pathological. Friendship could be genuine without full disclosure. Rivalry could operate as recognition. A relationship could contain large differences of ability or power without being non-reciprocal by definition.

The Year-1 standard therefore asked whether asymmetry remained **answerable**:

- could the weaker or less informed participant refuse?;
- could the relationship survive contradiction?;
- could the person retain purposes the relationship did not assign?;
- could development outlive the developer?;
- could role change expose value that utility had concealed?;
- could a psychological model remain corrigible by the person it described?

Year 2 introduces a more difficult condition.

Ayanokōji becomes better at modeling the people around him. Horikita becomes better at understanding classmates as trajectories rather than files. Ichinose becomes better at reading the social field. Sakayanagi's attention becomes personally rather than merely strategically selective. Ryūen increasingly distinguishes follower capacities. The school itself measures and classifies more aggressively. Intimacy therefore increases **relational legibility**.

That is not automatically good.

The more accurately one person knows another, the easier it becomes to help, protect, anticipate, comfort, coordinate, persuade, or develop the other person. It also becomes easier to choose the right timing, pressure, omission, incentive, fear, promise, or relational cue needed to produce a preferred response without issuing a direct order.

Year 2 therefore shifts the relationship problem from:

> **Can asymmetrical relationships become reciprocal?**

into:

> **Can reciprocity survive when one participant possesses a much stronger working model of the other and can use that model to shape the field in which the other chooses?**

The strongest Year-2 answer is:

> **Yes, but accurate recognition is not enough. A relationship becomes most reciprocal when the modeled person retains enough information, refusal capacity, independent support, independent purpose, and causal standing to make the modeler answer to an answer the modeler did not already own.**

This document calls that strongest state **counter-authored reciprocity**.

It does not require equality of intelligence, social power, information, or institutional office. It does require that the stronger model remain vulnerable to correction by the person it models.

That is why Ichinose becomes the year-end terminal case, Kei the strongest negative information test, Hiyori the cleanest participant-mode countermodel, Horikita the strongest contradiction-without-information-symmetry case, Sakayanagi the clearest example of love becoming indirect jurisdiction, and the successor Ayanokōji Group the cleanest social-succession case.

The artifact uses relationship labels only after structural description. Terms such as `friend`, `lover`, `rival`, `classmate`, `protector`, or `leader` are not sufficient explanations. Every major relation is treated as a directed, history-sensitive system.

---

## 0.1 A relationship is directed, not merely paired

`Ayanokōji -> Kei` is not analytically identical to `Kei -> Ayanokōji`.

He may provide protection while she provides ordinary intimacy. He may control decisive information while she possesses superior female-peer social infrastructure. She may depend on him emotionally while he acquires habits around her that survive the end of the romance. Each direction can therefore differ in:

- knowledge;
- leverage;
- dependency;
- protection;
- desire;
- disclosure;
- refusal;
- social alternatives;
- and perceived irreplaceability.

The same rule applies across the corpus.

Horikita's desire for Ayanokōji's recognition does not imply that he desires hers in the same form. Ichinose's attachment does not imply passivity. Sakayanagi's love and Ayanokōji's preference for her as a rival create different but mutually causal structures. Ryūen's need for a meaningful victory and Ayanokōji's desire to preserve Ryūen as a developmental competitor are reciprocal without being symmetrical.

This specialist therefore avoids undirected statements such as “they are dependent,” “they trust each other,” or “they are close” unless the direction and domain are specified.

---

## 0.2 Affection is not consent

Year 2 makes this control more necessary than Year 1.

Kei's love does not retroactively authorize the coercive origin of Ayanokōji's control over her or the later secrecy around the breakup timetable.

Ichinose's continued attachment does not authorize the V12 use of trust and timed disclosure to destabilize her cognition.

Sakayanagi's love does not remove Ayanokōji's causal responsibility for designing a clue field in which her inferred understanding of his preference helps produce the result he wants.

Kushida's later voluntary cooperation does not make Horikita's V05 preservation consensual at the moment Airi bears the irreversible cost.

The governing rule remains:

> **Later affection, development, recovery, or successful continuation cannot retroactively grant consent to the earlier method.**

---

## 0.3 Protection is not ownership

Protection is relationally positive when it expands future options, reduces coercive exposure, preserves exit, distributes support, or makes refusal safer.

Protection becomes ownership-like when the protector treats successful protection as title to:

- define the protected person's goal;
- decide what pain is acceptable for the person's development;
- conceal information required for informed choice;
- remain indispensable;
- determine the meaning and timing of separation;
- or redirect costs onto third parties without their informed participation.

Year 2 repeatedly combines both sides in the same act. That combination must not be simplified into either “he protected her, therefore it was good” or “the act was controlling, therefore no care was present.”

---

## 0.4 Dependency is multidimensional

Year 2 decisively destroys the single-axis model `dependent <-> independent`.

A person may be:

- behaviorally autonomous;
- emotionally dependent;
- socially well-supported;
- informationally disadvantaged;
- strategically useful;
- economically or institutionally constrained;
- psychologically attached;
- capable of refusal;
- and still able to contradict the relationship's strongest actor.

Kei is the clearest example. She can function publicly, study, coordinate peers, seek Satō's counsel, control social presentation, and survive the formal breakup while remaining acutely organized around Ayanokōji's judgment and expectations.

Horikita shows another split: independent judgment precedes demonstrated system independence after hidden security is removed.

Ichinose shows a third: intense attachment coexists with criticism, strategic autonomy, and a claim to reciprocal use.

Dependency is therefore assessed by domain, origin, alternatives, monopolization, visibility, exit cost, and compatibility with contradiction.

---

## 0.5 Recognition has four major Year-2 forms

### Epistemic recognition

Accurately understanding capability, motive, wound, desire, or likely behavior.

This is the form Ayanokōji often excels at.

### Social recognition

Treating a person as visible and consequential within a group. Yamamura's Year-2 arc makes the absence of this form especially important.

### Relational recognition

Treating another person as particular rather than fungible: someone whose loss, approval, presence, habits, or refusal has meaning beyond generic role utility.

### Moral recognition

Treating another person as possessing standing over the conditions that affect her: the right to know, refuse, contradict, reinterpret, leave, or generate an answer the stronger actor did not choose.

Year 2's central warning is that **epistemic recognition can grow while moral recognition lags**.

A person may be understood with extraordinary precision and governed more efficiently because of that understanding.

---

## 0.6 Reciprocity does not require equal power

Equal power is uncommon in COTE and not the governing criterion.

The relevant questions are:

1. Can the lower-power participant refuse without catastrophic punishment?
2. Is enough relevant information available for the refusal to be meaningful?
3. Can contradiction materially alter the relationship?
4. Does the stronger participant recognize limits on jurisdiction?
5. Can the lower-power participant maintain independent relationships and purposes?
6. Does the relationship survive role change?
7. Can the stronger participant's model be corrected by the other person's answer?

This last criterion becomes decisive in Year 2.

---

## 0.7 Three relationship states must remain distinct

### Authored continuation

A relation continues and the target becomes more capable, but the stronger actor's developmental script still organizes the target's interpretation of success.

Kei's breakup behavior is the strongest negative example. She acts with dignity, but partly because she believes that dignified independence is the answer Ayanokōji expects.

### Role-surviving / successor relation

The bond or social structure survives removal of its original role or center.

Examples include:

- Sudō's development after romantic rejection;
- Kei's growing horizontal support beyond the boyfriend/protector;
- Haruka–Akito–Keisei after Airi's expulsion and Ayanokōji's non-return;
- Sakayanagi–Yamamura extending beyond school;
- Airi continuing to shape Haruka from outside ANHS.

### Counter-authored relation

The target not only survives but generates an answer, purpose, or causal structure the original model does not possess and forces the stronger actor to respond.

Ichinose at Y2V12.5 is the strongest terminal case.

The hierarchy is therefore:

`persistence < functional independence < informed self-authorship < counter-authorship`

Each is valuable. They are not interchangeable.

---

## 0.8 Separation is an information test before it is an endurance test

Year 1 treated role change as the strongest stress test. Year 2 adds a prior question:

> **Did the person know enough to author the meaning of the separation before it occurred?**

Kei does not.

Horikita does not.

Sakayanagi largely does own her school withdrawal, even though Ayanokōji's influence contributes causally.

Ichinose refuses the terminal role Ayanokōji prepared for her and transforms the relation before separation can be defined on his terms.

Thus a person may successfully endure separation while still having been denied informed authorship of the separation itself.

# 1. Executive Year-2 thesis

Year 2 is a study of what happens when **being known becomes both intimacy and infrastructure**.

The cast becomes increasingly entangled. Private histories become shared. Cross-class friendships become ordinary. Romance becomes public. Formerly hidden selves become visible. Leadership depends more on differentiated expertise. Rivals become personally meaningful. Ordinary memories accumulate. Some characters begin to know one another well enough that direct instructions are unnecessary.

This produces the central relational paradox:

> **Greater intimacy can enlarge another person's freedom, but it can also make the person's motives easier to govern.**

Five longitudinal movements organize the year.

### Movement 1 — intimacy creates precision

Ayanokōji's relationships with Kei, Horikita, Ichinose, Hiyori, Hirata, Sakayanagi, and Ryūen all become more legible. That legibility supports care, ordinary participation, and more accurate recognition. It also gives him increasingly precise control surfaces.

V12 makes the danger explicit: a direct command is unnecessary when trust, inferred preference, fear of loss, or internalized developmental expectation reliably supplies the desired action.

### Movement 2 — horizontal ties reduce monopolized dependence

Kei–Satō, Horikita–Ibuki, Sudō–Onodera, Haruka–Akito–Keisei, Kanzaki–Himeno and the reform coalition, Hiyori's cross-class friendships, and Ryūen's differentiated follower relations all make dependence more plural.

Plural dependence is not independence from relationship. It is protection against one relationship becoming the only route to safety, recognition, information, or belonging.

### Movement 3 — contradiction becomes relational competence

The strongest relationships increasingly survive answers such as:

- “you are wrong”;
- “I hate you and will still cooperate”;
- “I will not forgive you”;
- “I will use you too”;
- “your victory did not develop us”;
- “I will follow you, but for reasons not reducible to fear.”

Contradiction is therefore not relational failure by default. In mature relations it becomes evidence that belonging does not require total submission.

### Movement 4 — role loss reveals value

Sakayanagi recognizes Kamuro's friendship after irreversible loss.

Ayanokōji's body repeats domestic routine after Kei is gone.

Haruka remains organized by Airi after ANHS removes Airi.

Sudō's development survives loss of Horikita as romantic goal.

Ryūen rejects an official victory because the absent rival's independent strength mattered more than the institutional result.

Irreplaceability is therefore clearest when value survives the role that once made the person useful.

### Movement 5 — counter-authorship becomes the terminal reciprocity test

Ichinose's Y2V12.5 answer transforms the year.

She does not become informationally equal to Ayanokōji. She does not deny his influence. She does not purify herself of attachment. Instead, she identifies the authored binary, generates an answer he does not possess, models his own compulsion to observe her, criticizes the ethics of unilateral development, and claims reciprocal causal rights.

The result is the year-level relationship principle:

> **To recognize another person most fully is not to predict her correctly. It is to remain answerable when she becomes capable of producing an answer that makes the prediction incomplete.**

# 2. Relational test framework for Year 2

Every major relationship is evaluated across the following dimensions.

## 2.1 Origin

How did the bond begin?

Voluntary proximity, coercion, institutional assignment, protection, rivalry, secrecy, shared interest, attraction, family power, class obligation, or some mixture?

Origin matters because later sincerity does not erase earlier leverage.

## 2.2 Information symmetry

What does each participant know about:

- the other's history;
- motives;
- future plans;
- relevant institutions;
- hidden risks;
- competing relationships;
- and the actual purpose of the current interaction?

Year 2 adds **timing** to this dimension. Truth supplied after a decision can no longer be altered is not equivalent to timely disclosure.

## 2.3 Power and leverage

Power is separated into at least:

- formal authority;
- informational advantage;
- causal leverage;
- social-network power;
- physical coercive capacity;
- economic/point resources;
- and emotional leverage.

A relationship may be reciprocal in one dimension and radically unequal in another.

## 2.4 Refusal capacity

Can a participant say no?

Can she survive saying no?

Does she know what she is refusing?

Will refusal alter future access, safety, belonging, status, or protection?

A spoken “no” is not sufficient evidence if the information needed to understand the choice was withheld.

## 2.5 Dependency architecture

Dependency is described as:

- vertical or horizontal;
- monopolized or plural;
- visible or hidden;
- temporary or self-reproducing;
- capability-producing or capability-suppressing;
- safely reducible or costly to reduce.

The goal is not zero dependency. The strongest relations support **answerable interdependence**.

## 2.6 Protection architecture

Ask:

- what is being protected?;
- from whom?;
- with whose consent?;
- at whose cost?;
- whether alternatives expand;
- whether the protector's indispensability increases;
- and whether the protected person can later reinterpret the intervention.

## 2.7 Strategic utility

Utility does not invalidate affection.

The question is whether the person remains valuable when the role changes or utility disappears.

## 2.8 Emotional disclosure

Who risks being known?

Who controls which truths become visible?

Who receives enough information to respond rather than merely be observed?

## 2.9 Contradiction

Does disagreement trigger expulsion from the bond, retaliation, humiliation, loss of protection, or reclassification as enemy?

Or can the relationship absorb contradiction as information?

## 2.10 Fungibility and irreplaceability

Being indispensable to a system is not the same as being irreplaceable to a person.

Year 2 therefore treats role loss as high-value evidence.

## 2.11 Role survival

Does the relation survive:

- breakup;
- rejection;
- class rivalry;
- exposure of hidden identity;
- leader removal;
- expulsion;
- school withdrawal;
- class transfer;
- or the collapse of the original social group?

## 2.12 Counter-authorship

Can the participant generate an answer the stronger party did not script?

Does that answer materially alter the stronger party's model or future behavior?

This is the strongest available Year-2 indicator of relational autonomy.

# 3. Year-1 -> Year-2 claim-transition ledger

## 3.1 PRESERVE

The following Year-1 claims remain canonical:

- affection is not consent;
- protection is not ownership;
- dependency is not the opposite of autonomy;
- strategic utility does not prove affection is false;
- reciprocity does not require equality of power;
- partial selves can form genuine friendships;
- ordinary time is diagnostic because it is not always required for strategic function;
- role change is a stronger test than intensity alone;
- a useful model is not the person;
- dissent can be a form of belonging.

## 3.2 STRENGTHEN

Year 2 strengthens these claims:

- horizontal support reduces monopolized dependency;
- contradiction is one of the strongest observable forms of relational agency;
- cross-class friendship can survive real competition;
- relational value is often clearest after role loss;
- development becomes more credible when skills and support transmit laterally to third parties;
- ordinary life creates non-fungible habit and memory even when explicit theory remains instrumental;
- knowing another person creates moral obligations about how that knowledge is used.

## 3.3 REVISE

Several Year-1 formulations are now too weak or too simple.

### “Development survives the developer”

Revise to:

> **Survival is necessary but not the strongest test. Counter-authorship is stronger because the developed person uses what she became to generate purposes or answers the developer did not own.**

### “Kei becomes independent”

Revise into separate behavioral, social, emotional, informational, and strategic dimensions.

### “Horikita becomes independent when Ayanokōji leaves”

Revise to: independent judgment and political authorship are established; post-transfer system reproduction is only beginning to be tested.

### “Ichinose is rescued or developed by Ayanokōji”

Revise to a two-way relation in which he influences, harms, protects, and tests her while she ultimately forces him to answer a model of **him**.

### “Sakayanagi–Ayanokōji is primarily intellectual rivalry”

Revise to include love, inferred preference, and private relational sovereignty.

### “Hiyori influences Ryūen”

Revise to distinguish accurate diagnosis and moral pressure from binding jurisdiction.

### “Disclosure means telling the truth”

Revise to include timing, relevance, and practical ability to change the decision.

## 3.4 DOWNGRADE

The following propositions are downgraded or restricted:

- formal consent alone proves legitimate relational authorship;
- continued participation proves the origin of the relation became legitimate;
- being needed proves irreplaceability;
- successful development licenses the developer's method;
- accurate prediction is evidence of relational entitlement;
- outward competence proves emotional independence.

## 3.5 REJECT

Year 2 rejects:

- romance cures Kei's dependency;
- Kushida's integration requires reconciliation;
- Ayanokōji Group rupture proves the earlier friendship was fake;
- Airi's later flourishing justifies expulsion;
- Sakayanagi can keep Ayanokōji-centered private priority politically costless;
- separation itself proves independence;
- Ryūen's follower loyalty proves coercion is a sufficient basis for good leadership;
- superior recognition grants legitimate jurisdiction over the recognized person.

## 3.6 OPEN

The following remain open at `Y2SL`:

- can Ayanokōji make timely and sufficient disclosure a stable relational practice?;
- can he voluntarily reveal weakness in a way that gives another person meaningful protective authority?;
- can Horikita reinterpret the class transfer after learning its hidden timetable without treating it merely as another lesson he authored?;
- can Kei's emotional recovery become independent of the answer she believes Ayanokōji wanted?;
- can Hiyori's diagnosis become durable constraint on Ryūen?;
- can Hirata choose particular attachment without universal-betrayal anxiety?;
- can Ichinose's reciprocal-use relation remain reciprocal under continuing global information asymmetry?;
- can Ayanokōji accept a counter-authored answer he strongly dislikes without redesigning the environment around it?;
- can post-school relationships become self-authored rather than merely continuations of school-era developmental projects?;
- can the former Sakayanagi polity build relationships that constrain its new exceptional center rather than merely depend on him?

# 4. The Year-2 relational progression

## 4.1 Y2V01–V02 — intimacy becomes infrastructure

The beginning of Year 2 expands the number of relationships that can carry political information.

Ayanokōji–Kei becomes increasingly domestic and explicit. Horikita increasingly trusts Ayanokōji while developing class-to-class reciprocal structures. Kei–Satō remains meaningful outside romance. Hiyori continues ordinary cross-class friendship. Ichinose and Kanzaki begin exposing the difference between solidarity and agreement. Ryūen recruits Katsuragi because expertise can matter more than ideological similarity.

The relationship system therefore begins moving from isolated dyads toward overlapping networks.

The key risk is also visible early: Ayanokōji's growing closeness supplies better information about how to pace another person's development.

## 4.2 Y2V03–V04 — desire and trust become exploitable surfaces

Kei's first kiss demonstrates that genuine desire and strategic emotional regulation can occupy the same act. Ichinose's trust crosses class boundaries under institutional threat. Ryūen's follower relations increasingly contain delegated expertise. Sakayanagi and Ayanokōji continue mutual recognition under widening political pressure.

The lesson is not that intimacy is false. It is that sincerity does not disable strategic use.

## 4.3 Y2V04.5–V06 — publicization, exposure, and self-owned continuation

This is the first major relationship-constitution hinge.

Kei's romance becomes public enough that hidden curriculum and ordinary love must coexist in the same analytical object. Ichinose confesses inside an information field she does not control. Horikita and Ibuki become chosen investigative partners. The Ayanokōji Group begins to register Ayanokōji's emotional distance.

V05 then violently tests whether belonging survives catastrophic disagreement. Kushida's hidden/public equilibrium collapses. Airi is expelled. Haruka's ordinary friendship with Ayanokōji becomes grievance. Horikita authors Kushida's preservation without Kushida's consent.

V06 gives the crucial repair distinction: **ongoing continuation can become self-authored without retroactively legitimizing the imposed origin**. Kushida chooses to cooperate for herself. Kei teaches Ayanokōji an unconditional-relational answer his optimization does not generate, while immediately exposing how little decisive truth he has shared with her.

## 4.4 Y2V07–V08 — horizontal support and cross-class moral friction

V07 turns Kei–Satō into one of the strongest horizontal-support structures in the corpus. Satō receives socially dangerous truth and remains. Haruka's relation to Airi becomes transformative precisely because Airi is outside the institution: Airi's continuing life destroys the equation `expelled = permanently ruined`.

The Ayanokōji Group survives only by becoming a different social object.

V08 broadens the principle. ANHS deliberately removes some class barriers during the school trip. Students become more aware that a rival can simultaneously be a friend whose future they value. `Y2V08-E036` captures the contradiction directly: students may want a cross-class best friend to graduate from Class A even when class competition structurally makes that impossible for everyone.

The result is **moral friction**. Knowing another person can make optimal competition psychologically and ethically harder.

## 4.5 Y2V09–V10 — relationships begin overrunning old models

V09 strengthens second-brain structures, plural opposition, and relationship-conditioned strategy. Kei's conflict with Ayanokōji demonstrates that severe private distress does not erase public competence. Horikita increasingly inhabits office rather than merely acting as a student being developed.

V09.5 is especially important because ordinary desire becomes increasingly self-owned. Kei–Satō receives direct best-friend interiority. Sakayanagi identifies love without treating it simply as weakness. Ichinose has wants that are not reducible to class function. Hiyori and Ayanokōji demonstrate participant-mode invitation with disclosed desire and preserved refusal.

V10 then makes model failure relationally productive. Ichinose can oppose Horikita's class without losing personal trust. Hiyori can judge Ryūen's victory as developmentally thin. Sakayanagi discovers that Kamuro had become a friend only after optimization has already made the relationship irrecoverable.

## 4.6 Y2V11 — recognition without ownership becomes explicit

Y2V11 provides the cleanest positive stopping rule of the year.

After arranging access between Sakayanagi and Yamamura, Ayanokōji permits multiple outcomes and withdraws:

> `ここから先は、オレの立ち入る領域じゃない。`

The significance is jurisdictional, not merely polite.

He can author access without claiming ownership of the answer.

This becomes the strongest positive local model for relationship mediation in Year 2. It is not generalized yet.

## 4.7 Y2V12 — intimacy becomes choice architecture

V12 supplies the decisive warning.

Kei experiences a visible request for permission while Ayanokōji is already internally in `希望を絶つための準備段階`. The appearance of reciprocity therefore coexists with hidden temporal authorship.

Ichinose's trust becomes an attack surface. Ayanokōji times disclosure to alter her reasoning rather than to enable her choice.

Sakayanagi reconstructs his preference from an authored clue field and makes a voluntary decision with severe fiduciary consequences.

The volume therefore revises the V11 stopping rule:

> **Withdrawing before the final act is not enough if earlier staging has already heavily authored the motives and information available at the point of choice.**

## 4.8 Y2V12.5 — separation and counter-authorship

The final fiction boundary differentiates the major relations sharply.

Kei gives the negative informed-choice result. She behaves independently while remaining emotionally organized around the developmental answer she thinks Ayanokōji wants.

Horikita asks Ayanokōji to stay immediately before discovering, in effect, that he has already chosen institutional separation. Her succession test begins under major timetable asymmetry.

Sakayanagi owns withdrawal, preserves personal futurity beyond school, and asks Ayanokōji to imagine his own life as self-authored.

Ichinose defeats the binary prepared for her and turns the observer into an observed subject.

The year therefore ends not with one global answer about “healthy” versus “unhealthy” relationships, but with a hierarchy of relational outcomes differentiated by information, refusal, role survival, and counter-authorship.

# 5. Ayanokōji and Karuizawa Kei — genuine romance inside an authored curriculum

Ayanokōji–Kei is the year’s strongest negative test of the proposition that affection naturally repairs coercive origins.

It does not.

The relation becomes more ordinary, more physically and emotionally intimate, more socially public, and more habit-forming. At the same time, Ayanokōji continues to treat the relationship as a developmental curriculum whose endpoint includes separation.

The correct Year-2 model must therefore hold several propositions at once:

1. Kei's affection is genuine.
2. Ayanokōji's ordinary participation is genuine.
3. The relationship contains real reciprocal learning.
4. Kei's emotional dependency remains severe.
5. Her horizontal social autonomy grows substantially.
6. Ayanokōji withholds decisive information about himself and the relationship's timetable.
7. The breakup is therefore behaviorally survivable without becoming fully informed reciprocal choice.

Reducing the relationship to either “fake experiment” or “normal romance” loses the central structure.

---

## 5.1 Year-1 inheritance — protection, chosen loyalty, and unresolved hidden curriculum

The Year-1 endpoint had already invalidated a total `host / parasite` model.

Kei had accepted protection under coercive conditions and organized survival around powerful protectors. But the rooftop sequence showed that immediate survival optimization did not explain everything: she refused the betrayal that would have exposed Ayanokōji even under extreme pressure.

Later partnership, ordinary contact, jealousy, gifts, naming, and the mutual confession made the relationship more than instrumental.

Yet Ayanokōji still possessed vastly more information about:

- how the relationship originated;
- his own capability;
- the White Room;
- his future plans;
- his developmental interpretation of Kei;
- and the degree to which he treated romance as a learning environment.

Year 2 tests whether increased intimacy reduces that asymmetry.

It largely does not.

---

## 5.2 Y2V01–V03 — domesticity is real, and so is regulation

Early Year 2 gives the relation repeated low-stakes reality.

Studying, cooking, phone contact, jealousy, private jokes, ordinary touch, and the first kiss matter because they are not reducible to exam necessity.

Y2V03 is especially diagnostic. Ayanokōji experiences the first kiss as novel and desirable while also using physical intimacy to regulate Kei's anxiety about Nanase.

That coexistence is crucial.

The strategic function does not prove the desire is false.

The desire does not make the strategic function disappear.

A mature model must therefore allow:

`genuine desire + real care + emotional learning + active regulation`

inside the same scene.

This becomes the default structure of the Year-2 romance.

---

## 5.3 Y2V04.5 — publicization changes the social constitution of the bond

Once the relationship becomes increasingly public, it can no longer exist only as a private protective arrangement.

Publicization has several effects:

- Kei's status is less dependent on maintaining a false Hirata relationship;
- the romantic bond enters the class's social knowledge;
- third parties gain greater standing to interpret Ayanokōji's treatment of Kei;
- and Kei's identity becomes less monopolized by a secret dyad.

This is a genuine autonomy gain.

But public visibility does not equal private transparency.

Ayanokōji's own history, future, and curricular interpretation remain largely inaccessible to Kei.

The relationship can therefore become socially legible while remaining epistemically opaque at its deepest level.

---

## 5.4 Y2V06 — Kei teaches a non-optimization answer

Y2V06 gives Kei one of her strongest reciprocal acts.

She asks whether Ayanokōji would have expelled her if she had low OAA, weak status, and no friends. He repeatedly answers the wrong question by analyzing hypothetical utility.

Kei wants a different category of answer: that he would protect **her** without needing to derive the answer from present usefulness.

His resulting realization is concise:

> `そこに理屈なんかは不要`

The scene matters because Kei is not merely receiving development. She teaches him a relational rule his optimization does not spontaneously generate.

The direction of influence reverses.

That is real reciprocity.

But the volume immediately places a hard limit on any triumphalist reading.

Kei tells him she has no meaningful secrets left and asks him not to hide things from her. Ayanokōji mentally inventories family, White Room, school plan, friendships, and romantic truths and recognizes that she knows almost none of what would matter most.

Thus the same volume produces both:

- **high relational learning**;
- **low informational reciprocity**.

This split remains central through the breakup.

---

## 5.5 Y2V07–V09.5 — Satō becomes the decisive autonomy node

Ayanokōji controls the timing of Kei's disclosure of her bullying history to Satō. That means even this apparently empowering disclosure is not free of environmental authorship.

Satō's response, however, is not his.

She stays.

The relationship becomes `親友`.

This matters because the disclosure contains exactly the kind of socially dangerous information Kei's earlier survival architecture taught her could destroy belonging.

Instead:

`dangerous truth -> peer knowledge -> continued attachment`

The result is a horizontal bond that does not depend on the romantic protector.

By Y2V09 and Y2V09.5, Satō can:

- hear Kei's distress;
- judge Ayanokōji's behavior independently;
- tell Kei when she thinks he is wrong;
- provide physical and emotional companionship;
- and remain loyal to Kei rather than to the logic of the romantic dyad.

`Y2V09_5-E089–E096` explicitly preserve this independent best-friend structure.

This is why the correct model is:

> **vertical emotional dependency plus growing horizontal autonomy.**

Kei is not globally independent.

She is no longer relationally monopolized.

---

## 5.6 Y2V09.5 — the curriculum becomes explicit

Y2V09.5 is the harshest pre-breakup evidence because Ayanokōji explicitly audits the relationship as a learning environment.

He distinguishes romantic form from acquired emotion, evaluates the cold-war interval as experiment, and continues to treat dependency itself as something that can be intensified and later tested.

This does not retroactively erase the ordinary life that has accumulated.

It does establish that the relationship remains partly governed by a private theory Kei does not share.

The asymmetry is therefore not simply “he knows more about the White Room.”

It is that he knows the **meta-purpose of the relationship as he understands it**, while she does not.

---

## 5.7 Y2V12 — visible permission, invisible timetable

Y2V12 gives the cleanest local example of why formal consent is insufficient.

Ayanokōji asks Kei for permission to meet the class leaders. She reads the request as evidence of trust and respect.

Internally, however, he is already in:

> `希望を絶つための準備段階`

The visible interaction is reciprocal in form.

The hidden temporal structure is unilateral.

This is why Year 2 adds **timing** to the consent framework.

A person cannot meaningfully authorize the direction of a relationship if the decisive future has already been privately scheduled and the information needed to contest it is withheld.

---

## 5.8 Y2V12.5 — behavioral autonomy fails as a sufficient test

The breakup chapter finally adjudicates the relation.

Ayanokōji confirms internally that separation was part of the relationship's architecture. He also hopes, before completing it, that his own forecast might fail—that some uncalculated feeling might stop him.

This wish matters. It proves that the relationship is not emotionally empty.

It does not change who controls the situation.

Kei reconstructs the architecture with painful accuracy. She wants to cling, beg, and promise anything. She does not because she believes that doing so would violate **his developmental expectation** that she learn to stand alone.

Her conduct is therefore agentic and authored at the same time.

She is choosing.

The norm according to which she evaluates the “correct” choice has been deeply shaped by the person leaving her.

Her refusal of explanation does not cure the information deficit because it occurs after the relationship's timetable has already been authored and under acute emotional distress.

The canonical Year-2 answer to the reciprocal-information question is therefore negative:

> **The romance ends without Kei having received enough timely information to co-author its meaning or endpoint.**

Yet the relation leaves unmistakably real residue.

Ayanokōji allows the public narrative that Kei dumped him, protecting her social position. The next morning his body begins preparing a second bowl of rice before conscious correction.

The correct result is not “he secretly loved her conventionally.”

It is narrower and more important:

> **ordinary repetition made Kei non-fungible in embodied memory even after the developmental script reached its intended endpoint.**

# 6. Ayanokōji and Horikita Suzune — judgment reciprocity under timetable asymmetry

Ayanokōji–Horikita becomes one of the strongest reciprocal relationships in the corpus if reciprocity is measured by **contradiction and political authorship**.

It remains deeply asymmetric if reciprocity is measured by **life-planning information**.

The relation therefore demonstrates why “Horikita is independent” and “Horikita is still dependent” can both be misleading without specifying the domain.

---

## 6.1 Year-1 inheritance — proxy theory already failed

By the end of Year 1, Horikita was no longer adequately described as Ayanokōji's proxy.

She could:

- detect hidden authorship;
- demand explanation;
- run her own tests;
- form independent ends;
- learn from other people;
- and transform Manabu's recognition from specification into release.

The unresolved problem was structural.

Ayanokōji retained hidden security, superior information, and a future theory of her class that she could not inspect.

Year 2 makes the difference between **judgment reciprocity** and **timetable reciprocity** explicit.

---

## 6.2 Y2V05 — consequential contradiction becomes indisputable

Horikita's decision to preserve Kushida is the strongest proof that Ayanokōji does not own her political judgment.

He intends a different expulsion outcome.

She rejects it.

Her decision changes the class permanently.

The decision is ethically costly and cannot be retrospectively purified by Kushida's later contribution. But as relationship evidence it closes the puppet model.

Horikita can produce an answer Ayanokōji does not want and force him to operate around it.

That is a high form of reciprocity.

---

## 6.3 Y2V06–V10 — answer dependence declines

Across the middle of the year, Horikita increasingly authors:

- bounded contracts;
- exam structures;
- student-council recruitment;
- alliances;
- role distribution;
- and class decisions.

Ayanokōji's direct solution becomes less necessary.

The relationship therefore changes from:

`hidden solver -> visible executor`

into something closer to:

`hidden strategic reserve <-> independently functioning political actor`.

That is a major development.

It is not the same as removing the reserve.

---

## 6.4 Ordinary closeness creates a different dependency vector

As institutional dependence declines, personal significance grows.

V10's spontaneous smile matters because Horikita becomes associated with interaction that is not required by strategy. The relation contains purposeless conversational value.

By V12.5, Horikita worries about Ayanokōji after the burden of expulsions and offers support if he is hurting. He does not enter that protective relationship. He defers.

This matters for `Y1H-035` and `Y1H-016`.

Horikita is increasingly willing to care for him as a person.

The corpus still lacks a case where he voluntarily reveals genuine helplessness and grants her meaningful protective authority.

---

## 6.5 Recognition desire is not obedience

`Second List` `Y2SL-E050` accurately compresses one real feature: Horikita wants Ayanokōji's recognition.

The mistake would be to infer that recognition desire explains her leadership or nullifies self-authorship.

By Year 2, the evidence shows both:

- she values his evaluation;
- she can consequentially reject his preferred answer.

Recognition is therefore an attachment vector, not sovereign certification.

---

## 6.6 V12–V12.5 — the separation-information failure

The strongest remaining asymmetry is not tactical competence.

It is that Ayanokōji controls the timetable of institutional separation.

Horikita increasingly imagines a future in which he remains nearby. At the final class celebration, she explicitly asks him to stay beside her and watch over the class.

He has already chosen the opposite future.

Her experience of loneliness after he silently leaves the celebration is real attachment evidence.

It is not proof of political incapacity.

The Year-2 relationship result is therefore:

> **Horikita is capable of standing against Ayanokōji's judgment before she is allowed to co-author the meaning of standing without his presence.**

That distinction is essential.

---

## 6.7 Romance-label discipline

V12.5 gives Horikita an unfamiliar bodily/emotional response during a playful “lover candidate” exchange.

The source establishes:

- novelty;
- salience;
- difficulty of immediate self-classification.

It does not require this specialist to convert the reaction into a definitive romance label.

At `Y2SL`, the authoritative classification is **epistemic novelty inside a relationship of growing personal significance**.

This is intentionally more precise than either “they are obviously romantic” or “there is nothing romantic present.”

# 7. Ayanokōji and Ichinose Honami — trust becomes counter-authorship

Ayanokōji–Ichinose undergoes the largest Year-2 relationship reclassification.

The Year-1 relation could still be described as trust across rivalry under paternalistic developmental judgment. Ayanokōji offered presence, protection, and a one-year horizon while privately retaining `介錯` jurisdiction over what should happen if Ichinose or her class failed.

Year 2 turns the target of that judgment into the strongest person capable of judging **him**.

---

## 7.1 The Year-1 promise creates a jurisdiction problem

The one-year promise is relationally meaningful because it establishes continuity and future attention.

It is also dangerous because Ayanokōji increasingly treats it as authorization to determine **when** Ichinose should encounter decisive crisis.

Y2V08 sharpens the frame through the language of `生か死` and `介錯`.

The issue is no longer “will he help?”

It becomes:

> **What standing does he have to determine the timing, form, and acceptable pain of another person's developmental threshold?**

---

## 7.2 Reform creates a relationship beyond the dyad

Y2V07–V09 materially reduce the one-to-one protector/subject framing.

Kanzaki, Himeno, Watanabe, Amikura, Hamaguchi, and others form a plural reform layer inside Ichinose's class. Ayanokōji helps create conditions for discussion, but the resulting political intelligence is not reducible to him.

The most important relational effect is that Ichinose's social world becomes capable of:

- protecting her;
- challenging her;
- interpreting her condition;
- and placing moral demands on Ayanokōji.

The `Second List` bonus story `あの頃から───` makes this explicit. Amikura recounts classmates collectively protecting Ichinose during an unstable period, then asks Ayanokōji not to hurt her unnecessarily.

The dyad acquires **third-party moral witnesses**.

This matters because developmental jurisdiction becomes less monopolized when the subject has a social world that can contest the developer's conduct.

---

## 7.3 Ayanokōji explicitly rejects total authorship credit

The same bonus story supplies one of the most important anti-omnipotence controls in the entire corpus.

When Amikura asks whether he restored Ichinose, Ayanokōji rejects the premise and credits Ichinose with standing back up through her own power.

This does not make his influence irrelevant.

It limits causal ownership.

The relation therefore already contains the seed of the Year-end principle:

> **influence can be real without making the influenced person's entire development the influencer's achievement.**

---

## 7.4 Y2V09–V10 — attachment becomes strategically competent

Ichinose's development does not require abandoning solidarity, kindness, or personal trust.

She becomes more adversarially competent while remaining recognizably Ichinose.

Direct class conflict with Horikita does not require personal hatred. Trust can survive opposition.

Y2V09.5 also shows that her desires can be personal rather than class-functional. The photograph project matters because it is not reducible to political obligation.

The target of development is acquiring more purposes of her own.

---

## 7.5 Y2V12 — protection and violation occupy the same architecture

V12 is the relation's ethical nadir.

Ayanokōji constructs a bargain that materially protects Ichinose's class from traitor-expulsion risk and produces class benefit.

He also withholds the actual developmental function of that bargain.

Ichinose becomes unknowingly entangled in Maezono's expulsion, and Ayanokōji times truth to destabilize the reasoning system that makes her formidable during the exam.

This is exactly why “he helped her class” is not sufficient ethical analysis.

The same structure can be:

- protective toward the polity;
- instrumental toward the person;
- and autonomy-defective in timing.

Trust becomes an attack surface because he knows which truths matter and when they will hurt most.

---

## 7.6 Y2V12.5 — `介錯` becomes a defeated binary

The terminal confrontation begins from enormous asymmetry.

Ayanokōji offers a two-option structure whose logic he controls. Ichinose can withdraw and let him take over, or remain and convert rupture into adversarial fuel.

He predicts the second with overwhelming confidence.

Crucially, he wants a third answer that he himself does not possess.

Ichinose produces it.

She identifies that neither option contains the true answer, preserves her refusal to sacrifice classmates, and articulates a method for maintaining the four-class balance through her own thought.

This is stronger than merely “choosing differently.”

The content of the answer is outside the developer's model.

That is why the event is canonical **counter-authorship**.

---

## 7.7 The observer becomes observed

Ichinose then reverses authorship again.

Ayanokōji believes he has forced the decisive encounter by coming to her room.

She reveals that she predicted he would come and prepared the encounter around his curiosity and developmental compulsion.

The person he studies has built a working model of **him**.

The asymmetry remains large.

But it is no longer one-directional.

---

## 7.8 Reciprocal use is not equality

Ichinose explicitly names the structure:

> `綾小路くんが私を利用するように、私もあなたを利用する。その権利はあるよね?`

Ayanokōji accepts that he lacks the right to deny the claim.

The sentence is important because Ichinose does not demand purity.

She does not say influence or strategic use must disappear.

She demands **reciprocal standing**.

The relation therefore moves from:

`developer -> subject`

through:

`developer <-> resistant subject`

into:

`two strategically aware participants with unequal information but reciprocal causal claims`.

That is not symmetry.

It is a much stronger form of answerability.

---

## 7.9 Intimacy-label discipline

V12.5 contains a mutually chosen intimate encounter and an “absolute contract” language of mutual need/use.

Because the characters are minors, this synthesis does not reproduce or elaborate physical detail.

Analytically, the important result is not to force the scene into a single conventional label.

At `Y2SL`, the relation contains:

- intense romantic attachment on Ichinose's side;
- strategic reciprocity;
- mutual causal acknowledgment;
- secrecy;
- significant remaining information asymmetry;
- and a continuing relation that does not map cleanly onto a simple public boyfriend/girlfriend category.

`Second List` `Y2SL-E054` appropriately compresses the relation as one in which Ichinose has learned more of Ayanokōji's nature and shares a secret relation.

---

The terminal principle is:

> **Ichinose does not escape Ayanokōji's influence. She becomes capable of making his influence answerable to a model and purpose he does not own.**

# 8. Ayanokōji and Shiina Hiyori — participant mode as a positive control

Hiyori is diagnostically important because the relation shows what Ayanokōji can look like when he is not primarily operating as protector, hidden developer, examiner, or sovereign stage-setter.

The relationship forms through books, quiet conversation, cross-class curiosity, and low-pressure presence.

Class competition remains real.

It does not own the bond.

---

## 8.1 Shared interest does not need a manufactured deficit

Ayanokōji does not need Hiyori to be threatened, isolated, indebted, or strategically dependent in order to sustain the relation.

The bond contains ordinary reasons for return.

That makes it one of the cleanest controls against the proposition that all his social relations require engineered need.

---

## 8.2 Y2V09.5 — disclosed desire plus preserved refusal

The clearest participant-mode sequence occurs when Hiyori reveals that the author of a book she gave Ayanokōji is her father and that she wanted **him specifically** to know.

Later Ayanokōji invites her out.

He worries that the invitation could feel like pity or pressure. When she asks why, he does not tell her what he thinks she secretly needs.

He says that **he wanted to invite her**.

He also recognizes her right to refuse.

The structure is therefore:

`I want this -> I disclose that authorship -> you may say no`

rather than:

`I know what you need -> I create the condition -> your predictable response completes the plan`.

That difference is why Hiyori is the strongest local positive countermodel.

---

## 8.3 Competition without emotional ownership

Hiyori can want Ayanokōji to graduate with her while remaining loyal to Ryūen's class.

The relationship demonstrates:

> **institutional rivalry can govern outcomes without owning affection.**

This principle becomes increasingly important across Year 2 because cross-class friendship generates moral friction without requiring classes to cease competing.

`Second List` `Y2SL-E055` adds only a cautious calibration: reading companion with faint affection.

That is appropriately narrower than forcing the relation into conventional romance.

# 9. Ayanokōji and Hirata Yōsuke — care becomes bounded rather than universal

The Year-1 relation began with unusual access obtained partly through coercive repair. Hirata trusted Ayanokōji because Ayanokōji could reach a wound and decision structure most classmates could not.

Year 2 improves the relation primarily through **limits**.

Hirata remains caring.

He becomes less likely to treat caring as jurisdiction over every painful outcome.

---

## 9.1 Mutual class care

Hirata increasingly performs mediation Ayanokōji does not own, remains functional through collective injury, and accepts that another legitimate actor may make a decision he dislikes.

The relationship becomes less:

`Ayanokōji repairs Hirata so Hirata can function`

and more:

`two differently constituted actors who care about overlapping people and can criticize one another's jurisdiction`.

---

## 9.2 `越権行為` as relational correction

Hirata's ability to name overreach is particularly important.

He can recognize when Ayanokōji's representative competence risks becoming unauthorized class sovereignty.

That makes Hirata more than a socially skilled subordinate.

He becomes a constitutional moral witness.

---

## 9.3 Care begins moving toward Ayanokōji

At the Year-2 endpoint, Hirata commits himself to supporting Kei and the class while also telling Ayanokōji that help is available if Ayanokōji becomes lost.

The direction of protection begins to reverse.

Ayanokōji still does not expose the kind of helplessness that would place him under Hirata's meaningful protective authority.

`Y1H-034` therefore remains only partially answered for Hirata's own particular-attachment problem, and `Y1H-035` remains open for Ayanokōji.

# 10. Ayanokōji and Sakayanagi Arisu — love, recognition, and indirect jurisdiction

Ayanokōji–Sakayanagi begins as unusually high epistemic recognition and becomes one of the clearest Year-2 demonstrations that **understanding another person can govern without commanding**.

---

## 10.1 Volume 0 changes origin, not chronology

Volume 0 reveals that Sakayanagi's nature-versus-nurture interest begins with childhood observation of Kiyotaka.

This is retrospective revelation.

It does not mean every school-age interaction should be reread as if the reader possessed that information prospectively.

It does establish that Sakayanagi's relation is self-chosen, durable, and older than the school rivalry.

---

## 10.2 Y2V09.5 — love becomes explicit without becoming surrender

Sakayanagi identifies romantic love and refuses the premise that love must simply be weakness.

Her desire remains competitive.

She wants to disrupt Ayanokōji's completed calculations, draw out emotion, and defeat the “created genius” while also loving him.

The relation therefore resists the binary:

`rivalry or love`.

For Sakayanagi, love becomes another reason the rival must remain capable of surprising and resisting her.

---

## 10.3 V12 — inferred preference becomes a relational veto

The decisive ethical problem arises because Ayanokōji does not directly order Sakayanagi to lose.

She reconstructs his preference from an authored clue chain.

She retains formal freedom.

She can win.

Yet her understanding of him is precise enough that his inferred preference becomes more powerful than an explicit command would need to be.

This is **indirect jurisdiction through mutual legibility**.

Her agency remains real.

His causal responsibility also remains real.

The case therefore defeats two simplifications simultaneously:

- “she chose it, so he is not responsible”;
- “he influenced it, so she had no agency.”

Both causation and authorship can be distributed.

---

## 10.4 Love creates fiduciary danger

Sakayanagi's personal choice is costly because she is not only a private individual.

She is a sovereign whose class depends on her.

Her private relation to Ayanokōji becomes politically causal enough to override the polity's strongest institutional interest.

This closes the Year-1 fiduciary question negatively.

Love is not the moral failure.

The failure is allowing private relational priority to become sovereign without adequate accountability to dependents.

---

## 10.5 V12.5 — personal futurity survives institutional defeat

Sakayanagi's withdrawal is not annihilation.

She owns the final decision, asks Ayanokōji to promise future contact after school, tells him that his present life should ultimately be his to decide, and extends the Yamamura relation beyond ANHS.

The result is a strong role-survival case:

`school sovereign -> former student -> future person with continuing relationships`

Personal self-authorship survives.

Institutional succession does not.

`Second List` `Y2SL-E053` correctly compresses the Ayanokōji relation as both person to defeat and person she loves.

---

# 11. Ayanokōji and Ryūen Kakeru — adversarial reciprocity and self-authentication

Rivalry is one of COTE's most important relationship forms because it can create recognition without requiring affection, moral agreement, or alliance.

Ayanokōji–Ryūen becomes increasingly reciprocal in Year 2.

---

## 11.1 Preservation becomes more than utility

Year 1 already made pure-disposability models difficult. Ayanokōji preserves Ryūen partly because Ryūen remains strategically useful and developmentally interesting.

Year 2 makes the relation more particular.

Ayanokōji's four-class design requires independent competitive centers. Ryūen is not interchangeable with a compliant high-ability substitute because the value lies partly in his capacity to remain difficult.

---

## 11.2 Ryūen wants meaningful victory, not only official victory

The reciprocity becomes clearest when Ryūen's own standard changes.

A favorable result is not enough if it does not authenticate his capability against a worthy opponent.

By V12.5, Sakayanagi's thrown official result is unsatisfying precisely because he knows she defeated him on merit first.

The rival's independent strength becomes part of the standard by which he judges himself.

This is adversarial recognition at a high level.

---

## 11.3 The relation remains ethically mixed

Reciprocal rivalry does not rehabilitate Ryūen's internal polity.

He can value Ayanokōji as a difficult external benchmark while still responding coercively to internal dissent.

The relationship must therefore be distinguished from the ethics of either participant's leadership system.

`Second List` `Y2SL-E056` frames Ryūen as fated rival/person to defeat, consistent with the mature state.

---

# 12. Ayanokōji and Nagumo Miyabi — from imposed proving field to bounded rivalry

Nagumo's relation to Ayanokōji begins inside institutional asymmetry.

Nagumo controls student-council authority, third-year patronage, and a system that can make unrelated students bear the consequences of his need for validation.

His problem is epistemic:

> **supremacy without credible counterforce cannot prove itself to the sovereign who designed the field.**

Ayanokōji becomes the person against whom he wants authentic measurement.

Year 2 improves the relation when the personal contest becomes more bounded and less extractive.

By the exchange-camp phase, the rivalry can be financed through Nagumo's own resources, carried with lower third-party externality, and treated as a contest between participants rather than an entire grade serving one person's need for proof.

The relational principle is:

> **Rivalry becomes more reciprocal when unrelated dependents stop paying for the rivals' need to authenticate themselves.**

`Second List` `Y2SL-E057` preserves the endpoint as recognition of Ayanokōji's ability and desire for competition.

# 13. Ayanokōji and Atsuomi Ayanokōji — the originating authorship relation

Volume 0 makes the father-son relation indispensable to any Year-2 relationship synthesis because it provides the genealogy of hidden curriculum, preference engineering, and opacity toward the author of one's environment.

It must be used carefully.

Genealogy is not destiny.

---

## 13.1 Atsuomi's authorship is environmental before it is relational

Atsuomi's deepest form of control is not emotional closeness.

It is the design of institutions, developmental conditions, incentives, and available futures.

The person is governed by the world through which options become thinkable.

This supplies a powerful retrospective analogue for Ayanokōji's later environment-first methods.

It does not prove conscious imitation.

---

## 13.2 Childhood opacity is a survival relation

`V00-RR-035` shows young Kiyotaka understanding more about Atsuomi than Atsuomi realizes and concealing that understanding.

Opacity toward the powerful author is therefore not merely adolescent social awkwardness.

It has deep developmental roots as a way of retaining unowned interior territory.

That genealogy helps explain why adult reciprocal disclosure is difficult.

It does not excuse withholding from people who do not possess Atsuomi's coercive authority.

---

## 13.3 Preference engineering and later relationship ethics

`V00-RR-054` supplies the political genealogy of preference engineering: create conditions in which another person comes to choose what the author wants.

Year 2 repeatedly shows Ayanokōji using structurally similar methods.

The ethical problem is not simply resemblance to his father.

The problem is whether he can become capable of recognizing a boundary his father did not: that successful authorship of conditions does not grant title to the person's answer.

Hiyori participant mode, the V11 stopping rule, Horikita's disagreement, and Ichinose counter-authorship are important precisely because they supply experiences of relationship that can compete with the originating model.

---

## 13.4 Opposition is not yet self-authorship

V12.5 reactivates Atsuomi's postgraduation plans and power.

Ayanokōji's opposition is real.

But a future defined only as “not what Atsuomi wants” would still be organized around Atsuomi.

The strongest future relational question is therefore whether Ayanokōji can form purposes and durable relationships whose meaning is not reducible to either compliance with or rebellion against the originating author.

At `Y2SL`, that remains open.

---
# 14. Horikita Suzune and Kushida Kikyō — covenant without reconciliation becomes self-authored cooperation

Horikita–Kushida is the year’s strongest proof that legitimate cooperation does not require affection, forgiveness, or a restored harmonious identity.

It is also one of the clearest cases where **later successful cooperation must not retroactively legitimate the route by which the relationship was preserved**.

---

## 14.1 Year-1 inheritance — knowledge as coercive jurisdiction

The relationship begins from irreversible knowledge.

Horikita knows the private Kushida. Kushida experiences that knowledge not as intimacy but as danger because the existence of a witness makes restoration of the old public/private compartmentalization impossible.

Year 1 therefore left them in hostile coexistence.

Horikita's anti-disposability impulse pushed toward integration.

Kushida did not authorize that project.

---

## 14.2 V05 — preservation is Horikita-authored, not Kushida-authored

The Unanimous Vote Examination destroys the old equilibrium.

Kushida's concealed history and hostile conduct become public. She retaliates through classmates' secrets. The universally friendly mask cannot simply be restored.

Horikita nevertheless chooses preservation.

This is independent political authorship.

It is not relational consent.

Kushida wants expulsion rather than salvation under Horikita's theory of her future usefulness. Airi bears the irreversible terminal cost.

The relationship therefore enters Year 2's central ethics distinction:

> **A correct or later-validated model of someone's capacity does not grant jurisdiction to force that person into the modeled future at other people's expense.**

---

## 14.3 V06 — the ongoing project changes ownership

The decisive improvement occurs when Horikita stops demanding gratitude, reconciliation, or class affection.

She tells Kushida to use her rare abilities for **herself**.

Kushida chooses the bargain:

> `私は私のためだけに１年半戦ってクラスに貢献する。`

The left-handed handshake is analytically powerful because it refuses sentimental falsification.

Hatred remains.

Cooperation becomes legitimate because its **continuing motive is hers**.

This is the correct answer to the inherited project question:

> **The preservation was not self-authored; the continuing contribution becomes self-authored.**

---

## 14.4 Exposure makes contradictory selves coexist publicly

The old structure required:

`public friendliness + hidden hostility + witness elimination`.

After V05, that structure is impossible.

The new structure is harder but more legitimate:

`known contradiction + bounded cooperation + visible self-interest`.

Kushida can remain socially skilled without pretending that skill equals universal affection.

The public and private registers do not merge into one purified personality. They become legible enough to operate in the same social field.

---

## 14.5 V07–V09 — contribution stops requiring Horikita's scripting

Kushida investigates threats to the class because class survival overlaps with her chosen future.

She ends the Yagami relation on terms she owns and threatens reciprocal exposure rather than remaining a passive information asset.

Horikita's later student-council recruitment improves on V05 because it offers reasons Kushida can appropriate: recognition, status, influence, renewed competitive route.

Kushida chooses.

The project therefore moves from:

`Horikita preserves Kushida because Horikita predicts future value`

into:

`Kushida uses the preserved future for goals she recognizes as her own`.

That is genuine development without reconciliation.

---

## 14.6 Boundary result

`Y1H-017`, `Y1H-018`, and `Y1H-019` are all answered by development in qualified form:

- public/private contradiction can coexist after secrecy collapses;
- hatred can coexist with legitimate cooperation;
- the ongoing Horikita–Kushida project can become Kushida-authored.

The permanent qualification is V05.

Later success does not make the origin consensual.

# 15. Horikita Suzune and Sudō Ken — development survives the loss of romantic reward

Horikita–Sudō is one of the cleanest positive Year-2 relationship arcs because it demonstrates that influence can cease to own the learner.

---

## 15.1 Year-1 inheritance — development partly organized around recognition

Sudō's early effort is substantially motivated by Horikita.

Her approval, attention, and imagined romantic possibility supply powerful incentives.

That does not make the development fake.

It leaves open whether the gains can survive if the original reward disappears.

---

## 15.2 Trajectory becomes self-owned

By Year 2, Sudō's study, athletic pacing, anger regulation, and social judgment increasingly function without constant Horikita reward.

His outdated OAA scores themselves become evidence that snapshot measurement lags development.

The key relationship shift is from:

`I improve because I want Horikita`

into:

`I have become a person capable of valuing the improved practices themselves`.

---

## 15.3 Romantic rejection is the role-change test

Horikita rejects Sudō romantically.

The relation does not collapse.

Sudō does not abandon study, discipline, class contribution, or respect.

His development survives the loss of the original hoped-for future.

That is stronger evidence of successful influence than continued devotion would have been.

The learner no longer needs the developer's romantic availability as the condition of maintaining the result.

---

## 15.4 Development becomes generative

Sudō eventually becomes capable of helping weaker classmates and transmitting learned discipline laterally.

Onodera's immediate teaching of anger regulation is also important because it shows Sudō himself can be developed by peers outside Horikita's original dyad.

The relationship therefore enters a mature network:

`Horikita -> Sudō -> other classmates`

and

`Onodera -> Sudō`.

Development is no longer vertically monopolized.

---

# 16. Horikita Suzune and Ibuki Mio — adversarial friendship and moral friction

Horikita–Ibuki is one of the cleanest examples of a relationship that becomes more personally meaningful **without requiring politeness, ideological convergence, or the end of class rivalry**.

---

## 16.1 Repeated cooperation creates chosen reliability

The relation moves through investigation, competition, blunt disagreement, and repeated moments where each can treat the other as a credible partner for a bounded task.

The low-ceremony register is analytically important.

Neither needs to perform warmth in order for the bond to be real.

---

## 16.2 Friendship changes the cost of political decisions

By V10, the possibility of Ibuki's expulsion changes Horikita's protection calculus.

That hesitation is not proof that friendship owns the policy decision.

It is proof that the relationship has made loss personally meaningful.

This is moral friction:

> **knowing another person can change the cost function of a decision without automatically dictating the decision.**

---

## 16.3 Cross-class attachment remains politically honest

Horikita and Ibuki remain members of rival polities.

The bond does not require pretending otherwise.

That makes it a useful contrast to relationship models in which personal affection is treated as requiring political softness.

The mature state is **adversarial friendship**: reliable attachment with preserved blunt contradiction and genuine conflicting institutional interests.

---

# 17. Horikita Suzune and Ichinose Honami — respect survives direct conflict

Horikita–Ichinose demonstrates that respect and political competition need not collapse into either alliance or hatred.

Year 1 had already shown that a temporary alliance could end without personal enmity.

Year 2 supplies a much harder test.

They become direct competitors. Ichinose's class develops greater adversarial competence. V12 culminates in Ichinose decisively defeating Horikita in the representative contest.

The defeat is personally painful and politically consequential.

The source does not require a conversion of the relation into hatred.

The governing principle is:

> **Respect does not entitle a rival to strategic softness. Competition does not require moral dehumanization.**

This is one of the most important cross-class relationship forms in a series whose institutional structure repeatedly tries to make personal and collective interest diverge.

---

# 18. Karuizawa Kei and Satō Maya — horizontal friendship as protective infrastructure

Kei–Satō is the strongest Year-2 example of friendship reducing monopolized dependency without demanding complete emotional independence.

---

## 18.1 Dangerous truth does not destroy belonging

The V07 disclosure of Kei's bullying history is the decisive event.

Ayanokōji authors the timing.

Satō authors the answer.

She stays.

The narration promotes the relationship to `親友`.

For Kei, whose earlier survival logic treated dangerous truth as a route to exclusion or domination, this is a major counterexample.

---

## 18.2 Satō becomes an independent moral witness

The friendship becomes structurally important because Satō is not simply another source of comfort.

She can judge Ayanokōji's behavior independently and side with Kei.

During romantic destabilization, Kei can therefore access a relationship that does not inherit the boyfriend's developmental theory.

That changes the power ecology of the romance.

---

## 18.3 Y2V09.5 — friendship becomes self-consciously chosen

`Y2V09_5-E089–E096` supply unusually explicit best-friend evidence.

Kei's interiority recognizes Satō as a real friend formed after arriving at ANHS. Handholding is not romantic confusion or social performance; it is something the friends want to do because they are close.

The relation therefore has its own ordinary-life grammar.

It is not merely “support for the main romance plot.”

---

## 18.4 Post-breakup social causality

At the Year-2 endpoint, Kei's wider friend network is organized enough around **her** that friends can become colder toward Ayanokōji after the breakup.

This is a high-value autonomy result because social attachment now has independent causal force.

The friends are not simply extensions of his protection system.

The relationship principle is:

> **Horizontal friendship is protective infrastructure when it gives a dependent person witnesses, alternatives, interpretation, and loyalty that do not pass through the dominant vertical bond.**

---

# 19. Shiina Hiyori and Ryūen Kakeru — diagnosis without constitutional jurisdiction

Hiyori–Ryūen is a relationship of high diagnostic value and limited formal power.

Hiyori can remain loyal to the class while refusing to become a miniature Ryūen.

She can:

- reject methods she considers wrong;
- identify developmental costs in apparent victories;
- distinguish leader success from class growth;
- and influence particular cases.

Ryūen increasingly respects her judgment.

But respect is not yet binding jurisdiction.

Tokitō remains the negative control. Open dissent can still encounter coercive retaliation.

The relation therefore supports a precise boundary:

> **Hiyori has moral and developmental intelligence inside Ryūen's polity; the polity has not constitutionalized that intelligence as a durable limit on Ryūen.**

`Y1H-026` remains strictly open.

# 20. Ichinose Honami, Kanzaki Ryūji, Himeno Yuki, and the reform network — loyal opposition becomes belonging

Ichinose's class provides one of Year 2's most important alternatives to the idea that cohesion requires agreement.

---

## 20.1 Isolated dissent is weak

Y2V05 shows Kanzaki capable of diagnosing serious strategic problems while remaining unable to translate diagnosis into legitimate constitutional change.

He can disagree.

The polity does not yet know how to use disagreement.

---

## 20.2 Reform becomes relational rather than merely ideological

Y2V07 begins a broader coalition.

Himeno's refusal to perform universal enthusiasm makes suppressed difference visible. Watanabe, Amikura, Hamaguchi, and others add perspectives that prevent reform from becoming merely “Kanzaki versus Ichinose.”

The result is a **second brain** rather than a rival sovereign.

---

## 20.3 Dissent becomes compatible with loyalty

Kanzaki does not need to stop caring about Ichinose or the class in order to oppose the current equilibrium.

Ichinose does not need to expel or humiliate critics to remain leader.

The relationship therefore answers `Y1H-021`:

> **Yes: loyal opposition can become part of belonging.**

The importance is relational before it is strategic.

A person can say “our current method is failing” without that statement becoming proof of betrayal.

---

## 20.4 Distributed care prevents leader-totalization

`あの頃から───` adds another layer. When Ichinose freezes during the accidental-message incident, female classmates collectively generate a protective cover story.

The leader is temporarily unable to author the answer.

The social network does.

This demonstrates that Ichinose-class solidarity is not only affection directed upward toward its central figure.

It can operate laterally and protect the leader.

That matters for succession and resilience.

---

## 20.5 Year-end hardening does not equal moral inversion

After V12.5, Ichinose is warmer and more dangerous at once.

She can preserve solidarity while becoming more capable of deterrence, strategic opacity, and confronting disloyal conduct.

The class has not become a culling polity.

The mature formulation is:

> **solidarity becomes more durable when boundaries and dissent can exist without making expendability the first answer.**

---

# 21. Ryūen Kakeru and his followers — voluntary legitimacy without protected dissent

Ryūen's follower relationships are a critical control against both “fear explains everything” and “loyalty proves the regime is healthy.”

Neither is sufficient.

---

## 21.1 The rooftop already broke the pure-fear model

Followers preserve Ryūen after defeat when bodily fear no longer explains the choice.

Year 2 makes the voluntary component increasingly explicit.

Komiya distinguishes the changed leader from the earlier ruler and describes the newer Ryūen as someone worth following.

Ishizaki and Albert continue to supply loyalty and ordinary social attachment.

---

## 21.2 Differentiated expertise improves the relation

Katsuragi's recruitment is significant because Ryūen wants him partly for capacities Ryūen himself lacks.

A subordinate is valuable not because he mirrors the sovereign but because difference improves the polity.

Delegation therefore becomes more real.

---

## 21.3 Tokitō proves the constitutional limit

Open dissent remains unsafe.

Ryūen can hear criticism and sometimes adapt, but the system does not reliably protect the critic from coercive retaliation.

The mature relation is therefore:

> **voluntary sociological legitimacy + differentiated follower attachment + growing delegation + unresolved authoritarian jurisdiction.**

This is relational development without constitutional completion.

# 22. Sakayanagi Arisu, Kamuro Masumi, Yamamura Miki, and Hashimoto Masayoshi — three failures of centralized recognition

These three relationships expose different limits of a polity organized around an exceptional sovereign's perception.

---

## 22.1 Kamuro — irreplaceability discovered after optimization has removed the choice

Kamuro begins as a blackmail-origin subordinate.

She becomes useful, familiar, and personally particular before Sakayanagi's model fully contains the category of friendship.

V10 forces the issue through irreversible loss.

Only after Kamuro is expelled can Sakayanagi adequately name what the relationship had become.

The lesson is severe:

> **a person can become irreplaceable before the optimizer possesses a variable for irreplaceability.**

The failure is not absence of perception in general.

It is that the model recognizes value too late to protect it.

---

## 22.2 Yamamura — service is not the same as being registered

Yamamura's problem is quieter.

She contributes while remaining socially under-registered.

What she wants is not abstract praise but evidence that her existence would make Sakayanagi **hesitate**.

Y2V11 creates an externally staged opportunity for that question to become visible.

The positive move belongs partly to Ayanokōji's stopping rule: he creates access, permits multiple outcomes, and refuses to own the answer.

The relation then acquires enough independent value to extend beyond school in V12.5.

This is a role-survival success.

---

## 22.3 Hashimoto — social intelligence without corrective trust

Hashimoto's political diagnosis is not foolish.

He correctly perceives that Sakayanagi's personal priorities may not align with the polity's overriding fiduciary objective.

His response is to turn trust into insurance.

People become exit options. Cooperation requires collateral. Betrayal is answered prospectively by willingness to betray first.

The problem is not lack of social intelligence.

It is lack of a relationship in which another person's correction can become authoritative **before** the model hardens into action.

Sakayanagi's polity gives Hashimoto too little protected dissent.

Hashimoto gives the polity too little loyal trust.

The result is constitutional rupture.

---

## 22.4 The comparative lesson

Kamuro, Yamamura, and Hashimoto expose three different recognition failures:

1. **late recognition** — the friend is named after loss;
2. **under-recognition** — the contributor is useful without being personally registered;
3. **non-corrective recognition** — the critic sees a real problem but lacks a trustworthy route for making the sovereign answer before betrayal becomes rational.

The common failure is centralized legibility without sufficiently answerable relationships.

# 23. Haruka Hasebe, Airi Sakura, Akito Miyake, and Keisei Yukimura — social succession after rupture

The Ayanokōji Group is one of Year 2's strongest demonstrations that a real friendship can break **because it was real** and later survive only by refusing restoration.

---

## 23.1 V05 — ordinary friendship becomes grievance

Airi's expulsion changes Ayanokōji's role inside the group.

He is no longer merely the quiet member whose hidden self others incompletely know.

He becomes a causal source of irreversible injury.

Haruka's hostility is therefore not evidence that the earlier friendship was fake.

The severity of betrayal is evidence that the relationship carried expectations worth betraying.

---

## 23.2 Non-forgiveness is legitimate relational agency

V06 refuses the easy repair script.

Haruka does not owe immediate forgiveness because Ayanokōji or Horikita can offer reasons for the class decision.

Akito's decision to remain close to Haruka prioritizes particular loyalty over frictionless reintegration.

This is an important Year-2 principle:

> **repair does not become more ethical by requiring the injured person to perform reconciliation on the timetable preferred by the polity.**

---

## 23.3 Airi becomes a counter-author from outside ANHS

The decisive change comes from Airi herself.

Haruka has treated expulsion as near-total life destruction.

Airi's visible post-school future disproves that totalization.

Haruka recognizes the error:

> `退学した人は全員不幸になると決めつけてた。`

The expelled person therefore changes the meaning of expulsion **from outside the institution that expelled her**.

This is counter-authorship in a social rather than strategic register.

ANHS removes Airi's student role.

It does not own her future.

---

## 23.4 The group survives by becoming something else

Haruka, Akito, and Keisei reconnect.

Ayanokōji consciously refuses re-entry and hopes the three-person bond becomes stronger without him.

The canonical outcome is succession, not restoration.

`Y1H-033` is answered:

> **Yes, the social structure survives the concealed central person—but only by ceasing to be the old Ayanokōji Group.**

This is one of the clearest demonstrations that continuity of relationship does not require continuity of form.

# 25. Dependency — from host models to plural interdependence

Year 2 makes dependency one of the clearest places where single-axis character analysis fails.

The series does not move toward a world in which mature people need nobody.

It moves toward a world in which dependence can become **plural, visible, bounded, corrigible, and survivable**.

---

## 25.1 Dependency is not intimacy by itself

Kei's host structure can intensify attention, loyalty, fear of loss, and emotional centrality.

Those effects do not by themselves establish relational recognition.

A relationship becomes more ethically serious when the dependent person can become **less dependent without becoming less valuable**.

That is why Ayanokōji's private interest in whether Kei can stand alone is not automatically unethical. The ethical problem lies in treating his right to test that question as self-evident and withholding information needed for Kei to co-author the test.

---

## 25.2 Vertical and horizontal dependence can move in opposite directions

Kei is the strongest example:

- vertical emotional dependence on Ayanokōji deepens;
- horizontal dependence on Satō and the female peer network strengthens;
- behavioral competence remains high;
- informational dependence remains severe.

No single “dependency score” can represent that state.

The relational model must be vector-valued.

---

## 25.3 Dependency can create capability without becoming permanent entitlement

Horikita's relation to Ayanokōji is developmental partly because access to him gives her information, challenge, and hidden security she could not initially reproduce.

The important question is whether that dependency becomes self-reducing.

By Y2SL, direct answer dependence is much lower.

The remaining hidden-security and timetable dependence is precisely why post-transfer performance remains an open succession test.

---

## 25.4 Dependency can be politically distributed

Ichinose's class supplies a positive network model.

The leader remains central, but classmates can protect her, criticize her, and form an opposition/reform structure. The polity therefore depends on many relational nodes rather than a single sovereign cognition.

Ryūen's class develops in this direction through differentiated expertise, but protected dissent remains weak.

Sakayanagi's class illustrates the failure mode: exceptional central recognition does not generate enough successor relationship infrastructure.

---

## 25.5 The Year-2 dependency standard

Ask five questions:

1. Is dependence monopolized?
2. Can the dependent person access independent witnesses or alternatives?
3. Does dependence build transferable capability?
4. Can dependence diminish without destroying belonging?
5. Can the dependent person contradict the source of support?

The mature alternative to both isolated autonomy and proprietary dependence is:

> **plural, answerable interdependence.**

---

# 26. Friendship — voluntary return, ordinary time, dangerous truth, and tolerated contradiction

Year 2 substantially strengthens the friendship model because friendships become more visible outside the strategic centers that initially made them possible.

---

## 26.1 Voluntary return remains the baseline

Friendship is strongly evidenced when people choose contact without immediate institutional necessity.

Ayanokōji–Hiyori is exemplary because books and conversation generate repeated return without manufactured threat.

Kei–Satō becomes especially strong because the bond sustains itself outside romance and class strategy.

Ishizaki's ordinary cross-class friendliness similarly matters because rivalry remains intact while leisure remains desirable.

---

## 26.2 Dangerous truth is a stronger test than pleasant compatibility

Kei–Satō becomes most diagnostic not through ordinary fun but through socially dangerous disclosure.

Satō receives information that could rationally destabilize Kei's social identity and remains.

The result teaches a broader Year-2 principle:

> **Friendship becomes especially credible when new information makes exit easier but the person voluntarily returns.**

---

## 26.3 Friendship can survive non-forgiveness

Haruka's relation to the successor group shows that reconciliation is not the only path to social survival.

She does not need to forgive Ayanokōji in order for her capacity for friendship to remain intact.

The social structure heals by changing center and form, not by requiring the injured person to erase grievance.

---

## 26.4 Friendship can exist across rivalry

Hiyori–Ayanokōji, Horikita–Ibuki, Horikita–Ichinose, Ishizaki–Ayanokōji, and the school-trip material all demonstrate that class competition does not exhaust interpersonal life.

This creates a constitutive tension in ANHS:

- institutional success is rivalrous;
- human knowledge creates attachments that make total rivalrous optimization morally costly.

That tension is not a flaw in the friendship evidence.

It is one of the school's most important social lessons.

---

## 26.5 Friendship does not require total self-disclosure

Year 1 already established that partial selves can form real bonds.

Year 2 preserves the point but sharpens the limit.

Incomplete disclosure becomes ethically significant when the hidden information materially changes:

- safety;
- consent;
- expected future;
- purpose of the interaction;
- or cost of leaving.

Hiyori can be a genuine friend without knowing the White Room.

Kei's romantic relationship cannot be fully reciprocal while she lacks the hidden curriculum and separation timetable.

The required disclosure threshold is relationship-specific.

---

## 26.6 Friendship as counter-curriculum

Ordinary friendship teaches things that optimization systems poorly represent:

- silence without failure;
- invitations with no strategic gain;
- jokes;
- embarrassment;
- handholding without romantic utility;
- reading preferences;
- concern for rivals;
- missing someone after a role ends;
- and choosing to remain near an imperfect person.

This is why ordinary-life evidence remains essential to reconstruction.

Crisis-only analysis overestimates strategic motive and underestimates the social habits through which people become particular.

# 27. Romance — mutual desire without automatic reciprocity

Year 2 contains several different romantic or near-romantic structures. They must not be flattened into one hierarchy of “who loves whom most.”

---

## 27.1 Ayanokōji–Kei — explicit romance, incomplete information

This is the formal girlfriend/boyfriend relation of Year 2.

Its ordinary reality is strong.

Its informational reciprocity is weak.

The key lesson is:

> **Mutual romantic participation does not by itself repair coercive history, hidden curriculum, emotional dependency, or unilateral control over the relationship timetable.**

---

## 27.2 Ichinose — romantic attachment becomes strategic agency

Ichinose's attachment to Ayanokōji remains intense.

Year 2 rejects the inference that such attachment must make her politically passive.

By the endpoint, attachment coexists with:

- refusal;
- criticism;
- strategic concealment;
- adversarial competence;
- and reciprocal-use claims.

The relationship remains secret and structurally unusual rather than fitting cleanly into the public girlfriend/boyfriend model.

The correct analytical emphasis is **agency inside attachment**.

---

## 27.3 Sakayanagi — love as self-authored power and fiduciary risk

Sakayanagi explicitly identifies love.

She does not experience it merely as contamination of rationality.

Love becomes another dimension of rivalry and desire to produce an unmodeled Ayanokōji.

But because Sakayanagi is a class sovereign, private romantic priority acquires public cost.

Her case therefore proves that love can be personally self-authored and politically irresponsible at the same time.

---

## 27.4 Horikita — preserve novelty without forced closure

Horikita's V12.5 response establishes unfamiliar relational affect.

It does not justify a definitive romance classification at the Year-2 boundary.

The correct state is:

> **growing personal significance + recognition desire + unfamiliar bodily/emotional response + unresolved self-classification.**

Future interpretation must not be imported backward into Y2SL.

---

## 27.5 Hiyori — faint affection does not overwrite friendship

`Second List` describes faint affection while preserving reading-companion friendship.

The guidebook calibration should not be used to transform every prior book conversation into courtship.

The primary relation remains participant-mode friendship with possible romantic valence.

---

## 27.6 Romance is not the endpoint of relational seriousness

Some of Year 2's strongest reciprocal relationships are non-romantic:

- Horikita–Kushida hostile cooperation;
- Horikita–Ibuki adversarial friendship;
- Kei–Satō best friendship;
- Ayanokōji–Ryūen rivalry;
- Ichinose–Kanzaki loyal opposition;
- Haruka–Airi continuing friendship after expulsion.

The corpus therefore rejects romance as a universal measure of relational depth.

# 28. Recognition — seeing the person without claiming title to the person

Recognition is the conceptual bridge between relationship and ethics in Year 2.

Ayanokōji, Sakayanagi, Horikita, Ichinose, Hiyori, and Ryūen all become better at recognizing particular capacities and motives.

The ethical issue is what follows from recognition.

---

## 28.1 Accurate recognition can enlarge agency

Horikita's recognition of Sudō's trajectory lets her treat current scores as stale rather than essential.

Recognition of Kushida's persistent social capacity creates a path to later self-authored contribution.

Ryūen's recognition of Katsuragi's differentiated expertise supports delegation.

Ayanokōji's recognition of Yamamura's unmet relational need makes a conversation possible.

These are constructive uses.

---

## 28.2 Accurate recognition can also govern more efficiently

The same principle cuts the other way.

Ayanokōji knows what Kei fears, what Ichinose values, what Sakayanagi will infer, and what Horikita is being prepared to become.

The better the model, the less overt coercion may be necessary.

Thus:

> **epistemic recognition is not moral recognition.**

The person is not fully recognized merely because she is predicted.

---

## 28.3 Irreplaceability often becomes visible too late

Kamuro is the clearest case.

Sakayanagi's model can optimize ability and political risk while failing to represent friendship until loss makes the category unavoidable.

Ayanokōji's post-breakup rice-bowl habit provides a quieter analogue: ordinary repetition registers value outside the explicit curricular model.

The body or grief may know before the theory does.

---

## 28.4 Recognition requires corrigibility

The strongest Year-2 standard is not “I understand you.”

It is:

> **I understand you, and your answer still has standing to tell me that my understanding is incomplete.**

This is why Ichinose's year-end counter-authorship outranks mere predictive success as relationship evidence.

---

# 29. Information, timing, secrecy, and consent — relationship as epistemic government

Year 2 makes information architecture one of the central relationship systems.

Secrets do not merely conceal facts.

They distribute decision rights.

---

## 29.1 What matters is not only truth but actionable truth

A person may technically be told something and still lack informed choice if the disclosure arrives after:

- alternatives have closed;
- emotional commitments have been engineered;
- social costs have become prohibitive;
- or the decisive outcome has already been institutionally scheduled.

The canonical Year-2 rule is:

> **Accurate information delivered too late to affect the choice is not equivalent to informed consent.**

---

## 29.2 Kei — the romance endpoint proves timing failure

Kei knows that Ayanokōji is unusual and secretive.

She does not know enough about his curriculum, internal timetable, biography, or transfer plan to co-author the breakup before it occurs.

Her terminal refusal of explanation does not make the earlier asymmetry disappear.

---

## 29.3 Horikita — political reciprocity coexists with strategic nondisclosure

Horikita knows enough to challenge Ayanokōji on class policy.

She does not know that he has converted her class into a succession experiment whose next phase requires his departure.

Thus high contradiction in one domain does not produce informed participation in another.

---

## 29.4 Ichinose — timed truth can be an attack

V12 is especially important because Ayanokōji does not merely conceal.

He selects the **moment of revelation** for psychological effect.

Truth itself becomes causal leverage.

This is why “at least he eventually told her” is ethically inadequate.

---

## 29.5 Secrecy can be protective and still require audit

Not every secret is illegitimate.

People have privacy rights. Protecting another person's confidence may be ethical. Revealing dangerous information indiscriminately can itself be harmful.

The relevant question is whether the hidden fact materially determines the other person's ability to consent, remain safe, interpret the relationship, or plan a future.

The standard is not total transparency.

It is **sufficient, timely, relationship-relevant disclosure**.

`Y1H-004` remains open globally because Ayanokōji has not shown this as a stable practice.

---

# 30. Protection, care, and ownership — the right to help is not the right to decide

Protection is one of the most morally ambiguous relationship languages in COTE.

Year 2 repeatedly separates sincere care from legitimate jurisdiction.

---

## 30.1 Protection that enlarges freedom

Positive protection tends to:

- reduce immediate danger;
- create more future options;
- distribute support;
- preserve evidence and witnesses;
- make refusal safer;
- or become unnecessary as the protected person grows.

Kei–Satō friendship and distributed care in Ichinose's class are strong examples because the protection does not require one sovereign protector to remain indispensable.

---

## 30.2 Protection that resembles ownership

Protection becomes proprietary when it:

- monopolizes alternatives;
- uses hidden danger to deepen reliance;
- converts successful rescue into entitlement over the future;
- or defines the protected person's “best interest” without sufficient standing.

Ayanokōji–Kei and Ayanokōji–Ichinose repeatedly approach this problem from different directions.

---

## 30.3 Horikita–Kushida — developmental protection can displace costs

Horikita's V05 decision is useful because it expands the ownership critique beyond Ayanokōji.

She protects Kushida's future because she believes in unrealized capacity.

Airi pays.

The case proves that benevolent developmental intention does not prevent another person's protection from becoming imposed jurisdiction.

---

## 30.4 Ayanokōji's mature positive stopping rule

Y2V11 gives the clearest local alternative.

Ayanokōji recognizes Yamamura's problem, creates access, and then states:

> `ここから先は、オレの立ち入る領域じゃない。`

Protection or facilitation stops before ownership of the answer.

V12 then proves that this rule is only locally generalized.

# 31. Contradiction and refusal — the strongest observable form of relational agency

Year 2 repeatedly treats “no” as developmental evidence.

But not every refusal is equally free.

---

## 31.1 Strong contradiction cases

High-quality Year-2 contradiction includes:

- Horikita rejecting Ayanokōji's Kushida policy;
- Kushida refusing reconciliation while cooperating;
- Ichinose rejecting Ayanokōji's binary;
- Satō judging Ayanokōji wrong to Kei;
- Hiyori judging Ryūen's developmental result;
- Haruka refusing forgiveness;
- Kōenji refusing unlimited class claims;
- Ryūen refusing to treat a thrown official result as meaningful victory.

These acts matter because the relationship or polity must continue to account for the speaker afterward.

---

## 31.2 Weak or compromised refusal cases

Kei's refusal of explanation at breakup is real behavior but epistemically compromised by prior nondisclosure and distress.

Tokitō can dissent, but dissent is physically unsafe.

Yamamura can want recognition, but needs an externally created opening before the relationship becomes contestable.

The correct standard is therefore:

> **A refusal is strongest when the person knows what she is refusing, can survive refusal, and remains a participant whose future still matters.**

---

## 31.3 Contradiction is not hostility by default

Horikita–Kushida, Horikita–Ibuki, Ichinose–Kanzaki, and Ayanokōji–Ryūen all show different forms of relationships whose stability depends partly on the ability to disagree without pretending unity.

This is one of Year 2's strongest relational corrections to harmony-centered models.

# 32. Fungibility and irreplaceability — value after the role ends

COTE frequently confuses strategic indispensability with relational irreplaceability at the level of character rhetoric.

The analysis must not.

---

## 32.1 System indispensability

A person may be hard to replace because of:

- social access;
- exceptional intelligence;
- political capital;
- athletic ability;
- point resources;
- leadership office;
- or hidden strategic security.

This is functional dependence.

It does not establish relational particularity.

---

## 32.2 Relational irreplaceability

Relational value is better evidenced when it survives role loss.

Examples:

- Sakayanagi recognizes Kamuro as friend after loss;
- Ayanokōji's body retains Kei's domestic routine after breakup;
- Haruka remains shaped by Airi after expulsion;
- Sudō retains Horikita-linked development after romantic rejection;
- Yamamura and Sakayanagi carry friendship beyond school;
- Ryūen experiences Sakayanagi's disappearance as loss of a meaningful rival rather than merely a favorable class outcome.

---

## 32.3 Kei remains deliberately dimension-specific

Year 2 undermines the claim that Kei is uniquely irreplaceable to Ayanokōji **as a romance-learning instrument**.

It simultaneously provides evidence that ordinary life has made her particular in embodied memory.

The correct answer is neither “completely replaceable” nor “proven singular beloved.”

Different dimensions return different results.

# 33. Role survival, succession, and counter-authorship — the year-level hierarchy

Year 2 turns role change from a test of durability into a hierarchy of autonomy.

---

## 33.1 Persistence

The relation or social structure continues.

This is the weakest positive result because the person may simply continue performing the original role.

---

## 33.2 Functional independence

The person can perform without the original protector, reward, leader, or developer.

Sudō after romantic rejection is a strong case.

Kei shows substantial behavioral/social independence but incomplete emotional independence.

---

## 33.3 Informed self-authorship

The person knows enough about the relevant structure to choose what the developed capacity is for.

Kushida's post-V05 cooperation eventually reaches this state in continuing form.

Kei's breakup does not fully do so because the relationship timetable was withheld.

Horikita's class-transfer succession test begins without informed authorship of the timing.

---

## 33.4 Counter-authorship

The person generates an answer or purpose outside the stronger model and forces the model to update.

Ichinose is the strongest Year-2 example.

Airi's post-expulsion future gives a social variant by defeating Haruka's totalization of what expulsion means.

Horikita's V05 disagreement gives a political variant: Ayanokōji must operate around a consequential answer he did not choose.

Kōenji offers an epistemic variant through insistence on discovering his own answers.

---

## 33.5 Comparative role-change matrix

|---|---|---|---|
| Kei as protected girlfriend | ex-girlfriend | social function, horizontal friendships, public control; private attachment unresolved | functional independence, incomplete informed self-authorship |
| Ayanokōji as Horikita-class hidden security | class transfer | Horikita's judgment; post-transfer polity result unknown | succession test begins |
| Kushida as universally friendly mask | exposed adversarial classmate/council actor | social competence and contribution | informed continuing self-authorship |

| Ryūen's opponent Sakayanagi | absent rival | meaningful-victory standard | adversarial role survival |

## 33.6 The terminal Year-2 hierarchy

The relationship system can therefore be summarized as:

`being preserved`

< `continuing to function`

< `choosing the meaning of continued function`

< `using that function for an independent purpose`

< `making the stronger model answer to an unowned result`.

This hierarchy will be reused by the ethics, class-politics, and full-synthesis documents. It is not a moral ranking of people. It is a ranking of how much relational authorship is evidenced in a particular structure.

# 36. Relationship language — high-value Japanese terms without lexical overreach

This specialist does not replace `Y2_09` or the later terminology index. It records only a small number of relationship terms whose semantic function is structurally important.

## 36.1 `親友`

“Best friend” / very close friend.

Its promotion in the Kei–Satō relation matters because it follows dangerous disclosure rather than merely pleasant proximity.

The label marks a relationship that survived information which could have made abandonment socially rational.

## 36.2 `友人`

Friend.

The term is deliberately less informative than the relation's structure. Ayanokōji's Year-2 ordinary-life development repeatedly demonstrates that “friend” is not reducible to strategic ally.

## 36.3 `宿主` / `寄生虫`

Host / parasite.

These terms remain important to Kei's self-model but must not be promoted into total ontology. They describe trauma-conditioned survival logic and dependency structure, not the whole person or the whole romance.

## 36.4 `介錯`

A term with mercy-killing / attending-final-act connotations in the Ichinose developmental frame.

Year 2 transforms it from paternalistic terminal judgment into a **defeated binary**. Ichinose's third answer demonstrates that the person under judgment can reject the judge's categories.

## 36.5 `立ち入る領域`

A domain into which one has standing to enter.

`ここから先は、オレの立ち入る領域じゃない` is the year's strongest explicit relationship-jurisdiction phrase.

The significance is not lack of knowledge but acknowledged lack of title over the answer.

## 36.6 `利用する`

To use.

Ichinose's reciprocal-use proposition matters because the term is not sanitized. Reciprocity does not require pretending strategic use has vanished. It requires refusing one-sided entitlement.

## 36.7 `必要不可欠`

Indispensable / necessary.

Year 2 repeatedly warns that indispensability is a system property, not automatic proof of love, irreplaceability, or legitimate control.

## 36.8 `信頼`

Trust.

Trust is both social capital and attack surface. Year 2's relationship ethics therefore judges not only whether trust exists but what the trusted person does with privileged access.

# 38. Strong counterarguments and their limits

## 38.1 “Every Ayanokōji relationship is manipulation”

This overstates the evidence.

Ayanokōji plainly manipulates people and environments. But Hiyori participant mode, ordinary domestic habit with Kei, spontaneous affect around Horikita, genuine interest in Ryūen as rival, and the desire for answers he cannot predict demonstrate relationship states not exhausted by manipulation.

The better claim is:

> **Ayanokōji often remains capable of manipulation inside relationships that also contain genuine desire, care, curiosity, or attachment.**

The coexistence is the problem.

---

## 38.2 “If the person eventually benefits, the method was justified”

Rejected.

Kushida's later contribution does not authorize V05.

Airi's later success does not justify expulsion.

Ichinose's later counter-authorship does not make the V12 attack legitimate.

Kei's behavioral survival does not validate the hidden breakup timetable.

Outcome success is not retroactive consent.

---

## 38.3 “Kei is either still a parasite or already fully independent”

False binary.

Her state is multidimensional:

- emotionally dependent;
- behaviorally capable;
- socially influential;
- horizontally supported;
- informationally disadvantaged;
- capable of refusal;
- and privately devastated at breakup.

All can be true simultaneously.

---

## 38.4 “Horikita is either Ayanokōji's puppet or completely independent”

False binary.

Independent judgment and political authorship are established.

Post-transfer system reproduction is not yet observed.

Personal recognition desire and hidden-security dependence remain without nullifying political agency.

---

## 38.5 “Ichinose's attachment proves dependence on Ayanokōji”

Incomplete.

Attachment is strong. Passive developmental jurisdiction is not.

By Y2SL, Ichinose can identify his method, reject his binary, predict his behavior, claim reciprocal use, and lead her class through a self-generated answer.

Intensity of attachment does not imply absence of agency.

---

## 38.6 “Sakayanagi chose freely, so Ayanokōji did not control anything”

Too narrow.

Formal choice remains real. Ayanokōji intentionally designs a field in which Sakayanagi's accurate model of his preference becomes causally decisive.

Agency and influence are not mutually exclusive.

The question is how much responsibility belongs to the actor who authors the informational environment.

---

## 38.7 “Ryūen's followers stay voluntarily, so coercion is no longer important”

Rejected.

Voluntary legitimacy is real.

Protected dissent remains weak.

Tokitō demonstrates that the sovereign can still answer contradiction through coercion.

---

## 38.8 “Friendship requires complete disclosure”

Rejected.

Hiyori can be a real friend without knowing the White Room. Partial selves routinely sustain genuine bonds.

The relevant question is whether hidden facts materially alter the other person's safety, consent, role, future, or ability to interpret the relationship.

---

## 38.9 “Reciprocity is impossible under large power disparity”

Too strong.

Ayanokōji–Ichinose remains unequal yet becomes substantially more reciprocal because Ichinose can make her model causally binding on him.

Ayanokōji–Ryūen is highly unequal in some capability domains yet reciprocal as self-authentication rivalry.

Reciprocity is answerability, not sameness.

---

## 38.10 “Counter-authorship absolves the original developer”

Rejected.

Ichinose's strong endpoint does not erase V12.

Kushida's self-authored cooperation does not erase V05.

A developed person can exceed a harmful method without making the method good.

---

## 38.11 “Romance is the deepest relationship category”

Unsupported.

Kei–Satō, Horikita–Kushida, Horikita–Ibuki, Ayanokōji–Ryūen, Ichinose–Kanzaki, and Haruka–Airi all carry high relational significance without being romance.

The series repeatedly treats rivalry, friendship, loyalty, sibling recognition, class belonging, and developmental opposition as equally capable of organizing identity.

# 39. Reconstruction and simulation constraints

This specialist can support novel-situation character modeling, but only if the reconstruction preserves relationship-specific topology instead of turning every actor into a global trait bundle.

---

## 39.1 Directed-state requirement

Before simulating any interaction, represent both directions separately.

Example:

`Ayanokōji -> Kei`

is not the same state as:

`Kei -> Ayanokōji`.

Record at minimum:

- known facts;
- believed motives;
- attachment;
- leverage;
- desired outcome;
- disclosure policy;
- refusal cost;
- and independent support.

---

## 39.2 Role-state requirement

The same pair behaves differently by role.

Ayanokōji–Horikita can operate as:

- classmates;
- political collaborators;
- hidden guarantor / visible leader;
- ordinary conversational partners;
- recognition seeker / evaluator;
- or post-transfer institutional rivals.

Do not generate one generic “their dynamic.”

---

## 39.3 Relationship history remains causal

Current warmth does not erase coercive origin.

Current hostility does not erase former friendship.

Current breakup does not erase embodied habit.

Current cooperation does not erase nonconsensual preservation.

Simulation must retain history as a constraint on what can be said, assumed, forgiven, or requested.

---

## 39.4 Do not convert inferred affect into explicit confession

Especially for:

- Horikita's V12.5 relational novelty;
- Hiyori's faint affection;
- Ayanokōji's ordinary residue after Kei;
- rivalry-specific attachment.

Where the source establishes salience but not explicit category closure, simulation must preserve ambiguity.

---

## 39.5 Kei-specific reconstruction constraints

Do not model:

- total helplessness;
- complete emotional recovery;
- romance as fake;
- romance as conventionally resolved;
- or social competence as proof of emotional independence.

Preserve:

`high social intelligence + horizontal support + abandonment fear + real affection + disclosure deficit + post-breakup unresolved attachment`.

---

## 39.6 Horikita-specific reconstruction constraints

Do not equate needing advice with obedience.

Preserve:

- consequential disagreement;
- recognition desire;
- bounded reliance;
- institutional office;
- increased ordinary significance;
- and lack of prior knowledge about the transfer timetable at Y2SL.

Do not simulate knowledge she has not received.

---

## 39.7 Ichinose-specific reconstruction constraints

Do not freeze her at Year-1 transparency or softness.

Preserve:

- solidarity;
- strategic opacity;
- romantic attachment;
- deterrence;
- refusal;
- reciprocal-use logic;
- distributed care;
- and ability to model Ayanokōji.

Do not turn counter-authorship into generic ruthlessness.

---

## 39.8 Hiyori-specific reconstruction constraints

Low temperature is not passivity.

She can disclose personally meaningful information, refuse, diagnose, and preserve cross-class friendship.

Do not invent coercive authority over Ryūen that the text has not shown.

---

## 39.9 Sakayanagi-specific reconstruction constraints

Preserve both:

- self-authored love/rivalry;
- and the political danger of private priority.

Do not make her automatically subordinate to Ayanokōji because she loves him.

Do not ignore the fact that inferred preference can become causal.

---

## 39.10 Kushida-specific reconstruction constraints

Do not write cooperation as redemption-coded warmth.

Preserve:

`known hostility + status motive + real social skill + self-interested contribution + capacity for sharp coercive behavior + legitimate bounded cooperation`.

---

## 39.11 Ryūen-follower reconstruction constraints

Use the mixed model:

`voluntary legitimacy + coercive sovereignty + differentiated expertise + growing delegation + weak protected dissent`.

Do not reduce followers to frightened minions or free constitutional citizens.

---

## 39.12 Ayanokōji Group successor constraints

Do not casually restore the five-person group.

At Y2SL, the meaningful social object is the successor structure formed after Airi's expulsion and Ayanokōji's refusal to return.

Haruka's non-forgiveness remains legitimate state, not an error waiting to be corrected.

---

## 39.13 Disclosure-gating rule

Before generating a high-stakes relationship decision, ask:

> **Does this character actually know the fact required to reason this way at Y2SL?**

If not, the model must not smuggle reader knowledge into actor knowledge.

This is particularly important for:

- White Room information;
- the origin and purpose of Ayanokōji's plans;
- class-transfer intentions;
- hidden relationships;
- and post-breakup internal states.

---

## 39.14 Counter-authorship rule

A good reconstruction of Ayanokōji must allow other characters to surprise him within evidence-supported bounds.

A model that makes every other character's “independent” action secretly part of his plan reproduces the exact epistemic error Year 2 repeatedly corrects.

## 40.1 What the answered questions do not authorize

An answered handoff question is not permission to universalize the answer.

For example:

- Kei's broader social identity surviving Ayanokōji does not prove emotional independence from him;
- Horikita's ability to disagree does not prove she knows the timetable governing their separation;
- Kushida's self-authored cooperation does not retroactively legitimate the costs imposed by V05;
- Ichinose's strategic competence does not make Ayanokōji's earlier use of her trust harmless;
- the successor Ayanokōji Group surviving does not restore Airi or erase Haruka's legitimate non-forgiveness;
- Sakayanagi's voluntary withdrawal does not make Ayanokōji's inferred-preference intervention non-causal.

Relationship development must therefore be stored as **specific capacities under specific informational and historical conditions**, not converted into adjectives such as “independent,” “healthy,” “loyal,” or “reciprocal” without qualification.

# 41. Open relationship questions at Y2SL

Year 2 closes many first-year uncertainties while creating more demanding second-order questions. These are the principal relationship questions that should remain live at the frozen `Y2SL` boundary.

## 41.1 Disclosure and relational jurisdiction

The broadest unresolved Ayanokōji question remains `Y1H-004`:

> **Can he tell another person enough of the truth, early enough, that the person can make an informed choice whose answer he has not already architected?**

The corpus now proves that he can:

- tell difficult truths;
- reveal highly sensitive facts selectively;
- ask permission;
- permit refusal;
- accept some outcomes he did not prefer;
- and explicitly recognize a domain as no longer his to enter.

It does not prove that these capacities have become a general disclosure ethic.

The difference between **truth disclosure** and **timely sufficient disclosure** remains fundamental.

## 41.2 Ayanokōji as a protectable person

`Y1H-035` remains the most important reciprocity limit on Ayanokōji himself.

He protects, diagnoses, trains, observes, receives care, and sometimes exposes preference or ordinary ignorance. None of these is equivalent to voluntarily entering a condition in which another person:

1. knows he is genuinely vulnerable;
2. possesses information or capacity he lacks;
3. may decide how to protect or correct him;
4. can refuse that role;
5. and can materially alter the outcome.

Until such evidence exists, reconstruction should not invent a mature reciprocal-protection grammar merely because he has become more emotionally legible.

## 41.3 Kei after the breakup

The romance has ended at the Y2SL boundary, but several relational questions remain deliberately unclosed:

- How quickly does behavioral independence become emotional independence, if it does?
- Which ordinary habits remain affectively loaded rather than merely habitual?
- How much of her performed strength was self-authored before the breakup, and how much was shaped by the independence curriculum she believed Ayanokōji wanted?
- What future relation, if any, can exist once the boyfriend/protector/developer role is removed?
- Can future contact occur with enough information symmetry to avoid recreating the old structure?

The current corpus supports neither “Kei is still fundamentally dependent” nor “the breakup cleanly completed her independence” as a totalizing endpoint.

## 41.4 Horikita and the meaning of separation

Horikita's relationship with Ayanokōji ends Year 2 with unusually high judgment reciprocity and unusually low timetable reciprocity.

Open questions include:

- how she interprets the fact of separation once its purpose becomes legible;
- whether recognition-seeking survives resentment or perceived betrayal;
- whether her unfamiliar V12.5 bodily response acquires a stable relational category;
- whether she can revise her model of him when his motives become less inferable from class interest;
- whether `Y1H-016` can ever close through evidence of genuine Ayanokōji failure rather than her own failure in his presence.

No answer may be imported from later narrative material into this document.

## 41.5 Ichinose after counter-authorship

Ichinose produces the strongest relational counter-authorship at the boundary, but one successful counter-authored scene does not automatically constitutionalize the relationship.

Future tests include:

- whether reciprocal use remains genuinely bilateral when interests diverge;
- whether Ayanokōji can accept an Ichinose answer he dislikes without building a new environment around it;
- whether Ichinose's knowledge of his nature is sufficient for future informed choice or remains materially incomplete;
- whether her distributed class support continues to prevent Ayanokōji from becoming the sole interpretive or emotional center;
- whether intimacy strengthens reciprocal causal standing or creates a new dependency surface.

The current answer is therefore **counter-authorship demonstrated**, not **relationship equality completed**.

## 41.6 Hiyori and Ryūen

`Y1H-026` remains strictly open.

Hiyori can:

- diagnose social conditions Ryūen misses;
- preserve relational texture within his class;
- communicate with low-temperature directness;
- and contribute to outcomes less purge-oriented than his earlier default.

The missing evidence is whether her moral judgment can **bind or redirect Ryūen himself** when his preferred course conflicts with hers.

Influence on the environment is not yet constitutional jurisdiction over the sovereign.

## 41.7 Hirata and particular attachment

The Year-2 corpus strengthens Hirata's capacity for particular care while preserving the old problem: can he choose one person's claim when doing so necessarily disappoints or disadvantages others he also feels responsible for?

His relation to Ayanokōji is especially useful because it increasingly includes mutual concern without making Ayanokōji his sole stabilizer. Yet `Y1H-034` remains partial because particular attachment has not been exhaustively tested against his universal-care reflex.

## 41.8 Sakayanagi after school-role separation

Sakayanagi's withdrawal creates unusually strong evidence that a relation can seek continuity after its institutional role disappears.

Open questions remain:

- whether rivalry can remain reciprocal without the ANHS competitive frame;
- whether love becomes less fiduciary-dangerous once she no longer governs classmates whose interests can be subordinated to it;
- whether Ayanokōji can relate to her without using her as a proof object or strategic benchmark;
- how her newer friendship with Yamamura changes a relational ecology previously organized by selective aristocratic recognition.

This document records the future-facing possibility only. It supplies no post-boundary answer.

## 41.9 Successor groups and imported centers

Year 2 repeatedly produces social structures that survive by changing their center:

- the Ayanokōji Group survives as a different successor object;
- Ichinose's class distributes reform rather than relying on one symbolic center alone;
- Sakayanagi's former polity reaches the boundary with a succession problem and willingness to accept an imported exceptional center.

The open relational question is whether successor structures become genuinely **polycentric** or merely replace one singular center with another.

That question belongs jointly to this relationship synthesis and the class-politics specialist that follows.

# 43. Canonical Year-2 relationship principles

The following propositions are the compact conceptual output of this specialist at `Y2SL`.

## 43.1 Reciprocity principle

> **Reciprocity is not equality of power. It is the capacity to make asymmetry answerable.**

Year 2 strengthens the standard:

> **The strongest reciprocity is counter-authorship: the other person can produce an answer that forces the stronger modeler to update rather than merely confirming the model.**

## 43.2 Dependency principle

> **Dependency should be evaluated by monopolization, alternatives, information, exit capacity, and the right to disagree—not by whether one person ever needs another.**

Plural, answerable interdependence is a more mature target than isolation.

## 43.3 Friendship principle

> **Friendship is most visible in voluntary return, dangerous truth, tolerated failure, ordinary time, and care that is not conditioned on strategic usefulness.**

Year 2 adds that friendship can cross class boundaries and preserve moral friction against the institutions that classify participants as opponents.

## 43.4 Romance principle

> **Mutual desire or an explicit romantic role does not by itself establish information reciprocity, equal jurisdiction, or completed independence.**

Romance may humanize, educate, expose, monopolize, protect, or become a control surface. Each function must be demonstrated separately.

## 43.5 Recognition principle

> **Accurate recognition becomes ethically reciprocal only when the recognized person retains the right to correct, exceed, refuse, or invalidate the model.**

Being known more accurately can enlarge another person's agency or make that person easier to govern.

## 43.6 Protection principle

> **Protection is least proprietorial when the protected person can know what is being done, refuse the protector, possess alternative support, and survive the end of the protective role.**

Beneficial outcomes do not by themselves grant retroactive relational jurisdiction.

## 43.7 Separation principle

> **Separation is an information test before it is an endurance test.**

A relationship cannot prove informed independence merely because one participant behaves capably after a separation whose purpose, timetable, or developmental architecture was hidden from them.

## 43.8 Contradiction principle

> **A relationship becomes more reciprocal when disagreement can remain inside the relationship without being translated into betrayal, defect, or evidence that the dissenter must be corrected.**

Horikita–Kushida, Horikita–Ichinose, Ryūen's increasingly differentiated follower field, and Ichinose's internal reform all illuminate different versions of this problem.

## 43.9 Role-survival principle

> **The end of a role reveals what the role had been carrying.**

Boyfriend/girlfriend, leader/follower, developer/subject, protector/protected, classmate/rival, and group-center roles can conceal both genuine attachment and dependency. What remains after role change is analytically more informative than the mere persistence of contact.

## 43.10 Counter-authorship principle

> **Development survives the developer only in a weak sense if the developed person merely continues the developer's intended curriculum.**

The stronger test is whether the person can use acquired capacities for an independently generated purpose.

The strongest current test is whether that purpose makes the developer **answer**.

# 44. Final synthesis and Y2SL boundary lock

Year 2 transforms relationship analysis by making **better understanding itself morally ambiguous**.

The central characters become more legible to one another. Ayanokōji learns more about Kei, Horikita, Ichinose, Sakayanagi, Hiyori, Ryūen, and others. Horikita becomes better at reading capacity through trajectory rather than static record. Ichinose develops strategic social perception. Sakayanagi's attention becomes increasingly personal. Ryūen learns to differentiate followers rather than rule only through undifferentiated fear. Friends and classmates accumulate dangerous knowledge about one another.

This increase in legibility creates real goods:

- more precise care;
- better protection;
- more accurate recognition;
- stronger cooperation;
- new ordinary pleasures;
- more durable friendship;
- better distributed leadership;
- and the possibility of being known beyond institutional labels.

It also creates more precise control.

The better one person understands another's fear, loyalty, attraction, pride, loneliness, ambition, shame, desire for recognition, or need to protect someone else, the easier it becomes to shape the environment so that the other person **freely performs the response the modeler wanted**.

That is why Year 2 cannot rank relationships simply by warmth.

A cold rivalry may contain strong reciprocal contradiction. A romance may contain genuine affection and weak information symmetry. Hatred may coexist with legitimate cooperation. Friendship may become politically disobedient by crossing class lines. Protection may be caring and proprietorial at once. Separation may be developmentally successful and still informationally unfair. Love may increase the risk that inferred preference becomes sovereign.

The strongest relational advances are therefore not the scenes in which everybody becomes closer.

They are the scenes in which **monopoly weakens**:

- Kei gains a friend to whom dangerous truth can be given without losing belonging;
- Kushida's continuing cooperation becomes hers rather than Horikita's imposed script;
- Sudō's development survives the loss of romantic reward;
- Ichinose's polity generates loyal opposition and distributed care;
- the former Ayanokōji Group survives by becoming a successor object that no longer requires Ayanokōji's return;
- Hiyori permits Ayanokōji a cleaner participant relation in which declared desire and refusal remain visible;
- Horikita can disagree consequentially with the person whose recognition she values;
- Ichinose can tell Ayanokōji that she too will use him and claim reciprocal causal standing.

The terminal Year-2 relationship hierarchy is therefore:

`persistence`

< `functional independence`

< `informed self-authorship`

< `counter-authorship`.

Persistence asks whether the relation remains.

Functional independence asks whether a person can function without the old dependency.

Informed self-authorship asks whether the person's choice belongs to them under adequate information, real refusal, and non-monopolized support.

Counter-authorship asks whether the person can generate an answer that the stronger participant did not already own.

This final level is why Ichinose's Y2V12.5 endpoint matters so much. Ayanokōji constructs a developmental binary and explicitly wants the possibility of an answer outside it. Ichinose supplies that answer, demonstrates that she has also been modeling him, claims reciprocal right to use the relation causally, and forces his model to update. The moment does not erase the history of violated trust or unequal information. It does establish the clearest Year-2 case in which superior modeling fails to remain unilateral.

Kei provides the opposite necessary lesson. Her growth is real. Her ordinary love is real. Her broader friendship network is real. Her ability to endure the breakup is real. Yet none of those facts make the relationship retrospectively informed. **Behavioral independence cannot substitute for disclosure.**

Horikita supplies another distinction. Her recognition-seeking is real, as is her increasing closeness to Ayanokōji. But she can disagree with him, author political decisions he would not make, and preserve her own reasons. **Attachment does not imply obedience.** At the same time, the hidden timetable of his departure means judgment reciprocity has not yet become full informational reciprocity.

Sakayanagi supplies the fiduciary warning. Love and recognition are not ethically trivial merely because they are sincere. When a leader treats another person's inferred preference as more binding than the constituency she governs, **private attachment becomes political jurisdiction**.

Hiyori supplies the low-temperature positive control. Not every meaningful relation needs crisis, ownership, rescue, or romance. Shared reading, quiet curiosity, explicit desire, preserved refusal, and ordinary return can create relational value precisely because no one needs to become the other's project.

The completed Year-2 relationship synthesis therefore ends with a principle stronger than the frozen Year-1 formulation.

Year 1 established:

> **Reciprocity is the capacity to answer back under conditions of difference.**

Year 2 adds:

> **The highest relational autonomy presently visible is not merely the right to answer back. It is the capacity to give an answer the stronger model did not own—and thereby make the modeler answer in return.**

That claim is canonical **only through `Y2SL`**.

The following remain intentionally unresolved at this boundary:

- general timely disclosure by Ayanokōji (`Y1H-004`);
- Horikita witnessing and interpreting genuine Ayanokōji failure (`Y1H-016`);
- Hiyori morally redirecting Ryūen himself (`Y1H-026`);
- Ayanokōji voluntarily exposing weakness that grants another person meaningful protective authority (`Y1H-035`);
- the durability and future form of multiple post-role relationships after the Year-2 endpoint.

No later narrative evidence has been used to close them.

**Boundary lock:** this specialist is canonical for Year-2 relationship synthesis at the Japanese `Y2SL` boundary. Source-local readings remain immutable at their own temporal boundaries. Volume 0 remains retrospective evidence at its mandated position. Second List remains documentary/paratextual calibration. Year-3 narrative evidence remains excluded.
