## Year 2 — Ayanokōji Kiyotaka
### Character, psychology, ethics, and voice
### The self-authored subject who becomes an environmental author: ordinary desire, relational jurisdiction, and the unfinished problem of letting other people own their answers

> **Canonical specialist synthesis at the complete Year-2 / Second List boundary.**
>
> This document is the primary Year-2 home for Ayanokōji Kiyotaka's psychology, freedom, self-authorship, developmental methods, ethical trajectory, and written Japanese voice. It does not replace the immutable source-local volume readings. It synthesizes them at `Y2SL`, preserves Volume 0's mandated retrospective position, and imports no Year-3 narrative evidence.

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

This specialist begins from the frozen Year-1 conclusion and asks what Year 2 actually changes.

The Year-1 primary thesis was already resistant to the most common reductions. Ayanokōji was not adequately described as emotionless, purely manipulative, secretly ordinary, or simply a White Room machine wearing a school-life mask. The mature Year-1 model was layered:

1. an ordinary adolescent layer accumulating biography, desire, embarrassment, attraction, friendship, leisure, and bodily experience;
2. a relational layer in which particular people acquire value beyond generic function;
3. a White Room / strategic layer organized around concealment, victory-conditioned fallback, threat management, and functional modeling;
4. a developing environmental-author layer in which he alters conditions so that other people's own actions become part of the causal mechanism.

Year 2 does not replace those layers. It makes their interaction much more consequential.

The governing question for this document is:

> **Can a person whose own development was overwhelmingly authored by others learn to value people without reproducing that authorship over them?**

Year 2 does not settle the question. It produces stronger evidence on both sides.

Ayanokōji becomes more capable of ordinary participation, more aware of desires that cannot be reduced to utility, more tolerant of ignorance and failure, more interested in people who generate answers beyond his model, and at least locally capable of stopping an intervention before he owns the answer.

At the same time, he becomes a more sophisticated author of environments. Intimacy gives him finer information. Trust becomes a causal resource. Timing becomes a jurisdiction. Formal freedom can remain intact while the available motives have been heavily shaped. By V12, the absence of a direct order no longer reliably distinguishes respect for autonomy from high-level control.

The central Year-2 paradox is therefore:

> **ordinary human intimacy increasingly matters to him for itself, while that same intimacy gives him more precise causal access to the people he cares about.**

The ethical question is whether the first development can become a limit on the second.

At `Y2SL`, that remains open.

## 0.1 No clinical diagnosis

This is literary and behavioral analysis, not psychiatric diagnosis.

The corpus supports descriptions of:

- developmental deprivation;
- conditioned threat response;
- strategic concealment;
- unusually high cognitive empathy;
- inconsistent reciprocal disclosure;
- difficulty granting others corrective or protective authority;
- pleasure in manipulation;
- spontaneous affect;
- ordinary ignorance;
- desire for recognition;
- and severe autonomy violations.

It does not license attaching a clinical disorder label and then using that label as a causal shortcut.

## 0.2 First-person narration is evidence, not omniscience

Ayanokōji's narration is unusually useful for mechanism, constraints, and decision structure. It is not the final court of appeal on the meaning of every person he observes.

His characteristic strengths and limits differ by domain:

| Domain | Reliability at Y2SL | Required caution |
|---|---|---|
| Observable mechanism | usually very high | he may omit scale, provenance, or preparatory authorship |
| Immediate tactical structure | very high | strategy can be described narrowly enough to understate responsibility |
| Other people's incentives | often high | model accuracy is not identity with the person |
| His own motives | partial, often layered | one sincere motive need not exhaust the action |
| His own emotion | frequently self-observational rather than fully named | affect may appear before interpretation |
| Future prediction | analytically sophisticated but falsifiable | Year 2 increasingly produces model-breaking answers |
| Moral legitimacy | not privileged | explanation, effectiveness, and ethical title remain separate |

The project must therefore preserve other first-person and character-local authorities rather than turning them into footnotes to Ayanokōji's model.

## 0.3 Flat delivery is not evidence of absent emotion

The written Japanese corpus now makes this control stronger than it was at the Year-1 boundary.

His public speech remains sparse, understated, and often deliberately non-showy. His interior voice can be comic, desirous, embarrassed, analytical, coercive, affectionate, uncertain, self-correcting, or bored.

Year 2 adds more participant-mode speech acts without replacing the sparse outward baseline.

## 0.4 Care does not automatically prove legitimacy

Ayanokōji often cares accurately.

He can identify:

- shame;
- fear;
- hidden capacity;
- loneliness;
- interrupted education;
- dependency;
- grief;
- friendship;
- and the conditions under which another person is likely to recover.

That accuracy can support excellent care.

It can also support extremely precise control.

The correct question is never simply whether the care was sincere. It is whether the cared-for person retained enough information, refusal capacity, independent purpose, and corrective standing to remain an author rather than merely the successful subject of care.

## 0.5 Later benefit does not retroactively create consent

This rule is especially important for Kei, Airi, Kushida-adjacent intervention logic, Ichinose, Sakayanagi, and the Horikita separation project.

A later person may:

- become stronger;
- appropriate a prior intervention;
- use the resulting competence for independent ends;
- forgive the method;
- or even become grateful.

None of those facts alone means the original method was informed, consensual, proportionate, or legitimate.

## 0.6 Volume 0 is retrospective primary fiction, not chronological permission to overwrite prior local truth

Volume 0 is read at its mandated position after Y2V08.

It reveals prior hidden state and recontextualizes later behavior. It does not retroactively turn every school-era choice into a predetermined White Room reflex.

The most important distinction is:

`origin/provenance -> constraint and genealogy`

not:

`origin/provenance -> destiny and exhaustive explanation`

## 0.7 Second List is a boundary audit, not a higher-order replacement for the novels

The official guidebook is valuable for:

- OAA visibility snapshots;
- official emphasis;
- relational topology;
- retrospective compression;
- and bonus fiction.

It does not override fiction when a profile phrase compresses a mixed relationship more aggressively than the narrative evidence permits.

# 1. Executive thesis

Year 2 strengthens the case that Ayanokōji is genuinely changing while simultaneously making it harder to interpret that change as moral reform.

The most important developments are not increases in intelligence or combat capability. Those are largely questions of revelation and visibility. The major developments occur in what he **permits himself to want**, what kinds of uncertainty he begins to value, how openly he recognizes environmental authorship as his own project, and what he does when another person's answer exceeds the model he had built for them.

The Year-2 progression can be stated in eight linked propositions.

### 1. Ordinary life becomes positive purpose rather than merely negative escape

He does not merely want to avoid White Room custody.

He increasingly values:

- friends;
- romance as experience;
- purposeless conversation;
- books and hobbies;
- food;
- travel;
- consumer desire;
- bodily failure;
- making rather than reproducing;
- shared memories;
- and the finite remaining time of school life.

The claim `学校生活を続けることが、今のオレにとって最優先すべきこと` in Y2V01 is therefore not incidental. The defended object is a lived environment with positive content.

### 2. Self-authorship becomes second-order rather than pristine

Volume 0 reveals that the route into ANHS was anticipated and partly prepared by adults.

That discovery requires a revision, not a cancellation, of his freedom project.

The mature formulation is:

> **Ayanokōji becomes more self-authored not because nobody else touched the conditions of his life, but because he generates purposes inside those conditions that cannot be reduced to the purpose of the prior author.**

Freedom is therefore tested by counter-authored meaning, not clean origin.

### 3. Environmental authorship becomes conscious and large-scale

Year 1 established the grammar.

Year 2 expands it from local developmental interventions into:

- authored visibility;
- developmental pacing;
- information architecture;
- procedural counter-authorship;
- relationship curriculum;
- culling;
- succession design;
- staged agency;
- temporal jurisdiction;
- all-class ecological engineering;
- bounded intervention;
- relational sovereignty;
- and separation experiments.

His distinctive power is not merely solving a game. It is changing the environment in which the game is solved.

### 4. The ethical problem shifts from overt coercion to jurisdiction

By late Year 2, direct command is often unnecessary.

A target can remain formally free and still act inside conditions heavily authored through:

- information asymmetry;
- timing;
- trust;
- dependency;
- incentive design;
- relational expectation;
- or control over which futures appear viable.

The governing ethical question therefore becomes:

> **How much of the choice architecture did he author, and could the affected person meaningfully answer the hidden author before the consequence became irreversible?**

### 5. Counter-authorship becomes the strongest evidence of autonomy

Mere persistence after the developer leaves is weak evidence.

Executing the lesson the developer wanted can still reproduce the developer's curriculum.

The strongest result is an answer the model did not contain.

That is why Ichinose's late-Year-2 trajectory matters so much: the person becomes more than the completed analysis and forces the analyzer to update.

### 6. Affect develops without becoming protective vulnerability

Ayanokōji can now be shown to experience:

- desire;
- pleasure;
- embarrassment;
- disappointment;
- loneliness;
- physical attraction;
- ordinary anxiety over rejection;
- spontaneous amusement;
- and an involuntary smile.

Yet `Y1H-035` remains open.

He has not voluntarily exposed helpless weakness in a way that grants another person meaningful protective or corrective authority over him.

### 7. His strongest Year-2 ethical progress is local stopping-rule competence

V11 matters because he can identify a domain that is not his:

> `ここから先は、オレの立ち入る領域じゃない。`

This is not moral transformation by itself.

It proves that he possesses a lower-jurisdiction repertoire: diagnose, open access, preserve alternatives, and stop before choosing the answer.

V12 then proves that this repertoire is not yet generalized under high stakes.

### 8. His written voice develops through permission, not personality replacement

The stable `オレ` remains.

The major change is modal.

He becomes more able to say or internally admit:

- I want this;
- I do not know this;
- I may fail at this;
- the other person may refuse;
- this future is not my jurisdiction;
- and the answer may be valuable precisely because I did not supply it.

The Year-2 voice is therefore not simply “warmer.”

It contains a larger repertoire of permissible speech acts while strategic compression remains fully intact.

# 2. Year 1 → Year 2 claim-transition ledger

The following transitions govern the specialist.

## PRESERVE

1. **Ordinary school life is a genuine self-chosen project.**
2. **The ordinary adolescent layer is not merely camouflage.**
3. **The victory/tools doctrine and functional modeling are genuine internal structures.**
4. **Ayanokōji possesses exceptional cognitive empathy and inconsistent autonomy restraint.**
5. **His strongest strategic talent is environmental/informational authorship rather than deduction alone.**
6. **Care and instrumentality can coexist in the same act.**
7. **He can value selected people beyond immediate utility.**
8. **He desires difficult, meaningful encounter and can imagine defeat as liberation from Atsuomi's claim.**
9. **His models are powerful and incomplete.**
10. **Flat public affect is not evidence of flat private affect.**
11. **A useful model of another person is not the person.**

## STRENGTHEN

1. **Ordinary life has positive content under severe external pressure.**
   Y2V01 explicitly prioritizes continued school life; later volumes add finite time, ordinary failure, hobbies, consumer desire, purposeless companionship, and creation.

2. **The desire for uncontrolled or incompletely controlled experience is genuine.**
   Y2V08 voluntary non-optimization, Y2V09.5 ordinary invitation risk and inefficient desire, and Y2V10 spontaneous affect are cumulative.

3. **Developmental authorship is a self-chosen purpose.**
   Y2V06 makes the anti-White-Room counter-experiment explicit; Y2V10 expands it to all four classes.

4. **He can accept some growth away from him.**
   `Y1H-002` becomes `ANSWERED_BY_DEVELOPMENT`: he actively wants Horikita's class to become capable of opposing him.

5. **Relationship particularity matters.**
   The corpus contains genuine affection, ordinary habits, remembered preference, grief-adjacent residue, friendship-specific desire, and differentiated relational registers.

6. **People who exceed his model are increasingly valuable to him.**
   Nagumo causes model revision; Horikita is valued for unmodeled outcomes; Ichinose becomes the strongest counter-author; Second List explicitly reinforces model obsolescence.

7. **Public performance and private capacity must remain separated.**
   OAA becomes the clearest institutional example.

8. **Ayanokōji possesses a lower-jurisdiction relational repertoire.**
   Hiyori in V09.5 and the V11 stopping rule provide positive models.

## REVISE

1. **“ANHS is a purely self-created escape outside Atsuomi's design.”**
   → The opportunity was partly architected; the purposes generated inside it are not thereby owned.

2. **“Self-authorship is demonstrated whenever the purpose is his own.”**
   → Too weak after Volume 0. A self-authored end can still be pursued through inherited preference-engineering grammar. `Y1H-007` is therefore `REFRAMED`.

3. **“Ayanokōji gets warmer.”**
   → Replace with: he acquires more participant-mode speech acts and more explicit ordinary purposes while retaining severe strategic compression and control capacity.

4. **“The ethical problem is whether he manipulates.”**
   → Replace with jurisdiction, information, refusal, temporal authorship, accountability, and counter-authorship.

5. **“Separation demonstrates independence.”**
   → Separation is only a succession test. Independence is stronger when the successor creates legitimate answers the developer did not supply.

6. **“Protection is evidence of moral progress.”**
   → Protection can coexist with ownership, coercive incapacity, hidden timetables, and third-party externalities.

7. **“The strongest autonomous outcome is successful functioning without him.”**
   → Counter-authorship outranks mere function because a person may function by reproducing the curriculum he authored.

## DOWNGRADE

1. “Cold versus warm” as a sufficient developmental axis.
2. “Manipulator versus sincere” as mutually exclusive categories.
3. “Strategic versus ordinary” as mutually exclusive modes.
4. Any use of OAA as latent-ceiling measurement.
5. A single self-description as exhaustive motive.
6. Ayanokōji's prediction as privileged future truth.
7. Later competence as retroactive consent.
8. Visible independence as proof of independently authored norm.

## REJECT

1. **He is emotionless.**
2. **All ordinary interiority is fake.**
3. **All people remain only tools in every domain.**
4. **Successful outcomes legitimate coercive methods.**
5. **Mutual romantic form guarantees conventional love.**
   Y2V09.5 directly rejects this forecast at the local boundary.
6. **The White Room explains every later choice as fixed programming.**
7. **He is socially omniscient.**
8. **Written Japanese voice can determine seiyū acoustics.**
9. **Every Ayanokōji intervention should be narrated as a grand hidden experiment.**
10. **Counter-authorship erases his responsibility for the environment he intentionally staged.**

## OPEN

The most important unresolved Ayanokōji questions at Y2SL remain:

- `Y1H-004` — sufficient disclosure for informed relational choice;
- `Y1H-035` — voluntary protective vulnerability;
- `Y1H-005` — accepting a costly autonomous answer without rerouting the environment;
- `Y1H-003` — particular value surviving loss of strategic utility at meaningful cost;
- `Y1H-006` — the eventual meaning of his desired legacy;
- `Y1H-039` — whether plural authorship can create durable limits on a superior modeler;
- and the post-transfer question of whether he can accept an answer that defeats not only his plan but his **theory of the plan**.

# 3. The four-layer Year-2 model

The four layers should be treated as simultaneously real rather than masks that take turns replacing one another.

## 3.1 Ordinary adolescent layer

This layer contains capacities and desires whose value does not require strategic conversion:

- wanting friends;
- feeling pleased when called a friend;
- domestic routine;
- cooking;
- attraction;
- jealousy-adjacent and romantic observation;
- consumer desire;
- food preference;
- hobbies;
- books;
- embarrassment;
- purposeless conversation;
- disappointment;
- bodily failure;
- and the wish to use limited school time as lived experience.

The decisive Year-2 change is not the existence of this layer. Year 1 already established it.

The change is **permission**. He increasingly allows a preference to justify itself.

Y2V09.5's yogurt-maker sequence is the cleanest small example. He begins cost-recovery analysis, recognizes that optimization would eliminate the purchase, and stops because he wants the object. The desire does not need a strategic defense.

## 3.2 Relational layer

This layer records particular people as more than generic social variables.

Year 2 adds substantial density:

- Kei becomes habitual domestic presence, romantic experience, object of desire, protected person, hidden curriculum, and eventually separated partner;
- Horikita becomes trusted leader, valued conversational partner, spontaneous-affect trigger, succession object, and future opponent;
- Ichinose becomes protected person, pressured developmental subject, trusted admirer, deliberately injured opponent, reciprocal user, and strongest counter-author;
- Sakayanagi becomes rival, interpreter, loved/recognizing other, and a person whose relational knowledge makes indirect jurisdiction possible;
- Hiyori becomes the cleanest low-control participant-mode friend;
- Ryūen becomes rival whose continued development is intrinsically meaningful;
- Amasawa becomes White Room-linked junior whose future need not be reduced to allegiance;
- and ordinary social residues persist after formal relationship transitions.

The relational layer is therefore not proof against instrumentality.

It is the field in which instrumentality becomes ethically harder to excuse because the other person is increasingly known as particular.

## 3.3 White Room / strategic layer

This layer remains exceptionally stable.

It includes:

- threat detection;
- concealment;
- optionality preservation;
- performance management;
- evidence control;
- ranking under scarcity;
- rapid functional classification;
- willingness to use severe coercion;
- and the tendency to convert an event into a mechanism that can be modeled.

Year 2 does not show this layer disappearing.

The correct developmental model is accumulation and conflict rather than replacement.

## 3.4 Environmental-author layer

This layer becomes fully self-conscious in Year 2.

Ayanokōji's distinctive strategy is often:

1. observe a limiting condition;
2. infer the target's likely response family;
3. alter information, incentives, timing, social topology, or access;
4. allow the target's own agency to produce the visible act;
5. evaluate whether the act changed the person/system as intended;
6. preserve optionality for the next branch.

This is not literal puppetry.

The target's agency is real.

The authorship of conditions is also real.

The layer becomes ethically dangerous precisely because a sophisticated author can truthfully say:

> I did not make them choose.

while omitting:

> I built the environment in which their characteristic choice became predictably useful to my purpose.

## 3.5 Why “hidden true self” is the wrong model

Year 2 gives no good reason to identify one layer as the authentic person and the others as performance.

The ordinary layer can be sincere while the strategic layer remains active.

Care can be sincere while jurisdiction remains excessive.

Romantic touch can be genuinely desired while the relationship is still conceptualized as curriculum.

An involuntary smile can be authentic while an undisclosed class-transfer experiment continues.

The person is the conflict among these capacities, not a secret core that makes the others false.

## 4.1 Principal developmental injury — deprivation of surplus purpose

The Year-1 formulation remains valid but Volume 0 gives it deeper provenance.

Ayanokōji's developmental environment supplied:

- task;
- evaluation;
- ranking;
- punishment;
- comparison;
- technical knowledge;
- and externally assigned purpose.

It starved:

- low-stakes preference;
- reciprocal contingency;
- purposeless time;
- ordinary social symbolism;
- hobbies chosen for themselves;
- relational protection that did not require usefulness;
- and the expectation that another person's feeling could alter the governing system.

The Year-2 counter-curriculum matters because these are not decorative deficits. They are missing causal categories.

A person who has rarely experienced a preference being valid merely because it is theirs will naturally find it easier to treat preference as another variable to manage.

## 4.2 Principal adaptive strength — causal decomposition without static essentialism

He is unusually good at separating:

- event from identity;
- displayed ability from possessed ability;
- emotional state from permanent essence;
- public role from hidden incentive;
- and current limitation from developmental possibility.

This allows him to see capacities others miss.

It also lets him design environments in which latent capacity becomes usable.

## 4.3 Principal adaptive danger — future-state privilege

The same strength creates his characteristic ethical failure.

He can discount the present person's experience in favor of the person he expects to produce later.

Pain becomes investment.

Ignorance becomes motivation.

Dependency becomes curriculum.

Separation becomes treatment.

A relationship becomes the training environment for a future self the present partner has not been told is the actual project.

## 4.4 Principal relational need — recognition without ownership

This Year-1 formulation becomes stronger after Volume 0.

He resists being owned by Atsuomi, Tsukishiro, Sakayanagi's superior information, institutional files, or another person's claim to decide his route.

The relationships he values most strongly tend to include some combination of:

- accurate recognition;
- difficulty;
- refusal capacity;
- unpredictability;
- and the possibility of answering back.

The paradox is that he often seeks precisely that relation while failing to grant equivalent informational standing to the other person.

## 4.5 Principal fear — recapture, predetermined meaning, and the sealed-core hypothesis

The explicit fear remains external recapture and loss of school freedom.

A deeper fear persists: ordinary experience may fail to alter the strategic architecture enough to matter.

Year 2 weakens the strongest sealed-core reading because new desires, spontaneous affect, and participant-mode relations are real.

But the fear survives in a revised form:

> **perhaps new purposes can accumulate without becoming strong enough to restrain the old method under decisive stakes.**

V11 and V12 are the clearest paired test of this question.

## 4.6 Principal ethical resource — curiosity about answers he does not own

Ayanokōji's attraction to unpredictability is morally ambiguous.

It can become experimental appetite.

It can also create the precondition for respecting another person as more than a completed model.

The ethical resource appears when surprise is permitted to change **him**, not merely enrich his data.

# 5. Freedom and self-authorship — from escape to second-order authorship

## 5.1 Negative freedom remains real

Ayanokōji still resists external ownership.

Y2V01 provides a clean statement when Sakayanagi offers a safer route and he refuses despite recognizing her competence:

> `おまえの実力は認めている。だが自分の運命は自分で決める。`

This is not distrust of ability. It is refusal of jurisdiction.

That makes his later willingness to author others' environments an explicit contradiction rather than an accidental inconsistency.

## 5.2 Positive freedom becomes richer

Year 2 adds positive content to freedom:

- continued school life;
- friends;
- romance;
- purposeless social time;
- hobbies;
- ordinary ignorance;
- making mistakes safely;
- wanting inefficient objects;
- creating rather than reproducing;
- being remembered;
- and encountering answers he cannot fully predict.

Freedom therefore becomes increasingly about what life is **for**, not merely who does not control him.

## 5.3 Volume 0 revises the clean-escape narrative

The route to ANHS was not untouched by Atsuomi and adult planning.

The mature distinction is:

- **prepared opportunity** does not equal **owned meaning**;
- **causal influence** does not equal **total authorship**;
- **genealogy** does not equal **destiny**.

Ayanokōji's later love of ordinary school life remains development because the source does not show the child already possessing that adult purpose in finished form.

## 5.4 Second-order authorship is the stronger Year-2 model

No serious human life is causally pristine.

The meaningful question is whether the person can generate purposes that exceed inherited design and can revise the methods inherited from that design.

Ayanokōji clearly achieves the first.

The second remains incomplete.

## 5.5 The class-separation project is self-authored and inherited at once

Y2V06 makes the project explicitly his.

He wants to test White Room education through a class developed by his own method and wants eventual opposition/defeat.

Volume 0 later recontextualizes the form: his life has long been organized by proof experiments, preference engineering, and environments designed to establish claims about human development.

Thus:

> **the end is self-chosen; the grammar of proving the end through authored people is not wholly new.**

This is why `Y1H-007` becomes a reframed question rather than a triumphal answer about self-authorship.

## 5.6 Freedom for self versus freedom for others remains the governing contradiction

He is extremely sensitive to illegitimate claims over his own future.

He is much less reliable at recognizing when accurate developmental knowledge becomes an illegitimate claim over another person's future.

Year 2 improves this asymmetry locally.

It does not eliminate it.

# 6. Ordinary life and counter-curriculum — desired uncertainty

The most important Year-2 human-development evidence often occurs outside the highest-stakes strategic scenes.

## 6.1 Y2V01 — school life becomes worth public cost

Ayanokōji changes his visibility policy partly because remaining at school is worth defending.

Friendship, cooking, tutoring, domestic mess, jealousy, grocery shopping, and ordinary embarrassment establish what is at stake.

## 6.2 Y2V03–V04.5 — embodied intimacy and social continuity

Environmental authorship is the capacity to alter the field in which another actor chooses rather than merely issue the choice directly.

Typical instruments include:

- information;
- visibility;
- timing;
- incentives;
- witnesses;
- dependency;
- social topology;
- institutional procedure;
- access;
- relational expectation;
- and anticipated audience response.

# 8. Ethics — from intervention to authored choice

Ayanokōji's ethics should be evaluated through method, not merely outcome.

## 8.1 The Year-2 intervention audit

For any high-impact intervention, test:

1. **Goal authorship** — whose end is being pursued?
2. **Epistemic warrant** — how strong is the model, and how falsifiable is it?
3. **Imminence/necessity** — was intervention actually needed now?
4. **Proportionality** — how severe is the imposed cost?
5. **Less-coercive alternatives** — were lower-jurisdiction methods available?
6. **Information** — did the affected person know the conditions relevant to choice?
7. **Refusal/exit** — could they say no without punishment engineered by the intervener?
8. **Jurisdiction** — what legitimate standing did Ayanokōji have to decide?
9. **Risk distribution** — who bears third-party cost?
10. **Dependency created** — does the method make future refusal harder?
11. **Transparency/provenance** — can the intervention be traced and contested?
12. **Reversibility/repair** — can a wrong model be corrected after action?
13. **Temporal authorship** — who controls when information and judgment arrive?
14. **Separation/succession** — does later independence reproduce the intervener's curriculum?
15. **Counter-authorship** — can the affected person generate a legitimate answer that changes the modeler?

## 8.2 Explanation is not justification

Volume 0 can explain why preference engineering, performance management, and control through environment feel natural to him.

That genealogy does not justify later use.

## 8.3 Effectiveness is not legitimacy

Ayanokōji is often right about what will produce a desired outcome.

The fact that the method works is evidence of capability.

It is not evidence of moral title.

## 8.4 Surface consent is insufficient

A person can sincerely choose inside an environment whose relevant facts, refusal costs, or relational pressures were hidden from them.

Year 2 therefore distinguishes:

- surface consent;
- informed consent;
- constrained consent;
- authored-choice consent;
- retrospective appropriation;
- and counter-authored consent.

## 8.5 V03 — mild paternalism through motivational opacity

Withholding rescue information from Ike preserves effort.

The harm is relatively bounded.

The mechanism is important because it reveals the general form: ignorance can be deliberately preserved because the developer believes the target will perform better without knowing.

## 8.6 V04 — protection can violate autonomy and create externalities

Incapacitating Nagumo may be protective.

It also removes immediate risk choice and has system-wide consequences.

The case separates protective motive from legitimate authority.

## 8.7 V05 — Airi and the ethics of culling

Airi's final request is sincere.

The environment is not neutral.

Ayanokōji knows:

- her guilt;
- Haruka attachment;
- weak self-assertion;
- her valuation of his judgment;
- and the procedural impossibility created by the class's prior branch.

The mature formulation is:

> **Airi authors the final act, but Ayanokōji authors much of the choice environment in which that act becomes psychologically unavoidable.**

This is one of the corpus's strongest demonstrations that agency and manipulation can coexist.

## 8.8 V07 — stage-setting and deniable responsibility

The Yagami operation preserves many independent acts.

The moral danger is that distributed agency can diffuse accountability so effectively that the stage-setter understates his contribution without making a literally false claim.

## 8.9 V08 — care and timing jurisdiction

Ichinose receives genuine comfort.

The hidden timetable remains his.

This is more ethically revealing than fake-care analysis because it shows that **accurate care can increase control capacity**.

## 8.10 V09.5 — pleasure in manipulation

The important claim is narrow but serious.

Ayanokōji recognizes that manipulating through speech can be fun.

This does not prove sadism or make every intervention pleasure-driven.

It does create an internal-restraint problem: control now has its own experiential reward.

External answerability and stopping rules therefore matter more, not less.

## 8.11 V11 — the strongest positive local model

The V11 rule is:

> open a future; preserve alternatives; stop before owning the answer.

This is the best evidence that Ayanokōji can practice developmental intervention without total curriculum.

`Y1H-001` remains only `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED` because one local stopping rule does not prove stable generalization.

## 8.12 V12 — the strongest negative model

V12 demonstrates that formal freedom can itself become a control surface.

Truth and falsehood can be mixed so that the recipient cannot audit them in time.

A valued person's inferred preference can become more causally powerful than a direct order.

Trust accumulated through care can be reversed into vulnerability.

The stronger stopping rule is therefore:

> **Leaving the last act free is not enough if the earlier design has made one answer predictably serve the hidden author's preference.**

## 8.13 V12.5 — separation does not settle the ethics

Kei behaves with strength partly because she understands the lesson he expects.

Horikita's class must function without him, but she was not informed in time to participate in the meaning of the transfer.

Ichinose's strongest recovery is the answer he did not supply.

The Year-2 ethical hierarchy becomes:

`beneficial outcome < functional independence < informed self-authorship < counter-authorship`

## 8.14 Protection versus ownership

Ayanokōji frequently protects people.

Protection becomes ownership when he assumes that superior knowledge of danger grants superior title to decide:

- what risk they may take;
- what truth they may hear;
- when they may break;
- whom they may become;
- or when the relationship must end.

## 8.15 The principal ethical virtue and failure still arise from the same capacity

### Virtue

He sees developmental possibility where static systems see defects.

### Failure

He mistakes developmental insight for jurisdiction.

Year 2 does not replace this Year-1 formulation.

It scales it from individual cases to class ecology and relational sovereignty.

# 10. Counter-authorship — the Year-2 limit on developmental ownership

## 10.1 Why independence is not enough

A developed person can continue functioning after the developer leaves and still be executing the developer's intended answer.

That is why the following hierarchy matters:

1. **persistence** — the relation or behavior continues;
2. **role survival** — function survives a role change;
3. **successor structure** — the system can reproduce competence without the original center;
4. **counter-authorship** — the person/system produces a legitimate answer the original model did not supply and forces the modeler to update.

Counter-authorship is the strongest Year-2 autonomy evidence because it makes the superior model answerable to the person.

## 10.2 Surprise is not automatically counter-authorship

Ayanokōji likes surprise.

That alone does not make the surprising person autonomous in the relevant ethical sense.

If every unexpected response is immediately converted into another valuable data point inside his experiment, surprise can become a product.

The stronger criterion is:

> **Does the unexpected answer acquire standing to change what Ayanokōji is entitled to do, not merely what he knows?**

## 10.3 Horikita — counter-authorship through consequential disagreement

Horikita repeatedly gives answers he does not dictate.

Her preservation of Kushida is the clearest early example because he must operate inside a decision he would not have selected.

This demonstrates meaningful judgment independence.

The stronger post-transfer question is whether the polity can generate a strategy that defeats or reinterprets the lesson he expected it to learn.

## 10.4 Kushida — appropriation without gratitude

Kushida's later cooperation is not valuable because she becomes grateful for preservation.

It becomes more autonomous when she adopts continued cooperation for her own self-interested reason while retaining hatred.

This is an important correction to developmental authorship: the target need not accept the developer's moral story to appropriate a future.

## 10.5 Sudō — horizontal generativity

Sudō's development increasingly propagates through peer relation rather than superior design.

This is stronger than simple improvement because the developed person becomes capable of helping generate capacity in others.

## 10.6 Airi and Haruka — institutional culling does not own the future

Airi's life after ANHS demonstrates that school elimination is not ontological failure.

Haruka's model changes because the expelled person produces a future outside the institution's ranking system.

This limits the jurisdiction of Ayanokōji's V05 culling logic even where the immediate class decision remains irreversible.

## 10.7 Ichinose — the strongest terminal case

Ichinose becomes the clearest example of a person who repeatedly exceeds a completed model.

Second List makes the route explicit:

- Ayanokōji rejects sole authorship of her recovery;
- her class supplies distributed care;
- her later development changes what he can infer;
- and the Year-2 endpoint supplies an answer beyond his forecast.

The strongest terminal principle is therefore:

> **Development survives the developer most convincingly where it becomes counter-authored.**

## 10.8 Counter-authorship as a psychological need

There is also a personal dimension.

Ayanokōji wants recognition that does not own him.

People who answer beyond his model may satisfy that need more deeply than people who simply admire or obey him.

This creates a possible route of moral development:

> the relation he most wants may require him to stop guaranteeing the conditions under which it succeeds.

Year 2 identifies the route.

It does not prove he can follow it under decisive stakes.

- leave large environmental consequences implicit;
- avoid grandiose ownership language.

This is not always lying.

It can still understate responsibility.

A reconstruction model should therefore **not** make him boast about manipulation simply because the analyst knows he staged the field.

## 12.6 Emotional uncertainty is analytic before confessional

Ayanokōji rarely needs to narrate a direct conventional label such as “I am deeply sad” for affect to be present.

He often:

1. notices a bodily or behavioral signal;
2. compares it with expectation;
3. tests possible labels;
4. rejects one interpretation;
5. leaves another open.

Y2V10's involuntary smile is exemplary.

The affect occurs before he scripts it.

He treats his own expression as evidence.

## 12.7 Self-correction is a development marker

One of the strongest linguistic signs of development is not warmer wording but the capacity to interrupt an old explanatory reflex.

The yogurt-maker sequence is structurally important for this reason: optimization begins, then desire is allowed to terminate the calculation.

The process is psychologically visible in language.

## 12.8 Public understatement and private breadth must remain separated

Do not put the interior essay into his mouth.

Spoken Ayanokōji usually gives less than the narration knows.

This distinction is essential for behavioral reconstruction.

## 12.9 V12.5 focalization trap

The breakup chapter contains a formal warning beyond ordinary selective narration.

An emotionally rich interior initially appears attributable to Ayanokōji but is revealed through `別れよう` to belong to Kei's hoped-for counterfactual.

The correct lesson is:

- Ayanokōji can selectively narrate;
- and the novel can also temporarily misalign perceived focal ownership.

No reconstruction should assign every first-person-like interior near him to him without source-level focalization.

## 12.10 Volume 0 child voice — function before preference

The child voice is not simply the adult voice reduced in age.

Its world is heavily organized around:

- function;
- input;
- performance;
- test;
- observation.

The Yuki preference interaction is diagnostic because Kiyotaka initially answers a preference question through nutritional/function information before being pushed toward “what do **I** like?”

The episode provides a genealogy for the adult tendency to move from objective function toward subjective preference only after explicit prompting.

It also blocks the “emotionless child” reading.

Appetite, curiosity, dislike, and preference exist.

What is starved is the expectation that feeling has relational causal force.

## 12.11 Relationship-specific voice does not create separate personalities

Kei, Horikita, Hiyori, Sakayanagi, Ichinose, Ryūen, and White Room-linked interlocutors elicit different speech permissions.

That is relationship-conditioned expression, not evidence that one register is the true Ayanokōji and the others are false.

## 12.12 Voice simulation constraints

Do not write Ayanokōji as:

- a robot;
- an emotionless monosyllable machine;
- an omniscient villain monologuing his plan;
- a permanently sarcastic teenager;
- a conventionally transparent boyfriend;
- or a person whose every ordinary joke secretly advances strategy.

The correct model is:

> **sparse outward baseline + broad interior register + relationship-specific permission + stress/strategic compression + increasing participant-mode speech acts.**

## 12.13 Open voice work

The following remain open at Y2SL:

- exact frequency distributions of pronouns, sentence-final forms, contractions, and honorifics;
- a machine-readable address-term crosswalk for every dyad;
- a formal Ayanokōji narration-mode classifier;
- and any acoustic performance analysis.

These open tasks must not be replaced by invented quantitative certainty.

# 15. Volume 0 — retrospective genealogy without determinism

Volume 0 changes the explanation of Ayanokōji more than it changes the local facts established before it.

## 15.1 The White Room is political before it is merely pedagogical

The institution is not just a brutal school.

It is embedded in political competition, patronage, elite production, and Atsuomi's project of power.

This matters because Ayanokōji's later environmental authorship now has an explicit political genealogy.

## 15.2 Kiyotaka is less reproducible, not less formed

The mature nature/nurture formulation is neither:

- “the White Room made everything he is”;
- nor “the White Room merely discovered a fixed genius.”

The source supports an interaction between unusual potential and extreme environment.

Its own researchers do not establish Kiyotaka as a reliably reproducible product.

## 15.3 Emotional capacity was not metaphysically absent

The child source contains:

- curiosity;
- appetite;
- dislike;
- preference;
- attention to other people;
- and selective protection/data-seeking behavior.

What is deeply constrained is not feeling itself but the social expectation that feeling should reorganize the environment.

## 15.4 Atsuomi's preference engineering is the key genealogy

The “sushi/curry” doctrine matters because it articulates a governing grammar:

> the strongest control can operate by changing the field in which another person comes to prefer the imposed outcome.

School-era Ayanokōji later modifies this grammar.

He uses staged agency, development, succession, and partial handoff.

But he does not invent the jurisdictional form from nothing.

## 15.5 The class-separation project is therefore genealogically compromised but not externally assigned

Kiyotaka genuinely chooses the counter-experiment.

Volume 0 shows why the **form** of turning other people into evidence for a developmental proposition should not be treated as pristine self-authorship.

## 15.6 ANHS remains an alternative only through plural counter-authorship

Once political genealogy contaminates any easy purity contrast, ANHS cannot be defended merely because it offers choice, leisure, or a different institution.

The stronger alternative consists in:

- dissent;
- independent evidence;
- meaningful exit;
- ordinary surplus purpose;
- bounded measurement;
- rival institutions;
- and futures not exhausted by institutional judgment.

In Ayanokōji's case, this becomes personal:

> **the school differs most meaningfully from the White Room when its people can produce answers he does not own.**

## 15.7 Claims Volume 0 does not settle

Volume 0 leaves open:

- exact Tsukishiro instructions/objective;
- complete blank-year mechanics;
- what Kiyotaka knew about adult preparation when entering ANHS;
- several Matsuo/Eiichirō/Nanase causal questions;
- post-White-Room lives of former students;
- and whether Ayanokōji can consciously identify and ethically constrain the Atsuomi-derived elements of his own authorship.

# 16. Strong counterarguments and their limits

## 16.1 Counterargument — ordinary life is still only data collection

### Strongest case

Ayanokōji frequently turns ordinary experience into analysis.

He explicitly enjoys learning through manipulation and sometimes treats romance or friendship as curriculum.

### Limit

The corpus contains ordinary desires that terminate rather than advance optimization:

- wanting continued school life;
- wanting an inefficient object;
- disappointment at losing the object;
- deliberate under-preparation for skiing;
- purposeless conversation;
- spontaneous smiling;
- habits surviving a relationship's end.

The stronger conclusion is not strategic purity.

It is that ordinary life produces purposes that can coexist with and sometimes interrupt optimization.

## 16.2 Counterargument — all care is manipulation because he always understands more

### Strongest case

Information asymmetry is extreme, and care often increases his leverage.

### Limit

The Hiyori participant mode, V11 bounded intervention, ordinary tutoring, rescue without manufactured crisis, and his own refusal to claim sole authorship of Ichinose's recovery demonstrate a wider repertoire.

The correct distinction is **care quality and jurisdiction**, not “care versus manipulation.”

## 16.3 Counterargument — Volume 0 proves he is simply repeating Atsuomi

### Strongest case

Environmental authorship and preference engineering have clear structural family resemblance.

### Limit

The ends differ substantially.

Kiyotaka develops ordinary and relational purposes Atsuomi did not assign, values unmodeled answers, and sometimes practices stopping rules that would make little sense inside total paternal sovereignty.

The mature claim is inherited grammar plus divergent purpose, not identity.

## 16.4 Counterargument — he is becoming morally better because he wants people to be independent

### Strongest case

Training-wheels withdrawal, distributed agency, V11 stopping rules, and acceptance of growth away from him are real.

### Limit

He often owns the timetable, information, and definition of what “independence” should look like.

Independence performed according to the developer's curriculum is weaker than counter-authorship.

## 16.5 Counterargument — V12 proves all earlier humanization was fake

### Strongest case

V12 contains severe trust weaponization and relational sovereignty.

### Limit

The source repeatedly shows authentic ordinary desire, care, and affect before and after.

The more difficult and better-supported conclusion is simultaneous development of human attachment and control capability.

## 16.6 Counterargument — V10's smile proves conventional emotional normalization

### Strongest case

The smile is involuntary and genuinely unplanned.

### Limit

The same volume explicitly expands his four-class developmental jurisdiction.

The smile proves spontaneous affect, not completed ethical or relational transformation.

## 16.7 Counterargument — Kei was merely a textbook

### Strongest case

Ayanokōji explicitly uses textbook/curriculum logic and later considers substitution.

### Limit

The fiction also contains real physical desire, ordinary attentiveness, domestic routine, concern, remembered preference, and emotional residue.

The guidebook's reductive language is legitimate official framing but insufficient as exhaustive literary ontology.

The mature formulation is coexistence of relationship and curriculum.

## 16.8 Counterargument — because his narration is selective, nothing internal can be trusted

### Strongest case

He underdiscloses competence and motive, and V12.5 includes a focalization trap.

### Limit

Selective narration does not make all statements equally unreliable.

Mechanism, present desire, observed response, and self-correction remain highly informative when routed carefully.

The correct method is domain-specific reliability, not total skepticism.

## 16.9 Counterargument — he is socially omniscient

### Strongest case

He models many people at extraordinary resolution.

### Limit

Year 2 repeatedly shows:

- revised estimates;
- ordinary knowledge gaps;
- model surprise;
- difficulty with low-stakes invitation;
- and people generating answers outside his forecast.

Superior social diagnosis is not omniscience.

## 16.10 Counterargument — counter-authorship absolves the stage-setter

### Strongest case

If another person genuinely creates the terminal answer, perhaps the developer did not control them after all.

### Limit

Causal responsibility for intentionally authored conditions remains even when the target later exceeds the model.

Counter-authorship limits ownership of the **result**; it does not erase responsibility for the **environment**.

# 17. Claim-status audit at the complete Year-2 boundary

This section states what the specialist is willing to treat as current character authority at `Y2SL` and what it is not.

The categories are deliberately not a single confidence score. A claim can be strongly supported and still ethically incomplete, relationship-specific, or bounded to one mode of action.

## 17.1 Confirmed at Y2SL

### A. Ordinary school life is a genuine self-chosen end

This is no longer plausibly reduced to camouflage or merely a convenient refuge from Atsuomi.

Y2V01 makes continued school life his explicit present priority. Later volumes repeatedly show value in activities whose importance depends on their inefficiency, uncertainty, embodiment, or social contingency: learning to ski without overpreparing, wanting ordinary invitations, buying and using a yogurt maker, reading, eating, shared travel, purposeless conversation, celebrations, embarrassment, attraction, and memories.

Volume 0 complicates the origin of his route into ANHS. It does not erase the later fact that he appropriates ordinary life as a purpose of his own.

### B. His self-authorship is real but second-order

He did not author the conditions that produced his capabilities, his initial escape route, or many of the alternatives available to him.

He nevertheless authors consequential later ends within and against those conditions.

The mature formulation is therefore neither:

- `Ayanokōji is fully self-created`

nor:

- `Ayanokōji is only executing someone else's curriculum`.

It is:

> **a heavily authored person becomes a second-order author of purposes, relationships, and environments.**

### C. Environmental authorship is his mature strategic grammar

Across Year 2 the recurring sequence is:

`diagnose limiting condition -> alter environment -> preserve or manufacture motive/pressure -> allow target agency to complete the mechanism -> observe the resulting answer`.

This grammar can produce:

- real development;
- real agency;
- useful uncertainty;
- lower visible coercion;
- and genuine answers he did not script exactly.

It can also preserve severe hidden authorship.

The target acting freely at the final step does not by itself establish that the preceding choice architecture was free.

### D. Care and calculation coexist without one automatically falsifying the other

Year 2 repeatedly rejects the binary `care OR use`.

Ayanokōji can:

- care about a person;
- understand them accurately;
- want their development;
- enjoy their company;
- experience attraction or emotional residue;
- and still incorporate their motives into a larger design.

This is central to Kei, Horikita, Ichinose, Sakayanagi, and Ryūen in different ways.

### E. His models are extraordinarily strong but not equivalent to people

He is repeatedly good enough to make high-resolution prediction a genuine causal resource.

He is also repeatedly confronted with:

- revised estimates;
- ordinary ignorance;
- unexpected affect;
- socially awkward low-stakes uncertainty;
- and people who generate answers not contained in his model.

The strongest Year-2 developmental evidence often appears precisely when the model is exceeded.

### F. Counter-authorship is the strongest autonomy test presently available

Survival is weaker than role survival.

Role survival is weaker than functional independence.

Functional independence is weaker than informed self-authorship.

Informed self-authorship is still weaker than **counter-authorship** when the person can generate an answer that forces the superior modeler to update rather than merely demonstrating the competence he intended to cultivate.

Ichinose at the Y2V12.5 boundary supplies the clearest terminal form of this test.

### G. The V11 stopping rule is real positive evidence

`ここから先は、オレの立ち入る領域じゃない。`

This is important because it expresses more than tactical withdrawal. It names a boundary of **jurisdiction**.

The evidence should not be downgraded merely because V12 later shows a severe failure to generalize it.

It should also not be promoted into a stable global trait.

The correct state is:

> **jurisdictional restraint has entered his available repertoire; it has not yet become a reliable governing norm.**

## 17.2 Strengthened beyond the Year-1 boundary

### A. The ordinary layer is generative, not merely restorative

Year 1 established that ordinary life mattered.

Year 2 strengthens the claim: ordinary life does not merely repair deprivation. It generates **surplus purpose**—new wants, memories, attachments, curiosities, inconveniences, and forms of value that the White Room did not assign.

### B. Spontaneous affect exists without strategic permission

The V10 smile is especially valuable because it is not presented as a chosen tactic or a rehearsed social response.

It does not prove conventional emotional normalization.

It does sharply weaken any model requiring affect to be merely simulated.

### C. He increasingly wants people to become generative rather than merely capable

The blank-sketchbook problem, the all-class developmental project, and the attraction of unpredictable answers all strengthen a shift from:

`can this person execute?`

toward:

`can this person produce an answer that is actually theirs?`

This development is ethically promising and structurally dangerous at the same time, because he often attempts to **design the conditions under which authentic generativity will emerge**.

### D. Low-control participant mode is a real relational capacity

Hiyori is the cleanest recurring example.

Ayanokōji can want company, issue an invitation, leave room for refusal, and participate without converting the encounter into an optimization problem.

This matters because it supplies positive evidence for a relational mode other than developmental custody.

It does not prove he applies that mode where stakes or attachment are higher.

### E. He can accept some consequential disagreement and growth away from himself

Horikita, Kushida, Sudō, and later Ichinose all contribute evidence that development need not culminate in obedience or gratitude.

`Y1H-002` is therefore answered by development.

The remaining question is whether he can accept an answer that is not merely independent but also costly, inefficient, unwanted, or destructive of a project he values.

## 17.3 Revised formulations

### Earlier formulation: freedom is mainly escape from external control

**REVISE.**

Year 2 makes freedom increasingly second-order.

The harder question is not whether Ayanokōji can choose his own end, but whether he can pursue self-chosen ends without reproducing the preference-engineering and authored-environment grammar that formed him.

### Earlier formulation: his central ethical issue is manipulation

**REVISE.**

“Manipulation” remains useful but too coarse.

The more precise Year-2 axis is **jurisdiction**:

- who chooses the end;
- who possesses decisive information;
- who controls timing;
- who can refuse;
- who bears externalities;
- who can audit the causal account;
- and who retains the right to answer differently.

### Earlier formulation: independence is sufficient evidence that development succeeded ethically

**REJECT.**

A person can become highly competent, survive separation, and even reproduce the intended curriculum while remaining evidence of successful authored development rather than strong evidence of autonomy.

Counter-authorship is the stronger test.

### Earlier formulation: greater affection should naturally reduce instrumental control

**DOWNGRADE.**

Year 2 repeatedly shows the opposite possibility.

Attachment can increase:

- information;
- motivational access;
- credibility;
- dependency knowledge;
- and the precision of timing.

Humanization and causal power can therefore grow together.

### Earlier formulation: romance with Kei establishes irreplaceability

**REJECT as a Year-2 boundary claim.**

Y2V09.5 reveals current substitutability rather than the hypothesized irreplaceability, while the relationship still contains real affection, bodily desire, routine, care, and residue.

The correction is not “Kei meant nothing.”

It is “real relationship does not equal demonstrated irreplaceability.”

### Earlier formulation: the Horikita relation is chiefly proxy leadership

**REVISE strongly.**

By Y2SL Horikita is an independent political actor, trusted collaborator, affective peer, and intended successor whose consequential disagreement has become part of the relationship's value.

The hidden separation timetable remains a serious asymmetry.

## 17.4 Rejected reductions

The following are not acceptable current-authority summaries:

- `Ayanokōji has no emotions.`
- `His ordinary personality is entirely fake.`
- `Every relationship is merely a tool relation.`
- `If someone improves, his intervention was therefore ethical.`
- `If he does not issue a direct command, the choice is autonomous.`
- `If another person acts voluntarily, he did not author the environment.`
- `Because Volume 0 explains provenance, later agency is predetermined.`
- `Because he understands someone accurately, he has legitimate authority to decide for them.`
- `Because his narration is selective, no internal statement is usable.`
- `Because he is highly predictive, he is socially omniscient.`
- `Because V11 shows restraint, V12 is an anomaly that can be ignored.`
- `Because V12 shows severe control, the earlier ordinary development was fake.`
- `His written voice simply becomes warmer over time.`

## 17.5 Unresolved at Y2SL

The corpus still does not establish:

1. whether ordinary attachment will reliably constrain high-control intervention when the project stakes are high;
2. whether the V11 jurisdictional stopping rule can generalize beyond favorable circumstances;
3. whether he can voluntarily disclose decisive information early enough for another person to make a genuinely informed consequential choice;
4. whether he can accept a costly autonomous answer without rerouting the environment toward his preferred ecology;
5. whether he can value a particular person through sustained strategic uselessness or opposition at real cost to himself;
6. whether he can grant another person meaningful protective or corrective authority over him;
7. whether he can expose weakness rather than merely permit others to observe bounded imperfection;
8. whether his desire for unpredictable answers can mature from a sophisticated developmental experiment into reciprocal coexistence;
9. whether a class can become independent of him in the stronger systemic sense rather than merely functioning after his withdrawal;
10. how much of his own inherited preference-engineering grammar he can consciously recognize as such.

These are not gaps to be filled by character archetype.

They are boundary conditions for future evidence.

# 19. Behavioral reconstruction constraints for Ayanokōji at Y2SL

`COTE_BEHAVIORAL_RECONSTRUCTION_PROTOCOL_THROUGH_Y2.md` is derivative and remains `active_provisional`. This section translates the canonical specialist into constraints for downstream reconstruction without making scenario output new evidence.

## 19.1 Behavior before speech

A canon-compatible reconstruction should resolve, in order:

1. what Ayanokōji knows and does not know;
2. whether the scene is ordinary, relational, institutional, adversarial, or acute-stress dominant;
3. what he currently wants;
4. whether the other person's answer matters instrumentally, relationally, or both;
5. what formal authority he actually possesses;
6. what epistemic advantage he possesses;
7. whether he has a demonstrated reason to intervene;
8. whether a lower-control route exists;
9. whether the V11 stopping-rule family is supported in this relation/domain;
10. only then, how he speaks.

## 19.2 Do not default every scene to mastermind mode

Low-stakes scenes require the ordinary-life correction.

If there is no exam, threat, coercive institution, hidden obligation, meaningful status contest, or evidence that he has turned the encounter into a developmental problem, the reconstruction should prefer ordinary response families before inventing a secret test.

A bookstore visit with Hiyori is not presumptively a social experiment.

A meal is not automatically information collection.

An invitation can be an invitation.

This is not naivety about the character. It is fidelity to the completed Year-2 evidence.

## 19.3 Do not default every high-stakes scene to direct coercion

Ayanokōji's mature control grammar is often environmental rather than imperative.

A reconstruction should consider:

- withholding or sequencing information;
- changing incentives;
- creating a comparison;
- preserving optionality;
- inducing another actor to make the visible move;
- changing who encounters whom;
- using an existing motive rather than manufacturing a new one;
- or declining visible ownership.

However, the output must label authored conditions as authored conditions. Indirectness is not ethical innocence.

## 19.4 Separate actor model from ground truth

When Ayanokōji says or thinks that another person is likely to respond in a particular way, downstream simulation must encode:

`AYANOKOJI_MODEL(target)`

not:

`TARGET_TRUE_STATE`.

Year 2 specifically rewards this discipline because model-surprise becomes developmental evidence.

## 19.5 Preserve simultaneous motives

Do not force a single hidden “true” motive when the source supports coexistence.

Ayanokōji may act because he:

- wants the class ecology to improve;
- cares about the person;
- wants to learn from their answer;
- wants ordinary continuity;
- dislikes a threat;
- enjoys manipulation;
- wants to test his own theory;
- and wants to preserve an exit route.

The relative weight may be underdetermined.

The correct reconstruction can preserve that underdetermination.

## 20.1 Principal developmental injury

The mature formulation remains continuous with Year 1 but becomes politically sharper after Volume 0.

Ayanokōji was developed in an environment that provided:

## 20.2 Principal adaptive strength

He distinguishes **surface performance from causal structure** unusually well.

That lets him detect:

- hidden ability;
- institutional blind spots;
- unstable incentives;
- dependency;
- latent leadership;
- unacknowledged desire;
- false equilibrium;
- and opportunities for growth.

## 20.3 Principal adaptive danger

The same diagnostic strength makes it easy for him to conclude:

`I understand the developmental situation better`

`I may author the conditions under which the other person will choose`.

Year 2 shows why that inference is ethically unsound even when the diagnosis and final outcome are excellent.

The strongest supported need remains **recognition without ownership**.

He is drawn toward people who:

- see more of him than the public mask;
- do not reduce him to institutional score;
- can oppose him meaningfully;

No one relation exhausts it.

## 20.5 Principal ethical developmental task

He already cares.

It is:

> **learn that accurate care, developmental insight, and causal power do not create title to another person's answer.**

The V11 stopping rule shows that he can formulate this distinction.

The V12 regression shows that formulation has not become stable governance.

## 20.6 Principal future-evidence test

The strongest future test is not whether he can create another successful person.

He has already demonstrated that.

It is whether he can remain in a relationship or polity where another person:

1. knows enough to judge him;
2. can refuse him at meaningful cost;
3. can protect or correct him when he is genuinely vulnerable;
4. can generate an answer he dislikes;
5. and can force his model—not merely his tactic—to change.

That is the strongest currently available operational definition of reciprocal authorship.

# 22. Final synthesis — more participant, more causal, not yet reciprocal enough

The most useful Year-2 correction is to reject the idea that Ayanokōji's development moves along a single axis from “cold” to “human.”

Year 2 changes him on at least two axes at once.

First, he becomes more of a **participant**.

He values ordinary life more concretely. He wants invitations. He experiences the awkwardness of possible rejection. He chooses activities he cannot perform perfectly. He buys things because he wants them rather than because they optimize a mission. He can enjoy food, books, trips, bodily attraction, purposeless conversation, celebration, rivalry, and the prospect of being remembered. He can smile before he has decided what the smile means. He can want an answer precisely because he does not already possess it.

Second, he becomes more **causal**.

He understands more people at higher resolution. He can work across classes. He can use institutions, incentives, timing, trust, secrecy, rivalry, love, and self-conception as components in an environmental design. He increasingly understands that the target's own agency is often the most powerful mechanism available. He can therefore leave the final visible act voluntary while still shaping much of the route into it.

These developments are not opposites.

They can reinforce one another.

The more a person matters to him, the more he may know about what would move them.

The more he values their independent answer, the more tempted he may be to design the perfect conditions under which that answer will emerge.

That is the central Year-2 danger.

The White Room deprived him of ordinary preference and reciprocal authorship while making him exceptionally good at authored development. ANHS gives him the chance to acquire the first without automatically losing the second.

Volume 0 makes this genealogy visible but does not make it destiny.

The strongest evidence against destiny is not that Ayanokōji becomes nice.

It is that he begins to value forms of life that authored systems are bad at producing on command:

- inefficient desire;
- accidental memory;
- embarrassment;
- unplanned affect;
- friendship without mission;
- purposeless conversation;
- bodily failure;
- and other people who answer incorrectly from the model's perspective but authentically from their own.

The strongest evidence that the inherited grammar remains alive is not that he is still strategic.

It is that his strategy increasingly operates through **preference and relationship architecture**.

V11 and V12 therefore belong together.

V11 proves he can recognize a boundary:

`ここから先は、オレの立ち入る領域じゃない。`

V12 proves he does not yet live by that boundary consistently.

The correct synthesis is neither triumph nor regression.

It is an expanded repertoire with unresolved governance.

By the end of Year 2, Ayanokōji can:

- participate without controlling every low-stakes scene;
- care without reducing care to utility;
- admire independent development;
- accept some disagreement;
- desire surprise;
- and explicitly name a jurisdiction he should not enter.

He can also:

- preserve decisive information asymmetry;
- use truth as a timed instrument;
- exploit trust as a causal channel;
- stage environments whose final choices remain formally voluntary;
- and subordinate another person's immediate experience to a developmental end he judges superior.

The question is no longer whether he is capable of human attachment.

The Year-2 corpus answers that strongly enough.

The question is whether attachment can become a **constitutional limit** on power rather than simply a richer source of information and motive.

Counter-authorship is the most demanding test the year discovers.

A developed person is not most independent when they survive the developer.

They are more independent when they can use what they have become for ends the developer did not choose.

And they are most diagnostically important when their answer forces the developer himself to revise the model.

At `Y2SL`, Ayanokōji increasingly wants such answers.

He has not yet demonstrated that he will always let them remain theirs.

That is the canonical Year-2 endpoint.
