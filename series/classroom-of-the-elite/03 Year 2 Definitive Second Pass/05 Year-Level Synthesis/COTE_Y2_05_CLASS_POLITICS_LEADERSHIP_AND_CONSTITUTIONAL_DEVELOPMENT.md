## Year 2 — Class Politics, Leadership, and Constitutional Development
### Four succession constitutions under pressure: authority, dissent, delegation, legitimacy, and the problem of exceptional-person dependence

> **Canonical specialist synthesis at the complete Year-2 / Second List boundary.**
>
> This document is the primary Year-2 home for comparative class politics: the four stable student polities, leadership legitimacy, information architecture, dissent, bounded obligation, weak-member treatment, delegation, generativity, resource governance, external diplomacy, provenance, concentration risk, and succession. It does not replace the immutable source-local volume readings or the canonical through-Year-2 polity ledger. # 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

The frozen Year-1 class-politics specialist ended with a deceptively simple result:

> **The four classes are not merely ranked containers. They are four incomplete political technologies for converting unequal human capacities into collective action.**

Year 2 does not replace those constitutions with new ones.

It forces each constitution to confront the question Year 1 could only partially pose:

> **What remains of the polity when the exceptional person on whom it depends is wrong, absent, unwilling, defeated, transferred, expelled, or no longer sovereign?**

That shift changes the primary unit of analysis.

Year 1 was dominated by the comparative problem of **leadership style**:

- Horikita's developmental aggregation;
- Ryūen's fear-centered asymmetric mobilization;
- Ichinose's solidaristic trust order;
- Sakayanagi's aristocratic optimization.

Year 2 increasingly asks whether those styles have become **reproducible political systems**.

A leader can improve while making the polity more dependent on her.

A polity can win while remaining constitutionally fragile.

A polity can lose while developing more robust internal correction.

A transfer can increase capacity while revealing failed succession.

A class can become more cohesive while becoming less capable of dissent.

A class can become less warm and more politically plural without becoming morally worse.

The specialist therefore uses a strict rule throughout:

> **leader development is not polity development.**

Polity development requires evidence that capacities which matter to collective survival can be distributed, contested, transmitted, corrected, and reproduced beyond one center.

## 0.1 Stable polity IDs, not moving class letters

The canonical longitudinal identities remain:

- `POLITY-HORIKITA`
- `POLITY-RYUEN`
- `POLITY-ICHINOSE`
- `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN`

Class letters are rank states.

They are not constitutional identities.

The same political system can move from D to A without becoming a different polity; likewise, a polity can retain elite personnel while losing the constitutional center that made those personnel collectively effective.

This specialist therefore never uses “Class A” as shorthand for “best constitution.”

## 0.2 Point totals are outcomes, not constitutional rankings

At the `Second List` boundary, `Y2SL-E084` gives the provisional ordering recorded in the canonical polity ledger:

| Stable polity | Provisional boundary state | Rank position | Constitutional shorthand |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `POLITY-HORIKITA` | 1233 | A / 1st | distributed developmental polity entering a genuine post-guarantor succession test |
| `POLITY-RYUEN` | 1040–1090 | B / 2nd | adaptive authoritarian polity with stronger legitimacy/delegation and unresolved sovereign dependence |
| `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` | 793 | C / 3rd | elite centralized polity after internal succession failure and imported re-centralization |
| `POLITY-ICHINOSE` | 714 | D / 4th | solidaristic polity with growing opposition/adversarial capacity and weak point conversion |

These totals are institutionally real.

They are not constitutional scores.

A class point total cannot by itself establish:

- whether dissent is protected;
- whether leadership can be corrected;
- whether weak members are treated as developable persons or disposable inputs;
- whether strategic knowledge is reproducible;
- whether formal consent is meaningfully informed;
- whether a result was authored by the visible leader;
- whether the polity can survive succession;
- whether members regard authority as legitimate;
- or whether irreversible costs were imposed by actors with standing to impose them.

The `Y1FF` and `Y2SL-E085` first-year-end point snapshots also remain numerically unreconciled. This document preserves that discrepancy and does **not** construct arithmetic Year-1→Year-2 point deltas from incompatible official snapshots. The detailed measurement/institutional audit routes to `Y2_06` and `Y2_07`.

## 0.3 Effectiveness, legitimacy, morality, and resilience remain separate variables

A polity may be:

- effective but illegitimate;
- legitimate to followers but ethically coercive;
- procedurally valid but epistemically misinformed;
- morally protective but competitively weak;
- competitively strong but succession-fragile;
- socially cohesive but dissent-poor;
- strategically distributed but dependent on a hidden emergency actor.

Year 2 makes these divergences impossible to collapse into one ranking.

## 0.4 A polity is not reducible to its visible leader

The relevant constitutional object includes:

- formal and informal leadership;
- social mediators;
- strategic specialists;
- dissenters;
- exceptional autonomous actors;
- weak or low-status members;
- private-point and protection arrangements;
- alliances and contracts;
- information flows;
- shadow authors;
- and the institutional rules above the class.

This matters especially for `POLITY-HORIKITA`, where Ayanokōji remains a hidden terminal-security layer through most of the year, but it also matters for the other classes. Ryūen's polity cannot be understood without Katsuragi, Hiyori, Ishizaki, Albert, Komiya, Tokitō, and the academic base. Ichinose's polity cannot be understood without Kanzaki, Himeno, Watanabe, Amikura, Hamaguchi, and the broad mutual-support network. Sakayanagi's polity cannot be understood without Kamuro, Yamamura, Hashimoto, Kito, Morishita, and the failure to convert a deep personnel field into a legitimate successor center.

## 0.5 Delegation, plural leadership, generativity, and succession are distinct

These terms must not be used interchangeably.

**Delegation** means the leader permits or assigns another actor to execute a bounded domain.

**Plural leadership** means meaningful domains are governed by more than one actor whose judgment has real causal standing.

**Generativity** means developed competence creates new competence elsewhere: a person teaches, recruits, corrects, reproduces, or transmits a capacity rather than merely performing it.

**Succession resilience** means the polity can continue producing political judgment after a central actor is removed.

A polity can delegate heavily while remaining succession-fragile.

Ryūen is the clearest case.

A polity can contain many talented people and still fail succession.

Sakayanagi-origin is the clearest case.

A polity can show strong generativity without yet completing the actual succession test.

Horikita is the clearest case.

A polity can legitimate opposition before that opposition becomes materially generative enough to replace leader-centered strategy.

Ichinose is the clearest case.

## 0.6 Dissent is constitutional only when it can survive membership

Disagreement alone does not establish a healthy polity.

The relevant test is whether the dissenter can:

1. remain a member;
2. speak without intolerable retaliation;
3. create information the leader must process;
4. alter policy before irreversible cost;
5. preserve legitimate standing after being wrong;
6. and become part of the system rather than merely an exception tolerated by the ruler.

Tokitō proves that dissent can exist under Ryūen without being protected.

Kanzaki and Himeno show dissent becoming compatible with solidarity.

Hirata, Sudō, Kushida, Kei, and Kōenji demonstrate different kinds of constraint inside Horikita's polity.

Hashimoto demonstrates what may happen when a polity lacks a trusted corrective channel and a broker instead treats betrayal as insurance.

## 0.7 Weak-member treatment is not a simple kindness axis

Every polity must decide what weakness means.

Possibilities include:

- temporary developmental state;
- membership entitled to protection;
- liability to be compensated for;
- exploitable vulnerability;
- replaceable resource;
- or reason for exclusion.

The constitutional question is not merely whether a polity ever sacrifices someone.

It is:

> **Who has standing to decide that another member's weakness has become an irreversible collective liability, what information is used, what alternatives exist, and who bears the remainder when the decision proves useful but morally costly?**

V05 and V12 make this question especially severe for Horikita's polity. Kamuro makes it severe for Sakayanagi-origin. Ichinose provides the strongest anti-culling norm but pays competitive-conversion costs. Ryūen increasingly preserves members without developing a general non-disposability principle.

## 0.8 Information governance is constitutional governance

A class cannot be politically assessed only through visible orders.

It matters:

- who knows the plan;
- who knows the purpose of the plan;
- who can verify authorship;
- who has access to private information;
- who may conceal information from whom;
- and whether a member can challenge the provenance of a decision.

The four polities differ sharply:

- `POLITY-HORIKITA` develops the most plural visible information structure while retaining the deepest hidden-author problem;
- `POLITY-RYUEN` usually makes sovereignty legible but centralizes decisive adversarial intelligence;
- `POLITY-ICHINOSE` begins with high-trust circulation and learns strategic opacity without abandoning belonging;
- `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` concentrates interpretive knowledge near the sovereign and underdevelops corrective trust.

## 0.9 Volume 0 is retrospective context, not a rewrite of student-polity chronology

Volume 0 reveals a political-developmental genealogy for Ayanokōji and the White Room.

It does not retroactively make Horikita, Ryūen, Ichinose, or Sakayanagi participants in that genealogy.

Its legitimate use here is comparative:

- it clarifies what a maximally authored developmental institution looks like;
- it sharpens the difference between producing capacity and producing self-government;
- it shows why exceptional output does not prove constitutional legitimacy;
- and it makes Ayanokōji's later four-class ecology more ethically legible without converting it into a White Room clone.

Detailed White Room/institutional analysis belongs to `Y2_07`; retrospective claim control belongs to `Y2_10`.

## 0.10 `Second List` calibrates the boundary; it does not certify future outcomes

The guidebook is unusually valuable here because it explicitly distinguishes current state from future task.

It confirms:

- Horikita's extraordinary Year-2 growth while leaving post-transfer stability unresolved (`Y2SL-E027`, `E081`, `E082`);
- Ryūen's stronger coordination and Katsuragi acquisition while warning of leader-dependence and academic weakness (`E034`–`E036`);
- Ichinose's unmatched no-expulsion unity while warning that strategy remains too dependent on her and point conversion remains weak (`E041`, `E042`);
- Sakayanagi-origin's collapse after sovereign withdrawal and absence of an internal successor (`E045`–`E047`);
- the reality of Ayanokōji's transfer into the former Sakayanagi polity (`E080`).

The guidebook does **not** reveal how those unresolved systems perform after the Y2SL boundary.

## 0.11 Ayanokōji's four-class project is not a fifth polity

Ayanokōji increasingly tries to preserve a competitive ecology in which all four classes retain meaningful paths to victory.

That is a developmental project **about** the four polities.

It is not itself a class constitution.

Its legitimate analytical role is as an external pressure and hidden authorship layer. Treating it as a fifth polity would erase the difference between:

- the political order members inhabit and partially authorize;
- and an individual actor's attempt to design the competitive environment among those orders.

# 1. Executive thesis — Year 2 turns leadership systems into succession constitutions

The shortest correct description of Year-2 class politics is:

> **Year 1 asks how unequal people can be organized. Year 2 asks whether the resulting political order can survive the person who organized it.**

Every major polity becomes a different experiment in dependence on excellence.

`POLITY-HORIKITA` develops the widest portfolio of distributed usable capacity. Horikita becomes independently authoritative; Hirata remains a social/moral counterweight; Kei sustains network coordination; Kushida's exposed social competence becomes self-owned rather than counterfeit; Sudō becomes peer-generative; Kōenji makes exceptional capacity available through bounded contract; multiple classmates increasingly solve local problems without Ayanokōji supplying the answer. Yet Ayanokōji remains hidden terminal security through the decisive Year-2 exam and controls the timing of his own departure. The polity therefore ends as the strongest **candidate** for distributed succession, not as a polity whose succession has already been proven.

`POLITY-RYUEN` becomes a substantially more sophisticated authoritarian order. Ryūen recruits expertise he lacks, delegates bounded domains, signs real contracts, updates failed models, preserves followers more often, and earns voluntary legitimacy that cannot be reduced to fear. But ultimate political judgment remains concentrated in him, dissent remains unsafe when it becomes personally challenging, and *Second List* explicitly identifies growing Ryūen-dependence as a developmental risk. The polity becomes better governed without yet becoming less sovereign-centered.

`POLITY-ICHINOSE` learns the most important lesson available to a solidaristic constitution: **criticism need not be disloyalty**. Kanzaki's isolated Year-2 dissent becomes a plural second brain through Himeno and others. The class learns concealment, adversarial response, and harder strategic boundaries without adopting culling as its governing answer. Ichinose herself becomes capable of counter-authoring Ayanokōji's developmental model. But leader-level counter-authorship arrives before polity-level strategic reproduction is complete. The class remains unusually dependent on Ichinose as both affective center and strategic nervous system.

`POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` supplies the negative succession control. It begins with the deepest elite talent pool and the strongest centralized strategic compression. It ends by demonstrating that **elite personnel are not a constitution**. Sakayanagi's private priority can override fiduciary class interest; corrective dissent becomes betrayal rather than trusted internal constraint; relational value becomes legible after irreversible loss; and when the sovereign exits, no internal successor reproduces her function. The class collectively finances and accepts Ayanokōji's transfer, which is real political agency—but it is **consented re-centralization**, not internal succession.

The four polities therefore cannot be ranked on a single political axis.

They differ in where they place the costs of coordination:

- Horikita pays for pluralism with slower aggregation, moral remainder, and historical dependence on hidden security;
- Ryūen pays for speed and strategic unity with weak protected dissent and sovereign concentration;
- Ichinose pays for membership protection and trust with conformity pressure and weak conversion into institutional score;
- Sakayanagi pays for elite compression with corrective-trust failure and catastrophic succession fragility.

The strongest Year-2 constitutional principle is:

> **A mature polity is not one whose strongest leader can solve every problem. It is one whose members can continue to produce, contest, transmit, and revise political judgment when the strongest leader no longer supplies the answer.**

That standard changes how victory itself must be read.

A win can be:

- genuinely distributed;
- leader-authored;
- hidden-author-authored;
- purchased through scarce resources;
- achieved through coercive mobilization;
- generated through solidaristic coordination;
- or distorted by private priorities and institutionally authored rules.

Year 2 therefore does not crown a constitution.

It turns **succession, correction, and provenance** into the measures by which all four constitutions become answerable.

# 2. Comparative constitutional framework

The Year-1 specialist used multiple constitutional dimensions. Year 2 retains them and adds stronger succession diagnostics.

| Dimension | Year-2 diagnostic question |
|---|---|
| **Membership** | What makes someone entitled to remain part of the collective? |
| **Authority** | Who can make binding decisions, and in what domain? |
| **Legitimacy** | Why do members accept the authority: rule, trust, fear, competence, loyalty, reciprocity, necessity? |
| **Information** | Where is strategic/social knowledge stored, and who may contest its interpretation? |
| **Dissent** | Can disagreement survive membership and alter policy? |
| **Weak-member doctrine** | Is weakness developable, protectable, compensable, exploitable, or disposable? |
| **Resource governance** | Who controls private points, protection rights, bargains, class capital, and scarce exemptions? |
| **Delegation** | Can bounded domains be genuinely owned by actors other than the leader? |
| **Generativity** | Does competence create new independent competence elsewhere? |
| **External diplomacy** | Can the polity cooperate, contract, recruit, or ally without losing identity? |
| **Provenance/accountability** | Can members identify who authored a decision and why that actor had standing? |
| **Succession** | Can the polity reproduce strategic/political judgment after center removal? |
| **Concentration risk** | What person or hidden subsystem could collapse the polity if removed? |
| **Counter-authorship** | Can members produce answers that force the leader/system to revise rather than merely execute? |

These dimensions should be read as a profile, not a scorecard.

## 2.1 Four kinds of legitimacy

Year 2 requires at least four legitimacy categories.

### Procedural legitimacy

Was the action authorized by recognized rules, office, contract, or collective procedure?

### Sociological legitimacy

Do members actually accept, follow, or preserve the authority?

### Epistemic legitimacy

Does the decision-maker possess enough reliable information to justify confidence in the judgment?

### Ethical legitimacy

Even if the decision-maker is informed and effective, does that actor have standing to impose this particular cost on this particular person?

These categories diverge repeatedly.

Ryūen acquires stronger sociological legitimacy without institutionalizing ethical or procedural limits on coercion.

Horikita can possess procedural office and real independent judgment while still owning morally costly decisions.

Ichinose can possess extraordinary sociological legitimacy while lacking enough distributed strategic capacity.

Sakayanagi can possess overwhelming epistemic confidence while violating fiduciary responsibility to the polity.

Ayanokōji can possess exceptional epistemic advantage while lacking ethical jurisdiction over the people whose choices he stages.

## 2.2 Succession is stronger than temporary leader absence

A leader can be unavailable for one task while a subordinate executes a plan.

That is not yet succession.

The stronger test asks whether the polity can:

1. identify the new problem;
2. generate an interpretation;
3. select among competing purposes;
4. authorize a decision;
5. mobilize resources;
6. accept correction;
7. update after failure;
8. and reproduce those capabilities again without the old center.

The former Sakayanagi polity fails this test most visibly.

Horikita's polity reaches the threshold at Y2SL.

Ryūen's polity has partial domain succession but not strategic succession.

Ichinose's polity develops a second brain but has not yet shown enough distributed generativity to declare the test solved.

## 2.3 Counter-authorship is the strongest developmental signal

A successor can remain inside the old leader's conceptual frame.

Counter-authorship is stronger.

It appears when the polity or member can generate an answer that:

- the original center did not supply;
- the original center did not predict;
- or the original center must revise around.

Ichinose's V12.5 third answer is the clearest leader-level case.

Horikita's V05 preservation of Kushida is an earlier case of independent political authorship, though its ethics remain mixed.

Ryūen's refusal to regard Sakayanagi's thrown result as the victory he wanted shows leader-level revision of the meaning of success.

The former Sakayanagi polity's collective transfer decision is agency, but it does not yet generate a new internal strategic constitution.

## 2.4 Constitutional health is not absence of conflict

Conflict can be evidence of political capacity.

A class in which nobody disagrees may be:

- genuinely aligned;
- socially conformist;
- afraid;
- dependent on one center;
- or deprived of information needed to contest the plan.

Year 2 increasingly treats **legitimate friction** as a resource.

The best question is not “does the class get along?”

It is:

> **Can disagreement create better knowledge without dissolving belonging?**

# 3. Year-1 → Year-2 constitutional claim transitions

The Year-1 polity specialist froze a set of propositions and questions. Year 2 changes them unevenly.

## 3.1 PRESERVE

1. **Stable polity IDs must remain separate from class letters.**
2. **Point totals are institutional outcomes, not total political quality.**
3. **Horikita's central problem is aggregation of heterogeneous capacity without ownership.**
4. **Ryūen's followers possess real agency; coercion does not exhaust loyalty.**
5. **Ichinose's solidarity is genuine political capacity.**
6. **Sakayanagi's centralized excellence contains fiduciary and succession risk.**
7. **Administrative/procedural sovereignty can override student competence.**
8. **Dissent is a capacity rather than merely a disruption.**
9. **The point economy is constitutional infrastructure because it changes which futures are affordable.**
10. **Information quality does not itself create information rights or jurisdiction.**

## 3.2 STRENGTHEN

1. `POLITY-HORIKITA` develops the strongest observed distribution of operational competence.
2. Ryūen's voluntary follower legitimacy is longitudinal and cannot be dismissed as post-rooftop anomaly.
3. `POLITY-ICHINOSE` can add strategic opacity and adversarial competence without abandoning solidarity.
4. Sakayanagi-origin's strategic concentration is simultaneously its strength and its succession catastrophe.
5. Dissent becomes more valuable when it remains compatible with belonging.
6. External recruitment can materially increase polity capacity without proving internal development.
7. Social infrastructure matters constitutionally even when it lacks formal office.
8. Provenance matters: who authored a result becomes part of political legitimacy.
9. Succession is a test of institutional reproduction, not merely individual replacement.
10. Development is strongest when new actors can generate purposes and answers that the developer cannot claim as outputs.

## 3.3 REVISE

### Earlier shorthand: “Horikita's class is becoming independent.”

Revised:

> Horikita and many classmates become independently capable; the polity's independence from Ayanokōji becomes genuinely testable only when he transfers.

### Earlier shorthand: “Ryūen's class is fear-based.”

Revised:

> Fear remains an available technology, but voluntary legitimacy, delegation, recruitment, contracts, and model updating materially change the constitution without constitutionalizing dissent.

### Earlier shorthand: “Ichinose's class is too kind.”

Revised:

> The polity has a strategic-conversion and distribution problem, not a simple kindness problem. Year 2 proves solidarity can coexist with concealment, criticism, deterrence, and adversarial response.

### Earlier shorthand: “Sakayanagi's class is resilient because it has elite personnel.”

Revised:

> A deep talent pool does not substitute for a succession constitution. Role allocation can be excellent while strategic reproduction remains absent.

### Earlier shorthand: “Delegation means distributed leadership.”

Revised:

> Delegation distributes execution. Distributed leadership requires independent judgment. Succession requires reproducible judgment after center removal.

### Earlier shorthand: “Collective consent proves independence.”

Revised:

> Collective financing and consent to Ayanokōji's transfer are real agency, but the action is consented re-centralization under leader-vacuum conditions, not proof of internally generated succession.

### Earlier shorthand: “Winning proves development.”

Revised:

> A victory must be audited for authorship, provenance, institutional conditions, and whether the polity can reproduce the causal competence that produced it.

## 3.4 DOWNGRADE

- scalar rankings of constitutional quality by class points;
- the idea that Ryūen's improved delegation has solved succession;
- the idea that Ichinose's second brain is already fully institutionalized;
- the idea that Horikita's A position proves independence from Ayanokōji;
- the idea that former Sakayanagi-class leadership is solved because Ayanokōji arrives;
- any inference that a beneficial later outcome cleans the coercive or sacrificial route that produced it.

## 3.5 REJECT

- fear alone explains Ryūen-class loyalty;
- solidarity necessarily prevents adversarial competence;
- Horikita remains Ayanokōji's proxy at the Year-2 boundary;
- Kushida's social capacity disappears when her mask collapses;
- Sakayanagi-origin can absorb sovereign exit without severe institutional damage;
- a class with many high-ability members automatically possesses succession resilience;
- class rank is a valid summary of constitutional quality;
- the four-class ecology grants Ayanokōji legitimate ownership of the four classes' futures.

## 3.6 OPEN

The major Year-2 boundary questions remain:

1. Can `POLITY-HORIKITA` sustain high-level performance after Ayanokōji becomes external rather than internal security?
2. Can Kōenji's contribution become durable without converting his self-authored autonomy into class ownership?
3. Can Ryūen's followers create a binding internal limit on the sovereign?
4. Can Ryūen's class reproduce strategic intelligence if he is absent?
5. Can Ichinose's reform coalition become materially generative rather than merely legitimate?
6. Can Ichinose improve point conversion without making culling or disposability the new governing norm?
7. Can `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` become more than another exceptional-person vehicle after importing Ayanokōji?
8. Can collective consent around Ayanokōji coexist with meaningful correction of him?
9. How should the incompatible official Year-1 point snapshots be reconciled?
10. Can any class preserve exceptional ability without allowing superior knowledge to become superior jurisdiction?

# 4. Political progression of Year 2

Year 2's constitutional progression is best read in six movements plus the retrospective and documentary boundaries.

## 4.1 Y2V01–Y2V02 — legibility becomes bargaining power, then contract

OAA and the cross-year pairing system make ability searchable.

The effect is constitutional because legibility changes who can make demands.

Different polities respond differently.

Horikita tries to organize equalized class-to-class cooperation and prevent expulsion while distributing work through Hirata and other classmates. Her model begins to treat the weak as members whose risk must be politically managed rather than simply as defects.

Ryūen treats the partner market as both price mechanism and intelligence surface. He then spends heavily to recruit Katsuragi—a form of expertise different from his own.

Ichinose converts trust into cross-year protection and later proves capable of bargaining with Sakayanagi rather than merely accepting the stronger class's frame.

Sakayanagi uses brand, money, and strategic asymmetry from a position of elite confidence.

Y2V02 makes the deeper issue explicit: institutions create contracts through which one person's ability can bind another person's risk.

Kōenji's island bargain becomes one of the year's cleanest constitutional cases. His contribution can benefit the collective **without granting the collective unlimited title over his future labor**.

The Ryūen–Katsuragi transfer similarly distinguishes legal consent from environmental pressure. The polity can acquire expertise; it cannot literally own the person.

## 4.2 Y2V03–Y2V04 — hidden authorship exposes the limits of internal constitutions

The uninhabited-island exam decentralizes students physically while centralizing official data in school-managed systems.

This makes attribution difficult.

Within classes, distributed actors can act independently.

Above classes, administrators can manipulate the evidentiary environment.

The Tsukishiro/Shiba record problem therefore establishes an institutional ceiling on class politics: even a competent polity cannot guarantee recognition if the authority above it can alter provenance.

For Horikita's polity, Kōenji's performance also demonstrates a constitutional paradox: self-sovereign excellence can be enormously useful when bounded by contract, yet remain unavailable for ordinary command.

For Ryūen, the Hōsen confrontation shows a leader increasingly able to define victory institutionally rather than physically. He can preserve followers, use timing, rely on delegated exam work, and refuse to equate bodily dominance with political control.

The period therefore differentiates **capacity**, **authority**, and **recorded outcome**.

## 4.3 Y2V04.5–Y2V06 — embeddedness makes political decisions morally attributable

V04.5 broadens ordinary relationships and makes the four classes less socially sealed than their competitive identities imply.

Komiya's account of the changed Ryūen strengthens voluntary legitimacy.

Cross-class friendships and ordinary ties make it harder to treat rival polities as abstract enemy blocs.

Then V05 forces each polity to reveal what membership means under authored scarcity.

For Horikita, preserving Kushida over Airi proves independent political authorship and creates permanent moral remainder.

For Ryūen, Tokitō demonstrates that dissent can survive as a person while remaining unsafe as an institution.

For Ichinose, Kanzaki demonstrates that solidarity can suppress strategic criticism without formal coercion.

Sakayanagi-origin remains strongest at centralized ranking and allocation, but its own deeper correction problem has not yet reached terminal form.

V06 supplies the constructive answer most clearly in Horikita's polity: local actors can diagnose, consult, decide, and teach without routing every problem through the hidden strategist. The `補助輪` / training-wheels logic turns succession from abstraction into explicit developmental criterion.

## 4.4 Y2V07–Y2V09 — plural agency and loyal opposition become viable

Horikita's polity increasingly uses actors with motives the leader does not own. Kushida becomes a contributor for self-interested reasons rather than coerced gratitude. The Ayanokōji Group begins surviving by reconstitution rather than restoration. Sudō's development becomes lateral and peer-generative.

Ichinose's polity changes even more constitutionally. Ayanokōji helps create the possibility of a reform relation, then explicitly stops before owning it. Kanzaki and Himeno become a broader second-brain structure. By V09 the polity can conceal resilience and defeat Ryūen's sabotage without abandoning its solidaristic membership norm.

Ryūen's failure against that changed class becomes positive evidence because he updates the model.

The period's governing political lesson is:

> **a polity becomes stronger not when disagreement disappears, but when disagreement and surprise can enter the collective model without requiring exclusion.**

## 4.5 Y2V09.5–Y2V11 — ordinary motives, scarce protection, and recognition complicate governance

The late year makes non-class purposes politically relevant.

Desire, friendship, rivalry, ordinary embarrassment, and private attachment cannot be treated as noise outside the constitution because leaders act through people who possess those motives.

Y2V10's protection economy makes scarcity explicit: protection, institutional value, strategic usefulness, and relational importance are not the same category.

Sakayanagi's Kamuro decision is the sharpest negative case. An optimization model can rank contributions accurately enough for a contest while lacking the category that would have recognized irreplaceability before loss.

Ryūen's polity receives its own official internal diagnosis through Hiyori: leader-authored victories may inhibit the development of classmates' own `上積み`.

Y2V11 then separates recognition from jurisdiction. The strongest political systems must allow perception of another person's value to stop short of claiming ownership over the person's answer.

This becomes relevant to every polity: the problem is not merely seeing talent, but deciding what rights follow from seeing it.

## 4.6 Y2V12–Y2V12.5 — sovereignty, fiduciary failure, and succession become irreversible

The final Year-2 exam creates the strongest simultaneous stress test.

`POLITY-HORIKITA` wins, but the victory contains Ayanokōji's terminal hidden intervention and Maezono's expulsion. The result proves competitive capacity while exposing unresolved provenance and jurisdiction.

`POLITY-RYUEN` is defeated by Sakayanagi on the merits of their contest. When the official result reverses because Sakayanagi chooses to lose, Ryūen refuses to treat the scoreboard as the victory he sought. Leader development occurs without solving polity succession.

`POLITY-ICHINOSE` demonstrates enormous leader-centered competence: Ichinose defeats Horikita in the relevant contest, then is personally destabilized through Ayanokōji's use of trust. V12.5 turns the crisis into the strongest counter-authorship result of the year, but *Second List* still refuses to infer that the entire class has reproduced her strategic function.

`POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` reaches the decisive constitutional failure. Sakayanagi's inferred private commitment to Ayanokōji overrides fiduciary class interest. Her subsequent withdrawal creates point loss, despair, and a leadership vacuum with no internal successor. The class collectively mobilizes resources and consent to bring Ayanokōji in.

The former Sakayanagi class therefore ends the year with real collective agency **inside a succession failure**.

That distinction is one of the most important in the entire Year-2 political architecture.

## 4.7 Volume 0 — retrospective negative control on developmental sovereignty

At its mandated position, Volume 0 reframes the reader's understanding of authored development.

The White Room shows an extreme case in which institutional authorship aims to eliminate surplus purpose and subordinate development to externally selected ends.

None of the four class polities is equivalent to that institution.

But the comparison sharpens the political standard:

> **producing capable people is not sufficient evidence that the system which produced them was legitimate, nor that the produced people owe the system their purposes.**

That standard becomes especially relevant to Ayanokōji's hidden ecology and to any class leader tempted to treat another member's realized capacity as proof that earlier jurisdiction was justified.

## 4.8 `Second List` — documentary boundary audit

The official profiles do not simply celebrate outcomes.

They identify future tasks:

- Horikita: growth real; post-transfer reproduction unresolved;
- Ryūen: coordination real; sovereign dependence and academic base remain weaknesses;
- Ichinose: unity real; strategic distribution and point conversion remain unfinished;
- Sakayanagi-origin: collapse real; imported Ayanokōji is a catalyst, not an internally generated succession answer.

The documentary boundary therefore strengthens the succession reading rather than closing it.

# 5. `POLITY-HORIKITA` — constitutional developmental pluralism under a real succession test

`POLITY-HORIKITA` is the most dangerous polity to overstate because Year 2 supplies unusually strong evidence of development **and** deliberately stops before the decisive system-reproduction result.

Its progression is best described as:

> **heterogeneous capacity under shadow sovereignty → visible independent leadership → distributed local agency → morally answerable constitutional office → removal of the hidden guarantor at the boundary**

The polity's great strength is not that it possesses the strongest individual.

It is that more of its members become capable of doing politically useful things for reasons and through methods that no single leader fully owns.

The unresolved problem is whether this plural capacity can replace the terminal security formerly supplied by Ayanokōji.

## 5.1 Frozen Year-1 constitution — distributed promise, hidden sovereignty

Year 1 had already produced a broad portfolio:

- Horikita — visible academic/strategic leadership;
- Hirata — interpersonal legitimacy and anti-disposability pressure;
- Kei — peer-network coordination;
- Kushida — extraordinary social reach under a false public/private equilibrium;
- Sudō — rapidly developing physical and academic capacity;
- Ike and others — contextual competence not captured by scalar ranking;
- Kōenji — exceptional but self-sovereign ability;
- Ayanokōji — concealed synthesis, deterrence, contingency repair, and security.

The problem was not talent scarcity.

It was **constitutional attribution**.

The class increasingly looked like a distributed polity while its worst contingencies could still be silently solved by an actor outside the visible chain of command.

Year 2 makes Horikita's own authorship substantially more real before it makes the polity's independence from Ayanokōji testable.

## 5.2 V01–V02 — leadership begins treating obligation as bounded

The first-year pairing crisis demonstrates Horikita's growth from individual optimizer toward constitutional coordinator.

She does not simply rank students by current ability and allocate them downward.

She must:

- prevent expulsions;
- coordinate information;
- recognize that weaker students create shared risk without losing membership status;
- work through Hirata and classmates;
- negotiate across class/year boundaries;
- and admit that not every personal choice is hers to command.

The Kōenji bargain in V02 is especially important because it creates a model of collective benefit without total political ownership.

Horikita does not obtain a permanent title to Kōenji's excellence.

The bargain defines a bounded exchange. His extraordinary contribution can discharge an obligation rather than becoming evidence that the class may demand infinite future labor.

That constitutionalizes a principle that later specialists should preserve:

> **exceptional capacity creates bargaining power and possible obligation; it does not erase person-level jurisdiction.**

## 5.3 V03–V04 — plural operation does not yet eliminate hidden security

The island examination allows many classmates to operate in dispersed groups.

That distribution matters.

But the worst institutional threat remains outside Horikita's effective knowledge. Ayanokōji still handles Tsukishiro-level danger beyond the public class constitution.

This creates a distinction between:

- **ordinary operational independence**;
- and **terminal contingency independence**.

Horikita's polity increasingly possesses the first.

It has not yet demonstrated the second.

Kōenji's performance also shows the productive potential of self-sovereign members. He benefits the class precisely because the polity can make a bounded deal with someone it cannot absorb into ordinary loyalty.

The class therefore begins learning that constitutional strength can include actors who preserve refusal.

## 5.4 V05 — Horikita becomes sovereign enough to be blameworthy

The Unanimous Vote Examination is the point at which any residual reading of Horikita as Ayanokōji's proxy becomes untenable.

She rejects his preferred solution and preserves Kushida.

This is independent political authorship.

It is also a decision whose burden cannot be outsourced back to Ayanokōji merely because he executes the terminal expulsion route.

The constitutional facts must remain simultaneous:

1. the school authors a coercive unanimity/expulsion environment;
2. Horikita independently decides that Kushida's future value warrants preservation;
3. Kushida does not consent to being preserved as Horikita's developmental project;
4. Ayanokōji identifies and operationalizes Airi as the expendable alternative;
5. the class participates in an irreversible ranking under time pressure;
6. Airi bears the terminal cost;
7. later Kushida contribution cannot retroactively grant consent;
8. later Airi flourishing outside school cannot prove that expulsion was a valid total judgment of her worth.

This is constitutional maturity in an uncomfortable sense.

A polity becomes politically adult not only when it can make its own decisions, but when it can no longer disclaim responsibility for the casualties those decisions create.

## 5.5 V06–V09 — distributed agency becomes generative rather than merely delegated

V06 supplies the strongest constructive polity mechanism of the year.

Sudō encounters a local social problem, asks for bounded advice, thinks, and reaches his own answer. Ayanokōji's line—`オレは何もしてない。おまえが自分で考えて自分で答えを出しただけだ`—is important because the class-level result is not “Ayanokōji solved it invisibly.”

The local actor owns the answer.

The follow-up `そうやってクラスは回っていく` turns this into a political theory: a class becomes self-sustaining when problems route to capable local actors rather than one universal solver.

The `補助輪` metaphor makes removal of support the intended criterion.

Sudō then becomes more than a success story. Onodera teaches him an anger-regulation technique; he uses it; his academic discipline survives romantic disappointment; and he begins helping weaker classmates. Development becomes **transmissible**.

Kushida's trajectory is similarly important. Her false equilibrium collapses, but the underlying social capacity does not disappear. Cooperation increasingly rests on motives she can own rather than on coerced gratitude or maintenance of an impossible mask. Horikita can therefore use a politically difficult person without demanding ideological conversion as the price of membership.

This is why “distributed developmental polity” is more accurate than “democratic class.”

The class still contains hierarchy and severe coercive history.

Its distinctive strength is that heterogeneous motives and capacities can increasingly remain inside the operating system.

## 5.6 Horikita's office becomes answerable rather than omniscient

Across the middle and late year, Horikita increasingly governs by:

- explicit negotiation;
- bounded bargains;
- specialist input;
- visible responsibility;
- acceptance of contradiction;
- role differentiation;
- and willingness to revise.

Hirata can challenge the moral meaning of victory.

Sudō can challenge assumptions and ask whether another path existed.

Kushida can cooperate while preserving dislike.

Kōenji can refuse while remaining a class member.

Ryūen can be a contractual partner without being assimilated into friendship.

Ichinose can be respected while becoming a direct rival.

The constitutional development is not elimination of authority.

It is a stronger distinction between:

- leader jurisdiction;
- specialist jurisdiction;
- member autonomy;
- and collective obligation.

## 5.7 V12 — successful representation exposes the last shadow constitution

The final examination is the polity's most severe provenance test.

Ayanokōji can occupy representative authority, produce a winning outcome, and expel Maezono through a sequence whose broader purpose is withheld from the class.

The result therefore combines:

- formal exam authority;
- strategic competence;
- class benefit;
- hidden developmental purpose;
- and irreversible membership cost.

Hirata's later language approaching `越権行為` is constitutionally important because it expresses the limit the polity still has not fully institutionalized:

> **representative authority does not automatically include sovereign jurisdiction over membership.**

A class capable of winning through an exceptional representative is not necessarily a class capable of auditing what the representative did in its name.

## 5.8 V12.5 / Second List — reaching A begins rather than completes the succession test

The class reaches the A position at precisely the moment Ayanokōji leaves.

This timing prevents triumphalist closure.

`Y2SL-E027` recognizes extraordinary growth.

`Y2SL-E081` retrospectively frames Ayanokōji's late conduct as preparation for the class to continue after transfer.

`Y2SL-E082` explicitly leaves that continuation unresolved.

Kōenji is separately flagged as a future variable (`Y2SL-E028`).

The correct Year-2 boundary claim is therefore:

> **Horikita's polity has developed enough independent capacity that removing the hidden guarantor becomes a meaningful experiment. The corpus has not yet observed the experiment's result.**

## 5.9 Constitutional strengths at Y2SL

- broadest demonstrated portfolio of usable capacities;
- comparatively strong visible correction ecology;
- leader can own decisions without pretending omniscience;
- peer-level generativity exists;
- antagonistic or self-interested motives can remain politically usable;
- Kōenji establishes bounded obligation as a real constitutional limit;
- social infrastructure survives exposure and conflict;
- public leadership is genuinely Horikita-authored rather than proxy rule;
- members increasingly distinguish winning from legitimate jurisdiction.

## 5.10 Constitutional liabilities at Y2SL

- no observed post-transfer reproduction yet;
- historical dependence on Ayanokōji's hidden terminal security;
- incomplete provenance around V12 and the transfer timetable;
- V05 and V12 leave serious expulsion/moral remainder;
- Kōenji remains only contractually integrated;
- A-rank may create incentives to defend status rather than continue experimentation;
- several actors are independently capable, but strategic synthesis after Ayanokōji remains unproven.

## 5.11 Boundary judgment

`POLITY-HORIKITA` is the strongest **positive candidate** for a succession-capable developmental constitution.

It is not yet the proven winner of the succession test.

Its Year-2 success is better stated this way:

> **the class no longer needs Ayanokōji to be the obvious first-line answer to ordinary political life; the boundary finally asks whether it can also survive without him as the hidden last-line answer.**

# 6. `POLITY-RYUEN` — adaptive authoritarian legitimacy without protected counter-sovereignty

Ryūen's Year-2 development is easy to misread in opposite directions.

One error treats him as essentially unchanged: still a violent dictator whose followers merely obey.

The other treats delegation, follower loyalty, and contracts as evidence that authoritarianism has dissolved.

Neither survives the complete record.

The correct progression is:

> **fear-centered sovereign → defeated but voluntarily preserved ruler → recruiter/delegator → contract-capable adaptive sovereign → leader whose success increasingly requires a meaningful proving field**

while the central constitutional continuity remains:

> **ultimate political judgment is still Ryūen-centered, and internal dissent is not safely protected when it challenges sovereignty.**

## 6.1 Year-1 inheritance — defeat reveals legitimacy fear could not explain

The rooftop destroyed Ryūen's claim to irresistible personal supremacy.

It did not destroy the polity.

Ibuki, Ishizaki, Albert, and later other followers demonstrate that membership and loyalty continue after fear alone should have become insufficient.

Year 2 therefore begins with a mixed inheritance:

- coercion remains real;
- follower agency is also real;
- legitimacy has become sociologically thicker than the leader's own earlier theory of fear admits.

## 6.2 V01–V02 — strategy becomes institutional intelligence

Ryūen adapts rapidly to the OAA/partner market.

He understands not merely individual ability but:

- price expectations;
- scarcity;
- signaling;
- recruitment behavior;
- and the intelligence revealed by who bids for whom.

The Katsuragi transfer is the more important constitutional development.

Ryūen spends enormous resources to acquire a capability he does not personally reproduce:

- defensive judgment;
- rule literacy;
- administrative caution;
- institutional competence.

This is a meaningful advance beyond the political assumption that difference from the ruler is disloyalty.

Katsuragi's value lies partly in **not being Ryūen**.

Yet the transfer also clarifies the difference between acquiring expertise and generating it internally.

The polity becomes stronger through import.

Succession is not solved.

## 6.3 Delegation distributes execution, not sovereignty

Katsuragi can own bounded domains.

Ishizaki, Albert, Hiyori, Kaneda, Komiya, and other members retain differentiated functions.

Ryūen can leave exam work to others while attending to separate conflict.

This matters.

The polity is less operationally bottlenecked.

But the constitutional equation remains:

`distributed execution != distributed final judgment`

No actor yet possesses a recognized standing to overrule Ryūen on the polity's governing direction.

## 6.4 V04–V04.5 — leadership becomes systemic and voluntarily followable

The Hōsen confrontation demonstrates that Ryūen no longer reduces political victory to bodily dominance.

He can use:

- institutional timing;
- emergency response;
- follower preservation;
- delegated exam roles;
- and adversary miscalibration.

Hōsen may dominate the immediate physical field without owning the political meaning of the encounter.

V04.5 then gives the strongest follower-side legitimacy evidence. Komiya distinguishes the present Ryūen from the earlier ruler who treated classmates as disposable and regards the changed leader as someone worth following.

This evidence must be allowed to revise the polity model.

Voluntary loyalty is real.

It does not create a veto.

## 6.5 V05 / V11 — managed dissent is not constitutionalized dissent

Tokitō is the decisive test.

In V05 he can criticize Ryūen and argue that the leader should step down.

Ryūen identifies, threatens, and humiliates him, but does not immediately purge him. Katsuragi can read the resulting arrangement as leaving a route for continued membership.

This is a development from pure purge logic.

It is best classified as **managed dissent**.

V11 prevents overclaiming.

Tokitō's continuing opposition encounters physical coercion and threatened pursuit. Intervention from outside the polity can interrupt the immediate event, but the class itself still lacks a protected constitutional mechanism by which dissent can bind the sovereign.

The distinction is exact:

> **Ryūen's polity has learned to contain dissent socially before learning to protect dissent politically.**

## 6.6 Contracts improve external constitutional behavior faster than internal restraint

Horikita–Ryūen agreements prove that the polity can engage in bounded, credible cooperation without pretending affection or ideological convergence.

This is genuine institutional maturity.

A rival can be:

- adversary;
- contractual partner;
- information source;
- and future opponent

at the same time.

But the strongest contractual protections operate externally.

Internally, the dissenter does not receive an equivalent rule-governed shield from the ruler.

That asymmetry is constitutionally diagnostic.

## 6.7 Failure can update the sovereign without empowering the polity

Ryūen's sabotage of Ichinose's class fails in V09.

His response is politically strong: he updates rather than defending a falsified model for pride.

This gives the polity excellent **leader-level epistemic corrigibility**.

It does not yet give the polity **institutional corrigibility**.

A system that depends on the ruler being smart enough to revise still depends on the ruler.

## 6.8 Hiyori identifies the developmental bottleneck

Hiyori's late-Year-2 diagnosis is one of the strongest internal critiques in the class.

The problem is not simply that Ryūen is harsh.

It is that repeated leader-authored victories can prevent classmates from acquiring their own durable `上積み`.

`Second List` confirms this as an official future-task field:

- `Y2SL-E034` records Hiyori's concern about excessive Ryūen-dependence;
- `Y2SL-E035` credits improved coordination and Katsuragi while warning that dependence on Ryūen is increasing.

The success of the leader can therefore create the failure of succession.

That is the polity's deepest Year-2 paradox.

## 6.9 Academic weakness exposes the cost of compensatory sovereignty

`Y2SL-E036` records a weak basic academic layer in cooperative/written contexts.

This matters politically.

A sovereign strategist can repeatedly invent tactics that compensate for broad portfolio weakness.

If those tactics work, the polity may become **more dependent on sovereignty because sovereignty successfully hides the need for distributed development**.

This is the authoritarian counterpart to Horikita's training-wheels problem.

## 6.10 V12–V12.5 — meaningful victory raises the leader's standard without solving succession

Ryūen loses to Sakayanagi on the merits of the final contest.

The official outcome changes because Sakayanagi voluntarily throws the result for a private reason connected to Ayanokōji.

Ryūen refuses to equate official victory with the victory he wanted.

This is real leader development.

His political identity increasingly seeks a result that authenticates his own developed capacity rather than any outcome he can exploit.

But the class remains strategically organized around him.

A better sovereign is still a sovereign.

## 6.11 Constitutional strengths at Y2SL

- rapid coordination;
- sophisticated adversarial intelligence;
- willingness to recruit complementary expertise;
- real delegation of bounded domains;
- credible contracts and external diplomacy;
- voluntary follower legitimacy beyond fear;
- strong leader-level model updating;
- improved member preservation relative to early Year 1;
- capacity to distinguish meaningful victory from scoreboard victory.

## 6.12 Constitutional liabilities at Y2SL

- dissent is not safely protected;
- physical coercion remains available against internal challenge;
- strategic sovereignty remains highly concentrated;
- Hiyori's critique lacks binding authority;
- weak academic base increases compensatory dependence on Ryūen;
- delegation does not reproduce the leader's strategic function;
- succession remains weak;
- voluntary legitimacy may stabilize authoritarianism rather than limit it.

## 6.13 Boundary judgment

`POLITY-RYUEN` becomes a **better authoritarian polity**, not a non-authoritarian one.

Its deepest Year-2 question is no longer whether followers possess agency.

They plainly do.

It is:

> **Can follower agency become counter-sovereignty—an internal capacity able to limit, replace, or survive Ryūen—or will agency merely make his sovereignty more effective?**

# 7. `POLITY-ICHINOSE` — solidaristic pluralism and the unfinished second brain

The Ichinose polity undergoes the most important correction to a simplistic “kindness versus strength” reading.

Year 2 demonstrates that solidarity can learn:

- secrecy;
- adversarial inference;
- internal criticism;
- deterrence;
- strategic hardness;
- and refusal

without making culling or fear the condition of seriousness.

Its progression is:

> **high-trust solidarism → conformity pressure exposed → loyal opposition legalized socially → second-brain pluralization → adversarial competence → leader-level counter-authorship with polity-level reproduction unfinished**

## 7.1 Frozen Year-1 constitution — trust is infrastructure, not innocence

Year 1 establishes genuine political capital:

- shared private points;
- broad mutual support;
- voluntary disclosure;
- survival through scandal;
- resistance to member sacrifice;
- exceptional sociological legitimacy around Ichinose.

Its weakness is not that people care about one another.

It is that norms designed for reciprocal actors can be exploited by actors who do not reciprocate, and that social capital does not automatically convert into enough institutional points or distributed strategic initiative.

## 7.2 V05 — conformity can exist without coercive punishment

Kanzaki sees `正常性バイアス` and the danger of treating agreement as proof of safety.

His dissent is strategically serious.

Yet one dissenter cannot convert analysis into policy because the social system associates “good member” too strongly with affirmation of the collective.

This is different from Ryūen's polity.

The cost of dissent is not primarily fear of bodily retaliation.

It is the softer pressure of threatening a morally valued cohesion.

That does not make the pressure unreal.

The class's great social asset can therefore become a constitutional blind spot.

## 7.3 V07 — constitutional reform begins when the outsider stops owning it

Ayanokōji helps connect Kanzaki and Himeno.

The decisive point is his stopping rule: `ここから先は第三者であるオレが立ち入る領域じゃない。`

If he remained the hidden designer of the reform, Ichinose's polity would merely acquire another external shadow constitution.

By stopping, he allows reform to become a class-owned relation.

This is one of the cleanest Year-2 positive cases of environmental authorship terminating before political authorship is appropriated.

## 7.4 V09 — opposition becomes a second brain

The reform structure expands beyond Kanzaki.

Himeno, Watanabe, Amikura, Hamaguchi, and others make disagreement plural.

The important achievement is not unanimity among reformers.

It is the opposite:

> **criticism becomes compatible with belonging.**

The class can now contain multiple strategic interpretations without construing disagreement as betrayal of Ichinose or of solidarity itself.

That is constitutional pluralization.

## 7.5 Strategic opacity develops without fear becoming the governing norm

The same period shows the class defeating Ryūen's sabotage.

The polity can conceal resilience and allow an opponent to operate on stale assumptions.

This corrects the Year-1 vulnerability in which transparent good faith could become an adversarial attack surface.

Strategic opacity therefore need not mean internal deception as a governing principle.

A polity can preserve high internal trust while refusing to make all information available to rivals.

## 7.6 Ichinose's personal assertiveness changes the constitution without replacing it

Ichinose's desire and relational vulnerability remain politically relevant.

But later Year 2 no longer treats desire only as liability.

It becomes:

- motivation;
- information;
- capacity for boundary setting;
- and eventually part of her refusal to accept a developmental binary authored by Ayanokōji.

The polity therefore does not become stronger by purging warmth from its leader.

It becomes stronger as warmth ceases to monopolize the available political language.

## 7.7 V12 — class protection and leader violation diverge

Ayanokōji's final-exam bargain can materially protect Ichinose's classmates from traitor-expulsion risk and yield class benefit while simultaneously exploiting Ichinose's trust as a personal attack surface.

This distinction matters for class politics:

> **collective protection does not automatically legitimate the method used against the representative person.**

A polity can receive a beneficial outcome from an intervention that remains autonomy-defective toward its leader.

`Y2_08` owns the full ethical judgment, but `Y2_05` must preserve the constitutional divergence.

## 7.8 V12.5 — the leader counter-authors before the polity fully reproduces

After the V12 collapse, the class initially returns to its familiar strength and weakness: consolation around Ichinose.

Himeno sees the danger.

Kanzaki is exhausted.

Hoshinomiya introduces adult rescue fantasies that could re-externalize political agency.

Ichinose's return changes the field.

She refuses the binary Ayanokōji prepared, produces a third answer, recognizes the reform dissent, sets harder boundaries, and refuses to promise what she cannot guarantee.

This is genuine leader-level counter-authorship.

But `Second List` prevents the stronger polity claim:

- `Y2SL-E041` confirms extraordinary unity, no expulsions, and weak point conversion while noting that reform remains unfinished;
- `Y2SL-E042` explicitly says the class needs more individual capacity beyond dependence on Ichinose's strategy;
- `Y2SL-E083` strengthens the claim that Ichinose herself exceeds Ayanokōji's prediction.

Thus:

> **the leader becomes counter-author before the polity becomes fully counter-authoring.**

## 7.9 Membership protection is a constitutional principle, not merely sentiment

Among the four polities, Ichinose's most distinctive doctrine is that current performance does not automatically determine entitlement to remain.

The class repeatedly resists culling.

That has costs:

- resources can be consumed protecting members;
- difficult capacity problems can be deferred;
- strategic disagreement can feel disloyal;
- point conversion can lag.

But the norm also supplies political strengths:

- high trust;
- mutual insurance;
- willingness to expose vulnerability;
- resilience after scandal;
- and a stable baseline against treating weaker people as disposable inputs.

Year 2's achievement is not abandoning that doctrine.

It is beginning to build **adversarial competence around it**.

## 7.10 Constitutional strengths at Y2SL

- strongest membership-protection norm;
- exceptional sociological legitimacy and mutual support;
- no-expulsion continuity;
- loyal opposition increasingly compatible with belonging;
- strategic opacity and adversarial response now available;
- leader capable of counter-authorship and harder boundary setting;
- reform network broader than one dissenter;
- class can remain solidaristic without equating solidarity with total transparency.

## 7.11 Constitutional liabilities at Y2SL

- weak conversion of social capital into class points;
- opposition is legitimate before it is fully generative;
- Ichinose remains unusually central as affective and strategic node;
- members may still suppress criticism to protect cohesion;
- adult actors can project rescue/grievance politics into the class;
- leader-level counter-authorship does not prove distributed polity-level strategy;
- the class has not yet shown how its anti-culling norm handles every future scarcity condition.

## 7.12 Boundary judgment

`POLITY-ICHINOSE` ends Year 2 as the strongest proof that **solidarity and political hardness are not opposites**.

Its unresolved question is reproductive:

> **Can criticism, strategic opacity, and independent initiative become common enough that Ichinose ceases to be the class's irreplaceable political nervous system without the polity abandoning its membership ethic?**

# 8. `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` — aristocratic optimization and the negative succession control

The former Sakayanagi polity is the most important negative control in Year-2 constitutional analysis.

Its collapse cannot be explained by lack of talent.

That is precisely why the case matters.

The polity begins with:

- elite personnel;
- high strategic intelligence;
- sophisticated role allocation;
- institutional confidence;
- long-standing positional advantage.

It ends with:

- the sovereign's private priority overriding class interest;
- severe point loss;
- despair;
- no internal successor;
- and collective importation of another exceptional strategic center.

The governing lesson is:

> **a polity can be extraordinarily good at allocating talent while being extraordinarily bad at reproducing the authority that allocates it.**

## 8.1 Frozen Year-1 constitution — the sovereign is also the interpretive system

Sakayanagi is not merely the strongest visible leader.

She functions as:

- primary interpreter;
- strategic planner;
- allocator;
- arbiter of relative value;
- and political identity of the class.

Year 1 already raised the core fiduciary question:

> **What constrains the sovereign when her private priority diverges from the polity's interest?**

Year 2 answers by demonstrating how weak the constraint actually is.

## 8.2 Katsuragi's exit removes an alternative institutional logic

Ryūen's recruitment of Katsuragi strengthens Ryūen's polity and weakens Sakayanagi-origin in a specifically constitutional way.

The loss is not merely a competent student.

Katsuragi represents:

- caution;
- rule literacy;
- defensive institutional reasoning;
- and a rival theory of elite leadership.

His transfer reduces the class's stock of internally legitimate alternative judgment.

The sovereign remains strategically formidable while the constitution becomes less plural.

## 8.3 Functional legibility exceeds relational legibility

Kamuro and Yamamura expose the polity's deepest interpretive asymmetry.

Sakayanagi can understand:

- task suitability;
- relative performance;
- strategic reliability;
- and role allocation

with great precision.

She is less reliable at registering when a person has become nonfungible through relationship.

That distinction is politically consequential because a class member can be correctly evaluated **as a resource** while being mis-evaluated **as a person whose relation to the leader changes what loss means**.

## 8.4 Hashimoto — corrective concern without a trusted constitutional channel

Hashimoto identifies a real problem: Sakayanagi may prioritize Ayanokōji over Class-A graduation.

His response remains his responsibility.

He treats betrayal as insurance and creates severe harm.

But his behavior also exposes a structural failure.

The polity lacks a trusted route through which a broker can transform fiduciary concern into binding internal correction before betrayal becomes his chosen instrument.

The interface is catastrophic:

`sovereign with weak answerable dissent + broker who treats trust as collateral`

A good constitution cannot rely on every critic being morally superior to the system that failed to hear him.

It must create channels that make correction possible before treachery becomes rationalized as the only hedge.

## 8.5 V10 — Kamuro's expulsion proves optimization can discover value too late

Kamuro is not the top institutional performer.

Sakayanagi allows a process that treats vulnerable candidates as sufficiently substitutable.

Only after loss does a category become undeniable:

**friendship / irreplaceability**.

The constitutional failure is not simply sadness.

It is model incompleteness with irreversible consequence.

A ranking system can be internally coherent and still omit the variable that should have changed the decision.

This becomes a class-politics version of the broader corpus principle:

> **the file is evidence about the person; the file is not the person.**

## 8.6 V11 — recognition can be opened from outside without becoming institutionalized inside

Yamamura's problem creates a chance for relational correction.

Ayanokōji helps open the encounter and then stops before claiming jurisdiction over its outcome.

The opportunity matters.

So does its provenance.

An outsider had to make the internal relation visible enough to receive repair.

The polity therefore gains a corrective event without yet demonstrating a self-generated corrective institution.

## 8.7 V12 — private relation overrides fiduciary class interest

The year-end contest supplies the decisive answer to the Year-1 fiduciary question.

Sakayanagi is winning on the merits of the contest.

Ayanokōji does not issue a direct command.

He constructs an interpretive route through which she can infer that he wants Ryūen preserved.

She understands the preference and voluntarily chooses to satisfy it.

Her agency is real.

The class-wide externality is also real.

The constitutional judgment is therefore:

> **the polity has no effective internal mechanism capable of preventing the sovereign's private relation from becoming sovereign over fiduciary duty.**

This is not reducible to selfishness.

It is a structural failure of constraint.

## 8.8 V12.5 — sovereign exit produces the clearest succession failure

Sakayanagi withdraws.

The class loses substantial points.

Members face despair and leadership vacuum.

No internal actor reproduces her interpretive function.

This is the strongest available proof that:

`deep talent pool != succession constitution`

The class can contain many excellent executors and still lack a mechanism for selecting, legitimating, and reproducing a new strategic center.

`Y2SL-E046` explicitly confirms the collapse/no-successor boundary.

## 8.9 Collective transfer financing is polity agency inside re-centralization

The class is not inert after Sakayanagi leaves.

V12.5 establishes:

- the 20-million-point transfer is completed;
- resources are collectively mobilized;
- students consent to and welcome Ayanokōji;
- the competitive field is reopened.

These facts matter.

They are evidence of political agency.

But the type of agency must be named correctly.

The polity responds to a leader vacuum by importing another exceptional strategic intelligence.

That is:

> **collectively authorized re-centralization.**

It may prove strategically effective later.

At Y2SL, it does not count as internally reproduced succession.

## 8.10 Constitutional strengths at Y2SL

- elite personnel remain available;
- members can coordinate resources under crisis;
- history of sophisticated role differentiation persists;
- collective transfer consent is real rather than purely imposed;
- late relational corrections create possibilities for less instrumental internal relations;
- the class can recognize a strategic vacancy and act to fill it.

## 8.11 Constitutional liabilities at Y2SL

- no internal successor to Sakayanagi;
- weak trusted dissent before betrayal;
- private sovereign priority imposed class-wide cost;
- relational value historically under-modeled;
- strategic interpretation over-concentrated in one person;
- transfer solution may reproduce exceptional-center dependence;
- collective consent occurs under severe scarcity and leader vacuum;
- the polity has not yet shown that the incoming center can be corrected or constitutionally bounded.

## 8.12 Boundary judgment

`POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` is the clearest Year-2 negative succession case.

Its failure is not “Sakayanagi was not talented enough.”

It is nearly the inverse:

> **the sovereign was so constitutively central that the polity never developed enough internal machinery to convert its many talented members into a replacement political center.**

# 9. Four-class ecology — plurality without ownership

Late Year 2 makes Ayanokōji's four-class developmental project explicit enough that it must be included in a class-politics synthesis.

It must also be carefully bounded.

Ayanokōji increasingly wants all four polities to remain viable enough to generate strong competition, development, and eventually an opponent capable of producing an answer he cannot supply.

There are defensible political values inside that ecology:

- preventing one class from becoming permanently dominant;
- preserving alternative leadership systems long enough to develop;
- maintaining multiple theories of merit, membership, coordination, and rivalry;
- increasing the chance that independent strong actors emerge;
- refusing to reduce the school to a single optimized constitutional model.

But there is a fundamental legitimacy problem.

The four polities do not collectively appoint Ayanokōji as designer of their equilibrium.

He can therefore be simultaneously:

- insightful about why plurality matters;
- effective at preserving it;
- and unauthorized to impose the costs by which he preserves it.

Maezono, Ichinose, Kei, Sakayanagi, and entire class point balances can become variables in a developmental field whose purpose they did not select.

The correct principle is:

> **plurality of constitutions is valuable without granting the observer ownership of the plurality.**

This matters especially at the terminal transfer.

Ayanokōji removes himself from Horikita's polity partly so that its independent development can be tested, while entering the former Sakayanagi polity as a new strategic center.

He relinquishes one constitutional position and assumes another.

The ecology therefore remains ethically and politically unresolved at Y2SL.

## 9.1 Ayanokōji is a cross-polity causal actor, not a neutral referee

He has relationships, loyalties, desires, rivalries, and developmental hypotheses inside the system.

His position cannot be treated as external objectivity.

He:

- trains and tests Horikita's polity;
- preserves Ryūen as a rival;
- destabilizes and later receives counter-authorship from Ichinose;
- influences Sakayanagi's terminal choice;
- and transfers into the resulting leadership vacuum.

Any full account of Year-2 class politics must therefore audit **provenance** whenever a class outcome aligns suspiciously well with his ecology.

## 9.2 The strongest answer to the ecology is counter-authorship

If a polity merely develops exactly as Ayanokōji expects, its competence can remain difficult to distinguish from successful external authorship.

The stronger evidence is when the polity or leader changes the problem.

Ichinose's third answer is the clearest current case.

Horikita's independent V05 judgment is another, though not an ethically innocent one.

Ryūen's changing standard for meaningful victory makes him less reducible to the role of “useful rival preserved for Ayanokōji.”

The former Sakayanagi polity's collective consent to transfer is real agency but remains structurally entangled with a vacancy Ayanokōji can exploit.

# 10. Leadership concentration — four different monopolies

The four classes do not simply vary in “how centralized” they are.

They concentrate **different political functions**.

## 10.1 `POLITY-HORIKITA` — distributed visible portfolio, hidden terminal concentration

Visible leadership becomes the most plural of the four systems.

Horikita does not need to own:

- every social relation;
- every peer dispute;
- every academic intervention;
- every negotiation;
- every motivational structure;
- or every act of refusal.

But Ayanokōji remains unusually concentrated at the point of terminal contingency and hidden causal design until the boundary.

Thus the polity's concentration risk is not obvious everyday micromanagement.

It is **last-resort sovereignty**.

## 10.2 `POLITY-RYUEN` — distributed expertise, concentrated final judgment

Ryūen can delegate extensively.

He can value Katsuragi precisely because Katsuragi knows things he does not.

But ultimate strategy, coercive judgment, and political direction remain concentrated.

The polity's concentration risk is therefore **visible sovereign judgment**.

## 10.3 `POLITY-ICHINOSE` — distributed social capital, concentrated affective-strategic center

The class contains broad mutual trust and an increasingly plural reform structure.

But Ichinose remains unusually central because she supplies:

- symbolic cohesion;
- interpersonal trust;
- public legitimacy;
- much of the strategic synthesis;
- and much of the class's emotional orientation.

The concentration risk is therefore **centrality through legitimacy and solidarity**, not fear or secrecy.

## 10.4 `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` — concentrated interpretation

Sakayanagi historically does too much of the work of deciding what events mean.

Other members can perform roles.

The sovereign supplies the model that tells the polity how those roles fit together.

The concentration risk is therefore **interpretive sovereignty**.

When she leaves, the polity loses not just a leader but a meaning-making function.

## 10.5 Comparative rule

The relevant question is not merely:

> How many people perform tasks?

It is:

> **How many people can generate, contest, and legitimate the interpretation from which tasks are assigned?**

By that standard, Year 2's apparent decentralization becomes much easier to audit.

# 11. Dissent, correction, and loyal opposition

Year 2 makes dissent one of the clearest differentiators among the four constitutions.

| Polity | Dominant dissent form | Safety | Ability to change policy | Boundary judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `POLITY-HORIKITA` | explicit disagreement, refusal, hostile cooperation, specialist correction | comparatively high | yes | strongest visible correction ecology |
| `POLITY-RYUEN` | managed dissent under sovereign discretion | low / conditional | sometimes | dissent exists but lacks protected standing |
| `POLITY-ICHINOSE` | loyal opposition / second brain | increasing | increasingly yes | major constitutional development |
| `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` | broker dissent, private grievance, alternative elite judgment | weak as trusted route | often too late | corrective trust failure |

## 11.1 Horikita — correction becomes compatible with authority

Horikita's strongest Year-2 leadership development is not that she becomes harder to challenge.

It is that challenges do not automatically strip her of authority.

She can be:

- contradicted;
- corrected;
- asked for reasons;
- forced to bargain with refusal;
- and made to confront moral remainder

without the polity collapsing into a succession crisis.

This is a strong sign that office is becoming larger than personal infallibility.

The qualification is V12: the class still lacks full provenance over Ayanokōji's terminal intervention.

## 11.2 Ryūen — dissent becomes socially survivable before politically safe

Tokitō can remain in the class after challenging the ruler.

That is meaningful development.

But V11 demonstrates that the dissenter does not possess a protected right against coercive enforcement.

Ryūen decides how much dissent the polity can tolerate.

Thus dissent may inform the sovereign without becoming a constitutional limit on sovereignty.

## 11.3 Ichinose — criticism becomes a form of loyalty

This is the largest Year-2 improvement in the Ichinose polity.

Kanzaki begins as an isolated dissenter worried that solidarity has become a normalcy bias.

The reform structure later expands.

The key change is normative:

> **one can criticize the class's inherited operating system and still count as someone acting for the class.**

This creates a second brain without requiring a second sovereign.

Its remaining weakness is practical scale: legitimacy of dissent develops faster than its capacity to generate enough independent strategic action.

## 11.4 Sakayanagi-origin — when correction becomes betrayal

Katsuragi's earlier opposition, Hashimoto's insurance politics, Kamuro's irreplaceability, and Yamamura's under-recognition reveal different failures of correction.

The polity often learns important information only when:

- an opponent has already exited;
- a broker has already betrayed;
- a friend has already been expelled;
- or an outsider has already staged the conversation.

This is not absence of intelligent members.

It is absence of a strong enough **trusted route from subordinate knowledge to sovereign constraint**.

## 11.5 Dissent as a pre-irreversibility technology

The strongest constitutional value of dissent is temporal.

Correction matters most **before**:

- expulsion;
- betrayal;
- transfer;
- irreversible resource commitment;
- or leader exit.

A polity that “learns” after the cost is paid may become wiser without becoming reliably corrigible.

# 12. Weak members, culling, and the politics of developmental time

The four polities differ most sharply when current weakness becomes expensive.

## 12.1 Horikita — developmental optimism with episodic disposability

Horikita increasingly understands ability as trajectory.

Sudō is the clearest success.

Kushida becomes an even harder case because Horikita preserves a person whose present behavior is politically destructive on the belief that future social capacity can be recovered.

But V05 exposes the cost of developmental jurisdiction.

Preserving one future can require sacrificing another person under the exam's authored scarcity.

V12 repeats the tension from a different route through Maezono.

The constitution therefore contains a genuine contradiction:

> **people are treated as developable until a sufficiently powerful strategist or scarcity mechanism decides that one person's removal is useful enough.**

The existence of development does not erase the need for limits on who may terminate another person's trajectory.

## 12.2 Ryūen — weakness remains strategically priced

Ryūen becomes less casually disposable toward followers.

He preserves more differentiated capacities and appears increasingly capable of loyalty toward people useful to the polity.

But the governing norm remains instrumental.

Weakness is not protected as a status in itself.

The polity asks what a person can contribute under command, contract, pressure, or future development.

This can be strategically realistic without becoming a rights-based membership doctrine.

## 12.3 Ichinose — membership before optimization

Ichinose's polity offers the strongest alternative.

The class repeatedly refuses to make institutional weakness the first argument for expulsion.

This creates high costs when collective resources are finite.

It also preserves something the other constitutions can lose:

> **membership is not merely a temporary license issued by the current contribution model.**

Year 2's important development is that the class begins adding strategic hardness **around** this principle rather than treating the principle itself as childishness to be discarded.

## 12.4 Sakayanagi-origin — optimization can confuse fungibility with value

The polity is strongest at ranking.

Kamuro demonstrates the failure mode.

The question “who contributes less?” can be answered without answering “whose loss changes the constitution in ways the ranking did not model?”

Optimization therefore creates a specific danger:

> **if the system's categories omit relational or developmental value, precision inside the categories can make irreversible error more confident rather than less likely.**

## 12.5 No class gets to infer personhood from survival value

Airi's post-school future, Kamuro's relational importance, Kōenji's elective participation, and the guidebook's own model limits all block the same inference:

> **institutional survival value is evidence about a student's position in a contest, not a total judgment of the student.**

That rule belongs centrally to `Y2_06` and `Y2_08`, but it is also a constitutional limit on class politics.

# 13. Information architecture, provenance, and shadow sovereignty

The four polities can be compared as information regimes.

## 13.1 Horikita — increasingly distributed knowledge, incomplete meta-knowledge

More classmates know and own useful domains.

Horikita herself becomes capable of integrating multiple information sources without demanding total central control.

But the polity still lacks full information about:

- Ayanokōji's developmental timetable;
- the purpose of his transfer;
- the full rationale behind some terminal interventions;
- and the extent to which visible victories were secured by hidden contingency control.

The key deficit is therefore **meta-knowledge**: the polity does not always know who authored the environment in which it acted.

## 13.2 Ryūen — centralized adversarial intelligence with legible authorship

Ryūen's polity has the opposite advantage and problem.

Members usually know where strategic sovereignty resides.

Authorship is relatively legible.

The cost is centralization.

The system may be easier to attribute and harder to constrain.

## 13.3 Ichinose — high-trust circulation learns selective opacity

The class's information architecture begins from unusually low internal transaction costs.

Members trust one another enough to share resources and vulnerability.

Year 2 adds a new capacity: not all politically useful information needs to be transparent to adversaries, and not all internal disagreement threatens the social order.

The reform coalition makes the information system more plural.

## 13.4 Sakayanagi-origin — sovereign map, controlled disclosure, weak correction

Information quality near Sakayanagi is often excellent.

The problem is not ignorance.

It is that the sovereign interpretation can become the last interpretation.

Hashimoto's conduct shows how brokers may respond when they do not trust the system to incorporate their concern.

Kamuro and Yamamura show how humanly important information may never enter the strategic model in time.

## 13.5 Provenance is a constitutional right problem

Year 2 increasingly suggests that good political order requires not merely accurate decisions but **auditable authorship**.

Members should be able to ask:

- Who made this decision?
- Under what authority?
- On whose information?
- With what hidden purpose?
- Who can contest it?
- When can contestation occur?
- Who owns the record after the fact?

This is why Ayanokōji's hidden successes are constitutionally ambiguous even when beneficial.

It is also why Tsukishiro/Shiba's record power sits above and threatens every student constitution.

# 14. Legitimacy — why no polity wins every category

A scalar “best leader” ranking is analytically inferior to a legitimacy profile.

## 14.1 Procedural legitimacy

Horikita increasingly operates through recognized class office, negotiation, contracts, and student-council office.

Ichinose's leadership also possesses strong recognized standing.

Ryūen often operates through de facto sovereign acceptance more than protected procedure.

Sakayanagi historically combines formal class leadership with an extraordinary degree of substantive discretion.

But procedural validity does not settle ethical legitimacy.

## 14.2 Sociological legitimacy

Ichinose remains strongest in broad voluntary social acceptance.

Ryūen's Year-2 development greatly increases his sociological legitimacy relative to the early fear regime.

Horikita's legitimacy grows as visible authorship, competence, and accountability grow.

Sakayanagi possesses strong elite authority but loses the polity at the point where private priority and succession failure become decisive.

## 14.3 Epistemic legitimacy

Sakayanagi and Ryūen often possess excellent strategic information.

Ayanokōji exceeds all four visible leaders in many hidden domains.

Horikita increasingly compensates for not knowing everything by using distributed expertise.

Ichinose's class increasingly develops a plural information system rather than relying solely on leader intuition.

The Year-2 lesson is that **epistemic superiority creates reasons to listen, not automatic jurisdiction to rule every domain**.

## 14.4 Ethical legitimacy

This is where effectiveness most sharply diverges from standing.

- Horikita's V05 choice is independently authored and morally burdened.
- Ryūen's coercive tools remain ethically problematic despite follower loyalty.
- Ichinose's anti-culling norm is ethically important even when competitively costly.
- Sakayanagi's private preference creates fiduciary failure despite strategic sophistication.
- Ayanokōji's hidden ecology repeatedly possesses knowledge without collective authorization.

`Y2_08` owns the full moral theory. Here the constitutional point is simpler:

> **a polity becomes dangerous when one kind of legitimacy is allowed to masquerade as all the others.**

## 14.5 Performance legitimacy

Winning can create real political support.

Repeated success demonstrates competence.

But Year 2 supplies multiple reasons performance cannot stand alone:

- Horikita's final victory contains hidden Ayanokōji authorship;
- Ryūen receives an official result he does not regard as the meaningful win;
- Ichinose's class can be socially robust while low in points;
- Sakayanagi can be winning on merit and still destroy fiduciary legitimacy through her chosen loss.

Performance is one source of legitimacy.

It is not the constitution.

# 15. Delegation, generativity, and reproduction

This is the central comparative section of the Year-2 polity analysis.

## 15.1 Delegation asks who can execute

Ryūen improves greatly here.

Horikita also delegates broadly.

Ichinose's class possesses distributed social work even before strategic reform.

Sakayanagi historically assigns roles with high sophistication.

Delegation alone therefore cannot explain constitutional maturity.

## 15.2 Generativity asks who can create more capacity

The strongest positive evidence appears in `POLITY-HORIKITA`:

- Onodera teaches Sudō;
- Sudō internalizes and later becomes capable of helping others;
- Kushida's social expertise survives identity collapse and becomes self-owned;
- local problems increasingly receive local answers;
- Horikita can recruit contributions without demanding ideological sameness.

`POLITY-ICHINOSE` shows emerging generativity:

- Kanzaki's critique becomes a plural reform network;
- dissent creates more dissent rather than merely one opposition figure;
- Ichinose produces an answer outside Ayanokōji's model.

`POLITY-RYUEN` remains mixed:

- Katsuragi and followers can own domains;
- Hiyori can diagnose;
- but the sovereign's strategic function does not clearly reproduce.

`POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` provides the negative control:

- role competence is plentiful;
- successor authorship is not.

## 15.3 Reproduction asks whether the constitution survives center removal

At Y2SL:

- Horikita: **unproven but genuinely testable**;
- Ryūen: **weak evidence**;
- Ichinose: **partial / second-brain structure incomplete**;
- Sakayanagi-origin: **internally failed at the boundary**.

This is deliberately not a rank table of overall merit.

It is a succession state table.

## 15.4 A constitution succeeds when it can create authors, not merely agents

An agent can perform a role.

An author can decide what the role is for.

Year 2's strongest distributed-development signal is therefore not “more people become useful.”

It is:

> **more people become capable of defining problems, generating answers, teaching others, refusing inherited purposes, and forcing the center to update.**

# 16. Contracts, alliances, recruitment, and transfer

## 16.1 Horikita–Ryūen — bounded cooperation under preserved antagonism

The two polities can enter contracts without pretending merger.

This is constitutionally useful because it separates:

- trust in the other person's character;

Mature politics can therefore occur between rivals who retain incompatible goals.

## 16.2 Ichinose and rival classes — respect without strategic transparency

Ichinose's Year-2 development shows that maintaining respect does not require revealing every vulnerability.

The polity becomes capable of selective opacity while preserving its underlying relational ethic.

## 16.3 Ryūen recruits rather than reproduces Katsuragi's expertise

The Katsuragi transfer increases capacity.

It should be counted as real political skill.

But imports must not be confused with internal development.

A polity that repeatedly buys missing constitutional functions may become stronger while remaining reproductively dependent.

## 16.4 Former Sakayanagi class imports a sovereign function

Ayanokōji's transfer is the terminal version of the same distinction.

The class recognizes a strategic vacancy and mobilizes collective resources to fill it.

That is agency.

It is also evidence that the vacancy could not be filled internally at the boundary.

## 16.5 Transfer creates a new polity state without erasing lineage

The stable identifier remains `POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` because longitudinal analysis needs to preserve the constitution that generated the transfer condition.

The incoming center may radically transform the polity later.

That later constitution cannot be projected backward into Y2SL.

# 17. Resource governance — money, protection, and the affordability of political principles

Detailed point mechanics belong to `Y2_06`, but class politics cannot ignore resources because values become politically meaningful when they must be funded.

## 17.1 Private points make constitutional preferences actionable

Private points can purchase:

- insurance;
- protection;
- transfer;
- cooperation;
- rescue;
- or strategic flexibility.

A polity's theory of mercy or mobility is therefore constrained by fiscal capacity.

## 17.2 Ichinose — mutual insurance

The polity's shared-resource norm turns solidarity into material infrastructure.

Its risk is sustainability under repeated scarcity.

## 17.3 Ryūen — strategic capital

Ryūen is willing to spend dramatically to acquire Katsuragi because collective resources can buy a missing political function.

The spending decision reflects a constitution comfortable treating capital as strategic transformation.

## 17.4 Horikita — bargaining rather than simple ownership

Kōenji's contract demonstrates that contribution can be priced through bounded obligation.

The polity also increasingly uses protection and points inside broader negotiated structures rather than a single redistributive doctrine.

## 17.5 Sakayanagi-origin — capital cannot replace succession

The terminal 20-million-point transfer proves the class can aggregate enormous resources around a political choice.

That does not make money a substitute for internal institutional reproduction.

Resources can import a solution.

They cannot prove the polity learned how to produce the solution itself.

# 18. Merit, measurement, and the constitutional meaning of `実力`

`Y2_06` will own the full ability/measurement synthesis, but `Y2_05` must establish the political consequence of Year 2's measurement regime.

OAA and special exams do not simply discover a neutral thing called ability.

They help determine which abilities become:

- visible;
- marketable;
- protectable;
- recruitable;
- expensive;
- targetable;
- or politically ignorable.

The four polities answer the resulting meritocratic problem differently.

## 18.1 Horikita — merit as developable and relational

The polity increasingly treats current ability as incomplete evidence about future contribution.

Its strongest examples are developmental.

Its strongest contradictions are expulsions where future possibility is terminated under scarcity or strategic judgment.

## 18.2 Ryūen — merit as strategic effect

Formal scores matter less than whether a person can alter the field.

This is why Katsuragi's institutional competence and Hiyori's diagnostic intelligence matter beyond scalar OAA.

It is also why Ryūen himself can possess low “social contribution” while exercising enormous social causality.

## 18.3 Ichinose — merit does not determine membership

The polity most explicitly refuses to make current comparative ability equivalent to entitlement to remain.

The unresolved task is converting that norm into enough distributed performance to survive the school's competitive system.

## 18.4 Sakayanagi-origin — merit as hierarchical allocation

The polity is strongest at ranking and allocation.

Year 2 demonstrates that ranking can still omit categories that matter to political legitimacy and relational stability.

## 18.5 Constitutional principle

> **The important political question is not whether unequal ability exists. It is who gets to translate a measurement of ability into a claim over another person's membership, obligation, risk, or future.**

# 19. Institutional ceiling — the fifth constitutional layer above the classes

The four classes do not govern a sovereign territory.

They operate inside ANHS.

That institution writes:

- exam rules;
- point systems;
- transfer prices;
- protection mechanisms;
- reporting channels;
- student-council powers;
- and the official records through which performance becomes consequence.

`Y2_07` owns the full institutional synthesis, but one polity-level fact must remain visible here:

> **internal constitutional quality cannot guarantee external procedural justice.**

## 19.1 Y2V03–V04 — record sovereignty can dominate class competence

Tsukishiro/Shiba demonstrate the extreme case.

A polity may correctly observe or infer an event and still fail to make that knowledge institutionally actionable if authorities above it can manipulate provenance.

This creates a distinction between:

- internal epistemic competence;
- and procedural sovereignty over the record.

No class constitution solves the second problem alone.

## 19.2 Student council is a constitutional laboratory, not a fifth class

Nagumo and later Horikita demonstrate competing theories of institutional office.

The student council can:

- shape opportunities;
- distribute benefits;
- create patronage;
- and alter the practical meaning of school rules.

But its political structure should not be merged into the four class constitutions.

It is an overlapping governance layer that can condition all four.

## 19.3 Teachers can carry private interests into formal office

Hoshinomiya's late-Year-2 behavior is especially relevant to Ichinose's polity.

A class's internal constitution can be affected by an adult whose personal history and grievances shape intervention.

Office therefore does not eliminate motive.

This is one reason the polity must distinguish assistance from capture.

## 19.4 The institution is not a single mind

Year 2 repeatedly shows conflict among:

- teachers;
- administrators;
- student-council actors;
- external political interests;
- and procedural channels.

“ANHS wants X” is often too coarse.

The four polities operate under a layered state whose offices can disagree and whose records can become political terrain.

# 20. Ethics and jurisdiction — constitutional implications without replacing `Y2_08`

This specialist does not issue the final ethical verdict on every expulsion or intervention.

It must nevertheless preserve the political distinction that makes those verdicts possible:

> **competence does not create unlimited jurisdiction.**

## 20.1 Horikita — correct developmental diagnosis can still externalize cost

Kushida's later usefulness may strengthen Horikita's diagnosis of latent value.

It does not retroactively make Airi's sacrifice consensual.

A constitution must therefore distinguish:

- being right about someone's future capacity;
- from being entitled to make others pay for preserving that future.

## 20.2 Ryūen — follower legitimacy does not legitimate every coercive act

People can genuinely choose to follow a ruler whose practices remain coercive.

Sociological legitimacy and ethical legitimacy therefore remain separate.

## 20.3 Ichinose — anti-culling norms can be politically costly without being politically childish

The polity's refusal to treat weak members as obvious expendables is a constitutional choice.

Its competitive costs are evidence relevant to evaluating the choice, not automatic refutation of it.

## 20.4 Sakayanagi — strategic superiority does not erase fiduciary obligation

A leader can understand the contest better than classmates and still violate the obligation owed to them when a private relationship overrides collective interest.

## 20.5 Ayanokōji — ecology without authorization

Preserving four viable polities may create real developmental value.

That value does not grant unilateral authority to impose the experiment's costs on individuals and classes.

The political problem is therefore not “was the model intelligent?”

It is:

> **who authorized the model to become the environment?**

The second brain creates alternative thought, but Ichinose remains the primary strategic/affective center.

# 25. Counterarguments and anti-simplification rules

## 25.1 “Horikita's class is democratic.”

Too strong.

The polity is plural, increasingly correctable, and unusually capable of preserving differentiated roles.

It also contains:

- leadership hierarchy;
- hidden Ayanokōji intervention history;
- institutionally forced scarcity;
- severe expulsions;
- bounded rather than universal consent;
- and an uncompleted succession test.

Use **constitutional developmental pluralism**, not “democracy,” unless a narrower context explicitly warrants comparison.

## 25.2 “Horikita's class is secretly an Ayanokōji dictatorship.”

Also too strong by the Y2SL boundary.

Ayanokōji remains a major hidden author, but Horikita can produce binding answers against his preference, classmates increasingly own domains, and the polity's visible constitution has real independent causal force.

The accurate problem is **historical hidden sovereignty plus unproven system reproduction**, not total proxy rule.

## 25.3 “Ryūen's class is a dictatorship, therefore followers are passive.”

False.

Followers possess real motives, differentiated competence, and voluntary loyalty.

The unresolved problem is not follower agency.

It is whether follower agency has enough protected standing to constrain or replace the sovereign.

## 25.4 “Ryūen became a good leader, so coercion no longer defines the polity.”

False.

Year 2 strengthens leadership legitimacy and competence while preserving coercive tools and unsafe dissent.

Development is not redemption.

## 25.5 “Ichinose's class loses because kindness is weak.”

Too crude.

The polity's problem is:

- point conversion;
- strategic distribution;
- conformity pressure;
- and central dependence on Ichinose.

Year 2 directly demonstrates that solidarity can acquire adversarial competence without becoming fear-based or culling-centered.

## 25.6 “Ichinose's second brain means the class is now decentralized.”

Premature.

Opposition becomes legitimate and plural.

`Second List` still says the class needs greater individual capacity beyond Ichinose's strategy.

## 25.7 “Sakayanagi's class fails because Sakayanagi is selfish.”

Insufficient.

Her private priority is decisive, but the deeper political failure is that no trusted corrective/succession mechanism can absorb sovereign error or exit.

A constitution that depends on the sovereign never having a conflicting private priority is fragile by design.

## 25.8 “Ayanokōji transfers in, so the former Sakayanagi class is fixed.”

Unsupported at Y2SL.

The transfer restores strategic possibility.

It also re-centers the polity around another exceptional actor.

Whether the result becomes more constitutional is future evidence.

## 25.9 “Class A is the best class.”

Category error.

Class A is an institutional ranking.

This specialist evaluates political organization.

## 25.10 “No coercion is the definition of good leadership.”

Insufficient.

A non-coercive leader can still fail through:

- paralysis;
- information neglect;
- inability to protect members;
- conformity pressure;
- or refusal to make necessary decisions.

The stronger standard combines:

- effectiveness;
- accountability;
- correction;
- developmental humility;
- bounded jurisdiction;
- and succession resilience.

# 27. Reconstruction constraints for class-polity modeling

This specialist is analytical evidence, not a script generator. When modeling a class in a hypothetical scenario, preserve the following constraints.

## 27.1 Model the polity, not only the leader

A scenario involving “Horikita's class” cannot be reduced to “what would Horikita do?”

Likewise for Ryūen, Ichinose, or Sakayanagi-origin.

The relevant causal network includes:

- specialists;
- dissenters;
- social mediators;
- hidden information;
- resource constraints;
- and institutional rules.

## 27.2 Ask which domain is at issue

A polity may be decentralized socially and centralized strategically.

It may be plural in ordinary life and sovereign under crisis.

It may be good at external contracts and poor at internal dissent.

Do not assign one global “centralization score” to every scenario.

## 27.3 Preserve actor-relative information

Do not give Horikita the full Ayanokōji timetable before Y2SL.

Do not give Ryūen's followers institutional protection they do not have.

Do not assume Ichinose's entire class knows every reform concern at the same level.

Do not let former Sakayanagi-class members automatically understand the full significance of their succession failure from an omniscient narrator's perspective.

## 27.4 Respect abstention on post-transfer outcomes

At the Y2SL boundary, do not state as established fact:

- that Horikita's polity succeeds or fails after Ayanokōji leaves;
- that Ayanokōji's new polity becomes superior;
- that Ryūen develops an internal veto;
- that Ichinose's second brain becomes fully generative;
- or that the former Sakayanagi polity learns to constrain its new center.

Those are future branches, not current facts.

## 27.5 Generate behavior from constitutional incentives

A faithful polity simulation should ask:

- who has standing to decide?;
- who possesses the information?;
- what dissent is safe?;
- what resources are available?;
- which member is considered replaceable or protected?;
- what external contracts exist?;
- who is indispensable?;
- and whether the proposed action strengthens or weakens succession.

That produces more faithful collective behavior than attaching adjectives such as “smart,” “kind,” “authoritarian,” or “elite” to the class.

# 30. Canonical Year-2 constitutional synthesis

Year 2 begins with four leadership systems.

It ends with four succession problems.

The difference is fundamental.

A leadership system asks:

> **Who can make this class act?**

A succession constitution asks:

> **Can the class continue producing judgment when that person no longer makes it act?**

`POLITY-HORIKITA` gives the strongest positive evidence for distributed developmental capacity. It increasingly converts heterogeneous people into local authors rather than merely instruments. Horikita's own judgment and institutional authorship become unmistakably hers. Yet the class enters its true succession test only when Ayanokōji leaves. Its Year-2 achievement is not proof of post-guarantor independence. It is that independence has become a serious empirical possibility.

`POLITY-RYUEN` demonstrates that authoritarian rule can become more intelligent, more legitimate, more contractual, more differentiated, and less casually disposable without ceasing to be authoritarian at the point of final judgment. Followers are agents, not puppets. The unresolved constitutional failure is that their agency still lacks enough protected standing to limit or reproduce the sovereign.

`POLITY-ICHINOSE` demonstrates that solidarity can become politically harder without betraying itself. Loyal opposition, strategic opacity, and adversarial competence can develop inside a membership-protective constitution. Ichinose herself reaches powerful counter-authorship. The class's next unresolved task is to turn that leader-level development into broadly reproducible strategic agency.

`POLITY-SAKAYANAGI-ORIGIN` demonstrates the catastrophic limit of elite centralization. The polity possesses many capable people yet cannot reproduce the sovereign function after Sakayanagi leaves. Its collective response—financing Ayanokōji's transfer—is real agency, but the chosen solution is another exceptional center. The class therefore proves that talent depth and succession depth are different things.

Across all four, Year 2 rejects three equations:

`leader competence = constitutional competence`

`victory = development`

`consent to a new center = internal succession`

It replaces them with harder tests:

- Can disagreement survive membership?
- Can lower-status information alter policy before irreversible cost?
- Can specialists own domains without the sovereign owning them?
- Can development generate new developers?
- Can the polity tell who authored the result?
- Can exceptional capacity remain bounded by jurisdiction?
- Can the class reproduce judgment when the center disappears?
- Can the developed polity produce an answer the developer did not own?

The strongest final formulation is therefore:

> **Year 2 transforms class politics from a contest among leaders into a contest among theories of political reproduction. The decisive resource is no longer simply superior strategy, solidarity, coercive coordination, or elite optimization. It is the capacity to make judgment portable without making persons fungible: to distribute expertise without erasing refusal, preserve dissent without dissolving coordination, protect members without disabling adaptation, and survive exceptional leaders without turning exceptional leaders into the constitution itself.**

The year does not show one class fully solving that problem.

That is not analytical incompleteness.

It is the correct Y2SL boundary.
