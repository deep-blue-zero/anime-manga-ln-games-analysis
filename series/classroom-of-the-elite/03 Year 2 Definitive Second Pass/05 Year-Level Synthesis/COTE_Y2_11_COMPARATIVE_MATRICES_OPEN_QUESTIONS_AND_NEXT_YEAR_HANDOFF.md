# Year 2 — comparative matrices and the boundary into Year 3

## 1. Responsibility and thesis

This is the principal analytical handoff from the completed `Y2SL` boundary into a **later** Year-3 execution. It consolidates six architecture-required matrix families and preserves the questions the next year must test. The operational release gate remains in the current entrypoint and delivery audit. This document's existence does not open the gate, and it contains no Year-3 answer.

Year 2 establishes that development can be real inside conditions another actor authored. It also establishes that development does not by itself legitimate those conditions. The comparison therefore separates **capacity, ownership, and answerability**. A person may grow more capable while becoming more dependent; a leader may become personally more corrigible while failing a fiduciary duty; an institution may generate plural purposes while retaining coercive control. Those are distinct results, not inconvenient exceptions to be averaged away.

The matrices compress canonical interpretations (`SI`/`IT`), with event facts (`TF`) and reported motives (`CI`) retained through the linked primary homes. Normative assessments are judgments (`VJ`), not disguised measurements. “Unproven” means the observed boundary lacks the required test; it does not mean impossible. No row ranks total human worth or supplies a single strength score.

## 10. Strongest counterargument: does the autonomy test undervalue beneficial coordination?

The strongest objection is that the author/subject distinction can overstate domination. People knowingly rely on teachers, partners, leaders and experts; nobody chooses all the conditions under which growth occurs. Kiyotaka repeatedly supplies real protection and opportunity, and other characters do not merely execute his instructions. It would be false to infer that every asymmetry destroys agency or that only fully informed, equally powerful partners can have meaningful relations.

The Year-2 answer accepts that objection's force. The matrices preserve beneficial assistance, local stopping rules, independent decisions, meaningful attachments and genuine developmental outcomes. They do not require freedom from every influence. Their stricter claim concerns **jurisdiction**: the power to determine another person's ends, information timetable and permitted answer should not follow automatically from superior understanding or a beneficial result. The decisive comparisons ask whether dependence remains revisable, whether refusal has standing, and whether the stronger actor can be corrected. Unequal capacity and answerability can coexist; Year 2 demonstrates some bounded forms without proving general completion.

This also limits the opposite argument. A response Kiyotaka did not predict is evidence against total model ownership, but it need not alter his values or make him emotionally reciprocal. Counter-authorship and the author's development require separate observations.
