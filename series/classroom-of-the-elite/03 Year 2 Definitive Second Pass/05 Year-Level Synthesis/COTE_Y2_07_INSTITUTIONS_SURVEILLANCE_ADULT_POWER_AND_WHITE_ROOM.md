# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 2 — Institutions, Surveillance, Adult Power, and the White Room
### Legibility without equal transparency: captured records, political permeability, counterforce, and the contest over who may author a student's future

> **Canonical specialist synthesis at the complete Year-2 / Second List boundary.**
>
> This document is the primary Year-2 home for ANHS governance, surveillance, record sovereignty, rule provenance, student-council jurisdiction, teachers and administrators, administrative capture, independent witness architecture, national political permeability, White Room institutional genealogy, Atsuomi's external authorship, and the question of whether ANHS can remain a genuine developmental alternative once its own political entanglement is known. It synthesizes the frozen Year-1 institution specialist, the canonical through-Year-2 institutional-rules ledger, the mandated Volume-0 retrospective-revelation ledger, immutable source-local readings, and the completed `Y2_01–Y2_06` specialist layer. It does not replace those source-local authorities and imports no Year-3 narrative evidence.

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

Year 1 established that Advanced Nurturing High School is neither an innocent school nor a simple second White Room.

It is a plural experimental institution whose rules can create development, opportunity, dependence, scarcity, disposability, privacy, mobility, and political leverage at the same time.

Year 2 makes the institutional problem more difficult in two ways.

First, ANHS becomes **more legible from below**.

Students are increasingly:

- measured through OAA;
- searchable across year groups;
- tracked through GPS and digital devices;
- ranked through scores and class points;
- allocated scarce protection;
- made economically mobile through private points;
- observed through guidebook files;
- and exposed to special examinations whose formal logic is increasingly explicit.

Second, the institution becomes **less cleanly self-contained from above**.

The reader learns that:

- Tsukishiro can insert a hidden twenty-million-point expulsion incentive into the student environment;
- Nagumo can lend student-council legitimacy to that parallel incentive while building a school-wide patronage network;
- an acting administrator can transform part of a legitimate examination into a private retrieval zone;
- teachers may carry old personal grievances into present professional decisions;
- national political actors can affect examination design;
- Volume 0 places both the White Room and ANHS inside the same wider political struggle;
- and the official archive can become richer without becoming omniscient.

The canonical Year-2 institutional thesis is therefore:

> **ANHS increases legibility downward faster than transparency upward. The governed become easier to measure, classify, locate, price, protect, sanction, and move while the actors capable of changing the environment, exploiting the record, or politically repurposing the institution can remain comparatively opaque.**

The resulting question is not merely whether a rule is written fairly.

It is:

> **Who authored the rule, who knew the operative rule, who could enforce it, who controlled the record, who could contest that record, who bore irreversible cost, and what independent counterforce existed before the cost became final?**

This specialist uses the following controls throughout.

## 0.1 ANHS is not one actor

Do not write `ANHS wants X` when the source establishes only that:

- a teacher wants X;
- Nagumo wants X;
- Tsukishiro wants X;
- a formal examination requires X;
- Chairman Sakayanagi permits X;
- an external political visitor causes X to be reconsidered;
- or a student class decides X inside a school-created rule environment.

Institutional analysis requires actor and jurisdiction.

## 0.2 Formal rule, implementation, enforcement, record, and authorship are different layers

The canonical Year-2 sequence is:

`rule text -> information distribution -> implementation -> enforcement -> record -> review -> political authorship`

A process can be fair at one layer and compromised at another.

## 0.3 Surveillance is not omniscience

GPS can locate devices without proving authorship.

OAA can report institutional observations without exhausting capacity.

A camera can record an event while framing and interpretation remain contested.

A dossier can be detailed while its compiler, source, and purpose remain opaque.

The correct question is always what the surveillance object actually proves.

## 0.4 Record integrity and event truth are distinct

Always distinguish:

1. what happened;
2. what evidence exists;
3. who has access to the evidence;
4. who is credible;
5. what can be institutionally enforced;
6. what becomes the official record;
7. what the reader later learns retrospectively.

Tsukishiro makes this distinction indispensable.

## 0.5 Office is a resource, not proof of neutral motive

Teacher, president, acting chairman, chairman, and political office grant jurisdictional capacity.

They do not automatically grant:

- impartiality;
- benevolence;
- complete information;
- or legitimate authority over every outcome the office can technically produce.

## 0.6 Counterforce is a positive institutional capacity

Independent witnesses, rival authorities, appeal routes, procedural triggers, public reasons, protected dissent, and record provenance are not signs that a good institution has somehow failed to be harmonious.

They are mechanisms by which concentrated power becomes answerable.

## 0.7 Stopping power and truth-forcing power are different

V04 proves that counterweights can stop Tsukishiro's private operation.

It does not prove that ANHS can compel a truthful administrative adjudication afterward.

This distinction remains active at Y2SL.

## 0.8 Formal freedom and practical access are different

A right may be genuine while practically unavailable because of:

- twenty-million-point cost;
- information asymmetry;
- patron control;
- class dependence;
- time pressure;
- or scarcity.

## 0.9 Secrecy is ethically and politically double-edged

Anonymous voting can protect individual will.

The same anonymity can obscure responsibility and make collective coercion difficult to diagnose.

Hidden information can protect a target from retaliation or create a parallel rule world invisible to the governed.

## 0.10 Volume 0 changes reader genealogy, not local Year-2 knowledge

Volume 0 establishes political origins and adult planning that most Year-2 students do not know.

Do not back-project that knowledge into Horikita, Kei, Ryūen, Ichinose, Hiyori, Chabashira, or other actors.

## 0.11 Authorship of access is not ownership of outcome

Volume 0 may show that adults anticipated, enabled, or partly prepared the institutional doorway through which Kiyotaka reaches ANHS.

That does not make later relationships, purposes, class constitutions, or counter-authored choices products they own.

## 0.12 Institutional comparison is normative, not geographical

After Volume 0, `White Room = inside / ANHS = outside` is inadequate.

The stronger comparison concerns:

- plurality;
- dissent;
- meaningful exit;
- unscripted relation;
- independent evidence;
- contestable authority;
- ordinary life;
- and whether participants can create purposes not exhausted by the institution's model.

## 0.13 This document does not absorb the neighboring specialists

- Exact ability/score interpretation belongs primarily in `Y2_06`.
- Four-class constitutions belong primarily in `Y2_05`.
- Relationship dependency and counter-authorship belong primarily in `Y2_04`.
- Normative intervention/violence judgments belong primarily in `Y2_08`.
- Retrospective-revision mechanics belong primarily in `Y2_10`.

`Y2_07` owns the **institutional powers and infrastructures that make those questions possible**.

## 0.14 Year-3 lock

No Year-3 narrative consequence is admissible here.

Year-2 transfer, political, White Room, and succession facts are held at the `Y2SL` boundary only.

# 1. Executive thesis — Year 2 turns institutional power into a problem of asymmetric visibility and counterforce

Year 1 discovered hidden rules.

Year 2 discovers that even when the rules become more visible, **the authors of the environment may remain less visible than the people being evaluated inside it**.

The school publishes OAA.

Students can search one another.

Ability becomes legible across cohorts.

At the same time, one incoming student has received a confidential dossier on the entire second-year cohort and a hidden mission from Tsukishiro.

The official examination produces one set of incentives.

The covert bounty produces another.

The school has therefore increased transparency about students while permitting a decisive opacity about power.

The island examination escalates the pattern.

The environment is saturated with GPS, watches, tablets, rankings, designated areas, and recorded movement.

Yet a powerful administrator can still attempt to create a low-observation extraction zone within that instrumented environment.

The response is revealing.

Ayanokōji does not solve the institutional problem merely by being stronger than Tsukishiro and Shiba.

He uses the formal rescue system to create a **provenance chain** that brings independent school personnel toward I2 for reasons they can verify themselves.

The operation ends when the private environment can no longer remain private.

Tsukishiro can still insist afterward that nothing happened.

Thus Year 2 establishes both the value and the limit of internal counterforce:

> **Counterforce can prevent concentrated power from completing an action before it can necessarily force concentrated power to confess the truth of the action.**

That becomes the institutional problem in miniature.

ANHS is not protected because its rulers are always good.

It is protected when no one ruler can make every relevant fact, procedure, witness, and future disappear into one private account.

Volume 0 then widens the frame.

The White Room is not revealed as a neutral educational laboratory later corrupted by politics.

Politics is constitutive from the beginning.

Naoe's factional struggle with Kijima, elite funding, hidden children, patron networks, research competition, Atsuomi's ambition, and Tsukishiro's operational role make the project a political developmental institution from inception.

ANHS is also politically situated.

Kijima's standing includes the school.

Atsuomi and Tsukishiro are interested in Kiyotaka's placement there.

By V12.5, elite political observation directly affects examination design.

The reader therefore loses the comforting geography in which Kiyotaka simply escapes from a political institution into a neutral school.

The Year-2 distinction becomes constitutional:

> **The White Room seeks to concentrate environment, purpose, measurement, record, and future under one developmental sovereign. ANHS remains dangerous precisely because it shares some of those technologies, but it remains a genuine alternative insofar as authority is divided, relationships are unscripted, records can be contested, exits and transfers exist, dissent can survive, and people can generate purposes the institution did not assign.**

The strongest institutional concept at the boundary is therefore **counterforce**.

Counterforce includes:

- independent provenance;
- multiple adult authorities;
- student-council succession;
- dissent inside a class or office;
- privileges that can be used against the person who granted them;
- witness architecture;
- procedural triggers that survive administrator preference;
- and persons whose futures do not disappear when an institutional file stops recording them.

A trustworthy institution is not one that never generates conflict.

It is one in which conflict can become **auditable before power makes itself irreversible**.

# 2. Frozen Year-1 baseline and Year-2 revision

The Year-1 specialist established several propositions that remain canonical.

## 2.1 Preserved Year-1 propositions

1. **ANHS is a plural experimental school, not a neutral container.**
2. **The school is not one coherent actor.**
3. **Surveillance can deter, document, mislead, invade, or be captured.**
4. **Administrative record control can outrank displayed performance.**
5. **Private points are political infrastructure.**
6. **Expulsion mechanisms encode a theory of disposability.**
7. **Teachers are political actors whose private histories may affect present authority.**
8. **Student government can approach rule authorship.**
9. **The White Room's effectiveness sharpens rather than resolves the problem of ownership.**
10. **Ayanokōji carries institutional habits beyond physical escape.**
11. **Ordinary life is evidence of purposes not exhausted by formal curriculum.**
12. **ANHS's strongest difference from the White Room is plurality, not innocence.**

## 2.2 Year 2 strengthens the baseline

Year 2 adds:

- public OAA legibility;
- parallel hidden incentives inside public exams;
- large-scale telemetry;
- a school-wide Nagumo patronage network;
- the strongest direct case of administrative capture at I2;
- a working model of independent witness/provenance resistance;
- a secret-ballot examination that protects will while manufacturing a collective deadlock;
- student-council succession from Nagumo to Horikita;
- teacher conflict of interest made explicit through Hoshinomiya;
- class transfer enacted as institutional reconstitution rather than theoretical possibility;
- direct national-political influence on school examination design;
- and Volume 0's revelation that ANHS and the White Room belong to an entangled political genealogy.

## 2.3 The Year-1 binary is revised, not inverted

The Year-1 specialist rejected both:

`ANHS is simply the opposite of the White Room`

and:

`ANHS is merely another White Room`.

Year 2 strengthens both rejections.

Volume 0 makes political independence an impossible criterion for calling ANHS an alternative.

V04, V05, V09, V12, and V12.5 make institutional innocence equally impossible.

The boundary test becomes:

> **Can the institution preserve plural authorship and meaningful counterforce even when its rules, records, offices, and political sponsors create strong incentives toward concentration?**

# 3. The six-layer Year-2 institutional model

The canonical through-Y2 institutional ledger uses five institutional layers. This specialist retains them and adds a sixth analytical layer for retrospective provenance.

## 3.1 Layer 1 — formal public rule

What students are officially told:

- OAA fields;
- exam conditions;
- scoring;
- penalties;
- secret-ballot rules;
- Protection Point effects;
- class-transfer prices;
- student-council offices;
- rescue mechanisms.

Formal rules matter.

Students repeatedly reason from them successfully.

## 3.2 Layer 2 — operational implementation

A right or rule becomes real through:

- who has information;
- who can afford access;
- which students can coordinate;
- how leaders allocate scarce protection;
- whether a report is filed;
- whether a dissenter can remain anonymous;
- whether a class can aggregate twenty million points.

Formal equality can therefore coexist with practical inequality.

## 3.3 Layer 3 — enforcement

Who can make the rule bite:

- automated scoring;
- teachers;
- administrators;
- student officers;
- class votes;
- witnesses;
- economic penalties;
- expulsion procedures.

Enforcement is distinct from legitimacy.

## 3.4 Layer 4 — record

What becomes institutionally real after the event:

- GPS logs;
- official scores;
- ballot totals;
- OAA snapshots;
- expulsion records;
- transfer records;
- teacher reports;
- administrator statements;
- official guidebook files.

Record can be accurate and incomplete.

Record can also be captured.

## 3.5 Layer 5 — discretionary political authority

Actors who can alter the environment without necessarily changing public rule text:

- student-council presidents;
- teachers;
- acting administrators;
- chairmen;
- politically connected adults.

This layer includes agenda, appointments, mobility access, information, emergency intervention, interpretation, and selective enforcement.

## 3.6 Layer 6 — external and retrospective authorship

Volume 0 and V12.5 require an outer layer:

- Naoe factional politics;
- Kijima's government-backed ANHS project;
- Atsuomi's White Room counter-project;
- elite capital;
- political observation;
- adult planning of Kiyotaka's future environment.

This layer changes the reader's institutional genealogy.

It does not automatically determine the local choices produced inside the school.

# 4. Year-2 institutional progression

## 4.1 Y2V01 — the governed become visible while the hidden rule remains invisible

Y2V01 creates the Year-2 institutional paradox in its purest form.

OAA exposes student profiles across classes and year groups (`Y2V01-E007–E013`).

The school openly acknowledges that its evaluations are fallible (`Y2V01-E013`).

Historical and current observations can remain separate rather than overwriting one another (`Y2V01-E011`).

This is a meaningful institutional improvement in legibility.

But before the ordinary students encounter this public system, an unidentified White Room student has already been given detailed second-year data by Tsukishiro (`Y2V01-E001–E003`).

Later the covert twenty-million-private-point reward is revealed (`Y2V01-E054`).

The public exam and hidden bounty therefore coexist.

The formal rule says:

> pair across cohorts and survive the examination.

The parallel rule says:

> produce Ayanokōji's expulsion for extraordinary private reward.

The institutional issue is not merely cheating.

It is **asymmetric rule membership**.

Participants who appear to inhabit the same examination may actually inhabit different incentive environments.

The local endpoint correctly refuses to identify every suspicious first-year as White Room-origin.

That refusal is itself institutional discipline:

> **The existence of a hidden actor does not license total suspicion toward every high-capacity or unusually informed student.**

## 4.2 Y2V02–Y2V04 — telemetry expands while causation remains contestable

The island exam creates one of the densest surveillance environments in the series.

Students can be tracked through devices, GPS searches, locations, rankings, designated areas, and communication infrastructure.

Yet V04 repeatedly demonstrates that:

`location != authorship`

`device record != causal proof`

`presence != guilt`

Ayanokōji refuses to treat Amasawa's presence plus broken-GPS evidence as sufficient proof of assault (`Y2V04-E014`).

Amasawa refuses a GPS shortcut because it could be bait for false attribution (`Y2V04-E025`).

Longitudinal GPS comparison does reveal a coordinated Nagumo support structure (`Y2V04-E029–E031`).

The same surveillance technology can therefore be:

- highly useful;
- strategically manipulated;
- causally incomplete;
- and politically transformative.

## 4.3 Nagumo — institutional opportunity becomes patronage

The island exam exposes the mature form of Nagumo's school-wide project.

Lower third-year classes cooperate partly because ordinary progression is effectively closed and Nagumo controls extraordinary mobility routes (`Y2V04-E030`, `E061`, `E138`).

This is not false mobility.

Nagumo genuinely expands options.

But the options are routed through a personal allocator.

The correct institutional judgment is:

> **A right becomes patronage when practical access depends on one discretionary intermediary.**

Nagumo's temporary incapacitation then demonstrates the structural liability of centralized coordination (`Y2V04-E142–E143`).

The network's power is impressive.

Its single-point dependence is equally real.

## 4.4 Tsukishiro's I2 operation — administrative capture inside a valid exam

The I2 confrontation is Year 2's most important institutional stress test.

Ayanokōji infers that the low-observation location and surrounding mechanics were selected to force him into a privately manageable confrontation zone (`Y2V04-E162`).

Tsukishiro demands voluntary withdrawal and extraction while Shiba participates (`Y2V04-E163–E169`).

The acting administrator possesses more than physical force.

He possesses:

- office;
- information;
- infrastructure access;
- staff cooperation;
- ability to alter conditions;
- and the expectation that the event can remain institutionally unreal afterward.

This is **administrative capture**:

> a legitimate institutional shell contains a privately authored operation whose objective is not the public objective of the institution.

## 4.5 V04's countermeasure — independent provenance

The most institutionally important part of Ayanokōji's response is not combat.

He routes a rescue report through Sakayanagi and the transceiver structure so that genuine missing/broken-watch conditions create an independently interpretable reason for school personnel to approach I2 (`Y2V04-E176–E178`).

Once outside observers are expected, Tsukishiro's environment ceases to be privately governable (`Y2V04-E179`).

This is the canonical Year-2 **counter-provenance** model:

> **When hostile authority can control the immediate record, durable resistance requires a fact to travel through a procedure that creates an independent witness or provenance chain.**

The result should not be romanticized.

Tsukishiro subsequently asserts that nothing happened and treats teacher resistance as weak (`Y2V04-E183`).

Thus:

`intervention capacity > truth-forcing capacity`

The institution contains counterweights.

It has not demonstrated an infallible administrative accountability mechanism.

## 4.6 Y2V05 — perfect counting does not make a decision politically complete

The Unanimous Vote Special Examination is an institutional paradox.

It protects individual voting privacy unusually strongly.

Votes are secret and only totals are revealed (`Y2V05-E024`).

Contracts and private-point payments binding a vote are prohibited (`Y2V05-E026`).

Chabashira explicitly says the system prioritizes individual will (`Y2V05-E050`).

At the same time, the class must transform private wills into unanimous collective output under time pressure.

The final question offers class points in exchange for expelling a classmate (`Y2V05-E062`).

The system can therefore count preference correctly while forcing preference into a coercive collective structure.

This produces a canonical Year-2 distinction:

> **Procedural accuracy is not political completeness.**

Anonymous voting protects the dissenter from direct identification.

It can also prevent the class from knowing who is imposing the veto.

Privacy and opacity are produced by the same mechanism.

## 4.7 Chabashira — institutional afterlife

V05 also reveals that school decisions outlive the moment in which the institution formally closes them.

Chabashira's old unanimous-vote crisis remains psychologically and professionally active years later (`Y2V05-E001–E008`, `E046–E048`).

Her student history becomes teacher policy.

The institutional lesson is broader than her individual psychology:

> **An institution can end a case administratively while the people formed by that case continue carrying it into later offices.**

This is one reason post-decision accountability cannot be reduced to whether the rule was followed correctly at the time.

## 4.8 Y2V06–Y2V08 — what the institution does not fully regulate matters

These volumes provide a useful institutional negative control.

Formal rules structure class points, exams, mobility, and school membership.

They do not fully determine:

- friendship repair;
- resentment;
- ordinary travel;
- health;
- leisure;
- social reintegration;
- or the meaning people assign to an expulsion after the institutional act is complete.

The governance blind spot is productive.

It allows development not already formatted as institutional output.

## 4.9 Volume 0 — the institutional ontology changes

Volume 0 changes the reader's understanding of both institutions.

ANHS is introduced as a government-backed Kijima achievement (`V00-E008`).

Naoe launches the project that becomes the White Room inside factional political conflict (`V00-E009`).

Elite capital, secret children, researchers, patron networks, and political ambition become constitutive infrastructure (`V00-E016–E021`, `E033–E036`).

The White Room is therefore not neutral education later captured by politics.

It is political before it is educational.

Atsuomi's later decisions intensify that structure:

- outcomes outrank humane process (`V00-E044–E046`);
- legitimate nonparticipation is absent (`V00-E050`);
- Kiyotaka becomes political proof and succession material (`V00-E061`, `E162–E163`);
- White Room personnel are imagined across national institutions (`V00-E147`).

At the same time, Chairman Sakayanagi accepts ANHS leadership and distances himself from Atsuomi (`V00-E146`).

The two institutions therefore occupy an entangled political field rather than sealed moral universes.

## 4.10 Y2V09 — student-council succession becomes constitutional experiment

Nagumo accelerates succession and Horikita enters the presidency as a politically distinct actor (`Y2V09-E021–E026`).

Ichinose can resign from student-council office without abandoning her class project (`Y2V09-E027–E032`).

Horikita then seeks Kushida's voluntary participation rather than treating ability as conscriptable property (`Y2V09-E033–E044`).

The office therefore becomes a test of whether institutional capacity can survive change in occupant without carrying the predecessor's entire political constitution with it.

## 4.11 Y2V10 — protection is finite institutional allocation

Year 2 repeatedly demonstrates that protection is not a universal right automatically attached to personhood.

Protection Points, leader protection, rescue mechanisms, and private-point alternatives create finite protective capacity.

The institution therefore makes protection a political allocation question:

> **Who receives formal protection, under what price or rule, and what kinds of value can be translated into survival?**

This is an institutional question before it becomes the ethical question owned by `Y2_08`.

## 4.12 Y2V11 — recognition without jurisdiction

Y2V11 supplies a constructive limit case.

Ayanokōji can recognize a problem, create access, and then explicitly stop before claiming indefinite authority over what follows.

The event matters institutionally because it shows a generalizable difference between:

- enabling a process;
- participating in a process;
- and owning the process.

The series does not yet show that this stopping rule governs every high-stakes environment.

## 4.13 Y2V12 — representative authority under incomplete knowledge

The year-end exam explicitly separates representatives and participants.

Hidden roles, asymmetric knowledge, social inference, and sanctions are part of the formal architecture (`Y2V12-E051–E065`).

This makes incomplete information an institutional design feature rather than accidental confusion.

A representative may possess formal authority while lacking full epistemic or ethical jurisdiction.

The exam therefore sharpens:

> **rule-enabled power != unlimited representative sovereignty**.

## 4.14 Hoshinomiya — office does not neutralize private grievance

V12 makes teacher conflict of interest explicit.

Hoshinomiya's old relationship with Chabashira and unresolved grievance become active willingness to subordinate present students to private revenge (`Y2V12-E001–E010`).

This is the cleanest Year-2 negative control against the assumption that professional office converts private motive into neutral service.

## 4.15 Y2V12.5 — external political permeability becomes observable

V12.5 changes the scale again.

The school alters examination architecture in the presence of major political observation.

Kijima's presence therefore has institutional consequence.

The school is not merely government-backed in genealogy.

It is **politically permeable in operation**.

This does not mean every exam is dictated by politicians.

It means the model `educators and students alone author the school environment` is false.

## 4.16 V12.5 — expulsion acquires an institutional afterlife

Kijima asks about follow-up after expulsion.

The question matters because ANHS routinely treats expulsion as terminal inside the competitive system.

External scrutiny reopens the person beyond the game's boundary:

- What happens after institutional membership ends?
- What duty, if any, remains?
- What evidence exists about later outcome?

The source does not fully answer those questions.

It makes them institutionally admissible.

## 4.17 V12.5 — witness architecture returns as adult accountability

The later handling of Hoshinomiya again emphasizes truth, proof, witnesses, and deterrence.

The solution is not merely a stronger private threat.

It is to create an environment in which later abuse becomes harder to isolate from independent observers.

This repeats V04 at a different scale:

> **Independent witness structure is one of the corpus's strongest counters to private sovereign power.**

## 4.18 V12.5 — transfer is institutional reconstitution

The twenty-million-point transfer right becomes actual rather than theoretical (`Y2V12_5-E173–E179`; `Y2SL-E080`).

The transfer has four layers:

1. formal mobility exists;
2. access is capital-gated;
3. collective financing converts individual movement into class-level political action;
4. the receiving class changes membership without the institution determining its new internal constitution.

The school can change jurisdiction.

It cannot guarantee the political meaning of that change.

## 4.19 Second List — the official archive becomes richer and visibly bounded

`Second List` gives official OAA, class reviews, transfer records, activity summaries, and editorial interpretations.

It therefore increases institutional knowledge.

But the guidebook simultaneously demonstrates the limit of the file.

OAA records institutionally legible output.

Class reviews can recognize leadership or strategic properties not captured by the scalar score.

The transfer record can establish membership without establishing whether the receiving polity becomes independent, dependent, or transformed.

The mature rule remains:

> **The official archive is evidence about the person and polity. It is not the person or polity.**

# 5. Surveillance taxonomy — who watches what, for whose purpose?

Year 2 requires several different surveillance types.

## 5.1 Public performance surveillance

OAA makes school-observed performance searchable.

Purpose:

- comparison;
- evaluation;
- development;
- recruitment;
- institutional legibility.

Risk:

- students become targets and commodities within the market created by the data.

## 5.2 Location telemetry

GPS, watch status, device signals, and movement history can establish:

- location;
- absence;
- pattern;
- coordination.

They cannot automatically establish:

- motive;
- identity of a device user;
- causal authorship;
- or guilt.

## 5.3 Dossier surveillance

Tsukishiro's White Room briefing creates a different form of surveillance.

The subject cohort is informationally exposed without reciprocal visibility into:

- who compiled the dossier;
- what sources it used;
- why it exists;
- who can act on it.

This is the clearest Year-2 example of **nonreciprocal legibility**.

## 5.4 Peer surveillance

Students observe one another through:

- rumor;
- visible conduct;
- private conversation;
- strategic watching;
- class networks;
- social inference.

Peer surveillance can be coercive.

It can also be counter-institutional when students detect manipulation the formal system does not capture.

## 5.5 Witness surveillance

A witness architecture differs from surveillance aimed at control.

Its purpose is to make later denial difficult.

The key feature is not continuous observation but **independent provenance**.

V04 and V12.5 repeatedly validate this form.

## 5.6 Self-surveillance and strategic observability

Ayanokōji, Kōenji, and others understand that institutions infer capacity from displayed evidence.

Subjects therefore manage the data-generating interface.

The surveillance relation becomes reflexive:

`observer measures subject -> subject anticipates observer -> subject curates output -> observer updates model`

The institution is not watching a passive object.

# 6. Record sovereignty — the power to decide what institutionally happened

Year 1's chess interference established the hardest record problem:

`displayed result != recorded result`.

Year 2 moves from that revelation toward a theory of provenance.

## 6.1 Event truth is not enough

An event can occur and still fail to produce institutional consequence if:

- no one reports it;
- evidence is inadmissible;
- the relevant authority controls the record;
- the victim fears retaliation;
- or the event is framed as something else.

## 6.2 Record truth is not enough

A record can be accurate about its object while incomplete about causal meaning.

Examples:

- GPS logs can record device location without proving assault authorship;
- ballot totals can accurately record votes without identifying motive;
- OAA can accurately record observed performance without exposing latent ceiling;
- transfer records can accurately record new membership without resolving political dependence.

## 6.3 Provenance is the missing middle

The corpus increasingly asks:

- who produced the record;
- under what conditions;
- through which chain;
- with what opportunity for alteration;
- and whether independent observers can reproduce or challenge it.

## 6.4 Counter-provenance is stronger than private accusation

At I2, Ayanokōji's plan works because the rescue condition can be independently interpreted.

The adults need not accept his private narrative first.

The procedure creates its own reason for them to appear.

That is stronger than simply possessing a secret recording that the captured authority might suppress or discredit.

# 8. Counterforce — the positive institutional technology Year 2 discovers

Counterforce is any mechanism by which concentrated authority becomes answerable to an independent source of judgment before irreversible cost becomes final.

It includes:

- independent witnesses;
- rival adults;
- student dissent;
- protected ballots;
- publicly defensible reasons;
- institutional rescue triggers;
- privileges usable against patrons;
- office succession;
- distributed evidence;
- and external scrutiny.

## 8.1 Counterforce is not the same as opposition

An opponent may seek only to replace one sovereign with another.

Counterforce is structurally stronger when it preserves a **separate standing** capable of constraining whichever actor occupies the center.

## 8.2 Counterforce must precede irreversibility

A perfect post-hoc explanation is weaker than a mechanism that can prevent:

- expulsion;
- falsified record;
- coerced withdrawal;
- or professional abuse

before the institutional consequence closes.

## 8.3 Counterforce may be internal

Mashima, Chabashira, Kiryūin, Sakayanagi, Horikita, Kiriyama, and protected dissent can all function as internal counterforce in specific contexts.

## 8.4 Counterforce may be external

Kijima's questioning and political observation demonstrate external scrutiny.

Externality does not guarantee justice.

It creates another center of attention the internal institution cannot fully ignore.

# 23. Administrative sovereignty versus procedural sovereignty

Year 2 requires two different meanings of sovereignty.

## 23.1 Administrative sovereignty

Power to:

- access systems;
- alter implementation;
- control staff;
- suppress information;
- change conditions;
- direct surveillance;
- and exploit institutional infrastructure.

Tsukishiro is the strongest example.

## 23.2 Procedural sovereignty

The capacity of a rule or process to continue constraining actors even when a powerful person would prefer otherwise.

Examples include:

- rescue triggers that bring independent adults toward I2;
- secret ballots that preserve private will from direct leader surveillance;
- transfer privileges that can be used outside patron preference;
- succession procedures that allow office to change occupant.

## 23.3 The strongest institution is not the one with the strongest administrator

The strongest institution is one in which:

> **procedure can survive the administrator, and the record can survive the administrator's preferred account of what occurred.**

ANHS demonstrates the first more strongly than the second by Y2SL.

# 26. Institutional failure taxonomy at Y2SL

## 26.1 `RULE_OPACITY`

A consequential operative rule is unavailable to affected participants.

Example: hidden bounty.

## 26.2 `ACCESS_ASYMMETRY`

A formal right exists but practical access is capital-, patron-, or information-gated.

Example: twenty-million-point mobility.

## 26.3 `IMPLEMENTATION_CAPTURE`

A formally valid system is operationally repurposed by a discretionary actor.

Example: I2 extraction environment.

## 26.4 `RECORD_CAPTURE`

The actor able to control the official outcome can override or suppress displayed reality.

Year-1 chess remains the frozen baseline; Year 2 preserves the risk.

## 26.5 `PROVENANCE_FAILURE`

A record exists but its chain of authorship cannot be independently validated.

## 26.6 `MOTIVE_OFFICE_CONFLICT`

Private grievance or objective enters professional institutional action.

Example: Hoshinomiya.

## 26.7 `PATRONAL_MOBILITY`

Freedom from one structure depends on custody by a discretionary allocator.

Example: Nagumo.

## 26.8 `PRIVACY_OPACITY_DUAL_USE`

A secrecy mechanism protects individual agency and simultaneously obscures political responsibility.

Example: V05 anonymous vote.

## 26.9 `SURVEILLANCE_OVERINFERENCE`

Accurate telemetry or measurement is promoted into unsupported causal or identity proof.

## 26.10 `POLITICAL_PERMEABILITY`

External political attention changes internal institutional design.

This is not automatically failure; it becomes a failure when influence is unbounded, unrecorded, or noncontestable.

## 26.11 `TERMINAL_JURISDICTION`

An institution treats exit/expulsion as the end of its epistemic or moral responsibility.

V12.5 reopens this as an unresolved problem.

## 26.12 `COUNTERFORCE_DEFICIT`

No independent actor or process can intervene before irreversible cost.

This is the most important systemic risk.

# 30. Strong counterarguments

## 30.1 Counterargument: comparing ANHS with the White Room trivializes the White Room's severity

**Response:**

The comparison is not an equivalence claim.

The White Room is far more totalizing in curriculum, exit, developmental purpose, and ownership.

The comparison identifies shared technologies—measurement, enclosure, scarcity, authored environment, developmental evidence—so the decisive differences can be specified rather than assumed.

## 30.2 Counterargument: Tsukishiro is an aberration, so he tells us little about ANHS

**Response:**

He does not represent normal school purpose.

He exposes a constitutional vulnerability: what an actor with sufficient administrative access can privately repurpose.

The fact that internal counterweights can stop him is equally important evidence about ANHS.

## 30.3 Counterargument: more surveillance simply makes the school safer and fairer

**Response:**

More information can improve safety and fairness.

Year 2 repeatedly shows that observation object, access, interpretation, and record provenance remain separate.

GPS can be accurate while causal attribution remains wrong.

## 30.4 Counterargument: V04 proves ANHS successfully self-corrects

**Response:**

V04 proves an important but narrower result.

Counterforce stops the private operation.

Tsukishiro still controls the post-event account enough to deny it.

Self-correction is operationally demonstrated and adjudicatively incomplete.

## 30.5 Counterargument: V05 is maximally fair because every vote is private

**Response:**

Privacy protects individual will.

The class still faces a manufactured unanimity problem under terminal consequence and time pressure.

Accurate procedure does not contain the complete political meaning of the choice.

## 30.6 Counterargument: Nagumo's patronage is acceptable because students receive real opportunities

**Response:**

The opportunities are real.

The question is whether access remains dependent on the patron and whether beneficiaries can use opportunity against his preference.

Expansion of options and decentralization of power are different achievements.

## 30.7 Counterargument: Kijima's observation proves healthy democratic oversight

**Response:**

It proves external scrutiny and political permeability.

Because the examination changes under observation, it also demonstrates observer effect.

Oversight is healthy only when channels of influence are visible and bounded.

## 30.8 Counterargument: Volume 0 proves ANHS was simply another Atsuomi experiment

**Response:**

It proves adult planning and political entanglement.

It does not prove ownership of downstream relationships, choices, or institutional counter-authorship.

## 30.9 Counterargument: the White Room's output justifies harsh methods

**Response:**

Effectiveness establishes causal capability.

It does not establish jurisdiction to totalize another person's development or treat casualties as acceptable proof cost.

The ethical conclusion is developed in `Y2_08`; institutionally, outcome sovereignty remains the key danger.

## 30.10 Counterargument: adult private motive is irrelevant if formal rules are followed

**Response:**

V12 shows that adult discretion and interpretation can affect the environment even without formally abolishing rules.

Office supplies causal capacity outside literal rule text.

## 30.11 Counterargument: an institution that needs counterforce is institutionally weak

**Response:**

Any institution with meaningful power needs mechanisms for correction.

A system with no counterforce may look orderly precisely because dissent, evidence, or rival jurisdiction has been suppressed.

## 30.12 Counterargument: a class-transfer right proves open mobility

**Response:**

It proves formal mobility.

Twenty-million-point access makes the right heavily capital-gated.

V12.5 demonstrates exercise under extraordinary collective financing, not ordinary equal accessibility.

# 31. A Year-2 standard for legitimate developmental institutions

Year 1 proposed a procedural, anti-proprietary standard.

Year 2 strengthens it into sixteen requirements.

## 31.1 Bounded purpose

The institution must be able to say what it is trying to develop without claiming authority over every dimension of the person.

## 31.2 Transparent measurement object

A score must identify what it measures and what it does not.

## 31.3 Rule provenance

Affected participants should know who authored a consequential rule or incentive, subject to defensible privacy limits.

## 31.4 Practical access

A formal right should be audited for whether ordinary subjects can actually exercise it.

## 31.5 Record integrity

Displayed events and official outcomes require trustworthy provenance.

## 31.6 Independent witness

No powerful actor should monopolize the only admissible account of conduct involving that actor.

## 31.7 Contestable authority

Office must contain procedures through which decisions can be questioned before irreversible cost where feasible.

## 31.8 Protected dissent

Disagreement must be able to remain inside membership without automatic retaliation.

## 31.9 Data humility

Institutional files should be treated as models of observed performance rather than total identities.

## 31.10 Proportionate consequence

The institution should not use maximal membership loss merely because a rule can be designed to make it available.

## 31.11 Protection without submission

Safety should not require becoming the protector's client or property.

## 31.12 Meaningful refusal

A subject should retain domains in which saying no does not simply become institutional failure.

## 31.13 Separated powers

Rule design, enforcement, record, and review should not collapse entirely into one actor.

## 31.14 Post-membership accountability

Expulsion or withdrawal should not automatically erase concern for institutional consequences inflicted before exit.

## 31.15 Plural developmental routes

The institution should permit multiple forms of competence and future purpose rather than one totalized developmental ideal.

## 31.16 Counter-authorship

The strongest criterion:

> **A developed person must be permitted to use what they became for purposes the institution did not choose, and their answer must be capable of changing the institution's own model or legitimate scope.**

ANHS partially satisfies this standard and repeatedly violates it.

The White Room violates its center by construction.

## 32.4 Resolve record vulnerability

If the scene involves misconduct, ask:

- who can alter it;
- who can witness it independently;

## 32.5 Resolve practical access

A nominal right may be unusable without:

- points;
- allies;
- information;
- office;
- or time.

Never generate `the school` as one mind unless a public rule genuinely establishes unified action.

- Tsukishiro's exact Year-2 objective;

- guaranteed administrative auditability;
- the scope of post-expulsion responsibility;

If a powerful actor is challenged, a source-faithful solution is more likely to involve:

- witnesses;
- competing authority;
- provenance;
- contract;
- procedural trigger;
- or distributed information

# 36. Final synthesis

Year 2 begins with an institution that promises more information.

OAA makes students searchable.

Ratings become public.

Annual records preserve change.

The school explicitly admits that its observations are not omnipotent.

This looks like a move toward transparency.

At exactly the same moment, the reader sees an opposite movement above the students.

An unknown White Room student receives a dossier on the second-year cohort.

Tsukishiro operates a hidden expulsion project.

A twenty-million-point bounty creates a parallel incentive world most participants cannot see.

The institution has therefore solved one information asymmetry by creating or tolerating another.

That is the Year-2 pattern.

The governed become legible faster than power becomes answerable.

The island exam then produces the most instrumented environment in the corpus so far.

Students can be located, ranked, tracked, and compared.

But telemetry does not abolish interpretation.

A broken GPS does not identify an assailant.

A location record does not prove motive.

A hidden administrator can still use the geography of the exam to create a private coercive zone.

The decisive countermeasure is not superior surveillance.

It is provenance.

Ayanokōji routes information through a rescue mechanism that will bring independent adults to I2 for reasons they can verify without first accepting his private story.

Tsukishiro's operation fails when the environment stops being private.

Yet his denial remains possible.

The institution can stop him more easily than it can force him to yield the truth.

This is why Year 2 cannot end with the comforting claim that ANHS contains good adults and therefore self-corrects.

Its stronger achievement is structural:

> **ANHS contains enough plurality that a powerful actor does not automatically own every route through which reality becomes institutional fact.**

The Unanimous Vote exam complicates the same principle.

The ballot is private.

The count is accurate.

Individual will is formally protected.

And the outcome can still become a coercive collective machine that converts a hidden preference into terminal membership loss.

The problem is not inaccurate procedure.

It is the assumption that procedural accuracy exhausts political meaning.

Nagumo offers the inverse case.

He expands mobility and individual opportunity.

His system genuinely frees some students from fixed class fate.

Yet freedom travels through the patron.

A student may escape one institution by entering another dependency relation.

Year 2 therefore refuses to use `more options` as a synonym for `less concentrated power`.

The student council's succession begins to address this problem.

Manabu, Nagumo, and Horikita do not merely represent stronger or weaker presidents.

They instantiate different constitutions of office.

Horikita's challenge is whether authority can become answerable procedure rather than personal prestige or custodial patronage.

The answer is promising and incomplete at Y2SL.

The adult layer grows equally difficult.

Chabashira demonstrates that institutional decisions can survive for years inside the people who later administer the system.

Hoshinomiya demonstrates that office does not dissolve private grievance.

Mashima demonstrates the importance of conscience joined to procedure and witness.

Chairman Sakayanagi demonstrates real but bounded protective office.

Tsukishiro demonstrates administrative access without trusted accountability.

Kijima demonstrates that political observation can reopen questions the institution normalized away while simultaneously changing the institution being observed.

Then Volume 0 removes the final illusion of a politically pure outside.

The White Room is born inside factional politics.

ANHS belongs to the rival political field.

Atsuomi treats education, family, finance, proof, and future political use as parts of one developmental project.

The White Room becomes a total institution not simply because it trains hard, but because it attempts to monopolize relevance.

The child does not get to decide which capacities matter, which relationships matter, whether participation matters, or what the resulting development is for.

Success is precisely what makes the institution most dangerous.

If it can prove that it caused extraordinary capacity, it becomes tempting to infer that it therefore owns the capacity and the future in which it will be used.

ANHS can no longer claim to be an alternative because it is politically clean.

It is not.

It can only be an alternative through **constitutional incompleteness**:

- multiple authorities;
- imperfect but contestable records;
- unscripted peer relations;
- real if unequal mobility;
- dissent;
- ordinary life;
- and procedures through which subjects can produce evidence and purposes the developmental sovereign did not already own.

This is why counterforce is the deepest institutional achievement of Year 2.

A plural institution needs actors capable of saying:

- that record is incomplete;
- that rule is not the whole rule;
- that office does not grant that jurisdiction;
- that witness exists independently of you;
- that privilege can be used against its allocator;
- that student is more than the institutional file;
- that expelled person still has a future;
- and that development does not become property simply because someone designed the environment in which development occurred.

The mature Year-2 institutional standard is therefore not anti-institutional.

It is anti-proprietary:

> **A legitimate developmental institution may measure, challenge, protect, rank, and cultivate people only while preserving the conditions under which its own model can be contested. It must separate rule from ruler, observation from identity, protection from submission, access from patronage, record from event truth, and causal authorship from ownership of the developed person's future.**

At Y2SL, ANHS meets that standard unevenly.

The White Room violates its center.

And the central institutional question remains open:

> **Can ANHS make counterforce durable enough that plurality is not merely the fortunate presence of unusually conscientious people, but a constitutional property capable of surviving the next powerful actor who tries to make the institution private?**

That is the canonical Year-2 endpoint.
