# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 2 — Japanese Narration, Voice, Genre, Humor, and Visual Paratext
### Stable voices, expanding permission: how form redistributes interpretive authority while the person keeps exceeding the file

> **Canonical specialist synthesis at the complete Year-2 / Second List boundary.**
>
> This document is the primary Year-2 home for Japanese first-person narration, focalization, written character voice, register, pronouns, address behavior, recurrent conceptual language, genre modulation, humor, illustration/form, dossier aesthetics, and official Year-2 paratext as literary framing. It synthesizes the frozen Year-1 specialist, the canonical through-Year-2 Japanese written-voice ledger, immutable source-local readings, Volume 0 only at its mandated retrospective position, and the completed `Second List` paratext audit. It does **not** claim anime/seiyū acoustic performance, does not promote editorial compression above primary fiction, and imports no Year-3 narrative evidence.

---

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

Year 1 established a durable formal principle:

> **Voice and image produce competing partial truths; no single register is the whole person.**

Year 2 preserves that principle and makes it more demanding.

The cast does not generally develop by replacing one voice with another. The stronger pattern is that a stable voice becomes capable of new speech acts, new objects of concern, new kinds of uncertainty, and new relationship-conditioned permissions.

Horikita does not become slangy in order to become caring.

Kei does not abandon `あたし` in order to become authentic.

Kushida does not lose social cushioning when the innocence of the mask collapses.

Ryūen does not become polite in order to become more politically sophisticated.

Ichinose does not need to sound like Ryūen in order to become strategically opaque.

Sakayanagi does not abandon refinement when grief and love become more legible.

Hiyori does not become loud in order to become consequential.

Ayanokōji does not need a new first-person pronoun to make ordinary desire, strategic severity, relational curiosity, and self-limitation coexist more openly.

The canonical Year-2 change model is therefore:

`stable idiolect`
`+ expanded permissible speech acts`
`+ relationship-specific register`
`+ stress deformation`
`+ new self-knowledge`
`= development without voice replacement`

This specialist adds one further Year-2 development:

> **As Ayanokōji becomes more capable of authoring environments, the novels become more explicit about forms that limit his narratorial sovereignty.**

Alternate perspectives, visible bodily reactions, adversaries' misreadings, guidebook files, formal hearings, illustrations, ordinary-life comedy, and finally the V12.5 focalization trap all force the reader to distinguish what Ayanokōji knows, what he says, what he causes, what another person experiences, what the publication emphasizes, and what the reader merely assumes belongs to his interior.

## 0.1 Written voice is not acoustic performance

This artifact analyzes written Japanese.

It may securely discuss:

- first-person self-reference;
- sentence structure;
- politeness level;
- recurrent endings where repeated evidence exists;
- lexical preference;
- address/name behavior;
- explicit self-correction;
- relationship-conditioned register;
- stress fragmentation;
- narrative focalization;
- and the difference between spoken dialogue and interior narration.

It does **not** infer from prose alone:

- pitch;
- timbre;
- vocal fry;
- breathiness;
- loudness;
- speaking tempo;
- regional accent;
- seiyū interpretation;
- or anime performance.

The written-voice ledger is a constraint layer for textual reconstruction, not an acoustic voice model.

## 0.2 Narration is evidence, not omniscience

Ayanokōji's first-person narration gives the reader privileged access.

Privileged does not mean complete.

The narration remains vulnerable to several different limitations:

1. **planning omission** — a plan may be revealed after it has succeeded;
2. **causal understatement** — he may describe his direct act narrowly while leaving the environmental effect implicit;
3. **motive underdescription** — behavior can demonstrate stake before he has vocabulary for it;
4. **emotional lag** — bodily or behavioral evidence can precede an accepted emotional label;
5. **model error** — another person can generate an answer his model did not contain;
6. **focalization ambiguity** — a passage that feels first-person-adjacent is not automatically his interior.

The first five are primarily limitations of the narrator/modeler.

The sixth becomes decisive at Y2V12.5 and belongs to the novel's point-of-view architecture itself.

## 0.3 Focalization is not the same as narrator honesty

The V12.5 breakup requires a distinction stronger than the simple phrase “unreliable narrator.”

A long interior sequence temporarily appears to describe Ayanokōji's sudden romantic reversal. The spoken `別れよう` then breaks the alignment, and the text reveals that the preceding romantic interior belonged to Kei's hoped-for counterfactual—her `妄想` / `願望`—rather than to Ayanokōji.

That is not best classified as Ayanokōji lying to the reader.

It is a **point-of-view trap** in which the novel temporarily misaligns perceived focal ownership.

Accordingly:

> **Narrator unreliability and focalization ambiguity are separate formal mechanisms.**

A reconstruction or synthesis model that fails this distinction can literally assign another character's desired interior to Ayanokōji.

## 0.4 Stable register does not imply stable moral or political function

The same surface register can carry different purposes.

Kushida's public competence can remain socially useful after her concealed innocence collapses.

Ryūen's roughness can carry intimidation, contract precision, or genuine recognition of a meaningful opponent.

Sakayanagi's politeness can carry domination, grief, affection, or withdrawal.

Horikita's precise register can carry judgment, request, uncertainty, leadership, or care.

The task is therefore not to infer essence from style. It is to identify **what a stable style is currently doing**.

## 0.5 One diagnostic line is rarely enough

Highest-value voice evidence remains:

1. repeated Japanese dialogue across ordinary and high-stress settings;
2. first-person interior narration;
3. explicit self-reference change;
4. repeated address/name change;
5. register shifts with clear triggers;
6. dialogue later confirmed by behavior.

A single particle, joke, honorific omission, or angry outburst should not be promoted into a complete personality claim without corroboration.

## 0.6 Humor is evidence, not exoneration

Year 2 repeatedly uses comedy to reveal:

- ordinary ignorance;
- bodily limitation;
- social incompetence;
- rivalry;
- desire;
- role incongruity;
- and the gap between institutional seriousness and lived school life.

The existence of humor does not morally neutralize coercion.

Chabashira's maid sequence can be genuinely funny and still raise a real consent problem around purchased labor.

Horikita/Ibuki banter can reveal intimacy without making their antagonism fake.

Ayanokōji's yogurt-maker failure can be comic and philosophically important at once.

## 0.7 Genre modes overlap rather than replace one another

Year 2 contains:

- school comedy;
- romantic comedy;
- relationship drama;
- examination puzzle;
- survival/territorial thriller;
- institutional procedural drama;
- political conspiracy;
- developmental horror;
- tragedy of membership;
- cultural-festival theatricality;
- and retrospective political prehistory.

The correct question is not “what genre is COTE really?”

It is:

> **Which genre is currently deciding what evidence becomes salient?**

An examination chapter makes rules and inference central.

A decimal volume makes purposeless time and voluntary company legible.

Volume 0 makes political procurement and developmental infrastructure legible.

The breakup chapter makes focal ownership itself part of the event.

## 0.8 Illustration is primary paratext, not prose fact

Narrative illustrations can:

- confirm visible body position or clothing;
- reweight a scene by editorial selection;
- create counterpoint against narration;
- restore embodied cost after abstraction;
- or deliberately aestheticize the system the prose is criticizing.

An image does not independently establish an interior motive absent from prose.

The safest visual chain is:

`literal image content`
`→ insertion context`
`→ editorial emphasis`
`→ bounded interpretation`

not:

`image pose`
`→ total psychological truth`.

## 0.9 *Second List* is four evidence systems, not one voice of God

The guidebook audit establishes four classes:

- `SL-DATA` — documentary chronology/system facts;
- `SL-RATING` — institutional quantitative evaluation;
- `SL-EDITORIAL` — retrospective official framing/compression;
- `SL-FICTION` — included narrative fiction.

These classes must remain separate.

An editorial caption can tell us what the official guidebook foregrounds.

It cannot automatically outrank a contradictory or more qualified primary-fiction sequence.

A rating can tell us what the school records at a stated time.

It cannot become a timeless theory of the person.

## 0.10 Volume 0 is retrospective primary fiction, not chronological permission

Volume 0 is primary fiction with high literary authority.

Its position in this Year-2 project is still retrospective.

The reader may use it to understand:

- White Room linguistic ontology;
- Atsuomi's political register;
- the child Kiyotaka's function-first cognitive environment;
- and the genealogy of later metaphors of curriculum, utility, and authored preference.

Earlier Year-2 characters do not retroactively know those facts.

The pre-Volume-0 reading state remains historically valid.

## 0.11 The file/person distinction remains binding

A profile, illustration, voice sample, OAA score, relationship diagram, first-person monologue, or retrospective caption can all be accurate without being exhaustive.

The Year-2 formal ethic is not anti-knowledge.

It is anti-totalization.

# 1. Executive thesis — Year 2 changes permission and redistributes narratorial sovereignty

The strongest Year-2 formal thesis is:

> **Year 2 rarely develops characters by replacing their voice. It develops them by expanding what their established voice can legitimately contain, while the novels increasingly distribute interpretive authority across alternate focalization, bodily evidence, ordinary-life genres, institutional records, illustrations, and other people's counter-models. Ayanokōji remains the dominant first-person interface, but he becomes less capable of monopolizing the meaning of the people and futures he can still causally influence.**

This principle appears at several scales.

Ayanokōji's `オレ` remains stable while his internal repertoire broadens.

He can still enumerate conditions, minimize his own causal authorship, and convert emotion into variables.

He can also want a trivial consumer product, worry about an invitation being refused, choose inefficient company, recognize that another person's future is no longer his `立ち入る領域`, and hope that his own prediction will fail.

The formal development is not replacement of the strategist by “the real teenager.”

It is increasingly explicit coexistence.

Horikita's polished speech similarly gains new permission.

The same syntax that once primarily judged others can now ask, negotiate, acknowledge uncertainty, accept openly self-interested cooperation, offer care, and state that Ayanokōji is important without turning into a new sentimental idiolect.

Kei's `あたし` remains socially quick and relationally demanding while stress can still fracture her interior. Her V12.5 behavioral composure therefore cannot be mistaken for emotional independence merely because the spoken breakup scene is controlled.

Kushida's public cushioning survives exposure. The collapse is in the innocence and secrecy of the public/private architecture, not in the social competence itself.

Ryūen's roughness acquires more precise contract language without becoming democratic speech.

Ichinose's warmth acquires opacity and counter-authored force without becoming a “dark voice.”

Sakayanagi's refinement can contain grief, love, and fiduciary failure without becoming colloquial.

Hiyori's quietness becomes more consequential without becoming indecision.

Nanase is the exceptional Year-2 case where self-reference itself becomes explicit action technology: `私 / ボク` is not merely flavor but a performed identity with behavioral effect.

The narrative architecture evolves in parallel.

Year 1 taught the reader to distrust the fantasy that Ayanokōji's first-person access solved him.

Year 2 intensifies the test.

- V03 turns performed identity and false records into plot mechanics.
- V05 moves into Horikita's interior so the Kushida decision cannot be annexed as an Ayanokōji twist.
- V06 lets Horikita focalization make distributed leadership visible from inside leadership.
- V09 gives Nagumo interiority just when his institutional order might otherwise look like one-note machinery.
- V10 lets Sakayanagi's unrecognized friendship become legible as revelation of a prior state rather than a sudden rewrite.
- V11 makes Yamamura's social invisibility a formal problem of whether existence enters other people's models.
- V12 repeatedly demonstrates how formal freedom can coexist with authored conditions.
- V12.5 finally weaponizes focal ownership itself.

The visual layer follows the same argument.

Year 2 repeatedly places a convincing bodily image beside a more troubling causal structure:

- quiet hand-holding alongside hidden developmental management;
- Ayanokōji's offered hand to Nanase alongside disposability language;
- convincing Kei intimacy immediately before `恋愛のカリキュラム`;
- Chabashira's visually marketable maid performance inside a consent problem;
- Kamuro/Yamamura faces inside a supposedly neutral allocation procedure;
- Kei's post-breakup composure beside interior collapse;
- Horikita's bodily anomaly before she can classify it;
- and final separation images before the political consequences can be known.

The image is not the correction to the text.

The text is not the correction to the image.

The formal achievement is the refusal to let one medium erase the other.

*Second List* then converts the entire year into a more sophisticated archive.

It supplies chronology, OAA snapshots, official character framing, class reviews, relationship captions, and bonus fiction. Yet the guidebook itself repeatedly demonstrates that richer files remain incomplete:

- Ayanokōji's OAA changes as visibility changes;
- Kōenji's recorded values underdescribe selectively available capacity;
- Ryūen's political competence exceeds the axis;
- Hiyori's quiet judgment is underdescribed by formal ratings;
- Kei's ordinary metrics fail to explain social coordination;
- Kushida's strong social metrics cannot determine moral use;
- Ichinose's own action exceeds Ayanokōji's prediction.

The Year-2 terminal proposition is therefore:

> **The more sophisticated the model becomes, the more important it is to preserve the person's capacity to invalidate the model. Voice, narration, genre, illustration, and paratext all produce knowledge; literary maturity lies in using that knowledge without mistaking representation for ownership.**

# 3. The Year-2 written-voice model — permission rather than replacement

The canonical through-Year-2 voice ledger supplies the governing architecture:

`BASE_SELF_REFERENCE`
`+ DEFAULT_REGISTER`
`+ PUBLIC/PRIVATE/STRESS/LEADERSHIP/INTIMACY register`
`+ ADDRESS_BEHAVIOR`
`+ LEXICAL_TENDENCIES`
`+ NARRATION_MODE`
`+ Y2_CHANGE`

The most important synthesis result is that developmental change usually occurs in the **contents and permissions** of those fields rather than in wholesale replacement of the voice.

A voice can become:

- more corrigible without becoming softer;
- more intimate without becoming less socially competent;
- more self-interested without becoming less publicly polished;
- more reflective without becoming linguistically refined;
- more adversarial without becoming rougher;
- more vulnerable without losing formal control;
- or more autonomous without becoming louder.

This matters for analytical reconstruction because stereotyped “developmental voice changes” often erase exactly the continuity the Japanese preserves.

A mature Horikita who suddenly speaks like an ordinary bubbly teenager is less accurate, not more developed.

A mature Ryūen who sounds politely consensus-oriented loses the point of his development.

A late Ichinose who becomes a generic cold antagonist turns strategic opacity into personality replacement.

A post-exposure Kushida who can only snarl treats one stress/private register as the total person.

The literary system is more conservative and more interesting:

> **people gain new things they can say without ceasing to sound like themselves.**

# 4. Ayanokōji's first-person form — one `オレ`, several operative permissions

Ayanokōji remains the formal center because his first-person narration creates the project's largest gap between access and mastery.

The stable `オレ` supports at least three major Year-2 modes.

## 4.1 Ordinary/comic `オレ`

This mode worries about or notices:

- food;
- consumer purchases;
- cooking;
- skiing;
- social mistakes;
- whether an invitation will be rejected;
- embarrassment;
- friendship;
- dating;
- bodily attraction;
- unfamiliar leisure;
- disappointment whose stakes are genuinely small.

V08's school-trip material and V09.5's yogurt-maker sequence are particularly important.

The comedy is not merely tonal relief. It shows a person capable of inhabiting events whose value does not need to be converted into strategy.

The yogurt maker is formally stronger than a grand declaration of freedom because the object is trivial. No heroic mission can explain why he wants it.

## 4.2 Analytical/strategic `オレ`

This mode remains stable and highly compressed.

It:

- enumerates conditions;
- distinguishes nominal from practical outcomes;
- models responses;
- manages information;
- thinks in probabilities;
- turns affect into decision variables;
- and often speaks about causal intervention through narrow descriptions of the immediate act.

V12 proves that strategic severity does not require theatrical villain language. The decisive control can occur through timing, relational inference, and information architecture.

## 4.3 Emerging participant `オレ`

The developmental addition is not warmth as a permanent tone.

It is the ability to speak or think in structures such as:

- I want this;
- the other person may refuse;
- this future is not mine to enter;
- this ordinary day matters because I want it;
- I want my prediction to fail;
- another person's answer can be valuable precisely because I did not provide it.

V09.5 with Hiyori is the clearest local participant model.

V11 supplies the strongest explicit stopping rule:

> `ここから先は、オレの立ち入る領域じゃない。`

The sentence is not linguistically spectacular.

Its importance lies in the speech act: the environmental author names a jurisdictional boundary on himself.

## 4.4 Causal understatement remains an invariant

Ayanokōji rarely needs to boast that he manipulated everyone.

He is more likely to:

- describe a small direct act;
- distinguish the other person's agency;
- characterize himself as setting a stage;
- and leave the scale of the environmental effect for the reader to reconstruct.

His V07 language around stage-setting is especially diagnostic.

This register can be technically accurate and morally incomplete at once.

The problem is not necessarily factual lying.

It is **compression of responsibility into the narrowest defensible causal description**.

## 4.5 Emotional uncertainty is analytic before it is confessional

Ayanokōji typically discovers affect as evidence.

V10's involuntary smile is exemplary because the expression occurs before he commands it.

He then observes the smile as a phenomenon requiring explanation.

This is compatible with a narrator whose training encourages classification before confession.

Bodily output can therefore have high evidentiary value without making every somatic anomaly a settled emotional label.

The same principle later applies to Horikita's V12.5 heart-rate anomaly.

## 4.6 V12.5 breaks the lazy “unreliable narrator” model

The breakup sequence temporarily gives the reader a romantic interior that appears to belong to Ayanokōji.

Then:

> `別れよう`

The spoken line ruptures the apparent interior.

The subsequent passage identifies the preceding sequence as Kei's hoped-for counterfactual.

The formal lesson is exact:

> **Before attributing an interior passage, identify the focal owner.**

This is stronger than the ordinary rule “Ayanokōji may omit things.”

The novel can also manipulate the reader's assumption about whose mind is being represented.

## 4.7 Volume 0 changes the genealogy of the adult voice without making it inevitable

The child Kiyotaka's environment is saturated with:

- function;
- input;
- performance;
- observation;
- test;
- and institutional outcome language.

Yuki's carrot question is diagnostic because Kiyotaka initially responds to preference through nutritional/function information before being pushed toward the question of what **he likes**.

This does not establish an emotionless child.

It shows an environment in which subjective preference has low expected relational effect.

The adult's movement from objective function toward subjective desire therefore gains genealogy without becoming determinism.

Volume 0's final wording that Kiyotaka will `入学を決める` preserves agency grammatically after extensive evidence of political preparation.

# 5. Alternate focalization — the formal anti-monopoly mechanism

Year 2 repeatedly ensures that Ayanokōji's causal power does not become narratorial ownership.

## 5.1 Nanase — self-authored psychological technology from inside

V01 flags the `ボク` / `私` distinction before explaining it.

V03 then gives Nanase's own account:

> `『私』の脆い心では、その『正義』を支えることは出来ない。`
>
> `だから『ボク』がそれを支える。`

The effect is formally important because the reader hears the identity technology from the user before Ayanokōji supplies his non-supernatural interpretation.

His explanation remains strong.

It does not erase her account of why the performance exists.

## 5.2 Horikita — independent judgment must be focalized to remain independent

V05's Horikita perspective is architecturally necessary.

If the Kushida decision appeared only from Ayanokōji's exterior observation, her independent judgment could be reduced to a surprise inside his story.

The move into her interior lets the reader experience the decision as **hers**, including its risk and ethical cost.

V06 extends the principle by focalizing distributed leadership from inside Horikita's scheduling, uncertainty, delegation, and dependence on specialized classmates.

The form itself therefore supports the claim that visible leadership is becoming real rather than merely theater around a hidden strategist.

## 5.3 Nagumo — interiority complicates institutional villainy

V09's opening monologue gives Nagumo a self-authentication problem:

- has his `実力` actually been tested;
- or has he become a `裸の王様`?

The perspective does not excuse patronage or hierarchy.

It prevents the political system from being read as consequence-free smugness.

The ruler's own interior contains a problem his public order cannot solve for him.

## 5.4 Yamamura — invisibility becomes first-person political evidence

V11 repeatedly demonstrates that social invisibility is not merely “shyness” observed from outside.

The line:

> `私は確かにココに存在している`

makes `存在` a demand to enter other people's models as a person rather than as useful unseen support.

Comedy in which others skip or misname her does not erase the cruelty. It demonstrates how ordinary social form can reproduce the fear her interior articulates.

## 5.5 Kei — the breakup returns the hidden curriculum to the learner's interior

V12.5's most important correction is not simply that Kei is sad.

Her own narration reconstructs the relationship as “half for me, half for him,” recognizes that Ayanokōji tends to absolutize his design, and interprets the breakup as scheduled from the beginning.

The learner therefore produces an account of the curriculum that the curriculum-author did not disclose to her while it was underway.

That perspective is indispensable to any ethical or relational reading of the breakup.

## 5.6 Sakayanagi, Ichinose, and other observers — Ayanokōji becomes the object

Year 2 increasingly allows other characters to model him:

- Sakayanagi reconstructs an unspoken relational message from his clue field;
- Ichinose predicts his arrival and later generates an answer he does not possess;
- Hiyori can distinguish his stated reasons from a simpler personal desire;
- Morishita notices provenance problems in his claimed advice;
- Kōenji refuses his framework of evaluation.

The formal movement is significant:

> **the narrator remains the dominant observer, but observation becomes reciprocal enough to produce model error.**

# 6. Focalization and truth-state discipline

For Year-2 retrieval, every narratively significant statement should be assigned at least one truth state.

| State | Meaning | Example type |
|---|---|---|
| `NARRATOR_DIRECT` | Ayanokōji explicitly narrates a proposition | plan, desire, evaluation |
| `FOCAL_OTHER_DIRECT` | another focal character directly narrates/interprets | Nanase, Nagumo, Kei, Yamamura |
| `SPOKEN_SELF_REPORT` | a character says something aloud about self or motive | confession, threat, promise |
| `EXTERNAL_OBSERVATION` | another actor observes behavior | smile, stress, posture |
| `BEHAVIORAL_EVIDENCE` | action supports or contradicts stated model | refusal, return, protection |
| `EDITORIAL_FRAME` | guidebook/caption/visual selection emphasizes a reading | Second List profile |
| `INSTITUTIONAL_RECORD` | school data records a state | OAA, class points |
| `RETROSPECTIVE_PRIMARY` | later primary fiction reveals genealogy/state | Volume 0 |
| `FOCALIZATION_AMBIGUOUS` | apparent interior ownership is not yet secure | V12.5 trap before reveal |

This prevents several recurring errors.

Ayanokōji's self-description is not automatically omniscient fact.

Another character's self-report is not automatically false because he models it differently.

An illustration is not automatically an interior claim.

A guidebook caption is not automatically primary-fiction causality.

A later retrospective source is not automatically earlier actor knowledge.

# 7. Japanese self-correction as development evidence

Japanese self-correction is unusually valuable in Year 2 because it can expose the exact moment a character revises the frame of an utterance.

Nanase's V01:

> `ボクは、じゃなかった───私は`

is initially evidence without explanation.

V03 later gives the local architecture.

After the confrontation, she begins by framing disclosure as Ayanokōji's entitlement—`知る権利`—then corrects toward her own desire to tell him:

> `そうですね、先輩には知る権利───いえ、私が知ってもらいたいと思っています`

The self-correction shifts the speech act from recipient entitlement to speaker authorship.

Ayanokōji's own Year-2 language repeatedly performs a related movement at a conceptual level:

- from utility toward desire;
- from certainty toward `不確定であってほしい`;
- from intervention toward `立ち入る領域じゃない`;
- from model completion toward recognition that another person can still create discovery.

Self-correction is therefore not verbal noise.

It is often the smallest textual unit in which changed jurisdiction becomes audible.

# 8. Recurrent conceptual vocabulary — language as an analytical state machine

`Y2_13` will become the full terminology/passages index. This specialist therefore does not attempt exhaustive lexical cataloguing. It instead identifies recurrent terms whose changing use reveals formal development.

## 8.1 `実力`

Year 2 opens with a chapter literally asking `実力とは` while OAA attempts formal measurement.

The surrounding fiction immediately splits the term into:

- possessed capacity;
- displayed capacity;
- measured capacity;
- politically available capacity;
- and rule-specific value.

The word remains contested throughout the year.

Nagumo later treats `実力` as something requiring meaningful adversarial proof rather than institutional supremacy alone.

The formal effect is to keep the series title itself unstable in a productive way.

## 8.2 `信頼` / `妄信`

Trust is not a uniformly virtuous noun.

V04.5's distinction between `信頼` and `妄信` turns solidarity into a constitutional question: can trust survive contradiction, or does harmony suppress dissent?

The vocabulary later supports the Ichinose-class reform problem.

## 8.3 `同調圧力`

Himeno's term names the cost of compulsory harmony directly.

Its value lies in refusing to treat friendly group affect as proof that dissent is socially free.

## 8.4 `舞台`

V07's `舞台` / `舞台をセッティングする` is the most exact Year-2 metaphor for environmental authorship.

The cultural-festival setting makes the metaphor self-reflexive: literal performances and covert causal staging occupy the same volume.

## 8.5 `補助輪`

V06's `補助輪` metaphor defines an anti-dependency ideal while preserving hierarchy.

Training wheels are successful when they become removable.

Yet the metaphor still belongs to the person who believes he understands the learner's trajectory from above.

## 8.6 `恋愛のカリキュラム`

The phrase is not reducible to “learning about romance.”

`カリキュラム` imports designed progression, stages, evaluation, and terminal objectives into an intimate relation.

Its force depends on the relationship being emotionally real rather than fake.

## 8.7 `宿主`

The biological host metaphor is stronger than ordinary reliance.

It keeps dependency conceptually available inside a relationship that also contains affection, social learning, and ordinary pleasure.

## 8.8 `解` / `正解`

Ayanokōji's answer-language reflects a trained preference for solvable structures.

V07's `揺るぎなき『解』` around self-preservation makes the mathematical flavor explicit.

The Year-2 arc then becomes increasingly interested in answers he does not supply.

## 8.9 `存在` / `価値`

V11 uses `存在` and `価値` to destabilize utility-centered recognition.

Yamamura demands social existence beyond usefulness.

Amasawa's value crisis after Yagami similarly forces separation among strategic utility, relational significance, and future worth.

## 8.10 `立ち入る領域`

The phrase is one of the cleanest Year-2 jurisdiction markers.

The issue is not simply epistemic inability.

`立ち入る` names entering a domain one may not have standing to enter.

## 8.11 `自由意思`

V10's career reflection makes free will concrete rather than absolute.

Ayanokōji has developed real new preferences, yet some future options remain externally constrained.

Language therefore protects the difference between self-authorship and unconstrained sovereignty.

# 9. Horikita Suzune — polished judgment gains corrigibility without losing precision

Horikita remains one of the strongest examples of development through stable syntax.

Her Year-1 voice already combined:

- complete sentences;
- controlled feminine endings;
- precise evaluation;
- judgment-oriented diction;
- limited social cushioning.

Year 2 does not make her verbally soft.

It expands the set of things that voice can do.

## 9.1 `対等` as political standing

V01 repeatedly makes `対等` a Horikita keyword.

The desired cross-year alliance is not merely cooperative or friendly. It is a relation of standing in which neither side possesses unilateral jurisdiction over the other.

This is voice as political theory.

## 9.2 Collaborative speech becomes possible without dependency becoming obedience

Horikita increasingly speaks in forms equivalent to:

- I need your judgment here;
- this remains my decision;
- I may be wrong;
- take responsibility with me;
- this task requires someone else's expertise.

The change is not loss of authority.

It is increased ability to make authority **answerable**.

## 9.3 Kushida cooperation preserves adversarial register

V06 is especially diagnostic because the compact does not counterfeit reconciliation.

Kushida states:

> `私は私のためだけに１年半戦ってクラスに貢献する。`

Horikita accepts the self-interested premise.

The left-handed handshake formalizes hostility rather than erasing it.

The voices remain clipped, skeptical, and negotiated.

That makes the cooperation more credible than a sudden warmth would.

## 9.4 Ibuki allows relationship-specific lowering of ceremony

School-trip and other Year-2 scenes with Ibuki show Horikita capable of:

- petty competition;
- banter;
- physical play;
- direct irritation;
- low-ceremony antagonistic familiarity.

This does not generalize into a new global colloquial register.

It is a **relationship-conditioned permission**.

## 9.5 Late care remains Horikita-shaped

V12.5's concern about Ayanokōji's heart arrives as observation, bounded directness, and practical offer.

The scene is powerful precisely because the care does not require a sentimental personality transplant.

## 9.6 Bodily novelty precedes linguistic classification

V12.5's heart-rate anomaly is an important limit case.

The body produces evidence before Horikita possesses a stable conceptual label for it.

The illustration reinforces the anomaly without converting it into definitive romance proof.

This parallels Ayanokōji's V10 smile:

> **in this series, affect can become bodily evidence before it becomes accepted vocabulary.**

`≠`

# 11. Kushida Kikyō — public cushioning survives the collapse of innocence

- strategic;

This is one of the strongest formal arguments against “true voice versus fake voice” essentialism in the corpus.

The strongest development is therefore:

## 16.4 Politeness continues to aestheticize danger

Sakayanagi remains a major case in which elegant speech can make coercive or proprietorial ideas aesthetically pleasurable.

The analytical method must not confuse composure with moderation.

# 26. Year-2 humor taxonomy

Humor performs analytical work throughout Year 2.

## 26.1 Competence-inversion humor

Ayanokōji can solve complex strategic environments and still fail at:

- ordinary consumer choice;
- cooking assumptions;
- skiing expectations;
- social timing;
- trivial leisure optimization.

The joke temporarily makes extraordinary competence locally irrelevant.

That is important in a series otherwise tempted by universal mastermind framing.

## 26.2 Bodily limitation and ordinary failure

Trips, falls, fatigue, awkward sports, weather, food, and exhaustion repeatedly make bodies resistant to abstract planning.

Asahina's V07 haunted-house fall is exemplary: planned spectacle collapses into a person needing help.

The performer becomes the vulnerable body.

## 26.3 Antagonistic-familiarity humor

Horikita and Ibuki are a major Year-2 source of comedy because their rivalry becomes sufficiently durable to support pettiness, banter, competition, and play.

The humor is relational evidence.

It does not require hidden affection to be analytically valuable.

## 26.4 Persona and self-theater

Kōenji's self-mythology, Ryūen's amused dominance, Amasawa's teasing intrusion, and other theatrical registers make public identity conspicuously authored.

Humor can therefore expose performance rather than prove insincerity.

## 26.5 Role-incongruity humor

Chabashira as maid is the clearest case.

The comedy depends on the collision among:

- teacher office;
- adult dignity;
- student economic strategy;
- and reader-facing spectacle.

The role inversion is funny because everyone understands what the ordinary role should have been.

## 26.6 Romantic/social miscalibration

Jealousy, bad answers, misunderstood expectations, invitations, gifts, and trivial dating problems let characters fail in domains where intelligence does not guarantee competence.

Kei often functions as a corrective interpreter of ordinary relationship norms for Ayanokōji.

## 26.7 Genre self-undercutting

Year 2 frequently allows comic school-life material to exist beside conspiracy or violence.

This can preserve plurality: White Room conflict is not allowed to become the only reality of school.

The same mechanism can also discharge discomfort too quickly.

## 26.8 Humor as evidence of desire

The strongest comic scenes often reveal what a character bothers to care about when the stakes are small.

A trivial want can be more diagnostic of self-authorship than a strategic declaration precisely because it is not useful.

# 27. Humor, ethics, and the problem of aesthetic discharge

The Year-2 method must preserve two claims simultaneously:

1. humor can humanize and reveal;
2. humor can aestheticize or prematurely discharge ethical tension.

## 27.1 Chabashira's maid sequence is the central case

The institutional rule makes her labor purchasable.

The class uses that fact strategically.

The scene is deliberately comic.

The illustration makes the adult maid spectacle visually memorable—exactly the commodity the in-story plan depends upon.

This self-reflexivity is formally sharp.

It also means the publication participates in the spectacle it depicts.

The correct reading is neither:

- “it is funny, therefore harmless”;
- nor “it is coercive, therefore the comedy is analytically irrelevant.”

Both functions exist.

## 27.2 Yagami's anti-climax is genre denial, not merely missing payoff

Yagami wants a direct comparative conflict.

A conventional showdown would grant him the genre he desires: mastermind versus mastermind, strength authenticated through centered duel.

V07 instead destroys him through distributed evidence, traces, bait, and his own inability to tolerate an Ayanokōji-coded invitation.

The anti-climax is therefore a formal argument about his psychology.

It is also narratively costly because the long buildup creates legitimate expectations of a more centered confrontation.

Both evaluations should remain.

## 27.3 Comedy around invisibility can remain cruel

V11's game scenes around Yamamura can be funny at the level of ordinary social awkwardness.

Because the narrative has already given her fear of being socially unregistered, the same joke also demonstrates the harm.

The comedy becomes evidence of structure rather than escape from it.

# 28. Performance as a Year-2 master form

Year 2 repeatedly asks whether a staged performance produces fake consequences.

Its answer is generally no.

The performance may be authored while its effects remain real.

Examples include:

- public OAA performance;
- Ayanokōji's authored mediocrity;
- Nanase's `ボク` identity performance;
- Horikita/Ryūen staged festival rivalry;
- the maid café;
- Chabashira's purchased performance;
- Tsubaki's false attribution;
- Kushida's continued public competence after exposure;
- relationship performance inside genuine intimacy;
- and selective public narratives after expulsions.

This leads to a governing formal distinction:

> **performed does not mean unreal.**

The correct questions are:

- who authored the performance;
- who knows it is a performance;
- which parts became internalized skill;
- what consequences it produces;
- whether refusal is possible;
- and whether the performer can later redefine the role.

The same logic applies to identity, leadership, romance, institutional records, and genre itself.

# 29. Visual paratext — a second system of legibility under Year-2 conditions

4. **ethical restoration** — returning a particular body to an abstract political mechanism;
5. **self-reflexive commodification** — making the reader participate in the same spectacle the plot analyzes.

The Year-2 addition is that image/text counterpoint becomes increasingly central to the overall argument about authored conditions.

## 34.5 Hiyori and Ryūen demonstrate domain mismatch

# 35. *Second List* — the file grows richer without becoming the person

*Second List* is the Year-2 formal culmination of dossier aesthetics.

Its value is substantial.

Its authority must remain typed.

## 35.1 `SL-DATA` stabilizes chronology, not causation

Annual-event tables, class-point snapshots, records, and system facts make the Year-2 sequence easier to retrieve.

They do not determine who authored every outcome or whether the resulting political order is legitimate.

A class-point table cannot tell us:

- whether leadership was distributed;
- whether dissent was safe;
- whether a victory depended on a hidden guarantor;
- whether someone consented to the strategy;
- or whether the polity can reproduce judgment after its leader exits.

## 35.2 `SL-RATING` describes institutional legibility

OAA values are strong evidence for what the school records.

The guidebook's own examples make naive numerical realism untenable.

## 35.3 `SL-EDITORIAL` reveals official emphasis

Profiles, captions, class reviews, Check Point pages, and relationship summaries matter because they tell us what the publication chooses to foreground retrospectively.

Editorial emphasis can:

- strengthen a reading;
- identify a commercially or narratively privileged relationship;
- compress a developmental arc;
- or foreground a future-facing question.

It cannot silently erase contrary primary-fiction nuance.

## 35.4 `SL-FICTION` restores lived experience inside the archive

The included stories carry higher literary authority than editorial compression because they return characters to narrative time.

The second bonus story is especially important for Ichinose because Ayanokōji explicitly recognizes that she stood back up by her own power and continues to produce discoveries after he thought his analysis was complete.

The archive therefore contains its own rebuttal to archival totality.

## 35.5 The terminal paratext proposition

*Second List* makes the file better.

It does not make the file sovereign.

> **A richer model can improve prediction without acquiring jurisdiction over the modeled person.**

# 38. Formal strengths of Year 2

Year 2's formal system is strongest where technique and theme become the same problem.

## 38.1 Information asymmetry is built into narrative form

The books do not merely tell the reader that actors possess unequal information.

They make the reader experience unequal information through:

- hidden exam rules;
- delayed plan revelation;
- false trails;
- alternate POV;
- institutional record uncertainty;
- unspoken inferred messages;
- and the V12.5 focalization trap.

The reader therefore participates in the same epistemic problem the characters face.

## 38.2 The series increasingly makes model error narratively productive

Ayanokōji remains extraordinarily good at prediction.

Year 2's strongest scenes are increasingly those where another person:

- surprises him;
- forces reclassification;
- generates an unowned answer;
- or reveals that his causal model had underdescribed a relation.

The narrative form thus begins to enact the thematic principle of counter-authorship.

## 38.3 Stable voice makes development more credible

Because Horikita, Kei, Kushida, Ryūen, Ichinose, Sakayanagi, Hiyori, and others remain linguistically recognizable, development cannot be reduced to authorial rebranding.

The same person must carry new judgment through old habits of speech.

That is formally stronger than simply assigning a “mature voice.”

## 38.4 Special-exam mechanics become literary form

The best Year-2 exams do not merely generate plot obstacles.

Their rules embody the thematic question:

- V01 turns public measurement into a market;
- V05 turns secret individual preference into unanimous collective coercion;
- V06 turns absence into a succession test;
- V09 makes improvement and marginal value more important than static rank;
- V10 turns protection into finite allocation;
- V12 makes relationship knowledge formally usable competitive capital.

Mechanics become characterization and ethics.

## 38.5 Genre oscillation prevents totalization

The White Room plot could reduce the series to conspiracy.

The point system could reduce it to game fiction.

Romance could reduce it to heroine selection.

The class war could reduce it to power scaling.

Year 2 resists each reduction by changing modes.

The result is not tonal incoherence by default. It is a formal insistence that no one system contains the whole life.

## 38.6 Illustrations repeatedly restore embodiment

The more abstract the mechanism becomes, the more valuable bodily image becomes.

Protection slots, expulsion votes, class-point totals, hidden curricula, and transfer plans all create pressure toward abstraction.

Illustrations repeatedly return the reader to:

- fatigue;
- tears;
- clothing;
- bodily asymmetry;
- distance;
- posture;
- touch;
- and visible isolation.

## 38.7 Retrospective form is increasingly disciplined

Volume 0 and *Second List* add enormous retrospective information without making prior prospective readings meaningless.

The project architecture mirrors the novels' strongest formal discipline by preserving what could be known at each stage.

# 39. Critical reservations

The Year-2 formal system also creates recurring analytical hazards.

## 39.1 Ayanokōji's diagnostic gravity remains unusually high

Even when other perspectives exist, the novels frequently allow Ayanokōji to supply highly persuasive explanations of other people's motives and development.

His accuracy is often excellent.

The danger is reader deference.

A strong explanation can become an unofficial omniscient narrator if later analysts stop asking whether another person's behavior or self-account requires revision.

## 39.2 Focalization ambiguity can become an interpretive trap beyond its intended scene

V12.5 proves that apparent interior ownership can be manipulated.

The lesson should improve attribution discipline.

It should **not** make every emotionally inconvenient passage permanently suspect.

Once focal ownership is textually secured, the passage returns to ordinary evidentiary treatment.

## 39.3 Emotional uncertainty can become unfalsifiable if every label is deferred

Ayanokōji often observes affect before naming it.

Horikita's late bodily anomaly has similar structure.

That does not authorize an analyst to suspend every emotional conclusion indefinitely.

Direct declarations, repeated behavior, stable desire, and convergent evidence must still be allowed to establish claims when the threshold is met.

## 39.4 Elegant language can sanitize domination

Sakayanagi, Tsukishiro, and Ayanokōji himself can express severe structures through controlled or analytically attractive language.

Compression is aesthetically pleasurable.

That pleasure can obscure externalized cost.

## 39.5 “Dark version” readings are encouraged by stress-register spectacle

Kushida's exposure, Ichinose's collapse, Kei's breakup, and other high-stress scenes are memorable.

Memorability can tempt readers to treat a stress register as the hidden true personality.

The longitudinal Japanese evidence repeatedly rejects that move.

## 39.6 Dossier aesthetics encourage power-scaling even when the story problematizes it

OAA gives readers an official comparison surface.

That surface can become the dominant fandom ontology even though the fiction repeatedly demonstrates:

- voluntary nonavailability;
- domain mismatch;
- hidden capacity;
- social competence;
- development;
- and rule-dependent value.

The form both critiques and feeds the desire to rank.

## 39.7 Fanservice and privacy ethics remain in tension

Year 2 continues to editorially foreground bodies in ways that can compete with the prose's interest in privacy, consent, and controlled visibility.

The correct response is neither to discard the images nor to pretend the tension disappears because the art is official.

## 39.8 Humor can move the reader past responsibility too quickly

School-life comedy is crucial to preserving ordinary humanity.

It can also make coercive or humiliating structures feel socially resolved before the affected person has been repaired.

Chabashira's maid sequence remains the clearest case.

## 39.9 Editorial romance weighting can exceed current fictional settlement

Guidebook and illustration emphasis can make one heroine relation feel narratively privileged.

That is useful evidence of framing and commercial/narrative attention.

It does not replace source-local standards for mutual knowledge, explicit choice, and current relational state.

# 42. A Year-2 taxonomy of formal contradiction

Apparent contradiction in COTE often belongs to one of several different classes.

## 42.1 Register contradiction

The same person sounds different in different relationships or stress states.

This may be ordinary register variation rather than inconsistency.

## 42.2 Self-knowledge contradiction

Later vocabulary names a state that earlier behavior already suggested.

Sakayanagi/Kamuro friendship is a strong example.

This is often revelation of prior state rather than retroactive character rewrite.

## 42.3 Narrator/model contradiction

Ayanokōji's prediction conflicts with another person's actual answer.

This can be evidence of counter-authorship rather than continuity error.

## 42.4 Record/event contradiction

Institutional data or administrative history conflicts with event evidence.

Year 2 explicitly establishes that records can be manipulated under hostile administration.

That raises skepticism without making every record false.

## 42.5 Image/text contradiction

An image foregrounds warmth, vulnerability, or spectacle while narration supplies a different causal or ethical frame.

This is often deliberate counterpoint.

## 42.6 Editorial/fiction contradiction

Guidebook compression appears sharper or simpler than the novels.

Primary fiction retains causal/literary authority; editorial emphasis remains evidence of official framing.

## 42.7 Temporal contradiction

A statement true at V03 can be false at V12 without either reading being erroneous.

Longitudinal state must be preserved.

## 42.8 Prospective/retrospective contradiction

Volume 0 or Second List can recontextualize a prior reading.

The earlier prospective truth remains historically valid at its information boundary unless later evidence directly proves the earlier claim false.

# 44. Relationship between form and counter-authorship

Counter-authorship is not only a theme of Year 2.

It increasingly becomes a formal property of the novels.

Ayanokōji's power depends partly on superior observation.

If the narrative gave only his voice, the reader could easily reproduce his politics of legibility: every character would become a function correctly diagnosed by the superior modeler.

Year 2 repeatedly resists that outcome through form.

- Nanase explains the psychological purpose of `ボク` from inside.
- Horikita owns the Kushida judgment in focalized space.
- Haruka refuses the class's preferred recovery narrative.
- Nagumo receives an interior problem not visible from his public sovereignty.
- Yamamura demands recognition of `存在` beyond usefulness.
- Kei reconstructs the hidden curriculum from the learner's side.
- Ichinose produces an answer Ayanokōji did not have.
- Morishita questions provenance.
- Kōenji refuses the evaluator's frame.
- illustrations restore bodies after strategic abstraction.
- Second List's fiction exceeds its own files.

The formal endpoint can therefore be stated strongly:

> **The series' increasing interest in counter-authorship is matched by an increasing refusal to let its protagonist own narratorial meaning merely because he often owns causal advantage.**

This does not dethrone first-person narration.

It makes first-person narration more interesting by giving it resistance.

# 48. Final synthesis — the Year-2 person becomes harder to own precisely as the file improves

Year 1 ended with a paradox of legibility.

The school, the strategist, the dossier, the illustration, and the narrator could all reveal something real while failing to possess the whole person.

Year 2 does not solve that paradox.

It intensifies it.

The institution acquires OAA and produces richer files.

Ayanokōji becomes more sophisticated at environmental authorship.

Relationships become denser and more information-rich.

The reader receives Volume 0's political genealogy and *Second List*'s retrospective archive.

By ordinary information-theoretic intuition, the cast should therefore become easier to reduce to stable models.

The opposite increasingly happens.

The more information accumulates, the more important the difference becomes between **accurate model** and **owned future**.

Ayanokōji's own written voice is the clearest example.

The `オレ` of Year 2 still thinks through probability, conditions, utility, curriculum, stages, and controllable environments. It can still understate causal authorship and treat another person's future as a problem to be optimized.

The same `オレ` increasingly contains wants that cannot be justified by function.

It can want a trivial appliance.

It can want another person to accept an invitation.

It can want a prediction to fail.

It can recognize a domain as not his to enter.

It can notice an involuntary smile before knowing what it means.

It can value a person's answer more because he did not supply it.

The formal development is therefore not revelation of a warm true self under a cold false self.

It is expansion of permission inside one continuous narrator.

The rest of the cast develops through the same logic.

Horikita's polished judgment becomes corrigible and caring without becoming gushy.

Kei's `あたし` remains socially competent while interior stress reveals what composure cannot settle.

Kushida's cushioning survives the collapse of innocence and becomes chosen public skill.

Hirata's gentle `僕` learns to name jurisdictional overreach.

Sudō grows without being linguistically domesticated.

Ryūen makes contracts and self-authentication more precise without ceasing to sound dangerous.

Ichinose makes warmth strategically opaque and eventually counter-authorial without becoming a generic dark antagonist.

Sakayanagi lets grief and love enter refinement rather than breaking refinement.

Hiyori makes low-pressure speech active.

Nagumo's casual hierarchy acquires a private proof problem.

Nanase turns grammatical self-reference into behavioral technology.

Yamamura makes the cost of low social visibility audible as a demand for `存在`.

The narration increasingly gives those voices forms capable of resisting protagonist annexation.

That is the deeper Year-2 formal movement.

Ayanokōji can continue to own extraordinary causal advantage without automatically owning the story's meaning.

Horikita receives focalized judgment.

Nagumo receives a private problem his public rule does not solve.

Kei receives the learner-side account of the romantic curriculum.

Ichinose gives an answer the modeler cannot provide.

Morishita notices provenance.

Kōenji rejects the evaluator's ontology.

Yamamura insists that usefulness is not enough for social existence.

The V12.5 breakup goes further by forcing the reader to confront the mechanics of focal attribution itself. An emotionally rich passage can feel like access to the protagonist and still belong to someone else's desire. The formal warning is severe: privileged reader access can reproduce the same overconfidence as institutional surveillance if the reader stops asking who actually owns the evidence.

Genre protects the cast from another kind of ownership.

The survival thriller cannot permanently reduce everyone to field assets because the decimal volumes return food, books, shopping, embarrassment, affection, and purposeless company.

The vote tragedy cannot permanently reduce a student to the selected loser because later scenes restore an out-of-school future.

The cultural festival turns staging into both commodity and causal theory.

The institutional hearing makes testimony and public reason dramatic.

Volume 0 turns school conflict into political prehistory and developmental horror without erasing the later ordinary life that makes another future thinkable.

The breakup and transfer aftermath refuse to end the year on a clean competitive scoreboard.

Humor performs the same anti-totalizing work at smaller scale.

A mastermind can fail to understand ordinary consumer desire.

A teacher can become a reluctant marketable maid.

A haunted-house performer can fall and need help.

Two antagonistic girls can become funny precisely because the rivalry is safe enough to support pettiness.

A socially invisible person can expose the cruelty of ordinary comedy when the joke reproduces the fear of being unseen.

Humor therefore humanizes without exonerating.

The visual layer makes the same argument through bodies.

Ayanokōji and Kei can look genuinely intimate while the hidden curriculum remains ethically real.

An offered hand to Nanase can represent recognition while the narration still thinks in disposability.

Chabashira can be visually spectacular while the scene remains a consent problem.

Kamuro and Yamamura can occupy a formally equal procedure while their faces reveal radically different human stakes.

Kei can perform a breakup successfully and still be shown alone inside collapse.

Horikita's body can register an unfamiliar response before her language can classify it.

Illustration does not reveal the final truth behind narration.

It creates another partial truth that the analyst must preserve.

*Second List* then gives Year 2 its most explicit archive.

It measures more.

It summarizes more.

It compares more.

It knows more.

Yet its own contents repeatedly demonstrate the limits of total filing.

Ayanokōji's visible score changes with visibility.

Kōenji controls how much of himself becomes measurable.

Ryūen's political capacity exceeds the axis.

Hiyori's quiet judgment exceeds formal description.

Kei's social coordination is not captured by academic ordinariness.

Kushida's social excellence does not dictate its moral use.

And Ichinose ultimately produces an answer outside Ayanokōji's prediction.

The archive becomes more sophisticated at exactly the moment the series becomes more interested in people who invalidate sophisticated models.

The terminal Year-2 formal proposition is therefore:

> **Development changes permission more often than voice. Knowledge becomes richer more often than complete. The narrator can understand mechanisms better than motives. The image can make a body visible without owning its interior. The guidebook can improve the file without becoming the person. Genre can reweight evidence without repealing prior truth. Humor can restore ordinary humanity without neutralizing harm. And the strongest sign that a model has remained a model rather than becoming a prison is that the represented person can still produce an answer the representation did not contain.**

This is why Year 2's increasingly elaborate machinery of observation does not make the series less literary.

The machinery becomes the literary problem.

The person becomes most visible at the point where visibility stops being enough.
