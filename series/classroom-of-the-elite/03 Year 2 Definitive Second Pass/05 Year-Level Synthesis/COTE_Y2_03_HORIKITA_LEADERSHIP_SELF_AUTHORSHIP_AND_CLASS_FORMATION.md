## Year 2 — Horikita Suzune
### Leadership, self-authorship, and class formation
### From distributed developmental leadership to constitutional office: bounded obligation, plural agency, and the unproven succession of the hidden guarantor

> **Canonical specialist synthesis at the complete Year-2 / Second List boundary.**
>
> This document is the primary Year-2 home for Horikita Suzune's leadership, self-authorship, political judgment, class formation, institutional office, and the succession problem created by Ayanokōji's departure. It does not replace the immutable source-local volume readings. It synthesizes them at `Y2SL`, preserves Volume 0's mandated retrospective position, and imports no Year-3 narrative evidence.

# 0. Scope, primary home, and interpretive controls

The Year-1 Horikita specialist ended with a carefully limited conclusion.

Horikita had become an **integrated self-author** and a **distributed, correctable developmental leader**. She had moved beyond treating ability as static scalar inventory, beyond treating leadership as personal superiority, and beyond treating Manabu's imagined approval as the endpoint of her identity. She had learned to coordinate unlike capacities, expose criteria, accept correction, and preserve the possibility that a weak or difficult person could become more valuable than present measurement predicted.

But the Year-1 document also left a structural problem unresolved.

Her visible polity existed above a shadow constitution.

Ayanokōji still possessed information, deterrence, strategic reserve, and contingency-control capacity that Horikita neither fully knew nor publicly governed. She could increasingly produce answers he had not supplied, but the class had not yet demonstrated that its visible institutions could reproduce their performance after the hidden author withdrew.

Year 2 is where that distinction becomes the central problem.

The governing question for this specialist is therefore not simply:

> **Does Horikita become a better leader?**

The stronger question is:

> **What kind of political order does Horikita build once her own judgment becomes independently authored, and how much of that order remains dependent on an invisible guarantor she cannot fully inspect?**

Year 2 supplies a strong answer to the first half and deliberately withholds the final proof of the second.

Horikita becomes a genuinely independent political actor. She authors contracts, alliances, preservation decisions, institutional settlements, role distributions, and strategic answers that are not reducible to Ayanokōji feeding her a solution. She becomes capable of being corrected by lower-status classmates. She allows self-interest, hostility, friendship, and dissent to remain politically usable motives rather than demanding loyalty as the price of membership. She accepts visible liability for choices that produce real casualties. She enters the student council presidency as an officeholder who can bind predecessor power rather than merely inheriting predecessor style.

At the same time, the class's strongest succession test begins only after the Year-2 boundary.

Ayanokōji still supplies hidden enforcement in V01, environmental design across the year, terminal security in V12, and unilateral control over the timing of his final transfer. `Second List` explicitly treats Horikita's post-transfer stability as a future question rather than a fact already certified.

The specialist therefore uses three separate independence predicates throughout:

1. **independence of judgment** — can Horikita choose an answer she can own even when Ayanokōji prefers another?;
2. **independence of political authorship** — can she create institutions, contracts, alliances, and distributed operating structures that are intelligible as hers rather than as execution of hidden instructions?;
3. **independence of system reproduction** — can the polity continue to perform after the hidden guarantor becomes an external actor rather than internal insurance?

At `Y2SL`:

- the first is **established**;
- the second is **established strongly enough to be canonical**;
- the third is **open as an empirical succession test**.

Any reading that compresses those three into a single yes/no claim loses the most important Year-2 distinction.

## 0.1 Self-authorship does not require causal isolation

This specialist preserves the Year-1 rule:

> **influence is not the opposite of self-authorship.**

Manabu remains formative.

Ayanokōji remains formative.

Sudō, Kushida, Hirata, Kei, Ibuki, Kōenji, Ryūen, Chabashira, the student council, and the class all become causal inputs into Horikita's development.

The relevant question is not whether those influences disappear. It is whether Horikita can metabolize them into judgments whose governing purpose is now hers.

That is why V05 matters so much. The choice to preserve Kushida is not evidence that Horikita has escaped Ayanokōji's influence. It is evidence that influence no longer determines the answer.

Likewise, becoming student-council president does not require rejecting Manabu's example. It requires treating the inherited office as something she must govern through reasons she can defend rather than as a role in which she reproduces his personality.

## 0.2 Visible leadership is not the same as system independence

Horikita's class finishes Year 2 in the provisional A position with 1233 class points. That is real institutional success.

It is not by itself proof that the class is independent of Ayanokōji.

A political system can contain:

- a legitimate visible leader;
- distributed operational competence;
- real internal dissent;
- auditable contracts;
- and still depend on an unseen actor for tail-risk protection.

Year 2 repeatedly shows exactly that configuration.

The proper succession claim is therefore not:

> “Horikita's class succeeded, so Ayanokōji was unnecessary.”

Nor is it:

> “Ayanokōji helped, so Horikita's leadership was fake.”

The stronger formulation is:

> **Horikita's class becomes developed enough that removing the hidden security layer becomes a meaningful experiment. The year ends when that experiment begins, not when its result is known.**

## 0.3 Constitutional leadership is not the same as democracy

This specialist frequently uses words such as **constitutional**, **plural**, **distributed**, **bounded**, and **answerable**.

None means that Horikita's class becomes a democracy in the ordinary institutional sense.

Horikita remains a strong agenda-setting leader. She can make decisions many classmates dislike. She can bargain on behalf of the class. She can prioritize one student's retention over another's. She can occupy institutional office with real authority.

The constitutional claim is narrower and more precise:

> **leadership increasingly operates through publicly intelligible limits on what the leader, the class, or a powerful member may claim from another person.**

The strongest Year-2 examples include:

- the limited scope of the Kōenji bargain;
- the teacher-observed Ryūen alliance;
- the refusal to treat class membership as ownership of Kōenji's full capacity;
- the Kushida compact, which preserves hostility and self-interest rather than demanding emotional submission;
- the Kiryūin/Kiriyama settlement, in which office constrains retaliatory predecessor power;
- and Horikita's willingness to let weak or low-status classmates correct her model.

Constitutionalization means **power is increasingly made answerable to scope, procedure, counterparty standing, and revisability**.

It does not mean all affected people possess equal authority.

## 0.4 Legitimate leadership is not automatically just leadership

Year 2 especially requires this distinction.

Horikita can make a choice that is genuinely hers, publicly own it, defend it coherently, and still impose serious moral cost on someone else.

V05 is the canonical example.

Her preservation of Kushida proves independent judgment.

It does not prove that the preservation was ethically clean.

Airi bears the terminal cost. Haruka bears continuing relational harm. Kushida herself did not initially consent to being preserved as Horikita's project.

The year therefore rejects the equation:

```text
independent choice = legitimate choice = just choice = good outcome
```

Those predicates must be evaluated separately.

## 0.5 Error is not disqualification

One of Horikita's most important developments is learning to remain a leader **after** a judgment becomes contestable.

She does not mature into infallibility.

She becomes increasingly capable of:

- saying what she decided;
- exposing the reason;
- accepting that another person may remain unconvinced;
- identifying which part of an earlier decision she now thinks was wrong;
- preserving the fact of injury;
- and continuing to govern without demanding retrospective moral unanimity.

Her V06 response to Haruka is exemplary. She does not falsely apologize for a judgment she still believes she would repeat. She instead identifies her earlier promise as reckless and accepts that the broken expectation was genuinely hers to answer for.

This is **corrigibility without counterfeit repentance**.

## 0.6 Volume 0 cannot retroactively rewrite Horikita's reader-level history

Volume 0 primarily changes the reader's model of Ayanokōji, Atsuomi, and the White Room lineage of environmental authorship.

It does not directly supply new Horikita development.

Its effect on this specialist is therefore retrospective and bounded.

The reader can now understand the hidden security layer above Horikita's visible polity as part of a deeper genealogy of authored environments and preference engineering. But Horikita cannot be made to know that genealogy before the source gives her access to it.

Volume 0 is therefore `RETROSPECTIVE_RECONTEXTUALIZATION`, not a secret Horikita character beat.

## 0.7 `Second List` is evidence about the boundary, not advance certification of the future

`Second List` does three important things for Horikita:

1. it officially recognizes her Year-2 leadership growth;
2. it shows how compact institutional ratings still under-describe that growth;
3. it explicitly marks the class's post-transfer stability as a future problem.

It does **not** certify the result of that future problem.

The guidebook therefore strengthens the distinction between independent judgment and independent system reproduction rather than closing it.

# 1. Executive thesis

Year 1 teaches Horikita that leadership cannot be reduced to superior personal ability.

Year 2 teaches her that **even distributed leadership is insufficient unless the relations among unequal people become governable**.

That is the major transition.

The Year-1 Horikita becomes capable of seeing trajectory rather than static weakness, coordinating specialists rather than monopolizing competence, accepting correction, and integrating influence without reducing independence to isolation.

The Year-2 Horikita increasingly asks a second-order political question:

> **What may one actor legitimately require from another, under what agreement, with what public reason, and with what right to dissent or refuse?**

This appears repeatedly.

With first-year Class D, she does not merely seek partners. She tries to create a future reciprocal relation rather than exploit a temporary bargaining asymmetry.

With Kōenji, she moves from frustration at unavailable talent toward bounded obligation: he owes what he agreed to, not every future use the class can imagine.

With Kushida, the year forces her to discover that preservation is not enough. A leader can save someone from expulsion and still fail to make the person's future self-authored. The V06 compact becomes more legitimate precisely when Kushida can contribute for `私のためだけ` rather than because Horikita demands gratitude.

With Ryūen, cooperation no longer requires trust or moral likeness. It can be legitimate because duties are explicit, reciprocal, witnessed, and limited.

With Ibuki, antagonistic familiarity becomes friendship-like enough to alter the moral cost of sacrifice without converting political duty into simple favoritism.

With the student council, Horikita learns that office is not personal sovereignty. The Kiryūin/Kiriyama settlement matters because she asks what the council is actually entitled to do, not merely what punishment would satisfy her preferred outcome.

With weak or low-status classmates, Year 2 repeatedly shows that leadership must remain falsifiable. Ike can produce a better tactical answer. Onodera can teach Sudō. Kushida can provide social infrastructure Horikita cannot reproduce. Hirata and Kei can exercise authority in domains where her own style is inferior.

With Ayanokōji, the issue becomes succession.

Horikita is no longer his proxy.

She becomes one of the clearest examples in the series of a developed person who can generate answers the developer does not own.

But the relation remains asymmetrical in a different dimension. He still controls crucial information about the class's future. He prepares her polity for his absence while withholding when the absence will begin. He can genuinely want purposeless time with her, genuinely care for her after defeat, genuinely value her independent judgment, and still retain temporal jurisdiction over the separation that most directly tests that independence.

That is why the Year-2 endpoint cannot be summarized as “Horikita no longer needs Ayanokōji.”

The stronger conclusion is:

> **Horikita becomes independently capable before the corpus can prove that the polity she leads is independently self-sustaining.**

Her mature Year-2 leadership model is best described as **constitutional developmental pluralism**.

“Developmental” means present weakness is not destiny.

“Pluralism” means collective capacity is distributed across unlike people with motives the leader does not own.

“Constitutional” means the leader increasingly recognizes boundaries on obligation, preserves contestable motives, makes important bargains inspectable, accepts correction, and treats office as a structure of answerable power rather than a license to convert superior judgment into unlimited jurisdiction.

The model is powerful and morally imperfect.

Its principal virtue is that it makes heterogeneous agency collectively usable without requiring everyone to become Horikita.

Its principal danger is that Horikita can still become convinced that preserving another person's developmental possibility gives her standing to impose the cost of that preservation on unwilling third parties.

Its principal structural vulnerability is the hidden guarantor.

Its strongest Year-2 achievement is that the guarantor can finally leave without the class obviously collapsing at the moment of departure.

Its strongest unresolved test is whether that confidence survives contact with a world in which he is no longer inside the polity at all.

# 3. Year-2 starting point — the leader already exists; the constitution does not

Year 2 begins after the major personal identity break of Y1V11.5.

Horikita is no longer trying to become a copy of Manabu.

She already knows that:

- she needs other people;
- ability changes;
- expertise is distributed;
- public criteria matter;
- Kōenji cannot simply be requisitioned;
- Ayanokōji is far more capable than his visible record suggests;
- and class leadership creates obligations that cannot be reduced to “choose the strongest person.”

What she does **not** yet possess is a fully articulated constitutional theory of how those unequal actors should relate.

Year 2 supplies that problem through increasingly explicit conflicts over:

- contract scope;
- information rights;
- preservation and sacrifice;
- office;
- protected dissent;
- class membership;
- friendship across enemy polities;
- and succession after hidden security exits.

The opening state can therefore be summarized as:

> **Horikita enters Year 2 with a developmental theory of people and an increasingly legitimate visible office, but without a proven constitution for the ownership, limits, and succession of collective power.**

# 4. Y2V01 — reciprocal political design replaces corrective leadership as the main scale

Y2V01 is the first proof that Horikita's Year-1 development survives the change of school year.

The cross-year pairing exam creates an asymmetric market.

Second-years face expulsion risk. First-years ordinarily face much lower penalties. High-ability first-years can therefore be priced, bought, recruited, or strategically withheld.

The other class leaders respond through constitutions already visible in Year 1:

- Sakayanagi uses prestige and points;
- Ryūen manipulates the market and treats recruitment as intelligence collection;
- Ichinose mobilizes trust and protects weaker students.

Horikita cannot simply imitate any of them.

Her answer is institutional.

She attempts a class-to-class agreement with first-year Class D based on no internal price hierarchy, no academic exclusion, and a future `対等な協力関係`—an equal cooperative relationship.

The significance is not that the plan succeeds perfectly.

The significance is **what she is trying to build**.

## 4.1 Rescue priority shows the Year-1 ability lesson has become operational routine

Horikita does not begin by maximizing the pairing ceiling.

She prioritizes preventing expulsion.

Strong first-year partners are valuable partly because they can rescue weaker second-years rather than merely amplify already strong students.

This is an important change in the meaning of leadership.

The early Horikita would have been tempted to treat weak students as a drag on the collective maximum.

The Year-2 Horikita treats the class as a polity whose leadership obligations include ensuring that present weakness does not automatically convert into abandonment.

That is not egalitarian denial of ability difference.

It is an anti-disposability presumption operating inside a competitive institution.

## 4.2 Delegation to Hirata becomes normal rather than remedial

Horikita immediately recognizes that academic pairing and relationship acquisition are different problems.

Hirata's social accessibility is therefore a distinct governing resource.

The important developmental fact is that she no longer experiences this as a humiliating concession to a capacity she lacks.

Distributed leadership has become the default architecture.

Horikita does not need to be the best recruiter in order to govern recruitment well.

## 4.3 Sudō's outdated OAA becomes a practical theory of developmental time

OAA still carries Sudō's earlier low academic state.

Horikita already knows that the file lags behind the person.

She estimates his current academic trajectory more favorably and takes his sustained spring-break work seriously.

The shift is conceptually important:

```text
current rating -> evidence about a trajectory
not
current rating -> permanent political worth
```

This is one of the cleanest demonstrations that the Year-1 lesson has become governing habit.

## 4.4 She can admire Ichinose without becoming Ichinose

Horikita recognizes Ichinose's trust-first method as impressive.

The self-authorship result is that admiration no longer automatically creates an imitation project.

She knows Ichinose can do things she cannot easily reproduce.

Her mature response is to construct a different institution fitted to her own temperament and resources.

That is a major contrast with the Manabu-oriented early self.

## 4.5 Nanase's proposed leadership coup reveals fiduciary reasoning

Nanase offers an apparent shortcut: displace Hōsen's authority and create the alliance through a more cooperative first-year leader.

The proposal would help Horikita immediately.

She rejects it because a factional seizure would damage the first-year class's own political future and undermine the reciprocal relationship she is trying to create.

The choice shows that her bargaining logic is no longer purely class-extractive.

She can recognize obligations to the institutional viability of a counterparty.

## 4.6 The volume still exposes the shadow constitution

Horikita's design does not itself solve Hōsen.

He possesses information she does not: the hidden bounty on Ayanokōji.

Ayanokōji's knife-trap countermeasure changes the feasible bargaining environment beneath the visible negotiation.

The result is the first Year-2 instance of the central distinction:

> **the political goal can be independently Horikita-authored while the enforcement layer remains partly Ayanokōji-authored.**

`Y1H-012` therefore remains partial.

# 5. Y2V02 — authority acquires scope

V02 turns the Year-1 question “who should lead?” into the more constitutional question:

> **what does leadership entitle the leader to require?**

Three Horikita developments matter most.

## 5.1 The student-council presidency becomes her goal

Horikita tells Nagumo that she intends to become student-council president.

Ayanokōji judges the statement genuine and concludes that the domain in which he should directly interfere with that path has passed.

The developmental meaning is subtle.

The presidency is not free of Manabu's influence. Nor is it free of Ayanokōji's earlier nudging.

But the goal is now sufficiently integrated into Horikita's own political identity that continuing to treat it as borrowed would erase the actual development.

## 5.2 Individual discretion limits class grouping authority

Horikita explicitly recognizes that class grouping guidance does not possess absolute coercive force.

Final choice belongs to the individual student.

This matters because her Year-1 leadership once tended to infer that collective need supplied obvious standing to direct capable members.

V02 makes a different principle explicit:

> **coordination authority can be real without being total.**

## 5.3 The Kōenji bargain creates bounded obligation

Kōenji offers present contribution in exchange for future freedom.

Horikita does not accept a vague covenant.

She defines conditions.

If he takes first place alone, he gains the promised freedom. If he fails, he owes measurable future contribution.

The contract is important not because it “tames” Kōenji.

It does the opposite.

It creates a legitimate way to obtain collective benefit **without claiming the person**.

The agreement recognizes:

- his capacity is his;
- his future freedom has value to him;
- the class can negotiate for specific performance;
- and the resulting obligation has a scope.

This becomes one of the most durable constitutional precedents of Year 2.

## 5.4 Ibuki demonstrates relationship-specific motivation rather than universal persuasion

Horikita does not become warm or charismatic in a generalized way.

With Ibuki, she can use rivalry, irritation, and provocation as motivational grammar because their relationship makes those speech acts legible.

This is an early Year-2 sign that leadership can become **audience-specific without becoming manipulative totalization**.

She does not need every collaborator to be moved by the same reason.

# 6. Y2V03-Y2V04 — dispersed authority and reputation-based enforcement

The island exam removes many ordinary visibility structures.

Leadership becomes less about standing in front of a classroom and more about whether agreements and reputations remain meaningful when the leader is not physically present.

## 6.1 Ayanokōji's non-intervention is itself evidence about Horikita's current state

In V03, Ayanokōji explicitly thinks that the current Horikita can identify her own limit.

He does not supervise her survival minute by minute.

This is not proof of total class independence.

It is strong evidence that Horikita has become a person whom even the hidden strategist can treat as capable of self-regulation under uncertainty.

## 6.2 The Kōenji contract has teeth without mechanical ownership

The bargain has no literal coercive mechanism forcing Kōenji to perform.

Its force partly comes from reputation and future reciprocity.

Breaking a promise to a leader who is increasingly recognized as legitimate would alter Kōenji's own future claims on collective assistance.

The political principle is important:

> **a polity can create meaningful obligation through reciprocal standing rather than through direct command.**

Kōenji remains free enough that his cooperation is meaningful.

The class remains organized enough that refusal has relational consequences.

## 6.3 V04's Ibuki cooperation makes reasoning contestable rather than hidden

Horikita and Ibuki coordinate under pressure against Amasawa.

The relationship remains adversarial.

What matters is that coordination occurs through explicit exchange rather than through one party silently authoring the other's response.

This is a small-scale model of Horikita's broader Year-2 constitutional style:

- disagreement remains visible;
- motives need not converge;
- and cooperation can still occur.

## 6.4 Kōenji's performance closes the first major bargain cycle

When Kōenji satisfies the negotiated condition, the legitimacy of the contract now depends on Horikita respecting the freedom she promised.

That later matters in V05 and V10.

A leader who treats contracts as temporary instruments loses the constitutional advantage of having made them explicit.

Horikita increasingly does the opposite.

# 7. Y2V04.5 — secrets reveal that knowledge itself can become coercive power

V04.5 is quieter in class-government terms, but it sharpens the Kushida problem before V05 forces a decision.

Horikita now exists inside a relationship where private knowledge has political force.

Knowing another person's secret is not neutral.

It can create:

- leverage;
- fear;
- dependence;
- strategic silence;
- and pressure that remains coercive even without a direct threat.

This is crucial preparation for V05 because it blocks an easy moral story in which preserving Kushida simply means “seeing the good in her.”

The problem is constitutional:

> **how can a polity retain a socially powerful actor whose knowledge of other people is itself a source of asymmetric power?**

The answer cannot be “trust her because Horikita decided to.”

V06 will have to produce a more legitimate relationship structure than that.

# 8. Y2V05 — consequential independence becomes morally expensive

V05 is Horikita's most important Year-2 sovereignty test.

The unanimous-vote exam forces a class to convert individual anonymous will into a collective terminal outcome.

The institutional design is already coercive: a single veto can block the class, but unanimity means that once the class accepts expulsion, somebody must bear the cost.

Horikita's class therefore enters a situation in which leadership cannot remain abstract.

## 8.1 The class recognizes Horikita's office without making her personally irreplaceable

The class gives the Protect Point to Horikita because losing its leader would impose unusually high institutional cost.

Later, a substantial minority is still willing to vote for her expulsion.

Those facts can coexist.

The analytical result is:

> **political legitimacy is not the same thing as universal affection.**

Horikita is valuable as governor without needing to become the emotional center of every classmate.

## 8.2 Promise-keeping with Kōenji becomes constitutional under pressure

When Kōenji's vote becomes inconvenient, Sudō proposes breaking the earlier agreement.

Horikita refuses.

She does not retroactively declare emergency power over the contract.

She changes the incentive through a new bargain instead.

Kōenji explicitly recognizes her reliability.

This is one of the strongest proofs that bounded obligation has become more than rhetoric.

## 8.3 Preserving Kushida proves independent judgment

Horikita explicitly refuses to outsource the decision to Ayanokōji.

She recognizes his competence and still asks whether leaving the answer entirely to him can be right.

Her answer is no.

This closes `Y1H-013`:

> **Horikita can consequentially disagree with Ayanokōji and sustain the disagreement.**

# 9. Y2V06 - responsibility becomes distributed agency

V06 is the volume in which the V05 decision stops being a one-night sovereignty test and becomes a problem of governance.

Horikita cannot reverse Airi's expulsion. She cannot require Haruka to forgive her. She cannot make Kushida grateful. She cannot make the class forget what the unanimous-vote examination revealed about its members. The only available question is what kind of order she builds from the damaged state she helped create.

The answer is substantially more constitutional than the V05 decision itself.

## 9.1 Chabashira permits the leader to be developmental rather than perfect

Chabashira's changed posture matters because Horikita is no longer being evaluated as a leader who must avoid error in order to deserve authority. The class has already experienced the danger of adults and leaders treating one irreversible mistake as the total definition of a person.

The relevant correction is not permissiveness. It is the capacity to distinguish:

- an error from an identity;
- a bad consequence from total political invalidation;
- and retrospective responsibility from ritual self-erasure.

This becomes important when Haruka confronts Horikita. Horikita does not offer a false apology that would imply she now believes the Kushida-preservation judgment itself was insincere or wholly wrong. She instead identifies a narrower error: she made a promise before she knew what she would actually be willing to do.

That distinction is politically valuable precisely because it is costly. A leader who apologizes for everything after resistance is not necessarily corrigible; she may merely be evasive. Horikita's mature form of correction is to preserve the judgment she still owns while revising the part of her conduct she now regards as indefensible.

## 9.2 The Kushida compact transfers ownership of the ongoing project

The strongest affirmative development in V06 is the transition from imposed preservation to self-authored participation.

V05 belongs to Horikita. Kushida did not authorize the rescue.

V06 begins to belong to Kushida.

Horikita abandons the idea that Kushida must cooperate because:

- the class morally deserves it;
- Horikita saved her;
- gratitude is owed;
- friendship should be restored;
- or the exposed private self should be purified into the public persona.

Instead, she accepts a contract in which Kushida contributes for Kushida's own future.

The mature constitutional principle is:

> **a polity does not need every useful member to love the polity or the leader; it needs terms under which the member has a reason of her own to participate.**

This is why the hostility of the compact is not a failure of reconciliation. It is part of its legitimacy.

Kushida is permitted to remain:

- angry;
- self-interested;
- skeptical of Horikita;
- socially skilled;
- and politically useful.

The left-handed handshake is therefore not sentimental closure. It formalizes a relation in which cooperation no longer depends on emotional counterfeit. Source-local anchor: `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para0470-0525`.

## 9.3 The anti-gag-order matters as much as the compact

Horikita also refuses the fantasy that the class can restore the old Kushida simply by suppressing knowledge of what happened.

A forced promise of silence would reproduce the very structure that made Kushida dangerous: public order purchased through privately concentrated information and coercive leverage.

The better order is messier.

People know more.

They may dislike what they know.

Kushida remains capable.

The class must operate with a changed public/private constitution rather than trying to recover the old one.

This is a major movement away from leader-centered repair. The leader does not get to decide that social peace requires everyone to pretend the rupture never occurred.

## 9.4 Haruka proves that dissent can survive membership

Haruka's confrontation is not a failed reintegration simply because she does not forgive.

Her continuing anger proves something politically important: the class can contain a member who regards the leader's defining choice as morally injurious and still has standing to say so.

Horikita does not purchase unity by demanding moral agreement. Source-local anchor: `COTE:Y2V06:spine14:para1160-1311`.

This does not eliminate the injury. It changes the constitutional meaning of the injury.

The polity is becoming capable of holding:

- a leader who still owns the decision;
- a victim-survivor who still condemns it;
- a preserved member whose continued participation has become self-authored;
- and classmates who must work without a single reconciled interpretation of the event.

That is a more mature political condition than compulsory consensus.

## 9.5 The Ryūen alliance makes inter-polity cooperation auditable

Horikita's sports-festival alliance with Ryūen is another important change because it is both adversarial and formal.

She does not ask him to trust her.

She does not pretend class rivalry has disappeared.

She authors the proposal, negotiates its scope, and converts it into a written, teacher-observed agreement.

This produces several mature features at once:

1. **authorship** - the proposal is Horikita's rather than Ayanokōji's hidden instruction;
2. **boundedness** - the agreement concerns a defined objective rather than general loyalty;
3. **auditability** - terms are externalized rather than residing in private memory;
4. **adversarial compatibility** - cooperation does not require the end of rivalry;
5. **future accountability** - violation can be evaluated against an explicit object.

This is constitutional leadership in a compact form.

## 9.6 The sports festival is a distributed-agency test, not proof of full independence

Ayanokōji is physically absent from the live event.

Horikita's class nevertheless functions through a broad distribution of capacity:

- Horikita coordinates;
- Hirata and Kei perform social leadership;
- Kushida uses a relationship network no one else can reproduce;
- Sudō and Onodera become the primary athletic pair;
- other classmates solve local problems rather than escalating every uncertainty to one hidden solver.

The class finishes first. Source-local route: `COTE:Y2V06:spine15:para0001-0295`; `spine16:para0380-0555`; `spine17:para0285-0292`.

This is strong evidence that direct Ayanokōji command is no longer required for substantial performance.

It is not yet proof of system independence.

Ayanokōji helped author the environment of the test, including his own absence. The proper inference is therefore narrower:

> **the class can now generate successful action without continuous hidden command, even though the hidden actor still influences the conditions under which that autonomy is tested.**

That distinction becomes the template for the rest of Year 2.

## 9.7 Peer teaching is the deepest succession evidence in the volume

The most important succession evidence is not Horikita successfully issuing more instructions.

It is classmates teaching one another.

Onodera's role in Sudō's anger regulation is especially diagnostic. Sudō was once one of Horikita's central examples of a person whom static merit judgment would have discarded or misread. By V06, he can receive a technique from a peer and deploy it under pressure.

The developmental chain has therefore changed from:

`leader -> weak member`

to:

`leader-built environment -> peer expertise -> self-regulating member -> collective capacity`.

That is what makes the class potentially generative rather than merely well-managed.

## 9.8 The training-wheels metaphor makes succession explicit

Ayanokōji's private training-wheels model is not Horikita's knowledge.

That asymmetry matters.

From his side, the class is entering a succession experiment. He intends to reduce internal support, eventually leave, and discover whether the polity can oppose him.

From Horikita's side, he is increasingly indispensable.

The same relation therefore contains two incompatible temporal models (`COTE:Y2V06:spine17:para0318-0337`):

- **Horikita:** valuable collaborator whose continued presence is assumed;
- **Ayanokōji:** temporary guarantor preparing withdrawal.

V06 establishes the developmental direction while also creating the future disclosure problem.

The class may become stronger because the training wheels are being removed. Horikita is not yet being told when removal will happen.

# 10. Y2V07-Y2V08 - constitutional pluralization and relational personhood

V07 and V08 matter less because they introduce a single new governing doctrine than because they broaden the number of domains in which Horikita's leadership can coexist with other people's authorship.

## 10.1 Y2V07 - causal authorship without total ownership

- social reach;
- presentation;
- logistics;
- customer interaction;
- practical labor;
- interpersonal judgment;

By this point, Hirata, Kei, Kushida, Sudō, Onodera, and other classmates are not merely people Horikita uses because she lacks time.

Their domains have political standing.

A mature Horikita decision increasingly asks:

> **Who has the relevant knowledge, relationship, competence, or legitimacy to act here?**

rather than:

> **How can the leader personally impose the correct answer?**

That change is one of the deepest Year-2 continuities with the Year-1 specialist.

Relationship does not automatically become veto.

It changes the cost function.

That distinction becomes explicit in V10 when the possibility of Ibuki's removal creates hesitation without producing an automatic doctrinal exemption.

## 10.4 The Sudō rejection tests whether development has become self-sustaining

Horikita rejects Sudō romantically.

The later corpus does not support that collapse.

This makes Sudō one of the strongest proofs that Horikita's influence can eventually stop owning the learner.

She mattered causally.

She does not remain the sole purpose of what he became.

That is an important contrast with the more difficult Kushida case, where self-authorship has to be deliberately rebuilt after an imposed rescue.

# 12. Y2V09 - strategic victory becomes constitutional office

V09 is one of the most important Horikita volumes in Year 2 because three previously separate capacities converge:

- strategic authorship;
- class leadership;
- institutional office.

The result is not merely that she becomes student-council president.

It is that she begins governing questions about what office may legitimately do.

## 12.1 The cooperative exam validates developmental investment

Horikita solves the central scoring architecture of the cooperative written examination independently.

Her class defeats Sakayanagi's despite lower raw correctness because the relevant problem is not simply aggregate academic ceiling. It is allocation, matching, calibration, and the conversion of differentiated capacity into institutional score.

The victory therefore validates a long-running Horikita thesis:

> **a class can become stronger by developing and coordinating people whose present scalar measurements do not look elite.**

This is the practical answer to her earliest meritocratic error.

The weak member is not valuable because weakness is morally beautiful. The weak member can become valuable because ability is domain-specific, developmental, relationally coordinated, and institutionally converted.

## 12.2 Becoming president does not mean becoming Manabu

The presidency is meaningful precisely because the desire for it has become Horikita's.

She does not need to purge Manabu's influence to prove self-authorship.

Nor does she need to imitate his personality to prove continuity.

The relevant test is whether she can occupy inherited office under reasons she can publicly own.

V09 supplies strong positive evidence.

## 12.3 The Kiriyama/Nagumo settlement is Horikita's first major constitutional act

The Kiryūin/Kiriyama/Nagumo dispute forces Horikita to distinguish:

- personal dislike;
- predecessor entitlement;
- formal authority;
- constituency-owned resources;
- retaliation;
- and legitimate remedy.

Her settlement does not ask which powerful person she likes most. Source-local route: `COTE_Y2_V09_DEEP_READING.md`, especially the Kiryūin/Kiriyama constitutional-settlement sequence and `Y1H-015` adjudication.

It asks what each officeholder is entitled to do.

The result is constitutional rather than charismatic:

- Kiriyama is made answerable;
- apology and responsibility are preserved;
- Nagumo is prevented from converting presidential power into retaliatory cancellation of a previously earned right;
- pooled resources are treated as bounded by the constituency from which they arose;
- and no invented punishment is added merely to demonstrate dominance.

The key maturity is **scope**.

Power is legitimate only within a defined jurisdiction.

## 12.4 Kushida's council recruitment shows how Horikita has learned from V05

Horikita's later recruitment of Kushida should be read beside the V05 rescue.

She does not say:

> I saved you, therefore serve me.

She identifies reasons Kushida can appropriate:

- status;
- influence;
- visibility;
- utility;
- movement toward Class A;
- and the opportunity to use a capacity that remains real after exposure.

The recruitment is therefore a small but important reparative development in Horikita's leadership method.

The person does not have to accept Horikita's moral framing in order to enter the institution.

## 12.5 Presidency strengthens Y1H-015 but does not completely close it

`Y1H-015` asks whether Horikita can use student-council power without reproducing predecessor blind spots.

V09 gives substantial affirmative evidence.

She can:

- bind a predecessor;
- reason from constituency and entitlement rather than personal preference;
- resist retaliatory use of office;
- recruit someone for reasons that person can own;
- and occupy the presidency without treating Manabu or Nagumo as a personality template.

The handoff state remains appropriately partial because the corpus does not yet provide every kind of institutional stress the office could face.

The correct conclusion is not "Horikita has solved institutional power."

It is:

> **the presidency is now demonstrably Horikita-authored, and her first major constitutional actions are compatible with the Year-1 model of bounded, correctable developmental leadership.**

# 14. Y2V10 - protection without possession

V10 is a particularly useful test of whether Horikita's developmental leadership has become capable of protecting people without treating protection as ownership.

One of the strongest signs of a constitutional polity is not that weak members become obedient.

When Ibuki becomes vulnerable, Horikita's accumulated relation with her matters.

Friendship does not prove:

- that Horikita will always protect Ibuki;
- that cross-class loyalty outranks class duty;
- or that prior rivalry has disappeared.

It proves that a strategically replaceable opponent has become a historically specific person whose loss now carries additional meaning (`Y2V10-E064-Y2V10-E071`).

It is not yet a categorical decision rule.

## 14.3 Kōenji remains protected by the scope of his bargain

Horikita continues to interpret the Kōenji agreement narrowly.

Class membership does not give her a general claim on his full capacity.

His past contribution does not create infinite debt.

And the leader does not use a protection mechanism merely because she possesses the power to do so.

The contract interpretation is tracked in `Y2V10-E102-Y2V10-E109`.

This preserves the central constitutional distinction:

`capacity to command or protect != entitlement to command or protect`.

## 14.4 Crisis rhetoric becomes truthful rather than omniscient

Under pressure, Horikita does not promise a certainty she does not possess.

She asks for trust while acknowledging uncertainty.

That is a meaningful leadership change.

Early Horikita often treated correct judgment as the foundation of authority. Mature Horikita can ask people to follow a decision whose outcome is not guaranteed, provided the reasons and responsibility remain visible.

This is political trust rather than technocratic certainty.

## 14.5 The low-stakes Ayanokōji exchange matters because it is not strategic proof

Ayanokōji's involuntary smile during an ordinary exchange with Horikita is important relational evidence (`Y2V10-E072-Y2V10-E079`; `COTE:Y2V10:spine14:para0390-0412`).

- she notices the anomaly;
- their relation has acquired a history not exhausted by leader/developer utility.

That matters later because recognition, care, and succession are operating inside a real relationship rather than a purely instrumental training project.

Early Horikita's self-concept tied authority too closely to demonstrable superiority.

Year-2 Horikita can remain politically real after being decisively outperformed.

## 16.2 Wanting Ayanokōji's praise does not cancel self-authorship

Horikita acknowledges that she wanted his recognition and praise (`COTE:Y2V12:spine17:para0473-para0542`).

Recognition from Ayanokōji matters to her because:

- he has seen more of her development than most classmates;
- she recognizes his unusual capacity;
- their relationship contains challenge, collaboration, and accumulated intimacy;
- and his judgment therefore has high personal weight.

But Year 2 has already established that high-value recognition is not the same as obedience.

The strongest counterexample is V05: she chooses an answer he would not choose.

The correct formulation is:

> **Horikita wants recognition from a person whose judgment she values while retaining the ability to make consequential decisions that his judgment does not authorize.**

That is attachment inside self-authorship, not evidence against it.

## 16.3 Ayanokōji's care is real and politically incomplete

After the defeat, Ayanokōji gives direct physical and verbal support.

He also directs Horikita toward her classmates as the people from whom future support should increasingly come.

That is developmentally coherent with the training-wheels project.

But the same scene exists inside a larger nondisclosure structure.

He knows the internal support relationship is ending.

Horikita does not.

Care and temporal asymmetry therefore coexist.

## 16.4 Terminal security remains hidden

The class's final result still depends on Ayanokōji after Horikita loses her representative contest.

This is why `Y1H-012` remains only partially answered.

Horikita has become capable of visible leadership and substantial independent authorship.

The class has not yet demonstrated that the terminal-security layer can disappear without altering outcomes.

## 16.5 Y1H-016 remains strictly open

Horikita witnesses herself fail.

She does not witness Ayanokōji genuinely fail.

That difference cannot be blurred.

`Y1H-016` requires a real Ayanokōji error, defeat, loss of control, or incapacity that Horikita must recognize and interpret.

Voluntary withdrawal does not count.

Concealment does not count.

A result he still guarantees does not count.

At `Y2SL`, the question remains an abstention zone.

# 17. Y2V12.5 - the training wheels leave; the succession test begins

- successful class coordination;
- strategic adaptation;

It does not prove the class can reproduce the result after Ayanokōji becomes external to the polity.

The outcome measures what the current system achieved, not what a future altered system will achieve.

Horikita speaks to Ayanokōji from within an assumed shared future.

She wants to become someone he recognizes.

This creates a radical timetable asymmetry.

It is not reciprocal enough that she has been given standing in the decision about when the internal developmental relation ends.

## 17.3 Loneliness is evidence of attachment, not evidence of political incapacity

When Ayanokōji leaves the celebration without telling her, Horikita experiences the absence as painful (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine21:para0097-para0105`).

That response should not be pathologized or converted into proof that she cannot lead without him.

The training-wheels metaphor is now enacted.

But it is not a clean constitutional succession.

Horikita has not been given the information required to co-author the meaning, timing, or preparation for Ayanokōji's departure.

The mature endpoint is therefore:

> **Horikita is more capable of standing without Ayanokōji than ever before, while being denied the truth that would allow her to author the meaning of his departure before it occurs.**

This is why Year 2 can simultaneously establish her independence of judgment and leave the ethics of the succession unresolved.

- preserve a person he would remove;
- author institutions he did not dictate;
- and continue to defend a judgment he regards as irrational by his model.

Recognition matters.

It is not the sovereign source of her judgment.


# 19. The mature Year-2 model - constitutional developmental pluralism

The Year-1 phrase **distributed, correctable developmental leadership** remains valid, but Year 2 adds a more political layer.

By `Y2SL`, Horikita's strongest governing form is:

> **constitutional developmental pluralism**

The phrase is intentionally composite.

- **developmental** because present weakness is not treated as total future worth;
- **plural** because different people may contribute through different capacities, motives, relationships, and local authorities;
- **constitutional** because increasingly important relations are bounded by scope, procedure, reciprocal standing, and the possibility of correction.

This is not a utopia and not a democracy claim. It is a description of how Horikita's Year-2 leadership increasingly makes power answerable.

## 19.1 Bounded obligation

The Kōenji agreements are the clearest example.

Horikita does not treat class membership as a deed transferring all private capacity to the collective.

An obligation becomes legitimate when:

- the object is defined;
- the exchange is intelligible;
- the counterparty retains standing;
- and the leader does not expand the claim after performance merely because further contribution would be useful.

This is a political theory of membership.

## 19.2 Auditable procedure

The Ryūen alliance and the student-council settlements externalize terms.

The mature Horikita increasingly prefers arrangements that can be inspected by actors other than herself.

That matters because private intention is not enough to make power legitimate.

A leader who says "trust me" while controlling all terms remains structurally dangerous even if she is benevolent.

Horikita's growing reliance on contracts, reasons, witnesses, and explicit role boundaries makes her power more contestable.

## 19.3 Distributed expertise

Horikita's polity becomes strongest when leadership stops pretending that rank predicts domain competence.

Examples include:

- Hirata's mediation;
- Kei's social calibration;
- Kushida's network and memory;
- Sudō and Onodera's athletic competence;
- Ike's local strategic correction;
- and Kōenji's voluntarily available ceiling.

The mature leader's job is not to absorb these capacities.

It is to make a political environment in which they can matter without requiring the people who possess them to become copies of the leader.

## 19.4 Legitimate dissent

Kushida can hate Horikita and still cooperate.

Haruka can condemn the V05 decision and still possess standing in the class.

Kōenji can remain ideologically detached while being bound by specific bargains.

Ibuki can preserve rivalry while becoming personally significant.

A mature Horikita order therefore distinguishes:

`disagreement / dislike / non-loyal motive`

from:

`treason / uselessness / expulsion-worthy nonmembership`.

That distinction is one of the sharpest breaks from her earliest political theory.

## 19.5 Visible responsibility

Horikita's authority increasingly requires her to be the person who can be blamed.

This is especially important in V05-V06.

She cannot place all moral responsibility on:

- the exam;
- Ayanokōji;
- Kushida;
- class necessity;
- or later utility.

She must say which judgment was hers.

Public responsibility does not cleanse the outcome. It is a condition of accountable leadership.

## 19.6 Corrigibility

A political system becomes stronger when the leader can update without losing identity.

Horikita's Year-2 correction repertoire includes:

- learning from lower-status members;
- admitting a promise was careless;
- accepting uncertainty;
- soliciting specialist judgment;
- and remaining in office after decisive personal failure.

This is not indecision.

It is the capacity to distinguish commitment from infallibility.

## 19.7 Office without predecessor imitation

The student-council presidency closes an important self-authorship loop.

Horikita can inherit an institution shaped by Manabu and transformed by Nagumo without becoming either man.

Her legitimacy lies in what she does with inherited power:

- define scope;
- preserve counterparty standing;
- constrain retaliation;
- make reasons public;
- and accept that office creates duties different from private preference.

Influence has become integrated rather than obeyed.

## 19.8 Relation enters judgment without becoming automatic capture

Year 2 also makes Horikita less willing to treat people as exchangeable units.

This is not simply "empathy."

It is a better representation of political cost.

Friendship with Ibuki, history with Sudō, adversarial cooperation with Kushida, attachment to Ayanokōji, and familiarity across classes all increase the amount of person-specific information inside a decision.

The danger is capture: private relation could override office.

The evidence through Y2SL instead shows **friction** more often than automatic capture. Relation complicates the decision before it determines it.

# 20. Self-authorship - from Manabu imitation to independently inhabited office

Horikita's self-authorship is best understood as a change in the status of inherited standards.

At the beginning of the series, Manabu functions not merely as an admired brother but as an external court of worth.

The problem is not that she has a role model.

The problem is that she lacks an independently governed criterion for deciding when the role model's values should be accepted, modified, or rejected.

Year 1 begins the correction.

Year 2 institutionalizes it.

## 20.1 Influence becomes material rather than command

By Year 2, Manabu's example remains part of Horikita's political vocabulary.

But it is material she works with.

She can:

- value discipline without reproducing emotional distance;
- seek the presidency without making office a memorial to him;
- appreciate Ayanokōji's judgment without making it final;
- learn from Ichinose's brightness without becoming Ichinose;
- learn from Ryūen's adversarial realism without adopting his coercive sovereignty.

The mature self is therefore composite.

Its independence lies in integration.

## 20.2 V05 is the decisive proof because the answer is costly

Cheap disagreement proves little.

Horikita's V05 choice matters because she understands that Ayanokōji would choose differently and acts anyway under catastrophic stakes.

She then bears the political cost of that authorship.

This is the strongest reason the specialist rejects the "puppet" model.

A puppet does not generate the decision that its supposed puppeteer regards as a mistake.

## 20.3 V09 shows self-authorship moving from judgment into institution

The presidency matters because Horikita no longer proves independence only by disagreeing.

She produces settlements.

She decides what an office may do.

She creates reasons others can contest.

This is a higher-order form of self-authorship: not only choosing a personal answer, but creating a repeatable public structure through which other people can have answers too.

## 20.4 Y2V12 proves self-authorship can survive failure

A self built entirely around being correct would collapse when decisively defeated.

Horikita's does not.

That is why V12 is not a regression to early inferiority anxiety even though recognition desire becomes explicit.

The desire matters.

It does not consume the rest of the self.

# 21. Horikita's theory of ability - from scalar rank to developmental coordination

Horikita's most important intellectual development across the first two years concerns what ability is.

Early Horikita repeatedly confuses:

- current performance;
- institutional measurement;
- social usefulness;
- and total future worth.

Year 2 demonstrates that she has substantially abandoned that compression.

## 21.1 Sudō - trajectory over snapshot

Sudō is the canonical example.

His administrative rating lags the person Horikita has watched develop.

She therefore possesses evidence that the institutional file is temporally stale.

By later Year 2, Sudō's development includes:

- athletic excellence;
- improved academic effort;
- anger regulation;
- decision-making under pressure;
- and the ability to continue after romantic rejection.

Horikita's role is causal but not permanently proprietary.

## 21.2 Kushida - dangerous capacity remains capacity

Kushida is the hardest ability case because the same social capacity that makes her extraordinarily valuable also made her extraordinarily dangerous.

Horikita's positive epistemic achievement in V05 is recognizing that exposure does not make the capacity disappear.

The later mistake would be to infer that capacity automatically justifies preservation.

The correct mature position is two-dimensional:

- Kushida's social ability is real and under-recognized by crude culling logic;
- the costs imposed to preserve that ability remain morally real.

## 21.3 Kōenji - withheld capacity defeats file realism

Kōenji demonstrates a different limit of measurement.

Low or modest visible institutional values can coexist with intentionally withheld ceiling.

The leader therefore cannot infer total capacity directly from file output.

But recognizing hidden capacity still does not create title to it.

That is why ability theory and constitutional theory converge in the Kōenji case.

## 21.4 Ike - local expertise can reverse prestige hierarchy

Ike's correction of Horikita is one of the most politically important minor-member moments in Year 2.

It proves that a leader who understands plural ability must be prepared for a low-prestige classmate to possess the decisive local answer.

The correct response is not humiliation or reluctant tolerance.

It is model revision.

## 21.5 Onodera - peer pedagogy proves capacity can propagate laterally

Onodera does not merely contribute athletic value.

She teaches Sudō a usable self-regulation method.

This means class capacity can be **generative**:

one member's competence changes another member's future competence.

That is a stronger polity than one that merely inventories talents.

## 21.6 Hirata and Kei - social governance is not secondary ability

Hirata and Kei repeatedly solve problems that cannot be reduced to test score or physical ability.

Horikita's mature leadership increasingly treats:

- mediation;
- emotional calibration;
- reputation;
- group maintenance;
- and social translation

as real capacities rather than decorative support.

The polity becomes more realistic because its model of ability becomes multidimensional.

# 22. Kushida - the defining rehabilitation and jurisdiction case

No Year-2 relationship tests Horikita's developmental politics more severely than Kushida.

The case must be held in two temporal stages.

## 22.1 Stage one - imposed preservation

V05 is Horikita-authored.

Kushida does not own the rescue.

The class pays for it through Airi's expulsion, Haruka's injury, and broader trust collapse.

Horikita's decision is therefore simultaneously:

- independently authored;
- developmentally perceptive;
- politically courageous;
- nonconsensual toward Kushida;
- distributively costly;
- and ethically unresolved.

No later result should delete any item in that list.

## 22.2 Stage two - self-authored continued participation

V06 changes the jurisdictional structure.

Kushida begins owning the ongoing project.

Her motive does not have to resemble Horikita's.

The class can now benefit from her because she has chosen a future that makes contribution instrumentally rational for herself.

This is why the mature relation is more legitimate than the rescue without becoming retroactive consent to the rescue.

## 22.3 Later council recruitment strengthens the transfer of authorship

Horikita later learns to recruit Kushida through appropriable reasons rather than moral debt.

This is one of the clearest cases where Year 2 shows a leader learning from a morally compromised earlier intervention.

The improvement is not:

`Horikita was right all along`.

It is:

`Horikita becomes better at constructing a relation in which Kushida can be the author of what happens next`.

## 22.4 The original debt remains in the polity

Airi is not restored by Kushida's success.

Haruka's pain is not invalidated by increased class points.

The class should therefore not narrate the Kushida outcome as a simple rehabilitation triumph.

Its mature institutional memory must be capable of saying:

- the preserved person became genuinely useful;
- the preserved person eventually owned her participation;
- and another member paid an irreversible initial cost.

That capacity to preserve incompatible truths is itself a test of political maturity.

# 23. Kōenji - membership without ownership

Kōenji is Horikita's sharpest constitutional problem because he combines extreme potential capacity with unusually weak identification with class goals.

A simpler leader has two obvious responses:

1. condemn him as selfish and attempt to coerce contribution;
2. abandon all expectations because coercion is difficult.

Horikita's Year-2 solution is more sophisticated.

## 23.1 Membership creates some obligations, not unlimited title

The class can legitimately negotiate for defined contribution.

It cannot assume that enrollment transfers ownership of all private capacity.

This is the core lesson of the bargain structure.

## 23.2 Contract transforms moral complaint into political claim

Without an agreement, "Kōenji should help more" is largely a moral demand.

With an explicit bargain, Horikita can say:

- this contribution was promised;
- this benefit was exchanged;
- this scope is enforceable through reputation and reciprocity.

The political order becomes stronger because the claim is no longer dependent on shared sentiment.

## 23.3 The contract also binds Horikita

This is the more important half.

If a bargain only binds Kōenji, it is not constitutional.

Horikita must also respect:

- the promised future freedom;
- the narrow scope of obligations;
- and the fact that additional capacity remains his unless separately bargained for.

V10's restraint is therefore as important as the earlier performance demand.

## 23.4 Second List makes Kōenji the future constitutional stress test

The guidebook explicitly identifies his future contribution as important after Ayanokōji's transfer.

The wrong inference would be:

> Horikita now needs him, therefore she can command him.

The correct Year-2 question is:

> **Can her polity create reasons or bargains under which extraordinary voluntarily withheld capacity becomes available without collapsing membership into ownership?**

The answer remains prospective at Y2SL.

# 24. Ayanokōji and Horikita - reciprocity under disappearing hidden security

The Ayanokōji-Horikita relation is one of the most developed in the corpus and one of the easiest to oversimplify.

It contains at least six simultaneous structures by Y2SL:

1. developmental history;
2. strategic collaboration;
3. hidden former guarantorship;
4. judgment reciprocity;
5. personal attachment and recognition;
6. radical timetable asymmetry.

No single label is sufficient.

## 24.1 Judgment reciprocity is real

Ayanokōji increasingly treats Horikita as someone whose answers are worth discovering rather than merely supplying.

Horikita increasingly treats Ayanokōji as expertise she can consult without surrendering the right to decide.

V05 is the decisive proof.

Their relation can produce disagreement that changes the causal path.

## 24.2 The guarantor role remains real even as it becomes less visible

Ayanokōji still supplies:

- deterrence;
- hidden information;
- environmental architecture;
- tail-risk protection;
- and final intervention capacity.

Horikita's public authority therefore grows inside a system whose shadow layer remains partly external to her jurisdiction.

This is why dependence cannot be answered with a simple yes/no.

## 24.3 Recognition desire is not obedience

Horikita wants his recognition.

The desire becomes more explicit late in Year 2.

It is psychologically and relationally important because his judgment has become personally significant.

But a person can value another's recognition without granting that person final authority.

Horikita's decisions repeatedly prove exactly that.

## 24.4 Ordinary affect makes the relation more than developmental utility

The V10 smile, low-stakes conversation, irritation, teasing, practical concern, and late-year care all show a relation with surplus meaning.

The late-year relation also contains an explicitly unclassified bodily response: Horikita experiences accelerated heart rate and unfamiliar emotional disturbance after playful relational framing, but the source does not require a definitive romance label (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine19:para0131-para0175`).

This matters because Ayanokōji's eventual departure is not the removal of a neutral consultant.

It is the loss of a person who has become woven into Horikita's ordinary and political future.

## 24.5 Timetable asymmetry is the unresolved ethical core

Ayanokōji knows the succession experiment is coming.

Horikita does not.

That information affects:

- planning;
- emotional preparation;
- institutional succession;
- interpretation of recent help;
- and the meaning of the relationship itself.

The problem is not that he chooses to transfer.

The problem is that he controls the information required for Horikita to author her own response before the event.

## 24.6 Y1H-016 remains the strongest relational abstention zone

The corpus cannot yet model how Horikita would respond to a genuine Ayanokōji failure with high confidence.

Her current relation contains:

- admiration;
- challenge;
- care;
- dependence on hidden capacity;
- and a growing expectation of reciprocity.

But no source-local Year-2 event requires her to interpret his actual incapacity or loss of control.

Reconstruction must branch or abstain rather than importing a future answer.

# 29. Student-council presidency - office as an ethical technology

The presidency deserves separate treatment because it changes the scale of Horikita's leadership.

Class leadership can sometimes be defended through shared outcome: everyone wants Class A.

Student-council office creates actors with different constituencies, prior rights, rival projects, and asymmetric institutional power.

Horikita therefore has to ask not merely:

> What result is best?

but:

> **Who has standing, what authority exists, what was promised, whose resources are involved, and what remedy is proportionate?**

That is constitutional reasoning.

## 29.1 Office makes reasons public

A presidential decision cannot rely entirely on "I know better."

Its legitimacy improves when affected actors can understand:

- the rule;
- the entitlement;
- the boundary;
- and the reason for intervention.

V09 provides meaningful evidence of this mode.

## 29.2 Office creates fiduciary duties beyond friendship

Horikita's private preferences do not exhaust presidential obligation.

The presidency therefore becomes a valuable stress test of self-authorship: can she act from a role she chose while accepting that the role limits what she may personally want to do?

Year 2 answers provisionally yes.

## 29.3 Office gives Horikita a non-Ayanokōji source of political identity

The presidency also matters relationally.

Ayanokōji is not the author of every political domain she now inhabits.

The more Horikita develops through office, constituency, peer negotiation, and institutional precedent, the less plausible it becomes to describe her as merely the finished product of one developer.

Her causal history is plural.

Her judgments are increasingly hers.


# 30. Ethics - anti-disposability, distributive burden, and corrective leadership

Horikita's ethical development is most coherent when separated into three questions:

1. **Who should be treated as disposable now?**
2. **Who bears the cost when someone is preserved?**
3. **Who gets to author the preserved person's future?**

Year 1 substantially revises the first.

Year 2 forces her to confront the second and third.

## 30.1 Anti-disposability is not a never-expel doctrine

Horikita learns that current weakness, low measurement, social inconvenience, or dangerous present state do not automatically determine future worth.

That does not produce a categorical ban on expulsion.

V05 itself disproves such a reading.

The mature principle is narrower:

> **before irreversible removal, the polity should represent developmental possibility and domain-specific value more accurately than a static file or momentary crisis does.**

This is epistemic caution, not absolute preservation.

## 30.2 Developmental optimism can externalize cost

Horikita's greatest Year-2 ethical danger is that belief in future development can become a reason to make someone else pay for the experiment.

Kushida is the canonical case.

The preserved person's possible future value is real.

So is Airi's irreversible removal.

Developmental leadership is ethically mature only when it asks:

- who bears the risk;
- whether the beneficiary consents;
- what alternatives exist;
- and whether the leader is using another person as the involuntary price of optimism.

## 30.3 Corrective leadership requires more than admitting error

Horikita's willingness to admit mistakes is important but insufficient by itself.

The mature ethical test is whether correction changes future practice.

Year 2 supplies affirmative evidence:

- she narrows responsibility for the careless promise;
- moves from moralized Kushida cooperation to self-authored motive;
- increasingly relies on explicit bargains;
- and accepts correction from actors whose status is lower than hers.

Corrigibility becomes institutional rather than merely emotional.

## 30.4 Jurisdiction is the bridge between ethics and constitution

Horikita's mature ethical question is increasingly:

> **What am I entitled to decide for this person?**

This question appears in different forms:

- Kōenji's private capacity;
- Kushida's continued participation;
- Ibuki's cross-class relation;
- council predecessor rights;
- constituency-owned resources;
- and Ayanokōji's hidden authority over the timing of succession.

The common issue is title, not merely effectiveness.

## 30.5 A good outcome does not erase dirty hands

Year 2 gives Horikita several successful outcomes after morally compromised decisions.

The specialist therefore preserves a strict rule:

> **later success may strengthen the prudential case for a decision without retroactively cleansing the original process.**

That rule applies especially to V05.

It is one reason the polity's future memory matters. A mature class should be capable of learning from a successful but ethically costly decision without turning success into innocence.

# 32. Written Japanese voice - stable precision, expanded permissible speech acts

Horikita's written voice remains one of the highest-confidence profiles in the corpus.

The Year-2 development is not a replacement of register. The voice ledger specifically tracks the stable feminine endings `～わ`, `～よ`, and `～かしら` alongside complete, judgment-oriented syntax.

It is an expansion of what the stable register is allowed to do.

## 32.1 Stable baseline

Preserve:

- complete sentences;
- controlled feminine sentence endings;
- judgment-oriented diction;
- explicit logical distinctions;
- precise evaluation;
- relatively limited social cushioning;
- and a preference for saying what she actually judges rather than decorating it for approval.

The voice should not become generically casual simply because the character has developed socially.

## 32.2 New Year-2 speech permissions

The same voice can now more naturally perform acts equivalent to:

- asking another person for judgment;
- stating that a decision is hers;
- admitting that part of her prior conduct was wrong;
- asking others to carry responsibility with her;
- acknowledging uncertainty;
- admitting she cannot do something alone;
- expressing practical concern;
- and valuing recognition without abandoning argumentative independence.

Development is therefore audible as **corrigibility and relational range**, not as a new personality register.

## 32.3 Kushida - cooperation must not sound warm by default

Horikita and Kushida remain adversarial even after cooperation becomes legitimate.

Reconstruction should preserve:

- skepticism;
- challenge;
- negotiated utility;
- recognition of competence;
- and the absence of counterfeit intimacy.

A soft reconciliation register would erase the very constitutional achievement of the relationship: cooperation works without requiring false affection.

## 32.4 Ibuki - lower ceremony is relationship-specific

Horikita can sound more petty, direct, competitive, or playful with Ibuki.

That variance should not be generalized to:

- council speech;
- class leadership;
- negotiation with rivals;
- or unfamiliar peers.

Audience-specific relaxation is evidence of a richer person, not evidence that the core idiolect disappeared.

## 32.5 Late Year 2 - care remains precise

Horikita's care for Ayanokōji is especially diagnostic because it arrives in her established mode:

- observation;
- concern;
- a practical offer;
- bounded directness.

She does not transform into a conventionally sentimental speaker.

This is exactly the kind of development the voice ledger requires: new acts inside old linguistic identity.

## 32.6 Simulation prohibitions

Do not write Year-2 Horikita as:

- permanently cold;
- a generic tsundere engine;
- suddenly slang-heavy;
- emotionally inarticulate in every domain;
- secretly pleading for Ayanokōji's approval in every conversation;
- or speaking to Kushida, Ibuki, classmates, council members, and Ayanokōji in one undifferentiated register.

Her mature voice is controlled, relationship-sensitive, and increasingly capable of acknowledging uncertainty without losing precision.

# 33. Behavioral reconstruction constraints

This specialist is source authority for Year-2 Horikita modeling, but simulated scenarios remain derivative.

Use `COTE_BEHAVIORAL_RECONSTRUCTION_PROTOCOL_THROUGH_Y2.md` for the operational layer.

## 33.1 Behavior before voice

A Horikita reconstruction should first resolve:

1. what role she actually occupies;
2. whether the problem belongs to her jurisdiction;
3. who possesses domain expertise;
4. what obligations are explicit;
5. what information she has;
6. whether a relationship creates special moral friction;
7. and whether the situation requires leadership at all.

Only then should dialogue be realized.

## 33.2 Default high-confidence behavior family

In a consequential leadership problem, a well-supported Horikita family is:

`identify governing obligation -> distinguish role from preference -> seek or permit relevant specialist input -> state reasons -> choose -> accept visible responsibility -> revise if new evidence defeats the model`.

This is a family, not a deterministic script.

Exact outcomes remain scenario-dependent.

## 33.3 The anti-command test

A crucial adversarial reconstruction test is:

> **If someone else legitimately owns a low- or moderate-stakes task, can Horikita advise, disagree, or participate without taking command merely because she could probably perform the task well?**

Year 2 requires the answer to be yes in at least some domains.

Failure here would regress her into a competence-as-sovereignty model the corpus has already rejected.

- practical reliance;

# 40. Final synthesis - independent judgment before independent reproduction

Horikita's Year-2 arc is not best described as the completion of independence.

It is the separation of several things that early Horikita, many surrounding characters, and simplified readings tend to collapse.

The first is **ability**.

Year 2 confirms that ability is not a single rank. Sudō develops beyond his file. Kōenji withholds a ceiling the file cannot capture. Kushida's social capacity survives moral exposure. Ike can locally out-think the leader. Onodera can teach. Hirata and Kei exercise governance through social domains. Horikita herself becomes a successful leader whose administrative metrics do not transparently explain her political performance.

The second is **leadership**.

Leadership is no longer personal superiority.

It becomes the maintenance of an order in which the right person can act, the wrong person can correct the leader, and useful contribution does not require identical motive.

The third is **self-authorship**.

Horikita does not become self-authored by eliminating Manabu or Ayanokōji from her causal history.

She becomes self-authored by metabolizing influence into judgments she can own, including judgments her strongest influence would not choose.

V05 is the decisive proof because the disagreement has real cost.

The fourth is **legitimacy**.

A self-authored decision is not automatically a just one.

Kushida's preservation demonstrates independent judgment and developmental perception while also demonstrating nonconsensual rescue and externalized cost.

V06 does not erase V05. It shows how the leader can improve the ongoing structure by returning authorship to the preserved person.

The fifth is **constitution**.

Year 2 repeatedly turns personality problems into scope problems.

How much may the class demand from Kōenji?

What may the president do to a predecessor?

What counts as constituency property?

Can Kushida cooperate without affection?

Can Haruka remain a legitimate dissenter?

Can a low-status member defeat the leader's local model?

Can friendship alter a cost without automatically overriding office?

Horikita's mature answers increasingly favor bounded obligation, auditable procedure, distributed expertise, preserved standing, and corrigibility.

That is why **constitutional developmental pluralism** is the strongest Year-2 label.

The sixth is **failure**.

V12 matters because Horikita loses completely.

The loss does not secretly prove she was superior.

It proves something more important for leadership: her political identity no longer requires personal invulnerability.

She can be wrong, outperformed, corrected, hurt, and still remain the person responsible for leading afterward.

The seventh is **relationship**.

Horikita does not become independent by becoming indifferent.

Ibuki matters more because history makes her specific.

Sudō remains developed after romantic rejection.

Kushida can cooperate while preserving hostility.

Ayanokōji's recognition matters deeply while failing to become sovereign over Horikita's judgment.

The mature self is relational without being reducible to relation.

The eighth is **succession**.

This is where Year 2 stops.

The class reaches A.

Horikita's judgment is independent.

Her presidency is hers.

Her class has distributed competence.

Her members increasingly teach, correct, bargain with, and resist one another without routing every answer through one hidden solver.

These facts make the removal of Ayanokōji's internal security layer a meaningful test.

They do not predetermine the test's result.

The asymmetry at the boundary is therefore exact:

> **Horikita has become independently capable before the corpus can prove that the polity she leads is independently self-reproducing.**

That distinction is not a caveat added to an otherwise complete triumph.

It is the architecture of the Year-2 ending.

Ayanokōji thinks of himself as training wheels and chooses when to leave.

Horikita experiences him as indispensable and imagines a shared future.

The class has been prepared for a test whose visible leader did not co-author the timetable.

The ethical and political significance of the departure therefore cannot be summarized as either betrayal or benevolent graduation exercise.

It is both a developmental intervention and a unilateral jurisdiction over succession timing.

That is why the strongest next question is empirical rather than rhetorical:

> **What survives when the hidden guarantor is no longer inside the polity?**

Year 2 has done enough work to make that question fair.

It has not answered it.

That is the canonical Horikita endpoint at `Y2SL`.
