# 1. Current Year-2 boundary thesis

The specialist corpus has not yet stabilized, so the following is a **boundary thesis distilled from the completed canonical ledgers**, not the final wording of `COTE_Y2_FULL_SYNTHESIS.md`:

> **Year 2 turns authorship from a hidden tactical advantage into an explicit political, relational, institutional, and developmental problem. Ayanokōji increasingly learns to design environments in which other people change; ANHS likewise becomes more capable of measuring, sorting, protecting, moving, and consuming student ability. The central question is therefore no longer whether a superior model can produce useful outcomes. It is whether successful authorship remains answerable to people who can refuse, correct, outgrow, survive separation from, or generate purposes outside the model that understood them.**

Several completed-year conclusions already support that formulation:

- **Ayanokōji:** ordinary participation and human attachment become more real at the same time that his capacity for environmental authorship becomes more precise. The unresolved ethical problem is whether the first development can constrain the second.
- **Horikita:** independent judgment and institution-building are demonstrated; a self-sustaining post-Ayanokōji polity is not yet demonstrated.
- **Kei:** behavioral and horizontal social autonomy can coexist with emotional dependency and insufficient informational reciprocity.
- **Ichinose:** Year 2 produces the strongest current example of **counter-authorship**—an answer that exceeds Ayanokōji's completed model rather than merely succeeding inside it.
- **Ryūen:** leadership becomes more adaptive and gains more voluntary legitimacy while the class's dependence on its sovereign remains a succession risk.
- **Sakayanagi:** highly efficient centralized talent allocation fails the succession test most visibly when private priority and sovereign withdrawal leave a vacuum.
- **Kushida:** post-exposure cooperation can be self-authored without retroactively legitimating the coercive conditions under which she was preserved.
- **ANHS:** institutional legibility increases faster downward than transparency upward. Students become more measurable and transferable while rule authorship, record provenance, discretion, patronage, and external political influence remain unevenly visible.
- **Ordinary life:** low-stakes life is not merely absence of strategy. It generates **surplus purpose**—desires, attachments, failures, pleasures, jokes, hobbies, habits, and future projects that an institution or interpersonal designer may enable without fully owning.

The mature Year-1 principle remains active:

> **The file is evidence about the person. The file is not the person.**

Year 2 sharpens it into a stronger test:

> **A model of another person is most ethically and epistemically credible when the person it models can still produce an answer the model did not own.**

This README does not attempt to prove that thesis in full. The planned specialist documents and full synthesis own that job.

# 3. Analytical reading order and temporal coordinates

## 3.1 Canonical analytical reading order

The correct analytical order is:

`Y2V01 → Y2V02 → Y2V03 → Y2V04 → Y2V04.5 → Y2V05 → Y2V06 → Y2V07 → Y2V08 → V00 → Y2V09 → Y2V09.5 → Y2V10 → Y2V11 → Y2V12 → Y2V12.5 → Y2SL`

Do not move Volume 0 to the front merely because its events occur earlier in story chronology.

Do not treat *Second List* as if its documentary summaries or bonus fiction had been available to the reader during the volume-local reread.

## 3.2 Four temporal coordinates

Whenever they diverge, retain:

```yaml
story_chronology: "when the event occurred in-universe"
publication_chronology: "when the material was published"
analytical_reading_position: "when the V2 project encountered the material"
character_knowledge_position: "when the relevant character could know it"
```

Volume 0 makes this rule non-optional. A chronologically earlier event can become known to the reader late without becoming prior knowledge for Year-2 characters or prior evidence for already-frozen Y2V01–Y2V08 readings.

## 3.3 Development versus revelation

Use the following distinction throughout Year 2:

- **development** — the person, relationship, institution, or polity actually changes over time;
- **revelation of prior hidden state** — later evidence exposes something already true but previously unavailable;
- **retrospective recontextualization** — later evidence changes how an earlier scene should be understood without changing what the earlier characters knew;
- **correction of prior inference** — the earlier analytical model was wrong or too strong;
- **relationship-specific expression** — a capacity appears in one relational context without licensing global generalization;
- **unresolved contradiction** — evidence remains in tension and the corpus should preserve rather than flatten it.

The canonical through-Year-2 character ledgers encode these transitions explicitly.
