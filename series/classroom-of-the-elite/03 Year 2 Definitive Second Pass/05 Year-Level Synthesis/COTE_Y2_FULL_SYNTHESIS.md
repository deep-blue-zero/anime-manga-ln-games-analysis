# Classroom of the Elite — Year 2

## 1. Development creates a claim that development alone cannot justify

Year 2 makes a demanding distinction between helping someone become capable and acquiring the right to decide what that capability is for. Its strongest characters repeatedly change the conditions under which others can act: they protect a threatened future, reveal a talent, make an alliance possible, expose a secret, arrange a confrontation, or remove someone from the institution. Those interventions sometimes produce genuine growth. They also create a temptation to treat the growing person as evidence of the intervenor's success, and therefore as a future the intervenor may continue to govern.

The completed year supplies substantial evidence against that claim of ownership. Horikita can reach a judgment Kiyotaka does not supply. Sudō can find pleasure in learning after the romantic reward that helped motivate him is unavailable. Kushida can continue cooperating for reasons she makes her own. Ichinose can answer outside the alternatives prepared for her. Ordinary friends can reconstruct their lives without returning to the person who once connected them. These are developments of particular people, not merely improvements in the performance of a system.

Yet the same year gives environmental authorship greater reach. Ayanokōji increasingly acts through other people's independent motives, rather than having to direct every action. Knowing that someone loves, fears, trusts, grieves or wants something can allow him to prepare conditions in which a nominally free choice will probably serve his own project. Independence at the level of an immediate decision can coexist with dependence on another actor's control of information, timing and alternatives. Year 2's central ethical problem becomes sharper precisely because the other characters are increasingly capable.

The resulting synthesis separates three questions. **What can this person now do? Who owns the purpose of doing it? To whom must the person arranging the conditions answer?** The first concerns capacity; the second concerns authorship of ends; the third concerns legitimate limits on power. Progress on one axis may enable progress on another, but it does not certify it. A capable class can remain vulnerable to a missing strategic center. A person can express a sincere preference while lacking crucial information. A leader can recognize another person more fully while still imposing a future on them.

This interpretation is a supported year-boundary model, not a hidden law attributed to every narrator. The corpus establishes events and spoken or narrated claims through Japanese sources; it distinguishes those facts from interpretations of motive and normative judgments about legitimacy. It also preserves strong counterarguments. Education inevitably involves asymmetry. Protection and coordination can be valuable before perfect information or equal power exists. Other people can appropriate an opportunity made for someone else's purpose. The test is therefore whether dependence can be revised and whether the stronger actor can be corrected, rather than whether anyone can grow without influence.

The continuous argument below integrates the specialist corpus. [Y2_01][s01] owns the detailed causal progression; [Y2_02][s02] and [Y2_03][s03] own the protagonist and Horikita arguments; [Y2_04][s04]–[Y2_10][s10] retain their domain responsibilities. [Y2_11][s11] owns comparison and the unanswered handoff. [Y2_12][s12] and [Y2_13][s13] provide the exact routes into evidence and Japanese language. This synthesis changes none of their historical source boundaries and makes no claim about Year-3 events.

## 2. A school that makes people visible also makes them available for use

The year begins by changing what information can do. OAA gives students an accessible institutional account of demonstrated performance. Ayanokōji can consider his own public numbers reasonable while possessing capacities they do not reveal. The profile is not necessarily inaccurate; its scope is limited. Once the inter-year partnership exam makes one student's result consequential for another's survival, that limited information becomes material for recruitment, pricing, avoidance and coercion. Measurement does not simply describe the students. It alters their relations.

This is the first reason the title's 実力 cannot become one universal strength scale. Possessed ability differs from displayed performance; displayed performance differs from what the school records; recorded ability may or may not be usable under a particular rule. Developmental ability concerns change over time. Political ability concerns making decisions and coordinating people. Generative ability concerns producing a worthwhile answer that the existing task or evaluator did not already specify. The school can observe some of these capacities well and others poorly. A totalizing interpretation of OAA would mistake a useful administrative instrument for a sufficient account of the person.

The original Japanese makes the distinctions explicit in different settings. V01 discusses how records accumulate and what early information about new students may fail to capture. V08 asks students to rank classmates from their own standpoint, permitting friendship or humor as grounds alongside competence. V09 gives Sudō language for enjoying study itself. Those are three different kinds of evaluation: institutionally recorded output, lived relational judgment and self-reported motivation. The fuller a student becomes across these dimensions, the less adequate a single score becomes as a theory of development. The relevant routes are `Y2V01-E010–E011`, `Y2V08-E009` and `Y2V09-E057`; [Y2_13][s13] preserves the language in JP-A02/A03 and JP-E10.

Points connect measurement to coercive consequences. They can buy relief, subsidize cooperation, finance movement and preserve a participant's future. They can also make another person's insecurity an opportunity for bargaining. V02's rescue price monetizes an option under threat of expulsion; it does not establish that the option is equally available to everyone. A formal contract can reduce betrayal risk, as in Horikita's inter-year and later class arrangements, while leaving the broader institution's unequal sanctions intact. The class letter records a changing position in that economy, not an enduring political identity.

The examinations deepen this problem by separating procedure from legitimacy. V05's unanimous-vote structure can preserve the anonymity of an individual ballot while subjecting the individual to severe pressure to join a collective decision. Unanimity records agreement at the moment the mechanism accepts it; it does not reveal all the alternatives, threats or obligations that produced agreement. V12 similarly turns inference, hidden roles, trust and the sanction of expulsion into usable strategic materials. A valid institutional route can still support an ethically disproportionate intervention.

The strongest objection is that institutions need legible outputs and enforceable rules. That is correct. Without records, prices or procedures, the school would not become a neutral space; informal force and private patronage could become harder to challenge. Year 2 shows the value of contracts, witnesses and independent corroboration. The criticism concerns the further move from a bounded measurement or lawful option to a claim that the resulting outcome exhausts merit or establishes justice. An exam can identify who succeeded under its conditions without deciding whose purpose deserves priority.

The school is therefore more than a sequence of games. It distributes power to make futures possible and to close them. [Y2_06][s06] owns the mechanical and measurement analysis; [Y2_07][s07] owns the authority and record questions. Their connection supplies the setting for the year's interpersonal conflicts: people become easier to know in some respects, and therefore easier to help, recruit and injure, while the most consequential authors of their situation may remain difficult to inspect.

## 3. Ayanokōji gains ordinary life without surrendering developmental jurisdiction

Ayanokōji's development is real because the year gives him things to value that are not reducible to the next competitive outcome. Friends, shared leisure, embarrassment, literary conversation, domestic proximity and the shrinking duration of school life enter his experience as particular things. V09.5's recognition of irreplaceable everyday time matters alongside the earlier contrast between having a social world and merely trying to construct one. His relationships can involve curiosity, pleasure, care and disappointment even where his account of them remains analytical.

Reducing these scenes to camouflage would make the interpretation less faithful. He does not need to cease calculating in order to enjoy company. Nor must every preference be profound to count as development. Ordinary life changes what he attends to and what he wants to repeat. It can also make him uncertain in ways that raw competitive superiority does not resolve. Those changes remain meaningful even when they fail to constrain a later harmful decision.

The opposite reduction is equally inadequate. Emotional novelty does not establish informed reciprocity. Ayanokōji can notice a new feeling while continuing to control the timetable on which another person learns the facts necessary to interpret their relationship. He can desire a class's development while arranging a crisis that denies its members a meaningful say in the project's purpose. He can protect an individual while understanding that protection primarily as part of an educational design. Warmth and authorship coexist; their coexistence is the problem to explain.

V06 gives the contradiction a particularly clear form. The training-wheel metaphor imagines his own removal as necessary to the class's eventual capacity. It is an anti-dependency aspiration expressed from the position of the person deciding when dependence should end. The subsequent desire to be defeated sits beside confidence that he would win if he became the opponent. He wants his model falsified while retaining the authority to stage the test. This is more complicated than either invulnerable self-certainty or a simple wish for self-destruction. It is a conflict between a known repertoire of control and a desired result that would exceed it (`Y2V06-E162–E166`; JP-E01/E02).

V07 makes that repertoire more sophisticated. Ayanokōji's contribution to the convergence around Yagami depends on people who already have reasons to investigate, confront, report or protect. Their agency is not an illusion. His method operates by arranging access, cues and circumstances so that those reasons become jointly effective. This limits the claim of omnipotence: a prepared setting depends on other people and on information he may not entirely possess. It also enlarges responsibility. A person need not issue every instruction to help author the conditions under which an outcome occurs.

V11 supplies an affirmative limit. After creating an opportunity for Sakayanagi and Yamamura to meet, Ayanokōji identifies what follows as outside the domain he should enter. That distinction matters because he could continue interpreting, steering or claiming credit. The Japanese speaks of a domain to intrude into, not merely something unknowable. Recognition licenses attention and assistance here without licensing possession of the answer. It is a concrete positive ethical instance, not an abstract virtue assigned by the analyst (`COTE:Y2V11:spine19:para0235`; JP-B07).

V12 demonstrates the limit's failure to generalize. The use of Maezono, the timing of devastating information and the encoded intervention in Sakayanagi's decision put relational knowledge back to work as jurisdiction. His own tool language is particularly severe because it names the person being used. A system in which the target still technically chooses can remain strongly authored by the person controlling what the target knows and what the choice means. The comparison with V11 prevents both a total condemnation that denies every bounded act and a redemption narrative that lets one bounded act cleanse the rest.

By V12.5, separation is no longer merely proposed. The breakup and the transfer close real institutional or relational positions. But leaving one role can inaugurate another form of authorship. Ayanokōji enters **Sakayanagi's former class**, financed through his points, Hashimoto's savings and the classmates' agreed contributions. The source explicitly identifies that destination; the closeout corrected three misnamings in Y2_01. His continuing project cannot be assessed as if he simply departed from power. He relinquishes one position while becoming an imported center in another polity (`COTE:Y2V12.5:spine22:para0075–0080`).

The strongest defensible terminal judgment is therefore asymmetric. His repertoire of ordinary participation and locally bounded care has expanded. His skill and ambition in shaping developmental environments have also expanded. The year does not establish that another person's adequately informed refusal can reliably change his ends at real cost, or that he will voluntarily confer protective authority by exposing a weakness he cannot simply withdraw. Those remain precise tests, not reasons to deny everything already demonstrated. [Y2_02][s02], [Y2_08][s08] and the [claim/revision ledger][claims] retain the detailed judgments.

## 4. Horikita's independence becomes a political problem larger than her own strength

Horikita's most important development is her growing authority to make a judgment rather than merely supply an answer another person has prepared. She increasingly proposes arrangements, recognizes differentiated capacities, accepts assistance and takes responsibility for choices. Her account of leadership no longer depends on imagining that she alone must embody every useful trait. That is a political development: a class can act through distributed people whose capacities differ from its leader's.

The V05 decision to preserve Kushida is the decisive complication. Horikita does not simply obey Ayanokōji, and her judgment matters precisely because it changes the decision's course. Yet independent authorship does not make the cost disappear. Preserving Kushida leaves another person to bear expulsion. Airi's final agency inside that situation does not prove that she had equal authority over its construction. Horikita's development and the ethical burden of her decision are simultaneous facts of the year, rather than competing interpretations from which the synthesis must choose only one.

The aftermath gives substance to both. Haruka's grievance is not a mistake that improved class performance cancels. Akito's attachment gives his own choices stakes beyond school optimization. Airi can develop a purpose outside the school without thereby consenting retrospectively to what happened. The cultural-festival return and the friends' renewed connection are important because the people affected do not merely reset to their former roles. Their lives continue through a wound that remains part of the political history (`Y2V07-E094–E105`).

Kushida likewise cannot be understood solely as a resource whose correct valuation vindicates the decision to retain her. She begins within an imposed preservation project, but later cooperation can acquire reasons she owns. The difference between coerced survival and chosen continuing participation is analytically necessary. If every later contribution were counted only as confirmation that Horikita or Ayanokōji optimized correctly, the analysis would reproduce the very ownership claim it is examining. Her capacity for choice is part of the outcome, including the possibility that her choices remain morally mixed.

The class's broader distribution of capacity gives Horikita's leadership credibility beyond that one decision. Hirata supplies care and mediation without being reducible to a warm substitute for formal command. Sudō's effort becomes increasingly self-sustaining, and Onodera's immediate peer teaching shows that development need not flow from an exceptional central tutor. Kei has social capacities that do not depend on the romance. Ibuki can cooperate through antagonism and bounded terms. These relationships allow Horikita to lead without becoming identical to the people whose work makes leadership possible.

That is why success during Ayanokōji's operational absence matters, but cannot bear an unlimited conclusion. The ability to execute without his visible participation differs from the ability to reproduce judgment after his hidden guarantees, information and environmental arrangements are absent. By the endpoint, the transfer makes the latter question materially unavoidable. The class has developed; the post-transfer system has not yet been observed. Predicting either effortless independence or inevitable collapse would import an answer that the boundary does not contain.

Horikita's student-council succession extends the same problem into office. Taking a title after Nagumo does not automatically replace the patronal relations, incentives or expectations attached to it. Her ability to recruit willing participation and to distinguish her judgment from either predecessor is meaningful. A durable alternative would also require practices that survive contested authority, inconvenient information and her own mistakes. Political maturity involves being correctable as well as being competent.

The result is a distinct subject rather than an illustration of Kiyotaka's developmental success. Horikita owns choices whose consequences she must answer for. Her relation to the hidden author remains unresolved in ways that do not erase that ownership. [Y2_03][s03] and the [succession/separation ledger][succession] distinguish her independent judgment, the class's distributed work and the future test of institutional reproduction; none is a synonym for the others.

## 5. Relationships develop on several axes at once

Kei is the clearest reason autonomy cannot be treated as a single state that a character either has or lacks. Her peer world, practical competence, social ties and capacity to act have developed. The romance nevertheless remains deeply asymmetric in information and in authority over its timetable. Ayanokōji's understanding of himself as conducting a developmental or romantic-learning project is not equivalent to Kei having enough information to choose the relationship on those terms.

Her final response is therefore not adequately described as either cure or total regression. She can perceive, speak, preserve composure in an encounter and rely on a wider social world while also experiencing grief and continuing attachment. The source's surname changes make the relationship's altered public form legible: his farewell calls her Karuizawa, and hers returns to Ayanokōji-kun, while her interior still retains Kiyotaka. Formal separation, internal attachment and future agency are distinct states. A later text must show what she does with them; this corpus cannot prescribe recovery through a particular new relationship.

The earlier Second List date fiction supplies a necessary counterweight to a purely external diagnosis. Kei describes what she wants from dating: direct talk, eye contact, touch and an open relationship. A carefully arranged encounter can be both enjoyable and lonely. Her evaluative standpoint exceeds the arrangement's logistical success. But because that fiction concerns an earlier point in the romance, it cannot serve as proof of endpoint reciprocity or reverse the breakup. Its contribution is her perspective, not a retroactive solution.

Ichinose's trajectory presents a different relation between attachment and agency. Solidarity is initially both a moral commitment and a political vulnerability. Kanzaki and Himeno expose the costs of a class culture in which disagreement can be swallowed by the demand for harmony. Their criticism does not make care worthless. It asks whether belonging can include the authority to identify a problem and to change the collective response. A class that protects everyone rhetorically can still burden the person whose judgment diverges.

Ichinose's development includes adversarial competence. She can become harder for rival leaders to read, detect their motives and preserve the ability to compete without abandoning every moral limit. This is not a simple replacement of kindness by pathology. Desire gives her self-directed stakes, and strategic opacity becomes part of her repertoire. She can remain attached while ceasing to provide the predictable answer another actor expects. Those changes matter independently of whether the reader endorses all her choices.

The terminal 介錯 encounter makes that independence explicit. Ayanokōji offers a structure in which he retains the authority to judge what should happen to her leadership and her future. Ichinose's third answer defeats the adequacy of that structure. It is evidence against complete ownership of her development and against the completeness of his model. It does not prove that she knows everything, that prior harm has been repaired, or that a mutually instrumental arrangement has become fully reciprocal. Counter-authorship is a real accomplishment with additional ethical tests still ahead.

Hiyori provides another alternative. Her quietness does not entail passivity, and a relationship need not be organized around rescue or competition to have consequences. Literary conversation, ordinary company and a wish to graduate together across class rivalry supply purposes that ranking cannot exhaust. Her understated interventions can alter a surrounding situation without seizing leadership. Precisely for that reason, the strict question about changing Ryūen's own moral decision must remain narrower than a general claim that she has influence. Valuing her company or competence is not yet accepting her as a governing moral constraint.

The wider ensemble prevents the relationships from collapsing into a map of Kiyotaka's attachments. Yamamura's refusal of disposability and growing permission to state a want matter as her own changes. Sakayanagi's loss of Kamuro reveals a particular attachment not captured by a replacement model, while her connection with Yamamura opens a different possibility. Satō's support for Kei is not exhausted by romantic rivalry. Ishizaki's ordinary friendliness shows a relationship beyond fear without proving that all his political allegiances have been transformed. Hirata's care remains valuable while its relation to particular attachment and the obligation to save everyone stays under examination.

Friendship also has an afterlife beyond an original center. Haruka and Akito need not recreate the former group or erase Airi to have a future. Their return to shared activity is not simply an input into Horikita's victory or proof of Ayanokōji's successful management. It has its own authors, grievances and purposes. The distinction is essential to the year's notion of generativity: people can produce worthwhile lives that do not restore the older arrangement through which another person first understood them.

[Y2_04][s04] and the [relationship ledger][relationships] preserve the directed dimensions of these relations: information, leverage, exit, reciprocal recognition, protection, dependence, disagreement and survival after a role changes. The full synthesis draws one consequence from their differences. More attachment can make a person less controllable by giving them a self-owned purpose, and more controllable by making that purpose available as leverage. A claim about the relationship's development must specify which process is occurring and for whom.

## 6. Four polities offer competing answers to exceptional ability

Horikita's class increasingly treats leadership as the coordination of differentiated contributions. That tendency is neither morally spotless nor institutionally complete. It includes the V05 sacrifice, continuing disputes about preservation, and reliance on hidden support. Its promise lies in the growth of judgment across several people rather than in the leader becoming a smaller version of an exceptional strategist. The endpoint tests whether that promise can survive the departure of the person who has sometimes insured the system against its hardest consequences.

Ryūen's polity develops along a different path. Defeat does not end his relevance as a leader; chosen loyalty, delegation and the recruitment of Katsuragi make his authority more complex than brute domination. Katsuragi can supply instruction, caution, analytic disagreement and practical friction that Ryūen's earlier style underused. Followers have reasons for their allegiance. A portrayal of them as interchangeable victims would fail to explain the durability of the political relationship.

Nevertheless, voluntary loyalty and protected dissent are different constitutional properties. The capacity to disagree while being useful to the leader does not necessarily include standing to veto him, redirect his governing aims or remain secure after a refusal he finds intolerable. The continuation of coercion against dissenting actors matters alongside the development of delegation. Ryūen's willingness to change a tactic or value another person's competence does not by itself establish that power has become answerable to them. His polity's improvement is real and incomplete in a specific way.

Sakayanagi's case separates individual recognition from fiduciary succession. Her philosophy privileges exceptional judgment, and the class can benefit from the precision of that judgment. Her recognition of particular attachments changes her as a person. Yet the year's withdrawal problem shows what happens when sovereign private priority outruns responsibility for the polity's ability to continue. A choice can be personally self-authored and relationally meaningful while failing those who depend on the officeholder's provision for succession.

The new imported center does not erase that failure. Ayanokōji's arrival in the Sakayanagi-origin class is a fact at the boundary. The classmates' participation in financing it supplies consent to that transaction. It does not automatically establish shared control of information, distributed judgment or the ability to reproduce leadership without another exceptional figure. Restoring competitive capacity through an outsider and generating succession from within are different responses to a vacancy. Year 3 must show the constitutional effects of the former; Year 2 has only established the new condition.

Ichinose's polity makes solidarity its governing promise. That promise can preserve belonging and create commitment unavailable to purely transactional coordination. It can also make disagreement costly, as Himeno's critique reveals. Development therefore requires more than making the leader strategically tougher. It includes the question of whether loyal opposition can identify a problem without being absorbed into a performance of unanimity. Ichinose's own counter-authored answer gives this polity renewed agency at the endpoint, but the translation of that answer into durable collective practice remains a future test.

These differences prevent class points from adjudicating the political comparison. A score can change without a class replacing its governing philosophy; a leader can leave while the polity persists as the people who must live with the departure. Stable polity names are consequently more informative than floating class letters. The Second List endpoint records Horikita at 1233, Ryūen at a provisional 1040–1090, Sakayanagi's former class at 793 and Ichinose at 714. The range is retained. Its adjacent first-year table belongs to the younger cohort, not to Ayanokōji's Year-1 endpoint. Neither table is an ethical ranking of constitutions.

Kōenji challenges these systems from the position of an exceptional member who resists capture. His ability and capacity to create his own answer are not adequately represented by labeling every refusal a deficiency in cooperation. At the same time, exceptional independence does not automatically legitimate refusing every bounded obligation. The relevant question concerns the relation between membership, a specific agreement and the authority claimed over his purpose. The paternal condition introduced in V12.5 makes freedom itself a contested object of someone else's design. His own response to that condition is unavailable at this boundary.

Nagumo supplies the larger-scale comparison. His system can create real mobility opportunities while centralizing their allocation through patronage, extraction, obedience and revocable advantage. Its promise is not empty merely because it creates dependency; its dependency is not benign merely because some beneficiaries gain. His personal desire for a rival also complicates his authority. The actor who appears to have confirmed his superiority through broad institutional dominance can still need a contest to know what that superiority means.

The comparison therefore produces no single best leader detached from purpose. It asks which polity can identify and use capacity, protect weakness, sustain opposition, recover after loss, and reproduce judgment without converting everyone into a dependent extension of the strongest participant. [Y2_05][s05], [Y2_11][s11] and the [class-polity ledger][polities] retain the comparative matrices. At Y2SL, distributed capacity has grown in several places; constitutional limits on superior authorship remain uneven.

## 7. Adult power determines the environment but does not exhaust it

The White Room is more intelligible after Volume 0 because its educational design has a political genealogy. Atsuomi's ambition, funding relations, willingness to suppress failures and treatment of children as research material belong to the institution's logic. Calling an outcome a masterpiece evaluates it against the author's ends. It does not prove that the outcome can be reproduced, that the exposed children consented, or that exceptional performance settles the legitimacy of the process.

The comparison with ANHS must retain both resemblance and difference. Both institutions design developmental conditions, measure participants and make consequential judgments about results. ANHS nevertheless permits a wider field of attachments, purposes, leisure, rivalry and institutional mediation. That space can produce ends that no central design fully specifies. It can also be manipulated by adults, by examinations and by students who learn how to exploit its incentives. School plurality is a condition of possible autonomy, not a guarantee that every intervention within it is legitimate.

The adult actors have purposes distinct from their offices. Chabashira's remembered examination wound and investment in her class make her neither a disembodied rule interface nor an all-knowing protector. Hoshinomiya's grievance creates a different conflict between personal motive and pedagogical role. In the V12.5 containment of her conduct, Ayanokōji's stated aim is to keep her from leaving the class position, rather than simply to enforce an abstract principle against wrongdoing. That distinction shows how evidence, deterrence and institutional preservation can serve an interested end while still limiting a dangerous intervention.

Witnesses and records remain valuable because private knowledge alone may not become effective institutional truth. V04's confrontation and later disputes over attribution show that observing an event, knowing who caused it, proving that cause and obtaining an enforceable response are different achievements. A hidden command can make a true report support a false attribution. More surveillance does not automatically solve the provenance problem. Independent sources of judgment matter most where a powerful interested actor can influence what gets recorded or believed.

The same discipline applies to identity. Amasawa's White Room origin, Yagami's role, Ishigami's connection to Atsuomi, Nanase's possible function and Tsukishiro's highest purpose are separate propositions. A resolved name is not a resolved motive. A declaration that adult authority approved an operation remains a declaration until independently supported. Ishigami's allegiance does not by itself prove White Room training. The proposed knight role for Nanase remains a hypothesis, and later political context cannot simply convert the Matsuo account into a wholly fabricated story.

These actors also have independent futures at stake. Amasawa's relation to an ordinary life outside the training institution cannot be reduced to her utility in Kiyotaka's struggle. Yagami's removal is not a blank authorization to infer every prior choice from one retrospective identity. Hōsen and Utomiya operate through different forms of force, obligation and information. Nanase's grief and action need to be read with the distinction between what she believes and what the reader can verify. The [identity/allegiance ledger][identity] and [institutional-actors ledger][actors] preserve those separations.

V12.5 makes political permeability visible without resolving it into either total capture or neutral accountability. A prime minister's observation, a father's plans and elite competition can alter the school's environment. The students' purposes nonetheless remain analytically consequential. An opening authored for political reasons can produce a life with reasons its author did not supply. Conversely, genuinely self-chosen school purposes may develop inside continuing coercive adult jurisdiction. Kiyotaka's granted period of noninterference is not equivalent to a secure right to choose his postgraduation life.

[Y2_07][s07] and [Y2_10][s10] therefore connect genealogy with limits on inference. They explain more of how the environment became available without claiming to possess every intention behind it. Institutional explanation becomes strongest when it can account for generated possibilities as well as control, and when it resists letting a powerful actor's self-description define the complete ontology of everyone else.

## 8. Retrospective explanation must preserve the earlier person and the earlier reader

Volume 0 changes the available causal model after V08. It reveals political design, research practice, paternal ambition and childhood conditions that earlier local readings could not fully know. That revelation matters. An interpretation that refused all retrospective revision would preserve earlier ignorance as if it were a virtue. But the permission to revise a current causal account does not include permission to rewrite what an earlier character knew, what an earlier passage established or what an earlier choice meant within its available possibilities.

Kiyotaka's capacity makes this distinction especially necessary. An unusual adaptive subject interacts with an exceptionally harsh environment. Early unfamiliarity with a domain can coexist with rapid absorption and later extraordinary performance. The institution's inability to straightforwardly reproduce him complicates its claim to have manufactured a universally transferable result. Neither pure innate exceptionalism nor pure environmental manufacture adequately accounts for the interaction. Performance evidence also remains distinct from evidence of emotional reciprocity or moral legitimacy.

The children around him expose the limits of an institutional success story. Shiro's desire for freedom, Yuki's particular attachment and the problem of what happens to children who do not remain useful cannot be subsumed under the strongest trainee's achievement. Tabuchi's objection about independent life and social integration identifies a practical failure that a training score would not repair. An institution that suppresses the denominator of failure can advertise success while concealing its human cost. Where this particular EPUB has malformed cohort or percentage text, the qualitative critique does not justify inventing a repaired number.

The same restraint protects earlier ethical differences. In V03, Ayanokōji can compare his underlying nature with his father's while distinguishing his offered hand to Nanase. Volume 0 gives that comparison genealogy; it does not turn every difference into an illusion. Conversely, a childhood capacity for curiosity or a particular response to attachment does not prove that the later adolescent has generalized answerability. The relevant propositions concern different times, relationships and tests.

Second List adds another kind of retrospective complexity. Its exclusive fiction provides access to scenes and perspectives; documentary tables record institutional measurements; editorial summaries frame the story; an interview reports an author's stated intention or interpretation. These layers can support one another without becoming interchangeable. A score table cannot negate a character's unmeasured capacity, and an editorial description cannot be treated as transparent access to every motive. A bonus story acquired at the boundary can concern an earlier event, as the Kei date and Ichinose/Amikura material do.

Two closeout corrections demonstrate why source surfaces remain necessary even when a large analytical corpus already exists. The guidebook's first-year points table was misrouted to Ayanokōji's earlier cohort; its heading and named students resolve it as the younger cohort during his Year 2. An early illustration row misidentified the companion behind Hōsen as Horikita; direct inspection and the adjoining introductions establish Nanase. The original local records remain intact, with explicit current-boundary correction routes. The purpose is recoverable revision, not the appearance of an error-free historical archive.

[Y2_10][s10] and the [Volume-0 retrospective ledger][retrospective] preserve the difference among development, revelation of prior state, recontextualization, correction, relationship-specific expression and unresolved contradiction. The full-year model is stronger because it records which kind of change has occurred. It does not make every new explanation a discovery that the earlier person never changed or that the earlier reader should already have known the answer.

## 9. Japanese form makes the problem of recognition part of reading itself

The year is not only making propositions about other minds. Its narration makes the reader practice the uncertain work of attributing them. Internal access, withheld plans, changes of focal ownership and the timing of revelation can draw the reader into confidence before supplying the conditions that qualify it. The important question is not whether the narrator is simply reliable or unreliable. It is what a particular passage gives access to, whose thought it is, and which inference the reader adds.

V12.5's breakup is the clearest terminal example. A passage can seem to supply Ayanokōji with a sudden romantic recognition, but the later identification of Kei's hoped-for interior changes the ownership of that apparent recognition. Quoting the emotional sentences without the あたし and 願望 disclosure would create evidence the source does not supply. The correction concerns focal ownership, not a warrant to distrust every ordinary feeling recorded elsewhere. [Y2_13][s13], JP-E09, provides the exact local route.

Forms of address likewise make relations legible without solving them. A surname can mark a changed boundary in an interaction while a given name persists in private thought. An institutional or paternal use of 必要不可欠 can claim dependence, while Horikita's use offers protection from the standpoint of the class. The same term does not carry the same obligation independent of the speaker. The written-voice ledger's repeated evidence is therefore more useful than a glossary that assigns one English moral meaning to each Japanese word.

The competing metaphors sharpen the distinction. Ayanokōji's training wheels promise eventual removal from a position of guidance. His direct tool language objectifies a named person in an intervention. Sakayanagi's suggestion of experimentation remains her hypothesis, while Atsuomi's sample vocabulary belongs to actual institutional planning. The noun alone cannot tell the reader whether a sentence is self-description, accusation, speculation or rule. Ethical interpretation depends on retaining those speech acts and their evidence limits.

Humor, ordinary conversation and genre shifts are equally consequential. The decimal volumes and quieter passages do not merely interrupt the examinations. They show what actors find amusing, awkward, interesting, tiring or worth doing without an immediate competitive payoff. A character reconstructed only from crisis competence becomes implausibly constant in ordinary circumstances. Kiyotaka's literary company, Kei's frustration with a date's design and the ensemble's peer learning expose preferences that a strategic summary would otherwise discard.

Those ordinary scenes are not outside the school or immune to strategic use. The same relational knowledge that makes someone recognizable can make them vulnerable. This is why the novel's movement among examination conflict, romance, comedy, institutional intrigue and reflective narration belongs to the integrated argument. The reader repeatedly encounters a person in one frame and must resist allowing that frame to exhaust them in another.

Illustrations add an editorial selection of bodies, proximity, vulnerability, scale and action. They can foreground a helping hand or the bodily arrangement of an encounter without independently certifying interior motive. The closeout's Nanase correction shows the importance of identifying the pictured actor before making a compositional argument. It does not reduce the visual layer to decoration; it makes visual claims accountable to the actual image. [Y2_09][s09] owns the written and visual analysis, with no inference of performed audio from prose or punctuation.

## 10. The strongest defense of authorship, and where it fails

A fair assessment of Ayanokōji and the school's other powerful actors must begin with the case for their interventions. The environment already contains coercion, opacity and unequal capacity. Refusing to act does not restore an untouched baseline. A capable intervenor can expose a threat, preserve a future, make practical cooperation possible or give someone access to a conversation they could not otherwise initiate. Some beneficiaries develop capacities and purposes they value. A theory that counted only the original asymmetry would miss those real benefits and the agency of people who accept assistance.

Nor does meaningful consent require equal intelligence, identical information about every subject or freedom from every dependency. Students rely on teachers; friends rely on one another; political communities allocate different roles. An unequal relation can be valuable and answerable. In several Year-2 cases, its value comes precisely from one person being able to supply something another presently lacks. The ethical criticism cannot rest on the mere existence of influence or superior understanding.

It becomes stronger when it identifies a further claim: that understanding another person, benefiting them, or anticipating their answer grants authority to set the purpose, withhold the relevant alternatives, and impose the acceptable timetable. The V11 stopping rule shows that assistance can distinguish itself from that claim. The V12 intervention shows how relational knowledge can intensify it. Comparing those cases makes the critique specific. It concerns the scope and revisability of power rather than treating every act of guidance as the same moral event.

A consequential defense also owes an account of necessity and distribution. That a strategy produces a desirable result does not prove that the harmed person had to bear its cost or that every alternative was worse. The person who designs the choice environment may possess unusually good information about feasibility; they may also control what everyone else can know about that feasibility. A claim that only their method could work should therefore not be accepted solely because they successfully made it work. The source's competing agents, ordinary contributions and model failures keep the counterfactual question alive.

The analysis must be equally careful not to turn counter-authorship into an automatic moral endorsement. Ichinose's unpredicted answer proves that she exceeds a supplied model. It does not prove purity of motive or complete reciprocal information. Horikita's independent decision proves authorship, but still produces an unchosen cost for Airi. Ryūen's followers can choose him without thereby possessing protected standing to alter his moral limit. Sakayanagi can choose a personally meaningful departure while failing a duty to the class. Autonomy and ethical adequacy require related but distinct judgments.

Several apparent contradictions consequently become more precise without disappearing. Ordinary affect and harmful authorship can coexist within Ayanokōji. Personal development and fiduciary failure can coexist within Sakayanagi. Solidarity and pressure to conform can coexist in Ichinose's polity. Delegation and coercive supremacy can coexist under Ryūen. These are not failures to decide what the text means. They are findings about different relations and kinds of authority operating simultaneously. A synthesis should eliminate a demonstrable misidentification or wrong destination, but it should not eliminate an actual ethical tension for the sake of a cleaner character verdict.

The resulting normative standard is demanding but bounded. Ask whether the person affected can know enough to make the relevant choice, whether an alternative or refusal has meaningful standing, whether dependence can change without punitive disposability, and whether the stronger actor can accept correction that alters their end. Year 2 provides local evidence of these possibilities. It does not establish them as a stable general settlement.

## 11. What the boundary knows, and what it must leave open

The completed year establishes actual growth, actual injury and actual separation. It also establishes changed institutional conditions: a transferred strategic center, altered leadership, external political visibility and a different field of class competition. These are not predictions. What follows from them is a new set of questions whose answers cannot be inferred from the mere existence of the conditions.

The inherited tracker preserves forty-two terminal adjudications: fifteen answered by development, three by revelation, seven reframed, thirteen partially answered and four strictly open. “Answered” is not a synonym for a favorable result. The negative finding about Kei's informational reciprocity and the negative finding about Sakayanagi's internally generated succession are part of the answer. A later reversal would require later evidence and a new transition, not a revision that pretends the Year-2 boundary already contained it.

The four strict opens have concrete missing conditions. `Y1H-004` requires voluntary disclosure early and complete enough to enable another person's consequential informed choice. `Y1H-016` requires Horikita to witness and interpret genuine Kiyotaka failure, error or loss of control. `Y1H-026` requires Hiyori to change Ryūen's own decision, moral limit or governing standard. `Y1H-035` requires Kiyotaka to expose weakness in a way that grants another meaningful protective or corrective authority. Affection, competence, controlled withdrawal, indirect influence and preference disclosure cannot be substituted for those conditions.

The partially answered questions retain their demonstrated components. Horikita's independent judgment does not have to be rediscovered merely because post-transfer reproduction remains untested. A local stopping rule does not cease to exist because its generalization is uncertain. Mobility's real opportunities remain relevant alongside dependency and abandonment. Institutional witnesses can have a genuine limiting effect even when their durability under capture or interested authority is unresolved. The boundary inherits developed people and partially altered systems, not blank hypotheses.

The twelve Year-2-generated `Y2H` tests in [Y2_11][s11] carry these problems into their new circumstances. They ask about the imported center in the Sakayanagi-origin polity, Horikita's class after departure, Kei's own future, Ichinose's durable terms, the standing of Ryūen's followers, Sakayanagi beyond office, Kōenji's response to a freedom condition, council practice, political permeability, Kiyotaka's answerability, ordinary successor purposes and unresolved identity/admission claims. They are prospective tests with discriminating evidence requirements, not forecasts dressed as findings.

The [behavioral reconstruction protocol][protocol] remains downstream of that boundary. Its calibrated hypothetical cases may constrain plausible behavior or require abstention. They cannot create an event that answers a handoff question. Support, determinacy, behavior permission and exact-voice permission remain separate; sparse evidence about an institutional role must not be expanded into an invented portable personality. No prospective validation pair exists merely because a hypothetical sounds coherent.

This full synthesis completes the integrated analytical responsibility of the year. It does not by itself establish the release gate. The [project status][status] and final [corpus map][readme] own the administrative state, including the protocol audit, manifests, checksums and exact published verification. Year-3 narrative evidence remains excluded throughout this artifact. After every closure requirement is verified, a later execution may begin Y3V01 under the governing method; no Year-3 analysis is performed here.

Year 2 leaves its characters with more capacity to act and more reasons that are their own. It also leaves powerful actors with better knowledge of how to use those reasons. The unresolved achievement is whether a developed person can retain authority over a future that the developer did not write—and whether the developer can learn to answer to it.
