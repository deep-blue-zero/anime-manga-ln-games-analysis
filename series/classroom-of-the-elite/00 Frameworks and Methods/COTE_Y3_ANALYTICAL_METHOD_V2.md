# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 3 Rolling Analytical Method v2.0
### Japanese-primary deep reading, provisional snapshots, environmental authorship, class-polity tracking, consent, and reciprocal development

## 0. Status and purpose

This document governs the sequential second-pass/deep-reading corpus for **Year 3** of 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』 / *Classroom of the Elite*.

Year 3 is still incomplete. That changes the archival rules.

This method therefore has two simultaneous jobs:

1. preserve the full provenance, source, locator, evidence, Japanese-language, and revision standards established in the Year 1 and Year 2 methods;
2. prevent a provisional corpus from presenting temporary conclusions as completed-year judgments.

The currently verified source boundary is:

- `Y3V01`
- `Y3V02`
- `Y3V03`
- `Y3V04`

Future volumes should be appended in publication order without changing the stable source-code system.

The Year 3 method inherits unchanged:

- original-Japanese primacy;
- source SHA-256 and normalized-text fingerprinting;
- deterministic spine/XHTML/paragraph locators;
- illustration locators;
- evidence/claim codes and confidence grading;
- prior-analysis correction;
- local versus retrospective reading;
- YAML and canonical filename standards;
- controlled longitudinal thread IDs;
- primary-home/anti-duplication rules;
- and synthesis → artifact → evidence → locator → source traceability.

Year 3 adds new analytical machinery because several problems become more explicit:

- class transfer and unstable class labels;
- visible versus hidden sovereignty;
- environmental authorship;
- preference engineering;
- sincere choice under deliberately manufactured conditions;
- separation from former dependents and allies;
- whether development survives the developer;
- personally irreplaceable relationships;
- reciprocal authorship, where other people change Ayanokōji in ways he did not design;
- and ordinary life as a possible counter-curriculum to every system trying to assign purpose to his development.

The governing archival rule is:

> **No Year 3 artifact may imply finality beyond its stated source boundary.**

The governing interpretive rule is:

> **Distinguish control over another person's action from control over the conditions under which that person sincerely forms a preference.**

# I. Core Year 3 theses to test, not assume

The current provisional synthesis suggests a movement from **designing the experiment toward discovering what cannot be designed**. Treat that formulation as a hypothesis.

Every new volume should test:

1. Is Ayanokōji becoming more publicly accountable, or merely more openly sovereign?
2. Does moving between classes increase his political risk enough to reduce his hidden authority, or does it enlarge the scale of his experiment?
3. Can Horikita's development persist when Ayanokōji is no longer structurally embedded in her class?
4. Does a class leader remain legitimate after the exceptional person who stabilized the class leaves?
5. Can Ayanokōji engineer a decision environment without thereby appropriating the chooser's agency?
6. When a person sincerely chooses the option he wanted, did he persuade them, manipulate them, coerce them, or author the scarcity that made the option rational?
7. Can a relationship become personally irreplaceable to Ayanokōji without becoming strategically possessed?
8. Does separation produce autonomy or merely expose hidden dependence?
9. Are his romantic and friendship relationships increasingly reciprocal in information, vulnerability, and capacity to alter him?
10. What experiences change Ayanokōji despite not being assigned a developmental purpose?
11. Can he accept another person's growth when it leads away from his plan?
12. Can he value a person whose usefulness declines?
13. Can he surrender optimization because another person's preference matters independently?
14. Does Year 3 increasingly distinguish public leadership from environmental authorship?
15. Is the school's competitive structure becoming something Ayanokōji wants to preserve, transform, transcend, or simply use?

The method must retain enough uncertainty for later Year 3 volumes to overturn current answers.

# XII. Public sovereignty versus hidden sovereignty

Year 1 and much of Year 2 allow Ayanokōji to exercise power while minimizing visible responsibility.

Year 3 should track whether that changes.

For every major operation distinguish:

### Hidden sovereignty

Ayanokōji determines outcomes while another actor receives public authorship.

### Delegated sovereignty

He shapes the plan but gives another actor meaningful implementation freedom.

### Negotiated sovereignty

He bargains openly with other actors whose refusal remains meaningful.

### Public sovereignty

His ability, allegiance, or responsibility becomes visible enough that affected parties can correctly identify him as the decision-maker.

### Institutional sovereignty

He gains or uses a formal office, rule-based power, or institutional position.

Ask:

> Does greater visibility produce greater accountability, or merely make domination more efficient?

# XIII. Environmental authorship protocol

This is mandatory in Year 3.

For every significant intervention by Ayanokōji—or any actor employing an equivalent method—record:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Desired outcome | What result does the actor want? |
| Direct command | Is the target explicitly ordered? |
| Information control | What does the target know or not know? |
| Incentive design | Which rewards or penalties are authored? |
| Scarcity | Is a constraint natural, institutional, or deliberately manufactured? |
| Alternatives | What other viable choices exist? |
| Refusal capacity | Can the target say no without catastrophic penalty? |
| Preference formation | Does the target sincerely come to prefer the desired option? |
| Post-choice autonomy | Who controls what happens after the choice? |
| Benefit distribution | Who gains from the arrangement? |
| Developmental result | Does the target become more independent? |
| Ethical classification | persuasion / bargaining / manipulation / coercion / preference engineering / manufactured necessity / unresolved |

Do not collapse all influence into coercion.

Do not treat sincere preference as automatically autonomous.

The key question is:

> **Who authored the conditions under which the preference became rational?**

# XIV. Multidimensional consent protocol

Year 3 requires more precision than a binary consent/nonconsent label.

Analyze at least:

1. **information sufficiency** — does the chooser know the relevant facts?
2. **alternative availability** — are viable alternatives actually present?
3. **exit possibility** — can the chooser leave later?
4. **material leverage** — are money, points, status, or survival at stake?
5. **institutional compulsion** — does the school force participation?
6. **emotional leverage** — are love, guilt, fear, loyalty, or dependency exploited?
7. **manufactured scarcity** — did another actor deliberately remove alternatives?
8. **temporal pressure** — is the chooser given meaningful time?
9. **post-choice ownership** — does agreement become interpreted as ongoing jurisdiction?
10. **capacity for revision** — can the chooser later change their mind?

Use descriptive matrices, not a single numerical morality score.

# XV. Irreplaceability and fungibility protocol

For major relationships, maintain an evidence-based status:

```text
interchangeable
useful_but_replaceable
high_value_but_replaceable
functionally_irreplaceable
personally_irreplaceable
claimed_irreplaceable_but_unverified
unresolved
```

Changing a status requires evidence.

Distinguish:

- tactical indispensability;
- emotional exclusivity;
- unique historical knowledge;
- romantic attachment;
- grief at loss;
- unwillingness to substitute another person;
- and willingness to sacrifice strategic advantage for the person.

Do not infer personal irreplaceability merely because a character is highly useful.

Do not infer replaceability merely because Ayanokōji continues to use instrumental language.

---

# XVI. Reciprocal-authorship protocol

A central Year 3 question is whether other people can alter Ayanokōji in ways he did not select.

Maintain evidence for:

- altered preference;
- emotional surprise;
- spontaneous attachment;
- changed plan;
- accepted strategic cost;
- revised self-understanding;
- surrendered optimization;
- apology or reconsideration not required by strategy;
- continued relationship despite reduced utility;
- allowing another person's goal to redefine his own.

Required question in every major relationship section:

> **What has this person changed in Ayanokōji that Ayanokōji did not intentionally arrange?**

This is the positive counterpart to environmental authorship.

A relationship can be highly asymmetrical while still containing genuine reciprocal influence. The task is to identify both.

# XVII. Succession and separation protocol

Continue `COTE_SUCCESSION_SEPARATION_LEDGER.md` with special Year 3 emphasis.

For every major separation:

- Was it voluntary?
- Was it planned by the departing person?
- Was the dependent party informed?
- Which functions disappear?
- Which functions had already been internalized?
- Who fills the vacuum?
- Does independence increase?
- Does collapse reveal that prior development was superficial?
- Does the departing actor secretly continue controlling the structure?

The key question remains:

> **Does development survive the developer?**

Year 3 should be especially suspicious of “independence” that remains sustained by invisible intervention from the supposedly absent actor.

# XVIII. Ordinary life and counter-curriculum

Year 3 should formally distinguish:

### Assigned experience

An experience given a developmental, strategic, institutional, romantic, or observational purpose before it occurs.

### Emergent experience

An experience whose meaning develops without a predetermined instructional outcome.

Track:

- meals;
- books;
- leisure;
- hobbies;
- travel;
- gifts;
- phone calls/messages;
- physical closeness;
- jokes;
- boredom;
- ordinary study;
- chosen companionship.

Ask:

- Was the experience arranged to teach someone something?
- Did Ayanokōji enter it for strategy and later value it intrinsically?
- Did the experience alter him without offering measurable benefit?
- Does the school or White Room attempt to appropriate ordinary life by assigning it purpose?

The governing hypothesis to test is:

> **Some of Ayanokōji's most significant human development may occur precisely in experiences nobody successfully designed as lessons.**

# XIX. Ayanokōji protocol for Year 3

Retain the four Year 2 layers:

1. ordinary adolescent;
2. relational;
3. White Room strategic conditioning;
4. environmental author/developmental director.

Add:

5. **person subject to reciprocal authorship**.

Do not treat evidence for one layer as cancelling another.

Ayanokōji may simultaneously:

- care;
- manipulate;
- enjoy;
- calculate;
- protect;
- engineer scarcity;
- value another person intrinsically;
- and retain a strategic theory of that relationship.

The analytical task is to determine which layer explains which action and whether the balance changes over time.

# XX. Horikita independence protocol

Year 3 is a particularly strong test of the Year 1/2 thesis that Horikita has become an autonomous leader.

For each volume record:

- decisions made without Ayanokōji input;
- decisions made against his preference;
- correct inferences about his motives;
- mistaken inferences;
- delegation;
- ability to manage internal dissent;
- ability to replace functions he previously supplied;
- willingness to preserve or remove members;
- relationship with former classmates after class transfer;
- and whether her leadership remains legitimate under loss.

Do not treat independent error as evidence of failed independence.

A genuinely autonomous leader must be capable of being wrong without ceasing to be autonomous.

# XXVII. Counterargument requirements

Every checkpoint synthesis should test at least these alternatives:

### Environmental authorship

Counterclaim: Ayanokōji may be coordinating rational choices rather than appropriating agency.

### Horikita independence

Counterclaim: continued unseen support may mean apparent independence is overstated.

### Irreplaceability

Counterclaim: emotionally intense language or exceptional effort may reflect strategy, guilt, novelty, or possessiveness rather than personal nonfungibility.

### Reciprocal authorship

Counterclaim: changing a plan because another actor behaved unexpectedly is not automatically emotional development.

### Ordinary life

Counterclaim: an experience need not be strategically useless to be emotionally genuine; strategy and attachment can coexist.

### Public sovereignty

Counterclaim: visibility may increase accountability, but it may also let exceptional power normalize itself as legitimate authority.

The method should record evidence for the strongest plausible competing reading.

# XXIX. Governing Year 3 question

The Year 3 method should ultimately help the series answer a harder version of the question inherited from Years 1 and 2:

> **Can a person who has become exceptionally capable of authoring other people's environments remain free without claiming authorship over their preferences—and can he become a participant in relationships where other people possess enough independence to change him in ways he did not design?**

Until Year 3 is complete, every answer to that question remains provisional by design.
