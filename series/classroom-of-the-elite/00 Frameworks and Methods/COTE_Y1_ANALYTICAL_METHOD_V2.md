# 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』
## Year 1 Second-Pass Analytical Method v2.0
### Japanese-primary deep reading, provenance, Library retrieval, and multi-document synthesis protocol

## 0. Status and purpose

This document governs the **second analytical pass over Year 1** of 『ようこそ実力至上主義の教室へ』 / *Classroom of the Elite*.

The project has already completed:

- a sequential first deep reading of every Year 1 volume;
- a review of the Year 1 Official Guidebook — *First File*;
- a complete Year 1 synthesis;
- subsequent Year 2 and Year 3 analysis that has produced later knowledge capable of recontextualizing Year 1.

Those earlier analyses are valuable as an interpretive record, but they are **not the governing evidence for the second pass**. The original Japanese novels are the evidentiary authority. The second pass must verify, revise, sharpen, or reject prior conclusions against the primary text.

The objective is not merely to rewrite the old analyses more elegantly. It is to build an **auditable analytical corpus** that can support:

- future Year 1 synthesis;
- cross-year character and relationship tracking;
- the eventual all-years *Classroom of the Elite* synthesis;
- exact retrieval through ChatGPT Library search;
- provenance and source verification;
- Japanese-language analysis;
- institutional, political, ethical, and psychological comparison;
- cross-franchise comparative work;
- and later adaptation analysis without contaminating the novel record.

The governing principle is:

> **Every important conclusion should be recoverable through a traceability chain:**
>
> **series/year synthesis → thematic or character document → volume deep reading → evidence entry → source locator → original Japanese passage or illustration.**

A second governing principle is:

> **The second pass must preserve development rather than flattening Year 1 into what later years eventually reveal.**

Later knowledge can create a retrospective annotation. It must not be silently projected backward into what a Year 1 volume itself establishes.

# I. Core analytical thesis to test, not assume

The first Year 1 synthesis produced a strong working formulation:

> **Year 1 is about the conflict between human development and human control.**

Ayanokōji repeatedly helps people grow because he understands them unusually well. He also violates their autonomy because he often understands growth as something an exceptional observer may engineer from outside.

The second pass should use this as a **hypothesis**, not as an answer to be reproduced automatically.

Questions to test across every volume include:

1. Does the text consistently distinguish **development** from **control**, or does that opposition become too simple in some cases?
2. When Ayanokōji intervenes, what exactly does he know, predict, manipulate, permit, or misunderstand?
3. When a person changes after his intervention, how much of the change belongs to his design and how much exceeds it?
4. Does the school genuinely cultivate latent ability, or does it merely reward students already capable of adapting to artificial pressure?
5. How does the meaning of **実力 / jitsuryoku** change from academic competence to social usability, institutional legibility, political influence, and developmental capacity?
6. When does protection preserve agency, and when does it become ownership or dependency?
7. How does the class system transform friendship, romance, reputation, and private injury into political resources?
8. Does the series treat expulsion as educational necessity, institutional cruelty, strategic fact, or all three?
9. Which characters become less legible when reduced to the school’s ratings, roles, or reputations?
10. What kinds of ordinary life remain outside, resist, or reconfigure the school’s competitive logic?
11. How does Ayanokōji’s first-person narration simultaneously disclose and conceal his emotional life?
12. How often does the novel invite admiration of effective manipulation while also providing evidence of its ethical cost?

These questions should guide retrieval. They must not force the evidence into a predetermined conclusion.

The project already knows later information. That makes epistemic discipline more important, not less.

- **what the volume establishes now**;
- **what a character can know now**;
- **what a first-time reader could reasonably infer now**;
- **what later material eventually establishes**.

The goal is to preserve both narrative experience and mature canonical knowledge.

# III. Source hierarchy

## Tier 1A — Original Japanese Year 1 light novels

These are the governing narrative evidence.

They control:

- event chronology;
- Ayanokōji’s narration;
- dialogue and Japanese voice;
- examination rules;
- character interiority;
- institutional information;
- relationship development;
- and the narrative meaning of illustrations embedded in the novels.

When a prior analysis conflicts with the Japanese novel, the novel governs.

## Tier 1B — Novel illustrations

Illustrations are primary paratextual/visual evidence, not substitutes for prose.

They can establish or sharpen:

- clothing;
- hairstyle;
- facial expression;
- bodily distance;
- posture;
- social presentation;
- editorial emphasis;
- and the visual framing of a scene.

They should not be used to invent dialogue, unseen causal information, or precise internal states absent from the text.

## Tier 1C — *First File* exclusive fiction

Any newly included fictional short story should be treated as canonical narrative evidence within the guidebook’s publication position.

It may refine Year 1 conclusions, especially about:

- ordinary life;
- relationships;
- emotional development;
- and end-of-year state.

## Tier 2A — *First File* direct factual data

Examples:

- student numbers;
- birthdays;
- club membership;
- explicitly stated background information;
- school facilities;
- exam results;
- institutional facts.

Treat these as strong official reference evidence unless contradicted by the primary novels.

## Tier 2B — *First File* ratings and institutional measurements

Ability charts, hypothetical ratings, or OAA-style reconstructions are **institutional snapshots**, not omniscient character truth.

Always ask:

- What is actually being measured?
- At what date?
- Is the student suppressing performance?
- Does the metric capture hidden social or strategic power?
- Does an aggregate score confuse convenience with total value?

## Tier 2C — *First File* editorial framing

Character summaries, headings, retrospective “technique” pages, and other official packaging can sharpen how the franchise frames Year 1.

They remain compressed interpretations.

The novels outrank them when the prose is more nuanced.

## Tier 3 — Prior Year 1 analyses

The prior transcript, volume analyses, *First File* review, and full-year synthesis are:

- hypothesis indexes;
- lists of scenes to reverify;
- records of earlier blind spots;
- and targets for correction.

They are **not proof**.

Every load-bearing prior conclusion should be categorized during the second pass as:

- confirmed;
- strengthened;
- weakened;
- corrected;
- rejected;
- or unresolved.

## Tier 4 — Later-year material

Year 2, Volume 0, *Second List*, and Year 3 are authoritative for later canonical facts but **non-governing for Year 1-local interpretation**.

They belong in retrospective annotations only.

## Tier 5 — Adaptations and external material

Excluded from the core Year 1 synthesis unless explicitly commissioned as a later comparison.

# IV. Corpus audit and source-identity protocol

Before analyzing a volume, perform a source audit.

For every EPUB, record:

1. exact filename;
2. byte size;
3. SHA-256 of the original EPUB;
4. internal title and metadata title;
5. language identity by inspection of actual text, not filename alone;
6. package/OPF path;
7. spine order;
8. chapter XHTML files;
9. image count;
10. illustration inventory;
11. presence of afterword or publisher material;
12. ruby structure and text-extraction behavior;
13. structural integrity;
14. duplicate/superseded-copy status;
15. normalized text SHA-256 if a deterministic spine-concatenated derivative is generated.

## 1. Dual fingerprint

Where practical, record both:

- `source_sha256` — hash of the exact EPUB bytes;
- `normalized_text_sha256` — hash of normalized base Japanese text in spine order.

This distinguishes:

- byte-identical copies;
- repackaged copies with the same text;
- genuinely different textual sources.

## 2. Source status values

Use one of:

- `verified_primary`
- `verified_duplicate`
- `verified_repackaged_same_text`
- `superseded_source`
- `questionable_language_identity`
- `partial_source`
- `unverified`

Do not silently replace a bad source with another copy without recording which file was superseded.

# V. Canonical locator protocol

EPUB page numbers are unstable. The corpus must use deterministic structural locators.

## 1. Locator format

Preferred compact form:

> `Y1V07|spine:09|xhtml:Text/chapter_05.xhtml|p:0042|anchor:「……」`

Minimum required fields:

- source code;
- spine index;
- internal XHTML path or stable manifest identifier;
- paragraph index within normalized base text;
- short Japanese anchor phrase where useful.

## 2. Chapter-level human-readable locator

In prose, also provide a readable form:

> `Y1V07, Chapter 5, rooftop confrontation, para 42`

The exact chapter title should be included where the EPUB supplies one.

## 3. Deterministic paragraph indexing

When extracting text:

- follow EPUB spine order;
- preserve Japanese base text from ruby;
- omit `rt` and `rp` annotation text from the normalized paragraph body;
- preserve paragraph boundaries;
- number substantive paragraphs deterministically;
- do not silently merge dialogue paragraphs.

## 4. Illustration locator

Use:

> `Y1V11.5|img:012|source:Images/p_173.jpg|context:Horikita post-haircut`

Where the image belongs to a specific spine item, record both.

## 5. Locator confidence

If an old analysis contains a claim but the exact paragraph has not yet been reverified, use:

- `locator_status: pending_backfill`

Do not invent a paragraph number or image index.

## 6. Source-map artifacts

Strongly recommended derived administrative files:

- `COTE_Y1_SOURCE_INVENTORY.md`
- `COTE_Y1_SOURCE_MAP.json`
- `COTE_Y1_SOURCE_CHECKSUMS.sha256`

These files contain metadata and locators only, not copyrighted source text.

# VI. Evidence and claim classification

Every evidence entry should use two fields:

1. **source layer**;
2. **claim type**.

## 1. Source-layer codes

### `LN-TEXT`
Original Japanese novel prose or dialogue.

### `LN-ILL`
Original novel illustration.

### `FF-FICTION`
*First File* exclusive fictional material.

### `FF-DATA`
Direct factual guidebook data.

### `FF-RATING`
Institutional/statistical rating or hypothetical measurement.

### `FF-EDITORIAL`
Official retrospective/editorial framing.

### `PRIOR`
Prior project analysis; retrieval aid only, never primary evidence.

### `LATER`
Later-year evidence used only in explicit retrospective annotation.

## 2. Claim-type codes

### `TF` — Textual fact
Directly established event, action, dialogue, rule, or unambiguous description.

### `CI` — Character interior/self-report
A character’s own internal interpretation, remembered motive, claim, or self-description.

Ayanokōji’s interior narration should not automatically be treated as omniscient truth about his own motives.

### `IR` — Institutional rule or record
Explicit school rule, point value, formal result, administrative statement, or official record.

### `VF` — Visual fact
Something directly visible in an illustration.

### `SI` — Strong inference
Not directly stated, but supported by multiple converging textual or visual signals.

### `IT` — Interpretive thesis
Higher-level literary, psychological, political, or thematic formulation.

### `UA` — Unresolved ambiguity
The text supports multiple live interpretations and does not yet settle them.

### `VJ` — Value judgment
Normative claim such as coercive, disproportionate, legitimate, abusive, humane, exploitative, or just.

The ethical standard must be stated rather than disguised as neutral fact.

### `RC` — Retrospective correction
Later primary material establishes that an earlier inference or interpretation was incomplete or wrong.

### `RR` — Retrospective recontextualization
Later material changes the significance of an earlier scene without making the earlier reading false.

## 3. Confidence grade

Optional but recommended:

- `H` — high confidence;
- `M` — medium confidence;
- `L` — low confidence.

Example:

> `Evidence ID: Y1V09-E031 | Layer: LN-TEXT | Type: SI | Confidence: H`

## 4. Negative evidence

When a strong interpretation depends partly on absence, record the absence carefully.

Example form:

> “Across the verified Year 1 passages reviewed, Ayanokōji repeatedly describes X but never explicitly claims Y.”

Do not convert failure to find something into proof that it does not exist unless the search scope is genuinely exhaustive.

# VII. Local-reading and retrospective-reading protocol

Because this is a second pass conducted after later years are known, every analysis must guard against retrospective flattening.

## 1. Local reading

The main body of each volume analysis must answer:

> **What does this volume establish at the moment of publication within the agreed reading sequence?**

Do not use later revelations to make characters appear more transparent than they are.

## 2. Retrospective delta

Each volume may contain a final section titled:

> `Retrospective annotations reserved for later-year integration`

During the Year 1 pass, this section should normally contain only:

- links/placeholders for future cross-year revision;
- no imported Year 2/Volume 0/Year 3 claims unless the user explicitly authorizes retrospective annotation now.

## 3. Later revision rule

When a later year genuinely revises Year 1, record:

- original Year 1 claim;
- evidence that supported it then;
- later source and locator;
- whether the change is correction or recontextualization;
- revised all-years interpretation.

Never rewrite the Year 1 artifact so that the original uncertainty disappears.

# VIII. Second-pass workflow for every volume

## Phase 1 — Source audit

Verify the EPUB and record source identity.

## Phase 2 — Full reread in spine order

Read the complete substantive text.

Do not begin with keyword search alone.

The objective is to reconstruct:

- narrative rhythm;
- chapter progression;
- ordinary scenes;
- jokes;
- transitions;
- and quiet character evidence that a search-driven analysis would miss.

## Phase 3 — Chapter map

Record for every chapter:

- chapter title;
- major scenes;
- POV/narrative mode;
- examination stage where relevant;
- key relationship changes;
- major Japanese formulations;
- illustration placement.

## Phase 4 — Targeted retrieval pass

After full reading, search the volume for:

- names and forms of address;
- recurring vocabulary;
- key examination rules;
- point amounts;
- explicit motives;
- repeated metaphors;
- pronoun/register changes;
- relationship terms;
- `実力`, `平等`, `自由`, `信頼`, `恐怖`, `道具`, `所有`, `守る`, `退学`, `過去`, `罪`, `許す`, and other volume-specific concepts.

Search is a retrieval aid, not a substitute for context.

## Phase 5 — Prior-analysis audit

Read the earlier volume analysis only after completing the fresh source reread.

Classify prior claims under:

- `CONFIRMED`
- `STRENGTHENED`
- `WEAKENED`
- `CORRECTED`
- `REJECTED`
- `NEWLY_FOUND`
- `UNRESOLVED`

The second pass must be willing to say that a memorable previous thesis was overstated.

## Phase 6 — Evidence ledger

Create evidence IDs before final drafting.

Required categories:

- decisive event;
- character turn;
- relationship change;
- institutional/rule evidence;
- ethical case;
- Japanese voice/language evidence;
- illustration/visual evidence;
- counterevidence;
- open question.

## Phase 7 — Draft the canonical Markdown artifact

The Markdown file produced for the volume is the canonical analytical artifact.

Do not treat the chat response as the archival source and migrate later if avoidable.

## Phase 8 — Quality and provenance audit

Before delivery, verify:

- YAML parses conceptually;
- source SHA matches the audited EPUB;
- every quoted Japanese phrase has a locator;
- every evidence ID is unique;
- no later-year spoilers leaked into the local analysis;
- no placeholders remain except explicitly labeled `pending_backfill` or `future_retrospective`;
- internal links resolve;
- the artifact contains no invented pagination.

# XI. Required per-volume analytical structure

The following is the default template. Sections may be merged when a volume genuinely does not support them, but the evidence functions should remain represented.

## 1. Scope, source, and provenance

Include:

- source identity;
- spoiler boundary;
- source audit summary;
- chapter count;
- illustration count;
- prior-analysis status;
- any source caveats.

## 2. Executive thesis

A concise statement of what problem the volume is fundamentally exploring.

Avoid plot-summary theses such as:

> “This volume is about the sports festival.”

Prefer a formulation such as:

> “The sports festival turns individual excellence into a test of whether strength can become socially usable without ceasing to be individual.”

## 3. Position in Year 1 architecture

Explain:

- what the previous volume left unresolved;
- what new scale or problem this volume introduces;
- what later Year 1 question it prepares without spoiling the answer.

## 4. Chapter and narrative architecture

Map the volume’s major movements.

Track:

- shifts between ordinary life and examination;
- concealed versus public causality;
- when information is withheld from reader or characters;
- narrative pacing;
- monologue placement;
- cliffhangers and retrospective explanations.

## 5. Examination or institutional mechanism

Where relevant, reconstruct the rules exactly.

Use the dedicated examination template in Section XIV.

## 6. Ayanokōji analysis

Always examine:

- outward speech;
- interior narration;
- competence deliberately displayed or suppressed;
- emotional reactions;
- strategic actions;
- relation to ordinary life;
- ethical boundaries;
- White Room conditioning;
- what he notices about other people;
- what he fails or refuses to notice about himself.

## 7. Major character developments

For every major character in the volume, track:

- starting state;
- pressure/event;
- choice;
- consequence;
- self-interpretation;
- external interpretation;
- whether the change is durable, provisional, or unclear.

## 8. Relationship architecture

Analyze relationships as structures, not just sentiments.

Track:

- affection;
- dependence;
- knowledge asymmetry;
- leverage;
- trust;
- ability to refuse;
- names/forms of address;
- mutuality;
- institutional consequences;
- what changes after the volume.

## 9. Class and political development

Ask:

- Who has authority?
- Who appears to have authority?
- Who controls information?
- Who bears costs?
- Who can veto?
- What kind of legitimacy does the class accept?
- How is leadership distributed?

## 10. Institutional analysis

Track the school as designer:

- rules;
- surveillance;
- scarcity;
- ranking;
- punishment;
- reward;
- hidden evaluation;
- teacher discretion;
- adult interference.

## 11. Ability and legibility

Distinguish:

- possessed ability;
- displayed ability;
- measured ability;
- socially usable ability;
- developmental ability.

Record where these diverge.

## 12. Ethics and autonomy

For each major intervention, ask:

1. Who selected the goal?
2. Who understood the risks?
3. Could the affected person refuse?
4. Was suffering necessary, foreseeable, or deliberately engineered?
5. Did the intervention increase later agency?
6. Did it create dependence upon the intervener?
7. Could a less coercive alternative plausibly have worked?
8. Does the narrative aestheticize effectiveness in a way that risks obscuring harm?

## 13. Japanese narration, voice, and register

Analyze only patterns supported by repeated evidence.

Record:

- pronouns;
- sentence endings;
- honorifics;
- address terms;
- slang;
- politeness;
- register changes;
- rhythm;
- emotional leakage;
- public/private differences.

## 14. Appearance, illustration, and embodied characterization

Track:

- hair;
- clothing;
- posture;
- body language;
- scars;
- illness;
- athleticity;
- spatial distance;
- visual status performance.

Distinguish prose fact from illustration emphasis.

## 15. Motifs and recurring language

Examples across Year 1 may include:

- tools;
- defects;
- ownership;
- parasites;
- doors;
- mirrors;
- hair;
- gates;
- points;
- fear;
- rumor;
- memory;
- names;
- ordinary food;
- visibility and surveillance.

Do not force a motif merely because it appears once.

## 16. Formal and literary strengths

Assess:

- viewpoint control;
- suspense;
- recontextualization;
- dramatic irony;
- integration of examination mechanics and character;
- humor;
- ordinary-life pacing;
- scene construction.

## 17. Critical reservations

State meaningful limitations.

Possible categories:

- contrived exam design;
- ethically questionable framing;
- overpowered-protagonist compression of ensemble agency;
- gender generalization;
- fanservice conflicting with thematic privacy;
- psychological claims that exceed credible evidence;
- institutional plausibility.

## 20. Primary-source locator ledger

Required table for all load-bearing claims:

## 21. Cumulative delta

Questions must be limited to what remains open **at this point**.

Do not answer them using later knowledge.

- later Year 1 synthesis once created.

# XII. Character-analysis method

Every major character should be read developmentally rather than through static archetype labels.

## 1. Identity and appearance

Record:

- name and readings;
- class/institutional position;
- age/year where known;

# XV. Ability, meritocracy, and measurement protocol

Year 1 repeatedly destabilizes the word **実力 / jitsuryoku**.

Every analysis should distinguish:

1. **Possessed ability** — what a person can do.
2. **Displayed ability** — what they choose or manage to show.
3. **Measured ability** — what the school records.
4. **Socially usable ability** — what a group can reliably convert into action.
5. **Developmental ability** — capacity to improve or become more usable over time.
6. **Political ability** — ability to build legitimacy, coalitions, information networks, or compliance.
7. **Moral ability** — capacity to exercise judgment under pressure without simply maximizing outcome.

Do not collapse these into one “power level.”

When using guidebook ratings, explicitly identify which dimension the rating plausibly captures and what it misses.

# XX. Prior-analysis correction protocol

The old volume analyses are not to be discarded. They are to be audited.

For each volume create a section titled:

> `Second-pass revision audit`

Use the following categories.

## `CONFIRMED`

The prior claim is strongly supported as written.

## `STRENGTHENED`

The claim survives and gains additional textual evidence or sharper formulation.

## `WEAKENED`

The claim is directionally useful but overstated.

## `CORRECTED`

A factual, linguistic, structural, or interpretive error is fixed.

## `REJECTED`

The source does not support the earlier conclusion.

## `NEWLY_FOUND`

The second pass identifies a major feature absent from the first analysis.

## `UNRESOLVED`

The evidence remains genuinely ambiguous.

## Correction policy

Do not hide correction in revised prose.

The corpus should preserve what changed and why.

This makes the second pass auditable rather than merely authoritative by assertion.

# XXX. Cross-year revision architecture

This Year 1 method is designed to become the first layer of an all-years corpus.

## 1. Year 1 artifacts remain immutable snapshots

Once the Year 1 package passes audit, later years should not silently rewrite its local conclusions.

Corrections to factual errors may be issued as:

- errata;
- or a new method/artifact version with explicit `supersedes` metadata.

## 2. Year 2 character ledgers supersede current-state use

Example:

```text
COTE_CHAR_LEDGER_AYANOKOJI_THROUGH_Y1.md
    ↓ superseded for current-state reference by
COTE_CHAR_LEDGER_AYANOKOJI_THROUGH_Y2.md
    ↓ superseded for current-state reference by
COTE_CHAR_LEDGER_AYANOKOJI_THROUGH_Y3V04.md
```

The earlier snapshot remains necessary to answer:

- what was established at Year 1’s end;
- what later material changed;
- how much the character actually developed.

## 3. Required delta section in later ledgers

Every later ledger must include:

> `Changes since prior snapshot`

with categories:

- confirmed continuity;
- genuine development;
- revelation of prior hidden state;
- retrospective recontextualization;
- correction of earlier inference;
- unresolved contradiction.

## 4. All-years synthesis comes last

The all-years corpus should not merely concatenate Year 1, Year 2, and Year 3 prose.

It should use the year snapshots to analyze:

- change;
- reversal;
- recurrence;
- institutional scaling;
- relationship transformation;
- and later redefinition of earlier concepts.

# XXXII. Counterargument and adversarial-rereading standard

Every major analytical document must include at least one section titled:

- `Strongest counterargument`;
- `Competing interpretation`;
- `What this reading may understate`;
- or `Evidence against the current thesis`.

Examples:

## Ayanokōji and development

If arguing that he manufactures dependency, also ask:

- where he permits independent action;
- where he declines to exploit someone;
- where his intervention genuinely enlarges later freedom.

## Horikita and rehabilitation

If treating her preservation of Kushida as principled anti-disposability, also examine:

- personal fixation;
- risk imposed on classmates;
- whether consent of those bearing the risk matters.

## Ichinose and solidarity

If treating no-expulsion leadership as morally admirable, also examine:

- strategic fragility;
- financial externalities;
- suppression of dissent;
- dependence on her personal legitimacy.

## Ryūen and leadership

If emphasizing voluntary loyalty after fear fails, also preserve the history of violence that produced the hierarchy.

A good thesis should survive contact with the strongest evidence against it.

# XXXIII. Quality-assurance checklist

Before a Year 1 artifact is considered canonical, verify:

## Source and provenance

- [ ] Exact Japanese EPUB identified.
- [ ] Source SHA-256 recorded.
- [ ] Spine order verified.
- [ ] Language identity verified from text.
- [ ] Illustrations inventoried.
- [ ] No invented page numbers.
- [ ] Exact quotations have locators.
- [ ] Derived normalized text, if used, has a recorded hash.

## Spoiler and epistemic discipline

- [ ] No Year 2/Volume 0/Year 3 evidence has leaked into the local analysis.
- [ ] Character knowledge and reader knowledge are distinguished.
- [ ] Ayanokōji self-report is not automatically treated as omniscient motive.
- [ ] Guidebook measurement is not treated as total character truth.
- [ ] Speculation is labeled.

## Literary analysis

- [ ] Whole volume reread occurred before targeted search.
- [ ] Ordinary scenes are not ignored in favor of strategic climaxes.
- [ ] Japanese voice claims have repeated evidence.
- [ ] Illustrations are analyzed where meaningful.
- [ ] Humor and adolescent ordinary life remain visible.
- [ ] Examination rules are integrated with character/theme rather than treated as isolated puzzle mechanics.

## Ethics

- [ ] Effectiveness is not equated with legitimacy.
- [ ] Explanation is not equated with justification.
- [ ] Coercion, consent, leverage, and autonomy are distinguished.
- [ ] The strongest defense of controversial actions is included.
- [ ] The human cost of “successful” strategy remains visible.

## Revision audit

- [ ] Prior analysis has been explicitly compared against the source.
- [ ] Corrections are visible rather than silently absorbed.
- [ ] Counterevidence is recorded.
- [ ] Open questions are volume-local.

## Searchability

- [ ] Canonical filename used.
- [ ] YAML metadata present.
- [ ] Japanese and English series names included.
- [ ] Major character names present in metadata.
- [ ] Distinctive scene keywords present.
- [ ] Controlled theme tags used.
- [ ] Related-artifact links present.

# XXXIX. What success looks like

The second pass has succeeded if, at the end of Year 1, the project can do all of the following:

1. retrieve any major Year 1 claim by filename and keyword;
2. locate the primary Japanese evidence behind that claim;
3. distinguish local Year 1 knowledge from later retrospective knowledge;
4. show exactly which first-pass conclusions were revised and why;
5. compare characters across volumes without flattening development;
6. explain examination mechanics without reducing the novels to puzzles;
7. analyze Ayanokōji without collapsing him into either “emotionless mastermind” or uncomplicated romantic humanization;
8. preserve the ethical cost of coercive success;
9. distinguish ability from institutional measurement;
10. support a future all-years synthesis in which Year 2 and Year 3 revisions supersede current-state character ledgers without erasing Year 1 history.

The ideal corpus should allow a future reader to move in either direction:

> **from primary text upward toward synthesis**, or
>
> **from a synthesis claim downward toward the exact source that justifies it.**

That bidirectional traceability is the central archival goal of this method.

- primary-home anti-duplication rules;
- explicit source hierarchy;
- epistemic and normative claim separation;
- cross-document audit;
- source and artifact checksums;
- and final reader-facing documentation written only after specialized analyses stabilize the corpus.

The present method adapts those principles specifically to *Classroom of the Elite* by emphasizing:

- first-person narratorial ambiguity;
- special-examination mechanics;
- institutional legibility;
- points and quantified human value;
- developmental authorship;
- protection versus ownership;
- class political constitutions;
- year-by-year superseding character ledgers;
- and the preservation of local spoiler boundaries despite later canonical knowledge.
