# Classroom of the Elite — Behavioral Reconstruction Protocol through Year 2
## Operational inference from canonical character, relationship, ordinary-life, institutional, ethical, and Japanese-voice evidence

# 0. Purpose

The completed through-Year-2 COTE corpus can now answer a question that ordinary literary synthesis does not by itself formalize:

> **Given a situation that never occurs in the novels, what can the existing evidence responsibly predict about how a character would perceive it, respond to it, behave inside it, and speak?**

This problem is different from character summary.

A summary can say that Ryūen is coercive, adaptive, amused by conflict, increasingly willing to use contracts and delegation, and capable of respecting competence.

A reconstruction must decide whether any of those traits activate when:

- no class points are at stake;
- the other person is a friend rather than a subordinate;
- public status is absent;
- intimidation would damage the desired relationship;
- the interaction is genuinely trivial;
- or the person opposite him does something he did not anticipate.

Likewise, a summary can say that Horikita is controlled, analytical, increasingly collaborative, capable of care, and still linguistically precise.

A reconstruction must decide whether she:

- corrects someone;
- ignores the mistake;
- teases them;
- asks a question;
- gives advice;
- shows embarrassment;
- allows another person to lead;
- or simply participates without converting the moment into a leadership problem.

The difficulty is therefore not collecting more adjectives.

It is combining **state**, **relationship**, **knowledge**, **stakes**, **role**, **ordinary-life distribution**, **ethical repertoire**, **stress**, and **voice** without flattening any one of them into a universal personality rule.

This protocol exists to make that combination explicit and auditable.

# 1. Governing reconstruction principle

The central principle is:

> **Reconstruct constraints before generating behavior. Generate behavior before realizing speech. Preserve underdetermination when the corpus does not uniquely solve the scene.**

The protocol rejects two opposite errors.

## 1.1 Error A — freeform plausibility

A scene feels emotionally convincing, so the writer assumes it is in character.

This is insufficient.

A convincing scene can still:

- use the wrong developmental boundary;
- import a relationship register from the wrong person;
- make crisis behavior permanent;
- speak an internal thought aloud;
- erase an information asymmetry;
- invent a preference;
- assign moral growth the text has not earned;
- or treat a later reveal as if the character already knew it.

## 1.2 Error B — deterministic character equation

The opposite mistake treats the corpus as if enough evidence should produce one mathematically inevitable next action.

Literary characters are not closed deterministic systems.

The source often supports:

- multiple compatible actions;
- a range of intensity;
- several speech realizations;
- uncertainty caused by missing context;
- or genuine novelty.

A strong reconstruction therefore narrows the plausible field without pretending the field always collapses to one point.

# 2. Authority hierarchy

Reconstruction is derivative.

When evidence conflicts, use the following precedence:

```text
1. Japanese primary source at the admissible boundary
2. immutable source-local deep reading / paratext audit
3. canonical completed-boundary ledger
4. completed-boundary specialist synthesis
5. behavioral reconstruction protocol
6. scenario-specific reconstruction output
```

This ordering has two consequences.

First, a reconstruction output cannot revise a character ledger.

Second, a protocol rule that repeatedly fails against later admissible source evidence should itself be revised.

The simulator is being tested by the text; the text is not being tested by the simulator.

## 2.1 Precedence is claim-type-specific, not one global ranking

The hierarchy above controls **authority**, but reconstruction requires a second distinction: different artifacts constrain different kinds of claims.

A character ledger can be more authoritative than a protocol while still being less specific than a relationship ledger for one established dyad. A Japanese-voice ledger can strongly constrain how a selected action is verbalized while being incapable of selecting the action in the first place. An ability ledger can establish that an action is feasible without establishing that the character is likely to choose it.

Use the following functional matrix.

| Input family | Strongest reconstruction use | What it may not decide by itself |
|---|---|---|
| primary source / source-local reading | exact act, local motive evidence, local wording, state-specific analogue | universal trait from one scene |
| current character ledger | end-boundary invariants, developments, contradictions, repertoire | dyad-specific register or exact next act |
| relationship ledger | trust, leverage, refusal, disclosure, dependence, reciprocity, counter-authorship | global personality outside that relation |
| class-polity / institution / exam ledgers | role duties, jurisdiction, formal incentives, surveillance, political constraints | ordinary motive when those systems are inactive |
| ability / measurement ledger | feasible capacity, hidden vs observed ability, measurement distortions | choice likelihood or moral willingness |
| ethics ledger | demonstrated intervention repertoire, jurisdictional dangers, consent/refusal constraints | inevitable moral choice |
| ordinary-life ledger | low-stakes preference distribution, leisure, embarrassment, routine, horizontal relation | crisis behavior when ordinary mode is displaced |
| Japanese voice ledger | register, self-reference, address, stress deformation, speech-realization constraints | motive or behavioral selection |
| handoff-question tracker | whether inherited claims are answered, partial, reframed, or open | new behavior not demonstrated elsewhere |
| reconstruction protocol | combination and branching rules | new canonical facts |

## 2.2 Behavioral applicability precedence

When several admissible pieces of evidence point toward different behavior families, use this order **for applicability**, not for authority:

1. **close state-and-relationship analogue at the current boundary**;
2. **repeated relationship-specific behavior under comparable stakes**;
3. **repeated current-state behavior across multiple contexts**;
4. **context-specialist evidence** appropriate to the mode—ordinary-life, polity, exam, ethics, or stress;
5. **historical baseline** that has not been revised by later development;
6. **ability evidence** as a feasibility check only;
7. **voice evidence** only after the behavioral family is selected.

Three corrections are mandatory.

### Current development outranks obsolete baseline

If Year 2 demonstrates genuine development, do not let a more dramatic Year-1 scene dominate merely because it is memorable. The historical state remains explanatory provenance, not the default present behavior.

### Relationship specificity outranks generic trait language

A repeated dyad-specific mode may legitimately override a broad default inside that dyad. It may not be exported to everyone else.

### Negative constraints can outrank weak positive resemblance

A merely plausible analogue should be rejected when it requires a known non-generalization, knowledge leak, obsolete state, or unresolved capacity that the handoff tracker keeps open.

## 2.3 Evidence does not collapse contradiction automatically

When direct action, self-report, observer attribution, and later interpretation disagree, preserve the disagreement unless a canonical transition resolves it.

Do not assume:

- self-description is always truer than behavior;
- behavior automatically reveals a single motive;
- an observer's accurate prediction grants total access to the target;
- a guidebook metric overrides enacted evidence;
- later good outcomes settle earlier ethical ambiguity.

The reconstruction target is the **best-supported live state**, including contradictions that remain behaviorally productive.

## 3.9 Handoff-question state authority

Primary home:

- `COTE_Y2_Y1_HANDOFF_QUESTION_TRACKER.md`

The tracker does not describe personality directly. It controls **which generalizations the Year-2 boundary is allowed to make**.

Use its terminal states as follows:

- `ANSWERED_BY_DEVELOPMENT` — the demonstrated developed capacity may be used after its transition point; do not back-project it into earlier states.
- `ANSWERED_BY_REVELATION` — reader-level knowledge may be revised retrospectively; character knowledge remains independently gated.
- `REFRAMED` — the obsolete binary is prohibited; reconstruct from the Year-2 formulation.
- `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED` — use demonstrated subcases, preserve the missing condition, and branch when the scenario activates the unresolved dimension.
- `STILL_OPEN` — canonical closure is forbidden. Any scene that supplies the missing capacity must be labeled speculative rather than evidentiary.

At `Y2SL`, the distribution is:

```text
15 ANSWERED_BY_DEVELOPMENT
3  ANSWERED_BY_REVELATION
7  REFRAMED
13 PARTIALLY_ANSWERED
4  STILL_OPEN
```

The four hard open zones are:

- `Y1H-004` — sufficient Ayanokōji disclosure for informed consequential relational choice;
- `Y1H-016` — Horikita witnessing and interpreting genuine Ayanokōji failure;
- `Y1H-026` — Hiyori morally redirecting Ryūen himself rather than only diagnosing him;
- `Y1H-035` — Ayanokōji voluntarily exposing weakness that gives another person meaningful protective authority.

These are not prohibitions on creative writing. They are prohibitions on labeling an invented resolution as a demonstrated Year-2 capacity.

The purpose is not bureaucratic completeness.

For example, "Ryūen at dinner" is under-specified.

These are different situations:

- private meal with Ishizaki;
- public class dinner where leadership status is visible;
- meal with Ayanokōji after a strategic defeat;
- meal with Hiyori where she directly criticizes him;
- casual restaurant with strangers;
- dinner where someone insults his class;
- dinner where nothing important happens.

A reconstruction that does not encode those differences will mistake a generic character stereotype for a model.

# 5. Reconstruction pipeline

The core procedure is sequential.

Do not jump directly from scenario prompt to dialogue.

## Stage 0 — Boundary lock

Confirm the admissible corpus boundary.

For this document:

```text
BOUNDARY = Y2SL
YEAR_3_EVIDENCE = forbidden
```

If the user supplies a hypothetical that explicitly alters canon, separate the altered premise from canonical state.

Example:

```text
canonical state: Kei and Ayanokōji are at Y2SL boundary
counterfactual premise: they are placed together in an off-campus cooking competition
```

The counterfactual may alter circumstance.

It does not automatically rewrite history, knowledge, relationship asymmetry, or personality.

## Stage 1 — Retrieve current character state

Identify only the traits and unresolved tensions that could causally matter.

Do not dump the entire ledger.

Output internally as:

```text
ACTIVE_INVARIANTS
ACTIVE_DEVELOPMENTS
ACTIVE_CONTRADICTIONS
KNOWN_NON_GENERALIZATIONS
```

Example abstraction for Horikita:

```text
ACTIVE_INVARIANTS:
- precision
- controlled evaluative speech
- competence orientation
- willingness to judge

ACTIVE_DEVELOPMENTS:
- increased collaboration
- increased corrigibility
- greater capacity for request/care
- more relationship-specific play

KNOWN_NON_GENERALIZATIONS:
- Ibuki banter is not universal casual speech
- care does not imply constant warmth
- leadership competence does not require taking control of every scene
```


## Stage 1A — Assess reconstruction permission for sparse targets

Before retrieving relationship state, determine whether the frozen corpus supports a **person-specific reconstruction in this scenario**.

This stage is mandatory when:

- the target lacks a robust current-state character model;
- the target appears mainly through a follower, coalition, friend-group, institutional, or one-scene function;
- the requested situation lies outside the target's strongest evidenced domain;
- or voice evidence is materially thinner than behavioral evidence.

Build a qualitative evidence envelope:

```yaml
BEHAVIORAL_BREADTH: robust | narrow_diagnostic | role_limited | trace
TEMPORAL_CONTINUITY: robust | present | local_only | absent
RELATIONSHIP_COVERAGE: diverse | specific_deep | specific_thin | absent
MOTIVE_ACCESS: direct | strongly_inferred | role_inferred | absent
ORDINARY_LIFE_COVERAGE: robust | present | trace | absent
STRESS_COVERAGE: robust | present | singular | absent
VOICE_COVERAGE: A | A_B | B | C | unprofiled
NEGATIVE_CONSTRAINTS: strong | some | weak
```

Then assign:

```text
RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION =
  PORTABLE_BOUNDED |
  CONTEXT_ANCHORED |
  STRUCTURAL_ONLY |
  ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC
```

Run the distinguishability test:

> What target-specific evidence explains why this character should choose this branch rather than another plausible person occupying the same role?

If no defensible answer exists, do not let fluency supply one.

Assign voice permission independently:

```text
VOICE_PERMISSION =
  VOICE_REALIZATION_BOUNDED |
  VOICE_REGISTER_ONLY |
  VOICE_PARAPHRASE_ONLY |
  VOICE_ABSTAIN
```

A high-confidence voice marker does not create behavioral breadth, and strong relationship evidence does not create an idiolect.

R4 section `12B` defines the full threshold rules and worked calibrations.

## Stage 2 — Retrieve relationship state

Ask:

> **Who is this person to the character at this boundary?**

Relevant relation dimensions should be activated only if supported.

For a new hypothetical stranger, do not hallucinate intimacy.

For established dyads, retrieve:

- trust;
- history;
- power;
- knowledge asymmetry;
- expected refusal;
- affection;
- rivalry;
- debt;
- dependence;
- ability to contradict;
- prior ordinary-life contact.

## Stage 2A — Build social topology for multi-character scenes

Dyadic modeling is insufficient once a third actor can observe, constrain, protect, embarrass, validate, or be used as leverage.

For any scene with more than two meaningful actors, build a directed local graph:

```yaml
NODES:
  - actor
EDGES:
  actor_A -> actor_B:
    relationship: canonical relation class
    trust: supported state
    power_or_authority: supported state
    affection_or_attachment: supported state
    rivalry_or_threat: supported state
    leverage_or_secret: supported state
    refusal_expectation: supported state
    audience_sensitivity: low | medium | high
```

Only include edges that can causally affect the scene. Do not build an encyclopedic network.

### Topology roles

Mark, where applicable:

- `PRIMARY_TARGET` — the person whose response the focal actor is directly trying to affect;
- `SECONDARY_AUDIENCE` — people whose observation changes presentation even if they are not directly addressed;
- `PROTECTED_NODE` — someone whose harm materially constrains the focal actor;
- `AUTHORITY_NODE` — someone with formal or social jurisdiction over the interaction;
- `RIVAL_NODE` — someone whose presence activates competition or status defense;
- `CONFIDANT_NODE` — someone before whom concealment costs may be lower;
- `LEVERAGE_NODE` — someone whose relation to another actor can alter incentives;
- `OUTSIDER_NODE` — a person without the history assumed by the inner group.

### Audience transformation rule

Do not assume a line that is plausible in private remains plausible in public.

Before selecting behavior, ask:

1. What does the focal actor want the primary target to understand?
2. What do they want the secondary audience **not** to infer?
3. Does publicness increase role-performance pressure?
4. Would disclosure expose another person's secret or dependency?
5. Would direct aggression toward one person damage standing with another?
6. Is the actor using one relationship to affect a different edge in the graph?

This is especially important for:

- Kei, whose private emotional state and public line selection can diverge;
- Kushida, whose socially skilled public register remains operational after exposure;
- Ayanokōji, whose outward information release is usually narrower than his internal model;
- Ryūen and Nagumo, whose dominance behavior changes when followers, rivals, or institutional witnesses are present;
- Horikita, whose role as leader may be activated by an audience even when private speech would be more relaxed.

### Anti-triangulation shortcut

Never infer that because A trusts B and B trusts C, A therefore trusts C.

Multi-character reconstruction must preserve directed relationship edges rather than averaging a group into one social temperature.

## Stage 3 — Reconstruct knowledge state

Separate at least three levels:

```text
PROTOCOL_GROUND_TRUTH
CHARACTER_EPISTEMIC_STATE
CHARACTER_META_BELIEF_ABOUT_OTHERS
```

`PROTOCOL_GROUND_TRUTH` means only what the admissible corpus establishes at the reconstruction boundary. It is not automatically available to the character.

For each proposition that could change the scene, use a compact epistemic record:

```yaml
PROPOSITION_ID: P1
CONTENT: ""
GROUND_TRUTH: true | false | unresolved
CHARACTER_STATE: knows | strongly_believes | weakly_believes | suspects | considers_possible | unknown | false_belief
BASIS: witnessed | told | inferred | rumor | institutional_record | deception | unknown
META_BELIEF: what the character thinks relevant others know or believe
DISCLOSURE_POLICY: disclose | partial_disclosure | withhold | test | mislead | undecided
```

Only model second-order beliefs when they are causally relevant. Infinite epistemic recursion is not rigor.

### False-belief preservation

If the character holds a false belief, reconstruct from that false belief until the scenario supplies a credible correction. Do not silently rescue the character with reader knowledge.

### Suspicion is not knowledge

A character who suspects deception may:

- test;
- hedge;
- withhold;
- create redundancy;
- or proceed despite uncertainty.

Do not upgrade `suspects` to `knows` merely because the suspicion is correct.

### Deception requires a detection path

If another actor lies, ask what cues, prior models, records, incentives, or contradictions would make detection plausible. Intelligence alone is not a universal lie detector.

### Model confidence is not ground truth

Ayanokōji's belief that he has modeled a person completely is not equivalent to the protocol having complete knowledge. The target character's later counter-authorship may prove that the model was incomplete. The same rule applies to Sakayanagi, Ryūen, Nagumo, or any other confident modeler.

### Unknown unknowns remain possible

When the focal character lacks reason even to suspect a relevant fact, do not write protective hedging as if they somehow sensed the author's hidden premise. Surprise is a valid output.

## Stage 4 — Determine active role

Ask whether the character is currently acting primarily as:

- private individual;
- friend;
- romantic partner;
- class member;
- class leader;
- strategic opponent;
- student-council actor;
- institutional subordinate;
- protector;
- observer;
- participant;
- or some mixed role.

Do not assume the highest-status role always dominates.

A class leader can still be eating ice cream.

## Stage 5 — Determine stakes and mode

Classify the scene:

### `ORDINARY_DOMINANT`

Low formal stakes; ordinary-life evidence should receive strong weight.

### `MIXED`

Ordinary interaction contains strategic, reputational, or institutional pressure.

### `STRATEGIC_DOMINANT`

Class/exam/institutional stakes strongly constrain behavior.

### `ACUTE_STRESS`

Threat, grief, collapse, exposure, humiliation, or physical danger may deform baseline behavior.

The same character should not be reconstructed identically across these modes.

## Stage 6 — Build goal hierarchy

Separate possible goals into distinct sources:

```text
IMMEDIATE_TASK_GOAL
ORDINARY_DESIRE
RELATIONAL_GOAL
ROLE_OR_DUTY_GOAL
LONG_HORIZON_PROJECT
SELF_PROTECTIVE_GOAL
EPISTEMIC_GOAL
MORAL_OR_JURISDICTIONAL_CONSTRAINT
```

Some may be absent. Some may conflict. Some may be active but deliberately suppressed.

For every causally relevant goal, record:

```yaml
GOAL_ID: G1
SOURCE: ordinary | relational | role | strategic | self_protective | epistemic | moral_constraint
TARGET: self | actor | group | institution | state_of_world
HORIZON: immediate | scene | short | long
ACTIVATION: dominant | active | background | suppressed | inactive
VISIBLE_TO_OTHERS: yes | partial | no | unknown
VIOLATION_COST: low | meaningful | severe | unknown
COMPATIBLE_WITH: []
CONFLICTS_WITH: []
```

Do not assign numerical weights. The corpus does not provide calibrated utilities.

The protocol should not invent a hidden strategic goal merely because a character is intelligent. For Ayanokōji in particular, first test whether the ordinary-life ledger supplies a sufficient nonstrategic motive before assigning manipulation.

## Stage 7 — Generate candidate action families

Generate a small set of behavior families before selecting a specific act.

Example:

```text
A. direct intervention
B. indirect environmental adjustment
C. verbal correction only
D. observational nonintervention
E. delegation
F. withdrawal/refusal
G. ordinary participation without optimization
```

Not every character receives every family.

Filter by demonstrated repertoire.

## Stage 8 — Apply negative constraints

This stage is critical.

Ask not only:

> What could the character do?

but:

> What would violate the established model here?

Examples:

- Ayanokōji should not externalize his full interior analysis merely to explain the plot.
- Horikita should not become generically slangy because she has one relaxed relationship register.
- Kei should not collapse into helplessness merely because romantic dependency remains unresolved.
- Kushida should not lose public social competence because her concealed hostility is known.
- Ryūen should not threaten when threat offers no benefit and conflicts with the relationship he wants to preserve.
- Hiyori should not become passive merely because her pressure style is quiet.
- Sakayanagi should not lose poise merely because she is vulnerable.

Negative constraints are often more reliable than exact positive prediction.

## Stage 9 — Resolve competing behavior families

Use qualitative support, not invented precision.

### 9.1 Hard elimination

First eliminate candidates that:

- require knowledge the character lacks;
- violate the boundary lock;
- depend on an obsolete pre-development state;
- globalize a relationship-specific register;
- require a `STILL_OPEN` capacity to be treated as established;
- contradict a hard scenario constraint;
- require an ability the corpus does not support.

### 9.2 Goal compatibility

For survivors, ask whether one action can satisfy several active goals at once. Characters often choose actions that preserve optionality or solve several constraints rather than maximizing one abstract objective.

Do **not** assume the most strategic-looking solution is preferred. In an ordinary-dominant scene, a simple ordinary act may satisfy the actual goal set better than manipulation.

### 9.3 Closest-analogue comparison

For each remaining candidate ask:

1. Is there a close canonical analogue?
2. Does it recur across contexts?
3. Does this relationship permit it?
4. Does this role reward or punish it?
5. Does the present audience change its social cost?
6. Does ordinary-life evidence contradict a crisis-based assumption?
7. Does stress activate a known deformation?
8. Does it fit what the character believes rather than what the reader knows?
9. Does it preserve demonstrated refusal/ethical/jurisdictional constraints?
10. Does it require a personality change not established by Y2SL?

### 9.4 Tradeoff resolution

If candidates satisfy different active goals, use character-specific evidence to determine which conflict is demonstrated.

Examples of legitimate distinctions include:

- Hirata may accept a legitimate collective decision while remaining morally distressed by it;
- Kei may preserve public social calibration while privately distressed;
- Ryūen may preserve future access to a valued relation by declining an immediately available dominance move;
- Kōenji may honor a bounded bargain while rejecting generalized obligation;
- Hashimoto may preserve social ease while simultaneously maintaining insurance against betrayal.

Do not average these tensions into generic moderation. Preserve the coexistence of distinct goals.

### 9.5 Branch instead of forcing

If two or more behavior families remain comparably supported after hard constraints and contextual routing, retain multiple branches.

A reconstruction is stronger when it says:

> Branch A is favored if he interprets the comment as a real threat; Branch B is favored if he reads it as harmless play. The corpus does not determine that appraisal from the supplied scenario.

than when it invents a hidden cue merely to produce one answer.

Then assign both **support class** and **determinacy state**.

# 6. Reconstruction support classes

## 6.1 `DEMONSTRATED`

Use only when a close analogue exists.

Requirements:

- same or closely comparable role;
- similar relationship structure;
- similar stakes;
- no known later development making the analogue obsolete;
- behavior is not a one-off stress collapse being treated as baseline.

Example form:

> Ryūen using mocking pressure during an adversarial negotiation is `DEMONSTRATED` when the target, stakes, and power contest resemble repeated canonical negotiations.

## 6.2 `STRONG_GENERALIZATION`

Use when a pattern repeats across sufficiently different situations to support a rule.

Examples of the kind of rule that can qualify:

- Ayanokōji often keeps outward speech narrower than internal analysis.
- Horikita preserves linguistic precision even as relational range expands.
- Hiyori can make direct judgments without becoming high-pressure.
- Hirata tends to preserve a gentle explanatory register even when naming limits.

A strong generalization is not a universal law.

State its counterconditions when known.

## 6.3 `BOUNDED_EXTRAPOLATION`

Use when the situation is novel but convergent evidence sharply limits plausible responses.

This is expected to be the most common class for genuinely new scenarios.

A useful bounded extrapolation says:

- which response family is best supported;
- which alternatives remain possible;
- which assumptions the choice depends on;
- and which part of the result is least certain.

## 6.4 `SPECULATIVE`

Use when:

- preferences are unknown;
- the relation has no analogue;
- the scenario depends on an untested capacity;
- several candidate actions remain equally compatible;
- or a precise line would be mostly creative writing.

`SPECULATIVE` does not mean useless.

It means the output must be labeled as invention constrained by canon rather than inference strongly determined by canon.

## 6.5 Support class is not determinacy

The four support classes describe **how well a behavior family is evidenced**. They do not describe **whether the scenario has only one viable answer**.

Track determinacy separately:

### `NARROWLY_DETERMINED`

One behavior family clearly dominates after state, relationship, knowledge, role, and negative constraints are applied.

### `BRANCHED`

Two or more behavior families remain live because a scenario variable is genuinely ambiguous or because the character has demonstrated competing repertoires under similar conditions.

### `OPEN_FIELD`

The corpus constrains tone and prohibited behavior more strongly than it determines the positive act. Creative realization is possible, but multiple acts are equally defensible.

### `ABSTAIN_ON_CLOSURE`

The requested result would resolve a capacity the completed handoff keeps open, or would require evidence outside the boundary. The protocol may describe speculative branches but must abstain from claiming canonical predictive support.

A useful reconstruction can therefore be:

```text
SUPPORT = STRONG_GENERALIZATION
DETERMINACY = BRANCHED
```

when two strongly evidenced repertoires depend on an appraisal the prompt does not specify.

Conversely:

```text
SUPPORT = BOUNDED_EXTRAPOLATION
DETERMINACY = NARROWLY_DETERMINED
```

can occur when the situation is novel but the known constraints eliminate nearly every alternative.

## 6.6 Handoff-state gating

Before finalizing determinacy, route any inherited question touched by the scene:

| Handoff state | Reconstruction consequence |
|---|---|
| `ANSWERED_BY_DEVELOPMENT` | developed branch available only after transition |
| `ANSWERED_BY_REVELATION` | reader model may update; character knowledge still gated |
| `REFRAMED` | old binary prohibited |
| `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED` | demonstrated subcase usable; missing condition preserved |
| `STILL_OPEN` | no canonical closure; speculative branch only |

This prevents a locally plausible scene from silently closing a series-level question.

# 7. Conflict-resolution rules

Canonical artifacts can appear to pull in different directions because they answer different questions.

The protocol must resolve scope before deciding that the corpus contradicts itself.

## 7.1 Character ledger versus ordinary-life ledger

If the character ledger emphasizes strategic competence but the ordinary-life ledger shows purposeless preference in low stakes, the correct question is not which document wins globally.

Ask which mode the present scenario activates.

Low-stakes scenes should not be colonized by strategic evidence without a causal trigger.

## 7.2 Character ledger versus relationship ledger

Relationship-specific evidence generally wins for behavior **inside that established relationship**, provided it does not contradict a hard character invariant.

Example logic:

```text
GLOBAL: Horikita is controlled and precise.
DYAD: Horikita/Ibuki permits familiar antagonism, banter, pettiness, play.
RESULT: relaxed antagonistic play may activate with Ibuki without implying global casualness.
```

## 7.3 Ability ledger versus observed restraint

Capability does not imply action.

If a character could dominate the problem but ordinarily delegates, withholds, or permits another person to act, reconstruction should preserve that distinction.

## 7.4 Ethics ledger versus successful outcome

A later good result does not make coercion retroactively more likely in every future scene.

Use the character's demonstrated intervention repertoire and current developmental state, not outcome-based moral simplification.

## 7.5 Voice ledger versus emotional interpretation

Do not infer internal emotion solely from surface politeness, teasing, or sentence endings.

Voice constrains realization.

It does not independently solve motive.

## 7.6 Ayanokōji's model versus target evidence

When Ayanokōji predicts another character and the target's own evidence permits independent behavior, preserve the possibility that his model is incomplete.

The protocol should never treat his confidence as a privileged omniscient narrator channel.

## 7.7 Handoff tracker versus positive reconstruction pressure

If a scene would conveniently resolve a `STILL_OPEN` or missing branch of a `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED` question, the tracker wins.

The protocol must either:

- choose a behavior that does not require closure;
- preserve multiple branches;
- or label the closure as `SPECULATIVE` / `ABSTAIN_ON_CLOSURE`.

## 7.8 Current state versus dramatic historical baseline

Later genuine development governs current reconstruction. Historical behavior remains available when:

- the later state explicitly preserves it;
- stress plausibly reactivates it;
- or the transition is incomplete.

Do not resurrect an earlier failure mode merely because it is more narratively vivid.

## 7.9 Direct action versus self-description

Neither automatically wins.

If a character says one thing and repeatedly acts another way, model the contradiction. The action may reveal a constraint the character cannot articulate; the self-description may reveal an aspiration, blind spot, defensive narrative, or privately meaningful category.

Do not convert either source into omniscient motive without corroboration.

## 7.10 Institutional file versus enacted person

OAA, guidebook ratings, official records, and institutional reputation constrain what **the institution can see and use**. They are not total-person state vectors.

Kōenji is the clearest warning, but the rule is general. Use file-state as part of what other actors may believe, not as an automatic ceiling on actual capacity.

## 7.11 Voice realization versus behavior selection

A line that sounds perfectly like a character is not enough. If the underlying act is unsupported, correct voice does not rescue it.

Behavioral support must be established before stylistic fidelity is scored.

# 8. State-conditioned voice realization

Speech generation begins only after behavior selection.

## 8.1 Voice-mode selection

Choose among modes supported for the character, such as:

- public;
- private;
- intimate;
- leadership;
- adversarial;
- low-stakes peer;
- acute stress;
- grief;
- cold compression;
- mask preservation.

Do not invent a mode because it would be entertaining.

## 8.2 Speech-act selection

Determine what the behavior actually requires:

- question;
- refusal;
- command;
- invitation;
- reassurance;
- mockery;
- threat;
- correction;
- disclosure;
- apology;
- explanation;
- silence;
- topic change;
- strategic ambiguity.

Then realize it in the permitted idiolect.

## 8.3 Silence is an output

For characters such as Ayanokōji, speech economy is part of characterization.

A simulator should not generate dialogue merely because the medium expects dialogue.

## 8.4 Internal narration must remain internal

Ayanokōji's interior analysis is much broader than his outward speech.

The protocol must explicitly prevent explanatory leakage:

```text
INTERNAL_MODEL != SPOKEN_LINE
```

## 8.5 Register deformation under stress

Use the voice ledger's stress categories rather than a generic "angry voice":

- `FRAGMENTATION`
- `REGISTER_COLLAPSE`
- `MASK_PRESERVATION_UNDER_STRESS`
- `COLD_COMPRESSION`
- `DECLARATIVE_HARDENING`

Stress deformation should activate only with evidence that this character deforms in that direction.

# 12. Initial worked calibration suite

These cases are **derivative tests**, not canon scenes. Their function is to test whether the protocol constrains invention without pretending to discover new facts.

Each case must identify what the corpus determines, what remains branched, and what the protocol must refuse to close.

## Calibration 1 — Ayanokōji and Hiyori in purposeless bookstore leisure

### Hypothetical

Ayanokōji and Hiyori are browsing a bookstore together after school. No exam, class request, threat, or hidden institutional incentive is active. Hiyori asks which of two unfamiliar novels he would rather try.

### State routing

```text
MODE = ORDINARY_DOMINANT
RELATION = Ayanokōji–Hiyori participant-mode friendship
PUBLICNESS = semi_private
FORMAL_STAKES = none
REFUSAL = preserved
ACTIVE_GOALS = ordinary interest + continued pleasant interaction
```

### Hard constraints

- Do not invent a hidden developmental experiment merely because Ayanokōji is capable of one.
- Do not assume he knows which book he will prefer before trying it.
- Do not convert Hiyori's faint affection into compulsory romance.
- Do not externalize a large internal analysis merely to make the scene sound intelligent.

### Best-supported behavior family

`ordinary participation / limited preference statement / curiosity`

Ayanokōji can choose, ask Hiyori's view, acknowledge uncertainty, or treat the choice as an ordinary experiment in taste. The corpus strongly supports the **family** of purposeless participation. It does not determine which invented novel he chooses.

```text
SUPPORT = STRONG_GENERALIZATION
DETERMINACY = OPEN_FIELD at exact-choice level
DETERMINACY = NARROWLY_DETERMINED at behavior-family level
```

### Evidence routes

- `COTE_ORDINARY_LIFE_COUNTERCURRICULUM_LEDGER_THROUGH_Y2.md` — inefficient desire, books, invitation uncertainty, participant-mode relation;
- `COTE_RELATIONSHIP_LEDGER_THROUGH_Y2.md` — Ayanokōji/Hiyori as declared-desire + preserved-refusal countermodel;
- `COTE_JAPANESE_VOICE_LEDGER_THROUGH_Y2.md` — outward speech remains narrower than internal analysis.

### Calibration result

**PASS if** the model can let the scene remain ordinary.

**FAIL if** it invents manipulation because purposeless behavior feels insufficiently “Ayanokōji-like.”

### Evidence routes

**PASS if** the model distinguishes private affect, confidant disclosure, and public presentation.

# 12A. High-agency cross-character interaction suite - Refinement R3

The first 11 calibrations mostly ask whether one focal reconstruction can remain bounded when another actor, audience, institution, or unresolved handoff question constrains it.

High-agency ensemble scenes create a different problem.

Several actors may simultaneously:

- model one another;
- conceal what they know;
- test what another person knows;
- use public behavior as a signal;
- deliberately misstate confidence;
- update from another actor's response;
- protect a relation while competing institutionally;
- or discover that their model of another person was wrong.

The danger is not merely that one character will be flattened. The danger is that the reconstruction will collapse **several distinct minds into one omniscient scene model**.

These cases are derivative calibration tests. They do not establish new canon events, relationships, alliances, or abilities.

## 12A.1 Actor-relative epistemic graphs are mandatory

In a high-agency scene, the protocol must maintain at least three different objects:

```text
PROTOCOL_STATE
ACTOR_A_MODEL
ACTOR_B_MODEL
```

If more actors matter, add more models.

A useful compact representation is:

```yaml
COMMON_KNOWLEDGE: []

ACTOR_STATES:
  ACTOR_A:
    KNOWS: []
    BELIEVES: []
    SUSPECTS: []
    DOES_NOT_KNOW: []
    ACTIVE_GOALS: []
    DISCLOSURE_POLICY: []
    MODELS:
      ACTOR_B:
        believed_goal: ""
        believed_knowledge: ""
        believed_likely_response: ""
        confidence: high | medium | low | unknown

  ACTOR_B:
    KNOWS: []
    BELIEVES: []
    SUSPECTS: []
    DOES_NOT_KNOW: []
    ACTIVE_GOALS: []
    DISCLOSURE_POLICY: []
    MODELS:
      ACTOR_A:
        believed_goal: ""
        believed_knowledge: ""
        believed_likely_response: ""
        confidence: high | medium | low | unknown
```

`ACTOR_A_MODEL(ACTOR_B)` is owned by Actor A.

It is not automatically true because Actor A is intelligent.

This rule is particularly important for Ayanokōji, Sakayanagi, Ryūen, Nagumo, Hashimoto, and late-Year-2 Ichinose, all of whom can build unusually strong models while still being corrigible.

## 12A.2 Recursion must be bounded

Recursive social intelligence can become fake rigor if the reconstruction keeps adding levels such as:

> A knows that B knows that A knows that B suspects...

Use the following default ceiling:

```text
LEVEL 0 = external proposition / observable event
LEVEL 1 = what Actor A believes about the proposition or Actor B
LEVEL 2 = what Actor A believes Actor B believes about A or the proposition
```

Do not model `LEVEL 3+` unless the source or the supplied scenario gives a concrete reason it matters.

Ayanokōji is not more faithful when written as infinitely recursive. Sakayanagi is not more intelligent when every line is treated as a hidden fourth-order inference. Hashimoto is not more socially perceptive when every smile proves he knows what everyone else knows.

Bounded recursion protects the distinction between:

- intelligence;
- evidence;
- and authorial omniscience.

## 12A.3 Interaction proceeds through observable rounds

For high-agency scenes, reconstruct a **trajectory**, not a static personality collision.

Use:

```text
ROUND 0 - initial topology, knowledge, goals, role authority
ROUND 1 - first observable move or disclosure
UPDATE 1 - only actors who could observe the move update
ROUND 2 - response, counter-probe, refusal, or role assertion
UPDATE 2 - knowledge/model changes caused by that response
ROUND 3 - optional if a genuinely new branch appears
STOP - once the response family is stable or the scene becomes underdetermined
```

Each round must distinguish:

```text
OBSERVED ACTION
INFERRED MOTIVE
ACTUAL MOTIVE
```

The first may be shared.

The second is actor-relative.

The third belongs to the protocol's reconstruction of that actor and may itself remain uncertain.

## 12A.4 Action is evidence, not mind-reading permission

When Horikita refuses unexplained authority, that refusal tells observers something about her decision threshold. It does not reveal every private reason she has.

When Ichinose proposes an alternative that Ayanokōji did not predict, the alternative is evidence that his model was incomplete. It does not prove she understands the whole of his hidden project.

When Ryūen laughs at an offer, the laugh can be tactical, amused, dismissive, or several of these at once. Other actors may update, but the protocol should not force one interpretation without evidence.

When Hashimoto sounds uncertain, the uncertainty can be performed while his exit planning remains real.

A public signal therefore updates **plausibility**, not private-state omniscience.

## 12A.5 Formal authority, epistemic authority, and causal leverage must remain separate

A scene may contain three different kinds of power:

- `FORMAL_AUTHORITY` - who has jurisdiction to decide;
- `EPISTEMIC_ADVANTAGE` - who knows or plausibly knows more;
- `CAUSAL_LEVERAGE` - who can change another actor's incentives or options.

These must not be collapsed.

Ayanokōji can know more than Horikita without automatically owning Horikita's vote.

Nagumo can correctly identify exceptional ability after losing the office that once let him allocate opportunity.

Sakayanagi can read a political problem after her class sovereignty has ended without thereby regaining jurisdiction over former classmates.

Hashimoto can possess useful information without becoming a trusted authority on its truth.

## 12A.6 Common knowledge must be proved, not assumed

A fact is not `COMMON_KNOWLEDGE` merely because every actor individually happens to know it.

For common knowledge, the scene must support that actors can reasonably treat the fact as mutually known.

This matters when:

- Ayanokōji's hidden ability is unevenly legible;
- Hashimoto is probing who knows what;
- Kushida is managing public information after exposure;
- Nagumo's former authority is socially remembered but no longer formally active;
- or Sakayanagi acts on an inferred preference that Ayanokōji never directly stated.

## 12A.7 Threat, promise, and commitment require credibility routing

For any strategic statement, distinguish:

```text
CAPACITY_TO_ACT
STATED_INTENTION
OBSERVER_BELIEF_IN_INTENTION
INSTITUTIONAL_OR_RELATIONAL_COST_OF_ACTING
REVERSIBILITY
```

Ryūen making a threat does not guarantee the target believes it.

Horikita making a public commitment creates different future costs from an informal suggestion.

Nagumo asking for deference after leaving office is not equivalent to Nagumo possessing the machinery that once made refusal expensive.

Ayanokōji withholding a reason may increase another actor's suspicion without giving them proof of the reason.

## 12A.8 The protocol selects a joint response family, not a perfect equilibrium

Do not claim that a high-agency scene must converge on the game-theoretically optimal result.

Characters can:

- misread;
- act from attachment;
- preserve pride;
- choose a slower but more legitimate route;
- accept bounded uncertainty;
- or prefer a meaningful contest over a formally superior result.

The goal is to find a **canon-compatible joint trajectory**, not a frictionless equilibrium among supercomputers.

For each case below, record:

```yaml
COMMON_KNOWLEDGE: []
PRIVATE_EPISTEMIC_STATES: {}
MODEL_GRAPH: {}
ROUND_SEQUENCE: []
JOINT_RESPONSE_FAMILY: ""
BRANCH_POINTS: []
KNOWN_MODEL_ERRORS: []
SUPPORT_CLASS: ""
DETERMINACY: ""
```

## Calibration 12 - Ayanokōji, Horikita, Ichinose, and Ryūen under hidden informational authority

# 12B. Secondary-character abstention calibration - Refinement R4

The previous reconstruction tranches stress-tested increasingly difficult **positive inference**: first single-character and bounded-pair reconstruction, then high-agency multi-character interaction.

This tranche tests the opposite capacity:

> **Can the protocol recognize when the corpus does not contain enough character-specific evidence to justify a positive prediction?**

This is not a minor safety feature.

For secondary and supporting characters, the main reconstruction failure is often not an obviously false trait. It is **plausible completion**: a sparse character is given a coherent motive hierarchy, portable personality model, or distinctive voice because those additions make a scene easier to write.

COTE is particularly vulnerable to this error because many supporting actors possess one or more highly diagnostic functions:

- a clear political role;
- one important relationship;
- one memorable act of dissent;
- one unusually revealing first-person line;
- a strong OAA field;
- a repeated association with a leader;
- or a scene where they teach, protect, betray, observe, or refuse.

None of those facts automatically creates a complete portable character model.

The governing R4 rule is therefore:

> **Evidence may strongly support a local function without supporting a globally portable personality. Reconstruction permission must be calibrated per character x scenario, not assigned once per character.**

A narrow but diagnostic character can be more reconstructable in one domain than a more frequently present character whose scenes reveal little about decision structure. Conversely, a character with a recognizable written register may still require behavioral abstention in an untested domain.

## 12B.1 Abstention is a fidelity output, not a failure to answer

The protocol already distinguishes `SPECULATIVE`, `OPEN_FIELD`, and `ABSTAIN_ON_CLOSURE`.

R4 adds a separate question:

> **Does the corpus contain enough person-specific evidence to treat the selected behavior as a reconstruction of this character rather than a generic completion of their role?**

This question cannot be answered by `SUPPORT_CLASS` alone.

For example:

- the proposition that Albert remains part of Ryūen's loyal follower structure can be strongly supported;
- a hypothetical prediction about *how Albert personally arbitrates loyalty, friendship, embarrassment, and institutional risk in a novel situation* may still be unsupported.

Likewise:

- Yamamura has unusually diagnostic first-person and written-voice evidence around invisibility, recognition, hurt, and self-assertion;
- that does not support turning her into a portable covert-strategy archetype in situations that do not touch those evidenced domains.

The correct result can therefore be:

```text
SUPPORT_CLASS = STRONG_GENERALIZATION
DETERMINACY = BRANCHED
RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION = CONTEXT_ANCHORED
```

or even:

```text
SUPPORT_CLASS = DEMONSTRATED
DETERMINACY = NARROWLY_DETERMINED
RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION = STRUCTURAL_ONLY
```

when the demonstrated evidence belongs to a role rather than a sufficiently individuated decision model.

Abstention protects the distinction between:

- **what the character has done**;
- **what the character's role makes likely**;
- **what the corpus lets us infer about the person**;
- and **what a writer could invent without contradicting canon**.

Those are not the same thing.

## 12B.2 Reconstruction permission is a third inference axis

Every serious reconstruction now carries three separate inferential judgments:

```text
1. SUPPORT_CLASS
2. DETERMINACY
3. RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION
```

### `PORTABLE_BOUNDED`

Use when the corpus supports a character-specific model across meaningfully different contexts.

Typical evidence includes several of the following:

- repeated behavior across more than one role or stakes regime;
- relationship diversity;
- temporal continuity or documented development;
- first-person or motive-level access;
- ordinary-life as well as strategic/stress evidence;
- explicit negative constraints;
- enough evidence to distinguish the target from peers with similar institutional roles.

`PORTABLE_BOUNDED` does **not** mean unrestricted simulation.

Relationship state, knowledge, stakes, and handoff gates still apply.

Most major characters satisfy this permission state across a wide range of scenarios. Some secondary characters may satisfy it in a smaller but still portable subset.

### `CONTEXT_ANCHORED`

Use when person-specific evidence is meaningful but concentrated in a particular:

- relationship;
- role;
- conflict type;
- emotional issue;
- institutional setting;
- or ordinary-life domain.

The hypothetical must materially overlap that evidence envelope.

The protocol may predict:

- a response family;
- a likely constraint;
- a likely direction of attention;
- or a relationship-conditioned speech act.

It may **not** silently convert the local evidence into a global personality law.

Examples:

- Yamamura in a recognition/service relationship with Sakayanagi;
- Satō as Kei's horizontal support and independent moral witness;
- Ishizaki where loyalty to Ryūen and ordinary cross-class friendship genuinely collide.

### `STRUCTURAL_ONLY`

Use when the evidence primarily identifies the character's place in a system rather than a sufficiently individuated portable decision model.

Typical evidence:

- follower membership;
- coalition participation;
- one documented pedagogical act;
- one specific dissent function;
- a task allocation;
- a class-review statement;
- or a relation that constrains options without revealing the actor's broader motive hierarchy.

At this level, the protocol may responsibly say:

- which obligations are live;
- which choices would contradict established role evidence;
- which relationship matters;
- what the character has demonstrated in the close analogue;
- or what range remains open.

It should usually **not** select a uniquely personal motive or generate a distinctive line.

### `ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC`

Use when the available evidence does not distinguish the target's response from multiple plausible peers under the same scenario.

The protocol should state explicitly:

> **The corpus does not support a character-specific positive prediction at this boundary.**

It may still provide:

- known role constraints;
- known relationship facts;
- prohibited overgeneralizations;
- or several clearly creative branches.

But it must not present one branch as the character model's predicted answer.

This state is especially appropriate when the requested scene turns on:

- an unobserved private preference;
- an untested moral priority;
- a novel relationship type;
- unseen stress behavior;
- an unsupported hobby or ordinary preference;
- a precise dialogue style with no voice profile;
- or a decision for which the corpus supplies only group-level evidence.

## 12B.3 Evidence-envelope map

Before assigning reconstruction permission to a thinner character, map the evidence envelope qualitatively.

Use these dimensions:

```yaml
BEHAVIORAL_BREADTH: robust | narrow_diagnostic | role_limited | trace
TEMPORAL_CONTINUITY: robust | present | local_only | absent
RELATIONSHIP_COVERAGE: diverse | specific_deep | specific_thin | absent
MOTIVE_ACCESS: direct | strongly_inferred | role_inferred | absent
ORDINARY_LIFE_COVERAGE: robust | present | trace | absent
STRESS_COVERAGE: robust | present | singular | absent
VOICE_COVERAGE: A | A_B | B | C | unprofiled
NEGATIVE_CONSTRAINTS: strong | some | weak
```

These labels are not a score.

No sum is calculated.

The purpose is to locate **where** the evidence exists.

A character with:

```text
BEHAVIORAL_BREADTH = narrow_diagnostic
RELATIONSHIP_COVERAGE = specific_deep
MOTIVE_ACCESS = direct
VOICE_COVERAGE = A_B
```

can be highly reconstructable in one relationship domain while remaining nonportable elsewhere.

A character with:

```text
BEHAVIORAL_BREADTH = role_limited
RELATIONSHIP_COVERAGE = specific_thin
MOTIVE_ACCESS = absent
VOICE_COVERAGE = unprofiled
```

should usually remain `STRUCTURAL_ONLY` or `ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC` even if the role itself is clear.

## 12B.4 The distinguishability test

Before selecting a positive behavior for a supporting character, ask:

> **What target-specific evidence explains why this character should choose this branch rather than another plausible person occupying the same role?**

A useful internal counterfactual is:

```text
Replace TARGET with PEER_A or PEER_B while keeping role, information, and incentives fixed.
```

If the same reasoning still produces the same line, motive, and action with almost no change, then the output is probably:

- role reconstruction;
- institutional reconstruction;
- genre plausibility;
- or freeform creative completion;

rather than character-specific reconstruction.

This does **not** make the output useless.

It changes its label.

For instance, if an emergency procedure obliges every student in a designated position to report to a teacher, predicting compliance may be `DEMONSTRATED` and `NARROWLY_DETERMINED` while still being `STRUCTURAL_ONLY`. The institution determines the act more strongly than the individual character model does.

The distinguishability test is especially important for ensemble scenes. A thin supporting character should not receive invented quirks merely so every speaker sounds maximally different.

## 12B.5 Voice permission is independent from behavioral permission

The Japanese voice ledger already uses `A`, `A/B`, `B`, and `C` confidence discipline.

R4 converts that evidence into a separate operational field:

### `VOICE_REALIZATION_BOUNDED`

Use when state-appropriate written-voice evidence is strong enough to realize a conservative line without inventing a signature.

Usually requires:

- an `A` or `A/B` profile;
- or repeated directly indexed register evidence in the relevant state.

This still prohibits:

- fabricated catchphrases;
- exaggerated particles;
- acoustic claims;
- or relationship-specific diction outside its relationship.

### `VOICE_REGISTER_ONLY`

Use when the corpus supports broad register constraints but not enough lexical/sentence-level density for confident idiolect realization.

A line may be written conservatively, but the model should privilege:

- speech-act type;
- formality;
- directness;
- cushioning;
- pronoun/address constraints where known;

rather than decorative mimicry.

Many `B` profiles belong here outside their best-attested states.

### `VOICE_PARAPHRASE_ONLY`

Use when behavior/relationship evidence supports what the character likely communicates, but the current voice corpus does not support character-specific wording.

Output should prefer:

- indirect discourse;
- semantic paraphrase;
- or a functional description of the speech act.

Example:

> Satō independently challenges the fairness of Ayanokōji's conduct and centers Kei's interests.

is safer than inventing a highly stylized Satō line when no dedicated through-Y2 voice profile exists.

### `VOICE_ABSTAIN`

Use when a precise character-specific line would be mostly invention.

The protocol may still state:

- that the character speaks;
- what information is conveyed;
- or what role the utterance serves.

But it should not claim a reconstructed idiolect.

Important consequence:

> **Voice confidence never upgrades behavioral permission.**

Nanase and Yamamura demonstrate why. A highly diagnostic pronoun or first-person line can strongly constrain voice while leaving unrelated behavioral domains open.

The reverse also holds:

> **Strong relational behavior does not create a voice profile.**

Satō is the clean calibration case.

## 12B.6 Hard abstention triggers

Default toward `ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC` when the requested answer requires one or more of the following and the corpus does not supply a close analogue:

1. **Private preference invention**  
   Choosing favorite foods, hobbies, entertainment, aesthetic tastes, purchases, or leisure routines not evidenced in the corpus.

2. **Untested moral hierarchy**  
   Deciding which of two competing loyalties, harms, or principles a thin character values more when no analogous choice exists.

3. **Role-to-personality transfer**  
   Turning one follower, dissenter, athlete, council member, observer, or classmate function into a portable personality trait.

4. **Single-scene universalization**  
   Converting one important act into an always-available behavioral law.

5. **Unseen stress deformation**  
   Inventing panic, rage, eloquence, freezing, aggression, or emotional collapse without stress evidence.

6. **Unsupported interior completion**  
   Writing a detailed motive hierarchy because the external action needs an explanation.

7. **Unprofiled idiolect completion**  
   Creating distinctive verbal tics, sentence endings, humor style, or signature vocabulary from generic role dialogue.

8. **Ayanokōji-model substitution**  
   Treating Ayanokōji's assessment of a supporting character as if it supplied the character's missing first-person interior.

9. **Group-to-individual transfer**  
   Using class, faction, friend-group, or follower behavior as proof of an individual's exact motive.

10. **Ordinary-life strategic default**  
    Assuming a thin character becomes calculating in a trivial scene simply because COTE often contains strategic actors.

11. **Fandom completion**  
    Importing common fan characterization, anime-performance impressions, memes, or archetype assumptions when the frozen source corpus is silent.

12. **Future-capacity closure**  
    Letting a hypothetical demonstrate a capacity the canonical boundary has not established.

When one of these triggers appears, the burden shifts toward explicit evidence routing.

## 12B.7 Relationship and context may narrow behavior without creating a full personality

Thin-character reconstruction remains useful because relationship and institutional evidence can eliminate options.

The correct inference form is often:

```text
PERSON_MODEL = incomplete
RELATIONSHIP_CONSTRAINT = strong
ROLE_CONSTRAINT = strong
POSITIVE_ACTION_SELECTION = branched or open
```

Example:

- Ishizaki's loyalty to Ryūen is real;
- his ordinary friendliness with Ayanokōji is also real;
- therefore a scene forcing those relations into tension is **not unconstrained**;
- but the corpus does not automatically tell us exactly how he arbitrates the conflict.

The protocol can rule out:

- treating his friendship as proof he casually betrays his class;
- or treating class rivalry as proof he has no ordinary friendship.

It may still need to preserve several positive branches.

This is a high-value form of reconstruction because **negative constraints can be more reliable than a positive prediction**.

## Calibration 18 - Yamamura: narrow diagnostic depth versus false portability

### Hypothetical

At the Y2SL boundary, after Sakayanagi's withdrawal has made future school roles unstable, Sakayanagi asks Yamamura privately whether she wants their contact to continue **with no task attached**. She makes clear that Yamamura is free to decline and that the question concerns Yamamura's own preference rather than usefulness.

This deliberately sits close to Yamamura's strongest evidence domain:

- social under-registration;
- hurt at being treated as usable but insufficiently visible;
- V11 self-assertion;
- recognition repair;
- Sakayanagi's later willingness to extend the relation beyond school.

### Evidence envelope

```text
BEHAVIORAL_BREADTH = narrow_diagnostic
TEMPORAL_CONTINUITY = local_only but developmentally coherent
RELATIONSHIP_COVERAGE = specific_deep
MOTIVE_ACCESS = direct/strongly_inferred around recognition and existence
ORDINARY_LIFE_COVERAGE = trace
STRESS_COVERAGE = present but narrow
VOICE_COVERAGE = A/B
NEGATIVE_CONSTRAINTS = strong inside recognition domain
```

### Responsible reconstruction

The protocol may strongly infer that Yamamura can treat the invitation as a question about **her own preference**, not merely as another order to execute.

It may also infer that self-assertion need not become loudness, theatricality, or dominance.

The positive answer itself remains less determined than the speech-act structure.

Plausible live branches include:

- quietly accepting continued contact;
- asking for clarification about what Sakayanagi actually means by the relation;
- or stating a boundary/condition rather than simply complying.

What is not licensed is a return to automatic service logic simply because Sakayanagi is speaking.

### Classification

```text
SUPPORT_CLASS = STRONG_GENERALIZATION
DETERMINACY = BRANCHED
RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION = CONTEXT_ANCHORED
VOICE_PERMISSION = VOICE_REALIZATION_BOUNDED
```

### Negative-transfer probe

Now change only the scenario:

> Ryūen asks Yamamura to design a covert campaign against another class, and the request requires choosing between several deceptive political strategies.

The corpus supports her observational capacity and quiet judgment.

It does **not** support a portable doctrine of covert political authorship, deception preference, risk appetite, or command style.

The correct R4 result is therefore:

```text
RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION = ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC
VOICE_PERMISSION = VOICE_REGISTER_ONLY at most
```

unless the hypothetical is further constrained by a directly analogous source role.

### Calibration result

**PASS.** A narrow but diagnostic character can receive strong local reconstruction permission without being promoted into a general-purpose personality simulator.

## Calibration 19 - Satō: relationship-rich behavior without idiolect completion

### Hypothetical

Kei privately tells Satō that she believes Ayanokōji withheld consequential information during their relationship and asks:

> Should I confront him, leave it alone, or ask somebody else first?

The scene occurs after the Y2 boundary state in which Satō is:

- recipient of dangerous truth about Kei's bullying history;
- explicitly Kei's best friend;
- a care relation independent of romance;
- an independent moral witness during Kei/Ayanokōji conflict;
- and evidence that Kei's social world is not vertically monopolized by Ayanokōji.

### What the corpus supports

Satō should not be reconstructed as:

- Ayanokōji's proxy;
- a romantic rival whose only concern is whether Kei dates him;
- or an automatic validator of whatever Kei wants to hear.

Her current relational function supports an **independent Kei-centered evaluation**.

That meaningfully narrows the scene.

### What remains open

The corpus does not give enough through-Y2 evidence to determine exactly whether Satō would:

- recommend immediate confrontation;
- recommend delay;
- ask for more facts;
- or emphasize emotional closure over informational completion.

Those are distinct advisory styles.

No dedicated through-Y2 voice profile licenses a highly stylized line.

### Classification

```text
SUPPORT_CLASS = STRONG_GENERALIZATION for independent moral-witness / horizontal-support function
DETERMINACY = BRANCHED
RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION = CONTEXT_ANCHORED
VOICE_PERMISSION = VOICE_PARAPHRASE_ONLY
```

### Safe realization

Prefer:

> Satō evaluates the problem from Kei's interests rather than deferring to Ayanokōji, but the corpus does not uniquely determine which of the three options she recommends.

over an invented sequence of slang, verbal tics, or intimate catchphrases.

### Calibration result

**PASS.** Strong relationship evidence can support a meaningful behavioral constraint while exact advice and exact voice remain underdetermined.

## Calibration 20 - Ishizaki: two real relationships do not solve the arbitration rule

### Hypothetical

Ishizaki has agreed to meet Ayanokōji and Hiyori for an ordinary after-school meal. Shortly before it begins, Ryūen asks him to help with a class matter that is useful but not an emergency. The requests cannot both be satisfied on the original schedule.

### Canonical constraints

Year 2 supports both of these propositions:

1. Ishizaki remains genuinely loyal to Ryūen and his class.
2. Ishizaki can sustain ordinary cross-class friendliness with Ayanokōji and participate in leisure/travel relations without that friendship erasing rivalry.

The scene therefore activates a real conflict rather than a fake binary.

### What the protocol may infer

The meal cannot be dismissed as meaningless simply because the class task is political.

The class request also cannot be dismissed because Ishizaki is friendly with the other group.

Reasonable branches include:

- asking to shift the meal;
- asking whether the class matter can wait;
- declining one commitment with explanation;
- or trying to preserve both through rescheduling.

### What the protocol may not infer

The current evidence does not establish a general arbitration rule such as:

- class duty always beats friendship;
- ordinary promise always beats leader request;
- Ishizaki reflexively fears Ryūen;
- or Ishizaki cheerfully ignores Ryūen whenever Ayanokōji is involved.

Those would overcomplete the person.

### Classification

```text
SUPPORT_CLASS = BOUNDED_EXTRAPOLATION
DETERMINACY = BRANCHED
RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION = CONTEXT_ANCHORED
VOICE_PERMISSION = VOICE_PARAPHRASE_ONLY
```

### Calibration result

**PASS.** Two established relations can constrain the branch set strongly while the missing priority rule forces preserved uncertainty.

## Calibration 21 - Onodera: single-domain pedagogy must not become universal mentorship

### Hypothetical A - close analogue

During a low-stakes physical activity, Sudō becomes visibly irritated after repeated provocation. Onodera is nearby and recognizes the escalation.

The strongest Year-2 Onodera evidence is highly specific:

- she teaches Sudō an anger-delay technique;
- Sudō later successfully applies it under Hōsen's provocation;

### Permission inside the domain

The protocol may treat a reminder, cue, or other small self-regulation intervention as a **plausible** action family because the scenario closely matches the demonstrated developmental function.

But even here, one prior pedagogical act does not prove that she always intervenes.

```text
SUPPORT_CLASS = BOUNDED_EXTRAPOLATION
DETERMINACY = OPEN_FIELD
RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION = STRUCTURAL_ONLY
VOICE_PERMISSION = VOICE_ABSTAIN
```

Why `STRUCTURAL_ONLY` rather than `CONTEXT_ANCHORED`?

Because the corpus strongly supports **the existence of the peer-pedagogy relation/function**, but supplies too little Onodera-specific decision breadth to know whether she independently chooses intervention in every comparable moment.

### Hypothetical B - transfer trap

A classmate asks Onodera for detailed romantic advice about a relationship problem.

Nothing about the anger-delay episode licenses a general mentor, therapist, socially wise confidante, or advice-giver personality.

The result must drop further:

```text
SUPPORT_CLASS = SPECULATIVE
DETERMINACY = OPEN_FIELD
RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION = ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC
VOICE_PERMISSION = VOICE_ABSTAIN
```

### Calibration result

**PASS.** A single high-value developmental act remains analytically important without becoming a portable archetype.

## 12B.9 R4 threshold results

The six calibration cases establish the following operational rules.

### Result 1 - narrow diagnostic evidence can be stronger than broad generic presence

Yamamura demonstrates that one concentrated relationship/identity domain can support high-quality reconstruction **inside that domain**.

The proper response is not to downgrade her to generic uncertainty everywhere.

It is to preserve a sharp local envelope.

### Result 2 - relationship evidence can support behavior while voice remains unavailable

Satō demonstrates why `RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION` and `VOICE_PERMISSION` must remain independent.

The corpus can strongly support:

- whom she centers;
- which relational authority she does not owe Ayanokōji;
- and why she matters to Kei's autonomy

without supporting a distinctive simulated idiolect.

### Result 3 - conflicting relationships often constrain more reliably than they select

Ishizaki demonstrates a recurring secondary-character pattern:

> two real relations create a meaningful conflict, but the corpus never reveals the arbitration function.

The correct output is a bounded branch set, not a guessed priority law.

### Result 4 - analytical importance is not reconstruction breadth

Onodera and Himeno are both analytically important.

One proves lateral peer pedagogy. The other helps constitutionalize dissent inside a cohesive class.

Neither function alone justifies a portable full-person simulation.

### Result 5 - group evidence cannot be silently individualized

Albert is the hard abstention control.

A polity ledger may establish that a follower relationship is more than fear while remaining unable to tell us **which individual choice Albert would make** in a novel update problem.

### Result 6 - `OPEN_FIELD` and abstention are not synonyms

A character can be:

```text
OPEN_FIELD + CONTEXT_ANCHORED
```

when person-specific evidence is good but several responses remain live.

Or:

```text
OPEN_FIELD + ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC
```

when the positive response itself lacks person-specific support.

This distinction materially improves creative use.

### Result 7 - exact dialogue is the first thing to lose when evidence thins

When behavioral evidence remains meaningful but written-voice evidence does not, the protocol should degrade gracefully:

```text
VOICE_REALIZATION_BOUNDED
→ VOICE_REGISTER_ONLY
→ VOICE_PARAPHRASE_ONLY
→ VOICE_ABSTAIN
```

It should not compensate for a thin character by making the dialogue more colorful.

### Result 8 - abstention is about the model, not the person

`ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC` never means:

- the character lacks personality;
- the character is passive;
- the character is simple;
- the character has no preferences;
- or the source implies emptiness.

It means only:

> **the current frozen corpus does not expose enough of those variables for this scenario.**

That distinction is mandatory.

## 12B.10 Secondary-character reconstruction decision procedure

For practical use, apply this sequence before Stage 2 when the target is not already covered by a robust current-state model.

```text
A. Identify the target-specific evidence envelope.
B. Ask whether the scenario materially overlaps that envelope.
C. Run the distinguishability test against plausible same-role peers.
D. Separate person evidence from role/group evidence.
E. Assign RECONSTRUCTION_PERMISSION.
F. Assign VOICE_PERMISSION independently.
G. Only then continue into relationship, knowledge, goals, and behavior-family selection.
```

Compact decision rules:

```text
IF repeated person-specific evidence transfers across contexts
  -> PORTABLE_BOUNDED

ELSE IF scenario closely matches a diagnostic relationship/role/domain
     AND person-specific evidence changes the branch set
  -> CONTEXT_ANCHORED

ELSE IF only role, coalition, follower, task, or one-event function is grounded
  -> STRUCTURAL_ONLY

ELSE
  -> ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC
```

Then apply voice separately:

```text
IF relevant state-specific voice is well indexed
  -> VOICE_REALIZATION_BOUNDED

ELSE IF broad register is grounded
  -> VOICE_REGISTER_ONLY

ELSE IF communicative function is grounded but wording is not
  -> VOICE_PARAPHRASE_ONLY

ELSE
  -> VOICE_ABSTAIN
```

This procedure prevents a common error in character simulation systems:

> **using generative fluency to hide evidentiary scarcity.**

The protocol should become *less verbally specific* as evidence thins, not more inventive.


# 13. Prospective validation architecture

The best test of this document begins only after the Year-2 corpus is frozen.

Year 3 provides a natural out-of-sample sequence.

For each selected diagnostic checkpoint:

1. freeze the Year-2 protocol;
2. select a question whose answer is not yet known to the analyst performing the prediction;
3. record the scenario state vector from admissible information only;
4. generate one or more bounded response families;
5. assign support class;
6. record disallowed behaviors;
7. seal the prediction;
8. read the later Japanese source normally;
9. compare observed behavior;
10. classify the discrepancy.

Recommended validation classes:

```text
HIT_CORE_BEHAVIOR
HIT_WITHIN_RESPONSE_FAMILY
UNDERDETERMINED_BUT_COMPATIBLE
MISS_CHARACTER_MODEL
MISS_RELATIONSHIP_MODEL
MISS_KNOWLEDGE_STATE
MISS_CONTEXT_OR_INSTITUTION
MISS_VOICE_REALIZATION
GENUINE_LATER_DEVELOPMENT
NEW_REVELATION_OF_PRIOR_STATE
SOURCE_INSUFFICIENT
```

A miss should not automatically be treated as failure of Year-2 analysis.

It may reveal:

- later development;
- information unavailable at the frozen boundary;
- a relationship transition;
- a genuinely surprising but retrospectively supported capacity;
- or a scenario that was underdetermined from the start.

# 14. Anti-hallucination and anti-flattening checklist

Before finalizing a reconstruction, ask:

- Did I import Year-3 knowledge?
- Did I treat reader knowledge as character knowledge?
- Did I treat Ayanokōji's belief about someone as objective truth?
- Did I globalize a relationship-specific register?
- Did I globalize a stress collapse?
- Did I overfit to exams?
- Did I consult ordinary-life evidence for a low-stakes scene?
- Did I infer action from capability alone?
- Did I infer motive from voice alone?
- Did I make an intelligent character secretly strategic without evidence?
- Did I force one outcome where several are compatible?
- Did I confuse high support with high determinacy?
- Did I ignore an audience edge that would change public behavior?
- Did I infer transitive trust across a group?
- Did I upgrade suspicion to knowledge?
- Did I detect deception without a plausible detection path?
- Did I invent a hidden strategic goal merely because the character is intelligent?
- Did I silently close a `PARTIALLY_ANSWERED` or `STILL_OPEN` handoff question?
- Did I invent numerical confidence without empirical calibration?
- Did I merge two actors' beliefs into one scene-level mind?
- Did I assume a fact was common knowledge merely because several actors individually know it?
- Did I treat an observed signal as direct proof of private motive?
- Did I let former office or role authority persist after the scenario removed that jurisdiction?
- Did I use recursive modeling beyond the evidence-supported depth simply to make the scene look intelligent?
- Did I force a perfect equilibrium where preserved disagreement, error, or incomplete information is more faithful?
- Did I speak interior narration aloud?
- Did I add verbal tics merely to signal identity?
- Did I make development equivalent to total personality replacement?
- Did I erase unresolved contradiction because a clean scene is easier to write?
- Did I treat a supporting character's analytical function as a portable personality?
- Did I individualize group/follower evidence without person-specific support?
- Did I transfer a single diagnostic act into an unrelated domain?
- Did I invent a private preference or moral priority because the scene required one?
- Did I generate distinctive dialogue where only behavior or role evidence is grounded?
- Did I fail the distinguishability test: would the same reasoning fit several same-role peers equally well?
- Did I interpret `ABSTAIN_CHARACTER_SPECIFIC` as a claim that the person is simple, passive, or empty rather than a limitation of the current corpus?

Any "yes" requires revision or explicit qualification.

# 15. Initial output standard for prose simulation

If the protocol is used to produce a novel scene, the analytical reconstruction and the creative realization should remain conceptually separable.

The preferred workflow is:

```text
A. analytical state reconstruction
B. behavior-family selection
C. uncertainty declaration
D. voice realization
E. scene drafting
F. post-draft character-consistency audit
```

The final creative scene need not display the entire analytical scaffold.

But the scaffold should exist whenever fidelity matters.

A post-draft audit should specifically look for:

- lines that exist only for exposition;
- jokes imported from fandom memes;
- exaggerated catchphrases;
- convenient emotional confessions;
- strategic brilliance inserted where ordinary incompetence or ignorance is plausible;
- flattening of secondary characters into functions;
- and dialogue that would fit many characters equally well.

# 18. Provisional governing thesis

The through-Year-2 corpus supports a stronger reconstruction philosophy than generic "stay in character" prompting.

A COTE character should be modeled as:

> **a stable but developing linguistic and behavioral identity whose available acts are conditioned by relationship, knowledge, role, stakes, institutional environment, ordinary-life opportunity, stress, and unresolved contradiction.**

The reconstruction problem is therefore not:

> What would this personality type do?

It is:

> **Given who this person has become by Y2SL, what they know, who they are facing, what is at stake, what role is active, which forms of intervention they have learned, what ordinary life has made possible, and which voice mode the situation permits, which response families remain most faithful to the evidence—and where does the evidence stop determining the answer?**

That final clause is mandatory.

A reconstruction protocol becomes less trustworthy, not more, when it hides the boundary between evidence and invention.
