# OREGAIRU V2 -- Character State Ledger

## Function

This ledger consolidates character-state findings from the complete V2 reread through Volume 14.5. It is not a collection of character essays. It is a longitudinal evidence structure for answering four questions:

1. What is each character demonstrably like at a given point in the story?
2. What changes, and what merely becomes newly visible?
3. Which claims belong to the character, which belong to another observer, and which remain interpretation?
4. What state should later specialist synthesis treat as current authority without erasing earlier ambiguity?

The ledger deliberately prevents Hikigaya Hachiman's narration from becoming a universal interior camera. Hachiman is an important witness, but his inferences about Yukino, Yui, Haruno, Hayama, Iroha, or anyone else retain the evidentiary status assigned in the volume reading. The same rule applies to Haruno's diagnoses, Hiratsuka's guidance, Yui's social readings, and every other character assertion.

Every major entry routes back to one or more canonical deep readings and, where useful, to primary-source locator IDs already validated in those artifacts. Those volume ledgers remain the authority for exact Japanese wording and fixed-page evidence.

# 1. Character-state evidence protocol

## 1.1 State dimensions

A character state is tracked across these dimensions:

- **self-conception** -- how the character describes or organizes the self;
- **desire** -- what the character wants, including desires only partly owned;
- **fear / wound** -- what outcomes or histories organize defensive behavior;
- **agency / authorship** -- ability to originate, refuse, revise, or own action;
- **relational method** -- how the character asks, helps, protects, withdraws, or imposes;
- **role / social position** -- offices, family roles, group status, and performed identities;
- **competence** -- actual practical, social, institutional, or interpretive ability;
- **reliance / autonomy** -- how the character gives and receives help;
- **performance** -- socially strategic presentation that may coexist with sincere motive;
- **unresolved contradiction** -- two supported tendencies that should not be prematurely harmonized.

## 1.2 Evidence hierarchy

The ledger uses the following authority order for character interiority:

1. direct character speech clearly about self;
2. behavior under meaningful stakes;
3. viewpoint narration belonging to that character, where available;
4. repeated behavior across contexts;
5. another character's observation;
6. another character's psychological interpretation;
7. literary inference from form, imagery, or pattern.

A lower category may become highly persuasive through repetition and corroboration, but it does not silently change class.

## 1.3 State-change rules

- New information about a past state is not automatically a past state change.
- Later outcomes do not convert early ambiguity into inevitability.
- Mixed motives are preferred over purity tests when the evidence supports both.
- Competence does not imply autonomy, and reliance does not imply incapacity.
- Social performance does not imply falsity.
- A self-description can be sincere and still incomplete or excessively harsh.
- Character growth may consist of changed procedure while temperament remains stable.
- The mainline final-choice state and the post-14.5 durability state remain distinct authority layers.

# 2. Executive series-level character thesis

The strongest cross-character result of the V2 reread is that Oregairu does not primarily depict people discovering hidden fixed essences. It depicts people becoming more capable of **authoring actions and relationships without requiring perfect self-knowledge first**.

The principal characters do not become personality inversions:

- Hachiman remains analytical, defensive, comic, and inclined to overengineer.
- Yukino remains severe, highly competent, proud, and socially awkward.
- Yui remains atmosphere-sensitive, affectionate, and motivated to preserve connection.
- Iroha remains strategic, image-conscious, flirtatious, and opportunistic.
- Haruno remains provocative, politically intelligent, and difficult to read.
- Hayama remains socially central, accommodating, and burdened by expectation.
- Hiratsuka remains interventionist, impatient, and mentor-like.
- Komachi remains mischievous, socially fluent, and deeply attached to her brother.

What changes is the relationship between those stable traits and agency.

The series repeatedly moves characters through versions of the following progression:

> role or defense -> contradiction -> explicit stake -> owned choice -> answerable consequence -> revised practice

The endpoint is therefore not purified authenticity. It is **greater authorship under acknowledged incompleteness**.

# 4. Hikigaya Hachiman -- character-state route

The dedicated Hachiman epistemic ledger is authoritative for narratorial reliability. This section records the broader character-state progression needed for cross-character comparison.

## CS-HAC-01 -- V01-V03: defensive autonomy with growing evidence of desire

**State:** Hachiman begins with a coherent but defensive self-conception: solitude is preferable to false relation, expectation is dangerous, and social kindness is likely to conceal pity, obligation, or convenience. These beliefs are not pure delusion. His social perception is already strong. The defensive problem is that he uses real social knowledge to deny favorable possibilities involving himself.

**Behavioral contradiction:** he repeatedly helps, notices, remembers, returns, and becomes pleased when relied upon. He forms a working orbit around Yukino, Yui, Totsuka, Zaimokuza, Komachi, and Hiratsuka before he is willing to call that orbit a meaningful network.

**State delta:** Yui's and Yukino's direct corrections begin breaking the debt/accounting model. Hachiman can recognize that his own reset logic may be self-defense, but still chooses it.

**Source route:** V01 sections 4-5; V01-E11, E17-E19. V02 sections 4-5; V02-L23-L28. V03 sections 4-5; V3-L26-L34.

**Status:** STRENGTHEN the early V1 claim that Hachiman is defensive; REVISE any claim that he is simply socially blind.

## CS-HAC-02 -- V04-V06.5: solver identity and self-cost become morally consequential

Hachiman's competence becomes operationally dangerous because he increasingly treats his own cost as the easiest variable to spend. He sees structures others miss, can design high-leverage interventions, and accepts becoming disliked if it resolves the stated task.

The key correction is that voluntary self-sacrifice is not private. Others observe it, depend on it, become hurt by it, and change their own behavior around it. Hiratsuka makes this externality explicit.

By Volume 6 he can ask for limited help and acknowledge Yui's distinct method, but still believes that if a socially costly act lands mainly on himself it is ethically cheap.

**Source route:** V06-L01-L02, L15-L23, L45-L54; Checkpoint B.

**State delta:** competence remains; moral accounting changes. The self is no longer a costless resource.

## CS-HAC-03 -- V07-V09: preservation crisis, failed telepathy, and becoming a requester

Kyoto reveals the jurisdiction problem in full: Hachiman can correctly infer multiple wishes and still deprive Tobe of the speech he intended to make. Yukino and Yui respond not by denying his intelligence but by rejecting what he did with it.

Volume 8 worsens the pattern through election engineering. He recognizes that his motive includes preserving a place he values, yet uses information and synthetic support to remove the problem rather than make the underlying desires answerable.

Volume 9 is the major state break. Hachiman admits uncertainty, recognizes that he has treated inference as truth, and becomes the Service Club's requester. His declaration that he wants the genuine is not a solved philosophy; it is the first sustained attempt to place his own desire inside the problem.

**Source route:** V07-E050-E067; V08-E027-E073; V09-E022-E079; Checkpoint C.

**State delta:** from solver with private jurisdiction to participant capable of exposing a request.

## CS-HAC-04 -- V10-V11: asking improves before refusal tolerance does

Hachiman now directly asks friends for time and help, protects Yukino's privacy, imagines future reliance on Totsuka, and enters more ordinary shared life. Yet Hayama's refusal shows the limit: Hachiman still treats another person's request for information as potential authority to pressure a reluctant target.

Volume 11 further improves self-description. He identifies his positive-label projections onto Yui and Yukino and admits that the ideal he wants may itself be an imposition. He nevertheless retains strong anti-reliance and anti-ambiguity instincts.

**Source route:** V10 sections 5.1, 5.6, 5.8; V11-E004-E008, E028-E038, E089-E093; Checkpoint D.

**State delta:** the form of asking is learned before the ethics of accepting refusal are fully internalized.

## CS-HAC-05 -- V12-V13: personal motive survives collapse of respectable excuses

Volume 12 strips away club duty, target request, and uncomplicated rescue. Hachiman finally identifies a personal reason: he promised Yukino he would help and still wants to act. This is growth because the motive can no longer pretend to be neutral.

Volume 13 tests whether that growth changes method. Hachiman distinguishes direct help from rivalry, improves collaborator consent, accepts refusal from helpers, relies openly on Totsuka, and states that he and Yukino cannot decide the whole triangle for Yui. But he still manipulates the institutional environment and tries to turn a motivational fact into proof against a structural diagnosis.

**Source route:** V12 sections 5.3, 7.5-7.6, 18.2; V12-E115-E123 and later Hachiman reason sequence. V13 sections 5.1, 7, 16; Checkpoint E incoming route.

**State delta:** ethical vocabulary advances faster than practice.

## CS-HAC-06 -- V14: from service to first-person desire

The decisive state change is not that Hachiman becomes direct in ordinary speech. He remains painfully indirect. The change is that he can no longer justify continued relation through duty, diagnosis, rescue, or another person's need. He states that continued involvement is his own will.

He offers reciprocal exposure rather than unilateral cost-bearing. The bridge method remains ethically imperfect, but after release Yukino remains, challenges him, receives space to refuse, and counter-requests.

Hachiman also accepts that one chosen relationship cannot be certified as permanently genuine. Continued doubt becomes a practice inside commitment rather than a reason not to commit.

**Source route:** V14 sections 5.1, 7, 9, 15-18; Checkpoint E.

**State delta:** desire is owned without becoming metaphysical proof.

## CS-HAC-07 -- V14.5: future assumption and anti-sanctification

Post-ending Hachiman can casually assume future meetings with Yukino even if their institutions diverge. He explicitly rejects her lowering academic ambition to match him. He remains delighted by reliance while rationalizing it comically, but rationalization no longer blocks the desired action.

His final major correction concerns preservation itself. He realizes he has begun to sanctify the current Service Club form. The mature state therefore rejects both negative absolutism and positive absolutism.

**Source route:** V14_5-E071-E087, E093-E106; final post-14.5 checkpoint; Hachiman epistemic ledger.

**Terminal state:** future-oriented attachment + procedural self-doubt + willingness to transmit without owning form.

# 5. Yukinoshita Yukino -- character-state ledger

## CS-YUK-01 -- V01-V03: competence, reformism, and first voluntary belonging

**Initial state:** Yukino is exceptionally capable, severe, reformist, proud, socially isolated, and inexperienced at being liked. V2 rejects the early shortcut that competence is already merely armor. It is real ability, ethical conviction, pride, and a social mode that later acquires defensive uses.

Yui's presence produces the first major relational expansion. Yukino can accept being treated as a friend, ask Hachiman for bounded help when helping Yui, accept Komachi's practical knowledge, and say that Yui's presence helps both club members.

The important early correction is:

> Yukino is not incapable of reliance. She is much less practiced at making herself the object of the request.

**Source route:** V01-E05-E06, E14-E15; V02-L07, L15; V3-L02, L13-L15, L21, L25.

**Unresolved:** family vulnerability and Haruno comparison are present but not yet sufficiently specified.

## CS-YUK-02 -- V04-V06.5: substitute role, self-revision, and the failure of overfunctioning

Volume 4 introduces the family concept of substitute role: Yukino is not simply Haruno's opposite but someone who sometimes enters a public position whose normal occupant is her sister. She also demonstrates a strong capacity for self-revision by apologizing when her model of Rumi's family distorts judgment.

Volume 6 turns competence into a problem. Yukino can do so much that support becomes substitution: Sagami retains title while Yukino absorbs operational responsibility. Hachiman's criticism matters because it invokes Yukino's own earlier ethic of leaving the final act to the subject.

Her recovery is not dependence replacing independence. It is authored interdependence:

- she promises Yui future reliance;
- confronts Haruno herself;
- uses leverage rather than rescue;
- distributes emergency work.

Volume 6.5 tests whether the correction holds when Sagami receives a second responsibility. It does, incompletely: Sagami remains the accountable office-holder while Yukino still risks over-supporting.

**Source route:** V06-L04, L13, L19-L22, L29, L36-L40, L51-L54; V06.5 section 5.2; Checkpoint B.

## CS-YUK-03 -- V07-V09: trust, personal stake, election authorship, and self-uncertainty

Volume 7 increases both trust and vulnerability. Yukino entrusts the Kyoto problem to Hachiman and is personally distressed by the method he chooses. Her objection is method-centered: she rejects his way of acting rather than reducing him to a bad person.

Volume 8 establishes Yukino as a chooser. Her student-council candidacy is explicitly her will. V2 therefore rejects readings that treat it as a disguised request to be stopped. The later line that the problem and reason to move have disappeared reveals a real dependence on externally structured reasons, but does not falsify the original self-description.

Volume 9 destabilizes Yukino's competence identity. She admits that she thought she understood herself and others better than she did. She rejects the idea that Hachiman is simply the same kind of person as she is. Most importantly, she makes a future personal request for help without defining its terms.

**Source route:** V07-E007-E009, E021-E022, E050, E060-E061; V08-E027-E029, E053-E055; V09-E047-E052, E068-E077 and Destinyland request sequence; Checkpoint C.

**State delta:** from competent person who believes correct perception should be possible to a person who can acknowledge self-unknowing and still request future relation.

## CS-YUK-04 -- V10-V11: voluntary disclosure, fallibility-compatible trust, and unstable self-location

Volume 10 and 10.5 show a quieter but important growth. Yukino can answer personal questions and voluntarily add more than asked, accept Yui's plural friendships, partially repair old Hayama history without erasing anger, support Iroha as a senior, and design contingency around Hachiman's possible failure.

This is competence used to preserve another person's authorship rather than replace it.

Volume 11 then intensifies the family and identity problem. Haruno claims Yukino has no self; Yukino herself uses drift/anchor imagery and nearly lets Yui's preservation proposal determine the next form. The crucial counterevidence is the ending: Yukino tells Hachiman not to decide her feelings and originates a request of her own.

**Source route:** V10 sections 5.2 and 5.9; V10.5 section 5.2; V11-E073-E083, E086-E096 and final request sequence; Checkpoint D.

**State delta:** unstable self-location does not erase subjecthood; it becomes the condition under which request authorship matters most.

## CS-YUK-05 -- V12: authorship before certainty and explicit self-critique of reliance

Volume 12 is Yukino's largest self-authorship advance before the ending.

She:

- admits incomplete self-knowledge;
- recovers a personal future desire;
- describes how nominal freedom left her without a model for choosing;
- admits fear of testing a determinate answer;
- decides to speak to her mother herself;
- asks Hachiman and Yui to witness the end rather than solve it;
- uses the prom as a competence test;
- later asks Hachiman not to take over;
- explicitly describes her own conduct as dependence / burden displacement.

Her self-critique is authoritative as self-description but not a theorem that all prior reliance was pathological.

**Source route:** V12-E018-E039, E040-E054, E092-E108, E117-E123; V12 sections 5.1, 10.1, 18.3.

**Frozen principle:** authorship can precede certainty.

## CS-YUK-06 -- V13: self-authored closure that still overreaches others' authorship

Yukino refuses direct help but voluntarily accepts Hachiman as a rival. This is a meaningful authorization distinction. She later recognizes that she had come to expect Hachiman to make the prom succeed and names that as dependence.

Her final wager request -- that Hachiman grant Yui's wish -- is genuinely self-authored. V2 nevertheless refuses to ratify it as the correct final answer because it also allocates Hachiman and Yui's future without their joint authorization.

**Source route:** V13 sections 5.2, 6, 7, 10, 16; Checkpoint E.

**State delta:** self-authorship becomes strong enough to generate a wrong or incomplete answer of her own. That is still more agency than passive compliance.

## CS-YUK-07 -- V14: reciprocal claim, explicit desire, and support without incapacity

Yukino does not merely receive Hachiman's bridge speech. She interrogates it, stays after release, rejects devaluation of herself, and converts his asymmetrical offer into a reciprocal request for his life.

The later stair exchange is the cleanest autonomy marker in the series: she says she can stand alone; he says he knows; she still takes the offered hand.

Yukino later gives conventional romantic naming to the relationship. This does not collapse the larger relation into one word, but it matters because she can now state a desire without requiring a project to make it legitimate.

**Source route:** V14 sections 5.2, 6, 7, 10, 18; Checkpoint E.

**Terminal mainline state:** capable of standing alone, choosing support, and making reciprocal claims.

## CS-YUK-08 -- V14.5: ordinary future planning without fusion

In the true post-ending stories, Yukino assumes continued meetings across possible institutional divergence, wants shared study because it helps motivation, marks a one-month anniversary by her own choice, and participates in Service Club succession without insisting that the old form be preserved.

She also teaches Komachi a practice rather than commanding Komachi to reproduce an identity.

**Source route:** V14_5-E069-E087, E098-E106; final post-14.5 checkpoint.

**Terminal durability state:** chosen interdependence + separate trajectory + capacity to transmit without preserving form.

# 6. Yuigahama Yui -- character-state ledger

## CS-YUI-01 -- V01-V03: atmosphere sensitivity becomes active relational construction

Yui begins socially observant and highly responsive to atmosphere, but V2 substantially upgrades her agency. From Volume 1 she is already doing relational work:

- converting Yukino's bluntness into closeness;
- giving Yukino a nickname;
- moving between social groups rather than accepting one identity;
- renegotiating Miura friendship;
- recruiting cooperation;
- naming the Service Club as a place she values.

By Volume 2 she creates communication channels, future study plans, direct questions, and protective interventions. By Volume 3 she can say she does not want the relation to end, while still using deniable or incomplete forms when a fully explicit declaration would force an irreversible answer.

**Source route:** V01-E08, E12-E13, E17; V02-L02, L05, L08-L09, L17, L23-L25; V3-L24, L29, L33-L34, L40.

**V2 correction:** Yui is not merely the empath or mediator. She is a builder of relational infrastructure.

## CS-YUI-02 -- V04-V06.5: social intelligence becomes operational and accountability-bearing

Yui identifies the causal role of guilt and exclusion risk in Rumi's situation and notices Rumi's imminent action before Hachiman does. She also makes a clear two-person outing request to Hachiman.

Volume 6 is the largest early upgrade. Yui admits possessiveness toward Yukino, becomes angry at both Yukino's overfunctioning and Hachiman's failure to help, tells Yukino to rely on *us* rather than abstract everyone, respects Yukino's need for time, and accepts distributed work with honest limits.

Her social intelligence is therefore operational: she knows how events will read, who can be recruited, how a request can be made usable, and when support becomes substitution.

**Source route:** V06-L07, L09, L16, L21, L34, L40; V06.5 section 5.3; Checkpoint B.

## CS-YUI-03 -- V07-V09: romantic desire, explicit ethical boundaries, and participation in conflict

Volume 7 makes Yui's romantic desire difficult to classify as generic friendliness. She creates dyadic opportunities, reacts to the false confession, briefly believes it might be real, and explicitly asks Hachiman not to use that method again.

Miura independently observes that Yui still reads atmosphere but now says what she wants more often.

Volumes 8-9 move Yui from preservation manager to conflict participant. She runs for president partly to protect the club, admits selfish desire, rejects Hachiman's attempts to privatize consequences, calls Yukino unfair, accepts being called cowardly in return, and insists that incomprehension is a reason to continue talking rather than stop.

**Source route:** V07-E013-E016, E023, E062-E067; V08-E031-E033, E056-E059; V09-E019-E024, E048-E052, E069-E080; Checkpoint C.

**State delta:** Yui can increasingly place her own want into the room even when it creates friction.

## CS-YUI-04 -- V10-V11: ordinary desire, plural belonging, and preservation becoming explicit proposal

Yui's ordinary-life role becomes more visible. She supports multiple friendships rather than requiring exclusivity, shares aftermath when work fails, and continues to articulate desire as a legitimate reason even when it is selfish or temporary.

Volume 11 is the turning point where preservation becomes explicit enough to be ethically judged. Yui states that she wants everything and offers a concrete future arrangement. The proposal is caring, agentic, and controlling at once because it attempts to preserve everyone by settling the structure in advance.

**Source route:** V10 section 5.3; V10.5 section 5.3; V11-E003, E007, E024, E032-E037 and final aquarium proposal sequence; Checkpoint D.

**State delta:** atmosphere management becomes owned desire rather than hidden motive.

## CS-YUI-05 -- V12: self-awareness becomes self-implication

Yui knows enough to interpret the hidden photograph and deliberately refuses to force Yukino to answer before she believes the relationship can bear the consequence. This is avoidance, but self-aware avoidance.

Her closing interior sequence is decisive. She understands that visible tears could activate Hachiman's rescue reflex, refuses to become a pitiable object, says she wants him beside her rather than functioning as a hero, and admits that she displaced the decisive choice onto Yukino.

Her self-description of dependence is therefore best frozen as **decision displacement**, not generic emotional weakness.

**Source route:** V12-E062-E076 and final Yui interlude; V12 sections 5.2, 10.7, 18.4.

## CS-YUI-06 -- V13-V14: refusing self-erasure and receiving an answer

Volume 13 establishes that Yui does not merely want Hachiman. She explicitly refuses Yukino's disappearance from her future and imagines lunches, trips, Aprils, and years/decades with Yukino regardless of romantic outcome.

Volume 14 then forces actual non-equivalence. Yui asks Yukino directly about her feelings, reciprocates after disclosure, rejects unilateral closure, and continues to say she wants everything. Hachiman gives her a real romantic answer rather than indefinite postponement. She cries privately. The pain is not erased by later continuation.

Her return to the Service Club is therefore not restored ambiguity. It is continued relation after known non-reciprocity.

**Source route:** V13 sections 5.3, 6, 14-16; V14 sections 5.3, 6, 7, 18; Checkpoint E.

**Terminal mainline state:** emotionally wounded but not structurally deleted; capable of sustaining friendship after receiving the answer she had demanded.

## CS-YUI-07 -- V14.5: post-loss subjecthood and straightforward relational speech

Yui remains active in planning, school life, and the future club. Her possible cram-school motive remains mixed/open; V2 does not promote observer teasing into her true romantic interiority.

Her most important post-ending function is practical: when Hachiman and Yukino overcomplicate whether Komachi should recruit members, Yui understands the difficulty and simply says the club should be listed and new people invited.

**Source route:** V14_5-E089-E096, E098-E106.

**Terminal durability state:** Yui retains an independent social function and future orientation after romantic loss.

# 7. Isshiki Iroha -- character-state ledger

## CS-IRO-01 -- V07.5-V08: strategic junior under imposed candidacy

Iroha is immediately legible as socially strategic. She uses softness, junior status, and image management as leverage. Volume 8 then places her in a genuinely coercive institutional situation: prank nomination means she neither wants the office nor wants the public humiliation of losing or withdrawing.

Hachiman changes her incentive environment until she chooses the presidency. The origin is compromised; the choice is still hers at the final decision point.

**Source route:** V08-E006-E009, E044-E050.

## CS-IRO-02 -- V09-V10: installed role becomes self-owned practice

Iroha initially leans heavily on the Service Club but gradually performs actual presidential work. Volume 9 makes Hachiman and Yukino stop doing the office for her: she must summon the council, state positions, negotiate, and own the event.

Volume 10 confirms that role ownership can emerge through practice even when the role began under manipulation.

**Source route:** V09 section 5.4; V10 section 5.7.

**State delta:** compromised origin no longer exhausts identity.

## CS-IRO-03 -- V10.5-V12: mixed motive becomes normal rather than disqualifying

Iroha can be manipulative, lazy, hardworking, image-conscious, grateful, caring, flirtatious, and institutionally responsible in the same story. V2 rejects the hidden-real-Iroha model.

In Volume 12 the prom is genuinely hers as well as Yukino's project. Her answer that the prom is for herself is valuable precisely because it is owned. She also catches herself becoming over-reliant on Yukino and treats that as a project-management problem rather than a reason to abolish all reliance.

**Source route:** V10.5 section 5.4; V12-E091-E105, E115-E116; V12 section 5.5.

## CS-IRO-04 -- V13-V14: procedural observer and defender of continuing possibility

Iroha becomes increasingly good at recognizing institutional ownership, beneficiary confusion, and the emotional intensity of Hachiman/Yukino exchanges. Her selfishness remains productive: wanting something for herself is not treated as a moral disqualification.

In Volume 14 she is comfortable offering pretexts for continued contact and refuses the convention that an unchosen person's emotional future must be administratively terminated the moment a couple forms.

**Source route:** V13 section 5.4; V14 section 5.4; Checkpoint E.

## CS-IRO-05 -- V14.5: transparent pretext plus epistemic humility

Iroha explicitly admits that a present is an excuse for continued contact. Strategy and sincerity therefore become almost textually fused rather than opposites.

She still pushes boundaries by scheduling before crisp assent, so V2 does not romanticize her social technique.

Her most important epistemic change comes when she stops herself while interpreting Komachi: she says she only really knows herself and can only imagine another person from that basis. This is unusually mature inference-labeling.

She also becomes part of the Service Club succession ecology despite formal nonmembership.

**Source route:** V14_5-E040-E052, E063-E068, E088-E106.

**Terminal state:** strategic + sincere + institutionally embedded + more explicit about the limits of interpretation.

# 8. Yukinoshita Haruno -- character-state ledger

## CS-HAR-01 -- V03-V05: performed warmth and high-information counter-gaze

Haruno enters through socially optimized warmth, teasing control, and an eldest-daughter public role. Yukino calls the public face a mask, but V2 does not infer that all Haruno warmth is false.

By Volume 5 Haruno is a high-information, high-risk interpreter. She often knows important facts and sees fault lines early, but packages information in provocations that shape how listeners interpret Yukino.

**Source route:** V3-L17-L20; V04 section 5.6; V05 section 5.4.

**Authority rule:** Haruno says != novel proves.

## CS-HAR-02 -- V06-V10: political intelligence and visible uncertainty

Haruno understands informal power, crowd psychology, leverage, antagonism, and the weakness of unenforceable rules. Hachiman repeatedly speculates that she provokes Yukino for growth, but those motives remain unproven.

By Volume 10, Haruno's uncertainty becomes visible. Her question about whether anything genuine exists sounds less like a verdict from above and more like a personally invested doubt.

**Source route:** V06-L10, L31-L32; V08-E011, E018-E020; V10 section 5.9.

## CS-HAR-03 -- V11-V12: humanized critic, supportive sister, and codependency diagnosis

Volume 11 makes Haruno less usable as an oracle because she becomes more visibly implicated. She is highly perceptive about stagnation and Yukino's identity problem, but when Hiratsuka offers to listen to *her*, Haruno retreats into humor rather than making a direct request.

Volume 12 expands both sides. Haruno supports Yukino's authored choice, offers family assistance, and expresses pride/sadness about change. She also delivers the codependency diagnosis with deliberate theatrical force.

The diagnosis has evidence behind it. Her proposed cure through independence remains a character hypothesis, not governing doctrine.

**Source route:** V11-E040-E054, E064-E070, E093-E096; V12-E045-E061 and dependency sequence; V12 section 5.4.

## CS-HAR-04 -- V13-V14: interior longing limits diagnostic sovereignty

Volume 13's Haruno interiority reveals that she wants proof of a single non-counterfeit ending. Her critique of false endings is therefore also autobiographical and defensive.

Volume 14 confirms some of her criticism: the trio's proposed closure is too convenient, and Hachiman has not actually settled his desire. But the volume explicitly rejects using Haruno's borrowed diagnosis as a total explanation.

**Source route:** V13 section 5.6; V14 section 5.6; Checkpoint E.

**Terminal state:** brilliant counter-gaze, politically and emotionally implicated, never promoted to narrator authority.

# 9. Hayama Hayato -- character-state ledger

## CS-HAY-01 -- V01-V04: prosocial centrality with an unresolved history of failed intervention

Hayama begins as genuinely prosocial. His limitation is structural: universal inclusion language sounds different from the center than from the margin.

Volume 2 complicates the easy harmony-preserver model. He willingly accepts temporary exclusion from his own group if it may help horizontal ties form. Volume 4 reveals that his optimism is not naive: he understands Hachiman's ugly plan and participates because he hopes pressure may reveal decency. His memory of an earlier failure makes present intervention partly reparative.

**Source route:** V02-L10-L14; V04 section 5.4.

## CS-HAY-02 -- V06-V09: preservation, complicity, and rivalry with Hachiman

Hayama repeatedly becomes the person who can confer social legitimacy, but also the person Hachiman models instrumentally. In Kyoto, Hayama admits he likes the current group and fears irreversible loss. He relies on Hachiman despite disliking the method, then apologizes for that complicity.

Volume 9 further separates Hayama from Hachiman's idealized image. He explicitly resists being treated as the better man and distinguishes actual reliance from Hachiman's inferred need.

**Source route:** V07-E030-E044, E059; V09-E011-E016.

## CS-HAY-03 -- V10: expectation saturation and partial role ownership

Volume 10 is Hayama's major character reclassification. He is neither simply fake nor merely trapped. He has a public role saturated by expectation, but he also claims parts of it as himself. Formal choice exists while experienced authorship remains constrained.

His relation with Hachiman improves through mutual de-idealization: neither is permitted to use the other as a purified opposite.

**Source route:** V10 section 5.4 and corresponding locator ledger.

**State delta:** social role can be both imposed and partly owned.

## CS-HAY-04 -- V12-V14.5: change accepted, proxy guilt rejected, ordinary accommodation

Hayama recognizes that present change may be better than expected stasis. In Volume 13 he tries to transfer old regret toward Yukino onto Hachiman, and Hachiman refuses to inherit it. This protects both men from proxy obligation.

By Volume 14.5 their third-year relation is neither friendship nor hostility. It is a workable accommodation in a broader social field.

**Source route:** V12-E081-E087; V13 section 5.5; V14_5-E055-E056.

**Terminal state:** socially central, role-aware, partly self-authored, still carrying expectations that the text refuses to solve simplistically.

# 10. Hiratsuka Shizuka -- character-state ledger

## CS-HIR-01 -- early series: interventionist teacher with a self-transformation model

Hiratsuka begins by forcing Hachiman and Yukino into a shared institutional environment and refusing Hachiman's static self-description. Her early interventions are more coercive than her later method, but the governing goal is already developmental: a request may conceal a deeper problem, and club activity should not merely perform friendship.

**Source route:** V01-E21; V02-L01; V3-L04-L05.

## CS-HIR-02 -- V06-V09: externalities, correction, and question-preserving guidance

Hiratsuka is the clearest adult to say that Hachiman's willingness to hurt himself does not privatize the cost. She also distinguishes solving a problem from prohibiting it and frames being corrected as evidence that someone is still watching.

Volume 9 is her strongest epistemic intervention: Hachiman may read psychology well without understanding emotion. She does not then supply the answer. She repeatedly preserves the question and admits the limits of teachers and adults.

**Source route:** V06-L49-L50; V07-E018-E020; V09-E027-E041.

## CS-HIR-03 -- V11-V14: iterative recognition and relation by procedure

Hiratsuka's mature model is ongoing revision: live time together, notice incongruity, think, update, and accept eventual separation. She offers Haruno listening without forcing disclosure.

In Volume 13 she reduces the intervention debate to method: the important issue is *how* Hachiman involves himself, not whether he can purify himself through withdrawal.

In Volume 14 she directly attacks borrowed labels that distort another person's feelings. Her final genuine formulation remains modal rather than doctrinal.

**Source route:** V11-E017-E018, E040-E061; V13 section 5.7; V14 section 5.5.

**Terminal state:** mentor authority that deliberately refuses final epistemic sovereignty.

# 11. Hikigaya Komachi -- character-state ledger

## CS-KOM-01 -- V02-V05: ordinary family reciprocity and low-risk relational engineering

Komachi demonstrates early that protecting someone from cost can itself deprive them of participation. She also creates low-risk social situations Hachiman would not initiate and supplies a relational identity model that allows one person to display different colors without becoming false.

**Source route:** V02 section 5.6; V03 section 5.7; V05 section 5.6.

## CS-KOM-02 -- V07.5-V08: framing without destination control

Komachi's manipulations usually change opportunity and framing rather than dictate final answers. She knows Hachiman through long ordinary exposure but explicitly grounds that knowledge in experience, not mysticism.

Her Volume 8 request gives Hachiman a protective pretext to act while leaving Yukino and Yui's roles unspecified. She also tells him to speak to them directly.

**Source route:** V075-E013-E015, E026-E027; V08-E037-E047.

## CS-KOM-03 -- V10-V12: secure reliance as an autonomy control case

Komachi's entrance exam is a clean example of support without substitution. Hachiman can reassure, wait, care, and make failure survivable. He cannot take the exam for her.

After success, increasing sibling distance is framed as appropriate development without implying relational annihilation.

**Source route:** V10 section 5.8; V10.5 section 5.6; V11 section 5.8; V12-E077-E078, E111-E113.

**State delta:** less dependence does not require less relationship.

## CS-KOM-04 -- V14-V14.5: inheriting authority without inheriting identity

Komachi recreates the Service Club and becomes president, but the narrative refuses to treat her as caretaker of a sacred old form. She may recruit different people and make different mistakes.

The new mug, Hachiman's formal address to her as club president, and Yukino teaching tea constitute transfer of office and practice rather than ownership of meaning.

**Source route:** V14 section 5.8; V14_5-E057-E068, E079-E106.

**Terminal state:** family-secure, institutionally autonomous, able to inherit without copying.

# 12. Yukinoshita mother -- family-authority ledger

## CS-YM-01 -- V11: gentle coercion is still coercion

Yukino's mother demonstrates that social control does not require shouting. Her language defines identity, trust, freedom, failure, and correct direction before asking Yukino what she wants. The question is therefore formally open and substantively framed.

**Source route:** V11-E073-E083.

## CS-YM-02 -- V12-V14: contested authority becomes direct family politics

Volume 12's family testimony complicates the simple controlling-mother model: Haruno was explicitly constrained as successor while Yukino was nominally left free, yet that freedom was insufficiently recognized and difficult for Yukino to author.

By Volume 14 Yukino steps forward to answer her mother and accept responsibility. The mother's opposition increasingly functions as a family mode of testing and socialization, not simple suppression. The final dinner invitation draws Hachiman into the family system rather than casting him as an escape from it.

**Source route:** V12-E019-E032, E040-E054; V14 section 5.7.

**Terminal state:** formidable authority with real coercive capacity, but increasingly engaged through answerable contest.

# 13. Secondary-character state ledger

These characters do not require equal volume coverage, but each supplies a distinct control case that later synthesis should preserve.

## CS-SEC-MIU -- Miura Yumiko

**Early:** status dominance and coercive friendship language are real, but Yui's choice to preserve the friendship prevents an easy fake-popular-girl verdict.

**Kyoto:** Miura becomes one of the best social observers in the group. She notices Yui's increasing assertiveness, understands Ebina's adaptation, and says directly that she likes the current ordinary happiness.

**V10:** desire becomes an acceptable reason. Miura admits wanting closeness, chooses a course partly for present relational opportunity, crosses a line with Yukino, and repairs through apology.

**Source route:** V01 section 5.8; V07-E023-E029; V10 section 5.5.

**Terminal use:** control case for bluntness without lack of subtlety and for desire that is neither pure nor invalid.

## CS-SEC-TOT -- Totsuka Saika

Totsuka begins as direct warmth Hachiman can accept because it does not activate his ordinary romantic threat model. Across the series he becomes increasingly legible as an autonomous friend with leadership, athletic future, uncertainty, and capacity to help Hachiman.

The major correction is from aestheticized safe object to reciprocal friend.

**Source route:** V01 section 5.6; V02 section 5.7; V03 section 5.4; V10 section 5.6; V09-E017.

## CS-SEC-ZAI -- Zaimokuza Yoshiteru

Zaimokuza is a long-running control against essence-based authenticity. His theatrical persona is constructed, his skill is limited, his career fantasies wander, and he remains ridiculous. Yet he repeatedly returns to writing.

His state is therefore best defined by durable practice rather than certification.

**Source route:** V01-E09-E10; V3-L22-L23; V10.5 section 5.5.

## CS-SEC-EBI -- Ebina Hina

Kyoto reveals the exaggerated fujoshi persona as a social adaptation rather than mere comic noise. Ebina wants to preserve a group and avoid direct romantic legibility, but transfers the cost of boundary enforcement to others.

Her claim that she neither understands nor wants to be understood remains a defensive self-theory, not objective ontology. Her paradox is that she values the current place enough to dislike the version of herself that manipulates its form.

By V14.5 she is part of the ordinary wider social field rather than an unresolved crisis object.

**Source route:** V07-E024-E026, E073-E078; V14_5-E055-E056.

## CS-SEC-KAW -- Kawasaki Saki

Kawasaki is an early demonstration that appearance is causally misleading. Her exhaustion, secrecy, and rule-breaking arise from family burden and future ambition rather than frivolity.

Her flaw is unilateral care: protecting family by excluding them from the decision while her disappearance becomes a burden of its own.

**Source route:** V02-L18-L19 and section 5.5.

**Terminal use:** early control case for care that becomes unanswerable when beneficiaries cannot participate.

## CS-SEC-RUM -- Tsurumi Rumi

Rumi is not a device for proving Hachiman correct or incorrect. She understands exclusion mechanisms, carries shame about her own participation, wants release from hierarchy more than generic popularity, and acts decisively under pressure.

Volume 9 deliberately gives her a life beyond Hachiman's moral trial. Her later smile neither absolves nor condemns the old intervention.

**Source route:** V04 section 5.5; V09-E013-E014, E025-E026 and section 5.7.

## CS-SEC-SAG -- Sagami Minami

Sagami remains vain, status-sensitive, defensive, and capable of blame shifting. V2 nevertheless rejects a static-villain model. Across the cultural-festival and sports-festival material she accepts repeated public responsibility, asks for cooperation, continues after social protection weakens, admits she is trying, wants effort recognized, and remains for post-event work.

This is behavioral development without moral purification.

**Source route:** V06-L03-L06 and cultural-festival sequence; V06.5 section 5.4.

## CS-SEC-MEG -- Shiromeguri Meguri

Meguri is the clearest early control case for distributed leadership. She openly lacks Yukino's technical dominance yet creates a system in which people want to help, effort is noticed precisely, and delegation does not erase office-holder authority.

Her recognition of Sagami matters because she says Sagami is trying, not that Sagami is excellent.

**Source route:** V06-L13 and section 5.6; V06.5 section 5.5.

## CS-SEC-TOB -- Tobe Kakeru

Tobe's shallow register does not make his desire shallow. He explicitly says this romantic pursuit is different from previous casual relationships and that rejection would hurt.

The Kyoto intervention preserves his long-term agency but steals the specific speech he intended to make. This distinction is central to the series' intervention ethics.

**Source route:** V07-E010-E011, E047-E058; V06.5 section 5.7.

## CS-SEC-TAM -- Tamanawa

Tamanawa is primarily an institutional/social-role figure rather than a deep psychological subject. The satire of jargon and process remains real, but the V9 reading preserves his institutional function: he represents a mode in which language can diffuse ownership while still organizing real participants and constraints.

**Source route:** V09 section 5.8 and social-system analysis.

# 14. Cross-character state matrix

## 14.1 Relationship to desire

| Character | Early tendency | Mature tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Hachiman | translate desire into principle, debt, duty, or withdrawal | state first-person desire while allowing doubt afterward |
| Yukino | make desire speakable through problem/role/competence | act under uncertainty and originate direct requests |
| Yui | use atmosphere, analogy, and deniable direction | state selfish desire and remain after receiving a non-matching answer |
| Iroha | use pretext and performance openly | continue using pretext while becoming explicit that it is pretext |
| Haruno | expose others' hidden desires while withholding her own positive request | reveal that the demand for authenticity is personally invested, without fully resolving her own future |
| Hayama | fulfill expected role and preserve group | claim some role ownership while admitting expectation saturation |
| Komachi | desire family continuity through comic engineering | accept continuity through changed forms |

## 14.2 Relationship to help

| Character | Problematic form | Stronger later form |
|---|---|---|
| Hachiman | unilateral solution, self-cost as cheap currency | disclosed motive, bounded help, reliance on collaborators, reciprocal exposure |
| Yukino | competence substitution | contingency, delegated work, recipient-authored help, chosen support |
| Yui | preservation that postpones decisive consequence | accompaniment after answer, practical relational maintenance |
| Iroha | dumping office work upward | owning office while still using support |
| Haruno | provocation from observational distance | occasional direct assistance to Yukino, though still guarded |
| Hayama | preserving group through indirect social management | accepting limits and refusing some proxy responsibility |
| Komachi | comic framing and nudging | inheriting office while making own decisions |

## 14.3 Relationship to social performance

The V2 corpus rejects performance=falsity across multiple characters.

- Yui's atmosphere sensitivity can coexist with sincere desire.
- Iroha's manipulation can coexist with care and real institutional ownership.
- Haruno's public polish can coexist with real family investment and loneliness.
- Hayama's expected role can be both imposed and partly self-owned.
- Ebina's persona can protect a sincere attachment to group life.
- Zaimokuza's theatrical identity can protect a real desire to write.

The mature distinction is therefore not real self versus performed self. It is:

> **Who owns the performance, what does it enable, what does it conceal, and can other people answer it?**

# 15. Major V1 -> V2 character revisions

## 15.1 REVISE -- Yukino as competence armor

Competence has defensive uses, but the early series does not reduce it to armor. It is also genuine talent, ethical conviction, pride, social language, and later a tool that can be redirected toward distributed support.

## 15.2 STRENGTHEN -- Yui as active agent

Yui is much more than mediator or atmosphere manager. She builds infrastructure, creates opportunities, asks questions, protects social channels, bears accountability, and eventually states desires that create conflict.

## 15.3 REJECT -- Haruno as omniscient oracle

Haruno is one of the best interpreters in the series and one of the most invested. Her insight quality does not elevate her into narrator authority. Later interiority makes this limitation stronger, not weaker.

## 15.4 REVISE -- Hayama as fake social ideal

Hayama is constrained by public expectation but not emptied by it. Some expected behavior is sincerely his. The important conflict is authored choice under saturation, not true self versus fake role.

## 15.5 REVISE -- Iroha as manipulator who later becomes sincere

The series never requires a clean conversion. Manipulation and sincerity coexist from the start and become more integrated as she owns her office and openly acknowledges pretexts.

## 15.6 REJECT -- Yui as passive self-sacrificing losing heroine

Yui avoids, accommodates, and sometimes displaces choice, but she also acts strategically, states desire, rejects self-erasure, asks for answers, and preserves an independent relationship with Yukino after romantic loss.

## 15.7 REVISE -- Yukino dependence as late revelation of incapacity

Yukino is highly capable throughout. The dependency problem concerns opaque burden displacement, identity, expectation, and authorship -- not inability to function.

## 15.8 STRENGTHEN -- Komachi as methodological control

Komachi repeatedly demonstrates secure reliance, epistemic humility grounded in long familiarity, low-coercion relational engineering, and finally institutional succession. She is structurally central to the series' distinction between closeness and incapacity.

## 15.9 REVISE -- secondary characters as static genre functions

Miura, Totsuka, Zaimokuza, Ebina, Kawasaki, Rumi, Sagami, Meguri, and Tobe each provide a distinct control case for the main themes. Later synthesis should not flatten them back into protagonist-support roles after V2 has established independent state evidence.

# 16. Character-state contradictions that must remain open

The following tensions are evidence, not problems to be edited away:

1. **Yukino is highly competent and can still rely opaquely on others.**
2. **Yui can preserve others' agency and can also preserve ambiguity for self-protective reasons.**
3. **Iroha can be sincere and manipulative in the same act.**
4. **Haruno can be diagnostically right and normatively overreaching.**
5. **Hayama can be burdened by a role and sincerely identify with parts of it.**
6. **Hachiman can perceive a social mechanism accurately and still behave badly because he overclaims what that perception authorizes.**
7. **Komachi can manipulate framing and remain comparatively low-coercion because she preserves destination choice.**
8. **Yukino's self-removal in V13 can be self-authored and still exceed her jurisdiction over Yui/Hachiman.**
9. **Yui's post-ending presence can be durable without proving romantic feeling disappeared.**
10. **The final Hachiman/Yukino relationship can be chosen and workable without being certified healthy in every future condition.**

These contradictions should be carried into the relationship, dependency/autonomy, social-role/performance, and final specialist syntheses.

# 18. Outgoing authority and next analytical route

## 18.1 Canonical character-state conclusions

The following propositions are now frozen for later synthesis unless new primary evidence requires revision:

1. Character growth in Oregairu is primarily procedural and relational rather than essence replacement.
2. Yukino's central arc is not independence -> dependence, but substitutionary competence -> authored interdependence.
3. Yui's central arc is not passivity -> confession, but atmosphere-sensitive relational labor -> owned desire that can survive a non-matching answer.
4. Iroha demonstrates that social performance, strategic motive, sincerity, and role ownership can coexist.
5. Haruno is a high-value but non-sovereign interpreter whose demand for authenticity is autobiographically invested.
6. Hayama demonstrates that social role can be both constraining and partly owned.
7. Hiratsuka's mature mentorship is question-preserving and procedural rather than doctrinal.
8. Komachi provides the cleanest recurring control case for autonomy that remains relational.
9. The post-ending state does not sanctify the chosen configuration; it tests whether character agency survives ordinary change.
10. Secondary characters retain independent evidentiary value and must not be reduced to functions of Hachiman's development.
