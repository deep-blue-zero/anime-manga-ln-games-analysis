# OREGAIRU V2 — Authenticity / `本物` Longitudinal Ledger

## 0. Purpose

This ledger consolidates the V2 reread's findings about **authenticity, falsity, social performance, sincerity, self-authorship, mixed motives, and the changing meaning of `本物`** across *Oregairu* from Volume 1 through Volume 14.5.

It is not a dictionary of every appearance of `本物` and `偽物`. It is a longitudinal argument-control document.

Its job is to prevent five recurrent analytical errors:

1. importing the Volume-14 ending backward into the early novels;
2. treating Hachiman's vocabulary as automatic authorial doctrine;
3. treating Haruno's diagnoses as privileged omniscience;
4. collapsing performance, indirectness, compromise, dependence, or mixed motive into falsity;
5. treating `本物` as a state that the story finally certifies rather than a desire whose governing procedure becomes more adequate over time.

The governing V2 method requires each authenticity claim to ask:

- **Who is speaking or thinking?**
- **What exactly is being classified as real, fake, sincere, performed, mistaken, or genuine?**
- **What evidence does that speaker possess?**
- **What emotional stake does the speaker have in the classification?**
- **Is the statement a self-description, other-description, narrator inference, social judgment, or textual fact?**
- **What later evidence preserves, strengthens, revises, downgrades, or rejects the claim?**
- **Does a claim about sincerity actually establish ethical adequacy, autonomy, truth, or durability?**

This ledger therefore treats authenticity as an evolving **problem of relation and interpretation**, not as a binary property.

---

# 1. Executive thesis

The V2 reread rejects the common compressed formulation:

> *Oregairu* begins with fake relationships and ends when Hachiman, Yukino, and Yui finally obtain a genuine one.

That formulation is too clean for the text.

The series begins **before `本物` becomes its mature governing vocabulary**, but the early books already undermine a simple authentic/fake binary. Zaimokuza's performed chuunibyou identity is obviously constructed yet still expresses real desire. Yui's belonging to Miura's group is socially negotiated, role-governed, and imperfect without therefore being unreal. Haruno's cultivated persona conceals and manipulates, but the text does not establish that everything beneath or within the persona is false. Hachiman himself performs "the loner," "the realist," "the person who does not expect anything," and "the expendable problem solver" until those performances become both protective strategies and partly owned identities.

The major conceptual transition occurs in Volumes 7–9.

By Volume 8 Hachiman can reject one important counterfeit mechanism:

> `ただ一方的に願望押しつけてたというか、勘違いしてただけで、それを本物とは呼ばない`

His point is negative before it is positive. A relationship or understanding created by **projecting one's wish onto another person and mistaking the projection for knowledge** cannot safely be called `本物`.

That insight does not yet tell him what the genuine is.

Volume 9 makes the decisive procedural change with:

> `俺は、本物が欲しい`

The declaration matters not because Hachiman has finally defined authenticity. He has not. It matters because he stops pretending that private analysis can replace a request. He names a desire he cannot solve alone and exposes it to Yukino and Yui's answers. The famous scene therefore changes the **method of knowing** before it supplies a semantic definition.

The post-Volume-9 material then keeps refusing premature certification.

- Volume 10 shows that post-`本物` Hachiman can still violate the answerability principle he has begun to learn.
- Volume 10.5 shows ordinary, enjoyable, strategically performed social life that cannot be reduced to either "fake" or "pure."
- Volume 11 explicitly states that reaching toward `本物` does not stop the protagonists from continuing to make mistakes.
- Volume 12 rejects the idea that pain, rupture, or severe honesty automatically proves authenticity.
- Volume 13 gives Hayama a competing model: something originating as a `紛い物` or `贋作` can acquire historically singular value that its compromised origin does not exhaust.
- Volume 14 converts the problem from abstract authenticity to concrete, asymmetric choice. The Hachiman/Yukino romance becomes explicit without erasing Yui, and Yui can remain uncertain whether the resulting relation is `本物` while being certain about her own love.
- The phrase `掛け替えのない、替えのきかない偽物` captures the late paradox better than any clean triumphalist formula: something may remain structurally compromised, impure, historically contingent, and yet become irreplaceable.
- Volume 14.5 goes further by not using `本物` or `偽物` at all. Hachiman instead recognizes that he may have been `神聖視` — sanctifying — the Service Club relationship itself.

The mature V2 thesis is therefore:

> **`本物` evolves from an over-demanding fantasy of relation uncontaminated by projection, role, compromise, or uncertainty into a demand for answerable, revisable, self-authored relation. The series never grants an external certificate that the protagonists now possess "the genuine." It ends by making that certificate less important than their capacity to choose, answer, revise, continue, and refuse to sanctify the form their relationship currently takes.**

A compact form:

> **The genuine is not the relation with no performance, selfishness, pain, dependence, or uncertainty. It is the relation in which those elements can increasingly be named, answered, negotiated, and revised without requiring any participant to disappear.**

Even that formulation should be treated as a V2 synthesis claim, not as a literal definition spoken by the novels.

# 2. Evidence and attribution protocol

## 2.1 Evidence classes

This ledger preserves the following distinctions.

### Textual fact

An event, utterance, lexical occurrence, structural feature, or directly represented behavior.

### Character self-description

What a character says about his or her own motive, identity, desire, fear, or relationship.

Sincere self-description can still be incomplete.

### Character diagnosis

What one character says another person or relationship is.

Examples include Haruno's diagnoses and Hachiman's classification of others' social behavior.

These require independent testing.

### Narrator observation

What Hachiman directly reports seeing or hearing.

Often strong, though selective.

### Narrator inference

What Hachiman concludes from observations.

His social inference is frequently excellent; his inference becomes less reliable when his own desirability, vulnerability, or need is at stake.

### Behavioral evidence

What a person repeatedly does, especially where behavior contradicts or complicates self-description.

### Analytical inference

A conclusion drawn from multiple evidence classes.

### Literary interpretation

A higher-order claim about the work's thematic or formal organization.

No literary interpretation should be silently converted into a fact about a character's conscious intent.

---

## 2.2 The speaker-attribution rule

The following are not interchangeable:

- Hachiman saying a relationship is false;
- Haruno saying a relationship is codependent or counterfeit;
- Hayama discussing a copy or imitation;
- Yui describing what she wants;
- Yukino describing her own choice;
- Hiratsuka offering Hachiman interpretive language;
- the narration showing a relationship continuing;
- the text using `本物`, `偽物`, `紛い物`, or `贋作`.

The project must not manufacture a single "Oregairu authenticity doctrine" by taking whichever speaker is most aphoristic and treating that voice as the author's final answer.

---

## 2.3 Literal vocabulary and neighboring vocabulary

The ledger tracks literal authenticity vocabulary where it becomes important:

- `本物`
- `偽物`
- `紛い物`
- `贋作`
- `神聖視`

It also tracks neighboring mechanisms even when those words are absent:

- sincerity and bluntness;
- performance and persona;
- expectation and projection;
- self-deception;
- idealization;
- role behavior;
- mixed motives;
- social pretexts;
- indirect requests;
- pain and proof;
- dependence and independence;
- romantic exclusivity;
- ordinary continuity;
- self-authored choice;
- answerability;
- revision.

Literal lexical continuity matters, but the conceptual history is broader than keyword frequency.

# 4. Stage A — Volumes 1–3: authenticity before `本物`

## 4.1 Do not make the early novels secretly about the later slogan

Checkpoint A correctly freezes Volumes 1–3 as an early Service Club formation stage. The trio is not yet a settled friendship, romance, dependency system, or "genuine relationship." It is an **emergent relational institution**.

Later vocabulary has seeds here, but the seeds are not identical to the mature concept.

The early Hachiman already possesses an anti-falsity ethic:

- he distrusts public sentiment;
- he notices face-saving;
- he is alert to status hierarchy;
- he identifies coercive friendship mechanisms;
- he sees when language and practical motive diverge;
- he dislikes expectations because he associates them with future disappointment;
- he preemptively interprets kindness downward so he will not mistake ordinary consideration for personal affection.

Some of this is perceptive.

Some is defensive.

The distinction is foundational.

---

## 4.2 Hachiman's early mistake: "I can avoid falsity by refusing positive interpretation"

Hachiman's early realism is not simply false cynicism.

He often sees things other people politely avoid naming.

His problem is that he treats **false-positive prevention** as if it were the same thing as truth.

When evidence might imply:

- affection;
- inclusion;
- personal significance;
- trust;
- attraction;
- or a future claim on him,

he prefers the interpretation least capable of exposing him to disappointment.

This creates an asymmetric epistemology:

> negative explanations are "realistic"; positive explanations require impossible proof.

That is already an authenticity problem before `本物` is named.

The later `本物` desire partly grows out of this early structure. Hachiman wants a relation so certain that he cannot possibly mistake kindness for affection or performance for commitment.

The V2 reread should therefore preserve two early truths at once:

1. Hachiman's suspicion of socially convenient stories is often justified.
2. His demand for certainty is already calibrated to protect him from being wrong in one specific, painful direction.

---

## 4.3 Zaimokuza: the fake/authentic binary breaks almost immediately

Volume 1's Zaimokuza material is disproportionately important for this ledger.

His chuunibyou identity is:

- theatrical;
- derivative;
- exaggerated;
- knowingly stylized;
- socially embarrassing;
- and obviously "performed."

Yet the performance carries:

- real creative desire;
- real vulnerability;
- real ambition;
- real pleasure;
- real fear of judgment.

The persona is not therefore a transparent lie covering a pure "real self."

It is a medium through which parts of the self become speakable.

This early case anticipates several later corrections:

- Iroha's persona can be strategic and still belong to her;
- Haruno's public style can manipulate without rendering every feeling counterfeit;
- Yui's atmosphere management can be sincere care and self-protective performance at once;
- Hachiman's loner persona can begin as defense and become part of how he actually inhabits the world.

The first V2 authenticity rule is therefore available long before `本物`:

> **Constructed does not mean unreal.**

---

## 4.4 Yui and Miura's group: imperfect sociality is not automatically fake

Yukino's early intervention into Yui's friendship problem does not solve coercive or uneven group dynamics by announcing:

> your popular-group friendship was fake all along.

Instead, Yui renegotiates her position while retaining multiple social worlds.

This matters because a simplistic later reading can make the Service Club the only authentic space and everything outside it mere performance.

The early text does not support that hierarchy.

Yui can be:

- constrained by atmosphere;
- worried about belonging;
- inclined to accommodate;
- genuinely fond of her friends;
- genuinely attached to the Service Club.

The presence of pressure does not erase affection.

The presence of performance does not erase belonging.

The presence of multiple loyalties does not make one loyalty counterfeit.

---

## 4.5 Haruno's early public persona

Volume 3 makes Haruno's cultivated presentation visible, but that evidence must remain narrow.

It supports:

- deliberate social styling;
- strategic warmth;
- control of atmosphere;
- ambiguity of motive;
- ability to make intimacy itself into pressure.

It does **not** establish:

- that Haruno's entire personality is fake;
- that every warm act is manipulation;
- that she possesses no sincere attachment;
- that "seeing through masks" makes her an omniscient authenticity judge.

This becomes crucial later, because Haruno will repeatedly supply powerful terms for interpreting the trio.

Those terms deserve attention.

They do not deserve automatic authority.

---

## 4.6 Stage-A freeze

At the end of Volume 3:

- `本物` is not yet a mature governing concept;
- Hachiman's anti-expectation stance is psychologically and epistemically defensive as well as perceptive;
- social roles have already been shown capable of carrying sincere motive;
- the Service Club is meaningful without needing to be certified as uniquely authentic;
- Yui's external friendships are imperfect without being dismissed as fake;
- Haruno's persona is strategic without proving total falsity.

**Outgoing state:**

> Authenticity has not yet been defined. The text has already made a simple performance-versus-truth model inadequate.

---

# 5. Stage B — Volumes 4–6.5: sincerity, harsh truth, and self-sacrifice are not enough

## 5.1 The prestige of painful truth

The middle-early volumes deepen Hachiman's association between truth and cost.

His methods repeatedly succeed because he is willing to say or embody what others would rather avoid.

This gives his anti-falsity ethic real force.

He can:

- expose incentive structures;
- accept reputational damage;
- break socially convenient narratives;
- produce results other participants are too conflict-averse to produce.

It is tempting to treat this as proof that Hachiman possesses the "genuine" method before he possesses the vocabulary.

Checkpoint B rejects that shortcut.

---

## 5.2 Cost-bearing is not authenticity certification

Hachiman's self-sacrificial interventions are often sincere.

He may genuinely:

- want the practical problem solved;
- prefer to bear cost himself;
- believe his reputation is expendable;
- think others will be protected if he becomes the villain.

But sincere motive does not answer:

- whether the affected person authorized the method;
- whether the intervention preserves the person's agency;
- whether the solution creates future relational damage;
- whether Hachiman's self-concept makes him underestimate the value of his own well-being;
- whether "I can bear it" is functioning as a way to avoid mutual vulnerability.

This distinction, established in the intervention ledger, constrains the authenticity ledger:

> **A sincere sacrifice can still be a bad relational method.**

Authenticity cannot function as retroactive ethical permission.

---

## 5.3 Tobe and the problem of "truth"

The cultural-festival / social-conflict material also demonstrates that exposing truth is not always equivalent to solving the underlying human problem.

A person's embarrassment, fear, or attachment does not become morally negligible because a sharper observer can name the structure accurately.

The V2 reread therefore refuses:

> bluntness = honesty = authenticity = goodness.

Hachiman's relationship to "truth" is strongest when it defeats euphemism.

It becomes dangerous when truth is imagined as valuable regardless of:

- ownership;
- timing;
- authority;
- cost distribution;
- and the affected person's capacity to answer.

---

## 5.4 Ordinary life already matters

The supplementary and interstitial material in this period matters because it prevents crisis scenes from monopolizing the authenticity question.

The Service Club is becoming real to its members through:

- repeated presence;
- jokes;
- food;
- shared labor;
- routine;
- mutual expectation;
- irritation;
- ordinary familiarity.

None of those is a philosophical declaration.

That is precisely why they matter.

The later story will repeatedly discover that a relationship's reality is not located only in what people say under maximum emotional pressure.

---

## 5.5 Stage-B freeze

By the end of the V04–V06.5 window:

- performance is still not equivalent to falsity;
- painful truth is not automatically morally superior to tact;
- sincere self-sacrifice is not automatically authentic care;
- the Service Club has accumulated real relational history before it has a theory of that history;
- Hachiman's desire for non-false relation is intensifying, but the later `本物` framework must not be imported backward as though already complete.

**Outgoing state:**

> The text has made "sincere" too weak a criterion. A motive can be sincere while its interpretation of the other person or its method of acting on that motive remains inadequate.

---

# 6. Stage C — Volumes 7–8: from anti-falsity to the first negative `本物` criterion

## 6.1 The Kyoto problem: social preservation can become relational evasion

The Kyoto material turns the authenticity problem acute.

Hachiman is asked to intervene in a situation where people want incompatible things while also wanting their group structure preserved.

This is exactly the kind of problem that tempts him toward a sacrificial shortcut:

- maintain the visible social arrangement;
- absorb the ugliness himself;
- prevent direct collision;
- call the outcome practical.

The result works in a narrow sense.

It also damages the Service Club.

Why?

Because Hachiman's method treats the people closest to him as observers of a decision rather than participants in deciding what kind of cost they are willing to bear.

The method preserves one surface while making another relation less speakable.

The series is therefore not saying that surface preservation is always fake.

It is saying that **surface preservation becomes dangerous when it prevents answerable relation among the people actually paying for it**.

---

## 6.2 Hachiman's intolerance of maintained appearances becomes morally ambiguous

By this stage Hachiman increasingly experiences certain polite or indirect arrangements as intolerable.

That impulse contains both insight and excess.

Insight:

- social language can conceal incompatible desire;
- "everyone is fine" can be a lie of convenience;
- group harmony can be maintained by making one person's actual wish unsayable;
- avoiding explicit choice can distribute hidden costs.

Excess:

- he is tempted to believe that if something is painful or indirect it is therefore less real;
- he still tries to solve authenticity through superior interpretation;
- he is prone to treating his own willingness to be hurt as epistemic evidence;
- he can confuse refusal of pleasant illusion with possession of truth.

---

## 6.3 Volume 8's decisive negative criterion

Volume 8 supplies the ledger's first major conceptual `本物` use:

> `ただ一方的に願望押しつけてたというか、勘違いしてただけで、それを本物とは呼ばない`

The formulation matters because of what it excludes.

It rejects:

- projection;
- unilateral wish;
- mistaken certainty;
- a story about another person that survives only because the other person is not allowed to answer.

This is a stronger criterion than "people should be brutally honest."

A person can be completely sincere about a projection and still be wrong.

The problem is not merely lying.

The problem is **mistaking one's own desire for knowledge of the other**.

That distinction becomes one of the V2 project's most important corrections.

---

## 6.4 What Volume 8 still does not establish

The negative criterion does **not** establish that `本物` means:

- complete transparency;
- no social performance;
- no selfishness;
- no dependence;
- no ordinary politeness;
- no indirectness;
- no compromise;
- no uncertainty;
- no competing desire;
- no pain;
- or romantic exclusivity.

Volume 8 tells Hachiman what one kind of false understanding looks like.

It does not yet give him a complete positive model.

---

## 6.5 Stage-C freeze

At the end of Volume 8:

> **`本物` is desired because projection and convenient misunderstanding have become intolerable, but the desired object remains underdefined.**

The first durable criterion is negative:

> **A relation cannot count as genuine merely because one participant has built a compelling interpretation of it. The other person must remain able to answer.**

That point leads directly into Volume 9.

---

# 7. Stage D — Volume 9: `俺は、本物が欲しい` and the turn from classification to request

## 7.1 Why the declaration is not a definition

The famous line:

> `俺は、本物が欲しい`

is often treated as if Hachiman finally states the series' answer.

The V2 reread rejects that treatment.

The scene is powerful precisely because he **cannot** define what he wants adequately.

The declaration is:

- desire ownership;
- vulnerability;
- an admission of insufficiency;
- a request made to other people;
- a surrender of some interpretive control.

That is already a transformation.

But it is not semantic closure.

---

## 7.2 From "I know what this is" to "I need you to answer me"

Before Volume 9, Hachiman repeatedly tries to solve relationships through private inference.

His characteristic movement is:

1. observe;
2. model the system;
3. infer what everyone really wants;
4. decide what outcome is feasible;
5. pay the cost himself;
6. avoid asking for reciprocal clarification if the answer might expose him.

The `本物` request interrupts that procedure.

The key change is not that he suddenly understands everyone.

It is that he admits his own model is insufficient.

He cannot obtain the desired relation by:

- reading more accurately;
- sacrificing more efficiently;
- becoming more cynical;
- or constructing a perfect test.

He has to speak.

And then other people have to answer.

---

## 7.3 Answerability as the procedural core

The strongest V2 formulation of Volume 9 is:

> **`本物` shifts from a classification problem to an answerability problem.**

A relationship becomes more adequate when:

- desire can be stated;
- another person's response is not predetermined;
- refusal is possible;
- misunderstanding can be corrected;
- the participants can revise what they thought the relationship was.

This does not eliminate role or performance.

It changes their authority.

A role can remain useful.

A pretext can remain necessary.

Indirect language can remain emotionally intelligent.

But none of those should be allowed to become a system for making the other person's answer unnecessary.

---

## 7.4 The declaration does not repair the past

Volume 9 does not retroactively make earlier interventions good.

It does not erase:

- Kyoto;
- election damage;
- accumulated mistrust;
- Yui's concealed romantic desire;
- Yukino's family pressures;
- Hachiman's tendency to preemptively interpret rejection;
- the triangle's asymmetry.

The scene reopens a relation.

It does not purify it.

---

## 7.5 Why the remainder of Volume 9 matters

The `本物` scene does not end the book.

That formal fact matters.

The characters must return to:

- work;
- planning;
- small conversations;
- logistical cooperation;
- hesitation;
- incomplete understanding.

The narrative thereby refuses a conversion-scene model.

Hachiman has not crossed a metaphysical threshold after which every act is genuine.

He has made a different kind of request.

Now the method has to survive ordinary life.

---

## 7.6 The chronology-routed Christmas coda

The previously omitted Volume-6.5 limited-edition Christmas drama CD is correctly admitted only in its chronology-valid post-Volume-9 position.

Its value for this ledger is narrow but important.

It shows:

- ordinary social warmth after the Christmas repair;
- continued awkwardness;
- playful role behavior;
- no solved triangle;
- no complete semantic definition of `本物`;
- the practical usefulness of the post-V9 answerability shift.

The coda therefore **STRENGTHENS** Checkpoint C without rewriting it.

The ledger must not treat package-level thematic language as literal first-person interior monologue by Yukino or Yui.

---

## 7.7 Stage-D freeze

At the end of the Volume-9 state:

- `本物` is explicitly desired;
- projection has been rejected as sufficient knowledge;
- Hachiman has made desire answerable rather than keeping it private;
- the trio has reopened the possibility of relation;
- no one has defined a pure final state;
- ordinary practice becomes the next test.

**Outgoing state:**

> **The method becomes clearer before the meaning does.**

---

# 8. Stage E — Volumes 10–11 and 10.5: post-`本物` life without purity

## 8.1 Volume 10: growth is real and incomplete

Volume 10 is important because it prevents the V9 declaration from becoming sentimental mythology.

Hachiman has changed.

He more often:

- asks for help;
- speaks requests;
- recognizes other people's limits;
- allows others to contribute;
- gives explicit reasons.

But he can still fall back into:

- privately deciding what answer is acceptable;
- structuring situations until someone produces that answer;
- treating insight as jurisdiction;
- avoiding a more vulnerable request.

The result is not:

> Hachiman learned nothing.

Nor is it:

> Hachiman is now genuine.

The stronger reading is:

> **The new rule exists, and he can still violate it.**

That is what growth looks like in the V2 reread: revised practice with relapse, not personality replacement.

---

## 8.2 Volume 10.5: ordinary life makes the purity model less plausible

Volume 10.5 contains no governing lexical use of `本物` or `偽物`.

That absence is analytically useful.

The characters:

- work;
- tease;
- manipulate socially;
- perform roles;
- enjoy themselves;
- mix professional and personal motives;
- use pretexts;
- show care without always naming it.

Iroha is particularly important.

Her presentation is:

- strategic;
- flirtatious;
- self-protective;
- socially intelligent;
- sometimes manipulative;
- often playful;
- also genuinely hers.

The text does not require an analyst to peel away "Iroha the persona" until a pure authentic Iroha appears.

The persona is part of her agency.

This reinforces the early Zaimokuza lesson at a more mature social level:

> **Performance can be owned identity rather than evidence of counterfeit selfhood.**

---

## 8.3 Volume 11: `本物` can keep being wrong

Volume 11 makes the anti-certification principle unusually explicit in its own framing:

> `未だ、彼の求める本物には手が届かず、本物はまちがい続ける。`

The important phrase is not only "has not reached."

It is the continuation of error.

Seeking `本物` does not grant:

- moral immunity;
- interpretive omniscience;
- correct romantic decisions;
- freedom from selfishness;
- freedom from social performance.

The desire itself must be lived through imperfect people.

That keeps `本物` from becoming a purity badge.

---

## 8.4 Valuable relation can become an excuse not to choose

By Volume 11 the Service Club is unquestionably valuable to all three.

That creates a new danger.

Earlier, the problem was that their bond could not be honestly spoken.

Now the bond's very value can become a reason to preserve ambiguity.

A meaningful relationship can be used to delay:

- romantic choice;
- family choice;
- self-authored futures;
- confrontation with non-equivalent desire.

This is a crucial late correction.

> **Something can be real and still be used evasively.**

Authenticity is therefore not a property that justifies preserving the relationship in whatever form currently exists.

---

## 8.5 Stage-E freeze

Checkpoint D's post-`本物` restructuring should be read as:

- the trio has a more answerable method;
- ordinary life has regained weight;
- performance persists;
- mixed motives persist;
- desire is increasingly visible;
- no one has solved the triangle;
- relation itself can become an object of idealization.

The next stage must ask whether independence, pain, or rupture can provide the missing certificate.

Volume 12 answers: not safely.

---

# 9. Stage F — Volume 12: pain is not proof

## 9.1 Diagnostic vocabulary arrives after the relationship already exists

Volume 12 gives the post-`本物` crisis sharper diagnostic language.

Dependence, autonomy, separation, and the possibility of unhealthy relational structure become harder to avoid.

This matters.

But the timing matters equally.

The diagnosis arrives **after** years of:

- chosen labor;
- friendship;
- mutual rescue;
- conflict;
- ordinary life;
- concealed desire;
- genuine development.

A late diagnostic term cannot erase that history merely by naming a mechanism.

---

## 9.2 The seductive authenticity test: "if it hurts, it must be true"

Hachiman has long associated pain with non-falsity.

Pain feels difficult to fake.

Cost feels evidentiary.

A choice that hurts all participants can therefore acquire moral prestige simply because it resists pleasant self-deception.

Volume 12 makes that logic untenable.

Pain can accompany:

- truth;
- avoidance;
- guilt;
- self-punishment;
- withdrawal;
- overcorrection;
- genuine separation;
- unnecessary separation.

Pain is evidence that something matters.

It is not a complete classifier of what should be done.

---

## 9.3 Independence is not authenticity certification

The same applies to independence.

A decision can look clean because it separates people.

That does not establish that it is more self-authored.

A person may "choose independently" because:

- she actually wants the separation;
- she believes separation is what maturity requires;
- she is trying to remove herself for someone else's benefit;
- she accepts another person's diagnosis;
- she wants to stop burdening others;
- she believes painful action is morally purer.

Those motives cannot be distinguished by the label "independence."

This point is routed forward to the dedicated Dependency / Autonomy Ledger.

For the present ledger the narrower conclusion is:

> **`本物` cannot be defined as maximal independence.**

---

## 9.4 Haruno's diagnostic force and limitation

Haruno is particularly important here.

She sees real things.

She is often better than the trio at naming:

- evasion;
- repetition;
- dependency mechanisms;
- idealization;
- social performance;
- emotional cowardice.

But V2 must preserve her position as a participant-observer.

Haruno:

- has history with Yukino;
- has stakes in Yukino's differentiation;
- has her own anger at family roles;
- often pressures people through diagnosis;
- can make a label performatively alter the relation it describes.

Her insight is therefore evidence.

It is not a verdict.

---

## 9.5 Stage-F freeze

Volume 12 removes several tempting shortcuts:

- pain does not prove authenticity;
- separation does not prove autonomy;
- diagnostic severity does not prove correctness;
- continuing together does not prove pathology;
- sincere self-removal does not automatically prove self-authorship.

**Outgoing state:**

> The authenticity problem can no longer be solved by choosing whichever option looks hardest, loneliest, or least comforting.

---

# 10. Stage G — Volume 13: counterfeit origins and historically singular value

## 10.1 Hayama supplies a rival model

Volume 13 matters because Hayama's authenticity language is not simply another version of Hachiman's.

The important vocabulary includes:

- `紛い物`
- `贋作`

Hayama's model opens a possibility Hachiman's earlier purity demand resists:

> something may begin as imitation, staging, substitution, or counterfeit and still acquire a history that makes it singular.

This does not abolish the distinction between original and copy.

It changes what follows from that distinction.

---

## 10.2 Origin does not determine total identity

The logic has broad application.

A relationship can begin because:

- a teacher forced students into a club;
- a request supplied an excuse to meet;
- a competition created repeated interaction;
- a false pretext kept people together;
- a staged event created opportunities for real labor;
- a role allowed feelings to be expressed indirectly.

None of that proves the later relation false.

A compromised origin can become the site of:

- real memory;
- chosen repetition;
- cost;
- affection;
- obligation;
- transformation.

The effect does not retroactively make the origin pure.

It makes origin insufficient as a total verdict.

---

## 10.3 The staged prom and real consequences

Volume 13's prom work is structurally useful for this reason.

A deliberately staged or instrumental mechanism can generate:

- actual coordination;
- real investment;
- institutional pressure;
- meaningful choices;
- new relationships;
- genuine consequences.

The project should therefore preserve:

> **artificial beginning ≠ unreal outcome.**

This is one of the strongest late-series attacks on a simplistic fake/genuine binary.

---

## 10.4 Historical singularity

Hayama's model introduces something the early Hachiman tends to undervalue:

**history can make relation singular.**

Singularity does not require:

- purity of origin;
- lack of performance;
- perfect understanding;
- exclusivity.

A relationship may become irreplaceable because of the particular sequence of:

- mistakes;
- revisions;
- memories;
- mutual effects;
- chosen returns.

This prepares Volume 14's paradoxical language.

---

## 10.5 Stage-G freeze

At the end of Volume 13:

> **Counterfeit is no longer an exhaustive judgment. A relation can retain structural impurity while becoming historically non-substitutable.**

That does not answer whether the protagonists should remain in their current configuration.

It does make "it began artificially" or "it contains performance" inadequate reasons to dismiss the relation as unreal.

---

# 11. Stage H — Volume 14: concrete choice, real loss, and the irreplaceable fake

## 11.1 Choice arrives where definition could not

Volume 14 finally forces decisions that the abstract authenticity discourse could defer.

The characters cannot preserve every possible future.

Hachiman's desire becomes concretely directed toward Yukino.

Yukino must answer.

Yui experiences real romantic loss.

The Service Club relation changes.

This is not the moment the story finally proves one relationship genuine and another fake.

It is the moment the story makes **non-equivalence** unavoidable.

---

## 11.2 Romance does not retroactively reveal the "true" relationship

A common route-reading error would be:

> Hachiman/Yukino was the genuine relation all along; Hachiman/Yui and the triad were therefore false or temporary.

The V2 relationship ledger rejects that.

Yui's relationship with Hachiman has:

- genuine affection;
- genuine attraction;
- ordinary intimacy;
- mutual influence;
- unrealized possibility;
- unequal romantic outcome.

Yui and Yukino have:

- genuine friendship;
- rivalry;
- guilt;
- accommodation;
- mutual dependence in ordinary practice;
- incompatible romantic desires.

The triad has:

- real history;
- real damage;
- real care;
- structural instability;
- continuation after romantic asymmetry becomes explicit.

Romantic selection is consequential.

It is not an authenticity sorting machine.

---

## 11.3 Yui's `そんなの、わかんない`

Yui's ability to answer, in effect, that she does not know whether the relation is `本物` while remaining certain about her own love is one of the most important late-series distinctions.

It separates:

- **first-person certainty of feeling**
from
- **third-person certainty of relational category**.

She knows she loves.

She does not claim omniscient knowledge of what the entire triadic relation "really is."

That is epistemically stronger than an imposed certificate.

---

## 11.4 `掛け替えのない、替えのきかない偽物`

The phrase:

> `掛け替えのない、替えのきかない偽物`

should be treated as a central authenticity paradox rather than an embarrassment that the final synthesis needs to resolve away.

It refuses the clean binary.

The relation may be "fake" under some structural criterion:

- born of imposed circumstances;
- maintained through pretexts;
- full of concealed motives;
- unable to satisfy every participant equally;
- shaped by role behavior;
- historically contingent.

And yet it can also be:

- irreplaceable;
- singular;
- deeply formative;
- something the characters refuse to discard.

This gives late `偽物` a very different function from early anti-falsity rhetoric.

"Fake" is no longer equivalent to "worthless."

---

## 11.5 The final romantic commitment is not external certification

Hachiman and Yukino choose one another.

That establishes:

- romantic direction;
- mutual answer;
- willingness to accept future burden;
- explicit non-equivalence relative to Yui.

It does **not** establish:

- complete understanding;
- permanent psychological health;
- immunity from dependence;
- freedom from performance;
- perfect communication;
- guaranteed durability.

The ending is stronger because it does not need those guarantees.

---

## 11.6 Pain is present but not made the proof

Yui's loss matters.

Hachiman and Yukino's choice imposes cost.

The trio cannot continue as though nothing has happened.

Yet the story does not say:

> because the choice hurts, it is genuine.

That reading would reproduce the Volume-12 error.

Pain is a consequence of incompatible desire.

The authenticity-relevant question is whether the characters can acknowledge that consequence without:

- erasing Yui;
- denying the romantic choice;
- pretending everyone's desire is equivalent;
- manufacturing a painless lie.

---

## 11.7 Stage-H freeze

At the end of the mainline:

- romantic choice is concrete;
- friendship continuity remains possible;
- `本物` is not externally certified;
- `偽物` can coexist with irreplaceability;
- pain is not the proof;
- purity is not the proof;
- exclusivity is not the proof;
- continued answerability matters more than semantic triumph.

**Outgoing state:**

> **The series reaches commitment without reaching an uncontestable authenticity label.**

---

# 12. Stage I — Volume 14.5: ordinary durability and the rejection of `神聖視`

## 12.1 Lexical absence is meaningful but not magical

The complete Volume 14.5 deep reading records zero occurrences of:

- `本物`
- `偽物`
- `依存`
- `共依存`

This does not mean the associated problems vanish.

It means ordinary continuation no longer requires constant explicit theorization through those terms.

The characters have to live.

---

## 12.2 Hachiman's terminal self-correction: `神聖視`

The late self-correction is crucial:

> `俺は俺たちという関係性に拘泥しすぎていたのかもしれない。`

and then:

> `いや、神聖視していたと言ってもいい。`

Hachiman recognizes that he may have treated "our relationship" as something sacred.

This is a direct threat to triumphalist readings of `本物`.

If the ending simply established:

> this form is finally the genuine one,

then sanctifying the form would be appropriate.

Volume 14.5 instead makes sanctification itself suspect.

The current relation has value.

It is not exempt from revision.

---

## 12.3 Anti-sanctification is not anti-commitment

Rejecting `神聖視` does not mean:

- the relationship is disposable;
- commitment is weak;
- nothing has been achieved;
- everyone should remain detached.

It means value does not require freezing form.

A durable relationship may survive because participants can let it change.

This is particularly important as:

- school roles change;
- Iroha and Komachi alter institutional continuity;
- Hachiman and Yukino are romantically non-equivalent to Yui;
- the Service Club can continue without reproducing its exact old configuration.

---

## 12.4 Ordinary life as durability evidence

Volume 14.5's ordinary scenes matter more than another philosophical declaration would.

They show relationships surviving:

- changed roles;
- embarrassment;
- routine;
- teasing;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- practical tasks;
- successor involvement.

This is evidence of durability.

It is not proof of eternal health.

The V2 project should prefer that modest claim.

---

## 12.5 Terminal freeze

The final mainline-plus-14.5 authenticity state is:

> **chosen but not certified; valuable but not sacred; historically singular but revisable; performed in places but not thereby false; uncertain in category but answerable in practice.**

That is a better terminal state than "they finally found the genuine."

---

# 13. Speaker-specific authenticity models

## 13.1 Hachiman — from purity demand to anti-certification

### Early model

Hachiman initially privileges:

- low expectation;
- suspicion;
- painful realism;
- nonparticipation;
- negative inference.

He often behaves as if a relation is safer and truer when it requires less hopeful interpretation.

### Middle model

He increasingly demands a relation not built on:

- projection;
- convenient misunderstanding;
- false preservation;
- one-sided interpretation.

This produces the negative Volume-8 criterion.

### `本物` request

Volume 9 turns the desire outward.

He admits that he cannot solve it by himself.

### Late model

He learns that:

- performance can be sincere;
- ordinary pretexts can carry real desire;
- pain proves less than he thought;
- independence proves less than he thought;
- a "fake" may be irreplaceable;
- even a valuable relation can be `神聖視`.

**Canonical Hachiman trajectory:**

> **anti-falsity -> purity demand -> projection critique -> answerable desire -> fallible practice -> anti-certification**

---

## 13.2 Yukino — authenticity as increasingly self-authored choice

Yukino should not be defined through Hachiman's model of her.

Her authenticity problem is strongly connected to authorship.

She has to distinguish:

- competence from identity;
- helping from substituting herself for others;
- family expectation from chosen obligation;
- "independence" as an ideal from the future she actually wants;
- self-removal from generosity;
- desire from the role of being the correct person.

Her most authenticity-relevant acts are not necessarily those in which she is bluntest.

They are the acts in which she can say:

- this is my will;
- this is my request;
- this is the consequence I choose;
- this is the relation I am willing to answer.

The V2 ledger therefore rejects:

> Yukino becomes authentic when she becomes independent.

A stronger formulation is:

> **Yukino becomes more self-authored when independence itself stops functioning as an externally supplied test of authenticity.**

---

## 13.3 Yui — sincerity with mixed motives

Yui is essential because she disproves the idea that mixed motive makes care fake.

Her behavior can contain at once:

- genuine concern for Yukino;
- genuine love for Hachiman;
- fear of losing the trio;
- jealousy;
- desire to preserve access;
- willingness to accommodate;
- reluctance to force a decisive answer;
- eventual willingness to ask for more.

None of those cancels the others.

Yui's late development is not purification into selfless friendship.

It is greater ownership of the fact that her kindness has desire inside it.

That makes her more, not less, legible.

Her late `そんなの、わかんない` is therefore important:

> she refuses a total category while maintaining certainty about her own feeling.

---

## 13.4 Haruno — authenticity as exposure, with diagnostic overreach risk

Haruno repeatedly values exposure.

She distrusts arrangements that seem too smooth.

She notices:

- imitation;
- dependency;
- role repetition;
- family scripting;
- pleasant evasion.

Her interventions often force hidden structure into the open.

This gives her exceptional thematic importance.

But Haruno's model can overprivilege:

- rupture;
- discomfort;
- diagnostic superiority;
- the exposure of ugliness.

She sometimes risks behaving as if a relationship that survives being wounded by insight is more real because of the wound.

Volume 12's anti-pain correction limits that model.

So does Volume 14.5's anti-sanctification.

Haruno sees real mechanisms.

She is not the court that certifies `本物`.

---

## 13.5 Hayama — authenticity through historical singularity

Hayama's V13 contribution is structurally distinct.

His `紛い物` / `贋作` language allows a copy or compromised thing to become singular through history.

This model is especially useful because it does not require origin purity.

It recognizes that value may arise from:

- repetition;
- investment;
- memory;
- accumulated consequence.

Hayama therefore supplies a late conceptual bridge between:

> "this was not pure in origin"

and

> "this has become irreplaceable."

---

## 13.6 Hiratsuka — language as approximation rather than certificate

Hiratsuka's adult role repeatedly pushes Hachiman away from totalizing formulas and toward language adequate enough to continue acting.

Her importance to the authenticity problem is not that she defines `本物` for him.

It is that she legitimizes:

- imperfect articulation;
- continued searching;
- desire that exceeds a single clean word;
- the obligation to speak even when language cannot guarantee perfect understanding.

That is compatible with the late anti-certification posture.

---

# 14. What `本物` is **not** allowed to mean in the V2 corpus

The following equivalences are rejected.

## 14.1 `本物` ≠ sincerity

A person can sincerely misunderstand another person.

A person can sincerely choose a harmful intervention.

A person can sincerely withdraw for reasons that are not fully self-authored.

Sincerity matters.

It is not enough.

---

## 14.2 `本物` ≠ bluntness

Yukino's harsh speech can be sincere.

Hachiman's sharp social diagnosis can be accurate.

Neither fact establishes that maximum bluntness is the most authentic relational mode.

Tact, indirectness, humor, and performance can all carry truthful relation.

---

## 14.3 `本物` ≠ absence of performance

This equivalence is broken by:

- Zaimokuza;
- Yui's atmosphere work;
- Haruno's layered presentation;
- Iroha;
- ordinary social life across the club.

The correct question is not:

> is this performed?

It is:

> **what does the performance express, conceal, permit, control, and allow others to answer?**

---

## 14.4 `本物` ≠ pain

Pain may accompany genuine choice.

It may also accompany:

- self-punishment;
- overcorrection;
- guilt;
- unnecessary rupture.

Volume 12 makes pain an inadequate standalone classifier.

---

## 14.5 `本物` ≠ independence

A relationship does not become fake because people rely on one another.

Nor does a choice become genuine merely because it maximizes separation.

The dedicated Dependency / Autonomy Ledger will adjudicate the more detailed forms of this distinction.

---

## 14.6 `本物` ≠ romantic exclusivity

Hachiman/Yukino's romantic selection is real and decisive.

It does not render:

- Hachiman/Yui;
- Yukino/Yui;
- the Service Club triad

retroactively counterfeit.

Different relations can be real in non-equivalent ways.

---

## 14.7 `本物` ≠ permanent form

Volume 14.5's `神聖視` correction is decisive.

A relationship does not prove its authenticity by remaining institutionally identical forever.

Succession and role change can be compatible with continuity.

---

## 14.8 `本物` ≠ complete mutual understanding

Volume 8 rejects unilateral projection.

Volume 9 requires answerability.

Neither produces telepathy.

The mature standard is not:

> understand the other perfectly.

It is closer to:

> **do not make your interpretation final when the other person can answer you.**

---

## 14.9 `偽物` ≠ worthless

Volume 13 and Volume 14 make this impossible to sustain.

A compromised or counterfeit structure may still become:

- singular;
- formative;
- irreplaceable.

This does not erase the compromised elements.

It prevents those elements from exhausting the judgment.

---

# 15. Cross-ledger reconciliation

## 15.1 With the Hachiman Epistemic / Narratorial Ledger

The two ledgers converge on one major result:

> Hachiman's growth is less "he finally understands people" than "he increasingly stops treating private inference as sufficient authority over relationship meaning."

Authenticity therefore has an epistemic component:

- projection is not knowledge;
- observation is not jurisdiction;
- inference should remain answerable.

---

## 15.2 With the Character-State Ledger

The character ledger establishes:

- social performance does not imply falsity;
- self-description can be sincere and incomplete;
- the endpoint is greater authorship under acknowledged incompleteness.

The authenticity ledger strengthens this.

The mature characters are not purified selves.

They are people who increasingly own:

- desire;
- role;
- contradiction;
- cost;
- choice.

---

## 15.3 With the Relationship-State Ledger

The relationship ledger establishes that romantic non-equivalence does not erase triadic history or friendship continuity.

The authenticity implication is direct:

> **A relationship's reality is not measured by whether it becomes the final romance.**

The triangle contains:

- genuine affection;
- unequal desire;
- evasion;
- competition;
- care;
- injury.

Those properties coexist.

---

## 15.4 With the Request / Intervention Ethics Ledger

Authenticity cannot authorize intervention.

Knowing that another person's stated position is incomplete does not give Hachiman, Haruno, Yukino, or anyone else unlimited right to decide for that person.

The mature cross-ledger rule is:

> **seeing through a performance is not the same thing as possessing authority over the performer.**

This is critical because authenticity discourse can otherwise become coercive:

> "I know what you really want, therefore I may override what you said."

Volume 8's projection critique and the intervention ledger jointly reject that move.

# 17. V1 → V2 authenticity revisions to carry forward

These are domain-level revision directions. The dedicated V1-to-V2 Revision Ledger will perform the formal claim-by-claim routing.

## 17.1 "Hachiman hates fake relationships"

**V1 tendency:** directionally useful but too broad.

**V2 status:** **REVISE**.

Hachiman does reject projection, convenient misrecognition, and preserved appearances that prevent answerable desire. But he also participates in:

- roles;
- pretexts;
- strategic presentation;
- socially useful fictions.

The mature distinction is not fake-looking versus sincere-looking.

It is whether the arrangement permits the people inside it to remain agents capable of answering and revising its meaning.

---

## 17.2 "`本物` means total honesty"

**V2 status:** **REJECT**.

The series repeatedly preserves:

- tact;
- role behavior;
- jokes;
- indirectness;
- socially strategic presentation.

The problem is not all concealment.

The problem is concealment or interpretation becoming a mechanism for making another person's answer unnecessary.

---

## 17.3 "`本物` means mutual complete understanding"

**V2 status:** **REJECT**.

Volume 8 criticizes mistaken projection precisely because complete private confidence can be false.

Volume 9's solution is not telepathy.

It is asking.

The late series continues to contain uncertainty.

---

## 17.4 "`本物` is Hachiman and Yukino's romance"

**V2 status:** **DOWNGRADE / REVISE**.

The romance is a major concrete answer to Hachiman and Yukino's desires.

It is not the entire semantic content of `本物`.

Treating it that way:

- erases Yui's genuine relationships;
- confuses romantic selection with authenticity certification;
- ignores V13's counterfeit-singularity model;
- ignores V14's paradox;
- conflicts with V14.5's `神聖視` correction.

---

## 17.5 "Haruno knows whether the trio is genuine"

**V2 status:** **DOWNGRADE**.

Haruno sees important mechanisms.

Her diagnoses must be tested against:

- first-person testimony;
- actual behavior;
- later ordinary life;
- her own stakes.

She is a counter-gaze, not an omniscient narrator.

---

## 17.6 "The ending proves the relationship is genuine"

**V2 status:** **REVISE**.

The ending proves more concrete things:

- Hachiman chooses Yukino;
- Yukino answers;
- Yui remains;
- the old equilibrium cannot remain unchanged;
- the relation continues into a changed form.

The novels deliberately preserve uncertainty about the total category.

That is not an analytical failure.

It is part of the answer.

# 18. Strong counterreadings and stress tests

A Phase-4 ledger should not merely state the preferred thesis. It should preserve serious alternatives for Phase 6 adversarial audit.

## 18.1 Counterreading: `本物` really is a purity ideal, and the ending simply compromises it

One could argue:

- Hachiman's V9 desire is intentionally impossible;
- later "fake" language demonstrates that the trio never achieves the ideal;
- V14 is therefore a pragmatic settlement beneath the standard he originally wanted.

This reading has textual force.

The V2 response should not deny it by definition.

Instead:

- preserve `本物` as an aspirational ideal;
- distinguish the ideal's early impossible form from the procedure the characters learn;
- ask whether the narrative endorses the impossible standard or educates Hachiman away from demanding certification.

Volume 14.5's anti-`神聖視` correction presently weighs strongly toward the latter.

**Status:** OPEN as a philosophical emphasis; not strong enough to overturn AH-028.

---

## 18.2 Counterreading: Hayama's counterfeit model proves the trio is not genuine

Hayama's language could be read as a concession:

> what they have is only a precious fake.

The V2 response:

- do not erase the negative word;
- do not turn it into secretly "real";
- also do not assume "fake" exhausts value once the text itself emphasizes irreplaceability.

The point is the paradox.

**Status:** PRESERVE paradox; reject binary forced resolution.

---

## 18.3 Counterreading: Yui's continuation is merely self-deception

Yui's willingness to remain could be interpreted as refusal to accept loss.

There is evidence of:

- hope;
- accommodation;
- desire to preserve proximity;
- difficulty letting go.

But a total self-deception reading underweights:

- her increasing ownership of desire;
- explicit awareness of asymmetry;
- ability to name what she wants;

# 22. Canonical Phase-4 authenticity thesis

> **Oregairu's authenticity problem is not solved by discovering an essence called `本物`. It is progressively restructured. Early Hachiman seeks protection from false hope through suspicion; Volume 8 identifies projection as counterfeit understanding; Volume 9 turns the desire for the genuine into an answerable request; the post-`本物` volumes demonstrate that performance, mixed motive, dependence, error, and ordinary social form persist; Volumes 12–14 reject pain, independence, origin purity, and romantic exclusivity as sufficient certificates; and Volume 14.5 finally warns against sanctifying the relationship itself. The mature achievement is therefore not certainty that the relation is genuine. It is a more self-authored, mutually answerable, historically singular, and revisable way of continuing without needing certainty to precede commitment.**

This thesis is **canonical for the Phase-4 authenticity domain**, subject to later Phase-6 adversarial Japanese-source audit.
