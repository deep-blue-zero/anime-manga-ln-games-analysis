# OREGAIRU V2 — Japanese Voice Longitudinal Ledger

## 0. Purpose

This is the eighth architecture-defined Phase-4 longitudinal ledger.

Its purpose is to consolidate the Japanese-language evidence that makes *Yahari Ore no Seishun Love Comedy wa Machigatteiru.* unusually resistant to flat thematic paraphrase. The V2 reread has repeatedly shown that important distinctions are carried not only by **what** characters say but by:

- first-person pronouns and self-reference;
- address terms and naming;
- politeness level and register;
- sentence-final particles;
- modality and conditionality;
- hedges and epistemic limitation;
- ellipsis, false starts, and unfinished clauses;
- benefactive and request grammar;
- imperatives and refusal strength;
- abstraction level;
- repeated lexical clusters;
- rhetorical questions;
- deliberate register collision;
- narrated versus spoken voice;
- attributed versus formally unattributed interiority;
- and, where source control permits, performed timing, hesitation, interruption, and prosodic form.

The ledger exists because several common interpretive shortcuts fail when tested against the Japanese text.

The series does **not** divide characters into those who speak directly and therefore truthfully versus those who speak indirectly and therefore falsely. It does not treat politeness as emotional distance, bluntness as authenticity, hesitation as weak agency, a stable honorific as proof that a relationship has not deepened, or a socially performed register as evidence of an empty self.

Instead, linguistic form becomes one of the main places where the series stages the problem of answerability.

A character may:

- know something but refuse to own it in first person;
- want something but convert desire into obligation grammar;
- state a firm decision in soft language;
- speak bluntly while still misunderstanding another person;
- use a pretext everyone recognizes as a pretext;
- ask a question that forces another person to expose herself while withholding an answer of one's own;
- or use an ordinary formula whose accumulated relational meaning is much larger than its literal semantic content.

The central V2 question is therefore not:

> **Which character finally learns to speak honestly?**

It is:

> **How does each character's existing voice become more or less capable of carrying owned desire, uncertainty, refusal, request, responsibility, and an answer another person can actually respond to?**

This ledger does **not** replace the forthcoming `OREGAIRU_V2_PRIMARY_SOURCE_LOCATOR_LEDGER.md`. Exact claim-to-EPUB/PDF recovery, comprehensive page/paragraph routing, and compact primary-source locator normalization belong there. Where the canonical deep readings already preserve exact Japanese and stable locators, representative examples are retained here; no missing locator is invented.

The post-Volume-9 Christmas drama-CD material is also carefully bounded. The Watari-authored prose rewrite governs exact quoted wording. The recording governs performed delivery where independently supported. The completed audit explicitly warns that prose and recording differ slightly and that the current pass did not generate a fresh verbatim Japanese ASR transcript. Audio-specific claims therefore remain limited to verified or historically cross-checked features such as timing, hesitation, interruption, and vocal warmth.

# 1. Executive thesis

The V2 reread supports the following series-level linguistic model:

> **Oregairu does not narrate growth as convergence toward one ideal style of transparent speech. Character voices remain recognizably themselves. Growth occurs when those voices become better able to mark what the speaker knows, what the speaker does not know, what the speaker wants, what belongs to someone else, what is being requested rather than inferred, and what remains open to answer. Linguistic maturity is therefore not maximal directness. It is increasingly owned, bounded, revisable, and answerable speech.**

That thesis develops in six broad stages.

## Stage A — V01–V03: language manufactures both distance and relation

The earliest volumes already establish the major voice architecture.

- Hachiman's `俺` voice collides registers: teenage slang, mock theory, literary allusion, otaku reference, parody formality, and sudden humiliation.
- Yukino's `私`, complete sentences, feminine endings, lexical precision, and premise correction make control audible.
- Yui's `あたし`, fillers, nicknames, casual particles, and unfinished clauses make social movement audible.
- Saika's use of `八幡` marks a kind of ordinary personal closeness Hachiman receives more easily because he considers it categorically safe.
- Haruno's `雪乃ちゃん`, teasing familiarity, and rapid calibration make practiced social fluency audible without proving that the warmth is false.

Naming is already an action. Yui does not merely report closeness; `ヒッキー` and `ゆきのん` propose it.

At the same time, Yukino's near-utterance `私のとも…` and self-correction to `うちの部員` shows that an institutional noun can be easier to say than an intimate relational one. The series therefore begins with a crucial asymmetry:

> **people may already be behaving relationally before they possess a comfortable linguistic category for the relation.**

## Stage B — V04–V06.5: categories, abstraction, and particularization become ethical

The middle-early volumes turn vocabulary into jurisdiction.

`みんな`, `一人`, `孤立`, `解決`, `解消`, `勝手に`, `知る`, `理解`, `わかる`, `頼る`, `承認`, and `虚像` do not function as interchangeable theme words. Their grammar repeatedly asks who authored a state and who is entitled to interpret it.

Two opposite linguistic motions become especially important.

Hachiman often moves **from person to abstraction**. He converts feeling into categories, accounting, systems language, and general rules.

Yui often moves **from abstraction to particular relation**. Her Volume-6 correction is exemplary:

> `誰かとかみんなとかじゃなくて……。あたしたちを頼って？`

The move from indefinite `誰か` / collective `みんな` to first-person plural `あたしたち` is not decorative. It particularizes responsibility and care.

Yukino's reliance grammar does something different again:

> `あなたを頼らせてもらっても、いいかしら`

The causative-benefactive `～させてもらう` makes reliance something she asks permission to do. Her characteristic feminine register remains intact; vulnerability enters **through** that register rather than by replacing it.

## Stage C — V07–V09: failed telepathy gives way to answerable speech

Kyoto and the election crisis reveal the danger of treating interpretive fluency as communicative sufficiency.

Hachiman's managerial lexicon—`ステークホルダー`, `効率`, `妥協`, `現状維持`, `主導権`, `解決`—makes intimacy administratively tractable. Yui's plain counterlanguage restores persons: `こういうの、もう、なしね`, `人の気持ち、もっと考えてよ`.

Volume 8 then permits `……わからん` to remain an answer rather than forcing certainty.

Volume 9 makes the linguistic correction explicit:

- `言わなくてもわかる` is fantasy.
- `言ったからわかる` is arrogance.

Words are necessary inputs but not guarantees. The important shift is that Hachiman becomes a requester:

> `一つ、依頼がしたい`

and then owns desire:

> `俺は、本物が欲しい`

The language does not prove he has defined `本物`. It proves he has stopped pretending the desire arrived impersonally from analysis.

## Stage D — V10–V11: simple language becomes harder to dismiss

Volume 10 tests `選択`, `選択肢`, `期待`, `本音`, `嫌い`, and `知りたい`. It also attacks a hidden-true-self model through Hayama's:

> `それも含めて、俺だよ`

Socially inherited behavior can be burdensome without being detachable from selfhood.

Volume 10.5 preserves Iroha's highly variable performed register and Yukino's `今は、まだわからない`: present-bounded uncertainty with future possibility.

Volume 11 then makes a decisive point about stylistic continuity.

Yui can say forceful things softly.

Yukino can make a vulnerable request while still sounding like Yukino:

> `……私の依頼、聞いてもらえるかしら`

No character has to acquire somebody else's register in order to become more agentic.

## Stage E — V12–V14: language becomes both a tool of diagnosis and an object of suspicion

The final movement repeatedly warns that compact terminology can become a shortcut.

Haruno's `共依存っていうのよ` performs diagnostic certainty. Hachiman later restricts the concept:

> `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。`

and Volume 14 goes further by rejecting a borrowed label as sufficient to decide lived feeling.

At the same time, the novels become formally more polyphonic. Yui, Iroha, Hayama, Haruno, and Yukino-coded or deliberately unattributed interludes interrupt Hachiman's explanatory monopoly.

The decisive lexical movement is from duty and execution toward first-person claim:

- `私にやらせて`
- `あたしは全部欲しい`
- `俺は関わり続けたい`
- `義務じゃなくて、意志の問題だ`
- `あなたの人生を、私にください`
- `あなたが好きよ。比企谷くん`

The series does not conclude that one clean sentence can contain the whole relation. It concludes that clean sentences can still matter inside a relation that exceeds them.

## Stage F — V14.5: ordinary formulae carry particular meaning

The post-ending material does not purify anyone's voice.

Hachiman still jokes, rationalizes, and hides warmth behind logistics.

Iroha still uses strategic pretext:

> `プレゼントは言い訳ですよ`

Yukino still uses modest, practical formulations:

> `一緒の方が頑張れると思うし`

and carefully marks self-owned initiative:

> `私が勝手にやっているだけだから`

The terminal institutional formula is conventional:

> `改めまして、比企谷部長。よろしくお願いします`

Its emotional force comes precisely from history. The formula does not exhaust the relation; it performs a real transfer of standing.

The late-series linguistic endpoint is therefore not perfect self-expression. It is this:

> **ordinary language becomes usable because the participants no longer require ordinary language to be either a total definition or a protective lie.**

# 2. Evidence protocol for Japanese voice

The ledger uses nine dimensions.

## 2.1 Self-reference and address

Track:

- `俺`, `私`, `あたし`, `あーし`, `わたし`;
- surname versus given name;
- honorifics and role titles;
- nicknames;
- kinship terms;
- stable address that accumulates changed meaning.

A change of address is evidence when it occurs. The absence of a change is also potentially meaningful, but it must not be treated mechanically. Yukino can become profoundly closer to Hachiman while continuing to say `比企谷くん`.

## 2.2 Register and politeness

Track whether speech is:

- colloquial;
- polite;
- overformal or mock-formal;
- institutionally professional;
- feminine-coded;
- deliberately cute;
- aggressively casual;
- or register-shifting within one exchange.

Politeness is not automatically distance. Colloquiality is not automatically intimacy.

## 2.3 Syntax, modality, and stance

High-value forms include:

- conditionals such as `なら`;
- `～てもいい` permissive modality;
- `と思う` stance marking;
- `けど` relational openness;
- desideratives such as `～たい`;
- `今は` / `まだ` temporal limitation;
- benefactive/request constructions;
- causative-benefactive `～させてもらう`;
- explicit `意志` claims;
- imperatives and negative imperatives.

The question is what kind of ownership the grammar assigns, not merely how strong an English translation sounds.

## 2.4 Hesitation and ellipsis

Fillers, pauses, false starts, and trailing clauses can mark:

- embarrassment;
- uncertainty;
- graduated commitment;
- leave-taking space for response;
- or avoidance.

They do not have one stable meaning.

## 2.5 Lexical fields

Repeated words are tracked longitudinally when meaning changes with context. Especially important are:

- `知る / わかる / 理解`;
- `期待`;
- `勝手に`;
- `普通`;
- `理由 / 言い訳 / 口実`;
- `依頼 / 頼る / 願い / 欲しい / 求める`;
- `選択 / 選択肢`;
- `ちゃんと`;
- `終わる / 終わらせる`;
- `本音`;
- `本物 / 偽物 / 欺瞞 / 紛い物 / 贋作`;
- `依存 / 共依存`;
- `義務 / 意志`;
- `歪む`;
- `一緒`;
- `神聖視 / 拘泥 / 完璧 / まちがい続ける`.

No lexical item is assigned one moral valence for the entire series.

## 2.6 Speech act

Distinguish:

- assertion;
- request;
- refusal;
- permission;
- invitation;
- apology;
- question;
- diagnosis;
- self-description;
- accusation;
- promise;
- formulaic greeting;
- joke;
- and indirect pretext.

A question can be coercive. A soft request can be forceful. A formula can be intimate.

## 2.7 Narration versus spoken voice

Hachiman's internal prose is often far more articulate than his face-to-face speech. His narratorial fluency therefore cannot be treated as equivalent to interpersonal communicative capacity.

Conversely, a spoken sentence may look simple precisely because the speaker has stopped using narration or abstraction to protect herself.

## 2.8 Attribution state

Later interludes require explicit classification:

- explicitly attributed first-person voice;
- strongly context-coded but formally unattributed voice;
- multi-character formal resonance;
- Hachiman narration;
- external character interpretation.

Do not turn a contextually likely attribution into textual fact.

## 2.9 Audio-performance evidence

Where an audio witness exists, distinguish:

- exact words;
- timing;
- pause;
- interruption;
- emphasis;
- warmth;
- performed embarrassment;
- and prose-only interior narration.

For the post-V9 Christmas coda, exact quotation remains prose-grounded. The earlier direct-listening note about conspicuous pauses in the gift exchange is retained only as historical auditory cross-check.

# 3. Series-level linguistic architecture

## 3.1 Growth is not voice homogenization

The most important series-level finding is negative:

> **maturity does not make everyone sound the same.**

Hachiman does not become Yui.

Yukino does not abandon controlled feminine syntax.

Yui does not become rhetorically severe.

Iroha does not stop performing.

Haruno does not lose ambiguity.

Hayama does not become aggressively confessional.

The meaningful changes occur inside persistent voice identities.

## 3.2 Directness and force are separate variables

Yui is the clearest control.

Her speech can be hedged, colloquial, and soft while carrying decisive content. By Volume 11, `三人で、行きたいの……` and `あたしは全部ほしい` are not weak propositions.

Yukino provides the inverse control. Her syntax may be crisp and controlled while the full emotional proposition remains unspoken.

Therefore:

> **surface directness is not a reliable scalar measure of agency.**

## 3.3 Indirectness is ethically graded

The series distinguishes:

- protective indirection that preserves answerability;
- mutually legible pretext that lowers expressive cost;
- defensive euphemism that hides the speaker's stake;
- and indirect architecture that substitutes for another person's choice.

The same broad category—“not saying it directly”—can therefore describe very different ethics.

## 3.4 Words are necessary but non-sovereign

Volume 9 supplies the governing communication pair:

> unspoken understanding cannot be assumed;

but:

> spoken language does not guarantee understanding.

Volume 14 preserves the same structure. Yui does not claim speech will perfectly transmit. She demands the effort to speak and answers that she will make the effort to understand.

## 3.5 Abstraction can illuminate and conceal simultaneously

Hachiman's systems language is frequently accurate. It is also frequently protective.

Haruno's compact diagnoses can reveal a mechanism while overclaiming moral authority.

Tamanawa's process language can name legitimate collaborative ideals while detaching process from decision.

The V2 rule is therefore:

> **technical or abstract vocabulary must be evaluated proposition by proposition, not psychoanalyzed away merely because it is abstract.**

## 3.6 Ordinary formulae can become highly particular

`また明日`, `よろしくお願いします`, `好き`, `知ってる`, and ordinary school-day or institutional language become powerful because of accumulated history.

The series does not need increasingly ornate language to mark increasing intimacy. In several of its strongest scenes, the movement is in the opposite direction.

## 3.7 Lexical meaning is relationally contextual

`勝手に` can condemn unilateral projection in one scene and mark self-owned initiative in another.

`普通` can reassure, normalize, or impose a script.

`終わらせる` can mean completing an event, ending a club form, stopping ambiguity, or destroying a desired relation.

`言い訳` can mean evasive rationalization or mutually transparent pretext.

A lexical ledger must therefore preserve **speaker + target + context + speech act**, not merely count occurrences.

## 3.8 Naming is action

Yui's nicknames, Saika's `八幡`, Haruno's `雪乃ちゃん`, Iroha's `先輩`, Yukino's stable `比企谷くん`, and Hachiman's final `比企谷部長` all do social work.

Names can:

- propose closeness;
- preserve hierarchy;
- invoke family role;
- create junior/senior framing;
- or transfer institutional standing.

They are not reducible to a closeness meter.

# 4. Hikigaya Hachiman — narration, abstraction, and owned desire

## 4.1 Baseline voice: `俺` and register collision

Hachiman's first-person `俺` voice is one of the series' central formal engines.

It moves rapidly among:

- ordinary adolescent colloquial speech;
- mock-academic theory;
- management vocabulary;
- game and technology metaphors;
- literary allusion;
- anime/manga and otaku reference;
- overformal parody;
- legalistic or contractual phrasing;
- and sudden remembered humiliation.

The velocity of those shifts is itself characterization.

But V2 rejects the easy claim that every joke or elaborate metaphor is a defense. Watari uses the register for comedy, genre texture, reader alignment, and genuine analysis too. Comic camouflage becomes a stronger claim only where the joke arrives at a personally threatening inference and functionally displaces it.

## 4.2 Internal fluency versus spoken difficulty

Early Hachiman can produce intricate internal models while becoming hesitant, abbreviated, or evasive in direct speech.

That gap matters throughout the series:

> **he is often better at explaining people to himself than making himself answerable to people.**

The late arc does not require him to lose interpretive intelligence. It requires him to stop treating private interpretation as a substitute for an utterance another person can answer.

## 4.3 Systems and accounting language

Volumes 2–7 repeatedly give Hachiman technical relational vocabulary:

- `カテゴリ`;
- `コミュニケーション`;
- `シチュエーション`;
- `リセット`;
- `デリート`;
- `チャラ`;
- `差し引きゼロ`;
- `ステークホルダー`;
- `効率`;
- `妥協`;
- `現状維持`;
- `主導権`.

The language performs two functions at once.

It often sees real structure.

It also makes irreversible feeling look like something that can be reset, balanced, administered, or optimized.

The linguistic danger is not intelligence. It is **conversion of a provisional model into jurisdiction**.

## 4.4 Defensive modality: desire rewritten as obligation

Hachiman repeatedly accepts a desired action while changing its grammatical reason.

The clean early control is Volume 7.5:

Yui:

> `また、一緒に遊ぼ？`

Hachiman:

> `罰ならしょうがねぇな`

He converts **want** into **must / cannot be helped**.

Similar structures recur through duty, requests, promises, responsibility, work, and contracts. Desire becomes easier to tolerate when it can be narrated as an externally legitimate obligation.

This is why Volume 14's correction matters:

> `義務じゃなくて、意志の問題だ`

The sentence does not abolish Hachiman's preference for procedural language. It marks the point at which he explicitly refuses to let duty carry the motive for him.

## 4.5 `勝手に`: from projection to self-owned risk

`勝手に` is one of Hachiman's most important longitudinal markers.

Volume 5:

> `勝手に期待して勝手に理想を押しつけて勝手に理解した気になって、そして勝手に失望する。`

The repetition identifies an authorship error. He conducted expectation, idealization, pseudo-understanding, and disappointment unilaterally.

Later volumes broaden the term. Hachiman can act `勝手に` as a way of saying that the action is **his** responsibility and not an order to Yukino. But the word remains ethically double-edged: acting on one's own initiative can still mean acting without another person's consent.

V14.5 supplies a positive contrast through Yukino's use of the same field for a gift that imposes no reciprocal debt.

The mature lesson is contextual rather than lexical:

> **self-owned initiative is not the same thing as unilateral authority over another person's future.**

## 4.6 `わからない`: from shield to discipline

Early uncertainty often motivates withdrawal or private inference.

Volume 8's `……わからん` is therefore small but important. Hachiman lets uncertainty remain shared.

Volume 9 then reframes understanding as process rather than possession.

By Volume 14, Hachiman can choose and still answer Hiratsuka:

> `どうですかね。わからないですけど`

and:

> `だから、ずっと、疑い続けます。`

Not knowing no longer automatically blocks commitment.

## 4.7 Desire vocabulary: `欲しい`, `求める`, `関わり続けたい`

Volume 9's `俺は、本物が欲しい` is linguistically decisive because of `欲しい`, not because the noun has become semantically settled.

The speaker owns desire.

The final movement sharpens that ownership:

> `俺は関わり続けたいと、思ってる。`

The `と思ってる` does not weaken the statement into indecision. It marks it as his present stance rather than a universal law.

## 4.8 Plain language is emotionally dangerous

Late Hachiman often becomes most embarrassed when the available sentence is ordinary.

He mocks the promise reason `いつか、助けるって約束したから` as clichéd and intellectually unimpressive.

He resists Yui's demand for a simple statement because simple language gives him fewer places to hide qualification.

The bridge scene's emotionally central propositions are correspondingly plain beneath his abrasive metaphors:

- he had no other way to stay involved;
- if he lets go he may never regain the relation;
- he wants to remain involved.

The continuing contract/economic metaphors do not invalidate those propositions. They are Hachiman's familiar transport mechanism for saying them.

## 4.9 Post-ending control: style persists after growth

Volume 14.5 is essential because it prevents a false stylistic teleology.

Hachiman still uses:

- self-mockery;
- bureaucratic and economic reasoning;
- game/media reference;
- mock legalism;
- recursive rationalization;
- abrupt colloquial particles.

Yet his behavior and some spoken propositions are more direct.

The correct V2 conclusion is therefore:

> **Hachiman grows from abstraction-as-jurisdiction toward abstraction plus owned first-person desire, not from complicated speech toward a permanently simple voice.**# 5. Yukinoshita Yukino — controlled femininity, precision, and increasingly speakable agency

## 5.1 Baseline register: `私`, complete syntax, and controlled feminine endings

Yukino's early voice is unusually stable at the level of surface register. She uses `私`, tends toward complete syntax, and repeatedly uses feminine sentence endings such as `〜わ`, `〜かしら`, and `〜もの`. Her speech is precise, corrective, and often organized around the proposition that the other speaker has framed the premise incorrectly.

That control is important, but it must not be misread as either emotional absence or perfect self-knowledge. The series repeatedly lets bodily reaction, hesitation, lexical correction, or an unfinished phrase exceed the neatness of the sentence that finally emerges.

The Volume 1 near-slip from an intimate label toward the safer institutional phrase `うちの部員` is an early model. What she can classify institutionally is easier to articulate than what she can name relationally.

## 5.2 Precision is a mode of relation, not merely distance

Yukino's corrections often sound adversarial because they are exact. But exactness is also one of her characteristic ways of taking another person's claim seriously.

She distinguishes:

- what someone actually asked from what Hachiman infers;
- what she wants from what others say she should want;
- what she knows from what she cannot yet know;
- what help she permits from what she wants to do herself.

The late series therefore does not replace her precision with emotional spontaneity. It makes precision carry more first-person ownership.

## 5.3 Requests soften without becoming vague

A recurring Yukino pattern is that personal requests produce a slight grammatical softening while preserving semantic specificity.

Examples frozen by the volume readings include:

- `手伝ってもらえると助かる、のだけれど……`
- `あなたを頼らせてもらっても、いいかしら`
- `……私の依頼、聞いてもらえるかしら`

The important features are not simply politeness. These utterances leave the addressee room to answer. The request remains bounded and attributable to Yukino rather than disguised as objective necessity.

This becomes especially important in Volume 12, where:

> `私の依頼はひとつだけ`

limits the requested act, and:

> `お願い。私にやらせて`

makes non-interference itself the object of a request.

The V2 implication is that Yukino's autonomy is partly audible as **better specification of what she is and is not asking others to do**.

## 5.4 `知らない`, `知っている`, and `わからない`

Yukino's epistemic vocabulary also develops without a triumphalist endpoint.

Volume 6 juxtaposes:

- `あなたのことなんて知らなかったもの`
- `でも、今はあなたを知っている`

The contrast matters because knowledge is presented as historically revisable. It is not an essence she suddenly possesses.

Later, `わからない` and `まだわからない` become increasingly legitimate statements. Volume 10.5's:

> `今は、まだわからない`

is especially important because `今は` and `まだ` turn uncertainty into a temporal state rather than a defect. She can own the present limit without making it permanent.

That grammar anticipates the mature series-wide rejection of certainty as a prerequisite for commitment.

## 5.5 `私の意志`, `私の気持ち`, `私の依頼`, `私の願い`

The strongest longitudinal Yukino pattern is the increasing density and force of first-person possessive constructions around agency.

Key forms include:

- `これは私の意志よ`
- `……私のこと`
- `……私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで`
- `……私の依頼`
- `これが私の願い`

The repeated `私の` matters because so much of Yukino's conflict concerns others interpreting, designing, protecting, or diagnosing her life. First-person possession is not proof that the resulting choice is wise. It is evidence that the choice is being linguistically claimed as hers.

This is why the Dependency / Autonomy Ledger's distinction must carry into linguistic analysis: **self-authored grammar is evidence of authorship, not an automatic certificate of ethical adequacy**.

## 5.6 Composure can break without revealing a hidden 'true' voice

Volume 11 gives some of the most important breaks in Yukino's control:

> `……私のこと`

and:

> `……私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで`

The ellipsis, fragment, and direct possessive language make the cost of speech audible.

But the correct interpretation is not that the composed Yukino was fake and this is the real Yukino underneath. Her controlled register remains hers. The breakdown matters because a normally organized voice must work harder to carry something it cannot fully compress.

The series' anti-hidden-self logic, reinforced elsewhere by Hayama's `それも含めて、俺だよ`, supports the same conclusion here.

## 5.7 Family register and boundary enforcement

Yukino's family scenes show that gentle or conventional syntax can carry coercive force.

The early `そう、ね。普通はそうよね` and later speech from Yukino's mother demonstrate why this ledger cannot equate harsh wording with stronger social power. Polite, soft, or normatively reasonable language can delimit the available answer more effectively than overt hostility.

Conversely, Yukino's own `さようなら` can function as a register boundary: a formally ordinary phrase acquires force because of where and how it is used.

## 5.8 Volume 14: ordinary syntax bears extraordinary commitment

Late Yukino does not receive an ornate final idiolect. Her most decisive romantic claim is syntactically ordinary:

> `あなたが好きよ。比企谷くん`

That ordinariness is aesthetically important. The series does not need a specialized philosophical vocabulary to certify the claim. The familiar `私`-centered, feminine, controlled voice can now carry it.

The bridge sequence likewise preserves her characteristic pressure for adequate articulation:

> `もっと他に言うことあるでしょう`

and then her own answerability:

> `私はちゃんと言うわ`

The result is not register collapse but **expanded carrying capacity**.

## 5.9 Volume 14.5: agency remains ordinary, playful, and role-compatible

The post-ending material prevents Yukino's romantic declaration from becoming a permanent solemn register.

She can:

- make ordinary future assumptions;
- say `一緒の方が頑張れると思うし`;
- own a gift with `私が勝手にやっているだけだから`, explicitly releasing reciprocal obligation;
- participate in the joking `ゆきのん` self-introduction under group pressure and embarrassment.

The last case is especially useful. A nickname originally created by Yui can become material for shared play without becoming Yukino's essential identity. Linguistic performance is inhabitable precisely because it can be temporary and knowingly staged.

### Yukino freeze

> **Yukino's linguistic growth is not a movement from cold precision to warm spontaneity. It is the increasing ability of a precise, controlled, recognizably feminine voice to say what belongs to her: what she knows, what she does not know, what she asks, what she refuses, what she wants, and what she is prepared to answer for.**

# 6. Yuigahama Yui — soft colloquialism, relational naming, and firm desire without register hardening

## 6.1 Baseline: `あたし`, fillers, contractions, and unfinished syntax

Yui's speech is conversationally soft from the beginning. The deep readings repeatedly note:

- `あたし`;
- casual contractions;
- fillers such as `あ`, `や`, and `その`;
- self-correction;
- trailing clauses;
- laughter used to manage exposure;
- particles and ellipses that leave interactional space.

This style makes her easy to underestimate if analytical confidence is unconsciously mapped onto syntactic firmness.

The V2 reread rejects that mapping.

## 6.2 Naming is one of Yui's earliest forms of agency

Yui actively makes relationships through names.

She gives Hachiman `ヒッキー`. She moves Yukino from `雪ノ下さん` toward `ゆきのん`. These are not trivial verbal decorations. They are proposals about relational distance.

Importantly, the other person can accept, resist, reinterpret, or later reuse the name. Naming is therefore an early example of Yui's characteristic mode: she changes relational atmosphere through low-formality linguistic acts rather than through abstract argument.

## 6.3 Hesitation is not the absence of decision

Across the early volumes Yui repeatedly uses forms such as:

- `と思う、けど`
- `たぶん`
- ellipses before desire language;
- partial restarts.

The wrong reading is:

> hedge = weak preference.

The better reading is:

> hedge = a pragmatic method for making an exposed claim while leaving the other person room and retaining some protection against rejection.

That method can coexist with clear volition.

Volume 5's:

> `もっと知りたい、な……`

is modest in surface force and unmistakably approach-oriented in semantic content.

## 6.4 Yui often supplies the least abstract and most operational proposition

A major cross-volume pattern is that Yui frequently interrupts Hachiman's theoretical vocabulary with a sentence that specifies the relational problem in ordinary language.

Examples include:

- `人の気持ち、もっと考えてよ`
- `こういうの、もう、なしね`
- `あたし、この部活、好きなの`
- `誰かとかみんなとかじゃなくて……。あたしたちを頼って？`

These are not philosophically unsophisticated statements. Their low abstraction is often their advantage. They tell the group what conduct or relation is actually at issue.

Accordingly:

> **low abstraction ≠ low conceptual intelligence.**

## 6.5 Particularization: from `誰か / みんな` to `あたしたち`

The Volume 6 line:

> `誰かとかみんなとかじゃなくて……。あたしたちを頼って？`

is one of Yui's most important linguistic acts.

It rejects generic social categories in favor of a particular relation. The pause before `あたしたち` matters because she is not merely proposing that Hachiman should become more socially dependent in general. She names *who* may be relied upon.

That pattern anticipates the later series' movement away from universal relational theory and toward first-person, second-person, and small-group accountability.

## 6.6 Directness rises; softness remains

Yui's later speech becomes unmistakably firmer without becoming Yukino-like.

Volume 11 provides a cluster:

- `三人で、行きたいの……`
- `ここがいいの`
- `あたしは全部ほしい`
- `あたし、ずるいんだ。卑怯な子なんだ`
- `あたしはもうちゃんと決めてる`

The syntax remains recognizably Yui: soft endings, conversational particles, pauses, emotionally ordinary vocabulary.

But the propositions are strong.

This is the ledger's clearest demonstration that **directness and firmness must be tracked separately**.

## 6.7 The interludes remove the Hachiman filter without making Yui transparent

Volume 12's Yui interludes materially change the linguistic evidence base because they allow sustained first-person language outside Hachiman's narration.

The repeated imperatives/desideratives around:

- `忘れて`
- `認めないで`
- `見逃して`
- `見落として`
- `見過ごして`

and the simple desire clauses:

- `欲しくなかった`
- `傍にいて欲しかった`
- `傷つけて欲しかった`

show a consciousness that is not confused merely because it is conflicted.

Yui can want incompatible things and say so.

Her problem is not absence of introspection. It is that honest introspection reveals plural desires that no clean solution can jointly satisfy.

## 6.8 `わかんない` can coexist with certainty of feeling

Late Yui is central to the series' epistemic humility.

She can be unable to certify whether the relation is `本物` while being certain that she loves. That asymmetry matters:

- first-person affective knowledge can be strong;
- total relational ontology can remain uncertain.

The colloquial `わかんない` is therefore not intellectual failure. In context, it is a refusal to claim more knowledge than she has.

## 6.9 Volume 14: communication becomes an ethic of effort

Yui's late language is among the series' most explicit about communication:

- `勝手に終わらせないでほしい`
- `それ、言わなきゃ絶対わかんないよ`
- `伝える努力してないだけじゃん`
- `全部欲しい`
- `だから絶対言って`

This does not mean she believes language guarantees understanding. Her later position is more demanding and more modest: one must make the effort to say, and then continue trying to understand.

Her final formulation is especially valuable:

> `その分、あたしがわかろうとするからいいの。ゆきのんもたぶんそうだよ`

The verb is not possession of knowledge but ongoing effort toward it.

## 6.10 Ordinary desire is not diminished by ordinary words

Yui's late wish:

> `こんななんでもない放課後にゆきのんがいて欲しい。ヒッキーとゆきのんがいるところにあたしもいたい`

is deliberately ordinary in vocabulary and socially difficult in implication.

It refuses two analytical shortcuts:

1. that emotionally important desire must be expressed in elevated language;
2. that romantic non-equivalence requires Yui's relational language to disappear.

### Yui freeze

> **Yui's voice matures by increasing propositional ownership without sacrificing softness. Hesitation, ellipsis, colloquialism, and accommodation remain part of her idiolect, but they increasingly carry explicit desire, refusal, self-critique, and demands for communicative effort. Her language is therefore the series' strongest warning against treating low-force surface form as weak agency.**

# 7. Isshiki Iroha — performed cuteness, transparent strategy, and owned multiplicity

## 7.1 Iroha's register is visibly constructed — and still hers

Iroha is the strongest test case for any theory that equates performance with falsity.

Her voice can include:

- elongated polite-casual endings such as `～ですよー` and `～ですかねー`;
- cute deference;
- mock rejection routines;
- blunt private evaluation;
- strategic compliments;
- whispered intimacy;
- sincere gratitude;
- institutional speech as student-council president.

The shifts are real. The error is to treat one layer as the authentic Iroha and the rest as costume.

## 7.2 Benefactive grammar can invert apparent power

Volume 8's:

> `先輩に乗せられてあげます`

is characteristic. The `～てあげる` framing allows Iroha to describe being persuaded as something she grants to Hachiman.

This is not merely cute affectation. It is agency management. She participates while preserving a verbal position from which the participation remains hers.

The same logic often lets Iroha accept help, flirt, complain, or cooperate without surrendering the right to narrate what the exchange means.

## 7.3 The rejection gag is a relationship-specific script

Iroha's recurring rejection performance is neither literal romantic truth table nor meaningless comic routine.

Its function changes with accumulated history. Because both participants know the script, repetition can itself mark familiarity.

The future locator ledger should preserve scene-specific wording where claims depend on exact variation, but this ledger freezes the higher-level point:

> **a repeated performed script can become a genuine relation-specific convention without ceasing to be a performance.**

## 7.4 Strategic ambiguity is not absence of sincerity

The Volume 10.5 line:

> `こんなことするの、先輩にだけですよ`

remains multiply interpretable by design.

The correct method is not to select one hidden sincerity beneath it. The sentence can simultaneously:

- create specialness;
- tease;
- manage deniability;
- express actual preference;
- test Hachiman's reaction.

Multiple pragmatic functions do not cancel one another.

## 7.5 `ずるい` and self-aware mixed motive

Iroha's use of `ずるい`, echoed elsewhere by Yui, belongs to a larger late-series vocabulary in which wanting advantage does not automatically invalidate feeling.

Iroha frequently knows she is managing the situation. That self-awareness can make performance more answerable rather than less genuine.

## 7.6 `プレゼントは言い訳ですよ` — pretext becomes transparent shared grammar

Volume 14.5 gives one of the clearest late formulations:

> `プレゼントは言い訳ですよ`

The significance is not that pretext disappears. Iroha names it.

A `言い訳` can become socially productive when participants understand the frame and retain room to answer. This is the linguistic counterpart to the Social Role / Performance Ledger's model of inhabitable role.

## 7.7 Epistemic limitation becomes explicit

Also in Volume 14.5:

> `私がわかるのは、わたしのことだけ。だから、わたし基準に想像するしかない`

This is among Iroha's most analytically valuable statements.

It separates:

- what she can know first-personally;
- what she must imagine about others;
- the unavoidable use of her own perspective as a baseline.

The formulation does not eliminate interpretation. It makes the interpretive limitation explicit.

This aligns Iroha unexpectedly with the mature Hachiman narrator model: interpretation remains necessary, but it should no longer masquerade as jurisdiction.

### Iroha freeze

> **Iroha's voice demonstrates that performance can be an owned mode of agency. Her development does not strip away strategic cuteness; it makes the strategy increasingly legible, relationally shared, institutionally competent, and epistemically bounded.**

# 8. Yukinoshita Haruno — strategic ambiguity, interrogative pressure, and diagnostic asymmetry

## 8.1 Social fluency is audible power

Haruno enters with rapid familiarity, nicknaming, easy shifts of tone, and the ability to define a social atmosphere before others have decided what it is.

Her `雪乃ちゃん` is not just affectionate morphology. It can position Yukino within an older-sister frame before Yukino has chosen the terms of the exchange.

Haruno's linguistic competence is therefore a form of situational power.

## 8.2 Questions are one of Haruno's strongest instruments

Late Haruno often speaks through questions:

- `それが比企谷くんのいう本物？`
- `雪乃ちゃんに自分なんてあるの？`

The interrogative form matters.

A question can:

1. force the addressee to formulate an answer;
2. destabilize the addressee's existing category;
3. imply that the questioner sees a contradiction;
4. allow the questioner to avoid committing to a complete positive theory.

This creates a recurrent **diagnostic asymmetry**: Haruno can demand that others justify themselves while keeping her own preferred endpoint partly unstated.

## 8.3 Naming a mechanism does not grant sovereign interpretation

Her introduction of `共依存` is linguistically compact and forceful. The whispered, declarative diagnosis performs certainty.

Yet the later text itself limits what the word can do:

> `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。`

That distinction must govern the Voice Ledger as well as the Dependency Ledger.

A diagnostic noun can organize observation. Its grammatical confidence does not automatically prove that the speaker owns the correct moral remedy or the complete meaning of the relation.

## 8.4 Playfulness and severity coexist

Haruno's teasing register is not a detachable mask over pure hostility. She can be socially playful, genuinely attached, competitive, resentful, perceptive, and coercive in overlapping ways.

The mature reading should therefore resist both extremes:

- “Haruno is only performing”; and
- “Haruno's sharpest words are the author's diagnosis.”

### Haruno freeze

> **Haruno's voice is powerful because it makes interpretation socially actionable: nicknames frame, questions compel, diagnoses compress complexity. Its danger is the ease with which linguistic acuity can be mistaken for interpretive sovereignty.**

# 9. Hayama Hayato — neutral mediation, role-compatible selfhood, and marked breaks

## 9.1 Controlled neutrality is a real skill and a real constraint

Hayama's ordinary voice is balanced, socially aware, and unusually capable of speaking across groups. The early readings already show that he can formulate bounded request goals more explicitly than Hachiman's stereotype of the popular group predicts.

This voice is not simply fake niceness. It is a competence that helps maintain a large relational network.

At the same time, expectation accumulates around it. “Hayama” becomes a social answer others expect before the person has necessarily answered.

## 9.2 Breaks in register signal stake, not a hidden authentic replacement

Marked moments such as:

- `ならっ！`
- `君にだけは、頼りたくなかった`

matter precisely because they depart from his controlled baseline.

They expose personal stake and accumulated frustration. But they should not be treated as the only authentic Hayama. The ordinary mediator voice also belongs to him.

## 9.3 `それが、俺だ` / `それも含めて、俺だよ`

Volume 10 gives the ledger's clearest anti-hidden-self formulation:

> `それが、俺だ`

and:

> `それも含めて、俺だよ`

The second statement is especially important. `も含めて` makes multiplicity explicit. Role, expectation, accommodation, resentment, and personal choice can all be included in selfhood.

This directly supports the Social Role Ledger's rejection of the simple mask/essence binary.

## 9.4 Negative directness does not dissolve relation

Hayama's `俺は君が嫌いだ` and Hachiman's reciprocal `俺もお前が嫌いだよ` are useful because plain hostility does not magically produce either intimacy or severance.

Directness supplies information. It does not dictate the relational outcome.

### Hayama freeze

> **Hayama's voice shows that socially managed speech can be both personally owned and burdensome. His strongest breaks do not reveal a different true self; they expand the set of things the ordinarily controlled Hayama is finally willing to include in speech.**

# 10. Hiratsuka Shizuka — adult register, pedagogical indirection, and answer-preserving guidance

## 10.1 Hiratsuka is direct without confiscating the answer

Hiratsuka's adult authority permits greater directness than the students usually possess. Yet her best interventions do not simply replace their judgments with hers.

Her language repeatedly distinguishes:

- observation from conclusion;
- prediction from emotional understanding;
- the need for words from the fantasy that words guarantee understanding;
- a useful conceptual frame from the answer the student must still produce.

This is why she functions as a linguistic countermodel to both Hachiman's overinterpretation and Haruno's diagnostic pressure.

## 10.2 Tense and aspect carry an ethics of revision

Volume 11's:

> `人の印象は日々更新され続けている`

is not only a psychological claim. The progressive-continuative form matters. People are not a solved object; impressions are being updated and continue to be updated.

That temporal grammar is almost a compact statement of the V2 method itself.

## 10.3 The final question remains a question

Volume 14's:

> `君の本物は見つかったか？`

receives no final doctrinal confirmation from Hachiman.

His response remains uncertain, and Hiratsuka does not overwrite it.

Adult guidance therefore culminates not in certification but in a question the younger person must continue answering.

### Hiratsuka freeze

> **Hiratsuka's mature pedagogical register is answer-preserving: it can name errors, sharpen categories, and demand articulation while leaving the decisive first-person claim with the person who must live it.**

# 11. Hikigaya Komachi — kinship shorthand, meta-performance, and institutional succession

## 11.1 `小町的にポイント高い` — affection and performance openly coexist

Komachi's recurring `小町的にポイント高い` is explicitly meta-performative. She can narrate her own sisterly manipulation while doing it.

This makes her a comic but important counterexample to the idea that acknowledged performance is necessarily deceptive.

Her familial intimacy gives her access to shorthand, teasing, guilt, reward language, and future-oriented assumptions that would sound far more invasive from an outsider.

## 11.2 Intimacy does not erase boundary problems

Because Komachi's intervention is affectionate, it is tempting to treat her future allocation as harmless. Volume 14.5 complicates that.

`勝手に` remains context-sensitive even inside family intimacy. Knowing someone well does not create unlimited authority to determine their future relations for them.

The Japanese voice evidence therefore reinforces the Request / Intervention Ethics Ledger: warmth and accurate prediction are not jurisdiction.

## 11.3 `比企谷部長。よろしくお願いします` — formula as real transfer

Hachiman's late formal address to Komachi:

> `改めまして、比企谷部長。よろしくお願いします`

is one of the most important institutional utterances in Volume 14.5.

Nothing about the phrase is lexically exotic. Its force comes from the altered role relation and accumulated history.

The conventional formula does real work:

- it recognizes Komachi's office;
- it releases the Service Club from being only the old trio's sacred arrangement;
- it marks succession without requiring identical reproduction;
- it demonstrates that formulaic language can be meaningful precisely because institutions make shared formulas possible.

### Komachi freeze

> **Komachi's voice joins intimacy and overt performance, while the ending gives conventional institutional language genuine transformative force. Formula is not the opposite of meaning; its meaning depends on who is entitled to say it, to whom, and under what changed relation.**

# 12. Secondary voices as analytical controls

The major-character analysis becomes more reliable when tested against voices that do not share the same narrative centrality.

## 12.1 Miura Yumiko — blunt colloquial care

Miura's `あーし`, clipped rhythm, and forceful judgments show that bluntness can carry protection and social attention.

Her register prevents the ledger from treating softening as the universal language of care.

At the same time, bluntness does not give her privileged interpretive access. It is a style of action, not epistemic authority.

## 12.2 Ebina Hina — persona can be suspended without becoming false

Volume 7 is especially useful. Ebina's public fujoshi persona gives way, in high-stakes conversation, to plainer declaratives such as self-description and refusal to be understood.

The change is meaningful because the protective/social function of the persona temporarily decreases.

But the V2 reading must not say that plain Ebina is real and performative Ebina is fake. The more defensible claim is that different contexts require different degrees of role mediation.

## 12.3 Tobe Kakeru — casual register does not imply shallow stakes

Tobe's `～っしょ`, `～わー`, `的な`, fillers, and casual rhythm are socially marked. Yet Volume 7 can place `結構マジ` inside that same register.

His speech is a useful control against classifying intellectual seriousness from polish.

## 12.4 Totsuka Saika — simple questions can outperform theory

Saika's use of `八幡` is an ordinary closeness marker whose importance derives from Hachiman's restricted naming environment.

Late, the simple question:

> `八幡はどうしたいの？`

cuts through elaborate strategic machinery.

The question is powerful because it asks for a first-person answer rather than supplying an interpretation.

## 12.5 Kawasaki Saki — clipped boundary speech

Kawasaki's `関係ない` is not analytically reducible to coldness. In context it can be a claim about standing: who is entitled to intervene and who is not.

This supports a broader series rule: short negative forms often need pragmatic classification before personality interpretation.

## 12.6 Zaimokuza Yoshiteru — performed grandiosity and exposed desire

Zaimokuza's chuunibyou grandiosity is one of the earliest demonstrations that constructed voice can carry genuine desire.

When manuscript anxiety becomes salient, the performance may thin, but that does not retrospectively convert the stylized voice into pure falsehood.

## 12.7 Sagami Minami — `ちゃんと` and contested adequacy

Sagami's repeated `ちゃんと` in Volume 6.5 matters because the word is evaluative without being self-defining. It points toward properness, adequacy, or doing things as one should, but the relevant standard must be inferred from context.

The same word later becomes central across other speakers. Its mobility makes it a series-level procedural term rather than a private character marker.

## 12.8 Tamanawa — institutional language without adequate decision

Tamanawa functions as an important negative control for borrowed managerial language. Imported or abstract terminology can create the appearance of procedure while delaying commitment and obscuring responsibility.

The problem is not loanwords themselves. Hachiman uses equally abstract vocabulary. The difference is whether the language clarifies action and ownership or substitutes for them.

## 12.9 Yukino's mother — polite syntax can close rather than open choice

Yukino's mother is the strongest reminder that politeness is not ethical softness. Controlled, socially reasonable language can structure the field so tightly that refusal becomes difficult.

The ethical question must therefore concern answerability and available alternatives, not merely politeness level.

### Secondary-voice freeze

> **Oregairu distributes ethical and epistemic value across many registers. Blunt, soft, formal, casual, strategic, awkward, intellectual, and formulaic speech can each succeed or fail. What matters is what the form is doing in the relation.**# 13. Address, naming, and relational deixis

Address terms in *Oregairu* are meaningful, but they are not a one-dimensional intimacy meter.

## 13.1 Stable forms can carry changing relations

Several forms remain comparatively stable even while relationships change dramatically:

- Yukino → Hachiman: `比企谷くん`
- Yukino → Yui: `由比ヶ浜さん`
- Saika → Hachiman: `八幡`
- Yui → Hachiman: `ヒッキー`
- Yui → Yukino: `ゆきのん`

If address alone were treated as a closeness scale, much of the series' development would become invisible.

The better rule is:

> **A change in address can be evidence of changed positioning; absence of change is not evidence of relational stasis.**

## 13.2 Nicknames are proposals, not objective measurements

Yui's nicknames create social possibilities. Haruno's `雪乃ちゃん` can impose a family frame. Komachi's kinship shorthand presupposes access others do not have.

The same morphological intimacy can therefore be:

- affiliative;
- teasing;
- presumptive;
- hierarchical;
- nostalgic;
- strategic.

Speaker, addressee, setting, and right-to-use matter.

## 13.3 Institutional address can become intimate through history

The final `比企谷部長` / `よろしくお願いします` sequence demonstrates the reverse movement. A formal title can acquire emotional force because it recognizes a changed institutional relation.

Thus the series does not move monotonically from surname-plus-honorific toward first name. Relational meaning is distributed across **the whole utterance and its history**, not encoded in one address token.

# 14. Recurring lexical systems

The following terms should be treated as longitudinal systems. No single English gloss is adequate in every scene.

## 14.1 `勝手に` — unilateral authorship

Across the series `勝手に` marks a recurring question:

> Who acted, inferred, expected, decided, or assigned meaning without the other person's answer?

Its uses include:

- Hachiman's critique of projecting expectations: `勝手に期待して...`;
- Yukino's protest against Hachiman deciding her feelings: `私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで`;
- Yui's `勝手に終わらせないでほしい`;
- Hachiman's rejection of proxy regret;
- Hachiman's own unilateral action;
- Yukino's late `私が勝手にやっているだけだから`, where unilateral ownership releases the recipient from reciprocal obligation.

Therefore `勝手に` is not always condemnation. Its core concerns **whose act is being claimed and whether another person's sphere is being appropriated**.

## 14.2 `わかる / 理解する / 知る` — knowledge without mastery

These verbs form one of the series' central epistemic networks.

Early Hachiman often treats social understanding as predictive competence. The later volumes separate:

- knowing facts;
- recognizing patterns;
- predicting conduct;
- understanding feeling;
- understanding enough to act;
- continuing to try to understand.

The mature series repeatedly legitimizes `わからない` when it marks an honest limit.

Yui's `わかろうとする` is especially important because the volitional/process form shifts value from **possessing the answer** to **continuing the work of understanding**.

## 14.3 `ちゃんと` — procedural adequacy without a fixed procedure

`ちゃんと` recurs across speakers and volumes.

It can mean something like:

- properly;
- seriously;
- adequately;
- in the way the situation demands.

But the standard is context-dependent.

Late uses—Yui demanding that Hachiman say what he wants, Yukino `私はちゃんと言うわ`, Iroha `もっとちゃんとしてください`—make it increasingly associated with **answerable completion of one's communicative share**.

Yet it should not be translated into a universal ethical formula. The important question remains: *properly according to what obligation in this scene?*

## 14.4 `頼る / 依頼 / お願い` — reliance, request, and permission

These words must remain distinct.

- `頼る` can name reliance.
- `依頼` can formalize an object of request.
- `お願い` can range from conventional request marker to emotionally exposed plea.

Yukino's `お願い。私にやらせて` is particularly instructive because the request is for space to act rather than help doing the action.

Yui's `あたしたちを頼って？` particularizes whom Hachiman may rely upon.

Hachiman's `一つ、依頼がしたい` in Volume 9 turns private longing into an utterance others can answer.

## 14.5 `普通` — normality as description, reassurance, and pressure

`普通` is unstable throughout the series.

It can:

- reassure;
- describe common expectation;
- invoke social norm;
- expose that a norm belongs to a particular speaker;
- become a program Hachiman uses to avoid exceptional vulnerability.

Yukino's `それがあなたにとっての普通なのね` is important because it reassigns the supposedly general category back to its speaker.

## 14.6 `言う / 言葉 / 答え` — speech necessary, speech insufficient

The series refuses both extremes:

- “if people really understand each other, words are unnecessary”; and
- “saying the right sentence solves the relation.”

Volume 9 explicitly makes words necessary without granting them magical sufficiency. Volume 14 intensifies the point: Yui demands speech because the unsaid cannot simply be known, while Hachiman insists `言葉一つじゃ足りねぇよ`.

The apparent contradiction is productive.

A word can be necessary and still not exhaust the relation.

## 14.7 `欲しい / 願い / 求める` — desire becomes attributable

Hachiman's `俺は、本物が欲しい` matters partly because `欲しい` places desire in the first person.

Later `願い` circulates among Yukino, Yui, Hachiman, and the contest structure. The crucial question becomes whether a wish is:

- spoken by the person who owns it;
- inferred by someone else;
- converted into a task;
- used to allocate another person's future.

The lexical field therefore connects voice directly to authorization.

## 14.8 `本音` — not an infallible inner transcript

`本音` does not mean perfectly reasoned final truth.

A genuine preference can be irrational, incomplete, contradictory, or difficult to defend. The term should therefore not be used to restore a simplistic surface/true-self binary.

## 14.9 `本物 / 偽物 / 紛い物 / 贋作 / 欺瞞 / 信実 / 真実`

These words overlap without collapsing.

The Authenticity / `本物` Ledger already freezes the conceptual result; the Voice Ledger adds a linguistic warning:

> **similar English words such as “genuine,” “fake,” “counterfeit,” “deception,” “truth,” and “sincerity” do not authorize treating the Japanese lexical field as one stable binary.**

Volume 10's playful `シンジツ` ambiguity and Volume 13's `紛い物 / 贋作` are especially useful reminders that form and nuance matter.

## 14.10 `終わる / 終わらせる` — event versus agency

Late volumes repeatedly distinguish something ending from someone ending it.

The transitive `終わらせる` foregrounds agency. This matters when characters describe the Service Club or relationship as if dissolution were merely happening rather than being actively chosen or imposed.

## 14.11 `一緒` — ordinary togetherness after grand diagnosis

By Volume 14.5, `一緒` becomes valuable precisely because it is ordinary.

The post-ending state does not require renewed diagnostic vocabulary. Ordinary future grammar can carry chosen continuation.

# 15. Grammar, pragmatics, and translation-risk register

This section freezes recurring translation risks for later synthesis.

## 15.1 Ellipsis is not empty space

Japanese ellipsis can encode:

- hesitation;
- politeness;
- embarrassment;
- invitation to infer;
- self-protection;
- interruption;
- refusal to overstate.

The analytical mistake is to assign one psychology to every `……`.

Yui's ellipses often preserve interactional space. Yukino's can mark strain inside controlled syntax. Hachiman's can precede a statement he is struggling to own. The function must be local.

## 15.2 `けど` does not always cancel what comes before

Sentence-final or trailing `けど` can leave room for the other person's answer without retracting the proposition.

The Christmas-coda Yui line `終わらなくても、いいと思うけど……` is a strong example. Her stance is present; the ending reduces imposition rather than erasing desire.

## 15.3 `と思う` can mark ownership, not mere uncertainty

English translations can over-soften `と思う` into “I guess” or treat it as weak conviction.

In many Oregairu contexts it performs an important ethical function: the speaker marks the proposition as *their stance*.

Hachiman's `俺は関わり続けたいと、思ってる` is stronger, not weaker, for being owned as what he thinks/wants rather than announced as objective necessity.

## 15.4 Benefactives encode relational positioning

Forms involving `～てもらう`, `～てあげる`, and related benefactive structure can distribute agency, indebtedness, permission, and perspective.

Yukino's `頼らせてもらっても` and Iroha's `乗せられてあげます` are not equivalent despite both involving interpersonal action. The grammar helps show who frames themselves as receiving permission, receiving benefit, or granting something.

## 15.5 Politeness is not submission; plainness is not equality

Politeness level must be read with institutional and relational power.

A polite parent may narrow a child's options. A junior can strategically manage a senior with polite-casual cuteness. A plain imperative from a close friend may preserve more answerability than a formally courteous social script.

## 15.6 Feminine endings are character voice, not evidence of passivity

Yukino's `〜わ / 〜かしら / 〜もの` remain compatible with authority, refusal, precision, and first-person desire.

Any translation strategy that systematically converts her into generic neutral firmness risks losing how agency develops **inside** her established gendered register.

## 15.7 Loanwords and abstraction are functionally variable

Managerial or theoretical vocabulary is not intrinsically evasive.

It can:

- sharpen distinctions;
- comicize emotion;
- create distance;
- coordinate action;
- delay decision;
- counterfeit competence.

Tamanawa and Hachiman offer useful contrasts. The semantic class alone cannot settle the function.

## 15.8 Repetition is often more significant than isolated wording

Repeated terms such as `勝手に`, `ちゃんと`, `わからない`, `頼る`, and `言葉` accumulate force because later scenes answer or revise earlier uses.

The later locator ledger should therefore cross-link repeated lexical clusters, not merely index quotations individually.

# 16. Formal polyphony, narrator position, and attribution discipline

## 16.1 Hachiman's first person is a filter, not the whole novel's voice

Watari's Volume 6.5 afterword explicitly notes that because the work is presented through Hachiman's first person, how characters are depicted changes with Hachiman's degree of understanding.

This is methodologically decisive.

Narrative wording about another character can contain:

- observation;
- metaphor;
- genre framing;
- inferred motive;
- self-protective classification.

The Voice Ledger therefore inherits the Hachiman Epistemic / Narratorial Ledger's domain-specific reliability model.

## 16.2 Unattributed passages must remain unattributed when the form requires it

Volume 10's record-like passages deliberately resist easy exclusive attribution. Resonance with Hachiman or compatibility with Hayama is not sufficient to convert formal ambiguity into a speaker label.

The archive should preserve a distinction among:

- formally attributed;
- contextually strong attribution inference;
- genuinely open attribution.

## 16.3 Interludes are not decorative

Volumes 12–14 increasingly decenter Hachiman through interludes and preludes.

This matters linguistically because they provide access to voices whose motives would otherwise arrive mediated through Hachiman.

Volume 12's Yui interludes are especially important. Volume 13 distributes interludes among Iroha, Yui, Hayama, Haruno, and a closing passage that is strongly Yukino-coded but formally requires attribution caution.

Volume 14 continues the polyphonic widening.

## 16.4 Perspective changes the available vocabulary

The interludes do not merely tell us new facts. They change the **lexical and syntactic medium** through which a problem is experienced.

Yui's first-person desire clauses are less theoretically mediated than Hachiman's narration. Iroha's external observations classify Hachiman/Yukino through socially legible categories. Haruno's voice foregrounds diagnostic interrogation.

A mature synthesis should therefore ask not only *what is said*, but *whose linguistic resources are currently making the scene intelligible*.

# 17. Audio and prosody boundary

The light novels remain the primary literary source for this ledger. Audio evidence is supplementary and separately classified.

## 17.1 What audio can add

Where a canonically relevant drama-CD performance is available, audio can support analysis of:

- pause placement;
- hesitation duration;
- tempo;
- overlap;
- vocal force;
- ironic versus plain delivery;
- how a line's written ambiguity is performed.

## 17.2 What audio cannot silently replace

Voice-actor performance should not silently overwrite Watari's prose semantics.

For the chronology-routed post-Volume-9 Christmas coda, the Watari prose rewrite governs exact lexical quotation in this project. Existing direct-listening notes about conspicuous pauses during the gift exchange remain **historical auditory cross-checks**, not newly generated ASR evidence.

## 17.3 High-value Christmas-coda examples

Three short lines demonstrate the value of keeping prose and performance distinct:

- Hachiman: `また明日、な` — ordinary future expectation with no theoretical hedge;
- Yui: `終わらなくても、いいと思うけど……` — stance plus mitigation plus open conversational space;
- Yukino's acceptance of the gift under the `御礼` frame — stated social pretext plus behavior that exceeds minimal obligation.

The coda therefore strengthens the main Voice Ledger thesis: ordinary language can become more relationally consequential without becoming semantically grand.

# 18. Cross-ledger reconciliation

The Japanese Voice Ledger is not an isolated linguistic appendix. It changes how the other Phase-4 ledgers should be read.

## 18.1 With the Hachiman Epistemic / Narratorial Ledger

Language provides the mechanism for the shift from interpretation-as-jurisdiction to interpretation-as-provisional-input.

Increasing `わからない`, first-person desire, and explicit request grammar do not make Hachiman less analytical. They put boundaries around what analysis can claim.

## 18.2 With the Character State Ledger

Growth appears as **greater first-person authorship inside persistent idiolect**.

This is why character development should not be measured by convergence toward one ideal communicative style.

## 18.3 With the Relationship State Ledger

Mature relation is answerable partly because utterances increasingly become answerable:

- requests are identified as requests;
- wishes are attributed to speakers;
- refusals are possible;
- uncertainty is admitted;
- one person is less entitled to supply another's missing sentence.

## 18.4 With the Request / Intervention Ethics Ledger

Grammar can expose jurisdiction.

Imperatives, benefactives, request forms, unilateral `勝手に`, inferred wishes, and question pressure all help show whether another person retains meaningful answer capacity.

## 18.5 With the Authenticity / `本物` Ledger

There is no linguistically pure authentic register.

Plain speech can be false. Performed speech can be sincere. Mixed motive can be openly voiced. `本物` itself remains a concentrated lexical event rather than a universal descriptor of every later scene.

## 18.6 With the Dependency / Autonomy Ledger

Autonomy becomes audible not as solitary diction but as authorship markers:

- `私の意志`
- `私の気持ち`
- `私の依頼`
- `私の願い`
- `俺は...思ってる`

Chosen reliance likewise becomes speakable as reliance rather than hidden necessity.

## 18.7 With the Social Role / Performance Ledger

The Voice Ledger supplies the linguistic mechanism behind **inhabitable role**.

A social script becomes less dangerous when speakers can:

- mark it as pretext;
- joke about it;
- refuse it;
- revise it;
- shift register;
- acknowledge the office or convention without pretending it is the whole person.

# 20. V1 → V2 Japanese-voice revisions

## 20.1 “Yukino becomes softer” → REVISE

Too coarse.

V2 formulation:

> Yukino's stable precision and feminine register acquire greater capacity for first-person agency, uncertainty, request, and desire.

## 20.2 “Yui is indirect because she does not know what she wants” → REJECT

Yui often knows multiple things she wants and uses softened language because assertion has social cost. Her interludes prove substantial self-awareness even where desire is conflicted.

## 20.3 “Hachiman hides everything behind sarcasm” → REVISE

His comic/abstract language has multiple functions. It becomes defensive when it specifically displaces or sanitizes threatening first-person content.

## 20.4 “Iroha has a fake cute persona and a real blunt self” → REJECT

The register shifts are real; the ontology is wrong. Cute performance, blunt evaluation, strategic flirtation, institutional competence, and sincere gratitude can all belong to Iroha.

## 20.5 “Haruno says what everyone else is afraid to admit” → DOWNGRADE

She often identifies real pressure points. But interrogative and diagnostic force must not be mistaken for complete authorial truth.

## 20.6 “More direct speech is automatically more genuine” → REJECT

Directness can be coercive, mistaken, hostile, simplistic, or strategically staged. Indirectness can be precise, courteous, vulnerable, or agency-preserving.

## 20.7 “First-name/nickname progression is the main linguistic intimacy marker” → DOWNGRADE

Oregairu relies heavily on stable address forms. Syntax, modality, request structure, shared scripts, ordinary future grammar, and willingness to state desire often carry more longitudinal information.

## 20.8 “The ending finally says plainly what the story means” → REJECT

The ending contains unusually plain first-person claims, but it also preserves uncertainty, anti-reduction, and ongoing interpretation.

# 21. Stress tests and open counterreadings

## 21.1 Does Japanese indirectness make this analysis overinterpret ordinary grammar?

**OPEN as a standing methodological risk.**

Many forms tracked here are ordinary Japanese resources. The claim is not that every `けど`, ellipsis, or `と思う` is symbolically loaded. Analytical weight requires recurrence, marked context, contrast with the speaker's baseline, or relation to a longitudinal lexical cluster.

## 21.2 Is Yui's soft register partly accommodation that remains self-erasing?

**PRESERVE as a partial counterreading; reject as total explanation.**

Her accommodation can defer conflict and protect others. But the late volumes give too much explicit first-person desire and refusal to characterize the whole register as self-erasure.

## 21.3 Does Yukino's increased first-person language prove full autonomy?

**REJECT.**

Grammar can establish ownership of the utterance. It cannot prove that the decision is free from family pressure, relational fear, incomplete information, or self-removal.

## 21.4 Does Hachiman's plain bridge language mean he has finally escaped abstraction?

**REJECT.**

Volume 14.5 deliberately preserves his old stylistic machinery. The achievement is coexistence between that machinery and owned desire.

## 21.5 Is Haruno's question form merely stylistic, not power?

**OPEN by scene, but longitudinally strengthened as a pressure mechanism.**

Questions are not inherently coercive. Haruno's repeated ability to put others in the respondent position while withholding her own complete answer gives the form recurrent asymmetrical force.

## 21.6 Are the interludes simply reader clarification rather than thematic polyphony?

**DOWNGRADE as an exclusive reading.**

They do clarify inaccessible motives, but the systematic redistribution of vocabulary, syntax, and evaluation also changes the novel's epistemic structure.

# 23. Terminal Japanese-voice freeze

The complete V2 reread supports the following series-level model.

Early *Oregairu* gives each major character a highly differentiated way of making social life speakable:

- Hachiman theorizes, jokes, classifies, and displaces;
- Yukino specifies, corrects, and controls;
- Yui softens, names, and particularizes;
- Hayama mediates;
- Haruno frames and interrogates;
- Iroha performs and strategically repositions;
- Hiratsuka teaches through corrective but answer-preserving language;
- Komachi makes intimacy itself meta-performative.

The series does **not** cure these voices.

Instead, it repeatedly exposes what each voice cannot yet carry.

Hachiman's abstraction cannot substitute for asking. Yukino's precision cannot let others own her desire for her. Yui's accommodation cannot indefinitely postpone incompatible wants. Hayama's mediation cannot erase resentment. Haruno's diagnosis cannot decide another person's remedy. Iroha's performance cannot become a license to deny her strategic agency.

The late volumes therefore produce a change in **linguistic responsibility**, not stylistic uniformity.

Characters become more capable of marking:

- **what I know**;
- **what I do not know**;
- **what I want**;
- **what I am asking you to do**;
- **what I am asking you not to do**;
- **what belongs to you rather than me**;
- **what I am inferring rather than knowing**;
- **what I will continue to try to understand**.

That is why some of the terminal series' most important utterances are remarkably ordinary.

`また明日`.

`好き`.

`わからない`.

`ちゃんと言う`.

`一緒`.

`よろしくお願いします`.

Their ordinariness is not an aesthetic retreat from the philosophical language of `本物`. It is evidence that the relation no longer needs every meaningful act to arrive as a philosophical diagnosis.

The canonical Phase-4 Japanese-voice thesis is therefore:

> **Oregairu's linguistic development is not a march from indirectness to directness, performance to sincerity, or complicated speech to plain truth. Distinct voices remain distinct. What changes is their capacity for answerable first-person speech: to own desire without universalizing it, admit uncertainty without abandoning relation, formulate requests without disguising them as necessity, refuse without making refusal the whole self, and use social forms without mistaking form for essence. Japanese voice is thus one of the novel's principal ethical technologies. The mature achievement is not perfect transparency but better-marked responsibility for who is saying, knowing, wanting, inferring, asking, and answering.**
