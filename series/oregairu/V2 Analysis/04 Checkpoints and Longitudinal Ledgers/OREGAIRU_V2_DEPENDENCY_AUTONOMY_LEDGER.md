# OREGAIRU V2 — Dependency / Autonomy Longitudinal Ledger

## 0. Purpose

This ledger consolidates the V2 reread's findings about **dependence, reliance, capability, self-authorship, refusal, decision displacement, support, neededness, interdependence, and the late `依存` / `共依存` discourse** across *Oregairu* from Volume 1 through Volume 14.5.

Its purpose is not to decide whether the Service Club is "codependent" in a colloquial or clinical sense. That question is too blunt for the evidence the novels actually provide.

The series repeatedly distinguishes states that compressed readings often collapse:

- wanting another person nearby;
- relying on another person's competence;
- asking another person for help;
- accepting help one could survive without;
- expecting another person to solve a problem;
- needing to be needed in order to feel valuable;
- allowing another person to make a choice one is afraid to make;
- losing practice in self-authorship because others overfunction;
- being institutionally constrained despite formal freedom;
- choosing interdependence while retaining independent capability;
- having one's options manipulated without direct substitution;
- refusing help;
- refusing abandonment;
- and transferring responsibility under the language of "freedom."

Those are not interchangeable.

The governing question of this ledger is therefore:

> **What capacity does a relationship enable, displace, or suppress; who authors the relevant choice; what kind of reliance is present; and can the person affected meaningfully answer, refuse, revise, fail, and continue?**

The ledger prevents six recurring analytical errors:

1. retroactively diagnosing all early closeness as `共依存` because the term appears late;
2. treating Haruno's diagnosis as narrator-level fact;
3. treating Yukino's and Yui's self-descriptions of `依存` as proof that every form of their reliance was pathological;
4. equating independence with health, authenticity, or moral superiority;
5. treating reliance as evidence of incapacity;
6. assuming that separation, romance, or success can by itself prove that a dependency problem has been solved.

The mature dependency analysis must remain compatible with the already-canonical authenticity finding:

> **Dependence is not falsity, independence is not authenticity certification, and a self-authored relationship may include chosen interdependence.**

# 1. Executive thesis

The V2 reread rejects the simple developmental formula:

> dependence -> independence -> healthy relationship

That is not the trajectory the novels establish.

A more accurate series-level movement is:

> **defensive self-sufficiency and unacknowledged reliance -> overfunctioning and displaced choice -> explicit diagnosis of reinforcing mechanisms -> experimentation with separation and altered intervention -> chosen interdependence under acknowledged capability, difference, and revisability**

The earliest books already complicate independence as an ideal.

Hachiman calls solitude rational and equality-preserving while behaviorally building a network he refuses to name. Yukino is highly competent and capable of bounded reliance, yet is much less practiced at making herself the object of a request. Yui is socially embedded and highly relational without therefore lacking agency; in fact, she often creates the communication channels through which other people become more capable of acting. Komachi demonstrates from early on that protecting someone from cost can deprive that person of participation, while helping can also create the conditions in which independent action becomes possible.

Volumes 4–6.5 make the first major autonomy problem visible: **competence can become substitution**.

Yukino's ability lets her absorb Sagami's operational responsibilities. Hachiman's ability lets him absorb social cost. Both forms of overfunctioning can produce successful outcomes while weakening the subject's ownership of the action. Yui's correction is not "never rely on anyone." She tells Yukino to rely on *us* rather than silently becoming the universal substitute, and the cultural-festival resolution improves when responsibility and labor are redistributed rather than monopolized.

Volumes 7–9 shift the problem from competence to **jurisdiction**.

Hachiman can correctly infer multiple motives and still act beyond what those people authorized. In Kyoto, entrusted judgment becomes operational license. In the election arc, Hachiman privately decides that preserving the Service Club is more important than Yukino's stated choice and engineers the environment accordingly. The resulting hollow club is the crucial negative case: nominal continuity can be preserved while relational authorship is evacuated.

Volume 9 does not solve dependence. It changes procedure. Hachiman stops treating interpretation as sufficient jurisdiction and becomes a requester. Yukino and Yui are given room to answer. The club's recovery is therefore less a restoration of "independence" than the beginning of **answerable reliance**.

Volumes 10–11 show that this procedural improvement is incomplete. Hachiman becomes more able to ask friends for help and imagine relying on them, but still struggles to accept refusal and still wants to determine what form another person's autonomy should take. Yukino becomes increasingly able to draw explicit jurisdictional boundaries, culminating in `私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで` and then in a request of her own.

Volume 12 introduces `依存` and `共依存` as explicit interpretive vocabulary. The label has real explanatory force. It identifies mechanisms involving:

- Hachiman's sense of value being reinforced by being needed;
- Yukino's expectation that Hachiman will somehow make things work;
- Yui's displacement of a painful decision onto Yukino;
- the trio's tendency to preserve relation by moving choice away from the person who most fears its consequence;
- and the possibility that helping and being helped have become mutually reinforcing roles.

But the novel immediately limits the diagnosis. Hachiman's formulation is decisive:

> `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。`

A mechanism is not the feeling inside it. It does not decide whether the participants love, resent, fear, need, admire, desire, or care for one another. Nor does it establish a unique remedy.

Volume 13 then performs an experiment on the diagnosis. Yukino refuses direct help. Hachiman changes from direct assistance to institutional rivalry. He improves collaborator consent, accepts refusals, and says `ありがとう、頼りにしてる` without treating reliance as humiliation. Yet his dummy-prom strategy still manipulates the choice architecture. He tries to prove that wanting to help someone who does not need him means the relationship is not `共依存`; Haruno correctly attacks that as insufficient proof. Changed motive language does not automatically dissolve reinforcing structure.

At the same time, Haruno's larger cure hypothesis does not become sovereign. Healthy reliance exists in the same volume. Yukino voluntarily accepts rivalry after refusing direct help. Hachiman relies on collaborators more cleanly than before. Hiratsuka states the procedural principle `大事なのは関わり方`: the issue is not involvement versus noninvolvement, but the form of involvement.

Volume 14 rejects both total separation and romance as magical cures. Hachiman finally strips away the claim that Yukino must need him in order for his continued involvement to be justified. He wants involvement because he wants it. Yukino does not merely receive this offer; after the bridge's procedurally imperfect beginning, she remains after release, interrogates his terms, rejects his self-devaluation, and makes a reciprocal future claim. Later, the series states the autonomy proposition with unusual clarity:

> `一人で立てるのに……`  
> `知ってる`

The offered hand is meaningful precisely because capability is already known.

Volume 14.5 tests whether this structure survives ordinary life. It does so without using `依存` or `共依存` at all. Yukino can want to study with Hachiman because `一緒の方が頑張れると思うし` while retaining her own academic trajectory. Hachiman explicitly rejects her lowering ambition to match him. Komachi inherits the Service Club without being required to reproduce its prior form. Most importantly, Hachiman recognizes that merely saying `好きにしたらいい` or `自分で決めればいい` can itself be an escape: noninterference may transfer responsibility rather than respect autonomy.

The mature V2 dependency thesis is therefore:

> **Oregairu does not cure dependence by maximizing separation. It learns to distinguish reliance from incapacity, care from jurisdiction, support from substitution, self-authorship from solitary action, and freedom from responsibility transfer. `共依存` remains a useful mechanism-level diagnosis where neededness, burden transfer, and displaced choice reinforce one another, but it loses authority as a total ontology or automatic separation prescription. The final positive model is chosen interdependence: people may need, support, influence, advise, rely on, and remain attached to one another while preserving meaningful capability to answer, refuse, revise, fail, and choose differently.**

A compact formulation:

> **Autonomy in Oregairu is not being untouched by other people. It is retaining authorship while being meaningfully involved with them.**

# 2. Dependency / autonomy protocol

## 2.1 Terms this ledger keeps separate

### Capability

Whether a person can perform the relevant action without another person's substitution.

Capability is domain-specific. Yukino can be academically and administratively exceptional while still struggling with family authorship or intimate request. Hachiman can solve social problems while being poor at self-interpreting desire. Yui can be socially skilled while displacing a decisive romantic choice.

### Reliance

Using, trusting, or benefiting from another person's presence, competence, labor, advice, or emotional support.

Reliance may be:

- chosen;
- reciprocal;
- bounded;
- temporary;
- role-based;
- ordinary;
- strategic;
- emotionally significant;
- or unhealthy.

The existence of reliance alone does not decide which.

### Dependence / `依存`

A stronger relational state in which another person, role, or structure has become important to one's ability to decide, act, regulate, or maintain a valued identity.

This ledger preserves speaker attribution. When Yukino or Yui says `依存`, that is strong self-description. It does not automatically generalize beyond the object and mechanism they identify.

### Codependency / `共依存`

A late-series character diagnosis for a mutually reinforcing structure in which helping, being needed, burden transfer, identity, and avoidance of direct choice may sustain one another.

It is not treated here as a clinical diagnosis imported from outside the text.

### Self-authorship

The ability to originate, own, refuse, revise, or accept the consequences of a choice.

Self-authorship does **not** require perfect self-knowledge.

### Autonomy

A broader condition in which the person retains meaningful standing as an author of action and relation.

For this corpus, autonomy is assessed across several dimensions:

1. **origin** — who initiated the action or desire;
2. **information** — what the subject understood;
3. **refusal** — whether no could have force;
4. **revision** — whether the subject could alter terms later;
5. **capability** — whether assistance replaced a capacity the subject actually possessed;
6. **consequence ownership** — whether the subject could experience success, failure, embarrassment, regret, or relinquishment as theirs;
7. **jurisdiction** — who had authority to decide the matter;
8. **voice** — whether the subject could state an answer rather than merely be interpreted;
9. **future openness** — whether the relationship or institution remained revisable after the choice.

### Interdependence

Mutual reliance compatible with preserved authorship.

The V2 corpus uses **chosen interdependence** for the late positive model because "independence" alone cannot explain the ending's explicit support under known capability.

### Decision displacement

Allowing or encouraging another person to make, trigger, or bear a choice one is unwilling to make directly.

Yui's V12 self-description is the clearest case.

### Substitution

Doing for another person what they are supposed to own or practice themselves.

Yukino's cultural-festival overfunctioning and some of Hachiman's intervention methods supply important examples.

### Responsibility transfer

Using "freedom," "do what you want," or "decide for yourself" to avoid the relational duty to give honest advice, context, or involvement when one's position makes those things legitimately relevant.

V14.5 makes this explicit.

---

## 2.2 Evidence hierarchy

Dependency claims are classified by source.

Strongest evidence includes:

- direct first-person self-description;
- explicit refusal or request;
- behavior showing incapacity, expectation, or burden transfer;
- repeated behavioral pattern across volumes;
- demonstrable change in what happens when support is removed or altered.

Weaker evidence includes:

- another character's label;
- Hachiman's private inference;
- symbolic or paratextual suggestion;
- outcome alone;
- the fact that two people spend large amounts of time together;
- the fact that one person enjoys being needed.

The same behavior may support several interpretations. Enjoying reliance may indicate affection, identity reinforcement, ordinary pleasure, dependency, or several at once.

---

## 2.3 Guardrails

This ledger adopts the following hard rules.

1. **Reliance does not imply incapacity.**
2. **Capability does not imply autonomy.** A person can be able to perform a task while lacking meaningful authorship over whether and why they perform it.
3. **Formal freedom does not guarantee practiced self-authorship.** Yukino's family history is the strongest case.
4. **Self-authored choice can still be ethically incomplete.** Yukino's V13 closure request is the clearest example.
5. **Refusal must be object-specific.** Refusing direct help does not necessarily refuse rivalry, advice, presence, or future relation.
6. **Love does not authorize method.**
7. **Support does not need incapacity as its justification.**
8. **Noninterference can be an autonomy failure when it functions as responsibility transfer.**
9. **Separation can be chosen, defensive, imposed, or diagnostic theater; the outcome alone does not classify it.**
10. **Romantic reciprocity does not prove health, and romantic nonreciprocity does not erase relational value.**

# 3. Series-level dependency architecture

The dependency problem changes across the series.

| Stage | Dominant problem | Typical error | Emerging correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| V01–V03 | defensive self-sufficiency and unnamed reliance | treating need as debt, weakness, or threat | bounded request and voluntary belonging |
| V04–V06.5 | overfunctioning | competence becomes substitution; self-cost treated as free | distributed work and explicit reliance |
| V07–V09 | jurisdiction and preservation | inference or trust becomes license to decide | answerability and requester status |
| V10–V11 | direct asking without full refusal tolerance | improved communication still used to steer outcomes | privacy, correction, explicit boundaries |
| V12 | diagnosis | `依存` / `共依存` risk becoming total explanations | mechanism separated from feeling and remedy |
| V13 | experiment on diagnosis | altered method treated as proof of health | `関わり方`, bounded reliance, consent distinctions |
| V14 | final choice | separation or romance treated as cure | support under known capability; owned desire |
| V14.5 | durability / succession | current form becomes sacred; noninterference mistaken for autonomy | revisable interdependence and responsible advice |

The movement is not decreasing connectedness. In several dimensions the protagonists become **more** connected, more future-oriented, and more willing to rely on one another.

What decreases is the legitimacy of using closeness as unspoken jurisdiction.

# 4. V01–V03 — defensive autonomy and emergent reliance

## 4.1 Hachiman: independence as protection from expectation

Hachiman's early autonomy language is partly ethical and partly defensive.

He values solitude because:

- it reduces exposure to humiliation;
- it appears to preserve equality by avoiding debt;
- it protects him from mistaking kindness for affection;
- it lets him reject before he can be rejected;
- it prevents other people from acquiring claims over him.

Those principles contain real observations. His error is not that every social suspicion is false. It is that he turns prior injury into a universal rule about favorable possibilities involving himself.

Behavior contradicts total self-sufficiency almost immediately. He notices, returns, helps, remembers, enjoys being relied upon, and becomes embedded in a network before admitting that the network matters.

**Freeze:** early Hachiman is not independent in the sense of being relation-free. He is **defensively anti-acknowledgment** about reliance.

## 4.2 Yukino: capable of reliance, inexperienced at needing openly

V2 rejects the idea that early Yukino is simply "unable to rely on people."

She can:

- ask Hachiman for bounded assistance;
- accept Komachi's practical knowledge;
- work beside Yui;
- admit that Yui's presence helps the club;
- adjust behavior after correction.

The more precise limitation is that Yukino is much less practiced at making **herself** the object of a request. Helping others fits her competence identity. Saying "I need or want you to help me" is harder.

This distinction becomes decisive later. The series does not have to transform Yukino from autonomous to dependent. It has to let her learn that being capable does not eliminate the legitimacy of a personal request.

## 4.3 Yui: relationality is not lack of autonomy

Yui is socially relational from the beginning, but V2 treats that as a form of agency rather than an automatic weakness.

She creates:

- communication channels;
- friendship language;
- future plans;
- cooperation;
- transitions between social groups;
- and low-risk ways for emotionally avoidant people to remain in contact.

Her early vulnerability is not "she needs people too much." It is that atmosphere sensitivity can lead her to defer decisive conflict and preserve arrangements past the point where their terms should be spoken.

That distinction matters because later `依存` does not retroactively make her entire social method pathological.

## 4.4 Komachi as early control case

Komachi demonstrates a model that will become central much later: strong attachment can coexist with autonomy.

She influences Hachiman, engineers opportunities, gives advice, and receives care. Yet the relationship's security makes differentiation possible. Her later entrance-exam arc will show the same principle more cleanly: support can make failure survivable without taking the test for her.

# 5. V04–V06.5 — overfunctioning, self-cost, and distributed interdependence

## 5.1 Yukino and Sagami: when capability becomes substitution

The cultural-festival arc supplies the clearest early case in which competence can undermine another person's authorship.

Yukino can perform the work. Sagami retains office while Yukino absorbs operational responsibility. The practical system functions because the most capable person overfunctions.

The problem is not that Yukino helps. It is that help begins replacing the office-holder's practice of responsibility.

Hachiman's criticism is powerful because it invokes Yukino's own earlier ethic: the subject should retain the final act that makes the resolution theirs.

The correction is therefore not "Yukino should stop supporting others." It is **support without substitution**.

## 5.2 Hachiman: self-sacrifice as distorted autonomy

Hachiman treats his own reputation as a cheap resource because he believes he owns it.

This initially resembles radical autonomy: if the cost is mine, I may spend it.

The series rejects that accounting.

Other people:

- observe the self-harm;
- become hurt by it;
- depend on it as a method;
- alter their choices around it;
- and acquire relational stakes in what Hachiman does to himself.

Hiratsuka's correction establishes that the self is not socially isolated enough for voluntary cost-bearing to remain private.

This is an important dependency insight: **autonomy does not mean that self-directed harm has no relational externalities.**

## 5.3 Yui's correction: rely on *us*

Yui's response to Yukino's overfunctioning is not independence ideology.

She wants Yukino to rely on particular people who have volunteered to share the burden.

That matters because indiscriminate delegation and chosen reliance are different. Yui particularizes the support relationship: not "everyone will help," but *we will*.

The resulting improvement distributes labor and preserves subjecthood better than either solitary competence or total rescue.

## 5.4 Checkpoint-B freeze

By the end of the cultural-festival movement:

- Hachiman's self-cost is no longer ethically private;
- Yukino's competence is no longer assumed to be autonomy-preserving merely because it succeeds;
- Yui's relational labor is established as active agency;
- chosen reliance has positive standing;
- and the series has already shown that **independence versus dependence is the wrong binary**.

# 6. V07–V09 — trust, jurisdiction, preservation, and becoming answerable

## 6.1 Kyoto: entrusted judgment is not unlimited authority

In Kyoto, Hachiman is trusted to exercise judgment.

He accurately infers several people's motives. Yet Tobe intended to speak for himself, and Hachiman preempts that speech through the false confession strategy.

This is a dependency/autonomy failure even though Hachiman's reading is socially sophisticated.

The crucial distinction is:

> **being relied upon for judgment does not confer jurisdiction over another person's decisive act.**

Yukino and Yui's objection is therefore not simply "do not sacrifice yourself." It is also that he used entrusted discretion to replace a choice that belonged to someone else.

## 6.2 The election: preservation becomes decision substitution

Volume 8 intensifies the problem.

Yukino states that running for president is her will. Hachiman nevertheless engineers the environment because he predicts that her victory would destroy the Service Club configuration he values.

His care and fear are real. His prediction may contain insight. Neither fact authorizes him to downgrade Yukino's declared will into a surface statement whose "real" meaning he can privately determine.

The outcome is diagnostically important: the club remains formally intact but relationally hollow.

Preservation without authorship is not successful relational continuity.

## 6.3 Volume 9: from unilateral solver to requester

Volume 9's major autonomy advance is procedural.

Hachiman admits that private interpretation is insufficient. He names his own desire and exposes it to Yukino and Yui.

This does several things at once:

- reduces his claim to epistemic sovereignty;
- gives the other two standing to reject or reinterpret his request;
- converts desire from hidden motive into contestable speech;
- and makes continued relation something that must be answered rather than merely preserved.

The resulting Service Club is not free of dependency. It is more **answerable**.

## 6.4 Christmas coda: ordinary reliance after repair

The chronology-routed Christmas material strengthens, rather than revises, Checkpoint C.

Its value for this ledger is modest but important: the repaired relationship can support ordinary joint action without immediately returning to crisis-scale substitution. Shared work becomes habit before it becomes proof of permanence.

# 7. V10–V11 — asking improves before refusal tolerance does

## 7.1 Hachiman learns to ask

Volume 10 contains a meaningful change in Hachiman's willingness to rely on others.

He can:

- ask for time;
- ask for help;
- imagine future reliance on Totsuka;
- inquire into Yukino's future rather than simply derive it;
- preserve information she gives him as private rather than common property.

These are autonomy-supporting improvements because reliance becomes explicit rather than smuggled through role or manipulation.

## 7.2 But refusal still lacks full standing

Hayama's refusal shows that Hachiman has not yet internalized the whole ethic.

He can formulate a request while still treating another person's refusal as an obstacle to work around when the information seems important enough.

Thus:

> **asking is not autonomy-preserving if no is treated as merely the beginning of persuasion.**

The mature intervention ledger later formalizes this as the requirement that refusal have force.

## 7.3 Yukino's jurisdiction boundary

Volume 11 gives Yukino increasingly explicit language for resisting other people's interpretations of her.

`私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで` is not simply an emotional complaint. It is a jurisdictional statement.

Hachiman and Yui may observe, infer, advise, worry, and ask. They may not convert those activities into ownership of Yukino's feelings or future.

Her subsequent request is equally important. She does not wait for them to discover the correct hidden answer. She originates speech.

## 7.4 Checkpoint-D freeze

The post-`本物` restructuring does not produce independent individuals who no longer need one another.

It produces:

- more direct inquiry;
- more explicit correction;
- more privacy;
- more ordinary reliance;
- and clearer boundaries around interpretive jurisdiction.

The governing state is **high significance plus answerable autonomy conflict**.

# 8. V12 — `依存` and `共依存` enter the text

Volume 12 is the lexical turning point, but it must not be allowed to erase the behavioral history already established.

## 8.1 Haruno's diagnosis has real evidence

Haruno's `共依存っていうのよ` lands because the trio has accumulated recognizable reinforcing mechanisms.

The diagnosis is not baseless provocation.

It helps name:

- Hachiman's attachment to being needed;
- Yukino's tendency to let Hachiman become a solver around her;
- Yui's preservation strategies;
- burden transfer;
- self-worth attached to relational function;
- and the way the three can use "helping" to postpone the decision about what they actually want.

The diagnosis therefore has **explanatory force**.

What it does not have is automatic sovereignty.

Haruno remains:

- a character;
- a participant in the family system;
- emotionally invested in the protagonists' answer;
- and later revealed to want proof of a single non-counterfeit ending.

Her diagnosis must be tested, not obeyed.

## 8.2 Hachiman's governing limitation: mechanism, not feeling

The most important dependency sentence in Volume 12 is:

> `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。`

The distinction is exact.

`仕組み` concerns arrangement, mechanism, structure, or system.

`気持ち` concerns felt motive and emotion.

The sentence prevents several invalid deductions:

- a codependent mechanism does not mean affection is fake;
- it does not prove there is no love;
- it does not prove every act of care is merely reinforcement;
- it does not tell us which participant's feelings are "real";
- it does not establish separation as the unique cure;
- it does not prove that removing the relationship would solve the underlying developmental problem.

This line should govern the entire V2 dependency synthesis.

## 8.3 Hachiman: being needed as reinforcement

Hachiman increasingly recognizes that being useful and being needed have fed his sense of value.

That matters because his solver identity is not only altruistic. It provides:

- role clarity;
- justification for closeness;
- an excuse not to state desire;
- and a self-worth mechanism that rewards intervention.

This is one of the strongest supports for Haruno's structural diagnosis.

But the later test will ask whether Hachiman still wants involvement when **need is removed as justification**.

That question is more probative than asking whether he ever enjoyed being needed.

## 8.4 Yukino: authorship before certainty

Volume 12 is not primarily Yukino becoming independent from Hachiman. It is Yukino practicing authorship.

She:

- admits uncertainty;
- recovers a future desire;
- recognizes that nominal freedom did not teach her how to choose;
- decides to speak to her mother;
- asks Hachiman and Yui to witness the result;
- uses the prom as a competence test;
- explicitly asks Hachiman not to take over.

Her lines:

> `あなたの力はもう借りないわ`

and

> `お願い。私にやらせて`

set an object-specific boundary.

The request is not "leave my life." It is "do not do this for me."

That distinction becomes essential in V13.

## 8.5 Yui: `依存` as decision displacement

Yui's self-description is also strong evidence:

> `あたしは彼女に依存したの`

The canonical V2 interpretation is narrow and specific. Yui allowed Yukino to carry or trigger a decision Yui was afraid to make herself.

That is **decision displacement**.

It should not be generalized into:

- Yui and Yukino's friendship is pathological;
- Yui cannot act without Yukino;
- Yui's attachment is false;
- Yui has no agency;
- or separation from Yukino is required.

In fact, V13–V14 will show Yui independently authoring the continuation of that friendship even after romantic loss.

## 8.6 Yukino's family history complicates "independence"

Volume 12 also reveals why autonomy cannot be reduced to formal freedom.

Yukino has not simply been over-controlled in the ordinary sense. Part of her difficulty comes from being "free" in a family system where Haruno's path was predetermined and where Yukino's own choices did not receive equivalent seriousness.

This produces a paradox:

> **freedom without relational recognition can fail to teach authorship.**

A person may technically be allowed to choose while lacking practice in having a choice heard, contested, supported, and lived through.

That insight directly anticipates V14.5's rejection of `自分で決めればいい` as a complete autonomy ethic.

# 9. V13 — diagnosis becomes experiment

Volume 13 is the project's most important dependency test because the characters begin changing behavior **in response to the diagnosis**.

## 9.1 Hachiman recognizes Yukino's ownership of consequence

He explicitly thinks that Yukino's:

> `選択も挑戦も失敗も諦観も`

should belong to her.

This is a mature autonomy formulation because it includes failure and relinquishment.

Autonomy is not merely the right to succeed by oneself. It includes the right to:

- choose;
- attempt;
- fail;
- revise;
- and give up.

## 9.2 Noninvolvement is not his chosen answer

Hachiman nevertheless says he has no option of simply not becoming involved.

This creates the core ethical conflict of the volume.

If Yukino's authorship matters and Hachiman's continued involvement is genuinely his own desire, then the problem cannot be solved by asking which one of them gets to dictate whether relation occurs.

Hiratsuka supplies the mature procedural correction:

> `大事なのは関わり方`

The important thing is **how one becomes involved**.

This is the clearest rejection of the binary:

> intervention / dependence = bad  
> withdrawal / independence = good

The series instead asks what form of involvement preserves the other's authorship.

## 9.3 Direct-help refusal and rivalry authorization are different

Yukino refuses Hachiman's direct assistance.

Hachiman then creates an independent rival prom.

Later Yukino voluntarily accepts the contest/wager.

These facts must remain separate:

- she does not authorize him to work inside her project;
- she does authorize a competitive relation between projects.

This is a major autonomy gain in the V2 architecture because it demonstrates **granular authorization**.

A refusal is not necessarily global relational rejection.

## 9.4 Hachiman's collaborator reliance improves

Volume 13 contains some of the clearest positive reliance in the entire series.

Hachiman:

- discloses more about the dummy project's sacrificial nature;
- accepts Kawasaki's refusal;
- asks her to continue supporting Yukino instead;
- accepts bounded help from Totsuka;
- thanks collaborators;
- says `頼りにしてる`.

This matters because the same volume contains dependency anxiety.

Any theory that classifies all reliance as pathology immediately fails to explain why Hachiman's ability to rely on collaborators is framed as growth.

## 9.5 Hachiman's attempted proof against `共依存` fails

Hachiman states:

> `あいつが助けを必要としてなくて、それでも俺が助けたいと思うなら……、それは共依存なんかじゃない。それが証明できればいい`

There is a real discovery inside this sentence.

His desire to act survives the removal of **being needed** as a condition. That weakens any total theory that his attachment is nothing more than the reward of dependency.

But the conclusion is logically too strong.

Wanting to help someone who does not need help does not prove that no reinforcing dependency mechanisms remain.

Haruno's narrow critique is correct:

> `雪乃ちゃんは助けを求めてない、君は勝手にやってる……だから、共依存じゃない……そんなの言葉の上だけ`

Changing the wording can leave the structure partly unchanged.

The correct V2 transition is therefore:

> **STRENGTHEN the evidence that Hachiman's motive exceeds neededness; REJECT the claim that this fact alone disproves `共依存`.**

## 9.6 Yukino's self-description is stronger than Haruno's label

Near the end Yukino says she had naturally come to expect Hachiman would somehow make the prom work:

> `そう思うくらい、……あなたに依存していたの`

This is strong first-person evidence.

It establishes that Yukino recognizes a dependency mechanism in **expectation and burden allocation**.

It still does not establish:

- inability;
- total emotional dependence;
- falsity of affection;
- or the necessity of permanent separation.

Indeed, the next volume will show that support can continue after incapacity has been explicitly denied.

## 9.7 Self-authored closure can still overreach

Yukino uses the wager to ask Hachiman to fulfill Yui's wish and confirms that this is her own wish.

This is genuine authorship.

It is also incomplete autonomy ethics because the request attempts to allocate Hachiman and Yui's future.

Hachiman's correction is decisive:

> `これは俺とお前が勝手に決めることじゃない。由比ヶ浜も勝負に加わってる`

No two members of the triad possess jurisdiction over the third.

This is one of the strongest reasons V2 treats self-authorship and ethical adequacy as distinct.

# 10. V14 — separation cure rejected; chosen interdependence established

## 10.1 The volume dismantles borrowed explanations

Volume 14 does not decide the dependency problem by proving Haruno wrong in every detail.

It does something more precise.

The protagonists stop letting the diagnosis choose for them.

`共依存` is reduced from total explanation to a bounded mechanism-level description. The characters must still decide:

- what they want;
- whom they want involved;
- what form of support they accept;
- what loss they will impose or bear;
- and what future they are willing to claim.

## 10.2 Hachiman removes need as justification

The decisive change in Hachiman's relation to Yukino is not that he proves she needs him.

He does the opposite.

He reaches a point where continued involvement cannot be justified by:

- club duty;
- a client request;
- rescue necessity;
- Yukino's incapacity;
- an old promise alone;
- or the diagnostic need to prove the relationship healthy.

He wants the relationship because he wants it.

This matters for dependency because it separates:

> **desire for involvement**

from

> **need to be needed as the moral license for involvement**.

The latter mechanism has not necessarily vanished forever, but it no longer governs the final choice.

## 10.3 The bridge scene is not autonomy-perfect

The bridge scene must not be sanitized.

Hachiman:

- engineers the encounter;
- physically stops Yukino for a moment;
- initially speaks in language that partly devalues himself;
- and knows she may not want the same thing he wants.

Those are genuine procedural defects.

But the scene does not end with physical or rhetorical capture.

His hold loosens. Yukino remains. She:

- questions his terms;
- rejects his valuation of herself;
- requires clearer speech;
- and makes a reciprocal claim.

The scene therefore transitions from a flawed opening into **answerable negotiation**.

That distinction matters more than trying to classify the entire confession as either coercive or perfectly autonomous.

## 10.4 Yukino's reciprocal request

Yukino's response culminates in a request for Hachiman's life.

The significance for this ledger is not romantic grandeur by itself. It is the movement from being the object of rescue or diagnosis into a person making a reciprocal claim.

She is not saying:

> I cannot stand without you.

She is saying, in effect:

> I want a future in which we continue to make claims on one another.

This is interdependence language, not incapacity language.

## 10.5 `一人で立てるのに` / `知ってる`

The later hand exchange is the cleanest canonical image of the final autonomy model.

Yukino observes that she can stand alone.

Hachiman says he knows.

The hand remains offered and accepted.

This exchange rejects a premise that has distorted much of the earlier series:

> help is legitimate only if the other person cannot manage without it.

The mature alternative is:

> **support can be chosen even when independent capability is acknowledged.**

This is why "chosen interdependence" is a better terminal description than either dependence or independence.

## 10.6 Yui: nonreciprocity without social expulsion

Yui's arc is also essential to the autonomy conclusion.

She receives a real romantic answer. She is hurt. Continued friendship does not erase the loss.

But neither Yukino nor Hachiman gets to decide that Yui must now disappear for everyone's health.

Yui has already refused that logic.

Her continued place in Yukino's life is independently authored. Her return to the club is not simply a restoration of the old ambiguity; it is continued relation after non-equivalence becomes known.

The ending therefore rejects **administrative severance** as an automatic response to asymmetrical desire.

## 10.7 Romance does not certify health

The Hachiman/Yukino answer cannot be used as a shortcut:

> they became a couple, therefore the prior dependency diagnosis was false.

That would repeat the same category error as treating separation as proof of health.

The more defensible conclusion is narrower:

- the couple explicitly acknowledges capability;
- support continues anyway;
- Hachiman owns desire beyond neededness;
- Yukino makes reciprocal claims;
- Yui retains subjecthood;
- the relation remains answerable and uncertain.

These are stronger autonomy indicators than romantic status itself.

# 11. V14.5 — ordinary-life durability and the critique of noninterference

## 11.1 Diagnostic vocabulary disappears

The complete V14.5 text contains zero literal occurrences of:

- `依存`;
- `共依存`;
- `本物`;
- `偽物`.

The absence should not be overread. It does not prove every mechanism vanished.

What it does show is that ordinary post-ending interaction no longer requires the old diagnostic vocabulary as a master frame.

The problem is tested behaviorally.

## 11.2 Reliance without incapacity

Yukino wants to consider another cram school with Hachiman and eventually says:

> `一緒の方が頑張れると思うし`

This is reliance language without `依存` language.

She can study alone. The shared environment is valuable because it supports motivation and proximity.

That is precisely the kind of case a binary model cannot explain. If reliance equals pathology, this looks suspect. If autonomy equals authorship under preserved capability, it looks ordinary and healthy enough.

## 11.3 Separate trajectories remain visible

Hachiman explicitly rejects Yukino lowering her academic ambition to match him.

The relationship therefore does not require institutional fusion.

They can:

- assume future meetings;
- prefer shared activity;
- and remain attached

while retaining different academic routes.

This is one of the strongest durability tests for chosen interdependence.

## 11.4 Komachi and succession

Komachi inherits the Service Club presidency.

But neither Hachiman nor Yukino gets to convert inheritance into identity capture.

The new club may recruit different people, make different mistakes, and become something else.

Hachiman's eventual recognition that he had begun to `神聖視` the existing relationship form strengthens the autonomy finding: preservation itself can become control if the old participants claim jurisdiction over what successors are allowed to make from the institution.

## 11.5 `自分で決めればいい` can be an autonomy failure

The most important post-ending correction for this ledger is Hachiman's recognition that simply telling Komachi:

- `好きにしたらいい`;
- `自分で決めればいい`

would be an escape and a transfer of responsibility.

This is a major philosophical correction.

A thin autonomy model says:

> Do not influence another person's choice.

The mature Oregairu model is harder:

> **Influence is inevitable in meaningful relationships. The ethical task is to give honest advice, disclose one's stake, avoid substituting one's will, and leave the other person's answer revisable.**

Thus noninterference can be just as evasive as overcontrol.

# 12. Hachiman dependency / autonomy route

## 12.1 Early state: defensive autonomy

Hachiman's early self-sufficiency protects against:

- hope;
- debt;
- humiliation;
- and the risk that kindness means something he wants it to mean.

His behavior nevertheless shows growing dependence on the existence of a social world he refuses to name.

## 12.2 Solver identity: being useful becomes a relational license

Across the middle volumes, usefulness gives Hachiman a way to remain close without asking for closeness.

The Service Club request structure is ideal for this defense:

- another person supplies the problem;
- the club supplies the reason to interact;
- Hachiman supplies the method;
- the result supplies proof that his presence was justified.

This structure can reinforce the belief that he deserves relation only when useful.

## 12.3 Being needed and self-worth

By V12 the neededness mechanism becomes explicit enough to support the codependency diagnosis.

Hachiman's growth is not learning never to enjoy being needed. It is learning that **neededness cannot be the only permissible reason to remain**.

That is why V13's failed proof remains developmentally useful. Even though "I want to help when she does not need me" does not disprove codependency, it reveals a motive beyond functional necessity.

## 12.4 Healthy reliance on others

Hachiman also has to learn the inverse skill: relying on other people without converting help into debt, weakness, or humiliation.

His `ありがとう、頼りにしてる` to Totsuka and his improved collaborator practice are crucial because they show that the cure for unhealthy dependence is not unilateral self-sufficiency.

## 12.5 Terminal state

By V14–14.5, Hachiman can:

- own desire;
- accept Yukino's capability;
- offer support anyway;
- assume a future without requiring institutional sameness;
- give Komachi advice without owning her answer;
- and recognize his own tendency to sanctify relational form.

He remains prone to overengineering, indirectness, and rationalization.

The terminal state is therefore not "autonomous Hachiman." It is **Hachiman with a more answerable relationship to dependence, influence, and desire**.

---

# 13. Yukino dependency / autonomy route

## 13.1 Competence is real and should not be psychologized away

Yukino's competence is not merely a defense.

She is actually capable.

The autonomy problem arises when competence becomes:

- overfunctioning;
- a reason others stop practicing responsibility;
- or a reason Yukino believes she must not become the object of care.

## 13.2 Family structure and underdeveloped authorship practice

Yukino's later difficulty choosing is not reducible to Hachiman.

Her family history produces a more complex problem:

- Haruno's route is heavily predetermined;
- Yukino is nominally freer;
- but her freedom is not equivalent to being taken seriously as a chooser;
- comparison with Haruno supplies a ready-made model of competence and life direction;
- family language can define "freedom" while shaping the available meaning of each answer.

Thus autonomy requires more than the absence of explicit commands.

## 13.3 Reliance as learned permission

Yukino repeatedly shows that reliance is possible but difficult to claim personally.

Her development includes learning that:

- asking for help is permissible;
- needing company does not invalidate competence;
- sharing work does not automatically mean surrendering authorship;
- and support can be chosen for reasons other than incapacity.

## 13.4 V12–V13 self-critique

Yukino's `依存` language is important because she recognizes a real expectation structure around Hachiman.

She had begun to assume he would somehow make things work.

The corrective value of the prom is therefore not "prove I do not need him emotionally." It is "practice owning a difficult choice and its consequences without making his rescue the default condition."

## 13.5 V14–14.5 terminal state

The final state combines:

- independent capability;
- explicit romantic desire;
- reciprocal claims;
- shared study preference;
- separate academic trajectories;
- and the capacity to teach Komachi without reproducing herself through Komachi.

The canonical formulation is:

> **Yukino becomes more autonomous not by needing less relation, but by gaining greater authorship over the relation she chooses.**

---

# 14. Yui dependency / autonomy route

## 14.1 Relational skill is not pathology

Yui's social orientation is often treated as evidence of weakness in compressed readings.

V2 rejects that.

She is one of the series' most active builders of relational infrastructure.

Her risk lies in accommodation becoming a way to postpone irreversible choice.

## 14.2 Preservation and desire

Yui wants:

- Hachiman;
- Yukino;
- the Service Club;
- ordinary time;
- and preservation of a social world in which those desires can coexist.

The problem is not that the desire is selfish. The problem arises when preservation begins deciding the structure before everyone has answered.

## 14.3 `あたしは彼女に依存したの`

Yui's V12 admission is a major self-critique.

She recognizes that she partly relied on Yukino to enact or trigger the decision Yui herself feared.

That is a dependency mechanism in decision ownership.

It does not erase the fact that Yui remains capable of:

- asking;
- refusing pity;
- recognizing her own motives;
- challenging Yukino;
- and continuing friendship after loss.

## 14.4 Refusing self-removal as solution

By V13, Yui explicitly rejects a future in which Yukino must disappear in order to settle the triangle.

This is autonomy-positive because Yui authors the friendship independently of romantic outcome.

## 14.5 Terminal state

Yui receives a painful nonreciprocal answer and remains socially present.

That continued presence should not be romanticized into proof of effortless health. Pain remains.

But it demonstrates that asymmetry does not automatically require relational deletion.

Her post-ending role confirms ongoing agency rather than suspended waiting.

---

# 15. Iroha as a test case: compromised origin can become owned role

Iroha is valuable to the dependency ledger because her presidency begins under coercive conditions.

She neither wants the office nor wants the humiliation of losing or withdrawing.

Hachiman changes the incentive environment until she chooses presidency.

This is not pure autonomy.

Yet later volumes show Iroha:

- performing actual presidential work;
- acquiring institutional competence;
- wanting projects for herself;
- noticing over-reliance on Yukino;
- converting imposed office into self-owned practice.

The broader lesson is:

> **autonomy is not only about pristine origin. It can also involve later appropriation, revision, and ownership of a role one did not freely originate.**

This parallels the authenticity ledger's conclusion that compromised origin does not determine final ontology.

---

# 16. Komachi as the cleanest positive control

Komachi provides the series' least crisis-distorted example of secure dependence and autonomy.

Her relationship with Hachiman includes:

- emotional reliance;
- advice;
- care;
- influence;
- practical help;
- and ordinary family intimacy.

Yet increasing independence is treated as development rather than relational betrayal.

Her entrance exam clarifies the structure:

- Hachiman can support;
- he can wait;
- he can reassure;
- he can make failure survivable;
- but he cannot take the exam for her.

Later, when Komachi inherits the Service Club, the same structure appears institutionally. Hachiman and Yukino can:

- teach;
- advise;
- hand over practices;
- express preferences;

without owning the successor institution.

Komachi therefore demonstrates the positive model before the protagonists can fully articulate it:

> **secure reliance can make autonomy easier rather than harder.**

---

# 17. Hiratsuka: autonomy through procedure, not abandonment

Hiratsuka's method evolves from comparatively coercive educational intervention toward question-preserving guidance.

Her strongest contributions to the dependency domain are:

- Hachiman's self-cost has relational externalities;
- being corrected means someone remains in relation;
- adults cannot supply the final answer;
- people may need repeated lived experience before they understand themselves;
- and `大事なのは関わり方`.

She neither teaches radical self-sufficiency nor paternalistic rescue.

Her mature model is developmental relation:

> be involved, expose contradiction, preserve the question, let the younger person answer, and accept that the relation itself will eventually change.

That model is one of the foundations for the final chosen-interdependence thesis.

# 18. Haruno: valuable diagnosis, overextended cure

## 18.1 What Haruno sees correctly

Haruno identifies several genuine mechanisms:

- role entrenchment;
- avoidance disguised as kindness;
- the reinforcement of Hachiman's neededness identity;
- Yukino's reliance;
- the trio's preference for arrangements that postpone choice;
- and the possibility that apparently selfless behavior can preserve the very structure causing the problem.

These insights should not be rejected merely because Haruno is provocative.

## 18.2 Where the diagnosis overreaches

Haruno's authority becomes weaker when she moves from:

> there is a reinforcing mechanism

to:

> therefore independence/separation is the correct cure and will reveal the truth.

The novels do not grant that second proposition narrator status.

Counterevidence includes:

- healthy reliance throughout the series;
- Komachi's secure dependence/autonomy model;
- Hachiman's growth through asking for help;
- Yukino's final support under known capability;
- Yui/Yukino friendship surviving romantic asymmetry;
- the final refusal to use separation as proof;
- Haruno's own autobiographical investment in finding one true ending.

## 18.3 Canonical Haruno transition

**PRESERVE:** Haruno identifies real reinforcement structures.

**DOWNGRADE:** Haruno as sovereign interpreter of what the structure means emotionally.

**REJECT as governing doctrine:** separation or maximal independence as the automatically correct remedy.

---

# 19. Hayama: expectation, public role, and constrained authorship

Hayama complicates autonomy in a different direction.

He appears socially powerful. Yet Volume 10 reveals a person whose public role is saturated by expectation.

He is neither simply fake nor simply trapped.

Parts of the role are imposed; parts are genuinely his; parts may have become inseparable from what he now wants.

This shows why autonomy cannot be measured only by visible power or freedom of action.

A person can have many available actions and still experience authorship as constrained by:

- family expectation;
- group dependence;
- reputation;
- social centrality;
- and the cost imposed on others by deviation.

Hachiman's later refusal to inherit Hayama's childhood regret is also important. Proxy guilt does not create jurisdiction over another person's present relationship.

# 20. The `共依存` evidence audit

## 20.1 What the diagnosis explains well

The diagnosis is strongest when applied to **mechanisms**, especially:

### Hachiman's neededness loop

Helping supplies value, role, closeness, and a reason to avoid naked desire.

### Yukino's expectation loop

Hachiman's repeated intervention can make it easier to assume he will somehow solve the difficult problem.

### Yui's decision-displacement loop

Preserving everyone can become easier when the decisive renunciation is routed through Yukino.

### Triadic avoidance

The Service Club can become a structure in which tasks, rules, requests, and preservation substitute for direct choice.

### Mutual role reinforcement

The more one person overfunctions, the more another person can underpractice; the more the latter relies, the more the former's identity as solver is reinforced.

These mechanisms are real enough that simply dismissing `共依存` would be analytically weak.

## 20.2 What the diagnosis does not establish

The evidence does **not** establish that:

- the characters' feelings are counterfeit;
- all three participants are equally dependent in the same way;
- Yukino is incapable without Hachiman;
- Yui lacks agency;
- Hachiman acts only because he wants to be needed;
- healthy reliance is impossible among them;
- the relationship must end;
- independence will automatically solve the underlying problems;
- romantic choice proves the mechanism gone;
- continued friendship proves the mechanism harmless;
- or Haruno has unique authority to classify the relationship.

## 20.3 The diagnosis is asymmetrical

Each character's mechanism is different.

| Character | Strongest dependency mechanism | Not established |
|---|---|---|
| Hachiman | self-worth / relational license through being needed and solving | inability to relate except through rescue |
| Yukino | expectation of Hachiman's intervention; difficulty practicing self-authorship under family/relational structures | general incapacity or desire for permanent rescue |
| Yui | decision displacement and preservation through others' choices | friendship itself as pathology |
| Haruno | investment in exposing false endings; interpretive role may itself become identity | neutral diagnostic sovereignty |

The label is therefore more useful as a **system map** than as a character essence.

# 21. Remedy audit

## 21.1 Total separation

**Supporting argument:**

Separation can interrupt reinforcement and force practice of independent action.

**Evidence in favor:**

- Yukino benefits from owning the prom;
- Hachiman must lose rescue as automatic justification;
- Yui must stop routing the choice elsewhere;
- changing structure can reveal hidden habits.

**Limit:**

The text does not establish separation as the unique or final remedy. In V14, the protagonists reject closure built primarily from borrowed diagnosis. V14.5 then shows durable reliance without diagnostic language.

**Judgment:** **REVISE / DOWNGRADE as universal cure.** Useful intervention in some domains; not governing endpoint.

## 21.2 Maximal independence

**Supporting argument:**

Each person should stand alone and make choices without relying on another.

**Counterevidence:**

- reliance is repeatedly framed positively;
- Hachiman grows by asking for help;
- Yukino can stand alone and still take the hand;
- Komachi's secure reliance supports independent action;
- V14.5 rejects `自分で決めればいい` as sufficient.

**Judgment:** **REJECT as mature series doctrine.**

## 21.3 Noninterference

**Supporting argument:**

Respect another person's choice by doing nothing unless explicitly requested.

**Counterevidence:**

- meaningful relationships create legitimate reasons to advise or act;
- responsibilities may be plural;
- silence can itself preserve harmful structures;
- Hachiman's promise and personal stake do not disappear because Yukino refuses one form of help;
- V14.5 explicitly frames pure "decide for yourself" as responsibility transfer.

**Judgment:** **REVISE.** Noninterference may respect a boundary in a specific domain, but it is not a complete relational ethic.

## 21.4 Answerable involvement

This is the strongest procedural remedy in the completed V2 ledgers.

It requires:

- disclose motive where relevant;
- distinguish one's reason to act from authority to decide;
- preserve meaningful refusal;
- avoid substitution when the subject can and wants to own the act;
- allow consequences to remain the subject's;
- accept revision;
- and remain willing to hear an answer one does not want.

**Judgment:** **STRENGTHEN.** This is the dependency-domain counterpart to the Request / Intervention Ethics Ledger.

## 21.5 Chosen interdependence

Chosen interdependence is the strongest terminal relational model because it explains evidence that independence theories cannot:

- Yukino can stand alone and still accept support;
- Hachiman can want involvement without needing Yukino to be incapable;
- Yui can remain close after romantic nonreciprocity;
- separate academic trajectories coexist with shared future assumptions;
- Komachi can inherit and revise the club;
- the relationship can change without being invalidated.

**Judgment:** **STRENGTHEN as terminal V2 synthesis, subject to Phase-6 adversarial audit.**

# 22. Autonomy matrix

The following dimensions should govern later specialist synthesis.

| Dimension | Failure mode | Mature positive indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Capability | another person's help replaces practiced capacity | support given despite known capability |
| Origin | desire/action exists mainly because someone else authored it | subject can state a first-person reason |
| Information | subject acts under concealed material conditions | relevant stakes disclosed |
| Refusal | no is ignored, punished, or treated as preliminary | refusal changes method or stops action in that domain |
| Revision | one choice becomes permanent jurisdiction | terms can be renegotiated |
| Consequence ownership | helper absorbs all failure/cost | subject can own success, failure, regret, or relinquishment |
| Jurisdiction | insight/competence/love treated as authority | reason to act separated from right to decide |
| Reliance | support becomes default substitution or identity trap | reliance can be named, bounded, and reciprocally chosen |
| Difference | disagreement treated as relational death | different desires can remain in relation |
| Succession | old relationship claims ownership of future form | practices can be inherited without identity replication |

No single row alone proves autonomy. The point is multidimensional assessment.

# 25. Cross-ledger constraints

This dependency ledger must remain compatible with the previously completed Phase-4 ledgers.

## Hachiman epistemic / narratorial ledger

Dependency diagnosis cannot promote Hachiman's inference into fact. His reliability remains domain-specific.

## Character-state ledger

Character development is procedural and relational rather than essence replacement. The endpoint is greater authorship under acknowledged incompleteness.

## Relationship-state ledger

Relationships are multidimensional. Trust, reliance, disclosure, romantic asymmetry, authorization, and ordinary presence can move independently.

## Request / intervention ethics ledger

Knowing about someone, caring about someone, having reason to act, and having authority to decide are distinct.

The mature intervention standard remains **answerable involvement**.

## Authenticity / `本物` ledger

Dependence is not falsity. Independence does not certify authenticity. Performance, mixed motives, reliance, and uncertainty may remain inside a meaningful relation.

The dependency ledger therefore cannot reintroduce a purity model under the language of autonomy.

# 26. What the series ultimately rejects

The mature V2 corpus rejects several attractive simplifications.

## 26.1 "Healthy people do not need others"

Rejected.

The novels repeatedly give positive value to reliance, mentoring, friendship, family care, collaboration, and chosen support.

## 26.2 "If you can do it alone, accepting help is weakness"

Rejected explicitly by the V14 hand exchange.

## 26.3 "If you love someone, intervention is authorized"

Rejected.

Love supplies motive, not jurisdiction.

## 26.4 "If someone says they are independent, they are autonomous"

Rejected.

Self-description must be tested against structure, practice, and whether choices are actually heard.

Rejected as a universal rule.

> **Oregairu's dependency problem is not that its characters care too much, rely too much, or fail to become self-sufficient enough. The recurring danger is that care, competence, neededness, fear of loss, and social role can harden into jurisdiction: one person solves for another, one person's weakness justifies another's place, one person's reluctance is converted into someone else's decision, or preservation of the relationship becomes more authoritative than the people inside it. The mature endpoint is therefore chosen interdependence: capability is preserved, support is permitted, desire can create claims without creating sovereignty, refusal has force, responsibility is not simply transferred in the name of freedom, and relationships remain revisable rather than sacred.**
