# Oregairu V2 — Checkpoint A: Early Service Club Formation

## Purpose of this checkpoint

This document freezes the state of *Yahari Ore no Seishun Love Come wa Machigatteiru.* at the end of **Volume 3**.

It is not a miniature full-series synthesis. It is not an argument about where Hachiman, Yukino, Yui, or the Service Club will ultimately end. It exists to preserve what a careful reader could responsibly say after Volumes 1–3, before the later cultural-festival, Kyoto, election, Christmas, prom, family, and final-choice material reorganizes the apparent meaning of these early relationships.

The governing rule is strict:

> **Volumes 1–3 may establish the frozen state. Volumes 4–5 may only reveal concepts that must not be smuggled backward into it.**

Accordingly, later findings such as the explicit distinction between chosen solitude and maliciously imposed isolation, `解消` versus `解決`, Hachiman's cultural-festival self-sacrifice pattern, Yukino's later family comparison structure, Hachiman's explicit recognition of his idealized `超人性`, and the mature epistemic problem of revising one's model of another person are **not evidence for this checkpoint**. They are listed only in the contamination audit so that the early snapshot remains genuinely prospective.

The result is an early Service Club that is already much richer than its opening premise suggests, but much less theoretically settled than retrospective memory tends to make it.

---

# 1. Executive frozen thesis

By the end of Volume 3, the Service Club has become an **adult-created institution that three students are beginning to convert into a chosen relational form without yet possessing a stable language for what that relation is**.

Each member enters the club with a different strategy for surviving relation.

**Hachiman** preserves himself through suspicion, preemptive rejection, narrow functional categories, and rules that keep expectation low. His social intelligence is often excellent when he analyzes structures outside himself. His self-interpretation becomes less reliable when another person's care might be particular to him or when he must admit that he wants continuity. His early problem is not simple loneliness. He has real solitary pleasures and a defensible critique of coercive sociality. The problem is that he repeatedly turns protection from humiliation into a rule against granting uncertain relationships behavioral standing.

**Yukino** enters with a sincere competence-and-reform ethic. She believes helping should increase another person's capacity rather than merely deliver outcomes, rejects the celebration of weakness, and treats effort and self-reliance as moral goods. Her ability is real, not merely defensive theater. Yet Volumes 1–3 also show that she is inexperienced at receiving affection, can misdiagnose people when her conceptual model is too abstract, can revise herself in response to evidence, and can ask for bounded practical help without surrendering authorship. Her difficulty is therefore **not** “she cannot rely on anyone.” At this point it is better described as a preference for legible, bounded forms of relation and a still-limited vocabulary for personal attachment.

**Yui** enters as the most socially fluent of the three, but “mediator” is already too passive a term. She reads atmosphere, anticipates exclusion, names relationships, asks for contact, maintains communication, gives Yukino a nickname, explicitly invokes friendship, claims the Service Club as an important place, imagines future continuity, asks why people are acting rather than merely accepting surface behavior, and repeatedly pushes Hachiman and Yukino toward continued relation. She does sometimes adapt excessively to others, but by Volume 3 she is already an **architect of relation**: someone who turns her own desire for continued connection into a social fact others must answer.

The triad is therefore not yet a settled friendship, romance, dependency system, or “genuine” relationship. It is an **emergent relational institution** whose members repeatedly use requests, club work, favors, celebrations, jokes, functional labels, and controlled ambiguity to remain in contact without having to state the full stakes of that contact.

The most important early tension is:

> **Can relation remain voluntary and particular without becoming a debt, a coercive claim, an impossible demand for certainty, or a threat to self-authorship?**

Volumes 1–3 do not answer that question. They establish it.

# 3. Hachiman — frozen state at the end of Volume 3

## 3.1 Initial worldview: valid critique fused to defensive overgeneralization

Hachiman begins as a boy whose social pessimism is neither pure truth nor pure delusion.

He correctly recognizes:

- classroom hierarchy;
- the coercive potential of friendship language;
- how `everyone` can erase the person who is not actually included;
- how status and public narrative distribute recognition unevenly;
- how group structure can differ from the friendship story its members tell about themselves;
- how shared contexts such as class, club, school, and repeated communication help keep relationships connected.

The early reread therefore rejects the easy formulation that Hachiman is simply an antisocial boy who must discover that normal friendship is good.

His critique of conventional youth has real objects.

But his defensive system repeatedly converts partial truths into universal rules.

The clearest early example is conscious preemptive rejection:

- V01-E02: `嫌われてしまうのが一番早い` — printed p. 26.

Hachiman knows that he can avoid romantic humiliation by making himself unlikable first. This is not an unconscious tendency invented retrospectively; the defensive logic is already available to him as self-description.

Volume 1's closing essay nevertheless makes a significant correction. He affirms his solitary first year without requiring that everyone else's youth be fraudulent:

- V01-E18: `決して否定しない。力強く肯定しよう` — p. 301.
- V01-E19: self-affirmation need not negate others' days — p. 301.
- V01-E20: the current place may someday appear to shine — p. 301.

Checkpoint A should therefore freeze his early movement as:

> **zero-sum self-legitimation → tentative pluralism.**

He does not abandon solitude. He becomes less dependent on proving that sociability is false in order to preserve the value of solitude.

## 3.2 Narratorial reliability is already domain-specific

By the end of Volume 3, it is already unsatisfactory to label Hachiman generically “unreliable.”

He is often highly reliable about:

- group structure;
- status;
- exclusion;
- material constraints once they become visible;
- instrumental consequences;
- the social mechanics of requests;
- how public narratives misattribute contribution.

His vulnerability is more specific:

- he notices relational possibility but resists allowing favorable uncertainty to affect behavior;
- he is prone to converting past humiliation into predictive certainty;
- he can confuse an alternative motive with proof that a more personal motive has no standing;
- he is more comfortable explaining why another person's care is **not** particular to him than testing whether it might be.

Volume 2 makes this especially visible after the dog-accident revelation.

The frozen three-stage model is:

1. **Notice a possibility.** Hachiman is often capable of this.
2. **Assign probability.** His internal estimate is frequently ambiguous.
3. **Grant the favorable possibility behavioral standing.** He is strongly defensive here.

This distinction is more precise than saying he is “oblivious.”

## 3.3 Hachiman is capable of collaboration when the problem is safely external

Volumes 1–2 provide important counterevidence to any later totalizing claim that Hachiman cannot ask for help.

He can:

- use Hiratsuka as an adult resource;
- ask Zaimokuza for specialized help;
- coordinate Yui, Yukino, Komachi, Taishi, Hayama, and others;
- research scholarship structures;
- accept that another person's expertise solves a problem better than his own;
- condition an intervention on Hayama's authorization.

The early difficulty is not simply **receiving assistance**.

It is closer to:

> **being the object of care when accepting the care would create an emotionally dangerous claim about his importance to another person.**

That distinction must remain frozen because it prevents later “Hachiman never relies on anyone” shorthand from erasing early evidence.

## 3.4 Pleasure in usefulness is ordinary at this checkpoint

Volume 1 gives the clean statement:

- V01-E11: `頼りにされるとそれなりに嬉しい` — p. 221.

Checkpoint A classifies this as **ordinary gratification in being trusted and useful**.

There is not yet enough evidence to diagnose:

- a need-to-be-needed complex;
- identity dependence on usefulness;
- compulsive self-sacrifice;
- codependency.

The correct action is to preserve the datum for longitudinal comparison, not reinterpret it through later pathology.

## 3.5 Expectation avoidance is more developed than self-sacrifice

Volume 3 makes one defensive principle especially clear:

- V3-L16: mutual nonexpectation feels comfortable — p. 120.
- V3-L32: he resolves not to expect at beginning, middle, or end — p. 239.

At Checkpoint A, Hachiman's more mature problem is therefore **expectation avoidance**.

Expectation is dangerous because it can produce:

- disappointment;
- obligation;
- evidence that another person's behavior mattered;
- a claim that Hachiman himself mattered;
- a future whose loss would hurt.

This is why safe categories matter so much. Saika's gender category supplies `絶対の安心感` (V3-L06, p. 45). Yukino and Hachiman can temporarily perform `恋人のように` precisely because they are not actually lovers (V3-L14, p. 119). Functional categories reduce the risk that proximity will impose a claim Hachiman cannot easily dismiss.

## 3.6 The “zero account” is the strongest early warning

The most consequential Hachiman intervention in Volume 3 is relational rather than institutional.

He tries to define Yui's kindness as debt from the accident and proposes a moral accounting in which repayment makes the balance zero:

- V3-L26: `チャラ` — p. 237.
- V3-L27: `差し引きゼロでチャラ` — p. 237.

The ethical problem is not yet later self-sacrifice. It is **unilateral interpretive authorship**.

Hachiman assigns:

- the origin of Yui's kindness;
- the motive behind it;
- the amount of debt;
- the value of his repayment;
- and the point at which the relationship should end.

Yui directly rejects his motive attribution (V3-L29, p. 238).

Checkpoint A should therefore preserve the early warning as:

> **Hachiman can become controlling not because he wants domination, but because he tries to eliminate uncertain relational claims by solving their meaning unilaterally.**

That is an early mechanism. It is not yet permission to call every later ugly solution the same thing.

# 4. Yukino — frozen state at the end of Volume 3

## 4.1 Competence is real before it is defensive

Yukino enters as genuinely exceptional.

Her skill, intelligence, discipline, verbal precision, and confidence are not a façade waiting to be exposed as false. Volume 1 supports competence as:

- actual ability;
- pride;
- ethical conviction;
- a mode of interaction;
- and a source of social distance.

This is why the V2 method refuses to let later knowledge rewrite early competence as “armor” in every scene.

At Checkpoint A the safer formulation is:

> **Yukino experiences competence as both ability and moral responsibility; its defensive function is possible but not yet governing.**

## 4.2 Her early ethic is reformist and autonomy-oriented

Two Volume 1 anchors establish her early philosophy:

- V01-E05: `だから変えるのよ、人ごと、この世界を` — p. 69.
- V01-E07: `魚の獲り方を教えるか` — p. 87.

She believes:

- weakness should not simply be affirmed;
- helping should increase capability;
- reform is preferable to passive acceptance;
- the helper should not merely substitute their labor for the other person's development.

This is a serious ethic, not only a personality quirk.

Yet the cookie case exposes its first limitation. Technical self-reliance is not identical to understanding what the requester actually wants. A perfectly autonomy-oriented method can still misdiagnose the problem.

The Kawasaki case repeats the broader lesson in Volume 2: everyone initially misreads the apparent delinquency problem until material constraints and Kawasaki's own goal are named.

Thus Yukino's early ethical strength already requires a counterprinciple:

> **respect for agency is not enough if the helper's causal model is wrong.**

## 4.3 She is not incapable of affection; she lacks practiced language for it

Yui rapidly destabilizes Yukino's expectations about intimacy.

Volume 1 shows Yukino:

- bewildered that bluntness can be received positively;
- watching Yui's confrontation with Miura;
- smiling at Yui's success;
- accepting that Yui may regard her as a friend;
- acting on Yui's important request;
- retreating from an almost-friend claim into `うちの部員` when claiming Hachiman.

Key anchors:

- V01-E14: `友達と思われるのも別に、構わない` — p. 272.
- V01-E15: `うちの部員を` — p. 276.

The linguistic retreat is more useful than a generic “tsundere” label. It shows that **institutional language is safer than personal language**.

By Volume 3, Yukino's relationship with Yui is particular enough that she wants to celebrate Yui's birthday regardless of whether Yui returns to the club:

- V3-L11: celebration without leverage — p. 96.
- V3-L21: gratitude is something Yukino herself wants to express — p. 162.

This is an important agency-preserving form of care.

## 4.4 Yukino can rely on others in bounded ways

Checkpoint A must explicitly reject the claim that Yukino “cannot rely on anyone.”

Volume 3 provides direct counterevidence:

- V3-L13: Yukino recognizes a limitation and asks Hachiman for bounded help — p. 118.
- V3-L25: Yukino says Yui's presence helps both her and Hachiman — p. 231.
- V3-L39/40, supplementary bonus material: Hiratsuka says she is relying on Yui; Yui is happy to be relied upon.

The early frozen distinction is:

> **bounded reliance can coexist with authorship.**

Yukino can define the purpose of an action, ask another person for competence she lacks, accept help, and remain the author of the underlying desire.

Whatever later dependency problem emerges must therefore be more specific than inability to rely.

## 4.5 Honesty is both a real trait and already becoming Hachiman's ideal

Checkpoint A must hold two propositions simultaneously.

First, Yukino really does value directness and resists convenient falsification.

Second, Hachiman has already begun converting this trait into an essence:

- V01-E16: `虚言だけは吐いたことがない` / subsequent trust — p. 284.
- V3-L15: `雪ノ下は噓を吐かない` — p. 119.

Volume 3 intensifies the problem when Hachiman claims to know Yukino's `本物の笑顔` (V3-L20, p. 152).

But Checkpoint A cannot yet say that his ideal will break or explain exactly how. It can only freeze the epistemic risk:

> **Hachiman increasingly treats a real pattern in Yukino's behavior as privileged access to a complete authentic essence.**

That is a prospective warning, not a retrospective verdict.

# 5. Yui — frozen state at the end of Volume 3

## 5.1 “Social mediation” is insufficient; Yui is an early relational architect

The architecture's shorthand calls for Yui's “social mediation.” The V2 reread supports that but goes further.

Yui does not merely smooth interactions after other people create them. She repeatedly creates the interaction itself.

Across Volumes 1–3 she:

- enters the Service Club despite its hostile atmosphere;
- names Hachiman `ヒッキー`;
- names Yukino `ゆきのん`;
- recognizes Yukino's bluntness as `本音` rather than simple cruelty;
- returns to Miura and renegotiates rather than fleeing the friendship;
- calls Yukino a friend;
- defines important requests as something entrusted to friends;
- claims the Service Club as another important place;
- asks Hachiman for direct contact information;
- maintains an independent message relationship with Yukino;
- imagines future continuity through school/university;
- identifies the exclusion mechanism in Hayama's group;
- asks the causal question in the Kawasaki case;
- returns after the Volume 3 distance rather than allowing disappearance to become the answer;
- explicitly rejects Hachiman's interpretation of her motive;
- says she does not want the relationship to end.

This is not passive niceness.

The frozen formulation is:

> **Yui is the early Service Club's primary relational constitutor: she repeatedly converts private wishes for connection into names, requests, routines, future assumptions, and explicit claims that other people must answer.**

## 5.2 Her atmosphere-reading is both strength and risk

Yui is highly attentive to:

- social rank;
- exclusion;
- embarrassment;
- when a person is hesitating;
- whether direct action will threaten belonging;
- when someone needs a softer route into relation.

This makes her effective at keeping multiple social worlds open.

Volume 1 is crucial here: Yui does not solve coercive friendship with Miura by declaring the popular group “fake.” She renegotiates her position and chooses both that world and the Service Club.

Checkpoint A therefore records **multiple belonging** as one of Yui's earliest positive models.

But atmosphere-reading also creates risk. She sometimes delays direct assertion until she can find a form that will not rupture the relation.

By Volume 3, however, ambiguity should not automatically be read as weakness. The reread supports a more precise hypothesis:

> **Yui sometimes uses partial, deniable assertion as an intermediate form: enough desire is exposed to invite an answer without making rejection immediately catastrophic.**

That remains a hypothesis, not yet a complete theory of her later behavior.

## 5.3 Yui supplies an early theory of imperfect beginnings

The game-club argument is one of Checkpoint A's most important conceptual anchors.

When Zaimokuza is attacked as a `偽物`, Yui argues that an incomplete or mistaken method does not make `好き` itself false:

- V3-L24: imperfect beginning/method does not invalidate desire — p. 214.

This is significant because it occurs before Hachiman tries to invalidate Yui's possible feeling through the dog-accident origin.

Yui therefore does not merely **embody** an alternative to Hachiman's purity test. She has already articulated one:

> **a flawed beginning or method does not automatically falsify present desire.**

Checkpoint A should preserve this as Yui's own conceptual contribution.

## 5.4 Yui rejects debt as the total meaning of her relation to Hachiman

Volume 2 establishes that Hachiman can recognize Yui's sadness and kindness as real (V02-L16) while still refusing to grant the kindness particular meaning.

At the end:

- V02-L24: Hachiman tells her to stop if she is acting kindly from guilt.
- V02-L25: Yui answers `そんなんじゃ、ないのに`.

Volume 3 then makes the disagreement structural.

Hachiman proposes a zeroed moral account. Yui rejects the diagnosis and says she does not want the relationship to end.

At Checkpoint A, Yui's stance can safely be frozen as:

> **the accident matters, but it does not possess unlimited authority over the meaning of everything that followed.**

Do not yet convert that into a mature theory of romantic choice.

## 5.5 Yui values reliance positively

Two early anchors are especially important:

- V01-E13: `友達じゃなきゃこんなの頼まないよ` — p. 271.
- V3-L40, supplementary: realizing Yukino relies on her makes her happier — p. 256.

Yui's model treats reliance as potentially:

- trust;
- recognition of competence;
- particularity;
- friendship.

This is a crucial positive baseline for any later analysis of dependence.

# 6. Relationship-state freeze

## 6.1 Hachiman ↔ Yukino

### Trust

**Moderate and increasing, but partially idealized.**

They trust each other's competence and can cooperate with relatively little social ceremony. Hachiman strongly believes Yukino's directness is stable enough to define her character.

### Disclosure

**Ideological and situational more than emotionally confessional.**

They know meaningful pieces of one another's worldview and habits, but Hachiman's sense of similarity outruns the actual breadth of their mutual knowledge.

### Ease

**High under silence, insult, work, and bounded role-play.**

They can inhabit the same space without requiring conventional friendliness. They can also simulate romantic proximity when its unreality is explicit.

### Conflict tolerance

**High.**

Mutual criticism is not itself a threat to relation.

### Reliance

**Competence-based and increasingly voluntary.**

Yukino can ask Hachiman for bounded help. Hachiman relies on Yukino's judgment. Neither fact yet constitutes pathological dependence.

### Romantic coding

**Hachiman's attraction is clear.**

Yukino's romantic state is not sufficiently established for a checkpoint claim.

### Primary risk

Hachiman increasingly equates Yukino with a transparent authentic essence defined by truthfulness, similarity, and noninstrumental expression.

### Frozen shorthand

> **adversarial intimacy with growing competence-trust and an emerging epistemic idealization risk.**

---

## 6.2 Hachiman ↔ Yui

### Trust

**Real but asymmetrical.**

Yui repeatedly grants Hachiman interpersonal standing. Hachiman trusts her enough to cooperate and care about her reactions but resists favorable interpretations of why she cares.

### Disclosure

**Increasing, but blocked around romantic significance.**

The dog-accident history becomes explicit. Yui directly contradicts Hachiman's guilt/debt explanation.

### Ease

**Warming but unstable.**

Their interaction is less effortless than Hachiman/Yukino's work-silence, precisely because Yui's attention can activate romantic danger.

### Conflict tolerance

**Developing.**

Yui can reject Hachiman's interpretation and remain in relation.

### Reliance

**Still relatively low in practical terms, high in continuity-building terms.**

Yui asks for contact, waits for Hachiman, and repeatedly creates future relation. Hachiman does not yet comfortably accept being important to her.

### Romantic coding

**Strongly active as an unresolved interpretive problem.**

Checkpoint A does not claim a completed confession. It does claim that Hachiman recognizes the possibility sufficiently to construct defenses against it.

### Primary risk

Hachiman treats alternative causal explanations as reasons to deny particularity and attempts to close the account without Yui's authorization.

### Frozen shorthand

> **particular care repeatedly offered; particular meaning repeatedly disputed.**

---

## 6.3 Yukino ↔ Yui

### Trust

**High relative to the short duration of the relationship.**

Their friendship advances faster than a Hachiman-centered summary would suggest.

### Disclosure

**Increasingly personal.**

Yui's direct relational vocabulary is met by Yukino's gradual acceptance and eventually self-authored gratitude.

### Ease

**Warm but not frictionless.**

Yukino remains embarrassed by friendship language; Yui can tolerate that awkwardness without treating it as rejection.

### Conflict tolerance

**Strong.**

Yui can absorb Yukino's bluntness. Yukino can be corrected by Yui's unexpected reception.

### Reliance

**Explicitly positive.**

Yui frames asking important things as friendship; Yukino acknowledges Yui's presence as useful and meaningful.

### Romantic coding

Not a governing checkpoint category. Their relation must be preserved as important in its own right rather than treated as derivative of the heterosexual triangle.

### Primary risk

The main early risk is not hostility but asymmetry in willingness to name the relationship openly.

### Frozen shorthand

> **a rapidly particular friendship in which Yui supplies explicit relational language and Yukino increasingly supplies reciprocal action.**

## 6.4 The triad

At the end of Volume 3, the Service Club has become more than a collection of two members plus recurring client.

Its continuity depends on:

- repeated presence;
- requests;
- institutional routine;
- Yui's social initiative;
- Yukino's structured willingness to participate;
- Hachiman's repeated return despite his suspicion of such places.

The club's first serious threat is not formal dissolution. It is **passive disappearance through unspoken distance** after Yui withdraws.

Hiratsuka's intervention makes the institutional structure carry the emotional problem without ordering Yui to return. Yukino then chooses to celebrate Yui regardless of return. Hachiman participates. Yui comes back.

This is crucial because it establishes the club's double function:

1. **institutional pretext** — tasks, requests, roles, reports;
2. **relational infrastructure** — a repeatable place through which people who cannot directly say “stay” can continue meeting.

At the end of Volume 3, however, triadic symmetry is not stable. Yukino repeatedly refers to Hachiman and Yui as `あなたたち` and leaves them together; Hachiman perceives an invisible line.

The only safe prospective conclusion is:

> **triadic asymmetry has become visible.**

It is **not yet legitimate** to call this permanent romantic self-removal, surrender, or sacrifice.

### Frozen triad thesis

> **The Service Club is an emergent three-person place whose institutional form lets attachment exist before all three members can safely name what the attachment asks of them.**

# 7. Early request and intervention ethics freeze

Checkpoint A contains enough cases to establish a real ethical grammar before the later high-cost interventions.

## 7.1 Founding intervention — Hiratsuka reforms Hachiman

**Authorization:** weak; imposed by adult authority.

**Benefit:** possible.

**Ethical lesson:** beneficial outcomes do not erase paternalism.

This matters because the institution that later mediates requests is itself founded by overriding the supposed beneficiary's wishes.

## 7.2 Yui's cookie request — autonomy theory meets purpose mismatch

**Client:** Yui.

**Yukino's method:** build competence rather than simply deliver a result.

**Problem:** the technical task is not identical to Yui's interpersonal goal.

**Hachiman's intervention:** small, reversible, instrumental deception that helps the desired social act occur without taking the whole task away from Yui.

**Lesson:** preserving agency requires understanding the actual goal, not merely refusing substitution.

## 7.3 Miura confrontation — inferred need without explicit request

The intervention helps Yui renegotiate her friendship, but the result cannot retroactively prove that inferred need always authorizes intervention.

**Lesson:** success is not blanket permission to override request boundaries.

## 7.4 Zaimokuza manuscript — clean authorized criticism

This is one of the best early control cases.

**Client:** explicit.

**Goal:** explicit.

**Method:** harsh but relevant criticism.

**Agency:** preserved.

**Lesson:** serious criticism can respect a request precisely because it does not protect the requester from the substance of what they asked to know.

## 7.5 Totsuka tennis request — Hachiman can refuse unsustainable obligation

Hachiman does not automatically accept every request. He can refuse a commitment he believes he cannot sustain.

This is important counterevidence against a later totalizing image of compulsory rescue.

## 7.6 Hayama group case — authorized cost-bearing intervention

Volume 2 provides the strongest early consent control:

- V02-L13: `葉山、お前が望むなら解決することはできるぞ`.

Hachiman identifies a structure in which Hayama is the hub. The intervention requires Hayama to remove himself temporarily so the other ties can become more direct. Hayama understands and consents to the cost.

**Lesson:** a disruptive intervention can remain meaningfully authorized even when the solution is ugly or risky.

## 7.7 Kawasaki case — causal diagnosis before solution

The Service Club initially responds to an apparent “delinquency” problem. The real constraints only become visible after Yui asks why Kawasaki is working and Kawasaki identifies money, university aspiration, and family burden.

Hachiman's scholarship solution is strong because it preserves Kawasaki's chosen goal while reducing the material constraint.

**Lesson:** emotional reconciliation does not substitute for resources, and autonomy-preserving help must be causally responsive to the subject's actual goal.

## 7.8 Volume 3 personnel intervention — bounded pedagogical manipulation

Hiratsuka converts the emotional problem of Yui's disappearance into a formal club-membership task.

Crucially:

- she pressures Hachiman and Yukino;
- she does **not** order Yui to return;
- the mechanism creates action without predetermining Yui's answer.

**Lesson:** institutional pretext can expand relational possibility without fully commandeering the absent person's agency.

## 7.9 Yukino's birthday action — care without leverage

Yukino explicitly wants to celebrate Yui even if Yui never returns.

This is one of the cleanest early positive cases.

**Lesson:** care can be self-authored and meaningful without functioning as a demand for reciprocity.

## 7.10 Hachiman's zero-account intervention — unilateral interpretive closure

This is the ethically most concerning early relationship case.

Hachiman decides what Yui feels and when the debt is settled.

**Lesson:** even an intervention aimed at freeing another person from obligation can become controlling if the helper alone defines the other's motive and the meaning of the relationship.

> **Helping should not be evaluated only by whether the immediate problem disappears. Ask who defined the problem, who authorized the method, who bore the cost, and whether the subject gained or lost authorship.**

That principle is grounded in Volumes 1–3 alone.

# 8. `本物` / authenticity freeze

## 8.1 Volumes 1–2 are explicitly pre-`本物`

The governing prose searches found:

- Volume 1: `本物` = 0.
- Volume 2: `本物` = 0.

Therefore, Checkpoint A must not rewrite the opening books as though Hachiman and the others are consciously debating the later named concept.

The precursor field is instead:

- `本音`;
- truth and lie;
- correctness and wrongness;
- friendship and conformity;
- understanding and projection;
- request and reliance;
- place and belonging;
- generalized versus particular care.

## 8.2 Volume 3 is the lexical threshold

Volume 3 contains three main-narrative thematic uses:

1. **Haruno's radiance:** is calibrated social brilliance `本物`?
2. **Yukino's smile:** Hachiman calls a non-flattering, non-deceiving expression `本物の笑顔`.
3. **Yui's possible affection:** Hachiman denies `本物` status if the feeling could counterfactually have arisen toward another rescuer.

The meanings have not unified.

At Checkpoint A the provisional `本物` domains are:

- authenticity of social presentation;
- noninstrumentality of emotional expression;
- particularity versus role-substitutability of affection.

No mature synthesis is authorized.

## 8.3 `偽物` complicates the purity model

The game-club case introduces explicit `偽物` gatekeeping. Yui's response supplies the strongest early counterprinciple:

> **deficient practice or mistaken beginnings do not automatically falsify desire.**

This prevents a simple early equation:

> true origin = genuine feeling; compromised origin = false feeling.

## 8.4 Frozen authenticity question

The end-of-V3 question should therefore be preserved as:

> **What makes an expression, desire, or relation belong particularly to the people now inhabiting it when its origin, role, performance, or motive is mixed?**

The series has begun asking this explicitly. It has not yet answered it.

# 9. Dependency, reliance, and autonomy freeze

## 9.1 `共依存` is not available

The later term is absent from the early evidence and must not be imported backward.

Checkpoint A does **not** describe the trio as codependent.

## 9.2 `依存` is not inherently pathological

Volume 2 uses dependence to describe how relationships remain connected through shared categories, communication, and repeated situations.

This establishes a crucial baseline:

> **relationships can depend on contexts without the people being pathologically dependent.**

School, club, class, messaging, and routine are relational infrastructure.

## 9.3 Self-reliance can become unilateral burden-bearing

Kawasaki's attempt to fund her future alone illustrates a problem distinct from “dependency.”

She protects her family by withholding the problem and bearing the cost herself. Her motive is caring, but the method prevents Taishi and the family from having standing in a decision that affects them.

Thus:

> **independence can itself become relationally unilateral.**

## 9.4 Healthy reliance has multiple early forms

Checkpoint A contains several positive examples:

- Yui treats important requests as friendship.
- Yukino asks Hachiman for bounded help.
- Yukino acknowledges Yui's practical importance.
- Yui is happy to be relied upon.
- Hachiman can ask others for expertise in external problems.

The governing distinction is not “dependence bad / independence good.”

It is:

> **Can another person matter and help without taking over authorship of the self or the decision?**

## 9.5 Expectation is the more immediate Hachiman problem

Hachiman's V3 rule of no expectation is a more accurate early target than “dependency.”

He seeks a relation in which:

- he cannot be disappointed;
- he cannot impose;
- the other cannot demand;
- neither person's future becomes a claim on the other.

That arrangement reduces coercion but also blocks ordinary reciprocity.

### Frozen dependency/autonomy thesis

> **By Volume 3, Oregairu has already rejected both simple self-sufficiency and simple merger. Its positive early model is bounded, authorized, agency-preserving reliance. Its unresolved problem is how expectation and particularity can enter such reliance without becoming ownership or debt.**

# 10. Social performance and relational naming freeze

## 10.1 Performance is not equivalent to falsity

The early books repeatedly destabilize a pure authentic-self / fake-role binary.

Zaimokuza's theatrical speech is constructed, yet the desire protected by it is sincere.

Haruno's social fluency is cultivated and calibrated, yet the mere fact of performance does not prove every expression false.

Yukino and Hachiman can perform a lover-like role because the role's unreality makes the interaction safe.

Institutional labels such as `部員` can permit Yukino to make a relational claim she cannot yet formulate as friendship.

Therefore, Checkpoint A freezes:

> **roles can coerce, conceal, protect, enable, or transmit real desire. Their ethical meaning depends on what they let people do and what they prevent people from saying.**

## 10.2 Names change risk

Relationship nouns and address terms matter because they change what can be expected.

Examples include:

- `ヒッキー`;
- `ゆきのん`;
- `友達`;
- `部員`;
- `恋人のように`;
- Saika's use of `八幡`.

Hachiman often prefers narrower functional categories because they lower relational stakes. Yui more often moves toward names that increase explicit personal standing. Yukino increasingly acts relationally while still preferring safer naming.

### Frozen language principle

> **At Checkpoint A, intimacy often advances through a mismatch between what people do for one another and the narrower labels they can safely tolerate.**

# 11. Japanese voice/register freeze

This checkpoint does not attempt a full voice index, but several early patterns are already stable enough for longitudinal tracking.

## 11.1 Hachiman

- `俺` narration;
- rapid movement among colloquial speech, mock-academic argument, literary reference, otaku jargon, exaggerated formality, and comedy;
- abstract systems/accounting vocabulary intensifies when emotional stakes rise;
- internal articulacy greatly exceeds interpersonal directness.

By Volume 3, reset, deletion, balance, and accounting metaphors are not decorative jokes only; they become ways of organizing relationship risk.

## 11.2 Yukino

- `私`;
- controlled, complete syntax;
- feminine endings including `〜わ`, `〜かしら`, `〜もの`;
- ideological vocabulary around correction, independence, rightness, weakness;
- personal requests soften her grammar without erasing precision;
- friendship becomes easier to enact than to name.

## 11.3 Yui

- `あたし`;
- contractions, hesitation, incomplete phrases;
- relational naming and address as active tools;
- greater willingness than Hachiman or Yukino to use explicit terms such as `本音`, `友達`, and `居場所`;
- hesitation can coexist with initiative.

Checkpoint A therefore rejects the simple equation:

> polished syntax = greater emotional maturity; fragmented syntax = lesser agency.

Yui's fragmented speech often accompanies the strongest acts of relational initiative in the early series.

# 12. Early Service Club polity

Although “polity” is stronger language than the series itself uses, it is useful descriptively if kept modest.

The Service Club is an institutional micro-order with:

- an adult founder;
- nominal rules;
- membership boundaries;
- clients;
- requests;
- competing theories of help;
- informal norms about who may define problems;
- recurring labor;
- repeated space;
- and increasingly private attachment carried by public/official tasks.

## 12.1 Hiratsuka's authority

Hiratsuka can force contact and define pedagogical tasks. Her authority is beneficial at times and paternalistic at others.

She is not an omniscient mouthpiece.

## 12.2 Yukino's normative authority

Yukino often supplies the explicit theory of help:

- reform;
- effort;
- independence;
- serious criticism;
- refusal to flatter.

Her authority is strongest when the problem is legible and weakest when her model misidentifies what another person actually wants.

## 12.3 Hachiman's diagnostic/instrumental authority

Hachiman often supplies:

- structural diagnosis;
- unconventional method;
- institutional exploitation;
- the willingness to use an ugly mechanism when others hesitate.

By V3, that talent already requires ethical scrutiny because his diagnosis can become unilateral authorship.

## 12.4 Yui's relational authority

Yui supplies a different form of knowledge:

- atmosphere;
- exclusion risk;
- willingness;
- whether someone actually wants a relation to continue;
- the difference between a technically solved problem and a person who has not yet been heard.

The early club therefore works best when these forms of intelligence correct one another.

### Frozen institutional thesis

> **The Service Club is strongest when competence, structure, and relational perception remain mutually revisable; it becomes dangerous when any one member treats their model of the problem as sufficient.**

This claim is supportable before Volume 4.

# 14. Contamination audit against Volumes 4–5

This section records **what later reading makes tempting to say**, then explicitly prevents those statements from entering the freeze.

No claim below is positive evidence for Checkpoint A.

## 14.1 Temptation: “Hachiman already distinguishes chosen solitude from exclusion”

**Blocked.**

Volume 4 makes this distinction explicit. Checkpoint A may say Hachiman's solitary life contains genuine value and that he recognizes social coercion. It may **not** pretend he has already formulated the later explicit distinction.

## 14.2 Temptation: “Hachiman's early interventions are about `解消` rather than `解決`”

**Blocked as terminology.**

The later distinction is analytically useful, but the early checkpoint should describe the actual intervention structures without importing the later lexical opposition.

## 14.3 Temptation: “Yukino's family has already made her Haruno's substitute”

**Blocked.**

Volumes 1–3 establish:

- Haruno's cultivated public role;
- political-family socialization;
- Yukino's emotionally loaded sibling material.

They do not yet establish the later explicit `代役` formulation or the full maternal authority structure.

## 14.4 Temptation: “Hachiman already knows Yukino is his aspirational `超人性` ideal”

**Blocked.**

Checkpoint A can observe idealization:

- “Yukino does not lie”;
- “I know her genuine smile”;
- rapid identification through solitude.

It cannot use the Volume 5 self-analysis that her solitary `超人性` was something he wanted to acquire.

## 14.5 Temptation: “Volume 3 already proves Yukino will self-remove romantically”

**Blocked.**

The invisible line and `あなたたち` are real. The motive is not yet established.

Checkpoint A freezes **triadic asymmetry**, not romantic surrender.

## 14.6 Temptation: “The mature `本物` thesis is already present”

**Blocked.**

Volume 3 starts the explicit lexical field but leaves at least three meanings unintegrated.

## 14.7 Temptation: “The trio is already codependent”

**Blocked.**

The early evidence contains:

- contextual dependence;
- ordinary reliance;
- desire for continuity;
- expectation avoidance;
- institutional pretexts.

`共依存` is neither lexically nor analytically authorized here.

## 14.8 Temptation: “Hachiman's later self-sacrificial method is already his master pattern”

**Blocked.**

The strongest early concern is unilateral **meaning-assignment** in the zero-account case. The later willingness to make himself the damage-bearing instrument of a solution requires later evidence.

### Contamination result

The Checkpoint A state remains stable after the V4–V5 audit **only after narrowing several later-sounding formulations**.

The key protected distinctions are:

- expectation avoidance ≠ mature self-sacrifice;
- idealization seed ≠ later explicit self-model;
- triadic asymmetry ≠ self-removal;
- reliance ≠ codependency;
- early `本物` domains ≠ mature unified doctrine;
- performance ≠ falsity;
- solitude with genuine value ≠ already-theorized chosen-solitude doctrine.

# 15. Frozen questions carried forward

Checkpoint A closes with questions, not answers.

## Hachiman

1. Can he grant favorable uncertainty enough standing to act without demanding certainty first?
2. Can expectation exist without becoming a debt or a trap?
3. Can he allow another person to define their own motive when his structural explanation seems plausible?
4. Will pleasure in usefulness remain ordinary, or become a condition of self-worth?
5. Can his external social intelligence be turned inward without converting introspection into another defense?

## Yukino

6. Can her reform/self-reliance ethic adapt when the problem is not primarily technical or effort-responsive?
7. Can she name personal attachment as directly as she can enact it?
8. What kinds of reliance remain easy, and what kinds become identity-threatening?
9. How much of Hachiman's “understanding” of her will survive greater knowledge?
10. What is the actual meaning of the emerging asymmetry between her and the Hachiman–Yui pair?

## Yui

11. Can her strategy of maintaining multiple belongings survive when those belongings make incompatible claims?
12. Will her indirect assertion remain an adaptive social skill or become a mechanism of postponement?
13. How far can her “bad beginning does not falsify present desire” principle extend?
14. Can she ask others to choose in ways that risk loss rather than only preserve possibility?

## Service Club / intervention ethics

15. When does interpreting the “real problem” become permission to overwrite the requester?
16. Can the club preserve the subject's authorship while still using indirect or manipulative methods?
17. What happens when the cost of an intervention is no longer low, reversible, or consensually allocated?
18. Can the club remain a place when the requests stop being external problems and become the members' own desires?

## `本物`

19. Will social-performance authenticity, noninstrumental expression, and particular affection converge into one concept?
20. Can something have a compromised origin and nevertheless become particular through later choice?
21. Is mixed motive compatible with genuineness?

## Dependency/autonomy

22. Can another person become necessary without necessity erasing freedom?
23. Can a person ask to be helped without surrendering authorship?
24. Can someone choose independence without unilaterally depriving others of relational standing?

# 17. Checkpoint conclusion

The earliest Service Club is not best understood as three damaged teenagers accidentally becoming friends, nor as a preformed romantic triangle waiting for later plot to reveal its destination.

It is a **relational technology under construction**.

Hiratsuka gives them an artificial institution. Requests give them reasons to interact. Yukino supplies an ethic of competence and reform. Hachiman supplies structural diagnosis and unconventional instrumental reasoning. Yui supplies names, continuity, social motion, and increasingly explicit desire for relation. Each strength corrects another member's blind spot; each can also become dangerous when treated as sufficient.

The first three volumes therefore establish several propositions that later analysis must preserve:

1. Hachiman's solitude has real value; growth does not require its repudiation.
2. His strongest early distortion concerns the meaning of care when it might make him particular.
3. Yukino's competence and self-reliance are genuine values, not merely pathology in disguise.
4. Yukino can already rely on others when reliance is bounded and does not threaten authorship.
5. Yui is an active author of the triad, not a passive lubricant between Hachiman and Yukino.
6. Yui–Yukino friendship is a primary relationship in its own right.
7. The Service Club begins to matter precisely because institutional form allows attachment before attachment can be openly named.
8. Helping is ethically unstable when the helper claims authority to define the other's real problem without permission.
9. Reliance can be healthy; the early books do not support a dependence/pathology binary.
10. `本物` becomes explicit only in Volume 3 and does not yet possess a single meaning.
11. Mixed or artificial forms can carry sincere desire.
12. The first serious threat to the club is not an external enemy but the possibility that members will let uncertainty and unspoken desire turn ordinary distance into disappearance.

The state should now be considered **frozen** for longitudinal comparison.

From this point forward, later volume analyses may explain, complicate, or overturn these early interpretations, but they should not silently rewrite what Volumes 1–3 themselves made available.

> **Checkpoint A governing sentence:**
>
> **By the end of Volume 3, the Service Club has become a place where three incompatible strategies of self-protection are learning to cooperate through requests, bounded reliance, and increasingly particular attachment—before any of the three yet possesses a stable language for what they are becoming to one another.**
