# OREGAIRU V2 — Hachiman Epistemic and Narratorial Ledger

## Function

This ledger consolidates the complete V2 reread's findings about **Hikigaya Hachiman as observer, interpreter, narrator, self-interpreter, and decision-maker**. It is not a general Hachiman character essay and it is not a list of occasions on which he is "right" or "wrong."

Its governing question is the one established by `OREGAIRU_V2_ANALYTICAL_METHOD.md`:

> **What kind of claim is Hachiman making, what evidence does he possess, what emotional stake does he have in believing it, and what later evidence changes our confidence?**

The ledger therefore distinguishes among:

- observation;
- social inference;
- psychological inference;
- self-description;
- other-description;
- desire denial;
- rationalization;
- self-deprecation;
- preemptive rejection;
- comic camouflage;
- moral justification;
- contradiction by behavior;
- correction by another person;
- retrospective self-correction;
- later confirmation;
- later rejection or narrowing.

The document is a **longitudinal routing layer**. Every major entry points back to a canonical volume deep reading, whose own primary-source locator ledger is the governing route to the Japanese text. The ledger may summarize those findings; it does not replace the per-volume evidence record.

# 1. Executive longitudinal thesis

Hachiman is never best described as a generically unreliable narrator. The V2 reread instead establishes a stable asymmetry:

> **He is usually strongest at observing external structure and weakest when a plausible interpretation of that structure would expose his own desire, vulnerability, or responsibility to another person's answer.**

His strongest recurring domains are:

- hierarchy and status;
- coercive social language;
- incentives and institutional structure;
- group-maintenance mechanisms;
- reputational risk;
- labor distribution;
- role performance;
- practical constraints;
- the difference between stated procedure and actual social function.

His most error-prone recurring domains are:

- whether another person's attention toward him is particular;
- whether care directed at him can be accepted without debt;
- whether an explanation of a relationship's origin exhausts its present meaning;
- whether another person's unspoken desire authorizes intervention;
- whether his own wish can be redescribed as neutral principle;
- whether self-inflicted cost is morally harmless because he chose it;
- whether a diagnostic label can substitute for deciding what he wants.

The deepest development is therefore **jurisdictional rather than merely epistemic**.

Early Hachiman often reasons:

> I have a plausible model -> the model explains the situation -> therefore I may act on the model without obtaining an answer that could invalidate it.

Middle Hachiman increasingly learns:

> I may have a strong model -> the model can still be wrong or incomplete -> uncertainty should sometimes produce a question rather than an intervention.

Late Hachiman finally reaches:

> Even when the model is useful, and even when the mechanism is real, **the model does not own another person's feeling or answer.** My own desire must be stated as mine, and the other person must remain able to refuse, correct, revise, or counter-request.

The endpoint is not epistemic certainty. Volume 14 explicitly ends with continued doubt, and Volume 14.5 adds the final anti-sanctification correction: even a relationship that has survived the series' central crisis becomes epistemically dangerous if Hachiman starts treating its present configuration as perfect, complete, or sacred.

The mature narratorial state is therefore:

> **high social perception + explicit uncertainty + owned desire + answerability + continuing revision.**

# 3. Master pattern dictionary

## 3.1 Observation

Hachiman frequently perceives public behavior with unusual precision. This includes coercion in friendship language, status effects, hidden labor asymmetry, reputational incentives, group exclusion, deniable social pretexts, and institutional bottlenecks.

**Do not downgrade a correct observation merely because the observer is cynical.**

The V2 correction to many fandom-level readings is that Hachiman's problem is not that he "doesn't understand people." He often understands **structures around people** extremely well.

## 3.2 Social inference

Hachiman's social inferences are strongest when:

- the mechanism is externally observable;
- the stakes do not center his own desirability;
- multiple behavioral signals converge;
- he leaves room for revision.

They become less reliable when he turns a local mechanism into a universal ontology: all groups sacrifice someone; approval-seeking makes an action worthless; change is superficial; cold indifference is the only kindness; ending a relation is cleaner than preserving ambiguity.

## 3.3 Psychological inference

Psychological inference is the main borderland between competence and overreach.

The recurring danger is **predictive success becoming mistaken for interior access**. Hachiman can often anticipate what someone will do while still misunderstanding why they do it or what the action means to them.

This becomes explicit in Volume 9 through Hiratsuka's distinction between reading `心理` and understanding `感情`.

## 3.4 Desire denial

Desire denial evolves through several forms:

1. **preemptive rejection** — make himself disliked so hope cannot become humiliation;
2. **type abstraction** — turn a personally threatening possibility into a general statement;
3. **debt explanation** — treat care as repayment for a past event;
4. **safe categorization** — accept intimacy only when a category guarantees it cannot "mean" romance;
5. **moral principle** — describe withdrawal as fairness, equality, independence, or noninterference;
6. **diagnostic substitution** — use `共依存` or another mechanism as a reason not to investigate desire;
7. **comic camouflage** — replace a direct wanting statement with genre language, jokes, or masculine euphemism.

The late-series change is not disappearance of embarrassment. It is that embarrassment no longer prevents first-person ownership: `俺は関わり続けたい`.

## 3.5 Rationalization

A Hachiman rationalization is usually not a wholly false proposition. The dangerous form is:

> **a true or defensible general principle used in a scene where its emotional function exceeds its logical warrant.**

Examples include:

- solitude is valuable -> therefore Rumi may be better left alone;
- expectations can hurt -> therefore only he should bear disappointment;
- complete understanding is impossible -> therefore he need not ask;
- autonomy matters -> therefore Yukino's explicit refusal can be routed around through another method;
- codependency may describe the relation -> therefore separation must be the correct answer.

## 3.6 Self-deprecation

Early self-deprecation often preempts external judgment: if Hachiman declares himself defective first, others cannot surprise him with the verdict.

V2 distinguishes this from **specific self-correction**. Statements such as `勝手に期待して`, `勝手に決めつけていた`, recognition of `思考停止`, and `神聖視` identify particular cognitive mechanisms and are therefore more epistemically valuable than generic self-hatred.

## 3.7 Preemptive rejection

This is explicitly grounded from Volume 1 onward. Hachiman consciously develops tactics that reduce the chance of romantic humiliation.

The pattern persists in more sophisticated forms:

- refusing favorable interpretations;
- assigning care to debt;
- imagining relationships as resettable or balanceable;
- insisting on easily severable ties;
- preferring hostile legibility to hopeful ambiguity.

## 3.8 Comic camouflage

Comic narration can simultaneously be:

- genuinely funny;
- genre commentary;
- a social-distancing device;
- emotional deflection;
- indirect confession.

V2 rejects the reductive rule that every joke is defense. The category is admitted only where the joke interrupts or displaces an emotionally threatening proposition.

High-value cases include:

- jokes immediately after recognizing romantic possibility;
- `男の意地` as a masculine euphemism for desire;
- mocking the ordinary promise `いつか、助けるって約束したから` because its simplicity embarrasses him;
- later Iroha and marriage jokes that preserve visible discomfort around being chosen.

## 3.9 Moral justification

Hachiman's most consequential narratorial errors often occur when a correct moral value becomes an authorization rule.

Examples:

- fairness;
- non-imposition;
- responsibility for one's own mistakes;
- protection of autonomy;
- willingness to absorb cost.

The series repeatedly asks whether a moral justification actually preserves another person's authorship or merely gives Hachiman a cleaner story about unilateral action.

## 3.10 Correction by another person

The most important correctors are not omniscient authorities. Their value lies in exposing specific blind spots:

- **Yui**: sees when Hachiman's understanding fails precisely where his own effect on others is concerned;
- **Yukino**: corrects his attempts to decide her feelings, his minimization of harm to himself, and later his sanctification of the old club form;
- **Hiratsuka**: repeatedly separates inference from feeling, assistance from ownership, and mechanism from personhood;
- **Hayama**: exposes the social cost and strategic character of Hachiman's self-sacrificial methods;
- **Haruno**: identifies real relational mechanisms but must itself be audited as participant interpretation rather than sovereign truth;
- **Komachi**: provides unusually secure relational evidence that often reveals how unnecessary Hachiman's defensive formality can be.

## 3.11 Retrospective self-correction

The strongest longitudinal marker of growth is not "being right more often." It is Hachiman's increasing ability to identify his own interpretive mechanism before catastrophe forces the correction.

Milestones include:

- Volume 5: `勝手に期待して`;
- Volume 6: accepts provisional `虚像` rather than demanding certainty;
- Volume 8: recognizes the monster-of-reason problem;
- Volume 9: admits `答えてほしくなどない` and the controlling impulse inside total understanding;
- Volume 10.5: catches himself imposing `幻想` on Yukino;
- Volume 11: names his unilateral assignment of Yui as "kind" and Yukino as "strong";
- Volume 12: limits `共依存` to mechanism rather than motive;
- Volume 14: identifies his own use of the diagnosis as `思考停止` / `現実逃避`;
- Volume 14.5: identifies `神聖視` of the Service Club's present configuration.

# 5. Major longitudinal transitions

## 5.1 From observation to jurisdiction

The first half of the series repeatedly demonstrates that Hachiman's observations can be accurate while his claimed authority is not.

The crucial distinction is:

> **descriptive competence does not automatically confer intervention rights.**

This is visible in:

- Rumi: accurate exclusion diagnosis, then engineered environmental rupture;
- Sagami: accurate labor/status analysis, then strategic humiliation;
- Kyoto: accurate stakeholder mapping, then compression of incompatible desires;
- Hayama: accurate expectation analysis, then forcing disclosure despite refusal;
- prom: accurate institutional design, then strategic manipulation of the option environment.

By the end, Hachiman's development is measured increasingly by whether the other person retains the ability to answer differently.

## 5.2 From unknowability as shield to uncertainty as method

Early:

> people cannot be fully known -> expectation is dangerous -> safer to withdraw or infer privately.

Middle:

> people cannot be fully known -> models must be provisional -> ask when the answer matters.

Late:

> people cannot be fully known -> commitment does not eliminate uncertainty -> keep revising after choosing.

This is one of the series' most important epistemological arcs.

## 5.3 From debt closure to open reciprocity

Hachiman repeatedly imagines relations through accounting:

- repay;
- zero the balance;
- clear obligation;
- restore prior distance;
- make the ledger end.

The post-V09 Christmas coda produces a concise counterargument through Yui: reciprocity need not terminate at zero.

Later ordinary-life material completes the shift. Anniversary gestures, study plans, future meetings, and club succession are not balanced equations. They are continuing practices whose meaning is negotiated over time.

## 5.4 From negative self-story to specific self-correction

Early self-deprecation often says, in effect:

> I am bad / unwanted / defective; therefore no favorable interpretation can obligate me to hope.

Specific self-correction instead says:

> I idealized her.
> I decided she was strong.
> I wanted the problem to dissolve.
> I used `共依存` to stop thinking.
> I sanctified the club's present form.

This specificity is epistemically productive because it creates a revisable claim rather than an identity verdict.

## 5.5 From being needed to wanting involvement

Volume 1 establishes that being relied upon feels good without pathologizing it.

Across the series, usefulness becomes increasingly entangled with permission to remain close. The late codependency debate correctly identifies this reinforcement loop.

But the final movement does not conclude that all involvement is merely neededness. After the mechanism is named and stripped of explanatory sovereignty, Hachiman still arrives at:

- a promise;
- then, more decisively, `俺は関わり続けたい`.

The development is therefore:

> **from "I may remain because I am useful" toward "I want to remain, and that wanting must stand on its own without proving the other person needs me."**

## 5.6 From certainty before action to action under uncertainty

Early Hachiman often wants either:

- enough certainty to control the outcome;
- or enough doubt to justify nonparticipation.

Later he increasingly acts while preserving epistemic limits:

- accepts provisional images;
- admits not knowing;
- recognizes projection internally;
- asks questions without demanding final answers;
- accepts that `本物` cannot be certified;
- allows a chosen relationship to remain open to doubt and change.

# 6. Corrections supplied by other characters

## 6.1 Yui

Yui is among the most consistent correctors of Hachiman's self-implicating epistemology.

Her corrections include:

- important things can be asked of friends;
- origin debt does not exhaust present relation;
- he understands complex systems yet fails to understand the effect he has on people near him;
- kindness does not require endless protection from consequences;
- reciprocity need not end at a zero balance;
- complicated understanding does not require silence—sometimes one can simply say what one wants.

Yui's epistemic function is therefore not merely emotional. She repeatedly challenges the rule that **complexity invalidates direct speech**.

## 6.2 Yukino

Yukino's most important corrections target Hachiman's jurisdiction:

- harm to him counts as harm even when he minimizes it;
- her feelings cannot be decided by his autonomy theory;
- she can be capable of standing alone and still choose a hand;
- later, the current Service Club configuration is not the sacred complete form Komachi must preserve.

Yukino's corrections become increasingly important because they occur from inside the relationship Hachiman is most tempted to theorize.

## 6.3 Hiratsuka

Hiratsuka supplies the clearest conceptual checks:

- prediction of psychology is not the same as understanding feeling;
- reducing options can be help only if final choice remains with the person;
- impressions must be continually updated;
- `共依存` should not become a borrowed word that distorts actual feelings;
- `本物` is offered modally, not as a certified doctrine.

Her pedagogical function is often to restore the clause Hachiman deletes when he turns a useful principle into an operational shortcut.

## 6.4 Hayama

Hayama repeatedly demonstrates that social intelligence can produce different ethics.

He corrects Hachiman by showing:

- self-sacrifice is also strategic and socially costly;
- expectation can imprison a socially competent person;
- being able to satisfy a role does not prove the role is authored;
- even something counterfeit-like may become historically singular;
- another person's regret should not be transferred as a moral burden.

Hayama is therefore best retained as a competing epistemic practitioner rather than an inferior foil.

## 6.5 Haruno

Haruno is diagnostically valuable but must remain evidence-classed.

She sees:

- Yukino's history of imitation and externally oriented choice;
- the reinforcing relation between Hachiman's neededness and Yukino's reliance;
- the trio's tendency to preserve itself through indirectness.

But V2 rejects making her omniscient. Her own need for a single non-counterfeit ending, her difficulty asking directly for help, and the fact that Hachiman sometimes converts her words into harsher diagnoses all require caution.

The correct ledger state is:

> **Haruno often supplies high-value hypotheses, not narrator-certified truth.**

# 8. High-value lexical and metaphor clusters

## 8.1 Reset / delete / zero

Early relationship management repeatedly borrows systems/accounting language. This indicates a desire for reversible state transitions: if emotional relations can be reset, Hachiman can avoid irreversible vulnerability.

## 8.2 `勝手に`

The repeated language of unilateral assignment becomes one of the strongest self-correction markers:

- expectation;
- deciding someone is kind;
- imposing an ideal of strength;
- interpreting another person's feelings.

The term is especially important because it tracks **authorship error** rather than mere factual error.

## 8.3 `わからない`

The meaning changes longitudinally.

Early `わからない` can authorize withdrawal or private inference.

By V9–V10.5, it increasingly marks a legitimate stopping point.

Late, it coexists with commitment: one can choose while continuing not to know everything.

## 8.4 `頼る / 任せる / 委ねる`

Reliance vocabulary tracks Hachiman's movement from defensive independence toward differentiated trust.

Important contrasts:

- pleasure in being relied upon begins as ordinary;
- receiving care can later feel dangerous;
- V10 opens the possibility that he may someday rely on Totsuka;
- V10.5 still shows the reflex that sweet care must not be relied upon;
- final material normalizes chosen support without incapacity.

## 8.5 `本物`

For this ledger, `本物` matters chiefly as an epistemic demand.

Hachiman initially risks turning authenticity into an impossible test requiring certainty or uncontaminated motive. The mature arc makes the concept less certifiable rather than more.

The endpoint `疑い続けます` is therefore not failure to solve the question. It is the final narratorial discipline against converting commitment into epistemic omniscience.

## 8.6 `共依存`

The term produces a late-series stress test for Hachiman's relationship with theory.

Sequence:

1. Haruno names a plausible reinforcement mechanism.
2. Hachiman recognizes himself in part of it.
3. He overextends the label toward self-condemnation and remedy.
4. He then restricts it: mechanism is not feeling or reason.
5. V13 shows him trying to disprove it by changing motive—an invalid inference.
6. V14 identifies his use of the label as `思考停止` / `現実逃避`.

This is perhaps the cleanest example of the series teaching Hachiman that **a useful model becomes dangerous when it is asked to do the moral work of choosing.**

## 8.7 `神聖視`

This is the terminal epistemic warning.

Hachiman's old error was often negative absolutization: people do not change; groups are false; hope is dangerous.

`神聖視` reveals the positive mirror image: after reaching a cherished relational state, he can also absolutize success.

The final correction therefore closes the loop:

> **cynicism and sanctification are both forms of refusing revision.**

# 10. Final Hachiman epistemic profile

## 10.1 What he sees accurately

By the end of the reread, high-confidence Hachiman competence includes:

- social hierarchy;
- institutional incentives;
- reputation and face;
- hidden labor distribution;
- deniable pretexts;
- how expectations constrain roles;
- how groups preserve themselves;
- when public procedure and private motive diverge;
- when another person is behaving differently around him, even if he resists the implication.

## 10.2 What he explains badly

Persistent weaknesses include:

- treating origins as exhaustive explanations;
- treating personally threatening possibilities as safer abstractions;
- universalizing local truths;
- assuming self-inflicted cost minimizes ethical harm;
- turning autonomy into nonintervention or intervention without sufficient attention to requested form;
- converting labels into verdicts;
- confusing predictive competence with relational jurisdiction.

## 10.3 What he refuses to name

Across most of the series, the central unnamed material is not simply "love." It includes:

- wanting to be wanted;
- enjoying being relied upon;
- wanting a particular person's continued presence;
- fear that a named relation will become answerable and therefore changeable;
- desire to remain involved even when usefulness is not required.

## 10.4 What later behavior reveals

Behavior repeatedly exceeds his theories:

- he returns;
- remembers;
- initiates;
- buys particular gifts;
- asks questions;
- relies on collaborators;
- accepts that another person may refuse;
- chooses continued involvement;
- remains capable of attraction and social complexity after choosing;
- permits the club he values to change beyond his ownership.

## 10.5 Terminal formulation

Hachiman's epistemic growth can be stated most compactly as:

> **He moves from using interpretation to avoid being answerable, toward using interpretation as a provisional tool inside relationships whose other participants retain the right to answer differently.**

His narrator does not become omniscient, purified, or consistently self-transparent.

He becomes more capable of:

- distinguishing observation from inference;
- distinguishing mechanism from motive;
- distinguishing motive from authorization;
- distinguishing privacy from concealment;
- distinguishing chosen reliance from incapacity;
- distinguishing commitment from certainty;
- distinguishing preservation of value from preservation of form.

The final achievement is not "knowing the truth about everyone." It is a willingness to **keep updating after choosing**.

# 11. Synthesis constraints generated by this ledger

Any later specialist or full-series synthesis must preserve the following constraints.

1. **Do not call Hachiman generically unreliable.** Name the domain and evidence class.
2. **Do not treat accurate social diagnosis as proof of ethical authorization.**
3. **Do not treat self-deprecation as equivalent to self-knowledge.** Prefer specific mechanism-level self-correction.
4. **Do not call favorable romantic evidence invisible to him when he explicitly notices it.** Distinguish possibility detection, probability assignment, and behavioral permission.
5. **Do not reduce solitude to trauma.** It is both a real preference and, at times, a defensive ideology.
6. **Do not reduce self-sacrifice to passivity.** It can be strategic, controlling, morally serious, self-protective, and identity-reinforcing at once.
7. **Do not let Haruno's labels replace narrator audit.**
8. **Do not treat `共依存` as false merely because Volume 14 rejects it as a totalizing decision rule.** Bounded mechanism evidence remains.
9. **Do not treat the final romantic choice as retrospective proof that earlier ambiguity was fake.**
10. **Do not treat post-ending stability as proof of perfection.** Volume 14.5 explicitly rejects sanctification.
11. **Do not erase Hachiman's residual flaws.** Indirectness, overengineering, embarrassment at being chosen, attraction to other people, and occasional boundary-testing remain compatible with mature commitment.
12. **Do not make uncertainty the enemy of authenticity.** The final narrator explicitly retains uncertainty after choice.

# 12. Open questions for specialist synthesis

These questions remain interpretive rather than evidentiary gaps and should be carried into `02_HIKIGAYA_HACHIMAN_NARRATION_SELF_DECEPTION_AND_GROWTH.md`.

1. How much of Hachiman's strongest social intelligence is enabled by distance, and how much survives once he is fully implicated in a relation?
2. Does his desire to be needed ever disappear, or is it instead integrated into a broader set of reasons for attachment?
3. To what extent should the bridge overengineering be read as residual pathology versus a formal culmination of his tendency to build institutional pretexts for intimacy?
4. Is Hachiman's final commitment to continued doubt primarily epistemic humility, moral vigilance, or another defense that has been rendered productive rather than avoidant?
5. How should his continued attraction/fluster around Iroha be incorporated into a mature account of romantic commitment without either trivializing it or turning it into route instability?
6. Is `神聖視` the final form of the same cognitive error as his early cynicism—namely, freezing a revisable social reality into an absolute theory?
7. How much of Hachiman's development consists of becoming more accurate, versus becoming more willing to let other people's answers constrain what he does with his accuracy?
