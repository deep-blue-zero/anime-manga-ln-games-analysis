# Oregairu V2 - Checkpoint E: Prom / Final-Choice Movement

## Purpose of this checkpoint

Checkpoint E freezes the mainline state produced by Volumes 12, 13, and 14 before Volume 14.5 supplies any post-ending ordinary-life stabilization.

Its task is not to retell the prom arc or to reduce the final three volumes to the romantic choice. The canonical V12, V13, and V14 deep readings already perform the sequential close reading. This checkpoint instead asks what the three-volume movement changes in the series' governing problems:

- authorship versus dependence;
- help versus substitution;
- motive versus authorization;
- `共依存` as mechanism, diagnosis, excuse, and proposed cure;
- the limits of self-removal as an autonomy strategy;
- Yui's right to an answer and to a future not exhausted by romantic loss;
- Hachiman's transition from proxy reasons to first-person desire;
- Yukino's transition from proving independence to choosing reciprocal involvement;
- the relation between pain and authenticity;
- and the final status of `本物` once the series can no longer defer concrete choice.

Checkpoint D froze the trio at a crucial threshold. The Service Club had become more answerable, more capable of ordinary reliance, and more willing to state conflicting desires, but it had not yet developed a stable procedure for living with those conflicts. Yui had stated that she wanted everything. Hachiman had admitted that his refusal of her preservation solution expressed **his own ideal**, not neutral logic. Yukino had rejected both Yui and Hachiman deciding her feelings and had become the requester in her own name.

Volumes 12-14 then force that improved but unstable system through a sequence of increasingly demanding tests:

> **authored request -> autonomy experiment -> explicit dependency diagnosis -> non-intervention boundary -> personal motive -> rival intervention -> closure bargain -> contested self-removal -> dismantling of diagnostic shortcut -> explicit romantic answer -> reciprocal future claim -> continued triadic answerability**

The central finding is that the movement does **not** vindicate either of the simplest cures available to the characters.

It does not establish:

> dependency is false, therefore nothing was wrong.

Nor does it establish:

> dependency is real, therefore separation is the cure.

Instead, it decomposes the problem.

Real reinforcing mechanisms exist. Hachiman can derive value from being needed. Yukino can come to expect him to solve things. Yui can displace difficult choice onto Yukino. All three can use kindness, role performance, and institutional pretext to delay consequences.

But the final volumes also establish that a diagnostic mechanism cannot decide what anyone feels, cannot prescribe one universal remedy, and cannot substitute for the harder work of allowing each participant to author a request, refusal, correction, loss, and continued relation.

The governing question of Checkpoint E is therefore:

> **Can the trio move from diagnosing why they are entangled to choosing how they will remain related without making another person's dependence, pain, or self-erasure the price of that choice?**

The answer is qualified but affirmative.

---

# 1. Executive frozen thesis

The strongest Checkpoint E formulation is:

> **Volumes 12-14 transform the final-choice problem from a search for the one correct relational diagnosis into a test of whether the characters can own their desires without using diagnosis, sacrifice, or institutional role as proxies for choice. Volume 12 names genuine dependency mechanisms but already limits their authority: `共依存` can describe a `仕組み`, not decide a `気持ち`. Volume 13 then tests whether Hachiman can change his way of intervening rather than merely rename the same compulsion; his dummy-prom strategy preserves more of Yukino's operational authorship and improves collaborator consent, yet still manipulates the environment and fails to prove that the underlying relation is healthy. Yukino's subsequent self-removal is genuinely self-authored but exceeds her jurisdiction because it attempts to settle Hachiman and Yui's future as well as her own. Volume 14 finally dismantles both the codependency label as a total explanation and separation as an automatic cure. Hachiman states a first-person desire to remain involved, Yukino converts his asymmetrical life-offer into a reciprocal request, Yui receives a real romantic answer while refusing the destruction of her friendship with Yukino, and the final Service Club continues after romantic non-equivalence becomes explicit. `本物` is therefore not certified by purity, pain, independence, or romantic exclusivity. The mainline ending chooses a difficult relation strongly enough to incur consequences while keeping that relation answerable to doubt, correction, and future revision.**

Twelve frozen propositions follow.

1. **Volume 12 establishes dependency as textual fact without establishing separation as textual cure.**
2. **Yukino's governing autonomy problem is authorship, not mere absence of reliance.**
3. **Hachiman's promise explains why he acts but does not, by itself, authorize how he acts.**
4. **Volume 13 demonstrates genuine procedural growth without proving structural health.**
5. **A person can refuse direct help while consenting to rivalry or bounded involvement. Authorization is granular.**
6. **Yukino's V13 self-removal is sincere and self-authored but not automatically legitimate for everyone it affects.**
7. **Yui's romantic loss is real and must not be dissolved into “everyone stayed friends.”**
8. **Yui-Yukino friendship becomes independently authored and survives the romantic answer without becoming romantically neutral.**
9. **Volume 14 rejects diagnostic reification: `共依存` cannot substitute for describing what people actually feel and choose.**
10. **The final autonomy model is chosen interdependence: capacity to stand, ask, refuse, help, be helped, and revise.**
11. **`本物` ends as an anti-certification problem. Hachiman chooses and then continues doubting rather than using choice as proof.**
12. **The final Service Club is continuation without restored innocence. The conflict is no longer hidden, but it is not magically resolved.**

The phase can be represented as:

> **authorship before certainty -> dependency named -> cure proposed -> cure contested -> intervention method revised -> closure self-authored -> closure jurisdiction challenged -> borrowed diagnosis dismantled -> desire owned -> loss answered -> reciprocity negotiated -> chosen support -> non-equivalent continuation**

That is the frozen mainline state before Volume 14.5.

# 2. Evidence boundary, chronology, and authority

## 2.1 Incoming state: Checkpoint D

Checkpoint D governs the incoming state through Volume 11.

The most important incoming conditions are:

- the trio is **connected after nonidentity, not reconciled through unanimity**;
- Yui is an active claimant whose care and self-interest coexist;
- Yukino has reclaimed authority over her own feelings and has initiated a request;
- Hachiman has become more capable of asking, revising models, and respecting privacy, but can still override answers he dislikes;
- ordinary life is already real even when the relationship cannot be fully defined;
- reliance has become more ethically viable, while Hachiman remains suspicious of needing or being needed;
- kindness can support another person, but can also become a technology for postponing consequences;
- and `本物` remains contested rather than doctrinally fixed.

The post-Volume-9 Christmas CD reconciliation strengthens rather than revises this incoming state. It establishes that the repaired Service Club can already produce voluntary ordinary continuation and differentiated care, but Checkpoint D's conceptual account remains unchanged.

## 2.2 Positive evidence window

Checkpoint E admits only:

- Volume 12;
- Volume 13;
- Volume 14.

These three volumes are especially suited to checkpoint treatment because Watari's Volume-14 afterword explicitly identifies them as a three-part final-volume structure. That paratextual fact does not settle interpretation, but it confirms that the three books form one deliberate terminal movement rather than three merely adjacent installments.

## 2.3 Explicit exclusion: Volume 14.5

Volume 14.5 is **not** evidence for this freeze.

No later ordinary-life scene may be used here to argue that:

- the chosen Hachiman/Yukino relation was immediately stable;
- Yui's pain had already become easy to live with;
- the final Service Club geometry was already comfortable;
- the Yukinoshita family conflict had already normalized;
- or the ending's anti-certification stance had secretly become certainty.

The purpose of Checkpoint E is precisely to preserve what Volume 14 establishes **at the moment of mainline resolution**, before later material can make that resolution appear more settled than it was.

## 2.4 Evidentiary hierarchy inside the movement

This checkpoint preserves the V2 hierarchy:

- explicit character speech is not narratorial truth;
- self-description is stronger evidence of self-understanding than third-party diagnosis, but can still be incomplete;
- Hachiman's inferences remain domain-dependent rather than automatically authoritative;
- Haruno's diagnostic claims are analytically important but not sovereign;
- later correction can revise earlier interpretation without retroactively erasing earlier ambiguity;
- and a successful outcome does not retrospectively make a questionable procedure clean.

This is especially important because the final movement contains unusually strong labels (`依存`, `共依存`, `自立`, `本物`) that tempt categorical reading.

# 3. Three-volume causal architecture

## 3.1 Volume 12: diagnosis enters after authorship has already entered

Volume 12 begins not with `共依存`, but with Yukino trying to author an answer.

Her initial request is not “help me with the prom.” It is:

> `あなたたちに、その最後を見届けてもらいたい`

She asks Hachiman and Yui to **witness the ending** of an attempt she intends to make herself (V12-E027). She simultaneously admits that this may not be the whole answer (V12-E029).

That sequencing matters. Yukino does not first receive a pathology label and then decide to become autonomous. She first recognizes an old desire, admits uncertainty, asks to be heard, decides to test that desire through action, and asks others to witness the attempt.

The prom later becomes one vehicle for that test, not the original definition of the request.

The core Volume-12 movement is therefore:

> **authorship before certainty**

Yukino can say, in effect, “I may still be wrong, but this is the choice I need to make as mine.”

Only after this autonomy experiment is underway does the explicit dependency vocabulary arrive.

## 3.2 Volume 12: the diagnosis identifies a real mechanism

The `共依存` sequence must be preserved at full evidentiary resolution.

Yukino first says explicitly that she is dependent upon Hachiman and Yui (V12-E157). Haruno then names the broader mechanism `共依存` (V12-E165). Haruno's sharpest question asks whether being relied upon feels good (V12-E168), prompting Hachiman to recognize that he seeks value through being needed (V12-E170).

This is not empty psychoanalytic decoration.

The diagnosis identifies a plausible reinforcement loop:

> **Hachiman feels useful when needed -> intervention becomes rewarding -> others learn that intervention will occur -> burdens migrate toward him -> being needed becomes further evidence of his value.**

Yui later names her own related mechanism: she depended on Yukino by pushing difficult choice onto her. Yukino and Yui therefore provide first-person evidence that decision burdens have been displaced within the relationship.

What Volume 12 does **not** establish is that every form of reliance among the trio is pathological, or that the clinically suggestive label automatically determines the remedy.

Hachiman himself supplies the decisive limit:

> `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。`

and refuses to let the label become his reason (V12-E182).

This is the first indispensable Checkpoint-E distinction:

> **Mechanism can explain persistence without deciding value, desire, or cure.**

## 3.3 Volume 12: non-intervention becomes explicit, so method becomes the next problem

Yukino's boundary is not implied. She tells Hachiman:

> `あなたの力はもう借りないわ`

and:

> `お願い。私にやらせて`

(V12-E156 through V12-E160).

The series therefore removes one of Hachiman's old excuses. He can no longer claim that the other person never clearly stated what she wanted.

When the prom is externally threatened, Hachiman's motive eventually reduces to an embarrassingly simple statement: he once promised that he would help her (V12-E184).

That matters because the motive is now personal rather than merely institutional. But motive and authorization must remain separate.

The unresolved question entering Volume 13 becomes:

> **What method can Hachiman use once the person he cares about has explicitly asked him not to take over?**

## 3.4 Volume 13: Hachiman changes the intervention form

Volume 13 does not permit a simple “he ignored her request” summary.

Hachiman recognizes that Yukino's choices, challenges, failures, and resignation should belong to her. Direct aid is refused. He then declares independent opposition, and Yukino subsequently accepts a contest/wager.

That creates a new authorization structure:

- direct assistance: refused;
- rivalry/competition: voluntarily accepted.

The dummy prom is therefore not best understood as Hachiman secretly doing Yukino's prom for her. It is **institutional choice-architecture manipulation**: he builds a worse competing proposal designed to make Yukino's real prom comparatively acceptable.

Her proposal remains operationally hers.

Yet this method is not ethically neutral. Hachiman deliberately alters the environment around Yukino so that her desired option becomes more likely to survive. Third parties are manipulated; institutional resources are consumed; the dummy project acquires real social consequences.

The volume's achievement is thus procedural, not exonerative:

> **He changes how he intervenes without proving that intervention itself is structurally healthy.**

## 3.5 Volume 13: collaborator consent improves materially

One of the most important controls against an overly cynical reading is Hachiman's treatment of collaborators.

He abandons the older logic of using uninformed people and apologizing later. He discloses the likelihood that some labor may be sacrificial, accepts Kawasaki's refusal, allows bounded participation, and can tell Totsuka `ありがとう、頼りにしてる`.

This matters longitudinally because it proves that “Hachiman still intervenes” is too coarse a measure of change.

His ethics improve along one axis even while remaining compromised on another:

- **collaborator agency:** improved;
- **target-level environmental manipulation:** still ethically contested.

That multidimensionality is central to V2.

## 3.6 Volume 13: the anti-codependency proof fails

Hachiman tries to reason that if Yukino does **not need** his help and he helps anyway, then their relationship cannot be codependent.

Haruno correctly attacks the logical weakness: changing the wording or subjective motive does not necessarily change the reinforcing structure.

This is one of the rare places where Haruno's critique should be strongly preserved.

Hachiman demonstrates something important:

> being needed is not a necessary condition of his desire to act.

He does **not** demonstrate:

> therefore the relationship contains no codependent mechanism.

Those propositions are not equivalent.

This is the second indispensable Checkpoint-E distinction:

> **Motive evidence can weaken one causal theory without proving the entire relation healthy.**

## 3.7 Volume 13: self-removal becomes the proposed cure

After the prom succeeds, Yukino explicitly says that she had come to expect Hachiman would somehow make it work and says:

> `あなたに依存していたの`

This is among the strongest first-person dependency evidence in the series.

The next move, however, is not automatically ratified simply because Yukino authors it.

She invokes the wager and asks Hachiman to grant Yui's wish. When Hachiman verifies whether this is genuinely her wish, she answers:

> `ええ、これが私の願い`

This must be respected as self-authored speech.

But it also has a jurisdiction problem.

The proposed solution is not merely:

> “I will withdraw.”

It implicitly allocates:

- Hachiman's romantic future;
- Yui's romantic future;
- Yui's friendship future with Yukino;
- and the structure of the triad.

Hachiman himself recognizes the limit:

> `これは俺とお前が勝手に決めることじゃない。由比ヶ浜も勝負に加わってる`

So Volume 13 reaches a critical V2 proposition:

> **Self-authorship does not grant unlimited authority over other people's futures.**

Yukino's choice is genuine and still procedurally overbroad.

## 3.8 Volume 13: pain and ending remain epistemically unstable

The final interlude tries to use pain as evidence that the chosen ending must be genuine, while admitting:

> `他に確かめる術を知らない`

The speaker lacks another way to verify the choice.

That makes the pain criterion epistemically fragile. Volume 12 already rejects a merely cold, cruel, sad `本物` as desirable simply because it hurts. Volume 13's closing voice therefore cannot safely be elevated into doctrine.

The phrase:

> `掛け替えのない、替えのきかない偽物`

captures the contradiction more productively.

The relationship can be judged counterfeit-like and still be singular, loved, and non-substitutable.

Volume 13 therefore ends not with “the relationship has been solved by ending,” but with a **closure regime whose authority remains contested**.

## 3.9 Volume 14: the proposed ending fails before the romantic answer succeeds

Volume 14 does not begin by revealing that Yukino's V13 wish was fake.

The better reading is that the closure bargain is real but cannot sustain itself once its premises are tested.

Yui and Yukino already know more than the bargain admits. Yui refuses unilateral closure. Hachiman behaves as though ordinary friendship after separation is implausible for him. Haruno's challenge lands partly because Hachiman himself is unconvinced by the explanation he has adopted.

The crucial move then comes from Hiratsuka.

She does not simply replace Haruno with a kinder diagnosis. She attacks the reductive use of diagnosis itself:

> `共依存なんて、簡単な言葉で括るなよ`

and:

> `そんな借りてきた言葉で誰かの気持ちを歪めるな`

Hachiman recognizes that he has used the label as `思考停止` and `現実逃避`.

This does not mean the earlier dependency evidence was false.

It means:

> **A mechanism became a borrowed total story, and that total story began doing the choosing for him.**

## 3.10 Volume 14: Yui forces first-person desire into the open

Yui's role in the resolution is not to bless the chosen couple or gracefully disappear.

She forces Hachiman to stop executing inherited wishes and state what **he** wants.

The eventual bridge proposition is:

> `俺は関わり続けたい`

This sentence matters because its grammatical subject is Hachiman.

He does not say:

- Yukino needs me;
- the club needs me;
- I promised to rescue her;
- codependency compels me;
- friendship requires me;
- or justice requires me.

He says that **he wants continued involvement**.

That is not automatically morally sufficient. It is, however, epistemically cleaner.

By Volume 14, personal desire becomes a legitimate reason to make a proposal because it is finally owned as personal desire rather than smuggled in as objective necessity.

## 3.11 Volume 14: the bridge is ethically imperfect and still answerable

The bridge must not be romanticized into perfect procedure.

Hachiman engineers the encounter and physically stops Yukino for a moment. That is coercive conduct and belongs in the ledger.

But the scene also changes procedure after that initial act:

- the physical hold ends;
- Yukino remains voluntarily;
- she interrogates his proposal;
- she challenges his inadequate valuation of her;
- Hachiman gives her explicit room to reject, discard, or refuse to answer;
- and Yukino insists on answering anyway.

Most importantly, she does not merely accept Hachiman's asymmetrical offer.

She makes the reciprocal request:

> `あなたの人生を、私にください`

That grammar matters.

Yukino converts:

> “I will involve myself in your life”

into:

> “Then give your life to me as well.”

The substantive conclusion therefore becomes reciprocal even though the encounter's beginning is procedurally flawed.

Checkpoint E freezes the bridge as:

> **a revision of Hachiman's intervention ethic, not its perfection.**

## 3.12 Volume 14: the cleanest autonomy image comes after the bridge

The later exchange is ethically cleaner than the bridge rhetoric itself.

Hachiman offers Yukino his hand.

She says:

> `一人で立てるのに……`

He answers:

> `知ってる`

and the narration explicitly confirms that he knows she can stand alone.

He offers the hand anyway.

She takes it.

This is the strongest autonomy image in the entire final movement because support is not legitimized by incapacity.

The logic is:

> **I know you can stand. I am still offering. You know I know. You are still accepting.**

That is chosen interdependence in its cleanest form.

## 3.13 Volume 14: romantic choice creates loss without requiring relational expulsion

Yui receives a genuine romantic answer.

Hachiman tells her that she does not need to wait for a future version of him. Her later private crying makes the cost explicit.

This prevents the ending from being sanitized as “everyone got what they wanted.”

But Yui also refuses to totalize that loss.

Her continuing `全部欲しい` means she still wants:

- Yukino;
- the ordinary shared space;
- access to the Hachiman/Yukino world;
- and honest acknowledgment of her own unresolved feeling.

The ending does not grant all those wishes in equivalent form, but neither does it require her to delete herself or Yukino from the future.

Yui's final movement is therefore:

> **romantic rejection without relational annihilation.**

## 3.14 Volume 14: the final Service Club changes what “continuation” means

The room reopens after the romantic choice.

This is not a restoration of innocence.

The trio now knows:

- Hachiman and Yukino have mutually chosen one another;
- Yui still loves Hachiman;
- Yukino knows Yui's position;
- Hachiman knows enough that protective ambiguity is no longer plausible;
- and nobody can return to pretending the geometry is symmetrical.

Yui enters not by silently swallowing the problem but by presenting it as a request/consultation.

She asks how she can remain close to her most important friend when that friend is effectively the partner of the person she loves.

Yukino answers:

> `……お話を、伺いましょうか`

Yui says the issue may continue indefinitely.

Yukino answers:

> `そうね。……きっと、ずっと続くわ`

The final image therefore preserves not a solved triangle but an **institution of continuing answerability**.

The relationship continues after the conflict becomes speakable.

# 4. Hikigaya Hachiman: from proxy reasons to owned desire

## 4.1 Incoming Checkpoint-D problem

Checkpoint D already established that Hachiman had become more capable of asking questions, revising social models, and recognizing his own projections. It also established that he could still use interpretation to override another person's answer.

The final movement therefore does not need to teach him that communication exists. It has to teach him a harder distinction:

> **another person's answer must be allowed to alter the intervener's jurisdiction, and the intervener's own desire must be stated as his rather than disguised as the other's need.**

## 4.2 Volume 12: diagnosis improves self-observation and worsens self-condemnation

Haruno's `共依存` formulation gives Hachiman language for something he could not previously describe well: the helper-side reward of being needed.

That insight should be preserved.

But Hachiman also begins overextending it. The cold explanatory force of the label tempts him to convert a complex relationship into a pathology story that can supposedly generate the right answer automatically.

His better move comes later, when Hiratsuka asks him to state why he wants to act after the prom cancellation.

He strips away theory and says:

> `……いつか、助けるって約束したから`

The sentence is still incomplete as an ethical justification. But it is the beginning of a crucial transition away from usefulness as self-definition.

## 4.3 Volume 13: method becomes an object of self-conscious design

Hachiman's Volume-13 reasoning contains a genuine ethical gain: he recognizes that Yukino's **failure**, not merely her success, belongs to her.

That is a large correction to his earlier rescue pattern.

He also begins treating collaborator consent much more seriously. Refusal can stand. Conditions can be negotiated. Reliance can be acknowledged directly.

Yet he still thinks strategically about the beneficiary's environment rather than simply accepting non-intervention.

This makes Volume 13 one of the clearest demonstrations that Hachiman's growth is **modular** rather than global.

He can improve in:

- collaborator ethics;
- information disclosure;
- willingness to rely;
- recognition of another person's right to fail;

while still manipulating institutions around a person whose outcome he cares about.

## 4.4 Hachiman's anti-codependency proof is a narratorial failure worth preserving

Hachiman wants his unwanted help to prove that his motive is not codependency.

The reasoning is emotionally revealing and logically insufficient.

It reveals:

- he wants to distinguish personal desire from being-needed reward;
- he wants the relationship not to be reducible to pathology;
- he is searching for a justification that will permit continued action.

It fails because:

- a relationship can contain multiple motives;
- wanting to help an independent person does not remove reinforcement elsewhere;
- structural dynamics are not disproved by one counterexample;
- and the desire to prove the diagnosis wrong can itself become a reason to preserve the same pattern.

V2 should retain this as an important Hachiman inference failure rather than smoothing it over because Volume 14 ultimately chooses continuation.

## 4.5 Volume 14: `共依存` becomes a rationalization audit

Hiratsuka's challenge changes the problem from:

> “Is the relationship codependent?”

into:

> “What are you using the word to avoid saying?”

Hachiman's recognition of `思考停止` / `現実逃避` is therefore not a rejection of psychology. It is an epistemic correction.

A borrowed label had become attractive because it offered:

- a total explanation;
- a morally respectable reason to withdraw;
- and a way not to state his own desire.

Once that shortcut is removed, he has to own the sentence:

> `俺は関わり続けたい`

This is the final major Hachiman narratorial transition.

## 4.6 First-person desire is not moral sovereignty

V2 must not overcorrect by treating first-person ownership as self-validating.

Hachiman's desire gives him standing to **propose** a future.

It does not give him authority to impose that future.

The bridge therefore has to be evaluated not only by what he says, but by whether Yukino can:

- remain or leave;
- reject the formulation;
- challenge the valuation embedded in it;
- demand reciprocity;
- and author a different request.

The scene becomes ethically stronger only as those answer-capacities emerge.

## 4.7 Hachiman's final doubt changes function

At the end, Hachiman still refuses certainty.

This is not stasis.

Earlier doubt often functioned as **pre-choice protection**:

> if motives are mixed, if understanding is incomplete, if the answer may hurt, do not choose.

The Volume-14 stance is different:

> choose, accept cost, and continue questioning afterward.

His `疑い続けます` therefore marks a change in the temporal position of skepticism.

Doubt moves from **before commitment** to **inside commitment**.

### Frozen Hachiman transition

> **Hachiman does not become ethically mature by ceasing to intervene or by proving his motives pure. He becomes more mature by distinguishing motive from authorization, allowing others' answers to constrain procedure, owning personal desire as personal desire, accepting that his choices will injure as well as help, and refusing to use the chosen outcome as proof that he was right all along.**

# 5. Yukinoshita Yukino: autonomy from self-proof to reciprocal authorship

## 5.1 Incoming Checkpoint-D state

By the end of Volume 11, Yukino can correct Hachiman when he speaks for her and can ask for help in her own name.

The unresolved problem is not whether she has a voice. It is whether she can build a stable self without making **separation from all support** the proof that the voice is hers.

## 5.2 Volume 12: `自分で` becomes an experimental method

Yukino's repeated desire to do things `自分で` is not merely pride.

It responds to several real injuries:

- her family did not take an earlier future desire seriously;
- she followed Haruno because she lacked another orientation;
- she feared testing the desire because a definite answer might require relinquishment;
- she had become accustomed to Hachiman and Yui taking some burdens from her;
- and she does not know whether her own choices remain hers when support is always available.

Her prom experiment is therefore analytically serious.

She needs evidence that she can initiate, decide, fail, and continue without another person silently becoming author in her place.

## 5.3 Her dependency self-description should not be downgraded

Because Volume 14 later attacks reductive use of `共依存`, there is a retrospective temptation to soften Yukino's Volume-12 and Volume-13 statements.

That would be a mistake.

When Yukino says she is dependent, and later says she had expected Hachiman to make the prom work, those are high-value first-person observations.

The later correction is not:

> “Yukino was never dependent.”

It is:

> **dependency is one fact about the relation, not the total meaning of the relation or an automatic instruction to sever it.**

## 5.4 Volume 13: self-removal is genuine agency with overbroad jurisdiction

Yukino's request that Hachiman grant Yui's wish should not be reduced to hidden insincerity.

She means it as an attempt to take responsibility for a relationship she believes has become unhealthy.

That is why the scene is tragic rather than merely evasive.

But V2 must distinguish:

- **authorship of one's withdrawal** from
- **authority to arrange the surviving parties' future**.

Yukino can choose to leave.

She cannot unilaterally determine what Hachiman owes Yui, what Yui wants from Yukino, or whether the triad can survive in another form.

Hachiman's triadic-jurisdiction objection is therefore ethically significant even though he is not entitled to decide Yukino's feelings either.

## 5.5 Volume 14: Yui's refusal is an autonomy correction, not selfish sabotage

Yui rejects Yukino's unilateral ending and insists that their friendship has standing independent of the romantic outcome.

This is important for Yukino's autonomy because it prevents “I will remove myself for your sake” from becoming unchallengeable simply because it is self-sacrificial.

Another person can say:

> your disappearance is not what I want from you.

That answer forces Yukino to confront the fact that self-removal can also become a way of deciding for others.

## 5.6 Reciprocal request replaces solitary proof

At the bridge, Yukino does not merely allow Hachiman to remain.

She responds with:

> `あなたの人生を、私にください`

The form is important because it changes the final autonomy model from:

> “I must prove I can do everything without you”

into:

> “I can author a claim on you while accepting that you are also making a claim on me.”

This is reciprocal exposure rather than dependency-by-default.

## 5.7 `一人で立てる` is not repudiated

The later stairs scene protects the integrity of Yukino's earlier autonomy project.

She says she can stand alone.

Hachiman says he knows.

The story does not reveal that her claim to competence was false. It preserves competence as the condition under which support can become chosen rather than necessary.

This is why the final model is better described as **interdependence after demonstrated capability** than as romantic rescue.

## 5.8 Family authorship remains unfinished

Romantic choice does not remove Yukino from her family problem.

By the end of Volume 14 she still:

- remains engaged with family expectations;
- participates in institutional responsibility;
- accepts that conflict with her mother may continue;
- and is not narratively extracted into a private couple world.

This matters because a rescue-romance reading would make Hachiman the answer to Yukino's selfhood.

The text instead incorporates him into a future in which Yukino still has to author herself across family, school, work, friendship, and romance.

### Frozen Yukino transition

> **Yukino's autonomy arc does not culminate in refusing help. It culminates in the ability to identify dependency without making it destiny, attempt independent action without treating isolation as purity, reject others' formulations of her feelings, make self-authored choices that can themselves be challenged, and finally accept support from a position of acknowledged capability while making reciprocal demands of her own.**

# 6. Yuigahama Yui: answer, loss, and non-erasure

## 6.1 Incoming Checkpoint-D state

Checkpoint D rejects the idea that Yui is merely a passive losing heroine.

She already:

- knows what she values;
- states that she wants the whole triadic ordinary life;
- completes her old request;
- proposes a preservation solution;
- acknowledges selfish/mixed motives;
- and remains after Hachiman rejects that solution.

Volumes 12-14 therefore test what happens when Yui's desire can no longer be preserved by ambiguity.

## 6.2 Volume 12: Yui recognizes her own decision displacement

Yui's hidden-photograph interlude is important because she knows enough to ask the decisive question and deliberately does not.

She fears that asking Yukino directly will make the relationship collapse.

Her later self-indictment is sharper: she says she depended on Yukino by pushing the choice onto her.

This is not simple cowardice.

It is a sophisticated recognition of how kindness and fear can cooperate:

> **If I let Yukino choose, I can preserve myself from being the one who forces the answer.**

That is why Yui's dependency language is best frozen as **decision displacement**.

## 6.3 Yui wants an answer, not rescue

The end of Volume 12 makes one of her strongest ethical claims.

She knows that crying visibly could activate Hachiman's rescue reflex. She refuses to become the pitiable girl whose pain compels him to change direction.

Her desire is not:

> protect me from being hurt.

It is closer to:

> **hurt me honestly if that is the answer.**

This substantially revises any account in which Yui's central wish is merely preservation of comfort.

She wants preservation **and** truth, even when those goods conflict.

## 6.4 Volume 13: Yui refuses route-totalization

Yui's promise to Yukino about future lunches, sleepovers, trips, Aprils, years, and decades is one of the most important anti-route statements in the series.

It means:

> a romantic outcome does not own the entire semantic future of the friendship.

This does not make romantic competition disappear.

It creates a second independent claim:

> **I want Hachiman, and I also want Yukino.**

That is why `全部欲しい` should not be dismissed as childish greed. It is the honest grammar of genuinely plural attachment under incompatible constraints.

## 6.5 Volume 14: Yui forces Hachiman to become a claimant

Yui's decisive contribution to the final resolution is procedural.

She refuses to let Hachiman remain merely the executor of Yukino's wish.

He must state his own desire.

This means the “losing” character performs one of the acts that makes the final chosen relationship more ethically legible.

Her agency is therefore not secondary to the romantic result.

## 6.6 The answer hurts, and the text allows it to hurt

Hachiman's answer forecloses the indefinite future option Yui had retained.

Her private crying matters because it prevents several evasions:

- friendship does not neutralize rejection;
- Yui's emotional intelligence does not make loss painless;
- being treated honestly does not make the answer pleasant;
- and the chosen couple's legitimacy does not depend on pretending nobody else is injured.

The final relationship acquires moral seriousness partly because its cost is not erased.

## 6.7 Yui's return is not evidence that rejection failed

Yui returns while still emotionally unresolved.

She knows the situation is `まちがってる` in some sense and moves toward the place where she wants to be anyway.

This should not be automatically pathologized as refusal to accept rejection.

She is not claiming romantic equivalence.

She is claiming continued social and emotional standing.

The final request makes that distinction explicit: how can she remain close to Yukino while loving the person who is now effectively Yukino's partner?

That is not denial of the answer. It is a request about **how to live after the answer**.

## 6.8 The final Yui position

Yui's role at the end is unusually difficult and therefore analytically important.

She is:

- romantically unsuccessful;
- still in love;
- still Yukino's closest friend;
- still part of the Service Club;
- no longer protected by ambiguity;
- and unwilling to solve pain through disappearance.

This is precisely the kind of non-equivalent continuation the earlier series could not imagine cleanly.

### Frozen Yui transition

> **Yui develops from an atmosphere-preserver who could defer choice through accommodation into an explicit claimant who can name selfish and caring desires together, demand an answer rather than rescue, endure a real romantic loss, refuse the destruction of her friendship with Yukino, and return with the remaining contradiction converted into an open request rather than a hidden burden.**

# 7. Haruno and Hiratsuka: diagnosis, provocation, and anti-reduction

## 7.1 Haruno's diagnostic contribution should be preserved

Checkpoint E should resist a common pendulum swing.

Once Volume 14 criticizes `共依存` as a reductive borrowed word, it is tempting to conclude that Haruno was simply wrong.

The evidence does not support that simplification.

Haruno correctly identifies several things:

- the trio's relation contains reinforcement loops;
- Hachiman can derive value from being relied upon;
- Yukino's reliance can become habitual enough that she expects intervention;
- the relation is not adequately described by a sentimental trust label;
- and all three can preserve dysfunctional patterns through roles that feel caring.

These are meaningful observations.

The error lies in extending them into a total theory of feeling and cure.

## 7.2 Haruno's authority decreases when her own desire becomes visible

Volume 13's Haruno interlude is methodologically important because the diagnostician becomes a subject.

She wants proof that a single `嘘偽りない唯一つの正しい結末` exists.

That makes her position more intelligible and less sovereign.

Her strict-purity demand is not the voice of the novel standing outside the problem. It is one participant's response to her own history of performance, compromise, family role, and uncertainty.

Her skepticism about counterfeit relationships therefore has autobiographical stakes.

## 7.3 Haruno can be locally right and globally wrong

This is the appropriate authority model for her across V12-14.

She can correctly say:

> Hachiman has changed the wording without necessarily changing the mechanism.

while being unable to prove:

> therefore separation is the only healthy endpoint.

She can correctly expose role performance without proving that performed selves are false selves.

She can correctly identify Hachiman's need to be needed without exhausting his attachment to Yukino.

The final movement is strongest when these claims are separated.

## 7.4 Hiratsuka's correction is methodological, not sentimental

Hiratsuka does not answer Haruno by saying:

> “No, you are all simply friends who love one another.”

Instead, she attacks the cognitive shortcut:

> do not wrap a person in an easy borrowed word and let that word distort the person's feelings.

This is consistent with her earlier Volume-11 principle that impressions of people must be continuously updated.

Her method asks Hachiman to:

- keep observing;
- keep thinking;
- verbalize his reason;
- distinguish explanation from motive;
- and preserve the other person's power to answer.

That method, rather than one final psychological theory, becomes the adult guidance that survives into the ending.

## 7.5 Hiratsuka also models healthy dependency termination

Her farewell provides a useful control.

Hachiman has depended enormously on Hiratsuka as teacher, institutional protector, ethical interlocutor, and person who repeatedly forces him to think more carefully.

Yet their relationship can end in its teacher-student form without being invalidated.

He stands under his own power when she offers a hand, then takes that same hand in gratitude before saying goodbye.

This parallels the Yukino hand scene but with a different outcome:

- capability recognized;
- help not required;
- connection acknowledged;
- role can end without relationship history being declared pathological.

### Frozen adult-countergaze transition

> **Haruno remains a high-value diagnostician whose strongest local observations survive, but her cure theory loses authority once her own purity longing becomes visible. Hiratsuka provides the stronger final method: do not let labels replace description, reasons replace motives, or diagnosis replace another person's answer.**

# 8. Request and intervention ethics

The V12-14 movement is one of the densest intervention-ethics sequences in the series.

## 8.1 Five distinct authorization states

The movement should not be compressed into “Hachiman helps Yukino despite being told not to.”

At minimum, it contains five distinct states:

1. **Witness authorization.** Yukino asks Hachiman and Yui to witness her self-authored attempt.
2. **Non-intervention boundary.** Yukino later asks Hachiman not to borrow his power and to let her do it.
3. **Rivalry authorization.** Hachiman declares himself an opponent; Yukino voluntarily accepts the contest/wager.
4. **Closure request.** Yukino uses the wager to ask Hachiman to grant Yui's wish.
5. **Reciprocal-life negotiation.** Hachiman rejects the closure premise, states personal desire, and Yukino answers with her own reciprocal request.

Each state grants and withholds different permissions.

## 8.2 Motive cannot substitute for authorization

The promise in Volume 12 is emotionally important because it shows Hachiman cares for Yukino beyond the pleasure of usefulness.

But:

> “I promised to help”

is not the same as:

> “I am authorized to choose any method of help.”

Similarly:

> “I want to remain involved”

creates standing to ask for continued relation, not standing to compel it.

V2 therefore freezes motive and method as separate axes.

## 8.3 Rivalry is ethically preferable to substitution in one respect

The dummy-prom strategy allows Yukino to remain the author of the real prom.

Hachiman does not secretly replace her labor or present his plan as hers.

Competition gives Yukino a structurally different role from the recipient of rescue.

That is genuine progress.

But rivalry also permits Hachiman to manipulate the institutional environment while claiming not to help.

The relevant judgment is therefore:

> **better than direct substitution, not cleanly non-intervention.**

## 8.4 Collaborator consent becomes a major positive control

The dummy-prom team demonstrates what improved Hachiman procedure looks like when the people affected are actually asked.

He increasingly:

- discloses the nature of the work;
- allows refusal;
- negotiates bounded contribution;
- distributes burden;
- thanks people directly;
- and accepts reliance without converting it into debt.

This provides concrete evidence that his ethics are changing rather than merely his rhetoric.

## 8.5 The closure wager exposes the jurisdiction problem

Yukino has standing to say:

> “I want to leave.”

She does not have unlimited standing to say:

> “Therefore Hachiman should fulfill Yui's romantic wish, Yui should accept that arrangement, and our friendship should take the shape I assign to it.”

Hachiman similarly lacks standing to reject Yukino's feelings for her.

This is why the trio cannot be ethically governed by any dyad acting alone.

The final movement makes **triadic jurisdiction** explicit.

## 8.6 The bridge begins below the final standard

Physical stopping and encounter engineering must remain in the ledger.

The scene should not be retroactively cleansed because Yukino eventually reciprocates.

A later yes does not transform every earlier procedural act into good consent practice.

The ethical gain occurs as the scene transitions from constraint into answerability:

- physical release;
- continued voluntary presence;
- challenge;
- permission to reject;
- reciprocal formulation.

This temporal distinction matters.

## 8.7 Reciprocal requests are stronger than rescue contracts

The bridge becomes more ethically legible because Yukino refuses to remain merely the recipient of Hachiman's life-offer.

She makes a counter-request.

The structure becomes:

> I want to remain involved with you.

answered by:

> Then place your life within my claim as well.

The relationship becomes mutually binding rather than savior/beneficiary.

## 8.8 The final Service Club converts conflict into an admissible request

Yui's final return is the clearest institutionalization of answerability.

Her problem is not solved before she enters.

She brings the unresolved problem into the club and asks to discuss it.

The room's function has changed from:

> solve external requests while our own relationship remains the unspoken condition

into:

> **our relationship itself can become the request.**

### Frozen intervention standard

> **The mature Oregairu standard is not non-intervention. It is answerable involvement: motive must be owned, authorization must be granular, refusal must have force, beneficiaries retain the right to redefine the problem, collaborators retain refusal rights, and no one participant may use self-sacrifice or care to author every affected person's future.**

# 9. Dependency and autonomy

## 9.1 Dependency is real at multiple points in the system

Checkpoint E preserves the following without euphemism:

- Yukino says she depends on Hachiman and Yui;
- Yukino later says she had depended on Hachiman to make the prom work;
- Hachiman recognizes that being needed can give him value;
- Yui says she depended on Yukino by transferring difficult choice;
- Iroha recognizes over-reliance and corrects it;
- the trio has historically used role division to avoid direct ownership.

Any synthesis claiming “there was never codependency” is therefore too weak.

## 9.2 `共依存` is strongest as mechanism language

The label is useful when it identifies feedback:

- one person rescues;
- another comes to expect rescue;
- the rescuer receives identity reward;
- both sides reproduce the pattern;
- third parties can route difficult decisions through the same system.

It becomes weaker when asked to answer questions outside its scope:

- Who is loved?
- Is the attachment real?
- Must the relationship end?
- Is all help unhealthy?
- Can a formerly reinforcing relationship change form?

Volume 14's critique is therefore best understood as a **scope correction**, not a factual reversal.

## 9.3 Self-removal is not synonymous with autonomy

Yukino's V13 solution assumes that ending the relation may end the dependency mechanism.

Mechanically, that is plausible.

Ethically and psychologically, however, it is incomplete.

A person can remove themselves while still:

- allowing another person's predicted need to determine the choice;
- deciding for others what would be good for them;
- using pain as evidence that the choice is correct;
- or mistaking isolation for authorship.

Autonomy therefore cannot be measured by distance alone.

## 9.4 The final positive controls

The final movement supplies several healthier forms of reliance:

### Yui's friendship condition

Yui agrees not to interfere with Yukino's autonomy experiment while requiring Yukino to ask for help if she genuinely needs it.

This preserves both non-substitution and access to support.

### Hachiman's collaborators

People can refuse or contribute in bounded ways. Reliance is negotiated rather than silently extracted.

### Hiratsuka farewell

A dependent student can become capable of standing without retroactively invalidating the support that got him there.

### Yukino hand scene

Hachiman knows she can stand; she knows he knows; help is offered anyway; she chooses to accept.

This is the cleanest direct answer to the atomistic model of independence.

## 9.5 Chosen interdependence

The final autonomy formulation should therefore be frozen as:

> **Autonomy is the preservation of authorship inside relation, not the elimination of relation.**

Its practical indicators are:

- ability to say “I don't know”;
- ability to initiate a request;
- ability to refuse direct aid;
- ability to choose a different kind of involvement;
- ability to fail without another person owning the failure;
- ability to accept help without being defined by incapacity;
- ability to challenge the helper's formulation;
- ability to make reciprocal claims;
- ability to remain connected after receiving an unwanted answer;
- and ability to revise the relation later.

The phrase `一人で立てるのに……` / `知ってる` condenses this model more cleanly than the technical language of `共依存` ever could.

# 10. `本物`, authenticity, and anti-certification

## 10.1 Volume 12: painful truth is not sufficient

The movement begins by explicitly rejecting a `本物` that is only cold, cruel, and sad.

This is important because it prevents the final arc from becoming a morality play in which maximum pain automatically equals maximum authenticity.

Yui's `本物なんて、ほしくなかった` is similarly contextual rather than doctrinal. She fears a mode of truth-extraction that destroys the relationships before the participants know how to live with the result.

The problem is therefore not truth versus falsity alone.

It is:

> **what procedure can make truth livable without making care an excuse for indefinite evasion?**

## 10.2 Volume 13: counterfeit becomes historically singular

Hayama's counter-theory is one of the most important contributions of the final movement.

Even a `紛い物`, `贋作`, or `偽物` can become so historically particular that “fake” no longer exhausts its identity.

This destabilizes a purity ontology.

A relationship can have:

- compromised origins;
- performance;
- mistaken expectations;
- avoidance;
- dependency;

and still acquire real history that cannot simply be exchanged for a cleaner equivalent.

The phrase `掛け替えのない、替えのきかない偽物` is therefore not mere despair. It names the failure of the binary itself.

## 10.3 Volume 14: romantic love is true and still not the complete definition

Yukino's:

> `あなたが好きよ。比企谷くん`

is explicit, conventional, and important.

V2 should not evade the simplicity of this sentence in order to preserve theoretical complexity.

But the sentence does not invalidate Hachiman's claim that one word is insufficient to exhaust the relation.

Both can be true:

- “I love you” is a true statement;
- “love” does not encode every dependency, history, rivalry, debt, respect, jealousy, family implication, and future obligation in the relation.

Watari's afterword reinforces this expressive plurality rather than supplying one hidden key.

## 10.4 Hiratsuka's final formulation remains modal

Hiratsuka suggests that an accumulation of empathy, familiarity, curiosity, pity, respect, jealousy, and something more toward one person **might** be called `本物`.

The grammar is not a metaphysical definition.

It is a possibility.

Hachiman immediately refuses easy certification.

## 10.5 `疑い続けます`: doubt after commitment

This is the final authenticity move.

The series does not end with:

> “I have proven that this is genuine.”

It ends with a person who has made a costly choice saying that he expects to keep doubting whether the choice is right.

That is why **anti-certification** is the strongest final `本物` formulation.

The standard becomes:

> **choose strongly enough to incur obligation, then remain willing to scrutinize the relation instead of using the fact of choice as evidence that the relation is beyond criticism.**

## 10.6 Revised V1 synthesis term: accountable continuation

V1's phrase **accountable continuation** remains valuable but needs precision.

“Accountable” can sound as though the mainline ending has achieved a stable procedural equilibrium.

Volume 14 does not establish that.

The stronger checkpoint formulation is:

> **chosen, answerable continuation under non-equivalence and uncertainty.**

This preserves:

- choice;
- responsibility;
- continuing conflict;
- Yui's unequal romantic position;
- Yukino's remaining family and dependency concerns;
- Hachiman's continuing procedural flaws;
- and the refusal to treat the ending as proof of perfection.

# 11. Prom, institutions, and social systems

## 11.1 The prom is not only a romantic metaphor

Throughout V12-14, the prom remains an actual governance problem involving:

- preliminary administrative approval;
- student ownership;
- parental minority objections;
- alcohol/sexual/safety concerns;
- online publicity;
- school reputation;
- future precedent;
- alumni/future-graduate standing;
- adult responsibility;
- and who bears downstream complaints.

Reducing the prom to the triangle would erase one of the arc's central tests of agency.

## 11.2 Iroha remains an independent institutional subject

The prom matters to Iroha for reasons not reducible to Hachiman, Yukino, or Yui.

She wants students who did the planning to have standing. She thinks about precedent and future students. She develops as an office-holder who can increasingly own institutional work.

This matters methodologically because Hachiman's strategic use of the prom affects a project that belongs to more people than the trio.

## 11.3 The dummy prom becomes real enough to incur responsibility

A deliberately weak proposal still produces:

- labor;
- collaborator relationships;
- publicity;
- stakeholder responses;
- and institutional learning.

This is another final-arc instance of the counterfeit paradox:

> **a fake project can have real consequences.**

The moral status of its origin does not erase the reality of the people working within it.

## 11.4 Yukino's mother changes role across the movement

She is not merely a villainous veto point.

She represents:

- parent/community responsibility;
- anticipated objection;
- reputational governance;
- family power;
- and a social world Yukino must learn to negotiate rather than simply flee.

Hachiman's more effective final institutional move is not defeating her but recognizing her as an intermediary capable of persuading the parent network.

This is a major shift from opponent logic to stakeholder logic.

## 11.5 The final project network prevents couple enclosure

After the romantic choice, the story returns to collective work.

The joint prom includes:

- Hachiman;
- Yukino;
- Yui;
- Iroha;
- classmates;
- collaborators from the dummy project;
- adults;
- family stakeholders.

The chosen dyad is therefore embedded in a social system rather than sealed off from it.

This matters for the ending's ethics: romance does not become the only relationship that still counts.

# 12. Relationship-state freeze at the end of Volume 14

## 12.1 Hachiman <-> Yukino

**Incoming Checkpoint D:** high mutual significance; increasingly direct personal inquiry; stronger privacy ethics; chosen reliance; explicit selfhood/autonomy conflict; Hachiman still tempted to protect through interpretation; Yukino increasingly capable of correcting that interpretation and originating help requests. Romantic content materially significant but unresolved.

**Checkpoint E additions:**

- Yukino defines an autonomy experiment and asks Hachiman/Yui to witness it;
- she explicitly refuses Hachiman's direct help;
- she later consents to rivalry;
- Hachiman intervenes indirectly through institutional choice architecture;
- Yukino explicitly identifies her dependence on him;
- Yukino attempts a self-removing closure through the wager;
- Hachiman states that the dyad cannot ethically decide the triad for Yui;
- the closure bargain fails to hold;
- Hachiman stops relying on `共依存`, promise, or rescue as final explanations and says he wants continued involvement;
- Yukino challenges and answers the proposal rather than merely receiving it;
- Yukino makes the reciprocal life request;
- she later explicitly says she loves him;
- practical help becomes ordinary and consented;
- family/institutional future enters the relation rather than disappearing.

**Frozen state:**

> **mutually chosen romantic and life involvement; reciprocal claims rather than savior/beneficiary roles; continued risk of over-involvement; Yukino's competence explicitly recognized; support increasingly chosen rather than justified by need; family and institutional conflict remain active; relation is selected without being certified as healthy, permanent, or complete.**

## 12.2 Hachiman <-> Yui

**Incoming Checkpoint D:** Yui's romantic desire highly legible; Yui active rather than passive; Hachiman knows their wishes do not perfectly overlap; dyad caring and future-capable but unable to remain ethically neutral about romantic consequence.

**Checkpoint E additions:**

- Yui recognizes and names her own decision displacement;
- two-person ordinary intimacy continues through Volume 12;
- Yui refuses rescue as substitute for an answer;
- she collaborates closely with Hachiman during the dummy-prom work;
- she repeatedly distinguishes certainty of love from certainty about the relation's ontology;
- she forces Hachiman to state what he wants;
- Hachiman gives a real negative romantic answer rather than indefinite ambiguity;
- Yui experiences genuine grief;
- she returns without claiming romantic equivalence;
- she converts the remaining problem into a request rather than hidden suffering.

**Frozen state:**

> **romantic non-reciprocity is explicit and painful; social intimacy and moral standing continue; rescue ambiguity is rejected; Yui is permitted both loss and future presence; the dyad no longer depends on pretending the romantic question is open.**

## 12.3 Yukino <-> Yui

**Incoming Checkpoint D:** real friendship, real rivalry, real care, competing futures, and enough conflict tolerance that neither harmony nor romantic victory can be treated as the sole condition of continuation.

**Checkpoint E additions:**

- Yui knows more about Yukino's romantic position than she initially makes answerable;
- Yui admits that routing choice through Yukino was a form of dependence;
- Yukino's V13 self-removal attempts to allocate Yui's future;
- Yui explicitly rejects Yukino disappearing from her life;
- Yui names a long future of friendship independent of romantic outcome;
- Volume 14 allows the two girls to negotiate the proposed ending directly;
- Yukino states her feeling and Yui reciprocates disclosure;
- after Hachiman chooses Yukino, Yui returns specifically because Yukino still matters;
- Yukino visibly welcomes Yui's return;
- the final consultation is jointly inhabited rather than treated as Yui's private defect.

**Frozen state:**

> **romantic rivalry is fully explicit but friendship has independent authorship; neither girl's care is reducible to self-sacrifice; Yukino cannot solve the triangle by disappearing for Yui, and Yui does not require romantic victory in order to claim Yukino as part of her future.**

## 12.4 The triad

The end-of-Volume-14 triad has the following features:

- romantic equivalence is gone;
- Hachiman and Yukino have mutually chosen one another;
- Yui's continuing love is known;
- Yui's loss is real;
- the old protective ambiguity is no longer available;
- `共依存` is no longer allowed to define the whole relation;
- the Service Club institution is recreated;
- Yui returns voluntarily;
- Yukino invites her into the conversation;
- and the remaining contradiction is framed as a problem that may continue indefinitely.

**Frozen state:**

> **continued triadic relation after acknowledged non-equivalence.**

This is not reconciliation through sameness. It is continuation after sameness has become impossible.

## 12.5 Hachiman <-> Hiratsuka

Frozen as:

- a successfully differentiating mentor relationship;
- proof that substantial dependence can end in one form without invalidating its history;
- final adult challenge against diagnostic reduction;
- a model in which capability is recognized before gratitude and continued attachment are expressed.

## 12.6 Hachiman <-> Haruno

Frozen as:

- adversarial interpreters with some shared sensitivity to false explanation;
- Haruno capable of exposing Hachiman's rhetorical evasions;
- Hachiman increasingly able to treat her claim as one interpretation rather than final truth;
- Haruno's purity demand understood partly as her own unresolved desire for proof.

## 12.7 Yukino <-> family

Frozen as:

- no romantic rescue from the family system;
- increased willingness to state future desires;
- continued engagement with mother/Haruno rather than escape;
- growing ability to accept conflict as part of authorship;
- Hachiman increasingly incorporated into, not substituted for, that future social world.

# 13. Japanese-language and voice freeze

## 13.1 `自分で`

Across Volume 12, `自分で` functions as Yukino's autonomy marker.

Its importance is procedural rather than atomistic. It means she needs the act of choosing and doing to pass through **her**, not that no one may ever participate.

Volume 14 does not negate this lexicon. It revises the relation between `自分で` and support.

## 13.2 `依存` / `共依存`

The lexical progression is crucial:

1. Yukino uses `依存` as self-description.
2. Haruno introduces `共依存` as system diagnosis.
3. Hachiman recognizes helper-side reward.
4. Hachiman says `共依存` is a `仕組み`, not a `気持ち`.
5. Yui uses `依存` for her own decision displacement.
6. Yukino later uses `依存` again for her expectation that Hachiman would solve the prom.
7. Volume 14 attacks using `共依存` as a total borrowed label.

The lexical history itself shows why the final synthesis cannot simply choose “diagnosis true” or “diagnosis false.”

## 13.3 `願い`

`願い` changes ownership across the movement.

It begins as something participants may try to grant for someone else. By the end, the decisive utterances are increasingly first-person claims:

- Yukino names her own wish;
- Yui names that she wants everything;
- Hachiman states he wants continued involvement;
- Yukino makes a reciprocal request for Hachiman's life;
- Yui returns with a final consultation whose object is her own continuing relation.

The movement is from **wish administration** to **wish authorship**.

## 13.4 `終わる / 終わらせる`

Ending loses its moral neutrality.

Across V12-13, ending can sound like proof of independence or willingness to accept painful truth.

V14 demonstrates that an ending can also be:

- a borrowed cure;
- a self-removal strategy;
- a way to avoid stating desire;
- or a decision imposed on absent participants.

The verb therefore stops functioning as automatic evidence of maturity.

## 13.5 `ちゃんと`

`ちゃんと` remains one of the final arc's procedural seriousness markers.

Characters repeatedly want to:

- speak properly;
- decide properly;
- explain properly;
- end properly;
- and answer properly.

The recurrence reveals a shared anxiety that **having a feeling is not enough; the way one acts on it matters**.

## 13.6 `義務` versus `意志`

Hachiman's late movement is linguistically a movement away from duty-like explanations and toward volition.

The force of `俺は関わり続けたい` comes from the explicit first-person subject and desire predicate.

The series does not thereby endorse every act that follows. It does make concealment behind obligation harder.

## 13.7 `一人で立てる` / `知ってる`

This exchange is linguistically sparse because the relational infrastructure has already been negotiated.

Yukino's phrase asserts capacity.

Hachiman's `知ってる` validates rather than contests that capacity.

The hand offered afterward therefore cannot honestly be narrated as rescue from helplessness.

## 13.8 `疑い続けます`

The aspectual force matters.

`続ける` makes doubt a continuing practice rather than a one-time obstacle.

Hachiman does not say he has solved uncertainty. He commits to living with it.

## 13.9 Yui's voice

Yui's first-person language becomes increasingly direct while preserving emotional complexity.

`全部欲しい` is especially important because it refuses the demand that she convert mixed desire into a morally cleaner single wish.

Her final consultation is syntactically awkward because the situation is awkward. Indirectness here opens a conversation rather than suppressing one.

## 13.10 Yukino's voice

Yukino's final direct `あなたが好きよ。比企谷くん` comes **after** the more structurally demanding reciprocal-life request.

That order matters.

The conventional confession is not the entire resolution. It is a simple true statement spoken after the relation has already been negotiated in more complex terms.

# 14. Adversarial tests and rejected simplifications

## 14.1 "Haruno was right: they were codependent, and separation was the cure"

**REJECT as total synthesis.**

Dependency mechanisms are strongly evidenced. Separation as the sole cure is not.

# 18. Checkpoint-E frozen thesis and outgoing boundary

## 18.1 Canonical frozen thesis

> **The prom/final-choice movement does not solve Oregairu by identifying a pure relationship and deleting the impure ones. It solves a different problem: who is allowed to author the reasons, methods, losses, and continuations by which these relationships change. Volume 12 makes dependency explicit but simultaneously limits diagnostic authority: `共依存` can describe a mechanism without deciding a feeling or cure. Volume 13 then reveals how difficult that distinction is to live. Hachiman changes intervention method, preserves more of Yukino's operational authorship, and improves collaborator consent, yet still manipulates institutional choice architecture and cannot logically prove the relationship healthy. Yukino's self-removing solution is genuinely hers, but because it attempts to distribute Hachiman and Yui's future as well as her own, authorship exceeds into jurisdiction. Volume 14 dismantles that closure regime by refusing to let a borrowed diagnosis, pain, sacrifice, or the idea of a clean ending choose for the participants. Hachiman finally states first-person desire rather than Yukino's need; Yui receives a real romantic answer and real grief without accepting the destruction of her friendship; Yukino remains capable of standing alone and nevertheless chooses reciprocal support. The final Service Club therefore does not restore innocence or symmetry. It institutionalizes a harder relation in which non-equivalent desires are known and may remain difficult indefinitely. `本物` is not proven. Hachiman's final answer is to choose and keep doubting. The mainline endpoint is consequently a chosen, answerable continuation under non-equivalence and uncertainty: attachment strong enough to incur obligation and loss, but not so sanctified that it becomes immune to refusal, correction, or future revision.**

Compactly:

> **The final movement does not cure dependency by separation or vindicate attachment by romance. It replaces proxy explanations with owned desire and tests whether people can remain connected while preserving one another's right to answer differently.**

Freeze the following and admit no later evidence yet:

## 18.3 What Volume 14.5 is now allowed to test

Because Checkpoint E is frozen first, Volume 14.5 may now legitimately test the **durability** of the mainline answer rather than help create it.

The next reading should ask:
