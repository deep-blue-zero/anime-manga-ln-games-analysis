# OREGAIRU V2 — Request / Intervention Ethics Ledger

## Function

This ledger consolidates the V2 reread's complete request-and-intervention ethics trail through Volume 14.5. It is not a ranking of characters by moral virtue and it is not a list of whether each plan “worked.” Its function is to preserve how *Oregairu* repeatedly separates five questions that Hachiman, the Service Club, adults, families, clients, and friends often collapse:

1. **Who owns the problem?**
2. **Who has reason to care or act?**
3. **Who authorized what kind of involvement?**
4. **Who chooses the method and bears its costs?**
5. **Who retains the right to define whether the result counts as help?**

The governing distinction is:

> **Caring about someone ≠ having reason to act ≠ having authority to decide.**

The series does not move toward a simple doctrine of non-intervention. Some of its most ethical acts are forceful: Yui prevents the Volume-9 conversation from ending; Hiratsuka repeatedly creates situations students would not create on their own; friends state preferences that influence one another; the Service Club changes institutional choice environments. Conversely, passivity can be irresponsible. Volume 14.5 explicitly recognizes that telling someone merely to “decide for yourself” may transfer responsibility rather than respect autonomy.

The mature standard is therefore **answerable involvement**: intervention should leave affected people with meaningful standing to answer, refuse, revise, reinterpret, or redirect what is being done in their lives.

Every ledger entry routes back to canonical V2 deep readings and frozen checkpoints. Exact Japanese wording, EPUB paragraph IDs, PDF pages, and evidence IDs remain governed by those source artifacts. This file is the canonical series-level ethical routing layer for later specialist synthesis, especially `07_HELPING_SELF_SACRIFICE_DEPENDENCY_AND_AUTONOMY.md`.

# 1. Ethical protocol

## 1.1 Required fields

Each major request/intervention is evaluated across the following dimensions.

- **problem owner(s):** whose life, work, relationship, office, or future is substantively at stake;
- **stated requester:** who asks for assistance, if anyone;
- **request scope:** what was actually asked, rather than what the helper later decides would be good;
- **inferred need:** any unstated problem the helper believes lies beneath the request;
- **authorization:** explicit, implicit, institutional, delegated, contested, absent, or later ratified;
- **authorization granularity:** permission to care, advise, participate, investigate, disclose, decide, coerce, or bear cost are not interchangeable;
- **method:** advice, teaching, information, pretext, environmental restructuring, deception, coercion, role assignment, substitution, confrontation, mediation, resource provision, refusal, or presence;
- **information quality:** what is known, inferred, concealed, fabricated, or withheld;
- **cost bearer(s):** social, emotional, reputational, institutional, material, physical, or opportunity cost;
- **agency effect:** whether the subject gains options, loses options, receives information, is forced into a role, has speech blocked, or retains final authorship;
- **value substitution:** whether the helper silently replaces the subject's goal with another value such as harmony, protection, growth, autonomy, reputation, or authenticity;
- **refusal handling:** whether a no changes the helper's conduct or is merely treated as another obstacle/data point;
- **reversibility:** whether the intervention can be undone without lasting social or institutional consequence;
- **outcome:** what actually happens;
- **relational externality:** what the method does to trust, disclosure, obligation, or future answerability;
- **ethical classification:** the V2 judgment at that chronology boundary.

## 1.2 Evidence rules

1. **A successful outcome cannot retroactively authorize a method.**
2. **A bad outcome does not automatically prove the method illegitimate.** Ethical procedure and empirical effectiveness are separate axes.
3. **Accurate diagnosis does not create jurisdiction.** Hachiman may correctly understand a social mechanism and still lack authority to choose for the people inside it.
4. **Benevolent motive does not create jurisdiction.** `傷ついてほしくない` can be sincere and still support paternalistic action.
5. **Self-borne cost does not create jurisdiction.** A person may consent to injure himself; he cannot thereby consent on behalf of everyone who must live with the consequences.
6. **Request from A does not authorize action over B without limit.** Multi-person cases require stakeholder separation.
7. **Refusal has moral force.** It is not merely information to be modeled around.
8. **Explicit request is not always necessary for every kind of care.** Emergencies, constrained expression, ordinary friendship, and institutional duty complicate a pure request-only model.
9. **Absence of request is not proof of absence of need.** Volume 4 makes this explicit through Rumi. The safeguard is not blindness; it is humility about what inference authorizes.
10. **Influence is compatible with autonomy.** Advice, persuasion, emotional importance, and known preference do not erase authorship if the subject retains meaningful capacity to answer and revise.
11. **Non-intervention can be trust.** It can also be abandonment or responsibility transfer. Context matters.
12. **Disclosure is not transferable property.** Learning something from one person does not automatically authorize sharing it with another.
13. **Formal questions can still be coercive.** The surrounding normative environment can heavily prewrite which answer appears acceptable.
14. **Pretext is not automatically deception in the morally relevant sense.** Stakes, transparency, exit rights, and whether the pretext lowers expressive risk or commandeers a life decision all matter.
15. **The helped person retains interpretive standing.** The helper does not possess final authority to announce that another person was saved, harmed, freed, or fulfilled.

## 1.3 Intervention classes

The corpus repeatedly produces several distinct intervention types. They should not be collapsed into “manipulation.”

| Class | Definition | Typical risk |
|---|---|---|
| **capacity support** | information, skill, resources, contingency, or assistance that preserves the subject's goal | helper overestimating what goal should be preserved |
| **substitutionary competence** | more capable helper performs the role instead of enabling its owner | learned dependence; loss of authorship |
| **choice architecture** | altering incentives/options while leaving a formal choice | informational distortion; hidden pressure |
| **coercive mediation** | imposing confrontation, shame, or cost to force movement | deliberative agency loss |
| **pretext / expressive infrastructure** | indirect frame that makes a difficult action easier to attempt | deniability becoming avoidance; unequal awareness |
| **self-sacrificial social engineering** | helper makes self the cost-bearing instrument to control group reaction | collateral cost; unconsented role assignment |
| **harm redistribution** | moving cost to someone judged better able to absorb it | resilience mistaken for permission |
| **recipient-authored request** | subject defines goal and solicits bounded participation | request overread as unlimited license |
| **non-intervention / entrustment** | helper deliberately refrains because subject can own the task | abandonment disguised as autonomy |
| **answerability restoration** | intervention prevents premature closure so actual stakeholders can answer | force used in the name of speech |
| **presence / witness** | staying available without taking over | passivity if material help is actually needed |
| **reciprocal claim** | both parties openly place themselves under consequences of continued relation | mutuality mistaken for equal power/cost |

## 1.4 Phase backbone

| Boundary | Intervention-ethics state |
|---|---|
| Checkpoint A, V01–V03 | request grammar forms; early positive controls coexist with paternalistic founding and interpretive closure |
| Checkpoint B, V04–V06.5 | intervention becomes aggressive; self-sacrifice, substitution, coercion, and incentive engineering are differentiated |
| Checkpoint C, V07–V09 + Christmas coda | accurate social prediction loses its claim to jurisdiction; Hachiman becomes requester; authorization becomes ongoing and domain-specific |
| Checkpoint D, V10–V11 | conflicting requests, refusal, information ownership, capacity-before-commitment, non-intervention, and recipient-authored requests become explicit |
| Checkpoint E, V12–V14 | explicit non-intervention boundary forces method redesign; motive is separated from authority; reciprocal claim replaces rescue logic |
| Final durability, V14.5 | mature autonomy permits influence and advice; non-intervention is no longer treated as the only safe ethical position |

The longitudinal movement is:

> **solve the problem -> ask whose problem it is -> ask who authorized the solution -> return answer capacity -> accept conflicting answers -> own one's own desire -> participate without claiming total jurisdiction.**

# 2. Executive full-series ethics thesis

The Service Club begins from a paradox. Its institutional purpose is to help people become capable of solving their own problems, yet its founding itself is paternalistic: Hiratsuka decides Hachiman needs reform and orders him into an arrangement he did not request. That contradiction is productive rather than accidental. Across the series, helping repeatedly becomes dangerous precisely when a helper's competence, care, status, or predictive intelligence makes it easy to confuse **ability to improve an outcome** with **authority to choose the outcome**.

Hachiman is the most sustained case, but the problem is not uniquely his. Yukino's competence can become substitution. Yui's kindness can become preservation. Haruno's insight can become invasive authorship. Yukino's mother can ask an open question inside a heavily prewritten normative frame. Even arguments for autonomy can become paternalistic when one person decides what another person's independence ought to look like.

The series gradually builds a more demanding standard.

A good intervention does not require perfect neutrality. Helpers can have preferences. They can persuade. They can say “I want you to do this.” They can create occasions, offer resources, reshape institutional options, or refuse to let an evasive conversation end. But the subject must remain a **participant in the meaning of the intervention**, not merely the object whose welfare is optimized.

The mature V2 principle is therefore:

> **Ethical help expands or preserves another person's capacity to author consequential choices while making the helper's own motives, limits, costs, and claims answerable.**

This is why the late-series positive controls are not scenes where nobody needs anybody. They are scenes where need and capability coexist:

- Yukino can stand alone and still take Hachiman's hand.
- Yui can care about Iroha and `信じて任せる`.
- Hachiman can promise Komachi that he will be waiting without guaranteeing her exam result.
- collaborators can know a plan may fail and still choose to participate.
- Yui can state a preference about the Service Club without deciding for Komachi.
- Komachi can be influenced by people she loves without losing authorship.

The terminal intervention ethic is **answerable involvement**, not purified independence.

# 3. Longitudinal request / intervention ledger

## Phase A — V01–V03: request grammar before high-cost manipulation

### RIE-001 — V01: Hiratsuka's founding intervention into Hachiman

**Problem definition:** Hiratsuka judges Hachiman's social and moral posture deficient.

**Requester:** none from Hachiman.

**Authorization:** institutional authority, low personal authorization.

**Method:** compel participation in the Service Club and assign Yukino a reformative function.

**Agency effect:** mixed. Hachiman is coerced into a setting that later becomes meaningful, but benefit does not erase that the beneficiary did not define the intervention.

**Ethical classification:** **paternalistic institutional origin**.

**Longitudinal importance:** the series' request institution is born from an override. Later ethical sophistication should not be read as though the club began from pure consent.

**Source route:** V01 §7.1; Checkpoint A §7.1.

### RIE-002 — V01: Yui's cookie request

**Problem owner/requester:** Yui.

**Stated goal:** make handmade cookies for someone she likes.

**Yukino's initial method:** technical instruction and effort discipline.

**Problem:** technical competence does not exhaust Yui's interpersonal goal.

**Hachiman's method:** a small, reversible instrumental misdirection using an imperfect cookie to alter the evaluative frame.

**Agency effect:** positive overall. Yui still chooses whether and how to act.

**Ethical classification:** **imperfect but constructive support; low-stakes deception with preserved authorship**.

**Source route:** V01 §7.2; Checkpoint A §7.2.

### RIE-003 — V01: Miura confrontation on Yui's behalf

**Request:** no clean request authorizing outsiders to repair Yui's group position.

**Method:** Hachiman/Yukino confront coercive group dynamics they infer.

**Outcome:** Yui's own speech becomes decisive.

**Agency effect:** ultimately expanded, but later ratification cannot become a universal doctrine of inferred-need intervention.

**Ethical classification:** **beneficial inferred-need intervention with authorization warning**.

**Source route:** V01 §7.3; Checkpoint A §7.3.

### RIE-004 — V01: Zaimokuza manuscript criticism

**Requester:** explicit.

**Goal:** be read and evaluated.

**Method:** direct criticism relevant to the request.

**Cost:** hurt pride, borne by requester as part of what he asked to know.

**Agency effect:** preserved.

**Ethical classification:** **clean authorized criticism baseline**.

**Source route:** V01 §7.4; Checkpoint A §7.4.

### RIE-005 — V01: Totsuka tennis request and refusal

Hachiman initially refuses a role he believes he cannot sustainably perform. This is crucial counterevidence to a theory that caring always imposes an obligation to rescue.

**Ethical classification:** **legitimate refusal / capacity boundary**.

Later cooperation around the match is collectively chosen; Yui is not conscripted, Yukino enters after being asked, and the group accepts the risk together.

**Secondary classification:** **costly cooperation with participant authorization**.

**Source route:** V01 §7.5–7.6; Checkpoint A §7.5.

### RIE-006 — V02: Hayama group conflict

**Requester:** Hayama.

**Method:** Hachiman identifies Hayama as group hub and conditionally proposes removing him so weaker horizontal ties must form.

**Authorization:** comparatively strong. Hayama understands the designed cost and consents.

**Cost bearer:** Hayama loses his expected group position temporarily.

**Agency effect:** peripheral members are forced into choices, but the client bearing the principal designed cost knowingly accepts the strategy.

**Ethical classification:** **authorized disruptive intervention; early positive baseline**.

**Source route:** V02 §7.1; Checkpoint A §7.6.

### RIE-007 — V02: Kawasaki “restoration” case

The intervention begins badly because Taishi's wish to have his sister “back” is treated as though it defines Kawasaki's problem. The club cycles through failed theories until Kawasaki's own material goal—earning money for university and family burden—becomes visible.

**Key transition:** intervention improves as problem definition moves from outsider pathology to subject-stated aim.

**Ethical classification:** **diagnostic correction from paternalistic restoration toward subject-responsive assistance**.

**Source route:** V02 §7.2; Checkpoint A §7.7.

### RIE-008 — V02: scholarship information

Hachiman does not give Kawasaki money, select her future, or moralize about sacrifice. He supplies institutional information that reduces a constraint while preserving her chosen educational goal.

**Ethical classification:** **capacity support through information; strong positive control**.

This also freezes a materialist rule: communication can reveal the problem, but emotional understanding does not itself create scholarships, money, time, or institutional options.

**Source route:** V02 §7.3–7.4.

### RIE-009 — V03: Hiratsuka's personnel-replenishment maneuver

**Problem:** Yui's disappearance is becoming passive relational decay.

**Method:** translate emotional crisis into club-membership task.

**Authorization:** coercive toward Hachiman/Yukino via institutional authority; Yui is not ordered back.

**Agency effect:** creates occasion without prewriting Yui's answer.

**Ethical classification:** **bounded pedagogical manipulation**.

**Source route:** V03 §7.1; Checkpoint A §7.8.

### RIE-010 — V03: Yukino's birthday action

Yukino chooses to celebrate Yui while explicitly separating the act from any demand that Yui return.

**Ethical classification:** **care without leverage; self-authored positive control**.

The act matters because it changes possibility without converting affection into debt.

**Source route:** V03 §7.2; Checkpoint A §7.9.

### RIE-011 — V03: game-club/Zaimokuza case

The club refuses to let desire alone certify competence, but also refuses the inverse proposition that competence possesses authority to certify whether desire is legitimate.

**Ethical classification:** **productive confrontation with self-produced problem; no clean rescue model**.

**Source route:** V03 §7.3.

### RIE-012 — V03: Hachiman's “zero-account” closure of Yui's debt

**Problem definition:** Hachiman decides Yui's kindness is debt from the accident.

**Method:** define the value of repayment, reduce account to zero, and infer the relationship should terminate.

**Authorization:** absent from Yui.

**Agency loss:** Yui loses authorship over the meaning of her own kindness and relationship.

**Ethical classification:** **unilateral interpretive closure / preemptive authorship**.

This is a major precursor to later social engineering. The helper attempts to free someone from obligation by deciding for her what she feels.

**Source route:** V03 §7.4; Checkpoint A §7.10.

## Phase B — V04–V06.5: intervention becomes structurally aggressive

### RIE-013 — V04: Yukino's explicit-request threshold in the Rumi case

`彼女が助けを求めるなら` is an attempt to preserve Rumi as subject rather than project.

**Strength:** recognizes authorization problem.

**Weakness:** assumes desire can always become explicit request despite shame, fear, age, and group pressure.

**Ethical classification:** **principled but insufficient request-only threshold**.

**Source route:** V04 §7.1–7.2.

### RIE-014 — V04: Yui's constrained-expression correction

Yui establishes that absence of request does not necessarily mean absence of desire for help.

This does not authorize unlimited inference. It forces the ethical problem into a harder form:

> helpers may need to notice what cannot yet be cleanly requested, while still refusing to convert inference into unrestricted jurisdiction.

**Ethical classification:** **anti-formalistic correction; remains procedurally OPEN**.

**Source route:** V04 §7.3.

### RIE-015 — V04: Hachiman's Rumi social-system disruption

**Method:** manufacture fear, create a forced-sacrifice scenario, expose distrust, break coalition structure.

**Authorization from Rumi:** absent.

**Collaborator authorization:** substantial; older students know their roles and can object.

**Cost:** children experience deliberate fear; old group form is destabilized; intervention is not fully reversible.

**Agency effect:** Rumi gains room to act, but her decisive act is unscripted rather than produced by Hachiman's exact design.

**Outcome:** isolation structure changes; friendship restoration is not achieved.

**Ethical classification:** **unauthorized social-system engineering with mixed agency outcome**.

**Key rule:** an intervention can create conditions for autonomous action without itself being fully autonomy-respecting.

**Source route:** V04 §7.4–7.7.

### RIE-016 — V05: Kawasaki's legitimate non-repair boundary

Kawasaki's claim that some people simply cannot get along establishes that ethical helping need not maximize closeness.

**Ethical classification:** **non-reconciliation as legitimate outcome**.

This later becomes important for Sagami/Hachiman and for romantic non-equivalence.

**Source route:** V05 §7.1.

### RIE-017 — V05: Yui's knowledge-before-help model

`困ってたら力になりたい` links caring to enough knowledge to identify actual trouble without claiming total access to interiority.

**Ethical classification:** **relational attentiveness with epistemic modesty; positive precursor**.

**Source route:** V05 §7.2.

### RIE-018 — V05: Hachiman's expectation prohibition

`俺にそういうの期待すんな` is a micro-intervention into the relationship itself. Hachiman predicts expectation will create harm and attempts to constrain what the other person is permitted to expect before fully hearing what she wants.

**Ethical classification:** **preemptive relational authorship**.

**Source route:** V05 §7.3–7.4.

### RIE-019 — V06: Sagami request mutates into substitutionary competence

Sagami asks for help functioning as cultural-festival chair. Yukino increasingly performs the role's work while Sagami retains title.

**Problem:** competence makes substitution tempting.

**Agency effect:** Sagami loses opportunities to become competent; Yukino acquires practical control without formal authorship; the institution learns reliance on Yukino.

**Ethical classification:** **benevolent substitution / accountability transfer**.

**Key rule:** completing a task successfully for someone may directly defeat the developmental purpose of helping them perform it.

**Source route:** V06 §7.1–7.3.

### RIE-020 — V06: committee common-enemy provocation

Hachiman publicly attacks the committee's labor asymmetry and becomes the target of dislike.

**Authorization:** unilateral.

**Cost:** primarily Hachiman's reputation, with relational cost to people who care about him.

**Agency effect:** committee members subsequently debate and work rather than merely enact a script.

**Ethical classification:** **unilateral social engineering + self-borne cost + partially agency-restoring outcome**.

This is a precursor to self-sacrifice as control, but it is not identical to the rooftop because the later group response is comparatively open.

**Source route:** V06 §7.4; Checkpoint B §4.6.

### RIE-021 — V06: Yui asks Yukino to rely on them

Yui asks for particular reliance, does not overpromise competence, and accepts Yukino's deferral.

**Ethical classification:** **consensual invitation to interdependence**.

**Source route:** V06 §7.5.

### RIE-022 — V06: emergency performance cooperation

Yukino explicitly asks Yui for help; Yui states limitations; Yukino offers backup; roles are accepted according to capacity.

**Ethical classification:** **chosen bounded reciprocal cooperation; strong positive control**.

**Source route:** V06 §7.6.

### RIE-023 — V06: rooftop recovery of Sagami

**Goal:** return Sagami to the chair role she asked to be helped to fulfill.

**Method:** attack her need for recognition, deploy a tactically useful false social claim, predict Hayama's intervention, assign villain/rescuer/victim roles, generate shame and anger, and use the group response to move Sagami physically back toward the event.

**Authorization:** Hachiman authorizes his own reputational cost only. Sagami, Hayama, friends, Yukino, Yui, and others do not authorize their assigned roles or collateral cost.

**Agency effect:** Sagami chooses to leave the rooftop, but deliberative conditions have been deliberately engineered through humiliation and rescue scripting.

**Ethical classification:** **coercive agency-restoration through unconsented role assignment and self-sacrificial social engineering**.

This is the first strong V2 support for **self-sacrifice as control**.

**Key rule:** restoring formal agency does not erase violation of deliberative agency on the route to that restoration.

**Source route:** V06 §7.7–7.8; Checkpoint B §4.7.

### RIE-024 — V06.5: nomination of Sagami as sports-festival chair

A real institutional position is used partly as rehabilitation. Sagami retains genuine ability to refuse and later becomes visible owner of the role.

**Ethical classification:** **paternalistic opportunity creation with meaningful exit**; ethically superior to rooftop coercion.

**Source route:** V06.5 §7.1.

### RIE-025 — V06.5: forced apology / reconciliation meeting

Public pressure forces grievances and concessions into speech.

**Benefit:** responsibilities become explicit and cooperation improves.

**Cost:** humiliation and performative language.

**Ethical classification:** **coercive mediation; cooperation achieved without proof of genuine reconciliation**.

**Source route:** V06.5 §7.2.

### RIE-026 — V06.5: Hachiman instrumentalizes Hayama's predictable decency

Hachiman admits using Hayama's instinct to intervene.

**Ethical classification:** **low-cost instrumentalization of another's moral disposition**.

The importance is structural: the same cognitive style later becomes more damaging when the stakes rise.

**Source route:** V06.5 §7.3.

### RIE-027 — V06.5: shoe-locker cost transfer to Tobe

Hachiman predicts Tobe's network will absorb reputational/social damage better and transfers the cost accordingly.

**Authorization:** absent.

**Ethical classification:** **effective harm redistribution without consent**.

This is decisive evidence that Hachiman's ethical problem cannot be reduced to “he always sacrifices himself.” Sometimes he spends the person he predicts can take it.

**Source route:** V06.5 §7.4.

### RIE-028 — V06.5: bureaucratic “mutually assured destruction” strategy

The committee uses safety rules, surveys, participation restrictions, and burdens to eliminate acceptable alternatives for opponents. Hachiman recognizes portions as `詭弁` and `建前`.

**Ethical classification:** **institutional incentive coercion / choice-set compression**.

It is strategically intelligent and not martyrdom theater; the control operates through bureaucracy.

**Source route:** V06.5 §7.5.

### RIE-029 — V06.5: Sagami's unscripted speech and Meguri's recognition

Sagami states her own grievance outside Hachiman's design: she is trying, has apologized, and wants effort recognized. Meguri then accurately acknowledges effort and asks whether Sagami wants to continue.

**Agency effect:** object of rehabilitation becomes speaker; continuation becomes Sagami's answer.

**Ethical classification:** **recipient speech restores authorship; recognition-with-choice is a strong positive control**.

The endpoint also validates non-reconciliation: Sagami and Hachiman need not become intimate for the intervention to have an ethically defensible stopping point.

**Source route:** V06.5 §7.6–7.8.

## Phase C — V07–V09: from predictive control to answerability

### RIE-030 — V07: Kyoto confession substitution

**Problem owners:** Tobe owns romantic desire; Ebina owns romantic boundary; Hayama/Miura have stakes in group continuity; Service Club owns none of those lives.

**Explicit request:** help Tobe pursue/confess.

**Competing inferred/expressed preferences:** Ebina wants romantic pressure avoided; Hayama wants the group preserved.

**Method:** Hachiman fabricates his own confession before Tobe can make his, redirecting rejection and preserving the group.

**Authorization:** `任せる` grants real judgment discretion but not informed permission for the specific false confession; Hachiman's secrecy indicates anticipated objection.

**Costs:** Tobe's speech opportunity; Ebina's direct answer; Hayama's moral participation; Yukino/Yui's emotional security; Hachiman's social standing.

**Ethical classification:** **technically successful multi-stakeholder optimization through unauthorized substitution**.

**Canonical rule:** accurate understanding of a relationship system does not confer jurisdiction over the people inside it.

**Source route:** V07 §7.1–7.7; Checkpoint C.

### RIE-031 — V07.5: indirection versus deception

Pseudonyms, games, punishment frames, jokes, and staged roles are not automatically equivalent to false confession.

**Positive criterion:** participants can recognize the frame, answer within it, and decline without the frame determining their future.

**Negative criterion:** concealed plan assigns serious costs or life consequences without participant standing.

**Ethical classification:** **pretext must be judged by transparency, stakes, and exit rights—not by indirectness alone**.

**Source route:** V07.5 §7.1–7.2.

### RIE-032 — V08: Iroha's presidency request and anti-coercion principle

Iroha has a legitimate double constraint: she did not choose nomination, does not want office, and does not want the reputational cost of losing.

Hachiman eventually states `一色を無理矢理生徒会長にさせる気はない` and recognizes that his reasons do not create the right to force sacrifice upon her.

**Ethical classification:** **major explicit anti-coercion advance**.

**Source route:** V08 §7.1–7.2.

### RIE-033 — V08: Iroha choice architecture with fabricated support

Hachiman changes the environment so the presidency becomes attractive: apparent support, retaliation value, rivalry, Hayama proximity, and reduced fear of failure. Iroha knowingly says she will let herself be carried by his framing.

**Positive:** formal choice remains; persuasion is partly recognized.

**Negative:** online momentum is fabricated, altering informational conditions.

**Ethical classification:** **choice architecture with deception; real methodological improvement but not clean consent**.

**Source route:** V08 §7.3–7.4.

### RIE-034 — V08: overriding Yukino and Yui by removing “need”

Hachiman applies his anti-coercion principle asymmetrically. Yukino explicitly says her candidacy is her own will; Yui also authors her candidacy despite contradictory motives. Hachiman then removes the external need for them to run and declares that they no longer need to do so.

**Ethical failure:** desire is translated into necessity; once necessity is removed, Hachiman treats the action as losing standing.

**Ethical classification:** **benevolent option foreclosure / protection through substitution of judgment**.

**Canonical rule:** intimacy can reduce respect for explicit preference when the helper believes familiarity gives privileged access to hidden good.

**Source route:** V08 §7.5–7.6.

### RIE-035 — V08: Komachi's protective pretext

Komachi gives Hachiman an acceptable reason to act “for Komachi” and “for Komachi's friends.” The pretext changes Hachiman's permission to acknowledge his own desire; it does not decide Yukino, Yui, or Iroha's answer.

**Ethical classification:** **agency-preserving pretext / expressive permission**.

**Source route:** V08 §7.7.

### RIE-036 — V08: distributed collaborator network

Hachiman asks Zaimokuza and others for cooperation; tasks are divided by competence; reciprocity allows help to move back toward Hachiman.

**Ethical improvement:** cost distribution and collaborator agency improve even though the larger political tactic remains deceptive.

**Classification:** **better collaborator authorization inside ethically mixed target intervention**.

**Source route:** V08 §7.8–7.9.

### RIE-037 — V09: private rescue of Iroha while excluding the club

Hachiman initially refuses formal Service Club involvement, privately takes responsibility, predicts Yukino's emotional reaction, withholds information, and tries to repair the event himself.

**Ethical classification:** **caring but jurisdictionally repetitive private rescue**.

The method is less spectacularly self-destructive than Kyoto but still decides who should be allowed inside the solution on the basis of predicted feeling.

**Source route:** V09 §7.1.

### RIE-038 — V09: Rumi establishes recipient interpretive standing

Rumi's evaluation of whether prior help helped becomes a major correction.

**Canonical rule:** **the helper does not possess final interpretive authority over the helped person's outcome**.

This does not create pure recipient subjectivism; broader ethical constraints still matter. It blocks helper self-certification.

**Source route:** V09 §7.2.

### RIE-039 — V09: Hayama's request baseline

Hayama explains he did not intervene for Iroha because he was not asked; Hachiman was.

# 4. Major longitudinal ethical transformations

## 4.1 From “real problem” diagnosis to bounded inference

Hiratsuka's early principle—that the stated consultation may not be the real problem—is indispensable to the Service Club's usefulness. Kawasaki's case proves it: if the club had accepted “delinquent sister” as the full problem, it would have failed. Rumi proves the complementary problem: people under shame or social pressure may not be able to formulate the request that a strict consent theory demands.

But Hachiman repeatedly converts diagnostic skill into jurisdiction. The progression of V2 therefore narrows the early principle:

> **A stated request may be incomplete. Inference may be necessary. Inference does not automatically authorize substituted choice.**

By V14.5, Iroha articulates the epistemic form explicitly: she can imagine Komachi from her own basis, but she knows she only truly knows herself. The mature helper may model; the helper must mark the model as a model.

## 4.2 From self-sacrifice to cost-accountability

The V1 vocabulary “self-sacrifice” was directionally useful but too broad. V2 differentiates at least four forms:

1. **self-borne cost that exposes a real problem but leaves group response open** — V6 committee provocation;
2. **self-borne cost used to script everyone else's role** — V6 rooftop and V7 Kyoto;
3. **cost shifted to another person judged able to absorb it** — V6.5 Tobe;
4. **shared, disclosed, bounded cost** — V10.5 and V13 collaborators.

The ethical defect is therefore not pain itself. It is **cost assignment without standing**.

A mature intervention asks not only “am I willing to pay?” but:

> Who else is being made to pay? Did they know? Can they refuse? Is their resilience being treated as permission? Does my sacrifice create a debt or social script that constrains them?

## 4.3 From competence to substitution and back to support

Yukino's cultural-festival labor provides the clearest substitution case. Her competence is genuine. The more effectively she performs Sagami's work, the less Sagami develops as chair.

The series then develops alternatives:

- scholarship information preserves Kawasaki's goal;
- emergency performance uses reciprocal bounded roles;
- Iroha's council increasingly owns its office;
- Yui trusts and entrusts rather than replacing Iroha;
- V10.5 audits capacity before commitment;
- Yukino's V12 request asks for witness rather than execution;
- `一人で立てる` / `知ってる` finally separates capability from permission to receive support.

The terminal rule is:

> **Helping is not proven by doing what the other person cannot do. It can consist of joining what the other person could do alone, provided the joining remains chosen.**

## 4.4 From hidden optimization to plural stakeholder ethics

Kyoto is the central failure of single-objective optimization. Hachiman selects preservation of the group as the dominant criterion and distributes costs across Tobe, Ebina, Hayama, himself, Yukino, and Yui.

Volume 10 generalizes the lesson: Miura can authorize help on her own desire but cannot authorize disclosure for Hayama. Volume 13–14 then turn the principle inward on Yukino: she can author self-removal but cannot unilaterally allocate Hachiman and Yui's romantic future.

Terminal rule:

> **Multi-person relational problems have plural owners. No participant's request automatically collapses the others into constraints to be optimized.**

## 4.5 From protection to answerability

Hachiman frequently predicts pain correctly. The problem is his inference that preventing another person's painful choice is therefore protective.

V5 blocks emotionally consequential speech. V8 removes Yukino/Yui's reasons to run. V9 initially excludes Yukino from Iroha's case to prevent a predicted trigger. V11 risks defining autonomy for Yukino. V12 begins with Yukino explicitly asking that Hachiman not take over.

The mature alternative appears through requests and refusals:

- Hachiman becomes requester;
- Yui prevents premature closure but does not prewrite the answer;
- Yukino accepts an unfinished request;
- Hayama's refusal in V10 should have force even when Miura's desire is real;
- Yui refuses Yukino's disappearance;
- Hachiman finally states his own desire rather than hiding behind rescue.

Terminal rule:

> **Protection that prevents another person's answer from having consequences can become a form of control.**

## 4.6 From request as permission slip to granular authorization

The series progressively destroys the idea that a request authorizes whatever a competent helper later decides is necessary.

Examples:

- `任せる` in Kyoto does not equal informed permission for false confession.
- Miura's request does not authorize Hayama's disclosure.
- Yukino's `助けてね` does not override her later `私にやらせて`.
- V13 refusal of direct help does not prohibit consensual rivalry.
- collaborator consent in the dummy prom does not create target or third-party consent.
- a reciprocal bridge request changes the relationship but does not make the bridge's initial physical stopping ethically vanish.

Terminal rule:

> **Authorization is granular, role-specific, revisable, and revocable.**

## 4.7 From non-intervention as cure to non-intervention as one technique

Volumes 11–12 make non-intervention newly important. Yui's `信じて任せる` and conditional friendship support Yukino/Iroha without substitution.

But V14.5 refuses to absolutize this gain. Hachiman recognizes that “decide for yourself” may be responsibility transfer. Yui demonstrates that one can state a preference without claiming jurisdiction. Komachi demonstrates that being influenced does not erase authorship.

The final autonomy model is therefore not social isolation:

> **People become authors through interaction. The ethical question is whether interaction supplies inputs and support or replaces the person's answer.**

# 7. Self-sacrifice ledger correction

## 7.1 PRESERVE — self-sacrifice can function as control

The V1 insight remains strongly supported by the cultural-festival rooftop and Kyoto. Hachiman's willingness to make himself disliked does not confine the cost to himself. By becoming the designated villain or false romantic claimant, he changes what other people are socially permitted or compelled to do.

Self-harm can therefore be an **interpersonal technology**.

## 7.2 REVISE — self-sacrifice is not the master category

V2 rejects the tendency to route every ethically ugly Hachiman action through martyrdom.

He also:

- shifts cost onto Tobe;
- uses Hayama's decency;
- exploits bureaucracy;
- fabricates support;
- overrides Yukino/Yui while personally trying not to hurt them;
- overrides Hayama's refusal without sacrificing his social identity in the same way;
- manipulates institutional choices through the dummy prom.

The higher-order category is **private assignment of standing, roles, and costs**.

Self-sacrifice is one recurrent mechanism inside it.

## 7.3 STRENGTHEN — willingness to bear cost is morally relevant but not exculpatory

Hachiman's willingness to absorb consequences distinguishes him from a purely exploitative manipulator. He is often acting under genuine concern and does not simply externalize all risk.

But V2 freezes:

> **consenting to one's own cost is not consent on behalf of the people whose options, emotions, reputations, or relationships are also being rearranged.**

---

# 8. Request ontology

## 8.1 A request is not a transparent window into “the real problem”

Yui's cookie request, Kawasaki, Rumi, and many later cases show that people may formulate only one layer of what matters. A request can be incomplete, strategically phrased, embarrassed, mistaken, or institutionally constrained.

## 8.2 A request is still morally consequential

The fact that requests may be incomplete does not make them disposable. A request creates standing and scope. It tells the helper what the requester is willing to make answerable at that moment.

## 8.3 The requester may not own every affected interest

Miura can request inquiry but not authorize Hayama's disclosure. Yukino can request self-removal but not author Hachiman/Yui's desire. Tobe can request confession help but cannot force Ebina's romantic availability.

## 8.4 Requests can be ongoing rather than transactional

The V09 `本物` request is accepted without full comprehension and remains open. Yui's final V14 request may continue indefinitely.

The mature Service Club therefore no longer treats a request as:

> input -> solver -> final output.

It can be:

> **an admitted problem that creates an ongoing relation of answerability without guaranteed resolution.**

## 8.5 Counter-requests can redistribute power

Yukino's V11 request takes authorship back from Yui/Hachiman. Yukino's bridge counter-request prevents her from remaining passive recipient of Hachiman's life-offer. Yui's V14 demand that Hachiman state his own desire prevents him from acting as neutral executor.

Counter-request is one of the series' strongest mechanisms for preventing helper/client hierarchy from hardening.

---

# 9. Refusal ledger

The treatment of refusal is one of the clearest maturity tests.

## 9.1 Early healthy refusal

Hachiman can refuse Totsuka's unsustainable request in V01. This establishes that requests do not automatically create duty.

## 9.2 Refusal ignored

Hayama's V10 refusal is explicit, but Hachiman continues through coercive inquiry. This remains one of the clearest late examples of knowing the ethical rule and applying it unevenly.

## 9.3 Refusal that changes role rather than ending relation

Yukino's V12 `私にやらせて` does not mean “leave my life forever.” V13 respects the direct-helper refusal while creating a different, mutually acknowledged rivalry role.

This is ethically important: respecting a no does not require erasing every possible relationship.

## 9.4 Refusal must be possible for collaborators too

V13's collaborator improvement is substantial precisely because Hachiman discloses that work may be wasted and allows people to decline.

## 9.5 Refusal and answerability at the bridge

The bridge is not clean because it begins with physical stopping. It becomes more answerable when Hachiman releases, Yukino remains, and explicit permission to reject/nonrespond is introduced. Her insistence on answering is then her own act.

Terminal rule:

> **A refusal is ethically meaningful only if it can actually change what the other person does.**

---

# 10. Pretext, deception, and indirectness

The V2 reread sharply rejects the equation:

> indirect = fake = unethical.

Oregairu's characters often need indirection because direct speech carries embarrassment, status risk, or relational danger.

## 10.1 Agency-preserving pretext

Examples include Komachi giving Hachiman a reason to act, the Destinyland outing, Valentine event, and many small social excuses. These frames lower expressive risk while preserving refusal and not predetermining major outcomes.

## 10.2 Shared pretext

Iroha's V14.5 `プレゼントは言い訳ですよ` makes the frame almost explicit. Shared awareness reduces epistemic deception, though social pressure can remain.

## 10.3 Deceptive choice architecture

V08 fake support and V13 dummy prom use false or strategically incomplete information to change what options appear viable. Formal choice remains, but the quality of consent is altered.

## 10.4 Substitutive stagecraft

Kyoto is worse because the staging does not merely make speech easier. Hachiman's false confession consumes Tobe's opportunity to speak and reroutes Ebina's response.

Terminal distinction:

> **Indirection is most defensible when it reduces the cost of expressing a choice; it becomes dangerous when it replaces the choice or silently determines which answers remain available.**

# 11. Institutional ethics

The series repeatedly demonstrates that intervention is not only interpersonal.

- scholarships alter material opportunity;
- festival committees allocate labor and title;
- sports-festival rules create incentive pressure;
- student-council candidacy and fake public support change political choice;
- the Christmas event requires office-holder legitimacy;
- the prom involves parents, school, precedent, community groups, publicity, and future cohorts;
- the Service Club itself is an institution whose continuation can be inherited rather than copied.

The mature institutional rule is:

> **Institutions should expand the set of choices people can responsibly author rather than requiring individuals to become sacrificial exceptions to make the system work.**

This is why the clean student-council model matters: Hachiman/Yukino can advise, but Iroha and the council must become the institution rather than outsource office to exceptional helpers.

It is also why the dummy prom remains ethically mixed even when romantically meaningful. Manipulating institutional stakeholders is not rendered private because the motive is personal.

# 12. Intervention and relational jurisdiction

The Relationship State Ledger defines **relational jurisdiction** as who acts as though they possess the right to define, preserve, end, or redesign a relationship. The intervention ledger supplies the procedural evidence.

Major jurisdiction errors include:

- Hachiman deciding Yui's debt/relationship is over;
- Hachiman preventing speech because expectation is dangerous;
- Hachiman treating Kyoto group preservation as the objective he may enforce;
- Hachiman removing Yukino/Yui's candidacy rationale because he predicts pain;
- Yui's V11 proposal attempting to preserve everyone through a rule;
- Hachiman defining what Yukino's autonomy should require;
- Yukino's V13 self-removal attempting to allocate Hachiman/Yui's future.

Major jurisdiction corrections include:

- Rumi retaining judgment over whether help helped;
- Hachiman becoming requester;
- Hayama's request/refusal distinction;
- Yukino's `私にやらせて`;
- V13 direct-help/rivalry split;
- Hachiman's statement that he and Yukino cannot decide the triad for Yui;
- Yui demanding Hachiman own his own desire;
- reciprocal bridge request;
- Komachi succession decision after hearing but not obeying others.

The terminal rule is:

> **Intimacy creates reasons to care and standing to speak; it does not create unlimited jurisdiction.**

# 14. Canonical terminal ethics model

The complete V2 reread supports the following final model.

## 14.1 Caring

Caring supplies **attention and motive**.

It may justify noticing, asking, offering, warning, or remaining available.

It does not by itself authorize redesigning another person's options.

## 14.2 Reason to act

A person may have strong reason to act even without being formally asked: danger, institutional duty, obvious constrained expression, existing relationship, previous promise, or material change can all matter.

Reason is therefore broader than request.

But reason still does not decide method.

## 14.3 Authority

Authority is granular. It may come from:

- explicit request;
- office/role;
- delegated judgment;
- mutual wager;
- emergency necessity;
- ordinary relational standing;
- ongoing consent.

No one source automatically authorizes every form of intervention.

## 14.4 Method

The most defensible methods tend to:

- disclose relevant stakes;
- preserve exit/refusal;
- reduce rather than fabricate informational asymmetry;
- return decisions to people/institutions that must live with them;
- share failure rather than privatize it;
- avoid treating competence as ownership;
- distinguish advice from command;
- expose the helper's own motive when it materially affects the case;
- preserve future revision.

## 14.5 Outcome

Outcome matters. A method that reliably harms people cannot hide behind procedural purity.

But outcome is not enough. The series repeatedly gives Hachiman effective solutions in order to prevent the moral critique from collapsing into “manipulation is wrong because it fails.”

The harder question is:

> **What kind of success leaves the people inside it still able to recognize the result as theirs?**

## 14.6 Relationship after intervention

The mature standard includes aftermath. Helpers remain answerable for collateral cost, misunderstanding, failure, or changed relationships. Volume 10.5's shared-apology logic and the final Service Club's willingness to host a problem indefinitely show that intervention ethics continue after the apparent solution.

## 14.7 Final canonical formulation

> **The mature Oregairu standard is answerable involvement. Motive must be owned; authorization must be granular; refusal must have force; problem ownership must remain plural where lives overlap; collaborators and targets retain separate standing; competence does not confer jurisdiction; self-sacrifice cannot authorize costs for others; influence is compatible with autonomy; and legitimate help should return or preserve the affected person's capacity to answer, revise, and live with the result.**

A shorter formulation:

> **Help is most ethical when it adds capacity without confiscating authorship.**

And the sharpest negative formulation:

> **Knowing what may happen to someone is not the same as having the right to make it happen—or prevent it—for them.**

# 15. Synthesis constraints for later documents

Any later specialist or full-series synthesis must preserve the following constraints.

1. Do not describe Hachiman as simply “learning not to manipulate.” He continues to manipulate institutions and social conditions late in the series; what improves is his authorization discipline, ownership of motive, collaborator treatment, and respect for answerability.
2. Do not describe Yukino's growth as learning not to need help. Her clean endpoint includes chosen support under acknowledged capability.
3. Do not describe Yui's kindness as inherently agency-preserving. Her V11 proposal is agentic and controlling; her mature strength is increasingly the capacity to state desire and preserve others' right to answer differently.
4. Do not use Haruno's diagnoses as normative authority. Her interventions can be epistemically useful while low in authorization.
5. Do not treat Hiratsuka as an oracle. Her strongest interventions provide methods, questions, options, and temporary pressure while returning the final answer.
6. Do not treat explicit requests as blanket permission. Scope and third-party standing remain separate.
7. Do not treat absence of request as proof that intervention is never warranted. Rumi and constrained-expression cases remain counterevidence.
8. Do not equate non-intervention with autonomy. Trust may require non-intervention; responsibility may also require advice, warning, presence, or a stated preference.
9. Do not let romantic payoff sanitize the bridge's initial coercive act.
10. Do not let bridge coercion erase the later procedural transition into voluntary reciprocal request.
11. Do not describe the dummy prom as simply “helping Yukino” or simply “violating Yukino.” Its role is consensual rivalry combined with institution-level choice architecture and third-party manipulation.
12. Do not treat the Service Club as a machine whose mature purpose is to solve every request. Its final form can hold a problem that remains unresolved.
13. Do not equate authored choice with choice free from influence. Komachi's final decision demonstrates autonomy through responsive influence.
14. Do not equate personal sacrifice with ethical nobility. Cost distribution and role assignment must remain visible.
15. Do not equate good procedure with emotional niceness. Serious criticism, conflict, refusal, and disappointment can be ethical precisely because they preserve another person's standing.

# 17. Phase-4 handoff

This ledger freezes the ethical-procedure layer needed by the remaining longitudinal documents.

The strongest outgoing propositions are:

- **problem ownership is often plural;**
- **request scope is granular;**
- **refusal must alter conduct;**
- **competence, prediction, love, promise, sacrifice, and success are not substitutes for authorization;**
- **non-intervention can be care but can also be abdication;**
- **influence does not negate autonomy when the subject remains capable of answering and revising;**
- **self-sacrifice is one cost-allocation mechanism, not the master category;**
- **the mature Service Club's function is not final problem eradication but continuing answerability.**

The next ledger should therefore treat `本物` not as a detached philosophical keyword but against this procedural history: whenever the series claims to want something genuine, the question must be whether the relation can survive **mixed motive, imperfect knowledge, real disagreement, refusal, and authorship distributed among more than one person**.
