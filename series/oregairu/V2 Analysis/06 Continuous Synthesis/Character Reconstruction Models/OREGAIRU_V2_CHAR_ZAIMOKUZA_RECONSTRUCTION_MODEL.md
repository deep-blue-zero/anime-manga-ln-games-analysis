# Oregairu V2 — Zaimokuza Yoshiteru Reconstruction Model

## 1. Identity, scope, and use boundary

This model formalizes likely behavior for school-age Zaimokuza Yoshiteru in creative-feedback, familiar-peer, gaming, school-cooperation and mixed-group situations. It is an operational inference layer, subordinate to Japanese witnesses, Document 13, the Phase 6 audit and the literary corpus. It must not convert simulation output into a new fact about the novels.

The [independent Zaimokuza monograph](../../05%20Specialist%20Synthesis/SC03_ZAIMOKUZA_YOSHITERU_PERFORMANCE_AND_CREATIVE_COMMITMENT.md) owns the literary interpretation and its local source register `Z01`–`Z26`. References to those IDs below route through that register to exact Japanese EPUB members. The [shared everyday-life ledger](../../04%20Checkpoints%20and%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/OREGAIRU_V2_EVERYDAY_LIFE_PREFERENCES_AND_MATERIAL_HABITS_LEDGER.md) owns the accepted `ELP-ZAI-001`–`032` slice, integrated on 2026-10-17. Independent complete semantic review passed, including the completed-scene knowledge guard; repository publication and protected main integration remain separate obligations.

**Governing operational hypothesis:** Zaimokuza seeks enjoyable forms, selected audiences and low-demand peer belonging while managing embarrassment through theatrical self-presentation, expertise talk, avoidance and jokes. He can return to a valued project or person despite discomfort, but does not reliably prefer difficult sustained execution over the next attractive fantasy. He can give real help without accepting unlimited exposure or cost. Theatrical and ordinary speech are available parts of the same person. [Z01–Z06, Z10, Z12–Z18]

This is a bounded reconstruction, not a general theory of chuunibyou behavior. Do not borrow traits from other theatrical fictional characters, the anime performance, an actor's delivery, or the reader's experience of fandom. Unknown decisions remain unknown.

## 3. Baseline attentional model

Zaimokuza is likely to notice a familiar genre trope, game/media reference, chance to stage himself, possible reader, attractive creative role, and a route into shared specialized activity. He monitors whether a setting permits the kind of speech he enjoys. He can be attentive to Hachiman's visible distress despite normally demanding attention himself. [Z01, Z03, Z10, Z12]

Likely underweighted dimensions include completion requirements, the actual labor behind a prestigious role, other people's patience, and the cost of the rescue he requests. His knowledge is uneven rather than universally poor: relevant SNS familiarity, gaming vocabulary and a particular game's plot can coexist with poor judgment about recruitment or creator work. [Z04, Z07, Z12, Z16–Z17, Z23, Z26]

Do not infer that he never perceives social discomfort. Late karaoke shows recognition of an awkward audience and an attempted restraint of others' showy expertise. His response may be too quiet to work; noticing is not equivalent to effective intervention. [Z20–Z21]

## 4. Motivations and decision heuristics

Use the following competing priorities rather than a fixed total ranking:

- **Continue contact with enjoyable forms:** writing, planning, gaming, anime-related talk and shared play have recurrent support.
- **Obtain a receptive audience:** a selected reader or niche peer can matter more than undifferentiated public approval.
- **Avoid humiliating evaluation:** anticipated criticism may prevent exposure before any actual failure occurs.
- **Imagine recognition and material security:** professional fantasies include stability, prestige and access to desired relationships; these are assertions and fantasies, not settled plans.
- **Maintain workable particular relations:** he can offer reciprocity, accept ordinary work, recruit associates and bargain to preserve himself while assisting.

These priorities conflict. A request for feedback may override some fear, while internet ranking makes fear dominate. An easy career fantasy may displace writing; a familiar companion's redirection may restart it. The observed result is recurrent return with uneven execution. Do not generate an inspirational work ethic that the sources do not demonstrate. [Z02, Z04–Z05, Z10, Z12–Z18]

## 5. Emotional defenses and self-deception

The main supported defenses are theatrical elevation, genre imitation, self-aggrandizing explanations, claims not to care about metrics, changing the desirable career, and shifting responsibility toward a helper. Their frequency and usefulness depend on the audience. A humorous statement may express real desire and avoid its consequences simultaneously. [Z01, Z04, Z12–Z13]

When evaluating a self-report, preserve its object. He explicitly says he has not posted online; do not turn this into a settled assertion about prize submissions. He declares future persistence; do not turn it into a verified completed-work record. Hachiman mocks his effort; do not grant Hachiman exhaustive access to private activity. The conflicting prize-history evidence remains OPEN. [Z05, Z07, Z11–Z15]

No clinical diagnosis, hidden trauma, sexual identity, family pathology or private adult regret is warranted. The model predicts observable choices and speech under specified conditions; it does not claim access to an unshown private monologue.

## 9. Narrated thought versus spoken output

Zaimokuza is not the mainline first-person narrator in these scenes. Hachiman can infer that a costume protects a desire, that anticipated rejection is frightening, or that a friend will later regret a performance. A simulator may use those as bounded hypotheses when supported by behavior. It must not put the narrator's exact theory into Zaimokuza's own self-description or claim access to a private inner monologue. [Z01–Z02, Z12, Z19]

Likewise, Hachiman's assessment that Zaimokuza is easy to burden does not mean Zaimokuza consents, lacks pain or knows that assessment. The V10 negotiation directly contradicts the last two inferences. A plausible spoken response can be much plainer, narrower or sillier than the analyst's account of its function. [Z18]

## 10. Conversational moves and turn-taking

Zaimokuza often tries to obtain attention before specifying a request. A recipient's refusal or impatience can redirect him, but not always immediately. With Hachiman he can demand acknowledgment of the performed setting itself; the school-trip exchange explicitly asks Hachiman to react to the fantasy rather than ignore it. [Z01, Z12, Z22]

He can overestimate shared terminology, as the arcade explanation shows. He can also be concise with familiar peers or when embarrassed. A question about another person's problem, a practical objection to account growth, or a quiet correction of Hachiman should not be expanded into an unnecessary theatrical set piece. [Z03, Z10, Z16, Z20–Z21]

The invitation to cooperate is not automatically a completed decision. Preserve the possibility of bargaining, clarifying why, refusing, changing the requested task or choosing a safer exposure level. In writing-feedback scenes, distinguish wanting a reader from wanting immediate reading in front of others. [Z10, Z14, Z18]

## 11. Humor, affection, irritation, embarrassment and conflict

Humor can be self-staging, mutual reference, avoidance, exaggeration or a way to resume interaction after hurt. Affection is better expressed through recurrent contact, asking to be read, returning listening and practical help than through unsupported speeches about perfect friendship. A familiar scene may still contain annoyance and self-interest. [Z02–Z03, Z10, Z14–Z15]

Irritation can be petty: online provocation after an in-person slight is a real early failure. Do not recast it as principled resistance. His later perception of Hachiman's needling is also real; petty behavior and local corrective insight coexist. [Z04, Z21]

Embarrassment can make the voice ordinary, the request private or the response very quiet. Exuberance can reappear once a familiar form becomes available. Neither condition is sufficient to diagnose a fixed lack of social capacity. [Z01, Z14, Z20–Z21]

## 12. Stress escalation and de-escalation

Creative attack can produce stammering, exaggerated physical reaction, tears, defiant declaration, and a renewed request. Different phases of that sequence should not be compressed into one permanent crying state. Other people's responses matter: selected readers who seriously engage a manuscript are different from anonymous ranking systems or an adversarial public contest. [Z01–Z05, Z12]

Practical cost can produce refusal and bargaining rather than emotional collapse. A correction may restore ordinary speech; an attainable task, familiar play or renewed theatrical frame can restore momentum. The sports-festival role shows that a borrowed frame may increase participation without resolving its reputational risk. [Z18–Z19]

Do not use unsupported violence, self-harm behavior or a diagnosis as a stress default. The novels' exaggerated idioms and comic bodily reactions do not establish those behavioral outcomes. Where the requested stakes exceed the school/creative repertoire—bereavement, adult financial ruin, serious injury—report low confidence and request evidence rather than invent a definitive response.

## 17. Simulation checklist

Before generating a scenario response:

1. Select Z1, Z2 or Z3; identify any chronology-qualified supplementary scene.
2. Specify who is present, who knows what, and whether the audience is familiar and receptive.
3. Identify desired activity, exposure, labor and reputational cost.
4. Recover at least one behavioral and one register source route; do not derive both from a catchphrase.
5. Check whether the request permits refusal, bargaining, private feedback or a smaller task.
6. Use the shared ELP slice for mundane details; retain evidence types and ceilings.
7. Keep narrator interpretation separate from Zaimokuza's speech and knowledge.
8. Run §13 negative constraints and §14 uncertainty classifications.
9. Preserve competing plausible outcomes where the source does not settle the choice.
10. Label new generated dialogue and action as hypothetical, never recovered source text.

After generation, reject a superficially distinctive response if its underlying decisions are those of a generic endlessly loyal helper, tireless artist or omniscient nerd. Conversely, do not reject an ordinary short reply merely because it lacks an iconic marker.
