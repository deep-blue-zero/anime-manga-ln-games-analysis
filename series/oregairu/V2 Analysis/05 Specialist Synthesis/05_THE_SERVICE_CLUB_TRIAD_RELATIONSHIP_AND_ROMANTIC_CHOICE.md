# 05 — The Service Club Triad: Relationship and Romantic Choice
## From protected ambiguity to acknowledged non-equivalence

## 0. Function, scope, and governing thesis

The central three-person relationship in *Oregairu* is often described as a love triangle. That description is not false, but it is structurally insufficient.

A conventional love-triangle model asks which two people form the eventual couple and treats the third primarily as romantic competition. *Oregairu* spends too much of its dramatic architecture demonstrating that the Service Club is more than that. Hikigaya Hachiman, Yukinoshita Yukino, and Yuigahama Yui form three genuine dyads, a shared institution, an ordinary-life routine, a problem-solving team, an emotional refuge, a field of rivalry, and eventually a system in which no pair can alter its future without changing the third person's available choices.

The definitive V2 triad thesis is therefore:

> **The Service Club becomes a three-person relational system whose institutional form first allows attachment to exist before the participants can safely name it, whose crises expose the costs of preserving relation by unilateral judgment or protected ambiguity, and whose mature form depends not on equal desire or perfect understanding but on answerability under known non-equivalence. The final Hachiman/Yukino romantic choice is real, exclusive in the relevant romantic sense, and painful for Yui; it does not retroactively falsify Hachiman/Yui intimacy, erase Yukino/Yui friendship, or reduce the prior triad to a temporary obstacle course. What survives is not the old triangle restored, but a changed relation whose members know that they do not want identical futures and nevertheless continue to grant one another standing.**

This formulation requires seven distinctions to remain active throughout:

1. **a triad is not the sum of three dyads;** the state of one pair changes the option structure of the third person;
2. **ordinary presence is relationship evidence but not proof of equality, permanence, or health;**
3. **preserving a relationship can be caring while still postponing an answer another person has standing to receive;**
4. **romantic asymmetry does not imply total relational asymmetry;**
5. **self-authored choice does not grant authority to choose on behalf of the other two participants;**
6. **the `本物` request changes the relation's procedure before it settles the relation's eventual form;**
7. **the final romantic answer removes protected ambiguity but does not abolish the history that ambiguity helped contain.**

The chronological architecture follows the six movements established in `01_SERIES_ARCHITECTURE_AND_VOLUME_PROGRESSION.md`:

- **V01–V03:** institutional pretext becomes relational infrastructure;
- **V04–V06.5:** differentiated competence becomes mutual consequentiality;
- **V07–V09:** preservation-through-control fails and answerability begins;
- **V10–V11:** ordinary warmth survives while incompatible futures become explicit;
- **V12–V14:** diagnosis becomes a jurisdiction problem, and romantic non-equivalence becomes answerable;
- **V14.5:** the relation proves capable of ordinary continuation and institutional transmission without identical preservation.

The central question is therefore not:

> Who was the “correct” romantic partner all along?

It is:

> **How does a three-person relationship become capable of surviving the fact that its members want overlapping but non-equivalent futures?**

# 1. Analytical model: the triad as an emergent relationship

## 1.1 Why pairwise analysis is necessary but insufficient

The three principal dyads are each independently real.

**Hachiman/Yukino** develops through adversarial recognition, idealization, rupture, methodological correction, mutual requests, contested intervention, and finally reciprocal romantic choice.

**Hachiman/Yui** contains particular care, ordinary intimacy, increasingly explicit romantic desire from Yui, Hachiman's recurrent refusal to grant favorable evidence full standing, and eventually a clear negative romantic answer that does not erase the intimacy preceding it.

**Yukino/Yui** develops as a friendship before romantic rivalry becomes explicit, survives political disagreement and incompatible desire, and remains chosen after the romantic outcome becomes non-equivalent.

Yet no one of these dyads can explain the late series in isolation.

If Hachiman and Yukino privately decide that Yui should be protected from a painful answer, they have altered Yui's available future without her consent. If Yui and Yukino discuss withdrawing or yielding to one another, they change what Hachiman is being invited to answer. If Hachiman treats his promise to Yukino as sufficient reason to intervene, the resulting act affects Yui's romantic and friendship positions as well. If Yui proposes a future in which “everything” can be preserved, she is not merely expressing a dyadic wish to Hachiman; she is proposing a structure for all three people. If Yukino requests that Hachiman grant Yui's wish, she is making a decision whose consequences extend beyond her own self-removal.

Thus the triad has **emergent properties**.

A practical V2 model should track at least eight dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| membership | who treats the three-person unit as a meaningful continuing relation? |
| dyadic state | what is independently true in each pair? |
| ordinary presence | do they continue choosing unforced time together? |
| request structure | who asks whom for what, and who has standing to answer? |
| option structure | whose available choices are expanded, narrowed, or silently pre-decided? |
| romantic information | what is known, suspected, denied, or left ambiguous? |
| conflict tolerance | can disagreement occur without dissolution or forced harmony? |
| future assumption | can the participants imagine continuation without pretending its form is guaranteed? |

The triad becomes mature not when every dimension becomes harmonious, but when disagreement in one dimension no longer requires silent control in the others.

## 1.2 The Service Club is a relationship technology

The institution matters because it creates a place where people who cannot initially say “I want to spend time with you” can nevertheless keep returning.

Requests supply legitimate pretexts. Tea supplies routine. Assigned work supplies joint objects of attention. The clubroom supplies spatial continuity. The teacher's original coercion paradoxically creates a domain in which later participation becomes increasingly voluntary.

This means the Service Club should not be treated merely as scenery for romance.

It is a **relationship technology**: a structured environment that lowers the cost of repeated presence while providing role language for emotions the participants cannot yet name directly.

Early Yukino can say `うちの部員` before she can comfortably say something more intimate. Yui can construct “the three of us” through invitations and naming before the others can fully endorse its implications. Hachiman can enter the room for a request without admitting that returning to the same people has become desirable in itself.

The institution therefore carries attachment before the characters can.

That same strength later becomes a risk. Because the club offers such a useful pretext, keeping the institution open can be mistaken for preserving the relationship. Volume 8 demonstrates the difference brutally: the room remains, the tea can still be poured, and yet the relation has become hollow.

The lesson is structural:

> **A shared form can support relation, but form cannot substitute for answer.**

## 1.3 The triad is asymmetrical from early on

V2 must resist a symmetrical “three equally uncertain romantic options” model.

The three relationships do not develop with equal content at equal speed. Yui's attraction to Hachiman becomes legible early. Hachiman and Yukino develop a distinctive adversarial-intellectual intimacy that neither shares in the same form with Yui. Yukino and Yui build an independently affectionate friendship. Hachiman's own romantic self-recognition lags behind the relational evidence available to him.

This asymmetry is not a flaw in the triad model. It is what the triad model must explain.

The system persists for so long precisely because the participants share enough overlapping goods—friendship, ordinary time, mutual recognition, club work, attachment, romantic desire, fear of loss—without possessing a common ranking of those goods.

The late conflict is therefore not caused by the sudden appearance of romance. Romance has been present for a long time. The conflict intensifies when the participants can no longer maintain the fiction that the same arrangement can satisfy all of them indefinitely.

# 2. V01–V03: institutional pretext becomes a three-person place

## 2.1 Hachiman and Yukino are not yet “the real pair” awaiting Yui's arrival

The earliest Service Club scenes make Hachiman and Yukino mutually legible through conflict. They recognize intelligence, competence, isolation, and ideological difference in one another. That matters.

But a retrospective route reading can overstate what exists at this point. Their early intimacy is adversarial and partly projective. Hachiman idealizes Yukino's supposed inability to lie. Yukino treats Hachiman as both difficult client and intellectual opponent. The dyad is important, but it is not already the completed truth of the series.

Yui's arrival changes the relational system rather than merely joining it.

She introduces a different social medium. Where Hachiman and Yukino can remain connected through argument, Yui creates names, invitations, friendliness, embodied proximity, and explicit statements that the place matters. She converts repeated interaction into recognizable social relation.

The relationship-state ledger correctly freezes this point:

> **Yui is an active constitutor of “the three of us,” not a mediator inserted between an already complete Hachiman/Yukino pair.**

This has large consequences for the ending. If Yui helped constitute the triad, her later romantic non-selection cannot make her prior place retroactively accidental.

## 2.2 The early club distributes different kinds of intelligence

The three members quickly reveal nonredundant capacities.

- **Yukino** supplies structure, standards, formal competence, and willingness to confront excuses.
- **Hachiman** supplies incentive analysis, adversarial imagination, willingness to model ugly motives, and tactical improvisation.
- **Yui** supplies atmosphere reading, relational translation, social reach, and the ability to make continued presence feel ordinary rather than exceptional.

The point is not that each person has one fixed “class role.” Their skills overlap and develop. But the club's problem-solving power already depends on plurality.

That plurality also creates relational education. Hachiman encounters modes of social action that are neither naïve conformity nor his own sacrificial workaround. Yukino encounters the fact that correctness may require translation, pacing, or a model of another person's fear. Yui encounters people willing to articulate standards and consequences that her ordinary social environment may prefer to soften.

The three-person form is therefore developmental before it is romantic.

## 2.3 Yui's absence in Volume 3 makes membership visible

One of the strongest early pieces of triadic evidence is negative: when Yui withdraws, the room changes.

Her absence demonstrates that membership has become particular. The club can physically exist with two people, but the loss of the third person is not neutral. When Yui returns, the restoration is not simply “the story resumes.” It establishes that the three-person arrangement is already something the participants can miss.

At the same time, Hachiman perceives asymmetry. The room is restored, but not all meanings inside it are equally named.

This is the first durable triad state:

> **an emergent three-person place whose institutional form allows attachment to exist before the participants can safely state what continued membership asks of them.**

## 2.4 Early romantic asymmetry should remain prospective

Later knowledge tempts a reader to classify every early scene by endpoint.

V2 resists that.

Yui's attraction to Hachiman is increasingly active and particular. Hachiman's response is not best described as established reciprocal romance. Hachiman/Yukino contains increasing specialness but not a fully acknowledged romantic contract. Yukino/Yui contains friendship that is already significant outside the romance.

The early triad should therefore remain **prospectively open but structurally unequal**.

This distinction allows the ending to be a real choice rather than an inevitable fact that the characters merely take fourteen volumes to notice.

# 3. V04–V06.5: distributed competence and mutual consequentiality

## 3.1 The trio becomes a functioning team

The middle-early series demonstrates why the Service Club is attractive enough to become dangerous to lose.

The trio increasingly solves problems through differentiated strengths. In the camp material, cultural festival, and surrounding requests, the members do not merely stand beside one another while Hachiman performs the decisive act. Yukino's organizational skill, Yui's social positioning, and Hachiman's tactical willingness combine in changing proportions.

The relationship thus acquires **instrumental trust**.

Each member becomes evidence that the others do not have to solve every problem alone.

That matters especially for Yukino and Hachiman, both of whom can convert competence into solitary burden-bearing. The club provides repeated demonstrations that reliance can be particular rather than generic. Yui's `あたしたちを頼って？` is especially important because it does not say “be dependent.” It proposes a specific plurality of people who can share a burden.

## 3.2 Mutual consequentiality introduces cost

As trust increases, actions that once would have affected only the actor begin to wound the group.

Hachiman's humiliating tactics no longer remain his private cost because Yukino and Yui care what happens to him and care what his methods do to the relationship. Yukino's overwork no longer looks like admirable competence alone because the others have standing to see its effects. Yui's possessiveness or jealousy matters because the friendships are no longer casual.

The club therefore becomes **cost-bearing**.

This is a critical transition. A relationship becomes ethically denser when “I consent to paying the cost” is no longer a complete defense because other people now share consequences.

Hachiman's early self-sacrifice fails this test repeatedly. He assumes that because he has chosen the humiliation, the intervention is his to authorize. But Yukino and Yui experience reputational, emotional, and relational externalities. The triad turns self-sacrifice into a jurisdiction problem before the later series gives that problem a full vocabulary.

## 3.3 The cultural-festival arc exposes substitution in two directions

The cultural festival creates a useful parallel.

Yukino's competence can substitute for Sagami's responsibility. Hachiman's willingness to become the villain can substitute for slower forms of accountability and recognition. Both interventions may produce instrumental success. Both risk reducing another person's space to act.

This matters for the triad because the three members learn the same lesson from different positions:

> **helping that succeeds can still deform authorship.**

The Service Club's later maturity depends on retaining this lesson when the “problem” is no longer an external client but the relation among the three members themselves.

## 3.4 Ordinary time begins to matter independently of requests

The .5 material and quieter scenes are important because they show the trio not only under crisis pressure but in leisure, joking, gifts, and repeated ordinary presence.

Crisis can force contact. Ordinary time demonstrates preference.

The evidence should not be exaggerated: choosing ordinary time together does not prove equal desire or permanent health. But it establishes that the club has become more than obligation. The participants repeatedly convert free time into shared time.

That ordinary baseline will later make the hollow post-election club especially legible. The tragedy is not that the club stops functioning. It is that a place known to support spontaneous warmth becomes formally intact and relationally depleted.

# 4. V07–V09: preservation-through-control fails

## 4.1 Kyoto makes the triad's ethical problem visible

The Kyoto confession request is a decisive relationship experiment because nearly everyone involved wants to preserve something.

Tobe wants to confess. Ebina wants to preserve the group without becoming romantically legible. Hayama wants to protect the current social arrangement. Yui and Yukino care about the request and about Hachiman's method. Hachiman predicts the likely damage and constructs a false confession that prevents the feared event.

Instrumentally, the plan works.

Relationally, it fails.

Hachiman decides what outcome should be avoided and pays the visible cost himself. But the intervention also denies Tobe the intended speech, lets Hayama outsource moral dirt, protects Ebina without requiring full authorship of the boundary, and injures the Service Club because Yukino and Yui did not consent to Hachiman's use of himself as disposable mechanism.

The lesson for the triad is not “never protect people.” It is:

> **prediction plus care does not equal authority to decide.**

That principle will later govern the romance itself.

## 4.2 Yui becomes a methodological boundary-setter

Yui's importance in this movement is not merely that she dislikes seeing Hachiman hurt himself.

She begins placing explicit limits on method. `こういうの、もう、なしね` is structurally important because it says the relational consequence is not solely Hachiman's to price. A person can consent to his own humiliation and still lack authority to impose the resulting relationship on others.

Yukino's criticism operates differently but converges on the same issue. Hachiman has treated his own interpretation of what everyone needs as sufficient procedural authority.

Thus the Service Club's conflict is epistemic and political as well as emotional.

## 4.3 The election arc proves that continuity can be false to the relation without being “fake” in an ontological sense

The election crisis is the series' clearest demonstration that institutional preservation is not the same thing as relational preservation.

Hachiman wants to keep the club together. Yui also wants continuation. Yukino wants to run for president and explicitly claims the candidacy as `私の意志`.

Hachiman's intervention prevents the feared dissolution. The club remains.

But Yukino's choice has been discounted.

The resulting room is hollow.

This is not because the tea, desks, or meetings are counterfeit objects. It is because the preserved form has become a poor container for the actual state of the participants. The institution exists while the members' authorship has been damaged.

That negative control should govern every later preservation attempt:

> **If the form survives by removing the participants' standing to choose its meaning, preservation has failed even when continuity is technically achieved.**

## 4.4 Volume 9 repairs procedure before it solves desire

The `本物` request is often remembered as the emotional climax that reveals the “true” relationship.

V2 treats it more carefully.

Hachiman says:

> `一つ、依頼がしたい`

and then:

> `俺は、本物が欲しい`

The crucial transformation is procedural. He becomes the requester. He admits that what he wants cannot be derived neutrally from analysis. The desire belongs to him.

Yui keeps the conversation from collapsing. Yukino accepts the request without pretending that she fully understands or already possesses the answer.

The trio therefore does not leave Volume 9 with a solved romantic configuration.

It leaves with a new covenant:

> **we may continue even though we cannot yet guarantee what the continuation will mean.**

This is why the Christmas coda matters. Negotiated scheduling, differentiated gifts, leisure, and `また明日` demonstrate that answerability can produce ordinary life before the triangle is resolved.

The `本物` request therefore belongs to the triad before it belongs to the romance. It changes how the participants are permitted to ask for relation.

# 5. V10–V11: ordinary warmth and explicit incompatibility

## 5.1 Post-`本物` ordinary life is a test, not a victory lap

Volumes 10 and 11 are structurally necessary because they ask whether the new procedural ideal survives ordinary imperfection.

The answer is mixed.

The trio can again laugh, work, drink tea, plan events, exchange ordinary remarks, and occupy the room without every moment becoming crisis. This strengthens the claim that Volume 9 was not an empty catharsis.

But Hachiman still overreaches. Yukino still struggles to locate her desire cleanly. Yui still uses accommodation to preserve options. Privacy, expectation, and role remain contested.

Thus “we asked for something genuine” does not convert the relationship into a purified state.

The triad remains made of the same fallible people.

## 5.2 The aquarium proposal is the moment preservation becomes explicit political design

Volume 11 transforms Yui's long-running wish for continuation into an articulated future proposal.

Her language is important:

> `三人で、行きたいの……`

> `ここがいいの`

> `あたしは全部ほしい`

> `あたしはもうちゃんと決めてる`

This is not indecision.

Yui knows what she wants: Hachiman, Yukino, the three-person relation, and the ordinary place they have made.

The problem is not lack of agency. It is **joint attainability**.

Yui's proposal tries to keep several goods that may not be simultaneously compatible. The proposal is caring because it refuses to treat Yukino as expendable. It is selfish because Yui wants Hachiman. It is protective because it seeks to preserve the club. It is strategic because the proposed arrangement delays an exclusive answer. It is emotionally honest because Yui names that she wants everything.

All of those can be true at once.

The ethical question is whether Yui's preferred arrangement can assign the future of all three people without requiring each person's distinct answer.

## 5.3 Yukino's response restores feelings jurisdiction

Yukino's late Volume-11 language is the critical counterweight:

> `……私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで`

She does not merely reject Yui's proposal because she secretly prefers Hachiman.

She rejects the assumption that her feelings can be inferred and allocated by the other two.

This is a decisive triadic rule:

> **No pair has jurisdiction to decide the third person's feeling merely because doing so would preserve the group.**

Yukino then becomes requester:

> `……私の依頼、聞いてもらえるかしら`

The movement from being discussed to making a request herself is more important than any immediate romantic interpretation.

## 5.4 Connected after nonidentity

By the end of Volume 11, the trio has crossed a threshold.

They know enough to understand that their desires are not identical. Yet the relationship does not dissolve.

That is the first mature evidence that conflict may be survivable without unanimity.

The triad is now:

> **connected after nonidentity, not reconciled through sameness.**

This principle will carry the final arc.

# 6. V12–V13: diagnosis becomes a triadic jurisdiction problem

## 6.1 `共依存` does not arrive as a neutral truth

The late-series dependency vocabulary is powerful because it names mechanisms the characters have actually experienced.

Yukino recognizes expectation and burden displacement:

> `そう思うくらい、……あなたに依存していたの`

Yui recognizes a different mechanism:

> `彼女が彼に依存したみたいに、あたしは彼女に依存したの。`

Hachiman recognizes that being needed can reinforce his own self-worth and willingness to intervene.

Haruno compresses the system into `共依存`.

V2 preserves the mechanism and rejects the totalization.

The later correction `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。` is crucial. A mechanism can be real without deciding what the feelings mean, whether the relationships are false, or what single cure must follow.

The diagnosis therefore changes the triad's question from:

> Are these feelings real?

to:

> **Which patterns of reliance are displacing authorship, and how can the participants change those patterns without pretending the relationships have no value?**

## 6.2 Separation is an experiment, not an obvious cure

Yukino's late attempt to reduce reliance on Hachiman is self-authored and serious. She wants to discover what she can do without automatically drawing on the person whose involvement has become structurally overdetermined.

That experiment has value.

But V2 rejects the inference that distance is therefore synonymous with autonomy.

The series has many counterexamples: Komachi's secure attachment, bounded requests throughout the early volumes, distributed Service Club work, and later capability-aware support. Autonomy concerns authorship, not maximum separation.

This matters because “the three should separate” can become another totalizing answer imposed by diagnosis.

If the triad's ethical failure was partly that people decided too much for others, a cure that simply declares the relation invalid repeats the same procedural error at a higher theoretical level.

## 6.3 Yui's decision-displacement confession is a triadic, not merely personal, insight

Yui's `あたしは彼女に依存したの` should remain narrow.

She is not announcing that her friendship with Yukino is pathological in essence. She identifies that she let Yukino's self-denial carry a decision Yui herself feared to force.

This is one of the clearest examples of how triadic structure can hide authorship.

If Yukino withdraws, Yui may receive greater access to Hachiman without ever having to say, “I choose a future that excludes Yukino's romantic possibility.” If Hachiman accepts Yukino's closure, he can avoid fully naming his own desire. If Yukino decides that giving Hachiman to Yui is the correct self-sacrificial answer, she can turn uncertainty into virtue while choosing for the other two.

Each person can therefore use another person's act to avoid owning a piece of the final choice.

The triad's late crisis is precisely the effort to stop doing that.

## 6.4 Hachiman's Volume-13 insight: the whole triangle cannot be decided by two people

One of Hachiman's most important procedural advances in Volume 13 is his recognition that he and Yukino cannot determine the whole system for Yui.

That statement matters more than whether his dummy-prom method is perfectly clean. His practice remains mixed. He still manipulates institutions and constructs a rivalry.

But the ethical vocabulary has advanced:

> **a dyadic agreement cannot automatically settle a triadic future.**

This is the point at which the Service Club becomes legible as an actual political unit: not because it has formal rules, but because multiple persons possess standing over shared decisions.

## 6.5 Yukino's closure request is self-authored and still overreaching

The V2 Yukino monograph freezes a difficult proposition: self-authorship is not identical to ethical adequacy.

Yukino's late request that Hachiman grant Yui's wish is genuinely hers. It is not simply Haruno controlling her, Hachiman projecting, or Yui coercing.

Yet the request also attempts to determine too much of Hachiman and Yui's future.

This is analytically important because it prevents the autonomy framework from becoming simplistic. A person can own a choice and still exceed her jurisdiction.

The same is true for Hachiman and Yui at other points.

The triad therefore needs two independent questions:

1. **Who authored this choice?**
2. **Whose life does the choice claim authority to settle?**

Late maturity depends on keeping those questions separate.

## 6.6 Rivalry becomes ethically preferable to disguised rescue

The dummy-prom rivalry is imperfect, but it has one important virtue over the earlier unilateral interventions: the structure is increasingly explicit about contest.

Hachiman no longer pretends to be merely executing Yukino's wish. He acts because he wants continued involvement. Yukino can refuse direct help while confronting a rival project. Collaborators can be asked rather than silently assigned. The object of conflict is externalized enough that the participants can answer.

This does not make rivalry intrinsically healthy.

It demonstrates that **openly contested involvement can preserve more agency than benevolent disappearance**.

That principle prepares the final romantic choice.

# 7. Volume 14: known non-equivalence replaces protected ambiguity

## 7.1 The final choice is a romantic answer, not a metaphysical verdict

Volume 14 gives Hachiman and Yukino a mutual romantic choice.

That fact should not be softened.

Hachiman eventually reaches a formulation no longer justified by duty, rescue, or another person's incapacity:

> `俺は関わり続けたいと、思ってる。`

Yukino refuses to remain the passive object of that proposal. She challenges his inadequate wording and counter-requests:

> `あなたの人生を、私にください`

Later she states ordinary romantic desire plainly:

> `あなたが好きよ。比企谷くん`

The choice is reciprocal.

But a reciprocal choice is not the same as proof that every previous relation was leading toward it by necessity. Nor does it certify the chosen dyad as healthy in every future condition.

V2 therefore describes Volume 14 as **romantic selection under retained revisability**, not revelation of a timeless hidden truth.

## 7.2 What changes for Hachiman/Yui

The answer necessarily creates a loss for Yui.

This is not a symbolic loss that can be immediately converted into spiritual growth. She wanted Hachiman romantically. She had reason to believe the relation mattered. She demanded an answer rather than indefinite rescue ambiguity. The answer is unfavorable.

Hachiman's response removes romantic equivalence.

That does not make Hachiman/Yui intimacy false.

The dyad had real ordinary time, care, attraction from Yui, conflict, repair, and possible futures. The negative answer settles one dimension: Hachiman does not choose the romantic future Yui wanted.

It does not settle every other dimension.

This is why “Yui lost” is true but radically incomplete.

## 7.3 What changes for Yukino/Yui

The final choice also changes Yukino/Yui.

They are now friends who know they have wanted the same person and who know the outcome is non-equivalent.

The text refuses to solve this by making Yui disappear or by requiring Yukino to renounce her own desire as repayment.

Yui asks about Yukino's feelings. Yukino answers. Yui reciprocates with her own truth. They refuse the fantasy that friendship requires desire to be absent.

The friendship therefore crosses a difficult threshold:

> **rivalry becomes explicit without friendship being declared counterfeit.**

This is one of the series' strongest arguments against purity models of relation. A friendship can contain jealousy, competition, asymmetrical gain, grief, and continued care.

None of those alone determines whether the friendship is real.

## 7.4 `勝手に終わらせないでほしい`: Yui refuses administrative closure

Yui's late demand:

> `勝手に終わらせないでほしい`

is a major triadic speech act.

The object is not only romance. It is unilateral ending.

The line rejects the idea that another person's self-sacrifice, diagnosis, or romantic selection automatically grants authority to terminate every shared relation affected by the decision.

This is precisely the procedural problem that has recurred since early Hachiman interventions.

The mature correction is not “never end anything.” It is:

> **the people whose relation is being ended have standing in how the ending is authored.**

Yui's later communication ethic sharpens the point:

> `それ、言わなきゃ絶対わかんないよ`

A person cannot demand telepathy and then call the resulting misunderstanding proof of relational failure.

The final triad is possible because speech is no longer treated as a guarantee of perfect understanding but as a required contribution to answerability.

## 7.5 The recreated Service Club is not a reset

When Yui returns to the club after the romantic answer, the temptation is to call the triangle restored.

That is structurally wrong.

The old triangle depended partly on ambiguity. The participants could continue because several painful questions remained protectively unresolved.

The final triad cannot rely on that ambiguity in the same way.

Hachiman and Yukino's mutual romantic choice is known. Yui's love and grief are known. Yukino/Yui friendship is affirmed. The Service Club is recreated. Yui enters voluntarily. Yukino invites her into conversation.

Thus the final configuration is:

> **continued triadic relation after acknowledged non-equivalence.**

The achievement is not restoration of the earlier state.

It is the discovery that the relation may be inhabitable after the state changes.

## 7.6 The final problem is ongoing by design

The ending does not convert the triad into a solved geometry.

Yui's feelings do not evaporate on command. Hachiman and Yukino do not become perfect communicators. Yukino/Yui friendship now carries historical asymmetry. Boundaries will need continued negotiation.

The mainline ending's maturity lies partly in refusing to make this complexity disappear.

A relationship can be chosen without being finished.

# 8. V14.5: ordinary continuation and anti-sanctification

## 8.1 Durability is tested at lower dramatic temperature

V14 establishes the meaning of the ending. V14.5 tests what that ending can sustain afterward.

This distinction is essential.

A dramatic climax can produce extraordinary honesty that does not survive ordinary life. The post-ending material therefore matters because it lowers the temperature: school planning, study, gifts, club succession, casual coordination, friendship, and repeated ordinary decisions.

The triad does not need to repeat the bridge conversation in every scene to prove continuation.

It needs to remain capable of being ordinary.

## 8.2 Yui remains a social subject after romantic non-reciprocity

Yui's post-ending function is not “the rejected girl who is still around.”

She continues to make practical judgments, coordinate, advise, and participate in friendship. Her motives may remain mixed. Residual romantic hope is not ruled out. Neither is deliberately chosen friendship.

The evidence supports continued agency without requiring a purified emotional state.

This is important because a weaker ending could preserve the triad only by making Yui emotionally harmless to the chosen couple. *Oregairu* does not fully do that.

Her continued presence retains some unresolvedness.

## 8.3 Hachiman's `神聖視` correction prevents the ending from becoming a new idol

The most important V14.5 correction is Hachiman's recognition that he has begun to `神聖視` the existing Service Club configuration.

This is the post-ending form of an old cognitive temptation.

Earlier Hachiman used cynicism to freeze relationship meaning negatively. Now he risks freezing a successful relationship positively.

Both moves reduce revisability.

The correction is therefore profound:

> **the current triad is valuable because of its history and practice, not because its exact present configuration has become sacred.**

The relation was never perfect. Its worth does not depend on perfection.

## 8.4 Komachi succession turns continuity into transmission

Komachi's succession as president, Iroha's continuing institutional presence, the possibility of new members, and Yukino's transmission of tea practice all relocate the meaning of the Service Club.

If continuity required Hachiman, Yukino, and Yui to occupy the same chairs forever, the club would become precisely the kind of preserved shell the series has already criticized.

Instead, continuity becomes transmissible.

The deepest thing preserved is not membership identity but a practice:

- requests can be brought;
- people can disagree;
- help should not automatically replace authorship;
- ordinary presence can matter;
- imperfect answers can be revised;
- care can survive changed form.

The terminal triad is therefore compatible with institutional succession because the relation no longer needs to own the institution's future in order to validate its own past.

# 9. The three dyads inside the triad

## 9.1 Hachiman ↔ Yukino: from recognition to reciprocal claim

Hachiman/Yukino is the selected romantic dyad, but its V2 importance lies in more than selection.

The relationship develops through:

- adversarial recognition;
- Hachiman's idealization of Yukino as someone who does not lie;
- disillusionment and revised perception;
- reliance and intervention;
- failure of telepathic assumptions;
- direct request;
- feelings-jurisdiction correction;
- autonomy experiment;
- rivalry;
- mutual future claim;
- capability-aware support.

The terminal image is not rescuer and rescued.

When support occurs after `一人で立てるのに` / `知ってる`, relation is explicitly separated from incapacity.

This dyad becomes mature enough to be chosen because it increasingly allows refusal and counter-request.

That does not mean it becomes self-sufficient as a social world. Yui, family, friends, institutions, and future obligations remain relevant.

## 9.2 Hachiman ↔ Yui: real intimacy under non-reciprocal romantic outcome

Hachiman/Yui should be protected from two opposite distortions.

The first is route erasure: because Hachiman ultimately chooses Yukino, his relation with Yui must have been superficial, compensatory, or doomed from the start.

The second is retrospective equalization: because Yui's route was credible, Hachiman must have possessed equal reciprocal romantic desire that the ending arbitrarily suppresses.

V2 supports neither.

The relationship contains real intimacy, repeated ordinary time, care, conflict, and a credible road not taken. Yui's romantic desire is highly legible. Hachiman's reciprocal romantic commitment is not established at the same level.

The final answer therefore creates real non-reciprocity without invalidating the history.

## 9.3 Yukino ↔ Yui: the friendship that makes post-choice triadic continuity possible

Yukino/Yui is arguably the most important relationship for understanding why the final triad does not simply collapse into “couple plus leftover friend.”

The friendship predates explicit rivalry. It includes birthday care, reliance, bluntness, affection, conflict, competitive energy, political disagreement, jealousy, self-removal, and direct discussion of feelings.

Because the friendship is independently authored, Yui can continue to value Yukino even when Yukino receives the romantic future Yui wanted.

Because the friendship is independently authored, Yukino can invite Yui back without the gesture needing to be read only as guilt or condescension.

The friendship does not eliminate asymmetry.

It gives the asymmetry somewhere relationally real to be negotiated.

## 9.4 Why no dyad should absorb the third

A mature relational reading therefore refuses three absorptions:

- Hachiman/Yukino romance cannot absorb Yui into “the loser.”
- Hachiman/Yui intimacy cannot absorb Yukino into “the obstacle.”
- Yukino/Yui friendship cannot be reduced to an arrangement whose meaning is determined by Hachiman.

The triad matters because all three dyads retain independent content even after one receives romantic priority.

# 10. Requests: the triad's changing constitutional language

## 10.1 Requests are more important than confessions alone

The Service Club is founded around requests, so it is appropriate that the central relationship's development can be tracked through changes in request structure.

Early requests are often client problems. The members solve someone else's problem while revealing their own methods.

Later, the members themselves become requesters.

This matters because a request contains an implicit ethics:

- the requester names a need or desire;
- the addressee receives standing to answer;
- the scope can be bounded;
- refusal remains conceptually possible;
- responsibility for the desire remains attached to the speaker.

The mature relation becomes increasingly request-based rather than solution-imposed.

## 10.2 Hachiman's V9 request changes authorship

`一つ、依頼がしたい` reverses his habitual position.

Hachiman is no longer the detached solver. He is the person asking.

`俺は、本物が欲しい` further marks desire as his.

This does not give him a right to a particular response. It gives the others a clearer object to answer.

## 10.3 Yukino's late requests recover her from being interpreted

Yukino's language increasingly uses first-person possession:

- `私の意志`;
- `私の気持ち`;
- `私の依頼`;
- `私の願い`.

These constructions matter because much of the late conflict involves other people explaining what Yukino really needs.

A request puts Yukino back in the position of claimant rather than object of diagnosis.

## 10.4 Yui's wishes become proposals that must accept answer

Yui's `全部ほしい` is a wish with real standing.

It is not thereby a jointly attainable settlement.

The mature shift occurs when Yui can continue to own the wish while also demanding an answer that may deny part of it.

That is a major relational achievement:

> **desire retains dignity without acquiring entitlement to outcome.**

## 10.5 The final triad is request-capable, not request-satisfied

This is the strongest constitutional metaphor for the ending.

The final relation is not one in which everyone received what they wanted.

It is one in which each person's desire became increasingly attributable, contestable, and answerable.

The members can ask without pretending that asking guarantees agreement.

# 11. Silence, ambiguity, and the ethics of not saying

## 11.1 Silence is not one thing

*Oregairu* repeatedly uses silence, ellipsis, indirectness, and incomplete naming.

These should not be moralized uniformly.

Silence can:

- protect someone from premature exposure;
- create room for thought;
- preserve dignity;
- delay an answer;
- hide motive;
- prevent refusal;
- keep an incompatible arrangement functioning;
- express a limit where language genuinely fails.

The question is what the silence does to other people's standing.

## 11.2 Early ambiguity is developmentally useful

The Service Club could not have formed if every implication had been forced into explicit speech immediately.

Institutional pretext, nicknames, joking, requests, and ordinary routine allow intimacy to grow under tolerable ambiguity.

Thus V2 rejects a crude equation:

> indirect = false.

Some ambiguity is enabling.

## 11.3 Late ambiguity becomes costly because the stakes have changed

By Volumes 11–14, the same ambiguity has different consequences.

The participants now possess substantial relational history and incompatible future desires. Not speaking no longer merely protects a fragile beginning. It begins to allocate costs and options silently.

This is why Yui's communication ethic becomes so important.

`それ、言わなきゃ絶対わかんないよ` does not abolish subtlety. It marks the point at which silence can no longer be treated as a neutral container.

## 11.4 The mature relation needs both speech and tolerance for incompleteness

The series does not endorse total transparency.

Hachiman still overthinks. Yukino still speaks through a controlled register. Yui still hedges. People still misunderstand.

The mature principle is more modest:

> **Say enough that the other person can answer; admit what remains unknown; continue the work of interpretation without claiming telepathy.**

This is answerability rather than transparency.

# 12. Jealousy, rivalry, and mixed motives

## 12.1 Jealousy does not invalidate friendship

Yui is jealous. Yukino experiences rivalry. Hachiman's choices create asymmetrical gain and loss.

A purity model would treat these facts as evidence that friendship has become false.

The text does not.

Yui can love Yukino and want Hachiman. Yukino can love Yui and choose Hachiman. Care and competition can coexist.

The relevant ethical test is not whether jealousy exists, but what authority it claims.

## 12.2 Rivalry becomes healthier when it is admitted

Unacknowledged rivalry often appears as self-sacrifice or benevolent withdrawal.

Yukino can tell herself that disappearing is the generous solution. Yui can allow Yukino's self-removal to decide what Yui is afraid to claim. Hachiman can present intervention as duty rather than desire.

Open rivalry is emotionally harsher but procedurally cleaner because it returns agency to the participants.

This is why the series can prefer a contested second prom to polite disappearance.

## 12.3 Mixed motive is the normal state of the triad

No major participant reaches the ending with purified motive.

Hachiman's continued involvement contains desire, promise, habit, neededness history, love, fear of loss, and strategic impulse.

Yukino's choices contain pride, family differentiation, attraction, care for Yui, desire for Hachiman, self-protective independence, and genuine vocational questions.

Yui's continuation contains friendship, love, grief, jealousy, care, ordinary attachment, and perhaps residual hope.

The presence of mixture does not prove falsity.

It creates the problem Document 06 will analyze directly: how authenticity can survive performance, selfishness, duty, and incomplete self-knowledge.

# 13. Ordinary presence as evidence

## 13.1 Why ordinary life matters

A relationship that exists only under crisis can be difficult to distinguish from circumstance.

The Service Club repeatedly receives ordinary-life tests:

- tea;
- idle conversation;
- shopping;
- studying;
- Christmas scheduling;
- gift exchange;
- clubroom silence;
- leisure;
- post-choice planning;
- succession work.

These scenes matter because the participants have alternatives. They repeatedly choose some form of shared presence.

## 13.2 Ordinary presence does not certify the relation

V2 nevertheless rejects an opposite overreach.

Repeated ordinary time does not prove:

- equal desire;
- absence of jealousy;
- romantic health;
- permanent compatibility;
- perfect authenticity;
- future permanence.

Ordinary presence is evidence of **durability and preference**, not metaphysical certification.

That distinction is especially important after Volume 14. Yui's continued presence proves she has not been structurally erased. It does not prove her grief has vanished. Hachiman/Yukino ordinary planning supports reciprocal futurity. It does not prove every dependency concern has been permanently solved.

## 13.3 Ordinary life is where changed form becomes real

The mainline ending could have declared that the three remain important to one another and stopped there.

V14.5 makes the claim more credible by showing relation in mundane operation.

The ultimate test is not whether the characters can produce one perfect climactic speech. It is whether the changed arrangement can survive boring Tuesdays, school schedules, gifts, new members, and practical disagreement.

The post-ending evidence says: provisionally, yes.

# 14. `本物` and the triad

## 14.1 The genuine request belongs to all three before it belongs to a pair

The Volume-9 request is often retroactively read as Hachiman asking for his eventual relationship with Yukino.

That is too narrow.

The scene occurs in the triadic crisis. Hachiman asks because the Service Club's preserved shell has become intolerable. Yui participates in keeping the conversation possible. Yukino receives the request while unable to provide a complete answer.

The request therefore concerns a way of relating before it concerns a selected romantic pair.

## 14.2 `本物` does not promise equal outcomes

The desire for something genuine does not entail that everyone's wishes will become compatible.

Indeed, the series' later development suggests the opposite: answerable relation may require accepting that a genuine answer hurts.

If `本物` meant “a state in which nobody loses,” Volume 14 would falsify it.

If it meant “the relationship selected at the end was always the only real one,” the earlier Hachiman/Yui and Yukino/Yui evidence would become inexplicable.

The stronger formulation is procedural:

> **The desire for `本物` increasingly demands that the participants stop using preservation, theory, sacrifice, or social form to prevent each other from answering.**

## 14.3 The triad shows why authenticity cannot mean purity

The final relation contains:

- romantic asymmetry;
- grief;
- selfish desire;
- friendship;
- obligation;
- institutional roles;
- awkwardness;
- performance;
- unresolved hope;
- affection;
- future uncertainty.

If authenticity required purity from these elements, the ending would fail its own test.

The triad instead demonstrates a stronger and harder possibility: relation can remain valuable when mixed motives are known and answerable.

## 14.4 V14.5 prevents the endpoint from becoming a certificate

The absence of repeated `本物` classification in ordinary post-ending life is meaningful.

The relation is being lived rather than continuously diagnosed.

Hachiman's anti-`神聖視` correction then closes the loop: even a relation that survived the mainline crisis should not be frozen into a sacred form.

Thus the `本物` architecture terminates not in certification, but in revisability.

# 15. Romantic choice: what Volume 14 resolves

The ending does resolve important questions. V2 should not evade them in the name of complexity.

## 15.1 It resolves Hachiman's romantic selection

Hachiman chooses continued intimate involvement with Yukino in a way the text frames romantically and mutually.

He does not choose the corresponding romantic future with Yui.

This is a real asymmetry.

## 15.2 It resolves Yukino's willingness to claim Hachiman

Yukino does not remain the self-removing person who decides that withdrawal is morally superior.

She answers, counter-requests, accepts support under acknowledged capability, and states love directly.

## 15.3 It resolves Yui's right to an answer

Yui is no longer protected indefinitely through ambiguity.

The answer hurts, but it is hers to receive.

This vindicates one of the series' central late ethical corrections: protection from pain cannot automatically outrank the person's standing to know what future is actually available.

## 15.4 It resolves the fiction of romantic equivalence

After Volume 14, the three-person relation cannot responsibly be described as an unresolved equal romantic triangle.

That equivalence is gone.

Any later analysis must begin from known non-equivalence.

# 16. Romantic choice: what Volume 14 does not resolve

## 16.1 It does not prove destiny

The ending does not make earlier ambiguity false.

A choice can be historically produced rather than eternally prewritten.

The Hachiman/Yukino relationship becomes the selected romantic future because of accumulated history, attraction, conflict, revision, and choice. That is different from saying every prior scene secretly contained the complete endpoint.

## 16.2 It does not erase Hachiman/Yui

Yui's loss does not retroactively convert her relationship with Hachiman into misunderstanding or debt.

The history remains part of both characters' lives.

## 16.3 It does not erase Yukino/Yui

The friendship remains independently authored.

The chosen romance cannot absorb or settle every claim in that friendship.

## 16.4 It does not certify Hachiman/Yukino as permanently healthy

The ending supplies strong evidence for reciprocity, communication, capability-aware support, and chosen involvement.

It does not provide years of adult evidence.

Family, vocation, communication habits, intervention instincts, jealousy, and future burden allocation remain live domains.

## 16.5 It does not eliminate residual ambiguity from Yui's future

The mainline does not establish whether Yui's future continuation is weighted primarily toward lingering romantic hope, friendship, habit, ordinary love, competitive persistence, or some changing mixture.

The correct status is OPEN for degree.

## 16.6 It does not dissolve the triad

This is perhaps the most important negative result.

Romantic selection changes the triad's form.

It does not abolish the triad's history or its ongoing social reality.

# 17. What survives the choice

## 17.1 Friendship survives without equality of outcome

Yukino and Yui remain friends after the romantic outcome creates asymmetry.

This is not morally trivial. Many relationship narratives solve rivalry by exile, demonization, consolation pairing, or convenient disappearance.

*Oregairu* instead leaves the friendship burdened by history and still active.

## 17.2 Ordinary intimacy survives in altered forms

Hachiman/Yui ordinary interaction does not vanish. Hachiman/Yukino ordinary futurity expands. Yukino/Yui coordination remains.

The same behaviors no longer carry the same possible meanings because the romantic answer has changed the field.

That is what changed form means.

## 17.3 The Service Club survives as practice

The club's deepest practices remain available:

- listening to requests;
- contesting methods;
- sharing work;
- disagreeing;
- allowing awkward people to remain present;
- revising bad solutions;
- returning after conflict.

These practices become transferable to Komachi and later participants.

## 17.4 The right to revise survives

The final relation is not locked.

Hachiman's anti-sanctification insight makes this explicit.

The trio's history is valuable enough to preserve memory of it and flexible enough that preservation does not require identical repetition.

## 17.5 Imperfection survives

This may be the most important final continuity.

They continue to be people who misunderstand, overcomplicate, hedge, get jealous, use roles, overreach, and need correction.

The ending does not eliminate the conditions that made the story possible.

It changes their procedures for living with them.

# 18. Triadic jurisdiction: the mature ethical model

The full relationship can be summarized through a jurisdiction framework.

## 18.1 Caring is not deciding

All three characters repeatedly care about one another.

That fact does not grant any one of them authority to choose what the others should want.

## 18.2 Prediction is not consent

Hachiman may predict that Yui will be hurt, Yukino will withdraw, or a group will collapse.

Prediction is information.

It is not the predicted person's answer.

## 18.3 Self-sacrifice is not a jurisdiction waiver

A person can volunteer to pay a cost and still impose consequences on the others.

Hachiman's humiliation, Yukino's self-removal, and Yui's willingness to endure ambiguity all demonstrate variants of this problem.

## 18.4 Self-authorship is not sovereignty over shared outcomes

Yukino's V13 closure request is genuinely authored and still overreaches.

Yui's V11 future proposal is genuinely authored and still cannot bind Yukino and Hachiman automatically.

Hachiman's V14 desire is genuinely authored and still requires Yukino's answer.

## 18.5 Mature relation requires multiple standing holders

The final relational procedure therefore resembles a plural constitutional order:

- each person can state a desire;
- each person can refuse an interpretation;
- each person can contest a proposed future;
- each person can leave some uncertainty unresolved;
- no pair automatically controls the third person's position;
- shared form remains revisable.

This is what “answerability” means at the triadic level.

# 19. Conflict tolerance as the strongest maturity signal

A weaker relationship model would measure health by harmony.

*Oregairu* increasingly suggests a different test.

Early conflict often threatens dissolution. Hachiman expects rupture. Yukino can respond to injury with distance. Yui often manages atmosphere to keep confrontation from becoming terminal.

Late conflict can be survived.

- Hachiman and Yukino can disagree about intervention and still continue.
- Yukino can refuse Hachiman's interpretation of her feelings.
- Yui can challenge both of them.
- Yukino and Yui can acknowledge romantic rivalry.
- Hachiman can give Yui an unfavorable answer.
- the three can meet again afterward.

Thus the mature achievement is not that conflict disappears.

It is that disagreement stops functioning as automatic evidence that the relationship must be false or over.

The triad becomes **conflict-tolerant without becoming conflict-indifferent**.

That is a more demanding form of stability than harmony maintained through silence.

# 20. Counterreadings and adjudication

## 20.1 Counterreading: “The whole story proves Hachiman/Yukino were the genuine pair and Yui was the obstacle” — REJECT

The ending supports Hachiman/Yukino romantic selection.

It does not support retroactive deletion of Yui's constitutive role, Hachiman/Yui intimacy, or Yukino/Yui friendship.

This reading confuses final romantic selection with total historical ontology.

## 20.2 Counterreading: “The trio should have remained permanently ambiguous because that was the most caring arrangement” — REJECT

The preserved ambiguity becomes increasingly costly because it allocates decision through avoidance.

Yui herself eventually demands an answer. Yukino rejects others deciding her feelings. Hachiman learns that protecting people from outcomes can overreach.

Permanent ambiguity would repeat the hollow-preservation problem.

## 20.3 Counterreading: “Yui's Volume-11 proposal proves she is manipulative” — REVISE

The proposal is strategic and can be jurisdictionally overreaching.

It is also caring, self-aware, and expressive of real plural desire.

“Manipulation” alone discards the ethical complexity.

## 20.4 Counterreading: “Yukino's self-removal proves she understood the correct pairing before the others” — REJECT

Her withdrawal is a late, contingent response shaped by dependency anxiety, care, self-restraint, and an attempt to produce closure.

The later text itself contests her authority to choose the whole triangle's future.

## 20.5 Counterreading: “The final triad is unhealthy because Yui remains” — REJECT as total claim; OPEN for future boundary difficulty

Yui's presence is not evidence of automatic pathology.

The mainline shows informed non-equivalence, friendship authorship, a real answer, and voluntary continuation.

Long-term boundary difficulty remains plausible and under-observed.

## 20.6 Counterreading: “The final triad is healthy because everybody remains friends” — REJECT

Continuation does not certify health.

The arrangement retains grief, jealousy, residual hope, communication difficulty, and future negotiation.

The strongest claim is durability under present evidence, not completed health.

## 20.7 Counterreading: “`共依存` proves the three should separate” — REJECT

The diagnosis identifies bounded reinforcement mechanisms.

It does not establish one unique cure, determine the truth of feeling, or erase counterexamples in which reliance and autonomy coexist.

## 20.8 Counterreading: “Romantic choice destroys the triad” — REJECT

It destroys romantic equivalence.

It changes the triad.

The later text supplies direct evidence of continued three-person relation and institutional continuity after that change.

# 25. Terminal triad model

The completed Japanese-primary mainline supports the following terminal model.

The Service Club begins as an imposed institution that accidentally gives three socially mismatched people a legitimate reason to occupy the same room.

Hachiman and Yukino first recognize one another through antagonism. Yui turns repeated contact into socially legible relation. Requests become pretexts for attachment. Tea becomes routine. Work becomes reliance. Reliance becomes cost-bearing. The three learn that another person's pain or overwork is no longer private merely because that person consented to it.

Then their characteristic strengths become characteristic dangers.

Hachiman's insight becomes unilateral solution. Yukino's competence becomes substitution. Yui's preservation becomes conflict deferral. The club's form becomes something they can mistake for the relationship itself.

Kyoto and the election arc expose the failure of preservation-through-control. Volume 9 changes the procedure: Hachiman asks. Yui sustains the difficult conversation. Yukino receives the request without pretending certainty. The relation becomes capable of continuing without having solved itself.

Volumes 10 and 11 prove that this is not enough. Ordinary warmth returns, but desire is not identical. Yui wants everything. Yukino refuses to let the other two define her feelings. The three-person unit discovers that connection can survive nonidentity.

Volumes 12 and 13 then convert emotional difficulty into a problem of authorship and jurisdiction. Dependency mechanisms are real. Yet diagnosis cannot decide feeling, and separation cannot be assumed to be the unique cure. Each character is forced to confront the fact that self-sacrifice may be self-authored and still decide too much for somebody else.

Volume 14 finally removes romantic equivalence.

Hachiman chooses Yukino. Yukino chooses Hachiman. Yui does not receive the romantic future she wants. She receives an answer, grief, and continued standing. Yukino and Yui remain friends. The club is recreated. The old ambiguity is gone.

The achievement is not that nobody loses.

The achievement is that **loss no longer requires deletion**.

V14.5 then applies the final correction. The current form cannot become sacred. New people may enter. Roles may change. The founding three may leave the chairs they once occupied. Their history remains valuable without owning the future.

The terminal formulation is therefore:

> **The Service Club matures from a protected space in which attachment can exist without full naming into an answerable three-person relation capable of surviving explicit non-equivalence. Its members do not become identical in desire, purified in motive, or perfectly transparent. They become more able to state what they want, refuse what others decide for them, accept that care does not confer sovereignty, receive painful answers, and continue without demanding that continuation preserve the old form. The final romantic choice privileges Hachiman/Yukino as a couple while preserving Hachiman/Yui history and Yukino/Yui friendship as real, independently meaningful relations. The triad survives not as unresolved romance but as revisable interdependence after the romance has been resolved asymmetrically.**

A compact formula:

> **protected ambiguity → answerable nonidentity → known non-equivalence → revisable continuation**

That is the mature Service Club relationship architecture.

# 26. Handoff to Document 06 — authenticity, performance, and mixed motives

With the series architecture, three principal character monographs, and triadic relationship system now stabilized, Phase 5 can move from **who the participants are and how their relationship changes** to the conceptual problem that repeatedly governs their self-judgment:

**`06_THE_GENUINE_AUTHENTICITY_PERFORMANCE_AND_MIXED_MOTIVES.md`**

Document 06 should inherit the following constraints:

1. **The final romantic answer is not an authenticity certificate.**
2. **The prior triad is not fake merely because it contained ambiguity, rivalry, and incompatible desire.**
3. **Yui's kindness can contain selfish desire without becoming counterfeit.**
4. **Yukino's competence and controlled register can be socially performed without becoming false.**
5. **Hachiman's abstraction can conceal desire and still contain genuine insight.**
6. **A role, pretext, or socially conventional form can carry real particular meaning.**
7. **`本物` must remain historically developing rather than receiving one retrospective dictionary definition.**
8. **Mixed motive is normal; the key question is whether motives and roles remain answerable and revisable.**
9. **Pain, bluntness, spontaneity, independence, and romantic success are not sufficient authenticity tests.**
10. **V14.5 anti-`神聖視` requires the final authenticity model to remain anti-certifying.**

The triad therefore hands Document 06 its strongest conceptual problem:

> **How can a relationship be genuine when it contains performance, selfishness, fear, duty, social role, incomplete knowledge, and outcomes that do not satisfy everyone?**

That is the next architecture-defined question.
