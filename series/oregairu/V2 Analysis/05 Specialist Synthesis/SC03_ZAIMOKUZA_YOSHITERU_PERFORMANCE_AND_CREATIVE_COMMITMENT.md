# Zaimokuza Yoshiteru — Performance, Creative Commitment, and the Chosen Audience

## 1. Responsibility and central finding

Zaimokuza Yoshiteru does not become a serious person by ceasing to be ridiculous. His grandiose self-presentation, improvised history, unstable career plans, self-serving requests, and fear of evaluation coexist with actual attachments: to particular kinds of entertainment, to writing and planning things, to people with whom he can share specialized pleasures, and to Hachiman as a reader and companion. The strongest claim is neither that his performance conceals a wholly different authentic self nor that perseverance guarantees a professional destination. **An invented persona can carry real commitments while also making those commitments easier to announce than to fulfill.** [Z01–Z06, Z12–Z15]

That distinction gives Zaimokuza an independent trajectory. He seeks audiences and associates who can receive what he enjoys; he wants recognition without unlimited exposure; he can ask for help, return help, bargain over its cost, and mobilize a small network. His literary importance does not depend entirely on supplying Hachiman with an analogy. The friendship makes these activities visible, but their objects—manuscripts, games, peer affiliation, anticipated work, embarrassment—belong to Zaimokuza. [Z02–Z04, Z08–Z13, Z16–Z19]

This study elaborates the Character State Ledger's `CS-SEC-ZAI`, the Social Role/Performance Ledger's §§4.4 and 12.2, and the Japanese Voice Ledger's §12.6. It strengthens Document 13's `PUB-GEN-04` and the Phase 6 disposition `AUD-GEN-04`: performance is not falsity. It also narrows an easy overreading of **durable practice**. Recurring returns to writing, drafts, plots and readers do not establish a disciplined production routine, completed novels, a verified submission record, or an adult literary vocation. The prize-history tension in §5 remains OPEN. No frozen volume reading is revised here.

## 2. The persona is an activity, not a diagnosis

Volume 1 introduces a laboriously composed figure: coat, fingerless gloves, theatrical laughter, grand historical identity, and elaborate accounts of equipment. Hachiman explains this through the contemporary category `中二病`; he explicitly distinguishes the slang from an actual illness. Zaimokuza's characterization therefore does not license a clinical diagnosis, belief that his fantasy powers literally exist, or the assumption that every statement is delusional. He changes his invented hierarchy when Yukino challenges the Service Club's obligations, improvises pronouns, and drops into hesitant ordinary politeness when she makes him face her. His performance is sufficiently flexible to reveal audience awareness. [Z01]

The flexibility matters more than finding a supposedly pure voice underneath. Hachiman marks a smile as Zaimokuza's rather than the swordmaster's in the manuscript aftermath. That is a meaningful focalized distinction: Hachiman experiences an unfamiliar kind of seriousness and warmth. It is not omniscient access to a separate unperformed essence. Conversely, Zaimokuza's return to theatrical delivery after distress does not disprove the seriousness of what he has just said. In Volume 3 he can admit inadequacy with `俺`, then reoccupy `我` and duel-like language while continuing to express commitment. [Z02, Z05]

Performance does several things at once. It claims distinction, turns ordinary encounters into recognizable genre scenes, offers Hachiman a script to answer, and sometimes gives Zaimokuza the nerve to act. It can also delay the actual request, evade uncomfortable information, irritate listeners, and substitute entitlement for negotiation. Calling it emotional armor is a supported interpretation of these changing uses, not Zaimokuza's comprehensive private explanation of himself. [Z01, Z03, Z06, Z12]

## 3. Wanting to be read is more secure than wanting a particular career

The Volume 1 manuscript request is concrete. Zaimokuza says he wants to enter a newcomer prize, lacks friends from whom to obtain comments, and asks the club to read. He rejects open internet posting because strangers would be merciless. That is a choice of audience and exposure level, not absence of any desire to communicate. The manuscript itself exists, but Yukino says it is unfinished and criticizes grammar, narrative predictability, unnecessary exposition and unmotivated undressing. Her objections are not all interchangeable: some concern readability and completion; some reflect her unfamiliarity with light-novel conventions. Hachiman's plagiarism question is another adverse judgment, not an independently adjudicated plagiarism finding. [Z01]

Zaimokuza initially seeks the shelter of a genre-literate ally and suffers conspicuously when Hachiman joins the criticism. Yet his next request is not to erase the criticism. He asks whether they will read again, then says that receiving comments on something he liked writing made him happy. The ordinary ending of `読んでもらえるとやっぱり嬉しいよ` carries particular force after his elevated diction. His self-report and renewed request provide firmer support for a communicative commitment than Hachiman's subsequent general theory of the writer's condition. [Z02]

The distinction is essential. Hachiman imagines someone who will keep writing even without recognition, but the directly demonstrated event is smaller: Zaimokuza has been hurt by this audience and nevertheless requests its continued attention. The scene establishes neither indifference to recognition nor unlimited resistance to rejection. Volume 10.5's anticipatory fear of online judgment would contradict those exaggerations. What endures is the attraction of making something and having a selected person encounter it, alongside considerable difficulty completing and exposing it. [Z02, Z12–Z13]

The promise to read also changes daily companionship. In Volume 1 the pair still share physical education, but now talk about illustrators, debut prospects and the fantasy of marrying a voice actress. Hachiman says the conversations are not especially meaningful or fashionable; he also says the time is no longer unpleasant. Zaimokuza receives more than an abstract defense of his dream: he acquires an ongoing conversational recipient. [Z02]

## 4. The game-club dispute preserves desire without certifying skill

Volume 3 prevents sentimental rescue. Zaimokuza announces that writing game scenarios seems a better route to stable employment than light novels. He has provoked someone online after avoiding confrontation in person; he then wants the contest canceled or shifted to a format he can win. The immediate difficulty is substantially self-produced. The Service Club's initial refusal is justified by the difference between helping someone work and removing consequences for him. His aspiration does not confer unlimited claims on their labor. [Z04]

The gaming students' criticism combines legitimate concern with illegitimate jurisdiction. Breadth of experience, study, technique, and the difference between consumption and creation matter. Zaimokuza does not answer every criticism, and Hachiman grants that he needs to work. But the accusation `偽物` moves from inadequate preparation to invalid desire. Yui objects to that transition. Her speech has additional relational significance for Hachiman, yet Zaimokuza's response belongs to him: he admits having nothing to boast about and insists on staking himself on what he likes. [Z05]

Zaimokuza also names examples of aspiring writers who found employment or remained unemployed after partial prize success. These are his reports about acquaintances, not verified occupational biographies, but they complicate a reading in which he has never heard of material risk. The same speaker who fantasizes about easy stable employment can acknowledge disappointment and still declare that he would write without becoming a professional. Desire survives contact with the idea of failure; competence and consistent effort remain separate questions. [Z05]

The scene refuses to reward seriousness with sudden mastery. Zaimokuza promptly makes a card-game error while enjoying his attack-name performance. The resulting comic deflation is not incidental: a moving declaration does not make him strategically reliable, and liking a form does not make every performance within it effective. A useful reconstruction must preserve this vulnerability instead of transforming him into an undiscovered genius. [Z06]

## 5. Repeated return, unfinished work, and an unresolved submission record

Later evidence supports recurrence while resisting a clean success narrative. Volume 5 has Zaimokuza offer a manuscript and claim that he always carries writing devices. Totsuka admires this; Hachiman doubts that acquiring tools means doing the work. Zaimokuza's claim establishes self-presentation and intended readiness. It does not verify continuous writing, every listed device's presence, or the manuscript's completion. Volume 6 presents an original class-play script, but showing that paper is not proof that the script was selected or staged. [Z07–Z08]

Volume 10.5 makes avoidance explicit. Zaimokuza imagines becoming an editor, understates the labor and barriers involved, and appeals to internet knowledge. When online fiction is suggested, he denies caring about ranks and reviews while describing exactly the exposure he dislikes. Hachiman mistakenly thinks he has uploaded work. Zaimokuza corrects him: he has not posted; he reacted to criticism of other people's works. This is fear before evaluation, not recovery from a newly established online rejection. [Z12]

The resolution is modestly behavioral. After further career detours Zaimokuza returns to the library, writes in a notebook, stops, fantasizes about anime direction, then resumes after Hachiman prompts him. The writing is observable; its eventual completion is not. Hachiman's image of movement through detours offers a generous interpretation of the scene, but it should not erase his own observation that Zaimokuza's energy repeatedly goes elsewhere. [Z13]

Volume 12 gives the recurrence more specificity. Zaimokuza produces a plot associated with their earlier library planning, asks Hachiman to read at home because immediate reading is embarrassing, and describes a final high-school challenge. He immediately qualifies finality: university and other stages may make different writing possible. A present time boundary can organize aspiration without determining the rest of his life. Hachiman, however, says he has never read a completed manuscript and again doubts how much writing is presently happening. Those reservations remain evidence about Hachiman's experience and appraisal, not a comprehensive inspection of Zaimokuza's private activity. [Z14–Z15]

### OPEN — prize submissions and screening failure

The supplied witnesses do not support a single unqualified submission history. Volume 7.5's autumn advice letter from `剣豪将軍` disputes the claim that ordinary Japanese guarantees passage through a newcomer prize's first screening, adding `ソースは我`. Hachiman recognizes a familiar repeat sender. The natural implication is personal experience of failure. In Volume 10.5, however, Hachiman describes a man who had not even submitted to newcomer prizes and could barely finish a manuscript. Zaimokuza's explicit correction concerns **online posting**, which is a separate channel. [Z11–Z12]

These statements may reflect comic hyperbole, narrator knowledge limits, an indirect or exaggerated letter, or a continuity inconsistency. The present evidence does not select among them. Retain the letter as character assertion, the later account as narratorial characterization, and the non-posting correction as explicit self-report. Do not claim either a documented prize rejection or no prize entry ever. A future correction requires collating the relevant editions and surrounding narrative evidence; it does not require inventing a missing submission. This tension limits a biographical detail without weakening the independently supported performance/commitment argument.

## 6. A peer world exists beyond Hachiman

Zaimokuza's early declaration that he has no friends is not an exhaustive inventory of his social life. Volume 3 shows him laughing with fellow arcade players. He reports messaging, conversation and trips outside the prefecture with people whose legal names or occupations he may not know. He finds `格ゲー仲間` more trustworthy than the word friend because it specifies what they share. Hachiman likes this functional clarity. The community has a real practice even when neither man wants to confer the broad label. [Z03]

This is more than a comforting inference that everyone secretly has friends. The relations are limited. Knowing a game together does not guarantee intimate disclosure, moral support in every domain, or professional cooperation. Zaimokuza's status at the arcade also exposes him to mockery and conflict. Nevertheless, the existence of the network prevents school awkwardness from becoming a total personality law. Volume 4 independently shows him enjoying company outside Hachiman's orbit. [Z03–Z04, Z09]

The later connection with Hatano and Sagami's younger brother is especially consequential. In Volume 13 Zaimokuza recruits people who previously belittled him and prepares a meeting with Hachiman and Yui. Hachiman does not know the history of their intervening association; the text does not supply a complete reconciliation scene. The demonstrable change is a workable relation across the old conflict, not moral purification of everyone involved. Zaimokuza can connect social worlds instead of existing only as the isolated person Hachiman tolerates. [Z17]

## 7. Reciprocal assistance has terms

Volume 8 gives the clearest reversal of the usual help direction. Zaimokuza finds Hachiman troubled in the library, offers to listen because Hachiman has heard so much of his own nonsense, and immediately agrees when asked for help. He then helps put books away, although this ordinary task disappoints his anticipation of a grand scene. Comic self-congratulation and actual reciprocity coexist. The act does not need a purified altruistic motive to count as help. [Z10]

The reciprocity is imperfectly received. Hachiman initially treats Zaimokuza as someone he can burden without feeling guilty and does not explain his own election motive fully. Zaimokuza asks why Yukino and Yui must not win. That question is a meaningful contribution to the meeting: support does not make the plan self-explanatory. His later understanding of account growth and operational suggestions demonstrate relevant familiarity, not sovereign expertise or ownership of Hachiman's aim. [Z10, Z16]

Volume 10 makes a limit explicit. Zaimokuza says he does not want exhausting or conspicuous marathon work. Hachiman withdraws the request; Zaimokuza then offers conditional cooperation for a particular ramen meal, restricts the visibility of his participation, and reserves the right to name Hachiman if criticized. This is not simple disloyalty. He can care enough to assist while refusing unlimited reputational exposure. It also does not prove that his participation validates Hachiman's separate coercion of Hayama. Authorization is local to the collaborator and the cost he accepts. [Z18]

Earlier sports-festival cooperation shows why these limits matter. Hachiman activates the swordmaster script to secure a speech whose embarrassment Zaimokuza will bear. Zaimokuza agrees and becomes invested, but Hachiman knows that the conspicuous role carries a cost. Hachiman's forecast of later regret is still a forecast; it is not proof of a private aftermath. The contrast with the later negotiated request concerns visible terms of cooperation, not a claim that all early helping was forced or all later helping fully informed. [Z19]

## 8. Social embarrassment is audience-dependent

The flamboyant coat does not mean Zaimokuza enjoys every kind of attention. Self-scripted visibility offers control; involuntary evaluation, unfamiliar girls, or being recruited into another person's spectacle can remove it. In Volume 1 he can be loud with Hachiman and almost unable to face Yukino. In Volume 12 he chooses a reader but restricts the reading occasion. These are different ways of regulating exposure, not evidence that the wish to be read was fake. [Z01, Z14]

Volume 14's karaoke makes the conditions unusually clear. Initially Zaimokuza and the gaming students are quiet and uncomfortable among Miura, Ebina and Yui. His whispered exchange with Hachiman is ordinary, economical and mutually intelligible. Once Hayama creates a shared musical bridge and makes participation less precarious, Zaimokuza takes the microphone and later joins conspicuously in the enthusiasm. The contrast rules out both permanent social incapacity and effortless sociability. He can participate when the local audience and activity become legible. [Z20–Z21]

This scene also prevents Hachiman from monopolizing embarrassment. Zaimokuza tries, ineffectively, to stop the gaming students' condescending display of expertise. Later he tells Hachiman that his own needling is the problem. Zaimokuza can detect a social failure even when he is not generally socially fluent. This does not turn him into a hidden expert in group management; his perception is local, fallible and sometimes too timid to change the interaction. [Z20–Z21]

## 9. Ordinary pleasures and material limits

The ordinary-life evidence is specific but uneven. Recurrent arcade participation, specialized peer contact, games and anime conversation, light-novel aspiration, a handheld console on the school trip, and later familiarity with a game Hachiman is playing establish an entertainment repertoire. They do not prove expertise in every anime, genre, platform or creative technique. His inability to supply non-anime and non-light-novel favorites in the hostile Volume 3 exchange concerns that occasion, not a lifetime prohibition on other reading. [Z03–Z05, Z22–Z23]

The lunch library is directly self-described as his usual place in Volume 8. Saizeriya recurs as a meeting setting, and he orders food and drinks there; Hachiman's inference that an earlier plate held focaccia with syrup must retain its inferential status. The ramen demanded in Volume 10 is a concrete negotiated selection, not proof of a favorite food or an unlimited appetite. His physical description and jokes about eating do not authorize invented medical conditions, eating routines or constant food motivation. [Z10, Z18, Z24]

Zaimokuza can refuse a teacher's summer invitation, with gaming, Comiket and deadlines given in Hiratsuka's report. That establishes a reported refusal and competing plans; it does not certify attendance at a particular event or the existence of a completed submission. His claim to carry multiple writing devices likewise remains a claim. Actual notebook writing is stronger evidence for the act than equipment ownership is for productivity. [Z07, Z09, Z13]

No sufficient source establishes a complete household routine, family income, reliable academic standing, future university, adult occupation, or eventual romantic partner. The text gives him ambitions and apprehensions rather than a forecast the analyst is entitled to finish. Volume 14.5's reference to how outsiders may not tease Iroha is principally Hachiman's generalization; it supplies no distinct post-14.5 Zaimokuza developmental scene. [Z25]

## 10. Japanese voice: stylization, ordinary speech, and chosen recipients

Zaimokuza's voice is distinctive because it changes under pressure. `我`, `貴様`, `お主`, historical elevation, announced techniques, elaborate coughs and laughter establish the theatrical repertoire. Their instability in Volume 1 is part of the joke: Hachiman comments that the pronouns themselves fluctuate. A model that gives him one impeccably maintained archaic sociolect would be less faithful than a model that allows rhetorical overreach, colloquial interruption and sudden ordinary phrasing. [Z01, Z04, Z06]

His usual `八幡` marks an established conversational route; it is not permission to give every new acquaintance an intimate form. `戸塚氏` and the introductions `秦野氏` / `相模氏` show another repertoire. Facing Yukino's direct correction produces hesitant `あ、はい` and partial polite endings. That condition is narrower than universal deference to women. Comfortable arcade interaction and the mixed-status karaoke scene make relationship, familiarity and audience at least as important as gender alone. [Z01, Z03, Z17, Z20]

Requests range from an imperative to read, through the unadorned `また読んでくれるか`, to embarrassed home-reading instructions. Refusal can be explicit and practical, as with attention and physical effort in Volume 10. In Volume 12 an inadvertent spoiler produces an ordinary apology before defensive teasing; exam-failure talk makes him abandon the grand voice and ask that the topic stop. These are diagnostic transitions, not isolated catchphrases to paste into every response. [Z02, Z14–Z15, Z18, Z23]

Hachiman's descriptions of a resonant or unusually good voice are textual narration. This study has not conducted fresh audio inspection and does not infer exact prosody or actor delivery from those descriptions. Nor does it treat Hachiman's private insults as dialogue Zaimokuza necessarily heard or as neutral psychological assessments.

## 12. Literary conclusion and separate reconstruction decision

Zaimokuza's durable object is not one unwavering profession. He wants to remain in contact with things he likes and with people who can receive them. That desire can be brave when he asks to be read again, evasive when he plans a safer career instead of exposing work, selfish when he asks others to remove consequences, reciprocal when he offers to listen, and bounded when he bargains over help. Those differences make him a character rather than a single illustrative proposition.

The performed identity is consequently neither a disease to cure nor a moral certificate. It gives Zaimokuza words, affiliations and sometimes courage; it also permits deflection and self-inflation. The series allows the same person to take detours without making every detour progress. Writing remains something he returns to, not a destiny the completed corpus proves he fulfills. Friendship similarly becomes demonstrable through particular acts without requiring the narrator to stop making jokes about the label.

**Operational assessment, separate from literary authority:** the directly reopened evidence supports an evidence-gated, bounded reconstruction candidate for school-age Zaimokuza in familiar peer, creative-feedback, assistance, gaming and mixed-school-group situations. The meaningful distinctions concern early/middle/late evidence, Hachiman versus corrective or unfamiliar audiences, performance versus ordinary register, conditional reciprocity and anticipatory evaluation fear. Admission requires the nineteen-part architecture schema, an integrated primary-first shared everyday-life slice, explicit unknowns, and source/scenario QA. The literary monograph by itself supplies none of those approvals. A post-14.5 adult-life model, all-purpose occupational expert, or private-family simulation remains unsupported.

## 14. Producer verification and remaining limits

The derived-first route included the complete Zaimokuza-specific sections in the Character State, Japanese Voice, Social Role/Performance, Authenticity and Request/Intervention ledgers; the relationship and everyday-life ledgers were checked for relevant distributed coverage.
