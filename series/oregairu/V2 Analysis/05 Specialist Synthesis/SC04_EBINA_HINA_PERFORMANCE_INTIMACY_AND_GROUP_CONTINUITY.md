# Ebina Hina: performance, intimacy, and group continuity

## 0. Argument and authority

Ebina Hina wants forms of participation that do not give other people unrestricted access to her. This is not the same as wanting no attachment. She makes things with classmates, claims friendships formed through a hobby, likes the people around her, and works to keep shared life possible. She also resists becoming romantically available or fully interpretable on somebody else's terms. Her exaggerated public manner can support those purposes while remaining a medium for actual interests. The fiction never requires a choice between entirely genuine fandom and entirely calculated disguise.

The central tension is between **selective participation and attempts to control the conditions of change**. Ebina can contribute generously to a shared activity while arranging distance from a different kind of claim. In Kyoto, preserving the present involves indirect requests and a solution whose costs spread beyond her preferred group. Later acknowledgment does not erase those costs. Later participation does not prove she has abandoned distance. A substantial account therefore needs her creative work, friendships, refusals, self-judgment and ordinary conduct alongside the crisis that makes her unusually visible to Hachiman.

This monograph elaborates Character State Ledger §CS-SEC-EBI, the Social Role/Performance and Japanese Voice ledgers, Specialists 08/11/12, the Phase-6 adverse controls and Document 13, especially PUB-SYS-06. Those derived artifacts provided retrieval hypotheses; targeted Japanese reopening tested them. Source events, Ebina's self-description, Miura's testimony, Hachiman's inference and this study's interpretation remain distinct. The source table in §10 gives exact witness routes. This literary study supplies no recovered private biography or operational simulation authority, and does not supersede frozen readings or checkpoints.

## 1. A hobby can make a world

During the Volume 4 discussion of Tsurumi Rumi's exclusion, Ebina proposes a life organized around interests that can extend beyond school. Events and shared hobbies might lead to other acquaintances and another place to belong. She explicitly says that BL helped her make friends. Her subsequent attempt to recruit Yukino into that enthusiasm returns the exchange to comedy, and Hayama and Miura cut it short. The sequence nonetheless gives her proposal independent content: exclusion from one school arrangement need not exhaust a person's possible social world. [SC04-E01]

Ebina does not reveal the identities of those hobby friends or a complete extracurricular history. Hachiman infers that she is drawing on experience; that is broader than the direct claim that she made friends through BL. The distinction matters. There is positive evidence for an interest with social consequences, but no license to invent a convention schedule, private community, collection or creative career.

Hachiman soon wonders whether her conspicuous enthusiasm functions as a defense that discourages boys. His formulation is explicitly speculative, and his premise that a “real” fan would conceal the interest is part of his own suspicious reasoning. The observation that boys can be put off has some support in Tobe's account; the conclusion that conspicuous enjoyment must therefore be fake does not follow. Pleasure, a recognizable social role and protection from unwanted interpretations can coexist. [SC04-E02]

Nor does distance mean automatic deference to the group's center. When Hayama suggests discussions to resolve Rumi's problem, Ebina firmly warns that apparently agreeable girls may resume exclusion afterward. Her warning interrupts an optimistic procedure with a concrete concern about what happens outside the supervised conversation. It is her assessment, including a sweeping claim about girls, not the novel's universal account of female nature. It also does not establish that she personally suffered the same history. What it establishes is willingness to contradict Hayama when she thinks his proposed interaction overlooks continuing social pressure. [SC04-E01]

## 2. Creative work, direction, and imperfect authority

The cultural-festival musical makes Ebina responsible for a collective object. She writes the scenario, becomes director and handles staging. Hachiman's comic horror at her BL-inflected Little Prince initially dominates the presentation, but other classmates have to consider whether the proposal can work in public. Ebina says that she distinguishes material suitable for this setting from material she would not present there. Hayama proposes strengthening the comedy, and the production proceeds. [SC04-E03]

This is contextual judgment rather than absence of performance. Ebina can enjoy provocative implications while adjusting the form in which they become a class project. Conversely, her statement that she understands the distinction does not certify every choice she makes. The initial proposal confuses classmates; casting produces resistance. Her authority creates participation and also exposes others to an image they did not choose. An account that elevates her into an ideally considerate artist would lose the scene's discomfort.

Hachiman objects to acting because he already has festival-committee work. With Hayama's support, that concrete workload constraint is accepted and the casting changes. Hayama's own attempt to use the revision to escape his role fails. One accepted refusal therefore demonstrates responsiveness without proving an invariant rule of consent. Ebina is neither an irresistible manipulator nor someone whose enthusiasm never imposes on others. [SC04-E03]

Her practical work continues beyond the pitch. She assigns Hachiman a limited reception job, directs attention to the posted schedule when he lacks information, and assesses the audience situation. Before the performance she calls Kawasaki into the circle, recognizing that making the costumes makes her part of the production. The exchange jokes about responsibility and brings Kawasaki forward despite her initial reluctance. It is evidence of inclusion and recognition, with a small element of social pressure still visible. The person called is Kawasaki; Hachiman's separate response to Yui's invitation must not be substituted for it. The successful audience turnout confirms an implemented project, not a professional artistic qualification. [SC04-E04]

The autumn sports-festival material in Volume 6.5 adds two useful controls. Ebina asks why she has been recruited, accepts partly because she is available and apparently valued, and clarifies what kind of competition the committee wants. Later she distinguishes expecting Hayama to cooperate from actually asking him. His probable willingness does not settle the matter without an offer. This is direct counterevidence to a total account in which Ebina only manages other people indirectly and never recognizes their assent. Her work with Kawasaki and Yukino on the festival clothing also supports collaborative competence, without identifying every garment as her own production. These scenes precede Kyoto; they cannot be presented as lessons learned from its aftermath. [SC04-E05]

## 3. Friendship with room to withdraw

Miura's Kyoto account is indispensable because it supplies a friend's remembered experience rather than only Hachiman's theory. Miura contrasts Yui's accommodation with Ebina's apparently opposite way of fitting in: Ebina establishes a manner the group learns to accept. Miura also recalls repeatedly pressing introductions because she misunderstood Ebina's refusal as shyness. Ebina's smiling withdrawal made clear that the pressure could cost the relationship itself. Miura now says she does not ask much about Ebina's private life. [SC04-E07]

This testimony changes what social ease means. Ebina's lively behavior does not make her generally available for whatever intimacy her friends consider appropriate. Miura has learned a boundary partly by getting it wrong. Her concern that Ebina might leave also shows that Ebina is valued for shared life, not simply tolerated as a joke. The group's warmth is real enough that losing one person would change it.

Hachiman extends Miura's testimony into a stronger psychology: Ebina would destroy or abandon what she has before accepting the risks of preserving it through negotiation. That is a plausible interpretation of the danger, not direct knowledge of her interior or an observed universal history. It should not swallow the evidence of her continuing work, affectionate company and later admission that she likes the present people. A capacity to leave can protect a legitimate boundary; it can also make others afraid to raise anything difficult. Which function dominates depends on the specific demand and available alternatives.

The right question is consequently not whether the public Ebina is false and the withdrawn Ebina real. Both are actions of the same person under different claims. A friend can accept her jokes, share her projects and matter deeply to her while remaining outside a private account she does not want to give. That arrangement has limits, but privacy is not itself a failure of friendship.

## 4. Kyoto: what she asks, what Hachiman does

Ebina's Service Club request contains more than an opaque comic diversion. She describes the group as changing and says she wants everyone to remain friendly as before. Male-pairing jokes frame that concern, but they do not wholly conceal the declared value of continuity. At this point Hachiman is tempted to infer a hidden meaning, then recognizes his own habit of overreading. The later conversation confirms that the surface commission did not exhaust her objective. It does not make every earlier inference certain at the time it was made. [SC04-E06/E09]

Several responsibilities must remain separate. Tobe wants to confess. Ebina wants freedom from the resulting romantic reconfiguration. Hayama wants to avoid losing a valued group; Miura fears losing Ebina's participation. Hachiman aggregates these different wishes and chooses the false confession that interrupts Tobe. Ebina is surprised, then delivers a categorical present boundary: she does not intend to date anyone, whoever confesses, and leaves. She is an acting participant at that moment, not merely the object of someone else's solution. [SC04-E08]

Her response does not promise future availability to Tobe. The temporal `今は` limits the statement's declared scope; it is not an invitation to wait until she becomes accessible. Nor does it establish a permanent orientation or a complete philosophy of romance. She need not provide the private reason somebody else would prefer in order to make the refusal meaningful.

The problem with the intervention is therefore not that Ebina owes Tobe intimacy or a compulsory personal explanation. The V07 deep reading's adversarial Test D explicitly rejects that entitlement. The problem concerns the third-party means by which competing intentions are displaced: Tobe's intended speech is preempted, the generalized answer is elicited through a false target, and Hachiman's companions confront the costs of his method without having agreed to it. Respecting Ebina's boundary does not require treating that arrangement as the only possible way to respect it.

Afterward, Ebina thanks Hachiman and says he understood what she meant. This confirms her protective objective and relief. Hachiman supplies the precise paraphrase about preventing Tobe's confession; the text does not show Ebina commissioning his exact tactic in advance. His inference that she probably also consulted Hayama remains marked as inference. Benefit, an indirectly framed request and retrospective thanks establish meaningful involvement. They do not make her the sole author of every decision or consequence. [SC04-E09]

## 5. Self-dislike and responsibility after success

The Kyoto conversation complicates the result from Ebina's side. She says that a relationship would not work with her as she presently is, joins a claim about failed understanding to an explicit wish not to be understood, and uses `腐ってる` across the border between fan vocabulary and self-judgment. The omitted argument in the first understanding clause leaves its direction uncertain; the unwillingness to be understood is explicit. The line does not authorize an analyst to diagnose her, declare her objectively corrupt, or invent an earlier injury that supposedly explains everything. Its importance lies in what she claims about the terms on which she can participate now. [SC04-E09]

She also says that she likes her present self, surroundings and the people who stay with her. The concluding self-dislike must be read against that positive attachment. She is not simply declaring that nothing matters. A strong interpretation is that the wish to keep something she values exposes an uncomfortable judgment of how she is keeping it. The scene supports that tension; it does not settle a single exhaustive cause of her self-dislike. Her reference to the situation being something she has not had for a while hints at a past contrast without supplying its biography.

Volume 9 gives the question of cost another form. When Hachiman suggests she help Tobe, Ebina first turns the suggestion into a pairing joke, then directly calls it strange and redirects attention toward Hachiman's own relationships. Her boundary survives; she is not obligated to provide romantic encouragement because the earlier crisis was avoided. She then quietly apologizes and asks whether the awkwardness around him might have followed from Kyoto. [SC04-E10]

The apology matters because she notices a possible consequence outside the group whose continuity she wanted. It prevents the reading that she calmly externalizes harm without recognizing anything. It is also limited: a private question and apology do not constitute a complete account of the intervention, a negotiated repair with every affected person, or a new commitment to transparency. Hachiman says the result is his choice rather than her responsibility. His ownership of the tactic is significant, but it is not an impersonal verdict absolving every participant of every part of the process.

At the end Hachiman suspects another small lie in her assurance about her own group's wellbeing. The inference is plausible within their history, yet remains his inference. Ebina's small adjustment of her glasses does not independently prove concealment. The passage permits a troubled continuity without giving readers an omniscient inventory of what is wrong. Neither apology nor continued social life closes the question.

## 6. Looking through a camera, then joining the photograph

The beach photography in Volume 13 joins practical contribution to a renewed conversation about distance. Ebina uses the camera, directs the subjects, refers to photographic concepts and takes pictures with her phone. Hachiman reports the technical terms loosely; they support a usable repertoire here, not professional training or equipment ownership. When he suggests that she should also be photographed, she initially deflects toward Yui and Miura and returns to pairing jokes. [SC04-E13]

Her later reference to love, romance and sex as troublesome is direct speech, but still situated speech. She says that people without that kind of interest in each other can discuss certain things. The immediate relation is with Hachiman. It is not a declaration that she lacks every form of sexual or romantic desire toward everyone. The scene supports an interpretation of non-romantic distance as enabling candor without settling an orientation.

Ebina explicitly distinguishes Hachiman from herself. She claims that ordinary observation reveals something about his situation, then acknowledges partial information from Yui and says she does not know why matters reached their present form. These limitations are part of her intelligence. Her suggestion that there might have been an easier way is a challenge from a particular position, not a revealed authorial answer. Hachiman finds their outlooks close but not identical and declines to equate his conclusion with hers. The relation allows recognition without making either person the other's complete explanation.

The end of the scene is an important counterexample to an attractive visual metaphor. Ebina is not condemned to remain behind the camera while other people inhabit the image. Yui and Miura call her to join them; she responds and runs over. Earlier evasion of the subject position was local. She can choose to enter an affectionate shared picture without thereby opening every part of herself to interpretation. The sequence makes selected visibility an action, rather than a categorical inability to be seen.

## 7. Ordinary sociability and an ending without cure

Smaller scenes prevent Kyoto from becoming Ebina's entire biography. In Volume 10, Yui reports that Ebina is probably taking humanities; Ebina herself jokes about multiplying element symbols when recommending sciences. The joke may also divert Tobe's attempt to follow her course. It is not evidence of chemistry expertise, and the tentative educational information remains Yui's report. Participation in the Volume 11 chocolate-making event likewise does not demonstrate a new romantic opening. Hachiman himself notes the absence of a clear basis for that conclusion and offers accompanying Miura as another possibility. [SC04-E11/E12]

Volume 14 gives firmer ordinary-life content. Speaking with Hatano and Sagami's younger brother, Ebina says that she also plays board games fairly often. She asks questions, mentions familiar examples, and listens without escalating the embarrassment when the two become intensely explanatory. Their later list of Catan, Scotland Yard, TRPGs and design ambitions belongs to them. It cannot become her list of favorite games or her intended profession. [SC04-E14]

Her conversational labor is real but not magical. The encounter remains awkward, and Hachiman is uneasy about the smile he cannot read. Ebina can be considerate without making every recipient comfortable or converting every conversation into a success. Her public competence is best described through such particular acts, not by ranking her as the group's secretly perfect social intelligence.

The true post-mainline material used here is Volume 14.5 Story5. Ebina continues to talk with Hayama and helps fill conversational gaps around Hachiman in the changed classroom arrangement. He has learned something about relating to both of them, yet is still anxious when alone with her. His uncertainty is evidence about his experience; it does not demonstrate that she is actively threatening him. Ordinary coexistence survives incomplete understanding. The ending supplies continuing participation, not a final confession, breakup, cure or transparent revelation. [SC04-E15]

## 8. Japanese voice and relationship-conditioned distance

Ebina's textual self-reference includes `私` and `わたし`; orthographic difference should not be turned into a separate psychological persona. Her friendly `はろはろ～` greeting recurs, but is not compulsory in every exchange. Familiar address includes `結衣`, `優美子`, `とべっち`, `隼人くん` and `ヒキタニくん`. Public committee interaction permits courteous acknowledgment and task clarification. [SC04-E01/E05/E06/E09/E13]

Comic turns can combine coupling terms, puns on `腐`, excessive hypothetical elaboration and quick reclassification of an objection. Serious turns can be shorter and firmer: an immediate contradiction, an explicit boundary, a quiet apology. The comedy and seriousness can occur within one exchange. The first is not automatically false and the second is not unrestricted interior truth.

In Volume 13, `比企谷くん` appears in the serious claim that Hachiman differs from her, while nearby joking turns retain `ヒキタニくん`. The distinction supports a contextual repertoire. It does not establish a single irreversible naming progression or prove every earlier use was an innocent mistake. Her knowledge disclaimer in the same conversation is as important to fidelity as her apparently perceptive claim. [SC04-E13]

Miura can redirect her, Yui is an easy familiar partner, Hayama is someone she can contradict and whose assent she says must be requested, and Hachiman permits a particular kind of candid distance. The game-club juniors receive ordinary questions rather than the full repertoire of sexualized jokes. These observations justify relationship conditioning. They do not supply unobserved family, employment, intimate-partner or severe-crisis registers. This monograph verifies textual wording and pragmatic context, not fresh listening, vocal delivery or prosody.

## 11. Reconstruction decision and literary conclusion

**Operational reconstruction: DEFERRED.** Ebina has distinctive prospective material for performance, task collaboration, friendship, refusal and Hachiman-conditioned distance. This literary monograph has not completed the required nineteen-part operational specification, a primary-first shared everyday-life slice, systematic era/counterpart register matrix or novel-scenario/source audit. Public comedy and the Kyoto boundary cannot substitute for those gates. Family, romantic reciprocity, severe conflict and broader private routines also remain thin; they must be bounded rather than invented if a future school-social model is attempted. This is a deferral of an uncompleted assessment, not a finding that no useful bounded model could qualify.

The literary result is complete within its narrower responsibility. Ebina's desire to preserve the group matters because she participates in something valuable: shared jokes, work, pictures, ordinary company and friendships whose loss would change her life. She is capable of generosity and indirect control, insight and self-protective refusal, enjoyment and self-dislike. None of these capacities cancels the others. Her autonomy does not depend on becoming fully legible, and her privacy does not exempt the means by which she manages other people's choices from criticism.

The most defensible longitudinal account is not a cure from performance into sincerity. Earlier creative work already shows active participation; Kyoto reveals a sharper cost; Volume 9 adds acknowledgment; later scenes show contribution with continuing limits. Ebina remains someone who wants to stay without becoming available on every term. The novel gives readers enough to take that wish seriously and enough to question its implementation, while leaving the person larger than either a comic mask or an explanation of Hachiman.
