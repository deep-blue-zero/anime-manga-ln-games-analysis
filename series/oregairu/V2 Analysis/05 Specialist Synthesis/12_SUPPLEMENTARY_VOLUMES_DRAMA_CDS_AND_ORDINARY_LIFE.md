# 12 — Supplementary Volumes, Drama CDs, and Ordinary Life
## What Oregairu looks like when crisis is not allowed to monopolize meaning

## Executive thesis

The supplementary corpus does **not** contain a secret second Oregairu hiding behind the fourteen numbered novels. Its most important function is more rigorous and, in some ways, more consequential: it tests whether interpretations derived from crisis survive when the characters return to ordinary work, food, jokes, errands, school events, family routines, gifts, photographs, birthdays, study plans, institutional paperwork, and repeated assumptions that they will see one another again.

The governing conclusion is:

> **Oregairu's supplementary material is strongest when it lowers dramatic temperature without lowering evidentiary value. It shows that relation is not constituted only by confession, rupture, sacrifice, or crisis. It is also constituted by repeated presence, future assumptions, small requests, transparent pretexts, shared work, revisable routines, ordinary irritation, and the transfer of practices from one person to another.**

That conclusion depends on strict source control. The corpus contains several materially different kinds of “supplementary” object:

- commercially published decimal-numbered books by Watari Wataru;
- older anime-disc bonus prose later selectively reworked into a commercial volume;
- a Watari-scripted drama CD whose story chronology is far later than the book that physically contains it;
- prose rewrites that can govern exact wording where an audio source governs delivery;
- four official anthologies containing both Watari-authored stories and stories by guest authors;
- scan-backed OCR whose searchable text is not itself quotation authority;
- author afterwords, songs, illustrations, and packaging that can frame interpretation without becoming diegetic fact.

These cannot be flattened into a single evidentiary tier.

The most important methodological rule of this document is therefore:

> **official ≠ authorial ≠ mainline-compatible ≠ canon-governing.**

A guest-author anthology story may be officially licensed and aesthetically revealing while remaining unable to establish what Yukino “really” thinks in Watari's mainline. A Watari-authored anthology story may supply strong post-ending evidence while still remaining supplementary rather than retroactively rewriting the uncertainty of Volume 14. A drama recording may provide excellent prosodic evidence while a prose witness remains the safer authority for exact Japanese wording. A commercial 6.5 may be the current canonical retail text while older 6.25/6.50/6.75 booklets remain historically broader ancestors whose omitted material cannot simply be presumed present.

Once those distinctions are preserved, the supplementary corpus produces a coherent full-series contribution.

The decimal volumes reveal that **ordinary time is a longitudinal variable**. Volume 7.5 shows that belonging accumulated through repeated work before Hachiman could name it. Volume 10.5 shows that an undefined relationship can nevertheless be inhabited and remembered. The post-Volume-9 Christmas coda shows that one difficult conversation can become `また明日`, gifts, leisure, and future-facing habit without resolving incompatible desire. Volume 14.5 shows that the ending survives only by becoming non-sacred: people may stay, leave, return, join late, inherit practices, revise the institution, and continue to make mistakes.

The post-Volume-14 Watari anthology stories reinforce the same model. Yukino can possess a family and vocational future not reducible to romance. Yui can remain in love, grieve an unequal answer, practice toward someone she still cares about, and remain a socially complete daughter and friend. Komachi can share Hachiman's last school year without turning sibling closeness into a pathology of dependence. Iroha can use an excuse whose function is openly understood without making the excuse false. The recreated Service Club can continue after romantic choice while remaining asymmetrical, playful, occasionally jealous, operationally useful, and institutionally transmissible.

The compact Phase-5 terminal proposition is:

> **The mainline tells us what the characters choose under pressure; the strongest supplements tell us whether those choices can be lived without pressure.**

They can—but only as revisable practices, not as certificates of purity or permanence.

# 1. Why supplementary material requires its own method

## 1.1 “.5” is a publishing label, not a chronology

The four commercial decimal volumes do not have one formal function.

Volume 6.5 is a revised commercial reconstruction built from an earlier bonus-publication genealogy and contains a major sports-festival through-line plus a Christmas prose/drama coda whose story chronology belongs after Volume 9. Volume 7.5 is an anthology published after Volume 7 whose pieces occupy several earlier moments, including material immediately after Volume 3 and before Volume 4. Volume 10.5, despite its side-story form, is a secure late-January-to-February sequence immediately after Volume 10. Volume 14.5 is a curated collection whose five stories occupy three distinct chronology bands: post-Volume-9 New Year, late Volume 14 after the joint prom, and true post-mainline third-year ordinary life.

A decimal point therefore cannot determine evidence routing.

The rule already established by the sequential readings remains mandatory:

> **publication binding does not override explicit story chronology.**

This matters because supplementary books are precisely where retrospective publication and internal chronology diverge most often. Treating “7.5” as mechanically after Volume 7 would make early Hachiman behavior respond to events he has not experienced. Treating all of 14.5 as a post-ending epilogue would import the final romantic choice into New Year scenes where it does not yet exist. Treating the Volume-6.5 Christmas coda as early sports-festival characterization would move a post-Volume-9 relationship state backward by several novels.

Chronology control is not archival fussiness. It protects literary ambiguity.

## 1.2 Supplementary authority has two dimensions

Every supplemental object needs at least two separate authority judgments.

The first is **textual/source-form authority**:

- Is the text a clean publisher-derived Japanese EPUB?
- Is it a scan-backed OCR package whose images govern wording?
- Is the source an audio performance whose transcript is separately available?
- Is the evidence song paratext, afterword, illustration, or packaging rather than narrative prose?

The second is **narrative authority**:

- Is it Watari-authored mainline prose?
- Watari-authored official supplementary prose?
- a script by Watari later rewritten into prose?
- a guest-author official derivative?
- paratextual commentary?

These dimensions can cross. Anthology 02's Watari story has relatively high narrative authority but qualified quotation authority because the current searchable witness is OCR derived. A guest story in Anthology 01 has clean quotation-grade text but lower mainline narrative authority because it is deliberately another author's interpretation.

## 1.3 Working authority classes for this document

This synthesis uses four functional classes.

**Class M — commercial Watari mainline/supplementary volume authority.** The commercial decimal volumes are part of the canonical Japanese novel corpus, but their individual stories still obey chronology routing.

**Class A — Watari-authored official supplementary authority.** This includes chronology-audited Watari anthology stories and the Watari-authored/scripted Christmas coda. These may strengthen or extend mature claims, especially after the mainline ending, but cannot be used to make an earlier prospective scene know its future.

**Class B — official guest-author derivative authority.** These stories are useful for franchise reception, professional-peer interpretation, genre play, and comparative characterization. They cannot override Watari's mainline characterization or settle contested character interiority.

**Class P — paratextual authority.** Afterwords, lyrics, packaging, illustrations, and performance choices may support formal, historical, or reception claims. They do not become fictional fact merely because they are official.

A source-quality qualifier is then added where needed: clean text, fixed-page scan, OCR-navigation-only, or audio/prosody.

The current collection manifest records twenty-four usable Japanese EPUB witnesses at the title root: eighteen commercial mainline books including 6.5, 7.5, 10.5, and 14.5; two commercial `結` alternate-story volumes; and four official anthologies. The mainline V2 project governs itself through the eighteen-book mainline set. `結`, `Shin`, and `Another` are not allowed to define mainline state in this document.

## 2.2 The 6.75 problem: genealogy without invention

The architecture explicitly requires 6.75 to be addressed. The correct V2 response is **not** to manufacture a nonexistent `V06_75` artifact.

The collection manifest records that 6.25, 6.50, and 6.75 were anime-disc bonus novellas later collected or reworked into commercial Volume 6.5. The V6.5 source audit further establishes that the commercial volume is **selective rather than exhaustive**: it reconstructs the sports-festival through-line but omits at least some Komachi, Yui, and Kawasaki material known from the ancestor booklets.

That produces two archival consequences.

First, the commercial 6.5 is the current governing retail text for the material it actually contains. Second, the unavailable 6.25/6.50/6.75 booklets remain publication ancestors whose missing pieces cannot be silently inferred from the commercial edition.

The original 6.75 afterword's proposition that failure may be forgotten, accustomed to, or recovered from but cannot be erased was preserved in the earlier V2 source audit as historically valuable ancestor paratext. It remains useful as provenance. It is **not** the commercial 6.5 afterword and therefore must not be cited as though the current commercial book said it.

This is a useful miniature of the entire supplemental method:

> **later incorporation can preserve a lineage without erasing differences between versions.**

That principle is formally similar to the series' own interest in institutional transmission, but the archival point should stand independently of the thematic analogy.

# 3. Volume 6.5: aftermath without purification

## 3.1 What the commercial volume contributes

Volume 6 ended with Hachiman's cultural-festival intervention demonstrating that successful problem-solving can become domination: he reads the institution accurately, assigns himself the disposable role, allocates reputational costs, and produces an outcome without giving everyone meaningful authorship over the method.

The commercial 6.5 sports-festival material does not answer that failure with a grand ethical conversion. Its contribution is smaller and more convincing.

Sagami remains Sagami. Hachiman remains capable of contempt, abstraction, and tactical social reading. Yukino remains efficient and severe. Yui remains socially connective. Meguri remains an institutional leader rather than a moral oracle. The school does not become an ideal community.

What changes is **practice**.

Sagami can participate after failure without the story pretending the cultural festival never happened. Meguri can recognize labor without relabeling failure as success. Yukino can distribute work rather than carry everything herself. Yui can make social reality usable for people whose analytic intelligence does not automatically give them ordinary social fluency. Hachiman can observe that affective action outside his design may have more causal force than the clean plan he prefers.

The V6.5 closing thesis therefore remains important to the final synthesis:

> **growth can consist of learning and repeating a new action without rewriting the past, purifying motive, or replacing the person.**

This is an early version of the later anti-purity architecture. It helps explain why the series can ultimately preserve Hachiman's cynicism, Yui's accommodation, Yukino's exacting standards, and Iroha's performance rather than demanding personality replacement.

## 3.2 Ordinary coexistence can be an achievement

The sports-festival aftermath also supplies a modest social endpoint that later becomes structurally important: two people who genuinely dislike one another may learn to pass without constantly reopening the wound.

That is not reconciliation in the maximal sense. It is not confession, forgiveness, friendship, or restored innocence. It is a livable social distance.

Oregairu repeatedly prefers this sort of qualified success to total emotional conversion. The same logic later appears in post-choice triadic continuation: Yui does not need romantic equivalence for her history to remain real; Yukino and Yui do not need rivalry to vanish for friendship to survive; Hachiman does not need full interpretive certainty before acting.

Volume 6.5 therefore contributes an early **distance ethic**:

> **not every repaired relationship becomes close; sometimes repair means that the past stops governing every future encounter.**

## 3.3 Masks and performance before the `本物` vocabulary dominates

Commercial 6.5 contains no governing `本物`/`偽物` opposition, yet it is highly relevant to the later authenticity problem. The volume repeatedly distinguishes behavior from essential identity and social masking from moral falsity.

That matters because the full-series authenticity synthesis must not read later `本物` language backward as though the early novels already possess a purity theory. Volume 6.5 instead supplies an early counterexample: a social performance can be instrumental, face-saving, or strategic without making every feeling carried through it counterfeit.

The later V2 conclusion—that performance is a morally neutral technology whose ethical status depends on authorship, answerability, and revision—has roots here before the series gives the problem its most famous vocabulary.

# 4. Volume 7.5: retrospective ordinary infrastructure

## 4.1 The book sharpens backward

Volume 7.5 is the clearest proof that publication order and story order must remain separate.

It was published after the Kyoto volume, but its pieces range across substantially earlier states. The source's own calendar and internal markers establish, among other placements, a birthday bonus immediately after Volume 3, summer material before Volume 4, autumn pieces around and after the cultural festival, and pre-Kyoto material.

The correct reading is therefore dual.

For characters inside the scenes, later Kyoto knowledge does not exist. For the historical reader, Kyoto has already happened, so the anthology can retrospectively expose the infrastructure that the Kyoto crisis damaged.

This produces one of the best publication-time formulas in the project:

> **story-time foreshadowing can be publication-time retrospective connective tissue.**

## 4.2 Belonging accumulates before it is named

The central ordinary-life contribution of 7.5 is that the Service Club's importance does not originate in one great crisis. It accumulates through:

- food and cooking arguments;
- writing and editing tasks;
- anonymous advice;
- magazine work;
- invitations;
- phone calls;
- group photographs;
- hair styling;
- shared games;
- studying;
- small errands;
- repeated use of the same room and seats.

The characters do not yet possess a stable language for what the club is. Hachiman may still treat an invitation as suspicious, convert desire into comic denial, or think of social ritual as `ままごと`. Yet the institutional repetition is already creating an environment that is particular rather than interchangeable.

This matters to the later triad synthesis because it prevents a crisis-only account of attachment. The club is not important solely because the three save one another at dramatic peaks. It matters because it becomes the location in which **ordinary co-presence becomes expected**.

## 4.3 Indirection can preserve rather than destroy agency

Volume 7.5 repeatedly uses low-stakes frames—anonymous submissions, games, jokes, pseudonyms, punishment pretexts, collaborative tasks, invitations—to make expression possible.

That is analytically important because Oregairu's mature ethics cannot simply prefer direct speech over mediated speech. The anthology shows why indirection exists. It can lower the cost of asking, permit a refusal without catastrophe, distribute embarrassment, or create a reason to remain together long enough for more specific preference to emerge.

The failure condition is not “the request was indirect.” It is that the frame begins silently deciding the other person's answer.

This distinction anticipates mature Iroha. Her post-ending pretexts can be transparent enough that speaker and listener understand what the excuse is doing. Hachiman's Kyoto false confession, by contrast, is a unilateral script whose strategic success depends on other people absorbing roles they did not meaningfully choose.

The supplementary corpus therefore contributes an important formal-ethical rule:

> **pretext is compatible with authenticity when it lowers expressive risk without confiscating the addressee's answer.**

## 4.4 7.5 also prevents a comforting origin story

The same volume that reveals ordinary belonging also reveals that Hachiman's coercive social-engineering toolkit exists early. Audience construction, reputational pressure, incomplete disclosure, role assignment, and willingness to make collaborators bear costs are not inventions of the cultural festival or Kyoto.

That is important for character continuity. The series does not present Hachiman as a pure early loner later corrupted by self-sacrifice. Multiple mechanisms coexist from the start: withdrawal, desire denial, strong social diagnosis, attraction to efficient indirect intervention, punitive impulses, and genuine capacity for ordinary attachment.

The anthology's ordinary-life softness therefore does not sentimentalize the early club. It makes the later crises more intelligible by showing both what had been built and what methods were already capable of damaging it.

# 5. The Volume-6.5 Christmas drama/prose coda: aftercare after `本物`

## 5.1 Chronology controls publication container

The Christmas drama material is physically associated with Volume 6.5 but explicitly takes place immediately after Volume 9. V2 therefore quarantined it from prospective 6.5 characterization and reopened it only after the Volume-9 checkpoint.

This routing decision is one of the most important source-control corrections in the entire project.

The coda demonstrates what happens **after** Hachiman's `俺は、本物が欲しい` request but **before** the later dependency crisis. It is the practical aftercare of the Volume-9 repair.

## 5.2 `また明日` is small because it needs to be

The coda's most consequential evidence is not a confession. It is the recovery of ordinary futurity.

Hachiman can say `また明日`. The trio can move from work to voluntary leisure. An initial scheduling problem can be negotiated rather than interpreted as rejection. Gifts can be chosen without perfect self-knowledge. Yukino and Yui can receive the same act differently. Yui can imagine a relationship that does not need to end merely because a ledger of reciprocal obligation has no clean stopping point.

That is what the mainline `本物` conversation needed to prove: not that everyone now understood one another, but that a different procedure could become **habitable**.

The coda therefore strengthens Checkpoint C by showing:

> **answerability can produce tomorrow.**

## 5.3 The coda does not solve the triangle

Its ordinary warmth is powerful precisely because the text refuses to convert it into route confirmation.

Hachiman still hides personal desire inside debt, gift theory, and practical purpose. He still doubts that relation can continue indefinitely. Yukino's use of a gift is a chosen response, not proof of a fully articulated romantic contract. Yui's continuity wish is meaningful but not a direct claim of reciprocal romantic success. The closing narration preserves wishes that may not be fulfilled.

The coda is therefore evidence against two opposite reductions.

It is not “just comedy,” because it shows that the repaired club can live outside crisis. It is also not a secret romantic solution, because differentiated desire remains unresolved.

## 5.4 Audio and prose have different jobs

The Watari prose rewrite governs exact searchable Japanese wording in the current V2 infrastructure. The drama performance remains valuable for:

- timing;
- pauses;
- overlap;
- embarrassment;
- comic escalation;
- warmth;
- delivery differences among Hachiman, Yukino, Yui, Hiratsuka, Totsuka, and others.

That material should be classified as **prosodic evidence**, not silently substituted for a textual quote. Song material such as `君とMerry Christmas` belongs to package-level emotional framing and cannot be treated as a literal transcript of a character's private psychology.

This distinction is especially important after Document 11: voice is evidence about stance and performance, but voice does not by itself determine truth.

# 6. Volume 10.5: an undefined relation can still be lived

## 6.1 Unlike 7.5, 10.5 is a coherent continuation

Volume 10.5 contains four stories linked across late January and February after Volume 10. Its internal chronology is secure enough that all four may update the post-Volume-10 state.

That formal difference from 7.5 matters. “Side-story collection” does not imply “retrospective miscellany.”

Watari's afterword explicitly foregrounds `日常`: days without large incidents still contain emotional movement, conflict, and later memory. This is useful paratextual confirmation of the volume's project, but the fiction itself already demonstrates the point.

## 6.2 Ordinary work becomes a truth test

The post-Volume-9 club has reopened. Volume 10 has shown that `本物` does not prevent Hachiman from coercively pushing when another person's uncertainty frustrates him. Volume 10.5 then asks whether the relation can function in lower-stakes environments.

It can.

People can collaborate without identical motives. Iroha can be strategic and still genuinely responsible for outcomes. Hachiman can recognize that he does not fully know another person's interiority. Shared work does not require a settled definition of the relationship. Care can include the possibility that the person one helps will fail.

The photograph becomes a particularly strong formal object. A photograph preserves **co-presence without forcing one interpretation of the co-presence**. The same image can later carry different meanings for Hachiman, Yukino, Yui, Komachi, or anyone else in the frame.

That is almost a material model of the V2 methodology itself: shared fact does not imply identical interpretation.

## 6.3 Finitude enters ordinary consciousness

Volume 10.5 also makes remaining time explicit.

Hachiman begins thinking about:

- his eventual graduation;
- Komachi entering high school;
- future club membership;
- the household changing;
- photographs that may become painful memories;
- the fact that the current configuration cannot remain unchanged forever.

The preservation problem therefore shifts.

The question is no longer simply “How do we keep this?” It becomes:

> **How do we inhabit this honestly while knowing the present form cannot remain?**

That question will become the governing post-ending problem of Volume 14.5.

# 7. Volume 14.5: the ending survives by refusing to become sacred

## 7.1 14.5 is three chronology bands, not one epilogue

The five stories occupy three distinct strata.

Stories 1 and 2 occur at New Year immediately after Volume 9 and before Volume 10. Story 3 occurs after the joint prom during the late-Volume-14 spring-break envelope. Only Stories 4 and 5 belong to the true post-mainline third-year state.

That distinction prevents the book from being used to make Volume 14 easier than it was. Early New Year comedy cannot be converted into proof that the final romantic outcome was already settled. The festival story cannot simplify the bridge or Yui's loss. Only the final two stories may update post-ending state.

## 7.2 Story 3: finite ordinary time becomes a reason to participate

The festival story occurs after Hachiman and Yukino's mutual choice but before the full new-term club configuration stabilizes.

Its importance is temporal rather than certifying. Hachiman recognizes that festivals end and generalizes that ordinary days are also finite. The crucial movement is behavioral: he does not remain outside the group turning finitude into melancholic observation. When they call, he answers and joins.

The boy who has often protected himself by standing outside social ritual begins to treat impermanence as a reason to enter it.

## 7.3 Stories 4–5: enormous promises become small procedures

The true post-ending material translates Volume 14's enormous language of lives and futures into repeatable ordinary acts.

Hachiman and Yukino can discuss study environments without demanding identical academic trajectories. Yukino can prefer proximity without lowering her ambitions merely to manufacture sameness. A one-month relational marker can matter without becoming a compulsory ceremonial test. Iroha can continue to flirt, maneuver, and make excuses without the story pretending romantic choice erased her social agency. Yui can remain present after a real loss and continue to shape the club's future. Komachi can inherit institutional standing without inheriting an obligation to reproduce the original trio.

This is the correct scale for testing the ending.

A relationship claim such as `あなたの人生を、私にください` is not validated by repeating maximal language every day. It becomes meaningful when the participants can negotiate ordinary futures in ways that retain separate capability and answerability.

## 7.4 `神聖視` is the final anti-certification device

Hachiman's recognition that he has begun to `神聖視` the Service Club's present configuration is the most important ordinary-life correction in the post-ending corpus.

He rejects the temptation to treat the existing form as perfect because it has survived so much. Their relation was never perfect. It was distorted, damaged, repaired, widened, and remains capable of further mistakes.

This protects the V2 ending from a new form of purity theory.

The series does not end by saying:

> we have finally found the perfect form and must preserve it.

It says something closer to:

> **what mattered must be carried forward in forms that remain open to change.**

## 7.5 Succession is transmission, not replication

Komachi receives the club's name, room, history, mug, advice, and tea practice. She does not receive a script requiring her to become Yukino or recreate the exact Hachiman/Yukino/Yui geometry.

Iroha can belong without formal membership. New people may appear late. Cups can change. Tea can remain. Joint-prom documentation can become student-council institutional memory. Yukino can teach a practice without claiming permanent ownership over what the institution must mean.

The terminal temporal model is therefore:

> **continuity through successive acts whose distant outcome cannot be guaranteed in advance.**

# 8. Official anthology story-level authority audit

The four official novel anthologies are especially useful because they force a distinction between **official publication** and **governing authorship**.

Several guest contributors state the distinction themselves. Sagara Sou explicitly frames his Yukino story as one possible Yukino generated by a reader's interpretation rather than the author's hidden answer. In the All-Stars afterword he goes further and describes anthology writing through the logic of secondary creation: canon, omitted space, and imagination are mixed deliberately. Ishikawa Hiroshi's All-Stars story is openly meta-fictional, centered on an Oregairu reader and the history of reading the series. These are not defects. They are exactly why the guest material is valuable as **reception and interpretive evidence** and inappropriate as a source of mainline facts.

Watari's own stories occupy a different tier. They are still anthology supplements rather than numbered mainline chapters, but they come from the governing author and can therefore strengthen post-ending character, family, and ordinary-life claims after chronology audit.

# 9. The four Watari anthology stories as a post-ending ordinary-life band

The four Watari stories are not one continuous serialized epilogue. Their exact ordering relative to one another and to Volume 14.5's true post-ending stories is not always recoverable. They do, however, form a coherent **early-third-year post-Volume-14 band**.

That band materially strengthens the Phase-5 synthesis while leaving Volume 14's governing choice intact.

## 9.1 Anthology 1: Yukino's father and the future beyond the couple

`斯くして、彼の前に新たな敵は現れる。` is told from Yukino's father's perspective and expands family futurity in ways the numbered mainline only gestures toward.

The most important addition is not the comic father-versus-boyfriend frame. It is the plurality of futures.

Yukino's father is himself a person who married into a family name, business, and political inheritance. He therefore embodies transformed inheritance rather than simple bloodline continuity. Haruno, now twenty, is preparing for an extended overseas study period; Mrs. Yukinoshita may resist, but the family discussion ultimately leaves Haruno's decision recognizably hers. Yukino plans to return to the family-owned apartment. Her father supplies supplementary background for why she had originally lived alone, linking the arrangement to the post-entrance accident period and family conflict.

Most importantly, Yukino eventually says that she will introduce Hachiman to her father someday.

This is meaningful post-choice durability. It is **not** a marriage contract, engagement announcement, or retrospective proof that the entire series had one predetermined romantic route.

The story strengthens Document 10's proposition that romance becomes one consequential future among family, study, work, and inherited institutional life rather than replacing those domains.

It also gives ordinary evidence for Yukino and Yui's independently authored friendship: visits continue in both directions, and Yui can stay over. The final romantic answer has not deleted the friendship through which both girls already possessed claims on ordinary life.

## 9.2 Anthology 2: Hachiman and Komachi's final shared school year

`やはり妹さえいればいい。` is the one Watari anthology story whose present searchable witness has qualified source authority. The embedded page scans are the exact-text authority; OCR is suitable for discovery and broad paraphrase unless a page is visually verified.

Its chronology is nonetheless secure enough for analytical use: Hachiman is in his final high-school year and Komachi is now attending the same school.

The story's contribution is deliberately mundane. It tests whether sibling closeness can remain strong while Komachi gains a wider independent school life.

Komachi can call herself a “professional little sister,” offer help, become involved with other younger siblings, and still acknowledge limits to how much responsibility she owns for another family's problems. Hachiman can respond instantly when she needs him without the story converting that response into proof that he must control her future.

The closing sibling joke imagines their connection on a lifetime scale while preserving the comic register that prevents solemn possession.

The result is a useful ordinary-life control for the dependency/autonomy synthesis:

> **secure attachment does not become pathological merely because it is durable, and differentiation does not require emotional withdrawal.**

## 9.3 Anthology 3: Yui after loss is still a subject of the future

`いつか、その甘さを好きになることができる気がする。` is told primarily through Yui's father. Yui is explicitly seventeen and newly a third-year high-school student.

The story matters because it refuses two convenient post-ending simplifications.

It does not erase Yui's continuing desire. She has begun repeatedly practicing sweets, and the emotional context strongly associates that effort with someone she still wants to please. Her father recognizes that his daughter has genuinely fallen in love and experiences the situation as both sweet and bitter.

But the story also refuses to make continuing desire equivalent to renewed romantic symmetry.

Yui remains a daughter, student, friend, cook-in-training, social actor, and person whose future cannot be reduced to whether Hachiman changes his answer. Her mother's advice about developing one's own taste through repeated practice gives the story a subtle individuation structure: care for another person may initiate practice, but the practice can become part of the self rather than remaining a permanent attempt to purchase an outcome.

A later fathers' conversation broadens the family problem. Parents can want to support, obstruct, warn, or prepare, but their daughters' feelings remain the daughters' feelings. Parental concern does not create ownership over the romantic result.

Watari's afterword explicitly pairs this story with the Yukino-father story from Anthology 1 and frames the characters' ordinary days, unseen pasts, and far futures as continuing beyond the formal end of the main novel.

The mainline-compatible conclusion is therefore:

> **Yui's loss remains real, and so does Yui's future.**

Continuing feeling is not proof of pathology. It is also not proof that the chosen romantic relation has reopened into equality.

## 9.4 Anthology 4: post-choice club life without a reset

`しかし、その言葉の裏には裏がある。` is the strongest anthology control for the final triad synthesis because it returns directly to Hachiman's perspective in late April of the new school year.

The recreated Service Club is operating. Komachi holds the new institutional center. Yukino and Yui remain present. Iroha moves through the club often enough to have an emerging customary seat. Joint-prom documentation is being transferred to the student council so the event can exist as institutional memory rather than one extraordinary intervention owned by the old trio.

The story repeatedly signals **same but different**.

Yui brings a peach pie she has practiced making. Yukino praises it. Hachiman's appreciation includes the history of effort inside the food, not merely objective taste. Yui says she will make it again. Yukino considers making sweets as well; competition shifts into the possibility of the two girls baking together at Yui's home. Yui says she will visit Yukino when Yukino returns to her apartment. Yukino flusters rather than rejects the future.

This is not a restoration of pre-choice romantic equivalence. Yui's food still carries desire. Yukino knows more than she did before Volume 14. Hachiman knows an answer has been given. Iroha can see and play with subtext. The relation survives because non-equivalence is now part of the information environment.

The student-council sequence then gives Iroha's pretext ethics a mature ordinary test. She deliberately pulls Komachi into work partly because she predicts Hachiman will come help his sister. When confronted, she admits that she hesitated to intrude and thought giving Hachiman an excuse would help. Hachiman replies that she could simply ask and he would help at least somewhat.

The manipulation is not morally blank, but it is radically different from Kyoto. The pretext can be exposed. Its purpose can be discussed. The target can state what would have been sufficient. No irreversible romantic future is silently allocated.

Most strikingly, Yukino and Yui repeatedly appear at the student-council door after Hachiman leaves the clubroom. They offer materials, food, help, and the possibility of waiting for his return. The repeated visits become comic enough for Iroha to complain about people who are “love-addled,” but the structural point is simpler:

> **post-choice attachment produces ordinary return behavior.**

The story ends by making interpretation itself comic. Iroha offers to “translate” women's language; Hachiman refuses because every interpretation may have another reverse side behind it. The final smile belongs only to Iroha.

This directly extends Document 11. Mature relation does not require a final decoder ring. People can remain partially unreadable while continuing to ask, tease, work, wait, and return.

# 10. What ordinary life can prove that crisis cannot

## 10.1 Crisis reveals priorities; ordinary life reveals durability

Crisis is analytically powerful because it forces choice. The cultural festival, Kyoto, the election, the `本物` request, the dependency crisis, the proms, and the Volume-14 choice all expose what characters will risk when delay becomes impossible.

But crisis also distorts.

A person may act with unusual courage, cruelty, self-sacrifice, panic, or decisiveness under exceptional pressure. If the analysis stops at the climax, it may confuse an emergency configuration with the stable relation.

Ordinary-life supplements ask a different question:

> **What happens the next day, next week, next month, or next school year when nobody is forcing the characters into a decisive scene?**

That is why `また明日`, a cup of tea, a returned visit, a photograph, a shared pie, study plans, birthday memory, school paperwork, a text asking for help, or an inherited tea routine can be evidentially important.

## 10.2 Future assumption is one of the strongest low-temperature markers

The supplementary corpus repeatedly encodes relation through **assumed futurity**:

- Hachiman says `また明日` after Volume 9;
- Yui asks to play again;
- Yukino accepts future group outings;
- photographs are taken because there will be a later viewer;
- Hachiman measures time remaining before graduation;
- Yukino and Hachiman discuss study environments;
- Yui promises to make food again;
- Yui plans to visit Yukino;
- Yukino says she will introduce Hachiman someday;
- parents imagine futures they cannot fully control;
- Komachi inherits a club expected to acquire members not yet known.

These are not guarantees. They are **behavioral wagers that another encounter is worth planning for**.

This is often more useful than dramatic declarations of eternity.

## 10.3 Ordinary disagreement is a health test

The supplements also show whether relation can survive trivial friction.

People can disagree about food, schedules, clubs, study, gifts, events, work, social plans, or interpretation without immediately treating disagreement as evidence the relation was false.

This is a major maturation from the earlier tendency to protect connection by preventing difficult answers. The final state is not conflict-free. It is more capable of **absorbing conflict without converting conflict into deletion**.

## 10.4 Ordinary life preserves mixed motives

Low-stakes scenes are especially good at defeating purity theories.

Yui can bake because she wants to improve, because she loves someone, because she likes sharing food, because her mother helps, and because the effort has become part of her life. Iroha can want help, want access to Hachiman, avoid intruding, enjoy manipulating the setup, and still genuinely carry student-council responsibility. Hachiman can help Komachi because he loves his sister, because he enjoys being needed, because he is competent, and because the task gives him somewhere to be.

The question is not which one motive is “the truth.”

The question remains whether motives can be owned, contested, and revised without silently allocating other people's futures.

## 10.5 Ordinary time reveals pairwise independence inside the triad

The post-ending supplements further weaken any model in which the Service Club is merely Hachiman plus two romantic options.

Yukino and Yui visit one another. They plan food together. Yui can worry about Yukino's living situation. Komachi and Iroha develop their own abrasive-affectionate junior relation. Hachiman and Komachi continue sibling life. Yukino's family and Yui's family develop their own futurity concerns. Iroha and Komachi interact institutionally without requiring Hachiman to be the sole relational bridge.

A mature triad can survive because its pairwise relations have substance beyond one romantic contest.

# 11. What the supplementary corpus does not authorize

## 11.1 It does not supply a hidden “true route”

The post-V14 Watari stories strengthen Hachiman/Yukino durability. Yukino plans eventual introduction to her father. Hachiman and Yukino remain mutually involved. That does not mean the anthologies turn Volume 14 into proof of metaphysical predestination.

Likewise, Yui's continuing feelings do not reopen romantic equivalence by themselves.

The governing mainline answer remains:

- Hachiman chooses Yukino romantically;
- Yukino reciprocally chooses Hachiman;
- Yui receives a real nonreciprocal answer;
- the triad and Yukino/Yui friendship continue in changed form.

Supplements may show how that configuration is lived. They do not erase the answer in order to recreate route suspense.

## 11.2 It does not certify relational health

Ordinary continuity is evidence of durability, not a medical certificate.

Iroha can still push boundaries. Hachiman can still rationalize. Yui may continue to hurt. Yukino and Hachiman may later distribute relational labor badly. Parents may overreach. Komachi may exploit Hachiman's inability to refuse her. The Service Club may someday need to change more radically than the current characters expect.

The correct conclusion is not “they stayed together, therefore the relation is healthy.” It is:

> **they acquired procedures capable of continuing under imperfection.**

## 11.3 It does not make author afterwords diegetic doctrine

Watari's afterwords repeatedly say that the characters' days continue in his imagination and that he may write further pasts, futures, or ordinary episodes. This supports the project's anti-finality reading at the level of authorial framing.

But an afterword does not decide what a character believes inside the fiction. Nor does a joke about future continuation establish a specific canonical life outcome.

Paratext may explain publication intent or formal interest. It should not replace narrative evidence.

## 11.4 It does not let guest authors speak for Watari

The guest anthology stories are valuable precisely because they are multiple readings.

A professional novelist can notice a Yukino possibility, invent a Hachiman future, exaggerate a ship, stage a comic marriage fantasy, or construct a meta-reader story. That tells us something about Oregairu's interpretive affordances and reception among fellow writers.

It does **not** tell us that Watari has secretly canonized that interpretation.

The guest afterwords themselves repeatedly understand the assignment as interpretive or derivative play. V2 should take them at that level of authority rather than promoting them beyond what their authors claim.

# 12. Phase-5 revisions produced by the supplementary audit

The supplementary pass does not overturn the eleven prior specialist syntheses. It changes their confidence and boundaries.

## STRENGTHEN

**Ordinary life is primary analytical evidence, not filler.** The club's relation becomes visible in tomorrow-assumptions, leisure, shared work, food, photographs, visits, studying, errands, and succession.

**The Service Club is a relational technology.** Its room, requests, tea, paperwork, and recurring routines create repeated co-presence before and after explicit romantic choice.

**Yukino/Yui friendship is independently authored.** Post-choice visits, shared food practice, mutual concern, and planning survive without requiring romantic equivalence.

**Yui remains a future-bearing subject after loss.** Continuing romantic feeling can coexist with agency, family life, practice, friendship, and an unsettled personal future.

**Hachiman/Yukino is durable without becoming final-form proof.** Family introduction, study discussion, apartment futurity, and ordinary mutual presence strengthen the chosen relation while leaving vocational and long-term form open.

**Transparent pretexts can be ethically useful.** Iroha's later excuses are not pure directness, but they can be exposed, answered, and revised.

**Institutional continuity is transmission rather than replication.** Prom documents, presidency, mugs, tea practice, and new membership show that a form can survive by becoming different.

**The ending's anti-certification thesis survives post-ending evidence.** The absence of repeated `本物` certification and the explicit `神聖視` warning make ordinary revisability the stronger endpoint.

## PRESERVE

**The Volume-14 romantic choice remains asymmetric.** Nothing in the supplements restores an unresolved equal triangle.

**Mixed motives remain normal.** Ordinary life intensifies rather than solves motive mixture.

**Hachiman remains epistemically limited.** The later material makes interpretation more answerable; it does not make him a reliable decoder of everyone around him.

**Performance remains morally neutral.** Iroha, Komachi, Hachiman, and others continue to use jokes, roles, and scripts after growth.

## REVISE / NARROW

**“.5 = after the previous number” is rejected.** Each constituent story needs chronology routing.

**6.75 is not a current standalone authority.** It remains publication ancestry and limited historical paratext unless the original booklet enters the locked corpus later.

**Audio is not interchangeable with prose.** Audio governs performance evidence; text governs exact wording where a verified text witness exists.

**“Official anthology” is not one canon tier.** Watari and guest stories are separated by authorship and authority.

**Post-ending continuation does not equal closure.** More ordinary evidence can expand uncertainty rather than eliminate it.

# 15. Final synthesis

The supplementary corpus changes what counts as a serious Oregairu scene.

A confession is serious. So is a failed school event. So is a false confession, an election, a prom, or a family confrontation.

But a tea cup can also be serious when somebody else will use it after the original member graduates. A photograph can be serious when the people inside it will later remember the same moment differently. `また明日` can be serious when tomorrow previously could not be assumed. A pie can be serious when it carries desire, practice, maternal instruction, jealousy, effort, friendship, and no guaranteed romantic outcome. A stack of prom documents can be serious when an extraordinary event becomes transmissible institutional knowledge. An excuse can be serious when a character knows it is an excuse, another character knows what it is doing, and both remain capable of renegotiating the request.

This does not mean every bonus comedy is secretly profound. The source hierarchy is exactly what prevents that inflation. Guest-author play remains guest-author play. Songs remain songs. OCR remains OCR until checked against the page. Afterwords remain paratext. Publication ancestry remains ancestry. The strongest ordinary-life claims come from places where **high-authority fiction, secure chronology, and longitudinal relevance coincide**.

Those places produce one repeated result.

The Service Club begins as an imposed institution. It becomes a reason to remain in a room. It becomes a protected form whose preservation can become hollow. It becomes a place where asking is possible again. It becomes a contested object under dependency diagnosis and romantic asymmetry. It becomes a recreated club after an explicit answer. Finally, in the strongest supplementary material, it becomes something that can survive its founders by transmitting practices rather than demanding replication.

The same movement appears at the scale of persons.

Hachiman does not stop using abstraction, comedy, pretext, or tactical social knowledge. He becomes somewhat more capable of exposing those methods to reply. Yukino does not cease being competent or proud. She becomes more able to claim what she wants while allowing support that does not require incapacity. Yui does not cease managing atmosphere or cease loving after receiving a painful answer. She becomes more capable of remaining a subject whose future is larger than the answer she did not receive. Iroha does not become ethically purified. Her performance can instead become more transparent and more negotiable. Komachi does not prove autonomy by leaving Hachiman behind; she inherits a wider world while the sibling bond continues.

The supplementary corpus therefore confirms the most durable Phase-5 formulation:

> **Oregairu does not end by discovering a relationship outside performance, selfishness, dependence, social form, uncertainty, or pain. It ends by making those elements more answerable—and the ordinary-life material matters because it shows that answerability can become habit.**

Habit is not permanence.

That is why Volume 14.5's anti-sanctification correction is so important. The present club is not a shrine. The current romantic relation is not a certificate. Yui's current grief is not her permanent identity. Komachi's presidency is not Yukino's copied role. Haruno's family position is not a destiny that determines Yukino's. A practice can be inherited and altered. A relationship can continue and still be wrong tomorrow. A promise can matter without specifying every future act.

The ordinary-life endpoint is therefore neither “happily ever after” nor “nothing was resolved.”

It is more exact:

> **The answer survives when people can keep choosing it in forms that remain open to correction.**

That is the proper outgoing state for Phase 6.
