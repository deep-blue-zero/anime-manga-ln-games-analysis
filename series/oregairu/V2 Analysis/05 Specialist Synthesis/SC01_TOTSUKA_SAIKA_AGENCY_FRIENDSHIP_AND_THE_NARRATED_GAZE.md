# Totsuka Saika: agency, friendship, and the narrated gaze

## 0. Argument and authority

Totsuka Saika's principal continuity is a commitment to tennis that repeatedly becomes a practical question: how to improve, strengthen a weak club, recruit people, lead them, arrange time, and carry an activity into a possible educational future. His friendship works through related acts of attention. He notices another person's condition, proposes something they can do together, offers assistance, and increasingly asks to understand the purposes of work he is being invited to share. Neither continuity requires Hachiman to authorize it. Hachiman's narration determines how much readers see, but it does not bring Totsuka's goals into existence.

This distinction qualifies the existing Character State Ledger's movement from an aestheticized safe object toward a reciprocal friend. **The movement is strongly supported as a change in recognition and use of friendship; it is not a complete replacement of one Totsuka by another, nor the end of Hachiman's aestheticization.** Totsuka already has goals, other friends, uncertainty, practical judgment and refusals in the early novels. Volume 10 makes the limitations of Hachiman's image unusually explicit. Volume 10.5 then shows how easily that image can again obscure Totsuka's tiring participation. Later reciprocity is meaningful because it develops within this imperfect relation, rather than purifying its history.

This is a literary monograph, separate from any operational reconstruction specification. The distinction between source fact, a character's self-description, Hachiman's focalized observation, and V2 interpretation governs every section. The mainline through V14.5 is the admitted boundary; the latest direct Totsuka material used here is V14. A name search of the complete V14.5 text, with whitespace removed for retrieval, finds no Totsuka/Saika occurrence. That negative search does not remove him from the fictional world or supply a new post-ending psychological state. Shin, Ketsu, alternate routes, adaptations and guest fiction do not fill the gap.

The existing method, architecture, Character State and Japanese Voice ledgers, Specialists 08/11/12, Phase-6 audit, and Document 13 supplied the initial retrieval route. Targeted Japanese reopening tested their suggestions rather than treating them as conclusions. The source table in §10 records the consequential routes. Exact literary facts remain subordinate to those Japanese witnesses; this monograph does not supersede the frozen readings, checkpoints, original specialists or Document 13.

## 1. A person who wants to become better at something

The early tennis episode makes a revealing contrast. Totsuka explains that he wants to become stronger. Hachiman responds, briefly and comically, that weakness would be acceptable because he would protect him. The joke is not merely evidence that Hachiman likes Totsuka. It places an athlete's stated aspiration beside a spectator's preferred image. The spectator's warmth could leave the athlete's reason for acting unrecognized. Totsuka's subjecthood is present in that mismatch from Volume 1, before Hachiman later announces that he has begun to see it. [SC01-E01/E02]

His activity is already more sustained than the image of an effortlessly lovable classmate suggests. He has persistently asked permission to use the courts at lunchtime. He says he does the extra practice because he likes tennis. When he requests Hachiman's membership, he describes the club's specific difficulties: few members, impending loss of the third-years, inexperienced first-years, and a lack of competition for regular places that weakens motivation. Whatever the limits of his proposed solution, he understands that improvement requires an environment and other participants, rather than only more personal enthusiasm. [SC01-E01/E02]

The request also exposes vulnerability. Totsuka is nervous, seeks a favorable answer, and says Hachiman's presence would help him work harder. None of that makes his tennis ambition a cover for an otherwise knowable private desire. The scene allows both a sporting purpose and a wish for companionship without letting the reader determine their complete proportions. When Hachiman refuses membership, Totsuka is disappointed but thanks him for listening. His goal survives the rejected proposal. Accepting this answer is a small but important distinction from treating care as an entitlement to another person's time. [SC01-E02]

His body does not supply a simple moral emblem. He falls during training, worries that Yukino has abandoned him because he is improving too slowly, and cites his limited push-up capacity. Yet he wishes to continue because the others have made time for him. Determination, limited strength, anxiety about disappointing helpers, and sporting competence can coexist. The episode neither proves that he is secretly an outstanding athlete nor licenses the opposite stereotype of universal physical helplessness. It shows someone trying to improve under conditions he finds difficult. [SC01-E03]

Volume 3 adds a significant independent investment: evening tennis school. School-club practice is mainly basic training, so he seeks further instruction outside it. Volume 9 confirms that the lessons continue. This repeated allocation of time is stronger evidence of commitment than a single enthusiastic declaration. It also gives his life a schedule that friendship must accommodate. The early outing takes place before a lesson; later an ordinary invitation cannot simply displace club practice, lessons and a practice match. [SC01-E06/E11/E15]

By Volume 10 the change is institutional as well as personal. Other members now join lunchtime practice because he has invited them in preparation for a novice tournament. He modestly doubts how well he is managing them, while Hachiman interprets his long example as consequential. The direct facts are the invitation, participation and directions he gives. Hachiman's approving theory of leadership is a further interpretation. It is plausible, but it should not become proof that all members are motivated in the same way. [SC01-E12]

## 2. A future connected to practice, with its outcome still open

The conversation about academic tracks matters because Totsuka can answer questions Hachiman has scarcely thought to ask. He has considered entrance subjects and tentatively names human sciences or sports sciences in Tokorozawa as possible destinations. He connects that interest to having played tennis for a long time. The dialogue names a location and fields rather than explicitly naming the university. Even an easily recognizable institutional allusion should not be rewritten as an unqualified proper-name declaration. [SC01-E12]

His choice also complicates a false opposition between independent planning and wanting a friend nearby. He momentarily considers following Hachiman into the humanities track, but explains that the subjects would remain compatible with his contemplated destination. Hachiman cautions against choosing merely to match him; Totsuka answers that he has been thinking about the requirements. The scene therefore permits relational preference inside an independently considered plan. It does not require friendship to be either the only motive or wholly irrelevant. [SC01-E12]

Hachiman's suggestion of recommendation admission exposes a difference in practical knowledge. Totsuka explains the exceptional level at which sporting selection works and distinguishes it from the school's circumstances. His reference to professional or Olympic aspirations describes the caliber of certain selection candidates; it is not a confession that he intends to become a professional athlete or Olympian. Similarly, his information about Hayama's coordination of club captains comes from a shared institutional world. He knows something about Hayama as a fellow captain that Hachiman's image of the popular boy had omitted. [SC01-E12]

The future supported here is a direction of interest, not a forecast. There is no warrant to award Totsuka a university place, specify a mature career, or make his adult personality the inevitable extension of his school commitments. What the fiction establishes is already substantial: he can value an activity over time, acquire knowledge of its institutions, consider educational routes, and remain unsure of the outcome. His uncertainty is part of a thought-through future rather than the absence of one.

## 3. Warmth is something he does

Totsuka's friendliness is often described through the effect it has on Hachiman: relief, delight, comic loss of composure. An independent account needs to ask what Totsuka chooses. Several early actions resist reduction to atmospheric comfort. He recognizes Hachiman's tennis form, approaches him, invites him to partner, asks for assistance, and remembers that they were classmates before Hachiman remembers him. Their initial asymmetry concerns attention, not whether Totsuka possesses an interior. [SC01-E01/E02]

The exchange of names in Volume 2 is especially useful because it includes both desire and adjustment. Totsuka is pleased when Hachiman calls him Saika. He asks whether he too can use the nickname `ヒッキー`; Hachiman refuses, and Totsuka tries `八幡` instead. A closeness marker is negotiated rather than imposed. Hachiman subsequently continues to use the surname frequently, so the event is not a perfectly symmetrical permanent renaming. Its significance is that an ordinary mode of personal address has become possible and wanted. [SC01-E04]

In the Kawasaki investigation, Totsuka contributes an independent proposal: a third-party adult may hear things that cannot be told to parents. He offers a reason based on the distinctive difficulty of family closeness. The intervention fails when Hiratsuka addresses the matter without yet knowing Kawasaki's material reasons. That failure should not erase the sense in Totsuka's proposal, or transform it into insight he never possessed. He supplies a plausible approach to a problem whose governing information remains missing. [SC01-E05]

The Volume 3 outing makes his intention more explicit. He has noticed Hachiman seeming low and wants to offer a change of mood. He acknowledges that he does not know what happened. Care and incomplete knowledge are thus simultaneously spoken. He does not need to disclose a total theory of Hachiman to take a small initiative on his behalf. Within the same episode he selects an activity he would like to try, divides the photo stickers to include Zaimokuza, and promises future opportunities for shared memories. His social action is inclusive rather than exclusively organized around one admired person. [SC01-E06/E07]

This is also friendship beyond Hachiman's field of view. Volume 1 mentions Totsuka's normal PE partner. Volume 4 makes another tennis friendship visible when Hachiman sees him meet a peer. Hachiman's distress reveals an unpromised exclusivity he had assigned to the relation. The other friend's identity and the exact ranking of these friendships remain unknown. The scene establishes that Hachiman is not the measure of Totsuka's whole social world. It does not establish that Totsuka ranks Hachiman low. [SC01-E02/E08]

## 4. Reciprocity means being allowed to help

The meal in Volume 9 complicates the image of effortless emotional safety. Totsuka notices Hachiman's fatigue and proposes food with that condition in mind. Hachiman initially misreads the suggestion as a response to Totsuka's own exercise. When Totsuka asks what is wrong, Hachiman minimizes the situation. Totsuka then invokes the election episode: Zaimokuza had apparently been permitted the kind of conversation he would like to share. His envy is of a perceived avenue for trust and assistance, rather than simply of Hachiman's general capacity to talk. [SC01-E11]

His worry that he may not be useful is an important limit on the idea that he supplies care without stakes of his own. He would like to be someone Hachiman can rely on. That wish is not shown as a pathology; neither should it be purified into total selflessness. Friendship matters to him partly because he wants an active place in it. Hachiman's concealment can deny that place while appearing to protect him from worry.

Totsuka is not always right about what he sees. His praise of Hachiman's solitary endurance can reward the very concealment that keeps him outside the problem. Hachiman recognizes that the admired act contains more calculation and dependence on others' kindness than Totsuka realizes. The scene therefore preserves a double asymmetry: Totsuka wants knowledge he has not been given, and he forms an admiring inference partly because he lacks it. His subsequent invitation to speak when troubled is valuable without being evidence of infallible emotional perception. [SC01-E11]

Volume 10 gives this desire for reciprocity a more direct form. After explaining tennis, admissions and captaincy, he includes himself among the things Hachiman does not know. His modest concern that he may seem unreliable sits beside demonstrations of actual responsibility. Hachiman's answer is not a claim to have decoded him; it is a statement that he may rely on him and wants to know more. [SC01-E12]

The narration then says Totsuka had probably been waiting for Hachiman to approach. That is Hachiman's retrospective inference, not Totsuka's confession of a hidden continuous plan. Keeping the attribution matters. The scene establishes a newly voiced prospect of reliance; it does not give the analyst authority to rewrite every earlier invitation as a stage in Totsuka's deliberate strategy. Nor does Hachiman's declaration that Totsuka is simply Totsuka erase the comic angel classifications that occur in the same passage.

## 5. Gentle speech, actual limits

Totsuka's warmth is compatible with disagreement. At the cinema outing he likes the film Zaimokuza dismisses. He corrects Hachiman's description of Yukino, even while acknowledging that her seriousness initially felt frightening. In the toy conversation he warns both boys that their imagined modifications are dangerous and rejects the suggestion that self-inflicted injury would be acceptable. His moral language is local, intelligible and short; it does not need to become a thesis about the whole series. [SC01-E09]

His boundaries also vary in effectiveness. Against Miura's early attempt to take over the court, he tries to distinguish practice from play, but her public pressure overwhelms his quiet objection. During the Volume 10.5 photo session he asks about the purpose and duration, becomes tired, and does not himself secure an endpoint before Iroha intervenes. An attempted boundary and an effective refusal are different outcomes. A monograph that simply labels him either compliant or assertive would lose this contextual variation. [SC01-E03/E14]

Volume 12 supplies a stronger refusal that preserves affection. Hachiman asks about a free day. Totsuka cannot abandon weekday club commitments, has evening lessons and a forthcoming practice match, and apologetically works through his schedule. He then insists that the invitation should be renewed and promises to contact Hachiman. He wants to go; he still does not make himself immediately available. The combination makes his ordinary friendship legible without requiring dramatic confrontation. [SC01-E15]

His health admonition in the same scene is similarly direct. He has noticed Hachiman sleeping repeatedly and objects to unhealthy gaming. It would be excessive to infer from the joking one-hour injunction a detailed personal gaming regimen. What is established is an ability to reproach someone he likes, prompted by visible behavior rather than privileged access to private distress. [SC01-E15]

Volume 13 is more consequential because Totsuka names the pattern in their collaboration that he dislikes. He does not want to be left not understanding what happened again. He asks what Hachiman actually wants, observes that the apparent prom objective and secrecy toward Yukino do not fit comfortably together, and acknowledges that answering in company may be difficult. His demand is neither an assault nor a hidden diagnosis. He wants enough first-person explanation to participate as someone who understands. [SC01-E16]

After Hachiman explains, Totsuka offers help bounded by his own club. If people are needed, he can mobilize tennis members; he cannot do everything. The later joint-prom preparation uses that network, with shifts arranged around participants' schedules. The offer thus has practical consequences rather than serving only as a comforting scene. Yet Totsuka's question does not establish that he has audited every effect of the dummy proposal on absent people. The same character had helped with the marathon tactic in Volume 10 while noticing that it might be unwelcome. He becomes a more explicit collaborator without becoming a moral authority who always refuses questionable methods. [SC01-E13/E16/E17]

## 6. The camera and the limits of recognition

Volume 10.5 is decisive counterevidence to a completed liberation from the narrated gaze. The recruitment interview begins with Totsuka coaching juniors. His practical role is audible in the words of a technical correction and encouragement. When Hachiman begins taking personal photographs instead of simply documenting practice, Totsuka asks about the apparent change of purpose. He hesitates, then reasons that the publicity might bring new students into the club. His eventual participation has an intelligible independent motive. It does not establish that he enjoys being posed. [SC01-E14]

Hachiman briefly recognizes a possible manipulation in persuading him, then converts guilt into another source of excitement. As the session continues, Totsuka asks whether more photographs are necessary. A proposed rest becomes the prospect of a further round. Iroha finally terminates the shoot. The pleasure of the narration and the fatigue of its subject are both part of the scene. To preserve only the comedy would lose the problem; to erase the comedy in favor of a universal condemnation would also simplify the work's form.

This passage revises the strongest version of the trajectory hypothesis. The Volume 10 recognition is not false because the later behavior fails its implications. It has widened Hachiman's knowledge and made future reliance speakable. But knowledge of an aspiration can itself be recruited into a renewed aesthetic project: the hope of new club members becomes a reason Totsuka is willing to tolerate embarrassment. Recognition is vulnerable precisely where it becomes convenient for the person who already controls the representation.

The model of the relation must therefore retain both Totsuka's choice and the imperfect conditions of that choice. He is neither an inert object nor someone whose compliance confirms full comfort. The independent subject persists even when the focalizer again represents him inadequately. Iroha's intervention further demonstrates that the problem cannot be reduced to what either member of the dyad feels privately; another participant can see a practical limit the photographer is not respecting.

Volume 14 retains aestheticized comedy even as Hachiman explicitly calls Totsuka his friend. This coexistence prevents a simplistic demand that mature recognition must abolish all aesthetic affection. The relevant question is whether affection leaves room for purposes, information, refusals and responsibilities that do not belong to the admirer. The source repeatedly answers that question unevenly. [SC01-E17]

## 7. Gender presentation without invented interiority

The text gives Totsuka male self-descriptions. Hachiman supplies much of the feminizing comparison, imagined romantic staging, angel imagery, and protective fantasy. These are not interchangeable evidence classes. Analysis can describe the narrator's attraction and the gender categories through which he explains it without assigning Totsuka an unspoken identity or reciprocal romantic desire. [SC01-E01/E04/E09]

Volume 3 provides particularly strong evidence for Hachiman's safety logic. He explicitly contrasts how he would distrust a girl's invitation with the security he attaches to accepting Totsuka's kindness. This is a self-description of his interpretive defense. It explains why direct warmth can be received more readily in this channel. It does not settle every meaning of the desire the narration playfully expresses, and it does not settle Totsuka's feelings. The comedy can disclose something about Hachiman while leaving the other person's interior genuinely undetermined. [SC01-E06]

Hachiman also says Totsuka does not much like being treated as a girl. That report should remain attributed to him. Direct speech supplies male self-identification and a refusal of the bicycle ride, but the analyst should not pretend those acts explain every reason for that refusal. Similarly, the childhood Mini 4WD recollection broadens the available picture of Totsuka; it is not proof that a particular hobby authenticates a masculine essence. His tennis, taste in films, embarrassment and tenderness need not be sorted into a single normative gender argument.

The framing is not harmless by definition, but neither does it exhaust the character. Totsuka sometimes corrects assumptions, voices his preferences, and accepts or refuses practical arrangements. These acts are the strongest way of restoring his independent subjecthood. A contrary method that reconstructs a supposed hidden gender or romantic identity out of other people's reactions would repeat the error of replacing his limited, specific statements with a more total image.

## 8. Ordinary time and the difference between a choice and a preference

Tennis is exceptionally well supported as a durable interest. Other mundane evidence should remain narrower. Totsuka chooses a horror film, explicitly likes that kind of film against Zaimokuza's judgment, and enjoys recounting its spectacular surprises. Being startled is compatible with enjoyment. This is enough to reject the automatic assumption that a gentle or easily embarrassed person dislikes horror; it is not enough to rank his complete film tastes. [SC01-E09]

The Mini 4WD conversation contains a remembered childhood activity, a named model and renewed interest in trying it with friends. It supports an available recreational interest rather than an invented present collection or daily routine. His readiness to borrow the original Little Prince for an assigned role supports conscientious preparation; it does not establish a general literary hobby. At Yui's birthday gathering he says he enjoyed himself, and the subsequent arcade activity shows ordinary sociability within that specific early chronological setting. It should not be relabeled as late-series transformation simply because it is published in Volume 7.5. [SC01-E09/E10]

Food evidence is particularly easy to overread. He orders iced coffee after Hachiman in Volume 5. In Volume 9 his meat suggestion is explained as concern for Hachiman's condition, while the final fast-food choice accommodates budget. Hachiman remembers him as a small eater and thinks he tended to prefer vegetables, but that is qualified narrator memory. One cannot turn the scene into a definite dietary rule, favorite dish or intrinsic meat enthusiasm. The packed lunch in Volume 10 does not identify who prepared it. [SC01-E09/E11/E12]

The ordinary-life profile thus contains both positive content and useful absences. Totsuka has sustained sporting time, a life outside the school club, some independently voiced entertainment tastes, concern for shared choice, practical generosity, study/rehearsal effort and finite availability. His favorite food, music, household skills, family circumstances, spending pattern, sleep routine and adult occupation are not established by this material. Leaving those fields unknown is a more accurate reconstruction than extrapolating a complete gentle-athlete lifestyle.

## 9. Japanese voice and relationship-conditioned address

Totsuka's voice is not perpetual hesitation. The prose frequently prints `ぼく`, while Volume 12's emphatic rescheduling uses `僕`. Orthographic variation should not be converted into a different psychological self. His early address to Hachiman is `比企谷くん`; the Volume 2 naming exchange establishes `八幡`. He generally retains surname-plus-suffix forms for other peers: `由比ヶ浜さん`, `雪ノ下さん`, `三浦さん`, `材木座くん`, `葉山くん`, and in the recruitment interview `一色さん`. These distinctions matter more than the indiscriminate repetition of a soft-sounding ending. [SC01-E02/E04/E05/E12/E14/E15]

His requests often reduce imposition by asking whether the other person has time or finds the proposal acceptable. That form can carry a definite wish: joining the club, walking together, selecting a film, sharing a meal, or understanding a scheme. With a trusted friend he can tease and correct. With the club he gives clear directions; with an unfamiliar collective, his captain's introduction uses a more polite exhortation. Relational gentleness and institutional projection are compatible modes of one speaker. [SC01-E02/E06/E09/E10/E12/E14]

The V13 question is important because it requests a first-person answer instead of supplying an elaborate theory. Its force depends on the prior history of not being told. It should not become a catchphrase emitted whenever Hachiman is troubled. Likewise the emphatic promise of future contact in Volume 12 belongs to the unusual pleasure of receiving an invitation; it is not a permanent register of excitement. These are textual and pragmatic observations. This study does not claim fresh direct listening or verified delivery/prosody from a recording.

Remaining questions concern the depth and independence of his relationships beyond Hachiman, his private responses to repeated feminization, the limits of his captaincy, and how far his explicit wish to be useful differs across friends. Those are genuine gaps. They do not prevent an independent literary account of the acts the fiction does show. Fresh sources would have to contain Totsuka's own speech or observable conduct in those contexts before the gaps could be narrowed.

The monograph primarily **elaborates** the distributed supporting-cast evidence and **strengthens** the tennis/reciprocity spine. Its material qualification is the non-linear recognition claim, documented here as SC01-C01 rather than silently written into a prospective freeze. The findings do not require a wholesale rewrite of Document 14, alter the mainline romantic choice, or promote reconstruction rules into literary authority.

## 12. Operational eligibility and literary conclusion

The evidence warrants a bounded school-age reconstruction responsibility after completion of its separate gates. Totsuka has a longitudinally supported attentional pattern, sustained independent commitments, several kinds of initiative and refusal, relationship-conditioned language, meaningful failure modes, and sufficient primary ordinary-life observations for a shared-ledger slice. A separate model must keep thin family, romance, adult-professional and postV14.5-specific contexts underdetermined. It must also retain the early/late difference in the effectiveness of boundaries instead of generating a uniformly gentle helper with a tennis accessory. This is a local eligibility decision under the architecture, not a global capability grade.

Totsuka's literary independence rests on a less spectacular proposition. He has things he wants to do and people he wants to do things with; time spent with one friend does not consume all his obligations or relations. Hachiman learns to ask for a share of that time and assistance, and Totsuka increasingly asks to know what his participation means. Their friendship becomes more usable and more articulate without ceasing to be funny, unequal in information, or occasionally careless. The most defensible recognition is therefore neither that Totsuka was only an image nor that the image has finally vanished. It is that a loved image remains answerable to a person whose work, uncertainty, choices and limits exceed it.
