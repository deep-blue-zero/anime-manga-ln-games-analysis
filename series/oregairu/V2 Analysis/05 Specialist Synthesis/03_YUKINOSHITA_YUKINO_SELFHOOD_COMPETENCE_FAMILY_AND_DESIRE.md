# 03 — Yukinoshita Yukino: Selfhood, Competence, Family, and Desire
## From corrective competence to self-authored, chosen interdependence

## 0. Function, scope, and governing thesis

Yukinoshita Yukino is unusually easy to misread because the series introduces her through several interpretive frames before it gives her enough direct, longitudinal self-description to test them. Hachiman sees a solitary equal, a severe truth-teller, an idealized person who does not lie, a possible mirror of himself, a person who needs protection, and eventually the person whose future he wants to remain involved in. Haruno sees a younger sister entangled with imitation, dependence, inherited family roles, and the danger of living through other people. Yui sees a friend who is strong, lonely, difficult, lovable, self-denying, competitive, and sometimes too willing to remove herself. Their mother sees a daughter inside a family structure of succession, public obligation, and future consequence. Each perspective contains evidence. None has the right to define Yukino completely.

The definitive V2 Yukino thesis is therefore:

> **Yukino begins as a genuinely competent, proud, reformist, socially isolated person whose ethics and identity are organized around correction, responsibility, and the belief that help should preserve the subject's capacity to act. Her central problem is not hidden helplessness. It is that competence, family comparison, inherited role, and limited practice in having personally consequential choices recognized make it easier for her to act for others than to claim desire for herself. Her growth is the increasing ability to distinguish capability from self-sufficiency, reliance from surrender of authorship, role from identity, and self-authorship from isolation. By the end she can originate requests, refuse specific help without refusing relation, acknowledge uncertainty without abandoning choice, make reciprocal claims, state romantic desire, accept support whose necessity has been explicitly denied, and imagine a future in which relation continues without fusion or replication.**

This document therefore keeps six distinctions active throughout:

1. **competence is not a defense merely because it can become defensive;**
2. **self-sufficiency is not the same thing as self-authorship;**
3. **reliance is not the same thing as dependence, and dependence is not the same thing as incapacity;**
4. **a choice can be self-authored and still ethically overreach someone else;**
5. **family influence can shape a choice without making the choice unreal;**
6. **romantic reciprocity does not rescue Yukino from the unresolved work of family, vocation, friendship, or self-revision.**

The chronological architecture follows the six movements frozen in `01_SERIES_ARCHITECTURE_AND_VOLUME_PROGRESSION.md`:

- **V01–V03:** corrective idealism, isolation, and first voluntary belonging;
- **V04–V06.5:** family role becomes visible; competence becomes substitution; reliance becomes speakable;
- **V07–V09:** trust, election authorship, relational injury, self-uncertainty, and future request;
- **V10–V11:** fallibility-compatible disclosure, unstable self-location, and explicit jurisdictional boundary;
- **V12–V14:** family choice, prom authorship, dependency self-critique, self-removal, reciprocal desire, and final romantic answer;
- **V14.5:** ordinary future planning, separate trajectories, succession, and chosen reliance without incapacity.

The result is not the story of an “ice queen” learning warmth, a dependent girl learning independence, or a hidden romantic self finally emerging from behind competence. The series does something harder. It allows Yukino's stable traits to remain stable while changing **who gets to author what those traits mean**.

# 1. The evidentiary rule: Yukino cannot be reconstructed primarily through Hachiman

The first methodological rule of a Yukino monograph must be negative:

> **Hachiman's narration is evidence about Hachiman's experience of Yukino before it is evidence about Yukino's interiority.**

That does not make his observations useless. He sees many real things. He recognizes her competence, isolation, harshness, social awkwardness, attraction to cats, family tension, overwork, moments of vulnerability, political will, and increasing significance to him. He often detects a change before Yukino names it. But the Hachiman epistemic ledger has already established that his self-implicating domain is precisely where projection risk rises. Yukino is one of the most important sites of that risk.

The early proposition `雪ノ下は噓を吐かない` is the classic example. Hachiman has evidence that Yukino values bluntness and avoids ordinary social flattery. He then converts that evidence into an identity axiom. When later information violates the axiom, the emotional injury is real, but the violated object is partly Hachiman's projection. V2 therefore rejects the route-reading move in which a later “true Yukino” is inferred backward from whatever Hachiman finally realizes about her.

The same discipline applies to Haruno. Haruno knows the family, reads Yukino quickly, and correctly identifies several mechanisms. Her statement that Yukino lacks a self cannot simply be promoted into narrator-level truth. It is a high-pressure family assertion made by a participant whose own life has been organized by inherited role, frustrated adulthood, and the desire to force something demonstrably non-false into existence. Haruno's interpretations are valuable counterevidence. They are not a sovereignty grant.

The same applies, differently, to Yui. Yui often understands Yukino's emotional movement better than Hachiman does, and her atmosphere sensitivity gives her unusual access to what is being avoided. But Yui is also a romantic rival and a person who sometimes delays decisive speech because she fears what answer it will force. Her reading of Yukino must remain relationally situated.

Accordingly, this synthesis gives highest weight to:

- Yukino's explicit first-person statements;
- repeated behavior across volumes;
- requests and refusals whose object is clearly specified;
- choices that remain hers after other people object;
- institutional actions for which she bears practical consequence;
- Japanese-language markers of self-attribution and uncertainty;
- longitudinal contradiction between what she says, does, revises, and later says again.

Hachiman, Haruno, Yui, Hiratsuka, Hayama, and Yukino's mother then become **counter-gazes**: they can reveal something Yukino misses, but no single gaze is permitted to absorb the person.

This is not merely a fairness rule. It changes the character fundamentally. Once Hachiman's interpretive monopoly is removed, Yukino stops looking like a competent object whose “real need” must be uncovered. She becomes a subject whose competence, desire, fear, dependence, and refusal do not always point in the same direction.

# 2. Stable character core: what does not disappear

The V2 character-state ledger deliberately resists personality-inversion models. Yukino does not become a different kind of person in order to grow.

Across the series she remains:

- exceptionally competent;
- proud;
- severe;
- verbally precise;
- socially awkward in domains where ordinary calibration is expected;
- capable of dry cruelty and genuine care in adjacent sentences;
- reformist rather than merely cynical;
- attracted to clear standards;
- uncomfortable with unearned praise;
- willing to accept difficult work;
- easily embarrassed by direct positive relational naming;
- inclined to make care actionable through work, correction, procedure, or bounded request.

These traits matter because several weak readings treat the early Yukino as a shell around the later one. V2 rejects that structure.

Her competence is real before it becomes armor. Her severity is sincere before it becomes a social defense. Her standards are ethical commitments before they become sources of overfunctioning. Her controlled feminine speech is her voice before vulnerability must travel through it. Her family role constrains her without converting every choice into imitation. Her desire for autonomy is not fake because it sometimes becomes overcorrective.

What changes is the relationship between these traits and **authorship**.

Early Yukino can use competence to solve a problem while leaving the personal meaning of the action unnamed. She can make a correct criticism while failing to model the experience of a less capable person. She can assist someone else while being much less practiced at making herself the object of help. She can function independently while still defining her available life partly through Haruno's prior route. She can care deeply while preferring institutional or practical language to intimate naming.

Late Yukino remains competent and precise, but those traits have greater carrying capacity. Precision can specify a boundary instead of only correcting someone else. Competence can be delegated instead of hoarded. Uncertainty can be stated without becoming paralysis. Desire can be grammatically possessed. Reliance can be requested. A romantic claim can be ordinary rather than encoded in a project.

The character arc is therefore not:

> cold -> warm

or:

> dependent -> independent.

It is closer to:

> **competence as identity and safe medium -> competence as revisable tool inside self-authored relation.**

# 3. V01–V03: corrective idealism before the family problem is fully visible

## 3.1 Volume 1: reformism is an ethic, not an “ice queen” pose

Volume 1 introduces Yukino as an ideological counterweight to Hachiman. His method protects the self through suspicion and withdrawal. Hers protects integrity through correctness and improvement.

Yukino sincerely believes that the world is wrong when ability attracts resentment and when weakness is socially affirmed rather than challenged. She does not merely use “self-improvement” language to keep people at a distance. The belief organizes how she helps.

This is why her first major failure is not hypocrisy but **model insufficiency**. She is capable of seeing what another person could do if they behaved differently; she is less practiced at modeling what it feels like to be a person whose fear, status, skill, or social position makes that action difficult.

Yui's cookie request becomes the first correction. Yukino attacks the excuse. Yui unexpectedly receives the bluntness as honesty, apologizes, and tries again. Yukino is destabilized not by conflict but by favorable reception. Hostility is legible to her. Being liked after being severe is stranger.

That reversal matters for the entire series. Yukino's isolation is not proof she wants no relation. It is partly the result of a social method calibrated to environments in which ability, standards, and directness have often produced resentment. When Yui treats those traits as desirable rather than threatening, the possibility space changes.

The early thesis should therefore be:

> **Yukino's correctness is both conviction and social language. The problem is not that the conviction is false; it is that correctness alone cannot represent every person's conditions of action.**

## 3.2 Institutional language becomes an emotional technology

One of Volume 1's most precise character moments is Yukino's near-use of an intimate label followed by retreat into `うちの部員`—“our club member.” The primary-source locator is V01-E15, `p-0040.xhtml#p030`, printed p.276.

The importance is not that “club member” is fake while “friend” would be real. The institutional term does real emotional work. It lets Yukino claim standing before she can comfortably name intimacy.

This becomes a series-wide pattern:

- role can precede personal language;
- work can precede confession;
- practical request can precede vulnerable request;
- institutional relation can become the container in which personal relation acquires history.

Yukino is therefore not someone whose real self is trapped behind roles. She is someone for whom roles can make relation **speakable and actionable before it is emotionally nameable**.

## 3.3 Volumes 2–3: care appears before Yukino can theorize it

The early books repeatedly show Yukino acting with more relational investment than her labels initially admit.

She notices. She remembers. She acts on Yui's requests. She can permit friendship language, however embarrassingly. She can value Yui's presence in the club. She can make a birthday gesture whose meaning is explicitly separated from leverage: she wants to express gratitude even if Yui never returns.

That birthday structure matters because it defeats a later temptation to read all Yukino care through dependence, competition, or Hachiman. The Yukino–Yui relationship already has independent ethical content. Yukino is capable of care that does not function as a recruitment mechanism, romantic strategy, or debt exchange.

Volume 3 also provides a critical control against the claim that Yukino “cannot rely on people.” When she recognizes that her own criteria are poorly calibrated for choosing a present for Yui, she asks Hachiman:

> `手伝ってもらえると助かる、のだけれど……`

The line is hesitant. The task is bounded. The request is real.

This establishes a distinction that must govern every later dependency reading:

> **Yukino can rely on others early. What is difficult is making her own desire, identity, family future, or emotionally consequential need the object of reliance.**

The series does not transform her from incapable independence into healthy dependence. It progressively changes the stakes of what she can ask another person to enter.

# 4. Competence: real ability, moral vocation, relational armor

Any definitive Yukino analysis must protect competence from two opposite reductions.

The first reduction romanticizes it: Yukino is the flawless capable girl whose later trouble results only from other people misunderstanding or obstructing her.

The second psychologizes it away: Yukino is actually fragile and dependent, and competence is merely armor covering the “real” wounded girl.

Both are too simple.

Yukino is actually capable. She has high academic ability, organizational skill, verbal control, fast practical judgment, work discipline, institutional intelligence, and the capacity to learn new social techniques. Other people rely on her because she can reliably do things they cannot do as well.

That capability also becomes ethically loaded. Yukino's early helping philosophy says, in effect, that assistance should not abolish the subject's opportunity to act. People should be made more capable rather than merely comforted.

The cultural-festival arc turns that ethic back on her. Because Yukino can perform the work, Sagami can retain office while Yukino increasingly carries the operational burden. The institution continues functioning precisely because the most competent person overfunctions.

The problem is not “Yukino works too hard” in the generic sense. It is that competence becomes **substitutionary**.

The role “person who can fix this” creates three distortions:

1. other people can stop practicing responsibility;
2. Yukino can treat successful completion as evidence that the arrangement is justified;
3. being useful can make it harder for Yukino to notice when she has ceased to preserve the other person's authorship.

This is why Hachiman's criticism lands. He invokes a principle already compatible with Yukino's own ethics: the subject must retain the final act that makes the resolution theirs.

Yukino's correction is not to become less capable. It is to learn more differentiated competence:

- delegate;
- ask;
- use leverage without replacing the office-holder;
- distinguish support from substitution;
- accept another person's limitation without withdrawing the request;
- preserve contingency rather than making herself the only thing holding the system together.

The late prom material then returns competence in a more mature form. Yukino owns the proposal, negotiates with adults, coordinates students, faces family authority, refuses direct substitution, accepts a rival project under distinct authorization, and bears consequence. Competence has become a training ground for self-authorship rather than proof she needs nobody.

The terminal proposition is therefore:

> **Yukino does not outgrow competence. She gains greater control over when competence should lead, when it should delegate, when it should stop, and when it should coexist with personal desire that competence cannot justify on its own.**

# 5. Isolation and belonging: Yukino is not cured by becoming socially normal

Yukino's isolation is real, but the series never treats ordinary integration as the cure.

She is uncomfortable with many low-stakes social conventions. She can be poor at flattering, smoothing, or pretending not to notice contradiction. Positive social attention often embarrasses her more than hostility. She can be unexpectedly expressive around cats, awkward around dogs, enthusiastic about narrow interests, and visibly destabilized by friendship language.

None of this requires the theory of a hidden “true Yukino” beneath a false school persona. The Japanese Voice Ledger is especially important here. Her controlled register remains hers even as affect widens. Her surface voice does not have to collapse for the reader to accept that she contains multiple emotional registers.

The series instead teaches Yukino that belonging does not require surrendering difference.

Yui is essential. She does not demand that Yukino become socially soft before friendship is available. She names friendship quickly, receives bluntness as sincerity, offers touch and nicknames, challenges plans, asks for reliance, and remains present through disagreement. Their friendship therefore becomes one of the strongest controls against a developmental model in which Yukino must become “normal” to be loved.

Hachiman provides a different relation. Their early compatibility arises partly because bounded work, insults, silence, and staged unreality lower the demands of ordinary intimacy. They can cooperate while believing conventional romance or friendship is implausible. This is genuine ease, but it contains a risk: because the relation feels legible under exceptional categories, both can underestimate how particular it has become.

Yukino's growth therefore does not move from solitude to sociality in a generic sense. It moves toward **plural belonging**:

- one can remain difficult and be a friend;
- one can disagree and still remain;
- one can hold an institutional role without that role exhausting identity;
- one can be romantically chosen without expelling a rival friend;
- one can graduate without demanding the Service Club remain unchanged;
- one can imagine different universities without treating different trajectories as relational failure.

Belonging becomes less dependent on sameness of method, outcome, or location.

# 6. Family: substitute role, imitation, freedom without recognition

## 6.1 Volume 4: `私は代役でしかない`

Volume 4 supplies the first compact family-role formulation. Yukino explains that public-facing family appearances are normally Haruno's function and says:

> `私は代役でしかないの`

The verified route is V4-L17, `p-0031.xhtml`, printed p.177.

`代役` is important because it introduces **role derivation** before the series develops the mature autonomy vocabulary. Yukino is not simply Haruno's opposite. In some public contexts she enters a role whose canonical occupant is her sister.

This is enough to establish comparison pressure and derivative positioning. It is not enough to prove Yukino has “no self.”

The distinction matters because the later family arc can otherwise be flattened into a revelation that every earlier Yukino trait was copied. That is not what the source supports. Yukino's competence, pride, bluntness, reformism, and work ethic have become hers even where Haruno supplies a model, a contrast, or an inherited family grammar.

## 6.2 Haruno as model, rival, warning, and counter-gaze

Haruno matters to Yukino on several levels at once.

She is:

- the elder sister whose family role was specified earlier and more strongly;
- a model of social competence Yukino does not naturally share;
- a political operator whose methods Yukino increasingly learns to appropriate;
- a source of resentment and comparison;
- a person with privileged family knowledge;
- a warning about what adulthood under inherited obligation can look like;
- a critic who sometimes sees Yukino's drift before Yukino can formulate it;
- a participant whose own wounds make her diagnoses non-neutral.

Yukino's growth is therefore not “stop imitating Haruno.” It is **differentiation through appropriation**.

She becomes more capable of negotiation, strategic social maneuver, family confrontation, institutional politics, and indirect leverage. Those capacities may resemble Haruno's. The difference is not that Yukino preserves a pure original self untouched by her sister. The difference is whether she can own what she uses and refuse what she does not want.

The mature self is not originless.

## 6.3 Volume 12: freedom without recognition is not practiced authorship

Volume 12 makes the family problem more precise than “controlling mother, oppressed daughter.” Yukino's account is paradoxical.

Haruno's future was comparatively predetermined by succession. Yukino was ostensibly freer. Yet that freedom did not amount to practiced self-authorship.

Yukino says she once wanted her father's work. She followed Haruno because she did not know how to act with her own freedom. Earlier expressions of desire were not seriously taken up. She admits that fear of receiving a determinate answer kept her from testing the wish.

The evidence spine is V12-E018 through E039, especially:

- V12-E020: old desire for her father's work;
- V12-E021: succession/maternal structure constrains the field;
- V12-E022: she followed Haruno because she did not know how to act;
- V12-E023–024: this is the first time the desire has been heard in this way;
- V12-E025: fear of a definite answer prevented testing;
- V12-E026: she resolves to decide by her own will and to be able to relinquish knowingly.

This produces one of the strongest autonomy propositions in the entire series:

> **formal freedom is not enough if a person has little practice making a choice that is heard, contested, supported, refused, and lived through as theirs.**

That is why the late Yukino problem cannot be solved by simply telling her “choose for yourself.” The phrase can be liberating in one context and abandonment in another. Self-authorship is relationally practiced.

## 6.4 The mother is authority, not a simple villain

Yukino's mother is formidable. Her speech can reshape the available decision environment without becoming overtly abusive. Her polished register, social legitimacy, family authority, and ability to frame what counts as reasonable make her power unusually effective.

But V2 rejects reducing her to a one-dimensional antagonist. The family scenes reveal:

- real social and institutional stakes;
- a concern with responsibility and consequence;
- a willingness to hear arguments under some conditions;
- role-based conduct that Haruno herself partially distinguishes from private feeling;
- an authority structure that Yukino must negotiate rather than simply escape.

The ending does not remove Yukino from this family through romance. It leaves family negotiation and long-term vocation partly open. That unresolved status is important. Hachiman does not become the magical route out of the Yukinoshita household. Yukino's adulthood must still include inherited structure.

# 7. V04–V06.5: self-revision and the failure of overfunctioning

The cultural-festival movement is the first major test of whether Yukino's ethics can be applied to herself.

Earlier, she wants help to strengthen the person helped. During the festival she becomes the high-capability substitute who prevents institutional failure by absorbing work.

This is not simple hypocrisy. The conditions reward her error.

- the task genuinely needs doing;
- she can do it;
- others benefit from her competence;
- failure has public consequences;
- the office-holder's weakness creates an immediate practical vacuum.

Under those conditions, substitution feels like responsibility.

The correction becomes possible because Yukino is also capable of self-revision. Volume 4 had already shown her apologizing when her model of Rumi's family situation distorts judgment. She is proud, but she is not epistemically frozen.

In Volume 6 the same capacity allows a more significant revision. She can recognize that competent support has become overfunctioning. She does not resolve this by refusing future reliance. Instead, the ending moves toward distributed work.

Yui's particularized request—rely on **us** rather than on an abstract somebody—is essential. Yukino's later request to Yui for help is equally important because Yui admits her limitation rather than performing competence she lacks. Yukino then supplies backup instead of withdrawing the request.

This is a small but unusually clean model of interdependence:

1. a task is specified;
2. help is explicitly requested;
3. limitation is admitted;
4. support is adjusted to capability;
5. responsibility remains distributed rather than silently transferred.

Yukino's request to Hachiman near the end of Volume 6 is even more important linguistically:

> `あなたを頼らせてもらっても、いいかしら`

The causative-benefactive form makes reliance something she asks permission to do. Vulnerability enters through her existing precise feminine register rather than replacing it.

The volume then gives the epistemic pair:

> `あなたのことなんて知らなかったもの`

and:

> `でも、今はあなたを知っている`

This is not a claim of complete knowledge. It is historical re-recognition. She can revise what she thought she knew without concluding that understanding is impossible.

Volume 6.5 then tests whether this correction holds under another Sagami responsibility. It holds incompletely: Yukino continues to support, but Sagami remains the accountable office-holder. V2 therefore freezes the development as **improved support without perfected boundary control**.

# 8. V07–V09: trust, political will, relational injury, and the right to remain uncertain

## 8.1 Kyoto: trust is not unlimited method authorization

Volume 7 gives Hachiman real discretion. Yukino entrusts the problem to him. He then uses that discretion in a way she strongly rejects.

The important point for Yukino is that her objection is method-centered. She does not simply declare Hachiman a bad person. She is personally hurt because trust in his judgment becomes operational license for a method whose costs and role assignments were not jointly authorized.

This matters because Yukino's late development will depend on the difference between:

- trusting someone;
- relying on someone;
- granting discretion;
- granting a particular intervention;
- surrendering authorship.

Those categories are not interchangeable.

## 8.2 Volume 8: `これは私の意志よ` must be taken seriously

Yukino's student-council candidacy is a crucial anti-retrospective control.

When Hachiman asks whether Haruno caused the decision, Yukino answers:

> `これは私の意志よ`

The verified locator is V08-E027, `p-0023.xhtml §032`, PDF p.219.

Later material makes it tempting to read the candidacy as disguised dependence, self-removal, a cry for Hachiman to stop her, or evidence that she does not know what she wants. V2 rejects that flattening.

The decision contains explicit volitional authorship at the time she makes it. The fact that her reason to proceed later collapses after Hachiman changes the institutional environment reveals something important about how external structure supports her choices. It does **not** retroactively make the first-person claim false.

This is one of the strongest cases in the series for preserving prospective ambiguity. A person can genuinely choose something and later discover that the choice depended on conditions they had not fully understood.

Hachiman's error is especially revealing because he grants Iroha a strong anti-coercion principle while discounting Yukino's stated will when her choice threatens the Service Club. Intimacy makes him more willing to substitute his forecast for her answer.

For Yukino, the episode teaches the opposite lesson: being interpreted by someone who knows her well can itself become a threat to authorship.

## 8.3 Volume 9: self-unknowing becomes compatible with request

Volume 9 does not repair the crisis by returning Yukino to certainty.

Instead, she becomes more capable of saying that she did not understand herself or the others as fully as she assumed. The competence identity is destabilized, but not destroyed.

This is an important step because Yukino's early standards can make ignorance feel like failure. The mature series needs another possibility: uncertainty can be a legitimate state from which relation continues.

When Hachiman makes the `本物` request, Yukino does not pretend she understands it completely. She accepts an unfinished request under incomplete understanding. That is already a more mature relation to uncertainty than “I must know correctly before I can act.”

Her later future request is compact and decisive:

> `いつか、私を助けてね。`

The locator is V09-E084, `p-0031.xhtml §142`, printed p.342.

The line is not blanket authorization. It does not say Hachiman may decide what future help means. It establishes something narrower and more consequential:

> **Yukino can imagine a future in which needing help does not disqualify her competence or selfhood.**

This is one reason the later “be independent from Hachiman” cure becomes unstable. The series has already framed future reliance as something Yukino can choose.

# 9. V10–V11: fallibility-compatible trust and the return of the first person

## 9.1 Volume 10 and 10.5: “I do not know yet” is progress

The post-Volume-9 phase is quieter but structurally important.

Yukino can answer personal questions and voluntarily add more than she was asked. She can let Yui's friendships remain plural. She can partially repair old Hayama history without pretending the past is harmless. She can support Iroha as a senior while preserving Iroha's office. She can build contingency around Hachiman's possible failure rather than making success depend on one indispensable person.

This is competence used differently: not to replace another person's authorship, but to preserve it under uncertainty.

Volume 10.5 gives one of the most important Yukino lines in the corpus:

> `以前はそう思っていたけれど。……今は、まだわからない`

The locator is V10_5-E013, `part0009:P223-P232`.

`今は` and `まだ` make uncertainty temporal rather than essential. Yukino does not have to solve her future immediately to count as a subject. She can say that an earlier self-conception no longer governs and that the replacement is not ready.

This is an enormous development for a character whose social authority is often built from knowing what is correct.

## 9.2 Volume 11: Haruno's “no self” claim is pressure, not verdict

Volume 11 intensifies the identity problem. Haruno's criticism is severe. Yukino also uses imagery of drift and anchoring. The temptation is to consolidate all of this into the thesis that Yukino has no self and has always merely imitated or depended.

The ending provides decisive counterevidence.

When Yui's proposed arrangement begins to determine the future, Hachiman rejects it partly in the name of Yukino's authorship. But even that protection risks becoming another act of interpretation over her.

Yukino interrupts:

> `……私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで`

V11-E158 routes this to `part0029.html#p0146`, printed p.316.

She then notes that Hachiman's own request is unfinished and asks:

> `……私の依頼、聞いてもらえるかしら`

V11-E160 routes this to `part0029.html#p0156`, printed p.316.

These lines matter more than an abstract debate over whether Yukino “has a self.”

The self here is enacted procedurally:

- reject another person's assignment of one's feelings;
- mark the unresolved request;
- originate a new request;
- require others to hear it.

The important V2 conclusion is therefore:

> **unstable self-location does not erase subjecthood. It makes authorship practices more, not less, important.**

# 10. Volume 12: authorship before certainty

Volume 12 is Yukino's largest self-authorship advance before the ending because it gives her a problem that cannot be solved by saying either “I am strong” or “I need help.”

She still does not possess a total answer to what she wants relationally. Yet she can begin acting in a way that produces a more answerable future.

## 10.1 She recovers a desire older than the romantic triangle

The decision to discuss her father's work is crucial because it prevents the character from collapsing into a route function.

Yukino has a vocational/family desire whose history predates the mature romantic crisis. It is tied to Haruno, succession, mother, recognition, fear, and the experience of nominal freedom without practiced choice.

This is one of the most important ways V2 broadens the character beyond Hachiman. Her selfhood problem is not created by romance. Romance exposes and intensifies a family-conditioned authorship problem already present.

## 10.2 She asks for witness, not rescue

Yukino's request is deliberately narrow:

> `……私の依頼はひとつだけ。……あなたたちに、その最後を見届けてもらいたい。それだけでいいの`

The source route is V12-E027, `part0010.html#p0340`.

This request matters because it changes the ordinary Service Club grammar. She does not ask Hachiman and Yui to solve the problem. She asks them to witness the outcome of her attempt.

The request recognizes relation without assigning solution ownership.

Yukino then commits to returning home and stating her future hope. She wants at least this much put into words so she can reach a state she can accept. Haruno, once convinced she is serious, offers cooperation. That support does not automatically undermine authorship because Yukino originates the aim and Haruno's help remains a response to it.

This is a positive family-side control against the idea that autonomy requires refusing all assistance.

## 10.3 The prom is a competence test, but not merely a competence test

Yukino uses the prom as a way to practice owning a difficult project and its consequences. The project has real institutional substance. It is not simply a romantic metaphor.

She has to:

- propose;
- organize;
- negotiate;
- withstand adult pressure;
- coordinate students;
- respond to institutional opposition;
- confront family authority;
- decide what outside help she will accept.

The prom therefore turns public competence into a laboratory for personal authorship.

But V2 also preserves the danger of overcorrection. Yukino can begin treating successful independent execution as proof that she is properly autonomous. That is not the mature endpoint.

## 10.4 `お願い。私にやらせて`: refusal is object-specific

When Hachiman begins moving toward solution mode, Yukino explicitly asks him not to take over:

> `あなたの力はもう借りないわ`

and:

> `お願い。私にやらせて`

V12-E156 routes the boundary to `part0024.html#p0413`.

The correct interpretation is granular.

She does not say:

- leave my life;
- stop caring;
- never help me again;
- I no longer want future relation;
- I must prove that I need nobody.

She says, in substance:

> **do not do this task for me.**

This distinction becomes essential in Volume 13, where direct-help refusal and rivalry authorization diverge.

## 10.5 `依存` is self-critique, not total diagnosis

Yukino also uses dependence language about herself. That self-description has high authority because it is hers. But the V2 method refuses to inflate it into a theorem.

Her later articulation makes the mechanism clearer: she had come to expect that Hachiman would somehow make things work. The dependence lies significantly in **expectation and burden allocation**.

This supports real self-critique.

It does not establish:

- inability to function;
- falsity of affection;
- total emotional dependence;
- retrospective pathology of every earlier act of reliance;
- separation as the only cure.

The distinction between mechanism and meaning is one of the most important protections against overreading Yukino in the final three volumes.

# 11. Volume 13: a self-authored answer can still be wrong or incomplete

Volume 13 is where the series decisively separates **having agency** from **making the ethically final choice**.

## 11.1 Refusing direct help does not mean refusing relation

Hachiman's initial task is to honor Yukino's direct-help boundary.

He does not simply join her prom team. Instead, he creates a rival institutional proposal. The method still manipulates the broader environment, but it preserves more of Yukino's direct operational authorship than takeover would.

Crucially, Yukino later accepts the rivalry/wager.

This creates a clean authorization distinction:

- direct assistance inside her project: refused;
- continued relation: not refused;
- institutional rivalry: later authorized;
- future help in every imaginable form: not globally settled.

The case defeats a recurring analytic error in which one refusal is treated as a total statement of relational desire.

## 11.2 `あなたに依存していたの`: expectation becomes speakable

Near the end Yukino says:

> `そう思うくらい、……あなたに依存していたの`

The verified route is `p-0034.xhtml§228`, PDF p.356.

This is strong first-person evidence that Yukino recognizes how much she had come to expect Hachiman to make the prom succeed.

The important point is not to argue the word away. V2 accepts the self-critique.

The equally important point is not to universalize it. Yukino's statement does not negate the earlier evidence that she can function independently, make choices, ask for bounded help, or refuse intervention. Dependence exists inside capability.

## 11.3 The closure request: agency can overreach

Yukino then uses the wager to ask Hachiman to grant Yui's wish, explicitly confirming that this is her own wish.

This is one of the most important Yukino moments precisely because V2 refuses both easy interpretations.

It is not passive surrender. She originates the request. She owns it.

It is also not automatically the ethically correct answer merely because it is self-authored. The request allocates a future involving Hachiman and Yui without their independent authorization.

Hachiman's triadic correction—this is not something the two of us may decide by ourselves—therefore does not erase Yukino's agency. It reveals that **agency is necessary but not sufficient for answerable relation**.

This is a major maturation of the series' autonomy ethic:

> **a person must be allowed to make their own mistake, but another person's authorship remains a boundary on what that self-authored mistake may decide for them.**

## 11.4 Self-removal is a late strategy, not Yukino's essence

The V1-to-V2 revision ledger freezes this point strongly. Yukino's self-removal is real and important, but it belongs to a specific late crisis.

It should not be projected backward over a character who has repeatedly:

- competed;
- pursued roles;
- expressed will;
- set boundaries;
- confronted Haruno;
- made requests;
- supported Yui;
- sought Hachiman's help;
- argued for her own methods.

The late self-removal strategy is better understood as a defensive solution generated from a distorted premise: if her reliance and desire are harming the others, perhaps the responsible answer is to withdraw herself from the equation.

Volume 14 will reject that premise without declaring the original concern insincere.

# 12. Volume 14: reciprocal claim, explicit desire, capability-aware support

Volume 14 gives Yukino three distinct kinds of agency that should not be collapsed into one romantic climax.

## 12.1 She first negotiates with Yui directly

Before the final Hachiman/Yukino resolution, Yukino and Yui speak about the proposed ending without requiring Hachiman to interpret one girl to the other.

This is essential to Yukino's development because it protects the Yui relationship from becoming a subsidiary of the romance. Yukino can state feeling to the person whose interests conflict with hers. Yui can reject unilateral closure. Their friendship can survive the fact that no solution gives both women the same romantic outcome.

Yukino's authorship therefore includes the ability to remain answerable to someone she hurts without converting that hurt into a reason to erase herself.

## 12.2 The bridge scene: she is not merely the recipient of Hachiman's confession

The bridge scene begins imperfectly. Hachiman engineers the encounter and physically stops Yukino briefly. His first formulation also risks recreating the helper/beneficiary asymmetry.

The scene becomes more answerable after physical release.

Yukino remains.

She questions his terms. She rejects his devaluation of herself. She pressures him for adequate articulation. When he gives her permission not to answer, she refuses the escape:

> `私はちゃんと言うわ`

She then converts his asymmetrical life offer into a reciprocal request:

> `あなたの人生を、私にください`

The locator is V14 `p-0032 §456`.

Grammatically and ethically, this matters. She does not merely accept being chosen. She makes a counter-claim.

The relationship is therefore not resolved as:

> rescuer chooses beneficiary.

It becomes:

> **two people make reciprocal future claims while retaining the right to contest what those claims mean.**

## 12.3 `一人で立てるのに……` / `知ってる`

The later stair exchange is the cleaner autonomy image.

Yukino says:

> `一人で立てるのに……`

Hachiman answers:

> `知ってる`

The Yukino line is at V14 `p-0036 §600`.

This exchange is more important for Yukino's autonomy than a simple “she learned to accept help” reading.

Her independent capability is explicitly acknowledged **before** support is accepted.

The old implicit equation would be:

> help is justified by incapacity.

The late alternative is:

> **help may be chosen because relation itself has value even when incapacity is absent.**

This is why “chosen interdependence” is a better terminal description than either dependence or independence.

## 12.4 `あなたが好きよ。比企谷くん`: desire becomes ordinary grammar

Yukino later states:

> `あなたが好きよ。比企谷くん`

The locator is V14 `p-0040 §140`.

The sentence is strikingly ordinary. It is not another conceptual riddle. It does not define `本物`. It does not contain a grand theory of love. It names a conventional romantic proposition.

That ordinariness is part of the character development.

Yukino's controlled, feminine, precise voice does not have to become someone else's voice to carry desire. The same register that once made intimate labels difficult can now state affection directly.

But the simple sentence arrives **after** the complex reciprocal negotiation. It adds clarity without exhausting the relationship's meaning.

The final choice therefore establishes romantic direction, not metaphysical certification.

# 13. Volume 14.5: ordinary futurity without fusion

The post-mainline material is valuable because it tests whether the large claims of Volume 14 can survive ordinary logistics.

Only the true post-ending stories should govern this test; the earlier New Year stories belong to the post-V09 chronology and the festival story belongs after the joint prom but before the final mainline stabilization.

## 13.1 Separate trajectories remain legitimate

Hachiman and Yukino discuss universities and cram schools without requiring identical destinations.

Hachiman explicitly rejects the possibility that Yukino should lower an academic target to match him. This is significant because romance does not become a reason for merger.

The relation must coexist with differentiated ambition.

## 13.2 `一緒の方が頑張れると思うし`: ordinary chosen reliance

Yukino eventually says that being together would help her work:

> `一緒の方が頑張れると思うし`

The source route is V14_5-E077, `part0021 ¶109-126`.

The line is modest. It does not claim incapacity. It does not ask Hachiman to alter his educational trajectory. It names a preference for shared environment because shared environment increases motivation.

This is one of the cleanest post-ending confirmations of the autonomy thesis:

> **support can improve performance and emotional life without replacing capability.**

## 13.3 `私が勝手にやっているだけだから`: initiative without debt

Yukino brings handmade cookies for the one-month anniversary. Hachiman panics because he has not prepared a reciprocal gift. She says:

> `私が勝手にやっているだけだから`

The route is V14_5-E086, `part0021 ¶220-225`.

This use of `勝手に` is important because the series often uses unilateral-action vocabulary negatively. Here Yukino marks the gift as **her own initiative** and releases Hachiman from reciprocal obligation.

The act is self-authored without becoming jurisdiction over him.

## 13.4 Succession: Yukino can transmit practice without reproducing herself

The final Service Club problem concerns Komachi.

Yukino does not argue that the club must preserve her presidency, membership structure, or exact culture. She points out that even during her own tenure new people entered and that Komachi may form irreplaceable relationships of her own.

Later, Yukino teaches Komachi to make tea.

That image is a compact terminal statement of Yukino's relation to inheritance.

She can pass on:

- practice;
- memory;
- technique;
- care;
- institutional history.

She does not need Komachi to become “the next Yukino.”

This is the positive counterpart to her own family problem. Mature inheritance is not replication.

# 14. Yukino and Hachiman: from mutual exceptionalism to reciprocal answerability

The Hachiman/Yukino relationship is central to Yukino, but it should not become the interpretive container for her entire character.

Its development is best understood as a movement through changing forms of **standing**.

## 14.1 Early: adversarial intimacy and idealization risk

The pair quickly develop unusual ease around work, insults, silence, and the belief that ordinary intimacy is unlikely. Their similarities are real, but Hachiman overstates them.

Yukino is not simply a female Hachiman. She has stronger reformist confidence, different relations to effort and responsibility, different family structure, different willingness to inhabit institutional roles, and a different relationship to social approval.

Hachiman's early idealization therefore creates both intimacy and risk. He makes Yukino's directness into an epistemic guarantee. Volume 5 breaks that guarantee.

The repair matters because Yukino does not simply apologize for failing his image. She helps construct a more provisional method: ask again, revise, do not treat one wrong model as the end of relation.

## 14.2 Middle: trust is real, but trust does not grant jurisdiction

Kyoto and the election arc demonstrate that Hachiman can care deeply and still override Yukino's authorship.

This is one of the series' most important romantic corrections. Greater intimacy does not automatically make private interpretation more legitimate. In fact, intimacy can make the helper more willing to believe they know the person's “real” good.

Yukino's `これは私の意志よ` and later `私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで` form a longitudinal boundary against that tendency.

## 14.3 Post-Volume-9: asking replaces telepathy

The relationship improves when Hachiman begins asking rather than deriving.

He asks about her future. She discloses voluntarily. He treats the answer as private rather than distributable information. Yukino can later ask him for future help without defining the exact terms.

The relationship becomes less “effortless” and more usable because both can survive being wrong about each other.

## 14.4 V12–V13: Hachiman must learn that desire to help is not authorization

Yukino's explicit refusal of direct help is the key test.

Hachiman's mature task is not to stop caring. It is to change the form of involvement.

This is why the rival-prom structure matters. It demonstrates that relation can continue after an object-specific refusal if the parties later authorize another form of interaction.

Yukino's self-critique of dependence then prevents the relationship from becoming a savior fantasy. She acknowledges expectation. Hachiman must acknowledge neededness as part of his own self-worth system.

The romance can survive only if neither party requires the other to remain permanently rescuer or rescued.

## 14.5 V14: reciprocal claims rather than discovered destiny

The ending does not reveal that Hachiman and Yukino were “the genuine pair all along.” It records a historically produced choice.

Their relation contains:

- early attraction;
- intellectual recognition;
- idealization;
- disappointment;
- repair;
- trust;
- unauthorized intervention;
- explicit refusal;
- reliance;
- rivalry;
- dependence;
- desire;
- negotiation;
- romantic reciprocity.

The result is meaningful because the history is not pure.

Yukino's contribution to the final relation is active. She stays. She interrogates. She rejects self-devaluation. She answers. She claims.

That is why the final Hachiman/Yukino state should be described as **reciprocal chosen involvement under acknowledged capability**, not rescue, destiny, or certification.

# 15. Yukino and Yui: friendship independent of romantic outcome

A Yukino monograph that treats Yui only as romantic rival is structurally incomplete.

The Yukino–Yui friendship begins before the final triangle is stabilized and develops its own history.

Yui is the faster relationship-namer. She offers nicknames, friendship language, touch, invitations, and low-risk continuity. Yukino reciprocates more often through action than label.

Over time the dyad develops:

- gratitude;
- gift-giving;
- direct requests;
- admitted limitations;
- conflict tolerance;
- rivalry;
- criticism;
- emotional knowledge;
- refusal of self-removal;
- post-romantic ordinary cooperation.

## 15.1 Yui teaches Yukino that reliance can be particular

During the cultural-festival phase, Yui's request is not “rely on someone.” It is closer to “rely on us.”

That particularization matters. Yukino's movement is from abstract competence toward chosen people.

The later direct work exchange—Yukino asks; Yui says what she cannot do; Yukino adjusts—is one of the healthiest early dependence controls in the series.

## 15.2 Rivalry does not cancel friendship

Kyoto, the election, Christmas, the aquarium, the prom, and final choice all place the girls in situations where their desired futures are not identical.

The friendship survives not because they suppress that difference forever but because they increasingly make the difference answerable.

Volume 11 is important because Yui can state a future she wants while Yukino can refuse to have her feelings decided. Volume 13 is important because Yukino's self-removal tries to settle Yui's future and Yui rejects the premise. Volume 14 is important because both girls can name incompatible romantic desire without treating friendship as automatically void.

## 15.3 Post-ending ordinariness is durability evidence

Volume 14.5 shows Yukino and Yui shopping for Komachi, coordinating a surprise, exchanging looks, jointly presenting a mug, and participating in ordinary club succession.

The text does not stage their friendship as a solemn post-romantic treaty.

That ordinariness matters. It does not prove Yui's pain vanished. It proves the friendship has enough independent authorship to continue after non-equivalence becomes known.

The terminal proposition is:

> **Yui is not the person Yukino must lose in order to become Hachiman's partner. She is a separately chosen friend whose continued standing prevents romantic resolution from becoming relational purification.**

# 16. Yukino and Haruno: imitation, accusation, and differentiated adulthood

Haruno is both indispensable and dangerous to Yukino analysis.

She has access to family history unavailable to most characters. She can often identify a mechanism before the younger trio can name it. She is socially and politically more practiced than Yukino. She also has strong personal stakes in whether Yukino reproduces, escapes, or transforms the family pattern Haruno herself inherited.

## 16.1 Haruno's accusation contains history

When Haruno says Yukino has no self, the statement lands because the series has supplied real supporting pressures:

- Haruno's role preceded Yukino's public role;
- Yukino sometimes describes herself as substitute;
- Yukino followed Haruno's path when she did not know how to use her freedom;
- Yukino can seek external reasons to move;
- Yukino sometimes wants an authoritative answer that relieves ambiguity.

But the statement is too total.

Against it stand:

- explicit election will;
- early reformist conviction;
- direct family desire;
- personal refusals;
- self-authored requests;
- political choices;
- willingness to disagree with Haruno;
- late romantic claims.

The best V2 conclusion is therefore not “Haruno is wrong” but:

> **Haruno identifies a real differentiation problem and overstates it into an ontology of selfhood.**

## 16.2 Yukino learns from Haruno without becoming Haruno

Late Yukino becomes more strategic. She negotiates, uses institutional leverage, thinks politically, and becomes more comfortable with social maneuver.

This should not be read as contamination by Haruno or evidence that her earlier bluntness was naive.

It is developmental appropriation.

Yukino can learn a technique from an elder sister without accepting the elder sister's entire life. That distinction is the same mature inheritance principle later embodied when Yukino teaches Komachi tea without demanding replication.

## 16.3 Haruno also becomes capable of supporting a Yukino-authored choice

Volume 12 gives an important corrective to the “cruel oracle” reading. Once Yukino states a serious family aim, Haruno offers cooperation and says she will support the choice whether it is right or wrong.

This is one of the series' cleanest positive formulations of answerable support:

> respect for authorship does not require certainty that the authored choice is correct.

Haruno remains provocative and difficult. But the sister relation cannot be reduced to domination.

# 17. Japanese voice: controlled femininity and increasingly speakable agency

Yukino's Japanese voice is one of the strongest pieces of evidence against a “mask falls off” character arc.

Her surface register is stable.

She uses:

- `私`;
- relatively complete syntax;
- feminine endings such as `〜わ`, `〜かしら`, `〜もの`;
- careful premise correction;
- semantic precision;
- controlled politeness;
- occasional formal closure when a boundary is being enforced.

She does not become agentic by speaking like Hachiman, Yui, Iroha, or Haruno.

## 17.1 Precision becomes first-person rather than merely corrective

Early precision is often directed outward:

- your premise is wrong;
- that request means something else;
- your excuse does not justify the behavior.

Late precision increasingly specifies the boundaries of Yukino's own standing:

- `これは私の意志よ`;
- `私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで`;
- `私の依頼はひとつだけ`;
- `これが私の願い`.

The repeated `私の` is not a magic authenticity marker. It is a longitudinal grammar of possession under conditions where other people repeatedly interpret, protect, diagnose, or plan around her.

## 17.2 Requests soften without becoming vague

Yukino's vulnerable requests often show slight hesitation or grammatical softening:

- `手伝ってもらえると助かる、のだけれど……`;
- `あなたを頼らせてもらっても、いいかしら`;
- `……私の依頼、聞いてもらえるかしら`.

The important point is not that softer speech is more sincere. It is that the request remains bounded and answerable.

This is why `お願い。私にやらせて` is so important. Non-intervention itself becomes a specified request rather than an implication Hachiman is expected to infer.

## 17.3 Composure breaks do not reveal a separate “real Yukino”

Volume 11 gives fragments and ellipses around especially consequential first-person speech. The cost of speaking becomes audible.

But V2 rejects the idea that the composed Yukino is false and the broken voice is true.

The controlled register remains hers. The break matters because the register must carry more than it usually carries.

## 17.4 Ordinary syntax can bear extraordinary commitment

`あなたが好きよ。比企谷くん` is aesthetically powerful because it is ordinary.

The line does not need philosophical ornament to become authoritative. It is a conventional romantic sentence spoken by the same character whose syntax has been stable for volumes.

Growth is therefore **expanded carrying capacity**, not voice replacement.

# 18. Helping as identity: from corrector to author of bounded support

Helping is not merely a Service Club activity for Yukino. It is one of the earliest ways she can make an ethical self visible.

She believes people should improve. She takes work seriously. She is offended by systems that reward avoidance. She is often willing to do more than her formal share because she experiences competent action as responsibility.

This makes helping both vocation and risk.

## 18.1 Early positive form: strengthen the subject

At her best, Yukino's helping ethic protects the recipient's agency. She wants the person to do the act that makes the outcome theirs.

The Rumi case supplies a partial version through the threshold `彼女が助けを求めるなら`: wait for the subject's request. V2 recognizes the principle while also noting its weakness—people under shame, fear, childhood pressure, or hierarchy may not be able to formulate explicit requests cleanly.

The late series therefore keeps the authorization concern but develops more flexible forms.

## 18.2 Failure form: competence replaces the subject

Sagami is the clearest early case. Yukino's competence makes substitution possible.

This is not a reason to condemn her work ethic. It is the reason later Yukino must distinguish **the value of accomplishing a task** from **the value of another person practicing responsibility**.

## 18.3 Mature form: bounded cooperation

Late Yukino can:

- request help;
- accept admitted limitation;
- preserve another person's office;
- refuse specific intervention;
- authorize a different form of involvement;
- negotiate family support;
- transmit practice to Komachi without dictating the successor form.

Her helping identity therefore survives. What changes is its jurisdiction.

# 19. Desire: what Yukino wants, and when she can say it

Yukino's desire should not be reduced to romance. The series gives her multiple desire domains whose visibility develops at different rates.

## 19.1 Ethical desire

Early Yukino wants a world in which ability is not punished and weakness is not simply affirmed. She wants improvement to be possible. This desire is ideological and sincere even where her methods are too severe.

## 19.2 Relational desire

She wants Yui's presence before she is comfortable naming friendship. She values Hachiman's competence and company before the relation can be named conventionally. She wants the Service Club to continue before its institutional form becomes sufficient to explain why.

## 19.3 Family/vocational desire

She once wanted her father's work. The family structure leaves that desire under-practiced rather than fully extinguished. Volume 12 recovers it as something worth stating and testing even if the answer is refusal.

This domain is essential because it demonstrates a Yukino future not exhausted by the romantic pair.

## 19.4 Desire for authorship

By the prom phase Yukino wants not merely a successful event but the experience of choosing, attempting, and bearing consequence herself.

That desire can become excessive when “doing it myself” turns into an independence test. Yet the underlying need for authorship is real.

## 19.5 Romantic desire

Yukino's romantic desire becomes increasingly difficult to deny before she names it, but V2 preserves prospective evidence discipline. Earlier warmth, jealousy, salience, embarrassment, or withdrawal should not be rewritten as explicit knowledge from Volume 1 onward.

What matters is that by Volume 14 she can own the proposition directly.

## 19.6 Desire for continued relation without merger

Volume 14.5 gives the least dramatic and perhaps most durable desire form: see one another, study in the same environment when useful, mark time, keep meeting across different institutions, let Komachi's club become its own thing.

The progression is therefore not from “unknown desire” to “romantic answer.” It is from **desire that often requires role, task, or another person's framework to become actionable toward desire increasingly attributable to Yukino herself across multiple domains**.

# 20. Independence, dependence, and the mature autonomy model

Yukino is the character through whom *Oregairu* most directly rejects the equation:

> independence = health.

The series also rejects the inverse:

> reliance = intimacy, therefore reliance is always good.

The mature framework is procedural.

## 20.1 Capability

Can Yukino perform the action herself?

Often yes.

Capability matters because help should not be justified by invented helplessness.

## 20.2 Authorship

Who chooses the goal, accepts the consequence, and retains the right to revise?

This becomes the central late question.

## 20.3 Authorization

What form of involvement has Yukino actually accepted?

The V12–V13 distinction between direct help and rivalry is the clearest case.

## 20.4 Burden allocation

Has Yukino silently come to expect someone else to make the outcome work?

Her `依存` self-critique says yes in a specific domain.

## 20.5 Relation

Does acknowledging capability require withdrawing support?

The stairs scene says no.

## 20.6 Terminal formula

The Dependency / Autonomy Ledger supplies the strongest concise statement:

> **Yukino becomes more autonomous not by needing less relation, but by gaining greater authorship over the relation she chooses.**

That formulation should govern later synthesis unless Phase 6 produces counterevidence strong enough to revise it.

---

# 21. Final choice and future: romance does not end the selfhood problem

Yukino's final choice matters enormously. It does not solve everything.

The mainline resolves:

- that Yukino and Hachiman choose reciprocal romantic involvement;
- that the relation can include support without presuming incapacity;
- that Yukino can state desire conventionally;
- that Yui's friendship retains standing despite non-equivalence;
- that self-removal is not the necessary cure for acknowledged dependence.

It does not resolve:

- Yukino's long-term vocational path;
- the eventual outcome of the family business question;
- the degree to which her mother will adapt;
- how Yukino's relation to Haruno changes across adulthood;
- whether her tendency to turn self-restraint into virtue fully disappears;
- how she and Hachiman negotiate future conflicts where help and autonomy again collide.

This incompleteness is aesthetically appropriate.

A weaker ending would make romance a release from family. Another weaker ending would make perfect independence a prerequisite for love. *Oregairu* instead leaves Yukino in a more difficult adult position: she can love someone, remain close to Yui, pursue education, confront family, inherit some practices, refuse others, and still be uncertain.

The self is no longer a secret answer to be discovered. It is something practiced across choices.

# 22. Major counter-readings and V2 judgments

## 22.1 “Yukino is actually weak; competence is only armor”

**REJECT as total explanation.**

Competence is repeatedly demonstrated as actual capacity. It can become armor and role capture without ceasing to be real.

## 22.2 “Yukino cannot rely on other people until late in the series”

**REJECT.**

Volume 3 already contains explicit bounded reliance. Volume 6 makes reliance more personal and reciprocal. The later development concerns emotionally consequential reliance and authorship, not the first discovery of help.

## 22.3 “The election proves Yukino wanted Hachiman to stop her”

**REJECT as governing reading.**

`これは私の意志よ` has direct first-person authority. Later loss of reason to proceed complicates the choice; it does not erase it.

## 22.4 “Haruno correctly reveals that Yukino has no self”

**DOWNGRADE to high-value participant diagnosis.**

Haruno identifies real imitation and differentiation pressures. Yukino's repeated first-person authorship prevents the total claim from becoming narrator truth.

## 22.5 “Yukino becomes independent in V12–V13 and is therefore ready for romance”

**REJECT.**

V12–V13 are authorship experiments, not purification. Yukino's self-authored closure itself overreaches. V14 explicitly values support after capability has been acknowledged.

## 22.6 “Yukino's dependence admission proves the relationship with Hachiman was unhealthy”

**REVISE.**

The admission establishes a real expectation/burden-allocation mechanism. It does not decide the entire relation's meaning or required cure.

## 22.7 “Yukino withdraws because she realizes Hachiman and Yui are the natural pair”

**REJECT.**

The late self-removal has mixed motives and is contested by Yui and Hachiman. It should not become a retrospective route verdict.

## 22.8 “Hachiman's final choice makes Yukino authentic”

**REJECT.**

Romantic reciprocity is an answer to one domain. Yukino's self-authorship develops before it and remains open after it.

## 22.9 “Yui becomes expendable once Yukino is chosen”

**REJECT.**

The Yukino–Yui friendship has independent history and post-choice durability.

## 22.10 “The final Yukino is the true Yukino without roles”

**REJECT.**

Late Yukino still uses roles, institutional language, controlled politeness, competence, strategic behavior, and inherited practices. Growth is ownership and revision, not role abolition.

# 23. Specialist claim freeze

The following propositions are frozen as the current Phase-5 Yukino synthesis claims. They remain subject to the later adversarial Japanese-source audit.

### YK-SYN-01 — Competence is real

Yukino's capability must not be reduced to compensatory armor merely because competence can become defensive or substitutionary.

### YK-SYN-02 — Corrective idealism precedes generic aloofness
