# Oregairu V2 — Volume 03 Deep Reading

## From reset to restart: particularity, safe intimacy, and the first appearance of `本物`

# 1. Source identity and publication position

## 1.2 Bonus-track provenance

The volume contains a prose bonus, `ぼーなすとらっく！「たとえばこんなバースデーソング」`.

Its own editorial note explicitly states that:

- it is a **novel-form rewrite** of the drama script included with the Volume 3 limited edition;
- the drama is set **immediately after the Volume 3 main story**;
- the prose rewrite differs somewhat from the audio version.

Accordingly, this artifact treats the bonus prose as **Watari-authored attached primary supplementary material** but keeps it analytically distinct from the six-chapter main narrative. Vocal-delivery claims from the actual drama CD remain available for a later track-level audio audit; they are not silently imported from V1 here.

## 1.3 Publication-position boundary

The prospective pass permits knowledge of Volumes 1–2 only.

At this point the reader knows:

- Hachiman was injured saving Yui's dog before high school;
- Hachiman has interpreted Yui's later kindness through that accident;
- Yui has begun making particular choices involving Hachiman but has not successfully defined them to him;
- Yukino and Yui have become friends through the Service Club;
- Hachiman and Yukino possess a distinctive low-contact compatibility built around distance, argument, and limited personal inquiry;
- Yukino's family position has been lightly disturbed by Kawasaki's reference to her father, but Haruno has not yet appeared;
- the later `本物`, `共依存`, prom, and final-choice frameworks do not exist for the prospective reader.

Volume 3 must first be read inside that epistemic boundary.

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

## 2.1 What problem does Volume 3 initially appear to pose?

The volume begins with Hachiman believing that the dog-accident problem has been solved through **reset**.

His logic is simple:

1. Yui's kindness was produced by guilt or obligation.
2. Clarifying that obligation removes the false premise.
3. The relationship can return to a safer distance.
4. Hachiman regains emotional equilibrium; Yui returns to her ordinary social life.

He explicitly treats this as a correct solution.

The narrative immediately tests it by removing Yui from the clubroom.

The resulting absence reveals something neither Hachiman nor Yukino can comfortably say: the club has already changed enough that her nonpresence has become perceptible as a **loss of ordinary structure**.

Hachiman looks at the door. Yukino tracks Yui's excuses and messages. Both remember the room as recently noisy. Neither simply resumes the original two-person arrangement.

The first prospective proposition is therefore:

> **A relationship can be declared reset before the people living inside it have actually returned to their prior state.**

## 2.2 The volume's immediate opposition: reset versus continuation

Hachiman's favored relational operation is initially technological/accounting language:

- reset;
- delete;
- restore distance;
- zero out obligation;
- end the matter.

The story gradually introduces another possibility:

- leave the past event intact;
- remove an illegitimate obligation from it;
- permit present choices to generate a new relationship.

This distinction is not yet the mature theory of `本物`. It is a more basic question of **whether history determines relational meaning permanently**.

## 2.3 What Volume 3 does not yet authorize

A prospective reader should not yet conclude that:

- Yukino is romantically sacrificing herself for Hachiman and Yui;
- Haruno's entire personality is fake;
- Yukino's family dynamics are fully understood;
- Hachiman consciously knows Yui loves him and simply refuses the fact;
- `本物` already possesses its Volume 9–14 meaning;
- the final invisible line is definitively the romantic triangle rather than a still-unrevealed factual asymmetry.

The volume strongly invites some of those later readings. It does not yet settle them.

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

Volume 3 is unusually coherent beneath its episodic rom-com surface. Its six main chapters repeatedly ask what kinds of relationships are safe enough for Hachiman to accept, what kinds of desire can survive an imperfect beginning, and who gets to define what another person's behavior means.

## 3.1 Chapter 1 — Yui's absence converts atmosphere into a problem

Yui becomes caught between Miura's group and the Service Club. Hachiman sees her hesitate and removes himself so she does not have to choose around him.

That act looks considerate and is partly considerate. It also repeats his characteristic unilateral assumption:

> if his presence creates pressure, the correct solution is to erase his claim rather than ask what the other person wants.

The more revealing development occurs afterward. Yui stops attending the club for roughly a week. Hachiman expects relationships in this condition to fade naturally because that is what happened to his previous classmates. Yukino, however, refuses to ask Yui directly whether she will return because she predicts Yui would say yes **even if she did not want to**.

This is one of Yukino's earliest sophisticated acts of agency-respecting care. She recognizes that a direct request can be coercive when addressed to someone strongly disposed to accommodate.

Hiratsuka then enters and refuses to let Hachiman and Yukino turn passive decay into moral neutrality. She declares that the club is not a friendship club or a simulation of youth; the assignment she has imposed is `自己変革` — self-transformation. She orders them to replenish the club with someone who has genuine will to participate.

The institutional task is artificial. Its pedagogical function is transparent enough to be legible: Hachiman and Yukino are being forced to confront what Yui's absence has done to them without Hiratsuka coercing Yui herself into returning.

## 3.2 Chapter 2 — Saika establishes the safe-affection control case

The Saika outing is much more important in V2 than it first appears.

Saika asks Hachiman to spend time with him. Hachiman explicitly reasons that if a girl gave him the same invitation, he would:

- search the surroundings for people running a punishment game;
- verify that no trap was visible;
- and decline anyway, just to be safe.

Saika is categorized as male. That category creates what Hachiman calls an **absolute sense of security**.

Because he believes he cannot legitimately interpret Saika's kindness as heterosexual romantic interest, he can accept:

- invitation;
- companionship;
- use of his given name, `八幡`;
- concern for his mood;
- a promise to make more memories together.

Saika later reveals that the entire outing was partly an attempt to cheer Hachiman because he had seemed down.

This gives the reread an unusually clean control variable:

> **Hachiman is not generally incapable of receiving care. He is much more capable of receiving care when the interpretive category removes the danger of romantic false-positive humiliation.**

That will matter when the volume constructs a second “safe intimacy” case with Yukino.

## 3.3 Chapter 3 — Yukino's affect range and the Yui encounter

The pet event broadens Yukino without requiring the crude model of a hidden “true Yukino” underneath a false public self.

She displays:

- intense knowledge and enthusiasm around cats;
- embarrassment about the degree of that enthusiasm;
- physical caution around dogs;
- a willingness to move closer to Hachiman when she feels unsafe;
- more spontaneous facial and bodily reactions than her school persona ordinarily permits.

The right V2 formulation is **register expansion** rather than mask removal. Yukino has a wider affective range than the role Hachiman usually observes at school.

Yui then unexpectedly encounters Hachiman and Yukino together. Her question about why they are alone together, her difficulty looking at Yukino, and her relief later when the apparent romantic explanation disappears all make the emerging triangle more socially legible without yet settling it.

The chapter's most important Yukino moment is her birthday plan. She has inferred Yui's birthday from `0618` in Yui's address. More importantly, she wants to celebrate Yui **even if Yui never returns to the Service Club**.

That condition matters.

The gift is not a recruitment incentive and not payment for future participation. Yukino says she wants to convey the gratitude she already feels. This is care deliberately separated from leverage.

Hachiman infers that Yui is probably Yukino's first friend. That is a plausible and later-supported interpretation, but it remains **Hachiman's inference**, not a direct textual declaration from Yukino.

## 3.4 Chapter 4 — bounded reliance, staged romance, and Haruno

Yukino recognizes that her own value criteria are poorly calibrated to choosing a present for Yui. Instead of pretending competence, she asks Hachiman for assistance:

`手伝ってもらえると助かる、のだけれど……`

The line is difficult for her to say, but she says it.

This is an important V1 correction. Yukino does **not** lack the ability to rely on other people in general. She can:

- identify a bounded practical deficit;
- identify another person's useful competence;
- request help;
- and incorporate that help into her own action.

Her later difficulty must therefore be defined more narrowly: asking for help when the object is **her own desire, identity, family position, or emotionally consequential choice** is much harder than asking for task assistance on behalf of someone else.

The shopping sequence then creates Hachiman's second safe-intimacy control case.

To stop staff from treating Hachiman as suspicious, Yukino proposes that for the day they behave **as though they were lovers**. Hachiman accepts precisely because the formulation guarantees the opposite of actual romance. His internal reasoning is explicit:

- Yukino does not lie;
- `恋人のように` means “like lovers,” not lovers;
- therefore nothing romantic can actually happen;
- therefore the performance is safe.

He even conceptualizes their certainty that nothing will happen between them as a kind of trust.

This parallels Saika almost exactly:

> **Intimacy becomes acceptable when its governing category contains its own disavowal of romantic possibility.**

This is also the first major seed of Hachiman's later idealization problem. `雪ノ下は噓を吐かない` is not an omniscient narrative statement. It is an axiom Hachiman currently uses to stabilize his model of Yukino.

Haruno then enters.

Her warmth, physical proximity, facial expressiveness, and frictionless sociability initially fit an idealized model of approachable femininity almost too perfectly. Hachiman becomes suspicious of the very lack of friction. He asks whether the radiance she projects is `本物`.

Yukino later confirms that Haruno's public-facing style is an `外面` and a `仮面` produced through years of being taken to greetings, parties, and other family-political obligations as the eldest daughter.

The key limitation is equally important:

> The text establishes a cultivated public persona. It does **not** establish that Haruno has no sincere warmth, no authentic affection, or no personality outside manipulation.

Hachiman contrasts Haruno with the smile he believes he has seen from Yukino — a smile that does not flatter, deceive, or evade. He calls that smile `本物`.

This is the first moment in which V2 must simultaneously credit and distrust him. He has recognized a real difference in social performance, but he also assumes with striking confidence that he knows which Yukino expression is her unmediated “real” one.

## 3.5 Chapter 5 — desire, method, and the argument Hachiman hears before rejecting it

The Zaimokuza/game-club chapter looked relatively comic in V1. In the V2 structure, it is one of Volume 3's most important preparatory arguments.

The game-club students attack Zaimokuza because his desire to make games is not supported by the “correct” history, training, breadth, and technique. They call him a `偽物`.

Hachiman initially agrees with much of their factual criticism. Zaimokuza is lazy, derivative, and insufficiently trained. But Hachiman objects when correctness of method is elevated into authority over desire itself.

He asks what is actually correct about outsourcing one's correctness to tradition and authority. The volume uses `依存` here negatively — dependence on inherited cultural capital and authority — but this is not yet interpersonal dependency pathology.

Yui then delivers one of the most important lines in the early series.

She admits she does not understand games very well. She says, hesitantly, that even if the way something began was not correct, and even if it was incomplete, the feeling of **liking** it is not therefore a lie, counterfeit, or mistake.

Crucially, the prose says she lifts her face and looks directly at Hachiman before speaking. Hachiman himself wonders who the words were directed toward.

This must be evidence-classified carefully.

**Textual fact:** the surface subject is Zaimokuza and creative aspiration.

**Strong literary implication:** staging and Hachiman's own question invite a second address toward the Hachiman/Yui problem.

**Not yet textually established:** that Yui has delivered a fully conscious coded confession.

The significance is nonetheless major. Hachiman hears, before the final confrontation, an alternative principle:

> **A compromised or imperfect beginning does not automatically falsify the desire that exists now.**

He later adopts almost the exact opposite rule.

The game debate therefore makes his final conclusion harder to excuse as merely the only conceptual option available to him.

## 3.6 Chapter 6 — moral accounting, particularity, and restart

The final clubroom scene is the volume's intellectual center.

Hachiman gives Yui a present, but immediately tries to redefine its meaning. He proposes that the dog rescue, her supposed consideration toward him, and his repayment should all be **cleared**.

His vocabulary is not incidental:

- `チャラ`
- `差し引きゼロ`
- no further need to care about him;
- therefore “this is the end.”

He is converting a relationship into a closed moral account.

The psychological benefit is explicit. Once the balance is zero, he can be free of:

- painful misunderstanding;
- mistaken hope;
- the need to interpret Yui's particularity;
- and the possibility that her kindness demands an answer.

Most importantly, Hachiman is not naively unaware of his own defensiveness. Immediately after declaring the account closed, he thinks that even this may itself be another painful misunderstanding and another misguided act of self-defense.

That matters for the narratorial ledger:

> **His problem is increasingly not inability to generate the counter-reading. It is inability to permit the counter-reading to govern his behavior.**

Yui directly rejects his premise. She says she has never thought of him through pity or simple consideration and begins `あたしは、ただ……` before failing to complete the statement.

Yukino then intervenes with a strikingly causal and almost juridical argument.

Hachiman does not remember helping **Yui specifically**, and Yui does not remember feeling the pity Hachiman assigns her. The beginning, Yukino says, is already “wrong.” She initially agrees that the existing structure can therefore be ended.

Hachiman radicalizes that premise.

Even if Yui's feeling were special, he refuses to call it `本物` because:

- it began in an accidental event;
- another rescuer could hypothetically have generated comparable gratitude or affection;
- he did not identify Yui as Yui when he saved the dog;
- therefore, he reasons, her feeling cannot be directed toward him as **him**.

This is more precise than a generic “genetic fallacy.”

Hachiman is applying two linked tests:

### Origin essentialism

A feeling caused by a contaminated or accidental origin remains permanently contaminated by that origin.

### Counterfactual particularity

If another person in the same causal role might have generated the feeling, the feeling cannot count as particular to Hachiman.

The second concern is not irrational. Romantic particularity really does matter. The error is treating a hypothetical interchangeable **origin** as proof that all later experience remains interchangeable.

Yui and Hachiman have accumulated interactions after the accident. Hachiman's rule erases that history.

His defense becomes explicit:

- do not expect at the beginning;
- do not expect midway;
- do not expect at the end.

Yui gives the simplest counterclaim: she does not want this to be the end.

Yukino then supplies the conceptual move Hachiman cannot make.

She says that if something has ended, it can begin again — **because Hachiman and Yui are not morally at fault for the accident**. One was the rescuer and one the rescued, but both were victims of the event. The causal responsibility belongs elsewhere.

This is the V2 sharpening of the V1 “restart” thesis:

> **Yukino does not purify the past. She reallocates responsibility so that the past no longer imposes a permanent debt relation on the present.**

Once “rescuer” and “rescued” cease to be moral obligations they must keep paying, Hachiman and Yui can decide what, if anything, they want to become now.

Yukino's final wording is important: `あなたたち` — **you two**.

She looks between them, smiles gently but somewhat sadly, and leaves quickly under the pretext of reporting successful personnel replenishment to Hiratsuka.

The prospective reader may infer emerging triadic asymmetry. It is too early to call this settled romantic self-sacrifice.

The volume ends with Hachiman looking at the place Yukino had occupied and sensing an invisible line between where he and Yui sat and where Yukino had stood. He says a fact or truth separates “us” from “her,” but the reader is not yet told what it is.

That unresolved line prevents the restart from becoming a simple two-person happy ending.

## 3.7 Bonus track — ordinary celebration as a stress test

The attached bonus prose begins by parodying Hachiman's solitude ideology: birthdays prove the correctness of being alone — **but wanting to celebrate someone is not wrong**.

That formulation deliberately echoes the main narrative's distinction between flawed form and meaningful desire.

The story then enlarges the social field around the club:

- Yukino makes the cake;
- Yui chooses karaoke and coordinates the gathering;
- Hachiman invites Zaimokuza and Saika;
- Komachi participates;
- Hiratsuka appears nearby and speaks with Hachiman.

The ordinary gathering is not proof that all tension has vanished. It shows that the restart is capable of generating **shared time**.

Two supplementary lines are especially important for V2.

First, Hiratsuka tells Yui that she is relying on her regarding Hachiman and Yukino. This should not be allowed to reduce Yui to caretaker of the two “difficult” protagonists, but it confirms that an adult observer already recognizes her as an active relational force.

Second, Yui says that merely being celebrated makes her happy and that realizing Yukino is **relying on her** makes her happier still.

That is a positive use of reliance before the series' later pathology vocabulary.

Near the end, Hiratsuka predicts that Hachiman may experience the current everyday life as deceptive or `偽物`. She does not command him to stop doubting. She tells him that his suspiciousness is evidence that he keeps thinking and hopes he eventually reaches his own answer.

Thus the bonus track itself already understands “fake” as an **open question**, not a label whose utterance automatically settles reality.

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

## 4.1 Observational accuracy: high around relational mechanics

Hachiman remains very good at noticing:

- Yui's hesitation between social groups;
- Yukino's changed reaction to Yui's absence;
- the similarity between his and Yukino's distance-management styles;
- Saika's attempt to cheer him;
- Yui's unusual attention to Hachiman/Yukino appearing together;
- Haruno's conspicuously calibrated sociability;
- the game-club students' psychological investment in authority and training;
- Yukino's attempt to create a path for him and Yui to resolve their conflict.

These observations should not be discounted merely because Hachiman is defensive.

## 4.2 Self-description: increasingly self-aware, still behaviorally closed

A major V3 gain is the difference between **metacognition** and **behavioral freedom**.

Hachiman often knows what kind of defense he might be performing.

At the end he openly entertains the possibility that his own “closure” is another mistaken act of self-defense. Yet he proceeds with it anyway.

This requires revising any model in which Hachiman's growth problem is simply “learn to realize that he is lying to himself.”

By Volume 3 he can sometimes realize the possibility in real time.

The deeper difficulty is:

> **Can he act while allowing a hopeful interpretation to remain genuinely possible?**

## 4.3 Reset/delete/accounting metaphors

Volume 3's most revealing narratorial metaphor cluster is not romantic language but **systems and accounting**.

Hachiman repeatedly wants to:

- reset;
- delete;
- restore the correct distance;
- clear debts;
- balance the ledger at zero.

These metaphors perform emotional work. They convert relationships into objects that can be returned to a known state.

The advantage is control.

The cost is that human relations are historically path-dependent. A reset does not actually erase what people have learned about one another.

## 4.4 The “safe category” mechanism

Two episodes now strongly support a precise model.

### Saika

Male category -> no legitimate heterosexual-romance inference -> kindness safe to accept.

### Yukino's pretend-lover proposal

Explicitly “as though lovers,” combined with Hachiman's axiom that Yukino does not lie -> proposition guarantees they are not actually lovers -> intimacy safe to perform.

The common mechanism is not simply “Hachiman likes impossible romance.” It is:

> **He can tolerate intimate behavior when the semantic category itself protects him from having to decide whether the intimacy is romantically particular.**

## 4.5 Yui as the dangerous interpretive case

Yui cannot be safely categorized.

She is:

- a girl;
- consistently kind;
- increasingly particular toward Hachiman;
- socially capable of kindness toward others;
- linked to him by an accidental moral history;
- unwilling to define her feelings fully;
- but visibly hurt when he dismisses their meaning.

This ambiguity is exactly what Hachiman's defensive epistemology cannot tolerate.

He therefore replaces probabilistic uncertainty with a categorical rule about origin.

## 4.6 Hachiman's `本物` test is already too strict

His third use of `本物` in the volume is effectively a **substitutability test**:

> if Yui might have felt similarly toward another rescuer, the feeling cannot be genuine toward Hachiman.

This criterion is much stricter than ordinary relational causality.

Most relationships are contingent:

- people meet through accidents;
- institutions;
- seating arrangements;
- family;
- work;
- school clubs.

Contingency of encounter does not imply interchangeability after history accumulates.

V3 therefore gives the later `本物` problem a very specific early pathology:

> **Hachiman tries to make genuineness require freedom from causal contingency.**

## 4.7 V3 narratorial ledger summary

| Domain | V3 assessment |
|---|---|
| External social observation | Usually strong |
| Recognition of group pressure | Strong |
| Recognition of performative sociability | Strong but may overclaim motives |
| Interpretation of his own defensiveness | Surprisingly self-aware |
| Estimation of Yui's romantic probability | Ambiguous / underdetermined |
| Permission for hopeful interpretation to guide action | Very low |
| Model of Yukino's truthfulness | Overconfident idealization seed |
| Moral accounting of accident | Internally coherent but relationally reductive |
| Counterfactual particularity test | Philosophically serious but overextended |

# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Hikigaya Hachiman

Volume 3 makes Hachiman less easy to call “oblivious.”

He:

- notices Yui's absence;
- notices Yukino's concern;
- recognizes Saika's care and accepts it;
- understands the staged-romance structure with Yukino;
- recognizes that Yui's statement about imperfect beginnings may have a second target;
- explicitly generates the possibility that his final act is self-defense;
- recognizes hypothetical romantic feeling in Yui strongly enough to argue that it would not qualify as `本物`.

His problem is therefore becoming one of **admissibility** rather than perception.

He can produce the interpretation. He refuses to let it create expectation.

## 5.2 Yukinoshita Yukino

V3 significantly expands Yukino beyond V1 shorthand.

She is capable of:

- wanting Yui back while refusing to coerce her;
- planning a gift without requiring Yui's return;
- asking Hachiman for bounded practical help;
- relying on Komachi's taste and social knowledge;
- expressing gratitude to Yui because she personally wants to;
- admitting that Yui's presence helps her and Hachiman;
- accepting physical affection from Yui while complaining about it;
- giving Hachiman and Yui a conceptual path out of the debt model.

The strongest correction is:

> **Yukino's problem is not inability to rely. It is much closer to difficulty making herself the object of the request.**

She can ask for assistance when helping Yui. Later volumes must show when that competence fails once the desire being protected is Yukino's own.

## 5.3 Yuigahama Yui

V1 described Yui as struggling to say what she wants. V3 requires a more active model.

She does hesitate, displace, and leave sentences unfinished. But she also:

- chooses to return to the club;
- accepts the awkwardness of re-entry rather than disappearing permanently;
- reacts to the possibility that Hachiman and Yukino are romantically involved;
- receives Yukino's gratitude and reciprocates closeness;
- deliberately speaks a principle about `好き` while looking at Hachiman;
- explicitly denies the pity/debt interpretation;
- explicitly says she does not want the relationship to end;
- accepts the proposed restart;
- in the bonus, says that being relied upon by Yukino makes her happy.

The better V2 formulation is:

> **Yui is capable of directional assertion but often chooses deniable, analogical, or incomplete forms when a fully explicit statement would force an irreversible answer.**

That is already strategy as well as fear.

## 5.4 Totsuka Saika

Saika becomes the clearest example so far of uncomplicated reciprocal care.

He notices Hachiman's mood, arranges time together to cheer him, uses `八幡`, and promises to make future memories with him.

Hachiman accepts this care because it does not trigger his ordinary romantic threat model.

This makes Saika analytically useful beyond the gender joke.

## 5.5 Zaimokuza Yoshiteru

Zaimokuza remains ridiculous, lazy, and derivative. V3 refuses to sentimentalize him into an undiscovered genius.

What matters is that his desire survives knowledge of his own inadequacy.

The game-club students possess legitimate expertise; their error is converting expertise into authority over whether another person's desire is real.

Zaimokuza's declaration that he will continue writing even if he never becomes a professional is an early example of identity being grounded in **practice and desire rather than external certification**.

## 5.6 Yukinoshita Haruno

Haruno enters as a problem of performance.

The source establishes:

- socially optimized warmth;
- unusual ease with proximity;
- teasing control of atmosphere;
- an eldest-daughter public role formed by family obligations;
- Yukino's explicit characterization of that public face as `外面` / `仮面`.

The source does not yet establish:

- that all Haruno warmth is fraudulent;
- that her entire personality is constructed;
- that her provocations have one stable motive;
- that Hachiman's suspicion is complete understanding.

## 5.7 Hikigaya Komachi

Komachi continues functioning as a logistical architect of low-risk intimacy.

She can:

- accompany Yukino and Hachiman shopping;
- translate taste and domestic/social knowledge;
- participate in the birthday gathering;
- create situations Hachiman would not initiate himself.

Her interventions are manipulative in the comic sense, but generally preserve reversibility and choice. This will later provide a useful contrast with Hachiman's higher-stakes social engineering.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Hachiman ↔ Yui

### Beginning state

- trust: damaged but not absent;
- disclosure: poor;
- ordinary ease: disrupted;
- Hachiman expectation: relationship should reset to safer distance;
- Yui expectation: unresolved;
- romantic possibility: recognized as dangerous, not settled.

### Mid-volume

- Yui returns voluntarily;
- Hachiman physically opens the door and creates a socially usable entry;
- awkwardness remains visible in chair spacing and silence;
- Hachiman/Yui can interact through third-party tasks before discussing themselves.

### End state

- Yui directly rejects the debt interpretation;
- Hachiman's closure attempt fails;
- Yui says she does not want the relationship to end;
- Yukino releases the pair from the accident-as-obligation model;
- Yui tentatively accepts the restart.

**Delta:** from unilateral reset toward mutually acknowledged continuation, without full emotional definition.

## 6.2 Hachiman ↔ Yukino

V3 deepens an unusual form of trust.

They share:

- compatible distance management;
- little appetite for personal questions;
- strong practical cooperation;
- confidence in each other's stable behavioral tendencies;
- staged intimacy precisely because both believe actual romance is impossible.

Hachiman's belief `雪ノ下は噓を吐かない` is particularly important. It creates safety now and becomes a vulnerability in the later series because it is not merely trust in conduct; it is an idealized proposition about identity.

**Delta:** increased functional intimacy and mutual readability, but still protected from explicit personal desire.

## 6.3 Yukino ↔ Yui

This relationship receives one of the volume's largest gains.

Yukino:

- refuses to exploit Yui's accommodation by requesting her return directly;
- plans a birthday expression even if Yui leaves forever;
- says her gratitude is based on her own desire to express it;
- says Yui's presence is helpful/needed;
- accepts Yui's physical embrace without pulling away.

Yui:

- returns despite awkwardness;
- receives Yukino's gratitude emotionally;
- embraces Yukino;
- in the bonus, explicitly enjoys being relied upon by her.

**Delta:** friendship becomes particular, reciprocal, and partially independent of Hachiman.

## 6.4 The triad

The club begins by demonstrating that three-person ordinary life has already become structurally different from the original Hachiman/Yukino dyad.

Yui's absence is not neutral.

Her return restores the three bodies to the room, but not immediately their prior ease.

At the end, Yukino repeatedly uses `あなたたち` to describe Hachiman and Yui, then physically leaves them together. Hachiman later perceives an invisible line between the pair and Yukino's former position.

**Prospective conclusion:** a triadic asymmetry has appeared.

**Do not yet conclude:** Yukino has accepted a permanent outsider role or romantically surrendered Hachiman.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

## 7.1 Hiratsuka's personnel-replenishment intervention

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Stated problem | Service Club lacks a motivated third member |
| Hidden problem | Hachiman/Yukino are allowing Yui's disappearance to become passive relational decay |
| Authorization | Institutional authority; coercive toward Hachiman/Yukino |
| Yui's agency | Preserved; Hiratsuka does not order Yui to return |
| Method | Convert emotional problem into club task |
| Cost | Forces Hachiman/Yukino into uncomfortable action |
| Reversibility | High |
| Ethical status | Manipulative but bounded and pedagogically transparent |

This is a useful baseline for later “protective pretext” analysis.

## 7.2 Yukino's birthday intervention

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Stated goal | Express gratitude / celebrate Yui |
| Condition | Explicitly not dependent on Yui returning |
| Authorization | Yukino's own decision; Hachiman/Komachi voluntarily assist |
| Agency effect | Expands relational possibility without demanding reciprocation |
| Cost bearer | Distributed and low |
| Ethical status | Strong example of care without leverage |

## 7.3 Zaimokuza/game-club intervention

The Service Club initially resists because Zaimokuza's problem is self-produced and his stated desire is partly to avoid consequences rather than improve.

The eventual contest does not solve his career prospects. It forces a confrontation over whether deficient method invalidates desire.

The case is ethically messy but analytically productive because it refuses two simplistic answers:

- desire alone guarantees competence;
- competence alone has authority to certify desire.

## 7.4 Hachiman's final “zero-account” intervention

This is not a formal Service Club case, but it is the most consequential intervention in the volume.

Hachiman unilaterally defines:

- the cause of Yui's kindness;
- the debt she supposedly carries;
- the value of his gift as repayment;
- the point at which the account becomes zero;
- and therefore when the relationship should end.

The problem is not self-sacrifice in the later sense. It is **interpretive authorship without permission**.

Yui objects to the diagnosis itself.

Yukino then intervenes by removing the moral debt structure, not by dictating what Hachiman and Yui must feel.

Her solution creates an option rather than selecting the option for them.

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 Relationship labels as safety technology

Volume 3 repeatedly distinguishes lived relation from named relation.

Examples include:

- Hachiman and Zaimokuza repeatedly denying that they are `友達` despite repeated interaction;
- game/online acquaintances whose real names may be unknown;
- Service Club `部員` as a safer institutional category;
- Yukino/Hachiman pretending to be `恋人のように` precisely because they are not lovers;
- Saika using `八幡`, a more intimate form of address than most peers.

The recurring issue is not simply whether a relationship is “really friendship.”

It is:

> **What risks become active once a relationship is named?**

Hachiman often prefers narrower functional categories because they reduce expectation.

## 8.2 The club as relational institution

V3 makes explicit what Volumes 1–2 only implied: the club's institutional form is now carrying private attachment.

Hiratsuka weaponizes that fact pedagogically.

Yukino later uses the personnel-replenishment report as a socially legitimate reason to leave Hachiman and Yui alone.

The institution can therefore:

- create contact;
- disguise desire;
- provide excuses;
- allocate tasks;
- and create exits.

It is not merely a setting.

## 8.3 Haruno and political-family socialization

Haruno's `外面` is explicitly connected to the Yukinoshita family's public life.

She did not simply decide to become socially polished in the abstract. The role was cultivated through repeated exposure to greetings, parties, and family obligations as eldest daughter.

This is the first strong evidence that **social performance can be an acquired institutional competence**, not just individual dishonesty.

## 8.4 Expertise and gatekeeping

The game-club dispute introduces another social system:

- tradition;
- professional aspiration;
- cultural capital;
- authority to define legitimate participation.

Hachiman's objection is not anti-expertise. He explicitly accepts that Zaimokuza should study and improve.

His objection is to turning expertise into a monopoly over who is allowed to count as sincere.

# 9. Authenticity / `本物` ledger

Volume 3 produces the single largest lexical change in the V2 reread so far.

Volumes 1 and 2 contained no explicit `本物` in the governing prose search.

Volume 3 contains **three main-narrative uses** of `本物`, plus significant `偽物` language in the game chapter and bonus prose.

This means the prospective chronology must be revised:

> **Volume 3 is not merely a retrospective precursor to the later “genuine” problem. It is the first volume in which the explicit lexical field becomes structurally important.**

But the meanings are not yet unified.

## 9.1 `本物` #1 — Is Haruno's radiance real?

Hachiman asks whether the brightness Haruno projects is `本物`.

Domain:

- social performance;
- public persona;
- charisma;
- strategic warmth.

Question:

> Is an expression produced through cultivated social competence false merely because it is calibrated?

The volume does not fully answer.

## 9.2 `本物` #2 — Yukino's smile as non-instrumental expression

Hachiman claims that he knows a `本物の笑顔`: one that does not flatter, deceive, or evade.

Domain:

- facial expression;
- emotional presentation;
- noninstrumentality.

Important V2 caution:

Hachiman may be right that Yukino's smile toward him differs from Haruno's public-facing style. But calling it the “real” smile already reveals his desire to sort Yukino into a transparent authentic essence.

This is an early seed of the later idealization problem.

## 9.3 `本物` #3 — Yui's affection and particularity

Hachiman refuses to classify hypothetical romantic feeling from Yui as `本物` if it originates in the dog accident and might have arisen toward another rescuer.

Domain:

- romantic feeling;
- causal origin;
- particularity;
- counterfactual substitutability.

This is much closer to the later series problem, but still not identical to it.

At V3, Hachiman's test is approximately:

> **A feeling is not genuine if it is caused by a role that someone else could have occupied.**

Later volumes will have to test whether `本物` moves away from purity of origin toward something built through action, speech, and accountable continuation.

## 9.4 `偽物` — gatekeeping in the game chapter

Zaimokuza is called a counterfeit creator because his methods and knowledge are incomplete.

Yui responds that incorrect or incomplete beginnings do not make `好き` itself counterfeit.

This produces a critical distinction:

> **The practice may be deficient while the desire remains sincere.**

That distinction directly precedes Hachiman's attempt to invalidate Yui's possible feeling because of origin.

## 9.5 Bonus-track `偽物`

Hiratsuka predicts that Hachiman may experience his new everyday life as deceptive or fake. She does not tell him the answer is already known. She asks him to keep thinking until he reaches an answer of his own.

This makes `偽物` an epistemic problem rather than an externally certified diagnosis.

## 9.6 V3 honmono provisional definition set

No single definition should yet govern the ledger.

At the end of Volume 3, `本物` can refer to at least:

1. **unperformed social radiance**;
2. **non-instrumental emotional expression**;
3. **particular rather than role-substitutable affection**.

The later series may integrate these. V3 itself has not.

# 10. Dependency / autonomy ledger

## 10.1 No `共依存`

The later codependency vocabulary is absent.

It must not be projected backward.

## 10.2 `依存` appears negatively, but not interpersonally

The significant main-narrative `依存` use occurs during the game debate. Hachiman attacks dependence upon inherited tradition and authority as a substitute for self-authored correctness.

This is conceptually relevant to the later autonomy problem, but it is **not** yet a diagnosis of interpersonal dependence.

## 10.3 Yukino already demonstrates healthy bounded reliance

The present volume contains direct evidence that Yukino can:

- recognize a limitation;
- ask for help;
- accept assistance;
- remain author of the underlying action.

This is an important baseline against later claims that she “cannot rely on anyone.”

## 10.4 Yui experiences being relied upon positively

In the bonus, Yui explicitly says that realizing Yukino is relying on her makes her happier.

That establishes an early positive relation between:

- reliance;
- trust;
- friendship;
- being particular to another person.

Later dependency pathology therefore cannot be defined simply as “one person needs another.”

## 10.5 Hachiman and expectation

The more immediate pathology in V3 is not dependence but **expectation avoidance**.

Hachiman describes mutual non-expectation as comfortable. At the climax he resolves not to expect anything at the beginning, middle, or end.

Autonomy, in his current model, risks becoming:

> nobody should possess a claim capable of disappointing me, and I should possess no claim capable of burdening them.

The volume is beginning to show the cost of that model.

# 11. Japanese language and voice

## 11.1 Hachiman — systems, accounting, and defensive qualification

V3's Hachiman voice repeatedly turns affect into technical or transactional vocabulary:

- `リセット`
- `デリート`
- `チャラ`
- `差し引きゼロ`
- `距離`
- `期待しない`

These terms make relationships sound stateful but mechanically reversible.

He also repeatedly hedges his own insight through comedy and self-mockery. Importantly, the hedging does not always erase awareness. Sometimes it contains a serious alternative explanation that he then refuses behaviorally.

## 11.2 Yui — hesitation as deniable assertion

Yui's voice remains full of:

- fillers;
- false starts;
- `その`;
- `えっと`;
- trailing clauses;
- softening `と思う、けど`;
- unfinished statements.

V1 treated this primarily as difficulty stating desire.

V2 should refine that.

Yui often uses hesitation to create **graduated commitment**. She can say enough to redirect the conversation without saying enough to force the other person into a final answer.

Her game-chapter line is exemplary: she states a general principle while looking directly at Hachiman, preserving both an explicit surface referent and a possible personal second address.

## 11.3 Yukino — logical grammar softens around personal requests

Yukino's ordinary voice remains:

- precise;
- conditional;
- classificatory;
- comfortable with `なら`, `だったら`, and causal structure.

Her final argument about the accident is almost legal in form: identify victims, identify causal responsibility, then remove an incorrectly assigned obligation.

But when asking for help, her syntax changes. `手伝ってもらえると助かる、のだけれど……` is hesitant and softened.

This contrast matters more than a generic claim that she is “formal.”

She is linguistically most secure when reasoning about systems and least secure when the sentence itself exposes need.

## 11.4 Haruno — social fluency as audible role competence

Haruno's speech quickly uses:

- familiarity;
- nicknaming (`雪乃ちゃん`);
- teasing;
- fast emotional calibration;
- playful intimacy.

The language supports Yukino's claim that the public persona is practiced social equipment.

Again, practiced does not mean wholly false.

## 11.5 Saika — `八幡`

Saika's use of Hachiman's given name remains socially significant.

It marks a level of ordinary personal closeness Hachiman receives easily precisely because he believes the category is safe.

## 11.6 Relationship nouns remain unstable

`友達` appears frequently, but Hachiman repeatedly resists its application to relationships he actually maintains.

This lexical instability should be tracked longitudinally. Hachiman often accepts:

- activity partner;
- acquaintance;
- club member;
- game friend;

more easily than an emotionally thicker category carrying expectations.

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertext

## 12.1 Saika comedy has a psychological function — but is still comedy

The repeated “Saika is male” joke supports the safe-affection interpretation unusually well because Hachiman explicitly states the logic.

V2 should still avoid converting every Saika joke into symbolic psychology. The gag also belongs to the series' early light-novel genre language and sexual-comic exaggeration.

Both functions can coexist.

## 12.2 Reset/delete jokes become conceptual machinery

The videogame/computer vocabulary begins as comic narration and becomes a serious relational model.

This is an example of a domain where Hachiman's otaku-reference style is not ornamental: it supplies the metaphors by which he attempts to govern affect.

## 12.3 The game chapter is metacommentary on legitimacy

The chapter asks:

- must one possess the correct lineage to create?
- does loving something count if one is mediocre at it?
- can training become another performance of authority?
- can fantasy survive knowledge of practical reality?

Because Yui's statement is staged toward Hachiman, the argument escapes the creative-writing case and becomes a rehearsal for the relationship climax.

## 12.4 The bonus-track birthday joke reverses itself

Hachiman's opening argument proves that birthdays are the beginning of human solitude. The narration then concedes that wanting to celebrate another person is not wrong.

The joke produces the volume's emerging pattern:

> a form may be ridiculous, artificial, conventional, or historically compromised while the desire enacted through it remains meaningful.

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial test

## 14.1 Is Hachiman's ending reasoning merely a “genetic fallacy”?

**V1 reading:** substantially yes — he invalidates current feeling because of origin.

**V2 adversarial correction:** the label is useful but incomplete.

Hachiman is worried about **particularity**, not merely causation. If Yui's affection is toward “the person who saved my dog,” he wants to know whether it is actually toward Hikigaya Hachiman.

That is a legitimate relational question.

What makes his reasoning defective is the stronger inference:

> because the initiating role was counterfactually substitutable, later feelings must remain substitutable.

The text does not establish that.

**Disposition:** V1 strengthened but narrowed.

## 14.2 Does Yukino “place herself outside” Hachiman/Yui?

The evidence is real:

- repeated `あなたたち`;
- slight sadness;
- quick departure;
- final spatial line.

Alternative explanation:

- she genuinely does need to report to Hiratsuka;
- she may simply recognize that Hachiman and Yui require private space;
- the sadness may involve friendship, exclusion, or knowledge the reader does not yet possess.

**Prospective disposition:** triadic asymmetry confirmed; motive unresolved.

## 14.3 Is Haruno's smile fake?

Yukino explicitly identifies a cultivated `外面` / `仮面`.

But a cultivated persona can still express real affect.

The strongest V2 claim is not “Haruno is fake.” It is:

> **Haruno has learned to control social presentation at a level Hachiman experiences as suspiciously frictionless.**

## 14.4 Is Yui's game-chapter statement a confession?

Surface reading: it is an argument about Zaimokuza's love of games.

Second-address reading: she looks at Hachiman; the content maps almost perfectly onto their unresolved accident problem; Hachiman explicitly wonders who the words are for.

The text intentionally supports double address.

Calling it a full explicit confession would overstate it.

Calling it irrelevant to Hachiman/Yui would underread the staging.

## 14.5 Is Yukino already unable to rely on people?

No.

Volume 3 directly contradicts that broad formulation.

She requests practical help successfully.

The later series must therefore specify **which kind of need** becomes threatening to her autonomy.

## 14.6 Is Saika merely a displaced-romance joke?

No.

The text explicitly links Hachiman's ease with Saika to freedom from the fear of misreading female kindness. The psychological reading is unusually well-supported.

But the sequence also functions as conventional genre comedy; the two readings need not compete.

## 14.7 Does `本物` already mean the mature series ideal?

No.

The word has entered the text, but its domains remain heterogeneous.

Any V2 synthesis that defines V3 `本物` using the final volumes without showing the lexical transitions would repeat the retrospective flattening the method is designed to prevent.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

This section permits full-series hindsight while preserving the prospective results above.

## 15.1 Volume 3 is the true lexical beginning of the `本物` problem

The V1 synthesis treated Volume 3 mainly as conceptual preparation for later authenticity language.

V2 corrects that chronology.

The term is already explicit.

What changes later is not the appearance of the question but its **scope and definition**.

V3 asks whether genuineness depends upon:

- unperformed expression;
- freedom from instrumental presentation;
- pure origin;
- or particularity to the person rather than the role.

Later volumes will progressively dismantle the purity criteria.

## 15.2 `雪ノ下は噓を吐かない` is a major Volume 5 seed

Hachiman currently treats Yukino's truthfulness as categorical enough to make staged romance safe.

With hindsight, this is not just trust. It is idealization.

Volume 5's crisis will hurt precisely because Yukino's ordinary human capacity for omission, silence, and imperfect disclosure contradicts an identity Hachiman helped construct.

V3 therefore supplies a clean prospective locator for the later revision.

## 15.3 Bounded reliance complicates the later dependency argument

Yukino asks for help in V3 without losing authorship.

Yui enjoys being relied upon.

This means the later `共依存` problem cannot coherently be summarized as “reliance is bad” or “Yukino must learn to need nobody.”

The relevant later distinctions must include:

- reliance versus surrender of authorship;
- expectation versus entitlement;
- mutual aid versus identity substitution;
- being needed versus requiring need as proof of relationship.

V3 already contains the healthy baseline.

## 15.4 Yui's later strategic ambiguity has an early prototype

Yui's `好きって気持ちに間違いなんてない` statement shows that her indirectness is not simply inability.

She can use a third-party case to say something personally consequential while retaining deniability.

With hindsight, this anticipates later forms in which Yui:

- understands more of the romantic structure than she states;
- chooses when to press and when to preserve ambiguity;
- acts socially rather than waiting passively.

## 15.5 Yukino's later self-removal should not be back-projected here

The `あなたたち` / invisible-line ending is tempting to read as the beginning of Yukino's sacrificial removal from the triangle.

V2 should resist that shortcut.

In V3, Yukino is also actively:

- preserving Yui's agency;
- requesting help;
- seeking gratitude expression;
- accepting friendship;
- intervening to create a new possibility.

Calling all of that “self-removal” would make the late-series concept explain too much.

The correct retrospective claim is narrower:

> **V3 introduces a spatial and linguistic asymmetry that later self-removal can build upon.**

## 15.6 Hachiman's later `本物` plea grows from an impossible early test

At V3, Hachiman wants feeling to prove that it belongs to him independently of contingent origin.

That desire is already close to an impossible purity test.

Later `本物` develops when he is forced to admit that:

- relationships arise contingently;
- motives are mixed;
- words are imperfect;
- understanding is incomplete;
- and yet people may still choose to continue and answer one another.

The later development should therefore be read not as discovery of an entirely new desire, but as **revision of the criteria by which he would recognize the desire as real**.

## 16.2 The largest V1 correction: chronology of `本物`

The V1 series-so-far synthesis was appropriately cautious about projecting the mature “genuine” concept backward. But it understated how early the explicit lexical field begins.

Volume 3 already uses `本物` three times in the main narrative.

Therefore the V2 longitudinal ledger should now read:

- **V1:** no explicit `本物` in governing prose search;
- **V2:** no explicit `本物` in governing prose search;
- **V3:** first explicit `本物` cluster, with multiple still-distinct meanings.

This is a meaningful revision, not a cosmetic one.

## 16.3 The second largest correction: Yui is already theorizing the problem

V1 positioned Yukino's restart argument as the primary conceptual answer to Hachiman.

That remains true at the climax.

But V2 finds that Yui supplies a crucial principle earlier:

> an incorrect or incomplete beginning need not make the present `好き` counterfeit.

Hachiman hears the alternative before he rejects it.

Yui is therefore not merely the emotional object whose feelings are interpreted by Hachiman and translated by Yukino. She is already a **participant in the theory of relationship**.

## 16.4 The third correction: healthy reliance already exists

V1 saw Yukino's difficulty requesting help as foundational.

V3 shows that the problem was stated too broadly.

A better cumulative hypothesis is:

> **Yukino can rely when the task is bounded and the goal remains externally legible. Reliance becomes threatening when asking would expose uncertainty about her own desire, identity, or life authorship.**

That hypothesis should now govern the next volumes until disproved.

# Volume 3 closing judgment

Volume 3 is the first *Oregairu* volume in which the problem later associated with `本物` becomes explicit in the text itself.

Yet it does not solve that problem.

Hachiman still imagines genuineness through purity tests:

- a smile should be free of social instrumentality;
- affection should not be transferable to another person occupying the same initiating role;
- an incorrect origin seems capable of invalidating everything downstream.

The other characters quietly undermine that model.

Yui says that an imperfect beginning does not make `好き` false.

Yukino separates accident responsibility from the relationship that formed around the accident and allows Hachiman and Yui to begin again without pretending the old beginning never happened.

Saika shows that Hachiman can receive care when interpretation feels safe.

Yukino demonstrates that reliance can preserve authorship.

The birthday gathering shows that conventional, artificial, even embarrassing social forms can generate real shared time.

The strongest cumulative proposition is therefore not yet “the genuine is accountable continuation.” That would be too mature.

Volume 3 earns a narrower conclusion:

> **History matters, but origin does not possess an unlimited right to dictate what later choices mean.**

Hachiman wants to reset.

Yui refuses the ending.

Yukino proposes a restart.

And for the first time, the series has begun explicitly asking what would make any of those relationships `本物`.
