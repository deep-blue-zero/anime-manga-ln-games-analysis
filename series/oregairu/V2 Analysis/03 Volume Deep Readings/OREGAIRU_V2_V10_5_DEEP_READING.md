# OREGAIRU V2 — Volume 10.5 Deep Reading
## 『やはり俺の青春ラブコメはまちがっている。10.5』

### Governing thesis

**Volume 10.5 turns the post-`本物` problem away from abstract definition and toward the archive of ordinary life. Its four linked late-winter stories show people becoming knowable not through final theories but through repeated, limited contact: Zaimokuza circles around exposure and still keeps writing; Yukino admits that the future once assumed for her is no longer certain; Iroha demonstrates that performance, manipulation, enjoyment, and care can coexist without collapsing into one “true self”; the Service Club builds a shared object while remaining unable to define itself; and Komachi and Hachiman discover that ordinary intimacy acquires value partly because time will end its present form. The photograph at the volume's center does not restore the Volume-8 fantasy of wordless total understanding. It preserves coexistence without requiring identical interpretation.**

A compact formulation is:

> **A relationship need not be fully defined to be real. It can instead be sustained through revisable participation, imperfect knowledge, shared work, and memories whose meanings do not have to become identical.**

That formulation remains **prospective and provisional**. Volume 10.5 contains no governing lexical occurrence of `本物` or `偽物`. The volume does not claim to define authenticity. It tests what ordinary life looks like after the trio has admitted that definition and total comprehension remain unavailable.

## 1.2 Constituent structure

The collection contains four numbered stories, followed by an afterword:

1. `そのうち、材木座義輝にでもできる簡単なお仕事がたぶん見つかる。`
2. `きっと、一色いろははお砂糖とスパイスと素敵な何かでできている。`
3. `絶対に破れない締め切りがそこにはある。`
4. `こうして、比企谷家の夜は更けゆく。`

This structure matters because Volume 10.5 superficially resembles 7.5: a decimal-numbered volume composed of side material rather than one conventional continuous novel. The source audit, however, produces the opposite chronology result.

## 1.3 Chronology audit: 10.5 is a linked late-winter sequence

Unlike Volume 7.5, Volume 10.5 is **not** a wide-ranging anthology that jumps between several earlier states.

The chronology is internally secure:

| Story | Story-time evidence | Cross-story links | Analytical status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | opens `この一月末から二月にかけて` | introduces Zaimokuza's free-paper discussion later recalled in Story 3 | post-V10 late-winter evidence |
| 2 | `立春も近い`; heater begins making abnormal noises | outing receipts and broken heater both reappear in Story 3 | post-V10 late-winter evidence |
| 3 | month has turned to February; free-paper deadline is mid-February | explicitly recalls Story 1; explicitly uses Story 2 receipts; heater disabled because its abnormality was reported | post-V10, early-to-mid-February evidence |
| 4 | Komachi's entrance exam is close | Hachiman says groceries were forgotten because of `フリペ制作の忙しさ` | post-Story-3, pre-exam evidence |

Volume 10 itself supplies the remaining bridge. Its marathon, normally held in February, is explicitly said to have shifted into **late January** that year; the final Haruno answer-check also occurs at `一月も末`. Volume 10.5 then opens across `一月末から二月`.

Therefore:

> **All four Volume 10.5 stories may update the post-Volume-10 state.**

This is a substantive source-control conclusion. 10.5 is not merely publication-time retrospective connective tissue. It is a coherent continuation of the late-winter ordinary-life phase after the Volume-10 course-choice crisis.

## 1.4 The afterword's paratextual framing

Watari's afterword explicitly calls attention to `日常` and argues that days without major incidents are still filled with emotional movement and conflict. He then asks what “he” thought and “she” felt inside such ordinary life, and what faces they might wear when later recounting those days as memories.

This is useful paratextual confirmation of the volume's formal project. It should **not** be treated as diegetic doctrine. But it justifies taking the small-scale events seriously rather than classifying 10.5 as disposable comedy.

## 1.5 Prospective evidence rule

Because the internal chain is post-V10, all four stories can positively modify the state established by Volume 10. Later Volume-11+ material is excluded from the prospective reading. The V1 reread is used only in Section 16 to identify historical claims that should be retained, narrowed, strengthened, or revised.

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

The incoming state is the post-Volume-10 state governed by Checkpoint C and `OREGAIRU_V2_V10_DEEP_READING.md`:

- the Service Club has reopened after the Christmas crisis but remains semantically unresolved;
- Hachiman has learned to ask and to rely, but he still converts “wanting to know” into coercive elicitation when another person's refusal obstructs him;
- Yukino's `わからない` is legitimate uncertainty, not a defect requiring Hachiman to solve her;
- Yui increasingly participates by stating desire and allowing conflict rather than merely protecting atmosphere;
- Iroha has become an increasingly persistent part of the trio's ordinary social environment without simply becoming “the fourth Service Club member”;
- Hayama has exposed the violence of socially prewritten answers;
- `本物` remains central but contested, and Haruno's warnings remain character assertions rather than settled diagnoses.

Volume 10.5 must therefore be asked:

1. Can ordinary life sustain the answerability procedure established in Volume 9?
2. Does Hachiman continue the Volume-10 movement from “I don't want to know more” toward “because I don't know, I want to know more”?
3. Can people be strategic or performative without becoming “fake”?
4. Can shared work preserve differentiated agency?
5. Can uncertainty about one's future be treated as legitimate rather than decoded as hidden failure?
6. Does the Service Club acquire a stable definition after the crisis?
7. What does a photograph preserve that a definition cannot?
8. Does “ordinary continuity” mean stasis, or can it contain impending departure?

The prospective freeze must **not** assume:

- that the trio is already codependent;
- that the romantic triangle has been consciously resolved;
- that the photograph predicts a later breakup or final pairing;
- that Iroha's ambiguous chemistry with Hachiman has a settled romantic status;
- that Yukino's career uncertainty already means dependence on Hachiman;
- that `本物` has a stable definition discoverable from the ending.

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

## 3.1 Four stories, one movement: ordinary time becomes visible

The four stories move from **future abstraction** to **embodied memory**:

1. Zaimokuza asks what he might become.
2. Iroha tests what a dyadic outing feels like.
3. The group produces a public object and then a private image.
4. Hachiman and Komachi experience domestic continuity while explicitly recognizing that the household and club will change.

The progression is therefore:

> **future possibility → present contact → shared production → remembered/anticipated past**

The volume's deepest subject is time. “Ordinary life” matters because it is not indefinitely repeatable.

## 3.2 Story 1: Zaimokuza's circular future is still movement

Zaimokuza arrives wanting to become an editor because being a light-novel writer appears humiliating, precarious, badly ranked, and unlikely to deliver the fantasy rewards he imagines. His reasoning is absurd. Yet the story makes something important visible beneath the joke: he fears exposure.

He claims to hate rankings, numbers, and reviews. Hachiman initially assumes he has posted something and been hurt. Zaimokuza admits that he has **not** posted; he has merely watched other works get criticized and imagined the same judgment arriving at him.

This is a clean preemptive-rejection case:

> **anticipated evaluation becomes sufficient reason to avoid the act through which evaluation could occur.**

The mechanism obviously resonates with Hachiman, but the text does not require Zaimokuza to function as a one-to-one allegory. His low-stakes absurdity simply lets the series display the mechanism without the romantic stakes that distort Hachiman's self-reading.

The story ends with Zaimokuza still circling: writer, editor, animation director, voice-actress marriage fantasy. Yet he goes back to writing. Hachiman recognizes that even someone who appears not to change can move through detours toward a destination.

The important longitudinal proposition is modest:

> **nonlinear movement can still be movement.**

The story refuses both “Zaimokuza has matured” and “Zaimokuza never changes.”

## 3.3 Story 1's communication principle: limited destination, not universal understanding

In discussing niche media, Hachiman thinks:

> `何だってそうだろう。言葉だって気持ちだって。`
>
> `喜ばせたい相手や伝えたい相手にさえ届くのであれば、それでいい。`

This should not be elevated into a final theory of intimacy. Its immediate context is a defense of works that do not need to be intelligible or pleasurable to everyone.

But publication-time significance is real. After Volumes 8–10, Hachiman can now imagine communication whose success criterion is **not universal comprehension**. A feeling need not become transparent to all observers. It may be enough for it to reach the intended recipient.

That is already different from the Volume-8 fantasy in which the ideal relation is one where understanding occurs without speech at all.

## 3.4 Story 1's future conversation: Yukino's uncertainty becomes legitimate

The career-research comedy causes everyone to discuss future work. Iroha and Yui readily admit that they do not have serious career plans. Yukino, by contrast, is assumed to be the obvious future professional.

Her answer is:

> `以前はそう思っていたけれど。……今は、まだわからない`

Hachiman is surprised because he had vaguely assumed that Yukino possessed a clearly ordered future. Then he catches himself:

> `そうした幻想を勝手に押し付けていただけなのかもしれない`

This is one of 10.5's strongest continuities with Volume 5. There he discovered that he had imposed an idealized “perfect Yukino” on her. Here the ideal becomes temporal: **future certainty and competence**.

The change is not that Hachiman fully understands Yukino. The change is that he can now identify his own expectation as a possible projection and then let her uncertainty stand.

## 3.5 Story 2: Iroha's trial date as mixed-motive contact

Iroha has Hachiman meet her without initially revealing that the outing is the “date-course” assignment he had casually agreed to consider. When he complains, she says:

> `普通に誘っても絶対に来ないじゃないですか`

Hachiman agrees.

This is ethically ambiguous but meaningfully different from Kyoto or the Volume-10 marathon. Iroha manipulates the invitation procedure because she predicts refusal. Yet Hachiman is not trapped after arrival. He can leave. He chooses to continue partly because he did in fact make a prior commitment.

The appropriate classification is:

> **low-stakes strategic pretext with retained exit capacity**

rather than either “fully transparent invitation” or “coercion.”

The outing is simultaneously:

- research for a Hayama date;
- use of Hachiman as a test partner;
- practical leisure;
- a site of genuine mutual observation;
- later material for the free-paper project;
- a situation in which Iroha's performed femininity becomes unusually visible;
- an encounter both participants eventually admit was enjoyable.

No single motive cancels the others.

## 3.6 Iroha: performed femininity without hidden-self reduction

Iroha repeatedly stages herself. She adjusts appearance before approaching Hachiman, creates cute expressions, weaponizes a loophole in a bowling bet, moves physically closer, whispers `こんなことするの、先輩にだけですよ`, and later returns to more obviously calculated behavior.

The narration knows she is doing this.

Yet the same narration also finds her attractive and acknowledges that the day was enjoyable.

This makes Iroha a strong case against a simple performance/falsity binary:

> **An action can be performed because the performer genuinely wants to perform it.**

Her persona is not necessarily a counterfeit layer concealing one authentic girl beneath it. Strategic femininity is one of the ways Iroha acts in the world.

The Sugar-and-Spice motif should therefore be read as **irreducible combination**, not secret essence. At the end Hachiman broadens the insight beyond Iroha: the girls close to him likely possess their own difficult “something” as well.

## 3.7 Story 3: the free paper converts mixed motive into accountable production

The free-paper project originates in a dubious institutional motive: Iroha wants to use leftover student-council budget before future allocations are reduced. She also attempts to expense locations visited during the trial date.

The project nevertheless develops into real work.

Yui's first response is particularly important. Instead of simply accepting or rejecting Iroha's emotional appeal, she says Iroha should research the process, ask knowledgeable people, obtain help, and then return. Her delivery is gentle, but the content is essentially:

> **prepare a viable request.**

This is request ethics maturing into institutional practice.

Iroha does return with printing specifications and an estimate. Hachiman explicitly says he has not yet agreed; they will hear the concrete plan and then decide whether it is possible. Yukino tests specifications and schedule. Yui considers available helpers. Only after constraints become legible does the group commit.

The method is much healthier than earlier Service Club crisis-solving because **capacity is discussed before obligation is accepted**.

## 3.8 The project's comic ethical flaw: Hachiman still says yes too easily

The process is not idealized. Iroha deploys tears/softness. Hachiman knows it is at least partly performed and still gives in. Yukino calls him `相変わらず甘い`.

So the sequence is not:

> perfect consent → perfect project.

It is:

> mixed-pressure request → explicit constraint analysis → differentiated contributions → contingency planning → shared responsibility.

That distinction matters. The healthy outcome does not purify the messy origin.

## 3.9 Story 3's central structural problem: the Service Club cannot write itself

When Iroha suggests including the Service Club in the publication, Hachiman says no. His public reasons are plausible: the club is obscure, it is not recruiting, and removing the page reduces workload.

But the narration identifies the real reason:

> `この部活を、今の在り方をどう呼んで、どうやって定義するべきか、その答えがまだわからない。`

This is decisive evidence against any claim that Volume 9 solved the relationship simply by naming `本物`.

They can:

- meet;
- work;
- tease;
- care;
- make tea;
- support juniors;
- take photographs;

and still not know what public category fits their relationship.

The lack of definition is no longer treated as proof of falsity. It is an unresolved feature of a relation that demonstrably exists.

## 3.10 The deadline crisis: failure becomes survivable

Hachiman falls catastrophically behind on his column. This produces two distinct responses.

Yui tells him that missing the deadline would not make him uniquely blameworthy. She will apologize with him. She says he can stop, because seeing him suffer is something she does not want.

This is notable because she does **not** turn care into “therefore you must continue.” She tries to make failure relationally survivable.

Hachiman's reaction is revealing. He still interprets sweet care as something he must not “rely on or entrust himself to.” That reflex causes him to recommit to finishing. So the older anti-dependence rule remains active inside him even when the people around him are offering a healthier form of shared responsibility.

Yui then acknowledges that she herself may be `ずるい`: she does not always stop him, cannot always help correctly, and has other feelings she cannot yet fully state.

Her growth is not purified selflessness. It is increasing ability to admit that care and personal desire coexist.

## 3.11 Yukino's contingency planning: trust that includes possible failure

Yukino's response is even more structurally important.

She admits she should have checked Hachiman's progress because she knew that **even he can make mistakes**. She then reveals a buffer plan: submit completed text, use dummy material for the remainder, revise during the next stage, and absorb the extra correction cost from budget already reserved for contingencies.

This is a strong countermodel to Hayama's social world.

Hayama's environment treats trust as an expectation that failure should never become visible.

Yukino's production practice treats trust as:

> **I believe your work matters enough to plan for the possibility that you may fail.**

That is more humane because failure does not become betrayal.

Hachiman then tells her directly:

> `割りと助かってる`

Her contribution is not abstractly praised as competence. He identifies what she is doing—scheduling, coordination, contingency—as something helping him and the group.

## 3.12 The photograph: shared object, nonidentical meaning

The project's most important epistemic sequence begins when Iroha explains why she takes ordinary photographs:

> `思い出って大事じゃないですか`

Hachiman contrasts this with his own habit of photographing only events or nonordinary occasions. Iroha's “ordinary” includes the unremarkable scene as something worth preserving.

At the end Yui asks for a Service Club photograph. Iroha initially declines to join and offers to take the picture of Hachiman, Yukino, and Yui first. Hachiman cautiously interprets this as possible consideration on her part.

Looking at the photograph, he thinks:

> `依然として、呼び方も定義もわかりはしない。言葉ではないが故に、きっと共有できるのだ。言葉にしてしまえば、おそらくは食い違ってしまう思いを、形にしてつなぎとめてくれる。`

The line demands a careful distinction from Volume 8.

Volume 8's rejected fantasy imagined:

> wordless total understanding; effortless comprehension; indestructibility.

Volume 10.5's photograph does **not** claim that the participants share one interpretation. It explicitly predicts `食い違い` if the meanings are put into words.

The photograph therefore preserves:

> **co-presence without semantic identity.**

It can record that these people were here together without deciding what “together” meant to each of them.

This is not telepathy. It is archive.

## 3.13 Story 4: family as the clean control case for continuity through change

The final story moves from school relationships into the Hikigaya home.

Hachiman and Komachi improvise a late-night meal, repeat a childhood pattern produced by their parents' work schedules, and walk together to a convenience store. Yet the scene is saturated with impending change.

Komachi's entrance exam is close. Hachiman has less than a year before he will leave the Service Club. The narrator explicitly imagines new people entering the club after him.

When Komachi becomes anxious, Hachiman says:

> `高校で待ってるからな`

This is an unusually clean version of supportive presence.

He does not promise the exam result. He does not take the exam for her. He does not reduce her options. He tells her where he will be.

The volume closes with:

> `季節が移ろうのならば、また、人のつながりも生生流転。`

and:

> `だから、今しばらくは。傍らのぬくもりとともに。`

The answer to preservation is not freezing the present.

> **Continuity can mean remaining warmly present while accepting that the form will change.**

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

## 4.1 External-social reliability remains high

Hachiman remains exceptionally good at reading practical structures:

- why Iroha's budget creates a deadline;
- what information a free paper needs;
- how to define a target audience;
- how page count, personnel, review cycles, and production constraints interact;
- when a request is unrealistically scoped;
- how Iroha deploys expressions strategically;
- how Yui makes a rebuke sound gentle;
- how Yukino's cold wording can contain genuine care.

The project sequence validates rather than undermines his operational competence.

## 4.2 Self-implicating interpretation remains less reliable

His weaknesses remain concentrated where he is personally desired or cared for.

With Iroha, he repeatedly interprets her behavior through Hayama. That inference has evidence: the outing's stated purpose is to prepare a Hayama date. But several of Iroha's behaviors become specifically Hachiman-directed, and the narration refuses to settle what degree of personal desire is present.

Hachiman therefore tends to choose the **lowest-self-implication explanation** when evidence permits several readings.

The correct V2 label is not “Hachiman fails to notice Iroha likes him.” The text does not prospectively settle that proposition. The stronger claim is:

> **Hachiman preserves Hayama as the default explanatory center even after Iroha's behavior exceeds the minimum necessary for Hayama-directed research.**

## 4.3 A major improvement: projection can now be recognized as projection

The Yukino career passage matters because Hachiman catches himself potentially imposing `幻想`.

Earlier volumes often required another person or later consequence to expose his idealization. Here he performs the correction internally:

> Yukino seemed like someone who must have a planned future → Yukino says she does not know → Hachiman recognizes that his image may have been self-authored.

That is real epistemic growth.

## 4.4 `わからない` no longer automatically triggers conquest

Across 10.5, Hachiman repeatedly leaves uncertainty unresolved:

- Yukino's future;
- Iroha's `素敵な何か`;
- the definition of the Service Club;
- what exact color he and Yukino see in the sunset;
- what the photographs will mean later.

He asks questions but does not always convert missing information into a puzzle he must forcibly solve.

That is an important difference from Volume 10's Hayama intervention.

## 4.5 The remaining anti-reliance reflex

Yui explicitly gives Hachiman permission to fail and promises to share the apology. Hachiman thinks that precisely because the words are sweet and kind, he must not `頼ったり委ねたり`.

This is not healthy independence. It is a still-active defensive rule:

> **Receiving care risks becoming dependence; therefore care must be repaid by renewed self-exertion.**

The volume does not reward that rule unambiguously. Yukino's later contingency plan demonstrates that reliance can be structured without erasing responsibility.

# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Hikigaya Hachiman

The largest Hachiman delta is not dramatic. It is ordinary.

He can now:

- spend time with people without every interaction being an emergency;
- help Zaimokuza think about a future without needing to rescue him;
- accept that Yukino may not know her own future;
- admit an outing with Iroha was tiring **and** enjoyable;
- work inside a distributed production structure;
- receive operational care from Yukino and emotional care from Yui;
- preserve a shared moment without defining it;
- imagine graduation before it happens.

The remaining problem is that he still often translates being cared for into an obligation to prove self-sufficiency.

## 5.2 Yukinoshita Yukino

V10.5 gives Yukino three important developments.

### A. Future uncertainty

`以前はそう思っていたけれど。……今は、まだわからない` is not mere indecision. It demonstrates that a former self-conception no longer governs automatically.

Her future has become a question she can leave open.

### B. Seniority toward Iroha

Yukino increasingly behaves as Iroha's senior rather than merely a Service Club member forced to tolerate her. She warns Iroha that official publication requires consideration because **Iroha** will bear the complaints. She checks production risks. She designs contingency.

This is competence in service of another person's authorship, not substitution for it.

### C. Care that plans for fallibility

The MAX Coffee, progress monitoring, apology, buffer, and revised schedule show a less brittle form of competence. Yukino does not need Hachiman to be flawless in order to trust his contribution.

That is as important for her as it is for him.

## 5.3 Yuigahama Yui

Yui continues the Volume-9 transition from atmosphere manager to accountable relational participant.

Her strongest 10.5 action is giving Hachiman permission to stop while promising to stay present for the consequence.

> `あたしも一緒に謝る`

This is exactly the kind of care earlier Hachiman's methods lacked: **shared aftermath**.

She also acknowledges mixed motive:

> `あたし、ずるいかも`

rather than purifying herself into the selfless girl who only wants everyone happy.

She remains operationally useful: finances, follow-up, social logistics, and smoothing institutional collaboration.

## 5.4 Isshiki Iroha

Iroha is the volume's most prominent character and perhaps its best argument against hidden-self essentialism.

She is simultaneously:

- manipulative;
- hardworking when sufficiently motivated;
- lazy;
- image-conscious;
- observant;
- institutionally opportunistic;
- grateful;
- flirty;
- strategic;
- capable of care;
- capable of exploiting others' care;
- genuinely interested in ordinary memory.

The story never reveals one of these as the “real Iroha” that invalidates the rest.

Her student-council development also continues. She does not simply dump the free paper on the Service Club. She contacts the printer, handles permissions, participates in interviews, checks material, and ultimately carries institutional responsibility for the publication.

## 5.5 Zaimokuza Yoshiteru

Zaimokuza remains ridiculous, but V10.5 upgrades him from static comic satellite to a useful longitudinal control case.

He is afraid of judgment before submitting anything. He changes career fantasies rapidly. Yet he still returns to writing.

His growth is precisely the kind of growth Hachiman has increasingly learned to recognize:

> **not a transformed essence, but repeated action across detours.**

## 5.6 Hikigaya Komachi

Komachi's role is quieter but structurally decisive.

She supplies:

- family continuity;
- preexisting mutual reliance;
- ordinary domestic labor;
- a relationship where support does not require interpretive conquest;
- explicit future transition.

Her entrance exam becomes the cleanest example in the volume of a problem Hachiman cannot solve for someone else but can accompany.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Hachiman–Yukino

State update:

> **provisional recognition → ordinary collaboration with increasing tolerance for uncertainty and direct usefulness recognition**

Important deltas:

- Hachiman hears `まだわからない` about Yukino's future and does not force a hidden answer.
- He recognizes his competent-future image of her as possible projection.
- Yukino admits she misread Hachiman's capacity.
- Hachiman tells her directly that her work is helping him.
- Yukino responds with visible embarrassment and then practical support.

Romantic coding is present in physical attention, blush, proximity, and Hachiman's sensory focus. Prospectively, however, the more important relationship change is epistemic:

> **they can admit that each has misread the other without treating the error as relational collapse.**

## 6.2 Hachiman–Yui

State update:

> **particular care → increasingly explicit permission for vulnerability, still complicated by Hachiman's anti-reliance reflex**

Yui:

- stays with him during the deadline;
- performs useful administrative work rather than merely encouraging him;
- says she will share the apology;
- tells him his suffering matters to her;
- admits her own motives are not wholly selfless or fully articulated.

Hachiman's response is warm but defensive. He still believes he must not yield to sweet care.

This makes the relationship more explicit while preserving its asymmetry.

## 6.3 Yukino–Yui

State update:

> **particular friendship → embodied ordinary ease and stable co-regulation**

The opening of Story 3 has Yui move her chair beside Yukino and share a blanket for warmth. Yukino normally complains about such closeness but permits it.

The significance is not erotic by itself. It is behavioral evidence of a friendship whose physical and social ease has increased markedly since early volumes.

The final photograph reinforces this: Yui and Iroha hold Yukino from both sides; Yukino looks bothered and embarrassed rather than alienated.

## 6.4 Hachiman–Iroha

State update:

> **senior/junior instrumental relation → recurring dyadic chemistry with mixed strategic and personal motives**

The trial date cannot be reduced to either “Iroha uses Hachiman for Hayama” or “Iroha is secretly dating Hachiman.”

What can be frozen prospectively:

- she knows ordinary invitations are likely to fail with him;
- she designs around that barrier;
- she chooses him as a test partner;
- she performs femininity more aggressively with him than she says she would with Hayama;
- she says the outing was enjoyable;
- she expects a `next time`;
- she later uses the outing as project research;
- she wants him to learn from it too.

This is **relationally particular** even if its romantic classification remains open.

## 6.5 Iroha–Yukino / Iroha–Yui

Both relationships now function as real senior-junior bonds.

Yukino gives structure, standards, and risk-aware protection.

Yui gives approachable social support and helps Iroha convert vague desire into a workable request.

Iroha's growing place in their ordinary life should not be mistaken for equivalence with the founding trio. Her own refusal to enter the first Service Club photograph recognizes that history has shape.

## 6.6 Hachiman–Komachi

The family relationship remains the volume's healthiest continuity baseline.

They can:

- tease;
- cook;
- remember childhood;
- depend on each other;
- imagine separation;
- state future presence.

Importantly, impending separation does not make the bond less real.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

## 7.1 Audit matrix

| Episode | Request owner | Target / affected party | Method | Authorization | Agency result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zaimokuza career consultation | Zaimokuza | Zaimokuza | research, challenge, comic confrontation | explicit | preserved; he keeps changing mind |
| Iroha trial date | Iroha + prior loose Hachiman commitment | Hachiman | concealed purpose / strategic invitation | partial; prior promise exists, full purpose delayed | Hachiman retains exit, then participates |
| free paper initial proposal | Iroha/student council | Service Club | emotional appeal + institutional motive | initially refused/conditional | group requires feasibility before commitment |
| free paper after preparation | Iroha/student council | Service Club | specs, estimate, schedule | explicit after constraint audit, though Hachiman is emotionally pressured | differentiated participation |
| deadline crisis | group | Hachiman | support, monitoring, contingency | Hachiman remains responsible for own work | failure made survivable |
| Komachi exam anxiety | Komachi | Komachi | presence/promise | no substitution attempted | authorship fully preserved |

## 7.2 The critical advance: capacity before commitment

Hachiman's joking line about work contains a serious procedural improvement:

> `キャパシティを正しく把握し` and, where necessary, reduce workload through negotiation.

That is almost the institutional analogue of the helping ethic developing since Volume 6.5:

> **do not treat willingness as infinite capacity.**

The group asks:

- how many pages;
- how many people;
- what deadline;
- what permissions;
- what budget;
- what review cycles.

This is healthier than accepting a vague moral burden and deciding costs afterward.

## 7.3 Iroha's manipulation remains visible

The project is not cleaned of coercion. Iroha uses:

- vulnerability performance;
- prior receipts;
- Hachiman's susceptibility to “little-sister-like” requests;
- deadline urgency.

The novel knows this is manipulative and makes it funny.

The V2 distinction is:

> **Manipulation can coexist with later accountable participation; the second does not erase the first.**

## 7.4 Yui and Yukino improve the ethics of aftermath

Yui:

> if the project fails, we apologize together.

Yukino:

> if Hachiman cannot finish, the process has a buffer.

These are important because earlier crises often made success the only morally survivable endpoint.

Here:

> **failure is incorporated into the social process before it happens.**

That reduces the incentive for self-sacrificial control.

## 7.5 Komachi as positive-control intervention

`高校で待ってるからな` is almost the purest positive helping line yet.

Hachiman offers:

- location;
- continuity;
- confidence;

without promising an outcome he cannot control.

It is presence rather than authorship.

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 Publishing, ranking, and preemptive self-protection

Story 1 satirizes creative labor while exposing a real social mechanism:

- ranking;
- review culture;
- prestige labor;
- employment gatekeeping;
- glamour versus actual workload;
- imagined access to celebrity.

Zaimokuza's fear shows how public measurement can deter participation before evaluation occurs.

This recalls but does not duplicate Hayama's expectation problem. Zaimokuza fears entering a system that can rank him poorly; Hayama is trapped in a system that already ranks him highly.

## 8.2 Student council as bureaucratic organism

Iroha's project is driven by fiscal incentives rather than pure educational idealism. Leftover budget must be used or future budget may shrink. The free paper therefore begins in institutional self-maintenance.

The novel treats this comically but realistically:

> institutions create incentives that can produce useful public goods for mixed reasons.

The final publication can be worthwhile even if its origin was budget preservation.

## 8.3 Professional language as comedy and conceptual tool

`決算`, `経費`, `入稿`, `台割`, `FIX`, `ターゲット`, `プロデューサー`, `ディレクター`, `AD`, and `下請け` create a parody of adult work.

But the vocabulary also externalizes the series' recurring relationship questions:

- who defines the concept;
- who owns the output;
- who bears revision cost;
- who checks;
- who gets blamed;
- what happens when the plan fails.

The work project becomes a low-stakes model of distributed responsibility.

## 8.4 The Service Club's institutional paradox

The Service Club can perform an institution's functions but cannot define its own social identity.

That paradox is now explicit.

It has:

- membership;
- routine;
- physical territory;
- repeated procedure;
- external clients;
- institutional history;

but Hachiman cannot write a public description of its present meaning.

Formal existence and relational definition therefore remain distinct.

# 9. Authenticity / `本物` ledger

## 9.1 Lexical audit

Across the four main stories:

- governing conceptual `本物`: **0**
- `偽物`: **0**
- governing explicit `本音` cluster: **0**

The dominant language is instead:

- `理解`
- `わかる`
- `言葉`
- `日常`
- `思い出`
- `写真`
- `定義`
- `共有`
- `将来`

This matters. 10.5 is not a volume in which characters lecture about the genuine. It is a volume in which they **live after having asked for it**.

## 9.2 Mixed motives remain compatible with meaningful action

Iroha is the central test.

The date and free-paper project are both multiply motivated. The narrative does not demand motive purification before treating them as meaningful.

This strengthens a proposition already emerging in Volumes 6.5 and 10:

> **impurity of motive does not by itself falsify action.**

## 9.3 Definition is not possession

The Service Club cannot define itself.

Yet the inability to define does not render the relation unreal.

This is a major post-`本物` correction to Hachiman's earlier tendencies. An undefined relation can still generate:

- obligations;
- affection;
- memories;
- shared labor;
- conflict;
- future pain.

## 9.4 The photograph is not wordless comprehension

The photograph deserves its own ledger distinction.

It does not communicate a proposition such as:

> “all three understand this relation in exactly the same way.”

It records a historical fact:

> **they occupied this moment together.**

Because meanings may diverge, the image's value lies precisely in not forcing one verbal definition onto everyone.

That is compatible with Volume 9's rejection of telepathic authenticity.

## 9.5 Prospective authenticity state after 10.5

The best prospective formulation is:

> **The genuine, if it exists, need not be a perfectly purified motive, a single public category, or a shared semantic definition. Volume 10.5 instead preserves the possibility that reality can reside in repeated, revisable participation among people who continue to mean different things by the same shared life.**

This remains inference, not textual doctrine.

# 10. Dependency / autonomy ledger

## 10.1 Healthy reliance becomes increasingly procedural

The free-paper project demonstrates a distributed model:

- Iroha initiates and interfaces institutionally;
- Yukino scopes, schedules, edits, and creates contingencies;
- Yui handles accounts/social follow-up and emotional support;
- Hachiman writes, frames audience, photographs, and solves practical gaps.

No one becomes unnecessary.

No one can plausibly complete the same project alone without a large cost increase.

This is **interdependence**, not evidence of pathology.

## 10.2 Hachiman still distrusts being supported

His response to Yui shows the remaining problem. He believes sweet words are precisely the ones he must not rely upon.

So dependence anxiety persists even as the actual relationship system becomes healthier.

## 10.3 Yukino's contingency plan is autonomy-compatible care

She does not take over Hachiman's writing. She restructures the process around his possible failure.

This preserves:

- his authorship of his work;
- her responsibility as project manager;
- the group's ability to complete the collective task.

It is therefore a strong positive control case for **help without substitution**.

## 10.4 Komachi/Hachiman: long-term dependence without loss of personhood

The sibling relationship demonstrates that dependence need not be transitional pathology. They have supported each other for years. The anticipated future is not total separation but changed form.

Komachi may enter Hachiman's school. Hachiman may later leave home. Their relation survives because it is not reducible to one physical arrangement.

# 11. Japanese language, voice, and recurring lexical systems

## 11.1 `わからない` as honest modal restraint

Yukino:

> `今は、まだわからない`

The adverbs matter.

- `今は` limits the claim to the present.
- `まだ` keeps future change possible.
- `わからない` states uncertainty rather than incompetence.

This is linguistically different from a categorical refusal. The line preserves futurity.

## 11.2 Hachiman's `幻想を勝手に押し付ける`

`勝手に` continues the lexical system established in Volume 5: unilateral interpretation recognized as something the interpreter does **to** another person.

Here the object is not moral perfection but future certainty.

That is an important expansion of the earlier self-indictment.

## 11.3 Iroha's performed register

Iroha's voice constantly shifts among:

- elongated casual politeness (`～ですよー`, `～ですかねー`);
- strategic cute deference;
- blunt evaluation;
- rapid-fire rejection-gag language;
- sincere gratitude;
- whispered intimacy.

The variability is the point. Her voice is a tool she consciously handles, but the existence of control does not render the resulting speech meaningless.

`こんなことするの、先輩にだけですよ` is particularly ambiguous because it can function simultaneously as:

- teasing;
- flirtation;
- strategic pressure;
- true statement about differential behavior.

No single English paraphrase should erase that multiplicity.

## 11.4 `ずるい`

The volume uses `ずるい` in two important ways:

- Iroha explicitly celebrates being “a little unfair” as feminine strategy.
- Yui accepts that she herself may be unfair in how she relates to Hachiman.

The lexical echo matters because it refuses the split between “strategic Iroha” and “pure Yui.” Both girls can possess personal desire that affects how they care.

The difference is not purity versus manipulation. It is how openly each person recognizes and manages mixed motive.

## 11.5 `定義` / `共有`

The key photograph sequence distinguishes two operations:

- `定義する`: define the relationship;
- `共有する`: share/preserve something of it.

Hachiman cannot do the first.

He thinks the photograph can do some version of the second.

That lexical distinction is central to the volume's epistemology.

## 11.6 `日常` / `思い出` / `写真`

The volume's strongest lexical field is archival.

`写真` becomes especially concentrated in Story 3. `思い出` links the date, ordinary snapshots, final group photograph, and afterword. `日常` is most conceptually active in Story 3 and paratext.

The semantic movement is:

> ordinary present → selected record → future memory.

## 11.7 `高校で待ってる`

Hachiman's line to Komachi uses a simple locative promise rather than an outcome guarantee.

`待ってる` says:

> I will be there.

It does not say:

> I will make you pass.

This is why it functions so well as a positive-control helping statement.

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertextuality

## 12.1 Meta-light-novel satire

Story 1 is openly self-referential: writing labor, editors, Gagaga Bunko, rankings, online fiction, voice actresses, deadlines, and publishing gatekeeping.

The jokes should not automatically be converted into a grand thesis about art. Their analytical value lies in how creative exposure becomes a safe comic analogue for interpersonal exposure.

## 12.2 Mother Goose: Sugar and Spice

The nursery-rhyme reference provides Story 2's title and recurring metaphor.

Hachiman initially treats “sugar” and “spice” as separable ingredients—sweetness versus strategic pain. But the story ends with the unclassifiable `素敵な何か`, then extends that unknowability to other girls close to him.

The intertext therefore supports **combination without reduction**, not a gender-essentialist proposition that all girls possess one mystical secret essence.

## 12.3 Workplace/deadline parody

Story 3 transforms teenagers into a mock production committee:

- producer Iroha;
- director Yukino;
- AD Yui;
- subcontractor Hachiman.

The joke is funny because it maps unexpectedly well onto their actual functions.

## 12.4 Seasonal quotation

`冬来たりなば春遠からじ` at the end paraphrases the familiar “If Winter comes, can Spring be far behind?” seasonal formulation associated with Shelley.

Here it does not simply mean optimism. The winter/spring movement is also temporal loss: spring means exams, graduation, new members, and eventual departure.

Change is hopeful and painful at once.

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial tests

## Test A — Is Volume 10.5 just “filler”?

**Reject.**

It advances no large external plot, but it materially updates:

- Yukino's future self-conception;
- Hachiman's projection audit;
- Iroha's student-council competence;
- Yui's care language;
- Yukino's contingency-based helping;
- the Service Club's unresolved definition;
- ordinary-life temporality.

Its smallness is the method.

## Test B — Does the photograph restore Hachiman's fantasy of wordless understanding?

**Reject, with caution.**

The wording may look similar because Hachiman values a nonverbal medium. But the logical structure differs.

Volume 8 imagined shared comprehension **without speech**.

Volume 10.5 explicitly says verbalized meanings would probably **differ**.

The photo preserves a common object across different meanings. It does not claim those meanings are the same.

## Test C — Is Iroha's date fake because it is practice for Hayama?

**Reject.**

The stated practice motive is real. So are enjoyment, observation, strategic use, later project research, and Hachiman-specific performance.

“Fake” assumes one motive must invalidate another. The story does not support that.

## Test D — Is the outing fully consensual because Hachiman stays?

**Reject.**

Iroha deliberately withholds the purpose because she predicts he would refuse. That is a procedural manipulation.

What makes it ethically lower-stakes than Kyoto or the marathon is retained exit capacity and a prior loose commitment, not perfect consent.

## Test E — Does Yukino's career uncertainty prove she has lost autonomy?

**Reject.**

Prospectively, the opposite is at least as plausible. She is no longer obligated to perform certainty merely because others expect it.

`まだわからない` can be an autonomy claim.

## Test F — Does Yui's willingness to share blame solve Hachiman's self-sacrifice problem?

**No.**

It creates a healthier environment, but Hachiman still turns her care into fuel for renewed self-exertion. His anti-reliance schema survives.

## Test G — Is Iroha now simply a fourth Service Club member?

**Reject.**

She is deeply embedded in their ordinary life. But her own choice to photograph the original trio first recognizes a history and boundary that remain distinct.

The correct model is **overlapping relational systems**, not automatic membership equivalence.

## Test H — Is the end nostalgic pessimism?

**Reject as too narrow.**

Hachiman anticipates future pain and departure, but the response is not withdrawal. It is:

> remain with the warmth for now.

The volume therefore joins finitude and affirmation.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

Full-series hindsight strengthens several patterns but must not replace the prospective state.

## 15.1 Ordinary life as evidentiary substrate

Later developments make the photographs, tea, deadline work, outings, and family nights feel like evidence for what the characters will eventually risk losing or choosing. But at this point, the text has not assigned a final relational form.

The proper retrospective claim is:

> **10.5 creates an archive from which later choice can acquire weight.**

Not:

> 10.5 secretly announces the ending.

## 15.2 Yukino's uncertainty

Later family/autonomy conflicts can make `今は、まだわからない` look like a direct setup. That is true structurally, but prospectively the line is broader: it frees Yukino from Hachiman's competent-future projection before it specifies what she will choose instead.

## 15.3 Yui's `ずるい`

Later romantic developments make her admission of mixed motive especially significant. Retrospectively it belongs in the longitudinal record of Yui learning to speak from personal desire rather than representing herself as only the guardian of group happiness.

But she has not yet fully named that desire here.

## 15.4 Iroha and multiplicity

Later Iroha material may clarify some motives, but 10.5 should retain her present plurality. Her value as a character partly lies in resisting the demand that every gesture be sorted into “real” or “performance.”

## 15.5 The Service Club photograph

Retrospectively, the photograph becomes powerful because later choices can change the group. Its original function remains simpler:

> **record the fact of shared ordinary presence before anyone can define its future.**

# 18. Cumulative delta and state freeze after Volume 10.5

## 18.1 Hachiman

**Strengthened:**

- recognizes some of his projections without external correction;
- tolerates `わからない` more often;
- accepts distributed work;
- increasingly values ordinary time;
- can preserve a shared moment without defining it;
- can offer presence instead of outcome control with Komachi.

**Still unstable:**

- being cared for still activates anti-reliance logic;
- Hachiman-specific romantic/self-worth interpretations remain defensive;
- he can still be manipulated through responsibility and care;
- accepting other people's uncertainty is easier in ordinary life than in high-stakes crisis.

## 18.2 Yukino

Frozen state:

> **chosen uncertainty + increasingly non-substitutionary competence**

She can say she does not know her future. She can help a junior by structuring rather than replacing. She can plan for Hachiman's fallibility. She can receive direct recognition of usefulness without converting the entire relationship into a competence transaction.

## 18.3 Yui

Frozen state:

> **care increasingly includes shared consequence and admitted personal motive**

She does not merely keep everyone smiling. She makes failure survivable, handles work, admits unfairness, and states that Hachiman's suffering matters personally.

## 18.4 Iroha

Frozen state:

> **strategic persona + emerging institutional authorship + particular relational access**

Her performance is not disproven by sincerity; her sincerity is not disproven by performance. She can create a bad plan, manipulate people into engagement, and then genuinely carry responsibility for the result.

## 18.5 Service Club

Frozen state:

> **socially real, operationally functional, emotionally particular, semantically unresolved**

The club cannot define itself in public language. That does not prevent it from functioning as a relationship system.

The central new archival image is the photograph:

> **shared form without compulsory shared meaning.**

## 18.6 `本物`

No new lexical definition.

The post-10.5 prospective state is:

> **If `本物` is to remain useful, it must accommodate relations whose participants are partially unknowable, whose motives are mixed, whose public definition remains unstable, and whose shared past may later be remembered differently.**

## 18.7 Dependency/autonomy

No prospective evidence supports upgrading ordinary reliance into pathology.

The strongest positive model is increasingly:

> **distributed reliance + fallibility + retained authorship + shared aftermath.**

## 18.8 Temporal state

This is the volume's largest cumulative addition.

By the end of 10.5, Hachiman has begun to measure the remaining ordinary time:

- one year until his own graduation;
- Komachi's transition into high school;
- possible future members of the club;
- photographs that will become painful memories;
- a household whose daily form will change.

The series' preservation problem is therefore becoming explicitly temporal.

> **The question is no longer merely “How do we keep this?” It is “How do we inhabit this honestly when we know its present form cannot remain?”**

### Frozen Volume 10.5 governing sentence

> **By the end of Volume 10.5, the Service Club still cannot define what it is, but its members have begun to discover that definition is not the only form of truth available to them: work can be shared without identical motives, care can include the possibility of failure, photographs can preserve co-presence without forcing one interpretation, and continuity can survive change when people keep choosing to participate while the ordinary time remains.**
