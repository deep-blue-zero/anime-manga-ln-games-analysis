# OREGAIRU V2 — Volume 10 Deep Reading
## 『やはり俺の青春ラブコメはまちがっている。10』

> **Prospective governing question:** Checkpoint C ended with the Service Club newly capable of asking, answering, disagreeing, and continuing without pretending that complete understanding had been achieved. What happens when that procedure meets a problem in which the requests themselves conflict, the person being investigated explicitly refuses disclosure, and social expectation has already narrowed what counts as a freely chosen answer?

> **V2 thesis:** Volume 10 tests **answerability under conflicting claims**. Hachiman has genuinely changed: he asks other people for help, accepts limits on collaborators, asks Yukino directly about her future, explicitly wants to know Totsuka rather than preserve an idealized image of him, and refuses to treat Yukino's private disclosure as information he owns the right to redistribute. Yet his old pathology survives in a more sophisticated form. Miura asks him to discover Hayama's academic track; Hayama asks him to stop. Hachiman chooses one request over the other, engineers physical and emotional conditions designed to weaken Hayama's ability to refuse, and converts Hiratsuka's pedagogical principle—adults may expand and narrow options while the student retains final authorship—into its coercive inversion: if Hayama has too many options, Hachiman will forcibly narrow them until the desired answer appears. The volume therefore does not present post-`本物` growth as linear. It puts a normative rule beside its violation. At the same time, Hayama reveals the inverse tragedy of Hachiman's expectation-avoidance: a person so able to satisfy others that the socially correct answer can cease to feel chosen. Miura, Totsuka, Yukino, Iroha, Komachi, and even Hayama himself then provide competing models of how partial knowledge, ordinary liking, inherited roles, advice, reliance, and present ownership can become meaningful without becoming pure. `本物` remains unresolved. The deeper question is whether a relationship can become more real while the other person retains the power to answer differently.

## 1.2 Internal architecture

Volume 10 is formally unusual. Its nine numbered chapters are framed and interrupted by three explicitly labeled “handwritten record / monologue” sections:

1. `第一の手記　或いは、それは誰の独白でもない。`
2. `① とうとう、比企谷小町は神頼みをする。`
3. `② 相変わらず、雪ノ下陽乃はかき乱してくる。`
4. `③ いつの間にか、一色いろはは居座っている。`
5. `④ それでも、三浦優美子は知りたいと思う。`
6. `⑤ いつかその日まで、戸塚彩加は待っている。`
7. `⑥ 颯爽と、雪ノ下陽乃は暗闇に去って行く。`
8. `第二の手記　もしくは、それは誰しもの独白でもある。`
9. `⑦ いつでも、葉山隼人は期待に応えている。`
10. `⑧ そうして、彼ら彼女らの過去と未来は交錯し、現在に帰結す。`
11. `第三の手記　であるならば、それは誰の独白だったのか。`
12. `⑨ なれど、雪ノ下陽乃はかく語りき`

This frame is not decorative. The three titles deliberately destabilize speaker ownership:

- nobody's monologue;
- everybody's monologue;
- whose monologue was it?

The first-pass reading attributed the notes too readily to Hachiman. V2 treats that as an overstatement. The Dazai intertexts undeniably connect to Hachiman's current reading and psychological vocabulary, but the second and third notes resonate strongly with Hayama's expectation burden, Haruno's desire to expose performance, and the larger cast's fear of being misrecognized. The third note's repeated `私は信頼されている` and image of becoming a `信頼の魔物` are particularly Hayama-coded, yet the novel never provides a stable diegetic speaker tag. **Speaker identity is therefore an intentional ambiguity, not a fact to be solved.**

Watari's afterword reinforces this treatment. It speaks of a small monologue that may remain in “someone” or “everyone,” and asks what happens when apparent similarity produces either affinity or a recognition of irreducible difference. The formal frame universalizes a problem without erasing character specificity.

## 1.3 Prospective evidence boundary

Sections 2–14 use only information available through Volume 10. Later uses of `共依存`, later romantic resolution, later Haruno claims, and later final-series formulations of `本物` cannot govern the prospective reading.

Checkpoint C supplies the incoming state:

- Volume 7 showed that preservation may be legitimate while predictive accuracy fails to confer jurisdiction over other people's relationship choices.
- Volume 8 showed Hachiman applying his own anti-coercion principle asymmetrically, honoring Iroha's right to choose while discounting Yukino's `これは私の意志よ` when her choice threatened the club.
- Volume 9 changed the procedure: Hachiman became a requester, Yukino and Yui were permitted to misunderstand and object, and the trio agreed to continue without pretending the request for `本物` had been completed.
- Ordinary life returned, but the request remained open.

Volume 10 is therefore the first major test of whether **ask rather than assume** survives once two legitimate requests point in opposite directions.

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

Volume 10 opens not with crisis but with **continuation**. New Year, Komachi's exams, birthday shopping, shrine fortunes, casual group overlap, student-council errands, course selection, and a school marathon all belong to ordinary seasonal life. That ordinariness matters. Volume 9's repair is no longer protected by emergency intensity. It has to function when there is no climactic Christmas event forcing everyone to speak.

The first handwritten record supplies the formal directive. The speaker quotes the opening of Dazai's 『人間失格』—`恥の多い生涯を送って来ました`—and remembers abandoning the book because it felt too exposing. Now the desire is to return to the unread remainder:

> `本の続きを。あのとき、読み進めることのできなかったその先を。`

The metaphor is well suited to the post-Volume-9 state. The characters have not rewritten the earlier pages. Kyoto, the election, failed interventions, family history, embarrassment, and rivalry remain. What changes is willingness to **read farther** instead of stopping where the text becomes personally threatening.

Several low-stakes scenes immediately show real procedural growth.

Hachiman directly invites Yui to help choose Yukino's birthday gift: `……明日とか、暇か？` This is small but important. Earlier volumes repeatedly required Service Club requests, accidental encounters, or external pretexts to legitimize time together. Here he simply asks. When Miura's group appears and Yui is visibly divided between social circles, Yukino tells her `またすぐ会えるじゃない`. The Service Club is not demanded as an exclusive proof of closeness. Yui is permitted to belong elsewhere without betraying the trio.

On the crowded train, Yukino silently catches Hachiman's coat hem. The gesture is physically intimate but practical, and neither turns it into a confession. Later, after admitting she spent New Year alone because `私はいてもいなくても大差ないもの`, she and Hachiman end with the modest future assumption `……今年も、よろしく`. Their relation contains enough continuity to imagine another year without yet naming what that continuity means.

But the volume's central problem enters through **course choice**. Academic streaming will change classes, trajectories, family expectations, and future proximity. Miura wants to know Hayama's track because she wants a chance to remain near him. Hayama refuses to disclose it and explicitly asks Hachiman to stop prying. Hachiman therefore encounters something Volume 9 did not solve:

> **What does answerability require when one person's sincere request can only be fulfilled by violating another person's sincere refusal?**

That is the prospective problem to freeze before any later resolution is imported.

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

## 3.1 Movement I — New Year makes ordinary reliance visible

Komachi's examination anxiety initiates the shrine visit. Hachiman distinguishes sharply between what he can do and what he cannot. He will not pray for outcomes he thinks can be improved through his own action; he prays for Komachi because her examination is ultimately hers. This is a useful baseline after the intervention-heavy middle volumes: there are outcomes even Hachiman recognizes he cannot own.

The fortune sequence literalizes shared vulnerability. Yui hands Komachi her good fortune and takes the bad one; Yukino ties fortunes together and jokes about averaging them. The scene does not erase risk. It turns risk into something that can be held socially.

The same chapter broadens the trio's social ecology. Yui can leave with Miura's group because Yukino expects return rather than demanding exclusivity. Hachiman asks Yui for help buying a present rather than waiting for an institutional pretext. Komachi transparently maneuvers Hachiman and Yukino into a shared return journey. The club's post-Christmas repair is becoming ordinary enough to survive overlapping relationships.

Yet Yukino's family absence remains troubling. `私はいてもいなくても大差ないもの` turns exclusion into an efficiency calculation: if her presence makes no difference, absence is easy to justify. Hachiman's immediate practical answer—then perhaps it is fine—is emotionally inadequate. Volume 10 will repeatedly ask whether a condition can be functionally workable while still failing the person living inside it.

## 3.2 Movement II — partial knowledge becomes sufficient for care

Birthday shopping directly revisits Volume 5's epistemic crisis. Hachiman knows that there remain many things he does not know: `知っていたつもりでも、知らないことはたくさんある`. But he and Yui can still choose a present. They do not need complete access to Yukino's “true self” to make a caring, revisable guess based on accumulated ordinary time.

The later encounter with Haruno, Hayama, and Yukino's mother deepens the point. Hayama accidentally uses `雪乃`, revealing a historical intimacy unavailable to Hachiman and Yui. Hachiman recognizes that there is accumulated time outsiders cannot touch: `余人がその思い出に触れることはできない`. The past is real without becoming public property.

The mother-daughter exchange again exposes Yukino's difficulty with choice. Her mother's beautifully polite “question” carries the force of a decision. Yukino looks toward Hachiman and Yui; Haruno immediately cuts off that route with `雪乃ちゃん、ダメだよ`. Haruno is not simply liberating her sister. She replaces maternal direction with a demand that Yukino prove independence. The scene keeps Yukino between two external expectations while the birthday gift itself offers a much gentler model: Hachiman and Yui act on partial knowledge, give something, and let Yukino decide what it means.

## 3.3 Movement III — rumor turns private relation into common property

The Hayama–Yukino dating rumor creates a social system with no single author. Everyone merely heard it; everyone merely repeats it; the absence of ownership makes accountability difficult. The rumor assigns an intimate relation to two people without requiring either person's consent.

Hayama reacts with unusual anger because the rumor converts private history and present contact into public entertainment. When Hachiman later asks about that history, Hayama changes the subject and Hachiman initially respects the boundary.

Iroha's now-routine presence in the Service Club provides a contrasting institutional movement. She has gone from reluctant installed president to someone who can simply occupy the club, route work through the correct channels, and later direct her own student council. Her relation to the trio has become ordinary rather than crisis-dependent.

The chapter also makes future separation unavoidable. Different academic tracks may split classes. Yui is visibly uneasy; Yukino jokes from the position of someone already often separated from them. The trio's “same place” can no longer be treated as an indefinitely stable container.

## 3.4 Movement IV — Miura's request reveals the conflict inside post-Volume-9 answerability

Hayama asks Hachiman whether someone told him to ask about the course choice. His contemptuous `……また、仕事だから、か？` attacks Hachiman's oldest moral camouflage: externalizing personal involvement as “Service Club work.” Then Hayama makes his own request:

> `そういう煩わしいの、やめてくれないか`

He asks Hachiman to stop.

This is the key complication. Miura's wish is not trivial. She wants the information because she wants a chance to stay near Hayama. But Hayama's refusal is also not trivial. He has reasons not to let his choice become a social command for everyone around him.

The Yukino–Miura confrontation makes the epistemic dimension explicit. Yukino rejects the premise that compulsory disclosure can settle public interpretation. Even if she told everything, why should the crowd believe it? Her conclusion is that the understanding of close people is sufficient:

> `私は近しい人が理解してくれているならそれだけで構わないから`

Miura answers with one of the volume's strongest formulations:

> `近しい人、ってやつ。……それになりたいから知りたいんじゃん`

Closeness cannot be a credential one must possess before asking. Sometimes asking is part of how closeness is built.

Hachiman recognizes the desire beneath the factual question: `ただ、気持ちが知りたい。理解、したいのだ.` He also formulates the ethical difficulty correctly: he is not confident that it is right to enter the space of someone who does not want to be entered. Crucially, he asks Miura whether she still wants to know if the process is intrusive. She does.

The scene is progress because desire is spoken rather than purified. It is also danger because **Miura's willingness cannot authorize Hayama's involuntary disclosure**. Volume 10 will not allow “a sincere request exists” to function as a complete moral warrant.

## 3.5 Movement V — Tobe and Totsuka dismantle role reduction

Hachiman investigates Hayama's course through friends and discovers that apparent closeness does not imply reciprocal knowledge. Tobe consults Hayama repeatedly, but Hayama has never consulted Tobe. Hachiman says `……信頼してんだな`; Tobe's answer reveals a one-way reliance structure. He depends on Hayama yet may not recognize when Hayama himself is in trouble.

This prompts an important self-correction: a person who consults nobody is not therefore a person with no problems.

Totsuka delivers an even more personal correction. He has a serious future, a club to lead, recruitment responsibilities, and plans beyond Hachiman's “angel” joke. He tells Hachiman, `八幡は知らないことばっかりだね`, then finally adds `あと、……ぼくのこととか`.

Hachiman's response is one of the clearest longitudinal reversals of the reread. Volume 5 paired Yui's `もっと知りたい` with Hachiman's explicit refusal: `俺は……もっと知りたいとは思わない.` Here he thinks:

> `でも、だからもっと知りたいと思うのだ。`

And when Totsuka offers future help, Hachiman answers:

> `俺だって頼りにしてる。まだわかんねぇけど、……でも、たぶん頼る`

He does not suddenly claim knowledge. He recognizes ignorance and names a future willingness to rely. The old defensive sequence—idealize, refuse knowledge, deny need—is beginning to reverse.

There remains a transitional danger in Hachiman's imagery of peeling off masks and cutting toward an interior self. He still imagines knowledge partly as penetration. But `戸塚は戸塚だ` interrupts the fantasy of a hidden final essence. Totsuka does not become more real by being stripped of roles. He becomes more real when Hachiman allows those roles—captain, student, friend, admired person—to belong to one subject rather than to Hachiman's joke.

## 3.6 Movement VI — Hiratsuka states the volume's clearest normative rule

The career consultation supplies the strongest explicit model of legitimate influence in Volume 10.

Hiratsuka tells Hachiman that a present course choice is one among many future opportunities. Teachers can help by giving students options and, importantly, by removing some options:

> `教師がしてやれるのは選択肢を増やすこと。……そして、選択肢を削ってやることだ`

Hachiman asks whether reducing choices is itself acceptable. Hiratsuka answers:

> `無論、最後に決めるのはそいつだ。我々はアドバイスするのが限界さ`

The distinction is exact:

- advice may reshape a decision environment;
- competence may identify bad options;
- adults may make consequences legible;
- **final authorship remains with the chooser.**

This becomes the ethical ruler against which the marathon intervention must be measured.

Komachi's exam anxiety offers a domestic version. Rational probability does not calm her. What finally helps is Hachiman's promise that even failure will not make her unsafe: `いざとなったらお前一人くらい俺がどうにかしてやるよ` followed by `だから、安心しろ`. He does not take the exam for her or dictate her desired school. He changes the cost of failure by making continued care credible.

This is one of the volume's healthiest helping models: **support can widen practical freedom by making a bad outcome survivable.**

## 3.7 Movement VII — Iroha demonstrates institutional development rather than rescue

Iroha now routes requests formally, delegates, gives instructions, and works through her own council. Hachiman explicitly compares the Christmas crisis with the present and notes that Iroha's motivation, the council's awkwardness, and his own relation to them have all changed.

His formulation is important:

> `きっかけは何でもよくて、こうやって少しずつ、重たいものを一緒に運んで行けるならあり方を変えてゆけるのだ`

A compromised origin does not permanently own a later practice. Iroha became president through manipulated choice architecture, but the office has become genuinely hers through repeated work. This extends Volume 6.5's behavioral-change argument. Origin matters; it does not possess unlimited veto power over later ownership.

## 3.8 Movement VIII — Haruno turns expectation into the hidden Hayama problem

Haruno repeatedly destabilizes Yukino and Hachiman, but Volume 10 also uses her to turn attention toward Hayama. When Hachiman asks what Hayama might have expected, Haruno answers:

> `見つけてくれることを、かな`

That claim is analytically powerful but must remain correctly classified. It is **Haruno's assertion/inference**, not omniscient access to Hayama. The second handwritten record soon echoes the desire to have one's clowning seen through and to be found by a person unusually sensitive to evil. The formal adjacency makes the hypothesis compelling without converting it into fact.

The note's self-condemnation—`期待をした自分が、理解したと思った自分が、理解されたと思った自分が許せない`—could fit Hachiman, Hayama, Haruno, or the broader volume's network of disappointed mutual models. Its strength lies in refusing final ownership.

## 3.9 Movement IX — Hachiman recognizes Hayama's expectation trap

Hachiman's most important Hayama insight is not that Hayama is “secretly like him.” It is that they are constrained in nearly opposite ways.

Hachiman protects himself by refusing expectation. Hayama is so competent at meeting expectation that other people's confidence becomes a cage. Hachiman finally calls the pattern a kind of self-sacrifice: meeting others' expectations and avoiding their pain can also require surrendering one's own options.

This is a valuable analogy, but it needs narrowing. Hayama is not simply another Hachiman. His social position, competence, family history, charisma, and pleasure in winning make the burden different. More importantly, later he will claim part of the role as his own: `それが、俺だ`.

The volume's question is therefore subtler than “expectations are fake.” It asks what happens when a role begins as social demand but becomes partly inhabited, valued, and defended by the person carrying it.

## 3.10 Movement X — the marathon shows methodological growth and ethical regression at once

Hachiman calls Totsuka: `……頼みがある`. The post-Volume-9 procedure survives. He does not simply assign Totsuka a task. Totsuka consents, Hachiman tells him not to overdo it, and Totsuka remains free to refuse.

Zaimokuza initially resists. Hachiman recognizes the burden and says he can forget it. Zaimokuza then supplies explicit conditional consent—ramen compensation and a self-protective condition if blame arrives. This is genuine growth from the cultural-festival and Kyoto patterns. **Collaborators are no longer silently converted into cost-bearing pieces.**

The target, however, is different.

Hayama has explicitly asked Hachiman to stop. Hachiman engineers a stretch of the race in which he can isolate Hayama, waits until fatigue may reduce his composure, and deliberately insults Miura to provoke anger. His own theory is explicit:

> `余裕がなくなった時、人は本音を漏らす`

He therefore designs conditions to reduce Hayama's capacity to maintain the refusal he has already expressed.

The coercive core becomes unmistakable when Hachiman borrows Hiratsuka's language of options and inverts its ethical limit:

> `葉山隼人には選択肢が多すぎて、絞り込めない。なら、その選択肢を無理やりにでも削ればいい。葉山の文理選択をこっちが決定できれば、三浦からの依頼は達成できる。`

Hiratsuka: narrow options through advice; **the person decides**.

Hachiman: forcibly narrow Hayama's options until **Hachiman determines the answer**.

The lexical echo makes the category error unusually clear. Volume 10 puts the norm and the violation in the same book.

## 3.11 Movement XI — Hachiman's brilliant inference fails, and Hayama answers back

Hachiman orders Hayama to choose sciences as a trap. He reasons that rejection will reveal the contrary choice. He then constructs a broader theory: perhaps Hayama wants academic separation to dissolve difficult relationships without direct confrontation.

The inference is socially elegant—and wrong.

Hayama rejects the relational theory. He does not want to reset or end the group; he explicitly says advancement does not automatically reset human relationships. The failure matters because it prevents Hachiman's social intelligence from becoming narrative omniscience. Even at his sharpest, he can model a person incorrectly.

The deeper exchange is Hayama's direct admission:

> `俺は君が嫌いだ`

He says he cannot bear feeling inferior to Hachiman, and may elevate Hachiman to “equal” status merely so that losing to him becomes tolerable. Hachiman recognizes the mirror: he has elevated Hayama into an absolute good person so that his own inferiority and resentment become easier to narrate.

This is one of the series' cleanest cases of reciprocal de-idealization. Neither man's flattering model of the other is altruistic. Both use the other as part of a story about themselves.

Yet Hayama's response also restores agency. He rejects Hachiman's command precisely because Hachiman gave it: `だから君の言う通りにはしない`. Hachiman accepts the answer rather than escalating further. The method was coercive, but the person targeted remains capable of surprising the strategist and changing the conclusion.

Hayama then insists on winning the race:

> `……いいや、勝つさ。……それが、俺だ`

This line prevents “Hayama's public role is merely fake” from surviving. He is burdened by expectation; he also possesses pride, competitiveness, and real attachment to the self that wins. A socially produced role can become partly self-authored without the history of pressure disappearing.

## 3.12 Movement XII — asking Yukino directly becomes the ethical contrast

After the marathon, Yukino treats Hachiman's injury. The scene carries strong dyadic romantic coding—physical proximity, care, embarrassment, and a ribbon-like knot on the bandage—but its most important analytical function is procedural.

Hachiman asks:

> `……なぁ。進路、どっちに進むか、聞いてもいいか`

Yukino notices the novelty:

> `あなたがそういうことを聞くのって初めてね`

Hachiman knows he has had earlier opportunities and deliberately maintained a boundary. This time he asks permission. Yukino answers and then voluntarily supplements the answer:

> `……一応、文系ということにはなっているわ`
>
> `ええ。だから、……とりあえずはみんな一緒ね`

The contrast with Hayama is deliberate. With one person Hachiman engineers disclosure after refusal; with another he asks and receives more than the minimum answer because the person chooses to give it.

His ensuing thought is one of Volume 10's best temporal formulations:

> `変わらないのは過去の事実だけだ。それは縛り付ける重荷にもなるけれど、繫ぎ止める杭にもなるのかもしれない。踏み越えた一歩がせめて足跡になればいい。`

Past fact is immutable. Its meaning and present use are not. It can bind, anchor, or become a footprint already crossed.

Yui's arrival prevents the scene from becoming a sealed dyad. She notices the intimacy, then rejoins. The triangle remains structurally present even as pairwise relations deepen unevenly.

## 3.13 Movement XIII — Miura's answer validates ordinary desire without purifying it

Once Miura learns that Hayama is probably in the humanities track, she decides to choose the same. Yukino questions the casualness of the decision. Miura's answer is strikingly ordinary: university entrance can be retried, but this particular relational opportunity cannot. She likes the group, including the troublesome parts.

Yui responds:

> `それで、いいんだ。もっと簡単でよかったんだ……`

Hachiman then thinks:

> `ふわふわした感情だって、本物に至る可能性は充分にある`

This is not an authoritative dictionary definition of `本物`. It is a **Hachiman inference**, and should remain labeled as such. But it is an important prospective development. The genuine need not begin from purified motive, certainty, or a metaphysically exceptional bond. An ordinary, “fluffy” feeling can potentially become something genuine through later action.

Miura's motive remains mixed: attraction, group attachment, fear of lost opportunity, ordinary teenage future-planning. Volume 10 does not treat that mixture as disqualifying.

## 3.14 Movement XIV — Yukino and Hayama revise history without erasing it

At the pub gathering, Hayama apologizes for the dating rumor. Yukino shifts to an older matter and acknowledges that there may have been better ways to handle their shared past. She apologizes for trouble and thanks him for having tried to care:

> `ただ、気遣ってくれたことには感謝している`

Hayama says she has changed. Yukino answers that many things are different now, looking toward Yui and Hachiman.

Her advice to Hayama is especially important:

> `あなたも過去に縛られなくていいと思うけれど。……誰かの背中を無理に追う必要もない`

Hayama answers:

> `……それも含めて、俺だよ`

This is not a defense of determinism. It is a claim of present ownership. A past influence may have constrained or shaped a person without becoming something that must be surgically removed for the current self to count as authentic. Volume 10 thus complicates the series' earlier fear of origin contamination: **what was once imposed can become part of what someone now chooses to carry.**

## 3.15 Movement XV — Hayama reveals the paradox of socially prewritten choice

Hayama corrects Hachiman's theory of why he hid his course. He does not want relationships to dissolve. His more important statement is:

> `それしか選びようがなかったものを選んでも、それを自分の選択とはいわないだろ`

The line crystallizes his problem. A formally available option can cease to feel chosen when social, familial, or reputational structure makes every alternative effectively illegible.

Hayama never plainly says “I chose humanities.” He tells Hachiman that sciences are wrong, and the binary school context makes humanities a very strong inference. V2 preserves the evidence classification:

- **textual fact:** Hayama rejects “sciences.”
- **high-confidence inference:** his track is humanities.
- **not established as fact:** the full content of family pressure or the exact professional future expected of him.

He then says:

> `俺は選ばない、何も。それが一番いい方法だと信じてる`

This is not literal passivity. “Choosing not to choose” becomes a relational policy designed to avoid imposing his overwhelming influence on others. It is also self-protective and self-satisfied by his own admission.

Volume 10 therefore pairs two distortions of choice:

- Hachiman turns knowledge into permission to decide for someone else.
- Hayama turns the impossibility of clean choice into a policy of refusing to choose at all.

Neither is presented as complete maturity.

## 3.16 Movement XVI — the final handwritten record and Haruno reopen `本物`

The third record repeats:

> `私は信頼されている。私は信頼されている。`

Trust becomes a monster because the socially trusted person begins to feel that betrayal is forbidden. The note asks whether a performed surface, repeatedly affirmed by others as one's “true self,” may eventually become real. It waits for someone to `見抜いてくれる`, then reaches the unstable lexical pair:

> `信実。あるいは真実。`

And finally:

> `本物なんて、あるのだろうか。`

The ambiguity is the point. Hayama fits the expectation/trust burden; Hachiman fits the desire to test relations and expose false understanding; Haruno fits the impulse to puncture apparently stable surfaces. The novel does not resolve the speaker.

Haruno's final scene then turns the question directly onto the Service Club. Hachiman knows Yukino's track but refuses to tell Haruno:

> `それを俺が言うのはフェアじゃない`

This is strong evidence of growth. Access to private information no longer automatically becomes a license to circulate it.

Haruno suggests Yukino trusts him. Hachiman rejects the simple label. Haruno answers:

> `そう、あれは信頼とかじゃないの。……もっとひどい何か`

Then:

> `それを本物とは呼ばない……、君の言葉だったね`

And finally:

> `本物なんて、あるのかな……`

V2 must preserve the authority state. Haruno's language is a **character assertion**, powerful because of her observational position and family knowledge, but not a narrator-certified diagnosis. The text does not supply a prospective term for the “worse thing.” Later terminology must not be retroactively inserted here.

The volume therefore closes not with a definition but with hesitation before a known ending. Hachiman touches the cold book and resists turning to the conclusion. Volume 10 has taught him more about choices, roles, history, and privacy; it has not guaranteed that the thing he named in Volume 9 exists in a form he can secure.

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

## 4.1 Clear improvement: ignorance no longer automatically triggers denial

The strongest narratorial growth is the shift from **refusing knowledge** to **wanting more while admitting incompleteness**.

Volume 5's defensive position was explicit: `俺は……もっと知りたいとは思わない.` Volume 10 reverses it around Totsuka: `でも、だからもっと知りたいと思うのだ.` The causal relation matters. He wants to know **because** he does not know, not because he thinks knowledge will produce total certainty.

His future `たぶん頼る` is equally important. Hachiman does not confidently declare dependence or emotional need; he admits that future reliance is possible. This is a much healthier uncertainty than his earlier certainty that he needs nobody.

## 4.2 Clear improvement: privacy becomes non-transferable rather than merely hidden

The final Haruno scene is one of the best positive controls in the volume. Hachiman has information Haruno wants. He refuses to provide it because it would be unfair. He therefore distinguishes:

- knowing something;
- having been told something;
- owning the right to redistribute it.

That distinction is exactly what his earlier intervention style frequently lacked.

## 4.3 Persistent weakness: another person's desire can still become authorization in his model

Miura's request is emotionally serious, and Hachiman correctly asks whether she still wants to know despite Hayama's resistance. But once she answers yes, Hachiman increasingly treats her will as sufficient grounds to cross Hayama's boundary.

This is a category error. **The requester's authorization governs the requester, not the target.**

Hachiman can ethically help Miura decide what *she* will do. It does not follow that he may force Hayama to disclose what Hayama has explicitly refused to disclose.

## 4.4 The marathon is domain-specific unreliability in action

Hachiman is socially brilliant during the race. He correctly predicts fatigue, anger, public role pressure, and Hayama's likely resistance to being commanded. He also constructs a wrong theory of Hayama's underlying goal.

This is exactly why the V2 model avoids calling him generically unreliable. His observations are often excellent. The failure occurs when accurate observations are assembled into a model that Hachiman then treats as jurisdiction.

The narratorial problem is not that he cannot read people.

It is that **being good at reading people tempts him to overvalue the authority of the reading.**

## 4.5 Hiratsuka's vocabulary exposes the slippage

No narratorial audit point is stronger than the echo between:

> `選択肢を削ってやることだ`

and

> `その選択肢を無理やりにでも削ればいい`

The omitted clause is the ethic. Hiratsuka immediately says the person ultimately decides. Hachiman carries the technique forward while dropping the authorship condition.

This is a precise example of Hachiman learning a true proposition and then applying it through an old control structure.

## 4.6 “Cold indifference is kindness” should not be promoted to doctrine

Near the end, Hachiman thinks that only accurate rejection may count as genuine understanding and that `冷たい無関心こそは優しさ` because uncomprehending affirmation can become a shackle.

There is insight here. Praise and expectation can indeed imprison Hayama. The third handwritten record demonstrates how “trust” can become coercive.

But the universalization is Hachiman's bias. The volume itself supplies counterevidence:

- Komachi is helped by unconditional reassurance.
- Totsuka waits, offers help, and invites reliance.
- Yui and Yukino allow plural belonging rather than indifference.
- Hayama appears to want someone to find him, according to Haruno and the formal interlude resonance.

The mature conclusion cannot therefore be “indifference is kindness.” More defensibly: **affirmation that refuses to see the person can become coercive; respectful non-possession may sometimes be kinder than imposed belief.**

## 4.7 The interlude speaker cannot be collapsed into Hachiman

The first pass's “Hachiman's Dazai notes” is too strong. Hachiman's reading frames the volume and the final scene places the book in his hands, but the titles themselves deny stable ownership. The notes should be treated as a formal field across several characters.

This matters because the volume's deepest insight about expectation is not “Hachiman discovers Hayama is secretly the same as him.” It is that several people occupy different versions of the same human problem: **wanting to be known without consenting to be reduced to the model by which one is known.**

# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Hikigaya Hachiman — from answerability as breakthrough to answerability under conflict

Hachiman begins Volume 10 with several real gains from Volume 9 intact:

- he directly asks Yui to spend time shopping;
- he asks Totsuka for help rather than silently assigning him a role;
- he allows Zaimokuza to refuse and set conditions;
- he admits he wants to know Totsuka better;
- he anticipates future reliance;
- he asks Yukino a personal question rather than deriving the answer;
- he refuses to pass Yukino's answer to Haruno.

These are not cosmetic changes.

But Volume 10 refuses the easy developmental story. Hachiman still wants the security of extracting the answer when another person refuses. Hayama's resistance activates the old machinery: isolate, pressure, provoke, infer, narrow options.

The resulting delta is not “Hachiman learned communication.” It is:

> **Hachiman has learned the form of asking, but he has not yet fully accepted refusal as an answer that can constrain him when he believes another person's request justifies continuing.**

## 5.2 Yukinoshita Yukino — greater voluntary disclosure, still unresolved family authorship

Yukino's volume is comparatively quiet, but several changes matter.

She:

- permits Yui's plural friendships without possessiveness;
- accepts Hachiman and Yui's birthday gift warmly;
- answers Hachiman's direct course question and voluntarily adds relational meaning (`とりあえずはみんな一緒ね`);
- repairs part of the old Hayama history without denying her anger or pretending the past never happened;
- publicly acknowledges that circumstances and relationships have changed;
- joins Miura's celebration rather than remaining outside that social world.

At the same time, `私はいてもいなくても大差ないもの` shows that family absence is still normalized through self-minimization. Haruno's accusation that Yukino is “again relying on people” cannot be accepted as fact, but it does show that Yukino's increased relational openness is visible enough to become contested.

The prospective state is therefore not dependence but **greater permission to disclose and be accompanied**, alongside unresolved questions about how she authors choices under family expectation.

## 5.3 Yuigahama Yui — plural belonging and ordinary desire

Yui's important contribution is partly structural. She can move between the Service Club and Miura's group without the volume treating one attachment as betrayal of another. She participates in choosing Yukino's gift, notices relational shifts, and recognizes the simple emotional truth beneath Miura's decision.

Her `もっと簡単でよかったんだ` is one of the volume's strongest corrections to the trio's tendency to over-theorize intimacy. Feelings do not have to arrive as philosophical certainty in order to matter.

The infirmary scene also increases romantic asymmetry. Yui arrives after a physically intimate Hachiman–Yukino interaction, registers it, and then rejoins the relational space. V10 does not resolve the triangle, but the three members' knowledge of pairwise intimacy is becoming less symmetrical.

## 5.4 Hayama Hayato — expectation, envy, role ownership, and non-choice

Hayama receives his strongest subject-status expansion so far.

He is not merely “the popular foil.” He is a person whose ability to satisfy social expectations creates a distinctive confinement. He refuses to recommend a course to Tobe precisely because his influence could distort another person's choice and later become a source of blame. That is a mature recognition of social power.

At the same time, his group relies on him without necessarily becoming people he can rely upon. Tobe's admission that Hayama has never consulted him captures the loneliness of the central role.

Hayama's hatred of Hachiman is especially important because it is not a moral rebuke. It contains envy and self-protection. He wants Hachiman elevated partly so that losing to him is tolerable. Hachiman's own idealization is the mirror image.

Hayama's `それが、俺だ` and `それも含めて、俺だよ` prevent a crude “true self versus social mask” reading. The role others expect can be burdensome and still become something he owns. The unresolved question is whether ownership arrived through free choice or adaptation to a path made nearly compulsory.

His most philosophically consequential line is the distinction between an option selected because there was no real alternative and a choice he can call his own. That is the inverse of Hachiman's childhood defense. Hachiman avoided being chosen; Hayama has been chosen by expectations so thoroughly that he struggles to locate an uncontaminated choosing self.

## 5.5 Miura Yumiko — desire as a reason, not a verdict

Miura's development is one of V10's quiet strengths.

She does not transform into a confessional heroine or lose her abrasive style. Instead, she becomes legible as someone who understands the stakes of ordinary attachment. She wants to know because she wants to become closer. She is willing to admit that the motive is selfish and that the opportunity may be temporary.

Her eventual course decision is not optimized long-term planning. It is a choice to value a present relationship opportunity. The text refuses to dismiss that as shallow.

Her apology to Yukino is equally important. She can cross a line, be called on it, and then repair without a grand moral conversion.

## 5.6 Totsuka Saika — from idealized object to autonomous friend

Totsuka is perhaps the clearest beneficiary of the volume's “you do not know the person you think you know” structure.

He has leadership responsibilities, an athletic future, uncertainty, and the capacity to help Hachiman. Hachiman's old angel jokes are not merely harmless comedy when treated as a total model; they can become a way of not seeing Totsuka's subjecthood.

The crucial delta is reciprocal future orientation. Totsuka is waiting for Hachiman to approach; Hachiman says he will probably rely on him. Their friendship becomes less one-directionally aestheticized and more usable.

## 5.7 Isshiki Iroha — office becomes identity through practice

Iroha now demonstrates that a role with a compromised origin can become authentically inhabited. Her presidency began through manipulation. Volume 10 shows her using the office, routing requests, directing peers, and accumulating competence.

This is highly relevant to Hayama. The series is increasingly skeptical of origin essentialism. A role does not become false forever because it began under pressure; later ownership matters.

## 5.8 Komachi — secure reliance as developmental infrastructure

Komachi's exam anxiety provides a useful control case. She is dependent on family in the ordinary sense, but Hachiman's reassurance does not erase her authorship. He cannot take her test. He can make failure less catastrophic.

Her recovery illustrates a form of reliance that increases rather than reduces agency.

## 5.9 Haruno — destabilizing interpreter whose uncertainty becomes visible

Haruno remains dangerous precisely because many of her readings are perceptive. But Volume 10 gives her final doubt a different texture. `本物なんて、あるのかな……` is not the triumphant pronouncement of someone who possesses the answer. It sounds lonely enough for Hachiman to notice.

Her `もっとひどい何か` is therefore best preserved as an **open diagnostic provocation**. It should structure later questions, not settle them.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Hachiman ↔ Yukino

**Trust:** increases behaviorally. Yukino answers a personal future question and voluntarily adds more than asked. Hachiman protects the disclosure from Haruno.

**Disclosure:** increases. Hachiman asks rather than infers; Yukino gives course information and relational framing.

**Permission to intervene:** remains conditional. Yukino's earlier `いつか、私を助けてね` does not become a blanket warrant.

**Proximity/ease:** increases through shrine visit, shopping aftermath, crowded train, injury care, and casual future language.

**Romantic coding:** strong but still not explicit reciprocal confession. The infirmary scene is one of the volume's clearest dyadic intimacy moments.

**Future assumption:** `とりあえずはみんな一緒ね` is explicitly temporary but meaningful. The category “together” now reaches beyond the clubroom into academic future.

## 6.2 Hachiman ↔ Yui

**Ease:** high enough for Hachiman to initiate shared shopping.

**Operational partnership:** stable. Yui helps choose Yukino's gift and continues functioning as a social interpreter.

**Romantic asymmetry:** remains significant. Yui sees or senses Hachiman–Yukino intimacy and stays inside the triangle rather than withdrawing.

**Conflict tolerance:** post-V9 gains hold; there is no need for Yui to manufacture uninterrupted atmosphere throughout V10.

## 6.3 Yukino ↔ Yui

Their friendship is now strong enough to permit plural social belonging. Yukino does not frame Yui's movement toward Miura as desertion. Yui later physically rejoins Yukino after the infirmary scene. Their relation remains warm without requiring exclusivity.

## 6.4 Hachiman ↔ Hayama

This is the volume's most transformed relationship.

They move from adversarial mutual interpretation into direct negative disclosure:

- Hayama: `俺は君が嫌いだ`
- Hachiman later: `俺もお前が嫌いだよ`

The hatred does not end relation. It arguably makes the relation more honest because each can finally deny the flattering model imposed on him by the other.

Yet the marathon also represents a severe boundary violation. Hachiman uses Hayama's body, fatigue, pride, and anger as instruments of disclosure. The later honesty cannot retroactively authorize that method.

The pair end with a paradoxical kind of equality: not equality of status, popularity, or moral virtue, but equality in their capacity to resent, misread, and need the other as a comparative reference.

## 6.5 Miura ↔ Hayama

Miura's desire becomes explicit without being reciprocally resolved. She wants proximity and makes a practical choice accordingly. Hayama's course secrecy was partly designed to prevent his influence from making other people's decisions for them, yet Miura chooses after receiving probable information anyway.

The relation therefore remains asymmetric but no longer passive on Miura's side.

## 6.6 Yukino ↔ Hayama

Their shared past is acknowledged as history rather than romantic destiny. Yukino can thank Hayama for care while also acknowledging that the old method may have been wrong. Hayama can say Yukino changed without reclaiming authority over her.

This is **repair without restoration**. The old relation is not recovered; it becomes a past that can be named without determining the present.

## 6.7 Hachiman ↔ Totsuka

This relation becomes more reciprocal and less idealized. Totsuka is not merely a refuge from difficult heterosexual desire; he is a person with a future who can be asked for help and relied upon.

## 6.8 Service Club ↔ Iroha / Student Council

The boundary remains porous after Volume 9. Iroha “sits around” rather than appearing only as crisis client. This is evidence that institutions can become parts of ordinary relational life without swallowing the trio's identity.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

Volume 10 is the first book in which the post-Volume-9 request ethic encounters **irreducibly conflicting authorization**.

## 7.1 Miura's request

**Problem owner:** Miura owns her desire to remain near Hayama.

**Explicit request:** discover Hayama's course choice.

**Underlying need:** preserve opportunity for relational proximity before academic sorting closes it.

**Authorization:** Miura authorizes Hachiman to act on her behalf.

**Ethical limit:** she cannot authorize disclosure on Hayama's behalf.

## 7.2 Hayama's counter-request

Hayama explicitly asks Hachiman to stop the intrusive inquiry. This is not inferred discomfort; it is stated refusal.

The existence of two requests means the Service Club can no longer assume “the client” is the only relevant moral subject.

## 7.3 Hachiman's chosen method

The marathon method has two ethically different layers.

**Collaborator layer:** improved.

- Totsuka is asked.
- Hachiman says not to overdo it.
- Zaimokuza can refuse.
- Zaimokuza sets compensation and self-protective conditions.

**Target layer:** coercive.

- Hayama has refused.
- Hachiman isolates him.
- waits for fatigue;
- provokes anger;
- weaponizes Miura's dignity;
- uses a forced-choice command to reveal information indirectly.

The method is therefore neither “same old Hachiman” nor clean growth. It is **localized ethical improvement around collaborators combined with continued jurisdiction over the target.**

## 7.4 Hiratsuka as normative countermodel

Her rule can be formalized:

> **Legitimate help may alter the choice environment; it must preserve the chooser's final authorship.**

Hachiman's later `無理やりにでも削ればいい` violates that condition.

## 7.5 Komachi as positive control

Hachiman does not choose Komachi's school or exam result. He uses family commitment to reduce the catastrophic cost of failure. Her agency increases because fear decreases.

## 7.6 Iroha as positive institutional control

The student council does increasingly more of its own work. Hachiman and Yukino contribute without replacing the office-holder. Iroha's role becomes more genuinely hers through practice.

## 7.7 Yukino's course disclosure as positive relational control

Hachiman asks. Yukino answers. She offers additional meaning. Hachiman later does not redistribute the information.

This is perhaps Volume 10's cleanest demonstration that **asking can produce more knowledge than coercion because the person retains control over what else to give.**

## 7.8 Outcome versus authorization

Hachiman succeeds in giving Miura useful information. The outcome does not sanitize the method.

This directly preserves Checkpoint B's rule:

> **Effectiveness does not confer authorization.**

And Checkpoint C's extension:

> **Answerability requires the other person to retain the right to answer differently.**

Volume 10 shows Hachiman applying that second principle unevenly.

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 Academic tracking as relational infrastructure

Course selection is not merely personal preference. It controls class assignment, peer proximity, university pathways, and family narratives. The school literally turns future orientation into spatial sorting.

This is why Miura's “stay near him” motive cannot simply be dismissed as romantic triviality. Institutional design can make one academic form consequential for relationship continuity.

## 8.2 Family capital and inherited profession

Hayama's “choice” is saturated by social expectation. The novel strongly implies that elite family history and professional continuity matter, but V2 must not invent exact family requirements beyond what the text states. What is established is Hayama's own criterion: if only one answer was realistically available, he does not experience selecting it as meaningful choice.

Kawasaki provides a different material case. Family finances and younger siblings constrain her educational route. Preference is never purely internal; economic structure belongs to the causal model.

## 8.3 Charisma as power and burden

Hayama refuses to recommend a track to Tobe because his recommendation could become a causal force in Tobe's life and later a source of regret. This is a sophisticated understanding of informal authority.

The same charisma generates dependence. People expect Hayama to solve atmosphere, avoid conflict, and remain admirable. “Trust” becomes a command to continue being the person everyone already expects.

## 8.4 Rumor as decentralized coercion

The Hayama–Yukino rumor shows how a group can violate privacy without a clear violator. Distributed repetition converts possibility into social fact while every participant preserves plausible deniability.

Hayama's response is not raw truth-telling. He uses public-stage competence to generate a counter-narrative by publicly thanking multiple girls. The rumor loses exclusivity because a more socially manageable story replaces it.

This is another case where social intelligence solves a public problem without granting the audience access to private truth.

## 8.5 Student council as developmental institution

Iroha's arc demonstrates that institutions can educate through repeated responsibility. Competence is not only an innate trait discovered by a producer-like Hachiman. It can emerge through office, practice, delegation, and shared burden.

## 8.6 The marathon as expectation ritual

The race is a school spectacle in which Hayama is expected to win because winning is part of his social identity. His decision to win after confessing that the role burdens him is therefore especially important. Social expectation and self-owned desire are not mutually exclusive categories.

# 9. Authenticity / `本物` ledger

Volume 10 does not contain a large conceptual `本物` cluster. Its dominant lexical field is instead:

- `選択`
- `答え`
- `期待`
- `理解`
- `信頼`
- `噂`
- `過去 / 現在 / 未来`
- `知る / 知りたい`
- `同じ / 違う`

That distribution matters. The volume is **testing conditions under which something might become genuine**, not producing a settled definition.

## 9.1 First major prospective use: ordinary feeling may become genuine

Hachiman's thought about Miura is:

> `ふわふわした感情だって、本物に至る可能性は充分にある`

Evidence class: **Hachiman inference**, not narrator-certified theorem.

Its significance is nevertheless substantial. `本物` is no longer restricted to a relation that begins from perfect insight, pure motive, or exceptional destiny. An ordinary feeling can potentially arrive there.

This strongly weakens origin essentialism.

## 9.2 The interlude reopens rather than resolves the term

The third record moves through `信実` and `真実` before asking `本物なんて、あるのだろうか`. The lexical instability is itself meaningful. Truth, faithfulness, sincerity, and genuineness overlap but are not made identical.

## 9.3 Haruno uses Hachiman's own word against the current club relation

`それを本物とは呼ばない……、君の言葉だったね` is not a final definition. It is a challenge. Hachiman himself cannot identify what exactly Haruno is naming or what the correct alternative would be.

Haruno's later `本物なんて、あるのかな……` deprives her of the position of someone who simply knows the answer and withholds it.

## 9.4 Prospective V10 formulation

The strongest prospective claim is not “`本物` = choice/privacy.” That shorthand is too compressed.

A better formulation is:

> **Volume 10 tests whether the desire for genuine understanding can coexist with another person's right not to be penetrated, and whether socially inherited roles or mixed motives can become real through later ownership without retroactively becoming freely chosen at their origin.**

The book ends with the problem open.

# 10. Dependency / autonomy ledger

The literal word `依存` is not a governing concept in Volume 10; its occurrence is not the thematic mechanism later terminology might tempt a rereader to impose. The more productive vocabulary is `頼る`, `相談`, `任せる`, `信頼`, and the practical distribution of burden.

## 10.1 Tobe → Hayama: reliance without reciprocity

Tobe relies on Hayama and consults him, but Hayama has never consulted Tobe. This is not proof that the friendship is false. It shows that a person can be central to a group while lacking an easy path to vulnerability inside that same group.

The risk is **asymmetrical usefulness**: the person everyone relies upon becomes harder to imagine as someone who needs care.

## 10.2 Hachiman ↔ Totsuka: future reliance becomes speakable

Hachiman's `たぶん頼る` is small but developmentally large. It is not dependence as identity. It is permission for a future act of asking.

This should be frozen as **chosen prospective reliance**.

## 10.3 Komachi ↔ Hachiman: secure attachment increases agency

Komachi's dependence on family is not framed as pathology. Hachiman's assurance that failure will not endanger her materially or relationally allows her to return to studying. The relation works because support does not replace performance of the task.

## 10.4 Yukino and the problem of Haruno's interpretation

Haruno says Yukino has returned to relying on other people. This is a **character assertion**. The volume itself shows Yukino asking less indirectly, disclosing more voluntarily, and allowing Hachiman and Yui to be present. None of those facts independently establishes dysfunctional dependence.

Later terminology must not be imported backward to convert “receives help” into “dependency pathology.”

## 10.5 Iroha: supported autonomy through institution

Iroha provides the strongest non-romantic positive control. The trio's earlier intervention did not leave her permanently passive. She now exercises office, makes requests through channels, directs peers, and owns results.

Support has become developmental when the recipient can increasingly act without the helper substituting for her.

## 10.6 Hayama: being relied upon can itself become a dependency system

The volume turns the usual direction of dependency analysis around. Hayama's group depends on him, but the pattern also makes his identity dependent on continuing to satisfy expectation. The third handwritten record's `信頼の魔物` is a useful literary image here: being trusted can become a demand not to betray the role.

This does not mean “trust is bad.” It means trust becomes coercive when it denies the trusted person's right to disappoint, refuse, or change.

## 10.7 Prospective ledger state

By the end of V10:

- reliance is increasingly speakable;
- healthy support leaves the task and final answer with the supported person;
- asymmetrical reliance can isolate the central helper;
- social trust can become role coercion;
- no general `依存` diagnosis is warranted.

# 11. Japanese language and voice

Volume 10 is unusually useful for longitudinal lexical analysis because several of its major ideas are carried through **repeated wording with altered ethical conditions**.

## 11.1 `選択` / `選択肢` — choice as the governing institutional word

The book repeatedly asks what makes an option a choice rather than merely a path taken.

Hiratsuka:

> `教師がしてやれるのは選択肢を増やすこと。……そして、選択肢を削ってやることだ`

followed by:

> `最後に決めるのはそいつだ`

Hachiman later:

> `その選択肢を無理やりにでも削ればいい`

Hayama finally:

> `それしか選びようがなかったものを選んでも、それを自分の選択とはいわないだろ`

The repetition builds a three-part distinction:

1. **options may be socially structured;**
2. **guidance may legitimately affect available options;**
3. **choice loses authorship when another party or social structure makes the answer effectively compulsory.**

The same word therefore carries pedagogy, coercion, and existential doubt.

## 11.2 `知る / 知りたい / 理解` — from credential to process

Miura's `それになりたいから知りたい` places knowing before achieved closeness: she wants knowledge partly to become a close person.

Totsuka's `八幡は知らないことばっかりだね` turns ignorance into relationship evidence rather than shame.

Hachiman's `だからもっと知りたいと思う` explicitly reverses Volume 5's desire denial.

Yukino's `近しい人が理解してくれているなら` retains a different model: close relation can protect against public misreading.

The volume does not collapse these into one doctrine. It stages a tension between:

- privacy from the public;
- desire to become close through inquiry;
- recognition that knowledge remains incomplete.

## 11.3 `期待` — from hope to assignment

Expectation is one of V10's densest words. Hachiman's early psychology treated expectation mainly as a risk of disappointment. Hayama demonstrates a different burden: expectation can assign a role in advance.

The second handwritten record condemns the self that expected to understand and be understood. Haruno says Hayama may have expected someone to find him. The third record repeats “I am trusted” until trust becomes monstrous.

The word therefore has at least three functions:

- **hope** that another will recognize one;
- **prediction** that another will behave in a certain way;
- **social command** that another must continue satisfying the prediction.

## 11.4 `本音` and the violence of elicitation

Hachiman explicitly believes fatigue will make `本音` leak. That wording matters because “getting the true feeling” is not morally neutral. The volume asks whether the conditions under which truth is extracted belong to the truth's ethical meaning.

A confession produced after deliberate exhaustion and provocation may be factually revealing while still being coercively obtained.

## 11.5 `嫌い` — negative truth as de-idealization

Hayama's `俺は君が嫌いだ` and Hachiman's later `俺もお前が嫌いだよ` are unusually clean because they do not pretend negative feeling abolishes relation. The pair can dislike each other and remain meaningfully entangled.

This is a useful correction to the series' early tendency to treat conflict as evidence that a relation has failed.

## 11.6 `それが、俺だ` / `それも含めて、俺だよ` — selfhood as incorporation

Hayama's repetitions reject a hidden-true-self model. Winning, expectation, inherited history, rivalry, and the effort to satisfy others may be burdens, but they are not simply detachable masks.

`それも含めて` is especially important: **including that** is me.

The mature problem becomes not stripping away everything socially produced, but determining which inherited structures the present person can own, revise, or refuse.

## 11.7 `シンジツ` → `信実` / `真実`

The handwritten records initially use `シンジツ`, visually suspending lexical specificity. The third later writes:

> `信実。あるいは真実。`

The first term evokes fidelity/sincerity/trustworthiness; the second truth/reality. The text then moves to `本物`, keeping the conceptual field unstable rather than equating all three.

This supports a V2 rule: do not translate every authenticity-adjacent word into one flattened concept.

## 11.8 Miura and Yui's low-abstraction register

Miura's `やっぱいいって思うんじゃん` and Yui's `もっと簡単でよかったんだ` matter partly because they are not philosophical formulations. Their ordinary speech interrupts Hachiman's tendency to turn desire into a theory before allowing it to count.

The novel repeatedly lets emotionally direct, low-abstraction speech carry truths the highly analytical characters complicate.

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertextuality

## 12.1 Dazai is not a prestige reference pasted onto the volume

『人間失格』 and 『走れメロス』 structure the book's problem of performance, trust, social role, and the fear of being seen. The intertext matters because characters do not merely quote Dazai; they **misread, reread, and change identification**.

A text once abandoned for exposing the reader becomes something to continue. The trustworthy Melos story is reread from the king's position: what does demonstrated fidelity mean to someone whose problem is that he cannot trust even after proof?

This is a sophisticated use of school-canon literature because it turns interpretation itself into character evidence.

## 12.2 Comedy continues to prevent theory from becoming pure solemnity

The book repeatedly punctures its own abstractions through:

- Hachiman's continuing Totsuka jokes;
- Zaimokuza's inflated roleplay and negotiated ramen compensation;
- Iroha's fake-romantic question about whether Hachiman is dating someone;
- birthday-shopping embarrassment;
- Komachi's manipulation of her brother;
- Miura's bluntness;
- Hachiman's defensive internal commentary around physical proximity.

The comedy is not merely tonal relief. It often reveals the distance between what a character can theorize and what he can emotionally tolerate.

## 12.3 The fake confession register returns as parody rather than catastrophe

Iroha can perform a romance-coded question—`先輩……。今付き合ってる人って、……いますか？`—in a context where Hachiman, Yui, and Yukino all understand that language itself can create a social event even when it is staged.

Compared with Kyoto, the scene is low-stakes and reversible. This reinforces the V7.5 distinction: **performance is not automatically deception in the ethically relevant sense.** Transparent play can create expressive room rather than seize another person's process.

## 12.4 Sports genre as forced-body truth test

The marathon temporarily shifts the series into a competitive-sports grammar: pacing, stamina, formation, rivals, victory. Hachiman exploits that grammar because the body creates conditions conversation normally cannot.

The ethical problem is precisely that he uses sport not simply to compete but to manufacture vulnerability. The genre mechanism becomes part of the intervention method.

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial tests

## 14.1 “Hachiman's marathon method is justified because Miura explicitly asked him to do it”

**Evidence for:**

- Miura understands that knowing may hurt.
- Hachiman asks whether she still wants the answer.
- She explicitly says yes.
- The information genuinely helps her make a decision.

**Counterevidence:**

- Hayama explicitly asks Hachiman to stop.
- Hachiman deliberately weakens Hayama's ability to refuse.
- Hiratsuka's same-volume rule preserves final choice with the person whose options are being shaped.
- Hachiman's broader theory of Hayama's motive turns out wrong.

**Judgment:** reject as a complete justification. Miura authorizes help concerning her choices; she does not own Hayama's privacy.

## 14.2 “Hayama is simply Hachiman's popular double”

**Evidence for:**

- both are burdened by their relation to others' expectations;
- both idealize/devalue the other to manage inferiority;
- both want to be seen beneath performance;
- both make self-satisfaction part of their moral choices.

**Counterevidence:**

- Hachiman historically refuses expectation; Hayama satisfies it;
- Hayama possesses charisma and informal power Hachiman does not;
- Hayama genuinely enjoys winning and claims the role;
- their intervention ethics and social strategies differ.

**Judgment:** revise. They are **inverted comparators**, not duplicates.

## 14.3 “Hayama's public self is fake and his hidden self is real”

**Evidence for:**

- he resents expectations;
- the interludes treat social performance as concealment;
- he withholds course information and admits hatred Hachiman's public model excluded.

**Counterevidence:**

- `それが、俺だ`;
- `それも含めて、俺だよ`;
- he wants to win;
- he cares about friends;
- he manages public narratives skillfully and intentionally.

**Judgment:** reject the binary. Hayama's public role is both imposed and inhabited.

## 14.4 “Miura's decision proves genuine love is simple and irrational”

**Evidence for:**

- Yui says it could have been simpler;
- Hachiman thinks a fluffy feeling may become `本物`;
- Miura prioritizes relational opportunity over maximally optimized career planning.

**Counterevidence:**

- Hachiman says *may become* genuine, not already is;
- Miura knows Hayama socially and has long-term group attachment;
- academic choice has multiple practical effects;
- the volume does not validate every impulsive desire.

**Judgment:** narrow. The scene rejects purity and excessive abstraction, not responsible deliberation.

## 14.5 “Haruno has correctly diagnosed the Service Club as pathological dependence”

**Evidence for:**

- Haruno has exceptional family/context knowledge;
- she says the relation is worse than trust;
- she says nothing has changed;
- Hachiman takes the comment seriously.

**Counterevidence:**

- she does not name a mechanism;
- she ends by doubting whether `本物` exists at all;
- Volume 10 contains multiple healthy acts of asking, refusal, privacy, and reliance;
- no governing `依存` framework is present.

**Judgment:** keep OPEN. The statement is a high-value warning, not a settled diagnosis.

## 14.6 “Hachiman has solved his knowledge problem because he now asks questions”

**Evidence for:**

- Totsuka;
- Yukino course choice;
- direct requests to collaborators;
- privacy protection from Haruno.

**Counterevidence:**

- marathon coercion;
- interpretive overreach about Hayama's motive;
- belief that exhaustion reveals truth he may therefore extract;
- occasional return to essence-stripping imagery.

**Judgment:** reject. V10 is precisely about inconsistent application of the new procedure.

## 14.7 “The handwritten records are Hachiman's private monologues”

**Evidence for:**

- Hachiman reads the Dazai texts;
- the final scene returns to the book in his hands;
- many formulations match his psychology.

**Counterevidence:**

- the titles explicitly destabilize speaker identity;
- trust/expectation imagery maps strongly onto Hayama;
- Haruno's language echoes the same field;
- the afterword generalizes the problem to someone/everyone.

**Judgment:** downgrade to a multiple-address formal device. Hachiman is one strong resonance, not secure exclusive speaker.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

Full-series hindsight clarifies why Volume 10 is easy to oversimplify.

Because later volumes intensify the vocabulary of dependence, choice, romantic decision, and Haruno's diagnoses, a rereader can make V10 look like the moment the series already knows its final answer. It does not.

What Volume 10 actually does is construct **the conceptual terrain** later conflicts can inhabit:

- Can a request remain legitimate when another person refuses the means required to fulfill it?
- Can closeness grow through asking without becoming entitlement to disclosure?
- Can a role formed under expectation become genuinely owned later?
- Can support shape options without taking final authorship?
- Can ordinary liking be significant without first becoming pure or philosophically exceptional?
- Can a person reject both total transparency and total indifference?
- Can past coercion be acknowledged without making later selfhood unreal?

Hindsight strengthens several patterns but should not rewrite their authority states.

### 15.1 Haruno's final warning becomes more important, not more omniscient

Later developments make `もっとひどい何か` historically significant. They do not make it a retroactive factual label. The correct V2 move is to preserve it as a **claim that later receives tests**, not as a hidden narrator annotation.

### 15.2 Hayama's choice problem becomes an important comparative control

Later questions about Yukino and Hachiman's choices benefit from Hayama's formulation: a socially compulsory answer may not feel like one's own selection. But V10 also refuses a fantasy of “pure choice” untouched by history. Hayama's `それも含めて、俺だよ` shows that present self-authorship may consist partly in choosing what to keep from a compromised inheritance.

### 15.3 Hachiman's privacy growth should remain real

Later failures do not erase the importance of `それを俺が言うのはフェアじゃない`. Hachiman has learned a durable distinction between access and ownership of another person's information. The fact that he violates a related boundary with Hayama in the same volume demonstrates uneven growth, not absence of growth.

### 15.4 Totsuka is a crucial longitudinal correction

The V5/V10 verbal reversal is one of the cleanest pieces of evidence in the entire reread:

- V5: Hachiman does not want to know more.
- V10: because he does not know, he wants to know more.

That is not merely romantic-plot development. It is a change in his model of relationship.

# 16. V1 revision audit

The first-pass Volume 10 analysis was often directionally strong, but V2 produces several significant revisions.

## 16.1 The three handwritten records

**V1:** treated them primarily as Hachiman's Dazai frame and then generalized outward.

**V2:** **REVISE / STRENGTHEN FORMAL AMBIGUITY.** Hachiman is a major resonance, but the titles explicitly resist exclusive speaker assignment. The second and third records are strongly Hayama-compatible and broadly cast-wide. Do not state “Hachiman says/thinks” for unattributed lines.

## 16.2 Shrine fortunes as shared vulnerability

**V1:** shared risk rather than isolated damage.

**V2:** **RETAIN.** Add the non-exclusive social point: Yukino explicitly allows Yui to move between groups because return is expected.

## 16.3 Yukino's family absence

**V1:** not simple independence; Hachiman's practical answer is incomplete.

**V2:** **RETAIN / STRENGTHEN.** `私はいてもいなくても大差ないもの` is self-minimizing logic under family conditions, not proof of later dependency.

## 16.4 Birthday gifts and partial knowledge

**V1:** ordinary partial knowledge can be enough for care.

**V2:** **RETAIN / STRENGTHEN.** This directly answers V5's crisis of interpretive ownership: one can act on revisable knowledge without claiming total comprehension.

## 16.5 Rumor as public property

**V1:** rumor decentralizes responsibility and converts Hayama/Yukino into communal narrative.

**V2:** **RETAIN.** Add Hayama's counter-story management as a form of public narrative substitution rather than truth disclosure.

## 16.6 Miura's intimacy theory

**V1:** “I want to know because I want to become close” is a major correction to Hachiman.

**V2:** **RETAIN / STRENGTHEN**, with a crucial limit. Miura's sincere desire does not authorize violation of Hayama's contrary request.

## 16.7 Totsuka and role reduction

**V1:** Hachiman has reduced Totsuka to an angelic role.

**V2:** **STRENGTHEN.** Add the explicit V5 reversal `もっと知りたい` and future `頼る`; this is more significant than first pass captured.

## 16.8 Hiratsuka's assistance model

**V1:** helping expands/reduces choices but does not author the future.

**V2:** **STRENGTHEN SUBSTANTIALLY.** The exact `選択肢を削る` wording is later repeated by Hachiman in his coercive Hayama strategy. The novel builds an internal normative contrast.

## 16.9 Hayama as self-sacrificing expectation-bearer

**V1:** expectation is Hayama's version of self-sacrifice.

**V2:** **RETAIN-NARROW.** The analogy is useful but not exhaustive. Hayama also owns part of the winning/public role and refuses to be reduced to pure victimhood: `それが、俺だ`.

## 16.10 The marathon

**V1:** both growth and regression.

**V2:** **STRENGTHEN / DIFFERENTIATE.** Collaborator ethics have improved more than V1 emphasized: Totsuka and Zaimokuza consent and set boundaries. Coercion is concentrated on Hayama, who has explicitly refused.

## 16.11 Hayama's hatred and Hachiman's “indifference” thesis

**V1:** honest rejection can feel like recognition.

**V2:** **RETAIN the de-idealization, DOWNGRADE the universalization.** `冷たい無関心こそは優しさ` is Hachiman's inference and likely carries his own expectation-avoidance bias. The volume's healthier relations are not indifferent.

## 16.12 Hayama's public performance

**V1:** performance does not automatically mean falsity.

**V2:** **RETAIN / STRENGTHEN.** `それが、俺だ` and `それも含めて、俺だよ` make role ownership explicit.

## 16.13 Hachiman asking Yukino about her course

**V1:** a smaller, healthier ask.

**V2:** **RETAIN / STRENGTHEN.** This becomes the clean ethical contrast with Hayama: ask → answer → voluntary extra disclosure → privacy respected.

## 16.14 Miura's “fluffy feeling” and `本物`

**V1:** mixed motives do not disqualify genuineness.

**V2:** **RETAIN-NARROW.** It is Hachiman's prospective inference about possibility, not an achieved definition.

## 16.15 Yukino/Hayama past repair

**V1:** old history can be revised without erasure.

**V2:** **RETAIN / STRENGTHEN.** Hayama's `それも含めて、俺だよ` adds a key point: inherited or pressured history may become part of present selfhood through ownership.

## 16.16 Haruno's final judgment

**V1:** warning, not final diagnosis.

**V2:** **STRENGTHEN CAUTION.** No prospective `共依存` label. `もっとひどい何か` remains unnamed, and Haruno herself doubts `本物`'s existence.

## 16.17 V1 shorthand “V10 genuine = choice/privacy”

**Disposition:** **REVISE.** Choice and privacy are central constraints but not the definition. V10 is better understood as testing whether genuine relation can coexist with partial knowledge, refusal, socially inherited roles, mixed motives, and non-pure choice.

## 18.12 Frozen Volume 10 thesis

> **Volume 10 asks what answerability becomes when other people's requests conflict. Hachiman has learned to ask, but he has not yet learned that wanting to know—and even being asked to know by one person—does not erase another person's right to refuse. Around him, Miura shows that ordinary liking can be a reason without becoming certainty; Totsuka shows that closeness grows by admitting how much remains unknown; Hiratsuka defines help as shaping options without owning the final choice; Iroha shows that an imposed beginning can become a self-owned role through practice; and Hayama reveals the inverse tragedy of Hachiman's expectation avoidance, a life so saturated by expectation that the socially correct answer no longer feels chosen. The volume therefore moves `本物` farther away from purity. Its central opposition is not truth versus falsity, but **authored choice versus socially prewritten answer**—and it repeatedly suggests that a relationship becomes more real not when another person is fully deciphered, but when that person retains the power to answer differently.**
