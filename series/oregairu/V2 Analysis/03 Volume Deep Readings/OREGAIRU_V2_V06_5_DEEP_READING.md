# OREGAIRU V2 — Volume 6.5 Deep Reading
## Sports-festival aftermath, behavioral change, recognition, and the ethics of learning to remain strangers

### Governing proposition

Volume 6.5 does **not** reverse Volume 6 by declaring that people can simply become different people. Its more exact movement is this:

> **The past cannot be erased; character does not need to be purified; nevertheless, repeated choices can alter behavior, behavior can become habit, and socially real change can emerge without requiring either reconciliation or a claim that someone’s underlying “essence” has been replaced.**

The sports-festival sequence therefore occupies a critical position in the series. Volume 6 discovers the danger in Hachiman’s willingness to allocate pain through roles. Volume 6.5 asks what remains **after** that kind of intervention succeeds publicly but leaves real social damage behind. Its answer is not confession, forgiveness, or friendship. It is much less sentimental and, in some ways, more mature: people may learn how to work together, accept bounded responsibility, recognize effort, maintain distance, and continue living beside a history that cannot be made not to have happened.

The volume’s strongest counterargument to Hachiman is also not supplied by a philosophical lecture. It is supplied by **Sagami’s unscripted emotional speech**. Hachiman constructs increasingly elaborate rational pressure against the athletic-club faction, only to discover that the impasse began in emotion and cannot be solved by pretending emotion is merely defective reasoning. Sagami, the person he has repeatedly reduced to status-seeking and approval hunger, becomes the person whose own voice breaks the stalemate.

That is a substantial correction to his method.

# 1. Source identity and publication position

## 1.1 Commercial Volume 6.5 is the governing text

The primary source for this reading is the commercial Japanese **『やはり俺の青春ラブコメはまちがっている。6.5』**. The fixed-page scan contains 492 physical pages. Its paper colophon gives **2014-07-27, first edition / first printing**. The EPUB metadata gives an electronic publication date of **2014-07-25**, with the supplied electronic state later based on the **2015-03-20 third printing**.

The commercial book itself states that Chapters ①–⑧ were created by **reconstructing, re-editing, expanding, and revising** the bonus novels originally supplied with the 2013 anime BD/DVD first-press releases. The `なかがき` is unusually explicit:

> `６・２５巻、６・５０巻、６・７５巻を再構成再編集し、加筆修正ディレクターズカットを加え、一冊にまとめ直した`

This is therefore not merely a bound photocopy of three earlier booklets. It is a later authorial/editorial state and will govern the V2 mainline reading.

## 1.2 The sports-festival sequence’s story chronology

Watari explicitly places the reconstructed material **between Volume 6’s cultural festival and Volume 7’s school trip**. That is the prospective boundary used here.

The commercial sports sequence is:

1. `① またしても平塚静は新たな命令を下す。`
2. `② 今再び城廻めぐりと巡り会う。`
3. `③ 思ったとおり、相模南は変わってない。`
4. `④ あくまでも雪ノ下雪乃は試し続ける。`
5. `⑤ 以上のことから、比企谷八幡は予感する。`
6. `⑥ それでも、城廻めぐりは見てくれている。`
7. `⑦ そして、最後の会議が踊りだす。`
8. `⑧ だから、彼らの祭りは終わらない。`

## 1.3 What 6.25 / 6.50 / 6.75 actually contribute

The ancestor audit corrects an important simplification. The three bonus volumes are **not** simply three neat pieces whose total contents equal commercial 6.5.

- **6.25** contains the material that becomes commercial Chapters ①–③.
- **6.50** contains the ancestors of commercial Chapters ④ and ⑤ **plus two additional stories not reproduced in the commercial sports-festival sequence**:
  - `だから、比企谷小町は頑張れる。`
  - `存外、由比ヶ浜結衣は人気がある。`
- **6.75** contains the ancestors of commercial Chapters ⑥–⑧ **plus another story not reproduced in the commercial sports-festival sequence**:
  - `密やかに、川崎沙希は問いかける。`

Accordingly, the correct genealogy is:

> **commercial 6.5 selectively reconstructs and revises the sports-festival through-line from the three bonus booklets; it does not reproduce every piece of fiction those booklets contained.**

Those omitted stories remain Watari-written supplementary fiction and should eventually be indexed separately. They are not silently folded into this volume-local character-state freeze.

## 1.4 The Christmas bonus is deliberately quarantined

The commercial book then appends:

`ぼーなすとらっく！ そのクリスマスキャンドルの灯が揺れる時……。`

But the provenance note explicitly says this is a prose rewrite of the **Volume 9 limited-edition drama-CD script**, that the story occurs **after Volume 9**, and that readers/listeners are recommended to finish Volume 9 first. It also warns that the prose rewrite differs slightly from the audio.

Therefore:

- it is **part of the commercial 6.5 publication object**;
- it is **not part of the V6→V7 prospective narrative state**;
- it is **excluded from Sections 2–10 and 18’s prospective ledgers**;
- its drama CD and song will receive their substantive reading at the proper post-Volume-9 point.

This is not pedantry. Including the Christmas material here would import post-Kyoto, post-election, and post-Christmas-event relationship knowledge into a point in the series where those events have not happened.

## 1.5 Visual-source treatment

The complete 492-page fixed-page scan was rendered and visually scanned. The book interleaves the revised sports-festival sequence with monochrome illustrations, graphic/chat-like inserts, and then a visually distinct Christmas/drama-CD package in the late pages. That material packaging reinforces why publication object and story chronology must not be treated as identical categories.

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

The allowed prospective knowledge for the main analysis is:

- Volumes 1–6;
- Checkpoint A as a frozen summary of V1–V3 only;
- the commercial Volume 6.5 sports-festival Chapters ①–⑧.

The following must **not** govern the prospective interpretation:

- Volume 7 Kyoto;
- Volume 7.5’s later chronological connective tissue;
- election / Christmas material;
- the post-V9 Christmas bonus appended to commercial 6.5;
- later explicit `本物` discourse;
- later `共依存` terminology;
- the final romantic resolution.

At this point in publication-order psychology, Hachiman has just learned several things in Volume 6:

1. assigning himself the role of sole legitimate casualty is not consequence-free;
2. other people can experience his injury as pain;
3. Yukino and Yui can create healthy forms of requested reliance;
4. complete understanding is impossible, but revisable interpretation is possible;
5. tactical success does not settle the ethics of the method.

Volume 6.5 now asks whether those lessons survive contact with **aftermath rather than crisis**.

The prospective thesis should therefore remain narrow:

> **A mistake can remain a mistake while people learn new practices around it. Repair need not mean erasure, conversion into friendship, or discovery of a pure hidden self.**

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

## 3.1 Phase A — Volume 6’s victory returns as social cost

The opening deliberately refuses to let the cultural festival end at its public success.

Sagami’s circle continues to circulate hostile stories about Hachiman. The atmosphere of Class F remains damaged. Hachiman’s first instinct is minimization: the hostility will probably decay by itself; there is no reason to expend effort because there is no `実害`.

Yukino immediately answers:

> `………実害なら、あるわ`

That sentence is crucial because it punctures Hachiman’s accounting. Reputation is not merely an abstract field he can spend without consequence. The volume later materializes the point through the garbage placed in his shoe locker.

The causal chain is explicit:

**V6 rooftop method → Sagami humiliation → peer protection / anti-Hachiman narrative → classroom hostility → direct harassment.**

The story therefore reclassifies “reputation damage” from tactical resource into a harm-bearing social fact.

## 3.2 Phase B — Meguri’s request creates a second festival laboratory

Shiromeguri wants her final sports festival to be exciting and successful. The Service Club is pulled into planning.

This matters structurally because sports festival repeats the institutional setting of Volume 6 while changing the problem:

- the committee is smaller;
- the event is shorter;
- the social wounds from the previous festival are still active;
- all three Service Club members now know more about what their methods cost.

The book is therefore a controlled sequel rather than a detached bonus episode.

## 3.3 Phase C — Yukino proposes Sagami

Yukino identifies Sagami as the candidate for sports-festival committee chair. Her rationale is neither simple forgiveness nor sentimental belief that Sagami has become good.

She identifies:

- prior committee experience;
- ambition / status sensitivity;
- attraction to visible positions;
- therefore, available motivation.

She then frames the appointment as a kind of remedial exposure:

> if something has been lost, something equal or greater may be required to compensate for it.

Hachiman interprets this as an attempt to restore Sagami’s confidence and public evaluation. Meguri accepts the importance of second chances but imposes a critical institutional condition: this is a real job, and a repeat of the cultural-festival failure would harm the event.

Already, the novel refuses the binary:

> “Sagami deserves another chance” **versus** “Sagami is untrustworthy.”

Both statements can be true.

## 3.4 Phase D — Hachiman’s initial theory of change

Hachiman rejects the idea that one speech, one failure, or one kind intervention transforms personality:

> `人は変われないのだ。もし、変われるとしたら手段は一つ。`

His proposed mechanism is painful conditioning:

> repeated injury leaves lasting marks; avoidance of the same pain changes behavior.

This is grim, but it is more sophisticated than simple “people never change.” Even at the beginning of the volume his position contains two layers:

- **essence skepticism:** a core self does not flip instantly;
- **behavioral plasticity:** experience can alter conduct.

The rest of the book develops the gap between those two claims.

## 3.5 Phase E — Sagami’s first repair is coercive and incomplete

Sagami is brought into the role. Tensions with her former friends / fellow committee workers must be addressed. A public conversation forces her to apologize and ask for cooperation.

She eventually says, in substance:

> she wants to make the event exciting; she is trying; cooperation would help; she will avoid imposing on club obligations.

The scene produces a working agreement.

Hachiman immediately sees the limitations. The exchange is partly ritualized, partly coerced, and strategically phrased. He reads Sagami’s apology as self-defense and the athletic-club side’s acceptance as a way to secure concessions.

His suspicion is not baseless.

But it is incomplete.

The important thing is that **an imperfectly motivated agreement still creates a usable social fact**. That proposition will recur throughout the volume.

## 3.6 Phase F — distributed competence replaces solitary substitution

The sports-festival problem increasingly requires different forms of competence:

- Yukino organizes and formalizes;
- Yui supplies social reach, recruitment, and intuitive audience/event sense;
- Meguri supplies institutional legitimacy and morale;
- Sagami occupies the visible chair role;
- Hachiman diagnoses incentives, procedural weaknesses, and adversarial strategies;
- other students provide labor, performance, and implementation.

This is not a utopian teamwork story. Hachiman remains manipulative, Yukino remains severe, Sagami remains status-sensitive, and multiple committee members remain resentful.

But the system no longer works by allowing one person to quietly do everyone else’s labor.

That is an important post-Volume-6 improvement.

## 3.7 Phase G — the conflict shifts from procedure to affect

The major late conflict concerns opposition from the athletic-club side to dangerous or burdensome events such as the proposed `チバセン`.

Hachiman recognizes a critical social mechanism:

> `人間は感情で動く。`

People form an affective conclusion and then build reasoning around it. He correctly observes that pure refutation will not dissolve an argument whose actual cause is resentment.

Yet he still tries to solve the problem by constructing rational traps.

The committee’s strategy uses:

- participation rules;
- outsider restrictions;
- safety language;
- an enormous survey burden;
- institutional procedure;
- mutually costly alternatives.

Hachiman himself recognizes some of this as `詭弁` and uses `建前` strategically.

The strategy approximates mutually assured destruction: opposing the committee must be made expensive enough that the other side chooses compromise.

It does not fully work.

## 3.8 Phase H — Sagami’s unscripted speech changes the meeting

The decisive rupture occurs when Sagami stops performing the committee chair role cleanly and speaks from grievance:

> `うちだってちゃんとやってるじゃない……`

and then:

> `今度はちゃんとやろうって頑張ってるじゃない！ なんでわかってくれないの？`

Hachiman calls the speech irrational, emotional, and outside his diagram.

That is exactly why it works.

He finally recognizes:

> the problem began in emotion; therefore emotion, not merely counter-logic, is capable of altering it.

The decisive action is not Hachiman sacrificing himself, assigning social roles, or delivering the perfect argument.

It is **Sagami becoming the author of the wound**.

That is the volume’s most important intervention-ethics correction.

## 3.9 Phase I — the sports festival ends without moral purification

The sports festival itself is energetic, compromised, and imperfect. Hachiman still cheats. The red team still loses. The event nevertheless becomes meaningful to Meguri and the others.

Afterward, the Service Club returns to ordinary time. Yukino and Yui can joke, anticipate next year, and treat the festival as shared memory.

Sagami is not absorbed into the club and not converted into Hachiman’s friend.

She is still doing post-event administrative work.

When they meet in the hall, she says only:

> `ねぇ、そこ、どいてくんない`

Hachiman moves.

He concludes that perhaps the two of them can become good at maintaining the relationship of **other people**.

That ending is one of the quietest and most important in the early series.

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

## 4.1 External social perception remains one of his strongest domains

Volume 6.5 repeatedly validates Hachiman’s ability to see:

- status incentives;
- how titles change the meaning of identical speech;
- why Sagami values visible leadership;
- how temporary committees create weak ties rather than durable friendship;
- why athletic clubs possess bargaining power;
- how institutional rules can be invoked as authority;
- why resentment often precedes its stated logic;
- how public humiliation changes group behavior.

His social cognition is not a joke. The committee repeatedly benefits from it.

## 4.2 But he initially minimizes harms that fall on himself

The `実害` exchange is the first direct correction.

Hachiman treats reputational hostility as something that will disappear if ignored. Yukino insists there is already concrete harm. The shoe-locker incident proves her right.

This extends Hiratsuka’s Volume 6 correction. Hachiman cannot preserve the fiction that harm to him is private, costless, or socially irrelevant simply because he can tolerate it.

## 4.3 The shoe-locker scene complicates the martyr model

Hachiman finds garbage placed specifically in his locker. His reaction is revealing:

- cold fatigue rather than dramatic self-pity;
- an attempt to classify the act rationally;
- acceptance that visible targets invite more aggression;
- immediate search for a tactical response.

Then he moves the garbage into **Tobe’s** locker.

This is ethically important.

Hachiman reasons that Tobe’s social insulation will convert the same act into something survivable—indeed, the result is partly comic because friends gather around him and Hayama normalizes the situation.

But Hachiman also says:

> `俺のための犠牲もまた必要なのだ。`

Volume 6.5 therefore prevents a one-note characterization of Hachiman as someone who only spends himself. Under pressure, he can **externalize cost onto another person** when he judges that person better positioned to absorb it.

That is rational from a damage-distribution perspective.

It is also unilateral.

Tobe never agrees to be the buffer.

## 4.4 Hachiman’s theory of change is partly right

His early thesis is that pain alters behavior more reliably than exhortation. Later, watching Sagami withdraw from her friends rather than seek consolation, he develops the model further:

- pain generates avoidance;
- repeated behavior becomes habit;
- habit becomes socially legible character;
- therefore behavior can produce objective change even if “essence” remains disputed.

This is not nonsense.

The novel gives him supporting evidence.

The error would be turning this into a complete theory of persons.

Pain is not the only thing changing Sagami. She also receives:

- another opportunity;
- institutional responsibility;
- requested cooperation;
- consequences;
- social recognition from Meguri;
- the chance to articulate frustration in her own words;
- continued work after the public event.

The causal system is plural.

## 4.5 His approval analysis can become contempt masquerading as ontology

Hachiman observes Sagami’s approval dependence accurately. But his language often slides from:

> “Sagami seeks recognition”

into:

> “therefore Sagami’s acts are of little value.”

That inference does not follow.

The volume’s strongest rebuttal is Meguri. Meguri recognizes effort without pretending Sagami is competent, pure, or transformed. Recognition here is not infantilizing applause. It is accurate social feedback:

> you have been trying; you have come this far; do you want to continue?

Sagami accepts.

Recognition can therefore function as **developmental information**, not only dependency fuel.

## 4.6 His model fails when affect refuses to stay noise

Hachiman knows in theory that people move through emotion. He says so directly.

But his planning still tries to make emotion irrelevant by constructing procedural cages.

Sagami’s breakdown exposes the contradiction. He calls it `感情論`, recognizes it as outside the diagram, and then admits that the original problem was affective; affect is therefore what changes the field.

This is not “logic bad, feelings good.”

The correction is:

> **If emotion is part of the cause, a solution that models only incentives and formal arguments is causally incomplete.**

## 4.7 Hachiman’s mask theory is unstable

He observes that people adopt different `仮面` and `ペルソナ` according to relational distance and says these are different from the `本来の自分`.

At first glance this sounds like a simple surface/true-self dichotomy.

The rest of the volume destabilizes it:

- Sagami’s chairman performance changes what she can do;
- Meguri’s soft demeanor and institutional authority are both real;
- Tobe’s comic social role can absorb hostility while still containing genuine distress;
- Hachiman himself adopts comic, adversarial, bureaucratic, and strategic roles.

The commercial afterword makes the methodological warning explicit: because the series is filtered through Hachiman’s first-person values, characterization changes as his understanding changes, and “front” versus “back” may itself be something the observer arbitrarily decides.

That authorial paratext strongly supports the V2 narratorial method without converting the afterword into omniscient interpretation of any particular scene.

# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Hikigaya Hachiman

### Gains

- accepts that the cultural-festival method produced concrete social consequences;
- works in a more distributed system than in Volume 6;
- develops a more nuanced behavior-versus-essence theory of change;
- recognizes that affect can be causally primary;
- respects Sagami’s right to make a costly choice he personally distrusts;
- can imagine a relationship improving without becoming friendship;
- experiences ordinary club life as something to which he has become accustomed.

### Persistent liabilities

- still treats himself as unusually expendable;
- still uses people instrumentally without permission;
- moves harassment onto Tobe;
- can mistake accurate cynicism for a complete account;
- still equates approval need with weakness too quickly;
- reaches coercive strategies before participatory ones;
- tends to understand emotional legitimacy only after it disrupts his plan.

### Volume-local state

Hachiman has not abandoned his worldview. He is becoming **more discriminating about where it fails**.

## 5.2 Yukinoshita Yukino

Yukino’s recommendation of Sagami is deliberately severe. It is not soft forgiveness.

She believes another opportunity can matter and is willing to expose Sagami to responsibility again. That carries paternalistic risk because Yukino initially thinks in terms of what may restore Sagami’s self-evaluation rather than beginning from Sagami’s own articulated desire.

However, several things distinguish this from Volume 6’s substitutionary competence:

- Sagami ultimately chooses the position;
- Sagami remains publicly responsible for it;
- Yukino does not quietly become chairman in her place;
- when Sagami decides to continue after the later crisis, Yukino accepts that choice and asks how to support the resulting strategy.

Yui correctly catches a residual danger: Yukino’s initial confidence still assumes Yukino herself will work heavily enough to keep Sagami from failing.

So the growth is real but incomplete.

## 5.3 Yuigahama Yui

Yui continues the pattern established in V1–V6: she is not merely emotional cushioning.

Her functions include:

- noticing when Yukino’s plan still assumes too much self-burden;
- reading how students will respond to events;
- proposing socially legible concepts such as turning appointments into “captain versus captain” spectacle;
- maintaining connections unavailable to Hachiman;
- recruiting / mediating;
- supporting Sagami without pretending nothing happened;
- participating in the final distributed planning rather than standing outside it.

The cumulative picture is increasingly clear:

> **Yui’s social intelligence is operational intelligence.**

The series does not simply give Hachiman “logic,” Yukino “competence,” and Yui “feelings.” Yui understands how events become attractive, how people will interpret them, and how requests can be made in a socially usable form.

## 5.4 Sagami Minami

Sagami is the volume’s principal subject.

The V2 reading should refuse two simplifications:

1. she was always secretly good;
2. she remains exactly the same and merely learns to hide it.

Neither fits the evidence.

Sagami remains:

- vain;
- status-conscious;
- sensitive to rank and public evaluation;
- resentful;
- capable of self-defense and blame shifting.

But she also:

- accepts a second public responsibility after humiliation;
- apologizes under pressure;
- explicitly asks for cooperation;
- promises consideration for others’ club obligations;
- continues after the social protection of her old allies weakens;
- withdraws rather than immediately soliciting sympathy;
- admits she is trying;
- articulates that she wants her effort recognized;
- chooses to continue after Meguri acknowledges that effort;
- remains engaged in post-event administrative work after the spectacle is over.

That is enough for **behavioral development**.

It is not enough for redemption.

The volume never asks Hachiman to like her.

## 5.5 Shiromeguri Meguri

Meguri becomes one of the most important countermodels of leadership so far.

She openly admits she is not an exceptional administrator. Her strength lies elsewhere:

- people want to help her;
- she notices effort;
- she can confer recognition without making recognition cheap;
- she accepts distributed competence rather than needing to be the best worker;
- she frames continuation as an invitation rather than a command.

Where Yukino’s early leadership tends toward competence substitution, Meguri’s leadership is **relationally distributed**.

Her recognition of Sagami is especially important because she does not say Sagami is excellent. She says she can see that Sagami is trying.

That level of precision is what makes the praise usable.

## 5.6 Hayama Hayato

Hayama remains a social stabilizer and a source of legitimacy. Hachiman explicitly tells him that his intervention in getting Sagami to accept was not actually necessary; Hachiman already intended to maneuver Sagami into the role and merely benefited from Hayama making the process easier.

The exchange is revealing less because of Hayama than because of Hachiman: Hayama’s social goodwill is another resource Hachiman is willing to model instrumentally.

## 5.7 Tobe Kakeru

Tobe unexpectedly becomes an ethical test case.

His broad social support allows him to metabolize the garbage incident differently from Hachiman. Friends gather, joke, react, and Hayama stays attentive. Yet the narration explicitly registers some real distress beneath Tobe’s performance.

The point is not that popular people cannot be hurt.

It is that **social buffering changes the meaning and durability of the same hostile act**.

Hachiman understands this and exploits it.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Hachiman ↔ Yukino

Volume 6.5 shows a noticeably more functional pair after Volume 6’s crisis.

They still argue, but the argument is increasingly about methods rather than whether the other person can be trusted to exist as understood.

Key movement:

- Hachiman notices and voices concern that Yukino may again overwork herself;
- Yukino is briefly surprised by the concern;
- Yui reinforces it;
- Yukino’s later role remains substantial but not substitutionary to the same degree;
- Hachiman and Yukino can collaborate in adversarial planning without pretending they agree morally on every step.

The relationship has acquired **conflict tolerance**.

## 6.2 Hachiman ↔ Yui

The dyad is comparatively ordinary in this volume, which is itself significant.

Yui can challenge his assumptions, contribute solutions, and joke with him without every interaction becoming a referendum on the accident or romantic origin problem.

That ordinary operational familiarity is precisely the sort of relational substrate the main crisis volumes often hide.

## 6.3 Yukino ↔ Yui

Their V3–V6 friendship continues to stabilize.

Yui can criticize Yukino’s plan directly—especially the assumption that Yukino will once again absorb work—and Yukino does not treat the criticism as betrayal.

By the post-festival club scene they can share a future-facing “next year” fantasy together.

This is not evidence that all tensions are resolved. It is evidence that the dyad now tolerates disagreement inside continued affection.

## 6.4 The Service Club triad

Volume 6.5 may be the strongest early example of the trio functioning as **distributed competence**:

- Yukino: structure, administration, formal argument;
- Yui: social reach, audience sense, relationship translation;
- Hachiman: adversarial diagnosis, incentives, institutional loopholes.

The key improvement over Volume 6 is not that Hachiman becomes nice.

It is that the solution system does not require him to be the only meaningful actor.

The decisive late breakthrough is not even his.

Sagami supplies it.

## 6.5 Hachiman ↔ Sagami

The dyad does **not** reconcile.

That is one of the volume’s strengths.

At the end:

- Sagami still dislikes him;
- Hachiman still does not admire her personality;
- neither apologizes into friendship;
- neither pretends the rooftop did not happen.

What changes is behavioral compatibility.

She can ask him to move without staging victimhood. He can move without retaliating or demanding recognition.

Hachiman’s phrase is effectively:

> they may become able to maintain the relationship of `他人` well.

This is not relational failure.

It is a legitimate social achievement.

## 6.6 Sagami ↔ Meguri

This may be the volume’s healthiest corrective relationship.

Meguri sees a person whom almost everyone else has reduced to either:

- incompetent status seeker;
- victim;
- problem;
- chairman function.

She recognizes effort while maintaining standards.

That recognition lets Sagami continue **without erasing the difficulty**.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

## 7.1 The nomination of Sagami

**Problem ownership:** mixed. The class atmosphere and sports festival are institutional problems; Sagami’s confidence is Sagami’s problem.

**Who defines the remedy:** initially Yukino and the Service Club.

**Authorization:** Sagami must ultimately accept the chair role; she is not physically or formally forced into it.

**Risk:** high. The proposal uses a real institutional job partly as rehabilitation.

**Assessment:** paternalistic but not fully agency-erasing. It is ethically superior to the V6 rooftop method because Sagami retains a genuine possibility of refusal and later becomes the visible owner of the role.

## 7.2 The forced apology / reconciliation meeting

**Problem:** committee cooperation.

**Method:** public pressure requiring Sagami and the athletic-club side to articulate grievances and concessions.

**Benefits:** produces an explicit agreement and makes responsibilities speakable.

**Costs:** humiliation; performative apology; incentive for strategic language.

**Assessment:** partially effective coercive mediation, not genuine reconciliation.

The novel itself refuses to romanticize the result. Hachiman recognizes the transactional subtext.

## 7.3 Hachiman’s use of Hayama

Hayama’s instinct to intervene is socially useful, and Hachiman admits he took advantage of it.

This is a relatively low-cost example of Hachiman treating another person’s predictable moral disposition as a tool.

The behavior matters because it is the same cognitive style seen in more damaging interventions, even when the immediate stakes are modest.

## 7.4 The shoe-locker transfer to Tobe

This is one of the clearest unilateral cost-shifts in the early series.

Hachiman correctly predicts that Tobe’s social network will reduce the damage. But the fact that Tobe is resilient or socially buffered does not create authorization.

The scene therefore belongs in the intervention ledger as:

> **effective harm redistribution without consent.**

It complicates any theory that Hachiman’s sole ethical problem is excessive self-sacrifice.

## 7.5 The “mutually assured destruction” strategy

The committee uses safety restrictions, participation rules, outsider restrictions, and survey burdens to make continued opposition costly.

Hachiman openly knows portions of the argument are sophistic (`詭弁`) and that `配慮` can be used as `建前` to steer opinion.

This strategy is institutionally intelligent and ethically dubious.

It attempts to solve a relationship conflict by engineering incentives so that one side loses acceptable alternatives.

That is control.

But unlike the V6 rooftop method, it does not require Hachiman to injure himself or Sagami personally. It is bureaucratic coercion rather than martyrdom theater.

## 7.6 Sagami’s emotional speech is the volume’s agency breakthrough

The planned strategy stalls.

Sagami then says what Hachiman did not script:

- she is trying;
- she apologized;
- she wants that effort acknowledged;
- she does not understand why the others refuse to see it.

Hachiman is explicit that this is outside his diagram.

The field changes because Sagami stops functioning as the **object** of rehabilitation and becomes the **speaker** of the grievance.

That distinction should be preserved for the series-level ethics synthesis.

## 7.7 Meguri’s recognition preserves choice

Meguri later tells Sagami that she sees her effort and asks whether she wants to keep going.

This is an unusually clean intervention:

- accurate rather than inflated praise;
- no denial of weakness;
- no command;
- no substitution;
- continuation remains Sagami’s answer.

When Sagami chooses to continue despite predictable pain, Hachiman ultimately recognizes that he has no right to veto the choice.

## 7.8 The ending models non-reconciliation as ethical success

Neither Hachiman nor Sagami is required to forgive, confess affection, or become intimate.

The outcome is a stable boundary.

That is particularly important in a series so concerned with closeness. Not every ethical relationship must maximize intimacy.

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 Reputation is a material resource

Volume 6 treated reputation as something Hachiman could spend. Volume 6.5 shows the debt coming due.

Reputation changes:

- who is believed;
- what actions are socially permitted against a person;
- whether harassment is treated as serious;
- whether peers buffer or amplify injury.

The garbage incident dramatizes a harsh social rule Hachiman recognizes:

> once a person has been publicly classified as a legitimate target, others feel freer to attack.

## 8.2 Social buffering explains the Tobe contrast

The same garbage moved into Tobe’s locker produces a different ecology:

- immediate witnesses;
- friends approaching;
- humor;
- Hayama’s attention;
- collective interpretation.

Tobe is not invulnerable. His distress is visible.

But he does not have to metabolize the event alone.

This is an important structural distinction between **individual resilience** and **networked resilience**.

## 8.3 Temporary institutions create contingent relationships

Hachiman explicitly observes the peculiar sociality of event committees: people who are not really friends spend intense time together and then disperse.

That temporary relational form explains both the power and fragility of the sports-festival group.

A committee can coordinate without producing intimacy.

That is not necessarily hypocrisy. It may simply be the appropriate scale of the relationship.

## 8.4 Status changes the force of speech

Hachiman repeatedly notes that identical propositions are heard differently depending on:

- speaker rank;
- popularity;
- role;
- institutional title.

This is why Hayama, Meguri, Yukino, Sagami, and Hachiman do not have interchangeable persuasive power.

The novel treats status not merely as teenage vanity but as a **communication infrastructure**.

## 8.5 Institutional authority can become rhetorical cover

The committee’s late strategy invokes school policy and safety concerns as leverage. Hachiman understands that few people will challenge an argument framed as institutional `配慮`.

This is an early example of a broader political theme:

> ostensibly neutral procedure can be used strategically to shape what outcomes remain available.

The tactic works because the school itself occupies a higher legitimacy level than either committee faction.

## 8.6 Meguri’s leadership is a distributed constitution

Meguri’s self-description matters. She does not claim exceptional personal competence. She says, effectively, that the student council around her makes the role possible.

Her authority therefore rests on:

- mutual affection;
- trust;
- recognition;
- willingness of others to supply what she lacks.

This is a very different institutional model from Yukino’s earlier assumption that competence legitimates absorbing other people’s work.

# 9. Authenticity / `本物` ledger

## 9.1 Lexical result

In the commercial Volume 6.5 sports-festival sequence:

- `本物`: **0 governing occurrences**
- `偽物`: **0 governing occurrences**
- `本音`: **0**
- `欺瞞`: conceptually present once in the ordinary social-mask discussion, but not as a developed `本物` opposition
- `建前`: **1 especially important strategic use** in the committee’s safety argument
- `噓`: several ordinary / social-mask uses

So this is not an explicit `本物` volume.

## 9.2 Yet it is deeply relevant to the later authenticity problem

The volume repeatedly tests whether impurity of motive invalidates action.

Sagami wants:

- status;
- approval;
- restored standing;
- fun;
- success;
- proof that she is trying.

Those motives coexist.

The novel does not purify them before allowing behavioral growth.

That is significant.

## 9.3 Behavioral reality versus essential reality

Hachiman’s most developed theory in the volume distinguishes:

- “essential” change;
- observable behavioral change;
- habitual change;
- social evaluation.

He insists that even behavior initially produced by pain avoidance can become objectively meaningful once repeated.

This creates a major precursor to the later authenticity problem:

> **Does an act have to originate from a pure essence in order to become real?**

Volume 6.5 increasingly suggests **no**.

## 9.4 Masks are not automatically falsehoods

Hachiman initially describes relational personas as masks different from a `本来の自分`.

But the afterword and narrative form complicate that binary. A role can:

- conceal;
- protect;
- coordinate;
- enable action;
- become habit;
- alter social identity.

The better V2 hypothesis is:

> a performed role is not automatically fake; its ethical significance depends on how it is chosen, what it enables, and whether it can be revised.

## 9.5 Prospective conclusion

Do **not** translate Volume 6.5 into the mature doctrine “the genuine means accountable continuation.”

What is earned here is narrower:

> **A relationship or action does not become meaningless simply because its motives are mixed, its repair is incomplete, or its participants remain flawed.**

# 10. Dependency / autonomy ledger

## 10.1 `依存` is not the governing vocabulary

The sports-festival sequence does not frame its conflicts through interpersonal `依存`.

The important vocabulary is:

- `頼る`
- `助かる`
- cooperation;
- division of labor;
- recognition;
- self-responsibility;
- institutional obligation.

## 10.2 Sagami explicitly asks for bounded cooperation

One of her meaningful developments is:

> `協力してもらえると助かる`

paired with a promise not to impose excessively on club obligations.

This is imperfectly motivated, but structurally healthy in one important respect:

> need is stated rather than silently converted into somebody else’s duty.

## 10.3 Yukino’s reliance becomes more distributed

Hachiman notices it is unusual for Yukino to rely proactively on Hayama. More importantly, the whole event requires her to work through other people rather than simply replacing them.

The improvement from Volume 6 is therefore not “Yukino learns dependence.”

It is:

> **Yukino increasingly permits an institution to remain plural.**

## 10.4 Meguri supplies the strongest positive interdependence model

Meguri openly depends on her student-council team and remains a legitimate leader.

This strongly rebuts any later temptation to equate dependence itself with dysfunction.

Her dependence is:

- acknowledged;
- reciprocal;
- role-compatible;
- non-possessive;
- socially productive.

## 10.5 Hachiman’s “I did not need you” line to Hayama

Hachiman tells Hayama that he would have gotten Sagami into the position without him, and that Hayama merely saved time.

This is not evidence that Hachiman has transcended reliance.

It is evidence of his continuing preference to conceptualize help as **replaceable utility** rather than relational obligation.

That preference remains psychologically important.

# 11. Japanese language and voice

## 11.1 Yukino’s `実害なら、あるわ`

The compression matters.

Hachiman says there is no real damage. Yukino does not moralize at length. She simply asserts:

> `実害なら、あるわ`

The particle `なら` takes his category and turns it against him: **if “actual damage” is the criterion, then actual damage exists.**

Her voice is corrective rather than consoling.

## 11.2 Sagami’s repeated `ちゃんと`

At the late meeting Sagami repeatedly reaches for `ちゃんと`:

- she is properly doing the work;
- this time she intended to do it properly;
- she apologized;
- she reflected.

`ちゃんと` is doing moral work. Sagami is asking to be judged not only by the old cultural-festival narrative but by **current effort under a shared norm of responsibility**.

Her speech is emotionally messy, but its central claim is coherent:

> do not freeze me at my worst performance if I am now performing the obligation differently.

## 11.3 `承認` changes meaning across contexts

Hachiman often treats `承認` as dependency and weakness. Yet the volume distinguishes several forms:

- compulsive need for approval;
- status recognition;
- institutional approval;
- accurate acknowledgment of effort.

Meguri’s recognition shows why the noun cannot be assigned one moral valence.

## 11.4 Hachiman’s abstract vocabulary

When analyzing Sagami, Hachiman reaches for high-abstraction terminology:

- `自己承認欲求`
- `自己同一性`
- `客観的変化`
- `本質的な変化`
- `感情論`
- `論理`
- `合理的`

The vocabulary creates analytic distance.

The novel then punctures that distance through comedy and through events his terminology fails to predict.

This is characteristic Hachiman narration: often incisive, often over-systematized.

## 11.5 `他人という関係性を上手に保つ`

The final formulation is linguistically striking because `他人` is not used as failure.

Hachiman imagines becoming **good at maintaining otherness**.

This is a relationally modest but ethically serious proposition:

> closeness is not the only valid destination of repaired interaction.

## 11.6 `意義` rather than praise

When Sagami chooses to continue despite likely pain, Hachiman does not suddenly call her good.

He says:

> `そこには意義がある。`

There is **significance / worth in the act**.

This preserves evaluative precision. He can recognize the action without rewriting the person.

## 11.7 The afterword as narratorial-method paratext

Watari explicitly states that because the work is filtered through Hachiman’s first person, character depiction changes according to his values and degree of understanding:

> `彼の理解の度合いによって、描写のされかたは変わる`

This does not authorize treating every Hachiman statement as false.

It authorizes exactly the V2 method:

> classify observation, inference, self-protective interpretation, and later correction separately.

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertextuality

## 12.1 Comedy repeatedly absorbs injury without deleting it

The Tobe locker scene is the best example.

Hachiman converts harassment directed at him into a situation that becomes socially comic around Tobe. Yet the prose still notes:

- Tobe’s underlying distress;
- Hachiman’s guilt;
- Hayama’s momentary hardening.

Comedy changes the social processing of harm. It does not prove the harm unreal.

## 12.2 Sports-festival escalation turns school administration into war theory

Hachiman narrates committee conflict through:

- mutually assured destruction;
- military pressure;
- bargaining;
- strategic sacrifice;
- rhetorical traps.

This is funny because the object is a high-school sports festival.

It is also diagnostically useful because Hachiman genuinely thinks in adversarial systems.

## 12.3 Chiba regional jokes remain identity texture rather than “themes”

`チバセン`, MAX Coffee, local references, and exaggerated Chiba pride continue to give the prose a specific geographic comic texture.

They should not be inflated into a grand theory of regional identity unless later recurrence warrants it.

## 12.4 The Christmas bonus is a publication coda, not a prospective V6.5 chapter

The Christmas prose and `君とMerry Christmas` materially belong to the book object, but their character-state evidence is post-Volume-9.

They are therefore logged here as **deferred supplementary evidence** only.

No lyric or Christmas relationship claim is admitted into this volume’s prospective ledgers.

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial tests

## Test A — Is Hachiman’s “people do not change” theory simply disproven?

**Too simple.**

The novel itself preserves part of the theory. Sagami does not become a different essence overnight. Her vanity, resentment, and approval sensitivity remain visible.

The stronger revision is:

> **continuity of personality does not prevent meaningful behavioral development.**

Hachiman moves from categorical denial toward that distinction.

## Test B — Is Sagami redeemed?

**No.**

There is no moral purification, no warm reconciliation, and no demand that the reader reinterpret Volume 6 as a misunderstanding.

What is supportable is **rehabilitation of agency and behavior**.

## Test C — Is Meguri’s encouragement merely poisonous kindness?

Hachiman worries that it is. His concern deserves weight: encouragement can persuade someone to remain in a painful role.

But Meguri does several ethically important things correctly:

- describes effort accurately;
- does not deny incompetence;
- does not erase past failure;
- asks rather than commands;
- leaves Sagami the decision.

The “poison” reading is therefore incomplete.

## Test D — Did public humiliation cure Sagami?

**Not established.**

Humiliation contributes to behavioral change, but the text gives a wider causal system: opportunity, responsibility, recognition, conflict, self-expression, and continued work.

Treating pain as the sole developmental technology would reproduce Hachiman’s bias.

## Test E — Does the emotional outburst mean logic is useless?

**No.**

The event still requires:

- safety planning;
- logistics;
- formal proposals;
- rules;
- scheduling;
- surveys;
- institutional authority.

The correction is that rational architecture cannot replace the affective cause of a social conflict.

## Test F — Is the Service Club now healthy?

**Too early.**

Distributed competence is healthier than substitutionary competence, but Hachiman still manipulates, Yukino still risks overfunctioning, and Yui still often supplies unacknowledged emotional labor.

The volume shows improvement, not completion.

## Test G — Are masks necessarily false?

**The volume itself undermines that conclusion.**

Performed roles can become real practices. The afterword further warns that “front” and “back” may be observer-imposed categories.

## Test H — Is “remaining strangers” a failure to reconcile?

**No.**

The ending offers a strong pluralist social ethic: some histories are better managed through respectful distance than compulsory intimacy.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

With full-series hindsight, Volume 6.5 becomes considerably more important than a “sports festival bonus volume.”

## 15.1 It supplies an early alternative to purity-based authenticity

Sagami develops without becoming pure.

The relationship between Hachiman and Sagami improves without becoming intimate.

The committee succeeds without becoming harmonious.

These are all early demonstrations that relational value can survive mixed motives and incomplete repair.

Later `本物` discourse should therefore not be read as appearing ex nihilo. Volume 6.5 has already tested whether **social reality can be earned through repeated action even when interior purity is unavailable**.

## 15.2 It shows a route out of Hachiman’s control problem

Volume 6’s rooftop climax succeeded because Hachiman authored everyone’s roles.

Volume 6.5’s decisive meeting shifts because Sagami speaks outside his plan.

The later series repeatedly asks whether Hachiman can allow other people’s desire to remain causally effective instead of absorbing it into his own solution architecture.

This volume gives an unusually early positive case:

> the solution becomes better when the subject surprises the strategist.

## 15.3 Behavioral continuity becomes relevant to Hachiman himself

Hachiman’s distinction between essence and learned behavior will eventually matter beyond Sagami.

His own development is not credible if “growth” means becoming a fundamentally different protagonist. The series instead repeatedly asks whether someone can remain recognizably Hachiman while learning different ways to:

- ask;
- wait;
- accept answers;
- distribute cost;
- remain involved.

Volume 6.5’s change theory therefore rebounds onto its narrator.

## 15.4 The ending’s distance ethic becomes a useful counterweight to later intimacy

Later Oregairu gives enormous weight to deep, difficult attachment.

Volume 6.5 preserves another ethical truth:

> not every repaired relation should become close.

That matters when evaluating “help,” “saving,” and the tendency of romantic readings to assume maximal intimacy is always the best outcome.

## 15.5 The Christmas bonus belongs later

Full-series hindsight makes the appendix highly meaningful: ordinary post-crisis club warmth, Christmas choice, concealed feelings, and the song’s explicit `本物` vocabulary all matter.

Precisely because they matter, they must **not** be used here.

They belong to the post-Volume-9 audit, where they can be analyzed without chronology violation.

# 16. V1 revision audit

## 16.1 RETAIN-STRONG — Volume 6 consequences are real

V1 correctly identified 6.5 as the volume that refuses to let Hachiman’s cultural-festival method disappear after public success.

The Japanese strongly supports this, especially:

- class hostility;
- Yukino’s `実害` correction;
- the shoe-locker harassment;
- Hachiman’s fatigue and response.

## 16.2 RETAIN-STRONG, NARROW — “Sagami rehabilitation, not redemption”

This remains an excellent shorthand.

V2 adds precision:

- her motives remain mixed;
- her behavior changes in multiple observable ways;
- recognition by Meguri is developmentally important;
- there is no forced friendship with Hachiman.

## 16.3 RETAIN-STRONG, REFORMULATE — Hachiman’s change theory

V1 was right that 6.5 complicates “people do not change.”

The more exact V2 formulation is:

> **Hachiman does not abandon essence continuity; he concedes that pain, repeated action, habit, and social evaluation can create objectively meaningful behavioral change.**

This is not a full philosophical conversion.

## 16.4 RETAIN-STRONG — emotion overturns an emotional conflict

V1 correctly identified Sagami’s outburst as the point that breaks the deadlock.

V2 sharpens why:

Hachiman already knows people are moved by emotion, yet his plan still seeks to make emotion irrelevant through procedure. Sagami’s speech exposes the causal incompleteness of his method.

## 16.5 RETAIN-STRONG — distributed competence

The Service Club and school ecology solve the sports-festival problem through multiple distinct competences rather than one martyr solution.

This is a genuine post-V6 improvement.

## 16.6 STRENGTHEN — the Tobe locker transfer

V1 treated the locker incident principally as evidence that Hachiman’s reputation abuse has consequences.

V2 adds an important ethical correction:

> Hachiman does not merely endure the harm; he deliberately transfers it onto a socially protected third party without consent.

This is an early case of unilateral cost allocation that does **not** take the form of self-sacrifice.

## 16.7 RETAIN-STRONG — the school becomes a social world

The sports festival expands Oregairu beyond the romantic trio into:

- committee politics;
- temporary friendships;
- student-government legitimacy;
- athletic-club interests;
- class reputation;
- networked resilience;
- weak ties.

That V1 observation remains strongly supported.

## 16.8 RETAIN-STRONG — Watari’s first-person methodological note

V1 correctly recognized the afterword as unusually useful analytical paratext.

The V2 methodology is almost directly anticipated by Watari’s statement that Hachiman’s degree of understanding changes how characters are depicted.

This supports—not proves—the need for domain-specific narratorial auditing.

## 16.9 CORRECTION — commercial 6.5 is not simply “all of 6.25 + 6.50 + 6.75”

This is the major provenance revision.

The commercial edition reconstructs the sports-festival through-line but **does not reproduce every story** from the three original bonus booklets. At minimum, the Komachi, Yui, and Kawasaki interludes identified above are absent from Chapters ①–⑧.

The older books therefore remain a broader supplementary corpus than the commercial main sequence.

## 16.10 CORRECTION — the Christmas bonus must be quarantined prospectively

V1 correctly noticed that the Christmas material occurs after Volume 9, but its analysis was still bundled into the 6.5 discussion.

V2 applies a stricter chronology rule:

> publication binding does not override explicit story chronology.

The prose rewrite, drama CD, and `君とMerry Christmas` are deferred to the post-V9 layer.

## 16.11 NARROW — the 6.75 afterword’s “cannot erase failure” statement

The original 6.75 afterword contains the valuable line that failure can be forgotten, accustomed to, or recovered from, but cannot be erased and made never to have happened.

That remains highly relevant to the sports-festival sequence.

However, it is **ancestor paratext**, not the commercial 6.5 afterword. The commercial book replaces that afterword context with its own broader first-person / “back side” methodological discussion.

The evidentiary tier must remain visible.

# Volume 6.5 closing thesis

Volume 6 ended by showing that successful intervention can become domination when one person assigns everyone else a role and spends himself to make the script work.

Volume 6.5 responds with a smaller and more durable idea.

Sagami is not absolved. Hachiman is not vindicated. The committee does not become a family. The past is not erased. The sports festival does not even end in the desired victory.

What changes is practice.

Sagami can act differently without becoming a different species of person. Meguri can recognize effort without calling failure success. Yukino can structure without doing everything herself. Yui can translate social reality into workable action. Hachiman can discover that emotional agency outside his plan may be more causally powerful than the plan itself. And two people who genuinely dislike one another can learn to pass in a hallway without reopening the wound.

The most defensible proposition is therefore:

> **Growth does not require rewriting the past, purifying motive, or replacing the self. Sometimes it means learning a new action, repeating it until it becomes livable, and building enough distance that yesterday’s failure no longer dictates tomorrow’s entire relationship.**

That is a much stronger bridge into the Kyoto material than “Sagami became nicer.”
