# OREGAIRU V2 — Volume 12 Deep Reading
## 『やはり俺の青春ラブコメはまちがっている。12』

### Governing thesis

**Volume 12 is the point at which the post-`本物` problem acquires an explicit diagnostic vocabulary without allowing that vocabulary to become the answer. Yukino asks Hachiman and Yui not to solve her life but to witness her attempt to choose it; the prom becomes the institutional vehicle through which she tries to prove that she can originate, own, and complete an undertaking herself. Haruno then names the trio's relational mechanism `共依存`, Yukino calls her own conduct `依存`, and Hachiman recognizes the pleasure he takes in being needed. Yet the novel immediately limits the diagnosis: `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。` A mechanism can explain how care, need, usefulness, and self-worth reinforce one another; it cannot tell Hachiman what he feels, cannot determine what Yukino truly wants, and cannot decide whether separation is the only ethical cure. The volume's decisive movement therefore runs from **diagnosis toward reason**: once work, club duty, rescue identity, and even codependency can no longer serve as sufficient explanations, Hachiman is forced toward the embarrassingly ordinary fact that he made a promise to help someone whose future matters personally to him.**

A compact formulation is:

> **Volume 12 finally names `共依存`, then refuses to let the name substitute for a choice.**

A second formulation is equally important:

> **Autonomy is not proven by needing nobody. It is proven when a person can define the problem, request the terms of assistance, refuse unwanted intervention, and remain the author of what happens next.**

The volume does not yet prove that Hachiman, Yukino, and Yui have achieved that standard. It shows all three struggling to distinguish authorship from withdrawal, care from rescue, and painful truth from the false moral comfort of simply calling separation “independence.”

## 1.2 Formal architecture

Volume 12 has seven numbered chapters framed by three interludes:

1. opening `Interlude…`
2. `① やがて、季節は移ろい、雪は解けゆく。`
3. `② こう見えて、雪ノ下陽乃は酔っていない。`
4. `Interlude…`
5. `③ 不意打ちに、比企谷小町はあらたまる。`
6. `④ 今日まで、その鍵には一度も触れたことがない。`
7. `⑤ やはり、一色いろはは最強の後輩である。`
8. `⑥ ふと、由比ヶ浜結衣は未来に思いを馳せる。`
9. `⑦ その選択を、きっと悔やむと知っていても。`
10. closing `Interlude…`

The three interludes are not decorative. The opening voice says that an answer must be spoken even though speech cannot guarantee transmission and even though the choice will be regretted. The middle interlude is explicitly Yui's first-person interiority and reveals that she has long recognized Yukino's feelings while choosing not to force them into speech. The closing interlude is again explicitly Yui's and turns the volume's diagnostic language inward: she refuses the role of the pitiable girl whom Hachiman will automatically rescue and admits that she used Yukino as the person onto whom she could displace choice.

## 1.3 Incoming state from Checkpoint D

Checkpoint D froze the following prospective state at the end of Volume 11:

- Hachiman is more capable of asking, limited reliance, and revising his social models, but still capable of overriding another person's refusal when his own interpretation feels persuasive.
- Yukino has asserted that other people may not decide her feelings for her and has originated a request in her own name.
- Yui is an explicit claimant who says she wants `全部`, not a passive mediator awaiting somebody else's choice.
- the trio's desires are now openly nonidentical;
- `本物` remains an aspiration/request rather than a settled doctrine;
- ordinary warmth is real, but kindness can become a means of postponing consequences;
- autonomy has begun to look dialogical rather than synonymous with self-sufficiency.

Volume 12 must therefore be read as the first test of what happens **after everyone has standing to answer**. It may not import later conclusions about the prom, the romantic outcome, or the final form of the trio.

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

At the publication boundary of Volume 12, several questions are genuinely open:

1. What is Yukino's request actually asking Hachiman and Yui to do?
2. Is Yukino's desire to act “by herself” an authored developmental choice, a defensive overcorrection, or both?
3. Can Hachiman respect Yukino's refusal of help without converting distance itself into proof of autonomy?
4. Can Yui remain a friend to Yukino while acknowledging that Yukino's desire may conflict with her own?
5. What evidentiary authority should attach to Haruno's eventual `共依存` label?
6. Does Yukino herself accept that label, or only describe specific behavior as `依存`?
7. Can an account of relational mechanism tell anyone what they ought to choose?
8. Does “helping” Yukino necessarily violate her authorship once she has explicitly asked Hachiman not to intervene?
9. Is the prom principally Yukino's wish, Iroha's institutional project, a test before her family, or a proxy through which several unresolved relationships act?
10. When Hachiman eventually chooses to intervene, does he possess authorization, a promise, a personal reason, or merely another sophisticated rationalization?

The prospective reading must not assume:

- that `共依存` is an omniscient narrator diagnosis;
- that Haruno's interpretation is equivalent to authorial judgment;
- that independence requires severing reliance;
- that Yukino's `依存してる` proves every prior act of receiving help was pathological;
- that Yui's `依存` self-description determines the objective structure of her friendship with Yukino;
- that Hachiman's promise to help is already a romantic confession;
- that “self-reliance” and “separation from Hachiman” are identical;
- that the prom is merely a romantic proxy with no independent institutional stakes.

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

## 3.1 Opening interlude: speech is necessary even when truth hurts

Volume 12 begins by refusing a comforting interpretation of Volume 11. The opening voice describes a long silence in which emotion has outrun theory. The key sequence is:

> `言わなければわからない。言ったとしても伝わらない。`
>
> `だから、その答えを口にするべきだ。`

The first clause rejects telepathy. The second rejects the opposite fantasy that speech guarantees transparent understanding. Yet the conclusion is still that the answer must be spoken.

The interlude then anticipates regret:

> `その選択を、きっと悔やむと知っていても。`

and ends:

> `冷たくて残酷な、悲しいだけの本物なんて、欲しくはないのだから。`

Speaker attribution should remain formally open at this point. The language resonates with the trio's shared problem and later Yui-centered material, but the text has not yet licensed exclusive attribution. What is prospectively clear is the thematic rule: **pain alone does not authenticate a choice**. A “genuine” that is merely cold, cruel, and sad is not automatically desirable because it hurts.

## 3.2 Chapter 1: the request is to witness an authored ending

The action resumes on **February 14**, directly after Volume 11's aquarium/Ferris-wheel confrontation. Hachiman and the girls sit in the cold and recount a year of shared memories. The scene is warm precisely because the memories belong to all three. Hachiman observes:

> `思い出というのは自分の意思にかかわらず、一緒に過ごした人も共有するものだ。`

Shared history is not privately owned. Yet the conversation demonstrates the difference between sharing memories and sharing an answer. They speak about what they want to speak about and leave the actual problem untouched:

> `話したいことだけを話して、話したくないことはそのままで。`
>
> `本当に言いたいことは何一つ、言わずに。`

Hachiman recognizes that future conditions may remain the same while the answers diverge: `それでも、同じ答えにはおそらくならないだろう`. Most importantly, he can no longer plausibly claim ignorance. He has accumulated too much evidence about Yui and Yukino for “I don't know” to function as an innocence defense.

His retrospective confession is severe. He did not really want an answer, solution, or conclusion. He wanted `解消`—for the problem to dissolve by itself:

> `俺はありとあらゆる解答も解決も結論も求めてはいない。きっと解消されることを望んでいた。`

This is a major Hachiman-narration correction. The wish for ambiguity was not merely philosophical humility. It was also avoidance of consequence.

Hachiman then does something the V2 method has been waiting for: he asks Yukino to speak for herself.

> `……雪ノ下。聞いていいか。お前の話`

Yukino answers that she still does not know what she wants in the total relational sense. She then discloses a much older desire: she once wanted her father's work. Her family structure matters. Haruno was bound as successor while Yukino was ostensibly left “free”; because Yukino did not know how to act with that freedom, she followed her sister. She says her mother has always decided things, that her own vague expressions of desire were not properly taken up, and that she had never seriously told Haruno.

The strongest self-critique is:

> `たぶんきちんとした答えを出すのが怖くて、確かめることをしなかったの。`

Yukino's problem is therefore not simply “dependence on Hachiman.” It predates him. She has a developmental history in which **freedom without recognition or decision practice** can itself be disabling.

Her new commitment is carefully worded:

> `今度は自分の意志でちゃんと決めるわ。誰かに言われたからとかではなく、ちゃんと自分で考えて納得して、……諦めたい`

`諦めたい` is easy to flatten into defeat. Here it means something closer to wanting a conclusion she has actually verified rather than carrying an untested inherited impossibility forever. She wants the right to find out whether the old wish can live—and, if it cannot, to relinquish it as **her** decision.

Her actual request is narrower than later events make it seem:

> `……私の依頼はひとつだけ。……あなたたちに、その最後を見届けてもらいたい。それだけでいいの`

She does **not** initially request that Hachiman solve the family problem, run a prom, defeat her mother, or even “help” in the ordinary Service Club sense. She asks for witness.

Yui immediately tests the request:

> `ゆきのんの答えは、それ、なのかな……`

Yukino does not claim certainty:

> `もしかしたら、違うのかもしれない……`

This is essential. Yukino is authoring a provisional answer, not announcing an omniscient self-diagnosis. Her further explanation is that she wants to prove she can do something properly by herself so she can `ちゃんと始められる`.

Hachiman then performs an interpretive move that should be preserved as **Hachiman inference**, not doctrine. Yukino calls the long-surviving wish her `本音`; Hachiman reasons that a wish that resists time, neglect, avoidance, and attempted abandonment may deserve to be called `本物`. This is stronger than the Volume-8 telepathy fantasy because it locates genuineness in persistence rather than perfect comprehension. But it remains his criterion.

The more important ethical conclusion is narrower:

> `雪ノ下雪乃が自分で選んで、自分で決めること。`

He agrees to watch. The chapter closes not with rescue but with a promise of witness.

## 3.3 Chapter 2: family speech, Haruno's cooperation, and the limits of “watching”

At Yukino's apartment, Hachiman and Yui obey the request: they stay silent while Yukino speaks first. Her opening `私たちのこと。……これからの私たちについて` momentarily sounds like the triad, but she clarifies that she means herself, Haruno, and their mother. The ambiguity is structurally useful: Volume 12 repeatedly places family and Service Club under the same question of who gets to define Yukino's future.

Yukino tells Haruno that she will return home and properly state her future hope to their mother, even if it cannot be granted, because she does not want to leave regret:

> `これは……せめて、これだけはちゃんと言葉にして、納得できるようにしたい`

Haruno's response is more supportive than a “cruel oracle” reading allows. Once convinced that Yukino is serious, she says she will cooperate and may speak to their mother on Yukino's behalf. She also warns that returning home will likely mean renewed parental management. The scene therefore does not oppose autonomy to all assistance. Yukino can originate the aim while Haruno offers strategically relevant support.

On the walk afterward, Haruno tells Hachiman:

> `雪乃ちゃんがそれを選んだなら、わたしはそれを応援するの。それが正解でもまちがっていても`

This is an important positive control: respecting authored choice need not require certainty that the choice is “correct.” Haruno's darker adult lesson—`そうやってたくさん諦めて大人になっていくもんよ`—is a character philosophy, not a narrative law.

Haruno also tells Hachiman to **watch** rather than intervene and links his reflexive care to being an `お兄ちゃん`. That charge will later become central. Yet the same chapter complicates Haruno herself: she says that even when drinking she remains observed by a detached, sober self—`酔えない`. She predicts Hachiman is similar. Her later diagnostic posture therefore emerges from someone who experiences life partly through self-surveillance and distance, not from an unlocated omniscient position.

## 3.4 Middle interlude: Yui already knows what she does not want to make answerable

The second interlude is unambiguously Yui's first-person voice. While helping Yukino pack, Yui finds the hidden ride photograph of Yukino and Hachiman. Her reaction is not discovery but confirmation:

> `──ああ、やっぱり。`

She finds the picture, Yukino's preservation of it, and even its concealment cute. She puts it back. Then the interlude exposes the full logic of Yui's postponement.

She could ask Yukino directly. She could tease, offer support, or force the latent feeling into public language. She refuses because she believes Yukino would deny and retreat, ending the present relationship. The verbs become a program:

> `認めないで、見逃して、見落として、見過ごして。`

Yui knows this is self-serving. She says asking Yukino's feelings is unfair, stating her own is unfair, and blaming Yukino because she is afraid of Hachiman's answer is the most unfair move of all. She admits she has long known there is a place between Hachiman and Yukino into which she cannot easily enter, while also knowing she wants to go there.

The interlude ends:

> `──本物なんて、ほしくなかった。`

This does not mean Yui never wanted a genuine relationship. It means that **an ideal called `本物` has become terrifying when its realization appears to require the present shared world to collapse into a determinate answer**. The scene therefore strengthens Volume 11's warning: authenticity itself can become a violent abstraction if it is understood only as “force the hidden truth out and accept whatever breaks.”

## 3.5 Chapter 3: Komachi as the first major separation control

Komachi's entrance examination provides a relational control case outside the romantic triangle. Before knowing the result, she formally tells Hachiman:

> `お兄ちゃん、ありがとう。お世話になりました`

Hachiman cries. The emotion contains happiness and a small grief because Komachi is becoming less dependent on him. Crucially, neither sibling interprets that development as a requirement to sever relation. The family bond survives changed distance, greater self-direction, and direct gratitude.

This matters because Volume 12 repeatedly tempts Hachiman to equate “independence” with “I should no longer be involved.” Komachi demonstrates another model: **a relation may become less necessary without becoming less real**.

## 3.6 Chapter 4: the key and Hachiman's premature equation of distance with autonomy

The chapter title—`今日まで、その鍵には一度も触れたことがない。`—turns the Service Club key into a formal object of relational jurisdiction. Hachiman realizes that although he has spent enormous time in the room, Yukino has always possessed the key. He has inhabited a place without owning access to it.

When Yukino chooses to pursue the prom work through the student council and says she wants to do it herself, Hachiman reasons that tomorrow he will have no work-based reason to come to the club. He labels the resulting distance `自立` and compares the sadness to Komachi's gentle separation:

> `きっと、自立とはこういう類いのものなのだ。`

The scene is emotionally convincing but epistemically unstable. The Komachi analogy is not proof. Hachiman wants to believe the ache is evidence that he is doing the right thing:

> `だから、この疼痛は正しいことの証だ。`

That is a classic Hachiman rationalization candidate. Pain does not validate a theory any more than suffering validated `本物` in Volume 9. The chapter ends with the club being locked and Hachiman emphasizing that the key belongs to Yukino. The image properly registers **jurisdiction and access**; it should not yet be promoted into proof that healthy autonomy requires Hachiman's absence.

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

Volume 12 is unusually valuable because Hachiman catches several of his own interpretive habits in real time while remaining vulnerable to others.

## 4.1 Strong observation: he recognizes his wish for dissolution

The Chapter-1 admission that he wanted `解消` rather than an answer is one of his strongest self-readings in the series. It explains why seemingly sophisticated respect for ambiguity sometimes functioned as avoidance. He had hoped time would do the moral work of choosing for him.

**Status:** STRENGTHEN — high-confidence self-observation supported by prior behavior.

## 4.2 Stronger method: ask Yukino, then let the answer remain provisional

Hachiman asks Yukino to tell her own story. He does not begin by supplying a diagnosis. He accepts `もしかしたら、違うのかもしれない` without demanding certainty.

**Status:** PRESERVE/STRENGTHEN.

## 4.3 Interpretive risk: persistent desire = `本物`

Hachiman reasons that what survives time and attempted abandonment can be called genuine. This is plausible and more mature than the desire for telepathic certainty, but it remains a criterion generated by Hachiman.

Persistent desire may be meaningful without being wise, healthy, or the whole self.

**Status:** OPEN — Hachiman inference, not governing definition.

## 4.4 Interpretive risk: distance = independence

When Yukino takes over the prom work and Hachiman stops visiting the club, he uses Komachi's growing independence as an analogy and interprets his own sadness as proof of correctness.

This may contain truth. It may also serve his recurring wish to transform emotional pain into a moral reason for self-removal.

**Status:** OPEN / likely partial rationalization.

## 4.5 Improved social perception: Hayama as another revisable relation

Hachiman notices that he now exchanges small acknowledgments with Hayama's group because **he began looking at them**. He distinguishes recognizing a person from actually knowing them. His Hayama relationship likewise remains odd and indirect: both speak obliquely, interpret one another, and “finish off” conversations before they become conversations. Yet Hachiman recognizes that this is something both participate in.

This extends the V10 epistemic improvement: people become more knowable through attention without becoming fully decipherable.

## 4.6 The codependency shock produces valid self-observation and excessive self-condemnation

Haruno's `共依存` label lands because it connects to something Hachiman already knows about himself: he likes being needed. His narration correctly identifies the loop in which requested usefulness supplies self-worth.

But the immediate reaction is also characteristically Hachiman: he leaps from mechanism recognition into disgust—`醜く`, `浅ましい`, `おぞましい`, `下劣`, `醜悪`. The extremity of the language should not be mistaken for increased accuracy. Hachiman often converts moral uncertainty into self-punishment because self-condemnation feels controllable.

His most important qualification is textual and explicit:

> `雪ノ下が俺に依存しているかの真偽はさておくとしても`

He does **not** claim to have proven Yukino's dependence simply because the label fits aspects of his own behavior.

## 4.7 Decisive correction: mechanism is not motive

Hachiman later gives the volume's governing epistemic limit:

> `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。言い訳にはなっても、理由にはなってくれない。`

This is not a rejection of the concept. It is a restriction on what the concept can answer.

A relational mechanism can describe:

- how one person's need rewards another person's usefulness;
- how help can preserve incapacity;
- how being relied upon can stabilize self-worth;
- how roles can mutually reinforce themselves.

It cannot, by itself, establish:

- what Hachiman feels for Yukino;
- what Yukino wants from Hachiman;
- what Yui wants them to do;
- whether all reliance is pathological;
- whether separation is the only remedy;
- what anyone ought to choose.

This is one of the most important analytical guardrails in the whole V2 reread.

## 4.8 Final reason: embarrassingly ordinary promise

Once every role-based reason is stripped away—work, club duty, Komachi, procedural responsibility, even `共依存`—Hachiman is left with a reason he finds humiliating precisely because it lacks cleverness:

> `……いつか、助けるって約束したから`

The narration mocks the phrase as ordinary, clichéd, lacking `ロジック` or `リリック`. That embarrassment is diagnostic. Hachiman is more comfortable with mechanisms than with attachment.

The promise does not automatically authorize any intervention he later chooses. It does establish that the impulse to act cannot be reduced to being needed.

**Status:** STRENGTHEN — personal attachment has escaped the work/utility explanation, but the ethics of intervention remain open.

# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Yukinoshita Yukino — authorship before certainty

Volume 12 gives Yukino one of her largest prospective advances, but the advance must be described correctly. She does **not** suddenly acquire total self-knowledge. She explicitly says she still does not know the complete answer. What changes is her willingness to originate actions under uncertainty.

Her sequence is:

1. admit she does not know the total relational answer;
2. recover an old personal desire that predates the Service Club;
3. identify a family pattern in which Haruno was constrained and Yukino was “free” without knowing how to use freedom;
4. admit she avoided testing the desire because a determinate answer frightened her;
5. decide to speak to her mother herself;
6. request that Hachiman and Yui witness rather than pre-solve the process;
7. use the prom as a project through which she can demonstrate competence and responsibility;
8. later refuse Hachiman's intervention when the project is threatened;
9. name her own conduct toward Hachiman and Yui as `依存`.

The word `依存` is important precisely because Yukino says it herself:

> `……わかってるの、依存してること。あなたにも由比ヶ浜さんにも、誰かに頼らないなんて言いながらいつも押し付けてきたの`

This should be taken seriously. It is first-person self-description grounded in behavior. But Yukino's formulation is not identical to Haruno's later `共依存`. Yukino is saying that she has displaced burdens and contradicted her self-image of not relying on people. She is not offering a clinical theory of the triad.

The danger is to take Yukino's self-critique and retroactively declare every prior act of reliance pathological. Volume 10.5 already established counterexamples: Yukino can ask for help with exit capacity, design contingency around another person's possible failure, and accept friendship without task substitution. The problem is therefore not `頼る` as such. It is reliance that becomes opaque, identity-bearing, or burden-displacing while the person insists she is independent.

Yukino's prom competence also matters. She is not helpless. The project plan is unusually strong; Iroha realizes how much work Yukino has silently absorbed. This is exactly why “dependency” cannot mean incapacity. Yukino can be extraordinarily capable while still using competence and other people relationally in unhealthy ways.

## 5.2 Yuigahama Yui — preservation becomes conscious self-implication

Yui's strongest Volume-12 material comes in the interludes, where the narration no longer filters her through Hachiman.

She already knows that Yukino's feelings matter. She recognizes the photograph as confirmation, not revelation. She also knows her own refusal to ask is self-protective. Her strategy is no longer naïvely “keep everyone happy”; it is conscious postponement:

> `認めないで、見逃して、見落として、見過ごして。`

The later interlude goes further. Yui refuses to cry in front of Hachiman because she knows pity activates his rescuer role:

> `あたしは、可哀想な子になんてならないんだ。だって、そしたらまた彼は助けてくれちゃうから。`

This is significant moral perception. Yui does not want Hachiman's protective reflex to substitute for his desire. Her devastating line is:

> `ヒーローじゃないのを知ってるから、ちゃんと傷つけて欲しかった。`

She wants the dignity of being someone whose romantic desire can receive an honest negative consequence rather than someone protected into indefinite hope.

Yui also turns `依存` inward:

> `彼女が彼に依存したみたいに、あたしは彼女に依存したの。`

and:

> `全部押し付けてきたのはあたしのほうだ。`

Again, this is first-person self-diagnosis, not omniscient classification. What Yui means is comparatively concrete: she let Yukino carry the burden of renunciation and used Yukino's restraint as permission not to state or resolve her own desire.

This confirms the V1 claim that Yui is neither passive loser nor pure sacrifice. By Volume 12 she understands that accommodation can itself place burdens on other people.

## 5.3 Hikigaya Hachiman — from usefulness to personal reason, but not yet clean intervention

Hachiman's most important movement is the progressive stripping away of respectable justifications.

- The Service Club no longer supplies work.
- Yukino explicitly asks him not to help with the prom.
- Haruno gives him a mechanism that could explain his need to be useful.
- Hiratsuka directly asks whether he still has a reason once Yukino has deliberately excluded him from the cancellation information.

Under those conditions Hachiman cannot honestly say “it's my job.” The remainder is personal attachment and a promise.

That is growth.

Yet the volume ends before the ethical question is solved. A promise to help does not make every form of help authorized. Hachiman has already learned in earlier volumes that good intention, superior prediction, and willingness to bear costs do not grant jurisdiction. Volume 12 therefore ends at a productive contradiction:

> **He has finally located a personal reason to act, but he has not yet shown that he can act without taking authorship away from the person he wants to help.**

That is the proper incoming question for Volume 13.

## 5.4 Yukinoshita Haruno — diagnostic force, family solidarity, and detached authorship

Haruno gains complexity rather than simply diagnostic authority.

Early in the volume she supports Yukino's authored choice even if it proves wrong. She offers to assist in the family negotiation. She expresses pride and sadness about Yukino changing. She also reveals her own inability to lose the observing self and simply become absorbed in experience.

Later, however, she presses a powerful interpretation onto the trio. She first describes them as three people who individually are ordinary but together fall into fixed roles, then asks Hachiman what the relationship should be called. His embarrassed answer—`三角関係`—makes her laugh. Her answer is:

> `共依存っていうのよ`

The staging matters. Haruno deliberately makes the word feel like revelation. Hachiman describes it as colder and more truth-like than any `本物`.

But Haruno is still a character with motives, history, style, and wounds. Her definition has evidence behind it. It is not a narrator-issued diagnosis.

Her own later claim that the codependency is now ending and Yukino will `独り立ち` should therefore remain **character assertion / hypothesis**. The novel itself does not stop there; Hachiman almost immediately rejects `共依存` as a sufficient reason not to act.

## 5.5 Isshiki Iroha — institutional ownership and self-correction

Iroha becomes essential because the prom is genuinely her project too. When Yukino asks what the prom is for and whom it serves, Iroha answers:

> `もちろん、わたしのためです！`

Hachiman cannot tell whether this is wholly candid or strategically performed, but Yukino accepts the answer because it is **owned**. Iroha wants to leave a precedent for future students and explicitly frames the present project as a move that can make later action possible.

She also catches herself becoming overdependent on Yukino:

> `ほんとに雪乃先輩に頼りきりになってそうです……`

This is a useful institutional parallel. Iroha does not respond by refusing all future help from Yukino. She recognizes a project-management risk and adjusts. That is closer to healthy reliance than the more absolute “I must do everything myself” interpretation that begins to surround Yukino.

## 5.6 Hikigaya Komachi — separation without relational annihilation

Komachi's thanks, examination, and eventual success form the volume's most stable developmental control. Hachiman can feel sadness at her independence, cry, worry, and still not interfere with the exam outcome. Later he imagines that they may not commute together even at the same school, and describes the emerging distance as appropriate.

Yet neither sibling treats independence as the end of mutual aid, belonging, or affection.

Komachi therefore quietly refutes a binary that the central trio will struggle with:

> **less dependence does not require less relationship.**

## 5.7 Hayama Hayato — change becomes visible even from outside the dyad

Hayama tells Hachiman that Yukino's present movement is better than the old stasis and admits that he had expected the old arrangement never to change. His perspective matters because he has known the Yukinoshita family longer than Hachiman.

But Hachiman again risks converting Hayama's approval into proof. “Hayama thinks it is better” is useful counterevidence against pure disaster, not final authority about Yukino's internal life.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Hachiman ↔ Yukino

**Incoming state:** post-Volume-11 answerability, explicit difference, Yukino-originated request.

**Volume-12 delta:**

- Hachiman directly solicits Yukino's account rather than supplying one.
- Yukino discloses family history and a preexisting vocational desire.
- she asks him to witness rather than solve;
- he agrees to respect her wish to act independently;
- he experiences non-participation as painful but interprets it as autonomy;
- when the prom is threatened, his intervention reflex returns;
- Yukino explicitly refuses his help: `あなたの力はもう借りないわ` and `お願い。私にやらせて`;
- he ultimately chooses to act again after locating a reason outside work and diagnosis.

The relationship therefore becomes more personal **and** more ethically difficult. Hachiman can no longer hide intervention behind club procedure. If he acts, he must own that he is acting because Yukino matters to him and then still answer the authorization problem.

## 6.2 Yukino ↔ Yui

The relationship is unusually intimate and unusually asymmetrical in knowledge.

Yui physically supports Yukino through the February-14 disclosure, hears her family story, helps her pack, and agrees to respect her attempt to act on her own. Yui also sets a boundary that becomes one of the healthiest formulations in the volume:

> if Yukino needs people, she must call; not as Service Club members but as friends.

This is **conditional non-intervention**, not abandonment.

Yet Yui knows more than she says. The hidden photograph confirms what she has long suspected. She deliberately leaves the knowledge unspoken because she fears Yukino will erase or deny the feeling and thereby destroy the present relationship.

By the closing interlude Yui admits that this silence used Yukino: Yukino carried the burden of withdrawal that Yui could not choose herself.

## 6.3 Hachiman ↔ Yui

Volume 12 substantially increases dyadic ordinary intimacy. Once the prom temporarily no longer needs their labor, Hachiman himself asks Yui to go somewhere. The outing begins under the pretext of buying Komachi a gift but becomes extended shared time.

At IKEA they discuss university, childhood dreams, living alone, and marriage. Yui says one childhood dream was `お嫁さん`. The domestic showroom is important because it offers a **possible-life image** without becoming a promise. Hachiman explicitly notices that every physical object is real and yet the staged room feels counterfeit:

> `どれも本物なのに紛い物。何がその差を分けるのだろう`

This should not be reduced to “Yui future = fake.” The showroom is fake because it has material form without lived history. The scene asks what converts a plausible arrangement into an inhabited life.

Yui and Hachiman also take a photograph together; Hachiman asks her to send it and locks the image on his phone. That is meaningful behavioral evidence of valuing the dyadic moment.

The final interlude then makes the cost explicit: Yui wants Hachiman beside her, not as the automatic hero who rescues the pitiable. She wants his choice even if it hurts.

## 6.4 The triad

Volume 12's most important triadic change is the **partial suspension of the Service Club as relational infrastructure**. Yukino deliberately shifts the prom outside club authority. Hachiman and Yui stop coming when there is no work. The club room is locked.

This is an experiment: what remains when “request” and “work” no longer provide an excuse for proximity?

The answer is not nothing. Hachiman and Yui continue relating; Yukino works with Iroha; all three remain mutually attentive. But the familiar triadic form stops automatically organizing them.

The novel therefore tests an old V1 thesis more precisely:

> The Service Club did not create the attachment, but it provided a procedural grammar that let attachment exist without being chosen directly.

Once that grammar is suspended, desire and avoidance become harder to disguise.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

Volume 12 contains several distinct requests. Keeping them separate is essential.

## 7.1 Yukino's first request: witness, not rescue

**Owner of problem:** Yukino.

**Declared need:** she wants to test a long-suppressed family/career desire and reach a conclusion she can accept.

**Authorization:** Hachiman and Yui are asked to `見届ける`.

**Method:** non-substitutive presence.

**Agency effect:** high. Yukino retains definition and execution.

This is one of the cleanest requests in the series.

## 7.2 Haruno's assistance

Haruno offers family-specific strategic support because she knows their mother and the household system. Yukino does not reject assistance in principle.

This is important counterevidence to “Yukino's growth = needing nobody.”

## 7.3 Prom ownership: Iroha supplies an independently owned institutional reason

When Yukino asks Iroha `何のために、誰のためにやるの？`, Iroha answers `わたしのため`. This matters because Yukino is trying to avoid repeating a structure in which she acts only because others assign her a role.

The prom can become Yukino's developmental vehicle without being invented solely for Yukino. Iroha has a project she owns, which allows Yukino to take responsibility for something **outside herself** while still testing her competence.

## 7.4 Yukino's second request: let me do this

When the project is threatened, Yukino says:

> `あなたの力はもう借りないわ`
>
> `お願い。私にやらせて`

This is explicit refusal/authorization boundary. Hachiman's earlier promise to witness does not automatically override it.

Her reasoning is psychologically loaded—she believes she will become worse if she continues depending on him and Yui—but the boundary itself is clear.

## 7.5 Yui's conditional friendship model

Yui says she will not interfere but asks Yukino to promise two things: do not overwork, and call if people are genuinely needed, because they are friends even outside the Service Club.

This is arguably the strongest autonomy-supportive helping model in Volume 12:

> **respect the person's attempt; preserve an explicit channel for requested assistance; do not make help contingent on institutional role.**

## 7.6 Hachiman's final intervention problem

After the school shifts to cancellation, Hiratsuka tells Hachiman that Yukino specifically chose not to inform him and asks whether he still has a reason to help.

That question prevents easy moralization. Hachiman cannot honestly claim authorization from Yukino.

His final answer is the old promise:

> `……いつか、助けるって約束したから`

This establishes motive, not jurisdiction.

Therefore the Volume-12 ethics ledger must end **OPEN**:

- Hachiman has a genuine personal reason.
- Yukino has given an explicit refusal.
- circumstances have materially changed because the project is now being externally blocked.
- Yukino's initial request did ask him to witness the end, not abandon her regardless of what happens.
- the form of intervention has not yet been shown.

Volume 13 must determine whether he can act in a way that reopens options for Yukino without simply taking the project back from her.

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 The Yukinoshita family: freedom can be another form of non-recognition

Volume 12 significantly revises any simple “controlling mother / liberated younger daughter” model.

Haruno was explicitly bound by succession expectations. Yukino was told she could be free. But Yukino experiences that freedom as a lack of serious recognition: when she tentatively expressed interest in her father's work, she was told not to worry about it because Haruno's role was already fixed.

The result is paradoxical:

> **Haruno was over-authored; Yukino was under-recognized as an author.**

Yukino then copied Haruno because imitation supplied structure where nominal freedom did not.

## 8.2 The prom as institutional conflict, not only romantic proxy

The prom conflict concerns real institutional questions:

- student self-government;
- parental authority over minors;
- reputational risk;
- online circulation of images;
- minority objection;
- risk management;
- school accountability;
- tradition versus innovation;
- whose preferences count when an event serves students, parents, teachers, alumni, and community.

The mother does not simply say “I dislike this.” She builds a governance case: concerns about drinking, sexual conduct, social media, student capacity to absorb risk, and the obligations of representatives to hear quieter voices.

Some of those concerns are substantively legitimate. The problem is partly procedural. The school had given preliminary approval, students reasonably invested effort, and the parent's intervention arrives through social influence and informal access rather than a transparent decision process.

Hachiman notices that the mother is treated as a `賓客`; her local status changes institutional gravity. Haruno later says her mother is partly acting because others asked her to, meaning even the powerful adult is carrying role obligations.

This mirrors the series' broader concern with socially prewritten answers: institutions can constrain authorship just as relationships can.

## 8.3 Minority voice and veto

Yukino's mother correctly insists that minority criticism cannot simply be ignored. Yet the institutional question is whether “someone objects” should operate as a de facto veto.

The novel does not solve this abstractly. Hiratsuka argues for continued negotiation and plan correction rather than instant cancellation. This is consistent with the series' emerging relational ethic: **an objection must be answerable, not automatically sovereign**.

## 8.4 Student leadership: Iroha as future-bearing institutional subject

Iroha's best argument is that present students are also future graduates and therefore possess standing to propose new traditions. She thinks beyond the immediate event: even failure can establish precedent, procedures, and imagination for later councils.

The prom is therefore also about whether institutions permit young participants to develop authorship by attempting something consequential.

## 8.5 Adult authority: Hiratsuka chooses not to become indispensable

Hiratsuka's possible transfer is disclosed in the same movement. She says that if students want the prom to survive beyond one year, adults cannot simply rescue it for them.

This is an institutional version of the same helping problem:

> **responsible support sometimes means building capacity that survives the helper's departure.**

The distinction is critical. That is not the same as refusing all help. Hiratsuka still creates time, supplies information, negotiates procedure, and later helps Hachiman find room to act.

# 9. Authenticity / `本物` ledger

Volume 12 has six governing `本物` occurrences and two `偽物` occurrences in the main literary sequence. Their meanings are not uniform.

## 9.1 Opening: a cruel `本物` is not desirable merely because it is true

`冷たくて残酷な、悲しいだけの本物なんて、欲しくはない` directly rejects suffering-as-authenticity.

**Ledger:** STRENGTHEN — genuineness cannot be defined by pain.

## 9.2 Rumi memory: known falsity can still contain care

Hachiman recalls someone reaching out despite knowing the form was `偽物`. The memory carried a faint hope precisely because the act was not invalidated by imperfect form.

**Ledger:** PRESERVE — performance/constructed form remains ethically separable from motive and effect.

## 9.3 Yukino's persistent wish

Hachiman says a desire that survives time, neglect, and attempts to forget may be called `本物`; what collapses under those pressures may be `偽物`.

**Evidence class:** Hachiman inference.

**Ledger:** OPEN. Persistence is evidence of significance, not sufficient proof of wisdom or moral priority.

## 9.4 Haruno's “real self” problem

Hachiman wonders whether a rare soft expression is `本物の雪ノ下陽乃`, then immediately concludes he still cannot know what Haruno's truth is because she layers performance and self-awareness.

**Ledger:** STRENGTHEN — there may be no simple hidden authentic Haruno beneath every role.

## 9.5 Yui: `本物なんて、ほしくなかった`

Yui's line is not a philosophical rejection of authentic relation. It is a frightened rejection of a form of “truth” that seems to require forced disclosure and relational loss.

**Ledger:** REVISE — Yui is not anti-genuine; she is resisting an authenticity procedure that threatens to make one answer annihilate the shared world.

## 9.6 IKEA: real objects, counterfeit home

`どれも本物なのに紛い物` is a nontechnical use with strong thematic resonance. The showroom has real furniture, bed, utensils, and kitchen but lacks the history and commitments that make a home inhabited.

**Ledger:** STRENGTHEN — authenticity cannot be reduced to correct external components.

## 9.7 `共依存` sounds more true than `本物`

Hachiman says Haruno's diagnostic word sounds colder and more truth-like than any `本物`. The sentence records the **psychological force of explanation**, not its complete authority. The risk is that an elegant diagnosis feels truer than messy lived experience because it offers a compact causal story.

**Ledger:** OPEN / adversarial caution.

### Current prospective `本物` position after V12

By the end of Volume 12, `本物` has been narrowed away from:

- telepathic understanding;
- motive purity;
- absence of performance;
- pleasantness;
- suffering;
- mere persistence;
- possession of the correct psychological label.

The strongest surviving candidate is procedural:

> **a relation in which desire can be stated without another person's subjecthood being erased, and in which explanatory labels do not predetermine the answer.**

That remains a candidate, not a final definition.

# 10. Dependency / autonomy ledger

This is the first volume where the dependency ledger moves from indirect behavior to explicit vocabulary.

## 10.1 Yukino's `依存`

Yukino says she is dependent on both Hachiman and Yui and has pushed things onto them while claiming not to rely on people.

**Classification:** first-person self-description + behaviorally supported partial diagnosis.

**Do not overextend to:** every instance of asking, trust, mutual aid, or friendship.

## 10.2 Iroha's `頼りきり`

Iroha recognizes project over-reliance on Yukino after noticing how many functions Yukino has absorbed.

**Classification:** operational dependency / single-point-of-failure risk.

This is a useful non-romantic control. The correction is not “never rely on Yukino again”; it is distribute responsibility and remain attentive.

## 10.3 Haruno's `共依存`

Haruno defines the Hachiman/Yukino relationship as codependent and emphasizes the helper-side reinforcement: Hachiman receives self-worth and satisfaction from being needed.

**Classification:** high-value character assertion with explanatory fit.

Haruno's evidence is not trivial. Across earlier volumes Hachiman repeatedly sought controllable usefulness, accepted personal cost, and felt disturbed when no longer needed. Volume 12 itself shows the ache when Yukino excludes him from the prom.

Yet Haruno's label is not final narrator authority.

## 10.4 Hachiman's qualified uptake

Hachiman says:

> `俺と雪ノ下の関係性は共依存と言えば共依存。`

but immediately adds:

> `雪ノ下が俺に依存しているかの真偽はさておくとしても`

That qualification must remain visible. Hachiman accepts that the model fits aspects of **his** behavior before claiming knowledge of Yukino's internal state.

## 10.5 Haruno's cure hypothesis: `独り立ち`

Haruno says the codependency is ending and Yukino will become independent and a little more adult.

**Classification:** character assertion / proposed cure narrative.

The volume itself provides counterpressure. Komachi demonstrates independence-with-continuing-relation. Yui offers non-interference plus an open channel for requested assistance. Hiratsuka supports without making herself the permanent solution.

Therefore:

> **independence is not yet proven to require relational severance.**

## 10.6 Hachiman's decisive limit

`共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。`

This moves the ledger from label to mechanism. A dependency mechanism can be real while the feelings inside it remain love, friendship, guilt, fear, desire, pride, care, loneliness, or several at once.

## 10.7 Yui's `依存`

Yui says she depended on Yukino because she let Yukino do the rejecting/renouncing that Yui herself could not do.

**Classification:** first-person self-description of decision displacement.

Again, the important mechanism is not “Yui needs Yukino too much.” It is **Yui outsourced a choice to Yukino's restraint**.

### Prospective V12 dependency conclusion

Volume 12 strengthens the V1 claim but makes it more exact:

> **`共依存` is no longer merely a plausible later label. It is now explicit textual vocabulary with real explanatory force. But the novel itself refuses to make the diagnosis governing. The term identifies reinforcement structures around neededness, burden transfer, and self-worth; it does not determine feeling, moral value, or the unique remedy.**

# 11. Japanese language and voice

## 11.1 `依頼` changes grammatical ownership

Yukino's request grammar is deliberately modest:

> `私の依頼はひとつだけ`
>
> `見届けてもらいたい`

The benefactive/request form asks for a limited act directed toward Yukino's own process. It does not grammatically command Hachiman to solve the underlying problem.

Later:

> `お願い。私にやらせて`

is even more direct. The request object is **non-interference / permission to attempt**.

## 11.2 `諦めたい` is volitional, not merely passive resignation

Yukino does not say simply `諦める`. The desiderative `諦めたい` makes even relinquishment an authored desire. She wants the ability to stop only after she has verified the answer herself.

## 11.3 `まだわからない` persists as legitimate grammar

Yukino's Volume-10.5 `まだわからない` continues into Volume 12's broader uncertainty. The series increasingly allows `わからない` to mean “not yet possessed” rather than “therefore someone else may decide.”

## 11.4 `自分で` becomes the volume's autonomy marker

Yukino repeatedly uses `自分で`, `自分の意志`, and related phrases. The lexical insistence reflects a character actively trying to build an authorship vocabulary that she feels she lacks.

The risk is that `自分で` slides from **self-authored** into **alone**. Yui's friendship condition is the linguistic counterweight: help can be called when needed without undoing authorship.

## 11.5 Haruno's diagnostic register

Haruno's `共依存っていうのよ` is short, intimate, almost pedagogical, and delivered as a whispered reveal. She makes the word perform certainty.

This contrasts sharply with Hachiman's later qualification-heavy prose. The narrative form itself teaches caution: compact labels feel authoritative; lived motives generate hedges.

## 11.6 `仕組み` versus `気持ち`

The sentence `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない` is lexically exact.

- `仕組み`: arrangement, mechanism, structure, system.
- `気持ち`: feeling, affective orientation, subjective motive.

The contrast is not “codependency is false.” It is “structural explanation and felt reason are different analytical levels.”

## 11.7 Yui's first-person prose

The interludes strip away Hachiman's observational filter. Yui's repetitions—`忘れて`, `認めないで`, `見逃して`, `見落として`, `見過ごして`—sound like self-instruction. Her simple final clauses (`欲しくなかった`, `傍にいて欲しかった`, `傷つけて欲しかった`) are less mediated than Hachiman's recursive abstractions.

That voice difference is evidence. Yui understands much of the situation not through theoretical vocabulary but through concrete desired action and feared consequence.

## 11.8 Hachiman's embarrassment around simple language

Hachiman calls the promise reason ordinary, clichéd, lacking logic and lyric. His elaborate narration is almost a defense against a sentence that cannot be made intellectually impressive.

This is a recurring stylistic principle of late Oregairu: **the closer Hachiman gets to desire, the more suspicious he becomes of plain language precisely because plain language offers fewer hiding places.**

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertext

Volume 12 remains heavily comic even while moving into explicit dependency language. The comedy is functional in several ways.

1. **Hachiman's pop-culture digressions** continue to interrupt affective exposure. The more intimate the setting—Haruno's possible boyfriend, IKEA domestic space, Yui proximity—the more likely he is to launch into exaggerated jokes.
2. **Iroha's genre fluency** lets her turn institutional work into romantic-comedy performance without making the work unserious.
3. **Zaimokuza** provides a future-choice parallel in comic register: he speaks grandly of the last high-school attempt at writing, then admits that different life stages may produce different writing. This supports a nonterminal model of choice: a present path need not annihilate every alternate self.
4. **The prom itself** imports a foreign coming-of-age ritual into a Japanese public-high-school governance structure. The mismatch is not merely exotic flavor; it creates the institutional dispute over localization, propriety, and who gets to define “high-school-like” behavior.
5. **Hachiman's “hero” language** becomes less purely parodic by the final Yui interlude. Yui explicitly says she does not want the hero role to protect her from being hurt. That transforms a long-running protagonist joke into a relational critique of rescue scripts.

The volume therefore continues a pattern established earlier: comedy can camouflage vulnerability, but no single gag should automatically be elevated into theme. It matters when the joke repeats at a structurally charged point or is later answered by behavior.

# 13. Illustration and paratext analysis

The fixed-page witness contains several full-page or near-full-page illustrations whose placement materially shapes emphasis. They are treated as paratextual evidence only.

## 13.1 The February-14 bench: Yui and Yukino share a frame without Hachiman

The early monochrome illustration around physical PDF p.18 places Yukino and Yui in the nighttime bench setting. The composition fragments bodies and emphasizes lateral separation within a shared environment rather than staging Hachiman as the visual center.

This is appropriate to Chapter 1's prose. Yukino's request is not primarily a dyadic confession to Hachiman. Yui is physically and ethically present: she takes Yukino's hand, asks whether the proposed answer is really Yukino's answer, and is herself implicated in whatever follows.

The image therefore supports the reading of Volume 12 as **triadic authorship conflict**, not a simple Hachiman-Yukino revelation scene.

## 13.2 Yui/Hachiman dyadic emphasis around the ordinary-life movement

The full-page illustration around p.190 juxtaposes a relaxed Yui with Hachiman. Its placement in the section where the old club routine is loosening gives visual weight to the growing dyadic ordinary-life channel.

The image should not be used as proof of romantic outcome. It is evidence of editorial emphasis: while Yukino attempts autonomous project work elsewhere, the book deliberately gives Yui and Hachiman visual space together.

## 13.3 Upside-down Yukino with project documents

The illustration around p.264 presents Yukino upside down relative to the page while holding a document and pen. Whatever the immediate gag or scene-level orientation, the visual effect is striking: the most administratively competent character in the volume is literally inverted.

A cautious paratextual reading is that the image emphasizes the tension between visible competence and unstable relational/self-definitional orientation. It does **not** prove psychological diagnosis and should not be treated as a visual symbol of `共依存` by itself.

## 13.4 Final Yui/Hachiman split image

The late illustration around p.357 is one of the volume's strongest editorial choices. Yui's tearful close-up dominates one side; Hachiman is shown physically moving away on the other. The prose interlude makes the emotional logic explicit: Yui hides her tears because visible suffering would reactivate Hachiman's hero role and prevent him from leaving.

The image therefore reinforces not “Yui is abandoned” as a final verdict but the specific Volume-12 problem:

> **Yui wants to be chosen or refused as a person, not rescued as a victim.**

## 13.5 Visual caution

The illustrations privilege emotionally legible moments and heroine framing because they are light-novel paratext as well as narrative art. They may sharpen attention to a scene but cannot settle:

- which relationship is `本物`;
- whether `共依存` is objectively correct;
- the final romantic outcome;
- whether tears imply moral victimhood;
- whether visual separation equals necessary ethical separation.

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial test

## 14.1 Reading A: Haruno finally gives the correct diagnosis — the trio is codependent

**Support:**

- Yukino herself says she is dependent on Hachiman and Yui.
- Hachiman recognizes that being needed gives him self-worth.
- Yui later says she depended on Yukino to carry a choice she would not make.
- the trio have repeatedly used fixed roles—solver, strong competent girl, atmosphere-preserver—to maintain the relation.

**Counterevidence:**

- Haruno is a character, not narrator authority.
- Hachiman explicitly qualifies uncertainty about Yukino's actual dependence.
- `共依存` describes a mechanism, not feeling or moral conclusion.
- earlier volumes contain healthy forms of reliance and shared work.
- Iroha and Komachi demonstrate that dependence/autonomy exist on a spectrum rather than a binary.

**Judgment:** **STRENGTHEN the mechanism; reject the totalizing diagnosis.** “Codependency” is now textually legitimate terminology, but it cannot be used as a master key that explains every attachment in the series.

## 14.2 Reading B: Yukino's desire to do the prom alone is straightforward healthy independence

**Support:**

- Yukino explicitly owns the goal.
- she has a history of having choices defined by family structure.
- she wants evidence that she can complete something on her own.
- the prom allows her to confront her mother with demonstrated competence.
- she directly refuses Hachiman's help.

**Counterevidence:**

- her language is partly self-punitive: `どんどんダメになる`.
- she moves from “I must author this” toward “I must not borrow your power,” which are not identical claims.
- Yui's friendship model allows autonomy plus requested help.
- Komachi's independence does not require severing care.
- Iroha recognizes over-reliance without concluding that reliance itself must end.

**Judgment:** **PRESERVE authorship, keep isolation-as-cure OPEN.** Yukino is right to demand control over her own experiment; the novel has not proven that self-authorship requires doing everything alone.

## 14.3 Reading C: Hachiman's final promise reason proves romantic love for Yukino

**Support:**

- all work-based reasons have been stripped away.
- Hachiman's agitation is intensely personal.
- he cannot tolerate the externally imposed end of Yukino's attempt.
- the old promise has unusual emotional force.

**Counterevidence:**

- Hachiman has made serious personal commitments to non-romantic people.
- “I promised to help” is attachment evidence, not an explicit romantic declaration.
- his rescuer/neededness schema is still active.
- Volume 12 has not yet shown what he will actually choose when romantic claims become direct.

**Judgment:** **OPEN.** Strong evidence that Yukino matters beyond work; insufficient prospectively to collapse that attachment into a fully articulated romantic choice.

## 14.4 Reading D: Yui is sacrificing herself for Yukino and Hachiman

**Support:**

- she hides knowledge of the photograph;
- she does not stop Hachiman when he goes to help;
- she suppresses tears;
- she recognizes Yukino's feelings.

**Counterevidence:**

- Yui explicitly says she wanted Hachiman beside her;
- she wants honest hurt rather than protective ambiguity;
- she admits she blamed Yukino and used Yukino's restraint;
- her silence protects her own hope as well as Yukino.

**Judgment:** **REJECT pure-sacrifice reading.** Yui's behavior combines care, fear, desire, avoidance, and self-implication.

## 14.5 Reading E: the prom is merely a metaphorical proxy for the romantic triangle

**Support:**

- Hachiman's intervention status parallels his relational status with Yukino;
- the project becomes a test of whether he can let her act without him;
- external obstruction forces him to decide whether to re-enter.

**Counterevidence:**

- Iroha independently wants the prom;
- student-government precedent matters;
- school/parent governance questions are real;
- substantial practical labor occurs outside the romantic triangle;
- Yukino's family/career conflict predates the trio.

**Judgment:** **REVISE.** The prom is a genuine institutional project that also becomes a relational stress test. Treating it as “only a metaphor” erases Iroha and the institutional layer.

## 14.6 Reading F: Yukino's mother is simply a villainous controller

**Support:**

- Yukino describes a history of maternal decision-making;
- the mother's soft rhetoric channels discussion toward a predetermined conclusion;
- her local influence gives her privileged institutional access;
- Yukino visibly contracts under her attention.

**Counterevidence:**

- the mother raises legitimate concerns about minors, online image circulation, risk, and representative responsibility;
- she is partly responding to requests from other parents/community actors;
- the school did reserve final approval;
- some participating students genuinely did not anticipate the exposure problem.

**Judgment:** **PRESERVE structural control critique without flattening the policy dispute.** Her power is most troubling where authority, social influence, and predetermined conclusion reduce answerability—not because every concern she states is irrational.

## 14.7 Reading G: if a relationship is codependent, separation is the morally correct answer

**Support:**

- Haruno proposes independence as the end of the codependent loop;
- Yukino fears continued reliance will make her worse;
- Hachiman's neededness can reward dependence.

**Counterevidence:**

- Hachiman explicitly distinguishes mechanism from feeling and reason;
- Komachi models differentiation without severance;
- Yui offers help-on-request as a third option;
- Hiratsuka models support that creates capacity without becoming permanent authorship;
- Iroha's over-reliance problem is addressed by role redistribution, not abandonment.

**Judgment:** **REJECT as a governing conclusion at V12.** Separation is one possible intervention against a reinforcement loop, not a moral theorem.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

Full-series hindsight makes Volume 12 dangerous to read prospectively because many later developments tempt the reader to turn every scene into foreordained romantic sorting. V2 resists that move.

Several later recontextualizations are legitimate:

1. **The prom becomes part of a longer sequence of relational choice.** That does not mean Yukino invented it as a disguised romance mechanism. In Volume 12 it is first a real Iroha project and a Yukino authorship test.
2. **Later dependency arguments may sharpen the mechanisms visible here.** They do not retroactively turn every earlier reliance into pathology.
3. **Later romantic resolution may clarify which desires Hachiman was unable to name.** It does not make `いつか、助けるって約束したから` an explicit confession at this publication boundary.
4. **Later judgments about separation and interdependence can test Haruno's cure theory.** They do not make her Volume-12 statement omniscient in advance.
5. **Yui's later choices can deepen the closing interlude.** They must not erase that Volume 12 Yui still actively wants Hachiman, wants the shared relation, and does not experience herself as simply stepping aside.

The strongest retrospective gain is methodological rather than outcome-based:

> Volume 12 is where the series itself gives us the rule that prevents later concepts from flattening earlier material: **`共依存` may describe a mechanism; it does not possess the feelings inside that mechanism.**

That sentence should govern the entire full-series dependency synthesis.

> **Volume 12 does not reveal that the trio's feelings were “really codependency.” It reveals that their feelings have been operating through a codependency-like reinforcement mechanism, then forces the characters to ask what remains when that mechanism no longer counts as an answer.**

# 18. Cumulative delta

## 18.1 Frozen Volume 12 thesis

> **Volume 12 names the mechanism the trio feared but refuses to let the mechanism author the choice. Yukino's request is not rescue but witness to a self-authored test; Yui recognizes that her accommodation has displaced choice onto Yukino; Hachiman recognizes that being needed has fed his sense of value. Haruno's `共依存` therefore lands because it identifies a real reinforcement loop, but Hachiman's later distinction—`共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない`—prevents the diagnosis from deciding what anyone feels or what the remedy must be. When Yukino refuses his help and the prom is externally threatened, Hachiman finally loses the procedural excuses that once made intervention easy. What remains is a promise and personal attachment. Volume 12 ends not by resolving dependency, but by making the next ethical test unavoidable: can he act on that reason without taking Yukino's authorship away from her?**

Compactly:

> **Diagnosis can explain why people remain entangled; it cannot choose for them.**

This is the largest post-Checkpoint-D change. The vocabulary of dependency has finally become explicit primary-text evidence, but the novel simultaneously limits what that vocabulary is allowed to prove.

## 18.2 Hachiman epistemic and narratorial delta

**STRENGTHEN:** Hachiman can now identify a desire for `解消`—for the problem to disappear—rather than pretending he merely sought a correct answer. This is unusually strong self-observation because it admits that apparent neutrality concealed avoidance.

**STRENGTHEN:** he asks Yukino to tell her own story and allows her answer to remain provisional. Her uncertainty no longer automatically licenses him to supply a hidden motive.

**PRESERVE WITH CAUTION:** his inference that a wish surviving over time may approach `本物` is meaningful but remains his inference, not a settled definition.

**REVISE:** his equation of distance with `自立` is at best partial. Komachi provides a healthier control case in which differentiation and continuing relation coexist.

**STRENGTHEN:** Haruno's `共依存` accusation produces a valid helper-side self-recognition: being needed can reward Hachiman and reinforce intervention. But his immediate movement into global self-loathing exceeds the evidence.

**MAJOR STRENGTHEN:** `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない` is now a governing epistemic limit. Hachiman explicitly separates mechanism from motive and diagnosis from reason.

**OPEN:** the promise `いつか、助ける` supplies a personal reason that survives the collapse of club-duty and usefulness explanations. Whether acting on that reason respects Yukino's authorship remains unresolved at the Volume-12 boundary.

## 18.3 Yukinoshita Yukino state delta

Yukino's central movement is **authorship before certainty**.

She does not discover one total answer. She:

- recovers a long-standing future desire;
- identifies a family structure in which Haruno was constrained while Yukino was nominally free but insufficiently recognized;
- admits that fear of a determinate answer made her avoid testing the desire;
- decides to speak to her mother herself;
- asks Hachiman and Yui to witness the end of that attempt;
- uses the prom as a vehicle through which to demonstrate competence and responsibility;
- later asks Hachiman not to take over when the project is threatened;
- explicitly names her own conduct toward Hachiman and Yui as `依存`.

Her self-description must be taken seriously. It is nevertheless not identical to Haruno's broader triadic diagnosis. Yukino says she has relied on and pushed things onto them; Haruno supplies the technical-sounding `共依存` label and a proposed cure through independence. Those are separate evidentiary acts.

The strongest prospective Yukino principle after Volume 12 is:

> **Needing help and authoring one's life are not opposites; the ethical issue is whether another person becomes the author in one's place.**

## 18.4 Yuigahama Yui state delta

Volume 12 makes Yui's self-awareness substantially harder to describe as mere accommodation.

She already knows enough of the Hachiman-Yukino relation to recognize the hidden photograph's implication. She deliberately does not ask because she fears that forcing an answer will collapse the shared world she wants to preserve.

That avoidance is self-serving as well as caring, and Yui knows it.

Her closing interlude then sharpens the point dramatically:

- she knows visible tears would activate Hachiman's rescue reflex;
- she refuses to become the pitiable girl whose suffering automatically changes his action;
- she says she has already been helped;
- she wants him beside her rather than functioning as her hero;
- she wants to be hurt honestly rather than protected through indefinite ambiguity;
- she says she depended on Yukino by pushing the decisive choice onto her.

This is not the voice of the least self-aware participant. It is one of the clearest self-indictments in the volume.

Her `依存` statement is best read prospectively as **decision displacement**: she allowed Yukino to bear the choice that Yui herself feared making. It does not establish that every form of Yui-Yukino reliance is pathological.

## 18.5 Haruno state and authority delta

Haruno becomes more—not less—complex in Volume 12.

Before supplying the codependency diagnosis, she:

- recognizes Yukino's answer as an answer;
- offers cooperation;
- prepares for likely maternal intervention;
- tells Hachiman to support Yukino whether her answer proves right or wrong;
- reveals something of her own detached relation to adulthood and family expectation;
- acknowledges that Yukino is an ordinary girl struggling with who she is.

Her later `共依存` formulation is therefore high-value character evidence, not reducible to cruelty. But it is not narratorial omniscience.

Her propositions must remain separated:

1. the trio's behavior can be described through a codependent reinforcement mechanism;
2. being needed may reward Hachiman;
3. the relation is not adequately described by simple trust;
4. Yukino's independence will end the mechanism;
5. adulthood requires giving up many things.

The first three receive substantial textual support. The fourth and fifth remain Haruno's cure theory and adult philosophy, not settled laws of the novel.

## 18.6 Relationship-state delta

### Hachiman ↔ Yukino

The relation becomes more explicitly personal and more ethically difficult at the same time. Yukino asks him to witness her attempt, later asks him not to intervene, and tries to prove that she can act without borrowing his power. Hachiman's reason for moving after the prom cancellation is no longer merely work or club duty; it is the promise he made to her.

The next problem is therefore not whether he cares. It is whether care plus promise can authorize action after a request for non-intervention.

### Yukino ↔ Yui

Yui possesses increasingly asymmetric knowledge of Yukino's feelings and hidden objects. She also understands that Yukino is bearing choices Yui herself has avoided. The friendship remains intimate, but intimacy no longer means symmetric disclosure.

### Hachiman ↔ Yui

The ordinary-life channel strengthens materially: a two-person photograph, future conversation, domestic-model-space interaction, and the handmade gift all produce genuine dyadic intimacy. The closing interlude simultaneously exposes the cost of Hachiman's hero role to Yui: rescue can protect her from pain while denying her the dignity of being chosen or refused directly.

### The triad

The Service Club is no longer the operative procedure that automatically structures intervention. The trio's relations continue, but their familiar institutional pretext is weakening just as individual desires become harder to hide.

## 18.7 Request/intervention ethics delta

Volume 12 produces four distinct request states that must not be collapsed:

1. **Yukino's first request:** witness the end of her self-authored attempt.
2. **Iroha's prom project:** an institutionally owned project with Iroha's independent reason for pursuing it.
3. **Yukino's second request:** do not take over; let her complete the proof herself.
4. **Hachiman's later action:** motivated by promise and attachment after an external cancellation threatens the prom.

The first three clarify authorization. The fourth deliberately reopens the problem.

The core unresolved question entering Volume 13 is:

> **Can a helper intervene for a personal reason after the target has asked for non-intervention without simply restoring the old logic of unilateral rescue?**

The answer cannot be derived from motive purity. It must be tested through method, options created or removed, costs assigned, Yukino's response, and whether her authorship expands or contracts.

## 18.8 Authenticity / `本物` delta

Volume 12 establishes several negative constraints:

- pain does not authenticate a relationship merely because pain is honest;
- known performance or temporary false form can still carry care;
- long persistence may be evidence of importance without becoming proof of final truth;
- a staged home can contain real objects while lacking the lived history that makes it a home;
- `共依存` can sound colder and more truth-like than `本物` without becoming a final answer.

Most importantly, the opening interlude rejects a `本物` that is only cold, cruel, and sad. The work is no longer asking merely whether the trio can uncover the truth. It is asking what kind of truth can be lived responsibly.

## 18.9 Dependency / autonomy delta

This is the first volume where dependency vocabulary becomes explicit enough to require a new ledger state.

**Textual facts:**

- Yukino says she is `依存`ing on Hachiman and Yui.
- Haruno calls the triadic mechanism `共依存`.
- Hachiman recognizes a helper-side reward in being needed.
- Hachiman qualifiedly accepts that his and Yukino's relation can be described as codependent while explicitly bracketing whether Yukino's dependence on him is established.
- Haruno predicts that Yukino's independence will end the mechanism.
- Hachiman later says codependency is a `仕組み`, not a `気持ち`, and cannot serve as the reason for his choice.
- Yui says she depended on Yukino by pushing the choice onto her.

**Prospective conclusion:**

> **Volume 12 confirms dependency mechanisms without establishing a theorem that independence requires separation.**

The strongest healthy controls remain Komachi's differentiation, Iroha's self-correction around over-reliance, Yui's conditional offer of friendship/help, and Hiratsuka's refusal to become indispensable.

## 18.10 Social-system delta

The prom becomes an actual governance problem rather than merely a romance metaphor.

The volume places in conflict:

- student ownership;
- preliminary administrative approval;
- parental minority objection;
- sexual/alcohol/safety concerns;
- online-publicity concerns;
- local stakeholder roles;
- precedent for future students;
- the distinction between hearing minority voices and granting veto power;
- adult support versus adult substitution.

The Yukinoshita family simultaneously becomes a model of a different governance problem: nominal freedom can fail to produce authorship when a child is not sufficiently recognized as someone whose desire needs to be heard and tested.

## 18.11 Japanese-language / voice delta

Several lexical distinctions are now longitudinally authoritative:

- `依頼` — locates ownership of a request.
- `自分で` — the volume's recurring authorship marker.
- `諦めたい` — an active wish to reach a conclusion one can accept, not merely passive defeat.
- `依存` / `共依存` — first explicit dependency vocabulary at governing scale.
- `仕組み` versus `気持ち` — mechanism versus feeling/motive.
- `言葉` — overwhelmingly more frequent than `本物`, reflecting the volume's insistence that reasons must be verbalized even when language is incomplete.

Yui's first-person closing prose is especially important because it removes Hachiman's mediation from her self-analysis.

## 18.12 Carry-forward questions for Volume 13

Volume 13 should test, prospectively and without importing later outcomes:

1. What method does Hachiman choose after Yukino has explicitly asked him not to take over?
2. Does his intervention expand Yukino's options, create a counter-option, or replace her authorship?
3. How does Yukino herself interpret his intervention: help, interference, rivalry, provocation, or something else?
4. Does the text continue equating `自立` with exclusion, or does it develop a model of autonomous interdependence?
5. Can Yui state and act on her own desire without routing the decisive choice through Yukino?
6. How is the prom conflict actually resolved, and who bears the costs of the method chosen?
7. Does `共依存` become operationally explanatory, or is it used as another story the characters tell themselves?
8. Can Hachiman articulate a reason for involvement beyond usefulness, being needed, and the old promise?
9. Does Haruno's proposed cure survive contact with the characters' actual behavior?
10. What new evidence would justify, revise, or reject the provisional distinction between **dependency as mechanism** and **attachment as motive**?

Checkpoint E should remain deferred until the prom/final-choice movement has accumulated enough evidence for a genuine prospective freeze.
