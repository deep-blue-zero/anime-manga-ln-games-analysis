# OREGAIRU V2 — Volume 11 Deep Reading
## 『やはり俺の青春ラブコメはまちがっている。11』

### Governing thesis

**Volume 11 reveals that care can become a technology of postponement. The Service Club's ordinary happiness is real: the members laugh, work, share food, notice absences, trust one another in limited ways, and deliberately create opportunities for other people to act. The crisis arises not because those practices are fake but because each member is tempted to use a genuinely valuable relationship to delay the consequences of increasingly incompatible desires. Yui finally turns preservation into an explicit proposal; Hachiman refuses the proposal because its desired conclusion would decide too much in advance; Yukino then rejects both of them deciding her feelings and becomes the author of a request of her own. The movement is therefore not from falsehood to truth but from protective preservation toward answerable desire.**

A compact formulation is:

> **Kindness becomes dangerous when it prevents another person's answer from having consequences.**

A second, equally important formulation is:

> **The opposite of coercion is not isolation. It is help whose recipient can still speak, refuse, revise, and originate the request.**

Volume 11 does **not** prospectively establish `共依存`. The words `共依存` and `依存` do not occur as governing concepts in the main nine chapters. The volume instead builds a pre-diagnostic field around `信頼`, `任せる`, `優しい`, `強い`, `わからない`, `答え`, `言葉`, `願い`, `委ねる`, and the problem of how other people define the self. Later terminology may recontextualize this material, but it must not be projected backward into the prospective reading.

## 1.2 Nine-chapter architecture

The main novel contains nine numbered chapters:

1. `冬は、その始まりを知るとき既に過ぎ去っている。`
2. `こうして、女だらけの戦いが始まる（男もいるよ）。`
3. `思いがけず、一色いろはの不在がもたらすものは。`
4. `そうして、男たちの一喜一憂が始まる（女もいるよ）。`
5. `ふと、平塚静は現在進行形と過去形について説く。`
6. `未だ、彼の求める本物には手が届かず、本物はまちがい続ける。`
7. `どうしようもなく、雪ノ下雪乃の瞳は澄んでいる。`
8. `どこまでも、由比ヶ浜結衣の眼差しは優しく暖かい。`
9. `春は、降り積もる雪の下にて結われ、芽吹き始める。`

The titles themselves form part of the argument. Winter is already passing before anyone recognizes its beginning; the central chapter says that reaching for `本物` has not stopped the characters from continuing to make mistakes; spring begins under accumulated snow rather than after the snow has simply vanished.

## 1.3 Publication and story-time position

Volume 11 follows the linked late-winter sequence of Volume 10.5. The Valentine event and Komachi's entrance examination place the principal action in February. There is no anthology-style chronology problem comparable to Volume 7.5: Volume 11 is a continuous mainline novel whose relational state directly updates the post-10.5 boundary.

The prospective reading therefore treats as incoming facts that:

- the Service Club is again operational and warm but remains semantically unresolved;
- Hachiman has become more capable of asking and limited reliance, while still retaining an anti-reliance reflex;
- Yukino's uncertainty about her future is legitimate and has not been solved by anyone else;
- Yui has increasingly acknowledged that her care has motives of its own;
- `本物` remains a request and contested aspiration, not a settled doctrine;
- ordinary life has demonstrated that people can remain connected without identical interpretations.

Later-volume labels and outcomes are excluded from Sections 2–14.

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

At the beginning of Volume 11, the Service Club's problem is no longer simple estrangement. The trio have a place they themselves have made. Hachiman explicitly experiences his seat in the club as a `居場所`; tea, cookies, work, jokes, and even Iroha's irregular presence have become ordinary infrastructure.

The prospective questions are therefore harder than those of Volumes 7–9:

1. Can a relationship remain honest when everyone recognizes more than anyone is willing to name?
2. Does indirectness lower expressive risk, or does it prevent desire from receiving an answer?
3. Can Yui continue to preserve the atmosphere without converting kindness into self-erasure or management?
4. Can Yukino receive help without someone else becoming the author of her choices?
5. Can Hachiman defend autonomy without once again speaking for the person whose autonomy he claims to defend?
6. Can `本物` remain an open request, or does the word itself become another idealized form used to avoid concrete choice?
7. Is the trio's warmth proof of relational success, or can real happiness coexist with unresolved conflict?
8. Can understanding remain revisable as Hiratsuka proposed, rather than becoming either telepathic certainty or despairing unknowability?

The prospective layer must not assume that:

- Haruno's descriptions are omniscient diagnoses;
- Yui is merely the losing heroine or selfless mediator;
- Yukino has already decided to withdraw;
- Hachiman has already romantically chosen between the girls;
- the aquarium animals have one-to-one symbolic assignments;
- suffering authenticates a relationship;
- ambiguity itself is morally defective.

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

## 3.1 Chapter 1: the present is already becoming past

The volume begins with a paradox of season and relationship. Winter does not announce its ending. By the time one recognizes the change, the season is already moving. The same logic applies to the school year, Hiratsuka's tenure, Komachi's entrance examination, and the Service Club's present form.

Hachiman looks at Hayama's group and recognizes something he once underweighted: their warmth is not mechanically produced by a good group composition. People actively fill gaps, maintain rhythms, and choose to preserve an atmosphere. Yui likewise says that everyone is trying to cherish `今`—the present.

That recognition is positive, but Hachiman immediately sees its temptation. He wants to surrender to atmosphere, expectation, and waiting: `期待して甘えて委ねて待ってていたい`. He then corrects himself. Merely waiting would be `不誠実`. The language is important because this is not an abstract essay about authenticity; it produces behavior. Hachiman asks Yui when she is free.

The invitation is a prospective romantic act, however cautious. Yui's answer is not a simple acceptance. She looks toward the club and postpones a reply. The causal problem is no longer whether she wants Hachiman's company. Volume 10.5 already made that plausible. The problem is what dyadic fulfillment would mean for the third person and for the triad.

The chapter therefore establishes the volume's governing conflict:

> **the present is valuable enough to preserve, but preservation cannot stop time.**

## 3.2 Chapter 2: Valentine indirectness as an enabling institution

Several requests converge on handmade chocolate. Miura wants a final ordinary high-school Valentine opportunity before exam preparation dominates the following year. Kawasaki wants a recipe her younger sister can reproduce. Iroha has her own motives. Hayama's social position makes direct acceptance of romantic gifts difficult because any visibly selective response produces interpretation and group consequences.

Hachiman's solution is an event that creates a public excuse. If many people make chocolate and Hayama is positioned as a taster, he can receive gifts without the act functioning as an explicit public selection.

This is not merely cowardice. It is one of the series' best demonstrations that **pretext can enable agency**. Miura can act. Iroha can act. Hayama can receive. The social environment becomes less punitive.

But Volume 11 immediately prepares the harder question: if pretext is valuable because it allows action without forcing a total definition, when does that same deniability become a structure for never answering what the action means?

## 3.3 Chapter 3: Iroha's absence reveals competing models of care

Iroha's absence from the clubroom becomes noticeable. This confirms something Volume 10.5 had already suggested: she is not one of the founding three, but she has become part of the ordinary ecology of the room.

Hachiman then performs one of his clearest self-audits. He admits that his tendency to intervene early with Iroha is partly an inability to leave matters to her. `任せることができない` approaches an inability to trust.

He contrasts himself and Yukino with Yui. Yui can `信じて任せる`: she evaluates Iroha's real capabilities and does not needlessly worry or intervene. That is a crucial positive-control case:

> **trust may be expressed through non-substitution.**

Yui is not less caring because she does less. In this case, her care recognizes Iroha as capable of carrying her own work.

The chapter also makes Yui's attachment to the trio explicit. She says that she likes relaxing together as three—then intensifies the statement: `すっごい好き`. This matters later because her final proposal cannot be reduced to a scheme for obtaining Hachiman. She is trying to preserve a relationship she openly values in its own right.

## 3.4 Chapters 4–5: the Valentine event proves warmth can be real inside ambiguity

The event works. People who would otherwise struggle to act can participate. Hayama says that under these conditions everyone can behave naturally: `これならみんな……、みんな自然に振る舞える`.

This does not prove that all ambiguity is healthy. It proves that a social fiction can sometimes expand action rather than replace it.

The event is also deliberately overpopulated. Miura, Ebina, Iroha, the Kawasaki sisters, Hayama, Tobe, Totsuka, Zaimokuza, Kaihin students, Meguri, Haruno, Hiratsuka, Hachiman, Yui, and Yukino inhabit overlapping purposes. There is no single purified motive that explains the event. It is successful precisely because the structure can carry mixed motives.

The Service Club's shared history becomes visible through taste-testing and memories of earlier cookies. Hachiman recognizes that some things become knowable not because they were instantly transparent but because people have accumulated time together and assigned that time importance.

This is one of the volume's strongest rebuttals to both total-knowledge fantasy and total-skepticism:

> **partial knowledge can become meaningful through history without becoming complete possession of the other person.**

## 3.5 Chapter 5: Hiratsuka's present-progressive grammar

The title says Hiratsuka lectures on present progressive and past tense. Her actual argument is relational and temporal.

`人の印象は日々更新され続けている。`

Impressions of people continue to be updated. If people live through time together and continue growing, they may come to understand more.

The decisive verb structure is not “understand once.” It is **continue updating**.

When Hachiman says he does not feel that he has grown, Hiratsuka tells him that change can be small. His current sense of `違和感` should not be suppressed. She first jokes in the register of “feel it,” then corrects herself:

> `感じるな、考えろ`

Do not merely feel it. Think.

The discomfort is not proof of corruption. It is information that his present model no longer fits perfectly.

Hiratsuka's most important temporal warning follows: people who keep moving do not constantly measure how far they have traveled; to someone who has stopped, another person's progress can feel like betrayal. This line gives the chapter title its full force. A relationship is always both present-progressive and becoming past. The desire to preserve a person at an earlier state can turn love or nostalgia into pressure.

Her own impending departure prevents the advice from remaining abstract. `いつまでも見ていてはやれない`—she cannot keep watching forever. Care therefore has to survive altered proximity.

## 3.6 Haruno as critic and participant

Volume 11 strengthens Haruno's diagnostic importance while reducing any justification for treating her as an omniscient oracle.

When Hiratsuka tells Haruno that if she truly has accumulated things to say, Hiratsuka will listen whenever she wants, Haruno visibly reacts and then retreats into joking. The scene reveals a person who attacks other people's indirectness while herself struggling to make a direct request for care.

Hachiman explicitly admits that he fundamentally does not know Haruno. He knows her social roles—Yukino's sister, Hayama's childhood acquaintance, Hiratsuka's former student, the socially perfect elder—but not her inner totality.

Haruno can therefore be perceptive without becoming authoritative. Her provocations may mix concern, resentment, identification, envy, boredom, family history, and her own difficulty asking for help.

## 3.7 Chapter 6: the word `本物` becomes part of the problem

After the event, Hachiman finally tells Yui and Yukino directly that the cookies are delicious and thanks them. The gratitude is genuine. The event was genuinely enjoyable. The trio's warmth is not exposed as fake.

Haruno then asks:

> `それが比企谷くんのいう本物？`

and:

> `こういう時間が君のいう、本物？`

Her challenge lands because the club has created a pleasant form in which difficult meanings can remain suspended. She calls the present version of Hachiman and his friends boring and says she preferred the earlier Yukino.

A crucial evidentiary correction is necessary here. The severe formulation `それは信頼などではない。もっとひどい、おぞましい何かだ` appears in **Hachiman's narration as his interpretation of the implication**, not as a direct Haruno quotation in this scene. V1 shorthand tended to attribute the phrase too directly to Haruno. V2 therefore classifies it as:

> **Hachiman inference / self-diagnosis, prompted by Haruno's challenge.**

The distinction matters because the novel has still not supplied a stable label.

The trio then voluntarily stay to help Iroha despite being dismissed. The cooperation is real. Hachiman also admits that finishing work would force him to think about what Haruno said, so staying becomes avoidance. The same action can therefore be both care and postponement.

This is the volume in miniature:

> **a good action can carry an avoidant function without ceasing to be good in every respect.**

## 3.8 Yukino's mother: freedom defined from outside

Yukino's mother arrives because Haruno has relayed information about Yukino's course and future. Her rhetoric is gentle. That gentleness is precisely what makes its coercive structure difficult to contest.

She says she thought Yukino was not “that kind of child.” She had trusted Yukino and therefore allowed her freedom. Perhaps the problem is the mother's own failure. She wants Yukino to live freely and `自分らしく`, but does not want her to go down a wrong path. Then she asks what Yukino wants.

The contradiction is structural:

> **Yukino is invited to choose after the acceptable identity and unacceptable direction have already been socially framed.**

Hachiman recognizes that the mother's phrase defines and negates Yukino at once. The mother even absorbs blame in a way that makes opposition difficult: if Yukino resists, the resistance can be reinterpreted as proof that the mother was wrong to trust her.

This is not a claim that every parental concern is abusive. The text gives insufficient basis for such a total judgment. What it does show is a communicative structure in which “freedom” is offered inside a pre-authored model of who Yukino is supposed to be.

## 3.9 Hachiman's anti-definition turn

After the encounter, Hachiman thinks through `俺らしい`, `彼女らしい`, `自分らしい`. If even the self cannot cleanly certify its own “self-likeness,” how can a relationship be definitively named?

He then makes one of his most important and most dangerous conceptual moves:

> `この感情も関係性も定義してはいけなかったのだ。名前を付けてはいけなかった。`

At first glance this sounds sophisticated. Naming can reduce plurality. A label can make one function eclipse others.

But Hachiman's next admission reveals the defensive use:

> once a form is made, he fears it can only be changed by breaking it.

And he wonders whether he and “she” have merely clung to shapeless words.

This means `本物` itself may be becoming a **formless protective ideal**. By seeking an unbreakable relation beyond ordinary names, Hachiman can postpone the concrete consequences of saying what any given desire is.

Volume 11 therefore does not simply move from social labels toward pure authenticity. It exposes the possibility that **refusal to name can also be avoidance**.

## 3.10 Chapter 7: Haruno attacks Yukino's self-authorship

Haruno is temporarily sent to live with Yukino. Yukino says she will handle her own situation and that it does not concern Haruno. Haruno replies:

> `雪乃ちゃんに自分なんてあるの？`

She argues that Yukino historically watched Haruno and decided what to do from that model; that being allowed freedom did not mean making choices; and that Yukino still does not know how to act.

These are high-value **Haruno character assertions**. They are not omniscient facts.

There is evidence supporting parts of the accusation: Yukino has idealized Haruno, competed with her, and at moments borrowed formulations from Hachiman or other people. But Haruno's account omits the environment that helped produce the behavior—family comparison, the elder sister's apparent perfection, and the mother's model of acceptable freedom.

Hachiman's self-awareness is important here. He wants to stop Haruno, and admits that the impulse is not purely “for Yukino” but also `自分のため`. Protective action carries his own need.

Yui then insists that both she and Yukino are thinking. Haruno answers with a statement that Yukino has only one place to return to. Again, that is not a neutral fact. It is part of Haruno's family-centered model of Yukino's possibilities.

## 3.11 Chocolate in the clubroom: desire becomes legible before it becomes sayable

Before Valentine's Day, Yukino brings handmade cookies. She has an explicitly prepared gift for Yui. Yui then asks whether hers is the only one.

Hachiman's reaction is revealing. He knows he is interpreting. He repeats `勝手に`: reading into the situation, becoming self-conscious, expecting, suppressing expectation. This is not the old Hachiman who fails to perceive romantic possibility. He perceives it immediately and attempts to strip it of behavioral authority.

When Yui asks `ヒッキーのは？`, Yukino eventually pushes the cookies toward him and says `よかったら`. Her embarrassment is unusually visible. Hachiman says they are good.

The episode matters because it shows all three already know enough for the problem to exist. The obstacle is no longer lack of data.

> **The crisis is what to do with knowledge that has become emotionally legible without being formally declared.**

## 3.12 Yui's home and Komachi: two control cases for intimacy

Yui's home provides a domestic contrast with Yukino's family. Yui's mother already knows `ヒッキーくん` from her daughter. The household is teasing, open, embarrassing, and socially porous. It is not presented as conflict-free perfection, but identity is not controlled through the same prestige-laden conditional rhetoric visible in Yukino's family.

Komachi then supplies the most stable relational control case in the volume. Hachiman asks whether she likes him. Their sibling bond is secure enough that affection, irritation, selfish requests, and teasing can be spoken without each answer threatening the existence of the relationship.

Hachiman recognizes a harder point: even after more than fifteen years of shared space, memory, and values, siblings can still misunderstand each other. This does not make their bond unreal.

The implication is not that romantic relations should function exactly like sibling relations. It is narrower and more useful:

> **truth-bearing intimacy does not require perfect understanding, and spoken desire does not automatically destroy a durable relation.**

## 3.13 Chapter 8: Yui deliberately reconstructs the triangle

On the morning of Komachi's entrance examination, Yui calls Hachiman and says directly:

> `ヒッキー、デートしよう！`

Hachiman had originally been the one to suggest an outing; he had delayed it. He goes.

At the aquarium he discovers that Yui has also invited Yukino without fully explaining the arrangement to either of them. Yukino offers to leave. Yui holds Yukino's arm and Hachiman's sleeve and says:

> `三人で、行きたいの……`

Hachiman asks whether “here” is really acceptable. Yui immediately answers:

> `ここがいいの`

The wording is multivalent, but V2 should not flatten it into a secret code whose “true” meaning is known. The immediate referent is the aquarium and the three-person outing; the relational resonance is strong because Yui is deliberately choosing the trio over the dyadic date Hachiman had originally offered.

Yui is therefore not a passive victim of the triangle. She actively reconstructs it before forcing the underlying conflict into speech.

## 3.14 Aquarium sequence: competing forms of connection

The aquarium is symbolically dense enough to tempt over-reading. The correct method is to preserve direct character statements separately from visual/literary inference.

### The penguins

A pair is described as staying together unless one dies. Yukino leaves the sightline abruptly; Yui remains and watches with a gentle, saddened gaze. This places exclusivity and pair-bond durability before them, but it does **not** prove which two characters the penguins “represent.”

### Yukino and the fish

This is much stronger because Yukino herself supplies the identification.

She notices that some are free. Then:

> `寄る辺がなければ、自分の居場所も見つけられない……。隠れて流されて、何かについていって、……見えない壁にぶつかるの`

Hachiman asks which fish she means.

> `……私のこと`

This is a direct Yukino self-description and therefore one of Volume 11's strongest autonomy/dependency passages. She describes herself as needing an anchor or point of support, hiding, being carried, following something, and colliding with an invisible wall.

The image should not be retrospectively converted into the clinical term `依存` or `共依存`. Prospectively it establishes:

- unstable self-location;
- reliance upon external bearings;
- fear of drifting;
- difficulty returning through an invisible boundary;
- awareness of the problem from Yukino herself.

Most importantly, Yukino is **not merely being diagnosed by Haruno here**. She has begun articulating her own condition.

### The three-person viewing

At the jellyfish/fireworks display Yui pulls Yukino close, and Hachiman appears in the glass reflection. Yui says:

> `３人で見れて、良かった……`

Hachiman briefly imagines that their feelings may not differ much. That is **Hachiman inference/fantasy**, not proof of identical desire.

The scene therefore works best as another instance of co-presence across possible interpretive difference, continuous with the photograph in Volume 10.5.

### The Ferris wheel

Yukino is frightened but says `みんな、一緒だし`; she reaches to Yui, who grips back. Ordinary conversation resumes inside a structure that moves while returning to the same point.

The narration describes the Ferris wheel as performing stability while rotating through a fixed circuit. The metaphor is unusually direct, but should still not be treated as a literal predictive diagram of the romantic ending.

Its prospective function is clear enough:

> **movement that guarantees return can feel safe while producing no durable change in position.**

Near the descent comes the line `もうすぐ、終わりだね`. Whatever its immediate referent, the placement makes the approaching end of the outing inseparable from the end-consciousness governing the whole volume.

## 3.15 Chapter 9: Yui makes preservation explicit

After the Ferris wheel, Yui leads the others away from the station and toward the water. She asks:

> `これからどうしよっか？`

Then clarifies:

> `ゆきのんのこと。それと、あたしのこと。……あたしたちのこと`

She gives Hachiman handmade cookies, connecting directly back to the first request of Volume 1. Hachiman refuses to classify them as mere repayment. The old debt is already settled. If their incorrectly begun relation has been ended and restarted, the meaning of the act can change.

Yui then says:

> `あたしは全部ほしい。今も、これからも。あたし、ずるいんだ。卑怯な子なんだ`

and:

> `あたしはもうちゃんと決めてる`

This is not a formal sentence “I love Hachiman.” But the romantic meaning is extremely strong in context: Valentine's Day, handmade cookies, the history of her first request, and the conflict she is constructing. Equally important, `全部` is broader than “I want the boy.” Yui has already explicitly said she loves the three-person ordinary life. Her desire includes Hachiman **and** continued relation with Yukino and the club.

This is why she experiences herself as `ずるい` and `卑怯`: the goods she wants are not easily co-satisfiable.

## 3.16 Yui's proposal: agency and control at once

Yui says that if everyone fully understood what everyone else thinks, the present arrangement could not continue. Therefore the final request concerns the three of them.

She invokes the old contest: the winner's wish will be granted. She says she knows the answer to Yukino's problem and offers her own conclusion:

> `あたしが勝ったら、全部貰う。`

and:

> `ずっと、このままでいたいなって思うの`

V1 was right that this is not passive self-sacrifice. Yui is constructing an outcome. She states a desired conclusion first and tries to use the contest's authority to make the path legitimate.

Hachiman recognizes the mechanism: the rule would become `強制力`, even an `免罪符`. It could allow everyone to say the result was produced by the game rather than by direct responsibility.

Yui's proposal is therefore ethically double:

- **agentic** because she finally states desire and takes strategic initiative;
- **controlling** because she tries to guarantee a final arrangement in which the relation and her desires are all preserved.

The correct reading is not “Yui becomes selfish and therefore authentic.” It is that a person previously idealized as kind finally makes visible that kindness and desire coexist—and that her desire can itself become managerial.

## 3.17 Hachiman's refusal: progress with jurisdiction residue

Hachiman nearly accepts. The proposal would preserve the happy time and distribute responsibility through an old rule. He then sees Yukino preparing to answer and decides that something is wrong.

He says:

> `雪ノ下の問題は、雪ノ下自身が解決すべきだ`

This is an important autonomy principle. It refuses an externally authored answer.

But the scene immediately complicates Hachiman's moral authority. He interrupts Yukino before she finishes and declares what she should do. In defending her authorship, he temporarily **speaks for her**.

Hachiman then indicts his own idealizations:

> `由比ヶ浜結衣は優しい女の子だ。そう勝手に決めつけていた。`

> `雪ノ下雪乃は強い女の子だ。そうやって理想を押しつけていた。`

Those positive labels helped him avoid responsibility. If Yui is infinitely kind, she can keep smoothing conflict. If Yukino is infinitely strong, she can keep surviving without demanding care.

Yet he immediately repeats the values—Yui is kind; Yukino is strong—because he still wants to treat those qualities as real and worthy of respect. The point is not that all character judgments are false. It is that **a trait becomes coercive when others are required to remain inside it for the observer's convenience.**

Then comes the volume's sharpest Hachiman self-disclosure:

> `それでも、俺は理想を押しつけたい。`

He does not claim to have purified himself of projection. He admits that his desired answer is itself an ideal he wants to press upon the people he values, because he does not want to return their care with a lie.

This is more mature than unconscious idealization, but it is not final ethical innocence.

## 3.18 Yukino's correction: autonomy becomes dialogical

Hachiman and Yui finally look to Yukino.

She says:

> `……私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで`

This is one of the most important lines in the V2 reread.

It corrects **both** preservation strategies:

- Yui cannot decide that her proposed arrangement is Yukino's answer.
- Hachiman cannot decide that autonomous Yukino must solve the problem in exactly the manner he prescribes.

Yukino then says that Hachiman's Volume-9 request remains unfinished and adds:

> `……私の依頼、聞いてもらえるかしら`

The volume therefore ends not with Yukino rejecting help, but by **re-authoring the structure of help**.

She becomes requester rather than problem-object.

Yui answers `うん、聞かせて` and steps closer with an extended hand. The relational mechanism is not independence from others. It is assistance that begins from Yukino's voiced request.

## 3.19 Final shadow: joined without solved equivalence

The final image is a vague, irregular, connected shadow. Its outline is indistinct, but its parts are `確かに結ばれて`.

This does not solve the romantic triangle. Yui's desire is clearer than before. Hachiman has said his wish does not perfectly overlap with hers. Yukino's actual request has not yet been stated.

The final image therefore repeats a mature formal principle already emerging in Volume 10.5:

> **connection does not require identical interpretation or identical desire.**

The volume ends at the point where the relation may finally have to carry difference rather than conceal it.

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

## 4.1 Strength: social mechanism remains highly legible

Hachiman remains excellent at detecting:

- how Hayama's acceptance of chocolate produces public implications;
- why an event can create deniable space for action;
- how Iroha's absence changes the clubroom ecology;
- how Yui and Yukino care differently;
- how socially positive labels become expectations;
- how his own idealization has served his avoidance;
- how the old contest could become coercive authority;
- how an easy conclusion could launder responsibility.

These observations should not be discarded merely because he is implicated in the triangle.

## 4.2 Continued weakness: favorable relational meanings are perceived then denied standing

The Valentine-cookie scene is a clean example. Hachiman immediately detects the possibility that Yukino's behavior toward him has special meaning. His internal response is not ignorance. It is suppression through self-disgust: he tells himself that noticing or expecting any special meaning is grotesque.

This continues the domain-specific unreliability identified much earlier:

> **he often perceives the relational evidence correctly while refusing to let the favorable interpretation authorize action.**

## 4.3 Improvement: he can state desire and initiate

Chapter 1 matters because Hachiman directly asks Yui when she is free. He also recognizes that waiting for atmosphere to decide everything would be insincere.

This is real growth from his earlier preemptive rejection posture.

## 4.4 Improvement: he recognizes projection as projection

When he describes Yui as kind and Yukino as strong, he explicitly uses the language of unilateral assignment: `勝手に決めつけていた`, `理想を押しつけていた`.

This is a continuation of Volume 5's `勝手に` self-indictment, now applied to relational roles rather than only to his image of Yukino.

## 4.5 Limitation: the anti-definition theory may rationalize avoidance

Hachiman's meditation that feelings and relationships should not be named is intellectually plausible. Labels can flatten multiplicity.

But his own reasoning exposes the motive: he fears that a named form can change only by breaking. The refusal to define therefore protects the current arrangement from having to face the consequences of definition.

This should be classified as:

> **mixed philosophical insight + avoidance rationalization.**

## 4.6 Major self-awareness: he knows his ideal remains an imposition

`それでも、俺は理想を押しつけたい` is crucial because it prevents a triumphalist reading of the climax.

Hachiman does not say:

> I have finally learned not to project.

He says, in effect:

> I know this is an ideal I am pressing upon others, but I would rather expose that ideal than hide behind a lie.

That is epistemically more honest, but still ethically contestable.

## 4.7 Immediate correction by Yukino

His claim that Yukino must solve her own problem is directionally autonomy-protective. Yukino's answer—`私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで`—shows its limit.

This is an unusually valuable narrator-correction because it arrives **inside the same scene**. The novel does not require a later volume to tell us that Hachiman's pro-autonomy intervention still contains jurisdiction.

## 4.8 Hachiman's final state

By the end of Volume 11, Hachiman has become better at:

- admitting desire;
- identifying projection;
- rejecting externally authored answers;
- tolerating conflict;
- recognizing that wishes may differ;
- continuing relation without requiring total overlap.

He remains weak at:

- waiting through another person's difficult answer without intervention;
- accepting ambiguity without calling it compromise;
- distinguishing a desired ethical ideal from a rule others must follow;
- receiving care without feeling compelled to purify or repay it.

The correct ledger transition is therefore **STRENGTHENED BUT UNFINISHED**, not “Hachiman learns authenticity.”

# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Hikigaya Hachiman

**Delta:** from answer-seeker who still coerces knowledge in Volume 10 to a participant who can expose his own desired standard—but still struggles not to impose it.

Newly strengthened:

- directly initiates an outing with Yui;
- recognizes waiting as avoidance;
- can distinguish trust from intrusive caretaking;
- admits limited knowledge of Haruno;
- names his own positive-label projections;
- rejects the contest as responsibility laundering;
- admits his ideal is still an imposition;
- remains present after Yui's desire becomes explicit enough to hurt.

Unresolved:

- anti-reliance and anti-ambiguity remain strong;
- he still interrupts Yukino;
- `ちゃんとした答え` can become a purity demand;
- his self-disgust remains a mechanism for suppressing reciprocal possibility.

## 5.2 Yukinoshita Yukino

Volume 11 is not simply “Yukino becomes dependent.” That formulation is too blunt and too late-term contaminated.

Her actual prospective movement is:

1. ordinary competence and care remain intact;
2. romantic feeling becomes more behaviorally legible;
3. family pressure intensifies the question of authored identity;
4. Haruno claims that Yukino has no self independent of models;
5. Yukino herself describes drifting without an anchor and striking an invisible wall;
6. she nearly allows Yui's proposal to determine the next form;
7. she rejects Hachiman deciding her feelings;
8. she originates a request of her own.

The final step is decisive. Volume 11 ends with **renewed subjecthood**, not completed self-removal.

The central Yukino formulation should therefore be:

> **She recognizes that her self-location is unstable and that she needs relation, but begins converting externally supplied answers into a request she can author herself.**

## 5.3 Yuigahama Yui

Yui receives one of the strongest V2 upgrades in the corpus.

She is not merely:

- atmosphere manager;
- mediator;
- passive romantic loser;
- selfless protector;
- “normal girl” foil.

In Volume 11 she:

- values the trio explicitly and intensely;
- recognizes Iroha's competence and trusts by not substituting herself;
- participates in event production;
- deliberately turns Hachiman's dyadic invitation into a triadic outing;
- gives the cookies from her first request a new meaning;
- says she wants `全部`;
- calls herself `ずるい` and `卑怯` rather than hiding self-interest under kindness;
- proposes a concrete outcome;
- risks Hachiman's refusal;
- remains present after refusal;
- accepts Yukino's new request.

Her flaw is not passivity but **preservation through strategic accommodation**. She wants Hachiman, Yukino, and the existing relation, and attempts to engineer a result that prevents total loss.

## 5.4 Isshiki Iroha

Iroha's Volume-10.5 competence carries forward. Her absence is felt. Her event work is increasingly institutional rather than merely opportunistic. She can use budgets, facilities, public justifications, and her council position to create an event that serves several constituencies.

Hachiman's concern for her reveals his own trust problem more than her incapacity.

Iroha therefore continues to operate as a useful autonomy control case:

> **being supported is compatible with becoming more capable when the support does not permanently replace the office-holder's agency.**

## 5.5 Hayama Hayato

Hayama's post-Volume-10 role is narrower here but important. He participates in the Valentine structure and describes its value: people can behave naturally inside the provided excuse.

His behavior is called a kind of deeply sincere dishonesty because he tries to satisfy the expectations surrounding him without creating selective romantic consequences. This should not be read as proof that Hayama is “fake.” It is another mixed-motive role performance in which care and concealment coexist.

## 5.6 Yukinoshita Haruno

Haruno becomes more human and less usable as an oracle.

She:

- recognizes structural stagnation;
- provokes the trio with high accuracy;
- notices Yukino's identity instability;
- weaponizes family history;
- says she preferred the earlier Yukino;
- herself reacts when Hiratsuka offers direct listening;
- retreats behind humor rather than making a request.

The V2 conclusion is:

> **Haruno is a gifted reader who is also inside the family and relational system she reads.**

Her insights are evidence. They are not self-validating judgments.

## 5.7 Hiratsuka Shizuka

Hiratsuka becomes the volume's clearest positive model of ongoing interpretation.

Her model is neither:

- total understanding;
- indifference;
- permanent supervision;
- purity through suffering.

It is:

> live time together, keep updating the model, notice incongruity, think about it, and accept that one day proximity will change.

She also offers Haruno listening without forcing disclosure. That is an important care model in a volume where so many people are trying to make others speak.

## 5.8 Komachi

Komachi remains the strongest low-stakes example of secure affection. Her relationship with Hachiman can contain selfishness, asking, irritation, affection, and uncertainty without every statement threatening dissolution.

Her entrance exam also foregrounds the limit of Hachiman's control. He can support, wait, and care. He cannot sit the exam for her.

## 5.9 Yukino's mother

Volume 11 strengthens the distinction between **gentle affect** and **agency-preserving communication**.

Yukino's mother does not need to shout to exercise control. Her language frames identity, trust, freedom, failure, and correct direction before asking Yukino what she wants.

This is a powerful social-system counterexample to the assumption that coercion must sound coercive.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Service Club triad

Incoming state: undefined but workable ordinary relation.

Outgoing state: **explicitly acknowledged conflict-bearing relation whose members now know that “staying the same” is itself a choice with costs.**

Dimensions:

| Dimension | V10.5 state | V11 delta |
|---|---|---|
| trust | growing through shared work | differentiated: Yui's `信じて任せる` becomes explicit positive model |
| disclosure | partial, ordinary | romantic/relational desire becomes strongly legible and partly verbalized |
| conflict tolerance | improved | major strengthening; Yui forces the issue and stays after refusal |
| permission to intervene | still mixed | final scene re-centers permission through Yukino's request |
| ordinary presence | stable | confirmed as genuinely valuable, not exposed as fake |
| future assumption | change acknowledged | preservation becomes impossible without answering difference |
| romantic desire | asymmetrically legible | Yui extremely strong; Yukino substantial behaviorally; Hachiman remains non-declarative |
| triadic attachment | real but undefined | Yui explicitly says she wants the three-person relation |
| definitional status | unresolved | anti-definition itself becomes suspect as avoidance |

## 6.2 Hachiman–Yui

This relationship becomes much harder to describe as one-sided.

Hachiman:

- initiates an outing;
- cannot treat her handmade cookies as neutral gratitude;
- recognizes that her desire has meaning;
- takes her proposal seriously enough to nearly accept;
- tells her the answer he himself wants rather than giving a socially convenient response.

Yui:

- turns the outing into a triadic day;
- gives the gift;
- states `全部ほしい`;
- predicts Hachiman's refusal;
- remains emotionally available afterward.

This is not a romantic resolution. It is a relationship in which Yui's desire can no longer plausibly be treated as invisible.

## 6.3 Hachiman–Yukino

Romantic coding and autonomy conflict intensify simultaneously.

The cookie sequence makes Hachiman highly self-conscious about Yukino's possible special attention. The illustrations also place them in unusually close, embarrassed visual compositions during the Valentine movement.

But the deeper development is procedural. Hachiman tries to protect Yukino from having her future decided by Yui, only for Yukino to tell him not to decide her feelings either.

This is a crucial rebalancing:

> **Hachiman's care becomes more ethically useful only when Yukino can correct it.**

## 6.4 Yui–Yukino

Their friendship is not a simple mask for romantic rivalry.

Yui explicitly wants the trio; Yukino prepares a Valentine gift for Yui; Yui recognizes Yukino's competence and suffering; Yukino admires Yui's ability to make the handmade cookies; they physically support one another at the aquarium and Ferris wheel; Yui's final proposal is partly an attempt to preserve Yukino within her desired future.

That very care creates conflict because Yui's proposed solution would also author an answer for Yukino.

Their final exchange is therefore important: after Hachiman's interruption, Yukino makes a request and Yui answers `聞かせて`, moving closer rather than withdrawing.

The friendship survives the exposure of nonidentical desires.

## 6.5 Hachiman–Iroha

The relation remains playful and ambiguous, but V11's major use of it is ethical rather than romantic. Hachiman's tendency to over-help Iroha exposes his inability to trust by leaving her responsible. Yui's contrasting care shows a better mode.

## 6.6 Haruno–Hiratsuka

This is one of the volume's most important adult relationships. Hiratsuka offers an open listening relation without demanding immediate disclosure. Haruno's reaction implies that even the critic who forces others toward speech may not know how to ask directly herself.

## 6.7 Hachiman–Komachi

The sibling relation becomes a control case for honest asking. Affection does not become less valuable merely because it is requested or stated. Their bond can survive difference and embarrassment.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

## 7.1 Valentine event: pretext that expands agency

The event passes the intervention test comparatively well.

- **Problem owners:** Miura, Kawasaki, Iroha and other participants retain distinct motives.
- **Authorization:** they ask or voluntarily participate.
- **Method:** create a shared activity rather than force a private confession.
- **Cost distribution:** institutional and social rather than concentrated in one sacrificial target.
- **Agency effect:** people gain opportunities to act.
- **Residual risk:** deniability can defer acknowledgment.

The event therefore remains a positive example of **pretext as expressive infrastructure**.

## 7.2 Hachiman and Iroha: care without trust

Hachiman recognizes that intervening before Iroha needs him may say more about his inability to entrust than about her need.

This matters for the helping ledger because it rejects a common moral shortcut:

> “I helped because I cared” is not sufficient.

One must also ask whether the care preserved the other person's authorship.

## 7.3 Yui's trust model

Yui's `信じて任せる` becomes one of the strongest positive formulations in the series so far. She can care about Iroha without compulsively replacing her work.

This is important because later dependency questions cannot be reduced to “relying on people is bad.” Volume 11 itself supplies an alternative: trust can mean recognizing competence and refraining from unnecessary substitution.

## 7.4 Hiratsuka with Haruno: invitation without extraction

Hiratsuka does not force Haruno to confess. She offers availability if Haruno truly has something accumulated to say.

That preserves:

- Haruno's choice of timing;
- Haruno's right not to disclose;
- availability of support.

It is a small but valuable model of care without jurisdiction.

## 7.5 Yukino's mother: question after pre-authorization

The mother's final question—what does Yukino want?—is formally open. The surrounding rhetoric heavily shapes which answer will count as responsible, self-like, or safe.

This demonstrates that **asking a question is not automatically agency-preserving**. The normative environment around the question matters.

## 7.6 Yui's final proposal

Yui is the requester and strategist. Her problem is that the proposed solution tries to protect everyone from irreversible loss by using the old contest as authority.

The ethical failure is not that Yui wants too much. Wanting is not coercion.

The failure is:

> **the solution would let a preexisting rule decide the meaning of other people's futures.**

## 7.7 Hachiman's refusal

Hachiman correctly identifies the contest as a possible `免罪符`. He rejects a mechanism that would let everyone say “the rule made us do it.”

But he then risks a parallel error by telling Yukino what autonomy requires before hearing her answer.

Thus:

> **an autonomy principle can itself become paternalistic if the supposed beneficiary is denied the final word.**

## 7.8 Yukino's final request

This is the volume's strongest helping structure.

Yukino:

- stops both others from deciding her feelings;
- recalls the still-open request structure from Volume 9;
- originates a new request;
- asks whether they will hear it.

The assistance is not rejected. Its authorship is changed.

This is why Volume 11 does **not** support an independence-as-isolation reading.

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 Valentine as a social signaling system

Chocolate is not merely private feeling in edible form. It is socially legible behavior governed by:

- obligation versus romantic giving;
- handmade versus purchased goods;
- public visibility;
- reciprocity expectations;
- group jealousy;
- school reputation;
- gender scripts.

Hayama's reluctance is therefore socially rational even if emotionally evasive. Receiving is itself a public act.

The event works by altering the signaling environment: it creates a category under which reception can occur without immediately becoming exclusive selection.

## 8.2 Exams and temporal pressure

Miura's request is intensified by the school calendar. “Next year” will not simply repeat this one; entrance exams alter available time and social rhythms. Komachi's exam makes the same temporal principle personal for Hachiman.

The volume repeatedly converts school institutions into reminders that the present form has an expiration date.

## 8.3 Hayama and role obligation

Hayama's “sincere dishonesty” continues Volume 10's analysis of socially prewritten identity. He chooses to inhabit the expected Hayama role fully rather than halfheartedly.

This is neither pure authenticity nor pure falsity. It is role ownership under constraint.

## 8.4 Yukino family: prestige and authored freedom

The Yukinoshita family exerts power less through visible punishment than through expectation, model identity, emotional framing, and future planning.

The mother's “freedom” is conditional upon remaining the kind of person she believes Yukino to be. Haruno's accusation that Yukino has never really chosen for herself emerges from the same family system, even as Haruno weaponizes it.

## 8.5 Yui family as contrast

Yui's household is socially open enough that Hachiman already exists in the mother's knowledge through Yui's stories. The contrast is not proof that one family is perfect and the other wholly pathological. Its analytic value is that it shows a different relation between family, peer intimacy, and embarrassment.

## 8.6 Service Club as informal institution

The club continues to solve requests by combining distinct competences:

- Yukino: planning, technical competence, standards;
- Yui: social access, emotional translation, atmosphere, trust;
- Hachiman: structural analysis, unconventional solution design;
- Iroha: institutional access and implementation.

The danger arises when the club's success at solving other people's problems becomes a structure for avoiding its own internal decision.

# 9. `本物` / authenticity ledger

## 9.1 Lexical state

Across Chapters 1–9:

- `本物`: **5** occurrences;
- `偽物`: **0**;
- `共依存`: **0**;
- `依存`: **0** as a governing label.

The `本物` cluster is concentrated in Chapter 6 and its title.

## 9.2 The chapter title is already a warning

`未だ、彼の求める本物には手が届かず、本物はまちがい続ける。`

The key point is not a dictionary definition. It is continued error.

Having articulated the desire for `本物` in Volume 9 has not produced moral clarity. The concept can be misunderstood, instrumentalized, or turned into an image whose preservation substitutes for action.

## 9.3 Haruno's question does not define the answer

Haruno asks whether the pleasant time is Hachiman's `本物`.

The question destabilizes his complacency. It does not prove that warmth, tea, friendship, or indirect Valentine behavior are inherently fake.

Indeed, the event's effectiveness and the trio's genuine pleasure are well-supported facts.

The problem is that **real pleasure can become evidence in an argument for permanent non-choice**.

## 9.4 Hachiman's agreement remains an inference

Hachiman interprets Haruno as implying that this cannot be genuine and thinks `同感だ`.

That is Hachiman's judgment at this moment, not the novel's omniscient conclusion.

His later anti-definition meditation shows why caution is necessary: he is tempted to protect the “genuine” by refusing any ordinary name or form. That too can become idealization.

## 9.5 `本物` as shapeless refuge

Hachiman wonders whether he and “she” have clung to words without shape. This is one of the most important longitudinal reversals.

Earlier, `本物` was valuable because it opposed complacent social performance. Now the ideal itself risks becoming **a way not to decide which ordinary, imperfect form a relationship will take**.

Thus Volume 11 adds a new warning:

> **Authenticity language can become evasive when “nothing less than the genuine” means never accepting the vulnerability of a concrete answer.**

## 9.6 Final-chapter movement away from definition toward answerability

The climax does not define `本物`. Instead it requires:

- Yui to state desire;
- Hachiman to state his desired standard;
- Yukino to reject both others deciding her feelings;
- Yukino to make a request;
- all three to remain present despite nonidentical wishes.

The prospective ledger transition is therefore:

> `本物`: **from contested ideal → ideal vulnerable to becoming protective abstraction → process increasingly tested by explicit incompatible wishes.**

# 10. Dependency and autonomy ledger

## 10.1 Do not import the later label

Volume 11 contains no governing `共依存` terminology. That absence is methodologically important.

It would be easy, knowing later developments, to rewrite every scene as an illustration of a diagnosis not yet supplied. V2 forbids that.

Instead we track behaviors:

- inability to entrust;
- over-care;
- need for external bearings;
- self-location through other people;
- fear of loss;
- protective postponement;
- transfer of choice;
- chosen reliance;
- direct request.

## 10.2 Hachiman: over-helping as distrust

Hachiman identifies his own pattern toward Iroha. Acting early because he expects failure prevents him from learning whether she can succeed.

This is a dependency-relevant mechanism on the **helper** side: being needed or controlling outcomes can substitute for trust.

## 10.3 Yui: trust by restraint

Yui's behavior toward Iroha is the positive comparator. She can care and still let Iroha carry responsibility.

This proves that the series does not equate closeness with intervention.

## 10.4 Yukino: direct self-description of external bearing

The aquarium line is the strongest prospective evidence of Yukino's current self-location difficulty:

`寄る辺がなければ、自分の居場所も見つけられない`.

She identifies herself with the image.

This is stronger evidence than Haruno's accusation because it is Yukino's own speech. But its content still does not equal a clinical diagnosis. The closest safe formulation is:

> **Yukino recognizes a vulnerability to drift and externally supplied orientation.**

## 10.5 Family structure and externally defined selfhood

Mother and sister both frame Yukino's self before asking her to produce it. Haruno's accusation that Yukino has “always watched me and decided” may be partly true; it is also embedded in the same family system that made Haruno the dominant model.

## 10.6 Yui's solution would preserve reliance by deciding its form

Yui's proposal is compassionate in intent and controlling in structure. It would let Yukino hand difficult choice to the contest outcome while preserving Yui's desired relation.

The problem is not help itself. It is **authorship migration**.

## 10.7 Hachiman's corrective is too individualistic if universalized

`雪ノ下の問題は、雪ノ下自身が解決すべきだ` contains an important autonomy truth. But taken literally as a universal rule it would make help suspect precisely when Yukino is learning to ask.

The final scene corrects that danger. Yukino does not announce solitary independence. She says:

> hear **my** request.

This is chosen reliance.

## 10.8 Final state

By the end of V11, the dependency/autonomy field should be frozen as:

> **The problem is neither needing others nor receiving help. The problem is when the need to help, preserve, or protect removes the recipient's authorship over the choice being made. The healthier alternative is not isolation but requested, revisable, answerable interdependence.**

This remains prospective and should be tested against later volumes rather than treated as a final verdict.

# 11. Japanese language and voice

## 11.1 Yui: softness no longer means lack of force

Yui's Japanese remains full of softeners, colloquial hesitation, and emotionally transparent particles, but Volume 11 repeatedly gives that register decisive content.

Examples:

- `三人で、行きたいの……`
- `ここがいいの`
- `あたしは全部ほしい`
- `あたし、ずるいんだ。卑怯な子なんだ`
- `あたしはもうちゃんと決めてる`

The contrast is central. Yui does not need to sound severe to exercise agency.

Her `ずるい` / `卑怯` vocabulary is also self-interpretation, not objective moral classification. She knows she wants goods that conflict and refuses to disguise that wanting as pure altruism.

## 11.2 Yukino: formal precision breaks into vulnerable first-person speech

Yukino's usual controlled register remains intact through much of the novel. Its breakdowns carry weight precisely because the baseline is so composed.

The strongest examples are:

- `……私のこと`
- `……私の気持ちを勝手に決めないで`
- `……私の依頼、聞いてもらえるかしら`

The last sentence is especially important. `聞いてもらえるかしら` retains Yukino's characteristic composed, feminine register while making a vulnerable request. Agency does not require abandoning her voice style.

## 11.3 Hachiman: `勝手に` returns as self-indictment

The recurrent `勝手に` family remains one of the clearest markers of his epistemic maturation:

- interpret on his own;
- expect on his own;
- assign kindness on his own;
- impose strength/ideal on his own.

But Volume 11 goes further with:

> `それでも、俺は理想を押しつけたい`

The adverbial `それでも` matters. Even after recognizing projection, he chooses to state an ideal. He is no longer pretending that his preferred answer arrived impersonally from logic.

## 11.4 Haruno: playful surface, interrogative violence

Haruno's lines often retain teasing or sisterly surface rhythm while placing the target under high interpretive pressure:

- `それが比企谷くんのいう本物？`
- `雪乃ちゃんに自分なんてあるの？`

The question form lets her expose contradictions without fully owning a positive answer of her own.

Hiratsuka's direct offer to listen reveals the asymmetry: Haruno is excellent at making others answer and poor at becoming requester.

## 11.5 Yukino's mother: gentle syntax as boundary enforcement

Her rhetoric combines:

- trust;
- self-blame;
- concern;
- `自分らしく`;
- `自由`;
- fear of the wrong path;
- a final question.

Nothing requires overt threats. The semantic sequence establishes the acceptable moral field before the answer is solicited.

## 11.6 Hiratsuka: tense and aspect as ethics

`人の印象は日々更新され続けている` uses continuing aspect to reject a static model of persons.

The chapter title's `現在進行形と過去形` is therefore not decorative grammar comedy. It expresses a relational ethics:

> a person one loves is always simultaneously the person one remembers and the person still becoming.

## 11.7 Lexical field

The volume's raw lexical emphasis is revealing:

- `言葉`: very high frequency;
- `答え`: extremely prominent;
- `わからない`: recurrent;
- `優し*`: recurrent;
- `好き`: recurrent;
- `三人` / `二人`: both structurally prominent;
- `本物`: concentrated rather than ubiquitous.

Volume 11 is therefore less a philosophical essay about `本物` than a novel about **speech, answer, kindness, and relational number**.

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertextual function

Volume 11 remains a romantic comedy while conducting serious relational analysis. That is not incidental tonal relief; comedy is one of the mechanisms by which characters keep difficult meanings socially survivable.

Examples include:

- Hachiman's exaggerated Valentine anxieties;
- cooking-event chaos;
- Iroha's teasing;
- Hachiman's childlike excitement over hammerhead sharks;
- Komachi's sibling banter;
- repeated genre-aware joking around dates, gifts, and romantic interpretation.

The aquarium shark sequence is particularly useful. Hachiman becomes transparently delighted, and Yukino responds by taking a photograph exactly as he requests. The scene momentarily removes all philosophical abstraction. They know something small about him because he shows it; she responds because he asks.

This is the ordinary-scale version of the relationship the grand `本物` language has struggled to describe.

Comedy also protects characters from overstatement. Iroha can test meanings and retreat. Yui can couch vulnerability in familiar speech. Hachiman can expose desire and then cover it with self-mockery.

The danger, as always, is when comic or genre cover becomes the only available channel and therefore prevents consequence.

# 13. Illustration and paratext analysis

Illustrations are treated as editorial/visual emphasis, not as evidence overriding prose.

## 13.1 Overall visual emphasis

Volume 11's visual material is strongly relational. The cover and character material emphasize Yui prominently, which fits a volume in which she becomes the most explicit architect of the final confrontation.

The grayscale inserts repeatedly isolate dyadic looks inside the triadic narrative rather than presenting the trio as visually interchangeable.

## 13.2 Hachiman–Yukino Valentine compositions

The insert corresponding to EPUB image `00013.jpeg` / fixed-page PDF p.147 vertically pairs Hachiman's embarrassed face with Yukino's similarly embarrassed close-up. The composition does not prove a confession. It editorially emphasizes reciprocal self-consciousness during a volume where romantic meanings are becoming difficult to suppress.

A later insert, EPUB image `00014.jpeg` / PDF p.189, places Hachiman and Yukino in one composition with different postures and sightlines. Again, the useful claim is paratextual: the volume repeatedly chooses to visualize their proximity at moments when the prose is destabilizing the “nothing to see here” interpretation.

## 13.3 Aquarium portraits

EPUB image `00020.jpeg` / PDF p.286 gives Yui a close, gentle direct gaze; EPUB image `00021.jpeg` / PDF p.295 gives Yukino an isolated profile against aquarium space.

The asymmetry is editorially suggestive without being a romance scorecard:

- Yui is visually associated with looking toward another person;
- Yukino is visually associated with reflective self-location and boundary imagery.

That corresponds to the prose's chapter-title emphasis—Yui's warm gaze, Yukino's clear eyes—but should not be used to decide whose desire “wins.”

## 13.4 Character-profile inserts

The Yui/Yukino profile-style spreads later in the volume place their words against stylized individual portraits. Their inclusion reinforces that the story is increasingly asking each girl to become legible as a subject rather than merely as one side of Hachiman's romantic choice.

## 13.5 Final visual principle

Volume 10.5 used a photograph to preserve co-presence without identical interpretation. Volume 11's illustrations move from paired embarrassment to individualized aquarium portraits, while the prose ends with joined but indistinct shadows.

The visual progression is therefore consistent with—but does not independently prove—the literary movement:

> **distinct subjects remain connected without becoming visually or semantically identical.**

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial tests

## Test A: Is Haruno simply correct?

**Strongest case for:**

- she identifies stagnation before the trio can articulate it;
- she sees Yukino's externally oriented selfhood;
- her challenge pushes Hachiman toward the central conflict;
- later behavior gives her warnings real predictive value.

**Counterevidence:**

- Hachiman admits he does not know Haruno;
- Haruno herself deflects when offered listening;
- she says she prefers the earlier Yukino, which may reflect her own attachment to an older role;
- her family participation helped produce the very instability she criticizes;
- the harshest `おぞましい何か` sentence in Chapter 6 is Hachiman's inference, not her direct diagnostic line there.

**Verdict:** **Haruno is diagnostically powerful but not governing authority.**

## Test B: Is the trio's ordinary happiness fake?

**Strongest case for:** Haruno challenges it; Hachiman senses mismatch; the pleasant form enables avoidance.

**Counterevidence:**

- the event produces real agency for several participants;
- the trio genuinely enjoys shared memory and work;
- Yui explicitly loves the ordinary three-person time;
- Yukino and Yui's friendship and mutual care are behaviorally real;
- Hachiman's gratitude is sincere.

**Verdict:** **Reject.** The happiness is real. Its use as an argument for indefinite non-choice is the problem.

## Test C: Is Yui simply sacrificing herself for Yukino?

**Strongest case for:** she tries to preserve Yukino; she may accept an arrangement painful to herself; she cries.

**Counterevidence:**

- `全部ほしい`;
- `もうちゃんと決めてる`;
- she wants the trio and Hachiman;
- she invokes the contest strategically;
- she proposes a concrete outcome that would benefit her own continued proximity;
- she calls herself sly/cowardly precisely because she recognizes self-interest.

**Verdict:** **Reject as a master reading.** Yui is a caring, strategic claimant whose proposal mixes love, fear of loss, and control.

## Test D: Does Yukino's fish metaphor prove pathological dependence?

**Strongest case for:** direct self-description of lacking an anchor, drifting, following others, hitting a wall.

**Counterevidence:**

- no `依存` / `共依存` governing term is used;
- Yukino is capable of ordinary competence and care;
- she explicitly rejects others deciding her feelings;
- she ends by making a request herself.

**Verdict:** **Strong evidence of unstable self-authorship and external orientation; insufficient prospective basis for a clinical/pathological label.**

## Test E: Is Hachiman's refusal the morally correct answer?

**Strongest case for:** he rejects responsibility laundering and protects Yukino's authorship.

**Counterevidence:**

- he interrupts Yukino;
- he declares what she must do;
- he admits he wants to impose an ideal;
- Yukino immediately tells him not to decide her feelings.

**Verdict:** **Necessary correction, incomplete method.** The scene deliberately gives Yukino the final correction.

## Test F: Does `本物` now mean explicit definition?

**Strongest case for:** the trio can no longer survive purely through unnamed implication; Yui forces speech.

**Counterevidence:**

- the final state is still not fully named;
- Hachiman and Yui's wishes are nonidentical;
- Yukino's request content is withheld;
- a label can itself flatten multiplicity;
- the series continues to value indirect forms that expand agency.

**Verdict:** **No.** Volume 11 moves toward answerability, not toward a rule that every relation must be exhaustively defined.

## Test G: Does the aquarium prove the final romantic pairing?

**Verdict:** **Reject.** The aquarium intensifies dyadic and triadic relational coding, but its prospective function is to expose exclusivity, drift, shared viewing, fear, and cyclical motion. One-to-one symbol assignment exceeds the evidence.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

This section alone permits later-series hindsight. It does not alter the prospective freeze above.

## 15.1 Later terminology should recontextualize, not overwrite

Later volumes will supply stronger dependency terminology. The V11 reread shows why that later diagnosis can feel persuasive: over-helping, external bearings, preservation, helper identity, and transfer of choice are already visible.

But Volume 11 also supplies counterevidence that must survive the later label:

- Yui's `信じて任せる`;
- Yukino's explicit self-observation;
- Yukino's rejection of others deciding her feelings;
- the final request structure;
- Hiratsuka's non-extractive offer to Haruno;
- ordinary help that increases rather than replaces agency.

Later diagnosis must therefore explain the mechanism without retroactively turning all reliance into pathology.

## 15.2 V11 is the necessary bridge between `本物` and later choice

With full-series hindsight, the temptation is to read Volume 9's `本物` request as already containing the ending. Volume 11 strongly resists that.

The characters can say `本物` and still:

- hide behind kindness;
- avoid definition;
- impose ideals;
- try to preserve incompatible goods;
- speak for one another.

The word does not solve them. It creates a standard they must learn how to enact without making it another purity fantasy.

## 15.3 Yui's proposal should not be rewritten as simple noble defeat

Later outcomes can make Yui's Volume-11 behavior look like an inevitable prelude to loss. Prospectively she is more formidable than that. She formulates an answer, uses the contest, admits desire, tests Hachiman, and survives refusal.

Her later pain should not erase her agency here.

## 15.4 Yukino does not end Volume 11 by disappearing

Later self-removal movements can tempt a rereader to see V11 as their completed origin. But the actual endpoint is the opposite in one crucial respect:

> **Yukino says do not decide my feelings, then makes a request.**

Any later retreat must therefore be read as a subsequent movement that modifies this newly asserted subjecthood, not as proof that Volume 11 ended with withdrawal already complete.

## 15.5 Hachiman's idealism remains ethically unstable

Later choice may vindicate aspects of Hachiman's refusal to accept a convenient arrangement. It should not retroactively sanitize `理想を押しつけたい`.

One of V11's achievements is that Hachiman can finally admit the normative desire he is bringing into the relationship. That makes him more answerable. It does not make the desire neutral.

## Closing proposition

Volume 11 does not destroy the Service Club's ordinary happiness in order to reveal a hidden “truth.” It asks whether the happiness is strong enough to survive the truth that its members do not want exactly the same future.

The answer is not yet known.

But the volume produces the procedural condition under which an answer can become theirs:

> **Yui may want everything. Hachiman may want an uncompromised answer. Yukino may refuse both descriptions and ask for help anyway. None of those wishes becomes legitimate by erasing the others.**

Spring begins not when the snow is denied, but underneath what has accumulated.
