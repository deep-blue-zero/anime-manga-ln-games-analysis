# OREGAIRU V2 - Volume 13 Deep Reading
## 『やはり俺の青春ラブコメはまちがっている。13』

### Governing thesis

**Volume 13 asks whether changing the method changes the relationship - or only changes the excuse. Hachiman stops directly substituting for Yukino and instead creates a rival prom, recruits helpers with much greater disclosure, accepts refusals, and relies on others openly; yet the entire dummy-prom strategy still engineers the institutional choice architecture so that Yukino's project becomes the option most likely to survive. Hachiman attempts to treat this unwanted assistance as evidence that their relation is not `共依存`, but the novel itself attacks that proof: changing the description of an intervention does not automatically change the reinforcing structure underneath it. At the same time, Yukino converts the contest into a self-authored closure procedure, Yui explicitly refuses the idea that romantic loss requires the death of her friendship with Yukino, and Hayama supplies a rival authenticity model in which even something born as a counterfeit may become irreplaceably singular through history. The prom is saved, but the relationship is not ethically solved. The trio now understands enough about authorship to know that two people cannot legitimately decide the third person's future; nevertheless, each remains tempted to let ending, pain, competition, or self-removal perform the choice that direct desire still makes difficult.**

A compact formulation is:

> **Volume 13 asks whether changing the method changes the relationship - or only changes the excuse.**

A sharper intervention-ethics formulation is:

> **Volume 13 turns diagnosis into experiment, and the experiment fails to prove what its designers want: neither unwanted help nor separation can by itself distinguish care from codependency, because the decisive test remains who authors the choice and whether the other people affected are allowed to answer.**

This volume therefore should not be read as the story finally proving or disproving `共依存`. It is the volume in which the characters begin **acting experimentally on the diagnosis**, and thereby expose the limits of diagnosis as a substitute for choosing.

## 1.2 Formal architecture

Volume 13 is formally more polyphonic than the preceding volumes. Its eight numbered chapters are interleaved with six separate `Interlude…` sections:

1. opening `Interlude…`
2. `① しみじみと、平塚静はいつかの昔を懐かしむ。`
3. `② どうしても、一色いろはには確かめたいことがある。`
4. `Interlude…` - Iroha viewpoint
5. `③ 最後まで、由比ヶ浜結衣は見守り続ける。`
6. `Interlude…` - Yui viewpoint
7. `④ あらためて、比企谷八幡はかたりかける。`
8. `⑤ いつともしれず、エンドロールは流れ始める。`
9. `Interlude…` - Yui viewpoint
10. `⑥ 人知れず、葉山隼人は悔いている。`
11. `Interlude…` - Hayama viewpoint
12. `⑦ レンズ越しに、海老名姫菜が見る風景は。`
13. `Interlude…` - Haruno viewpoint followed by Yui viewpoint
14. `⑧ せめて、もうまちがえたくないと願いながら。`
15. closing `Interlude…` - formally unattributed; context strongly suggests Yukino, but this remains an attribution inference rather than textual fact.

The interludes are analytically indispensable. Hachiman constructs the dummy prom and supplies most of the strategic language, but the novel repeatedly withdraws from him to show how Iroha, Yui, Hayama, and Haruno interpret the same relational machinery. The reader is therefore structurally prevented from treating Hachiman's explanation of his own method as the final account of what that method means.

## 1.3 Incoming state from Volume 12

Volume 12 froze five governing conditions:

1. Yukino has explicitly asked Hachiman not to take over: `あなたの力はもう借りないわ` / `お願い。私にやらせて`.
2. Hachiman has reduced his motive for acting to a personal promise: `いつか、助けるって約束したから`.
3. `共依存` is now legitimate textual vocabulary, but Hachiman himself supplies the governing limit: `共依存は仕組みだ。気持ちじゃない。`
4. Yui has recognized her own decision displacement and does not want pity-based rescue.
5. The prom has independent institutional ownership through Iroha and Yukino; it is not merely a romantic metaphor.

The incoming ethical question is therefore exact: **can Hachiman act on personal attachment without stealing Yukino's authorship?**

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

At the publication boundary of Volume 13, the following questions are genuinely unresolved:

1. Does Yukino's refusal prohibit all Hachiman involvement, or specifically direct assistance/substitution?
2. If Hachiman competes against Yukino rather than helping her, does that respect her authorship or merely evade the wording of her refusal?
3. Can a deliberately inferior dummy proposal be an ethical intervention if it manipulates institutional choice architecture rather than Yukino's personal decision directly?
4. Does Yukino voluntarily accept the competition, and if so, what exactly does that authorize?
5. Can Hachiman's collaborators meaningfully consent when much of their work is designed to fail or be discarded?
6. Is the dummy-prom experiment capable of proving the absence of `共依存`?
7. If Haruno says the altered method changes only the words and not the result, is she correct about mechanism even if her larger cure hypothesis remains contestable?
8. Does Yukino's attempt to end the relationship count as self-authorship, self-removal, self-sacrifice, or some unstable combination?
9. Can Yui accept romantic loss while preserving a future friendship with Yukino?
10. Does a relationship become `本物` because it is painful, because it is chosen, because it is singular, or because it survives revision?
11. Who has authority to declare the Service Club relation ended - Yukino, Hachiman, Yui, any dyad, or only all affected participants through continued interaction?
12. Can the prom be institutionally successful while the relational “experiment” remains inconclusive?

The prospective reading must **not** assume:

- that the dummy prom is merely a disguised direct rescue;
- that Yukino's acceptance of a contest authorizes every tactic Hachiman later uses;
- that Haruno's diagnosis is narrator truth;
- that Hachiman's claim “unwanted help disproves codependency” is logically valid;
- that Yukino's decision to end the relation is narratively ratified as healthy;
- that pain authenticates the final answer;
- that the end of one romantic possibility requires the end of Yui-Yukino friendship;
- that “prom saved / relationship lost” is already a complete description of the endpoint.

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

## 3.1 Opening interlude: an answer chosen because no better answer can be found

The opening `Interlude…` establishes a voice caught between the desire for correctness and awareness that the chosen response may remain wrong. The speaker repeatedly looks back, knows the choice may be mistaken, and nevertheless says that this was the only answer available. The language distinguishes `最適解` from `正解`: an optimal or workable solution is not necessarily the one correct answer.

The interlude's governing image is relational distance:

> `つかず離れず傷つかず`

The prior arrangement sought to remain neither too close nor too distant, preserving relation while minimizing injury. But the voice also confesses that it did not even know what it wanted to say. The closing wish is therefore not “avoid pain” but `今度こそはただ正しくありたい` - this time, at least, to be right.

Speaker attribution should remain open. The phrasing resonates with Yukino's later closure attempt, Hachiman's fear of repeating his errors, and Yui's contradictory wish for both ending and continuation. Formally, the safest classification is **voice ambiguity / anticipatory frame**.

## 3.2 Chapter 1: Hachiman recognizes that Yukino's mistakes should belong to Yukino

Hachiman begins Volume 13 knowing that a “model answer” exists. He rejects the idea that such an externally legible answer can automatically become `俺の、俺たちの答え`. That linguistic resistance is important: after Volume 12's diagnostic vocabulary, he refuses to let a socially available explanation settle what the trio should choose.

His conversation with Hiratsuka clarifies the autonomy issue. Hachiman recognizes that Yukino's `選択も挑戦も失敗も諦観も` ought to belong to her. The phrase is stronger than merely saying “she should succeed alone.” It grants her ownership of **failure and relinquishment** as well as success.

Yet Hachiman cannot convert that recognition into noninvolvement. He states:

> `少なくとも、関わらないって選択肢はない`

and then goes further:

> `雪ノ下の意思がどうであろうと、俺の行動には関わりがない`

This is the volume's first major ethical warning. Hachiman is correctly separating **his own agency** from Yukino's right to choose, but he risks turning that distinction into a license: her will does not determine whether he acts. That formulation is formally compatible with respecting another person only if his action does not usurp their choices. The rest of the volume tests whether he can maintain that boundary.

Hiratsuka supplies the governing correction:

> `大事なのは関わり方`

The question is not simply action versus nonaction. It is **how one becomes involved**. Hachiman himself understands the complication: helping ordinarily presupposes that the recipient wants help, while here their intentions conflict. Hiratsuka invokes the Service Club's old rule for competing ideas: if their justices collide, compete. This does not authorize arbitrary manipulation. It supplies a procedural possibility in which neither side must immediately submit to the other's answer.

## 3.3 Chapter 2: direct assistance is refused; competition is subsequently accepted

Iroha initially blocks Hachiman's attempt to intervene. She sees that Yukino is trying seriously and asks the correct beneficiary-centered question:

> `それが、雪乃先輩のためにならなくても、ですか？`

This question prevents “help” from being defined exclusively by Hachiman's motive. If intervention undermines what Yukino is trying to accomplish, good intention is insufficient.

When Hachiman finally speaks to Yukino, he asks directly:

> `プロム、俺が手伝ってもいいか`

Yukino's response is a renewed refusal. She says their previous pattern ends with her depending completely upon him and that she wants that pattern changed. Hachiman says responsibility for the relationship is shared and eventually makes his personal motive explicit:

> `俺は、お前を……助けたいと思ってる`

Yukino answers:

> `ありがとう。でも、もういいの。……それだけで充分よ`

This is not ambiguous authorization. She appreciates the desire and rejects the proposed aid.

Hachiman then says:

> `わかった。もう言わない。お前を手伝ったりしない`

and immediately introduces the loophole:

> `だが、対立しないとは言ってない`

This move should neither be romanticized nor flattened. His **initial declaration** of competition is unilateral: `俺が勝手にやる`. He is not yet asking permission. But Yukino subsequently chooses to opt into a formal wager. The terms become reciprocal: each pursues a prom by their own method, and the winner receives one demand from the loser. Yukino verifies the terms and declares, with characteristic competitiveness:

> `負けず嫌いなの、私`

The authority state is therefore layered:

- **direct help**: explicitly refused;
- **Hachiman independently acting in the institutional field**: declared without permission;
- **the wager/contest as a relational procedure**: subsequently and explicitly accepted by Yukino.

This is a more ethical arrangement than unilateral rescue because Yukino retains her project and gains the ability to defeat Hachiman. But acceptance of competition does not retroactively bless every tactic either party might use.

## 3.4 Iroha interlude: outsider perception identifies both intimacy and closure

Iroha watches Hachiman and Yukino's exchange and thinks:

> `あんなのほとんど告白だ。それか痴話喧嘩か別れ話`

This is valuable because Iroha is a socially perceptive external observer, but it remains a **character interpretation**, not narratorial fact. What she accurately perceives is the exceptional intimacy and exclusionary intensity of the dyadic exchange: she almost feels erased from the scene.

More importantly, Iroha sees Yukino afterward as simultaneously energized and tearful. Yukino says:

> `これで最後だから。……これでちゃんと終わりにできる`

The contest is therefore already double-coded for Yukino. It is not merely a fair competition. It is a **closure mechanism** through which she hopes to complete something she has decided must end.

That goal is self-authored in one sense, but it should not yet be called ethically final. A person can author an answer and still author a mistaken answer.

## 3.5 Chapter 3: Iroha defines a healthy ending as work, not disappearance

Iroha explains why the prom matters personally. Hiratsuka is leaving. The older students she relies upon will graduate. Iroha has few close friends. She wants a real occasion to send people off and says plainly that the project is for herself: `わたしが後悔しないため` / `わたしのため`.

Her selfishness is not treated as moral contamination. It becomes part of an intelligible, relational motive. She wants to create an ending through the troublesome labor of planning, negotiating, and actually carrying out an event. This offers a positive contrast to both silent disappearance and abstract “closure.”

Iroha's demand that Hachiman `もっとちゃんとしてください` is similarly important. She does not ask him to become pure, transparent, or selfless. She asks him to act more properly within relationships.

Komachi supplies another autonomy control. Hachiman does **not** attempt to manage her graduation trip for her. He recognizes that she is no longer a small child and trusts her. The sibling relation therefore demonstrates that differentiation can occur without relational annihilation.

Yui then asks to help Hachiman with the dummy-prom work. Unlike Yukino, she is not being assigned a role without consent. She volunteers.

## 3.6 Yui interlude: she recognizes the contest as an excuse for closeness

Yui's first interlude calls the contest a `言い訳`. Her insight is not that the contest is meaningless; it is that Hachiman and Yukino still do not know how to become closer without creating a reason such as conflict, a request, work, or competition.

Her most important structural observation is that she and Yukino are `同じで...反対`: similar and opposed at once. Both want something involving Hachiman and the triad; their desired outcomes cannot simply be merged.

Yui also believes she understands what Yukino's earlier request will lead to and sees only one path for granting it, but internally insists that path is `絶対ダメ`. She wants to remain connected `どんな形でも`. She knows even that wish may become another excuse.

This is not passive longing. Yui has enough information to understand the choice architecture, enough self-awareness to recognize her own rationalizations, and enough desire to resist an ending that would erase the rest of the relationship.

## 3.7 Chapter 4: the dummy prom as engineered choice architecture

The dummy-prom strategy is conceptually precise. Hachiman observes that the current institutional decision is binary: accept or reject Yukino's prom. He wants to introduce a second prom proposal so that the decision becomes **A versus B**. If B is sufficiently objectionable, “reject all proms” may become psychologically less salient.

In his own reasoning, the additional option can function to **erase the rejection option**.

Yui immediately notices the flaw: the adults can reject both. Her correction is important because it prevents Hachiman's cleverness from appearing omnipotent.

Zaimokuza later names the logic in terms of `ダブルバインド` and `誤前提提示`. Whatever the exact technical adequacy of that terminology, the ethical structure is clear: the dummy prom is **choice-architecture manipulation**.

But it is crucial to identify the target correctly. Hachiman is not trying to make Yukino personally choose a predetermined romantic or educational answer. He is manipulating an **institutional decision environment** occupied by parents and school authorities so that Yukino's already-authored prom proposal becomes comparatively acceptable.

That is still ethically contestable. It is deception-adjacent and strategically coercive. But it differs materially from Volume 7, where Hachiman directly preempted Tobe's ability to confess, or Volume 8, where he treated Yukino's own stated candidacy as inferior evidence to his motive theory.

## 3.8 Hachiman begins correcting his collaborator ethics before the plan succeeds

The dummy prom also reveals genuine growth.

At first Hachiman considers gathering helpers by obscuring how disposable their work may be and apologizing later. He notices Yui's disapproval and stops. The narration recognizes:

> `きっと俺は既にまちがえている`

Rather than treating that awareness as proof that only the outcome matters, he changes the recruitment method.

Totsuka's question is simple and devastating:

> `八幡はどうしたいの？`

He does not ask for a technical briefing alone. He asks Hachiman to state the desire behind the operation.

Hachiman eventually answers in his own words. The prom itself is not his real object. Yukino does not want his cooperation. Nevertheless:

> he wants **her prom** to be realized.

He then tells the potential collaborators the dummy proposal may be sacrificial and their work may be discarded. This dramatically improves informed consent.

Kawasaki refuses because she is already helping Yukino. Hachiman accepts the refusal without punishment or manipulation and asks her to keep helping Yukino. Totsuka offers bounded assistance. Hachiman replies:

> `ありがとう、頼りにしてる`

and the narration recognizes the value of having someone he can honestly rely upon.

This is one of Volume 13's strongest positive deltas. Hachiman's **method toward collaborators** has improved even while his **method toward the institutional decision system** remains manipulative.

## 3.9 Chapter 5: a project designed to fail becomes real social infrastructure

Recruitment continues through the Gaming Club and other students. Not everyone initially wants a prom. Hachiman persuades them partly through future interests: if prom becomes institutionalized, later versions could reflect their own tastes. The participants eventually articulate reasons of their own.

The dummy project is therefore not pure altruism and not pure exploitation. It is a coalition built from mixed motives.

Hachiman's strategy requires enough reality to become credible. Contacts, planning work, organization names, location research, and future possibilities are assembled so observers will believe the rival prom **could** exist. The central principle is not whether it can actually be held but whether decision-makers think Hachiman's group might try.

This is strategic ambiguity and legitimacy theater.

Yet the project produces real by-products. Later cooperation with Tamanawa becomes plausible. Participants acquire experience. Networks form. A deliberately staged institution can therefore create **non-fake consequences** even when its immediate stated purpose is instrumental.

That result matters to the series-wide performance ledger: an artificial beginning does not automatically make every later product counterfeit.

## 3.10 Yui and Hachiman: ordinary intimacy beneath instrumental work

The dummy-prom labor places Hachiman and Yui in extended ordinary proximity: design work, food, a net café, a film, joking, shared fatigue, and a trip to the shore for visual material. The work pretext is real, but the ordinary intimacy cannot be reduced to work.

Yui chooses the sea partly because it connects to the sunset they have repeatedly seen from school and the Service Club. The location is therefore an archive of shared ordinary time rather than a generic romantic set piece.

Hachiman's film metaphor gives three possibilities: finish the movie, restart it, or continue pretending not to see what is happening. But the credits begin regardless. Time makes non-choice consequential.

Yui's following interlude is exceptionally explicit in its contradiction. She asks for delay and simultaneously promises proper ending. She wants the lie she is telling herself to become true. She asks that the relationship be properly ended with Yukino - and finally asks:

> `終わらせないで`

The contradiction is the evidence. Yui is not too confused to know what she wants; she wants **incompatible goods** and knows that fact.

## 3.11 Chapter 6: Yui refuses Yukino's self-removal; Hayama rejects inherited responsibility

Yui tells Yukino openly that she is helping Hachiman. Yukino says she knows. Yui says she intends to `ちゃんとする` and therefore Yukino's request will not be granted. Yukino replies that she hopes Yui's wish will be granted.

Yui then asks whether Yukino actually knows what Yui wants. Yukino answers that it is probably the same as her own.

Yui refuses that presumed equivalence.

Her counterargument is embodied and future-facing. She wants lunches, sleepovers, trips, April, years, and decades. She intends to keep doing things with Yukino. This is decisive evidence that **romantic competition does not exhaust the Yui-Yukino relationship**. Whatever happens with Hachiman, Yui does not consent to a solution in which Yukino removes herself from their future friendship.

Hayama supplies another responsibility correction. His childhood regret centers on Yukino's old isolation: she said she could manage alone and refused help, and he regrets not doing more. But when Hayama implicitly places that unfinished responsibility onto Hachiman, Hachiman answers:

> `その後悔はお前のもんだろ。勝手に俺に託すんじゃねぇよ`

This is an excellent anti-proxy-responsibility principle. Another person's regret does not create Hachiman's obligation.

The irony is deliberate: Hachiman has spent much of the series taking on burdens partly because others' failures or desires made him feel responsible. Here he can finally tell Hayama that inherited guilt is not a legitimate command.

## 3.12 Hachiman's codependency “proof” is not logically sufficient

Hachiman eventually states his experiment:

> `あいつが助けを必要としてなくて、それでも俺が助けたいと思うなら……、それは共依存なんかじゃない。それが証明できればいい`

The emotional discovery inside this sentence is important. Hachiman wants to help even when being needed is not the condition. That weakens the idea that his attachment can be wholly explained as a reward loop of being depended upon.

But the inference is too strong.

A person can want to help someone who does not need help **and still participate in a codependent or controlling pattern**. The motivational fact does not prove the structural diagnosis false. Volume 13 knows this, and Haruno later attacks exactly the logical gap.

Hayama asks whether Hachiman knows the name of the feeling. Hachiman calls it `男の意地`. This is classic comic camouflage and desire denial: a deliberately inadequate masculine label covering a motive he refuses to name directly.

## 3.13 Hayama interlude: a rival theory of counterfeit and authenticity

Hayama privately calls Hachiman's explanation a `大嘘`. He then asks Haruno about `共依存`, which exposes the degree to which the diagnosis has become an interpretive object shared among several characters.

Haruno rejects substitutes:

> `そんなの、紛い物じゃない。私が見たいのは本物だけ`

Hayama offers the counterposition:

> `そこから成長する想いだってある`

Haruno rejects even that possibility. Hayama, however, reaches one of the most important authenticity formulations in the volume:

> `たとえそれが紛い物であったとしても、この世でただ一つの歪な贋作であるならば、誰も偽物などと呼べないはずだ`

This is not narrator doctrine. It is Hayama's answer to Haruno. But it substantially expands the `本物` ledger.

The proposition is: **origin impurity does not settle present ontology**. Something may begin as imitation, compromise, role, or counterfeit and through singular history become unrepeatable. That does not prove it morally healthy. It does challenge purity-based authenticity.

Hayama also sees something psychologically important in Haruno: she wounds or disenchants what she cares about before someone else can do so, denying herself the vulnerability of hope. When he asks whether she hates the people involved, she answers:

> `いいえ、大好きよ`

Haruno's severity therefore should not be flattened into indifference. Her insistence on exposure may itself be entangled with attachment and defensive pessimism.

## 3.14 Chapter 7: outside observers, photography, and the limits of interpretation

The chapter title explicitly frames Ebina through the lens: `レンズ越しに、海老名姫菜が見る風景は`. The photography sequence creates a formal metaphor for outside observation without making the observer omniscient.

Hachiman recognizes that Ebina's interpretation contains insight while not necessarily matching his own conclusion. This is a healthy epistemic development: **third-party interpretation can be informative without being sovereign**.

The same principle should govern Haruno.

The beach photography also gives Yui and Miura a space of friendship independent of Hachiman's internal drama. The fixed-page imagery emphasizes distance, cold shore, posed bodies, and observers behind lenses. These are useful paratextual emphases but should not be made into one-to-one romantic symbols.

Tamanawa reappears as another longitudinal correction. Hachiman notes that people change. More importantly, the supposedly fake dummy-prom infrastructure can now support a plausible future collaboration. Stagecraft has generated institutional capital.

## 3.15 Haruno confrontation: altered wording does not necessarily alter mechanism

Hachiman asks Haruno to leak the dummy-prom plan to the relevant parent network. She asks why he is doing any of this after hearing her diagnosis.

Hachiman's answer is deliberately autonomy-oriented: Yukino did not ask; he is doing it on his own; it is `自己満足`. The implicit proof is that acting without being needed should sever the codependency loop.

Haruno answers:

> `雪乃ちゃんは助けを求めてない、君は勝手にやってる……だから、共依存じゃない……そんなの言葉の上だけ`

and argues that the result may be unchanged.

On this narrow point, her criticism is strong. Hachiman himself admits the move contains wordplay/evasion. **Renaming an intervention “self-satisfaction” does not by itself prove the reinforcement mechanism has vanished.**

But Haruno then extends that insight into a cure theory: Yukino chose independence and ending, so Hachiman should simply watch.

Yui refuses the extension. Mere watching or distance can allow everything to die. Staying near enough to participate may be necessary even to end something properly.

This is an important V2 distinction:

- Haruno may correctly identify a **logical weakness** in Hachiman's proof;
- that does not make her proposed **separation cure** automatically correct.

Hachiman and Yui share the phrase `ちゃんと終わらせる`. Yet when Haruno asks whether any ending is acceptable - even one nobody wants - Hachiman says yes. That is ethically worrying. “Ending” risks becoming another purity object, valued because decisiveness feels morally superior to ambiguity.

Haruno calls Hachiman's `奉仕精神` explanation a lie. He responds that if there are more adequate words, they are not words for Haruno. This is a meaningful privacy boundary. Refusing an external diagnostician access to one's deepest motive is not itself dishonesty.

## 3.16 Haruno interlude: the diagnostician wants proof too

Haruno's own viewpoint sharply narrows any temptation to treat her as the detached possessor of truth.

She imagines the four-person table as too large. She rereads a book whose ending she already knows, hoping that perhaps after the end mark there might be a true ending she somehow missed. Her desire is explicit:

> `嘘偽りない唯一つの正しい結末`

and:

> `誰かが、証明してくれればそれで構わない`

Haruno therefore wants **someone else to prove** that a true ending exists.

This does not invalidate her observations. It changes their evidence class. Her diagnoses emerge from a person with an invested epistemic desire: she wants demonstration that genuine relation is possible and resists accepting convenient substitutes.

## 3.17 Yui confronts Haruno: feeling can be certain without defining the relation

Yui returns to challenge Haruno's account, partly to protect Hachiman. Haruno interprets Yui less through jealousy than through `庇護欲`: a desire to protect.

Haruno then revises the codependency map and says that Hachiman depends on Yui while Yui enjoys that dependence; she calls that connection the most severe part of the structure.

Again, this is a **high-value character diagnosis**, not textual fact.

Yui rejects the label. Haruno asks whether what Yui feels can be called `本物`. Yui answers:

> `そんなの、わかんない`

But Yui does know the force of her own feeling:

> `あたしの全部が、痛いくらい、好きだって悲鳴を上げてる`

This should be handled carefully against Volume 12. Volume 12 rejected pain as sufficient proof that an answer or relationship is genuine. Yui is not claiming that pain proves the relation morally `本物`. She is saying that pain makes **the existence and intensity of her own love** impossible for her to deny.

That is a first-person epistemic claim, not a universal authenticity criterion.

## 3.18 Chapter 8: the dummy plan loses, then changes the institutional speaker

The dummy-prom information reaches Yukino's mother. She recognizes almost immediately that it is a decoy. She also identifies the strategic weakness: adding choices does not resolve the root conflict.

Hachiman understands that the initial tactic has “lost.”

His final move is more sophisticated than simply making the dummy proposal scarier. He reclassifies Yukino's mother from opponent to **intermediary**. Rather than defeating her, he asks her to persuade the other parents.

The most ethically ambiguous card is his own name. Hachiman realizes his name has no inherent argumentative force, but the hearer may attach meaning to it because of the traffic accident and prior family contact. He thereby deploys relational/social history without explicitly threatening anyone.

This is manipulation through **implication and social capital**. It is also less destructive than blackmail: he does not state a threat or demand a quid pro quo. The tactic works because Yukino's mother already occupies the network through which concerned parents can be persuaded.

The prom ultimately proceeds using **Yukino's modified proposal**. Hachiman's dummy does not replace her project. Institutionally, the result preserves Yukino's authorship of the event proposal while acknowledging that Hachiman has altered the surrounding conditions under which it can survive.

That is precisely why the ethical answer remains mixed rather than binary.

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

## 4.1 Strong social and institutional diagnosis remains a major competence

Hachiman is exceptionally good at reading systems in Volume 13. He identifies:

- how informal `忖度` can block a formally approved project;
- how a binary decision can be altered by adding a rival option;
- how parent networks transmit concern;
- how organizational labels create perceived legitimacy;
- how strategic ambiguity can make a weak project look institutionally live;
- how speaker identity changes persuasive force;
- how Yukino's mother is more useful as intermediary than enemy.

These are real competencies. V2 should not downgrade them simply because his motives are emotionally entangled.

## 4.2 His autonomy principle is stronger than his authorization practice

Hachiman explicitly states that Yukino's choices, challenges, failures, and resignation should belong to her. This is one of his strongest autonomy principles yet.

Yet he also says her will does not determine whether **he** acts. The statement is philosophically defensible at the abstract level - two agents each possess agency - but dangerous in relational practice because one person's “independent action” can reshape another person's option set.

Volume 13 is therefore a test of whether Hachiman understands **relational externalities of self-authored action**.

## 4.3 He improves his collaborator ethics in observable ways

The most important behavioral evidence of growth is not his internal theory but how he treats collaborators:

- he recognizes that misleading people and apologizing later is wrong;
- he tells them the work may be discarded;
- he states the underlying purpose more honestly;
- he accepts Kawasaki's refusal;
- he honors role boundaries;
- he receives Totsuka's bounded support;
- he says `頼りにしてる` without converting reliance into self-abasement.

This is not cosmetic. It is a meaningful procedural improvement over earlier cost-allocation behavior.

## 4.4 “Unwanted help disproves codependency” is an inference, not a valid proof

Hachiman's most important reasoning error is the leap from motive to mechanism:

- premise: Yukino does not need/want his help;
- premise: he wants to help anyway;
- conclusion: therefore the relation cannot be codependent.

At most, the premises show that **being needed is not a necessary condition of his desire to act**. They do not establish that the intervention cannot reinforce problematic patterns.

Haruno's criticism is therefore analytically useful even if her broader diagnosis remains non-sovereign.

## 4.5 `男の意地` is deliberate desire camouflage

When Hayama asks Hachiman to name the feeling motivating him, Hachiman calls it `男の意地`.

This should be classified as:

- comic camouflage;
- desire denial;
- socially legible masculine euphemism;
- not an adequate psychological explanation.

The volume itself makes the inadequacy obvious through Hayama's response and the surrounding relational intensity.

## 4.6 Hachiman correctly rejects proxy guilt

His rejection of Hayama's childhood regret is one of his cleanest ethical insights:

> `その後悔はお前のもんだろ。勝手に俺に託すんじゃねぇよ`

Hachiman distinguishes **another person's regret** from **his own reason for acting**. This is an important correction of his historical tendency to absorb responsibility simply because he can imagine a way to pay the cost.

## 4.7 He still risks moralizing decisiveness

When Haruno asks whether even an unwanted ending would be acceptable, Hachiman says yes. This is not necessarily a stable doctrine, but it reveals a danger: after long pathological ambiguity, **ending itself** can begin to look morally clean.

Volume 13 repeatedly warns against that simplification. Iroha values endings because they are authored through shared work. Yukino seeks ending partly as proof of autonomy. Yui wants ending and continuation simultaneously. Haruno wants a “correct” ending. These are not equivalent.

# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Hikigaya Hachiman

**State change:** Hachiman becomes procedurally better without becoming ethically settled.

He now:

- recognizes Yukino's ownership of failure as well as success;
- asks for permission to help before being refused;
- distinguishes direct assistance from rivalry;
- changes recruitment tactics when he notices they are exploitative;
- discloses sacrificial work to collaborators;
- accepts refusal;
- relies openly on Totsuka and others;
- rejects proxy guilt from Hayama;
- keeps his deepest motive private from Haruno rather than accepting her demand for a label;
- explicitly recognizes that Hachiman and Yukino cannot decide a triadic relationship without Yui.

But he also:

- acts despite Yukino's stated will;
- constructs a manipulative institutional choice architecture;
- treats a motivational fact as proof against a structural diagnosis;
- remains attracted to ending as a morally cleaner state than ambiguity;
- continues to hide desire behind `男の意地`, `責任`, `奉仕精神`, and procedural language.

The V13 Hachiman is therefore neither “relapsed” nor “cured.” He is a person whose ethical vocabulary has outpaced the consistency of his practice.

## 5.2 Yukinoshita Yukino

**State change:** Yukino increasingly authors her method, but her chosen endpoint is self-removal-oriented and remains contestable.

She:

- repeats that she does not want Hachiman's direct help;
- explains why prior reliance felt totalizing;
- voluntarily accepts the contest and its wager;
- becomes energized by rivalry rather than reduced to passive rescue recipient;
- explicitly enjoys and values the time spent with Hachiman and Yui;
- says she did not know it was permissible to rely on someone;
- calls her own prior relation `依存`;
- uses the wager to make an explicit request about Yui.

Her endpoint is genuinely self-authored speech:

> `由比ヶ浜さんのお願いを叶えてあげて`

Hachiman checks:

> `それがお前の願いでいいか`

and she answers:

> `ええ、これが私の願い`

This matters. Yukino is not merely being dragged out of the triangle.

But authorship is not the same as ethical finality. Her request allocates Hachiman toward Yui and thereby tries to settle the future of other people. It can therefore be simultaneously:

- self-authored;
- caring toward Yui;
- self-removing;
- potentially paternalistic toward the people whose desires she is organizing.

The volume itself keeps that ambiguity alive.

## 5.3 Yuigahama Yui

**State change:** Yui becomes the strongest explicit defender of **relationship after romantic non-equivalence**.

She:

- volunteers to help Hachiman;
- recognizes the dummy contest as a relational pretext;
- understands Yukino's self-removal trajectory;
- works competently as art director and collaborator;
- admits she wants ordinary dyadic time with Hachiman to continue;
- says both “end it properly” and “don't end it”;
- directly refuses the idea that losing Hachiman romantically would require losing Yukino;
- challenges Haruno's codependency diagnosis;
- can say `わかんない` about `本物` while remaining certain that she loves.

Her future-oriented declaration to Yukino - lunches, sleepovers, trips, April, years, decades - is especially important. Yui refuses to let the romantic triangle totalize female friendship.

That is a major correction to route-like readings of her character.

## 5.4 Isshiki Iroha

**State change:** Iroha becomes an increasingly authoritative observer of both institutional and emotional procedure.

She owns the prom for her own reasons, recognizes Yukino's determination, challenges Hachiman's beneficiary assumptions, perceives the Hachiman/Yukino exchange as intensely relational, and wants endings to be made through actual work rather than merely declared.

Her selfishness remains productive rather than disqualifying. `わたしのため` is a mature enough reason to care about an institution.

## 5.5 Hayama Hayato

**State change:** Hayama moves from mirror/rival toward explicit theorist of inherited guilt and imperfect authenticity.

His refusal to let the school organization officially join Hachiman's dummy while allowing individuals latitude is a sophisticated institutional compromise.

His childhood regret about Yukino remains unresolved, but Hachiman refuses to inherit it. Hayama's own interlude then shows that he knows he has used Hachiman as a proxy because he lacks a path to redemption.

Most importantly, Hayama supplies the “unique counterfeit” theory: singular history may produce something that cannot meaningfully be dismissed as fake even if its origin was compromised.

## 5.6 Yukinoshita Haruno

**State change:** Haruno becomes less, not more, omniscient as the text gives her interiority.

Her codependency critique remains powerful. She correctly observes that changing labels does not automatically alter mechanism. But her viewpoint reveals that she herself wants proof of a single true ending. She rereads the already-ended book, looking beyond the end mark.

Her insistence on exposing counterfeits is therefore not purely clinical. It is bound to her own longing, disappointment, and defensive refusal to hope cheaply.

## 5.7 Hiratsuka Shizuka

**State change:** Hiratsuka continues as the most stable procedural adult.

Her key insight is `大事なのは関わり方`. She neither tells Hachiman to abandon Yukino nor authorizes rescue. She reframes the conflict into a form the young people can own and asks him to trust what they have built.

Her impending departure also gives the volume's endings theme material weight.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Hachiman - Yukino: from refused aid to consensual rivalry

The relationship changes through a sequence that should remain disaggregated:

1. Hachiman wants to help.
2. Yukino refuses.
3. Hachiman declares independent opposition.
4. Yukino voluntarily accepts a contest/wager.
5. Both pursue separate methods.
6. Hachiman's dummy alters the institutional environment around Yukino's project.
7. Yukino's proposal survives.
8. Yukino invokes the wager to seek closure.

This is not straightforward consent to help. It is **consent to rivalry**, followed by an intervention whose effects exceed the narrow dyadic contest.

The endpoint is equally layered. Yukino admits the relationship was enjoyable and comfortable. She says she did not know reliance was permitted. She then calls the current relation counterfeit-like and seeks its end.

Her negative judgment is a **character self-judgment**, not an authorial theorem.

## 6.2 Hachiman - Yui: ordinary partnership becomes more explicit while ending approaches

Hachiman and Yui work together for much of the volume. Their collaboration is low-friction, intimate, and increasingly honest at the practical level. Yui sees his methods, corrects them, makes him ask properly, and remains beside him.

The net-café and movie sequence demonstrates that ordinary dyadic warmth is not imaginary. This makes Yui's eventual loss more ethically serious: the relation she wants is built from actual shared life, not merely romantic projection.

## 6.3 Yukino - Yui: romantic conflict does not cancel friendship

This is arguably the volume's most important relationship correction.

Yukino tries to use self-removal to grant Yui's wish. Yui refuses the premise that Yukino can settle Yui's future by disappearing. Her answer is not abstract morality but future practice: she intends to keep eating, sleeping over, traveling, entering April, and accumulating years with Yukino.

The Yui-Yukino relationship therefore becomes explicitly **non-substitutable by the romantic contest**.

## 6.4 Hachiman - Hayama: proxy responsibility is refused

Their relationship develops through a cleaner moral boundary. Hayama cannot redeem his childhood failure by handing responsibility for Yukino to Hachiman. Hachiman refuses that inheritance.

This is a notable reversal of earlier Hachiman, who often accepted costs others tacitly wanted him to bear.

## 6.5 Trio: dyadic closure authority is explicitly denied

The most important line for the triadic ledger comes near the end:

> `これは俺とお前が勝手に決めることじゃない。由比ヶ浜も勝負に加わってる`

Hachiman explicitly recognizes that he and Yukino cannot decide the relation alone because Yui is an affected participant.

This substantially complicates any reading in which Yukino's closure request automatically settles the trio. The volume itself introduces a jurisdictional limit on dyadic solution-making.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

## 7.1 The beneficiary problem is explicit from the beginning

Iroha asks whether Hachiman's involvement is still justified if it is not good for Yukino. This restores the recipient's standing at the moment Hachiman is most tempted to define “help” by his own desire.

## 7.2 Direct aid is refused

Yukino's `もういい` / desire to do the work herself is clear. Hachiman does not possess authorization to become her collaborator in the real prom.

## 7.3 Rivalry creates a new authorization structure

The contest is different. Yukino voluntarily agrees to it and verifies the wager. That gives Hachiman standing as a rival project leader, not as Yukino's helper.

The distinction matters:

- he may pursue his project;
- she may pursue hers;
- each can win;
- the contest itself becomes mutually acknowledged.

However, a wager between two students cannot authorize manipulation of every third party. External stakeholder ethics remain separate.

## 7.4 The dummy prom manipulates institutions, not Yukino's personal answer

Hachiman's core tactic is to change the decision set facing parent/school stakeholders. This is a form of engineered pressure. It should be classified as:

- strategic social engineering;
- choice-architecture manipulation;
- partial deception/ambiguity;
- not direct substitution for Yukino's work;
- not equivalent to interpersonal consent.

The moral evaluation is therefore mixed.

## 7.5 Collaborator consent is substantially improved

Hachiman's clearest growth is here. He abandons the idea of exploiting helpers through ignorance. He discloses the possibility that their work will be wasted, states the actual strategic purpose, and accepts refusal.

This is precisely the kind of improvement the V2 method was designed to capture: **same strategic Hachiman, better authorization discipline**.

## 7.6 Third-party consequences remain real

The dummy proposal intentionally triggers parent anxiety. It uses community organizations and potential participants as credibility signals. Hachiman's own name is eventually used as a socially meaningful signal because adults may associate him with the prior accident/family contact.

The plan does not remain ethically private simply because Hachiman says he is doing it `勝手に`.

## 7.7 Promise and desire explain action but do not authorize method

Volume 12 ended with promise-based motivation. Volume 13 shows why that distinction matters. Hachiman may genuinely want to help for reasons independent of being needed. That makes his attachment harder to reduce to pathology. It still does not answer whether each tactic is permissible.

## 7.8 Yukino's final request is self-authored but triad-affecting

Yukino explicitly confirms that asking Hachiman to grant Yui's wish is **her** wish. That deserves full evidentiary weight.

But the request concerns two other people's romantic futures. Therefore the same autonomy principle Hachiman has learned applies back to Yukino: she can choose to withdraw herself, but she cannot single-handedly determine what Hachiman or Yui must desire, nor whether Yui consents to losing her.

This is not a contradiction in Yukino's subjecthood. It is the next level of the ethics problem: **self-authorship can still produce actions that constrain other subjects**.

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 Prom governance is not reducible to romance

The prom remains a real institution with:

- students;
- school administration;
- parent stakeholders;
- community organizations;
- precedent for later cohorts;
- publicity/reputation concerns;
- logistical credibility;
- competing project proposals.

Hachiman's strategy only works because these systems possess their own incentives.

## 8.2 `忖度` reveals governance by anticipated objection

One of the volume's institutional concerns is the way formal approval can be hollowed out by anticipated pressure. Actors self-restrict because they expect objections from more powerful stakeholders. The conflict therefore concerns not only explicit vetoes but the **social production of non-action**.

## 8.3 The dummy prom is opposition research as governance strategy

Hachiman converts the dispute from “prom or no prom” into “which prom.” The tactic resembles political agenda-setting: actors do not merely choose among preferences; the available menu is itself strategically constructed.

This is a sophisticated social-system insight and an ethically non-neutral one.

## 8.4 Yukino's mother is not merely an antagonist

The final move works when Hachiman stops treating Yukino's mother as an opponent and identifies her as the person best positioned to persuade other parents. This is a major improvement in institutional reasoning: the actor who blocks a project may simultaneously possess the authority needed to legitimate it.

## 8.5 Tamanawa demonstrates institutional learning

Tamanawa's reappearance matters because he has changed after earlier failure. Hachiman explicitly recognizes change in others. The dummy project's contact with him may support later real collaboration.

This is evidence that institutional performances can have developmental consequences beyond their original purpose.

## 8.6 Iroha's prom remains its own youth-governance project

Iroha's motive to create a proper send-off for departing people gives the prom independent social value. Her leadership should not disappear behind Yukino/Hachiman analysis.

# 9. Authenticity / `本物` ledger

## 9.1 `本物` is no longer a purity test the text can sustain

Volume 13 repeatedly places authenticity against compromised origins:

- the dummy prom is deliberately fake yet creates real work and relationships;
- Hachiman's excuses can be false while his desire underneath them remains real;
- Yui does not know whether her relation is `本物` but knows she loves;
- Hayama proposes that a unique counterfeit may become unrepeatable;
- the closing interlude calls the relation both counterfeit and irreplaceable.

This makes any equation `fake origin = fake relation` increasingly untenable.

## 9.2 Haruno retains the strict-purity position

Haruno says she wants only `本物` and rejects `紛い物`. Her standard is valuable as pressure against rationalization, but the volume gives it counterarguments rather than ratification.

## 9.3 Hayama's “unique counterfeit” is the strongest rival formulation

Hayama's proposition should be preserved in the longitudinal ledger as a distinct character theory:

> a distorted imitation may become so singular through history that calling it merely fake loses explanatory power.

This aligns with earlier evidence that roles, performances, and compromised beginnings can acquire owned meaning over time.

## 9.4 Yui separates certainty of feeling from ontology of relationship

Yui cannot answer whether the relation is `本物`. She can answer that she loves. That is epistemically disciplined: first-person feeling is more accessible than total relational ontology.

## 9.5 Pain-as-proof reappears at the end and should be downgraded

The closing interlude - contextually likely Yukino, though formally unattributed - says that the more painful the answer is, the more the speaker can believe it is genuine.

But the voice immediately admits:

> `他に確かめる術を知らない`

It does not know another way to verify.

This directly conflicts with Volume 12's opening refusal of a cold, cruel, merely sad `本物`. Pain therefore should be classified as a **fragile personal verification heuristic**, not a valid series-level criterion.

## 9.6 `掛け替えのない、替えのきかない偽物`

The final paradox is crucial. The relation can be called counterfeit while also irreplaceable and non-substitutable.

This undermines binary ontology. If something is singular, historically constituted, loved, painful, and nonreplaceable, “fake” may describe a perceived structural flaw without exhausting what the relation is.

# 10. Dependency / autonomy ledger

## 10.1 Yukino's dependency claim receives first-person weight

Near the end, Yukino says she was surprised that she naturally expected Hachiman to get the prom through and then says:

> `そう思うくらい、……あなたに依存していたの`

This is stronger evidence than Haruno's diagnosis because it is Yukino's own retrospective self-description.

It should still be scope-limited: she is describing the degree to which she had come to expect Hachiman's intervention, not declaring every form of reliance on him pathological.

## 10.2 Hachiman demonstrates healthier reliance on collaborators

`ありがとう、頼りにしてる` is not a small line. Hachiman can now explicitly depend on Totsuka and others without treating reliance as humiliation or moral debt.

The volume therefore contains **dependency concern and positive reliance at the same time**. Any framework that treats all need as pathology cannot explain the text.

## 10.3 Competition may reduce substitution without proving structural health

The contest prevents Hachiman from taking Yukino's real project away from her. That is a genuine autonomy gain.

But because his dummy is designed to make her project succeed, the deeper reinforcement question remains open. Reduced substitution is evidence of improvement, not proof of cure.

## 10.4 Yui refuses autonomy-through-disappearance

Yui's decades-long friendship claim is one of the strongest anti-isolation pieces of evidence in the series. She does not believe healthy differentiation requires Yukino to vanish from her life.

## 10.5 Hayama's proxy-regret episode separates care from inherited obligation

Hachiman's refusal to inherit Hayama's regret protects his own authorship. He may help Yukino, but not because Hayama's past failure commands him.

## 10.6 Yukino's closure attempt remains an autonomy experiment, not a proven cure

Yukino wants to show she can choose and end the relation. That is self-authored.

But ending may become a **performance of independence** just as helping can become a performance of usefulness. Volume 13 does not yet prove that separation is the only or healthiest way to stop dependency reinforcement.

# 11. Japanese language and voice

## 11.1 Hachiman's strategic lexicon remains managerial

The dummy-prom sections repeatedly use a vocabulary of options, proof, responsibility, victory, opposition, and design: `選択肢`, `勝負`, `責任`, `証明`, `ダミー`, `対立`, `言い訳`.

The recurrence matters because Hachiman continues to translate emotional conflict into solvable procedural form. What changes is that he now sometimes catches the ethical consequences of doing so.

## 11.2 `勝手` remains ethically double-edged

Hachiman repeatedly frames his action as something he does `勝手に` - on his own initiative and responsibility. Earlier volumes often used `勝手` as self-indictment for unilateral interpretation. Here it becomes a claimed autonomy defense.

The lexical reversal is important:

- `勝手に` can mean taking responsibility for one's own action;
- it can also mean acting without the other person's consent.

Volume 13 deliberately exploits both senses.

## 11.3 `ちゃんと` becomes a cross-character procedural demand

Iroha, Yui, Yukino, and Hachiman repeatedly seek to do things `ちゃんと`: end properly, ask properly, choose properly, say things properly.

The word carries moral aspiration without specifying one outcome. It is procedural rather than doctrinal.

## 11.4 `終わらせる` is not semantically stable

For different speakers, ending can mean:

- completing an event;
- graduating/saying goodbye;
- closing a relational form;
- stopping ambiguity;
- permitting a new beginning;
- destroying something one still wants.

Therefore repeated `終わる / 終わらせる` language should not be treated as a single thematic proposition.

## 11.5 `紛い物 / 贋作 / 偽物` broaden the counterfeit field

Hayama and Haruno's exchange expands the language beyond the simple `本物 / 偽物` binary. `紛い物` and `贋作` foreground imitation, derivation, and compromised origin. Hayama then challenges whether singularity can transform the status of a counterfeit.

## 11.6 Yui's directness increases without eliminating hedging

Yui can say she loves with bodily certainty while still answering `わかんない` to abstract authenticity. This contrast is a voice-level marker of maturation: she is more willing to state what she knows and withhold claims she cannot justify.

## 11.7 Yukino's final `願い` is syntactically explicit

Hachiman asks whether this is truly her wish. Yukino answers affirmatively. The dialogue therefore gives unusually strong first-person authorship evidence even though the ethical interpretation of the wish remains open.

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertextuality

## 12.1 Strategy language remains comic camouflage

Hachiman's tendency to treat social life as game theory, political maneuvering, or tactical operation remains funny because it is disproportionate to the school-romance setting. But the comedy should not obscure that the strategic models are often genuinely accurate.

## 12.2 `男の意地` is deliberately inadequate genre language

Hachiman's masculine-pride label turns a deeply vulnerable attachment into a conventional rivalry phrase. The joke functions as emotional camouflage.

## 12.3 The movie/credits structure literalizes genre pressure

The net-café film and `エンドロール` imagery create a metafictional problem: characters can pretend not to see the ending, restart, or delay, but narrative time continues. This does not force one romantic outcome. It makes **non-choice itself temporal action**.

## 12.4 Fake project / real consequences is a romantic-comedy structural joke

The dummy prom is an intentionally fake event that requires increasingly real labor, relationships, images, meetings, and institutional contacts. The joke mirrors the authenticity problem without providing a simplistic allegorical answer.

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial test

## 14.1 Alternative: Hachiman simply violates Yukino's refusal

**Supporting evidence:** Yukino explicitly asks him not to help; Hachiman says her will does not determine his action; the dummy is designed to save her project.

**Counterevidence:** Hachiman does not join or take over Yukino's project. Yukino subsequently opts into the contest/wager. His collaborators are increasingly informed. The final institutional result remains Yukino's proposal.

**V2 judgment:** **REVISE to a more precise claim.** Hachiman violates the spirit of complete noninvolvement, but he does not simply repeat direct substitution. He constructs an external rival intervention that preserves Yukino's operational authorship while manipulating the environment around it.

## 14.2 Alternative: the contest proves their relationship is not codependent

**Supporting evidence:** Yukino does not need Hachiman's help; he acts anyway; they compete rather than one depending upon the other; Yukino's project remains hers.

**Counterevidence:** Haruno correctly notes that altered wording can leave the reinforcing pattern intact. Hachiman himself admits the proof contains wordplay. Yukino later says she still naturally expected him to get the prom through.

**V2 judgment:** **REJECT as proof.** The contest is evidence of changed method, not decisive evidence about the total mechanism.

## 14.3 Alternative: Haruno's codependency model is therefore completely correct

**Supporting evidence:** multiple characters use `依存`; Yukino acknowledges dependence; Hachiman enjoys being relied upon; Yui recognizes decision displacement; Hachiman's proof fails.

**Counterevidence:** Haruno is an invested character; her interiority shows a strong desire for a single true ending. She extends mechanism diagnosis into a separation cure without proof. Healthy reliance exists elsewhere in the same volume.

**V2 judgment:** **RETAIN as high-value mechanism hypothesis; reject sovereignty.**

## 14.4 Alternative: Yukino's choice to end the relationship is the mature autonomous answer

**Supporting evidence:** she authors it, uses the wager, states her wish clearly, and sees ending as necessary to escape prior dependency.

**Counterevidence:** Yui explicitly rejects self-removal; Hachiman says a dyad cannot decide the triad; Yukino's wish attempts to allocate Hachiman toward Yui; the closing interlude admits uncertainty and uses pain as weak proof.

**V2 judgment:** **OPEN / not ratified.** Self-authorship is real; correctness remains unresolved.

## 14.5 Alternative: Yui is selfishly refusing to let Yukino go

**Supporting evidence:** Yui wants Hachiman and Yukino both; she asks for delay; she resists Yukino's closure.

**Counterevidence:** Yui explicitly separates friendship from romantic outcome and imagines a future of ordinary life with Yukino independent of Hachiman. She is not merely preserving competitive access to him.

**V2 judgment:** **DOWNGRADE.** Yui's desire is selfish in the ordinary mixed-motive sense, but her defense of continuing friendship is independently grounded.

## 14.6 Alternative: pain proves the final answer is genuine

**Supporting evidence:** the closing interlude says pain helps the speaker believe the chosen answer is `本物`.

**Counterevidence:** the same interlude admits no other verification method; Volume 12 explicitly rejects cruel sadness as authenticity proof; the speaker remains uncertain whether the answer is correct.

**V2 judgment:** **REJECT as series-level criterion; retain as character verification heuristic.**

## 14.7 Alternative: the dummy prom is fake and therefore analytically secondary

**Supporting evidence:** it is designed to be sacrificed.

**Counterevidence:** it creates real work, real consent problems, real contacts, real future collaboration possibilities, real photographs, and real institutional pressure.

**V2 judgment:** **REJECT.** Its falseness is exactly why it is an important authenticity experiment.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

Full-series hindsight is permitted here only to test how Volume 13's evidence was structured, not to overwrite its prospective ambiguity.

## 15.1 The volume is better read as an experiment in method than a diagnosis-resolution chapter

The first pass tended to move quickly from Volume 12's `共依存` diagnosis toward Volume 13 as the phase in which the characters attempt separation. V2 shows that the more precise action is **method substitution**:

- direct help is refused;
- Hachiman adopts rivalry;
- rivalry becomes consensual;
- the dummy manipulates institutional conditions;
- collaboration ethics improve;
- the resulting prom remains Yukino's project.

The experiment does not prove health, but it does produce genuine procedural change.

## 15.2 “Self-removal” remains valid but must be made multi-dimensional

Yukino does attempt to remove herself from the romantic/triadic problem as the solution she currently believes will restore proper boundaries.

But she is not simply sacrificing herself in mute passivity. She is:

- choosing;
- competing;
- enjoying rivalry;
- speaking her dependency interpretation;
- invoking the wager;
- issuing an explicit request.

The better formulation is **self-authored self-removal attempt**, with its authority over others still contested.

## 15.3 Yui's friendship claim prevents route reduction

Hindsight makes it tempting to treat Yui's role only through romantic loss. Volume 13 prospectively blocks that reduction. Her refusal to abandon Yukino is one of the strongest statements of female friendship in the mainline.

## 15.4 Haruno becomes less authoritative when her own longing is visible

Later readers often retroactively turn Haruno into the spokesperson for the novel. Volume 13's own formal architecture works against that. Her private need for somebody to prove a true ending exists makes her a participant in the authenticity problem.

## 15.5 The final interlude is deliberately unstable

Even if contextual attribution to Yukino is strong, its epistemology is weak by its own admission. The speaker chooses pain because no better test is known and prays the ending is correct. Hindsight must not transform prayer into proof.

**V2 state: REVISE.**

> **The prom is saved while Hachiman and Yukino place their relation under a declared closure regime whose authority remains contested by Yui, by Hachiman's own triadic-jurisdiction insight, and by the uncertainty of the closing interlude.**

**V2 state: PRESERVE + STRENGTHEN + qualify.**

Yukino explicitly seeks an ending and asks Hachiman to grant Yui's wish. But V2 must preserve that this is self-authored speech rather than passive surrender. The problem is not lack of agency; it is whether one person's agency can legitimately settle other people's answers.

## 16.7 New V2 claim: Hayama supplies a singular-counterfeit authenticity model

# 18. Cumulative delta

## 18.1 Hachiman epistemic/narratorial ledger

**New gains**

- recognizes Yukino owns failure and resignation as well as success;
- distinguishes his own motive from Hayama's inherited regret;
- accepts collaborator refusal;
- relies openly on others;
- recognizes Haruno's diagnosis as one interpretation rather than total truth;
- recognizes dyadic actors cannot settle a triadic relationship without the third participant.

**Persistent risks**

- translates attachment into strategy;
- treats independent motive as potential license for involvement;
- mistakes motivational evidence for structural proof;
- hides desire behind `責任`, `男の意地`, `奉仕精神`;
- sometimes moralizes decisiveness/ending.

## 18.2 Character-state ledger

- **Yukino:** strong self-authorship; explicit dependency recognition; rivalry energizes her; closure becomes chosen but not ratified; reliance is newly acknowledged as something she had not known was permitted.
- **Yui:** operational collaborator; explicit romantic claimant; explicit future friend to Yukino; refuses route-totalization; challenges external diagnosis.
- **Iroha:** owner of prom meaning; external relationship observer; procedural conscience around endings.
- **Hayama:** rejects simple purity ontology; names compromised relation as potentially singular; acknowledges proxy-regret behavior.
- **Haruno:** diagnosis remains important, but private longing for proof of one true ending becomes explicit.

## 18.3 Relationship-state ledger

- Hachiman/Yukino: **refused direct help -> consensual rivalry -> indirect institutional intervention -> declared closure attempt**.
- Hachiman/Yui: **ordinary dyadic partnership intensifies under approaching ending**.
- Yukino/Yui: **romantic conflict becomes explicit while future friendship is explicitly defended by Yui**.
- Trio: **authority to terminate the relation is no longer safely assignable to a dyad**.

## 18.4 Request/intervention ethics ledger

Volume 13 supplies one of the clearest distinctions in the entire V2 reread:

> **A recipient can refuse direct help while still consenting to rivalry; a rival can preserve the recipient's operational authorship while still manipulating the environment around her; collaborators can consent to the manipulative project more ethically than the institutional targets do; and a good motive can remain insufficient authorization.**

The ethical analysis must therefore remain multi-axis rather than labeling the whole plan “help” or “control.”

## 18.5 Authenticity ledger

The volume weakens purity ontology further:

- fake project -> real consequences;
- false excuse -> real attachment;
- compromised origin -> possible growth;
- counterfeit -> singular and irreplaceable;
- pain -> subjective evidence but not proof;
- `本物` -> unresolved and speaker-dependent.

## 18.6 Dependency/autonomy ledger

Volume 13 confirms real dependency mechanisms while simultaneously demonstrating healthy reliance. The strongest prospective conclusion is:

> **Autonomy cannot be measured by the absence of need. It is better measured by whether people can author requests, refuse roles, accept bounded reliance, survive another person's different answer, and revise the relationship without one person becoming the sole manager of everyone else's future.**

## 18.7 Final prospective freeze before Volume 14

At the exact end of Volume 13:

- the prom will proceed through Yukino's proposal;
- Hachiman's dummy strategy has altered the decision environment but has not proven his codependency thesis;
- Yukino openly states that she had depended upon Hachiman;
- Hachiman and Yukino both speak as though their current relation should end;
- Hachiman explicitly says the two of them cannot decide the triadic relation without Yui;
- Yui has already rejected a future in which romantic resolution means abandoning Yukino;
- Yukino asks Hachiman to grant Yui's wish and confirms that this is her own wish;
- the final voice treats pain as evidence because it lacks another verification method and prays that the ending is correct;
- the relation is simultaneously called counterfeit and irreplaceable.

The resulting V13 freeze is therefore **not** “prom saved, relationship solved.” It is:

> **The institutional problem has been made solvable by strategic intervention, while the relational problem has been moved into an explicitly contested closure procedure. Everyone has more authorship than before, but everyone is still capable of using that authorship to decide too much for somebody else.**

That is the state Volume 14 must inherit prospectively.
