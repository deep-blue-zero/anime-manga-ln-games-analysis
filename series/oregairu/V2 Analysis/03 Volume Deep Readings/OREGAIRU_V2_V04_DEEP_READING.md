## 1.2 Narrative position

Volume 4 follows the Volume 3 reset/restart crisis and its attached birthday material. The immediate relational state is therefore unusually important:

- Yui has returned to the Service Club.
- Hachiman has been told that a mistaken beginning need not permanently determine what follows.
- Yukino has demonstrated that she can ask Hachiman for bounded practical assistance.
- Yui and Yukino are now unmistakably particular friends rather than merely co-members of an institution.
- `本物` has entered the mainline vocabulary, but without a unified definition.
- Hachiman still actively regulates his distance from Yui to prevent what he calls `勘違い`.
- Haruno has appeared, and Yukino has named her public presentation `外面` and `仮面`, but the family system remains largely opaque.

Volume 4 moves the analytical scale outward. The first three volumes primarily examined whether individual relationships could survive wrong beginnings, awkward motives, social performance, and incomplete understanding. Volume 4 places the same questions inside a **group capable of manufacturing a victim**.

## 1.3 Chapter architecture

The eight main chapters are:

1. `こうして比企谷八幡の夏休みが過ぎてゆく。`
2. `どうしても平塚静からは逃げられない。`
3. `誰に対しても葉山隼人はそつがない。`
4. `唐突に、海老名姫菜は布教を開始する。`
5. `ひとり、雪ノ下雪乃は夜空を見上げる。`
6. `不覚にも、比企谷八幡は水着を持ってきていない。`
7. `最後に鶴見留美は自分の道を選び取る。`
8. `そして、雪ノ下雪乃を乗せた車は走り出す。`

The sequence is revealing. The volume begins with Hachiman's preferred model of low-stakes, severable relations; moves through an involuntarily isolated child; constructs a deliberately cruel social experiment; gives Rumi the decisive act; and ends with Hachiman knowingly withholding information from Yui about the Yukinoshita family car.

The central movement is therefore not simply “Hachiman learns groups are bad.” It is:

> **from believing that distance prevents injury, through destroying a coercive form of proximity, to discovering that his own emerging relationships already contain facts he is willing to conceal because those relationships can now be injured.**

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

## 2.1 The opening problem: Hachiman wants relations that cannot hurt

Before Rumi appears, Volume 4 states Hachiman's relational ideal with unusual clarity.

He takes reassurance from a world that continues to function without him. Then he explains why being `かけがえのない`—irreplaceable—is frightening: if an irreplaceable person or relation is lost, the loss cannot be undone; failure acquires real stakes.

His preferred alternative is explicit:

`何かあればたやすく切れて、誰も傷つかない。`

A relationship that can be severed easily if anything happens, such that nobody is hurt.

At the publication boundary, this should not yet be translated into the later vocabulary of codependency, self-sacrifice, or inability to choose. It is already sufficient on its own. Hachiman wants **contact without irreplaceability**.

The narrative immediately demonstrates the instability of that model.

Before articulating it, he has already been shaken by seeing Saika happily interacting with another friend. His reaction is not resentment toward Saika. It is the recognition that he may have privately assigned himself a degree of particularity that Saika never promised:

`俺だけが仲がいいなんて、いつからそう思ってたんだろう。`

He then generalizes through memories of relationships in which someone was his “best friend” without him being theirs.

The safe relation has already become unsafe because Hachiman has begun to care about **relative place**.

## 2.2 Yui tests the same defense directly

Yui proposes summer activities involving the familiar group. When Hachiman hesitates, she tentatively offers:

`い、嫌なら……二人、でも……`

The prose gives the moment unmistakable bodily consequence: Hachiman's heartbeat accelerates; he breaks eye contact; and soon afterward he states that consciously maintaining distance so that he will not “misunderstand” is difficult.

This strengthens the three-stage model established in the Volume 2 V2 audit:

1. notice possible personal/romantic meaning;
2. estimate how likely that meaning is;
3. permit the possibility to influence one's behavior.

Volume 4 leaves little doubt about stage 1. Hachiman perceives the charge. It still does not prove that he confidently knows Yui's romantic intention. What it does prove is that **he treats uncertainty itself as dangerous enough to require distance-management**.

## 2.3 What Rumi initially appears to offer Hachiman

Rumi is easy for Hachiman to classify because her external condition resembles a history he understands: she is physically among peers but socially excluded.

His first response, however, is not immediate rescue. He initially rationalizes nonintervention by saying that there is value in confronting solitude and that a person can learn things precisely because friends are absent.

The proposition is not entirely false. Volumes 1–3 have already shown that Hachiman's own solitude created real interests, skills, and independence. The narratorial problem is application.

At this point he does not yet know whether Rumi's solitude is chosen or coercively imposed, but he uses a true proposition about chosen solitude to justify `放置`.

Later he makes the distinction explicitly:

`孤立すること、一人でいること自体は別にいいんだ。問題なのは、悪意によって孤立させられていることだ。`

Being alone is not itself the problem. Being **made** alone through malice is.

This is an important prospective self-correction. Volume 4 does not simply vindicate Hachiman's loner ideology or refute it. It forces him to discriminate among kinds of solitude.

## 2.4 What the prospective reader should not yet assume

Do not yet conclude that:

- Rumi is simply “young Hachiman.”
- Yukino and Rumi share the same psychological structure because both were excluded.
- Hachiman consciously designs the plan to avenge his own childhood.
- Hayama's past failure is definitively about Yukino.
- Hayama's `Y` has one established referent.
- Yukino's mother's authority is fully explained by domination rather than a more complicated family structure.
- Rumi's final action proves her relationship with the girls is “really” genuine.
- Volume 4 has now defined `本物` for the entire series.
- Hachiman's plan is already the same unilateral self-sacrificial control seen later.

The text supplies evidence that later makes several of these readings powerful. The prospective artifact must preserve the difference between **available suspicion** and **later explanatory consolidation**.

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

## 3.1 Chapter 1 — Hachiman's ideal of dispensability fails before the plot begins

The volume starts by giving Hachiman summer freedom and then demonstrating that his supposedly low-attachment life has acquired relational density.

He notices Saika, experiences a small shock at discovering that Saika has other close relations, and responds by rehearsing the old asymmetry: the person one considers a best friend may not reciprocate that rank.

Yui then appears, and the narration slides almost seamlessly from “I like relations that can be cut without injury” into a conversation whose stakes are precisely that Hachiman does not want to misread Yui.

This opening is structurally important because it prevents the Rumi plot from being read as detached social theory. Hachiman is entering a story about forced exclusion **while already becoming someone for whom inclusion and particularity matter**.

## 3.2 Chapter 2 — Hiratsuka manufactures an environment of contact

The summer volunteer camp is another artificial institution in the series' growing archive of contrived spaces:

- the Service Club itself;
- school requests;
- the birthday operation;
- now a residential volunteer camp.

Hiratsuka again does not directly command the adolescents to become friends. She places them in a setting where work, meals, sleep, bathing, leisure, and child supervision force multiple social worlds to overlap.

This matters because Volume 4 repeatedly shows that institutional or staged situations are not inherently fake. They can disclose genuine reactions precisely because they create conditions in which people must act.

## 3.3 Chapters 3–4 — Rumi's exclusion becomes legible as a rotating group mechanism

Rumi describes a system in which someone is periodically excluded, the trend eventually fades, and ordinary interaction resumes—until another person becomes the target.

The mechanism is not a stable villain oppressing a stable victim. Rumi herself once distanced herself from a friend who was being excluded and later became the next target.

This is where Hachiman's *Kokoro* memory becomes analytically useful. He invokes Sensei's claim that there are not simply molded, permanent villains; ordinary people may become bad at a decisive moment.

Hachiman extends the idea into a theory of group moralization:

- an ordinary irritation is redescribed as evidence of another person's bad character;
- the new description permits the group to experience exclusion as justice;
- shared condemnation validates each member;
- fear of becoming the next victim creates pressure to locate a new sacrifice.

The strength of this analysis is that it explains how cruelty can be **distributed without any one participant having to understand herself as cruel**.

Its risk is typical Hachiman overextension. A mechanism that explains this group can become, in his narration, an indictment of `みんな` as such. V2 must retain both levels.

## 3.4 Yui identifies the missing problem: a person can want help and be unable to request it

Yukino's initial condition for intervention is explicit:

`彼女が助けを求めるなら、あらゆる手段を持って解決に努めます。`

V1 treated this partly as a limitation: Yukino waits for an explicit request that shame or distrust may prevent.

The V2 reread keeps that point but adds an important defense of Yukino's position. She is attempting to preserve **authorization**. After three volumes in which helping already carries the danger of defining another person's good for them, “if she asks” is not mere rigidity. It is an ethical boundary.

Yui then demonstrates why that boundary is insufficient by itself.

She recalls that Rumi admitted distancing herself from a previous victim and proposes that Rumi may feel she has no right to accept rescue for herself. Yui generalizes from experience:

`話し掛けたくても仲良くしたくても、そうできない環境ってあるんだよ。それでも罪悪感は残るから……`

There are environments where a person wants to approach or befriend someone but cannot make herself do it; the guilt remains anyway.

Yui then confesses that approaching a person whom everyone else is avoiding takes real courage because one immediately imagines becoming the next target.

The passage is a major character statement. Yui is not morally good because she lacks awareness of cowardice. She is morally significant because she knows **exactly what accommodation costs** and keeps trying to cross that boundary anyway.

Yukino looks at her as though dazzled.

## 3.5 Hachiman's re-evaluation of Yui is textually earned, not retrospective wishful reading

The prose explicitly reinterprets Yui's kindness in light of this confession. Her kindness is not described as maternal innocence ignorant of human ugliness. It emerges from awareness of the ugly impulse to withdraw, protect oneself, and comply with atmosphere.

That means the V1 characterization of “kindness informed by cowardice” survives strongly.

What V2 adds is temporal precision: Yui is not merely supplying emotional insight into Rumi. She is also supplying **counterevidence to Hachiman's Volume 2–3 model of kindness**. Kindness can be particular and morally costly even when the person is broadly kind and even when she has previously failed to act perfectly.

## 3.6 Chapter 5 — Yukino's family role becomes structurally visible

The night conversation with Hachiman contains the first sustained map of Yukino's public family role.

She explains that Hayama's father is counsel to the Yukinoshita company and his mother is a physician. When Hachiman remarks on the burden of family-level social ties, Yukino answers:

`そう言った外向きの場に出るのは姉の役割だから。私は代役でしかないの。`

Public-facing family appearances are Haruno's role. Yukino describes herself as only a substitute.

Then she admits:

`それでも、……今日は来られてよかったわ。無理だと思っていたから。`

She does not explain why she believed attendance would be impossible. Hachiman waits but does not press her. The scene ends with his belief that there are comfortable relationships made possible by **not knowing too much about one another**.

This is an important bridge toward Volume 5. In the moment, noninquiry can plausibly be read as respect for privacy. It will later become harder to separate respectful nonintrusion from a defense against destabilizing knowledge.

## 3.7 The maternal projection scene shows Yukino can correct herself

When Rumi discusses parental concern, Yukino coldly interprets parental attention as `支配`, `管理`, and `所有欲`—domination, management, and possessive ownership.

Hachiman answers from ordinary household experience that mothers interfere because they care. Yukino looks down and replies:

`そう、ね。普通はそうよね。`

Then she apologizes to Rumi:

`ごめんなさい。私が間違っていたみたい。無神経な発言だったわ。`

The narration itself remarks that this may be the first proper apology Hachiman has seen from her.

The correct V2 conclusion is narrower than “Yukino's mother does not love her.” Volume 4 establishes that **Yukino's lived model of maternal management is sufficiently abnormal that ordinary maternal interference does not occur to her as the default interpretation**. The cause and moral quality of that difference remain open.

Just as importantly, Yukino can recognize that her own experience has distorted her judgment and revise it publicly. Her commitment to correctness is therefore not merely a weapon aimed outward; it can bind her as well.

## 3.8 Chapters 6–7 — the four helping models collide

The camp ultimately places four distinct theories around Rumi.

### Hayama: reintegration through benevolent social capital

Hayama believes he can make the environment friendlier by drawing Rumi into interaction and modeling inclusion.

His kindness is real. The problem is structural: attention from an admired older boy can change Rumi's status rather than neutralize status. His social capital cannot simply be transferred without altering the hierarchy in which the child is embedded.

### Yukino: intervention conditional on the subject's agency

Yukino is willing to use `あらゆる手段`, but only if Rumi asks.

This protects authorization but risks requiring exactly the speech that shame and guilt make difficult.

### Yui: diagnose the barrier to requesting and create an opening

Yui understands why the request may not be sayable and why group atmosphere can defeat an individual's wish to act.

Her weakness is not lack of insight but lack of an immediately available structural method.

### Hachiman: abolish the mechanism that produces the victim

Hachiman no longer tries to manufacture friendship. He proposes to put the children under enough fear that they expose their willingness to sacrifice one another, thereby destroying the group's existing solidarity.

His own distinction is exact:

`問題は解決しない……でも、問題の解消はできる。`

The healthy relationship will not be created. The pathological structure can be made incapable of operating in its current form.

## 3.9 The plan is coercive, but its consent structure must be stated accurately

V1 correctly called the method ethically ugly. It frightened children and deliberately instrumentalized fear. But it also risked making Hachiman look more unilateral here than the scene actually is.

The high-school participants do not unknowingly become pieces in his scheme.

- Yukino finds the proposal terrible, sees no better available method, and consents.
- Hayama explicitly accepts while stating that he is betting on the opposite hypothesis—that the girls' better nature will emerge under pressure.
- Miura and Tobe complain about their unpleasant roles but knowingly participate.
- Hayama asks Hachiman to direct the operation.
- Hachiman recognizes that Hayama's role is especially unpleasant and feels obliged to answer his willingness seriously.

The targets—the children—do not consent to being manipulated. The collaborators substantially do.

That difference will become essential when V2 reaches later plans in which Hachiman withholds the decisive structure from people personally affected by it.

## 3.10 Hachiman and Hayama run the same experiment with opposite priors

The plan produces one of the cleanest ideological contrasts in the early series.

Hachiman predicts that extreme pressure reveals selfish betrayal.

Hayama predicts that extreme pressure may reveal cooperative decency:

`本性というなら俺はそっちの本性を信じたい。根は良い子たちだと思うんだよ。`

They agree on method while disagreeing on what “true nature” is likely to mean.

The result refuses to award either man total victory.

The girls initially behave exactly as Hachiman expects. They select Rumi, then begin turning on one another. The collective `みんな` fragments into individuals protecting themselves.

Then Rumi uses her camera flash to disable the threatening older students and directs the other girls to run.

She saves people who had just offered her as the sacrifice.

## 3.11 Rumi's choice prevents Hachiman from owning the meaning of the plan

Rumi's final act matters because Hachiman did not design it.

The chapter title says she chooses her own road. Yukino later emphasizes that Rumi moved forward `ちゃんと自分の意志で`—through her own will. Hachiman arranged the conditions, but he did not script the action that gives those conditions their most morally interesting result.

Yui's role is also easy to underweight: immediately before Rumi acts, Yui notices something and stops Hachiman from ending the charade. She helps preserve the space in which Rumi's own action becomes possible.

The successful component of the intervention is therefore distributed:

- Hachiman creates the structural rupture.
- the other older students knowingly perform the threat.
- Yui notices Rumi's developing response.
- Rumi authors the decisive choice.

## 3.12 The ending returns the volume's problem to the Service Club

After the camp, Yukino asks Hachiman:

`あなた、本当は誰のために解決したかったの？`

He answers Rumi and internally insists that he was not asked to solve anyone else's past.

The question is not proof that his stated motive is false. It is textual authorization to **audit projection**.

Later, Haruno arrives in the Yukinoshita family car. Her line that Hachiman “belongs to Yukino” and her probing of Yui are playful but also show that she perceives relational tensions quickly.

The moment Haruno says their mother is waiting, Yukino's register changes. She formally apologizes to Komachi, says she cannot go with them, and ends with the unusually final `さようなら`.

Then Yui pulls Hachiman's sleeve and begins to ask about the car. Hachiman tells her cars like that all look the same and that pain prevented him from remembering.

The narration immediately says:

`心にもないことを言った。`

He recognized it at once.

The volume that dismantles one group's false solidarity ends with Hachiman **knowingly maintaining an omission inside his own emerging relational system**.

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

Volume 4 is one of the earliest volumes where Hachiman's social analysis is simultaneously most impressive and most dangerous. The correct V2 question is not “is he reliable?” It is: **what kind of proposition is he making, and what personal interest enters its application?**

## 4.1 Observation: strong

Hachiman is highly reliable when identifying observable structure:

- Rumi is excluded while remaining physically embedded in the class.
- the target of exclusion has rotated.
- former friendship does not protect a person from becoming the next target.
- group members use shared interpretations to justify behavior.
- social attention from Hayama changes status rather than simply adding kindness.
- the children's coalition breaks under threat.
- Yukino's behavior changes around family references.
- the family car is familiar.

These are not fantasies produced by cynicism. The text repeatedly corroborates them.

## 4.2 Generalization: often insightful, sometimes inflated

His *Kokoro*-derived analysis—that ordinary people can become cruel when interests or status feel threatened—fits Rumi's account well.

The inflation occurs when a local mechanism becomes a total ontology of groups. His hatred of `みんな` is emotionally legible, but the novel has already supplied groups that do not operate through sacrificial victimhood: the Service Club at its best, Komachi's family relations, Saika's club relations, and even Hayama's own uneven but not wholly fraudulent network.

## 4.3 Rationalization: his initial nonintervention toward Rumi

The most important early narratorial correction occurs when Hachiman uses the real value of solitude as a reason to ignore Rumi before establishing whether her isolation is voluntary.

His later distinction between chosen solitude and malicious imposed isolation shows that the earlier “she may learn from this” account was incomplete.

This is a recurring Hachiman pattern:

> **a proposition can be philosophically defensible and still function as a defensive rationalization in the scene where he deploys it.**

## 4.4 Self-description: he wants low-stakes relations because loss frightens him

Unlike some earlier defenses, the `かけがえのない存在` passage is unusually transparent. Hachiman openly identifies the risk: irreplaceability makes failure and loss consequential.

This should be entered into the epistemic ledger as relatively high-confidence self-description, not immediately reclassified as self-deception.

The contradiction is behavioral rather than semantic. He wants easy severability while already reacting to Saika's other friend, Yui's possible invitation, Yukino's family opacity, and the car connection.

## 4.5 Romantic uncertainty: continued stage-1 recognition

`勘違いしないために意識的に距離を保つ`

is strong evidence that Hachiman is not oblivious to Yui's possible meaning. It still does not prove that he assigns a high probability to a romantic interpretation.

The V2 ledger therefore retains:

- **possibility detection:** strong;
- **probability judgment:** uncertain/defensively biased;
- **behavioral permission:** strongly restrictive.

## 4.6 Projection: textually invited, not factually settled

Yukino's question about whom Hachiman truly wanted to save, Hachiman's own long memories of exclusion, and his unusually intense hatred of sacrificial group peace all support a projection reading.

But the narration explicitly denies that he can infer other people projecting their pasts onto the request, and the text does not supply an omniscient correction.

V2 classification:

**Analytical inference, medium-high confidence retrospectively; not textual fact prospectively.**

The same caution applies to Hayama and Yui. Hayama openly admits remembering a similar situation in which he did nothing; Yui openly connects Rumi's problem to her own experience. Their projection is more textually explicit than Hachiman's.

## 4.7 The lie about the car is a genuine epistemic threshold

The final lie differs from earlier noninquiry.

At night with Yukino, Hachiman does not ask a question she appears unwilling to answer. That can plausibly be respectful restraint.

At the end, Yui begins asking about a fact Hachiman knows. He supplies information he internally knows to be false.

This is not “I will not intrude.” It is **active management of another person's access to shared relevant information**.

The distinction becomes crucial for Volume 5, where knowledge, concealment, idealization, and the right to explanation become the central conflict.


# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Hikigaya Hachiman

### New explicit relational fear

Volume 4 gives the clearest statement yet that Hachiman's preferred distance is organized around **loss prevention** rather than a simple positive preference for solitude.

Being unneeded reassures him because it keeps failure reversible. Being important to someone creates a category of mistake he cannot dismiss.

This is a major delta from Volume 1. His early loner identity could still be narrated as independence. By Volume 4, the narrative shows that **dispensability itself is emotionally attractive because it reduces stakes**.

### Greater self-correction

He begins the Rumi problem by defending nonintervention, then later distinguishes chosen solitude from imposed exclusion. He does not explicitly confess “I was wrong before,” but the conceptual refinement is real.

### More sophisticated intervention

The Rumi plan operates at a higher level than the Volume 2 Hayama-group solution. Hachiman now deliberately modifies the **rules under which a social system can reproduce itself**.

He is no longer simply moving one node in a network. He creates a stress test intended to reveal and destroy a group's hidden coordination logic.

This competence deserves recognition independent of whether the method is morally acceptable.

### New capacity for coordinated work

The planning conversation with Hayama is strikingly easy for Hachiman. He notes that task-focused conversation is comfortable because:

- the topic is externally supplied;
- harshness can be justified by the process;
- emotional interpretation is less necessary;
- competence rather than personal intimacy organizes exchange.

This becomes an early instance of **work as protective intimacy**. Hachiman can cooperate closely when the relationship does not have to name itself.

## 5.2 Yukinoshita Yukino

### Family role enters the foreground

Yukino is not merely Haruno's younger sister. She identifies a family division of labor:

- Haruno is the normal public-facing representative;
- Yukino appears in that world as substitute.

The word `代役` matters. It places Yukino's public participation inside a pre-existing role whose canonical occupant is someone else.

This is not yet enough to say that Yukino has “no self.” It is enough to establish a structural source of comparison and derivative identity.

### She is capable of self-revision

Her apology to Rumi is a significant character delta. She recognizes that her own model of parental concern has distorted her judgment and says so without converting the apology into self-defense.

This strengthens an important V2 correction to simplistic readings of Yukino's “correctness”: she is not committed to always being right. At her best, she is committed to the proposition that **being wrong should produce correction**.

### Her criterion of authorized help remains serious

`彼女が助けを求めるなら` is not mere coldness. Yukino remains wary of forcing a solution on someone who has not asked for it.

The limitation is practical: a person trapped by shame or group pressure may be unable to utter the request.

### She begins to see Hachiman's value without romanticizing the method

After the operation, Yukino states all the ugly facts: the children were frightened and their relations damaged. She nevertheless credits Hachiman with arranging conditions in which Rumi could move by her own will.

This is a sophisticated judgment because it neither purifies the method nor denies its beneficial consequence.

## 5.3 Yuigahama Yui

Volume 4 substantially strengthens the Phase-1 hypothesis that Yui is **an analyst and active social agent**, not merely emotional atmosphere.

### She supplies the missing causal variable

When the group asks why Rumi has not requested help, Yui identifies guilt and the fear of being the next excluded person. This is not a vague empathic feeling. It is a causal model of constrained behavior.

### She exposes her own compromised history

Yui does not claim a morally pure perspective. Her explanation works because she understands the desire to distance oneself when helping another person threatens one's own standing.

This makes her kindness more rather than less significant. It is practiced against a known counterpressure.

### She notices Rumi's agency before Hachiman does

During the climax, Yui stops Hachiman from ending the performance because she notices Rumi about to act.

This is another reason the “Hachiman solves Rumi” summary is too protagonist-centered. Yui helps protect the space for Rumi's self-authored decision.

### Romantic pursuit becomes more explicit

At the fireworks, she asks Hachiman to make the remaining item—going out together, just the two of them—come true.

Hachiman's `そのうち、適当にな` preserves possibility without creating obligation. Yui has nonetheless advanced beyond generalized friendliness into an invitation that the narration itself cannot comfortably neutralize.

## 5.4 Hayama Hayato

Volume 4 is the first volume where Hayama becomes a true ideological counterpart rather than simply the socially successful boy whom Hachiman distrusts.

### His optimism is not naivety

Hayama understands Hachiman's plan and its ugliness. He agrees to play the villain anyway. His disagreement concerns the hypothesis being tested: he wants to believe that pressure may reveal the girls' decency rather than their selfishness.

### His past becomes morally active

He later admits that the operation reminded him of an earlier similar scene in which he did nothing.

That admission makes his intervention partly reparative. Unlike Hachiman, Hayama explicitly identifies the old failure being reactivated.

### His history with Yukino becomes structurally important but remains underdetermined

The novel establishes:

- shared elementary school;
- family ties;
- his father's professional relation to the Yukinoshita company;
- his belief that many outcomes might have differed had Hachiman attended the same school;
- his surprising claim that he still probably could not have become Hachiman's friend.

This is enough to establish history and ideological incompatibility. It is not enough to reconstruct the precise event or assign the `Y` referent.

## 5.5 Tsurumi Rumi

Rumi should not be reduced to a device for Hachiman's development.

Her key traits are:

- strong observational awareness of the group mechanism;
- shame about her own earlier participation in exclusion;
- refusal to romanticize old elementary-school friendships;
- desire for relief from hierarchy more than generic loneliness;
- capacity for decisive action under pressure.

The climax matters because she is **not restored by becoming socially agreeable**. She does not suddenly love her classmates or accept the older students' worldview. She chooses an action whose meaning exceeds the roles available to her.

## 5.6 Haruno

Haruno remains analytically dangerous because her social warmth is both effective and difficult to classify.

In Volume 4 she:

- arrives as the family's socially effortless representative;
- immediately reads Yui's relationship to Hachiman;
- teases Yui with the idea that Hachiman is Yukino's;
- converts the mention of their mother into immediate compliance from Yukino.

The last point establishes power. It does not yet establish Haruno's complete motive or Mother's complete moral character.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Hachiman ↔ Yui

**Trust:** recovering after Volume 3, but Hachiman remains defensive.

**Disclosure:** Yui increasingly states concrete wants; Hachiman still replies through indefinite or distancing language.

**Romantic legibility:** significantly increased.

**Permission to intervene:** Yui remains willing to challenge Hachiman and to physically arrest his action when she notices Rumi preparing to choose.

**Future assumption:** Yui explicitly asks for a future one-on-one outing.

**Key contradiction:** Hachiman wants the relationship available enough to continue and distant enough not to demand interpretation.

The fireworks request is the clearest relational delta. Yui does not merely hope they will accidentally spend more time together. She asks for the unresolved possibility to be realized.

## 6.2 Hachiman ↔ Yukino

Their nighttime interaction becomes one of the first scenes in which silence is not simply absence of intimacy but a **shared rule of intimacy**.

Yukino discloses enough to signal that attending the camp mattered and that family expectations almost prevented it. Hachiman waits, then deliberately does not ask further.

At this stage, the restraint is experienced by both as comfortable. Volume 5 will test whether this mode scales once the withheld information directly concerns the relation between them.

After the Rumi operation, Yukino gives Hachiman unusually unbarbed recognition. She can name both the ugliness and the value of what he did.

**Trust:** rising.

**Disclosure:** partial and bounded.

**Mutual recognition:** substantially increased.

**Shared history:** still opaque.

## 6.3 Yukino ↔ Yui

This relationship continues to deepen independently of Hachiman.

Yui's confession about social cowardice produces one of Yukino's most visibly admiring reactions. Yukino's quiet `とても、あなたらしいと思うわ` is not a generic compliment. It recognizes Yui's imperfect but persistent way of being good.

Yui also continues physical familiarity—tugging Yukino's clothing, inviting her into activities, attempting to include her in fireworks.

**Trust:** high relative to early series.

**Physical/social ease:** increasing.

**Difference:** now a source of admiration rather than merely incomprehension.

## 6.4 Hachiman ↔ Hayama

The relation becomes simultaneously easier and more antagonistically lucid.

They discover extremely effective work compatibility. Neither requires friendship language for the coordination to function.

At night, Hayama then makes a remarkable declaration: even if Hachiman had changed many outcomes in his elementary-school world, Hayama thinks they still could not have gotten along.

Hachiman concludes that he may have recognized Hayama properly for the first time, just as Hayama has recognized him.

This is not closeness in the conventional sense. It is **accurate mutual opposition**, which the series increasingly treats as a meaningful relational form.

## 6.5 Yukino ↔ family

Volume 4 sharply raises the salience of family authority.

Two facts now coexist:

- Yukino lives and functions with significant independence in school life.
- a summons from her mother, mediated through Haruno, immediately changes her behavior and language.

The mature V2 analysis should not resolve this contradiction prematurely. Autonomy is domain-specific here just as Hachiman's narratorial reliability is domain-specific.

## 6.6 Hachiman ↔ his own place in other people's lives

Saika's other friendship, Yui's invitations, Hayama's assessment, Yukino's recognition, and the car secret all move Hachiman away from the fantasy that his social relations are functionally disposable.

He still prefers that fantasy. The volume increasingly gives him evidence against it.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

Volume 4 deserves a high-resolution intervention audit because V1's strongest conceptual vocabulary can otherwise flatten it into a prototype of later Hachiman behavior.

## 7.1 What is the actual request?

No formal Service Club client asks for Rumi to be “fixed.”

The adult environment notices her social isolation, and the older students decide whether to intervene.

This creates an immediate authorization problem: they are discussing another person's life before receiving a clear request from her.

## 7.2 Yukino's authorization threshold

Yukino's `彼女が助けを求めるなら` should be read as an explicit attempt to solve that problem.

**Strength:** preserves Rumi as subject rather than project.

**Weakness:** assumes that desire can be translated into explicit request under conditions where shame, fear, and guilt constrain speech.

## 7.3 Yui's correction

Yui demonstrates that absence of request is not equivalent to absence of desire.

This does **not** mean the helpers now possess automatic permission to do whatever they think best. It means an ethical framework requires some way to account for **constrained expression**.

That is a harder problem than either “never intervene unless asked” or “infer the true need and intervene.”

## 7.4 Hachiman's intervention taxonomy

**Stated goal:** stop the mechanism that keeps Rumi uniquely at the bottom.

**Method:** manufacture fear, create a forced-sacrifice scenario, expose distrust, break group solidarity.

**Authorization from Rumi:** absent.

**Authorization from high-school collaborators:** substantial.

**Cost bearers:**

- Rumi and classmates experience deliberate fear;
- classmates lose the prior coalition structure;
- Hayama/Miura/Tobe bear the discomfort of performing cruelty;
- Hachiman bears moral authorship of the plan but not unique visible social injury.

**Reversibility:** limited. Once distrust is exposed, the previous group form cannot simply be restored.

**Agency effect:** mixed.

- The children are manipulated.
- Rumi gains room to act because the old structure is destabilized.
- Rumi's decisive act is not dictated by Hachiman.

**Immediate result:** “林立,” not repaired friendship.

## 7.5 Why this is not yet mature self-sacrifice-as-control

The plan contains control but not the later characteristic structure in which Hachiman privately decides that **he** will absorb a cost and assumes others will accept the emotional consequence.

Here:

- he openly proposes the strategy;
- Yukino objects before consenting;
- Hayama negotiates the hypothesis and consents;
- Miura/Tobe know their roles;
- Hachiman himself is not the sacrificial object.

The important continuity with later Hachiman is instead **epistemic authorship**: he believes he can identify the system's governing mechanism and design conditions that force a result.

The important discontinuity is consent among his collaborators.

## 7.6 Does the plan work?

Depends on the criterion.

**If the goal is friendship restoration:** no.

**If the goal is elimination of Rumi as unique sacrificial target:** substantially yes.

**If the goal is Rumi's autonomous social growth:** partially, but only because Rumi adds an unscripted action.

**If the ethical standard requires informed consent from the person being helped:** the intervention remains deeply problematic.

The volume deliberately prevents simple outcome consequentialism from settling the question.

## 7.7 Yukino's post-hoc judgment is the best internal ethical summary

Yukino neither says “you saved her, therefore the method was good” nor “the method was bad, therefore nothing good happened.”

Her formulation separates:

1. method;
2. structural consequence;
3. Rumi's independent will;
4. Hachiman's role in arranging the occasion.

This four-part separation should become a model for later V2 intervention audits.

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 `みんな` as distributed moral cover

Hachiman's hatred of `みんな` reaches a new level in Volume 4.

The group allows acts that would feel vicious at individual scale to be redescribed as atmospheric inevitability:

- “everyone thinks this”;
- “this is just how it is”;
- “she did something to deserve it”;
- “I am not the one deciding.”

The concept is important because it shifts responsibility without eliminating action.

No mystical entity called “everyone” commits the exclusion. Individuals do, under a system that reduces the felt cost of owning the act.

## 8.2 Hierarchy matters more than simple friendship

Rumi does not describe the absence of friends as the whole problem. Being ignored publicly places her at the bottom. The suffering is therefore relational and status-based.

That distinction explains why “just make new friends” is inadequate. The group has attached a public social meaning to her isolation.

## 8.3 Hayama's social capital is nonneutral

Hayama's effort to include Rumi shows that benevolent high status changes the system even when deployed gently.

This does not make his kindness hypocritical. It means power is present in benevolence.

The same action—an older student approaching a child—has different meaning depending on who performs it and how the existing group interprets the attention.

## 8.4 Yui identifies atmosphere as constraint, not destiny

Yui's social intelligence is particularly strong because she neither romanticizes individual freedom nor treats atmosphere as total determination.

She knows that atmosphere can make a desired action frightening. She also demonstrates, through her own history with the Service Club, that people can sometimes cross it.

Her ethic is therefore neither Hachiman's destruction nor Hayama's harmony. It is **acknowledged pressure plus attempted action**.

## 8.5 “林立” is not community, but it may be less coercive than false unity

Hiratsuka's description of the post-intervention children as `林立`—standing separately like trees—is exact.

The volume does not present fragmentation as an ideal social endpoint. It presents it as preferable to a unity maintained through recurrent sacrifice.

This is a crucial limit on romanticizing Hachiman's solution. Freedom from a coercive group is a **cleared field**, not automatically a healthy relationship.

## 8.6 Institutions can create contact without dictating intimacy

The camp itself is a counterexample to Hachiman's group pessimism.

It is artificial, adult-organized, and compulsory for many participants. Yet it produces:

- effective Hachiman/Hayama cooperation;
- Yui/Yukino mutual recognition;
- Yukino/Hachiman disclosure;
- shared leisure;
- Rumi's encounter with older adolescents who take her problem seriously.

The moral problem is not “constructed social form = fake.” It is what the form requires people to surrender and what forms of choice it makes possible.

# 9. Authenticity / `本物` ledger

Volume 4 is the first volume in which V2 must actively resist converting a powerful local statement into the final theory of the series.

## 9.1 Lexical inventory

The EPUB contains five occurrences of `本物` and one of `偽物`.

At least one `本物` occurrence is ordinary/taxonomic rather than philosophical: Hachiman speculates about what a “real” instance of Ebina's enthusiast type would do. This matters because longitudinal lexical counting cannot treat every token as the same concept.

The Rumi climax, however, unmistakably continues the Volume 3 authenticity problem.

## 9.2 The negative criterion

Yui wonders whether Rumi and the girls were “really” close after all.

Hachiman rejects that immediately:

`誰かを貶めないと仲良くしてられないようなのが本物なわけねぇだろ。`

A bond that requires degrading someone else cannot qualify as `本物` for him.

This is already a meaningful normative criterion: group continuity alone does not authenticate a relationship.

## 9.3 The new positive possibility

He then adds:

`偽物だってわかってて、それでも手を差し伸べたいって思ったなら、そいつは本物なんだろ、きっと。`

This is the sentence V1 summarized as authenticity becoming an act rather than a pedigree.

The core insight survives. The wording matters.

- `偽物だってわかってて` — the actor does not need to misrecognize the compromised relation as pure.
- `それでも` — the choice matters despite that knowledge.
- `手を差し伸べたいって思ったなら` — the internal desire to extend a hand, not successful social rehabilitation, carries the potential authenticity.
- `きっと` — Hachiman still hedges.

## 9.4 The text immediately refuses doctrinal closure

Yukino answers uncertainly:

`そう、なのかしらね。`

Hachiman retreats:

`いや、知らねぇけど。`

He then reverses *Kokoro*: if an ordinary person can suddenly become bad, perhaps an ordinary person can suddenly become good.

Again he adds `たぶん`.

When Yui asks whether that means they ultimately do not know whether it was genuine, he agrees and invokes Akutagawa's `藪の中`: the truth remains inaccessible.

This sequence requires a major V1 narrowing.

The best V2 formulation is:

> **Volume 4 proposes that a knowingly chosen act inside a compromised relationship may provide stronger evidence of genuineness than the purity of the relationship's origin or public form. The proposition is explicitly tentative, and the characters do not claim epistemic certainty about Rumi's interior motive.**

“Authenticity as act rather than pedigree” remains an excellent retrospective compression if its uncertainty is preserved.

## 9.5 Relationship-level and act-level authenticity separate

The scene also introduces a distinction V1 only implied:

- a relationship may remain `偽物`;
- an act or desire within it may nevertheless be `本物`.

This becomes extremely important later. Mixed or damaged relational structures need not contaminate every feeling inside them.

But Volume 4 does **not** yet prove the inverse proposition—that one genuine act transforms the whole relationship into something genuine.

## 9.6 Honmono chronology through Volume 4

**Volumes 1–2:** no explicit governing `本物` vocabulary in the V2 searches.

**Volume 3:** `本物` enters around public presentation, unmanipulated expression, and personal particularity.

**Volume 4:** the term is extended toward moral action under acknowledged relational falsity.

The mature `本物` problem is therefore developing cumulatively rather than arriving fully defined in Volume 9.

# 10. Dependency / autonomy ledger

## 10.1 No explicit `依存` in the main lexical search

The EPUB search finds no `依存` token in Volume 4. This is analytically useful: the volume's dependence problem is enacted through **group membership, social hierarchy, trust, authorization, family role, and need**, not yet named through the later pathology vocabulary.

## 10.2 Rumi: group dependence without secure belonging

Rumi's classmates depend upon group atmosphere to determine permissible conduct, yet the group does not provide durable security. Membership is maintained partly by demonstrating distance from the current victim.

This is a form of social dependence that produces insecurity rather than support.

## 10.3 Hayama: social centrality as responsibility

Hayama's status means others expect him to stabilize situations. His benevolence is partly enabled by that position and partly constrained by it.

Volume 4 begins to suggest that being socially needed can itself restrict choice—a theme later applied far more strongly to both Hayama and Hachiman.

## 10.4 Yukino: public role as substitute

`私は代役でしかない` introduces a different autonomy problem. Yukino is not describing practical reliance on another person. She is describing **role derivation**: her public family identity is positioned as substitute for Haruno's.

This is more relevant to later authorship questions than a generic “Yukino is dependent” claim.

## 10.5 Hachiman: fear of becoming irreplaceable

Hachiman's opening model is anti-dependence in an extreme form: relationships should not become important enough that their loss creates irreversible damage.

The volume's irony is that his behavior already contradicts that preference.

## 10.6 Healthy reliance baseline remains intact

Nothing in Volume 4 overturns the Volume 3 finding that bounded requested reliance can be healthy.

Indeed, the Rumi discussion deepens the distinction:

- help requested and chosen;
- help desired but difficult to request;
- help inferred and imposed;
- help that creates conditions for self-action.

The later dependency debate must preserve these distinctions instead of treating all reliance as one variable.


# 11. Japanese language and voice

Volume 4 yields unusually useful linguistic evidence because several of its central conflicts concern the difference between collective language, personal agency, uncertainty, and social role.

## 11.1 `みんな` — “everyone” as both ordinary pronoun and ideological object

`みんな` appears frequently and cannot be assigned one stable negative meaning.

Sometimes it is ordinary group reference. Sometimes it is Yui's inclusive social vocabulary. Sometimes Hachiman treats it as the name of a coercive abstraction.

His climactic attack is not literally against every plural relation. It is against the way `みんな` can erase individual authorship:

> there is no entity called “everyone” who chooses; individuals hide inside the collective description.

V2 should therefore track who says `みんな`, about which group, and whether the word expands belonging or disperses responsibility.

## 11.2 `孤立` / `一人` / `ぼっち`

The lexical distinction around aloneness becomes conceptually precise.

- `一人でいる` can be chosen and neutral or positive.
- `孤立させられている` grammatically foregrounds being **made** isolated.
- Hachiman's habitual `ぼっち` remains both comic self-brand and social category.

The passive causative structure in the Rumi distinction matters. The ethical problem is not a visual count of how many people stand beside someone. It is who authored the state and through what pressure.

## 11.3 Yui's hesitations are not absence of thought

Yui's dialogue around Rumi contains:

- pauses;
- self-correction;
- `たぶん`;
- embarrassed laughter;
- concern about sounding like a bad person.

The surface register is uncertain, but the causal content is sophisticated. Her voice is a useful warning against equating fluent declarative speech with analytical depth.

Yui often thinks relationally **through hesitation**.

## 11.4 Yukino's conditional help language

`彼女が助けを求めるなら`

uses a clear conditional. It is not “I will help because I know she needs it.” Her willingness to deploy `あらゆる手段` is subordinated to the subject's request.

This grammatical architecture reflects her ethical architecture at this point: powerful intervention after authorization.

## 11.5 Hachiman's distinction: `解決` versus `解消`

This is one of Volume 4's most important lexical pairs.

`解決` implies solving the problem.

`解消` can mean eliminating, dissolving, or removing the condition.

Hachiman openly concedes that his plan does not produce the former. It produces the latter.

This is not simply cynical wordplay. It is a real intervention category that should remain in the longitudinal ethics ledger. Later Hachiman plans often succeed because he is willing to change what counts as the problem rather than satisfy the original relational ideal.

## 11.6 Epistemic hedging around `本物`

The genuine-scene sequence is saturated with uncertainty markers:

- `きっと`
- `そう、なのかしらね`
- `知らねぇけど`
- `たぶん`
- `よくわかんない`
- `藪の中`

Any synthesis that quotes only `そいつは本物なんだろ` and drops the hedges changes the epistemic status of the scene.

The V2 honmono ledger should preserve the hedge cluster as part of the evidence itself.

## 11.7 Yukino's `代役`

`代役` is stronger than merely saying Haruno “usually handles” family appearances. It defines Yukino's own public-facing role comparatively: a stand-in for an already assigned role.

That word becomes an early linguistic anchor for the later self-authorship problem.

## 11.8 `そう、ね。普通はそうよね`

Yukino's maternal correction is linguistically quiet. The pause and `普通は` matter.

She does not say Hachiman's description is universally correct. She acknowledges it as the normal model from which her own experience appears to deviate.

## 11.9 `さようなら`

At the end, Yukino does not use the lighter school-day departure register one might expect among newly close peers. She gives a faint `さようなら` after becoming formal with Komachi.

The line carries more finality in context than a mechanical dictionary translation of “goodbye” conveys. It marks a sudden register boundary between the summer peer-world and the family role into which she is being recalled.

## 11.10 Hachiman's explicit lie

`心にもないことを言った。`

is unusually valuable because the narrator classifies his own speech. We do not need to infer whether the car response is evasive, mistaken, or sincerely uncertain. He tells us he said something contrary to what he knew.

That makes the scene a high-confidence epistemic-ledger entry.

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertext

## 12.1 *Kokoro* is not decorative literary signaling

The middle-school book report and later remembered quotation supply the volume's philosophical experiment.

Hachiman takes from *Kokoro* the proposition that the frightening thing about people is not permanent villainy but the capacity of ordinary people to become harmful under the right pressure.

The Rumi plot then stages that proposition socially.

At the end, Hachiman attempts a reverse inference: if ordinary goodness can fail suddenly, perhaps ordinary badness can also give way suddenly to goodness.

The intertext therefore changes function across the volume:

1. warning about betrayal;
2. explanatory model of group cruelty;
3. basis for a tentative possibility of moral action.

## 12.2 `藪の中` corrects the temptation to make *Kokoro* into a closed theorem

Hachiman ultimately jokes that the truth is “in a grove,” invoking Akutagawa.

The literary joke is structurally serious. It prevents his own interpretive system from closing over Rumi's motive.

The volume allows a theory of genuineness to emerge and then immediately marks the interior truth as inaccessible.

## 12.3 Summer rom-com imagery is not separable from the ethical plot

The volume deliberately floods the reader with conventional summer-romance material:

- swimsuits;
- camp cooking;
- bathing jokes;
- ghost-story test of courage;
- night conversations;
- fireworks.

Hachiman repeatedly mocks the genre machinery even as it creates experiences he values.

This matters for the series-wide authenticity problem. A conventional, artificial, or genre-coded setting can still host nontrivial choices. “Cliché” is not equivalent to “false.”

## 12.4 Hachiman's jokes often expose the direction of threatened feeling

The Saika material, Yui's swimsuit, Yukino's body-image comedy, and the repeated deflection into pop-culture references all continue the pattern established in Volumes 1–3: emotional or sexual charge is frequently followed by associative comedy.

V2 still refuses the overclaim that every joke is camouflage. Here, however, the placement often makes the regulatory function visible.

## 12.5 Ebina complicates the meaning of “real” fandom/persona

One `本物` occurrence is Hachiman's generic speculation about what a “real” version of Ebina's type would do. That usage is useful precisely because it is **not** the same as the later relational `本物`.

The term can be taxonomic, aesthetic, ethical, or relational depending on context. Corpus-wide lexical analysis must not flatten them.

# 13. Illustration and paratext

The PDF was rendered page-by-page and visually scanned in full. The illustrations reinforce relational arrangements but do not override prose or settle ambiguous motives.

## 13.1 Summer color material

The front material foregrounds swimsuits, camp leisure, and the ensemble cast. This presents Volume 4 initially as a conventional summer-event volume even though its central narrative becomes a study of exclusion and coercive group formation.

The contrast is useful: the “青春イベント” surface and the social-ethical interior coexist rather than cancel one another.

## 13.2 Yukino under the night sky — PDF p.181 / printed p.175 vicinity

A major monochrome illustration enlarges Yukino's face against the night sky while Hachiman and another Yukino figure occupy deeper spatial planes.

The composition matters in three ways:

- Yukino visually dominates the page while remaining introspective rather than interactive.
- Hachiman is physically present but positioned behind/below rather than inside a mutual face-to-face composition.
- the star field materializes the prose's movement into past light, memory, and things that remain visible after their originating moment.

It supports the chapter's atmosphere of partial disclosure and noninquiry. It does not prove a romantic confession that the prose withholds.

## 13.3 Yui at the fireworks — PDF p.279 / printed p.273 vicinity

The fireworks illustration isolates Yui's flushed, open expression opposite a cropped Hachiman.

Unlike group illustrations elsewhere in the volume, this image emphasizes **dyadic romantic legibility** at exactly the point where Yui's request is becoming explicitly one-on-one.

The prose remains authoritative: Hachiman does not answer with a commitment. The illustration makes it harder, however, to reduce the request to generic group friendliness.

## 13.4 Rumi and the camp images

The camp illustrations repeatedly distinguish Rumi spatially from the surrounding children or older students. The formal grammar of separation therefore corroborates the prose's hierarchy/exclusion problem.

Care is still required: an isolated placement in illustration can be visual emphasis rather than literal social geography.

## 13.5 Paratextual limit

Ponkan⑧ can foreground:

- tenderness;
- embarrassment;
- romantic charge;
- isolation;
- character contrast.

The images cannot establish:

- Rumi's exact motive;
- Hayama's `Y`;
- whether Yukino is romantically jealous;
- whether Hachiman consciously projects his childhood;
- the moral truth of the intervention.

Those remain prose-level questions.

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial test

## 14.1 Alternative reading A: Hachiman simply proves that groups are rotten

**Evidence for:**

- Rumi's classmates rotate exclusion.
- the fear scenario quickly produces betrayal.
- Hachiman's `みんな` critique is narratively forceful.

**Evidence against:**

- Rumi's own final action contradicts deterministic selfishness.
- the high-school collaborators coordinate voluntarily.
- Yui repeatedly crosses group pressure rather than being totally determined by it.
- the camp itself creates meaningful cooperation.

**V2 ruling:** weakened. Volume 4 criticizes sacrificial group solidarity, not collective life in general.

## 14.2 Alternative reading B: Hayama's optimism is naïve and Hachiman is simply correct

**Evidence for:** the children initially betray one another exactly as Hachiman predicts.

**Evidence against:** Rumi's unscripted rescue fulfills the possibility Hayama wants to preserve: pressure can also produce unexpected prosocial action.

**V2 ruling:** neither model wins completely. The experiment yields evidence for both pessimistic and hopeful priors.

## 14.3 Alternative reading C: Rumi's rescue proves the friendships were secretly genuine

Yui herself raises this possibility.

Hachiman rejects it because a relation requiring degradation of another person cannot count as genuine by his criterion.

**V2 ruling:** the volume explicitly refuses this sentimental reading.

The possible `本物` is Rumi's desire/action, not the prior group form.

## 14.4 Alternative reading D: “authenticity becomes an act rather than a pedigree” is now the series' doctrine

This was one of V1's strongest formulations.

**Counterevidence:** the characters immediately hedge and finally concede that they do not know Rumi's interior truth.

**V2 ruling:** retain as a high-value hypothesis, downgrade from doctrine to **new candidate criterion**.

## 14.5 Alternative reading E: Hachiman's plan is already his later self-sacrificial pathology

**Counterevidence:** he is not the sacrificial object, and his collaborators are substantially informed and consenting.

**V2 ruling:** reject as overprojection.

The continuity lies in social engineering and confidence in structural diagnosis, not yet in the mature pattern of privately assigning his own injury as the cost everyone else must accept.

## 14.6 Alternative reading F: Yukino is helpless under her family

**Evidence for:** `代役`, inability to explain why camp attendance seemed impossible, mother's summons, register change, `さようなら`.

**Counterevidence:** she remains highly autonomous in many school contexts and deliberately chooses actions throughout the camp.

**V2 ruling:** family authority is powerful and autonomy is constrained, but “helpless” is too broad.

## 14.7 Alternative reading G: Yukino's maternal interpretation proves her mother is loveless

**Counterevidence:** Volume 4 gives Yukino's reaction, not an omniscient account of Mother's intent.

**V2 ruling:** reject. The source supports atypical/experienced-as-controlling maternal management; motive remains open.

## 14.8 Alternative reading H: Hachiman's noninquiry toward Yukino is mature respect

**Evidence for:** Yukino withholds an answer; he does not force disclosure.

**Counterevidence:** Hachiman explicitly likes relationships that remain comfortable through mutual nonknowledge. Later he actively lies about the car.

**V2 ruling:** genuinely ambiguous at this point. Respect for privacy and defense against destabilizing knowledge coexist as live explanations.

## 14.9 Alternative reading I: Hachiman's Rumi intervention “preserves agency” and is therefore ethical

**Counterevidence:** Rumi never consents to being placed in a staged threat scenario.

**V2 ruling:** no. The outcome contains an agency-expanding effect, but the method remains paternalistic/coercive.

The important ethical distinction is that Rumi authors the decisive action **despite** not having authored the experiment.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

The full series makes Volume 4 much more important than a summer-camp bridge, but hindsight must be applied with discipline.

## 15.1 `本物` now begins to move from expression to chosen action

Volume 3 introduced `本物` around social mask, unmanipulated smile, and whether affection is particular rather than transferable.

Volume 4 asks a new question:

> Can something genuine occur **inside a relation already known to be false or compromised**?

That is a major conceptual expansion. Later volumes increasingly abandon purity as a workable criterion, but Volume 4 only opens the door.

## 15.2 The Rumi plan is an early intervention-ethics control case for later Hachiman

With full hindsight, the most useful aspect is not “here is the first self-sacrifice.” It is almost the opposite.

The plan provides a comparison case where:

- Hachiman explains the method;
- collaborators object;
- collaborators consent;
- another ideological position is explicitly allowed inside the same plan;
- the subject's unscripted action changes the meaning of the result.

When later Hachiman stops granting people that degree of participation, V2 will be able to identify the deterioration precisely.

## 15.3 Yui's Volume 4 social analysis anticipates her later resistance to totalizing labels

Yui already knows that:

- good people can act badly under social pressure;
- failure to help does not erase later desire to help;
- guilt can constrain one's willingness to receive care;
- mixed motive does not make care meaningless.

This is highly relevant to the later `共依存` crisis. Yui's mature relational intelligence does not suddenly appear in the late series. Its basis is visible here.

## 15.4 Yukino's `代役` is an early authorship anchor

The late-series question “what does Yukino herself want?” becomes much clearer when read back through `私は代役でしかない`.

Her difficulty is not merely dependence on Hachiman. It emerges in a life where important roles already seem occupied, inherited, compared, or externally legible through Haruno.

But hindsight should not turn the line into destiny. Volume 4 Yukino is already making choices, correcting herself, and expressing preference.

## 15.5 Hachiman's comfortable nonknowledge anticipates Volume 5's crisis

The night-sky scene feels gentle because Hachiman does not press Yukino.

Full-series hindsight shows why the rule cannot remain sufficient. A relationship can respect privacy without using “I do not need to know” as a general shield against information that would revise one's image of the other person.

The car lie then makes that problem immediate.

## 15.6 The final car scene links the Yui debt and Yukino idealization structures

Hachiman has just spent Volumes 2–3 trying to neutralize one accident-generated relation because he fears obligation contaminating affection.

Now he recognizes that Yukino's family is connected to that same accident and withholds the recognition from Yui.

Retrospectively, the scene matters because Hachiman is not merely protecting privacy. He is protecting a relational equilibrium from information that may create another system of debt, expectation, or disappointment.

Volume 5 will reveal that the emotional wound is less “Yukino caused the accident” than **the collapse of Hachiman's image of a Yukino who does not conceal**.

## 15.7 Hayama's earlier failure becomes a durable moral wound

Later Hayama material makes his Volume 4 admission more important: he once watched a similar exclusion scene and did nothing.

His social harmonizing is therefore not merely native talent. It is plausibly also a response to a remembered failure to intervene.

V2 should nevertheless wait before assigning the exact childhood event beyond what later primary sources actually establish.

# 16. V1 revision audit

The historical V1 reading of Volume 4 was among the stronger early analyses. Its central structure survives, but several claims require narrower evidentiary language.

## V1-4A — “Volume 4 is a controlled experiment on Hachiman's social philosophy”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED.**

The same operation is explicitly accepted by Hachiman and Hayama under opposite hypotheses of human nature. The structure is even more experimentally legible than V1 suggested.

## V1-4B — “The decisive development is that a false relationship can still produce a genuine act”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED + NARROWED.**

The text directly juxtaposes `偽物` and `本物` around Rumi's action.

However, Hachiman immediately hedges with `きっと`, `知らねぇけど`, and `たぶん`, and the conversation closes with epistemic uncertainty.

Revised claim:

> A knowingly chosen act inside a false relation becomes a **candidate sign** of genuineness; the volume refuses certainty about the actor's interior truth.

## V1-4C — “Authenticity is becoming an act rather than a pedigree”

**Disposition: RETAIN-NARROW.**

Excellent retrospective shorthand, too categorical as a publication-position claim.

## V1-4D — “Chosen solitude and imposed exclusion are categorically different”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED.**

The Japanese explicitly contrasts ordinary `一人でいる` with being `悪意によって孤立させられている`.

V2 adds that Hachiman first fails to make this distinction and rationalizes nonintervention, so the insight is also a narratorial correction.

## V1-4E — “Yui's kindness is informed by knowledge of cowardice”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED.**

The text directly supports this. Yui explains the fear of talking to an excluded person, admits the temptation to distance oneself, and acknowledges residual guilt.

V2 further upgrades Yui from emotional witness to **causal analyst**.

## V1-4F — “Yukino waits for an explicit request and therefore misses constrained desire”

**Disposition: RETAIN-NARROW.**

Yui does expose the limitation. But V1 underweighted why Yukino's condition is ethically serious: it protects authorization and avoids defining another person's good without permission.

## V1-4G — “Hachiman destroys the coercive structure rather than repairing it”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED.**

`解決` versus `解消` is explicit in the Japanese.

## V1-4H — “Hachiman delegates cruel roles to Hayama/Miura/Tobe”

**Disposition: NARROWED.**

True descriptively, but potentially misleading ethically. The older students substantially know and consent to their roles. Hayama explicitly negotiates the premise and asks Hachiman to direct.

The nonconsensual manipulation is primarily directed at the children.

## V1-4I — “Rumi is rescued through agency rather than reintegration”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED.**

The chapter title foregrounds her choosing her own path; Yukino explicitly says Rumi moved through her own will.

“Rescued” should still be used carefully: her social life is not repaired.

## V1-4J — “Hachiman's method is an act of projection”

**Disposition: RETAIN AS INFERENCE, DOWNGRADE FROM FACT.**

Yukino explicitly asks whom he really wanted to save, and Hachiman's own childhood memories make projection highly plausible. But the text does not omnisciently settle the question.

Yui and Hayama's autobiographical projection are more directly acknowledged than Hachiman's.

## V1-4K — “Hayama becomes a genuine ideological counterpart”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED.**

Same method, opposite theory of human nature; highly efficient work coordination; admitted past failure; explicit prediction that he and Hachiman would not have become friends.

## V1-4L — “Yukino's family situation reframes her independence”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED + NARROWED.**

`代役` and the maternal summons make family-role constraint unmistakable.

V1 was too close to treating parental care as established domination. Volume 4 gives Yukino's experienced frame and behavior, not a final moral diagnosis of her mother.

## V1-4M — “The maternal scene is Yukino's first unambiguous apology”

**Disposition: NARROWED FOR TRACEABILITY.**

Hachiman himself says it may be the first proper apology he has seen from her. Unless the full earlier corpus is exhaustively searched for every apology construction, V2 records it as **one of the earliest explicit proper apologies and the first Hachiman explicitly marks as such**.

## V1-4N — “The car scene converts nonintrusion into active concealment”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED.**

The narrator explicitly labels his own response contrary to his actual knowledge.

## V1-4O — “Yui controls relational momentum”

**Disposition: STRENGTHENED AND EXPANDED.**

V2 adds:

- causal diagnosis of constrained help;
- stopping Hachiman so Rumi can act;
- explicit one-on-one future request;
- physical intervention in Haruno/Hachiman proximity;
- beginning the car question at the end.

She is not simply preserving atmosphere. She repeatedly **opens the next relational question**.

# 18. Cumulative delta through Volume 4

## 18.1 Hachiman

**Strengthened:**

- external social-system analysis;
- sensitivity to status, exclusion, and sacrificial group mechanisms;
- capacity for complex intervention design;
- ability to cooperate effectively when the relationship is organized as work.

**Newly explicit:**

- fear of irreplaceable relationships;
- preference for easily severable ties because they minimize irreversible loss;
- distinction between chosen solitude and imposed isolation;
- `解消` as an intervention distinct from genuine solution;
- willingness to destroy a structure rather than rehabilitate it;
- active concealment of relevant information from Yui.

**Revised:**

The self-sacrifice/control master hypothesis should **not** yet govern this volume. The better early-stage descriptor is:

> **structural diagnosis + engineered rupture + high confidence in his right to manipulate the target environment, moderated by meaningful consultation with collaborators.**

## 18.2 Yukino

**Strengthened:**

- commitment to agency-preserving help;
- ability to revise herself when evidence exposes projection;
- particular admiration for Yui;
- capacity to recognize Hachiman's useful contribution without sanitizing his method.

**Newly explicit:**

- public family role as Haruno's `代役`;
- recognition that her maternal model differs from what she calls `普通`;
- family authority can abruptly override peer-world plans.

**Held open:**

- exact nature of Mother's control;
- precise childhood history with Hayama;
- whether late self-removal has meaningful roots here or would be retrospective overreach.

## 18.3 Yui

**Strengthened:**

- relational intelligence as causal analysis, not merely empathy;
- moral courage grounded in awareness of cowardice;
- independent Yukino friendship;
- active romantic pursuit of Hachiman.

**Newly explicit:**

- desire can be constrained by atmosphere without disappearing;
- guilt can make receiving help feel illegitimate;
- she can intervene to preserve another person's opportunity to choose.

The “Yui as preserver” hypothesis should continue to be rewritten toward:

> **Yui as builder and manager of relational possibility, sometimes through accommodation and sometimes through active disruption of the existing atmosphere.**

## 18.4 Hayama

Volume 4 establishes him as a genuine competing social intelligence.

He is:

- neither naïve nor hypocritical;
- willing to accept ugly methods under constrained circumstances;
- more optimistic than Hachiman about what pressure can reveal;
- burdened by a remembered failure to act;
- historically entangled with Yukino;
- capable of precise mutual recognition with Hachiman without requiring friendship.

## 18.5 Rumi

Rumi adds a new autonomy category to the corpus:

> **a person can be manipulated into a situation and still author an action within it that the manipulator did not design.**

This does not retroactively legitimate the manipulation. It prevents the helper from owning the subject's agency.

## 18.6 `本物`

The honmono ledger now has a clearer longitudinal progression:

- **V1–V2:** no explicit central `本物` vocabulary.
- **V3:** authentic presentation, non-instrumental expression, personal particularity.
- **V4:** possible genuineness of a knowingly chosen act inside acknowledged relational falsity.

The most important methodological note is **uncertainty**. Volume 4 refuses to treat interior truth as fully recoverable.

## 18.7 Intervention ethics

Volume 4 creates the first large-scale controlled comparison among:

- benevolent reintegration;
- request-conditioned help;
- empathic diagnosis of constrained request;
- structural destruction.

No model receives total vindication.

This should remain the baseline against which later Hachiman interventions are judged.

## 18.8 Social systems

The series' critique of groups becomes more precise:

> The problem is not plurality itself. The problem is a collective form that distributes responsibility, requires conformity, and preserves peace by repeatedly locating someone who can bear exclusion.

The camp itself, the Service Club, and Hachiman/Hayama's cooperation demonstrate that structured collective activity can also expand agency.

## 18.9 Forward questions for Volume 5

1. What does Hachiman's car lie protect: Yukino's privacy, Yui, himself, or the current relational equilibrium?
2. How will Hachiman respond when new information revises his idealized image of Yukino?
3. Can “not knowing” remain ethical once another person's hidden history directly affects one's own relationship with them?
4. Does Hachiman's new `本物` criterion survive a case involving concealment by someone he trusts?
5. How far does Yukino's family role actually restrict her autonomy?
6. Will Yui's one-on-one invitation remain indefinitely deferred, or become a demand for an answer?
7. Does Hayama's remembered failure concern Yukino, and what can legitimately be inferred before later volumes say more?
8. Can the Service Club develop an intervention model that creates conditions for agency **without** coercively staging the subject's crisis?
9. Will Hachiman continue treating relation-breaking as a legitimate form of `解消` when the relationship at risk is his own?

## Volume 4 thesis

> **Volume 4 does not prove that false relationships become genuine through good acts. It discovers a more difficult possibility: even when people know a social form is compromised, an unscripted choice within it may still carry genuine moral weight. Hachiman learns to distinguish chosen solitude from imposed exclusion and demonstrates that he can destroy a coercive system, but he does not yet know how to build a healthy one. More importantly, the volume refuses to let him own Rumi's agency: he creates the crisis, Yui protects the opening, and Rumi chooses what to do. The summer ends by turning the same problem inward. Hachiman recognizes a fact capable of destabilizing his own relationships and chooses concealment. The question of authenticity has moved from other people's groups into the information architecture of the Service Club itself.**
