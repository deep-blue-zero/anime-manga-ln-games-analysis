# OREGAIRU V2 - Volume 14.5 Deep Reading
## 『やはり俺の青春ラブコメはまちがっている。14.5』

### Governing thesis

**Volume 14.5 does not add one uniform epilogue to Volume 14. Its five stories occupy three different chronological strata, and that routing changes what the book is allowed to prove. The two New Year stories belong immediately after Volume 9 and preserve the unresolved plausibility of both Yukino and Yui before the final-choice movement; the festival story belongs after the joint prom but before the final Service Club scene and turns Hachiman from an observer of finite time into a willing participant in it; only the Iroha story and the newly written final story constitute genuine post-mainline ordinary-life evidence. Those post-ending stories do not certify that Volume 14 produced a pure or permanently stable relationship. Instead, they translate its enormous language of lives and futures into small repeatable procedures: remembering a birthday, accepting a deliberately transparent pretext, comparing cram schools, refusing to let Yukino lower her academic ambitions to match Hachiman, marking one month without demanding reciprocal ceremony, deciding how much authority older members should exercise over Komachi's club, giving new members and quasi-members mugs, and teaching someone else how to make tea. The most important self-correction is Hachiman's recognition that he has begun to `神聖視` the present Service Club configuration. He explicitly rejects that sanctification: their relationship was never perfect, has always been distorted and frayed, and continues to widen while they `まちがい続ける`. Volume 14.5 therefore tests the ending not by preserving its form, but by permitting succession, new attachments, changing personnel, and ordinary imperfection. Its strongest final proposition is not that the answer has been proven genuine. It is that continuity can survive without turning the chosen answer into a shrine.**

## 1.4 The chronology audit is mandatory, not optional

A superficial reading of “14.5” as “the epilogue after Volume 14” would contaminate the longitudinal ledgers. The internal dating establishes three distinct strata.

### Story 1: January 1, post-Volume 9 / pre-Volume 10

Hachiman states that Komachi takes him to the New Year shrine; Yukino and Yui join them; Yui later leaves for Miura's group; Komachi doubles back; and Hachiman and Yukino return together (`part0007 ¶8-11`). This is the New Year immediately following the Christmas movement already analyzed after Volume 9. It belongs **before** the central events of Volume 10.

### Story 2: January 2, post-Volume 9 / pre-Volume 10

The story explicitly opens after Hachiman completes the promise made to Yui at the previous day's shrine visit to shop for Yukino's birthday present (`part0008 ¶4`). It therefore follows Story 1 by one day and remains in the same pre-Volume-10 window.

### Story 3: after the joint prom, during spring break, before the new term

Hachiman says they have survived the `合同プロム` and finally reached spring break (`part0009 ¶37-39`). Komachi is still anticipating entry into Soubu High, while Hachiman is “almost” a third-year (`part0013 ¶50`, `¶72-75`). This places the story **inside the late Volume-14 temporal envelope** after Hachiman and Yukino's mutual choice and after the joint prom, but before the new school year and before the final post-transition Service Club state is fully established.

### Story 4: mid-April, third year

The narration dates the scene to just past the midpoint of April (`part0014 ¶2-4`) and explicitly says Hachiman is now a third-year (`part0014 ¶68-71`). The Service Club is already operating in the post-mainline configuration and planning Iroha's birthday surprise (`part0014 ¶103-109`). This is unambiguously **post-ending ordinary life**.

### Story 5: late spring, roughly one month into the new Service Club

The story opens as spring approaches early summer (`part0017 ¶2-4`). Hachiman is a third-year; Komachi has entered Soubu; the recreated Service Club has been operating for about a month; Hachiman is now a university-entrance-exam student. The later one-month anniversary between Hachiman and Yukino anchors the romantic chronology as well (`part0021 ¶206-217`). This is the strongest **post-mainline stabilization witness** in the volume.

## 1.5 Authority routing rule

The chronology audit produces a strict evidentiary rule:

- Stories 1-2 may **supplement** the post-Volume-9 / pre-Volume-10 state, but cannot revise Checkpoint E.
- Story 3 may supplement the late-Volume-14 state, but cannot be used to make the Volume-14 resolution prospectively easier or more certain than the novel itself made it.
- Stories 4-5 alone may update the post-Checkpoint-E ordinary-life state.

This is the governing methodological correction to the V1 framing of “14.5 as post-ending coda.”

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

The relevant prospective freeze is unusual because the volume itself moves backward and forward in story time.

For Stories 1-2, the reader must return to the immediate post-Volume-9 state. Hachiman has asked for `本物`; the Service Club is again inhabitable; the Christmas coda has shown that ordinary continuity can resume; but the romantic future is not decided. Yukino and Yui are both emotionally salient. No Volume-14 outcome may be imported into those scenes.

For Story 3, the reader may know the late Volume-14 state up through the joint prom and Hachiman/Yukino mutual choice, but the story is still an interstitial festival scene. It does not possess authority to rewrite the exact procedural and epistemic difficulty of the bridge, Yui's loss, or the final Service Club consultation.

For Stories 4-5, Checkpoint E is frozen and immutable. The reader may know:

- Hachiman and Yukino have mutually chosen romantic and life involvement;
- Yukino's independent capability remains explicit;
- Yui has received a real non-reciprocal romantic answer and has suffered real loss;
- Yui nevertheless retains friendship standing and ordinary Service Club presence;
- `共依存` has been reduced from a total explanation to a bounded mechanism;
- `本物` has not been certified;
- Hachiman has chosen to continue doubting inside commitment;
- the final Service Club exists under known asymmetry rather than protected ambiguity.

The post-ending question is therefore **not** whether those things “really happened.” It is whether they can produce workable ordinary procedures once crisis no longer forces everyone into honesty.

Volume 14.5 is not permitted to turn later comfort into retrospective proof that the Volume-14 choice was objectively correct. It can only test durability after the fact.

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

## 3.1 Story 1: Komachi's sister-in-law game preserves unresolved Yukino salience

The first story is comic, but its chronology makes the comedy diagnostically useful. After the shrine visit, Yui departs to join Miura's group and Komachi returns for a forgotten charm, leaving Hachiman and Yukino to travel together. Hachiman describes the short trip as disproportionately vivid: the weak tug at his sleeve and Yukino's small goodbye wave remain difficult to forget (`part0007 ¶9-11`).

That is strong behavioral/narratorial evidence of Yukino-specific salience **before** the final-choice movement. It is not evidence that the ending was already metaphysically predetermined. The same period contains substantial Yui intimacy, as Story 2 will show.

Komachi then turns the household conversation into an absurd “future sister-in-law candidate” exercise. Hachiman's immediate objection is more important than the joke: `ご本人に許可なく勝手にそんな私的な候補挙げるの、ぜひやめて？` (`part0007 ¶44`). The line is comic self-protection, but it also lands directly on one of the V2 project's strongest ethical concerns: third parties do not obtain jurisdiction over another person's intimate future merely because they care about the outcome.

Komachi names Yukino as the strongest candidate and supplies comically mercenary reasons, but she ends with the much broader statement `小町はお兄ちゃんが幸せならそれでいい` (`part0007 ¶82`). Komachi therefore functions less as a route oracle than as a family member trying to imagine Hachiman's future happiness through whichever relational forms she can picture.

## 3.2 Story 2: Yui remains an entirely plausible future before choice

Story 2 is the necessary counterweight to any retrospective reading of Story 1. The next day, Hachiman returns from shopping with Yui for Yukino's birthday present. He describes the shopping time, casual conversation, and moments of physical proximity in warm sunset imagery (`part0008 ¶9`). The same sequence becomes tense when Haruno and Hayama appear and dark when Yukino and her mother arrive. The affective geography is therefore not “Yukino light / Yui false.” Yui occupies a genuinely warm, easy, desirable relational register.

Komachi now calls Yui a top-class sister-in-law candidate (`part0008 ¶25`). Hachiman rejects the narrowing premise in a revealing way: `由比ヶ浜は誰と結婚しても、いいお嫁さんになるに決まってる。相手を俺に限定して語る必要性はない` (`part0008 ¶30`).

This is not a clean romantic rejection. It may contain distancing, preemptive deflection, genuine admiration, and the familiar Hachiman move of universalizing a personally threatening possibility. V2 therefore classifies it as **Hachiman self-implicating narration with multiple plausible functions**, not as decisive evidence that he already knew Yui could never be his partner.

Komachi again says Hachiman's happiness is what matters (`part0008 ¶77`). Her closing reflection is deliberately form-open: someday there will probably be someone who takes Hachiman's hand, `どんな形かはわかりませんが` (`part0008 ¶88-89`). That openness is the right prospective freeze. The story recognizes a relational future without specifying its final geometry.

## 3.3 Story 3: the festival converts finite time from an object of observation into a reason to participate

Story 3 occurs after the joint prom, during spring break. Its structure is outwardly comic and event-like because it rewrites a live-reading script, yet the temporal argument is unusually direct.

Hachiman attends because Komachi brings him. At the entrance, when he might otherwise make the decision for her, he explicitly checks her preference: `ここは当人の意志を確認するべきだろう` (`part0009 ¶61`). That is a small but clean continuity with the late-series authorization ethic.

The larger ethical imperfection is that Yui and Komachi deliberately route the invitation through Komachi because they know Hachiman would refuse a direct invitation. Hachiman reconstructs the choice architecture himself: Yui says `ヒッキーに言ったら断るから`; Komachi knows that if the request comes from his sister on the day itself, he is unlikely to refuse (`part0011 ¶94-103`).

This is manipulation. It is also low-stakes, transparent once discovered, and directed toward optional leisure rather than extracting vulnerable information or solving another person's life. V2 therefore treats it as another example of **pretext whose ethical meaning depends on stakes, exit capacity, and what it forecloses**. The series has not reached a world in which all invitations become procedurally pristine.

The festival then produces a series of comic role performances: Yui improvises an idol introduction; Iroha observes; Yukino, under group expectation and embarrassment, performs a painfully cute `ゆきのん` self-introduction; Hachiman's narration effectively short-circuits (`part0011 ¶402-413`, `part0013 ¶1-14`). The scene matters less as romantic proof than as evidence that the post-choice group can still produce shared play, embarrassment, mutual teasing, and care.

The ending supplies the story's real temporal proposition. Hachiman watches Yukino, Yui, Iroha, and Komachi walking ahead. Their shadows overlap without remaining fixed. He asks how many more times he will see all of them together. Graduation is approaching. The festival will end. Then he generalizes: because ordinary days also end, `なんてことない日常さえも、祭りのひとつ`; everyday life is also their festival (`part0013 ¶155-171`).

Crucially, he does not remain behind to contemplate the image. He answers their call—`ああ、今行く`—and walks toward the final act (`part0013 ¶176-178`).

The change is procedural:

> **Finitude no longer provides a reason to withdraw before loss. It provides a reason to enter the event while it is still happening.**

This is a meaningful late-Volume-14 corroboration, but because of chronology it cannot be used to retroactively make the mainline choice less difficult.

## 3.4 Story 4: Iroha tests whether mixed motive still has standing after romantic choice

Story 4 is the first clean post-ending ordinary-life witness. It begins in mid-April. Hachiman is now a third-year. He sees Iroha among her new classmates and momentarily re-recognizes a fact familiarity had dulled: she is socially independent of him and genuinely attractive (`part0014 ¶14-19`).

When their eyes meet, Iroha gives him a small covert wave; Hachiman becomes self-conscious about how to respond in front of her friends (`part0014 ¶20-26`). Iroha then uses “going to the student-council room” as an excuse to separate from the group and returns specifically to sit with him. Her repeated phrase `こういう時` makes the function of the excuse deliberately legible (`part0014 ¶34-52`).

This is not a revelation that Iroha's previous persona was false. It is the opposite. Role, excuse, flirtation, enjoyment, and genuine preference all coexist in one action.

Iroha later talks about boys manufacturing small reasons to start conversations. Hachiman, partly joking and partly defending the entire class of small bids for contact, insists that a small action can change the world (`part0014 ¶60-65`). The story then stages precisely such a bid.

Hachiman remembers Iroha's birthday and gives her a Max Coffee (`part0014 ¶83-100`). The Service Club already has a proper surprise planned, and he has a separate actual present, but Iroha does not let this first gesture become a closed transaction. She hands him her water in “exchange,” declares the coffee gift void, and schedules a weekend encounter. Her explicit line is `プレゼントは言い訳ですよ` (`part0016 ¶1-12`).

The line is almost a post-ending miniature of the series' entire pretext problem. Here the excuse is not masking a secret institutional objective. The excuse itself is openly functioning as **social infrastructure for continued contact**.

Yet V2 must preserve the caveat: Iroha says she is looking forward to the weekend without waiting for a clean explicit yes, and Hachiman describes the plan as being decided `なし崩し` (`part0016 ¶12-16`). This is not a model of perfect authorization. Ordinary life remains full of pressure, assumption, flirtatious maneuver, and relational asymmetry.

The closing line matters because it refuses a singular revelation. `さりげなくてなにげない、いつも通りの積み重ね`—casual, ordinary repetitions—move the heart and weave the future (`part0016 ¶19-20`). Future is produced by accumulation, not one climactic certification.

## 3.5 Story 5: succession, not preservation, becomes the final problem

The newly written final story begins as spring shades toward early summer. Its opening discussion of friendship is deliberately unsentimental: relationships expose a person to other people's partners, friends, enemies, and obligations; Hachiman's third-year class now places him beside Hayama and around Ebina; even old discomfort becomes an ordinary social ecology (`part0017 ¶2-33`).

Komachi is now a Soubu student and the president of the recreated Service Club. After roughly a month, Hachiman becomes preoccupied with what will happen when the older trio graduates. His first instinct is recognizably protective: perhaps the club needs new members so Komachi will not be “left behind.”

The problem is that this thought silently assumes the current club is the normative template of what must be preserved.

The story builds toward Hachiman recognizing exactly that mistake. He cannot simply tell Komachi to preserve the club because she is likely to honor his wish even against her own developing preference. But he also catches the opposite evasion: saying “decide for yourself” can become responsibility-shifting when an older member actually has relevant experience and care to offer. The choice is not between controlling advice and total silence.

This becomes the volume's final institutional ethics problem: **How can a predecessor transmit something valuable without turning succession into replication?**

The answer unfolds through Yukino, Yui, Iroha, Komachi, mugs, and tea rather than through another abstract theory.

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

## 4.1 Stories 1-2: retrospective danger is highest where Hachiman universalizes personal threat

The New Year stories are an excellent control case for the V2 reliability model because they show Hachiman before the final choice but are published after it. A retrospective reader can easily convert every Yukino-salient thought into foreshadowing and every Yui deflection into proof of absence. The text does not support that simplicity.

Hachiman's vivid memory of Yukino's sleeve tug and goodbye wave is high-confidence **behavioral/narratorial salience**. He notices it, retains it, and admits it is difficult to forget (`part0007 ¶11`). The evidence does not require a theory about hidden destiny.

His response to Yui is more epistemically unstable. In Story 2, he warmly describes their shopping time and physical nearness, then immediately turns Komachi's Hachiman-specific marriage proposal into a universal claim that Yui would be a good wife for anyone (`part0008 ¶9`, `¶29-30`). That move is characteristic of Hachiman's self-implicating domain: a personally threatening possibility is abstracted into a safer general proposition. The general proposition may still be true. Its rhetorical function is the unstable part.

Accordingly:

- `由比ヶ浜は誰と結婚しても、いいお嫁さんになる` = character judgment with high sincerity;
- `相手を俺に限定して語る必要性はない` = possible distancing/rationalization;
- “therefore Hachiman has already rejected Yui romantically” = **not established**.

## 4.2 Story 3: Hachiman's finitude language is more reliable because behavior follows it

Hachiman has always been capable of beautiful elegiac interpretation, but V2 asks whether language predicts action. The festival conclusion passes that test better than many earlier monologues.

He recognizes that the group will not remain together in its present form; he frames ordinary life as finite festival; then, instead of preserving the image through distance, he moves toward them (`part0013 ¶155-178`). His action therefore corroborates the narration.

This is a real delta from early Hachiman, who often converted anticipated loss into preemptive nonparticipation. It does not mean he has become fearless about endings. It means he can now know an experience will end and participate anyway.

## 4.3 Story 4: Iroha exposes Hachiman's continuing embarrassment around being visibly chosen

Hachiman's Iroha narration remains a mixture of acute perception, attraction, comic camouflage, and defensive overanalysis. He repeatedly and explicitly calls her cute (`part0014 ¶14-18`, `¶31`). He becomes embarrassed by the secret-looking wave and by her return to him. These are direct affective observations.

What the story does **not** establish is romantic reciprocity comparable to his chosen relationship with Yukino. Hachiman enjoys Iroha, notices her attractiveness, is flustered by flirtation, and accepts being maneuvered into future contact. None of those facts require the conclusion that Volume 14's romantic choice is unstable or false.

The correct V2 category is:

> **continued susceptibility to another person's charm and bids for contact after romantic choice.**

That is ordinary human complexity, not automatic route reopening.

## 4.4 Story 5: `神聖視` is a high-value self-correction

Hachiman's most important narration in Volume 14.5 occurs when he realizes why he imagined Komachi being “left behind.” He had treated the current Service Club as though its exact configuration were the complete, best, finished form. He names this explicitly:

`俺は俺たちという関係性に拘泥しすぎていたのかもしれない。いや、神聖視していたと言ってもいい。` (`part0021 ¶175-176`)

This self-correction is unusually reliable for three reasons.

First, it explains a concrete prior behavior: his reluctance to advise Komachi about future recruitment.

Second, Yukino supplies a viable countermodel immediately beforehand: the room is large enough for more people; even under Yukino's difficult presidency others came; Komachi can have irreplaceable encounters of her own (`part0021 ¶161-172`).

Third, Hachiman then applies the correction prospectively. He no longer treats preserving the exact old group as the goal. His behavior toward Komachi shifts from “protect the room I valued” toward “acknowledge that her tenure should create experiences not authored by me.”

This is therefore one of the strongest **self-recognized narratorial correction** entries in the entire reread.

## 4.5 `俺たちの関係性が完璧だったことなどあるだろうか。否`

The next paragraphs matter because they block a triumphalist reading of Volume 14. Hachiman explicitly asks whether their relationship was ever perfect and answers no (`part0021 ¶181-182`). He describes it instead as something that has always been distorted, frayed, sometimes severed, tenuously reconnected, and still expanding while `未だにまちがい続けながら` (`part0021 ¶183`).

This is not retrospective self-condemnation. Nor is it the declaration that error itself is morally good. It is an anti-sanctification statement:

> the value of the relation does not depend on reconstructing its past as a hidden path toward perfection.

That is fully compatible with Checkpoint E's anti-certification stance.

# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Hikigaya Hachiman

Volume 14.5's true post-ending contribution to Hachiman is not that he becomes socially normal. He remains evasive, embarrassed, cynical, easily flustered, and capable of turning a simple emotional fact into three paragraphs of comic rationalization.

The more important change is what those defenses now coexist with.

### He permits future assumptions

At the cram-school cafe, Yukino imagines meeting after separate university lectures. Hachiman initially punctures the fantasy with realism, but then says the institution itself need not matter: `大学違っても待ち合わせはするだろうし` (`part0021 ¶28-43`). The line is phrased casually, but the narration identifies it as carrying a wish that this will indeed happen.

This is a major post-ending transformation in scale. Volume 14's bridge used the enormous rhetoric of lives. Volume 14.5 lets Hachiman assume something much smaller and in some ways more demanding: **we will probably keep arranging to see each other next year even if circumstances diverge.**

### He resists dependency through aspiration-lowering

Hachiman's statement about university choice is especially important. He says Yukino will not lower herself to his academic level to attend the same university; if she tried, he would actively stop her (`part0021 ¶32-40`).

That is clean evidence against a fusion model of intimacy. Continued relationship is imagined alongside divergent institutional paths. He wants proximity without making sameness of destination a condition of love.

### He accepts reliance and proximity while still rationalizing them

When Yukino wants to visit other prep schools together, she eventually admits that being together would help her maintain motivation (`part0021 ¶99-126`). Hachiman's internal reaction is delighted and wildly over-rationalized. He invents practical justifications and then collapses into `絶対そうします`.

The joke matters because the old Hachiman often used rationalization to deny desire. Here the rationalization no longer prevents the desired action. It becomes comic face-saving around an action both parties are openly choosing.

### He recognizes limits on his authority over Komachi

The Service Club succession problem forces him to distinguish three bad shortcuts:

- order Komachi to preserve what he loved;
- say “do whatever you want” and abandon his responsibility to advise;
- imagine his own preferred configuration as the only valuable one.

His eventual bow—`改めまして、比企谷部長。よろしくお願いします` (`part0023 ¶124-143`)—is symbolically important because he addresses Komachi as the present office-holder rather than treating himself as owner of the institution's true meaning.

Hachiman's final V14.5 state is therefore:

> **more capable of future-oriented attachment, more capable of transmitting without owning, and still constitutionally unable to do any of this without embarrassment and comic camouflage.**

## 5.2 Yukinoshita Yukino

Yukino's post-ending characterization is one of the clearest gains over V1 because ordinary scenes expose how reciprocity works when no crisis demands it.

### She imagines shared future without requiring institutional sameness

Her university fantasy is tentative: separate lectures, meeting afterward, talking over food (`part0021 ¶18-25`). This is not a grand marriage projection. It is exactly the kind of ordinary future image the earlier Yukino often struggled to generate for herself.

When Hachiman says they can meet even at different universities, she accepts the possibility rather than converting the future into a competition or sacrifice (`part0021 ¶40-44`).

### She asks for shared environment directly, if awkwardly

Yukino initially disguises her desire to visit another cram school with Hachiman inside an argument about “environment.” Eventually she says `一緒の方が頑張れると思うし` (`part0021 ¶107-126`).

This is reliance language without `依存` language. More importantly, it does not rest on incapacity. Yukino can study alone. The proposed shared environment is desirable because it supports motivation and ordinary proximity.

### Her one-month gift is explicitly self-authored

Yukino gives Hachiman handmade cookies for their one-month anniversary (`part0021 ¶196-217`). When he panics because he has prepared nothing, she immediately says `私が勝手にやっているだけだから` (`part0021 ¶220-225`).

That line is deceptively important. She does not transform her gift into a debt. She leaves Hachiman room not to have anticipated the ritual. Only after he remains uneasy does she playfully permit later reciprocation.

The relationship is therefore being built through **initiated bids that do not automatically become obligations**.

### She participates in succession rather than preserving ownership

Yukino's answer to Hachiman's fear for Komachi is not “preserve our club.” She points out that even her own presidency did not prevent new people from arriving and that Komachi may encounter people who become irreplaceable `私たちみたいに` (`part0021 ¶161-172`).

Later, she teaches Komachi how to brew tea (`part0023 ¶188-203`). This is one of the volume's cleanest images of continuity:

> practice transmitted to a successor without demanding that the successor reproduce the predecessor's relationship structure.

Yukino's post-ending delta is therefore better described as **reciprocal future-making + transmissive competence**, not dependency cured by romance.

## 5.3 Yuigahama Yui

Yui's role in 14.5 is substantially stronger than a “gracious loser remains friends” reading permits.

Stories 1-2 first preserve the prospective fact that Yui was genuinely plausible before the final choice. Story 2's warm shopping scene and Hachiman's admiration prevent the ending from rewriting her into a relationship that was never live.

In the true post-ending material, Yui's most important function is speech.

When Iroha asks what should happen with the future Service Club, Hachiman and Yukino have become cautious because they understand how easily their preferences can pressure Komachi. Yui simply says: `いいじゃん、載せようよ。新入部員じゃんじゃん入れよう` (`part0023 ¶35-39`).

Iroha's internal narration is unusually perceptive here: Yui understands the caution and the unspoken considerations, but is willing to say the simple desire anyway. This is not ignorance. It is a relational competence Hachiman and Yukino still often lack: **knowing complexity without requiring complexity to paralyze speech**.

Yui also refuses the preservation premise. She reminds Iroha and Komachi that she and Hachiman themselves joined midstream; future membership need not arrive at entrance ceremonies or reproduce the same origin (`part0023 ¶50-59`).

Her discussion of prep school is more ambiguous. She says she can ask Hachiman where he is going and perhaps use that as a reference for her own choice (`part0021 ¶255-269`, `part0023 ¶1-10`). Iroha and Komachi are tempted to frame this romantically. V2 does not grant them authority to decide Yui's motive. The practical and romantic readings may coexist.

That ambiguity should **not** be inflated into evidence that Yui intends to preserve romantic hope indefinitely. The stronger evidence is simpler: she remains future-facing, chooses participation, and can speak for continuation without asking the chosen dyad to pretend her loss never happened.

## 5.4 Isshiki Iroha

Iroha receives one of the volume's richest post-ending treatments.

Story 4 confirms the model already developed in Volumes 10 and 10.5: performance does not negate sincerity. She uses student-council duty as an excuse to leave friends, uses gift exchange as an excuse for another meeting, and openly tells Hachiman that the present is itself a pretext. The action is strategic precisely because she genuinely wants continued contact.

Story 5 then adds **epistemic humility**. When Iroha tries to infer why Komachi is reluctant to recruit, she catches herself:

`……いや、お米ちゃんがほんとのところ、どう思ってるかは知らないけど。`

`私がわかるのは、わたしのことだけ。だから、わたし基準に想像するしかない。` (`part0023 ¶45-47`)

This is a remarkable contrast with Hachiman's old interpretive habits. Iroha still infers, but she labels the inference as projection from her own experience rather than privileged access to another person's true motive.

She also becomes part of the Service Club's succession ecology despite not technically being a member. The joke becomes material: when mugs are distributed, one paper cup would now be economically absurd, so Iroha receives a matching mug too (`part0023 ¶144-164`). The institution acknowledges habitual belonging that exceeds formal membership.

Her V14.5 state is therefore:

> **strategic, flirtatious, institutionally embedded, epistemically more modest, and socially future-facing.**

## 5.5 Hikigaya Komachi

Komachi is the volume's hidden structural center. She dominates the first two story titles, brings Hachiman into the festival, enters Soubu, becomes Service Club president, and becomes the person through whom continuity must be rethought.

Her early sister-in-law games should not be mistaken for narrative omniscience. She nominates Yukino, Yui, Haruno, and increasingly absurd alternatives because the comedy is about her desire to imagine Hachiman's adulthood and secure her own continuing place in it. Her governing statement in both stories is his happiness, not route correctness (`part0007 ¶82`; `part0008 ¶77`).

The post-ending Komachi is more important institutionally. She recreates the Service Club but is not yet sure whether she wants new members. Hachiman's task is not to tell her what the “true” club should be. The final story deliberately transfers office, tools, and practice while preserving her right to build something different.

The new mug marks her presidency; Hachiman formally addresses her as `比企谷部長`; Yukino teaches her tea; and Komachi decides, after hearing Iroha's serious concern, that the club may be listed in recruitment materials (`part0023 ¶107-180`).

The succession is therefore not merely sentimental. Komachi receives both **authority and inherited practice**.

## 5.6 Hayama, Ebina, and the ordinary social field

Story 5's opening quietly normalizes Hayama. Hachiman and Hayama are seated near each other in the third-year class and have developed a mode of interaction in which neither expects complete communication (`part0017 ¶12-31`). This is not reconciliation into friendship. It is a workable social accommodation.

That matters because the series' ending does not isolate Hachiman into one chosen relationship. He remains embedded in a wider social field: Hayama, Ebina, Iroha, Komachi, Yui, Yukino, classmates, and institutional obligations. Ordinary adulthood is foreshadowed as management of overlapping ties rather than purification into one dyad.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Hachiman-Yukino: from life claim to ordinary repeated future

Volume 14 made their mutual claim explicit in maximal language. Volume 14.5's strongest contribution is to lower the register without reducing the commitment.

They discuss different universities and still assume meetings. They compare cram schools. Yukino says being together would help her work. Hachiman refuses the possibility that she should reduce her academic ambition to match him. She gives him one-month cookies. They are embarrassed by how to mark one year and ten years because neither can actually picture those horizons clearly.

This is precisely what ordinary stabilization should look like if Checkpoint E's autonomy model is to survive:

> **shared future without identical path; support without incapacity; ritual without mandatory reciprocity; commitment without epistemic certainty about ten years.**

The one-month-anniversary exchange is especially useful because it combines tenderness with comic uncertainty. When Yukino suggests a one-year anniversary, Hachiman cannot picture his life that far away. When she panics and suggests ten years instead, the proposal becomes absurdly distant. His final thought is not a promise that they will definitely be together then. It is that he cannot imagine forgetting this moment even after decades (`part0021 ¶228-244`).

V2 therefore resists turning the scene into a contractual guarantee. Its evidentiary value lies in **future inclusion becoming mundane enough to joke about**.

## 6.2 Hachiman-Yui: continued standing without restored ambiguity

Volume 14.5 does not supply a new romantic answer between Hachiman and Yui. Checkpoint E remains authoritative: Yui's love and loss are known.

What the volume supplies is evidence that the resulting social future is not immediately uninhabitable. Yui is active in the club, participates in Komachi's celebration, gives institutional advice, and remains part of the older trio's collective position. Story 3 also depicts Hachiman, Yui, Yukino, Iroha, and Komachi enjoying an event after the joint prom.

This should be classified as **durable ordinary presence**, not erased loss and not romantic reset.

## 6.3 Yukino-Yui: friendship survives as an active relationship, not ceremonial forgiveness

The final story repeatedly shows Yukino and Yui coordinating naturally: they shop together for Komachi's gift, plan the surprise, divide attention, exchange glances, and jointly present the mug (`part0023 ¶83-123`). Their relationship is not treated as a solemn post-romantic truce.

That is an important durability test for Checkpoint E. Yui's refusal to let romantic non-reciprocity erase Yukino from her future now produces ordinary cooperative behavior.

Again, this does not prove pain disappeared. Volume 14.5's lower stakes simply show that friendship is behaviorally active afterward.

## 6.4 Hachiman-Iroha: continued flirtatious access becomes a boundary test, not a route verdict

Story 4 demonstrates that a chosen romantic relationship does not remove every other charged interpersonal dynamic. Iroha can still seek Hachiman out, manufacture an excuse for a weekend meeting, and make him visibly flustered.

This is analytically valuable precisely because it prevents a sanitized ending.

The open question is boundary procedure. Iroha's future invitation is not cleanly negotiated; Hachiman's assent is delayed and partly assumed. There is no evidence here that Hachiman intends to violate his relationship with Yukino, and no evidence that attraction/fluster equals romantic reciprocity. But there is evidence that post-choice life will require continuing boundary work rather than relying on the mere fact of having chosen once.

Status: **OPEN as a low-stakes post-ending boundary problem; no route reopening inferred.**

## 6.5 Komachi-Iroha: succession gains its own peer-ish senior/junior axis

The festival establishes that Iroha and Komachi are still relatively new acquaintances after the joint prom (`part0011 ¶87`). By the final story, they have developed a bickering but functional relationship. Hachiman notes that Iroha will remain at Soubu a year longer than the older trio, making her a valuable continuity figure for Komachi (`part0013 ¶96`).

Story 5 realizes that potential. Iroha brings future institutional questions; Komachi responds; they tease one another; Iroha receives a mug; and Komachi formally asks for her future help (`part0023 ¶144-180`).

The Service Club's future therefore does not depend only on preserving the old trio. It already contains a **new horizontal/near-peer continuity network**.

## 6.6 The Service Club: institution becomes transmissible rather than sacred

The key relationship-state change is institutional.

Hachiman initially thinks of the club's future through absence: when the older members leave, Komachi will be left behind. Yukino corrects the premise by imagining new arrivals. Yui says recruit them. Iroha frames the issue as something that must be decided before attachment to the present becomes captivity to the past. Komachi finally permits the club to be listed.

Then practice is transmitted:

- Komachi receives the president's mug;
- Iroha receives a mug despite technical nonmembership;
- Yukino teaches Komachi tea;
- Hachiman formally recognizes Komachi as president;
- the room is imagined changing in personnel, objects, and shape.

The club's continuity is therefore **procedural and cultural**, not identical.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

## 7.1 The volume repeatedly tests whether concern becomes unauthorized authorship

Volume 14.5's low stakes do not eliminate the central ethics of the series. They relocate it.

In Story 1, Hachiman objects to Komachi nominating intimate partners without their permission (`part0007 ¶44`). The joke is minor, but the principle is familiar.

In Story 3, Hachiman explicitly checks Komachi's desire before deciding whether to enter the festival (`part0009 ¶61-64`). Yet Yui and Komachi have themselves manipulated Hachiman's invitation conditions because they know a direct invitation would fail (`part0011 ¶94-103`). The contrast shows why intervention ethics cannot be reduced to “did anyone manipulate anyone?” It matters what is at stake, whether the target can exit, whether the manipulation seizes life decisions, and what costs are imposed.

## 7.2 Story 4: transparent pretext is not automatically clean consent

Iroha's `プレゼントは言い訳ですよ` is among the clearest statements in the series that a pretext can be knowingly shared. Hachiman understands that the present is not the real reason for continued contact.

This makes the maneuver more answerable than earlier false institutional excuses. But Iroha still schedules the weekend before obtaining a crisp yes. The narration calls the process `なし崩し`.

V2 therefore freezes a nuanced proposition:

> **Shared awareness of the pretext reduces deception; it does not erase pressure or make the procedure automatically ideal.**

## 7.3 Story 5: Hachiman identifies the succession double bind

Hachiman's fear about the club creates one of the best mature formulations of advice ethics.

If he says “keep the Service Club,” Komachi may preserve it because he wants it preserved, displacing her authorship.

If he says “decide for yourself,” he may simply avoid the discomfort of offering a view, leaving Komachi to bear the entire burden of a decision about an institution whose history he helped create.

Hachiman recognizes both failures (`part0021 ¶159-185`).

The answer is neither command nor abdication. He eventually gives Komachi acknowledgment, experience, and trust without defining the future club for her.

## 7.4 Yui models speech without jurisdiction

Yui's `載せようよ。新入部員じゃんじゃん入れよう` is an opinion, not a command. Iroha's narration specifically identifies that Yui understands the caution but says the simple thing anyway (`part0023 ¶35-39`).

This is an important mature distinction:

> **Having an opinion about another person's decision is not the same as owning the decision.**

Hachiman and Yukino have sometimes become so afraid of influence that silence appears morally safer. Yui shows that answerability requires actual inputs. Komachi can hear a desire without being reduced to it.

## 7.5 Komachi's final decision has identifiable causal inputs without losing authorship

Komachi eventually says she is leaning toward listing the club because Iroha took the question seriously (`part0023 ¶173-180`). That is not independent decision in the sense of decision without influence. It is authored decision after hearing other people's concern.

This is precisely the autonomy model toward which the V2 reread has been moving:

> **authorship is compatible with being influenced, advised, supported, and emotionally connected, provided the subject remains capable of answering and revising.**

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 Third-year Hachiman is socially embedded whether he wants to be or not

The opening of Story 5 refuses the fantasy that romantic resolution removes Hachiman from social systems. Seat assignments place him beside Hayama; classmates talk around and through Hayama; Ebina enters the conversational field; Hachiman develops low-effort scripts to survive casual interaction (`part0017 ¶12-33`).

The result is not sociability as triumph. It is **institutional familiarity**. Three years of school have produced repeatable accommodations even with people Hachiman does not deeply choose.

## 8.2 Service Club succession is a governance problem

Iroha's request about whether the Service Club should appear in school-information materials appears bureaucratically trivial, but it creates an institutional decision:

- if the club is listed, future students may enter;
- if it is omitted, the institution may effectively close after the current members;
- Komachi is now the office-holder whose preferences matter most;
- older members have standing as predecessors but not ownership;
- Iroha has student-government responsibility for accurately representing school activities.

The series therefore closes one of its longest arcs by turning relationship meaning into **succession governance** rather than another emergency.

## 8.3 Formal membership is not the whole of belonging

Iroha's mug is a small institutional joke with serious implications. She points out that she is not a Service Club member. Yukino answers, effectively, that at this point technical status is beside the point and giving one person a paper cup would be absurd (`part0023 ¶153-164`).

The group recognizes habitual contribution and presence beyond formal category.

This parallels the series' broader resistance to labels that claim exhaustive authority. Formal category matters, but it does not always exhaust lived relation.

## 8.4 Succession requires accepting unknown future members

Yui reminds the younger girls that she and Hachiman themselves joined after the school year was underway (`part0023 ¶50-59`). The implication is institutional as much as personal: recruitment cannot be imagined only through standardized timing or by searching for replacements who resemble the founders.

A durable institution must be able to admit people its founders cannot predict.

## 8.5 The room becomes a site of transmitted culture

The final image of tea is especially important because it locates continuity in a repeatable practice rather than a preserved object.

Hachiman predicts that cups will change in number and shape, the room will change, and eventually no material trace of the older members may remain. Yet while he thinks this, Yukino teaches Komachi to brew tea (`part0023 ¶191-203`).

The proposed continuity is not magical permanence. It is cultural transmission:

> **someone learned a practice from someone, and may later perform it after the teacher is gone.**

# 9. Authenticity / `本物` ledger

## 9.1 Lexical result: the key terms disappear

Across the complete Volume 14.5 EPUB text:

- `本物`: 0
- `偽物`: 0
- `共依存`: 0
- `依存`: 0

That absence must be interpreted carefully because Stories 1-3 are not all post-ending. It would be methodologically wrong to say “after Volume 14 the characters never discuss `本物`” on the basis of the whole collection.

The stronger claim is narrower:

> **In the true post-ending Stories 4-5, ordinary relational life proceeds without needing the diagnostic or authenticity vocabulary that dominated the crisis volumes.**

This does not prove those questions have been solved. It shows they are no longer required to mediate every ordinary interaction.

## 9.2 `神聖視` is the volume's anti-authenticity-keyword

The most important conceptual word in Story 5 is arguably `神聖視`—sanctification (`part0021 ¶175-180`).

Hachiman realizes that once a relationship has been painfully chosen, there is a temptation to retroactively treat its achieved configuration as sacred: the best form, the completed form, the form successors ought to preserve.

Volume 14.5 rejects that move explicitly.

That does **not** amount to a new definition of `本物`. The text never uses the term here. V2 should resist smuggling the old vocabulary back into a passage that has deliberately moved beyond it.

The stronger formulation is:

> **whatever value the chosen relationship has, that value does not authorize sanctifying its present arrangement.**

## 9.3 “We keep making mistakes” is not romanticization of error

`未だにまちがい続けながら広がるもの` (`part0021 ¶183`) can easily be sentimentalized into “mistakes are what make relationships genuine.” That is too loose.

The passage's actual argumentative function is negative: imperfection does not disqualify the relationship from being valuable, and past errors do not make the current form a perfected endpoint.

The claim is therefore:

- relationships may remain valuable through correction and continuation;
- error is not evidence of falsity by itself;
- error is also not evidence of authenticity by itself.

## 9.4 The final authenticity stance remains anti-certification

Checkpoint E freezes Hachiman's final mainline posture as `疑い続けます`: choose, then continue scrutinizing.

Volume 14.5 does not replace that with certainty. Instead, it shows a compatible ordinary behavior:

- future plans remain contingent;
- one year is hard to picture;
- ten years is almost absurd;
- club personnel will change;
- present traces will disappear;
- another person's motive is repeatedly treated as only partially knowable;
- ordinary bids for contact remain mixed in motive.

The ending thesis therefore **survives** non-crisis material without becoming a doctrine of perfection.

# 10. Dependency / autonomy ledger

## 10.1 The diagnostic vocabulary is absent, but the autonomy problem remains active

Volume 14.5 contains no literal `依存` or `共依存`. The disappearance should not be read as proof that every dependency mechanism identified in V12-V14 has vanished. Instead, the post-ending stories test the question behaviorally.

The strongest result is that reliance repeatedly appears without incapacity.

Yukino can study on her own, yet says that being in the same environment as Hachiman would help her work (`part0021 ¶107-126`). Hachiman can make his own cram-school choice, yet happily imagines seeing Yukino around that choice. Komachi can run the Service Club, yet receives advice, a mug, Iroha's ongoing institutional help, and Yukino's tea instruction. Iroha can manage her own social life, yet deliberately uses her institutional role to create private time with Hachiman.

None of these examples requires a pathology label.

## 10.2 Hachiman explicitly protects Yukino's independent academic trajectory

The cram-school conversation supplies unusually clean evidence. Hachiman observes that Yukino may apply to some private universities, but she would not deliberately lower her target to match his level. He adds that if she tried, he would `普通に全力で止めてしまう` (`part0021 ¶34-40`).

This is stronger than simply saying “Yukino can stand alone.” It demonstrates what post-ending attachment does when a shared future and individual aspiration potentially diverge.

Hachiman's desired answer is not “choose the institution that maximizes proximity to me.” It is “pursue the appropriate academic choice; we can still meet.”

That is one of the clearest mature autonomy patterns in the series.

## 10.3 Yukino's shared-environment request is chosen reliance

Yukino's `一緒の方が頑張れると思うし` can be understood only if V2 keeps the distinction between reliance and dependency pathology intact.

She does not say she cannot study unless Hachiman is present. She does not demand that he change his target. She expresses that shared environment would make effort easier and more pleasant.

The answer is therefore:

> **support can increase motivation without replacing competence.**

That is exactly the kind of chosen interdependence Checkpoint E left open as the post-mainline test.

## 10.4 Hachiman's concern for Komachi risks becoming controlling care, and he notices

The final story's first impulse is classic Hachiman: he sees a possible future pain—Komachi alone after graduation—and wants to organize conditions to prevent it. His mature improvement is not lack of protective desire. It is recognition that his desire can become an external script for Komachi.

The line `俺が奉仕部を残してほしいと言ったなら、きっと小町は自分の意志にかかわらず、それを守るだろう` (`part0021 ¶159-160`) directly identifies how family love and trust can reduce apparent choice.

This is an important refinement of the dependency ledger:

> **an offer or preference can be coercive without any threat when the relationship gives the speaker's wish disproportionate weight.**

Hachiman therefore needs a method that preserves both guidance and Komachi's authorship.

## 10.5 “Decide for yourself” can also be an autonomy failure

The subsequent correction is equally important. Hachiman says that simply telling Komachi `好きにしたらいい` or `自分で決めればいい` would be an escape and a form of responsibility transfer (`part0021 ¶181-185`).

This prevents the V2 autonomy framework from collapsing into libertarian noninterference.

People with experience, care, and relational standing may owe one another honest input. The ethical question is whether that input remains answerable rather than determinative.

The mature model becomes:

> **neither command nor abandonment; offer experience, desire, and support while leaving the recipient capable of choosing otherwise.**

## 10.6 Succession demonstrates autonomy through transformed inheritance

Komachi does not preserve the Service Club exactly as Hachiman experienced it. Nor does she reject everything inherited.

She receives:

- the room;
- the institutional name;
- the office of president;
- a new mug;
- older members' stories and opinions;
- Iroha's continuing help;
- Yukino's tea practice.

But she also gains future unknown members and her own decision about recruitment.

This is autonomy as **transformative inheritance**, not originlessness.

## 10.7 Frozen post-14.5 autonomy proposition

The volume strengthens the final series model:

> **A person can be capable of standing alone and still rationally choose shared environments, advice, traditions, affection, and support. Autonomy is not the absence of relational influence; it is the continuing ability to answer what inherited influence becomes.**

# 11. Japanese language and voice

## 11.1 Hachiman's register remains defensive even when his behavior becomes direct

The post-ending stories do not replace Hachiman's voice with conventional romantic sincerity. His inner narration remains saturated with:

- abrupt colloquial particles;
- otaku/game references;
- self-mockery;
- exaggerated bureaucratic/economic reasoning;
- mock legalistic objections;
- recursive rationalization;
- sudden tonal collapses when embarrassed.

This matters because character growth is not expressed through voice homogenization. The Hachiman who can say “we'll probably meet even at different universities” is still the Hachiman who immediately hides the warmth of that claim behind logistics.

## 11.2 `勝手に` changes meaning by context

The volume repeatedly uses the semantic field of unilateral action, but the moral valence depends on what the action claims from another person.

Komachi's unauthorized sister-in-law nominations are rejected because they allocate other people's intimate futures. Yukino's `私が勝手にやっているだけだから` about anniversary cookies does something different: she marks the gift as **her own initiative** and specifically releases Hachiman from reciprocal obligation (`part0021 ¶220-225`).

The same adverb can therefore signal either problematic unilateral authorship or legitimate self-owned action. Context and jurisdiction matter.

## 11.3 `一緒` becomes ordinary future grammar

The cram-school sequence is saturated with awkward variants of togetherness. Yukino assumes they will inspect another school together, becomes embarrassed when Hachiman questions the assumption, and eventually says `一緒の方が頑張れると思うし` (`part0021 ¶99-126`).

The language is modest. It does not say “I need you.” It says, roughly, that together would help.

That lexical modesty is appropriate to the volume's post-climax work: large life claims are being converted into repeatable adverbial and practical forms.

## 11.4 `神聖視`, `拘泥`, `完璧`, `まちがい続ける`

The central succession passage contains a tightly linked lexical cluster:

- `拘泥` — clinging/fixating;
- `神聖視` — sanctifying;
- `至上で最高で完璧` — supreme, best, perfect;
- explicit denial that their relationship was ever `完璧`;
- `歪み`, `綻び`, `絶え` — distortion, fraying, severance;
- `まちがい続けながら広がる` — continuing to expand while continuing to be wrong/make mistakes.

This cluster is stronger than importing `本物` into the passage. The text's chosen vocabulary is about **anti-idolatry of relational form**.

## 11.5 Iroha's `言い訳` is deliberately double-voiced

Iroha's `プレゼントは言い訳ですよ` (`part0016 ¶8`) is delivered flirtatiously, but the word `言い訳` carries series-wide weight because excuses have often served as protective structures around desire.

The post-ending difference is not that excuses disappear. It is that this one is nearly transparent to both participants. Iroha is not asking Hachiman to believe the present is the whole reason.

The line therefore demonstrates an evolved use of indirectness:

> **an excuse can become shared social grammar rather than a false account everyone must pretend is literal.**

## 11.6 Iroha's epistemic disclaimer is linguistically precise

`私がわかるのは、わたしのことだけ。だから、わたし基準に想像するしかない` (`part0023 ¶47`) is one of the clearest explicit limitations on mind-reading in the series.

The first clause limits knowledge to self; the second does not prohibit inference but names its basis as self-referential analogy. That is exactly the distinction the V2 method has often had to impose externally on Hachiman's narration.

Iroha articulates it herself.

## 11.7 `よろしくお願いします` as transfer rather than confession

Hachiman's formal `改めまして、比企谷部長。よろしくお願いします` (`part0023 ¶139`) is striking because its language is conventional and institutional. The emotional content is enormous, but he chooses a social formula rather than attempting to compress the whole history into one original speech.

This is not communicative failure. Everyone present understands that the formula carries more than its literal office meaning.

The scene therefore complements the late-series language thesis:

> ordinary formulae can bear particular meaning without becoming exhaustive definitions.

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertextuality

## 12.1 Comedy remains the medium of post-ending intimacy

Volume 14.5 is significantly more comic than Volumes 12-14, but that tonal change should not be dismissed as fanservice or evidence that serious conflict has evaporated.

Comedy performs several analytical functions:

- it allows Hachiman to acknowledge attraction without turning every observation into a crisis;
- it lets Yukino and Hachiman negotiate anniversaries without adopting a new personality register;
- it permits Komachi to discuss marriage futures without claiming serious authority;
- it exposes lingering awkwardness around intimacy;
- it lets Iroha test boundaries through playful pretext;
- it makes institutional succession emotionally bearable.

The result is not “everything is fine now.” It is evidence that the relationships have enough resilience to carry awkwardness in a lower-stakes form.

## 12.2 The sister-in-law tournament is anti-oracular comedy

Komachi's candidate lists could easily be mined as route commentary. The story itself undercuts that reading by expanding the list into absurdity. Her game is about her own hopes, brother obsession, economic fantasies, and desire for family continuity.

The comedy therefore teaches the evidence rule:

> **a character speculating about someone's romantic future is evidence about the speculator before it is evidence about the future.**

## 12.3 The festival story preserves event-origin form

Story 3's live-reading origin helps explain its ensemble density, meta-performance, exaggerated self-introductions, and festival spectacle. V2 should not mistake every event-script joke for normal conversational baseline.

At the same time, the rewrite deliberately adds narrative reflection around temporality. The event origin and novel function can coexist.

## 12.4 Idol self-introductions as temporary role play

Yui's easy performance and Yukino's embarrassed imitation demonstrate different relationships to public persona. Yukino does not discover a hidden idol self. She deliberately performs a convention under social pressure and then becomes intensely embarrassed.

Hachiman's extreme reaction is character comedy. Its analytical value is the persistence of embodied attraction and delight after the mainline choice, not a symbolic theory of the “true Yukino.”

## 12.5 The title's `まちがい続ける` matters more than a victory-lap ending

The final story title—`けれど、きっと彼女たちもまちがい続ける。`—and Hachiman's later description of relationships that continue to expand while mistakes continue form a deliberate refusal of perfectionist closure.

The series title grammar survives into the coda not because nothing was learned, but because learning does not eliminate future fallibility.

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial tests

## 14.1 Alternative: “14.5 proves Hachiman/Yukino are now a perfectly healthy couple”

**Rejected.**

Evidence supports mutual affection, ordinary planning, shared future assumptions, and non-fused academic autonomy. It does not support perfect relational health. They remain awkward, indirect, embarrassed, and uncertain about the future. Story 4 additionally reminds us that Hachiman remains responsive to Iroha's flirtation and that ordinary boundary management is not automatically solved.

More importantly, Story 5 explicitly rejects perfection: `俺たちの関係性が完璧だったことなどあるだろうか。否`.

## 14.2 Alternative: “The one-month anniversary proves permanent commitment”

**Downgraded.**

It proves that Yukino marks the relationship, initiates a ritual, and imagines continuation. Hachiman is moved and cannot imagine forgetting the moment. But both characters admit that even one year is difficult to picture concretely; ten years becomes a comic overcorrection.

The scene's strength lies in ordinary future inclusion, not contractual certainty.

## 14.3 Alternative: “Iroha's weekend invitation reopens the romantic choice”

**Rejected as unsupported.**

The text supports Iroha's desire for particular access, Hachiman's attraction/fluster, and a somewhat coercive flirtatious scheduling method. It does not establish Hachiman making a competing romantic commitment.

Post-choice attraction to or enjoyment of another person is not evidence that the chosen relationship has been nullified.

## 14.4 Alternative: “Yui is still pursuing Hachiman romantically through cram school”

**OPEN / insufficiently determined.**

Yui says Hachiman's choice of prep school may be useful because their academic direction is similar. Iroha and Komachi can imagine a romantic angle. Yui denies that framing.

Given the evidence hierarchy, V2 cannot convert the observers' teasing into Yui's true motive. Practical and romantic motives may coexist. The stronger post-ending finding is that Yui remains socially and institutionally future-facing.

## 14.5 Alternative: “No `本物` vocabulary means the authenticity problem is solved”

**Rejected.**

Lexical disappearance is not metaphysical proof. Story 5 instead contains explicit anti-sanctification and continuing-error language. The ordinary volume is valuable precisely because the characters can act without needing to settle ontology every day.

## 14.6 Alternative: “The old Service Club survives intact through Komachi”

**Rejected.**

The final story directly attacks this preservation model. Komachi's club will have different members, different cups, different relationships, and eventually no physical trace of the founders. What survives is partially transmitted practice and institutional memory, not identity of form.

## 14.7 Alternative: “Hachiman's reluctance to tell Komachi what to do is mature noninterference”

**Partly rejected.**

His concern about over-influence is mature. But he himself recognizes that `自分で決めればいい` can function as responsibility transfer. Mature relation requires honest advice as well as restraint.

## 14.8 Alternative: “The festival proves the entire post-ending group is stable”

**Downgraded.**

The festival is a late-Volume-14 interstitial after the joint prom, not a remote future epilogue. It proves immediate ensemble participation is possible. It cannot establish long-term stability and cannot be used to rewrite the prospective difficulty of the mainline ending.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

## 15.1 What later knowledge legitimately clarifies

Once the complete mainline is known, Stories 1-2 gain a particular value: they show how much a later ending can distort memory of earlier possibility.

Story 1 makes Yukino's salience unmistakable. Story 2 makes Yui's warmth and plausibility unmistakable. Reading both after Volume 14 invites a false asymmetry—one as “true foreshadowing,” the other as “obvious losing route.” V2 deliberately rejects that retrospective flattening.

The earlier period is best understood as one in which Hachiman's attachments are differently structured, unevenly acknowledged, and not yet converted into an owned choice.

## 15.2 Story 3 retrospectively clarifies a major Hachiman evolution

The festival scene is especially powerful when placed against earlier Hachiman. His old defensive method often anticipated the end of a relation and withdrew investment accordingly. Here he explicitly recognizes the end and walks toward participation.

Retrospective comparison strengthens the development claim, but it should not erase the scene's immediate comic/event texture.

## 15.3 Stories 4-5 test rather than validate Checkpoint E

Checkpoint E's model predicts several things if the mainline resolution is durable:

1. Hachiman/Yukino attachment should not require Yukino to reduce her independent trajectory.
2. Yui should retain social standing without romantic ambiguity needing to be restored.
3. ordinary influence should be possible without returning to total control or total nonintervention;
4. `共依存` and `本物` should not need to mediate every interaction;
5. the chosen configuration should remain revisable rather than sacred.

Stories 4-5 provide evidence consistent with all five, with caveats.

The biggest caveat is that low-stakes ordinary life is easier than crisis. Durability is supported, not proven for every future stressor.

## 15.4 The ending's largest retrospective gain is institutional, not romantic

The most important new insight from V2 is that 14.5's final story is not merely “Hachiman and Yukino dating after the ending.” Its deepest structural function is to prevent the conclusion from becoming a closed romantic monument.

The older trio are already becoming predecessors. Komachi and Iroha will remain. Unknown people may enter. Tea can be taught. Mugs can multiply. The room can change.

The ending is therefore forced to face a question romance alone cannot answer:

> **Can something remain valuable if it stops looking like the form in which you first learned to value it?**

Story 5 answers yes, but only if transmission is allowed to include transformation.

# 18. Cumulative delta and final state after Volume 14.5

## 18.1 Chronology-routing delta

The most important archival result is methodological:

> **Volume 14.5 is not one temporal block.**

The canonical routing is now:

- **Story 1 — Jan 1:** post-V9 / pre-V10 supplement;
- **Story 2 — Jan 2:** post-V9 / pre-V10 supplement;
- **Story 3 — spring break after joint prom:** late-V14 interstitial supplement;
- **Story 4 — mid-April:** post-mainline ordinary-life evidence;
- **Story 5 — late spring:** post-mainline ordinary-life evidence.

Checkpoint E remains frozen and is not retroactively rewritten by Stories 1-3.

## 18.2 Hachiman

Post-ending state:

> **future-facing attachment without sanctification**

Strengthened:

- can assume future meetings even if institutions diverge;
- rejects Yukino lowering her academic trajectory for proximity;
- accepts chosen shared environment;
- recognizes his own ability to over-influence Komachi;
- recognizes that total nonintervention can also be abdication;
- identifies and rejects `神聖視` of his present relationship configuration;
- formally transfers Service Club standing to Komachi;
- can acknowledge finitude and still participate.

Still unresolved / ordinary imperfections:

- rationalization remains his primary face-saving language;
- Iroha can still maneuver him into social plans without a crisp authorization exchange;
- attraction and embarrassment around other people do not disappear;
- long-term future remains difficult for him to imagine concretely.

## 18.3 Yukino

Post-ending state:

> **self-authored aspiration + chosen reliance + transmissive competence**

She does not fuse her academic future to Hachiman's. She can say shared environment would help. She can initiate anniversary ritual without making it compulsory reciprocity. She can imagine future meetings. Most importantly, she does not treat the old Service Club as property to be preserved. She imagines Komachi's own encounters and teaches a practice that can survive her departure.

## 18.4 Yui

Post-ending state:

> **known romantic loss + retained institutional and friendship agency**

Yui is not erased or frozen into noble suffering. She helps shape the club's future, coordinates with Yukino, speaks desires that Hachiman and Yukino overcomplicate, and remains capable of imagining unknown future members.

Her cram-school motive remains open enough that V2 does not certify either “purely practical” or “continued romantic pursuit.”

## 18.5 Iroha

Post-ending state:

> **transparent pretext + future-weaving social action + epistemic humility**

Iroha continues to demonstrate that strategic performance and genuine desire can coexist. She also articulates one of the cleanest limits on interpersonal knowledge in the entire series: she knows herself and can only imagine others from that basis.

Her continued access to Hachiman is a boundary-management question, not evidence that the final romantic choice has been undone.

## 18.6 Komachi

Final state:

> **successor with inherited practice, not custodian of a shrine**

Komachi receives the Service Club's name, room, history, advice, mug, and tea practice. She does **not** receive an obligation to reproduce Hachiman/Yukino/Yui's relationship.

This distinction should govern all later succession analysis.

## 18.7 Service Club

The final ordinary-life state is:

> **institutional continuity through changing membership, transmitted practice, and permission to become different.**

The club is no longer identical with the original trio. Indeed, treating that configuration as supreme would repeat Hachiman's `神聖視` error.

Iroha can belong without formal membership. Komachi can become president without becoming Yukino. Future members can arrive late. Tea can remain while cups change.

The final image therefore replaces preservation of artifacts with preservation-through-practice.

## 18.8 `本物`

Volume 14.5 supplies **no new lexical definition**.

That absence is itself useful when bounded correctly. The true post-ending stories show people making ordinary choices without invoking `本物`, `偽物`, `依存`, or `共依存` as master vocabulary.

Checkpoint E's anti-certification endpoint therefore survives the ordinary-life test:

> **the relationship can be lived without needing to be repeatedly declared genuine.**

But the stronger anti-sanctification warning must accompany that claim:

> **living the choice does not make its present form sacred, perfect, or permanently authoritative.**

## 18.9 Dependency/autonomy

The post-ending control cases strengthen the distinction between pathology and chosen reliance:

- Yukino can stand on her own and still prefer shared study environment;
- Hachiman can want future proximity without demanding the same university;
- Komachi can receive inherited practice without losing authority;
- Yui can influence Komachi without deciding for her;
- Iroha can belong without formal dependency or formal membership.

The final model is therefore:

> **autonomy as authorship under influence, not isolation from influence.**

## 18.10 Time and succession

Volume 10.5 first made remaining ordinary time explicit. Volume 14.5 completes that temporal movement.

Story 3 says ordinary life is festival because it ends. Story 4 says futures are woven through repeated small actions. Story 5 says the room, cups, people, and material traces will change. The afterword similarly describes ten years not as the result of a ten-year master plan but as one task leading to another until distance accumulates (`part0025 ¶8-13`).

The series' final temporal model is therefore not preservation.

It is **continuation through successive acts whose distant outcome cannot be guaranteed in advance**.
