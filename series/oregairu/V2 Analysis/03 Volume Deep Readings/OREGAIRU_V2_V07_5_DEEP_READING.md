# OREGAIRU V2 — Volume 07.5 Deep Reading
## 『やはり俺の青春ラブコメはまちがっている。7.5』

> **Prospective governing question:** What does ordinary life reveal about the Service Club when the anthology is published after Kyoto but much of its material is set before Kyoto—and in two major cases before the cultural festival itself?

> **V2 thesis:** Volume 7.5 is not a hidden continuation after Kyoto. It is a **retroactive map of the ordinary relational infrastructure that Kyoto has just endangered**. Its most productive positive model is not “pure honesty” but **low-stakes, consent-preserving indirection**: games, jokes, transparent pseudonyms, distributed work, invitations, and social pretexts can reduce expressive risk without necessarily taking choice away from the person being addressed. Its strongest warning is SIDE-B: Hachiman already possesses a coercive social-engineering toolkit before the cultural festival—audience construction, incomplete disclosure, role assignment, reputational pressure, and willingness to make collaborators bear costs. That toolkit can be used to remove an abusive power and restore other people's agency, which means the series does not condemn technique simply because it is manipulative. The ethical distinction is **what the intervention makes possible, who authorized it, whose agency it restores or overrides, and who is made to pay**. Published after Volume 7 but largely set earlier, 7.5 makes visible that belonging had already been accumulating through ordinary work long before Hachiman could name the Service Club as a place to which he might want to return.

## 1.4 The most important publication-order correction

A V1 claim described SIDE-A's trust discussion as if it “anticipates” Kyoto. That is true **inside story chronology**, but false as a statement about the historical reader's experience. Volume 7 had already been published when 7.5 supplied that scene.

The correct formulation is:

> **SIDE-A is story-time foreshadowing but publication-time retrospective connective tissue.**

A reader encountering the books on release first experiences the Kyoto trust breach and only later receives the earlier scene in which Hachiman explicitly defends distrust as psychological self-protection. The anthology therefore **sharpens backward**.

That distinction is methodologically non-negotiable in this V2 pass.

# 2. Prospective reading freeze

Volume 7.5 requires two prospective freezes operating simultaneously.

## 2.1 Publication-time freeze

The August 2013 reader already knows Volume 7. Therefore the anthology may legitimately be read as a post-Kyoto publication that recontextualizes prior states. The reader knows that:

- Hachiman's false confession preserved Hayama's group while damaging Service Club trust;
- Yukino and Yui rejected his method;
- the volume had framed the climax through `信頼`, `任せる`, `妥協`, `現状維持`, and incomplete disclosure rather than a mature `本物` doctrine.

7.5 can speak *to* that knowledge because of publication order.

## 2.2 Internal-story freeze

Within each story, however, characters cannot know later events. Thus:

- B.T. cannot be made to “respond” to the cultural festival or Kyoto;
- SIDE-B cannot be treated as if Hachiman has learned V4–V7 lessons;
- SIDE-A cannot be made to contain post-Kyoto self-awareness;
- the autumn short stories may sit near V6/V7 material, but must be read only through what their internal placement permits.

This is especially important for SIDE-B. Because its method resembles later Hachiman interventions, hindsight makes it tempting to call it “self-sacrifice-as-control already complete.” Prospectively, that is too strong. What is already present is the **toolkit**: model the social system, manipulate audience conditions, conceal part of the plan, assign roles, exploit another person's likely response, and accept some personal cost. The later pathology is not yet identical because the ethical object differs sharply: SIDE-B targets an abusive alumnus whose presence is suppressing current students' agency.

## 2.3 Lexical freeze

Across the seven pieces, searches produce **zero conceptual occurrences of `本物` and zero `偽物` occurrences**. The anthology's active vocabulary instead clusters around:

- SIDE-A: `結婚`, `幸せ／幸福`, `普通`, `信じる／疑う`, `理解`, `偽る`, division of labor;
- B.T.: `遊び`, `みんな`, invitation, `緩衝地帯`, expectation, indirect permission;
- SIDE-B: `逃げる`, `戻る`, `帰る`, `場所`, `演出`, `世間`, `厳しい`, `効率`, reliance and role allocation;
- S.S.2: `優しさ` and its possible cruelty;
- S.S.3: `効率`, effort, motivation, and support.

Thus 7.5 should not be retrofitted into a hidden treatise on `本物`. Its major question is more concrete:

> **How can people stay near one another without either forcing full exposure or using indirection to seize control?**

# 3. Narrative and causal architecture

Because 7.5 is an anthology, its architecture is thematic rather than one continuous causal arc. The collection repeatedly stages a tension between **direct statement and enabling frame**.

## 3.1 S.S.1 — ordinary domesticity versus romanticized domesticity

The first short story looks trivial: Hiratsuka anonymously asks the Service Club for a simple dish that will appeal to men. Yui proposes meat-and-potatoes; Yukino proposes Japanese-style hamburger steak. Hachiman rejects both as overly targeted and supplies a much less glamorous answer: simply cooked meat and rice, eventually rendered as ginger pork.

The serious point is small but useful. Hachiman says that if the question is marriage, cooking becomes `毎日のこと`. His frame is **sustainability rather than symbolic performance**. A domestic act that can be repeated without exhausting the person performing it may matter more than a spectacular sign of ideal femininity.

This becomes a miniature preface to SIDE-A, where the collection repeatedly contrasts idealized “wife” imagery with ordinary work that actually sustains a shared task.

The transparent pseudonym is also typical of all four mail stories. Everyone can infer who the sender is, but the apparatus permits embarrassment to remain partially veiled. The point is not anonymity in a strong sense. It is **face-saving indirection**.

## 3.2 SIDE-A — marriage as a laboratory for difference, trust, and shared work

SIDE-A begins with Hachiman's comic contempt for marriage, but the opening argument is more interesting than the joke:

> `嫌いなのに無理してもお互い不幸なだけだ`

and then:

> `本来の自分を偽ることはするべきじゃないし、人間どうしたって価値観は違う`

If living together requires suppressing genuine differences, he reasons, that cannot automatically count as happiness.

This is not yet a mature relationship philosophy. It is partly Hachiman defending his own refusal to accommodate. Yet the premise is sound enough to matter: **difference does not have to be erased in order for a relationship to continue**.

The magazine assignment then turns the abstract question into work. Hachiman and Yukino immediately think in pragmatic production terms—deadlines, layouts, templates, efficiency—while Yui supplies the “ordinary high-school” perspective they lack. Hachiman's line that Yui's `普通さ` is valuable is revealing, and his internal observation goes further: he thinks Yukino is significantly “saved” by that quality.

This is one of the anthology's cleanest models of **complementarity without hierarchy**. Yui's social normality is not the opposite of Yukino's intelligence or Hachiman's system thinking; it is a resource neither possesses in the same way.

### The cooking contest and domestic imagination

The cooking sequence should not be treated as a romance scoreboard.

Yui's cooking is poor; Yukino's paella is technically excellent; Hiratsuka wins the joking “mother's taste” criterion with a simple, sustainable dish. Hachiman even eats Yui's unpleasant hamburger mainly so Totsuka will not have to. That is a comic, low-stakes self-sacrificial reflex, but elevating it into the mature V6–V7 pathology would be disproportionate.

The more interesting contrast is that **technical perfection and livable domesticity are not identical categories**. Hachiman has difficulty making “wife-like” commentary about Yukino precisely because ordinary excellence does not translate cleanly into the quiz's stereotype.

### Trust as defended vulnerability

The most important SIDE-A exchange comes from Komachi's final marriage question: what should someone do if a spouse is coming home late and might be cheating?

Komachi's answer is:

> `信じる`

Hachiman objects. His internal argument is explicit:

> `信じることが必ずしもすべての場合においていいことだとは俺は思わない。`
>
> `信じないこと、つまりは疑うことは自身の心の防御にも繫がる。`

For Hachiman, distrust is not merely cynicism. It is armor. Giving up doubt exposes the self to injury.

This retrospectively deepens Volume 7's trust vocabulary. In Kyoto, Hachiman would define trust more procedurally—shared understanding plus accumulated reliable acts until one can `信じて任せる`. SIDE-A supplies the **earlier defensive theory beneath that definition**: entrusting someone matters because the protection of doubt has been lowered.

Again, release order matters. The reader sees the breach first, then learns how much Hachiman himself conceptually associates trust with vulnerability.

### Wedding dresses and differential expressive risk

The wedding-dress sequence is heavily romantic-coded paratext and prose, but it rewards precision.

Hachiman finds Yui's exposed legs and neckline difficult to look at directly and registers their mutual embarrassment. Yukino receives a long, aestheticized description; everyone catches their breath, and she is visibly embarrassed. By contrast, Hachiman can straightforwardly tell Hiratsuka `綺麗ですね`.

This does not prove a neat ranking of desire. A better V2 reading is:

> **The more relationally ambiguous the addressee, the harder direct evaluative speech becomes.**

Hiratsuka is beautiful but occupies a comparatively safe adult/teacher relationship. Yui and Yukino carry higher interpretive stakes. Hachiman can see salience more easily than he can give that salience interpersonal standing.

### The group photograph

Yui proposes a photograph. Yukino initially says `私は遠慮しておくわ`; Yui catches her, then pulls Hachiman inward as well. Hachiman protests lightly. Hiratsuka says `たまには、こういうのも悪くないだろ？`, and Hachiman internally accepts: `まぁ、たまになら`.

This is useful precisely because it is **not perfectly consent-clean**. Yui physically pressures both reluctant participants. Calling every friendly push “healthy agency” would be sentimental.

But the scale and reversibility are very different from Kyoto. The stakes are low, refusal remains socially available, the act does not determine anyone's future, and the resulting inclusion is accepted rather than experienced as a violation.

The scene therefore helps define a more defensible distinction:

> **Relational prompting can be imperfect without becoming jurisdiction over another person's life. Ethical analysis needs scale, stakes, reversibility, and the person's ability to answer—not a binary manipulation/not-manipulation label.**

### Division of labor as healthy interdependence

The article's final production phase is perhaps SIDE-A's strongest positive model. Yui makes illustrations. Yukino handles editing/layout. Hachiman writes the column. Their work is explicitly described as `それぞれの適性に合わせた分業`.

No one proves independence by doing everything. No one has to become interchangeable with the others. Competence is differentiated and coordinated.

The scene also shows incomplete relational infrastructure: it may be the first time Hachiman and Yukino speak by phone, and he realizes he does not even have her email address. Emotional closeness and practical connectivity are advancing unevenly.

Komachi then simply stays nearby while Hachiman works. Her presence makes him less likely to give up. She does not command, threaten, or solve the task. This is **noncoercive external regulation**: another person's nearby expectation helps him sustain effort while leaving authorship intact.

The closing column moves Hachiman slightly beyond the story's opening cynicism:

> `結婚にしても将来にしても、先のことはわからない。`
>
> `だが、幸福を願う権利は誰しもが持っている。`
>
> `来たるべきときのための努力は怠ってはならない。`

The househusband joke immediately deflates any grand conversion. Still, this is a real movement: uncertainty does not invalidate the right to hope or the value of preparation.

## 3.3 S.S.2 — kindness can preserve a person by refusing to tell him the truth

S.S.2 contains two small but useful arguments.

First, a nervous new tennis-club captain asks how to lead. Yukino jokingly proposes authoritarian domination; Yui produces the actual answer: leadership does not have to mean standing in front and pulling everyone. A leader can be supported by others.

That is a compact distributed-leadership counterpoint to Yukino's tendency toward self-sufficient overfunctioning.

Second, Zaimokuza asks how to pass a light-novel newcomer prize. Yukino says that telling someone something harsh can be kindness. Hachiman instead sends an apparently encouraging response telling Zaimokuza that the gatekeepers are wrong and he should continue forever.

Yui calls Hachiman kind. Yukino answers:

> `……優しさって、残酷ね`

The joke works because Hachiman's “encouragement” avoids the painful truth while potentially condemning Zaimokuza to endless failure. The anthology is therefore already distinguishing:

- comfort from help;
- reassurance from correction;
- non-injury now from welfare over time.

This is not yet the later full ethical architecture, but it is a clear seed: **a method can feel kind locally while being cruel longitudinally**.

## 3.4 B.T. — pretext as a buffer for voluntary intimacy

The bonus track is one of the best early relationship documents, but only if its chronology is respected. The EPUB explicitly states that it occurs immediately after Volume 3's birthday-related material.

Hachiman has enjoyed the day. He admits it was not boring. Then Yui asks him to keep hanging out.

His immediate response is a lie about a strict curfew. The narration is unusually explicit:

> `誘われたらとりあえず断る。これはぼっちの安定行動であり回避本能でもある。`

The motive is expectation management. Hachiman fears that accepting might reveal the invitation to have been mere social courtesy. He refuses first so he cannot discover he was not really wanted.

This is the early **expectation-avoidance** pattern in unusually clean form.

Yukino catches the lie and tells him that being invited is rare enough that he should accept. Yui then assumes Yukino is included too. Yukino asks:

> `ねぇ、それって私も含まれているの？`

Yui answers emphatically:

> `あったりまえじゃん！`

Yukino is surprised, blushes, and says she was only startled. This is strong evidence that inclusion matters to her. It should not be inflated into “Yukino's deepest desire is belonging,” but it decisively refutes any early portrait of her as simply indifferent to group inclusion.

Hachiman still resists. The asymmetry becomes clearest when Totsuka says he would be happy if Hachiman came. Hachiman's behavioral permission changes immediately. The problem is therefore not a universal inability to accept invitations. **He can accept care more easily when its meaning feels safe and legible.**

At the end, Yui wins the right to choose the losing team's punishment. Komachi and Yukino explicitly delegate the decision to her. Yui then asks:

> `……また、一緒に遊ぼ？`

Hachiman describes the tiny distance between them as `遊び` / `ゆとり`, a `緩衝地帯` that prevents friction and exhaustion. He answers:

> `……ま、罰ならしょうがねぇな`

The pretext matters. It lets him say yes without having to directly assert, “I want to spend time with you.” The obligation is transparent and playful; Yui knows it is a frame, and Hachiman knows she knows.

That makes this one of the best examples of **consent-preserving indirection** in the early series:

> **A pretext can lower the expressive cost of a voluntary choice without changing the available choices.**

It should not be romanticized as universally healthier than direct speech. Yui's first direct invitation was already valid. The pretext is useful because it helps Hachiman cross his own defensive threshold.

The ending widens the invitation to the group. Yukino says she is not good at “everyone together,” but if they are willing to have her, she will join when free. The Service Club is beginning to possess an **ordinary-life horizon** outside problem solving.

## 3.5 S.S.3 — Yui's practical social intelligence

S.S.3 explicitly occurs after the cultural festival. Kawasaki asks, through a transparent pseudonym, how to help Taishi study for entrance exams.

Hachiman's answer is technically sound: repeat old tests, aggressively review mistakes, memorize where useful, and practice until test-taking techniques become usable. Yukino agrees that there is no royal road.

Yui then adds the dimension they miss. She proposes that Kawasaki study with Taishi, help when he gets stuck, avoid constantly ordering him to work, and tell him enjoyable stories about high school after study sessions.

The narration is itself surprised by how good the answer is.

This is not fluffy emotional support opposed to “real” competence. Yui is solving **motivation, co-regulation, and future salience**. She understands that a technically efficient study plan still requires the learner to want to continue.

This strengthens the V2 formulation of Yui as relational/operational intelligence rather than mere mediator.

## 3.6 SIDE-B — the intervention toolkit before self-sacrifice becomes the master frame

SIDE-B is the anthology's most analytically consequential story.

### The request is narrower than the solution

The judo-club members explain that many members are quitting. A graduated alumnus has returned to “help” and imposes humiliating, excessive, retaliatory training. Yukino immediately asks whether the problem is the alumnus. Club president Shiroyama says that removing him seems impossible: internal status, prior strength, a non-judo faculty adviser, and hierarchy make direct confrontation ineffective.

He therefore reframes the formal request as **new member recruitment**, because without enough people the club cannot even field a team.

Yui suggests bringing quitters back. After witnessing practice, all three Service Club members reject that approach. Hachiman says the abnormality is not merely the alumnus's behavior but the culture that accepts silent obedience. His strongest prospective ethical line is:

> `嫌なものを避けるというのは人として、生き物として、あまりに当たり前のことだ`

and:

> `責めるべきは、去った人間を責める風潮であるべきだ`

This is important because Hachiman is defending **exit rights**. Leaving an abusive arrangement is not automatically betrayal or weakness.

### Problem redefinition is ethically justified—up to a point

The formal request is recruitment, but recruitment without changing the environment would expose new students to the same abuse. Hachiman, Yukino, and Yui therefore redefine the effective problem.

That redefinition is ethically defensible. A helper need not fulfill a stated request literally when doing so would reproduce the harm that caused the request.

But SIDE-B then demonstrates that **correct problem definition does not automatically legitimate every method**.

### Hachiman's plan: make the audience into a weapon

The solution is a public judo tournament that appears to be recruitment theater. Hachiman says the most powerful outsider is effectively `世間様`: the imagined judgment of society.

The public event has two layers:

- overt purpose: make judo visible and recruit;
- covert purpose: create an audience before whom the alumnus can be made ashamed enough to stop returning.

Crucially, Hachiman states:

> `ただその裏で走らせる計画については一切触れていない。反対されても面倒だし`

The judo members are not told the covert plan.

This is already the later intervention problem in embryo: **incomplete disclosure because informed collaborators might object**.

Hachiman then uses Yui's social network and Hayama's popularity as “casting.” His vocabulary becomes explicitly production-oriented and instrumental: attention, casting, efficiency, audience management. The technique is recognizably the same kind of system cognition that later appears in Kyoto.

### Yui is not merely executing Hachiman's plan

Yui's recruitment of Hayama is itself instructive. She works conversationally, pulling Tobe into the exchange and letting the group's own pressure make Hayama's participation easier. Hachiman cannot tell how much is calculation and how much is her natural social fluency.

Again, the series refuses a clean opposition between “manipulative Hachiman” and “pure Yui.” Social coordination always affects choice. The difference is degree, transparency, and stakes.

### Iroha's first appearance: performed dependence as leverage

Iroha arrives to retrieve Hayama for soccer practice. Her soft voice, sleeve-grabbing, apparent helplessness, and selective dependence immediately alter the social field. Tobe offers to return instead; she politely rejects him. Miura recognizes the status maneuver and intervenes.

Hachiman instantly identifies Iroha as dangerous, but this is still first-impression inference. Prospectively, what can be said with confidence is that Iroha already understands how **feminine dependency can function as social leverage**. Miura understands the same language and counters it with direct territorial authority.

The scene is also valuable because it destabilizes Hachiman's tournament plan in a way he did not anticipate. His social models are powerful, not omniscient.

### Yukino chooses to intervene physically

When Hayama is pulled away, Yukino volunteers to take the middle match:

> `私が出ればいい話だから`

Hachiman and Yui try to stop her. Yukino refuses. When Hachiman proposes going first instead, she grabs his clothes and asks:

> `それになんの意味があるの？`

She has inferred enough of his plan to understand that he needs to remain available for the final confrontation. She then states:

> `それに、あなたに気にかけてもらうまでもないわ`
>
> `要は一度たりとも触らせなければいいのよね`

This is a particularly useful autonomy example. Yukino is **not assigned** the risk by Hachiman. She chooses it, challenges his impulse to substitute himself, and protects the plan's objective without requiring him to carry every burden.

Yui still negotiates safety—at minimum, she gets Yukino changed into appropriate clothing. The three are functioning as a team with disagreement inside cooperation.

### The climax: effective, punitive, and ethically dirty

Hachiman gets the alumnus onto the mat by exploiting shame in both directions: it would be embarrassing for a college judoka to join a frivolous high-school event, but after being publicly called onto the stage it would also be embarrassing to refuse.

He then manipulates Shiroyama into treating a clean throw as an accidental slip. Shiroyama recognizes the earlier cue `演出` and participates. Hachiman's narration is explicit:

> `なぜなら、「演出」だから。`

Shiroyama immediately pays a cost. The alumnus protests; Hachiman invokes the rule against challenging the referee; Shiroyama is visibly stressed and loses vocal confidence.

Hachiman later acknowledges this directly:

> `ちょっと酷い役を押し付けてしまった`

The social engineer has made another person morally complicit without full prior authorization.

Hachiman then attacks the alumnus's status in front of the audience. It does not even matter whether the crowd actually hears him. It matters that the alumnus thinks they may hear.

The narration states the purpose without euphemism:

> `公衆の面前での痛罵。先輩としての品格を、プライドを貶めること。`

Hachiman then constructs a psychological story: the alumnus probably failed or suffered a setback at university, lost confidence and status, and retreated to his old high school to dominate juniors. This model is plausible. The alumnus's final `そんなの全部わかってんだよ` confirms that some part of Hachiman's accusation lands.

But V2 must keep the evidence categories separate:

> **The specific university-failure narrative remains Hachiman's psychological inference.**

The alumnus's response does not independently verify every detail.

The cruelest line is:

> `戻ってきたんじゃなくて、あんた、逃げてきたんだ`

Hachiman attacks not only conduct but the meaning of the man's attempted return.

### Outcome and ethical ledger

The outcome is effective:

- no meaningful new-member recruitment occurs;
- several quitters return;
- they return because the alumnus stops visiting.

Thus the stated request is not literally fulfilled, yet the club's underlying capacity problem improves.

This matters for the series' ethics. SIDE-B is not a simple lesson that manipulation fails. **Manipulation works.** The question is what it achieves and what it costs.

The ledger is mixed:

- **agency restored:** students who left because of abuse can voluntarily return;
- **abusive external power removed:** the alumnus stops occupying the club;
- **Shiroyama cost:** forced into a misleading referee decision with inadequate disclosure;
- **target cost:** deliberate public humiliation and psychological attack;
- **Hachiman cost:** bodily injury and some social exposure, but he is not primarily “sacrificing himself” to absorb all consequences;
- **Yukino cost:** voluntarily assumed physical risk, not assigned by Hachiman;
- **Yui contribution:** social recruitment, logistical support, and information follow-up.

The story therefore supplies a better precursor to later arcs than the old claim “self-sacrifice already existed here.”

> **The coercive intervention toolkit predates the cultural festival. The mature self-sacrifice/control fusion does not yet exist in the same form.**

### The ending: place before possession

Afterward, Hachiman reflects that the alumnus probably saw Sōbu High as a `帰ってきたい場所`: familiar, comfortable, a place into which one could escape.

Then, while hearing clubroom voices and school sounds, he wonders:

> `俺にもいつか。`
>
> `戻りたいと、そう思う場所ができるのだろうかと。`

The literary implication is strong: the Service Club is already becoming such a place.

But the text does **not** explicitly say “the Service Club is my home.” V2 should preserve that gap. Hachiman is capable of imagining the category before he is capable of identifying his own attachment to the nearest candidate.

## 3.7 S.S.4 — low-stakes acceptance and Yukino's unguarded cat desire

The final short story is mostly comedy. Komachi asks what to do when Kamakura demands an arm pillow. Yukino becomes visibly animated, supplies detailed cat-behavior knowledge, and the final answer is essentially “you get to sleep with a cat, so tolerate it.”

Its analytical value is modest but real. Yukino's cat enthusiasm is one of the series' safe domains in which desire does not require elaborate defense. The mail format also lets Komachi make a domestic complaint public while retaining the ritual fiction of a pseudonym.

The story is not a major thematic key and should remain proportionate.

# 4. Hachiman narration audit

## 4.1 Strong domain: systems, incentives, and social pressure

7.5 repeatedly confirms Hachiman's high competence in external social analysis.

He accurately recognizes:

- why sustainable daily cooking differs from symbolic “wife” cooking;
- why Yui's ordinary social perspective fills a gap in himself and Yukino;
- why invitation ambiguity makes him defensive;
- why a technically correct study plan still needs practice;
- why judo-club hierarchy prevents direct correction;
- why a public audience changes the alumnus's incentives;
- why Hayama's participation will attract bodies and spectators;
- why quitters will not return while the harmful condition remains.

These are not “unreliable narrator” failures.

## 4.2 Weak domain: receiving invitations as evidence of being wanted

B.T. provides perhaps the cleanest early specimen of Hachiman's domain-specific unreliability.

Observation: Yui directly invites him.

Possible inference: she wants him to stay.

Hachiman's defensive inference: this might be social courtesy; accepting could expose him to humiliation.

Behavioral rule:

> `誘われたらとりあえず断る`

His problem is not failing to notice the invitation. It is refusing to grant favorable relational evidence enough behavioral standing to act upon it.

Totsuka's invitation bypasses the problem because Hachiman treats Totsuka's affection as safe rather than as a dangerous claim about his own social worth.

## 4.3 Desire denial by comic redirection

SIDE-A provides many examples in which Hachiman registers Yui or Yukino as physically/romantically salient and immediately surrounds the observation with comedy. The wedding-dress sequence is not proof that every joke conceals romance. Rather, it shows a repeated mechanism:

1. perception occurs;
2. bodily/visual salience is admitted;
3. direct interpersonal meaning is avoided;
4. a joke redirects the interpretive load.

This should remain an evidentiary pattern, not a license to translate every joke into hidden confession.

## 4.4 Overgeneralization: `逃げたらもう戻らん`

Before observing the judo practice, Hachiman says that once people run away, they basically do not return. He recognizes Yui's return to the Service Club as a special case.

The story itself complicates the claim. Several judo members return once the abusive alumnus disappears.

The better rule is not “people who leave never come back.” It is:

> **Return depends on whether the condition that made departure necessary has changed and whether return remains socially possible.**

SIDE-B therefore contains its own correction to Hachiman's essentializing language.

## 4.5 Psychological inference versus textual fact in the alumnus diagnosis

Hachiman observes real evidence:

- the alumnus emphasizes how harsh the world is;
- he returns repeatedly to the high school despite being a college athlete;
- he humiliates weaker juniors;
- he is highly sensitive to public judgment.

Hachiman then infers a narrative of university failure and compensatory retreat.

That inference is plausible and partially resonant with the alumnus's response. It is **not independently established biography**. The ledger must therefore classify it as a strong Hachiman psychological inference, not textual fact.

## 4.6 Self-awareness about collateral cost already exists

After the tournament Hachiman says he gave Shiroyama a rather cruel role and therefore avoids him to spare further discomfort.

The proposed remedy—distance—is debatable. But the acknowledgment matters: Hachiman already knows that his interventions can make collaborators pay.

That means later repetition cannot be explained simply as ignorance. The longer arc increasingly concerns **what he does with knowledge he already possesses**.

# 5. Character deltas

## 5.1 Hachiman

7.5 does not move Hachiman forward in one chronological line, but it exposes several already-existing layers:

- early expectation avoidance is explicit in B.T.;
- he can accept intimacy through pretext when direct acceptance feels dangerous;
- he values non-erasure of difference in SIDE-A, even while using that principle defensively;
- he can coordinate distributed work rather than insist on sole competence;
- his social-engineering toolkit is highly developed by SIDE-B;
- he already recognizes collateral cost to Shiroyama;
- he can imagine having a place to which he wants to return before he identifies his own current belonging.

The biggest correction to a linear “loner becomes self-sacrificer” model is that **several mechanisms coexist early**. Expectation avoidance, social-system competence, punitive intervention, comic desire denial, and tentative ordinary belonging are not consecutive replacements. They overlap.

## 5.2 Yukino

The anthology substantially strengthens the early Yukino record.

B.T. shows that being automatically included by Yui surprises and pleases her. SIDE-B then gives much stronger evidence of embodied ease:

- Yui directly asks what is wrong;
- Hachiman explicitly says unnecessary reserve between them has decreased since Yui's birthday;
- Yui touches, brushes, ties, and styles Yukino's hair;
- when the misunderstanding is explained, Yukino chooses to keep the hairstyle because it is cool and, implicitly, because she likes it;
- she blushes when inspecting the matching style;
- she allows Yui's hug even while verbally complaining.

This is not merely “Yui persistently invades Yukino's space.” Yukino's acceptance has become behaviorally meaningful.

SIDE-B also complicates any image of Yukino as a helpless overfunctioner. She voluntarily enters the judo match after reasoning that Hachiman's role in the plan matters more. She is willing to take risk, but the risk is **self-authored** and challenged by the others rather than imposed upon her.

SIDE-A then demonstrates differentiated competence: Yukino edits and structures, Yui illustrates, Hachiman writes. She can participate in interdependence without ceasing to be highly competent.

## 5.3 Yui

7.5 is unusually strong for Yui because it shows her in low-pressure functional contexts.

Her contributions include:

- direct invitations;
- automatic inclusion of Yukino;
- creating ordinary group futures;
- visually and physically producing friendship through hair styling and photographs;
- recruiting Hayama through social-network fluency;
- offering the strongest practical answer to Kawasaki's study question;
- understanding motivation and co-regulation;
- drawing the article illustrations;
- following up on the judo club afterward through her information network.

This further displaces the weak label “mediator.” Yui is a **builder and maintainer of relational infrastructure**.

The anthology also shows the risk in that strength. She sometimes assumes inclusion and physically pulls people into low-stakes activities. The mature analysis should therefore distinguish her generally agency-enabling style from the possibility of overstepping; “warmth” is not automatic moral authorization.

## 5.4 Komachi

Komachi functions repeatedly as a low-coercion relational engineer.

She understands Hachiman's defenses, recognizes Yui's desire to ask for another outing, and builds the game/punishment frame that makes acceptance easier. She also stays beside Hachiman while he works, and her mere presence helps him continue.

Her interventions work because she usually manipulates **framing and opportunity rather than destination**. She can be mischievous and pushy, but she does not need to substitute her desired answer for the participants' own.

## 5.5 Hiratsuka

Hiratsuka appears in a more ordinary, comic mode, but the anthology still develops her as an adult who creates institutions in which the students can do more than solve emergencies. She gives them the mail-advice system and the marriage-magazine task, both of which generate practice in collaborative judgment.

Her own marriage anxiety is played broadly, but SIDE-A also lets Hachiman speak direct praise to her in a way he struggles to do with peers. Their relationship remains emotionally meaningful while comparatively low in interpretive threat.

## 5.6 Hayama, Miura, and Iroha

SIDE-B provides an early miniature of the later social field.

Hayama is already easy to recruit once group expectation coheres around him. Hachiman observes that atmosphere can make refusal difficult. This is an important precursor to Volume 7's concern with preservation and group pressure.

Miura demonstrates direct protective/territorial intervention when Iroha tries to remove Hayama. She understands a social language Hachiman can diagnose but does not inhabit naturally.

Iroha's cameo is too early for a mature character thesis, but her capacity to make dependency, softness, and junior status produce leverage is immediately legible.

# 6. Relationship-state changes

## 6.1 Hachiman–Yui

B.T. gives an early, high-quality baseline:

- Yui wants another shared outing badly enough to ask twice;
- Hachiman refuses the first invitation because accepting is emotionally risky;
- a playful obligation lets him accept;
- he experiences the distance between them as a buffer against friction;
- Yui accepts the outing becoming group-based rather than insisting on exclusivity.

This is early romantic/relational evidence, but chronology matters: it belongs immediately after V3.

SIDE-A, set later, shows more embodied salience and easier collaboration. Yui can call him about work; he recognizes her unique contribution; she pulls him into the photograph; his attention to her wedding dress is strongly gendered.

The relationship has moved from “is this interaction legitimate at all?” toward **ordinary task interdependence with romantic ambiguity still difficult to state directly**.

## 6.2 Hachiman–Yukino

SIDE-B gives one of the earliest instances of Yukino protecting Hachiman from unnecessary self-substitution: when he proposes taking the judo match himself, she asks what purpose that would serve and chooses to go instead.

SIDE-A shows practical intimacy developing unevenly. Hachiman can work alongside Yukino, understands her competence, and speaks to her by phone, yet does not even possess her email address.

The wedding-dress scene increases romantic coding but remains pre-Kyoto ambiguity, not a confession.

## 6.3 Yukino–Yui

This is the anthology's clearest relational gain.

The B.T. establishes the importance of being included. SIDE-B later shows that inclusion has become embodied ease and direct concern. Hachiman himself marks the birthday as a relational threshold: `無用な気遣いや遠慮` has diminished.

Yui's hair styling is especially important because it is intimate but non-romantic in the governing evidence class. Yukino is initially confused, then explicitly declines the offer to undo it and keeps the style. That is **accepted care through touch**.

The V2 frozen shorthand becomes:

> **Yukino–Yui by early July: increasingly particular friendship in which Yui can initiate touch, direct concern, and shared style, while Yukino increasingly receives rather than merely tolerates the relationship.**

## 6.4 Triad

7.5 reveals how much of the triad is built through ordinary work rather than crises.

The group photograph, magazine assignment, email responses, shared outings, and judo event all give them reasons to be together that are neither pure friendship declaration nor emergency rescue.

The triad's institution is increasingly capable of generating its own ordinary continuity.

That is why the anthology's publication after Kyoto is painful: the reader has just watched Hachiman damage the very trust system that these earlier scenes show was accumulating quietly.

# 7. Request and intervention ethics

Volume 7.5 is especially valuable because it supplies several methods at different levels of ethical risk.

| Case | Request owner | Method | Authorization | Agency effect | Ethical assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mail advice | sender | pseudonymous advice | explicit request | preserves sender choice | Low-risk; face-saving indirection |
| B.T. outing | Yui / group | direct invitation + game/punishment pretext | Yui explicitly receives choice of punishment; Hachiman can refuse | enables Hachiman to say yes | Mostly agency-preserving; frame reduces expressive risk |
| SIDE-A magazine | Hiratsuka / school | division of labor | explicit collaboration | distributes competence | Strong healthy reliance model |
| SIDE-A group photo | Yui | physical prompting | Yukino/Hachiman initially resist | temporarily reduces immediate refusal control | Benign but imperfect; low stakes/reversible, should not be idealized |
| S.S.3 study advice | Kawasaki | technical + relational advice | explicit request | expands options | Strong example of aid without substitution |
| SIDE-B judo | Shiroyama/judo club | covert tournament theater + public pressure | formal request = recruitment; covert plan not disclosed | restores quitters' option to return; overrides target; implicates Shiroyama | Effective but ethically dirty; correct problem diagnosis does not sanitize means |
| SIDE-B Yukino match | Yukino | self-chosen participation | self-authorized despite peers discouraging it | preserves her own authorship | Important contrast with Hachiman substituting himself |

## 7.1 Indirection is not the same as deception

The anthology repeatedly uses pseudonyms, games, jokes, and staged roles. Treating all such structures as `偽物` would erase the text's distinctions.

A useful V2 rule emerges:

> **Indirection is agency-preserving when participants can recognize the frame, answer within it, and decline without the frame determining their future for them.**

B.T.'s punishment frame fits this model reasonably well. Kyoto's false confession does not.

## 7.2 Correct ends do not erase authorization problems

SIDE-B has a better outcome than a simplistic anti-manipulation reading would predict. Abuse stops; former members return.

Yet Hachiman still:

- hides the covert plan from the clients;
- makes Shiroyama participate in a false ruling;
- deliberately attacks another person's public dignity;
- relies on a psychological inference he cannot fully verify.

The series is therefore already capable of holding two propositions simultaneously:

1. **The intervention may be substantively beneficial.**
2. **The method may still impose unauthorized moral costs.**

That distinction becomes foundational for later analysis.

# 8. Social-system analysis

## 8.1 The judo club as hierarchy captured by retired authority

SIDE-B offers a compact institutional study.

The abusive alumnus's authority does not come from formal office. It comes from:

- seniority;
- prior competitive success;
- embodied skill;
- a coach who lacks technical expertise;
- a culture that romanticizes hardship;
- internal reluctance to challenge a respected graduate;
- stigma toward those who quit.

The result is **informal authority stronger than formal authority**.

This is why ordinary complaint channels fail. The alumnus can describe abuse as training and hierarchy as tradition.

Hachiman's solution attacks not technique but legitimacy. He changes the relevant audience from “juniors who owe deference” to `世間様`—the imagined public whose judgment the alumnus cannot control.

## 8.2 Exit and return

The story's strongest institutional insight is that exit is not failure when the institution has made participation harmful.

Former members return only after the abusive condition changes. This directly refutes a moral culture that treats staying as virtue regardless of environment.

The relationship to SIDE-B's title is therefore important. “Not yet knowing where one should return” is not a sentimental command to return anywhere that once felt familiar. The alumnus's attempted return is destructive because he uses the old place to escape his present and recovers status by dominating those still inside it.

A good `帰る場所` must be capable of receiving return **without requiring someone else to become smaller**.

## 8.3 Ordinary institutions matter

The email-advice system and magazine assignment are less dramatic, but their institutional role is significant. Hiratsuka keeps generating legitimate reasons for the trio to remain together after a request ends.

The Service Club thus does not merely solve problems. It produces **repeated, structured co-presence**. Repetition is how strangers become part of one another's ordinary environment.

# 9. Authenticity / `本物` ledger

## 9.1 Lexical state

Across the seven main pieces:

- conceptual `本物`: **0**
- `偽物`: **0**
- mature “genuine relationship” doctrine: **not available**

The anthology instead experiments with structures that later authenticity discourse will have to evaluate.

## 9.2 Performance can carry truth

Several apparently “constructed” forms are not false in any simple sense:

- transparent pen names protect face while conveying real problems;
- the punishment game gives Yui a safe frame for a real invitation;
- the magazine assignment is artificial work that produces real collaboration;
- the group photo is staged but records real belonging;
- the judo tournament is staged, but the recruitment layer and the underlying abuse are both real.

The problem is therefore not performance itself.

A more precise future question is:

> **Does the frame help a person express and choose, or does it let someone else choose while hiding the substitution?**

## 9.3 SIDE-A and the refusal of purification

Hachiman's opening marriage argument—that people have different values and should not have to falsify themselves into compatibility—is crude but significant.

This is an early anti-purification premise: relationship need not require making difference disappear.

It should not yet be called `本物`. It is one of the conditions a later theory will need to incorporate.

# 10. Dependency, reliance, and autonomy ledger

`依存` is not the governing vocabulary of 7.5. More useful are ordinary forms of `頼る`, `任せる`, and distributed function.

## 10.1 Healthy reliance: SIDE-A division of labor

Yui draws. Yukino edits. Hachiman writes. Komachi's presence helps Hachiman finish. No one loses personhood by relying on another's competence.

This is **chosen interdependence before the series has any need to call it that**.

## 10.2 Delegation: B.T.

Yukino delegates a quiz answer to Yui because she judges Yui more likely to know it. Later Yukino and Komachi explicitly delegate the punishment decision to Yui because Yui produced the winning point.

Delegation here is bounded, visible, and reversible.

## 10.3 Exploitative reliance: SIDE-B Shiroyama

Hachiman relies on Shiroyama to bring the alumnus and later induces him to cooperate in the “slip” ruling. But the second reliance crosses a line because Shiroyama did not knowingly accept the full moral role beforehand.

This gives the ledger a useful distinction:

> **Reliance becomes exploitative when one person's competence or position is used for a purpose they were not given meaningful opportunity to evaluate.**

## 10.4 Yukino's voluntary burden

Yukino entering the match is reliance-compatible autonomy. She is not being used because she recognizes the objective and elects to bear a bounded risk. Hachiman actually attempts to stop her.

That scene is strong counterevidence to any later theory that simply equates taking on burdens with unhealthy dependency or control.

# 11. Japanese language and voice

## 11.1 Hachiman: defensive modality around invitations

The B.T. is especially useful linguistically because Hachiman turns relational desire into obligation grammar:

- Yui: `また、一緒に遊ぼ？`
- Hachiman: `罰ならしょうがねぇな`

He does not answer the semantic content with an equivalent direct desire statement. He changes the modality from **want** to **must/it can't be helped**.

That is classic Hachiman defensive language: accepting the act while rewriting the reason so the self need not stand behind desire.

## 11.2 Yukino: controlled concession rather than effusive belonging

Yukino rarely says “I want to go” in B.T. Instead:

> `私でよければ、暇なときに付き合うわ`

The conditional and concessive structure preserves dignity while still making participation available.

Similarly, after Yui's hair styling she says:

> `別に構わないわ`
>
> `……涼しいし`

The pleasure is behaviorally visible while speech supplies a practical reason. Yukino too uses **instrumental language to carry attachment without fully naming it**.

## 11.3 Yui: directness is increasing

Yui's voice contains fillers and softeners, but 7.5 repeatedly shows direct relational acts:

- `あったりまえじゃん！`
- `また、一緒に遊ぼ？`
- direct concern: `ゆきのん、どしたの？`
- practical study advice in conversational language.

Her plainness is not lack of sophistication. Her language is often the fastest route from perceived relational need to an actionable response.

## 11.4 `遊び` as serious relational vocabulary

B.T. uses `遊び` repeatedly and then explicitly theorizes it as room/latitude. The ordinary word carries an important social function: a relationship can have **space that is not obligation, confession, request, or crisis**.

That “wasted” space is precisely where voluntary familiarity develops.

## 11.5 SIDE-B vocabulary: stagecraft and public judgment

The repeated vocabulary of `演出`, `世間`, audience, casting, efficiency, and performance shows Hachiman conceptualizing social reality theatrically.

He does not merely persuade individuals. He **authors the conditions under which they will interpret themselves**.

That is the linguistic seed of the “administrative jurisdiction” problem identified in Volume 7.

## 11.6 `逃げる／戻る／帰る`

SIDE-B is saturated with movement verbs. `戻る` occurs exceptionally often because the story separates several meanings of return:

- returning to a club after leaving;
- returning to an old institution for refuge;
- returning because the harmful condition has been removed;
- wanting a place to which one can return.

The text therefore refuses a moral equation of **return = courage** and **departure = cowardice**. Context determines whether return restores life or repeats avoidance.

# 12. Comedy, genre, and intertext

7.5 is much more openly comic than the adjacent mainline crisis volumes, and that is analytically important rather than disposable.

The anthology uses:

- fake advice-column anonymity;
- marriage-game-show parody;
- meta light-novel publishing jokes;
- Totsuka jokes;
- game-center competition;
- Hachiman's househusband fantasy;
- judo tournament sports parody;
- cat comedy.

The lighter mode demonstrates character under **lower stakes**. A person who appears one way in crisis may reveal a different relational capacity when no catastrophe needs solving.

The author afterword explicitly defends this scale. It says that what looks like an ordinary daily moment from outside can become, for the person living it, something that changes the rest of life. That is paratext, not proof of any individual interpretation, but it strongly validates treating ordinary scenes as architecturally meaningful rather than as filler.

The collection's comedy also keeps moral concepts from becoming slogans. “Trust,” “kindness,” “marriage,” “leadership,” and “return” are tested through jokes precisely so no single character can deliver a final doctrine unchallenged.

# 14. Alternative readings and adversarial tests

## 14.1 “7.5 proves pretexts are healthier than honesty”

**Rejected as too broad.**

Pretexts can lower expressive risk, as B.T. shows. But the anthology also includes deceptive stagecraft in SIDE-B that creates unauthorized costs. Indirection is ethically neutral until we ask what it allows participants to choose.

## 14.2 “SIDE-B proves Hachiman's later self-sacrifice pathology already fully exists”

**Narrow substantially.**

The social-engineering toolkit clearly exists. Hachiman also bears bodily risk. But the main ethical mechanism is punitive pressure directed at an abusive outsider so others can regain the option to participate. It is not yet the same as destroying his own relationship standing to decide an intimate group's future.

## 14.3 “Hachiman is simply right about the abusive alumnus”

**Split evidence classes.**

He is right that the alumnus's conduct is harmful and that removing him changes club participation. His detailed theory of university failure remains inference.

## 14.4 “The group photo is pure evidence of healthy consent”

**Rejected.**

Yui pushes both Hachiman and Yukino after initial resistance. The scene is affectionate and low-stakes, but that does not erase the pushiness. What makes it ethically mild is scale, reversibility, and subsequent acceptance—not perfect prior authorization.

## 14.5 “Yukino's blush proves she desperately wants a friend group”

**Narrow.**

The evidence strongly supports that Yui's automatic inclusion matters to her and that she is pleased/embarrassed by it. “Desperately” or “deepest desire” goes beyond the scene.

## 14.6 “SIDE-A is direct foreshadowing of Volume 7”

**Only in internal chronology.**

Publication history makes it retrospective connective tissue. This correction is one of the V2 pass's most important methodological gains.

## 14.7 “The Service Club is already explicitly Hachiman's home in SIDE-B”

**Literarily strong, textually unspoken.**

The ending places his question about a return-place beside the clubroom's sounds. The implication is deliberate. Hachiman still does not name the club himself.

# 15. Retrospective second-pass reading

Full-series hindsight may now be admitted explicitly.

## 15.1 7.5 reveals that the later “genuine” problem grows out of ordinary mechanisms

The mature series will eventually ask for more direct desire, answerability, and relationships that survive conflict. 7.5 shows why that answer cannot simply be “abolish all pretexts.”

Some of the trio's healthiest early contact occurs through frames:

- a club;
- a request;
- a game;
- a punishment;
- a magazine assignment;
- a photograph;
- an email column.

These forms are not necessarily obstacles to authentic relation. They are **scaffolds**. The later problem is whether the participants can eventually revise or leave the scaffold rather than becoming trapped inside it.

## 15.2 SIDE-B and Kyoto use related tools for ethically different objects

With Volume 7 already read, the structural resemblance is unmistakable.

SIDE-B Hachiman:

- aggregates incentives;
- constructs a stage;
- hides part of the plan;
- exploits shame;
- assigns moral cost to another participant;
- produces an effective outcome.

Kyoto Hachiman:

- aggregates incompatible relationship desires;
- constructs a confession stage;
- withholds method from Yukino and Yui;
- exploits his own social expendability;
- substitutes his act for Tobe's;
- produces the preserved-group outcome.

The resemblance makes the difference more important.

SIDE-B primarily removes a coercive condition and reopens options for judo members. Kyoto forecloses a relationship action on behalf of people whose desires differ.

Thus the mature criterion is not “manipulation bad.” It is closer to:

> **Does the intervention expand the subject's ability to choose, or does the intervention treat the helper's model of the subject's welfare as authority to choose for them?**

## 15.3 The Service Club has already become ordinary before Hachiman can say so

The final SIDE-B question about a place to return is retrospectively one of the series' clearest early belonging seeds.

Later arcs make the Service Club's form itself something Hachiman, Yukino, and Yui try desperately to preserve. 7.5 shows why. It is not valuable only because of dramatic confessions or rescues. It has accumulated:

- seats;
- tea;
- jokes;
- shared labor;
- irritation;
- phone calls;
- errands;
- games;
- invitations;
- photos;
- silence;
- repetitive presence.

The institution becomes emotionally costly to lose because it first becomes ordinary.

## 15.4 Yui's later role is already present in miniature

Hindsight strongly validates the “relational architect” formulation. Yui repeatedly turns social perception into concrete opportunities. She does not merely keep everyone happy. She changes the infrastructure of what people can do together.

This makes later readings that reduce her to “the girl who wants the trio not to change” inadequate. Her preservation instinct is real, but so is her repeated production of **new relational states**.

## 15.5 Yukino's later autonomy problem must not erase her early agency

Later dependency/self-removal language can tempt a retrospective reader to interpret every burden Yukino takes as pathology.

SIDE-B resists that flattening. Yukino chooses the match because she understands the collective objective and rejects Hachiman's unnecessary substitution. Her competence and willingness to act are not themselves symptoms.

A mature autonomy analysis must preserve the difference between:

- chosen contribution;
- compulsive overfunctioning;
- imposed substitution;
- self-removal on behalf of others.

# 18. Cumulative delta

Volume 7.5 should enter the longitudinal corpus not as “what happened after Kyoto,” but as a **publication-time retrospective supplement with internally distributed chronology**.

Its cumulative contributions are:

1. **Chronology discipline becomes mandatory.** Publication order and story order diverge sharply; “foreshadowing” must always specify which axis is meant.
2. **Expectation avoidance receives direct early wording.** Hachiman does not fail to perceive Yui's invitation; he refuses first because accepting might expose him to the possibility that he was not truly wanted.
3. **Pretexts are morally differentiated.** A transparent game can lower expressive risk without replacing choice. A covert social performance can achieve a good outcome while still imposing unauthorized costs.
4. **Yui is further upgraded from mediator to relational architect.** She builds invitations, group futures, motivation, practical support, information flow, and embodied friendship.
5. **Yukino's desire for inclusion is real but should remain proportionate.** She is surprised and pleased to be automatically included; by SIDE-B, friendship with Yui has become substantially less guarded.
6. **Yukino's competence is not equivalent to pathology.** SIDE-B shows chosen, informed contribution that actually prevents Hachiman from needlessly substituting himself.
7. **The Hachiman intervention toolkit predates the cultural festival.** SIDE-B already contains staging, incomplete disclosure, audience manipulation, targeted psychological pressure, and collateral role assignment.
8. **Self-sacrifice is not yet the best master concept for SIDE-B.** The core is punitive social engineering aimed at removing an abusive authority. Hachiman bears cost, but the primary ethical question is not his martyrdom.
9. **Outcome and method separate cleanly.** SIDE-B works: former members return. The effective outcome does not retroactively authorize Shiroyama's uninformed complicity or the humiliation method.
10. **The anthology supplies an early exit-right principle.** Leaving a harmful institution is not betrayal; return becomes plausible when the harmful condition changes.
11. **SIDE-A offers a strong healthy-reliance model.** Different people do different work according to competence; nobody has to become self-sufficient or identical.
12. **Trust is explicitly tied to vulnerability.** SIDE-A's Hachiman sees distrust as a defense against being hurt; after V7 publication, that makes the Kyoto entrustment breach retrospectively sharper.
13. **`本物` still does not govern the discourse.** The collection is about ordinary cooperation, trust, play, kindness, performance, return, and place—not yet the mature conceptual vocabulary.
14. **The Service Club is becoming a return-place before Hachiman can identify it.** The text positions his question about `戻りたい場所` inside the sensory environment of the clubroom, but he leaves the answer unstated.
15. **Ordinary time is analytically central.** The afterword's own paratextual thesis—that apparently trivial daily scenes may alter a life—fits the anthology's structural role without dictating any single interpretation.

The strongest compact formulation to carry forward is:

> **7.5 reveals that Oregairu's later crisis is not born because the characters lacked relationship. It is born because relationship had already been quietly made out of ordinary repetition—games, work, food, jokes, invitations, touch, shared inconvenience, and places one returns to. Hachiman's central danger is therefore not that he cannot understand social systems. It is that the same intelligence capable of removing an abusive constraint can become, when turned inward on intimate relationships, a claim to jurisdiction over choices that were never his to make.**

The mainline now resumes with Volume 8, where Kyoto's damaged trust stops being an aftereffect and becomes an institutional crisis inside the Service Club itself.
