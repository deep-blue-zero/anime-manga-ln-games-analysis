# Oregairu V2 — V1 Claim Audit

## Purpose

This document converts the first-pass *Oregairu* deep-dive into a **testable hypothesis ledger** for the V2 second-pass reread.

It is deliberately **not** a replacement synthesis.

The first pass produced many strong interpretations. The danger would be either:

1. treating those interpretations as settled truth and rereading the novels only to confirm them; or
2. discarding the first pass entirely and wasting a large body of accumulated analytical work.

V2 takes a third approach.

> **Every mature V1 conclusion becomes a hypothesis with a defined burden of proof, counterevidence target, source-backfill requirement, and revision condition.**

The historical transcript is therefore evidence about **what we previously believed and why**. It is not primary evidence about the novels. The original Japanese prose, illustrations, drama-CD audio, and other properly classified sources remain governing.

# 2. Claim-disposition vocabulary

Every V1 claim in this audit receives one or more of the following dispositions.

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **RETAIN-STRONG** | V1 claim is well motivated and should enter V2 as a high-confidence hypothesis, but still requires primary-source verification. |
| **RETAIN-NARROW** | Core insight is likely sound, but V1 phrasing risks overgeneralization. V2 should narrow domain, timeframe, or causal scope. |
| **REOPEN** | Plausible but insufficiently tested. V2 must actively search for counterexamples. |
| **OVERCLAIM-RISK** | V1 may have converted a useful interpretation into a totalizing explanation. |
| **SOURCE-BACKFILL** | Claim needs exact Japanese locator, chapter, wording, audio timing, illustration locator, or publication provenance. |
| **PROSPECTIVE-CHECK** | V2 must test whether the claim was genuinely available at the relevant publication point or only visible retrospectively. |
| **SUPPLEMENTARY-ONLY** | Claim derives from drama CD, anthology, song, 14.5, *Ketsu*, or another layer that must not govern mainline prose without corroboration. |
| **DEFERRED** | Material remains outside the governing mainline corpus for now. |
| **POSSIBLE-REVERSAL** | V2 should be prepared to abandon the claim if close rereading does not sustain it. |

These are audit dispositions, not verdicts.

# 3. Executive audit

## 3.1 The V1 synthesis is strong enough to preserve

The strongest V1 thesis is:

> *Oregairu* studies three adolescents who turn desire into technique because technique feels safer than asking.

V1 then gives each member a characteristic technique:

- Hachiman: unilateral problem-solving and self-sacrifice;
- Yukino: competence, correction, and eventually self-removal;
- Yui: atmosphere management, accommodation, and postponement of irreversible answers.

This remains a highly productive model. It explains why the Service Club matters as more than a setting: it gives personal desire an institutional disguise called **work**.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + RETAIN-NARROW.

Why narrow it?

Because no protagonist uses only one strategy.

- Hachiman sometimes asks, cooperates, follows, waits, or offers bounded help.
- Yukino sometimes reaches directly, chooses, apologizes, requests aid, or pursues her own project.
- Yui sometimes confronts, insists, withholds, strategizes, or knowingly accepts conflict.

V2 must therefore treat the three formulations as **dominant defensive tendencies**, not immutable personality laws.

---

## 3.2 The V1 account of 「本物」 is compelling but must be reconstructed longitudinally

V1’s mature conclusion is that 「本物」 does not mean a relationship purified of:

- performance;
- self-interest;
- dependence;
- jealousy;
- mixed motives;
- social roles;
- misunderstanding.

Instead, V1 argues that a relationship becomes more genuine as participants:

1. acknowledge mixed motives;
2. state their own desires;
3. permit others to answer;
4. accept consequences;
5. revise the relationship rather than preserving a false form.

The final shorthand is **accountable continuation**.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + SOURCE-BACKFILL + PROSPECTIVE-CHECK.

This should become one of V2’s central hypotheses, but V2 must not read early uses of truth, sincerity, falsehood, mistake, or honesty as already meaning the mature 「本物」 concept.

The reread must determine:

- when 「本物」 enters explicitly;
- which earlier lexical fields anticipate it;
- which do not;
- whether “accountable continuation” is truly text-generated or a V1 philosophical synthesis imposed across heterogeneous scenes;
- whether Volume 14.5 strengthens or complicates it.

---

## 3.3 V1’s strongest methodological insight: Hachiman is domain-specifically unreliable

The first pass repeatedly rejected a simplistic “unreliable narrator” label.

V1’s more precise model is:

> Hachiman often sees external social mechanisms accurately while becoming least reliable when an interpretation would force him to acknowledge his own desire, importance, vulnerability, hope, jealousy, or need to be chosen.

Examples identified across V1 include:

- reading clique structure accurately while misreading Yui’s particularity;
- understanding Kawasaki’s financial motive while reducing Yui’s care to guilt;
- recognizing other people’s performances while treating his own loner identity as transparent;
- correctly identifying structural risk while assuming the right to decide everyone’s tolerable losses;
- seeing that understanding is incomplete while using epistemic humility as a reason not to ask.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + RETAIN-NARROW.

V2 must classify his reliability by domain rather than simply label particular passages “true” or “false.”

# 4. Hachiman claim ledger

## H-01 — Cynicism functions as a closed epistemology

**V1 claim:** Hachiman’s error is not that all cynical observations are false. He gives negative explanations privileged status while treating favorable interpretations as inadmissible.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

**V2 test:**
- identify scenes where Hachiman accurately accepts favorable evidence;
- identify when negative interpretations are inferentially better rather than merely defensive;
- distinguish realistic caution from preemptive rejection.

**Primary-source targets:** early volumes, especially dog-accident reasoning, Yui withdrawal/restart, Orimoto material, post-Kyoto narration.

---

## H-02 — His romantic “denseness” is frequently active false-negative control

**V1 claim:** Hachiman is not simply unable to perceive romantic interest. He detects possibilities and suppresses them because a false negative is safer than a humiliating false positive.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + RETAIN-NARROW.

**Risk:** V1 may underweight moments of genuine uncertainty. Perceiving *a possibility* is not the same as correctly knowing another person’s feeling.

**V2 question:** When is he avoiding an inference he already finds probable, and when is he responsibly refusing certainty?

---

## H-03 — He projects ideals onto Yukino and then experiences correction as betrayal

**V1 claim:** Volume 5 makes explicit that Hachiman admired an idealized Yukino: solitary, truthful, invulnerable, self-sufficient. The traffic-accident concealment wounds that ideal more than it constitutes moral wrongdoing by Yukino.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + SOURCE-BACKFILL.

**V2 test:** track all earlier signs that Hachiman’s image of Yukino is aspirational self-projection rather than simply attraction.

---

## H-04 — “People cannot understand one another” becomes both insight and evasion

**V1 claim:** Hachiman’s epistemic humility is valid when it restrains entitlement, but evasive when he uses it to avoid asking questions whose answers may hurt him.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

**V2 contrast:** Volume 10’s Hayama privacy problem must be kept distinct from the Service Club’s communication failures. “You are not entitled to know” and “you should ask rather than decide” are not contradictory.

---

## H-05 — Hachiman’s self-sacrifice is often a form of control

**V1 claim:** By choosing himself as expendable, Hachiman assigns roles and costs to others without asking whether they accept the solution.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-NARROW + OVERCLAIM-RISK.

This is one of V1’s most important claims and one of the easiest to overstate.

V2 must distinguish:

1. **voluntary self-bearing of cost;**
2. **self-sacrifice that indirectly hurts observers;**
3. **self-sacrifice that assigns roles to others;**
4. **strategic risk-taking under constrained options;**
5. **genuine martyr fantasy;**
6. **protective action that is costly but not pathological.**

The label “self-sacrifice” must never substitute for scene analysis.

---

## H-06 — He excludes his own wellbeing from moral accounting

**V1 claim:** In the cultural festival especially, Hachiman treats harm he can personally tolerate as if it is not harm at all.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

**Counterevidence target:** scenes where he does protect himself, redirects harm, or refuses cost.

Volume 6.5 already complicates the pure-martyr reading by showing that he can redirect social damage onto Tobe when useful.

---

## H-07 — He weaponizes social intelligence

**V1 claim:** Hachiman often reads atmosphere, incentives, shame, and group hierarchy more accurately than insiders, then uses that information coercively.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

**V2 need:** distinguish successful tactical prediction from omniscient social mastery. His plans sometimes work because the plot supplies unusually legible systems.

---

## H-08 — His methods can consume other people’s integrity

**V1 claim:** The judo story is especially important because Shiroyama becomes complicit in a deceptive performance without full information.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + SOURCE-BACKFILL.

**V2 significance:** this prevents “he only hurts himself” from becoming a serious description of his early methods.

---

## H-09 — He mistakes voluntary action for exclusive ownership of consequences

**V1 claim:** Hachiman correctly resists patronizing descriptions of his choices as passive victimhood, but then incorrectly infers that others have no standing to respond to the consequences.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

**Primary audit:** Volume 8 / Hayama confrontation.

---

## H-10 — His desire for total understanding contains a controlling impulse

**V1 claim:** The Volume 9 「本物」 plea includes a wish to understand so completely that uncertainty disappears.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-NARROW.

**V2 question:** Is “epistemic possession” too strong a philosophical gloss, or does the Japanese support it? Track the exact modality of his desire.

---

## H-11 — His growth is better measured by requests than by sociability

**V1 claim:** The most important movement is not “loner becomes social.” It is:

> from solving by being hated → to relying on others → to admitting “I want something” → to asking Yukino to remain involved without needing rescue.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

This should be one of V2’s core longitudinal measures.

---

## H-12 — Personal desire becomes a sufficient reason only very late

**V1 claim:** Hachiman repeatedly needs external justification:
- client request;
- Komachi;
- promise;
- contest;
- work;
- prom;
- rescue.

Volume 14 becomes decisive because he finally claims **want** rather than usefulness.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + PROSPECTIVE-CHECK.

V2 should track every earlier moment where he acts merely because he wants to; those may complicate the lateness of the claim.

---

## H-13 — Hachiman’s humor often functions as emotional conversion

**V1 claim:** jokes, genre references, sexual humor, false syllogisms, and literary allusions frequently metabolize threatening feelings into analysis or comedy.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-NARROW + SOURCE-BACKFILL.

V2 must not pathologize every joke. Some jokes are simply jokes, genre play, or voice.

---

## H-14 — The novels risk flattering the punitive outsider fantasy

**V1 claim:** Hachiman’s schemes are narratively exhilarating and sometimes reward the fantasy that the alienated boy secretly understands everyone better than the social world does.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG as a critical question, not an internal-character fact.

V2 should ask when the text itself critiques this fantasy and when narrative causality rewards it.

# 5. Yukino claim ledger

## YK-01 — Yukino begins from corrective idealism, not generic “ice queen” aloofness

**V1 claim:** her early severity is ideological. Ability entails responsibility; help should develop capability rather than hand over outcomes.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

**V2 need:** recover exact Japanese vocabulary around correction, help, effort, weakness, ability, and change.

---

## YK-02 — Competence is both genuine strength and relational armor

**V1 claim:** Yukino converts problems into work because work lets her contribute without confessing need.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + RETAIN-NARROW.

**Counterevidence:** scenes where competence is simply competence, enjoyment, pride, or ethical conviction.

---

## YK-03 — Her self-sufficiency is aspirational, not fake

**V1 claim:** later evidence does not reveal that early Yukino was secretly helpless. It reveals that genuine ability coexists with family-conditioned fear of dependence and comparison.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

This distinction is essential to avoid reducing her to a hidden dependent heroine.

---

## YK-04 — Family makes competence morally loaded

**V1 claim:** Haruno’s prior occupation of the “brilliant daughter / public proxy / family instrument” role means Yukino experiences asking for help as possible evidence that she has no self of her own.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + SOURCE-BACKFILL.

V2 must distinguish:
- what the novels explicitly state;
- Hachiman’s inference;
- Haruno’s provocation;
- our systemic interpretation.

---

## YK-05 — Her growth is differentiation, not becoming Haruno

**V1 claim:** Yukino increasingly uses capacities associated with Haruno—negotiation, political intelligence, social maneuver—without becoming her sister.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

---

## YK-06 — Reliance, trust, expectation, request, and surrender of authorship must be separated

**V1 claim:** Yukino’s late arc is not “stop depending on Hachiman.” It is learning that:
- trusting him;
- expecting him to notice;
- asking him;
- accepting help;
- allowing him to decide for her

are distinct acts.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

This is a governing V2 analytical distinction.

---

## YK-07 — “Self-removal” is a late defensive strategy, not Yukino’s essence

**V1 claim:** Volume 13 shows Yukino treating her disappearance from the romantic structure as a gift to Yui and Hachiman.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-NARROW + OVERCLAIM-RISK.

V2 must not back-project “self-removal” onto all earlier restraint, independence, or mediation.

---

## YK-08 — Her final wish is loving and controlling at once

**V1 claim:** asking Hachiman to grant Yui’s wish is genuine care but still decides Yui’s happiness and Hachiman’s proper action.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

V2 should test whether “controlling” is the best word or whether “sacrificial authorship” is more precise.

---

## YK-09 — Her relationship with Yui is structurally independent of the romance

**V1 claim:** Yui offers Yukino friendship not earned through competence; Yukino offers Yui seriousness, admiration, and chosen loyalty outside ordinary group fit.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

V2 should expand this substantially because V1 remained Hachiman-centered even when it recognized the girls’ independent bond.

---

## YK-10 — Autonomy is authorship, not isolation

**V1 claim:** mature independence means being able to identify desire, request or refuse help, own decisions, and renegotiate relationships—not never needing anyone.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

This is one of V1’s best conceptual claims.

---

## YK-11 — Romance does not rescue Yukino from family

**V1 claim:** the ending draws Hachiman into her family/social future rather than extracting Yukino into a private anti-family space.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG + SOURCE-BACKFILL.

---

## YK-12 — “I can stand alone / I know” is a core anti-codependency image

**V1 claim:** the offered hand matters precisely because both parties affirm Yukino’s independent capacity.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG.

V2 should verify exact context and avoid treating one exchange as proof that all dependency problems are solved.

# 13. V1 source and method weaknesses

## 13.1 OCR-first reading

The first pass often worked from scanned PDFs with usable but imperfect OCR.

Consequences:

- Japanese wording was checked selectively rather than systematically;
- lexical recurrence was difficult to search longitudinally;
- ruby/furigana and punctuation may have been flattened;
- chapter boundaries and text-page mapping were less stable than EPUB;
- prose versus illustration-page relationships were not consistently recorded.

**V2 correction:** EPUB becomes the primary textual/search layer; PDF becomes fixed-page and visual verification.

---

## 13.2 Locator quality is uneven

V1 frequently recorded printed page numbers and short Japanese quotations, which is much better than no provenance.

However, it did not consistently preserve:

- chapter;
- EPUB spine item;
- distinctive Japanese anchor;
- PDF page;
- scene context;
- evidence classification;
- competing reading.

**V2 correction:** every major claim receives a primary-source locator ledger entry.

---

## 13.3 Japanese voice was recognized but underdeveloped

V1 made good high-level observations:

- Hachiman: irony, qualification, defensive abstraction;
- Yukino: precision and prosecutorial register;
- Yui: casual speech, hesitation, relational cues;
- Haruno: calibrated warmth;
- Zaimokuza: theatrical register shifts.

But it did not systematically track:

- pronouns;
- address-term transitions;
- sentence endings;
- modality;
- hedging;
- ellipsis;
- imperatives;
- politeness shifts;
- repeated lexical clusters;
- verbal echoes.

**V2 correction:** Japanese Voice Ledger becomes a first-class longitudinal artifact.

---

## 13.4 “Self-sacrifice = control” risks becoming too universal

V1 correctly discovered that Hachiman’s sacrificial strategies often allocate costs without consent.

But if used indiscriminately, the phrase can turn every costly act into domination.

**V2 correction:** each intervention must record:
- client;
- problem owner;
- authorization;
- method;
- cost bearer;
- reversibility;
- agency gain/loss;
- Hachiman’s motive evidence;
- consequences.

---

## 13.5 “Yukino self-removal” is temporally late

The final synthesis’s phrase is powerful, but it can make earlier restraint look like a single continuous pathology.

**V2 correction:** treat self-removal as a late-stage solution emerging from:
- family pressure;
- Haruno’s diagnosis;
- Yui friendship;
- romantic conflict;
- prom contest;
- shame around expectation.

Do not presuppose it in Volumes 1–6.

---

## 13.6 V1 underweighted Yui as strategist

The final synthesis corrects this more than the early analyses did, but the project’s conceptual language still often casts:
- Hachiman as analyst,
- Yukino as principle,
- Yui as relational mediator.

That division risks reproducing Hachiman’s own underestimation of Yui.

**V2 correction:** track Yui’s:
- tactical timing;
- social prediction;
- selective silence;
- coalition management;
- agenda setting;
- deliberate ambiguity;
- boundary crossings;
- acts of refusal.

---

## 13.7 Supporting women perform substantial emotional labor around Hachiman

V1 explicitly recognizes that Yui, Yukino, Komachi, Hiratsuka, Iroha, Miura, and Haruno repeatedly force Hachiman toward truths he avoids.

**Disposition:** RETAIN-STRONG as a formal/narrative critique.

**V2 correction:** do not reduce their independent arcs to developmental services for Hachiman.

---

## 13.8 V1’s full-series synthesis sometimes compresses distinct causal mechanisms

Examples:

- “care into technique” may unite scenes whose motives differ;
- “self-sacrifice” may include tactical risk, shame, service, compulsion, and love;
- “preservation” may include Yui’s kindness, strategy, fear, friendship, romance, and ordinary preference;
- “competence” may include Yukino’s joy in mastery as well as defensive control.

**V2 correction:** synthesis terms are outputs of evidence clustering, not categories imposed in advance.

# 16. Highest-priority adversarial tests for V2

## Test A — Is “self-sacrifice as control” actually the best master concept?

**Confirming evidence expected:**
- unconsented role assignment;
- unilateral cost allocation;
- repeated refusal to let others choose him;
- solutions that preserve others by making him disposable.

**Counterevidence sought:**
- consensual costly acts;
- situations where sacrifice expands rather than reduces agency;
- moments when self-cost is simply the least harmful available option.

**Revision trigger:** if “control” explains only a subset, V2 should replace the master phrase with a taxonomy.

---

## Test B — Is “Yukino self-removal” a coherent late arc or retrospective compression?

**Confirming evidence expected:**
- repeated tendency to solve conflict by withdrawing her own claim;
- Volume 13 sacrifice to Yui;
- shame around being expected/needed.

**Counterevidence sought:**
- explicit pursuit;
- possessiveness;
- boundary-setting;
- self-authored choices that preserve relationship.

**Revision trigger:** restrict “self-removal” to specific late decisions rather than character essence.

---

## Test C — Does 「本物」 really converge on “accountable continuation”?

**Confirming evidence expected:**
- rejection of perfect telepathy;
- mixed motives retained;
- conflict tolerated;
- desire stated;
- answer permitted;
- post-answer continuation;
- 14.5 ordinary-life durability.

**Counterevidence sought:**
- passages defining 「本物」 in stronger purity, exclusivity, or emotional-certainty terms;
- unresolved contradiction between Hachiman’s ideal and the ending.

**Revision trigger:** redefine final thesis as one possible mature practice rather than the series’ singular definition.

---

## Test D — Does Hachiman/Yukino truly reach the deepest level of his self-conception?

**Confirming evidence expected:**
- not merely attraction but desire for Yukino’s judgment, work, opposition, and future presence;
- inability to accept her disappearance after usefulness ends;
- confession structure.

**Counterevidence sought:**
- equivalent or competing future-self language around Yui;
- mainline evidence that V1 underweighted embodied/domestic compatibility.

**Revision trigger:** describe the romantic choice more historically and less hierarchically.

---

## Test E — Is Yui primarily a preserver, or is that framing itself Hachiman-centric?

**Confirming evidence expected:**
- atmosphere management;
- deferral;
- “everything”;
- avoidance of exclusion.

**Counterevidence sought:**
- agenda setting;
- confrontation;
- risk-taking;
- strategic use of ambiguity;
- decisions that knowingly destabilize harmony.

**Revision trigger:** replace “preserver” with a more active social-political model.

## Test F — How authoritative is Haruno’s diagnosis?

**Confirming evidence expected:**

**Counterevidence sought:**
- Haruno projection;

V2 must measure:

# 20. Phase-1 conclusion

The first pass should **not** be characterized as an obsolete reading.

Its core strength is that it already discovered the series’ main epistemic and ethical problem:

> people repeatedly convert desire into a form that lets them avoid owning desire.

The V1 synthesis then generalized this into a theory of:
- self-sacrificial solving;
- competence;
- accommodation;
- performance;
- dependency;
- authorship;
- and the genuine.

V2’s job is not to find a more elaborate vocabulary for the same conclusion.

It is to determine, from the Japanese novels again:

1. where that model is exactly right;
2. where it is too neat;
3. where characters act against their dominant pattern;
4. where V1 confused Hachiman’s interpretation with textual fact;
5. where late concepts were projected backward;
6. where Japanese wording changes the conceptual map;
7. where visual or audio evidence complicates prose;
8. and where the final synthesis should be revised rather than merely expanded.

The proper starting stance for Phase 2 is therefore:

> **V1 supplies the questions. The Japanese text gets to answer them again.**
