# Normative framing: depiction, evaluation, and moral development

## 1. Responsibility

This protocol governs how the analysis moves from represented conduct to a claim about the work's evaluative stance. It does not provide a verdict on Mushoku Tensei, diagnose a character, classify its readers, or authorize graphic reproduction of sexual material.

The central question is not simply whether misconduct appears. It is **what the text invites readers to notice, desire, excuse, criticize, overlook, or reconsider, through which mechanisms, at which source boundary**.

## 2. Separate the levels

| Level | Question | Principal evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Represented event | What occurs, and what remains imagined/reported? | Scene and exact witness. |
| Character evaluation | What does a participant judge or rationalize? | Speech, thought, behavior, revision. |
| Social evaluation | What do local institutions or other characters permit? | Situated norms and reactions. |
| Narrative framing | How is attention, sympathy, pleasure, and distance organized? | Voice, focalization, tone, structure, access. |
| Implied normative pattern | What evaluative pattern is supported across scenes? | Convergent and conflicting narrative mechanisms. |
| Creator intention | What was intended by whom and when? | Textual inference and separately dated commentary. |
| Reception | How did particular people respond? | Actual identified responses. |
| Social effect | Did the work change audience attitudes or conduct? | Evidence beyond close reading; usually not available. |

Neither a protagonist's belief nor a fictional society's custom is automatically the implied normative pattern. A critic's experience is real reception evidence without being the only possible interpretation.

## 3. Terms that must not be collapsed

Distinguish depiction, explanation, sympathy, eroticization, comic treatment, narrative accommodation, normalization, approval, justification, and endorsement. Several may coexist; none is a synonym for all the others.

For a claim of **endorsement**, specify the endorsed proposition: this act is permissible; this relationship is desirable; this excuse is valid; or this class of behavior need not be morally scrutinized. Do not move between those propositions mid-argument.

Use **normalization** cautiously for a demonstrated pattern of ordinary treatment, minimized harm, or unchallenged expectation. It is not a proven causal effect on viewers. Use **tonal contradiction** only after identifying the incompatible evaluative pressures; mixed tone is not automatically incoherent.

Sexual preferences, fantasies, attempted acts, completed acts, coercion, and later restraint require separate records. Age, developmental status, authority, dependency, knowledge, and consent matter independently. Clinical categories and colloquial labels cannot be substituted for each other without a separate justified inquiry.

## 5. Evidence that is diagnostic, not dispositive

Sympathetic focalization can coexist with critique. A comic scene can have a critical target without eliminating the possibility of erotic or minimizing framing. A character being struck or embarrassed can be a genre routine rather than a sustained reckoning. Lack of punishment can reflect narrative realism, omission, indifference, or approval; it does not decide which.

Likewise, eventual romantic or material success is not automatically a reward **for** an earlier act. Identify a causal or rhetorical connection. Temporal sequence alone is inadequate. A later good deed does not cancel an unrelated victim's harm, but a continuing flaw does not make unrelated growth imaginary.

Distinguish the analyst's discomfort from a formal argument about narrative positioning. Both can be recorded, with different claim classes. Do not assume the author's intent controls every reader's experience or that one response proves a universal effect.

## 6. Testing moral development

For a proposed change, identify:

1. Earlier belief/practice and the persons affected.
2. Trigger or learning mechanism, when represented.
3. What the character now understands differently.
4. Repair, restraint, responsibility, or changed practice actually demonstrated.
5. A later comparable opportunity, including a costly case or changed relationship.
6. Persistence, relapse, limits, and possible alternative explanations.

Track recognition, behavior, and generalization separately. A self-critical speech supports recognition; it does not prove transferred practice. Abstention when temptation or opportunity disappears is not a clean test of restraint. A later episode can retrospectively complicate an earlier assessment without being evidence that its intended endpoint was always known to the original audience.

Important alternatives include aging, reduced opportunity, increased security, changed partner selection, a different focalizer, greater power, editorial deletion, and a shifted genre register. Treat these as possible explanations, not presumptive debunkings of genuine growth.

## 7. Scope-matched counterreadings

Test propositions at scene, volume, phase, continuity, and series levels. One clear scene can defeat an absolute claim about every scene; it cannot necessarily settle the prevailing pattern. A late supplement may change a supplemented-series reading without erasing the meaning available to a reader of the main novels alone.

A useful conclusion may be dimensional: recognition improves, some practices change, an erotic/comic convention remains, and affected-person access stays uneven. State which part is established, inferred, contested, or unevaluated. Do not use nuance to avoid a supported critical conclusion or to manufacture equivalence between unequal evidence.

## 8. Matched-case and negative-case checks

Compare similar boundary problems across different characters, genders, status relations, and contexts where the text supplies meaningful analogues. Differences may reveal a consistent principle, protagonist favoritism, a deliberate double standard, or insufficiently comparable cases.

Avoid cherry-picking only sensational scenes. Sample routine care, refusal, help, repair, exploitation, violence, and nonsexual uses of power. The sexuality inquiry remains important but must not define all moral personhood in the series.

Count events only under a declared unit and coverage convention. Raw frequency, an invented morality score, and percentages of pages do not by themselves measure endorsement or literary importance.

## 9. Optional ethical-framework integration

The owner's PACTRIH framework may be used later if explicitly commissioned and its governing source is retrieved. Do not reconstruct its definitions from memory or turn its ordinal dimensions into an aggregate morality score. Character evaluation, narrative framing, and artist endorsement remain separate even when a comparative ethics instrument is used.

## 10. Output and update rules

The normative ledger owns event-level framing records and longitudinal comparisons. The central claims ledger owns revisions to load-bearing propositions. A specialist study owns the sustained argument; it references those records rather than creating a competing evidence chronology.

A claim must specify witness, range, target proposition, strongest support, strongest counterevidence, and what could change the conclusion. A definitive whole-series verdict is blocked by relevant unread or unverified sources. A strong bounded conclusion need not wait for every obscure supplement in the franchise.
