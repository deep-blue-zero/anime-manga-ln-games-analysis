# Discourse and hypothesis method

## 1. Purpose

Investigate the strongest recoverable versions of important interpretations, not a caricatured contest between fans and critics. Separate what a text says, what a reader infers, the evaluative premise behind that inference, and the extent to which a claim actually circulates.

The project is not obliged to defend the series, to condemn it, or to finish at a position midway between extremes. It should distinguish demonstrably false factual claims, plausible competing interpretations, value disagreements, and questions the available evidence cannot settle.

Do not assume that critics literally reason 'depiction equals endorsement.' A critic may instead make a substantial claim about erotic/comic framing, victim access, narrative rewards, or accumulated patterns. Recover the actual argument before evaluating it. Apply the same standard to defenses such as 'it is a redemption story' or 'he is a child now.'

## 2. Claim types

| Type | Evidence needed |
|---|---|
| Textual/factual | Exact edition, event, wording, and scope. |
| Interpretive | A sustained explanation of form, characterization, causality, or structure. |
| Normative/aesthetic | A stated value or criterion plus accurate descriptive premises. |
| Authorial intention | Textual inference and/or dated first-party commentary, with attribution limits. |
| Editorial history | Identifiable versions and evidence of the process, not just a difference. |
| Adaptation | Actual source/adaptation comparison in the relevant medium. |
| Reception prevalence | A defined corpus/sample and honest population limits. |
| Audience effect | Evidence about effects on actual readers; not inferred from literary form alone. |

A statement like 'this material makes the work unenjoyable for me' does not require falsification by a better plot explanation. Its factual premises can be checked; its preference is not a textual error. A broader claim about what the work endorses is a different proposition.

Use `OPEN`, `STRENGTHENED`, `WEAKENED`, `REFORMULATED`, `REJECTED_FOR_DECLARED_SCOPE`, or `UNDERDETERMINED` for local adjudication state. These are not authority labels or calibrated probabilities. Preserve the original proposition when narrowing it; do not quietly change the claim until it becomes unfalsifiable.

A broad claim should be decomposed into dimensions and levels. Different hypotheses below can both be supported. They are not mutually exclusive multiple-choice answers.

Broaden beyond controversy when actual reception warrants it: wish fulfillment and effort, competence and luck, family and dependency, pacing, worldbuilding, reader identification, second chances, the role of humor, and genre innovation. Do not invent historical influence claims from familiarity or popularity; those need historical evidence.

## 5. Support and disconfirmation

Define the scope and possible counterevidence before conducting a targeted search. A character's regret may support recognition but not necessarily narrative critique or durable change. A later relationship outcome may support a reward reading only if the proposed causal/rhetorical connection is demonstrated. Similar-looking scenes may differ in focalization or knowledge.

An absolute claim can be defeated by one valid counterexample. A tendency claim needs broader scope-matched evidence. Count independent scenes or publication events, not the number of times one passage is quoted by fans or ledgers.

Do not create a fake Bayesian probability or a single endorsement score. State qualitative confidence, decisive evidence, and what remains discriminating. A hypothesis with no possible distinguishing evidence may be a value commitment or an underspecified question rather than a testable textual claim.

## 6. Sequential exposure controls

Keep two activities separate: the primary reader accumulates source-grounded observations; the discourse analyst compares them with claims. Share neutral analytical questions, not a constant stream of partisan takes.

A small predeclared hypothesis catalogue can guide attention without determining findings. Place spoiler-bearing claims and retrospective quotations in a separate quarantine. Do not load it into a prospective reader unless that exposure is explicitly allowed and recorded.

Discourse work can begin at a bounded checkpoint after enough primary reconstruction exists. It need not wait for every volume, but its claim scope must match the admitted source. Full-series conclusions cannot be obtained by importing later summaries into an earlier-stage reader.

## 7. Reception corpus design

Before collection, define the question, language(s), media/editions, time window, platforms, search vocabulary, sampling strategy, and stopping rule. A purposive sample aimed at argumentative variety is appropriate, but it is not representative polling.

Record actual claim sources rather than only the most liked or inflammatory posts. Include sustained critical arguments, sustained defenses, mixed evaluations, and interpretations that do not center the controversy. Separate original arguments from reposts and derivative summaries. Avoid assuming a critic/fan identity when the author gives none.

For each item retain a stable ID, original URL, date, language, platform, accessible context, work/edition discussed, claimed reading boundary, relevant short excerpt/paraphrase, and provenance. Preserve only necessary personal identifiers; screenshots of private conversations need separate publication permission.

English and Japanese are language strata, not two coherent cultural minds. Subdivide when relevant by platform, date, source medium, declared familiarity, and argument type. Do not infer that an English-language critic has not read the novels, or that a Japanese commenter represents national tolerance. Record such facts only when evidenced.

Use a practical stopping rule such as several successive targeted searches yielding no materially new argument mechanisms within the declared sample. Report the rule and remaining gaps. Do not claim exhaustive reception coverage.

## 8. Translation and rhetorical fidelity

Preserve what the person actually argues before steelmanning it. Store the original formulation and the charitable reconstruction separately. Explain when a slogan compresses a more specific argument or when context changes its apparent force.

Translate charged terms with their register and ambiguity intact. Do not turn an insult into a diagnosis or flatten a nuanced Japanese post into an English culture-war slogan. Quote minimally and accurately; lengthy copied reviews are unnecessary.

Separate a disagreement about events from one about narration, an interpretive inference, or an ethical/aesthetic criterion. An argument may have correct facts and an unpersuasive inference, or a valuable interpretation built on one repairable factual error.

## 9. Adjudication format

A substantial claim study should provide: the exact bounded claim; strongest supporting reconstruction; strongest substantive counterreading; source observations and their interpretive steps; disputes over evaluative criteria; a scope-specific finding; and residual questions.

Do not force equal space or equal confidence when the evidence is asymmetric. Correct errors plainly. Do not describe people as morally suspect merely for enjoying or disliking the fiction. A theory of audience harm or normalization requires evidence beyond identifying the work's content.

Keep intent, textual effect, aesthetic success, and moral assessment separable in the conclusion. A work may intend critique and execute it unevenly; a reader may reasonably reject its aesthetic bargain without misreading the plot.

## 10. Integration and completion

The discourse ledger owns reception hypotheses and their provenance. The central analytical claims ledger owns shared literary proposition revisions. Claim studies link both. A reception synthesis describes the observed argument landscape and sampling limits; it is not a vote on which side has more posts.

Completion means adequate coverage for the declared question, not agreement among all readers. A final outcome may be supported, rejected, reformulated, or unresolved. Remaining legitimate value disagreements should be named rather than disguised as failures of evidence.
