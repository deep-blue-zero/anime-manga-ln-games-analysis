# Testimony, precaution and agency through V14

V14 warrants a targeted checkpoint because new testimony changes the grounds on which earlier advice can be trusted. The change does not authorize retrospective rewriting of any entering freeze. The [V14 reading](../02%20Sequential%20Readings/MT_V14_DEEP_READING.md#d-diagnostic-close-readings) owns observations001–036 and exact locators. This artifact specifies how the longitudinal account should handle the disclosure. It complements the [V12 loss and household checkpoint](MT_V12_LOSS_AND_HOUSEHOLD_CHECKPOINT.md) and [V01–V10 cumulative checkpoint](MT_V01_V10_CHECKPOINT.md); the required V15 cumulative review remains prospective. Prior franchise/model exposure is declared, so these are source-bounded retrospective discriminations, not clean predictions.

## The minimum warranted revision

Rudeus first accepts a detailed explanation of how Paul and Roxy could have survived without him. The reader has not witnessed that alternative expedition. Hitogami's specificity produces guilt and renewed trust before the cellar instruction; the typography and final expression increase unease. A visitor then interrupts compliance and supplies private identifying knowledge, observable abilities, a body with matching features, a journal and a concrete warning. Rudeus's later discovery of the distinctive rat is stronger evidence than the visitor's confidence alone. It gives good reason to reassess trust and take precaution, while leaving separate claims to be tested (V14:028–034).

The following separation is necessary, not merely cautious wording:

This is not a demand that Rudeus wait for certainty before protecting anyone. The warning is specific, plausible enough to matter and cheaply testable relative to the possible harm. The distinction concerns what the test proves and how a protective response treats other people. C003, C009, C010 and C014 should therefore change together without collapsing into a new all-explaining villain claim.

## Help and control before the disclosure

The expedition establishes a second limit on the elder's advice. Rudeus survives and helps Nanahoshi through capabilities distributed among several people. Sylphiette donates health, Roxy manages practical absences, Nanahoshi explains what he had not understood, Cliff helps an unknown stranger and chooses a useful reward, Elinalise organizes action, and Zanoba risks injury. Prior technique and an experimental prosthesis matter in combat. Perugius's intervention is decisive and arises through an enemy's intrusion, not the group's intended request. Total self-reliance would be a poor description of the represented success (V14:010–024).

That network is not inherently benign. Roxy is excluded categorically. Perugius restricts assistance and maintains prejudice after granting a debt-based exception. Moore's useful medicine coexists with a coercive regime. Atofe's reward takes away the very ordinary life for which Rudeus wants strength. Some guards choose service and others regret captivity. Thus the alternative to isolation is not indiscriminate trust or unlimited indebtedness. It is discriminating collaboration with attention to terms, exit, information and cost (V14:002/012/017–025).

Nanahoshi's gratitude provides a particularly useful contrast. She fears she cannot repay before leaving; Rudeus proposes smaller, possible future help without making residence or romance the price. He understands her wish better by imagining his own family lost to him, yet does not require her to adopt his destination. This is a local expansion of practical empathy, with counterevidence nearby: he passes trapped guards' appeals, uses potentially lethal force and benefits from Kishirika's instrumental use during escape. C002, C007, C011–013 must retain both the gain and its limits (V14:013/019–023).

## Protecting a family that still has a voice

V12 established a household through particular choices amid insecurity; V13 made the limits of marital permission explicit. V14 adds urgency, not a waiver. Before the expedition, Rudeus combines real childcare/recovery needs with his own fear of seeing wives die. Roxy's spoken agreement is represented. Some motives assigned to Sylphiette remain his speculation. After the visitor, both wives offer support and ask for openness, while he conceals the warning and locks rooms from outside. Those are actual competing actions and preferences, even when the precaution has an intelligible purpose (V14:015/033–034).

The elder's advice can correct one mistaken belief—Eris's departure as simple rejection—without authorizing another assumption about her or the wives. His testimony and prior independent interludes support revisiting that belief. They do not complete the conversation Rudeus has not had. The letter is begun; its wording, sending and reception are unshown. Eris's own interlude gives her a purpose and a costly intention to protect him, but not knowledge of his present family. The cumulative analysis must test eventual exchanges directly, not count the elder's confidence as successful agreement (V14:031/034–036).

The six canonical ledgers now separate present chronology from disclosed history and reported future, preserve affected-person speech, and track the specific corroboration. Eight existing model/index pairs receive bounded revisions. Rudeus's new selector describes a young recipient of testimony; it does not give him the elder's memories, powers or settled moral outlook. Eris's actual new rank belongs to her independent state. Five other packages remain unchanged after review.
