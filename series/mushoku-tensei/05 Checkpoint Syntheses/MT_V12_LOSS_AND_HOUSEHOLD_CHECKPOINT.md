## What the new evidence changes

The rescue resolves a location problem without resolving its human costs. Competent preparation, mutual protection and successful tactics coexist with Paul's rash first attack, the later plan's uncertain alternatives and Rudeus's inability to interpret the final movement. Loss cannot be assigned to a single character's self-blame merely because that character narrates it. Conversely, protective sacrifice does not rewrite Paul's earlier assault, coercive separation or renewed demand that his son risk death. C001, C005, C010, C014 and C015 require these simultaneous distinctions (V12:011–017/028/034).

V11 differentiated knowing a fact from understanding a person. V12 sharpens that problem when Zenith cannot supply an ordinary conversational account. Her initial silence does not warrant a claim that personhood is gone. Learning routines, selecting armor and preferring a room challenge Rudeus's totalizing description without proving recovered memory or a hidden explanation. The medical consultation establishes a local limit of knowledge and a care plan, not universal incurability. The ledger must leave cause, subjective experience and prognosis open (V12:018/032; C003/C007/C009/C012).

Roxy's first-person survival prevents her rescue from becoming an account of passive waiting. Teaching and reconciliation are already her purposes before her pupil appears. Her later care cannot be reduced to pure selflessness, exclusive opportunism or a necessary sexual cure. Her offer, his distress and acknowledged roughness, the false experience claim, late marriage knowledge, listening, and refused temporary arrangement are different evidence. The text itself distinguishes temporary relief from solving grief. A model that erases either help or cost fails its own source (V12:007–010/019–024; C002/C007/C008/C012/C014).

## The household's agreement and its unfinished terms

Sylphiette explicitly exercises a decision that Norn cannot make for her. She welcomes Roxy and insists on equal standing, while her private account reveals fear of displacement. Neither cancels the other. Rudeus's reciprocal double standard and the contradicted pregnancy premise constrain the apparent symmetry of the settlement. Roxy's requirement to ask Sylphiette first and her earlier refusal show boundaries within desire. Agreement to the new marriage does not give universal bodily access; Sylphiette's later pain limit supplies a direct countercase (V12:023–024/029–032; C002/C007/C013/C014).

Norn can perceive Aisha's constrained happiness and still overstep in claiming to speak for Sylphiette. She objects to betrayal, then preserves affection and asks for lessons without retracting all disagreement. Aisha can perform impressive hospitality, need room to embrace her mother and criticize her brother. These ordinary family distinctions prevent competence, gratitude or love from becoming a single hierarchy of maturity. Lilia's care is chosen and skilled, yet service rank, discipline and unequal labor remain visible. Rudeus's refusal to permit objection to the care arrangement is an actual constraint, not dissolved by a warm shared-home image (V12:022/026–027/029–033; C005/C007/C012/C013).

Birth and grave visit extend responsibility rather than certify completion. Lilia's experience and Aisha's assistance contrast with Rudeus's loss of composure despite magical power. Lucy does not replace Paul, and a pledge to resist hair-based prejudice does not eliminate the social pressure producing relief at her hair color. Rudeus's final self-recognition identifies an ongoing task. The imagined father at the grave supplies no recovered last advice or posthumous approval (V12:033–034).
