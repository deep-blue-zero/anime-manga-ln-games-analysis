# V01–V15: capability, shared judgment and incomplete repair

The first fifteen volumes repeatedly separate what a person can do from what that person can recognize, feel and permit others to decide. Early magical learning already depended on books, time and a teacher; later research and combat make the composition of competence increasingly explicit. The latest five volumes add a sharper difficulty. A person can recognize another's reasons and still refuse those reasons a part in choosing the action. Rudeus understands Nanahoshi's debt before pressuring her, knows Eris was misread before misreading her again, and wants to protect his wives while denying them information they have asked to share. His development is visible in actual corrections, but those corrections often originate with other people.

This does not make the work's hopeful outcomes empty. Norn can return to study, Roxy can build a profession and a household place, Sara and Rudeus can repair an injury without restarting a romance, and Eris can choose a family through reasons of her own. Those results become more intelligible when repair has several forms and participants. The cumulative claim is that capability and attachment require practices of shared judgment if they are to become dependable care. The corpus depicts some such practices and repeatedly shows their fragility under shame, urgency, hierarchy and fear.

## 1. Capability is distributed, and its moral use remains a separate question

The early teaching and travel findings survive. Roxy helps Rudeus cross an enclosure he could not cross by magical output alone; teaching Eris requires adjustment rather than simple possession of knowledge; Ruijerd and practical intermediaries supply kinds of safety the child lacks. Later the university exposes differences among strength, mana, fine control and method. Research succeeds through contributions that cannot be assigned to one inventor merely because one narrator describes them (V01:005–008; V02:006–011; V03:005/019–020; V08:013–016; V10:004–007/021).

V11–V15 both repeat and deepen this distinction. The rescue decision depends on family deliberation and available routes; labyrinth competence requires listening to specialists and revising an uncertain procedure. Paul's death and Zenith's continuing condition make successful extraction an incomplete measure of rescue. V13's devices retain experimental limits, while ordinary fatherhood depends on other caregivers. V14's medicine is found through Cliff's unguaranteed charity and its escape through another power's intervention. V15's armor gives the most explicit assembled capacity: systems, construction, materials, supervision, practice and labor. It has real effects and still loses (V11:016/020/022–024; V12:006/010–018; V13:011/013; V14:017/020–023; V15:014–015/019–022).

The stronger merit reading is therefore bounded, not discarded. Practice changes what people can do. Eris's adaptations against Nina and Orsted are specific, learned and consequential. The failure lies in making competence a sufficient title to authority over others or a promise that valued people cannot be lost. The diary makes that limit unusually stark: power can accumulate through a life increasingly unable to sustain relation. Present Rudeus's answer is partly technical preparation and partly a living network. Neither part alone secures the result (V14:035–036; V15:005–007/021–023).

## 2. Shared information and shared judgment are not identical

Earlier checkpoints distinguished absent, concealed, misinterpreted and acknowledged-but-unacted information. That taxonomy remains necessary. Norn's independent account changes the reader's understanding before Rudeus learns its full contents. He can nevertheless stop trying to produce an instant cure and remain with her. The result does not require that he possess her entire interiority. By contrast, later discoveries of her council work show that permission from him can follow work already accomplished, rather than creating the competence being recognized (V11:007–011; V13:032).

V15 supplies a harder case than mere ignorance. Nanahoshi receives the diary and the danger in fuller form than the wives initially do. She reasons critically and proposes a different course. Rudeus recognizes why she would resist betraying Orsted, then applies pressure anyway. The problem is not solved by adding facts, because some relevant reasons are already available and consciously subordinated. Roxy's refusal of selective confidence gives a contrasting case: she requires Sylphiette's inclusion, and Rudeus changes the actual conversation. The difference is whether the other's judgment can alter the procedure (V15:011–012/018).

This distinction narrows any account in which confession is automatically maturation. Confession can be selective, delayed or strategically placed. It also narrows a wholly cynical account: actual inclusion and specified employment terms are observable changes. Rudeus's later request for family time and pay gives more determinate obligations than an open-ended offer of his life. The source leaves their future implementation untested, while showing that he can now ask. That is a completed act with an incomplete practical horizon (V15:029–032).

## 3. Independent purposes keep relationships from becoming a reward schedule

The V01–V10 record already gives strong counterevidence to treating others as attachments acquired through the protagonist's progress. Roxy's family communication, Ruijerd's obligations, Eris's training, Sara's defensive speech, Sylphiette's desire to stand beside Rudeus and Nanahoshi's different home all matter on their own terms. V11 adds Norn's private reflection and religious questioning, Aisha's preferences inside service, and helpers whose refusal to disclose history is not an invitation to invent it (V05:023; V06:024–026; V07:020–025; V09:029–032; V10:021–022; V11:009–012/026/032–034).

Roxy's rescue does not originate her will to live or her professional future. V13 then gives her teaching and recognition a route outside the marital household. Sylphiette's acceptance of Roxy in V12 is a choice with privately represented cost, not proof of effortless tolerance. V15 extends both women's decision-making: they contest a comfort-only role, contribute to preparation and rescue, and make room for Eris through discussions outside Rudeus's supervision. The new interlude also lets Roxy err and Sylphiette feel anger. Independence is stronger when it includes fallibility than when it merely distributes ideal virtues among supporting figures (V12:007/009/030; V13:002/017/023–024; V15:008–009/014/020–022/033–035).

Eris's place is particularly instructive. Her achieved combat purpose leaves an ordinary-life problem; usefulness in battle has not supplied a secure everyday identity. The household begins to recognize her strength without proving that its roles are fixed. Norn can learn from her and welcome her while retaining an objection to plural marriage. Aisha can carry out learned service and also insist on a personal relation her brother cannot replace with another provider. These are not complete exits from hierarchy, but they prevent hierarchy from exhausting what the characters do (V15:017–018/025–027/033–036).

## 4. Repair has different endpoints and different costs

The corpus does not use one reliable sign of completion. Bodily recovery, apology, safe return, a new role, mutual recognition and an ongoing treatment solve different problems. V09's intimate recovery cannot settle every boundary. V12's return with Zenith cannot undo Paul's death or restore ordinary conversation with her. Lucy's birth and the graveside commitment provide new obligations rather than compensation that balances the loss. V13's Sara meeting can repair understanding while ending the possibility of resumed romance. These are material accomplishments precisely because their boundaries remain visible (V09:025–029; V12:016–018/025/033–034; V13:025–027).

V14 and V15 make future-oriented rescue similarly plural. The tea allows Nanahoshi to continue, not to become safely native to the world. The rat corroborates a specific warning, not all the elder's history. Eris's marriage and Rudeus's restored arms are achieved, but the origin story remains undisclosed to two wives, the guardian unsummoned and ordinary service untested. Zenith's final smile has emotional importance without evidencing recovered speech. A hopeful interpretation loses precision if it treats these remaining obligations as already discharged; a critical interpretation loses precision if it calls all local repair illusory (V14:023/029–034; V15:024–032/036).

## 5. Form makes unequal knowledge available for judgment

First-person narration repeatedly invites participation in Rudeus's fear, shame and relief while exposing gaps in the account. The work does not solve those gaps by making every outside view infallible. Norn's account changes the brother-centered explanation; Roxy's earlier fear outlasts a corrected proposition; Sylphiette's perspective supplies the cost behind consent; Eris's interludes give purposes the recipient of her words does not hear. V15 then makes the narrator read an alternative narrator who is also himself. The diary is materially partial and interpretively compromised, but cannot simply be dismissed when present evidence has made its warning credible (V10:022/034; V11:009–010; V12:030; V14:029–036; V15:001–007).

Chronological rearrangement does specific work. A rescue rewind reveals action already taken by others. The last interlude supplies Eris's reasons for a choice whose outcome the reader already knows. Those scenes correct a possible misunderstanding without converting revelation into a new event at the time it is narrated. The source/knowledge ledger therefore remains essential to literary explanation, not merely to continuity checking. A character cannot use an explanation that only the reader has yet received (V15:020/033–036).

Comedy, visual emphasis and paratext require equally precise treatment. Comic outcomes can preserve a real refusal or ignored preference; pictures can foreground affection or independent agreement without adding an unshown action. The V15 bilingual maxim does not justify harmonizing distinct formulations into a single authorial endorsement of compliance. The strongest bounded aesthetic achievement here is the repeated revision of an apparently sufficient explanation through another person's account. A limitation under the criterion of affected-person access is that many costs still reach the reader through comedy, limited speech or protagonist-centered interpretation. The analysis can identify both without asserting authorial intent or reception prevalence (V13:015/020/037; V15:028/034–038).
