# V03 — care, calculation, and mutual reliance

## Developments through V02 (spoilers through V02)

Rudeus's second life has brought unusual magical ability, schooling and relationships without resolving his treatment of other people. Roxy helped him leave the family enclosure and later continued her own study and paid teaching. His friendship with Sylphie combined shared learning with attachment and violated boundaries. At age seven, Paul forcibly separated them and sent him to Roa for five years of paid tutoring, alongside sword lessons from Ghislaine. The benefit of the later employment does not establish that force or the no-contact term was necessary; Sylphie's current state remains unknown.

In Roa, Rudeus became tutor to Eris, two years older than him. His plan to stage a kidnapping with Philip's approval became a real abduction and nearly ended in death. Practical literacy, calculation and magic helped the children escape, but Ghislaine saved them when his abilities proved insufficient. Eris's recognition of him did not produce automatic compliance. More durable learning developed through Ghislaine's experience, patient exercises, teacher coordination, rest and adapting unfamiliar tasks to Eris's existing sword skills. Ghislaine, in turn, gained literacy, arithmetic and elementary magic. Gifts and two birthday celebrations made reciprocal care visible, while the household's resources and political ambitions continued to shape their options.

Rudeus's tenth birthday joined recognition and constraint. Eris initiated a celebration and staff gift; Hilda's prior hostility changed into an embrace and marriage pressure. Philip reported that succession custom had taken his sons into his brother's household and proposed using Eris and Rudeus to regain power. Later that night, Rudeus exceeded Eris's limited permission for closeness. She stopped him and left. His reflection recognized her care and his disregard for it, and he apologized when she returned. She forgave him on that occasion and set a five-year boundary. His immediate interpretation of that future as a guaranteed reward left recognition entangled with entitlement. A later comparable test of his new promise had not occurred by V02's close.

The household's future was interrupted by an unexplained regional catastrophe. Several distant observers offered partial or speculative accounts of the abnormal sky. Arumanfi attacked Rudeus on suspicion; Ghislaine's recognized standing and oath stopped him. Expanding light then engulfed the group, and Rudeus held Eris until he lost consciousness. V02 did not disclose their destination. Six months later, Roxy reached the emptied region and refugee camp, where lists distinguished the dead from the missing. Paul's message said Norn was with him and sought Zenith, Lilia and Aisha; it assumed Rudeus could return and assigned him a northern search. Roxy read that message; Rudeus was not shown receiving it. She chose to search for him herself, without evidence of success yet.

The internal “Forest Goddess” extra showed Ghislaine alive after displacement, disoriented and urgently seeking the children. Her intervention in a distant war, combined with misidentification and contingency, saved Vigo and helped decide the conflict. His gratitude became a local cult over the following century, with an altered form of her name. That framing future belongs to V02 itself, but neither Vigo nor the reader was told whether she eventually returned home or found Eris. The [V02 reading](MT_V02_DEEP_READING.md) owns these observations and their limits. Missing people, unreceived messages and competing causal guesses remain unresolved at entry to V03.

## Story synopsis of V03 (spoilers through V03)

Rudeus awakens from the regional catastrophe first into a white dream space, in the body he remembers from his former life. A figure calling himself Hitogami offers advice: trust the man nearby and help him. Rudeus distrusts the figure's manner and claims, but on waking finds that he and Eris have been carried to the far northeastern Demon Continent. Their rescuer, Ruijerd, belongs to the feared Superd people. Rudeus cautiously speaks with him; Eris initially panics at a figure associated with childhood threats, then accepts his friendship. Ruijerd promises to escort both children home.

At a Migurd village, the pendant Roxy gave Rudeus identifies him as her pupil. The gatekeeper turns out to be her father, Rowin, who eagerly asks for news after years without contact. Rudeus learns that Roxy could not use her people's telepathic communication and left the village. Her parents give the travelers money and a sword. Ruijerd also tells his history: as a wartime leader he accepted and imposed Laplace's gifted spears, which he says drove his people into destructive violence; his son broke his weapon, and he later helped overthrow Laplace. He wants to restore his people's reputation. Rudeus accepts that goal alongside the journey home, although his trust combines sympathy with the need for a powerful protector.

Travel teaches the group to combine Rudeus's magic, Eris's swordsmanship and Ruijerd's experience. In Rikarisu, Rudeus disguises Ruijerd and constructs a story that lets them use the frightening name Dead End while being taken for impostors. They register as novice adventurers. Their combat power does not exempt them from low guild rank, and ordinary jobs pay poorly for a party of three. Rudeus worries about money and maintaining the performance. Eris enjoys parts of the adventure but privately fears they will never return. When she confides in him, he offers comfort and deliberately refrains from exploiting her vulnerability.

Following another dream instruction, they accept a child's request to recover her missing pet. Ruijerd's tracking leads to animals held by kidnappers who collect rewards by pretending to find them. When one captive kicks Rudeus, Ruijerd kills him. Frightened by the protector's readiness to kill, Rudeus persuades him to refrain, appealing both to reputation and to the children's fear. He then uses threats himself to recruit the surviving pair, Jalil and Veskel, into a scheme: they will complete low-rank jobs in Dead End's name while the trio completes the higher-rank jobs registered to them. It brings money and advancement, but evades the guild's rules. Eris protects Rudeus when Ruijerd angrily challenges the arrangement, expressing a confidence in him that makes admitting uncertainty harder.

The partnership initially prospers. Veskel's competent service produces real goodwill, and Ruijerd begins to recognize good work by people he had dismissed as simply evil. Yet success encourages Rudeus to underestimate risk. On a forest job, the trio follows three inexperienced adventurers whom Ruijerd wants to protect. Rudeus delays rescue to make their eventual intervention seem more impressive. He mistakes the severity of the danger, and Gablin dies before they intervene. Kurt, the survivors' leader, thanks them and accepts responsibility for entering dangerous ground. Ruijerd misreads Rudeus's delay as respect for the younger adventurers' independence. Rudeus knows that was not his motive: another person's death has exposed the consequences of treating rescue as an opportunity to accumulate gratitude.

Back in town, Nokopara discovers the job-swapping scheme and demands payment for silence. Rudeus's evasions eventually fail. Cornered, he contemplates abandoning the others and prepares to devastate the town to escape with Eris. Before he acts, Ruijerd reveals his identity and performs the feared villain, taking the blame and frightening Nokopara into letting them leave. Public fear overwhelms the goodwill they had built; guards assume the children were innocent victims, ignoring Rudeus's attempt to explain his own responsibility. Ruijerd leaves town, and the children follow.

Their reunion changes the terms of the partnership. Ruijerd promises protection without requiring repayment through reputation work. Rudeus nevertheless chooses to continue helping him, partly because persecution recalls his own experience of exclusion, while acknowledging that their situations differ. Ruijerd shaves his hair to reduce recognition. The party adopts regular consultation, and Eris proposes resuming market research, a useful practice Rudeus had neglected. During roughly a year of southward travel, they learn, work, train and manage recurring conflicts. Eris studies the local language and becomes a stronger fighter; Rudeus learns both the limits of his own combat and the usefulness of relying on others. Misconduct and prejudice persist, sometimes contained by companions rather than overcome internally. By the ending, Dead End is an A-rank party arriving at Wenport, with the crossing home still ahead.

The internal extra returns to the moment of the original displacement in the Asuran royal palace. Ariel, a popular princess who has preferred privileged comfort to a dangerous succession struggle, dismisses her protector Derrick's commitment. He overhears an assassination plot, but the immediate attack comes from a monster displaced into the palace, not a demonstrated execution of that plot. Derrick is fatally injured protecting Ariel. She recognizes his loyalty and accepts his request that she seek the throne. A young, unnamed white-haired girl falls from the sky and kills the monster; Derrick understands this as an answered prayer before dying. The extra establishes Ariel's changed intention and another consequence of the catastrophe, while withholding the newcomer's identity and subsequent history.

Sections C–D supply the source map and distinguish events, participant accounts and interpretation.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-001 — A distrusted guide and an old self

**Locator:** `part0006.html` p28–181. **Representation:** dream, interior judgment and speech. **Claims:** `MT-C-003,010`.

The white space returns Rudeus to the bodily identity associated with his former shame. He initially treats his second life's accomplishments as an undeserved dream, while the speaker's unstable appearance makes authority difficult to grasp. Hitogami claims divine status, knowledge and limits on reincarnation, and offers a culprit; none is independently verified here. Rudeus contrasts unearned advice with Roxy's experienced help. He also assumes Buena remains safe, a belief the reader can question from V02.

The strongest charitable account is that helpful advice deserves a limited trial even from an unpleasant messenger. Its useful result does not establish benevolence or every accompanying explanation. Rudeus is free to decide in a narrow sense, but alternatives are constrained by fear, ignorance and survival. **Minimum:** situated reliance and justified uncertainty coexist; neither absolute trust nor a proved deception follows. High for represented access, unresolved for motive and cosmology.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-002 — Recognition across inherited fear

**Locator:** `part0006.html` p217–384, p388–452; `part0008.html` p1–27. **Representation:** recollection, encounter and dialogue. **Claims:** `MT-C-007,009`.

Rudeus tests a feared classification against Ruijerd's manner and practical care. His decision to speak creates knowledge that the warning alone could not. Eris's panic then contradicts his assumption of simple fearlessness. Her apology for an earlier refusal arises in terror; it cannot retroactively make that refusal wrong. Comfort, familiar provocation and introductions let her act again: she recognizes Ruijerd as a friend and offers Rudeus a covering.

One can credit Rudeus's mediation without accepting his self-congratulation about national tolerance or his reduction of Eris to someone he must think for. His retelling of V02's abduction also omits his original scheme. **Minimum:** responsive care expands immediate options, while selective self-presentation and paternalism remain. High.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-003 — Roxy's work travels beyond her

**Locator:** `part0008.html` p136–281; `part0010.html` p157–231. **Representation:** encounter, book-mediated information, parental reports and gifts. **Claims:** `MT-C-001,007,008`.

The village first presents suspicion and a threat of force. Rudeus's language and etiquette partly help, but he mislabels an official and invents a gender explanation for different greetings. Roxy's pendant supplies an unplanned credential. Her father asks for news; the teacher's absent labor has produced both a knowledgeable pupil and an actual link to family.

The guide explains her inability to use Migurd telepathy; her parents acknowledge that village life did not suit her. Their worry does not turn into a demand that she return. Rudeus initially prioritizes his own age question, then recognizes parental concern and wonders about Paul. News from an earlier letter is not her verified present location. **Minimum:** competence depends on inherited work and relationships, and Roxy's departure has a reason beyond serving her pupil. High for interaction; family history remains reported.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-004 — Testimony does not remove a protector's danger

**Locator:** `part0010.html` p18–152. **Representation:** conflict and Ruijerd's remembered account. **Claims:** `MT-C-002,003,009`.

Before the wartime testimony, Ruijerd already threatens extreme violence to retrieve children, and the elder questions how that would restore his name. His history joins victimization with responsibility: he imposed the gifted spears on reluctant followers and accepts responsibility for the reputation that followed. His son's resistance, rather than an uncomplicated heroic will, breaks the sequence in his account.

Rudeus's emotional conviction is meaningful evidence of chosen trust, not independent verification of the spears' mechanism. Practical dependence also motivates agreement. Ruijerd's long failure to meet others of his people does not prove extinction. **Minimum:** sympathy can rationally support collaboration while leaving historical causation and current thresholds for violence open. High for that distinction.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-005 — Teamwork requires limiting one's own strength

**Locator:** `part0010.html` p235–335; `part0012.html` p1–154. **Representation:** practical training, dialogue and illustrated action. **Claims:** `MT-C-001,008`.

Rudeus asks to participate so the children will not be helpless if their protector is incapacitated, adopting a firm first-person stance before returning to politeness. Actual competence still requires supervision. His magic is initially too destructive for the team's purpose and the materials they need; Ruijerd admits limited experience with magical teammates. Eris transfers Ghislaine's instruction into protected live combat, while Rudeus sometimes feels left out.

The enlarged-projectile plate emphasizes disproportion rather than universal mastery. Food and weapon care show expertise outside spectacular fighting; Eris's enjoyment of camp life contradicts his assumption that a noble daughter cannot adapt. **Minimum:** observed role negotiation and practice explain improvement more precisely than either solitary genius or total dependence. High.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-006 — The reputation script excludes its own subject

**Locator:** `part0012.html` p245–434, p439–510. **Representation:** planning, fantasy, conversation and a public-viewpoint shift. **Claims:** `MT-C-009,011`.

Rudeus first imagines manufacturing danger for a heroic rescue, then constructs the layered identity of someone pretending to be the Superd he actually is. The plan uses laughter to replace terror. A brief external view shows how the trio looks to the guild; his returning first person judges the performance successful. That limited immediate change is real.

Yet he withholds the full deception from Ruijerd because he anticipates resistance. A technically truthful statement does not provide informed agreement. Eris's pleasure in a chosen hood also cannot prove she welcomed a formerly coerced household performance. **Alternative:** concealment may be a workable emergency protection. **Minimum:** it gives access while reproducing one person's control over others' interpretation. High; long-term efficacy unproved at this point.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-007 — A guild measures something other than combat power

**Locator:** `part0012.html` p527–713, p718–776; `part0014.html` p5–70. **Representation:** institutional exposition, forms and direct mockery. **Claims:** `MT-C-001,009`.

F-rank work, fees, advancement, restrictions and reporting make power insufficient for earning. Literacy assistance and flexible names also make this institution an access mechanism, not merely an obstacle. Rudeus has Eris write her own contract after explanation. Cards distinguish current bodily ages—ten, twelve and 566—from his private age arithmetic.

He had questioned whether tolerating mockery aimed at his partner demonstrated personal recovery; direct humiliation now revives earlier fear. Eris, conversely, restrains herself in this setting. Nokopara's teasing contains useful advice about tracking. **Minimum:** formal rank, actual skill, social competence and emotional security are distinct variables. High. The explicit ban on job trading becomes consequential later.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-008 — Affection can motivate excessive violence

**Locator:** `part0014.html` p137–144, p169–308. **Representation:** refusal, assault, intervention and repair. **Claims:** `MT-C-002,007`.

Eris yields the castle visit when Rudeus says he is tired, a concrete act of consideration. At the inn, Kurt ignores her avoidance and damages her valued hood. Her defense escalates into severe violence against all three boys and continued attacks after incapacity. Rudeus initially enjoys learning that his gift matters, then intervenes and heals. Ruijerd misclassifies the disparity as an ordinary children's quarrel.

**Value judgment:** Kurt's intrusion does not justify unlimited retaliation or attacking uninvolved companions. **Alternative:** fear and loss help explain escalation, but the later inferred stress is not full access to her mind. **Minimum:** care, learned restraint and serious overreaction coexist across contexts; declaring all development either complete or false is unsupported. High.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-009 — Practical worry and a comparable moment of restraint

**Locator:** `part0014.html` p312–413; `part0016.html` p1–16. **Representation:** financial calculation, dream, disclosure and interior decision. **Claims:** `MT-C-002,010`.

Budgeting makes Rudeus reassess his former parents' expenses and Paul's response to school fees. This is specific recognition of previously invisible labor. It does not eliminate the hunting alternative he himself identifies. Hitogami's new advice arrives when uncertainty makes relief especially attractive.

Eris's question about returning home corrects his assumption that she is simply enjoying an adventure. He comforts her and consciously declines to exploit her vulnerability, providing a real test beyond V02's promise. His restraint remains attached to a temporary “until return” condition; later conduct must decide its reach. He considers the wider disaster, then postpones the thought. **Minimum:** recognition and an actual inhibited action are supported, comprehensive responsibility is not. High.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-010 — Service exceeds performance

**Locator:** `part0016.html` p21–105, p106–209, p664–690, p736–742. **Representation:** client focalization, tracking, completed work and valuation. **Claims:** `MT-C-007,009`.

Meisel's encounter shows courtesy and listening becoming meaningful to the recipient. Ruijerd's unplanned reassurance succeeds beyond Rudeus's script. His tracking requires learned signs, not merely a supernatural eye; the pet's size also defeats Rudeus's assumption that the child exaggerated. The completed job meets a real need, and he later defends the value of her small payment.

The same investigation involves unauthorized entry and preemptive detention despite his admission that a legitimate explanation might exist. Her gratitude includes an exception for a good “demon,” rather than a demonstrated abandonment of group stigma. **Minimum:** real service, partial recognition and coercive means must all remain visible. High.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-011 — A prohibition secured through fear

**Locator:** `part0016.html` p223–361. **Representation:** stereotyped appraisal, killing, fear and argument. **Claims:** `MT-C-002,009`.

Rudeus's selection of a captive relies partly on gendered assumptions drawn from his sister, qualifying his earlier claim to impartiality. Ruijerd kills the restrained man after he kicks Rudeus. The narrator distinguishes fear of this protector's threshold from mere discovery that death exists. He worries that their different standards could eventually threaten him or Eris.

The no-killing argument moves from public reputation to the fact that the children themselves are witnesses who do not want to fear him. Ruijerd accepts that appeal. **Alternative:** a protective code is intelligible in a violent setting; it still does not make every retaliation proportionate. **Minimum:** an effective practical limit is negotiated without establishing shared universal ethics. The killed man's later reported wrongdoing does not retroactively become Ruijerd's reason. High.

### MT-E-LNJP-V03-012 — A lucrative bargain and unequal consent

**Locator:** `part0016.html` p364–660. **Representation:** intimidation, attributed confession, decision lists and dispute. **Claims:** `MT-C-002,009,011`.

Rudeus immediately reuses the fear he has criticized, threatening the surviving captives and exploiting Ruijerd's identity. His explicit risk/return list treats killing, official referral, abandonment and recruitment primarily as management options. The pair's account of Rowman's exploitation is corroborated by two coerced speakers, not an unconditioned investigation.

## E. Character deep readings and state changes

Rudeus's strongest practical development is recognizing that he cannot make a group safe by privately optimizing every outcome. His initial role assembles language, accounting, disguise and magic into useful coordination. It also treats others' reactions as controllable resources. The two central reversals expose different failures: a living person's risk exceeds his estimate, and witnesses do not follow his expected script. Explicit self-criticism is therefore consequential, but it is neither the only cause of change nor proof that the tendency has vanished. His companions intervene, forgive on incomplete information, enforce limits and supply knowledge.

Money anxiety brings a specific reappraisal of parents and paid support; direct mockery still produces fear after the earlier village threshold was crossed. These domains should not be collapsed. Likewise, the old dream body is associated with shame, but the volume supplies no controlled explanation of how bodies determine personality. Rudeus's spoken politeness, rough performances and private judgments vary with audience. At reunion, describing politeness as armor makes the wish for direct disclosure significant even though the disclosure remains incomplete.

Eris is not simply carried through his plan. She adapts to camp life, comforts, refuses, learns, protects him, misplaces confidence, overreacts, and supplies a neglected practical idea. Her private fear becomes available by speech, not comprehensive interior access. The same affection that motivates care can motivate disproportionate retaliation. Her faith in Rudeus protects him from an angry adult while making public uncertainty harder. Her learning is task-specific: embodied sword practice works where it does not for him; language becomes desirable through need. Some household lessons transfer, others need a changed setting.

Ruijerd's past is disclosed rather than newly occurring. In the present he changes practices: accepting limits on killing, reassessing wrongdoers through work, recognizing younger adventurers' self-understanding, sacrificing reputation, altering appearance and consulting a leader whose judgment has failed. His code is neither random aggression nor a complete moral solution. It misclassifies children's violence, excuses Rudeus for the wrong reason and depends on an unstable child/warrior boundary. Nevertheless his willingness to protect without payment and to learn is directly consequential.

Roxy has no new present-tense encounter here. Her guide, gift and parents reveal effects of earlier work and conditions of departure; the volume does not rewrite her V01 intentions or establish her present route. Jalil and Veskel are capable workers who also committed exploitation; their useful service does not repay every prior victim. Kurt's pride and responsibility correct Rudeus's dismissive appraisal, while his later panic shows that gratitude does not erase prejudice. Meisel and the older client have their own practical concerns and evaluations.

Ariel and Derrick require separate local keys and motives. She combines exploitative privilege, fear and newly accepted obligation; he combines genuine loyalty, competence, idealization and an ambitious political program. ## H. Normative-framing findings

Under an attentive-care and preventable-harm criterion, delaying rescue to maximize gratitude is wrong; the represented death supplies unusually direct consequence. Kurt's self-responsibility is a distinct perspective and does not erase Rudeus's opportunity, motive or warning. The failed plan is not morally equivalent to accidentally lacking the skill to save someone.

Under proportionality and bodily-autonomy criteria, Ruijerd's killing of a restrained aggressor and Eris's continued attacks after incapacity exceed immediate protection. Their tenderness remains real. Rudeus's later threats exploit precisely the fear he asks Ruijerd to prevent; no-killing rhetoric alone therefore cannot establish consistent restraint. The contemplated flood is assessed as a decision approaching execution, not as either an accomplished massacre or an inconsequential joke.

Rudeus's nighttime restraint is a genuine improvement in a comparable opportunity. Continued privacy violations later prevent extrapolating it to settled respect. Companions' effective intervention is worth recognizing as a social safeguard; calling it his internally achieved reform would misattribute the work. Eris's affection or reciprocal behavior never supplies retroactive consent to a specific rejected act.

Reputation deserves a separate normative axis from conduct. Persecution is not justified by one member's wrongdoing, and the guards' account is materially false. Conversely, criticizing prejudice does not require erasing Rowman's killing, extortionate tactics or fraudulent work reports. Similarly, Ariel's privilege and exploitation are not cancelled by grief and a new political intention. The volume warrants these scene-level findings; creator intention, audience effects and an overall endorsement verdict remain unestablished.
