# V02 — teaching, recognition, and displacement

## Developments through V01 (spoilers through V01)

Rudeus has begun a second life with memories of his former isolation and death, but with a child's body and dependence on a new household. Books, repeated magical experiments, Roxy's teaching, Paul's sword instruction and the labor of his family have made him unusually capable. His fear of leaving home persisted despite that ability: Roxy took him outside for a graduation exercise, and he subsequently tested the household gate himself. She left to pursue her own training and later reported a court position and further magical advancement in a letter. His gratitude to her coexists with sexual objectification and theft; practical progress has not settled his treatment of other people.

He befriended Sylphie after intervening in bullying and taught her reading and magic. She asked to learn, practiced, set a physical boundary, and remained cautious after he violated that boundary and apologized. Their renewed closeness did not erase that history. His wish to study with her and pay their fees combined care with assumptions about her future. Paul became concerned about their dependence on one another and arranged a tutoring job for Rudeus in Roa, alongside sword instruction from Ghislaine in exchange for literacy and arithmetic. He incapacitated and sent away his seven-year-old son without prior agreement, imposed five years without contact with Sylphie, and faced her attempt to prevent the departure. Neither the success of this plan nor the children's later choices is established at the V01 boundary.

At home, Paul's paternity of both Zenith's and Lilia's expected children had threatened the household. Rudeus used a knowingly false allegation to protect Lilia's immediate place, then admitted the fabrication privately to Zenith. Lilia's account distinguished that recent encounter from earlier forced conduct by Paul. Zenith chose to let her stay, and Norn and Aisha were born. The extra story returns to domestic time before Rudeus's departure, giving Zenith her own account of betrayal and a particular decision to feed Aisha. The household continues through care and work, with injuries, dependency and future obligations unresolved. These inherited events and their qualifications belong to the [V01 reading](MT_V01_DEEP_READING.md), especially its synopsis and Sections C–H.

## Story synopsis of V02 (spoilers through the complete volume)

Seven-year-old Rudeus reaches Roa to teach Eris, the nine-year-old daughter of Philip Boreas Greyrat. The appointment depends on Paul's family connections and promises university funding after five years. Eris rejects a younger teacher and physically overpowers him. Hoping to make her recognize the usefulness of literacy, arithmetic and magic, Rudeus proposes a staged kidnapping with Philip's approval. The arrangement becomes a real abduction when the butler Thomas introduces men serving another noble's interests. Eris is badly hurt. Rudeus heals her and uses his practical skills to escape, but also frightens her and makes assistance conditional on obedience. When they are caught again, his magic nearly gets them clear; Ghislaine's intervention saves them from a lethal attack. Back at the house, Eris allows him to use her name, and he becomes her tutor. Philip reports the betrayal and manages the public account to advertise Ghislaine's protection while concealing Rudeus's politically sensitive position.

Teaching remains difficult. Eris wants magic but avoids reading and arithmetic; the household's violent and humiliating habits compound the problem. Rudeus gets one degrading form of request abolished, yet also violates Eris's boundaries himself. Progress comes through continued work: Ghislaine's accounts of hardship make basic skills useful to Eris, Rudeus prepares exercises, and Ghislaine teaches both children swordsmanship. When the schedule leaves no rest, Rudeus coordinates a regular day off. An outing introduces prices, expensive books and the difference between earning money and ordering a relative to pay. Eris wants to give him something, while he tries to teach her to manage resources. He also sees Perugius's floating fortress and begins reconsidering stories he had treated as remote legend.

Eris's tenth birthday requires a public dance. She struggles and withdraws, but returns to practice; Rudeus helps by connecting the unfamiliar steps to her existing sword training. Their successful, eventually improvised dance gives her pleasure and brings him politically motivated attention. A smaller gathering afterward recognizes the reciprocal bonds among the three learners and teachers: Rudeus gives wands, and Ghislaine gives Eris an inherited ring. Seeing Eris cherish those gifts prompts Rudeus to refrain from an intended sexual intrusion that night. On a later walk through the house, he sees an unexplained sphere in the sky. Sauros regards it as benign without knowing what it is.

During the following years, Rudeus studies history and languages while Eris advances in swordsmanship. His foreign-language work depends on Ghislaine's assistance and a laboriously prepared book Roxy sends him. Roxy's accompanying letter describes her own work and discomfort at a figurine of herself that has reached the court. Ghislaine's separate viewpoint reveals how literacy and elementary magic change her life after exploitation and hunger; she admires Rudeus's patient teaching but also makes fallible assumptions about him and the people around him.

Eris organizes Rudeus's tenth birthday. He secretly discovers the plan and performs surprise and gratitude, only to provoke stronger household reactions than he intended. His parents have been invited but cannot come, according to Eris, because of trouble in the forest and illness at home. Hilda, previously hostile, embraces him as family. He receives a powerful staff. Philip subsequently explains a succession custom under which his sons were taken into his brother's household, and proposes using a marriage between Eris and Rudeus to advance his own ambitions. Rudeus avoids committing to that scheme. Later, when Eris offers limited closeness, he exceeds her stated boundary. She stops him and leaves. His reflection reaches genuine recognition of her care and his disregard for it; he apologizes when she returns. She forgives him on that occasion and sets a five-year boundary. His immediate interpretation of that future as a guaranteed reward leaves the change in his thinking incomplete.

The household's future is then interrupted. Roxy and several distant powerful figures observe an abnormal sky; their interpretations differ, and Perugius orders his servant Arumanfi to act against suspicious people. Roxy also ends her court employment, refusing the prince's coercion and repelling an attack as she leaves. Near Roa, Rudeus is testing his new staff with Eris and Ghislaine when Arumanfi attacks him as a suspected cause of the anomaly. Ghislaine's intervention and oath stop the attack. Moments later, expanding light engulfs the region. Rudeus tries to protect Eris and keeps hold of her until he loses consciousness; their destination is not shown.

The epilogue moves six months forward to Roxy reaching the emptied region and its refugee camp. She learns of widespread displacement and finds lists of the dead and missing. A message from Paul says Norn is with him and asks for help finding Zenith, Lilia and Aisha; he assumes Rudeus can return and assigns him a search elsewhere. Roxy independently chooses to look for Rudeus, planning to join travelers heading toward dangerous regions she can help explore. Her hope does not establish that she has found him.

The internal extra, “Forest Goddess,” follows Ghislaine after displacement into a distant forest. While urgently seeking the children, she encounters a desperate military mission led by Vigo. Her intervention, mistaken identities and the armies' mutual suspicion help decide a war. Vigo later honors his rescuer, and over a century that practice becomes a local cult whose goddess bears an altered version of her name. The story explains how that public legend emerges while expressly withholding whether Ghislaine eventually returns home or finds Eris. The complete unit map and diagnostic evidence follow in Sections C and D.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-001 — An appointment already structured by inequality

**Locator:** Episode 1, `part0006.html` p61–70, p115–158; `part0008.html` p17–85; `part0010.html` p167–203. **Representation:** interaction, speech and interior commentary.

Ghislaine first mistakes a question for mockery, then apologizes and explains transport and shops. The guest's tea and her standing position distinguish household treatment before anyone summarizes rank. Rudeus gains an interview through Paul's kinship, not an open demonstration of superior teaching. He proposes a staged kidnapping to produce a willing pupil; Philip accepts the experiment. His outwardly modest account of Sylphie also conceals a possessive internal account.

The question is whether initiative already constitutes responsible teaching. The strongest favorable reading credits ingenuity and willingness to work; the limiting evidence is that the pupil's reasons have not been heard and her agreement is to be manufactured. **Strong inference, high confidence:** resourcefulness and supported access coexist with manipulation. Ghislaine's literacy gap cannot support a general judgment of incompetence. Links: `MT-C-001,002,007`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-002 — Force fails as a pedagogical script

**Locator:** Episode 1, `part0010.html` p23–113, p128–149, p167–203. **Representation:** reciprocal violence and self-appraisal.

Eris strikes Rudeus; he hits back to teach what pain feels like and finds himself overpowered. He abandons direct force because it will perpetuate resistance, then proceeds with the fear-based plan. His conclusion is strategic, not a stated general commitment to noncoercion. Later he compares Paul's provision of work and shelter favorably with his former eviction, while recognizing that his own willingness to work matters.

A developmental reading can credit a changed appraisal of Paul and recognition that domination will not teach. But the switch to engineered fear does not respect Eris's choice, and Rudeus's counterfactual cannot prove Paul's intervention necessary. **Strong inference, high confidence:** he is revising tactics and his interpretation of paternal support; broader ethical change remains unestablished. Links: `MT-C-002,006`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-003 — The staged lesson becomes actual danger

**Locator:** Episode 2, `part0010.html` p216–400, p404–516, p525–668; `part0012.html` p1–41. **Representation:** plan, observed injury, escape and fear.

The numbered plan gives the reader a script against which the filthy confinement and excessive violence register as departures. Rudeus perceives the danger but continues to use it pedagogically: he temporarily withholds complete healing, invents an imminent threat and makes rescue conditional on obedience. Literacy, calculation and adaptive magic do help both children escape. Nevertheless, recapture and a thrown sword exceed his expectations; Ghislaine saves them.

The strongest defense is that a frightened child with practical advantages must secure cooperation during an emergency. That explains some demands for quiet; it does not make invented threats, deliberately intensified helplessness or the prior scheme necessary. **Value judgment:** those uses of dependency are wrong under a noncoercive-care criterion. **Strong formal inference:** effective skill and loss of control are jointly represented. His fear of death rebuts mastery without cancelling his contribution. Links: `MT-C-001,002,008`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-004 — Recognition and the managed public account

**Locator:** Episode 2/interlude, `part0012.html` p45–105, p115–142. **Representation:** Eris's speech; Philip's report; household interaction.

Eris loudly permits familiar address rather than becoming the subdued child Rudeus expects. The naming concession establishes a changed relationship while preserving her forceful manner. Philip subsequently explains Thomas's betrayal, the alleged patron's evasion of punishment, and the decision to credit Ghislaine publicly while concealing Rudeus's politically inconvenient ancestry. The investigation itself is not shown. Sauros objects to withholding gratitude but delivers that objection by striking Philip.

One reading sees the family's warm recognition superseding calculated politics. Yet Sauros's affection does not abolish household violence, and the public story deliberately selects facts for deterrence. **Strong inference, high confidence:** recognition, employment and publicity follow different rules. Rudeus's generalization about a violent “world” is his inference from a local household. Links: `MT-C-007,009`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-005 — A request shaped for the patriarch's pleasure

**Locator:** Interlude, `part0012.html` p143–269. **Representation:** coercive instruction, comic performance, narrated reform.

Rudeus asks that Eris request teaching herself. Sauros turns that principle into a humiliating, gendered performance he enjoys; Philip further directs it, while the demand would be unacceptable for a boy. The narrator notices Eris's anger and ultimately gets the practice abolished. His reasoning includes fear of her retaliation and only briefly entertains the spectacle's appeal.

The comic excess can expose the adults' hypocrisy, and ending the routine is a real local improvement. An uncomplicated emancipation reading overstates the motive and ignores Eris's cold response afterward. **Strong inference, high confidence:** the scene distinguishes speaking for oneself from performing a request under authority. **Value judgment:** imposed humiliation does not become voluntary because the desired instruction follows. Links: `MT-C-002,007,009`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-006 — Learning to use another teacher's knowledge

**Locator:** Episode 3, `part0012.html` p274–330; `part0014.html` p1–24, p29–133, p137–239, p278–297. **Representation:** ordinary lessons, boundary violation, narrated preparation and Ghislaine's report.

A month later, fear has not secured attendance in literacy and arithmetic. Rudeus violates sleeping Eris's bodily boundary and is struck; that defensive response must be distinguished from her earlier unprovoked aggression. He obtains Ghislaine's help, initially through feigned tears. Her account of logistical dependence and hardship makes arithmetic intelligible to Eris. Rudeus prepares exercises; Ghislaine supplies actionable sword advice and acknowledges what she cannot fix. A near-level rival makes progress perceptible.

Rudeus still claims the kidnapping had to come first. The source supplies no comparison that proves this counterfactual. **Strong inference, high confidence:** teaching now works through distributed expertise, learner interests and repeated practice. Eris's stated ambition also stops him projecting his former passivity onto her. These are genuine improvements in instruction alongside misconduct. Links: `MT-C-001,002,007,008`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-007 — Rest and practical money are part of education

**Locator:** Episode 4, `part0014.html` p312–411, p415–470, p500–557, p581–639, p708–733. **Representation:** staff meeting, outing and dialogue.

Eris judges his pay too low and wants to give him a book; he refuses a gift charged to someone else's resources and proposes an allowance. Neither he nor Ghislaine can immediately explain every use of price knowledge. His admission of that limit leaves learning partly exploratory.

The strongest alternative is efficient behavior management by a tutor who also wants a holiday. Both motives are stated; neither erases the altered conditions. Ghislaine remains responsible for protection during the break. **Strong inference, high confidence:** reduced strain and wider practical options contribute to cooperation; obedience is not a sufficient account of improvement. Links: `MT-C-001,007,008`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-008 — Dance changes the teaching problem

**Locator:** Episode 5, `part0014.html` p850–883; `part0016.html` p1–149, p167–218, p226–234; `part0018.html` p1–19. **Representation:** refusal, observed practice and public performance.

Eris initially declines help, then returns to practice; Rudeus cannot establish why. He identifies a mismatch between her fast sword-trained movement and the requested rhythm, adapts Ghislaine's exercises and helps her perform. Their later improvised dance produces enjoyment even though it is not formally correct. Public praise for Rudeus's surname simultaneously attracts politically motivated attention.

A heroic-tutor account has support in his diagnostic work. It is incomplete because the technique comes from shared training, Eris supplies skill and renewed effort, and Edna supports the breakthrough. **Strong inference, high confidence:** capability depends on the fit between person, task and teaching, not a single talent score. Pleasure and status are different outcomes of the same party. Links: `MT-C-001,007,008`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-009 — Gifts interrupt one harmful intention

**Locator:** Episode 5, `part0018.html` p23–109, p158–184. **Representation:** private celebration, ritual, inward restraint and uncertain observation.

Wands recognize Eris and Ghislaine as learners; Ghislaine responds solemnly and, prompted about birthday gifts, gives Eris an inherited ring. Later, seeing Eris asleep cherishing the gifts interrupts Rudeus's intention to touch her sexually. This is an actual opportunity followed by restraint. The ring's protective meaning is fulfilled figuratively through his response, without establishing supernatural causation. The subsequent unexplained red sphere is declared benign by Sauros because that is preferable to worrying, not because he knows its nature.

The strongest growth reading rightly identifies recognition of cherished relationships. The later birthday scene prevents treating it as a general rule already learned. **Strong inference, high confidence:** one restraint event is real and relationally specific. Neither a protective charm nor an optimistic belief guarantees safety. Links: `MT-C-002,007`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-010 — The history reader and the labor behind fluency

**Locator:** Episode 6, `part0018.html` p208–352, p356–497; background book summary `part0014.html` p687–700. **Representation:** embedded histories, narrator interpretation, letter and learning.

Rudeus reads accounts of wars, enslavement, treaties and divine origins. He identifies a heroic account's euphemism for hostage-taking, making the reader's critical task visible without certifying his whole historical interpretation. Language learning then exposes his own need for mediation: an unfamiliar book remains opaque until Roxy sends a substantial handwritten guide. Her letter also says she failed the same household's interview and that an intrusive likeness of her has reached her workplace.

Talent and retained knowledge matter, but the comparative cases—Ghislaine's help with beast language, an accessible parallel story and Roxy's dictionary—make support causally specific. **Strong inference, high confidence:** apparent individual expansion rests on others' skilled labor. **Unresolved:** the embedded cosmology and broad institutional histories are not independently verified world facts. Links: `MT-C-001,007,009`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-011 — Ghislaine's account expands and limits understanding

**Locator:** Episode 6, `part0018.html` p499–682. **Representation:** explicitly marked first-person focalization.

Ghislaine remembers exclusion, exploitation and hunger despite sword mastery. Patient instruction lets her imagine returning to her community differently; she values rational foundations and proposes a different sword-school teacher for Rudeus if he wants one. She also identifies the household's ethnic fetishizing gaze and revises her earlier failure to understand price research. This is a mind with its own learning history, not a tutor's accessory.

Her account praises Rudeus and partly minimizes his misconduct. She conjectures about Eris's feelings and Roxy's departure without access to their complete reasons. The reader can compare those judgments with scenes she did not narrate. **Strong inference, high confidence:** alternate focalization provides indispensable experience and independent fallibility. It does not install a reliable external moral judge. Links: `MT-C-001,003,007`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-012 — A performed gratitude changes household feeling

**Locator:** Episode 7, `part0018.html` p724–784, p788–907, p909–982, p990–1038; `part0020.html` p1–17. **Representation:** preparation, concealed performance and celebration.

Eris organizes Rudeus's birthday and participates in the food preparation. He discovers the surprise and rehearses a response to avoid disappointing her, but artificial tears provoke reactions he cannot contain. The passage briefly invents a war before explicitly cancelling that comic possibility. His parents' absence is reported as work and illness; Hilda's sudden protection does not establish that they abandoned him. The costly staff is a household gift initiated through Eris's concern for his work.

The performance may be kindly in purpose, but its effects defeat his claim to read the room. His acceptance of an expensive gift also qualifies his earlier refusal of Eris's offer. **Strong inference, high confidence:** genuine care can circulate through mixed motives, imperfect knowledge and class resources. Ghislaine's willingness to model for a figurine serves her own wish to preserve her appearance, which differs from his sexualized intent. Links: `MT-C-001,007,009`.

### MT-E-LNJP-V02-013 — Family protection can also recruit a political asset

**Locator:** Episode 7, `part0020.html` p25–136. **Representation:** Philip's explanation and proposal; Rudeus's appraisal.

Philip reports that his sons were transferred to his brother under the family's succession custom. This newly supplied account explains Hilda's hostility and grief without granting direct access to her mind. He accepts the custom while retaining political ambitions and treats Eris and Rudeus as possible means to regain power. His offer of control over his daughter makes the instrumental logic explicit. Rudeus declines largely because he fears political entanglement.

Affection need not be fake for this strategy to operate. Conversely, providing shelter and opportunity does not neutralize the proposed disposal of another person's choices. **Value judgment:** treating a daughter as inducement violates her agency. **Strong inference, high confidence:** family membership can protect and recruit at once. The explanation is local and reported, not a universal law of nobility. Links: `MT-C-007,009`.
