# V04 — crossed accounts, shared work and the costs of trust

## Developments through V03 (spoilers through V03)

Rudeus's second life has given him unusual magic, useful schooling and important relationships without resolving his treatment of other people. Roxy taught him and helped him leave the family enclosure. Paul later forced him away from Sylphie into a five-year tutoring appointment in Roa. There, patient practice and collaboration with Ghislaine helped Rudeus and Eris learn; a staged kidnapping nearly killed them, and a later violation of Eris's limited permission exposed the difference between valuing another person and respecting a particular boundary. Their household future ended in an unexplained regional displacement. Rudeus and Eris arrived in the northeastern Demon Continent; Ghislaine was separately shown alive, searching for them. V02's later Roxy episode established a devastated home region and Paul's search message, but Rudeus has not been shown receiving it.

The feared Superd warrior Ruijerd rescued the children and promised to escort them home. Rudeus accepted advice from a dream figure calling himself Hitogami while distrusting his claims. Roxy's pendant and guide helped the travelers reach her parents, who supplied news, money and equipment. Her father explained that she had left a village whose telepathic communication she could not share. Ruijerd reported a wartime history of manipulated weapons, collective catastrophe and responsibility, and hoped to restore his people's reputation. Rudeus agreed to help, combining sympathy with his need for a protector.

In Rikarisu, Rudeus disguised Ruijerd and made Dead End a recognizable adventuring party. The guild's novice rank prevented strong combat ability from immediately yielding adequate paid work. A pet-recovery job led to kidnappers; Ruijerd killed a captive who kicked Rudeus. Rudeus secured a promise against further killing, then used threats himself to recruit the survivors into a prohibited job-swapping scheme. Their service could genuinely help clients while their reporting deceived the guild. Eris offered care and support, but also committed excessive violence and expressed a confidence in Rudeus that made admitting doubt difficult. During one vulnerable night, he comforted her and consciously refrained from exploitation, a real but limited change from V02.

Apparent success failed in the forest when Rudeus delayed helping younger adventurers to increase their gratitude. Gablin died. Kurt accepted responsibility for entering danger, and Ruijerd mistakenly credited Rudeus with respect for warrior independence; neither knew his actual motive. Later, Nokopara exposed the job scheme and demanded payment for silence. Rudeus's attempted evasions failed, and he began preparing to devastate the town to escape with Eris. Ruijerd interrupted by revealing his feared identity, threatening the blackmailer and taking the blame. The town expelled him and treated the children as innocent victims despite Rudeus's protest.

At reunion, Ruijerd offered protection without demanding reputation work in return, and Rudeus freely chose to keep helping him. Their trust was real but their knowledge remained unequal: Ruijerd understood readiness to kill the extortionist, not the full contemplated flood. A new consultation routine included Eris and immediately recovered useful market research that Rudeus had neglected. Roughly a year of travel brought paid work, training, language learning and better coordination. It also brought continued prejudice and intrusive behavior, sometimes contained by companions rather than internally abandoned. The trio reached Wenport as an A-rank party; the sea crossing home remained ahead.

V03's palace extra returned to the original displacement. Ariel's protector Derrick died saving her from a monster that the narrator identifies as teleported into the palace. She accepted his dying request to seek the Asuran throne. An unnamed white-haired girl fell from the sky and killed the monster, which Derrick perceived as an answered prayer. Her identity and later history, the ultimate cause of the catastrophe, and many missing people's fates remain unestablished. These events are reader knowledge, not information supplied to Rudeus. The canonical V03 reading owns the observations and their source limits.

## Story synopsis of V04 (spoilers through V04)

At Wenport, Rudeus is eleven and Eris thirteen. Their year of travel has made them more capable, but Ruijerd's Superd identity turns an ordinary sea fare into an enormous official charge. Rudeus refuses to leave him behind. The party considers earning more, entering a dungeon or finding smugglers; Ruijerd's account of traps rules out the dungeon, while Rudeus privately considers selling Eris's treasured staff gift. Another dream brings advice from Hitogami, whose claimed explanation of the previous year's events does not resolve Rudeus's distrust.

Following that advice, Rudeus meets and feeds a hungry stranger who calls herself the demon empress Kishirika. He initially treats her appearance and extraordinary claims as a child's performance, behaving intrusively toward her. Her gift proves real: an eye that shows possible near futures. Pain and confused vision give way to a week of practice. The eye lets him beat Eris in a sparring match but cannot overcome Ruijerd's responsive fighting. Eris's distress reveals how the gift changes the meaning of her own sustained effort. Rudeus then tries to sell his staff without consulting his companions. Ruijerd stops him and explains why preserving an important gift matters to its givers as well as its owner. They instead accept a smuggling arrangement offered by Gallus, a man Rudeus recently helped, with a further rescue job to perform at the destination.

An interlude follows Roxy, Elinalise and Talhand searching for displaced people. Roxy has her own career, friendships, memories and romantic wishes. Misleading reports about Dead End, her fear of Superd, a short timetable, diverted attention and her own mistakes prevent her from recognizing Rudeus's party. She even observes Eris and Ruijerd training without understanding whom she has found. Their near encounter remains a missed meeting, not a reunion known to either side.

Ruijerd crosses covertly while Rudeus nurses seasick Eris. He struggles with intrusive desires but, without his protector's supervision, actually refrains and returns to caring for her. A later exchange pressures Eris over the terms of that care, leaving the earlier restraint meaningful but limited. At Zantport, the approaching three-month rainy season closes travel options. Rudeus leaves Eris at an inn and enters the smugglers' warehouse to recover Ruijerd and carry out Gallus's job.

Finding abducted, injured children changes the operation. Ruijerd kills the captors while Rudeus releases and heals seven children; Rudeus accepts this division of labor and wants no captor escaping to retaliate. He returns alone to free a large white dog that the children identify as their sacred beast. Doldia warriors arriving to recover the beast misunderstand his presence and incapacitate him. Ruijerd meanwhile meets the children's people and helps recover many more captives from a ship. A confrontation with port authorities delays his return. Assuming Rudeus can look after himself, he does not immediately collect his companion from confinement.

Stripped and denied a useful hearing, Rudeus spends a week in the village jail, eventually sharing it with Geese. Their comic hierarchy games develop into practical companionship: Geese gives him a vest and advice. When fire spreads through the village, Rudeus first sees a chance to escape. He nevertheless saves an endangered child and chooses to help the people who detained him. Rain magic and cooperation with the local fighters repel kidnappers. The last pursuer turns out to be Gallus, whose rescue commission concealed a plan to damage rival smugglers, divert the Doldia defenders and take valuable captives himself.

Gallus kills the accompanying warriors and nearly kills Rudeus. The new eye supplies warning without enough understanding or speed to ensure survival. Intervention by the sacred beast and a trick from Geese create the opening for victory. Gallus is captured alive. Rudeus becomes an honored guest; Gyes apologizes for his treatment, while Eris retaliates on his behalf. The rescued community's account of Asuran demand makes Rudeus suspect that the familiar Boreas household may be connected to this trade, but he does not know its servants' origins and conceals the suspicion from Eris.

During the rainy season, the party helps guard the village. Eris defends Ghislaine against her brother Gyes's contemptuous memories, resisting the urge to hit him. She befriends Minitoona and Tersena and teaches them language. Those friendships develop and repair a quarrel partly outside Rudeus's hearing. He practices magic, makes a Ruijerd figure, plays with the sacred beast and continues intrusive behavior that others must stop. When the rain ends, the village equips the travelers for departure. Geese accompanies them toward Millis, revealing cooking skill and a painful account of losing his place in an adventuring party. After a month in the forest and a further mountain passage, they reach human territory, still short of home.

The internal extra follows the displaced young mage now called Fitts at Ariel's court. Recurrent nightmares coexist with learned court service and exceptional magic. Ariel and Luke offer practical care; Ariel also uses Fitts's conspicuous presence to conceal her political preparations. Her teasing exploits a dependent subordinate's difficulty refusing, before a quieter exchange acknowledges their different fears and offers companionship. An assassin interrupts that night. Fitts protects Ariel, kills the attacker and survives poisoning. Recognition of that strength does not end the assassination attempts: an eventual foreign-study escape is announced, while the actual journey lies outside the episode. Fitts's nightmares cease after that night, but the narration does not isolate one cause or establish complete recovery. The name behind Fitts remains undisclosed; the extra's promised future intersection with Rudeus is not yet an accomplished meeting.

## E. Character deep readings and state changes

**Rudeus** now supplies several meaningful countercases to his own earlier methods. He accepts experts' limits, helps an insulting stranger, saves a child without delaying for credit, listens when the staff's relational value is explained and refrains during Eris's illness without outside enforcement. The mechanisms differ: attention to danger, practical uncertainty, gratitude, an explicit promise and another person's testimony cannot be reduced to one conversion. His use of the eye also puts a new gap between sensing and understanding on the page. Learning includes painful feedback and accepting a useful subordinate role in collective action.

Continuing failures are equally specific. He conceals decisions, demands gratitude, cooperates in killings, misrepresents his prior intentions and intrudes where a father must intervene. The sympathetic self-account of being unable to kill needs comparison with the orders he gives and the violence he accepts. Conversely, calling himself effortless or cowardly understates sustained practice and actual intervention. These are not reasons to adopt either the best or worst version of his self-description. His ordinary pleasure in food, architecture, tools, craft, animals and companionship prevents the character model from becoming only an emergency-response or misconduct model.

**Eris** moves among roles: companion, learner, excluded worker, caregiver's dependent, defender of a teacher, peer teacher and friend. The sea illness temporarily reduces her capacity without defining her. Her protest over Ghislaine and her language teaching materially expand what she initiates. In the peer quarrel, keeping some restraint does not eliminate violence, but the friend-specific reason gives more than Rudeus's impression alone. Her friendships repair themselves through actions partly outside his access. These V02–V04 contrasts warrant a first bounded model of learning, particular loyalties, refusal and peer interaction, with several motives still inferred and no general nonviolence claim.

**Ruijerd** is both a rescuer and a fallible classifier. The staff conversation shows perception of relational cost that Rudeus lacks. Willingness to revise the smuggling decision and to ask Gyes about guard work shows practical adjustment. Yet naked confinement becomes tolerable because he calls the child a warrior, and the very trust that once relieved Rudeus can now assign unrealistic responsibility. Gustav's disagreement prevents that judgment from being the volume's unopposed wisdom. His massacre of captors and nonlethal ship arrests also show why protective force must be evaluated in specific circumstances, rather than inferred from a fixed label.

**Roxy** receives independent focalized time. She mediates companions, pursues a search, remembers exclusion and ambition, wants romance, makes errors and pays costs. Her old-party recollections, the ballad's distortion and the missed meeting form a small history of being known incorrectly. The narrative need not make her foolish or faultless to create the near miss. **Geese** likewise combines help, cheating, social skill, weak combat and excellent practical labor. His reported loss of a niche explains a refusal without proving its superstition true. His account remains bounded to what he says and demonstrates; unnamed associates are not imported from memory.

**Fitts, Ariel and Luke** now have a daily relation as well as a crisis role. Luke's ordinary comfort is distinct from his refusal to help resist Ariel's invitation. Ariel both protects and instrumentalizes; her nightmare disclosure is not a proof that the relationship is free of hierarchy. Fitts's service is accompanied by fear of abandonment, persistent learning and formidable defense. The narrative supplies a public alias, not a license to resolve an identity question from later familiarity. The assassin has demonstrated skill and enslavement but almost no interior access; do not invent loyalty or consent to explain the plot.

## G. Focalization, voice and form

The repeated 「すれ違い」 titles organize more than a missed reunion. Each new account supplies a misalignment: Kishirika's appearance against power, Rudeus's received advantage against Eris's effort, Roxy's feared gaze against Ruijerd's curiosity. Later, both captor and rescuer read the dog encounter incorrectly, and the palace nobles reconstruct a false biography from a true story. The reader gets enough access to correct specific errors, without being granted all history or identity.

Lists promise control: three options announced before their content, day-counted imprisonment, weekly/monthly rain summaries and the Seven Great Powers. Their efficacy differs. The time markers make ordinary learning and friendship visible; the ranking preserves historical uncertainty rather than certifying current strength. The long battle expands perception into successive forecasts, mistaken interpretation and narrow timing. Bracketed future visions are information the character must still use, not guarantees supplied by the narrator.

Names also do work. Rudeus claims Ruijerd's name when protecting children, whereas the public's compressed nickname muddles his own publicity plan. The prison senior/junior routine is playful hierarchy interrupted by sincere thanks; it cannot serve as a universal measure of dominance. Palace Fitts is a role around which rumors accumulate. Polite dialogue, rough performance and inward game references differ in purpose; no pronoun automatically distinguishes sincerity from deception.

The illustrations have distinct emphases: the altered eye foregrounds bodily cost; closeness after sparring favors connection; the Merlin composition externalizes Rudeus's struggle; the jail plate enlarges comic performance; Eris's crying protest grants her feeling unusual frontal force; the cooking refusal uses a comic split; the departing cart opens a route. The absence of an interior plate in the Fitts extra is a coverage fact, not evidence that its events matter less.

## H. Normative-framing findings

The volume warrants positive judgments about immediate rescue, actual illness restraint, careful teaching, practical companionship and particular apologies. Each judgment specifies the conduct rather than awarding a redeemed identity. Under an autonomy criterion, the later care pressure, voyeurism, coerced sexualized teasing and exploitation remain wrong even where narration supplies comedy or warmth. Under a proportionality criterion, rescue does not automatically justify every killing or retaliatory blow. The local treaty's existence likewise does not make its officials' conduct just.

Three comparisons are especially diagnostic. First, V02's boundary violation, V03's night comfort and V04's ship care distinguish actual restraint from absence of opportunity and outside prevention; the later village scenes defeat transfer to every circumstance. Second, V03's delayed rescue and V04's immediate fire rescue share reward language but differ at the decisive point of action. Third, Ruijerd's warrior expectations and Ariel's useful-subordinate relation each show that recognition of capability can coexist with unmet care needs, though their powers and intentions differ.

The strongest favorable interpretation is that the work gives practical routes toward more attentive cooperation. The strongest critical qualification is that heroic success and comedy repeatedly return attention to the protagonist's relief while some victims receive little voice: killed captors, dead local warriors, abducted children and the enslaved assassin do not all receive equal interiority. That imbalance is a formal finding, not proof that every act is endorsed. The extra explicitly names dependence during Ariel's teasing and then gives comfort; that juxtaposition can support criticism without requiring the later comfort to be insincere. No creator-intention, reception-effect or whole-series verdict follows.

## I. Themes, world, chronology and knowledge

**Usefulness and belonging** becomes a distinct, supported question. Geese lacks a place when his general skills are redundant; Eris seeks a task when camp roles exclude her; Fitts fears being discarded if inadequate; Ruijerd expects a warrior to manage alone. Helpful recognition can therefore grant access and impose burdens. The evidence supports opening C013, not a claim that all affection is conditional or every skilled person abandoned. Gifts, food and unsolicited aid supply counterexamples to an exclusively transactional world.

**Misrecognition and partial histories** now extend from ethnicity and publicity into remembered family, teaching, institutions and trauma. Gyes's earlier sister does not exhaust Ghislaine's later conduct. The Seven Great Powers and the sacred-beast tradition require speaker attribution. Reported automatic ranking changes and the road's legendary origin are not experimentally verified mechanisms; actual passage is dry and relatively safe. Sound magic and foresight have demonstrated local limits. These findings belong in the existing form/world ledger rather than a premature encyclopedia.

The opening explicitly resolves the previous age uncertainty: Rudeus eleven after a recent birthday, Eris thirteen. Near departure she is soon fourteen; no later birthday is narrated here. Eye practice takes a week, the job wait fifteen days, confinement about a week, the rainy stay three months, forest travel a month and the mountain passage three days. Do not force rounded or overlapping spans into a precise calendar. The Fitts extra covers the catastrophe and roughly the following year, so its publication position is not a claim that every event occurs after the main journey's ending.

Rudeus still has not been shown receiving Paul's missing-family message or Roxy's search knowledge. He remembers an unsent letter and plans future contact. The wider catastrophe, Boreas political weakness and an eventual court escape belong to reader access and selected palace actors, not the travelers' current knowledge. Geese's former party/couple and Fitts's earlier identity remain unresolved by explicit naming. A contradictory or incomplete self-account is retained as such rather than corrected by a remembered future revelation.
