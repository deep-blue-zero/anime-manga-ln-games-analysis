# V15 — defeat, consultation and the terms of continued life

## Developments through V14 (spoilers through V14)

Rudeus begins a second life with memories of a failed first one. Roxy's teaching helps turn unusual magical capacity into practical skill and makes leaving the house possible. Friendship with Sylphiette brings affection and learning alongside an overridden refusal and subsequent apology. The Lilia pregnancy crisis ends with Zenith choosing continued family life; Norn and Aisha grow into unequal household positions. Paul's concern about dependence leads to a forced separation. Rudeus becomes Eris's tutor and learns to adapt through cooperation with Ghislaine and others.

The displacement catastrophe sends Rudeus, Eris and Ruijerd on a long journey home. Their survival depends on practical cooperation and protection beyond magical strength. Rudeus's attempts to control outcomes sometimes fail badly; a manipulated rescue opportunity ends in a child's death. Hitogami offers useful guidance without establishing his ultimate purpose. Reunion with Paul produces a specific reconciliation without automatically restoring Norn's trust. Orsted nearly kills Rudeus, and Nanahoshi's intervention contributes to his survival. Return to the devastated homeland cannot restore its earlier people or relations. Eris leaves to train beside him, but her brief message is understood as rejection. The reader knows more about her aim than Rudeus does.

Northern adventuring brings work and companionship amid distress and bodily difficulty. A failed encounter with Sara, defensive speech and missed opportunities leave a painful rupture. Soldat offers help during an acute crisis. University life later gives Rudeus collaborators with independent aims: Zanoba's craft project, Cliff's work on Elinalise's curse and Nanahoshi's return research use different skills. Increased status also enables coercive behavior. Sylphiette's identity is disclosed through an arranged encounter; renewed affection, bodily recovery and marriage give their relationship a new practical form. Work, dependence, information and permission remain uneven.

Norn and Aisha join the household. Norn's own account explains fears her brother does not fully know; Aisha's skilled work coexists with effort, desired leisure and injury over inherited rank. A rescue appeal and Norn's request lead Rudeus to leave his pregnant wife and travel with Elinalise. Newly disclosed teleportation records shorten the journey. In the labyrinth expedition, Roxy is rescued after prolonged independent survival. Zenith is recovered, but Paul dies protecting Rudeus in the hydra fight, and Rudeus loses his left hand. Zenith's altered communication and unknown condition call for care without a settled diagnosis.

Roxy shares grief, helps Rudeus and expresses her own desire. He acknowledges intimacy outside his marriage; she refuses a temporary arrangement. At home, Sylphiette chooses to welcome her and insists that Norn cannot decide for her. Equal standing is an expressed aim rather than a completed removal of insecurity. Lilia helps care for Zenith and at Lucy's birth. Rudeus becomes a father and renews commitments at Paul's grave.

V13 follows ordinary work: childcare, teaching, research, gifts and lessons. Rudeus receives a collaboratively made prosthetic hand and learns king-level water magic; Sylphiette reaches saint level. Norn has already done substantial council work when her brother learns of it. Aisha's garden knowledge corrects his first appraisal. Zenith's smiles and initiated care matter without proving recovery. Roxy earns professional respect, and an escort assignment enables Rudeus and Sara to repair companionship. Sara explicitly closes romance. Cliff and Elinalise marry. Nanahoshi's experiment summons watermelon instead of cabbage; her continuing illness remains unexplained, and Perugius is proposed as an expert contact.

V14 brings that visit. Perugius's floating castle offers instruction and unequal hospitality: Roxy is barred as a demon, Ariel struggles for political support, and Zanoba gains access through art. Elinalise privately tells Rudeus about an ancient labyrinth rescue, recovered emotion, missing memory and a difficult curse history. Cliff already knew; Sylphiette remains uninformed about that history. The comparison supplies uncertainty about Zenith, not a cure. Nanahoshi's collapse prompts exhausting care from Sylphiette and practical support from Roxy. Hearing Nanahoshi's wish to return to her own family moves Rudeus toward action.

Rudeus, Zanoba, Cliff and Elinalise seek Kishirika on the Demon Continent. Cliff's unsolicited charity finds her, but obtaining medicine exposes the party to Atofe's coerced reward of binding service. Rudeus refuses to abandon his family for strength. Their desperate escape uses learned techniques and mutual protection, causes serious injuries and succeeds when Atofe's soldiers intrude into Perugius's castle. His intervention rescues the party through a motive different from its plan. Tea improves Nanahoshi's condition but must continue. A debt-based figurine concession does not remove Perugius's prejudice. Ariel's royal test remains unanswered.

After returning, Rudeus begins a diary to make resolutions harder to forget. Hitogami then offers an unverified account in which Paul and Roxy would have survived without his intervention, drawing renewed trust before a request to check the cellar. An old visitor claiming to be Rudeus from about fifty years later interrupts him. The visitor describes a rat-borne illness, family deaths, misunderstood Eris relations and failed retaliation against Hitogami. These are reports of another course, not present deaths. He advises consulting Nanahoshi, writing to Eris and distrusting Hitogami without openly opposing him. His incomplete time travel is fatal.

Rudeus hides the encounter from his concerned wives and locks family rooms while investigating. Matching bodily features support the visitor's identity, and a rat with the specified crystalline teeth corroborates a particular warning. Disease mechanism and much of the reported future remain untested. The old diary is unread; an Eris letter is begun without shown contents or delivery. In the independent interlude, Eris defeats Nina through a learned change in technique, gains Sword King and full transmission, and intends to return. No reunion occurs. Nina and Gino continue their own training.

## Present-volume story synopsis — spoilers through V15

Rudeus reads the diary left by his dying older self. It describes a possible life in which Roxy contracts a fatal disease, Cliff dies during an unsuccessful search for a cure, and Rudeus retreats into drink, cruelty and delayed apologies. Sylphiette leaves with Ariel and is killed after the failed struggle for the Asuran throne. The diarist becomes powerful and useful to rulers, but research cannot replace the people he has lost. He misjudges Eris, who eventually dies protecting him; more companions are killed, and his search for revenge on Hitogami consumes decades. The final time-travel experiment is uncertain and desperate. These events belong to the visitor's reported future, not to the household currently alive around the young Rudeus. Reading nevertheless makes the losses feel immediate. He seeks comfort from his wives, recognizes disturbing possibilities in himself and resolves to act before those possibilities become his life.

Sylphiette and Roxy notice his evasions. They ask to participate in protecting the family instead of being confined to comforting him. A discussion of Eris produces conditional willingness to meet her, not permission to decide everyone's feelings in advance. Rudeus then meets Hitogami in a dream. The god admits hostility and claims that Rudeus's descendants will help Orsted kill him. He demands that Rudeus kill Orsted in exchange for leaving the family alone. Rudeus knows the promise cannot be enforced, but fears an enemy who can reach him invisibly more than an enemy he can physically confront. He sends Eris a letter explaining the household and their earlier misunderstanding, offering marriage or friendship depending on her wishes, and adds that he is going to fight Orsted.

Rudeus tells Nanahoshi more than he has told his wives. She questions the diary, reads it herself and develops a tentative account of time travel and the displacement disaster. She urges him to speak to Orsted. Rudeus instead presses her to help lure Orsted into an ambush, invoking the family he wants to save despite understanding her gratitude to Orsted. At the Sword Sanctum, Eris receives the letter. Isolte helps read it, and the friends disagree about what its marriage offer means. The warning about Orsted decides Eris's immediate course: she and Ghislaine leave to reach Rudeus.

Over roughly three months, Rudeus and his friends build a large suit of magical armor and prepare the battlefield. Cliff supplies systems, Zanoba contributes construction, Roxy supervises and arranges a useful contact, and Sylphiette helps with magic and practice. Others provide contacts and training or refuse participation for reasons of their own. Hitogami's technical advice proves useful without making his promises trustworthy. Ordinary family life continues inside the preparation. Elinalise's pregnancy becomes known to Cliff, Roxy announces her own pregnancy to the household, and Aisha's small rice harvest gives Rudeus a meal that recalls his previous world. Before leaving, he tries to confide only in Roxy. She insists that Sylphiette be included. He finally explains the immediate threat and the planned fight to both, although his reincarnation remains undisclosed.

The ambush wounds Orsted and forces a serious response, but Rudeus's preparations cannot overcome him. Orsted destroys the armor, severs an arm and exhausts Rudeus's remaining options. Rudeus begs for his family's lives and then tries a final desperate attack. Eris arrives before he is killed. The narrative retraces her arrival at the house, where Aisha welcomed her and the wives explained what had happened. Eris, Ghislaine, Sylphiette and Roxy have come together. Eris's years of training let her resist and buy time, while the others heal or protect Rudeus and plan a retreat. Their effort still cannot secure victory. Orsted offers to protect the family if Rudeus works for him against Hitogami. The offer follows explicit threats and gives no absolute guarantee, but Rudeus accepts it as a possible way to live. Orsted heals him and gives him a bracelet intended to obstruct Hitogami's access.

Recovery does not immediately solve the reunion with Eris. Rudeus mistakes her anxious silence for rejection and delays speaking to her. When they finally discuss the past, they acknowledge the pain of their separation. Eris proposes a duel whose outcome would decide whether she stays, but Rudeus abandons that form of proof and tells her he loves her. They agree to marry through conversation. Roxy and Sylphiette welcome her; Norn retains her objections to plural marriage while demanding that her brother treat Eris properly. Ghislaine plans to pursue justice for Sauros, and Rudeus proposes connecting her with Ariel, without completing that meeting in this volume. His first intimate night and morning with Eris transform the earlier memory of waking alone, though differences in pace, communication and household roles remain.

Rudeus visits Orsted again, initially concealing the visit from his wives; Eris follows and accompanies him. Orsted explains aspects of his own mission and offers a protective summoning arrangement. Rudeus discloses his reincarnation in Eris's presence. Orsted gives an account of Laplace's history and reincarnation factors, separating those factors from personal identity, and suggests that the infant body might otherwise have been stillborn. Rudeus finds relief in that explanation, while its evidential status remains an inference from Orsted's knowledge. He negotiates time with his family and compensation instead of accepting unlimited service, and hands over the diary, which Orsted initially skims before allowing time for a full reading. Outside, Eris asks whether he is still the Rudeus she knows. Her acceptance helps him affirm continuity, but he still intends to tell Roxy and Sylphiette himself at a future opportunity.

The closing interlude returns to the days before Eris's proposal. Her quietness comes into view as insecurity: she can fight, but cannot see where she belongs beside Sylphiette's domestic competence and Roxy's profession. An unsuccessful attempt to find work exposes a misunderstanding between her and Roxy. At the household entrance, Eris intervenes when a pupil tries to pressure Norn through claims about his father's influence. Sylphiette and Roxy were also ready to help; they recognize a place for Eris's particular strength without being reduced to helpless beneficiaries. A quiet encounter with Zenith helps Eris decide she wants to remain. Zenith's smile and Eris's imagined response do not establish restored speech. By ending with Eris's reasons for joining the household, the volume gives her decision an account separate from Rudeus's relief at being loved.

## D. Diagnostic close readings

### MT-E-LNJP-V15-001 — A diary changes time without changing the current household into its contents

**Source:** spine8 `text/part0007.html` p6–14; spine10 `text/part0009.html` p2–25; spine12 `text/part0011.html` p24–45.

Rudeus has written but not sent the letter at the opening. Reading begins among ordinary happiness, then loses reliable dates and moves through changed paper and omitted family members. These material and narratorial signs qualify the diary's apparent continuity. It is a selected account by a later self, now received by an earlier one. The embedded voice can supply testimony about a possible course of events without becoming direct narration of the present timeline. Current Rudeus's recognition that its ugliness is possible for him matters more than a categorical distinction between an innocent young self and an unrelated monster. C003/C009/C014; do not code an omission as a death or a reported ability as a present skill.

### MT-E-LNJP-V15-002 — A rescue mission fails despite work, danger and useful allies

**Source:** spine10 `text/part0009.html` p28–78.

The diarist reports Roxy's disease, Cliff's diagnosis and a mission with Cliff and Zanoba to obtain a restricted cure. Copying time, institutional restriction, escape and poisoned injury all matter; determination and technical ability do not remove delay. Cliff dies, Rudeus kills a human in this reported branch, and Roxy is already dead when the survivors return. Elinalise's later departure and pregnancy likewise belong to the diary. The pattern warns against equating effective effort with guaranteed rescue, but cannot independently certify disease science or every remembered cause. C001/C010/C012/C013/C015.

### MT-E-LNJP-V15-003 — Grief does not erase the diarist's choices toward the living

**Source:** spine10 `text/part0009.html` p80–184.

The diary moves from isolation and rejected comfort to sexual betrayal, delayed apology and a delayed search for Sylphiette. Fear of mistreating her does not prevent another injury. Its later account mixes encountered bodies and violence with guides, rumors, imprisonment and accounts of a failed coup. A single malicious enemy is not a sufficient explanation for every intervening choice or political event. The current reader can reasonably fear this future without adopting the diarist's self-justification. Sexual harm is summarized without reproducing graphic content. C002/C005/C009/C014; Sylphiette's reported death must not replace her present agency.

### MT-E-LNJP-V15-004 — Living wives answer a fear whose source they have not been told

**Source:** spine10 `text/part0009.html` p187–247; spine12 `text/part0011.html` p1–19.

Yet Rudeus conceals the cause of his distress, and a promise of affection does not authorize the future actions he fears. ### MT-E-LNJP-V15-005 — Research continues where relation fails

These do not erase service hierarchy or sexualized joking, but supply actions distinguishable from a grand vow. C001/C008/C011/C014; technical accumulation and relational recovery are different achievements.

### MT-E-LNJP-V15-006 — The diary's Eris is first a suspect and only later legible as a protector

### MT-E-LNJP-V15-008 — Sylphiette's viewpoint turns protection into a disputed household procedure

## E. Character deep readings and state changes

## F. Relationships, agency, and institutions

The wives object to precisely that division. Nanahoshi receives more information, yet her contrary recommendation is pressed aside. The household's admission of Eris involves several decisions, not a single grant of permission by its male center. ## G. Focalization, voice, and form

The diary is more than exposition. It lets the first-person narrator read another first-person version of himself, recognize continuity and resist particular explanations. Dates disappear, details are omitted, capabilities are summarized without methods and hostility contaminates attribution. A present scene with living wives interrupts the apparent authority of the dead future. The alternative life has emotional force precisely because it is neither an externally verified chronicle nor an arbitrary fantasy the reader can dismiss. The text provides reasons to take it seriously and reasons to separate its propositions.

Viewpoint changes then test the habits that reading alone cannot correct. Sylphiette narrates affection as something that can conceal a problem; Eris's letter scene supplies an audience beyond the writer's intention; external access briefly follows Orsted into the ambush. The rescue chapter rewinds to disclose a coalition formed outside Rudeus's knowledge. The final interlude returns further within the recent sequence to explain Eris's earlier silence and decision. Source order is therefore not story chronology, and a knowledge ledger must not give an earlier character information first supplied to the reader later.

The book also qualifies its competent observers. Nanahoshi labels hypotheses; Rudeus recognizes some post hoc reasoning; Roxy misreads willingness to teach; Eris misreads a gesture; companions' confidence about Orsted's motives is affected by the curse. These are different mechanisms, not a general rule that every statement is equally unreliable. Particular acts remain available for judgment: a person can be healed, a letter sent, an arm severed, a question asked and a refusal overridden even while a cosmic explanation stays uncertain.

Comedy repeatedly coexists with an affected person's limit. The rice meal shifts attention from Roxy, the school trial makes incompetence appear where miscommunication matters, and sexual gender reversal makes force humorous without making pacing irrelevant. The comparison between V06's empty morning and V15's visible training is a formal revision with emotional specificity. It signifies presence and the possibility of future conversation, not completion of every conversation. The epigraph's bilingual divergence and the illustrated women's handclasp widen the question of what a defeat or a happy ending includes.

## H. Normative-framing findings

The diary's violence and abandonment require neither graphic repetition nor a claim that loss mechanically causes abuse. Grief contextualizes conduct; the account itself records choices, delays and self-justifications. Present Rudeus's recognition that he could become that person is morally relevant as self-implication, but it cannot be used as a substitute for evaluating what he now does to others.

Pressure on Nanahoshi is the clearest current counterexample to a smooth maturation account. Rudeus understands her gratitude, still invokes his family's survival to recruit her into a betrayal she opposes, and feels successful afterward. His fear is credible and the power imbalance with Hitogami extreme. Those facts explain the narrowing of options without making the friend's reluctance disappear. Conversely, abandoning the hostage idea is a genuine boundary within this plan even though its motives are mixed. An analysis that credits only pure motives would miss change; one that treats any abandoned worse option as full repair would overstate it.

The wives' requested participation, Roxy's touch limits, Eris's conditional proposal and Norn's maintained objection all require event-specific treatment. Prior consent to marriage does not retroactively authorize groping. Rudeus's assent during intimacy should be retained alongside the ignored preference about pace. Eris's threat at the household entrance is explicitly a bluff, but intimidation and unequal force remain represented means; identifying the bluff narrows intent rather than declaring the scene morally empty. These findings distinguish source framing, affected-person response and analyst judgment.

The initial Orsted arrangement is coercive; later negotiation improves its terms. Protection and healing are concrete benefits, while threats and lack of alternatives remain part of its origin. The useful current question is whether the specified safeguards become usable practices, not whether a new patron must immediately be classified as wholly good or wholly deceptive. Zenith's ambiguous expression deserves similar restraint: emotional meaning for Eris does not authorize making a nonconversing person's consent or recovery explicit on her behalf.

## I. Themes, world, and chronology
