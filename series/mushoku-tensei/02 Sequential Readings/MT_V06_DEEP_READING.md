# V06 — returning without a shared account

## Developments through V05 (spoilers through V05)

Rudeus's second life has produced exceptional magical skill and important relationships without resolving his treatment of other people. Roxy taught him, helped him cross the boundary of the family enclosure and left to develop her own abilities. He befriended and taught Sylphie, violated a stated boundary and later apologized. Paul's coercive separation placed him in Roa as Eris's tutor. Their education depended on practice, adaptation, other adults' work and Ghislaine's instruction. Rudeus and Eris developed reciprocal affection alongside aggression, unequal power and a serious violation of limited permission. An unexplained regional displacement destroyed their anticipated household future and stranded them on the Demon Continent.

Before that separation, Paul's infidelity and Lilia's pregnancy had created a household crisis. Rudeus used a selective account to argue against Lilia's expulsion. Zenith chose to keep her and later cared for both infant daughters, Norn and Aisha. Lilia's gratitude coexisted with employment dependence and her disclosed history of an earlier assault by Paul. The family's continued life was an actual arrangement, not proof that its injuries had disappeared.

Ruijerd rescued the displaced children and offered to escort them home. His account of a wartime catastrophe explained the Superd people's feared reputation, which Rudeus promised to help improve. Roxy's guide, pendant and parents provided further help. The three formed Dead End. Paid work and cooperation developed beside concealment and attempts to manage other people's responses. Rudeus delayed rescuing younger adventurers to increase gratitude, and Gablin died. Later exposure of a prohibited job-swapping scheme led to extortion. Feeling trapped, Rudeus prepared to devastate the town to escape with Eris. Ruijerd intervened, exposed his identity and took the blame. Their renewed partnership did not include complete disclosure of that contemplated violence.

Travel brought training, language learning, practical consultation and unevenly shared responsibilities. Hitogami's advice had useful results despite Rudeus's doubts about the dream figure's identity and motives. A later instruction led him to feed Kishirika and receive an eye that could see possible near futures. The gift required calibration and could not replace bodily skill or sound interpretation. At Wenport, a prohibitive Superd fare blocked the sea crossing. Ruijerd stopped Rudeus from secretly selling Eris's staff gift, and they instead accepted Gallus's smuggling arrangement. Roxy, searching with Elinalise and Talhand, passed close by; rumor, fear and mistakes prevented a meeting.

Rudeus cared for seasick Eris without exploiting one unpoliced opportunity, then put pressure on a later care exchange. At Zantport, he and Ruijerd discovered abducted children in the smugglers' warehouse. Ruijerd killed captors with his agreement while he freed and healed children. Returning for their sacred beast led to wrongful imprisonment by rescuers who misunderstood his actions. In jail he met Geese. During an attack on the beastfolk village, Rudeus abandoned escape to help a child and then the community. Gallus proved to be organizing the attack; Rudeus survived the fight through Geese's and the sacred beast's help. Gyes apologized. A three-month rainy season allowed guard work, training and friendships. Eris developed independent peer ties, taught language, defended Ghislaine without striking Gyes and partly moderated a quarrel. Rudeus's appreciation of her growth coexisted with continued intrusion needing outside restraint.

The party reached Milishion, where Geese left temporarily. Rudeus planned letters home but interrupted an apparent abduction. The people he attacked were members of Paul's group rescuing displaced people from enslavement. Father and son met with different information: Paul assumed his capable son knew the whole Fittoa region had disappeared and had neglected the search; Rudeus did not know the scale of the disaster or that guild notices awaited him. Their reunion became an argument and fight. Paul struck first, Rudeus retaliated excessively, and Norn defended the father who had cared for her. Only afterward did Rudeus receive the full missing-family account.

Paul's retrospective account showed sustained care for Norn, collective rescue work, dependence on others' money and labor, and deterioration into drinking and neglected duties as useful news dwindled. It also confirmed his earlier assault of Lilia and recalled a particular correction of his parenting by Zenith. These histories did not cancel each other. Geese challenged Paul's assumptions about the journey and a talented child's needs. Rudeus, meanwhile, could formulate an apology but could not initially return. Eris's awkward embrace helped him; Ruijerd prevented her attempted revenge. The next meeting remained stiff until an innkeeper drew attention to Paul's distress. Rudeus recognized an analogy with a friendship he had destroyed in his former life, deliberately began the reunion again, and asked for an embrace. Both men cried and renewed their relationship.

Reconciliation supplied new choices and information. Paul reported Zenith and Lilia's negotiated domestic arrangements and Sylphie's continued education, including silent healing Rudeus could not perform. Her present location remained unknown. Rudeus expressed a stronger intention to respect Eris's refusal and chose to finish escorting her home while increasing attention to the displaced. Paul supplied money and advice. Norn still rejected her brother, Eris still resented Paul, and Rudeus continued to misread Vera and Shela despite the reader's access to their histories and protective work. Eris had seen the refugee notices but assumed Rudeus knew already. Admiration itself had helped prevent communication.

Eris's independent goblin hunt revealed an adventure wish beyond the efficient return journey. Cliff's magical talent proved poorly coordinated in practice; her own pride also led them into danger. Using Ghislaine's and Ruijerd's lessons, she found people in the forest and helped a knight defend a threatened child. Her first human killings involved real courage, assistance and inward fear concealed by outward composure. The rescued knight, Therese, later helped the party when a customs official rejected Ruijerd's letter and refused a demon's passage. Therese was also Zenith's sister. Her authority, gratitude and kinship secured passage and supplies, while her stated demon prejudice remained. Particular help did not reform the general rule.

Roxy returned to her parents but still could not share their telepathy. Tears and an embrace made their care accessible through another route, and she stayed three days. She then accepted evidence that she had missed Rudeus, revisited old companions and chose to continue searching for the still-missing family. Her praise of an effortless pupil understated the help his journey had required. In Eastport, Rudeus's nostalgic appetite prompted an excessive attack on a struggling cook. His companions removed him and he regretted the outburst; a separate viewpoint showed the cook closing his inherited business and accepting military recruitment. A repaired family relationship had not prevented misconduct elsewhere.

The palace extras have followed the displaced protector called Fitts, Ariel and Luke through service, fear of abandonment, political danger and flight. Fitts's original identity remains unestablished. V05's final inquiry supplies testimony about border assistance and an attack in which guards die supporting Fitts's defense. The investigator Gustav wrongly reports Ariel dead. When later testimony exposes the error, he knowingly leaves it uncorrected. The final framing confirms the report is false and announces later consequences without supplying them. By the V05 boundary, Dead End has reached the Central Continent, the family search remains unresolved, and information is still dispersed among people who cannot be treated as sharing it.

## Story synopsis of V06 (spoilers)

Rudeus, Eris and Ruijerd continue across the Central Continent toward Asura. The journey is now more manageable, but route choices still depend on terrain, time, travel skills and Eris's aversion to another sea crossing. Hitogami appears again in a dream. Rudeus asks directly about his missing family and receives a vision of Aisha pursued by soldiers in Shirone. Hitogami tells him to conceal his identity, hear Aisha's story and then write to an acquaintance at the royal court. Rudeus withholds the source of this information from his companions, partly because he fears drawing Ruijerd into an unknown scheme. He nevertheless asks for their cooperation. Ordinary travel continues around the secret: he experiments with cooking rice, finds that nostalgia exceeds his culinary skill, and learns that his companions do not share all his tastes.

In Shirone, Rudeus assumes Roxy still works at court and writes to her before finding Aisha. When he encounters the predicted pursuit, his first reading of the situation is incomplete. Aisha appeals for help, and he intervenes, discovering that the soldiers can counter his spells. A risky escape injures him, but he gets his sister to safety. Aisha explains that she and Lilia have been confined for years and prevented from contacting Paul. She also criticizes the brother whom her mother has praised as perfect. Rudeus, ashamed of the conduct behind that criticism, continues using an alias and hopes that rescuing Lilia will earn her admiration.

Ginger, a royal guard, arrives with what appears to be an invitation from Roxy. It is a trap. Roxy has left Shirone, and Prince Pax wants to use her pupil as bait. An anti-magic barrier leaves Rudeus unable to escape through his usual strength. Lilia is also threatened, while Ginger and other soldiers have been coerced through captive relatives. The eventual rescue combines several initiatives that Rudeus neither planned nor initially understands. Ruijerd frees the soldiers' families. Ginger brings Rudeus's figurine to Prince Zanoba, whose enthusiasm for its craftsmanship makes him demand apprenticeship. Zanoba then uses extraordinary physical power to seize Pax and force the court to act. His helpfulness does not make him harmless: retrospective history discloses lethal violence tolerated because of his military usefulness, and his immediate justification for intervening centers on dolls and their maker.

Pax and Zanoba are both exiled under the public description of study abroad. Lilia and Aisha receive an escort to Paul. Rudeus declines royal employment and evaluates the result as favorable while remaining uncertain how much Hitogami determined it. Lilia wants Aisha to accompany and eventually serve him; he refuses the proposed separation from her parents and objects to the sexualized terms of that future. Aisha independently asks to join the journey. Rudeus tries to defend his brotherly reputation without revealing himself, only to discover at their farewell that she already knows who he is. His attempt to direct her understanding has underestimated her judgment.

As the party approaches the mountains, Ruijerd recognizes Eris as a fully fledged warrior. Her progress comes from sustained practice, although Rudeus still struggles to see her beyond the pupil he first taught. An encounter on the road breaks their growing confidence. A stranger named Orsted recognizes Eris and Ruijerd but apparently has no knowledge of Rudeus. The companions react with fear; Rudeus does not. Orsted initially means to pass them, but Rudeus stops him to ask about the displacement. When he admits contact with Hitogami, Orsted abruptly attacks. Ruijerd and Eris try to protect him and are rapidly defeated. Rudeus improvises with silent magic but cannot overcome the difference in skill or heal his own injuries. He survives only after Orsted's companion, Nanahoshi, intervenes and Orsted heals him, as Eris later reports.

In another dream, Hitogami offers explanations involving curses, including a claim that the Superd people's reputation has a magical component that is fading. Rudeus notices an inconsistency in his informant's knowledge but does not resolve it. After waking, he experiences his near death as strangely dreamlike, while Eris remains frightened and Ruijerd troubled. He begins practicing a magic-disruption technique to improve his chances of escaping another encounter. Asked directly by Ruijerd, he finally discloses the dream advice and passes on the curse account with a warning against accepting it unquestioningly. The possibility that his work can still help his people moves Ruijerd to tears.

They reach the devastated Fittoa region. Having completed his escort, Ruijerd leaves to pursue his own search and work. The farewell insists on mutual indebtedness: he rejects Rudeus's claim to have achieved almost nothing and names the particular people whose responses changed. At the refugee camp, Eris reunites with Ghislaine but learns that Philip and Hilda died after displacement and Sauros was executed. Alphonse wants her to secure support for reconstruction through an aristocratic arrangement that threatens her welfare. Ghislaine opposes it. Rudeus ends their discussion by insisting that Eris must decide her own future. Meanwhile, a clerk corrects his mistaken reading of the missing-person list: Sylphie has been reported alive, although no contact address is available. Zenith remains missing from his perspective.

Grief and fear of another separation lead Eris to ask Rudeus to become her family. They become intimate, but do not establish a shared understanding of what follows. Her subsequent viewpoint reveals both her love and a persistent belief that she burdens a person far more capable than herself. Recognizing his vulnerability makes her want to become strong enough to stand beside him. She leaves with Ghislaine for sword training, hoping for a future partnership, and deliberately conceals her destination to prevent him from following. Her brief note about their unequal standing means something very different to Rudeus. He reads it as rejection, spends a week in distress while helping with reconstruction, and concludes that he has failed to grow. He then sets out north to search for Zenith.

The interlude gives Roxy an important lead that Rudeus does not yet possess. After a chance meeting with Kishirika, she chooses information about the missing family over a magical reward for herself. Kishirika reports Zenith alive near the labyrinth city of Rapan on Begaritt, but admits that she cannot see the circumstances clearly. Roxy and Talhand head toward Paul; Elinalise and Badigadi arrange a separate journey to tell Rudeus. Even this coordination contains a mistaken assumption: Roxy believes Kishirika will sail with them, but she stays behind. The final extra returns to Lilia and Aisha's escorted journey. It confirms Aisha's recognition and teasing, supplies Lilia's history of assault and insecurity, and shows real maternal protection and affection alongside her continuing determination to direct Aisha's future service. Their embrace repairs an immediate emotional distance without settling the larger question of who chooses that future.

## E. Character deep readings and changes

Rudeus's most consistent advantage is not control of events but the ability to do useful things within relationships that supply missing capacities. V06 makes this proposition harder to evade: an ordinary carpenter, trained soldiers, Ruijerd, Ginger, Zanoba, Nanahoshi and even an attacker who later heals him affect what he can accomplish. His rice experiment and imperfect disruption practice show both interest and persistence. These do not vanish when his concluding self-account says he has achieved nothing. That statement is a state-dependent interpretation formed after a relationship shock, not an adequate summary of the observed journey (003, 010, 016, 018, 026).

His judgments nevertheless repeatedly make partial knowledge feel complete. He assumes a court acquaintance remains in place, interprets a favorable ending as evidence of converging alternatives, defends his image through an alias and supplies Eris's note with the direction of inequality he most fears. These are different errors with related mechanisms: desire, familiarity or threat makes one account available before other people can correct it. He is also capable of inquiry and revision. Asking the clerk corrects the Sylphie misunderstanding; listening to Ruijerd changes the terms of the farewell; direct questioning finally produces disclosure of Hitogami. The model therefore needs conditions under which inquiry remains possible, rather than a blanket label of unreliable or honest (004, 011, 013, 019–021, 026).

Eris's development now has a much fuller interior account. Her effort is not simply admiration converted into obedience. She wants to contribute, fears replaceability, resents a gifted shortcut that seems to erase her work and uses teachers' concrete techniques to improve. She also provides care that her own burden narrative overlooks. The experience of Orsted becomes different in her mind from Rudeus's: he seeks sufficient power to escape, while she begins to identify learnable technique within an overwhelming opponent. Recognizing his vulnerability does not immediately create a better partnership, because she concludes that distance and self-improvement must precede equality and assumes he will understand this without its explanation (014, 018, 024–025).

This both strengthens and limits the V05 model. Trust can suppress communication not only because she assumes he already knows a fact, but because she credits him with interpretive capacities he does not have. Her short note is not a lack of literacy established by the scene; it is a deliberately minimized message shaped by fear he will follow. Her public decisions—rejecting the aristocratic plan, leaving with Ghislaine and discarding the Boreas name—have independent force. Her future hope remains centered on Rudeus, but her actual training choice is not his instruction. Neither dependence nor autonomy adequately describes all these relations by itself.

Ruijerd combines concrete care with classification and pride. He responds to physical sickness, rescues hostages, intervenes against Orsted and respects the younger travelers' developing competence. Defeat does not erase those acts. His question about Hitogami identifies the actual anomaly rather than the insecurity Rudeus first assumes troubles him. Tears at a possible route out of inherited disgrace show a need that the strong-warrior persona concealed. At departure, he insists on equality without pretending their resources were equal. His praise is relational recognition and encouragement, not an independent certification that Rudeus is a completed person (002, 010, 019–020).

Lilia's extra changes the explanatory account most substantially. It gives her insecurity, gratitude and maternal control a remembered history while also providing a counterexample within that history: her father refuses to impose the expected marriage after she is harmed. Her later certainty about Aisha is consequently neither an inevitable cultural script nor proof of lovelessness. She has a genuine emotional anchor in Rudeus, fears exploiting her child to preserve it, then uses a true instance of protection to settle a different question about the child's future. The resulting embrace is important because the child actually needs expressed affection. It is insufficient as a complete answer to the control problem (012, 029–031).

Aisha is observant and strategic within a constrained childhood. She discovers means of communication, tests adult accounts and uses teasing to change an unequal conversation. Her own idealization of the rescuer and inherited sexualized vocabulary limit a picture of fully independent discernment. Zanoba's craft sensitivity likewise has a narrow domain: he can perceive details other people miss while treating human life with devastating disregard. Ginger's independent action and the soldiers' parallel plan prevent their royal subordination from being mistaken for absence of agency, although hostage coercion sharply restricts their options (005–010, 013, 029).

Roxy accepts practical responsibility even when it postpones a desired reunion. She probes a vague answer, recognizes the problem of informing a distant traveler and divides the work with others. Her imagination still fills gaps incorrectly, and her idealized view of Paul and Zenith is not the reader's full account. Paul himself receives no newly witnessed present-day decision here: reports place the reunited family together, while Lilia's history adds affected-person access to an already established past wrong. Updating his model must add that evidence without inventing a new reform or regression after V05 (027–030).

## F. Relationships, agency and institutions

The return ends one three-person arrangement without resolving all the obligations it generated. Ruijerd can leave because the escort has an endpoint; gratitude does not entitle Rudeus to continued protection. Rudeus and Eris fail to establish a comparably shared endpoint. He believes intimacy begins a settled future. She believes separation makes that future possible. Their reciprocal care is real, and their interpretations of it remain unequal. In both cases, the right to choose must be distinguished from the quality of communication surrounding that choice (020, 023–026).

Lilia's relation to Aisha is another directionally unequal pair. Lilia transmits useful knowledge and protects life; Aisha seeks reasons, resents imposed purpose and later chooses admiration within the available frame. Her approval of the admired employer does not demonstrate a free comparison among futures. Rudeus's immediate refusal to take her limits one arrangement, but the closing instruction to serve him survives. This is a change in affection's expression more clearly than a change in authority (012–013, 029–031).

Institutions repeatedly convert people into resources. Shirone protects a dangerous prince because he can be useful, tolerates a younger prince's slave-market connections until his coercion threatens valued personnel, and disguises exile through formal language. Fittoa's council weighs Eris's availability against land and household restoration. These are not identical harms, and the source gives them different registers, but each exposes a conflict between a person's welfare and the utility assigned by an authority. The refusal of one transfer or rescue of particular captives does not abolish the surrounding arrangement (007–010, 022).

The interlude offers a different use of unequal resources: private connections make a sea route available, while companions share the cost of delivering information. That exception benefits the search without changing access for everyone. The same analytical distinction used for Therese in V05 remains necessary. Cooperation can be meaningful without becoming institutional reform (028).

## G. Focalization, voice and form

The central formal operation is a delayed redistribution of knowledge. Rudeus first experiences apparent improvisation at court; retrospective explanation reveals other plans already running. His experience of an inexplicable attack remains less informed than either adversary's unexplained knowledge. Eris's interior section then replays admitted episodes through a different account of dependence, before the narrative returns to his confident morning voice and sudden collapse. The sequence grants the reader enough information to reject his breakup explanation while leaving him without it. Dramatic irony does not make his pain comic merely because its premise is mistaken.

The note's phrase about being out of balance, `釣り合いが取れません`, leaves the direction and practical consequence underspecified. Eris's preceding thought identifies her own inadequacy and intention to train; Rudeus supplies his inadequacy and rejection. The problem is therefore not a mysterious untranslated word. It is the conjunction of semantic compression, different self-concepts and deliberate withholding of the destination. Their earlier conversation about being strong has already rehearsed this difference between shared vocabulary and shared meaning (018, 025–026).

Another recurring word, warrior, functions as permission, recognition and a distribution of expected care. Eris values the designation as evidence of competence. Rudeus initially hears it through a task classification, then learns it addresses his treatment of her. Ruijerd later uses it to mark the end of guardianship. The word has practical effects without fixing a universal maturity threshold. Physical age, local adulthood convention, remembered biography and emotional capacities remain separate (014, 020, 023).

Comic self-presentation is variable in its effects. The prison's imaginary comfortable-apartment pitch marks fear without solving confinement. Cooking enthusiasm exposes ordinary fallibility. The lengthy morning boast makes the collapse in register sharply visible, but also shows how readily Rudeus mistakes personal fulfillment for mutual understanding. Zanoba's comic excess sits beside lethal biography, while Lilia's closing tenderness sits beside a continuing coercive plan. Register can invite affection without cancelling the represented conduct or demanding a single tonal verdict (003, 008–010, 026, 031).

Illustrations select visible affect and spatial relations; they do not add hidden motives. Aisha's distressed pose precedes fuller disclosure of the guards' coercion. The imposing stranger and masked companion image withholds explanation through scale and concealment. Ruijerd's backlit departure frames an autonomous path. The late image of Kishirika examining the distance can be read beside the failure of complete sight. No cinematic camera, actor's tone or unseen panel sequence is inferred from these static images.

## H. Normative framing and consequential comparisons

V06 contains several genuine limits on advantage: prompt rescue after Aisha's appeal, refusal to slander Roxy for escape, resistance to Lilia's proposed dependent arrangement and insistence on Eris's decision authority. It also contains continued intrusive conduct, rationalization and selective concern. Treating all these as proof of a single general moral advance would ignore the different relationships, knowledge and opportunities.
