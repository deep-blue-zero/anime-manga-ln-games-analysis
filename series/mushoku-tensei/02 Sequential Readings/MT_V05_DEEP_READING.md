# V05 — partial knowledge, capable people and particular repair

## Developments through V04 (spoilers through V04)

Rudeus's second life has given him exceptional magic, useful teaching and lasting relationships without resolving his treatment of other people. Roxy helped him learn and leave his family enclosure. Paul then forced him away from Sylphie into a five-year tutoring appointment in Roa. There, Rudeus and Eris learned through practice, reciprocal support and Ghislaine's instruction. Their closeness included gifts and comfort as well as a serious violation of Eris's limited permission. An unexplained regional displacement destroyed the household's anticipated future. Rudeus and Eris appeared far away on the Demon Continent; Ghislaine was separately shown alive and searching. Roxy later found their home region devastated and Paul's message about missing relatives, but Rudeus has not been shown receiving that information.

Ruijerd rescued the children and promised to escort them home. Rudeus also accepted useful advice from the dream figure Hitogami while doubting his identity and motives. Roxy's pendant, guide and parents supplied practical help. Ruijerd described a wartime catastrophe that shaped the Superd people's feared reputation. Rudeus agreed to help restore that reputation while relying on his protection. In Rikarisu, their Dead End party combined useful adventuring service with concealment, threats and prohibited job swapping. Rudeus delayed rescuing younger adventurers to increase their gratitude, and Gablin died. Later exposure of the scheme brought extortion. Feeling trapped, Rudeus began preparing to devastate the town to escape with Eris. Ruijerd interrupted, revealed his feared identity and took the blame. Their subsequent reconciliation renewed reciprocal trust without complete disclosure of Rudeus's contemplated violence.

Consultation, paid work, language learning and training improved the party's coordination during roughly a year of travel. Eris supplied useful advice and comfort while showing excessive faith and aggression of her own. Rudeus demonstrated particular restraint but continued intrusive conduct. By Wenport, he was eleven and Eris thirteen. An enormous official fare for Superd blocked their sea crossing. Rudeus refused to abandon Ruijerd but secretly considered selling Eris's staff gift. Ruijerd stopped the sale and explained its relational value, changing Rudeus's decision before harm. They accepted a smuggling arrangement from Gallus, a stranger whom Rudeus had recently helped.

Following another Hitogami instruction, Rudeus met the hungry Kishirika, misread her appearance and received an eye that could see possible near futures. The gift required painful calibration and practice. It let him beat Eris but did not supply Ruijerd's bodily skill, reliable interpretation or automatic victory. Roxy, traveling with Elinalise and Talhand to search for displaced people, came close to meeting the party. Rumor, fear, incompatible priorities and mistakes instead produced a missed encounter. Her observation of Eris and Ruijerd's training remained unrecognized by both sides.

Rudeus cared for seasick Eris during the covert crossing and actually refrained from exploiting her vulnerability without Ruijerd present. A later exchange put pressure on the terms of her care, limiting any claim of lasting reform. At Zantport, he and Ruijerd found abducted children in the smugglers' warehouse. Ruijerd killed captors with Rudeus's agreement while Rudeus released and healed the children. Returning for their sacred beast led to mistaken arrest by its rescuers. Ruijerd recovered many other captives and collected Eris, but overestimated Rudeus's capacity to endure or escape confinement alone.

In jail, Rudeus met Geese, whose clothing, conversation and practical knowledge mattered before his later combat help. When fire and kidnappers attacked the village, Rudeus abandoned an escape opportunity to save a child and then help the community. The immediate rescue preceded his language about earning a favor. Gallus proved to be behind the new attack: his earlier commission had diverted defenders and undermined competing smugglers. Rudeus nearly died fighting him and survived through the sacred beast's and Geese's intervention. Gyes apologized for the mistaken detention. The broader account of abduction and Asuran demand made Rudeus question the origins of Boreas household servants, but he lacked their histories and concealed the suspicion from Eris.

The three-month rainy season brought paid village protection, practice, craft and friendships. Eris defended Ghislaine against Gyes's childhood memories without hitting him, taught language to Minitoona and Tersena, and partly moderated a peer quarrel. Her friends initiated a reconciliation that Rudeus could not fully hear. His observation of her independent growth coexisted with continued privacy violations requiring outside intervention. Ruijerd's respect for Rudeus as a warrior sometimes became excessive demands for self-sufficiency. Geese later joined their road journey temporarily and described losing his place in a former party; his excellent cooking and refusal to teach Eris exposed the connection between useful work and belonging. The travelers reached the Millis border, still short of home.

The palace extras follow another displaced survivor, publicly called Fitts, who protects Ariel after her decision to seek the Asuran throne. Daily service and exceptional magic coexist with nightmares and fear of being discarded. Luke and Ariel provide actual comfort, while Ariel also uses Fitts politically and makes refusal difficult through dependent teasing. Fitts protects Ariel from an enslaved assassin and survives poison. Nightmares cease after that night, but attacks continue and a future foreign-study escape is announced. Fitts's earlier name, the ultimate displacement cause and many missing people's fates remain unestablished within the admitted account. Palace knowledge and Roxy's search information do not silently become Rudeus's knowledge.

## Story synopsis of V05 (spoilers)

Rudeus, Eris and Ruijerd reach Milishion after their journey through the Great Forest. Geese leaves the group, having made himself useful through food, knowledge and companionship, and urges Rudeus to visit the adventurers' guild. The travelers choose a hotel and discuss money, Ruijerd's reputation and the next stage of their return. Rudeus plans overdue letters home. Eris instead takes a day to attempt the goblin hunt she has long imagined, while Ruijerd visits an old acquaintance.

Before finishing his letters, Rudeus sees men carrying away a child. He intervenes, believing he has found another kidnapping. In the ensuing fight he encounters a swordsman whose skill and appearance are familiar: Paul, his father. The apparent kidnappers belong to Paul's search party, which has been freeing people enslaved after the Fittoa displacement. Father and son have approached the encounter with radically different knowledge. Rudeus has struggled to return from the Demon Continent but does not know that the entire region disappeared or that messages await him at guilds. Paul assumes his exceptionally capable son knows all this and has chosen enjoyable adventuring over searching for his family.

Paul's account takes the reader back through the year and a half since the disaster. He found himself with Norn, rushed toward home, lost time caring for her illness and discovered a devastated region. With help from Alphonse and resources obtained through family connections, he organized a search and rescued many displaced people. The work exposed him and his companions to danger and opposition. As useful reports dwindled and his own family remained missing, he began drinking heavily and neglecting his responsibilities. Norn remained dependent on him, even as her presence prevented him from abandoning everything for a desperate search alone.

The reunion initially makes this situation worse. Rudeus presents his travels as a lively story; Paul hears a confession of indifference. Accusations escalate into a fight. Norn intervenes to protect the father who has cared for her, seeing Rudeus as an attacker. Only afterward does Rudeus receive the full account of the disaster and the continuing absence of Zenith, Lilia, Aisha and Sylphie. He returns to the hotel deeply hurt, unable to carry out the calm apology he thinks he should make. Eris is furious on his behalf. Ruijerd prevents her from attacking Paul, and she instead offers an awkward but effective embrace.

Geese meanwhile challenges Paul's account. He describes what travel on the Demon Continent demands, points out the limits of a child's knowledge, and makes Paul imagine a reunion that never happened because his son died. Paul begins to recognize that exceptional ability does not eliminate a child's need for care. Their next conversation begins with apologies but remains painfully stiff. An innkeeper prompts Rudeus to look at his father's distress. Remembering a friendship he destroyed in his previous life by rejecting a visitor's cheerful story, Rudeus deliberately starts the reunion again. He asks Paul to open his arms, says that he wanted to see him, and the two finally embrace and cry.

Their renewed conversation provides family news, including Sylphie's continued study after Rudeus left and Philip's earlier marriage proposal. Rudeus decides to complete Eris's journey home while making the search for displaced people a greater priority. Paul supplies money and advice; Ruijerd has obtained a letter from an old beneficiary that might solve the prohibitive sea fare imposed on Superd. Reconciliation remains local. Eris still resents Paul's treatment of Rudeus, and Norn refuses to accept her brother despite a shared meal and their father's efforts. A village's gratitude for a monster hunt likewise fails to produce the broader acceptance of Superd that Rudeus hopes to promote.

An interlude returns to Eris's independent day. She meets Cliff, a gifted young magician whose pride exceeds his practical experience. Their goblin hunt reveals his poor coordination and her own susceptibility to provocation. Lost in a forest, Eris uses lessons from Ghislaine and Ruijerd to locate other people, then intervenes when assassins threaten a knight and a young girl. She kills for the first time and helps save them, although the knight's assistance is necessary. The knight, Therese, remembers her rescuer. Cliff admires Eris, has his proposal rejected, and learns how highly she values Rudeus. The reader also learns that the attack belongs to a struggle inside the Millis church.

Two months later, a hostile customs official refuses to honor Ruijerd's letter. Rudeus's appeals to noble status do not win passage for his companion. Therese intervenes through her official authority, her debt to Eris and her recognition that Rudeus is her sister Zenith's son. The party receives passage and practical assistance. Her kindness does not amount to acceptance of demons generally: she explicitly retains the convictions of her order and makes a personal exception.

Roxy's interlude parallels the reunion through a different obstacle. Returning to her village, she still cannot participate in Migurd telepathy and doubts that spoken welcome communicates her parents' deepest feelings. Her mother's tears and a shared embrace change her response, and she stays for several days. Other testimony finally makes her acknowledge that she missed Rudeus earlier. She reconnects with Nokopara, learns of an old companion's death and continues searching for the still-missing family members instead of pursuing a meeting with her pupil.

The two extras extend the volume's concern with partial understanding. In Eastport, Rudeus's nostalgia for familiar food turns into an excessive attack on a struggling cook; after his companions remove him, he recognizes that he went too far. The cook's own account reveals consequences Rudeus does not see: the man closes an inherited restaurant and accepts a military recruitment offer. Another extra follows Gustav's investigation of a rumor that Ariel has died while escaping Asura. Testimony describes loyal border officials, a costly attack and Fitts's protective magic. Gustav mistakes an unidentified victim for Ariel. When later testimony exposes the error, he knowingly leaves the false report uncorrected. The volume ends with information still dispersed and consequences still unfolding, rather than with the missing family or the political crises resolved.

### MT-E-LNJP-V05-001 — Arrival is maintained through ordinary shared work

**Locator:** spine7 `text/part0006.html` p6–25, p32–110, p118–203. Representation: narration, quoted in-world guide, dialogue and routine.

The grand literary image of Milishion meets entry fees, commerce, class divisions and a slave market. Geese's practical knowledge and the group's habitual cleaning make arrival possible without a fresh command for every task. His departure occasions an admission of loneliness alongside appreciation for cooking and stress relief. Rudeus calls a meeting and asks about altered priorities; rest is explicitly connected to his distress at repeated killing of monsters. The question is whether growing competence has removed dependence. It has instead made some interdependence ordinary. A rival reading can emphasize his continuing leadership and comfort preferences; both remain, so routine cooperation should not be equated with equal power. **Minimum:** practiced coordination and useful companionship are actual supports, while guidebook magnificence is a partial account. High local confidence; C001/C007/C008/C012/C013; state, relationship and form ledgers.

### MT-E-LNJP-V05-002 — Child protection survives a bad experience, but judgment can still fail

**Locator:** spine9 `text/part0008.html` p1–54, p57–140, p144–232. Representation: choice, fantasy, self-explanation and action.

Rudeus worries about Eris's independent hunt through an imported sexualized adventure script, while Ruijerd treats her demonstrated ability as relevant. He ultimately permits the plan. Later, seeing a child carried away, he remembers his own wrongful detention but rejects abandoning children because of it. Party duty and reputational aims coexist. The crucial temporal difference from the V03 forest is that he does not manufacture a dangerous delay to increase gratitude. Yet he then refuses an explanation and attacks people whose activity he has misidentified. A generous reading sees principled intervention; a critical one sees recurring self-appointed authority. Neither cancels the other. **Minimum:** immediate aid is a countercase to invariant reward-driven delay, not proof of sufficient knowledge or proportionate force. High; C002/C003/C011, model003 and normative/knowledge routes.

### MT-E-LNJP-V05-003 — Seeing and identifying are different achievements

### MT-E-LNJP-V05-005 — Rescue work has real beneficiaries and real limits

**Locator:** spine11 `text/part0010.html` p123–165, p168–262. Representation: retrospective action and attributed reports.

Haste contributes to Norn's illness and forces a lengthy care stop. Finding Fittoa destroyed nearly defeats Paul; attending to Norn restores a concrete obligation. Search organization grows from his wish to find family into aid for others. Alphonse's resources and Zenith's relatives' status help, while freeing enslaved survivors brings conflict with property claims, attacks, deaths and criticism within the search party. Most survivors want return, but the account does not make everyone interchangeable. Names in reports are not direct death scenes. **Alternative:** the account can look like retrospective self-justification. Its admissions of error, limited resources and contested methods nevertheless prevent dismissing the work as fictitious. **Minimum:** care is materially effective and uneven, dependent on collective resources and legally coercive institutions. High for his represented account, qualified on reported distant facts; C001/C007/C009/C010/C013.

### MT-E-LNJP-V05-006 — Exceptional ability becomes an imagined exemption from needing help

**Locator:** spine11 `text/part0010.html` p265–340; spine13 `text/part0012.html` p273–410. Representation: Paul's withdrawal and later disputed explanation.

As useful news dwindles, Paul imagines his missing family's deaths, drinks and stops attending adequately to work. Those feared deaths are not established facts. Norn's dependence anchors him even while it blocks the desperate solo search he imagines. When Geese later invokes Rudeus's age, Paul initially answers through ability: his son is stronger and cleverer and therefore should have accomplished more. Geese must describe specific unfamiliar conditions and incomplete knowledge before the equation becomes unstable. **Alternative:** Paul has a legitimate grievance about missed information and competing emergencies. That supports criticism of particular omissions, not attributing knowledge he never established. **Minimum:** crisis and the capacity-to-obligation inference explain the accusation without justifying it. Strong bounded inference; C012/C013, Paul model and comparison with V04 warrior expectations.

### MT-E-LNJP-V05-007 — The first reunion distributes blame through incompatible accounts

**Locator:** spine11 `text/part0010.html` p357–564; spine13 `text/part0012.html` p1–80. Representation: dialogue, violence, disclosure and Norn's intervention.

Rudeus gives an entertaining, partly embellished account intended to present his journey favorably. Paul hears evidence that the journey was easy and interrupts the account before relevant imprisonment details. Rudeus has actually neglected some possibilities and initially assumed others were not displaced; the father also wrongly assumes that guild messages reached him. Sexual jealousy supplies another false explanation. Paul strikes first, and Rudeus's retaliation becomes excessive. Norn protects the only parent who has cared for her through the disaster. Only afterward does the son receive the region-wide account. His claim never to have touched Eris cannot be treated as a clean history of conduct; at most its immediate sexual boast uses a narrower sense than the earlier violations. **Minimum:** factual omission, false motive attribution and violence have different owners. High; C002/C003/C009/C010, normative and relationship ledgers. Mutual misunderstanding does not make the initial blows symmetrical.

### MT-E-LNJP-V05-008 — Being able to formulate an apology does not mean being able to return

**Locator:** spine13 `text/part0012.html` p84–213. Representation: private thought, bodily distress and care.

Rudeus crumples the unused letter paper, recognizing his wish to reassure his father and show competence. He can propose a rational conversation yet becomes sick at imagining it. His favorable summary that he avoided fatal mistakes is too broad against the V03 record. Ruijerd listens; Eris's response is first retaliatory, with an attempted armed departure that Ruijerd stops. Her subsequent embrace is awkward, physically specific and effective for Rudeus. The calluses on her hands make her own effort visible inside his experience of support. **Alternative:** the scene assigns a girl the labor of soothing a boy through Ruijerd's gendered prescription. ## E. Character deep readings and changes

Rudeus's most substantial development here is a particular capacity to receive care and reinterpret another person's distress. He does not discover that apologies exist; he discovers why the apology he can formulate is not yet enough. Food, rest, Eris's embrace, Ruijerd's intervention, Geese's work and the innkeeper's prompt all condition what looks, at the instant of resolution, like his own insight. Remembered experience becomes useful through a reversal of positions: the cheerful visitor he once attacked now resembles the role he has occupied with Paul. This is a more exact explanation than either a second-life adult simply acting maturely or a child simply being forgiven. His longing for a father is active, and his superior-age self-description does not make that need disappear.

The new understanding remains selective. He can accept that Norn sees a threatened caregiver, yet misread Shela's fear and Vera's dress. He declares a more explicit intention to respect Eris's refusal, yet the volume does not provide a matched, sustained test that could establish permanent restraint. His later attack on the cook is a new context in which expectation becomes entitlement, with companions again supplying the stopping point. The relevant change is not a scalar rise or fall in goodness. It is a newly available way to repair one relationship, alongside other habits and powers that remain dangerous. Even the claim that his heart can no longer break is evidence of how relief invites overgeneralization.

Paul's interior account gives the analysis a stronger basis for a separate model. The same man who exercises skilled practical care can demand too much of the person he admires. Norn's immediate vulnerability makes his responsibility concrete; Rudeus's exceptional performance makes vulnerability easy to forget. Paul's remembered correction by Zenith, later resistance to Geese and eventual counterfactual comparison of his children show that being corrected is possible but not automatic. His initial violence is not justified by grief or by Rudeus's actual omissions. His rescue work is likewise not nullified by that violence. Renewed affection helps him resume work, but the staff's account of the previous month prevents a flattering reinterpretation in which no neglect occurred. His prior assault of Lilia must stay in the model's moral history without being converted into a rule that every current relation is sexual exploitation.

Eris's interlude makes her apparent simplicity less sustainable. The commanding posture can conceal anxiety, support deliberate composure or enact pride. She wants an adventure for its own sake, notices information, evaluates practical spell use and deploys lessons from several teachers. At the same time, she accepts a taunt, injures Cliff and needs other people's restraint or assistance. Her rescue is not merely a prelude to helping Rudeus: it creates a debt and affects a church conflict beyond his knowledge. Her commitment to him does influence what she reports and forgets. The independent episode therefore supplies both autonomy and a mechanism by which attachment narrows communication. Her refusal of Cliff is unambiguous regardless of his estimation of his worth.

Ruijerd's conduct revisits the unstable child/warrior boundary. Here he works to preserve a father–son meeting and helps direct care toward Rudeus, in contrast with V04's overestimated self-sufficiency. Yet he injures Eris while restraining her and offers a gendered account of who should comfort whom. His old rescue produces an enduring friendship, while his limited interest in rank or naming complicates the letter's use. A narrow model can account for protective duty, practical instruction, status indifference and changing classifications, but cannot turn him into an impartial ethical authority.

Roxy's return develops the distinction between ability, recognition and belonging through her own history. Her communication difference is a persistent fact; an embrace makes parental care newly accessible without eliminating it. She also accepts an error after resisting the implication that she missed her pupil. That correction should not make her subsequent praise infallible. Her account of Rudeus's effortless passage resembles the very mistake that injured him in Paul's mouth, although her immediate purpose and action differ. Her decision to search for the other missing people has genuine practical direction. Pride, affection, embarrassment and duty are simultaneously supported motives.

The women around Paul are not interchangeable tests of his or Rudeus's character. Shela manages scarce resources and schedules while avoiding threatening attention. Vera deliberately creates a different field of attention and advocates for both her sister and the young son. Their responses continue after the men's emotional resolution. Norn's refusal is based on the father she experienced and the violence she saw, not an absence of moral feeling. Sylphie's newly reported education establishes differentiated competence and sustained work before displacement, but not her current identity, location or wishes. Zenith and Lilia gain reported domestic negotiation, not direct new interiority. Geese's intervention and renewed search are substantial choices, while his evasions still leave gaps. Fitts's protection remains bounded by witness access; Ariel's escape does not establish her future government. These limits belong to the account, rather than being vacancies to fill with franchise memory.

## F. Relationships, agency and institutions

The father–son repair changes both directions of the relationship. Paul learns that a competent son still needs to be welcomed and helped; Rudeus learns to read his father's shame and choose renewed contact. Neither can order Norn or Eris to make the same transition. Eris's care helps the reunion happen, but her desired punishment is refused. Norn's attachment to Paul supports a different account of the fight, and Rudeus's acknowledgment of it becomes a practical limit on forced reconciliation. The continuing discomfort of Vera and Shela shows why a scene's successful central relationship cannot stand for all present relationships.

Financial and institutional arrangements are not background decoration. Shela's control of funds, Alphonse's support, Zenith's relatives' privileges, the guild notices and the tariff system determine which choices can be attempted. The family's resources are neither inexhaustible nor universally available. Letters and names matter only when recipients recognize their authority. Rudeus's invented aristocratic account exposes how readily access can depend on status while also showing its limit: the duke will accept children of the right family and still exclude their demon protector. Therese supplies a remedy through a second office and particular obligations. It helps these travelers while preserving the unequal system.

The same distinction appears in advocacy. Villagers can thank Ruijerd and refuse an object meant to generalize that gratitude. Therese can owe Eris her life and still describe herself as bound to fight demons. Smiley can risk himself for Ariel while previously carrying out lethal delays for other nobles. These are not identical moral situations, but together they test a claim that one encounter automatically transforms a general category. Personal ties can interrupt a rule without revising it.

## G. Focalization, voice and form

V05 repeatedly puts an explanatory account before, after or outside the person who would need it. Paul's retrospective chapter gives the reader a preparation Rudeus lacks. Vera's explanation arrives through Paul, then later scenes let the reader watch Rudeus confidently miss it. Eris's interlude returns to an already elapsed day and makes her own adventure the cause of later assistance. Roxy's viewpoint reveals that a teacher's admiration can misdescribe her pupil's dependence. The restaurant extra and Gustav's inquiry extend the device beyond family: a viewpoint change can expose a consequence, while an embedded witness can report an action accurately and still misidentify its subject.

This does not mean every proposition is equally doubtful. The source directly distinguishes Eris's inward fear from Cliff's impression of effortless mastery. It explicitly marks Gustav's final report as false. Conversely, Geese's explanation for arranging imprisonment remains an attributed retrospective account, and the disguise mechanism remains inferred. The reading needs a hierarchy of evidence rather than a blanket unreliable-narrator label. The final inquiry makes the distinction especially legible by staging claim, testimony, inference, contradiction and the decision not to revise.

Written register often shows the difference between knowing a social form and achieving its purpose. Rudeus can speak politely while the father–son exchange remains rigid. Paul's noble greeting momentarily changes Eris's available response. Therese combines formal jurisdictional authority with a strategically timed apology. Smiley and Ariel share a cover identity whose fiction is transparent to both. Rudeus's overt reenactment succeeds because the other person can enter it, unlike earlier concealed schemes. The recurring word-field of reunion is therefore enacted through more than a greeting or a factual declaration.

Comedy has several local effects. The warehouse disguise and sexual distraction complicate heroic self-presentation. The awkward meal makes conversational skill fallible. Cliff's misreadings and Eris's aggression are treated with broad comic energy that can reduce the felt weight of injury, even while adult restraint remains necessary. The food extra returns to exaggerated authority after the emotionally serious embrace and then exposes the recipient's humiliation. No uniform audience response or authorial intention follows from calling these scenes comic. Their sequence lets sympathy and criticism remain simultaneously available.

Illustrations reinforce selected positions without providing a separate omniscient record. The father–son recognition image interrupts combat through paired faces; Norn's outstretched protection visualizes her loyalty. Geese's ordinary seated conversation contrasts with the emotionally concentrated embrace. The block-throwing Norn image keeps refusal visible after reunion. Eris's dynamic combat composition emphasizes mastery, which the following prose qualifies with fear and assistance. Therese's smiling embrace and Rudeus's question bubble preserve an uneven response. Design plates and the opening preview organize presentation but do not prove motives, future events or performed voices.

## H. Normative framing and consequential comparisons

The analytical criteria here are informed agency, proportionate force, fair hearing, noncoercive care and responsibility for foreseeable or recognized harm. These are analyst criteria, kept separate from a character's judgment, a local law, comic narration or an unverified claim about the author or audience. The volume provides neither a single endorsement nor a single condemnation of its whole cast.

The rescue opening is a useful matched limit on C011. Rudeus chooses prompt intervention despite his prior wrongful arrest, unlike the calculated V03 rescue delay. That deserves recognition as an actual difference in conduct. His refusal to hear the supposed abductors then repeats the epistemic failure of people who once misidentified him. A good reason to help is not a license to stop learning the situation. Paul likewise has real care obligations and legitimate missing-information concerns, but cannot turn the son's assumed knowledge into established fault or justify striking him first.

Eris's care and violence must be evaluated separately. Her sympathy is specific and practical; she notices hurt the men are tempted to minimize. Her proposed revenge and later injury to Cliff exceed what that sympathy licenses. Ruijerd's restraint prevents immediate harm, but the incomplete account of Eris's bruise does not establish that every degree of force was necessary. Her intervention against assassins presents a different threat, with an endangered child, active armed attackers and help from a knight. Killing an attacker in flight remains a further question within that episode. Different conditions prevent an automatic common verdict on every blow.

The consent-related evidence is also differentiated. Rudeus's new refusal-respecting resolve is more explicit than an age condition, but it remains an intention. His correction of an infidelity accusation coexists with instructing Vera to dress differently because of his own gaze. The reader has already been given the protective reason he misses. Therese's affectionate handling is not equivalent in context to earlier coercive conduct, yet his discomfort is still evidence of a boundary; warmth or kinship does not establish welcome. Medicine changes Eris's options on the sea journey, reducing the dependency that had made prior care exchanges ethically fraught. No graphic reconstruction or sexualized validation scenario is necessary to compare these conditions.

Paul's newly admitted assault and current rescue labor occupy the same history. Retaliatory motive increases specificity about culpability rather than being excused by a young-self label. Helping survivors later neither cancels that harm nor becomes unreal because he caused a different harm earlier. The report of Zenith and Lilia negotiating domestic terms adds agency after the V01 crisis but does not prove that the crisis, deception or unequal resources were acceptable to everyone. Avoid converting a workable arrangement into retrospective consent to how it arose.

The final extras are decisive tests of generalization. Rudeus's cook-directed outburst follows his successful reunion; his own criticism and companions' intervention identify excess. The recipient's lost business cannot simply be counted as a beneficial career outcome, since the source presents shame and outside recruitment rather than a freely chosen improvement explained from his settled perspective. Gustav has a different relation to harm: once he recognizes the likely error, the failure to verify and correct becomes explicit self-interested omission. C014 therefore distinguishes unavailable knowledge from avoidable error and from knowingly leaving a false report in use. The distinction would collapse if all three were called mere misunderstanding.

## I. Themes, world and chronology

The strongest emerging theme is that being valued for ability can both admit someone to a relationship and obscure what they need. Paul's demanding account of his talented son, Eris's assumption that Rudeus has read the notices, Cliff's expectation that talent warrants welcome, Roxy's inflated professional reputation and Randolph's mismatched vocation are different mechanisms. They do not reduce to one law of usefulness. Norn's care, Eris's embrace, Ruijerd's old friendship and Roxy's parents provide substantial counterevidence to the claim that belonging is only transactional.

Information has a material route. Notices must be encountered and understood; assumptions about shared knowledge can prevent communication; political reports have patrons and costs; witnesses observe from particular positions. The trajectory from a crumpled letter to a falsely completed investigation makes communication a form of work whose completion cannot be measured by the existence of a message alone. The food episode adds embodied memory as another powerful but fallible guide: a familiar smell can restore a past pleasure and generate an unjust expectation of the present.

At the opening Rudeus identifies his body as eleven, and the elapsed displacement interval is approximately a year and a half. Cliff is twelve and explicitly between Rudeus and Eris in age. V04 had described Eris as approaching fourteen; this later age formulation is retained as a timing tension rather than silently reconciled through an invented birthday. Rounded later references to two years and Rudeus's roughly twelve years should remain approximate rather than forcing a precise birthday calendar. The main Milishion stay culminates in a week after revised planning; Westport follows a reported two-month journey. Eris's interlude returns to the day of the first reunion, and Roxy's return is placed around the party's departure from Millis. Paul's section covers the intervening disaster period retrospectively. Ariel's extra has its own departure/investigation sequence; its relative placement should not be converted into a day-exact alignment with Rudy's route.

Institutional findings are local: church factions disagree about demons, different knight orders have distinct offices, tariff and property rules constrain rescue and passage, and officials can use delay as violence's enabling condition. The book does not establish a complete universal legal system. The new colophon identity and package date remain bibliographic facts, not story chronology. The cause of displacement, the missing family's present fates, Fitts's prior identity and the full later consequences of Ariel's escape remain unresolved at this boundary.

## L. Exit freeze and next questions

The current account closes V05's evidence boundary, not the whole narrative. Rudeus and Paul have achieved a specific reunion and renewed practical coordination. Norn and Eris have not inherited the same reconciliation. The search remains open, and the party reaches the Central Continent with help that does not dissolve demon prejudice. Roxy has revisited her family and corrected a missed identification, then chooses continued search. Ariel's reported death is false; the investigator's omission is explicit, while its later consequences remain only announced. These conclusions are held independently of any remembered continuation.

The entering freeze registered questions, not outcome predictions. V05 supplies diagnostic answers about overestimated capacity, immediate aid, new family information, independent work and partial perspective. It gives an explicit new refusal-respecting intention without a sufficient persistence test. Consultation becomes more visible in resources, departure and Eris's involvement, but not universally accurate information sharing. No count of supportive passages can turn those results into a successful blinded forecast.

Future eligible evidence should test whether the new care-based recognition transfers to an unfamiliar person or whether it remains confined to a valued relationship; whether a recognized mistake produces repair before outside pressure; whether a capable companion's expressed need changes a demand; whether a personal exception alters a broader exclusionary practice; and whether recipients can refuse care or obligation without losing belonging. These are discriminating questions with competing possibilities, not registered outcomes. The V05 cumulative checkpoint is complete as a bounded synthesis after this reading.
