## E. Character and relationship interpretation

**Rudeus.** His central practical pattern is converting shame into bounded exercises: literacy, mana repetitions, martial drills and field notes. The initial promise to be serious is neither empty nor sufficient. He can learn through failure, protect a targeted child, care for infants and admit a clear wrong; he also seeks asymmetrical advantage, steals a teacher's intimate property, overrides a child's refusal, manipulates his mother's judgment with a fabricated accusation and imagines shaping Sylphie's future. The first-person voice sometimes corrects itself, sometimes produces a second excuse. It cannot be treated as either always reliable or always exculpatory. A claim that his magical or social confidence proves ethical maturation would conflate domains. The infancy/adulthood distinction remains materially unsettled as a moral framework, but the prior-life memory and sexualized interiority are explicitly represented, not something inferred only from childish behavior. `001,003,005,008–016`.

**Lilia.** Palace injury, termination and the risk of being used as a political lure make her employment choice intelligible; pay and remittances constrain her options. Her fear of the infant is narrated from outside his self-account. She changes assessment when his behavior changes, then expresses disproportionate lifelong obligation after he intervenes in the pregnancy meeting. The earlier forced encounter with Paul and the later invitation are distinct episodes; her self-blame for the latter cannot erase the former or the current employment hierarchy. Her contemplated assignment of Aisha to serve Rudeus is a projection about a future child, not Aisha's choice. `004,013`.

**Zenith.** She heals and teaches, chooses an expensive book tuned to her son's interest, helps at the village clinic and performs domestic labor, judges her husband's broken promise, and chooses to care for Lilia's infant. Her extra explicitly resists the idea that she felt nothing: she had considered leaving and remains shaped by Milis monogamy. She also notices Rudeus's unusual sexual gaze and worries about Sylphie's ability to say no. Her projection that she will correct him later remains a future intention, not a demonstrated intervention. `003,006,013,014`.

**Roxy.** Her apparent youthful appearance is Rudeus's perception; her exact age is not established here. She teaches with adaptive questioning and material demonstrations, has paid work among villagers, knows institutional exclusion, repairs her own practical mistakes, and chooses advancement elsewhere. She is a crucial facilitator of Rudeus's first venture outside, but she does not know his full remembered history or set out to heal it. Respect for her pedagogical work coexists in his narration with sexual objectification and theft. Her subsequent letter reports a new court appointment and water-king skill as her own trajectory, not a reward for teaching him. `006–008,015`.

**Paul.** Martial competence and parental love are supported, as are striking a child, a prior sexual assault reported by Lilia, adultery, harassment and a later coercive separation. His point-of-view sections reveal specific worries about strength, parenting and dependency; they do not automatically vindicate his chosen means. He apologizes after learning he misjudged the bullying incident and later pauses to hear Rudeus after Sylphie's distress. These are bounded behavior changes, with contrary conduct still present. `006,010–016`.

**Sylphie.** She protects a delivered lunch through bullying, chooses to learn, practices persistently, sets a physical boundary, stays cautious after an apology, asks for ordinary treatment, and protests separation with her own magic. Her proximity to Rudeus provides protection and also narrows her alternatives in this village. He first mistakes her sex and later interprets her closeness through romantic-game frames; neither supplies direct access to her adult goals. Her fear of abandonment is observed, but Paul's prediction of irreversible dependency remains a hypothesis, not an established future. `009,011,012,015,016`.

**Ghislaine, Norn, Aisha, Rolls.** Ghislaine accepts a teaching exchange and asks for literacy, while Paul's letter mocks her; her own interiority remains thin. The infants' expressed needs and the adults' arrangements are visible, but their future autonomy is unavailable. Rolls contributes village defense, conveys concern about his daughter's isolation, and is involved in Paul's separation plan; his actual words to Sylphie at departure are not fully reported. Do not build adult models from these appearances. `009,014,016`.

Directional relations matter. Rudeus values Roxy for training and the safe crossing, whereas she values the work and seeks her own improvement. Sylphie wants friendship and physical safety, whereas Rudeus combines care, fear of loss and future-oriented possessiveness. Zenith's compassion for Lilia does not make betrayal painless; Lilia's gratitude does not erase the employment relation. Paul and Rudeus each experience closeness and resist the other's control. See the [relationship ledger](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/MT_RELATIONSHIP_AND_AGENCY_LEDGER.md) for directional records.

## F. Prose form, themes, and the illustrated object

The main voice is first-person and saturated with contemporary game, internet and erotic-genre comparisons. These give a compact account of how Rudeus classifies new information, but often substitute a familiar fictional script for another person's lived position. His first guesses about poverty, Roxy's age or motives, Sylphie's sex, Lilia's dislike and the bullying child's wound are revisable; the prose sometimes stages the revision. In other places, especially possessive fantasies, self-interruption supplies a brief check without resolving the underlying desire. Rapid joke/retraction rhythms can create reader distance from a thought, deflect its cost, and keep a comic register in circulation at once. Which function predominates depends on the scene.

Viewpoint shifts make a useful control. Episode 2 gives Lilia a history before Rudeus knows her; the Paul interlude restages his earlier slap as a failure of a sincerely held rule; Lilia's second section revises the family meeting's assignment of blame; the late Zenith extra narrates her faith and choice. These are not an omniscient moral referee: each passage retains its focal person's rationalizations and blind spots. Episode titles promise comedy or a familiar genre slot, while the chapter contents often include injury, work, fear or rupture. The abrupt stolen-garment disclosure after a lyrical farewell is a particularly consequential tonal cut. `004,008,010,013,014`.

The images form a second presentation channel. The front cover/character art foregrounds the adventuring ensemble before the prose has earned their relationships, and the front character chart offers names and relations as paratext. The grayscale plate at `text/part0019.html` presents the bathing reveal as a startled multi-panel gag, emphasizing Rudeus's surprise; the prose supplies Sylphie's refusal, tears and continued distance, which a gag image alone cannot represent adequately. The plate at `part0025.html` introduces Ghislaine as a visually imposing sword teacher; Paul's written insults about her literacy should not be inferred from that image. The back design sheets at `part0029`–`part0033` are character reference art, not extra episodes or proof of later plot. Claims of sexualized or coercive framing thus rest primarily on the prose sequence and its viewpoint distribution, with the relevant plate as a limited additional cue. No illustration is reproduced here.

Recurring mechanisms include **doors/gates** (domestic enclosure, supervised crossing, an imposed departure), **books versus practice** (manuals and experiments, Roxy's embodied teaching, Ghislaine's wish to read), and **care as work** (Lilia's wages, Roxy's paid service, childbirth, feeding, village defense). The weather magic serves exploration and agricultural livelihood, then risks a horse and crops during a demonstration. The social world imposes constraints through aristocratic schooling, species prejudice, guild wages, the servant/employer relation and family religious commitments. Roxy's reported historical account of a persecuted group and Rudeus's reading of its danger remain reports and inferences, not certified world history. See the [form/themes/world ledger](../03%20Longitudinal%20Ledgers/MT_FORM_THEMES_AND_WORLD_LEDGER.md).

## G. Normative framing and claim limits

At the scene level, Sylphie's explicit refusal makes the boundary clear; the eventual apology and her later distance keep the injury visible, while the gender-reveal gag and Paul's partial normalization of early sexual curiosity pull attention back toward male embarrassment. **Strong bounded conclusion:** the volume supplies both critique of the override and comic accommodation of it. This does not amount to a whole-series endorsement claim, nor to a claim that her subsequent friendship repairs the violation. Rudeus's earlier sexualized treatment of Lilia and Roxy, and theft from Roxy, have weaker direct reckoning. Their existence cannot be negated by his genuine gratitude. `004,008,011,012`.

The family meeting likewise produces a caring outcome through a knowingly false accusation. A criterion of truthful consent-sensitive judgment makes the lie objectionable even if it helps prevent the immediate economic danger to Lilia and the infant. Lilia's retrospective self-blame and her earlier assault history complicate a simple allocation of responsibility, while Zenith's retrospective account places her chosen care, not the unverified allegation, at the center of her own change.
