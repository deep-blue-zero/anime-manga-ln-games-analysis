## Explanatory domains

Cliff's self-concept combines real diligence with the expectation of exceptional achievement. Family instruction asks for humility while confirming a great future. This tension helps explain why he studies seriously yet evaluates peers through hierarchies that protect his own standing. The source does not support the alternative that he is merely a boastful fraud: elementary study, repeated effort and acceptance of unwelcome facts contradict it. Conversely, earned magical knowledge cannot stand in for fighting experience, social understanding or expertise in every topic.

His attention favors meaningful purpose and visible accomplishment. Zanoba's stated goal inspires him through a mistaken political analogy. Rudy initially looks wrong for the hero Cliff expects; subsequently observed feats and ordinary beginner study force a limited revision. He can accept ability without becoming fond of its possessor. This distinction makes the model falsifiable: a representation that instantly converts new evidence into friendship would fail, as would one that prevents all factual updating because he is proud.

Affection establishes another durable motive. He wants to defend Elinalise and work toward removing her constraint, and he accepts terms that Rudy expects him to reject. His defense can be rash: striking first against a group does not demonstrate practical competence. His later listening offer and continued inquiry show a different way of helping, without proving that every confident promise can be fulfilled. The theory Rudy provides remains qualified hearsay; persistence is an action, cure is an unachieved outcome.

Regulation is variable. Pride inhibits asking an admired fighter for instruction and can provoke conflict over insults, yet he deliberately interrupts defensiveness to thank a rescuer. Curiosity can redirect his attention during an adventurer conversation, and Elinalise's intervention can calm him. These observations support specific transition mechanisms, not an invariant temper or a diagnosis. Relational trust and a concrete research need eventually make asking Rudy for help possible despite dislike.

Ordinary life is evidenced in disciplined study, irritation at noise, conversation, medical/religious listening and repeated unsuccessful research. These activities give him a life beyond rival or romantic partner. Written speech can be declarative, evaluative and confident, but includes deliberate thanks, a qualified offer and an explicit request for information. It must not become a stock boast in every turn. Family negotiation, long-term household conduct, private leisure preferences and unfamiliar institutional leadership remain insufficiently evidenced.

Moral reasoning includes defending a valued person's dignity, willingness to accept a burdensome condition and offering care within a trained role. It also includes disproportionate or poorly calibrated confrontation and idealization. Elinalise's choice remains hers; his promise creates a commitment for him, not authority over her. The model permits useful support and mistaken advice to coexist. It neither endorses his social hierarchy nor reduces every act to selfish status seeking.
