## Explanatory account

Elinalise's practical agency depends on work, contacts and movement. She participates in an extended search and takes responsibility for carrying information to someone whose own search would otherwise continue without the lead. The V04 near miss includes her diversion but also several independent errors; reducing it to her sexuality discards the scene's causal structure. V06 coordination and V08 delivery establish a completed undertaking, while her assurance about Zenith remains testimony rather than a substitute for seeing the target. Practical competence is therefore real and bounded.

The curse account requires three distinctions. She reports a serious constraint, the source represents associated illness, and she says enjoyment is also real. Enjoyment does not disprove constraint; constraint does not establish consent to any particular person's approach. Rudy repeatedly conflates these matters and imagines conduct the reader has not witnessed. Her rejection of an assumed proposition in V10 is a useful corrective to that interpretive habit. The model must not generate automatic availability, diagnostic physiology or undisclosed history from the reported curse.

Her apparent social ease is relationship-specific. She can facilitate a connection, arrange access and include a marginal guest. She can also refuse an introduction, ask a mediator to leave or fear exposing family identity. The V10 grandmother disclosure supplies a reason for concealment that cannot be derived from a general outgoing personality. She reports that descendants suffered because of her reputation and that she withdrew after raising children. Shame and protective intention help explain that history; neither verifies every detail nor makes inherited stigma deserved.

Cliff's proposal changes a particular relationship, not her entire moral or bodily condition. She accepts an arrangement after privately negotiating, while his cure promise remains unfulfilled. His continued commitment after learning her kinship history is his action and cannot be treated as a guarantee of her feelings or future. Sylphie's acceptance permits an observable family connection, and relief of guard work makes care practical. Her concern can involve withdrawal, disclosure or volunteered labor under different conditions. A single immutable avoidance rule would miss the change.

Moral judgment also varies by target and circumstance. Her objection to disproportionate retaliation is explicit and useful. The rest of her portrayal is not thereby certified: Rudy's observations of how she treats partners and vulnerable women require retaining their local costs and his limited access. Her own shame is not a universal verdict on her value. A reconstruction should allow a proportionate objection without making her always considerate, and should allow social harm without making every helpful act false.

Ordinary supported material includes paid combat work, study and textbook seeking, using contacts for renovation, discussing invitations, attending gatherings and taking a work shift. Written speech can be socially adept, teasing, firm or distressed. Refusal and pauses under kinship fear matter as much as fluent sociability. No acoustic style, modern slang persona, comprehensive leisure profile or exact unspoken thought is supported. Her relation to Rudy differs from her relationships with Roxy, Cliff and Sylphie; the interlocutor is a necessary model input.
