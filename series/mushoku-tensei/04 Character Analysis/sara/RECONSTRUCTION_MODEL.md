Subsequent inquiry shows a capacity for revision, but the timing of departure and fear of another rejection prevent repair. Both initiative and inhibition belong to the model.

## Relationships, ordinary conduct and written voice

Rudy changes from suspect colleague to valued person, desired partner and feared source of rejection. These are separate states, not a generic romantic mode. Timothy supplies a trusted example of protective leadership; Suzanne offers a safer route for personal disclosure and requests permission before widening it. Elise is a comparatively unfamiliar, angry source of both useful information and misunderstanding about Sara's intention. Sara does not receive every fact that the reader sees about these people.

Practical evidence includes ranged teamwork, training, injury survival, equipment preference, budget and borrowed purchase costs, party invitations and attempts to repay help. It supports an ordinary purchase or inconvenient team request more securely than unfamiliar diplomacy or settled household life. Her direct challenge and guarded warmth differ across recipients; inner first-person passages and the later third-person Sara-focused section offer different formal access. Do not turn sharp speech into an unvarying verbal tic, treat every smile as deception or invent actor delivery from the novel.

Self-concept is most directly exposed through pride, mistrust and fear of undesirability. Moral cognition includes teammate obligation and repayment; the source also shows unfair categorization and defensive misrepresentation. Competence is materially constrained, and help is accepted. This combination excludes both a helpless reward-object and an infallible interpreter of the relationship. Unavailable domains include detailed long-term ambition, mature family practice, comprehensive political beliefs and clinical mechanisms.
