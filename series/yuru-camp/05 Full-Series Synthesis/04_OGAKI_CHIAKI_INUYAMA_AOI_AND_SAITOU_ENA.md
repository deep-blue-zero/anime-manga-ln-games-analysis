# Ōgaki Chiaki, Inuyama Aoi and Saitō Ena

Substantive provisional character reference based on complete V19 transaction a2843050 and the separately committed older-primary admission 662e4d11. All 12 bounded complements and ER-006 are now canonical ledger inputs; their old story epochs and later admission remain distinct. It is a character reference through a current corpus boundary, not a finished-series account or a behavioral model.

## 1. Responsibility, evidence and temporal boundaries

Chiaki, Aoi and Ena have lives and activities that are not exhausted by what they do for Rin or Nadeshiko. Chiaki works, proposes routes, experiments with materials and eventually tries a solo camp; Aoi cooks, jokes, receives attention and learns road cycling; Ena researches gear, chooses how to spend, works, refuses invitations, rests, makes things and pursues her own curiosity. Their contributions to a group matter, but contribution is only one part of the evidence. A subject can want an activity, fail at a task, receive help, make an unhelpful joke or choose not to participate without disappearing into a supporting function.

This document reconstructs those three subjects separately before comparing them. Its longitudinal responsibility is to distinguish an earlier baseline, later demonstrated capacity, repeated practice and a new occasion. It does not need three matching transformation arcs. Aoi's road cycling begins much later than her cooking; Chiaki's solo experience does not retrospectively make early organization an expression of a hidden solitary identity; Ena's actual camp participation does not turn every earlier refusal into preparation for inevitable recruitment.

## 2. Ōgaki Chiaki: practical participation beyond the organizer label

### 2.1 Early initiative is a distribution of tasks, not a total personality

The early accepted baseline presents Chiaki as an organizer working within material limits. That broad account remains partly extracted under E03-03/CS05. It is a useful contrast with the quieter Rin, but it should not do the work of a complete early biography. Being the person who proposes an outing does not establish that Chiaki performs all research, controls the others' wishes or has more technical ability in every domain. Her initiative is clearest when the account names an actual contribution and its recipient.

V03 supplies one such contribution: Chiaki sends Rin route information derived from family travel, receives thanks and offers a group invitation which Rin refuses. The practical value of the information and the refusal coexist. Chiaki can be useful to someone whose preferred trip configuration differs from hers; Rin does not have to accept the invitation for that usefulness to count. The information has a local, remembered provenance rather than arising from an unexplained status as the group's travel expert. This is DE of particular help and invitation, with a bounded IN about practical contact across differing preferences. It does not establish equal familiarity with every friend. (E03-01; RS05; CM04; V03 literal0144–0147.)

The Christmas cooking sequence makes another distinction visible. Aoi leads the meal and gives Chiaki the onion and egg-distribution tasks; the later tomato version explicitly uses the onion Chiaki has already sautéed. Calling the meal Aoi's is appropriate at the level of initiative and direction; calling every performed step Aoi's would erase Chiaki's hands. Chiaki also asks about the dish and the choice of sukiyaki, making her an engaged recipient and conversational participant rather than merely a loud reaction figure. These are observations within one local sequence, not independent confirmations of a general service disposition. (E04-03/05; CS89; RS87–88; CM128; V04 literal0095–0108 and0109–0114.)

This modest scale is important. The scene does not need to show Chiaki becoming a different person. It adds usable detail to what practical participation means: receiving a task, performing preparatory work, asking questions and enabling another person's planned dish. A retrospective character account becomes more accurate by retaining those small acts than by assigning a single leadership rank.

### 2.2 Paid work and consequential failure belong to her own ordinary life

V05's holiday work exchange gives Chiaki a material and temporal life separate from the outings she helps arrange. She is the liquor-store worker reporting full shifts through January4. Rin's December31–January4 holiday window is a different message. ER-003 corrects the frozen attribution and prevents Chiaki's labor from becoming evidence about Rin's employment. The practical consequence is unequal availability: leisure plans are negotiated around actual schedules rather than being freely interchangeable. Neither the messages nor the workplace images establish total earnings, a household class or every shift she ever works. (E05-04; CS49; RS40; CM53; literal0017–0020.)

The same distinction prevents an overly flattering competence story. In V06 the Chiaki/Aoi/Ena outing encounters serious cold because preparation did not adequately account for weather and altitude. Nearby campers provide shelter and Toba intervenes after Rin's remote escalation. Chiaki's capacity to organize a trip does not make the original plan safe. Conversely, shared failure should not be assigned wholly to her because an organizer is narratively convenient to blame. The accepted observations locate collective underpreparation, recognition and externally supported recovery. (E06-01/02; CS12; RS11; CM09; V06 literal0130–0154.)

This is a strong counterexample to two reductive profiles. A comic planner is not necessarily harmless: cold exposure is a different domain from an unsatisfactory sausage. A fallible planner is not therefore incapable of useful planning: V03's route information survives the later mistake. The warranted IN is domain-specific and conditional. Chiaki's actions become legible through information, equipment, companions and available support, not through a stable numerical competence score.

V08 then supplies a specific planning contribution that survives the correction of an older adult-centered summary. Rin asks to take her scooter; Ena supports the request; Chiaki proposes leaving the scooter at camp and using the car for sightseeing; Toba grants permission with caution. Chiaki originates that mitigation, while permission remains the teacher's act. The proposal neither makes her author of the whole Izu journey nor eliminates fatigue and road risk. It does show a peer adapting arrangements around someone else's desired experience rather than treating group travel as a single compulsory mode. (E08-04; RS44; CM57; ER-004; V08 literal0010–0014, decisive0013.)

### 2.3 Experiment, narration and making require different evidence

The accepted V10/V12 analyses preserve Chiaki's food experiments, later recipe revision and performed account of the trio's outing to the absent Nadeshiko. These are substantial parts of her characterization, but their broad ledger routes E10-03/E12-02 remain extracted. They support a qualified account of iterative practice in the accepted corpus; they do not newly certify every ingredient, technique, magazine source, speaker or evaluation in the uninspected range. This document therefore uses them as an analysis-led lane, not as a source of fresh exact Japanese or a hard general rule. (CM17/22; RS20; V12 §§2–4,9.)

Two distinctions survive that limit. First, trying and revising food is not the same risk problem as the V06 cold failure. A recoverable taste experiment does not retroactively make every outdoor error low stakes. Second, comic retelling is a relationship event with an absent listener. Nadeshiko receives an account, including exaggeration and correction, rather than acquiring firsthand memories of the outing. Chiaki's performance can be funny without proving habitual dishonesty in work, planning and every other setting. Aoi's correcting part must also remain hers.

V19 provides a more tightly verified independent making sequence. Chiaki uses an online pattern and tools to cut leather, punch holes, stitch and finish two covers. The process receives enough attention for the result to be more than a claimed off-panel hobby. Her `できたー！` at spine141 accompanies her holding the finished objects; it is self-directed satisfaction, not a technical lesson delivered to an audience. The comic ending arrives after one day, and later conversation exposes uncertainty about what to make next. (E19-16; CS81; RS83; JV66; preferred spines140–142,147.)

This episode adds an actual capacity while retaining its scale. Using a public pattern is not inventing the design; completing two covers is not comprehensive craft mastery. The first-day joke is especially useful contrary evidence against a smoothly escalating development narrative. Chiaki can complete what she set out to make and immediately face the ordinary problem of choosing another project. Pleasure in a bounded result does not need to become a vocational identity or a moral lesson about persistence.

Her later phone display to Aoi/Mei also keeps reception separate from manufacture. The friends can encourage further activity without having performed the tool work. Their cycling and her craft proceed at different speeds, under different bodily and material constraints. Shared communication connects those activities without converting them into one collective project.

### 2.4 Solo experience, local help and institutional work

The accepted V13 account gives Chiaki a first solo camp and a tentative experiential connection with Rin's practice. That whole solo route remains analysis-extracted under E13-03/CS27/CM24. It is reasonable to preserve the completed experience as accepted analytical history, while withholding newly precise procedural or voice claims without their narrower source check. One solo attempt expands her repertoire; it does not show that solitude has become her dominant preference or that she now knows Rin's inner life.

The separate Aoi encounter in V13 has a primary route. Chiaki supplies an improvised brake-holding aid and shares an unhurried view with her. The practical task matters precisely because it is limited: stabilization is not puncture repair. Quiet company is likewise a real interaction without requiring a revelation that the apparently noisy subject was secretly quiet all along. Both practical ingenuity and ease in a less performative configuration can be observed without replacing one caricature with another. (E13-04; CS30; RS23; CM26.)

Mei's subsequent enrollment increases the importance of how a group's arrangements respond to an actual newcomer. Chiaki participates in recruitment and support planning, and V15 literal0137 depicts her writing the promotion application. Enrollment, the numerical threshold, application-writing and completed school approval are four different states. Through V19 the last remains open. Chiaki performs institutional work; this does not supply authority to declare its administrative outcome or a completed succession. (E14-03/04; E15-04; CS35; CM32; CR07/08/46/52.)

The V12 imagined coercive junior labor is therefore not an enacted policy toward Mei. V16's hammock and hobby material also retains bonus/fantasy boundaries: the impossible portal, handlift touring and contemplated hobbies cannot establish travel, licenses or a new dog. Keeping the joke as a joke does not discard its characterization. It preserves initiative, playful overstatement and material limitation at the level the source actually supplies. (E12-03; E16-15; CS25/48; CM51–52.)

### 2.5 Voice, limits and the strongest rival account

Chiaki's verified language is thinner than her activity record. In V10 `詳しいっすね` addresses Toba during the stove exchange. It supports one colloquial polite teacher-directed turn, not an exclusive pronoun or an all-purpose speech generator. The same -ssu family cannot identify an unnamed worker as Mei: speaker identity and recipient context have priority over a familiar ending. In V19 the brief craft celebration supports expressive satisfaction in another mode. Neither sample certifies her whole narration voice, everyday self-reference distribution or speech under serious interpersonal strain. (JV16/66.)

The strongest economical rival is that Chiaki is simply the comic organizer whose mistakes and reactions keep scenes moving. That captures a recurring formal role, but it leaves out paid work, useful route information, concrete preparation, a peer mitigation proposal, bounded quiet help and completed craft. The opposite rival—an always improving practical leader—fails on consequential underpreparation, unevenly verified recipe/solo routes and the anticlimax after the finished covers. A better bounded IN is that Chiaki repeatedly turns interest into proposals, experiments, tasks and shared accounts while relying on resources and other people's capacities. Her practice is active and fallible. The available record does not warrant a claim that she always learns the right lesson, becomes a comprehensive leader or organizes everyone else's private life.

## 3. Inuyama Aoi: cooking, play and later beginner learning

### 3.1 The earlier subject is not explained by the later bicycle

Aoi's early state is comparatively thin in the original backfill. E03-03/CS06 preserves group participation and conversational calibration at extracted status. That limitation should remain visible rather than being filled by the much later cycling arc. Road cycling supplies new scenes, actions and capacities beginning in V13; it is not an explanation of her V03 knowledge or an invitation to describe an already expert early Aoi.

The older retrieval matrix also identifies early teasing and personal birthday reception as useful accepted-analysis leads, with insufficient newly isolated primary scope for the exact details. This document does not invent fresh quotations or use those leads to certify a complete private life. They identify a real asymmetry in the evidence: Aoi is more precisely recoverable as a cook, a sister making a travel request, a participant in failure and a later beginner than across every ordinary family, work or romantic context.

Her cooking nevertheless deserves independent treatment now. The Christmas scene is not merely a preliminary version of cycling mentorship, nor an abstract sign that she is the group's mother. It is a sequence of decisions and labor: actual first preparation, delegation, participant reactions, a menu explanation, a tomato remake and an announced further course interrupted by lack of fuel. That sequence gives both capacity and practical limits at an earlier epoch. (E04-03–06; CS88; RS87–90; CM128–130.)

### 3.2 Meal leadership includes other hands and received pleasure

Aoi identifies a preparation style, asks Chiaki to sauté onion and later uses it in the tomato version. The dishes are actually eaten. Nadeshiko praises her cooking and Rin explicitly thanks Inuyama; Aoi replies `おそまつさま`. The profile must include her receiving appreciation as well as providing food. Not every reaction has the same mode: Rin's extended tasting narration is partly reader-accessible interiority, whereas her later thanks is directed speech Aoi receives. (V04 literal0098,0102–0106,0111–0114; E04-03; JV74/76.)

The request `炒めといてくれへん？` is a source-verified regional form used for a specific peer task. It shows Aoi directing work without making all the work hers. Her announced cheese-pasta finish establishes an intention, not its accomplished preparation inside the newly inspected0095–0122 scope. When gas runs out, Nadeshiko's missing replacement, Chiaki's suggested resource, Toba's exhausted supply and Rin's proposed errand become separate contributions and limits. Cooking competence does not include an infallible surrounding fuel system.

The menu rationale has a different source mode. After Chiaki asks why sukiyaki, Aoi recounts an earlier household conversation. Grandmother speaks the maxim about eating it together on special days; present Aoi supplies `ちゅー訳や`. ER-006 preserves the explicit occasion reasoning but removes the maxim as a literal sample of Aoi's voice. Nadeshiko's present appraisal of year-end pot-sharing remains correctly attributed. The correction does not erase Aoi's ability to transmit family knowledge. It gives that ability a traceable source and avoids inventing hereditary talent from a remembered sentence. (E04-05; RS88; JV75; CR54; V04 literal0106–0108.)

The later V17 curry remake has another precise direction. Nadeshiko attributes the oden-to-curry idea to Aoi while actually making/eating it with Ena and Ema. Aoi matters in her absence through transmitted knowledge. The current conversation verifies the attribution; it does not show the original lesson, put Aoi at Nozori or give her authorship of that particular pot. These separate occasions support a bounded RP of culinary contribution and circulation, with different means of access, rather than a permanent domestic role imposed on every configuration. (E17-12; RS55; CM78; preferred spine148 within137–150.)

### 3.3 Play and correction do not form opposite identities

Aoi's humor cannot be reduced to calm truth-telling. In the primary V12 junior-labor fantasy she tells Chiaki `パワハラはだめよ`; the line is comic boundary-setting toward an imagined abuse, not proof that juniors have actually been compelled. In V18 Heya129 she frightens Nadeshiko with a fabricated mutual-finance conspiracy and admits that it was detected: `やっぱバレてもうたか`. The intervening challenger's portrait remains unnamed. Toba's separate plain explanation is hers. (E12-03; JV18; E18-19; CS71; RS69; JV56/57.)

Heya130 then gives Aoi and Chiaki a collaborative false etymology. Ena observes to Rin that incorrect history is being made, and Rin assents. Those observers' knowledge is not automatically a correction received by Nadeshiko. The invented origin cannot be reused as linguistic history. Nor should the strip make every Aoi explanation unreliable: the Christmas request and admitted beginner statement have their own direct support. (E18-20; CS72; RS70; JV58; preferred161–162.)

The V19 mushroom joke reverses the easy allocation of prank author and gullible recipient. Nadeshiko deliberately invents the book-cultivation story, Chiaki and Aoi believe it, and Nadeshiko privately recognizes the opportunity created by food-related credibility. Aoi is the recipient here. This is a useful contrary instance to an infallibly perceptive corrector profile; it does not establish universal gullibility, and the private admission is not disclosed to her. (E19-19; CS85; RS86; CM127; spines159–160.)

These scenes support relationship- and mode-specific play. They give no basis for an unqualified moral contrast between the honest Ena and deceptive Aoi, or between the clever older student and an always credulous Nadeshiko. Formal performance distributes temporary positions, and later knowledge can reverse them. A voice profile must retain the actual admission and request forms without turning regional speech into a guarantee of either truth or joking.

### 3.4 A later capacity begins with equipment, effort and recovery

V13 establishes an actual road cyclist. The gifted bicycle, practice, route extension, fear, puncture and pickup belong to Aoi, as the accepted V13 V1.2 and CR06 specify. Imagined Nadeshiko cycling elsewhere in that volume is a separate mode. The gift enables action; it does not transmit a finished competence. Her extended ride produces both pleasure and a limit that she cannot solve unaided. Chiaki's local brake aid and family transport keep helpers' tasks distinct. (E13-04; CS29/30; RS23/24; CM25/26.)

The expert-road-cyclist father story is retracted as an April Fool joke. It cannot provide a pedigree for later technical success. At V14, however, Aoi privately recalls particular puncture instruction from her father. The retracted broad history does not invalidate that separately supported instruction, just as the instruction cannot restore the broad pedigree. Here she inventories uncertainty about tube/valve compatibility and refrains from an uncertain fix. The help she actually arranges is teacher-truck pickup. Restraint and obtaining transport are practical acts; neither is a demonstrated completed repair. (E14-01; CS31; RS26; CM27; CR15; literal0075–0078.)

This sequence provides an especially valuable knowledge boundary. The reader receives Aoi's doubts and recalled instruction as thought. Mei is not shown receiving the same reasoning or family history. A later account can describe Aoi as privately aware of limits while still withholding a claim that she taught Mei those specific limits. A temporal profile must record the new capacity and the absence of that transfer separately.

V15 makes the comparative scale explicit. Aoi names herself a recent beginner; Mei feels relief at a near-peer pace after the sports-club experience. The line `初心者やからな` matters because it directly constrains the tempting expert-mentor shorthand. Relative seniority, a previous ride and willingness to help are not comprehensive technical mastery. Toba's express pickup option makes continued effort compatible with stopping rather than turning completion into an obligation. The broad later execution arc remains E15-03 at extracted status; the precise beginning and retreat provision have primary routes. (E15-01/02; CS34; RS28; CM30; JV23; literal0012–0015,0026–0029.)

The defensible IN is co-learning with unequal local experience. It is not that both riders always have identical capacities, nor that support proves the trip risk-free. Aoi can be more experienced in a particular situation, learn something herself and remain dependent in another. That distribution fits the cooking/fuel distinction as well: an ability is attached to a task and conditions rather than to a universal rank.

### 3.5 V19 tests continuation through work, soreness and choice

V16–V18 do not supply a new main-story cycling performance for Aoi or Mei. Their absence from those tests cannot be read as a plateau or as invisible improvement. V19's final main chapter supplies the next actual practice evidence: commuting and repeated riding around supermarket shifts, praise for persistence, soreness, slower progress and negotiated rest. This is a newly shown continuation of an activity under daily constraints. Its chapter placement does not make every scene chronologically subsequent to the Rin/Ayano reunion; the Golden Week activities have parallel/retrospective framing. (E19-15; CS82/83; RS81/83; CM121–122; spines135–136,138–139,143,151–153.)

Aoi proposes effort with a provisional destination rather than guaranteeing the lake: `行けるとこまで頑張ろか`. When conditions change, she offers stopping or rest. Mei supplies her own response, asking for a pause and a little further climbing. The revised endpoint becomes a nearer social photograph rather than a completed Motosuko ride. Aoi initiates that idea, Mei accepts it and Saki appears at the house window. Rin is away; receiving the image does not physically join her to the cyclists. (E19-14/15; RS81/85; JV67/69; spines132,153,157–158.)

Mei's private question about trying Karisaka from a fresher starting point preserves her own evaluation. The previous fifty-kilometer approach is a condition she now considers; it is not a completed retry or an announced plan Aoi has heard. The school-club senior's later recognition also belongs to Mei's history and present agency. Her welcome values continued road cycling at one's own pace without requiring sports-club membership. Aoi and Mei can receive it together without having identical biographies. (E19-15/17; CS83; RS82; JV68/70; spines144–146,152,154–156.)

These scenes support a limited developmental statement. Aoi has more repeated practice and participates in a workable pace negotiation with a junior who also initiates. They do not establish an achieved distant destination, an expert training regime or uninterrupted improvement. Soreness and changed plans are part of the observed practice, not defects that a progress account can omit. The alternative of portraying self-paced cycling as the opposite of all competitive sport is also too strong: a member of the demanding club offers the welcome. The work distinguishes enjoyment and configurations without making every institution a single antagonist.

### 3.6 Family, receiving help and voice remain independent lanes

The sister relationship has an exact earlier route. Aoi asks Toba to include her younger sister and discusses bedding; Toba conditions participation on parental approval and offers equipment. Akari is Inuyama Akari, not a Kagamihara. The request evidences Aoi's initiative and a particular family tie, but not a complete account of either sister's preferences or a standing obligation to attend every outing. The mistaken older verification reference to Sakura entrusting Nadeshiko remains withdrawn under ER-002. (E08-02; CS17; RS15; V08 literal0007–0010.)

Receiving a bicycle, accepting transport, enjoying food and receiving appreciation belong alongside giving help. The evidence should not select only moments when Aoi supplies something to others. At the same time, a complete family biography would require more than the available gifts, recollections and requested inclusion. Her father's specifically remembered instruction, grandmother's menu rationale and bicycle provision are distinct source occasions; no total family ethos is inferred from putting them beside one another.

Her Japanese samples show regional forms across different practical and comic settings: the meal request, the flashback bridge, `忘れとった` when recognizing overlooked altitude/weather, the greeting `おはよーさん`, the beginner statement to Mei and the later admission that a story was exposed. This is enough to support a recurrent regional voice at a bounded level. It is not an exhaustive dialect distribution or permission to generate exaggerated generic Kansai. Her peer request, senior-to-junior self-positioning, greeting and admission each retain a recipient and situation. The grandmother quotation cannot fill a gap in Aoi's own syntax. (JV11/13/23/56/67/74–76.)

The strongest rival is a stable reassuring helper who cooks and corrects while other people generate activity. That account misses her own teasing, her later adventurous beginner practice, received assistance and choices under fatigue. The rival of an expert who changes hobby domains fails more directly on explicit beginner status, compatibility doubts, pickup and near-peer pacing. A bounded synthesis can recognize a person who contributes, plays, pursues a new activity and manages limits without treating her as either a universal caretaker or an exemplary finished learner.

## 4. Saitō Ena: chosen participation, personal interests and reciprocal connection

### 4.1 Mediation is an action in a relationship, not her whole purpose

Ena's early practical mediation is specific. In V02 she encourages Rin to give the souvenir herself; Rin then initiates a shared meat-camping plan and Nadeshiko volunteers research. Ena's prompt matters without making either friend a passive object. V03's `違うジャンルの楽しさ` offers Rin another category for group enjoyment after the refusal, and Rin's eventual reply remains consideration rather than attendance. The source supports a low-pressure reframe in that exchange, not a universal therapeutic script or complete noninterference in others' choices. (E02-02; E03-02; CS03; RS04/06; JV07/08.)

The wider accepted scene includes playful persuasion, while the earlier retrieval matrix retains extra detail about a caught small lie as a navigation lead rather than a newly isolated primary claim here. There is no need to invent the exact line to qualify the broader point. Ena participates in trying to change a friend's mind; respectful optionality and influence can coexist. Her effectiveness cannot be generalized into omniscient knowledge of everyone else's motives.

V04's work lead supplies independent initiative in a different dyad. Nadeshiko describes difficulty finding work; Ena proposes the short-term card job and explains online entry; Nadeshiko eagerly accepts the proposal. Rin is present but does not mediate the help. The invitation is neither actual completed employment nor an enduring authority over Nadeshiko's finances. It gives Ena a practical resource to offer and Nadeshiko a choice to take. (E04-04; CS91; RS89; CM129; literal0100–0101.)

This distinction prevents a retrospective account from crediting every favorable connection to Rin/Nadeshiko's central friendship. Ena has a workplace route of her own and brings information from it. The later actual V05 work is a subsequent witness with different actions; counting the earlier lead and later work as two independent demonstrations of job performance would be a mistake.

### 4.2 Work, research, spending and refusal make a personal baseline

The V05 lunch conversation places Ena beside Nadeshiko as another worker. She brings her own food and reports the indoor task, including the difficulty of reading addresses and handwriting. Nadeshiko reports her outdoor work and asks questions. Ena's comic mock-deferential formulation about working in warmth remains Ena's speech, not Nadeshiko's. The conversational relation is reciprocal without requiring identical experiences or a new direct view of every indoor procedure. (E05-09; CS93; RS92; CM132; literal0010–0016.)

Ena is also the one who reports watching camping videos and researching tents. Nadeshiko's first answer to a snow-capable-tent question is tentative; Ena supplies the further explanation. The knowledge direction complicates the assumption that the more visibly enthusiastic camper always teaches the optional participant. At this boundary the exact technical account is manga-internal reported information, not an independently validated outdoor instruction. Ena considers buying after wages; no tent is actually purchased in that scene.

Nadeshiko then invites her to Outclub and Ena retains her going-home-club preference. The refusal need not be converted into an inevitable future recruitment step, and it does not erase interest in camping equipment or a proposed shared shop stop. Choice of institutional membership, interest in an activity and occasional participation are different variables. They remain different when Ema later becomes an actual camper without established Outclub enrollment.

V06 adds a strongly personal material preference. Ena reports that she has already ordered Chikuwa's tent after looking for a dog sleeping bag. She describes the displayed product and postpones buying a tent for herself, expecting to use friends' equipment. The displayed ten-thousand-yen price and order report make the desire concrete, but catalogue images are not delivery or setup. Her borrowing expectation has no named lender's consent and is not yet an actual loan. Pet-directed spending and delayed self-equipment are both her choices. They do not prove a complete financial biography or an inexhaustibly self-denying character. (E06-03; CS94; RS93; CM133; literal0019–0022.)

The conversation also keeps Rin's modes apart. Rin outwardly gives a restrained `別に…` while privately emphatic about wanting to see Chikuwa in it. Ena's `漏れちゃってるよ リン` teases the visible mismatch. It does not give her supernatural access to the exact private sentence. Familiarity can support a locally accurate observation without establishing that Ena always interprets Rin correctly. (JV86/87.)

Work later limits co-presence without becoming an injury to the relationship. In the V13 park messages Ena reports an afternoon shift and subsequent days off, apologizes for the mismatched camp schedule and receives Chiaki's reply that everyone works and the constraint is mutual. Her `あきちゃん` and safe-travel wish have a specific recipient. Chiaki's first solo camp is still a reported/prospective trip in this new bounded scope; its full execution belongs to the accepted analytical route elsewhere. Ena then rests with Chikuwa rather than treating every available interval as a duty to support someone. (E13-05; CS97; RS98; CM138; JV88; literal0089–0090.)

Her older V09 license wish remains an extracted aspiration, not acquired mobility. No V16 parental station ride or V17 train/taxi competence can supply missing evidence of Ena driving. Cost, working time, gear interest, pet preferences and rest establish a more concrete person precisely when they are not collapsed into a global independence score. (E09-01; CS18; CM14; E16-06.)

### 4.3 Curiosity and consent have observable local forms

The V13 park art sequence gives Ena an active encounter which is not reducible to recruiting a junior. Ema first draws the landscape and then makes a portrait of the sleeping Ena/Chikuwa without prior permission. When Ena notices, Ema apologizes and explains the dog's appeal. Ena responds warmly and praises the drawing. She asks about the phone-connected tablet, requests a trial, receives Ema's permission and actually draws. (E13-06; CS97/98; RS99; CM139/140; literal0091–0099.)

The request `ちょっと描いてみていい？` and Ema's `あどうぞ` are two turns from two speakers. They establish permission for the device trial, not retroactive permission for the earlier portrait. The distinction is analytically useful because it prevents a pleasant encounter from becoming an idealized claim of flawless consent in every act. Ena asks, experiments and asks price; Ema provides knowledge and grants access. The approximate price is a character report, and no purchase or permanent equipment transfer follows. (JV89.)

The new scope ends as Ena's work alarm begins farewell. It stops before later name, school and career dialogue. The analyst may index the artist retrospectively as Ema, but cannot give Ena that later name knowledge at literal0097 simply because the identity is now familiar. Ema's private thoughts about posture and breed remain the reader's access, not facts Ena receives. The absence of a formal introduction in this span does not invalidate the actual exchange; it limits its knowledge state.

This early contact supplies a better foundation for Ena/Ema than the corrected-away V15 advice attribution. In V15 Ena offers the home/Chikuwa invitation. Rin gives Ema the conditional camping answer. ER-001 removes one piece of support from an overbroad Ena adviser profile while preserving Ena's genuine part in providing a particular place, activity and social access. Different useful acts belong to different people. (E15-05; CS37; RS29/30; JV24/25.)

### 4.4 V16–V17: initiative, dependence and received care

In V16 Ena cuts Rin's requested hair, jokes about its changed possibilities and receives thanks. Ema's polite admiration observes dexterity; neither the haircut nor the compliment certifies a professional qualification. Rin's concise, blunt replies belong to this familiar dyad. Ena's imagined absent friends are part of her joke, not actual objections those people have communicated. (E16-01; RS32; JV26–28.)

Ena independently chooses to join Nadeshiko's journey, participates in alternatives and shares the eventual trip. Early-morning sleep makes her rely on Ema's navigation and preserved scenery, while her questions, thanks and later activity remain visible. Temporary difficulty at one part of a journey is not global incompetence or absence of agency. Conversely, a profile that cites only her useful interventions would conceal the dependence and the chosen rest. (E16-03/04/06/07; RS34–37.)

V17 makes reciprocal assistance more explicit. Ema forgets the chair she tested at home. Ena offers hers, persists past polite refusal and explains `ごろ寝で寛ぐのも好きだから`. The direct statement of her reclining preference is decisive for a local interpretation: giving the chair helps Ema while leaving Ena an arrangement she also enjoys. It is neither necessary to deny the generosity nor to invent complete self-sacrifice. (E17-04; CS60; RS51; CM66; JV42; spines044–046.)

At the bungalow Ena makes paracord gear for Chikuwa and later sleeps; Ema notices exposure, brings additional bedding and wraps her. The two actions occur at different times and do not create a contract of repayment. Ena is a receiver of care as well as a provider. The unnamed person who taught the paracord skill cannot be identified as Chiaki merely because Chiaki has useful paracord evidence elsewhere. Particular source attributions take priority over the convenience of a neat club teaching chain. (E17-11; RS51/53; CM76/77; spines122–134.)

She also contributes particular knowledge. After Ema points to snow as an additional reason for the bright night, Ena explains reflectivity; Nadeshiko registers its unfamiliarity from her own experience. Observation, explanation and reception are separate contributions. Ena's explanation is not Ema's, and useful practical knowledge does not require the speaker to be the main initiator of every trip. (E17-12; RS55; spines145–146.)

The later trip leaves the earlier counterevidence intact. Ena participated in V06 underpreparation along with Chiaki and Aoi. The later bungalow comfort depends on borrowed equipment, changed shelter, ordinary provisions and contributions from several people. Social ease and pet-gear interest do not prove environmental mastery, and the successful later arrangement does not rewrite the earlier mistake. Her completed participation can be positive without certifying every future activity as suitable or safe.

### 4.5 Voice and transmitted knowledge need recipient boundaries

Ena's verified language includes a reframe to Rin, familiar teasing and address, the self-description behind a chair offer, a request to Ema and explicit correction of invented history. These are different kinds of act. `ウソだから` in V12 retracts an origin story and prevents its plausible details from becoming biography. In V18 her comment to Rin about fabricated history identifies a false account without necessarily informing Nadeshiko. Exact shared childhood origins, full family context and a universal truth-teller disposition remain unsupported. (JV07/19/42/58/87–89.)

V17 also records Ena reporting Rin's fire reluctance, reading and free-time view. That is an information edge from an absent friend through Ena, not a new direct conversation between Rin and Ema. Ema's private question about whether camping suits her does not become an opinion Ena has heard. Later Ema actually asks to camp again and the seniors agree with a nearer future destination proposed. The later explicit request is a new public act, not evidence that the earlier private thought was already shared. (E17-07/11/14; RS53/56.)

A character account should therefore preserve Ena's access and limits symmetrically. She knows enough about Rin to relay particular views; she is not a transparent window onto every reason behind them. She recognizes some falsehoods; this does not make her a narrator of all truths. She can welcome a junior, lend, ask, nap and be cared for without those being stages in a fixed professional role as everyone's mediator.

The strongest rival profile is an exceptionally perceptive helper whose own wishes barely matter. It explains some gentle prompts but fails on research, spending, explicit refusal, curiosity and her stated pleasure in reclining. A passive optional-participant profile fails on the work lead, invitations, device request, haircut, chair offer, craft and knowledge contribution. The bounded IN is selective and reciprocal participation: Ena retains particular interests and choices while contributing to configurations she elects to enter. Its recurrence is supported across different contexts, but it remains a literary account, not a guarantee of her response to every future request.

## 5. Comparison without fixed role assignments

### 5.1 They distribute initiative, error and correction

The convenient trio of organizer Chiaki, stabilizing Aoi and mediating Ena describes recurrent emphases but fails as an exhaustive explanation. Chiaki also receives tasks, supplies quiet local help and makes objects for herself. Aoi initiates play and new cycling, receives assistance and is sometimes fooled. Ena makes practical offers, pursues material interests and rests. All three can take part in the same consequential planning error. The differences become more useful when indexed by task, recipient and epoch rather than converted into exclusive jobs.

Several contrasts have good DE foundations. Aoi directs a dish in V04 while Chiaki performs delegated work; Ena supplies an independent employment lead during that same gathering. At V06 the practical contrast is shared cold underpreparation, not one member's total expertise. At V08 Chiaki authors a mitigation around Rin's desired transport; Ena supplies support and Toba permission. At V19 Chiaki's craft and Aoi/Mei's ride are linked by messages while proceeding through separate processes. These cases do not prove perfect equality. They show why agency must be assigned locally. (E04-03/04/06; E06-01/02; E08-04; E19-14–17.)

Correction also travels differently. Aoi's explicit altitude recognition concerns an actual mistake; her junior-labor objection concerns fantasy; Ena's retraction concerns a false biography; the Heya etymology observers concern performed misinformation. Calling all these “the group's corrective function” would lose the object, recipient and stakes. It would also make it too easy to pool reader knowledge with what the characters actually learn.

### 5.2 Work and materials keep participation concrete

Chiaki's holiday shifts, Ena's seasonal and later work reports, Aoi/Mei's ride around supermarket shifts and the group's dependence on fuel, transport and equipment keep free time from becoming a frictionless resource. The evidence does not supply complete household budgets. It does repeatedly show schedules, prices, desired purchases, omitted supplies and access conditions entering decisions.

The forms of support differ. Aoi's requested preparatory labor is not the same as receiving a gifted bicycle. Ena's anticipated borrowing is not the same as her actual chair loan. Chiaki's technical aid is not a full repair, and a teacher's permission is not a guarantee of road access. The material-culture account should preserve these differences before making claims about mutual care. An observed transfer can be helpful, pleasurable, practical and ordinary at once; it does not automatically prove a single moral motivation.

### 5.3 Newer participants remain subjects

Mei's presence is not simply evidence that Aoi became a good mentor or Chiaki achieved succession. She joins by choice, brings cycling interest, evaluates pace, proposes rest followed by more riding and privately asks a conditional question about her earlier limit. Ema is not evidence of Ena's inevitable recruitment success. She draws, works, relays specialist advice, changes travel decisions, forgets equipment, gives care, names cost and asks to repeat. The three older subjects encounter choices and capacities that belong to those newer people. (E14-01/03/04; E15-01/02/05; E16-04–10; E17-03/04/08/11/14; E19-15/17.)

The institutional distinctions remain explicit through V19. Mei's existing Outclub membership is separate from sports-club membership and from the school's formal circle-to-club approval. Ema's first actual camping trip and repeat wish do not establish enrollment or a second completed camp. The application and imagined wood future do not supply a literal junior handoff. Retaining these open states does not diminish the actual adaptations already observed.
