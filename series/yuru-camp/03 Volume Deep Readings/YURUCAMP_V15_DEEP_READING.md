# 1. Executive orientation

Volume 15 gives the V14 cycling plan exactly the kind of stress test the prospective method requires. The plan does not collapse, but neither is its success produced by simple toughness.

Aoi and Mei ride far enough to become tired, encounter construction detours, move from comfortable cycling roads into sustained climbing, stop repeatedly for food and recovery, reach points where their speed drops dramatically, and eventually encounter a hard infrastructural boundary at the Karisaka Tunnel toll road. The support vehicle remains available throughout and is ultimately used to recover the cyclists after the endpoint of their ride. None of this is treated as humiliation or failed adventure.

That distinction is the central V15 result.

> **The cycling camp succeeds because its difficulty is real and its exits are real.**

Volume 14 proposed a trip in which different participants could carry different burdens while still converging on one shared camp. Volume 15 now shows the architecture functioning under pressure. Aoi and Mei receive the bodily experience of the route. Chiaki and Toba move luggage by car, establish camp, and pursue their own Karisaka-area leisure. Rin follows a separate solo route and happens to intersect the others near the tunnel. The group reconverges without retroactively pretending that everyone had the same journey.

This is the strongest confirmation yet of the project's narrow `MODULAR_GROUP_TRAVEL` concept. It should now be treated as a mature recurrent pattern rather than an Izu/Oigawa-specific convenience.

The second major result revises how Aoi's emerging seniority should be described. V14 positioned her as the more experienced cyclist in relation to Mei, but V15 refuses to transform that relative advantage into expert status. At a break Aoi explicitly says:

> `私もこないだロードを始めたばっかの初心者やからなぁ`
>
> (`V15 / 0014.jpg`)

Mei answers that they are at about the same level and that riding at the same pace is fun:

> `私も同じくらいなんで 一緒のペースで走れて楽しいんス`

Aoi immediately turns the statement into a comic threat about giving no more quarter. The joke matters because it contains the correction inside the relationship: Aoi is a `先輩`, but she is not a finished cycling authority. The pair's relation is therefore best described as **asymmetric in social position, shallowly asymmetric in experience, and increasingly peer-like in embodied learning**.

This substantially strengthens the V14 `RECIPROCAL_TRANSMISSION` hypothesis. Aoi can still offer route-reading, pacing, and road-bike tips, but Mei is not a passive pupil. Her enthusiasm changes the trip; her own reactions help regulate pace; and both riders discover what prolonged climbing and fatigue feel like together. The senior does not stand outside the experiment and teach it. She participates in the same uncertain system.

The third major result concerns **supported autonomy**. Before the main climb, Toba explicitly tells the cyclists that if they think they cannot continue, they should give up without worrying because the car can come get them immediately (`V15 / 0028.jpg`). That contingency remains available rather than ceremonial. Later, after genuine exhaustion and the infrastructural limit around the tunnel, the support side becomes the pickup mechanism.

The important inference is not “help makes challenge easy.” The ride is visibly hard. Rather:

> **Support can increase the amount of difficulty a person can responsibly choose because withdrawal remains available without shame.**

This is a stronger formulation than the earlier generic language of “care without takeover.” The support car does not tell Mei and Aoi when to stop. It makes stopping recoverable.

A fourth result is the volume's handling of **success**. Mei finishes the trip saying:

> `初サイクリングキャンプ めっちゃ楽しかったっス!!`
>
> (`V15 / 0133.jpg`)

That statement comes after fatigue, climbing, reduced speed, repeated rests, food stops, a hot spring, and an endpoint that is partly defined by road infrastructure rather than heroic conquest. The pleasure therefore cannot be reduced to effortless mastery. The trip gives Mei enough friction to become meaningful without converting suffering into proof of worth.

The fifth major result belongs to Ema and is deliberately quieter. V14 showed that Ema could remain socially adjacent to the Outclub and outdoor material culture without becoming a camper or club member. V15 preserves that freedom. Her chapter centers on illustration, dogs, observation, Chikuwa, conversation with Ena, and uncertainty about Golden Week. Only near the end does she ask Ena:

> `キャンプってやっぱり面白いですか？`
>
> (`V15 / 0159.jpg`)

Ena's answer is unusually useful counterevidence against any conversion narrative:

> `ハマる人はハマるけど 人によるかも`
>
> `時間の過ごし方がわからないって人もいるし`

Camping may strongly suit some people and not others; some people do not know what to do with the time. Ena then says:

> `興味あるなら一回やってみたら分かると思うよ`
>
> (`V15 / 0160.jpg`)

At the V15 boundary, Ema has moved from **non-camping participation** to **self-authored curiosity about trying camping once**. She has not joined the Outclub, adopted a camper identity, or accepted anyone else's judgment that she ought to camp. This is one of the cleanest examples of repertoire expansion under preserved difference so far: exposure can alter possibility without dictating identity.

The sixth major result is negative but important. Volume 15 does **not** pay off the V12 firewood inheritance hypothesis. A junior now exists; the club has an inter-cohort future; but the specific material object still does not cross cohorts in the depicted evidence. `SPECIFIC_V12_FIREWOOD_INHERITANCE` therefore remains `OPEN`.

Similarly, V14 established that Mei's membership gives the Outclub enough people to be eligible for promotion from `サークル` to formal `部` status. V15 does not provide a sufficiently explicit administrative scene to claim that formal institutional promotion has been completed in practice. Eligibility is real; actual administrative transition remains unconfirmed at this boundary.

The strongest V15 freeze is therefore:

> **Volume 15 validates adaptive succession by putting it under physical pressure. Mei does not inherit an already-mastered practice from an expert senior; she and Aoi undertake a difficult ride as near-peers in cycling experience, while the older group supplies logistics, recovery capacity, and a common camp rather than controlling the riders' effort. The trip succeeds through pacing, detours, food, rest, mechanical and infrastructural limits, and an available pickup path—not through denial of vulnerability. Ema's later curiosity about camping arises by a different route and remains explicitly optional. The new cohort is becoming part of the series' outdoor world without being required to reproduce the old cohort's identities or trajectories.**

# 2. Pass A — low-interpretation reconstruction

## 2.1 Chapter 82 — `イヌ子とメイの山梨縦断 サイクリングロードの旅`

The cycling camp begins by distinguishing road conditions rather than treating “cycling” as one homogeneous activity. Aoi and Mei are visibly more tense beside automobile traffic. The move onto separated cycling-road infrastructure changes the emotional and procedural character of the ride: there is more room to hold pace, look around, and converse without constantly managing passing vehicles.

Construction interrupts the assumed route. The riders do not respond by forcing their way through or treating the detour as narrative failure. They receive directions and reroute. This is a direct continuation of the series' long-standing interest in infrastructure as causal: a planned line on a map is not the traveled road.

The first major relationship clarification occurs during a food/rest stop. Mei explains that she is relieved by how the ride is going. Aoi answers:

> `私もこないだロードを始めたばっかの初心者やからなぁ`
>
> (`V15 / 0014.jpg`)

Mei says:

> `私も同じくらいなんで 一緒のペースで走れて楽しいんス`

Aoi's mock-dark response—roughly, “I've really been underestimated; from here on, no more holding back”—keeps the exchange comic. The scene establishes three facts without collapsing them:

1. Aoi is Mei's school/club senior.
2. Aoi has somewhat more road-bike experience.
3. Aoi still identifies herself as a beginner.

This is not expert instruction. It is **guided co-learning**.

The chapter repeatedly gives bodily management narrative time. Stops are not excised as uninteresting. Food, water, pacing, road surface, route availability, and fatigue are all part of the journey's visible structure.

Meanwhile, Chiaki and Toba travel in the support car with luggage. Their movement is not merely offscreen transport. They have a parallel day organized around the Karisaka region, campsite setup, sightseeing/planning, and food. The split therefore meets the project's narrow definition of modular group travel: one shared trip is intentionally built from differentiated movement modes and experiences that later reconverge.

Late in the chapter the remaining route becomes more intimidating. The pair are told that the final stretch contains substantial climbing. Toba's vehicle appears beside them and the contingency is made explicit:

> `ダメだと思ったら気にせずギブアップしてくださいねー`
>
> `すぐ迎えに行きますからー`
>
> (`V15 / 0028.jpg`)

Aoi and Mei do not immediately accept the ride. They choose to continue.

**Low-interpretation result:** the cycling pair begins successfully, but the manga defines successful continuation through route adaptation, equalized pacing, rest, and an explicit recovery option rather than through a promise that the riders will finish every planned kilometer unaided.

## 2.2 Chapter 83 — `千明と美波の雁坂のんあるキャンプ`

The chapter shifts attention away from the cyclists and gives the support module its own ordinary substance.

Chiaki and Toba establish camp in the Karisaka area. Their time includes campsite procedure, local-geography curiosity, drinks/food, and small leisure decisions. This matters structurally: the support role is not portrayed as suspended existence while “the real adventure” happens on bicycles.

The chapter also gives Toba a non-alcoholic register without treating it as identity replacement. Non-alcoholic or cocktail-adjacent flavor experimentation becomes another available routine. As elsewhere in the series, consumption is not forced into one moral category: pleasure, sensory comparison, adult routine, comedy, and practical circumstance coexist.

Butter-cookie and drink comparison is particularly characteristic of the manga's way of handling preferences. Differences in flavor and texture become something to notice rather than something that must be ranked into a single objective winner. This is a small material example of a broader series habit: **comparison need not terminate in homogenization**.

The chapter crosscuts back toward the cycling pair as the route becomes harder. Their bodily sense of elapsed time and distance begins to diverge from the clean abstraction of the plan. Fatigue produces jokes and distorted expectations. The humor does not erase physical cost; it makes the cost socially manageable.

A separate scooter journey also enters the same regional geography, setting up the later accidental intersection with Rin. The crucial point is structural rather than coincidental: multiple characters can occupy overlapping places through different itineraries without having planned a synchronized group excursion.

**Low-interpretation result:** the support side remains a full leisure module, while the cyclists' experience becomes progressively less map-like and more bodily.

## 2.3 Chapter 84 — `激走！イヌ子とメイの雁坂ヒルクライム`

The hill climb is the volume's strongest competence stress test.

Aoi and Mei encounter sustained grade rather than the comparatively forgiving rhythm of the cycling roads. The manga gives visible attention to gears, body position, luggage, cadence, rests, and energy intake. Advice is useful but not magical. Technique changes what can be attempted; it does not abolish fatigue.

Aoi offers road-cycling tips, but the chapter continues to resist expert inflation. Her knowledge is recently acquired and situated. The pair test suggestions against their own bodies. When the climb becomes difficult, they slow rather than converting the ride into a competition over who can suppress discomfort.

Food and drink become explicit energy management. Stops for local food and later hot-spring recovery are not side quests detached from the ride. They alter how the riders feel and what they can plausibly continue doing.

The climb also changes Mei's relation to difficulty. Her earlier enthusiasm now has friction to push against. Importantly, the manga does not reveal that enthusiasm was naïve and therefore false. She can be exhausted and still like the activity. Likewise, Aoi can become tired without her V13–V14 cycling trajectory being invalidated.

The hot-spring stop is particularly useful evidence. Recovery does not merely restore a numerical stamina meter. It changes motivation, attention, and bodily self-assessment. The manga keeps the body as an active participant in decision-making.

By the end of the chapter, the cycling module and support module reconverge at camp. No one needs to narrate the cyclists as failures for arriving tired. Their fatigue is expected information about what they did.

**Low-interpretation result:** the climb turns cycling competence from route enthusiasm into embodied self-management. Knowledge helps, but rest, food, changing pace, and willingness to recognize limits remain part of competence rather than exceptions to it.

## 2.4 Chapter 85 — `はらぺこと雁坂の晩ごはん`

The evening meal converts differentiated daytime experiences into shared camp time.

Cooking is materially explicit: ingredients, preparation order, heat, cheese, sauce, vegetables, and improvised adjustments receive panel space. As usual, food is neither pure symbolism nor mere decoration. It is labor, sensory pleasure, recovery, comedy, local routine, and a way of reconverging after separate movement.

The group does not execute dinner with frictionless perfection. Heat and melting behavior require adjustment. Hunger survives the first round strongly enough that additional food becomes desirable. The response is not shame over having planned “wrong” but another round of practical improvisation.

The multi-cheese sequence is especially revealing as material culture. Named cheeses enter as objects with different tastes, textures, and prestige associations, but the comic loss of clean identification prevents the scene from becoming a connoisseurship exam. The group eats, compares, guesses, and enjoys.

For Aoi and Mei, appetite is inseparable from exertion. The day's ride changes how dinner is experienced. This is another example of the manga making bodily state causal without making athletic hardship morally superior.

Night conversation begins turning toward the next day's route and the Karisaka Tunnel. The road ahead is not just “more kilometers.” Tunnel infrastructure, bicycle access, grade, and pickup possibilities matter.

**Low-interpretation result:** dinner is where the trip's differentiated modules become shared time again, but the manga preserves the fact that participants arrive with different bodily histories from the day.

## 2.5 Chapter 86 — `トンネルまであと少し`

The next morning provides the clearest evidence against reading V15 as a simple endurance triumph.

Aoi and Mei are sore. Their speed can fall to a visibly low level. Yesterday's effort has consequences that sleep does not erase. They nevertheless continue toward a self-chosen endpoint.

The Karisaka Tunnel toll-road infrastructure finally creates a hard boundary. Bicycles cannot simply translate determination into passage. The road system has rules and access conditions that the riders do not control.

Rin's scooter route intersects the cyclists near the toll-road area (`V15 / 0121–0122.jpg`). The encounter is striking precisely because it is not the product of one unified itinerary. Rin has her own route and recent solo-camping history. The shared geography produces accidental contact among otherwise independent trajectories.

After the cyclists reach their limit/goal area, the support arrangement becomes concrete rather than hypothetical. Pickup is discussed and used. This fulfills one of V14's most important prospective tests: the support vehicle was not narrative insurance that would disappear to preserve a heroic finish.

The use of pickup is not framed as disgrace. There is no moral accounting in which every kilometer not ridden cancels the experience. The pair have ridden far, climbed substantially, learned the route bodily, and reached an infrastructural boundary. Recovery by car is part of the planned architecture.

The chapter also opens future cycling possibility. Roadside cycling infrastructure, route information, and harder future destinations remain imaginable. Difficulty expands the map of possible next practices rather than proving that the current practice was a mistake.

Near the end, after the group's departure, Aoi and Mei continue riding on a section that gives them a different seasonal/scenic experience than expected. Mei's final evaluation is direct:

> `初サイクリングキャンプ めっちゃ楽しかったっス!!`
>
> (`V15 / 0133.jpg`)

The line should not be inflated into permanent identity. It is nevertheless strong direct evidence that the difficulty did not retrospectively convert the trip into failure.

**Low-interpretation result:** the cycling camp reaches a successful subjective endpoint through a combination of persistence, limits, infrastructure, and pickup. The manga treats recoverability as part of adventure design.

## 2.6 Chapter 87 — `イマジナリードッグとGWの予定`

The final chapter sharply changes scale and activity.

Ema's problem is illustration rather than camping. She wants to draw dogs convincingly and confronts the gap between generic mental images and observed specificity. Different breeds, body shapes, fur, movement, and expression complicate the imaginary category “dog.”

This is a small but methodologically resonant sequence: observation corrects abstraction.

Chikuwa becomes an especially useful live reference. Ema's interaction with Ena is organized around drawing, grooming, and dog knowledge before camping enters the conversation. This prevents the later camping question from feeling like an institutionally scripted recruitment beat.

Ema asks Ena:

> `キャンプってやっぱり面白いですか？`
>
> (`V15 / 0159.jpg`)

Ena answers:

> `ハマる人はハマるけど 人によるかも`
>
> `時間の過ごし方がわからないって人もいるし`

This is unusually explicit internal counterevidence against a universal “camping improves everyone” thesis. Enjoyment is person-dependent, and unstructured time can itself be uncomfortable.

Ena's next statement preserves experiment without pressure:

> `興味あるなら一回やってみたら分かると思うよ`
>
> (`V15 / 0160.jpg`)

The recommendation is experiential and conditional. It does not tell Ema what conclusion she should reach after trying.

The chapter closes comedically with the girls returning after spending time outside and discovering someone else contentedly watching television. Even inside a chapter that raises possible camping interest, ordinary indoor leisure is allowed to remain enjoyable rather than being degraded into an inferior life.

**Low-interpretation result:** Ema's relation to camping changes from distance to curiosity, but the change occurs through a non-Outclub relationship and is explicitly framed as something whose suitability can only be discovered individually.

> **Planning creates a recoverable field for improvisation; it does not eliminate uncertainty.**

## 3.3 Is Aoi actually competent under prolonged conditions?

**Yes, but only if competence remains bounded.** She can pace, advise, notice problems, continue through difficulty, and make use of support. She explicitly calls herself a beginner and does not possess omniscient technical authority.

# 4. Difficulty without ordeal

V15 permits a more precise distinction between **difficulty** and **ordeal**.

The cycling trip is difficult in at least five senses:

1. **Traffic difficulty** — riding near cars changes attention and comfort.
2. **Route difficulty** — construction and detours invalidate simple map-following.
3. **Gradient difficulty** — climbing turns equivalent map distance into unequal bodily cost.
4. **Recovery difficulty** — exertion persists into the next morning as soreness.
5. **Infrastructure difficulty** — the tunnel/toll-road boundary cannot be defeated by willingness.

Yet the manga does not organize these difficulties around a doctrine that suffering validates the participant.

No character says the ride only counts if the support car is never used. No character treats eating, bathing, resting, slowing, or pickup as moral weakness. There is no prestige hierarchy in which the cyclist who hurts most is the best cyclist.

This supports a new strong formulation:

> **Yuru Camp△ can make leisure strenuous without turning leisure into ordeal culture.**

The distinction is analytically valuable because it prevents “iyashikei” or “laid-back” from being misread as the absence of physical challenge. The series permits demanding experiences; what it resists is using suffering as a compulsory proof of authenticity.

**Status:** NEW STRONG `IN`; adjudicate at V13–V15 checkpoint.

# 5. Supported autonomy becomes recoverability architecture

The project has repeatedly used `SUPPORTED_AUTONOMY` to describe situations where help increases rather than replaces self-authored action. V15 gives the concept its clearest logistical form.

Toba's message before the climb is not “you must stop if I become worried.” It is:

> if you think you cannot continue, give up without worrying; we can come get you.

This matters because the decision threshold remains with the riders.

The support system consists of:

- luggage carried by car;
- adults with vehicle capacity;
- a common destination;
- communication;
- road-access knowledge;
- the promise of pickup;
- actual later pickup/reconvergence.

The support is therefore not merely emotional reassurance. It is **infrastructure**.

A useful revision is:

> **Supported autonomy is strongest when support does not predetermine the choice but changes the cost of changing one's mind.**

The ability to stop safely allows Aoi and Mei to attempt more than they could responsibly attempt if every kilometer had to be completed under their own power regardless of condition.

This is not risk elimination. It is **recoverability architecture**.

**Decision:** `SUPPORTED_AUTONOMY` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN**.

`CARE_WITHOUT_TAKEOVER` remains qualified by the older V07 paternalism counterexample; V15 does not erase it.

# 6. Aoi: seniority without expert inflation

Aoi receives another major character-state increment, but the increment is best understood as **role growth under retained beginner status**.

## 6.1 Relative seniority

In school/club hierarchy, Aoi is plainly senior to Mei. She also has earlier road-bike experience.

## 6.2 Explicit beginner identity

Her statement at `V15 / 0014.jpg` prevents the reader from converting relative experience into objective mastery.

> `私もこないだロードを始めたばっかの初心者やからなぁ`

This is direct evidence and should control later character modeling.

## 6.3 Practical teaching without expert posture

Aoi can still transmit what she knows: pacing, road-bike habits, climbing adjustments, route awareness, and confidence. Teaching does not require pretending to have completed the learning process.

## 6.4 Shared vulnerability

Aoi becomes tired too. She benefits from rest too. She is subject to the same slope, road, weather, and bodily constraints.

## 6.5 Character-model consequence

A future Aoi character model should not produce a generic “competent senpai” voice that confidently knows every bicycle answer. The evidence supports a more specific behavior:

> **Aoi is increasingly willing to act as the experienced party while openly remaining inside the learner category.**

This is highly compatible with her established comic register. She can shift from useful practical talk into mock menace or exaggeration without losing credibility.

**Character-state decision:** `AOI_CYCLING_ROLE` → **STRENGTHEN**.

**Expert-status decision:** `AOI_BICYCLE_EXPERT` → **REJECT**.

# 7. Mei: newcomer agency and the refusal of blank-slate succession

Volume 14 already prevented Mei from being a blank successor. V15 makes the point much stronger.

Mei's agency appears in several forms:

- she wants to cycle-camp;
- she rides rather than merely observes;
- she values equal pacing with Aoi;
- she experiences fear, fatigue, appetite, and accomplishment directly;
- she continues through difficulty without being ordered to prove herself;
- her evaluation of the trip remains strongly positive;
- her practice has caused the seniors' group to undertake a new activity architecture.

The result is not “the juniors learn how the Outclub camps.” It is closer to:

> **A junior enters an existing camping institution and helps produce a hybrid practice the institution had not previously centered.**

This is why “legacy” remains an inadequate master term. Legacy language often implies a stable object handed downward. V15 instead shows **institutional plasticity**.

The Outclub persists because it can absorb difference without requiring sameness.

**Decision:** `INTER_COHORT_CONTINUITY` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN**.

`LEGACY_AS_MASTER_THESIS` → **HOLD / probably too broad** pending checkpoint.

# 8. Reciprocal transmission

V15 provides enough evidence to distinguish three transmission forms.

## 8.1 Vertical transmission

Aoi and the older group possess more camping history. Mei receives logistical knowledge and practice access from them.

## 8.2 Horizontal co-learning

Aoi and Mei are both road-cycling beginners. They learn the climb, pacing, and bodily consequences together.

## 8.3 Reverse institutional influence

Mei's interest changes what the older group plans and does.

Together these produce a more mature concept:

> **Practice transmission in Yuru Camp△ is not simply expertise flowing downward. It can be vertical, horizontal, and institutionally reverse at the same time.**

This is one of the strongest V13–V15 checkpoint candidates.

# 9. Modular group travel: third major case

The project has intentionally kept `MODULAR_GROUP_TRAVEL` narrow. V15 now supplies a third high-confidence case.

The qualifying features are all present:

1. **One shared trip exists.**
2. **Movement is intentionally differentiated.** Aoi/Mei ride; Chiaki/Toba drive with luggage.
3. **Different modules have different experiential content.** Cycling-road/hill-climb embodiment differs from campsite/support-side leisure.
4. **The modules remain interdependent.** The vehicle carries gear and enables pickup.
5. **Reconvergence is planned and occurs.** Dinner/camp becomes shared time.
6. **Later divergence remains possible.** Rin's independent scooter route intersects without being retroactively absorbed into the planned module structure.

The strongest formulation remains:

> **A shared trip can be intentionally built from unequal modes of movement and unequal logistical burdens, provided the differences are coordinated around a common destination and a credible reconvergence/support plan.**

V15 adds one important clause:

> **The reconvergence plan may include recovery from an incomplete movement module without treating that incompleteness as social failure.**

**Decision:** `MODULAR_GROUP_TRAVEL` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN / mature recurrent pattern candidate**.

# 10. Infrastructure is not background

V15 is unusually strong evidence that roads and leisure infrastructure function as active causal systems.

The volume repeatedly makes visible:

- separated cycling roads;
- automobile traffic;
- construction closures;
- detours;
- grades;
- roadside food/rest facilities;
- campsite access;
- hot springs;
- tunnel/toll-road restrictions;
- support-car access;
- road information and cycling-route signage.

A map cannot predict the lived route without these systems.

The tunnel is the clearest example. Motivation does not override access rules. The endpoint is partly produced by infrastructure.

This strongly supports the long-running principle:

> **Place in Yuru Camp△ is causal before it is symbolic.**

**Decision:** `PLACE_GEOGRAPHY_AS_CAUSAL_SYSTEM` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN**.

# 11. Food, appetite, and bodily causality

V15 is not a “food volume” in the narrow sense, but food becomes one of the strongest bridges between exertion and shared life.

Food functions as:

- ride pacing;
- energy replenishment;
- local curiosity;
- hot-spring/rest-stop pleasure;
- camp reconvergence;
- cooking procedure;
- hunger comedy;
- cheese comparison;
- post-exertion sensory intensity.

The key analytical caution remains necessary:

`FOOD_ALWAYS_EQUALS_CARE` is still rejected.

Some food is care; some is logistics; some is tourism; some is appetite; some is comedy; some is simply delicious.

V15 strengthens a more precise proposition:

> **Food often makes bodily state narratively legible.**

After the climb, appetite tells us something about what the riders have done. Dinner is experienced differently because the daytime modules were different.

**Decision:** `FOOD_AS_MIXED_FUNCTION_PRACTICE` → **STRENGTHEN**.

# 12. Ema: observation, optionality, and a new route toward camping

Ema's V15 chapter is important partly because it does not begin from camping.

Her practical problem is visual representation. She wants to draw dogs better. Generic “dog” knowledge proves insufficient; breed, fur, body, movement, and individual behavior matter.

That observational process is consistent with the manga's wider epistemic style:

> abstraction → encounter with particular material reality → revision.

Chikuwa gives Ema a live object of attention. Ena gives her a social route into dog knowledge. Camping emerges later, almost incidentally, through conversation about how people spend time.

Ena's answer deserves to be preserved as high-value negative evidence:

> `ハマる人はハマるけど 人によるかも`
>
> `時間の過ごし方がわからないって人もいるし`

This explicitly rejects a universal suitability model.

Ema's question therefore changes her state without determining her future:

- V14: camping-adjacent but not interested enough to join; dislikes bugs; works at Caribou; can support others materially.
- V15: asks whether camping is actually fun and receives permission to treat it as an experiment rather than an identity decision.

The correct state is:

> **curious, not converted.**

**Decision:** `EMA_PRACTICE_REPERTOIRE` → **REVISE / expand**.

Do not infer Outclub membership.

# 13. Ena as a low-pressure transmission node

Ena's role in Chapter 87 is analytically useful precisely because she is not recruiting.

She does not say:

- camping is obviously fun;
- everyone should like it;
- Ema is missing out;
- the Outclub is where Ema belongs.

Instead, she gives an empirically modest answer: some people get into camping, some do not; some find the time difficult to structure. If Ema is interested, trying once will teach her more.

This is a highly characteristic form of transmission:

> **experience is offered as information rather than obligation.**

It strengthens `LOW_PRESSURE_INVITATION_GRAMMAR` and `SUPPORTED_AUTONOMY` without requiring a formal mentor relation.

# 14. Japanese voice and relationship language

## 14.1 Mei's `～っス` remains stable under strain

Mei's junior register persists during enthusiasm, fatigue, and evaluation. The form is not merely an introductory gimmick. Her final line—`めっちゃ楽しかったっス!!`—shows the same informal-respectful style carrying a strong first-person judgment.

**Voice decision:** `MEI_SU_REGISTER` → **STRENGTHEN**.

## 14.2 Aoi's dialect and comic reversals remain integrated with competence

At `V15 / 0014.jpg`, Aoi can state her beginner status plainly and immediately flip into exaggerated mock aggression. Technical/practical scenes do not neutralize her established comic voice.

## 14.3 Ena's camping answer is linguistically non-prescriptive

`人によるかも` is especially important. It leaves variability inside the proposition itself. `興味あるなら` likewise conditions the suggestion on Ema's own interest.

This is not merely content; the grammar of the exchange preserves optionality.

## 14.4 Senior/junior labels do not map cleanly onto expertise

Mei can call Aoi `先輩` while the two remain near-peers in cycling skill. Social hierarchy and domain competence are analytically separable.

This should be preserved in future character modeling.

# 15. Visual rhythm and form

## 15.1 Long roads create temporal duration

The cycling arc repeatedly uses road depth, repeated pedaling panels, route maps, and changing landscape to make distance occupy page time. The reader does not simply receive “they rode far” as exposition.

## 15.2 Gear and body remain co-visible

Helmets, handlebars, bags, bicycles, gradients, food, sweat, posture, and vehicle support remain materially legible. Cycling is represented as a relation among body, machine, infrastructure, and route.

## 15.3 Crosscutting preserves differentiated experience

Switches between cyclists, support crew, and Rin do not collapse the modules. The cut itself makes simultaneity without sameness visible.

## 15.4 Food procedure slows the post-ride sequence

The dinner chapter deliberately gives time back to ordinary handwork after long-distance movement. This creates a formal reconvergence: fast/effortful movement gives way to chopping, heating, melting, waiting, eating, and talking.

## 15.5 Chapter 87 sharply reduces geographic scale

After a volume dominated by long roads, mountains, and regional movement, the dog-drawing chapter returns to school/home-scale observation. The contrast prevents the cycling arc from becoming the only kind of meaningful activity the volume recognizes.

# 16. Humor and low-shame regulation

V15 repeatedly uses comedy to prevent correction or limitation from becoming social humiliation.

Examples include:

- Aoi's mock threat after Mei says they are at similar ability;
- exhaustion jokes;
- distorted time/distance perception;
- food excess and cheese confusion;
- the absurdity of “imaginary dog” representation;
- returning from a thoughtful outdoor conversation to someone happily watching television.

Humor does not erase consequences. The cyclists still hurt. Roads still close. The tunnel still blocks passage. The joke changes the **social temperature** of limitation.

The mature formulation remains:

> **Correction and limit-setting in Yuru Camp△ are often concrete and consequential without being organized around shame.**

**Decision:** `LOW_SHAME_SOCIAL_REGULATION` → **STRENGTHEN**.

# 17. Ordinary-life narrative sufficiency

V15 contains a conspicuous physical challenge, but its state changes still arise through ordinary procedure:

- choosing a road;
- accepting a detour;
- taking a break;
- eating;
- shifting gears;
- deciding whether to continue;
- setting camp;
- cooking;
- recovering;
- asking for pickup;
- observing a dog;
- asking another person what camping is like.

The cycling arc therefore does not contradict `ORDINARY_LIFE_NARRATIVE_SUFFICIENCY`. It clarifies it.

Ordinary life in this project need not mean low exertion. It means that consequential change can occur through ordinary practices rather than exceptional crisis.

**Decision:** `ORDINARY_LIFE_NARRATIVE_SUFFICIENCY` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN**.

`ORDINARY_FLOURISHING` remains `HOLD`. The manga continues to value a wide repertoire of ordinary pleasure and competence, but the project should not prematurely transform that pattern into a total normative philosophy.

# 18. Shared relational history and separate experience

The modular trip offers a useful extension of the shared-history model.

Aoi and Mei have the cycling route bodily. Chiaki and Toba have the support-side camp experience. Rin has a separate scooter/solo route. The characters can later know parts of one another's days through conversation and accidental intersection.

Shared history therefore remains non-identical:

> **people can belong to the same relational history without having occupied the same experience.**

V15 strengthens this claim without requiring a perfect record of what everyone did.

No V15 evidence requires revision of the project's romance/yuri status.

# 21. Counterevidence and rejected shortcuts

## 21.1 REJECT — “The cycling trip succeeds because Aoi is already an expert”

Aoi explicitly calls herself a beginner. Her relative seniority is real but narrow.

## 21.2 REJECT — “Mei merely follows Aoi's pace and instruction”

Mei states that their similar pace is part of what she enjoys. Her interest is one of the causes of the trip itself.

## 21.3 REJECT — “Using the support car means the cycling challenge failed”

The pickup function was part of the plan from the beginning and is treated as ordinary contingency.

## 21.4 REJECT — “A real cycling adventure requires finishing every planned kilometer under bicycle power”

The manga itself does not use that criterion. Infrastructure and bodily state legitimately modify endpoints.

## 21.5 REJECT — “Supported autonomy means adults quietly decide the safe limit”

In V15 the riders retain the stopping decision while adults make stopping recoverable.

## 21.6 REJECT — “Ema's curiosity proves she was always destined to become a camper”

V14's non-camping preference was genuine, and V15's conversation explicitly says suitability varies by person.

## 21.7 REJECT — “Ema's growing camping interest implies Outclub membership”

No such institutional decision appears.

## 21.8 REJECT — “The new cohort simply reproduces Rin/Nadeshiko or Chiaki/Aoi”

Mei's cycling and Ema's illustration/dog route are distinct. Mechanical generational pairing remains unsupported.

## 21.9 DOWNGRADE — “V15 completes the club's formal transition to a `部`”

V14 established numerical eligibility. V15 does not supply sufficient administrative confirmation.

## 21.10 PRESERVE / OPEN — “The V12 firewood reaches the junior cohort”

Still not shown.

## 21.11 HOLD — “Difficulty is now the series' central value”

The volume values challenge, but it also values stopping, recovery, food, observation, television, and person-specific preference. Difficulty is one available mode, not a master good.

## 21.12 REJECT — “Camping is presented as universally enjoyable”

Ena explicitly says it depends on the person and that some people do not know how to spend the time.

> **Volume 15 turns V14's adaptive-succession proposal into embodied practice. Mei's arrival does not create a one-way mentor chain in which an expert senior transmits a finished tradition. Aoi explicitly remains a cycling beginner, and the pair learn the route together while the older group supplies luggage transport, camp logistics, and a non-shaming recovery path. Construction, slope, fatigue, food, hot-spring recovery, soreness, tunnel access, and pickup all matter. The trip succeeds not because vulnerability disappears but because the activity is designed so that difficulty can be chosen without making withdrawal catastrophic.**

A second freeze concerns support:

> **V15 gives supported autonomy its clearest logistical form. Toba does not decide the cyclists' limit for them; she makes a change of mind recoverable. The support car therefore expands rather than diminishes self-authored challenge. This is not independence as self-sufficiency. It is autonomy backed by infrastructure.**

A third freeze concerns transmission:

> **Inter-cohort learning is now visibly reciprocal. Mei receives camping knowledge and support from the older group, but her cycling interest changes what the older group does. Aoi is simultaneously senior and learner. The institution survives not by reproducing itself unchanged but by allowing new participants to alter its repertoire.**

A fourth freeze concerns Ema:

> **Ema remains the strongest counterexample to absorption. She can work at Caribou, contribute practical knowledge, care about dogs and illustration, talk with Ena, and become curious about camping without joining the Outclub or being told that camping should suit her. Ena explicitly says suitability depends on the person and recommends trying only if Ema is interested. The state change is curiosity, not conversion.**

The most concise V15 longitudinal update is:

> **A practice becomes sustainable not when everyone can do it unaided, but when people can try, stop, reroute, recover, and still regard the experience as their own.**
