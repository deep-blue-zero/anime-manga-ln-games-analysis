# Yuru Camp△ Volumes 13–15 Adversarial Checkpoint
## Adaptive inter-cohort continuity, multidirectional transmission, recoverable difficulty, and a social world that expands without absorption

> **Governing rule:** Description first. Pattern second. Interpretation third. Theme last.

---

# 0. Purpose, scope, and checkpoint procedure

This document is the mandatory adversarial checkpoint after the sequential deep readings of Volumes 13, 14, and 15. It does not replace those readings. Its function is to decide which claims survive comparison across the tranche, which claims must be narrowed, which provisional concepts deserve promotion, which earlier formulations have become misleading, and what must remain open before Volume 16 is read.

The spoiler boundary is **Japanese manga Volumes 1–15 only**. Volume 16 and later material is excluded. Later franchise knowledge must not be used to rescue, complete, or retrospectively beautify claims that are incomplete at this boundary.

The immediate baseline is `YURUCAMP_V10-V12_CHECKPOINT.md`. That checkpoint froze several important propositions:

- **repertoire expansion under preserved difference** as the central longitudinal working model;
- **configuration flexibility** as its broader social consequence;
- **modular group travel** as a narrower travel architecture rather than a universal metaphor;
- **situated uncertainty management** as the preferred competence model;
- **supported autonomy** as self-direction within support systems rather than self-sufficiency;
- **separate experience becoming shared relational history through retelling**, while explicitly denying that retelling is a transparent archive;
- **ensemble decentralization** and lateral network widening;
- leisure as materially and infrastructurally embedded;
- an `OPEN` hypothesis of **material inheritance / club institutional time** generated by the V12 firewood sequence;
- **ordinary-life narrative sufficiency** as a strong formal property;
- `ordinary flourishing` as `HOLD`;
- and Rin–Nadeshiko romance/yuri as unsupported through V12.

Volumes 13–15 are unusually well suited to test those claims because their structure changes sharply across the tranche.

- **Volume 13** is a spring mosaic of parallel activity: Rin solo camping, Nadeshiko and Sakura's blossom routine, Ena's work/dog/drawing encounter, Chiaki's first solo camp, and Aoi's first substantial road-bike experiment.
- **Volume 14** closes the blossom movement, enters the new school term, introduces actual next-cohort institutional participation through Nakatsugawa Mei, differentiates Mizunami Ema's route away from club membership, and designs a cycling/support-car camping trip.
- **Volume 15** stress-tests that plan under actual bodily and infrastructural difficulty, while preserving Ema's separate trajectory and making her curiosity about camping explicitly conditional rather than compulsory.

This gives the checkpoint a useful sequence:

> **distributed possibility → institutional entry → embodied stress test**

Every major claim is evaluated through the same sequence used by the preceding checkpoint:

1. state the claim precisely;
2. identify its strongest direct and recurrent evidence;
3. ask what the corpus would look like if the claim were wrong;
4. identify counterevidence, non-payoffs, and scope limits;
5. compare across characters and situations rather than relying on one emblematic scene;
6. assign a claim transition: `PRESERVE`, `STRENGTHEN`, `REVISE`, `DOWNGRADE`, `REJECT`, or `OPEN`.

Evidence classes remain:

- `DE` — direct evidence;
- `RP` — recurrent pattern;
- `IN` — interpretive inference;
- `TH` — thematic hypothesis;
- `CA` — counterevidence / ambiguity.

Two methodological warnings are especially important in this tranche:

> **Do not turn relative experience into expertise.**

and:

> **Do not turn continuity into replication.**

The first controls Aoi's cycling role. The second controls the entire junior-cohort analysis.

# 1. Executive checkpoint result

The V13–V15 tranche strongly confirms the twelve-volume model, but it also changes the language needed to describe transmission, succession, support, and difficulty.

The strongest fifteen-volume formulation is now:

> **Yuru Camp△ increasingly develops people, practices, and institutions by expanding what can be tried without requiring earlier identities, preferences, or relationships to be repudiated. By Volume 15 this process has crossed a cohort boundary. New participants do not merely inherit an established camping culture: they selectively enter it, refuse parts of it, introduce practices of their own, and cause the existing group to reorganize around different bodies, skills, schedules, costs, and interests. Continuity therefore depends less on replication than on the capacity to remain coherent while being changed.**

This preserves **repertoire expansion under preserved difference** as the central longitudinal model. The tranche now shows that the model operates at four levels:

1. **individual practice** — Chiaki can try solitude without becoming Rin; Aoi can add road cycling without becoming a cycling expert;
2. **social configuration** — simultaneous solo, family, work, dyadic, club, support, and incidental-contact arrangements remain compatible;
3. **knowledge circulation** — information moves through family, weak ties, stores, teachers, peers, juniors, and trial rather than only from expert to novice;
4. **institutional continuity** — Mei joins an existing club but immediately changes what that club does, while Ema remains socially and materially connected without joining it.

The most important new mature formulation is therefore:

> **adaptive inter-cohort continuity**.

This is now preferable to broad **legacy** language. “Legacy” suggests a stable inheritance handed from one cohort to another. The actual sequence is less static:

- V12 imagines future juniors;
- V13 makes possible future students narratively visible;
- V14 produces an actual junior member;
- V14–V15 show that junior's own cycling interest changing the group's activity architecture;
- Ema demonstrates that proximity to the same social world does not require institutional absorption.

The best current claim is not that the older cohort leaves behind a fixed legacy. It is that the institution begins to persist **by permitting difference to alter it**.

A second major adjudication concerns knowledge. V13's term **informal pedagogy** was correct locally but is now too directional as the umbrella concept. V13 shows camping etiquette moving from grandfather/acquaintances to later practitioners. V14 adds practical influence before formal roles and retail knowledge moving through Ema. V15 adds near-peer co-learning and reverse institutional influence from Mei to the senior group.

The broader mature claim should therefore be:

> **distributed, multidirectional knowledge circulation**.

Pedagogy remains one mode inside that system. Other modes include:

- vertical teaching;
- weak-tie norm transmission;
- family provision and advice;
- store/work knowledge;
- peer co-learning;
- observation and imitation;
- self-testing;
- and newcomer influence on what an established group learns next.

A third major adjudication concerns **modular group travel**. The checkpoint had deliberately kept the term narrow after Izu and Oigawa. Volume 13 correctly does not qualify. Volume 14 designs a third case, and Volume 15 executes it under stress. Aoi and Mei cycle; Chiaki and Toba carry luggage and establish camp by car; the vehicle provides actual recovery capacity; differentiated experiences reconverge without being retroactively homogenized.

`MODULAR_GROUP_TRAVEL` can therefore be promoted to a **mature recurrent pattern**, still with strict scope limits. Mere separation does not qualify.

A fourth major result is the refinement of **supported autonomy**. V13 already shows Aoi's self-authored cycling depending on family equipment, maintenance, roads, safety gear, and father pickup after a puncture. V14 turns support into trip design. V15 makes the support car operational and explicitly leaves the stopping threshold with the riders.

The checkpoint therefore promotes **recoverability architecture** as a strong subclaim of supported autonomy:

> **Support can increase the amount of difficulty a person can responsibly choose when it reduces the cost of changing course without predetermining the choice.**

This does not replace the broader supported-autonomy concept. It identifies one especially clear mechanism by which support preserves agency.

A fifth result concerns difficulty itself. V15's cycling arc is strenuous, but eating, resting, slowing, bathing, rerouting, and pickup are not framed as moral failure. The same anti-ordeal logic is compatible with earlier retreat and support cases. The checkpoint therefore promotes **difficulty without endurance moralization** as a strong secondary pattern, while refusing to turn “difficulty” into a central series value.

The most important character-level correction is Aoi. Across V13–V15 she clearly becomes more competent and more influential in cycling contexts, but V15 explicitly blocks expert inflation:

> `私もこないだロードを始めたばっかの初心者やからなぁ`
>
> (`V15 / 0014.jpg`)

Aoi is best modeled as a **competent beginner with relative seniority**, willing to help, teach what she knows, admit uncertainty, and learn beside Mei. `AOI_BICYCLE_EXPERT` is rejected.

Mei, correspondingly, is not a blank recipient. Her presence creates the strongest evidence yet for **newcomer agency**. She receives knowledge and logistical support, but her own preference changes the older group's plan. The institution is not merely reproduced through her.

Ema supplies the strongest counterexample to absorption. She can draw, work at Caribou, acquire outdoor-product knowledge, help the cycling trip, care about dogs, talk with Ena, and become curious about camping without joining the Outclub. Ena's answer—`ハマる人はハマるけど 人によるかも` and `興味あるなら一回やってみたら分かると思うよ` (`V15 / 0159–0160.jpg`)—makes variability and conditional experimentation explicit.

Several claims remain deliberately unpromoted:

- the **formal administrative promotion** from `サークル` to `部` is not confirmed; V14 establishes numerical eligibility, not completed bureaucracy;
- the **specific V12 firewood handoff** is still not shown and is downgraded from “strong inheritance candidate” to a lower-priority object-specific `OPEN` hypothesis;
- **campsite stewardship** remains locally strong but insufficiently recurrent for thematic promotion;
- **ordinary flourishing** remains `HOLD`;
- broad **legacy** is not the preferred current language;
- romance/yuri remains unsupported through V15.

The tranche's strongest methodological result is therefore:

> **The series' continuity is increasingly visible not in sameness, completion, or self-sufficiency, but in recoverable experimentation: people can enter, decline, try, reroute, learn, stop, return, and alter a shared practice without turning difference into failure.**

# 2. Tranche architecture: V13 possibility → V14 entry → V15 stress test

The three volumes form a coherent analytical progression even though they do not constitute one continuous conventional dramatic arc.

## 2.1 Volume 13 — possibility becomes distributed

Volume 13 takes capacities that were once concentrated in the central Rin/Nadeshiko contrast and distributes them across the ensemble.

- Rin remains positively attached to solo camping while imagining vehicle camping as another possible mode (`V13 / 0011.jpg`).
- Chiaki completes a first solo camp and says she now understands some of Rin's attraction to it (`V13 / 0123.jpg`).
- Aoi tries road cycling and experiences speed, fatigue, mechanical failure, and pickup (`V13 / 0135–0158.jpg`).
- Ema and Mei enter the narrative horizon as future high-school students with their own interests rather than as pre-labeled successors (`V13 / 0102–0105.jpg`).

The volume's architecture is **parallel experimentation**. It establishes that the social network can support different practices without converging them into one event.

## 2.2 Volume 14 — possibility crosses an institutional boundary

Volume 14 converts the future-cohort question from hypothetical to actual.

Mei joins the Outclub. Her membership is enough for Chiaki to say the group now has the number needed to rise from `サークル` to `部` (`V14 / 0129.jpg`). But the same volume prevents a static-inheritance reading:

- Mei already has road-cycling interest;
- she rents before buying;
- she wants to combine cycling and camping;
- her preference changes the group's next trip;
- Ema does not join, yet remains connected through Caribou and support logistics.

The volume therefore turns **future institutional time** into **selective inter-cohort participation**.

## 2.3 Volume 15 — the new arrangement is tested physically

Volume 15 matters because V14's plan could have remained an attractive abstraction. Instead, the trip is tested against:

- traffic;
- construction;
- detours;
- sustained climbing;
- hunger;
- fatigue;
- next-day soreness;
- road restrictions;
- unequal experience;
- and actual use of support infrastructure.

The plan survives, but not because everyone becomes tougher than expected. It survives because the trip was designed with differentiated roles and recoverability.

This is why the tranche cannot be summarized as “the club gets juniors.” The stronger architecture is:

> **a new cohort begins to enter the system, and the system demonstrates that it can change shape around them without either dissolving or forcing them into old roles.**

# 3. Audit of the V10–V12 checkpoint's forward questions

The prior checkpoint left thirty-two open questions. V13–V15 answer some directly, partially answer others, and leave several untouched. The highest-value resolutions are below.

## 3.1 Does modular group travel recur outside Izu/Oigawa?

**Yes, decisively.**

V13 does not qualify and therefore protects the concept's scope. V14 prospectively designs a cycling pair plus support/luggage car. V15 executes that architecture, including actual recovery support.

**Decision:** `STRENGTHEN` to mature recurrent pattern; scope remains narrow.

## 3.2 Does configuration flexibility face anything harder than smooth coexistence?

**Partially.**

The tranche does not produce major interpersonal conflict, but it introduces operational strain:

- work schedules;
- club recruitment pressure;
- divergent institutional choices;
- gear cost;
- route difficulty;
- physical fatigue;
- different transport modes;
- and unequal expertise.

The network remains coherent despite those differences. This is stronger than mere simultaneous leisure, though it is not yet a high-conflict social stress test.

**Decision:** `STRENGTHEN`, with hard interpersonal strain still open.

## 3.3 Do Chiaki/Aoi/Ena and other non-central configurations continue receiving independent narrative weight?

**Yes.**

V13 gives Chiaki solo camping and Aoi cycling. V14 shifts major state change toward Aoi, Mei, Ema, and Chiaki. V15's main physical arc centers Aoi/Mei/Chiaki/Toba, while Ema/Ena carry the closing chapter. Rin is only partly intersecting; Nadeshiko receives no major V15 state revision.

**Decision:** `ENSEMBLE_DECENTRALIZATION` → `STRONGLY STRENGTHEN`.

## 3.4 Does future-junior material become actual succession?

**Yes institutionally, but not materially.**

Mei joins the Outclub. That is actual next-cohort participation. The specific V12 firewood does not pass to her.

**Decision:** split the old hypothesis permanently:

- `INTER_COHORT_CONTINUITY` → strongly supported;
- `SPECIFIC_FIREWOOD_INHERITANCE` → open, lower priority.

## 3.5 Does succession preserve successor autonomy?

**Yes, strongly.**

Mei brings cycling rather than simply receiving camping. Ema does not join and is not punished or narratively discarded. Ena explicitly describes camping suitability as variable by person.

**Decision:** `STRENGTHEN`.

## 3.6 Do work, money, and material access become more explicit constraints?

**Yes.**

V13 retains work-schedule friction. V14 adds bike/gear cost, rental as trial access, Caribou employment, family provision, and the possibility of part-time work. V15 keeps food, luggage, vehicle support, roads, access rules, and bodily recovery materially causal.

**Decision:** `MATERIAL_INFRASTRUCTURE_OF_LEISURE` → `STRONGLY STRENGTHEN`.

## 3.7 Does family transmission recur outside the Shima family?

**Yes, but with a precision requirement.**

Aoi's bicycle comes through her grandmother/family, and family support supplies maintenance, fitting/safety context, and pickup. This strengthens family-enabled practice. V15 does **not** show a detailed family technical-teaching sequence sufficient to say that Aoi's cycling skill is directly inherited.

**Decision:** `FAMILY_TRANSMISSION_WITHOUT_DESTINY` → `STRENGTHEN`, with provision/access distinguished from pedagogy.

## 3.8 Does a higher-consequence mistake stress the competence model?

**Not in the strongest possible sense.**

There is no V13–V15 analogue of a severe safety failure. Instead, the tranche stress-tests competence through speed recognition, puncture, uncertain tube compatibility, route distance, fatigue, detours, and access restrictions.

**Decision:** situated uncertainty management strengthens; catastrophic-error stress test remains open.

## 3.9 Does Toba face the difficult adviser problem?

**Partially, and cleanly.**

The cycling trip gives Toba a meaningful safety-support role, but she does not unilaterally terminate the riders' activity. She makes pickup available and leaves the decision threshold with them.

This is strong evidence for supported autonomy, but it does not erase the older V07 paternalism counterexample.

**Decision:** Toba as enabling fallible adviser → `STRENGTHEN`; “care never takes over” remains rejected.

## 3.10 Does retelling/shared-history architecture recur?

**Yes, modestly rather than centrally.**

V13–V14 use photos and messages to make separate blossom/solo experiences socially available. V15's differentiated modules reconverge into shared camp time. The tranche does not add a retelling-distortion case as strong as V12.

**Decision:** `PRESERVE / modest STRENGTHEN`.

## 3.11 Does place continue to constrain through transit, regulation, season, and bodily effort?

**Yes, strongly.**

Spring changes available practices and school structures; road bikes change experienced distance; Golden Week booking affects campsite planning; traffic and cycling-road separation alter riding experience; construction reroutes the trip; climbing changes bodily cost; the Karisaka Tunnel/toll-road system imposes a hard access boundary.

**Decision:** `PLACE_GEOGRAPHY_AS_CAUSAL_SYSTEM` → `STRONGLY STRENGTHEN`.

## 3.12 Does food remain irreducibly multifunctional?

**Yes.**

Across the tranche food operates as tourism, solo routine, reunion, novelty comedy, recruitment interface, portable exertion support, bodily recovery, camp reconvergence, and appetite marker.

**Decision:** `FOOD_AS_MIXED_FUNCTION_PRACTICE` → `STRONGLY PRESERVE / ENRICH`.

## 3.13 Does low-shame correction face a stronger practical test?

**Yes.**

Aoi admits limits; Mei fears distance; pickup remains available; rest and slowing are normal; Ema can decline one institutional route; no one treats support use as character failure.

**Decision:** `LOW_SHAME_CORRECTIVE_GRAMMAR` → `STRENGTHEN`.

## 3.14 Does ordinary flourishing receive enough explicit normative evidence for promotion?

**No.**

The tranche strongly values ordinary life as narratively sufficient, but Ena's `人によるかも` is especially useful against universalization: even camping itself does not suit everyone.

**Decision:** `ORDINARY_FLOURISHING` → `HOLD`.

## 3.15 Does romance/yuri status change?

**No.**

The tranche adds intimacy, familiarity, new friendships, and senior/junior relations, but no distinct confession grammar, erotic framing, exclusivity, jealousy, or relationship-specific romantic markers sufficient to revise the prior status.

**Decision:** `PRESERVE UNSUPPORTED` through V15.

## 3.16 Questions materially unanswered by this tranche

The following prior questions receive little or no useful new evidence and should not be falsely “resolved”:

- a second clean Nadeshiko solo overnight;
- further explicit Rin `寂しさ` evidence;
- longer-term Rin–Ayano development;
- stronger long-distance Ayano/Nadeshiko maintenance practices;
- repeated cross-volume weak-tie returns comparable to the Iidas;
- a severe high-consequence mistake under the competence model;
- mature bridge symbolism;
- a stronger explicit normative theory of flourishing.

These remain open without penalty.

# 4. Central model through Volume 15: repertoire expansion under preserved difference

The central model not only survives; V13–V15 broaden the range of evidence supporting it.

The claim should now be stated as:

> **Characters can acquire new practices, social configurations, skills, and forms of participation without treating earlier preferences or identities as defects that had to be corrected.**

V13 supplies three especially clean cases:

- Rin remains a committed solo camper while imagining vehicle camping;
- Chiaki enjoys solo camping without ceasing to be Chiaki;
- Aoi adds road cycling while remaining a novice who accepts help.

V14 crosses the model into cohort structure:

- Mei adopts cycling and camping in a hybrid way;
- Ema acquires outdoor-material knowledge without becoming a camper;
- the club makes room for a newcomer whose practice was not previously central.

V15 then prevents the model from turning into effortless self-expansion:

- Aoi and Mei discover limits together;
- the cycling practice remains worthwhile despite fatigue and recovery support;
- Ema's state change stops at curiosity rather than being forced into adoption.

### Wrong-world test

If repertoire expansion actually meant progressive convergence toward one preferred identity, we would expect later volumes to make differences disappear:

- Rin would become less attached to solitude;
- Chiaki's successful solo camp would be treated as conversion;
- Aoi would become a self-sufficient expert;
- Mei would simply learn “the Outclub way”;
- Ema would be recruited or narratively left behind.

The tranche repeatedly does the opposite.

### Decision

`REPERTOIRE_EXPANSION_UNDER_PRESERVED_DIFFERENCE` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN / central longitudinal model**.

A useful subordinate formulation is:

> **What spreads is practical possibility, not one correct identity.**

# 5. Configuration flexibility: now stable across asynchronous, institutional, and modular forms

The V10–V12 checkpoint introduced **configuration flexibility** because “modular group travel” was too narrow to explain all forms of non-co-presence.

V13 vindicates that distinction. Its spring mosaic is almost a demonstration case for configuration flexibility without modular travel:

- Rin alone;
- Nadeshiko with Sakura;
- Ena with Chikuwa and work;
- Chiaki solo camping;
- Aoi cycling independently;
- messages and chance overlaps connecting them lightly.

V14 adds institutional differentiation:

- Mei enters the Outclub;
- Ema uses the library and Caribou instead;
- Aoi/Mei cycle;
- Ema can support without sharing the same practice identity.

V15 adds coordinated differentiation under one trip while Rin remains independently mobile outside the planned modules.

The concept therefore now covers a stable social property:

> **Belonging can survive changes in co-presence, role, transport, schedule, and institutional membership without demanding constant synchronization.**

### Scope limit

Configuration flexibility does **not** mean all arrangements are equally deep or equally durable. It also does not prove the absence of future conflict. The claim concerns the network's demonstrated capacity to sustain multiple configurations without automatically reading separation as relational failure.

### Decision

`CONFIGURATION_FLEXIBILITY` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN / mature strong IN-RP**.

# 6. Adaptive inter-cohort continuity replaces “legacy” as the preferred current term

The V12 firewood sequence generated a future-facing institutional question. At that point “legacy” was deliberately withheld.

V13–V15 now provide enough evidence to answer the institutional part, but the answer is not “legacy” in the simple sense.

## 6.1 What is directly established

- V13 makes a next cohort visible as actual prospective high-school students.
- V14 shows Mei joining the Outclub.
- V14 establishes that her membership reaches the numerical threshold Chiaki associates with promotion from `サークル` to `部` (`V14 / 0129.jpg`).
- V14 shows Mei bringing road cycling into club planning.
- V15 executes a trip materially reorganized around that interest.
- Ema remains outside membership while continuing to participate in adjacent social/material networks.

## 6.2 Why “legacy” is too static

A legacy model would emphasize what the seniors preserve and pass downward. The evidence emphasizes something else:

- newcomers select rather than merely receive;
- not every newcomer enters the same institution;
- the member who does join introduces a new practice;
- seniority and expertise do not align cleanly;
- the old institution changes in response.

The stronger formulation is:

> **Inter-cohort continuity is adaptive when a shared institution persists by allowing later participants to alter its repertoire without erasing prior practice.**

## 6.3 Is “institutional plasticity” useful?

V15's phrase **institutional plasticity** captures the mechanism, but it is more abstract than necessary as the primary controlled term. The checkpoint therefore treats it as descriptive vocabulary, not a new top-level thesis.

Preferred hierarchy:

- **primary:** `ADAPTIVE_INTER_COHORT_CONTINUITY`
- **secondary mechanism:** institutional plasticity / capacity to change around newcomers
- **not current authority:** broad `LEGACY` master thesis

### Decision

- `MATERIAL_INHERITANCE / CLUB_INSTITUTIONAL_TIME` → **REVISE** into separate institutional and object-level claims.
- `ADAPTIVE_INTER_COHORT_CONTINUITY` → **NEW / STRONGLY SUPPORTED / mature tranche-level claim**.
- `LEGACY_AS_MASTER_TERM` → **DOWNGRADE / not preferred current authority**.

# 7. Distributed knowledge circulation: pedagogy becomes multidirectional

V13 correctly identified **informal pedagogy** as a major new pattern. The campground-etiquette scene is explicit: beginners may not know norms without experienced people; acquaintances teach acquaintances; Rin recalls her grandfather's rule (`V13 / 0018–0020.jpg`).

But V14–V15 show that “pedagogy” alone is too vertical.

## 7.1 Vertical transmission

Examples include:

- grandfather → Rin;
- experienced acquaintances → later campers;
- Caribou staff/work environment → Ema;
- older Outclub members → Mei's camping access.

## 7.2 Practical influence before formal teaching

Aoi helps Mei with a puncture before their senior/junior relation exists. She does not give a formal lesson or intentionally recruit Mei into cycling. Yet the encounter helps make road cycling visible and reachable.

This is influence without planned pedagogy.

## 7.3 Horizontal co-learning

V15 explicitly identifies Aoi as a recent beginner. Mei says their similar pace is enjoyable (`V15 / 0014.jpg`). They learn the embodied route together.

## 7.4 Reverse institutional influence

Mei's preference alters what the established group does. Knowledge/practice does not only flow from old member to new member; the newcomer changes the senior group's next learning problem.

## 7.5 Low-pressure invitation as transmission

Ena's advice to Ema is neither instruction nor recruitment:

- camping suits some people and not others;
- if Ema is interested, trying once is a way to find out.

This is transmission as permission to test, not pressure to adopt.

### Decision

`INFORMAL_PEDAGOGY` → **REVISE / SUBSUME** under:

> **DISTRIBUTED_MULTIDIRECTIONAL_KNOWLEDGE_CIRCULATION**

This becomes a strong recurrent pattern, with pedagogy retained as one subtype.

# 8. Modular group travel: promotion after the third major case

The project has deliberately resisted calling every separation “modular.” That restraint is what now makes promotion meaningful.

A qualifying modular-group-travel case requires:

1. one shared trip or shared trip project;
2. intentionally differentiated movement, roles, or experiential modules;
3. meaningful independent content within those modules;
4. coordination or interdependence among them;
5. planned or credible reconvergence;
6. no requirement that all participants have the same experience for the trip to remain socially shared.

Izu qualifies.

Oigawa qualifies.

V13 does not.

The V14 plan qualifies prospectively, and V15 executes it:

- Aoi/Mei cycle;
- Chiaki/Toba carry luggage and travel by car;
- the support side has its own campsite/local experience;
- luggage and recovery make the modules materially interdependent;
- the group reconverges for camp/food;
- later pickup does not invalidate the cycling module.

The V15 case adds an important new clause:

> **A movement module can end through recovery support without the shared trip or the module's experiential value being treated as failed.**

### Wrong-world test

If modular travel were only a one-off workaround, it should disappear when the cast composition and activity change. Instead, the same basic architecture reappears with different participants, different transport, different risk, and a different logistical problem.

### Decision

# 9. Supported autonomy: recoverability becomes a strong subclaim

The twelve-volume checkpoint defined autonomy as **self-direction within support systems**, not self-sufficiency. V13–V15 provide a particularly clean longitudinal sequence.

The distinction matters. Recovery support is not the same as covertly deciding for someone.

### Decision

- `SUPPORTED_AUTONOMY` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN**.
- `RECOVERABILITY_ARCHITECTURE` → **NEW / strong longitudinal subclaim**.

# 10. Difficulty without ordeal: strong secondary pattern, not central theme

Volume 15 names a distinction that is compatible with several earlier competence/autonomy cases:

- next-day soreness;

- rest is not cheating;
- bathing is not cheating;
- slowing is not cheating;

### Decision

`DIFFICULTY_WITHOUT_ORDEAL_CULTURE` → **NEW / STRONG IN, secondary pattern**.

> **The manga permits strenuous leisure without moralizing endurance.**

# 37. Fifteen-volume checkpoint freeze

## 37.1 Central longitudinal model

> **Repertoire expansion under preserved difference remains the strongest longitudinal model through Volume 15.**

It now applies not only to the early solo/group contrast but to mobility, skill, institutional participation, and newcomer agency.

People can add options without declaring earlier selves mistaken.

## 37.2 Social architecture

> **Configuration flexibility is now a stable property of the ensemble.**

Parallel lives, divergent institutional choices, modular travel, work schedules, and separate routes can coexist without automatically threatening belonging.

## 37.3 Inter-cohort architecture

> **The preferred current formulation is adaptive inter-cohort continuity, not legacy.**

Mei's arrival proves that a next cohort can enter the institution. Her cycling interest proves that continuity does not require replication. Ema proves that proximity to the same social world does not require institutional absorption.

## 37.4 Knowledge architecture

> **Knowledge circulates multidirectionally.**

Family, acquaintances, weak ties, work, teachers, peers, seniors, juniors, trial, and observation all participate. Vertical pedagogy remains real, but it is no longer sufficient as the master description.

## 37.5 Competence architecture

> **Situated uncertainty management remains the preferred competence model.**

The tranche strengthens it with a new domain. Competence includes admitting beginner status, escalating beyond one's knowledge, responding to bodily limits, adapting routes, obeying infrastructure, and using support.

## 37.6 Autonomy architecture

> **Supported autonomy now has a clearly demonstrated recoverability mechanism.**

Support can expand self-authored difficulty by making retreat, pickup, rerouting, or a changed mind workable without predetermining the decision.

## 37.7 Difficulty architecture

> **The manga can make leisure difficult without treating suffering as proof of authenticity.**

This is a strong secondary pattern, not a central doctrine of the series.

## 37.8 Material/institutional architecture

> **Leisure remains infrastructurally embedded, and institutional continuity is now real even though formal `部` status and the specific firewood handoff remain open.**

Money, jobs, rental, family provision, roads, rooms, shops, campsites, vehicles, and access rules remain causal.

## 37.9 Place and mobility architecture

> **Place remains causal before symbolic, and mobility mode is now a mature variable in how place is experienced.**

Distance is embodied rather than merely mapped.

## 37.10 Ordinary-life architecture

> **Ordinary life is narratively sufficient.**

Large state changes continue to occur through work, planning, membership, renting, food, routes, room use, practical help, fatigue, and conversation.

`Ordinary flourishing` remains `HOLD`.

## 37.11 Relationship architecture

> **The network can widen and cross cohorts without demanding exclusivity or sameness.**

Rin–Nadeshiko remains a secure close friendship; Aoi–Mei becomes a new activity relation; Ema–Mei survives divergence; Ema–Ena develops as a weak tie; none of these requires flattening relationship depth into equality.

Romance/yuri remains unsupported through V15.

## 37.12 Strongest full checkpoint formulation

> **Through Volume 15, Yuru Camp△ increasingly treats durable participation as the ability to remain oneself while becoming capable of more arrangements. Rin can remain solitary and curious; Chiaki can understand solitude without becoming Rin; Aoi can become useful without becoming an expert; Mei can join without becoming a copy; Ema can remain outside the club without becoming irrelevant. The social world supports this difference through flexible configurations, distributed knowledge, material infrastructure, low-shame correction, and recoverable exits. Continuity is therefore not produced by everyone arriving at the same identity. It is produced by a system that can survive people choosing differently—and can sometimes become richer because they do.**

A second formulation captures the tranche's practical ethic:

> **The manga repeatedly separates challenge from ordeal and autonomy from self-sufficiency. People can try difficult things because they can ask, rent, rest, reroute, admit limits, accept pickup, and change their minds. Support does not automatically reduce authorship; when designed as recoverability, it can enlarge the range of self-authored action.**

A third formulation captures the cohort transition:

> **The future of the Outclub is no longer hypothetical, but it is not a frozen legacy. The first demonstrated form of continuity is adaptive: a junior arrives with a different practice, the seniors reorganize around it, and another nearby newcomer remains free not to join at all.**
