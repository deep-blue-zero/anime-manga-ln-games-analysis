## 0.2 The terminal event is departure, not overnight success

The main Gunma sequence ends at s132 as Nadeshiko, Ena and Ema leave Yokokawa toward Takasaki. They have not reached the planned campsite, slept in the bungalow, visited Nozori Lake, completed Haruna day2, eaten the deferred kamameshi or returned. Ema has chosen and prepared for a first camping journey and is participating in its travel portion; the evidence does not yet establish her first camping-night experience or evaluation. The special that follows explicitly recalls the remaining spring break and therefore cannot supply that missing Gunma payoff.

# 1. Orientation after reconstruction

V16 develops the entering Ema question through a concrete intermediate state. She does not simply announce that camping has become her identity. She contributes a destination, transmits seasonal risk information, arranges borrowed equipment, recommends a bungalow, guides two sleepy seniors and uses her existing illustration practice to make a museum visit shared. Much of her new agency appears before any depicted camping night. The useful state change is **curiosity → a chosen and prepared journey**, with camping enjoyment and membership still withheld.

Nadeshiko's competence becomes more specific at the same time. Her illustrated itinerary demonstrates effort, remembered interests and enthusiasm, yet its original bus/train connections do not work. Ena and Ema help revise it. Nadeshiko welcomes companions, thanks Ema and accepts altered transport, a shorter trail and deferred activities. Her admission that she likes old trains without knowing much about them matters: interest does not depend on encyclopedic expertise, while camping experience does not guarantee expertise about a new railway itinerary. The earlier-season special repeats a substantial route error without negating her cooking ability.

Rin's changed Golden Week is equally particular. The bookshop owner's family visit unexpectedly frees her time. Ayano's proposed tour and Rin's memory of prior shared travel prompt Rin to ask to ride together. This is initiative, not merely acceptance of another invitation. The special then gives extended positive private lakeside attention and an explicit wish for a quiet solo-use site. Company and solitude remain simultaneously available goods within one character.

These findings strengthen the accepted model of repertoire expansion under preserved difference. They do not prove that every trial succeeds or that every newcomer becomes a camper. Most of the volume is still planning and travel; its chief new overnight experience remains beyond this source boundary.

## 2.1 Ch88: domestic preparation, haircut, another person's camping history and a changed work calendar

Nadeshiko prepares tablet, stylus, snacks and drink to plan her Golden Week solo camp (s005–006). Sakura's recalled television report about a retro railway museum in Gunma activates a specific remembered interest. The decision to research Yokokawa is actual; the imagined itinerary is not travel already completed.

The chapter then shifts to Ena's haircut gathering. Rin requests a substantial cut. Ena theatrically regrets losing a subject for hair arrangements and presents an exaggerated SNS example. Ema admires Ena's dexterity, addresses her as Saitō-senpai and then becomes the next possible hair model. Her startled response is mild comedy, not refusal of the relationship. Hair play, dog contact and observation give her an activity here before any camping decision (s008–010;E16-01).

Ema privately recalls Rin's suggestion that trying once would reveal whether camping suits her. A black-bordered Caribou memory gives the subsequent desire a specific object. Ema asks Ibigawa about a Nozori Lake photograph; Ibigawa distinguishes it from Nojiri, describes scenery, difficult access, a cheap bungalow and seasonal road closure. Ema says her parents are indoor people and she has never camped. Ibigawa offers equipment; another short-haired apron-wearing coworker offers items with a comic exception for table/chair feet. That worker remains unnamed and must not be identified as Mei from a junior-sounding -っす register. Ema privately wants to see the scenery someday. Desire for this place precedes any declared camping identity (s010–014).

Rin's haircut finishes. Ena has purchased three bun extensions for 999 yen; the chibi reactions of absent friends to bunless Rin are imagined possibilities, not actual knowledge communicated to those friends. Rin appreciates easier washing; her father wears an extension at dinner. The ordinary family gag keeps the hairstyle change at a practical and comic scale (s014–017;E16-01).

Rin returns a missed call to bookshop owner Takeda in a polite employee register. He explains that the shop will close May 2–6 so he and his wife can visit the grandson born in February and asks her to work April 30/May 1. Rin agrees. She had intended to work through Golden Week; the free interval is produced by her employer's family plans rather than an initial leisure choice (s018–019;E16-02).

Ayano then sends photographs from last week's Atsumi Peninsula camp. Rin counters with Chichibu and waraji-katsu; Ayano replies with Irago seafood. The exchange is playful reported travel, not the new tour happening now. Ayano proposes meeting the three friends on May 5 and says she already told Nadeshiko about riding to Yamanashi. Rin warns about Route1, then learns Ayano plans a northern Suwa/Route152 route suggested by a schoolfriend. Narrow-road imagery prompts Rin's concern; Ayano says she will camp and take several days. Rin recalls previous riding, food, lake, bridge photograph and fist bump. After this memory she changes her mind and actually texts, 私も一緒に走りに行っていい？ (s020–026;E16-02). The sequence supports self-initiated companionship without showing the future ride's completion.

A next-morning scene reveals Nadeshiko asleep across a doorway/floor after planning late. Sakura's discovery supplies the delayed punchline. Dog/Rin silhouette and weapon-storage bun interstitials close the chapter's unit; neither establishes a literal new pet or weapon practice (s027–030).

## 2.2 Ch89: a solo plan becomes three people's revisable itinerary

At school Nadeshiko still needs the second campsite and seeks Rin. An unnamed girl says Rin is working; Ena instead calls Nadeshiko to the library table where Ema is drawing. Nadeshiko shows her illustrated plan. Its visual elaboration is real work, but elaboration alone does not establish transport feasibility (s031–034;E16-03).

The proposal combines Koumi Line scenery, Karuizawa, a bus to Kumanotaira, the Abt trail, Megane Bridge, café/onsen, the Yokokawa museum and kamameshi, then onward rail/bus travel toward a campsite near Ikaho. Nadeshiko has found a less popular site partly because its postal reservation and municipal application are inconvenient. Ena asks about check-in. Timetable checking exposes the problem: Nadeshiko's earliest original arrival is after13:00 while the bus departs12:55 and runs once daily. Ema identifies the single-service constraint. Ena sketches an earlier Kofu departure/taxi/hurried walk/brief museum alternative; Ema notes the proposed leisurely outing would become training and offers a more direct museum option. Nadeshiko says she wants both a leisurely trail and museum, and will rethink (s035–041;E16-03).

This is not a scene in which junior correction defeats a foolish senior. Each proposal is tested against the experience Nadeshiko wants, and the group does not simply adopt the most punishing feasible schedule. Ena then asks to join May 2–4 because she has time and the discussion interests her; Nadeshiko welcomes her. Ema supplies Ibigawa's Nozori recommendation, including the pizza across the lake. The seniors invite Ema; she is surprised. Her destination information has helped change the trip before she accepts a participant role (s042–045;E16-04).

At home Ema asks Ibigawa to borrow gear. Ibigawa agrees, proposes light equipment for train/bus walking and asks whether the group understands early-May Nozori cold: roads have only recently reopened, snow may remain and night temperatures can be winterlike. She offers a winter bag but recommends a bungalow and checking companions' kit. Ema forwards the warning. Nadeshiko checks the weather and confirms a winter bag; Ena recalls her January Yamanakako cold crisis and helpers. Ema then proposes the cold-sheltering bungalow and both seniors agree. This is a consequential accommodation revision authored by the least camping-experienced participant, based on another person's specific knowledge (s046–049;E16-05).

Ema privately considers down/fleece layers and asking her mother for travel money. The source shows a prospective financial need, not the loan granted. Nadeshiko phones Rin; they discover their schedule misunderstanding. Nadeshiko says she would have invited Rin, explains the now-three-person group and hears about Rin/Ayano's separate ride. They arrange to exchange stories at Nadeshiko's home on May 4 night. Their separate trips remain; no actual reunion is shown (s050–052;E16-05).

May 2 06:20 at Kofu begins actual departure. Sakura wakes Nadeshiko in her car; Ena is already outside, dazed by early waking. Ena later explicitly says she traveled in her father's car. Ema is awake and Sakura asks this responsible-looking junior to look after the two older girls. This joking inversion is not formal guardianship or proof Ema can manage every outdoor risk (s053–054;E16-06). Pizza dream and bear-map interstitials remain paratext (s055–056).

## 2.3 Ch90: a capable junior, a sleeping senior and room gained through revised transport

At 10:00 Karuizawa, Nadeshiko cheers the wrong station name, Kobuchizawa. Ema corrects her. Train flashback shows Ema drawing and noticing Yatsugatake while the seniors are sleepy; she records scenery and has already called the taxi. Nadeshiko privately recognizes her capability (s057–060;E16-06).

Nadeshiko directs the driver to Clubman near Usui Lake. The original full Kumanotaira route has been shortened. Ema follows the map, notices the Gunma/Annaka border and road curves; Nadeshiko asks whether she gets carsick and Ema says she is fine. The taxi driver offers a brief Kumanotaira station-remains stop while warning long waiting increases fare. Nadeshiko thanks him and asks Ema whether she wants to join the walk; Ema chooses to. Ena sleeps in the car. On the stairs Ema visibly exerts herself. At the remains, she reads historical information on her phone while Nadeshiko imagines the old station's greetings (s061–069;E16-06).

Nadeshiko explains that walking the trail in reverse makes it continuously downhill; Ema plainly prefers the easier way. A selfie is digitally amended with a circular sleeping-Ena insert. The picture records a joke about absence; Ena did not physically stand at that station. The driver offers Megane Bridge and tells the girls about his past family camping, including Akagi/Haruna. His recommendations are manga-internal dialogue, not verified contemporary travel advice (s069–072).

Ena wakes, initially believing she is still at Kofu. Ema points out they are already in Gunma and identifies the bridge. Delayed recognition becomes an enthusiastic three-person roadside pose rather than a conflict over her sleep. At 10:43 the driver leaves them at Clubman. Nadeshiko explicitly thanks the others for making the taxi cost shareable. She plans a short café rest, one-hour trail and roughly one-hour museum before the 13:10 train. Ema privately thinks the timing is rough; she does not speak that thought as a rebuke (s073–076).

The café receives broad interior attention: wood, curved ceiling, car imagery, drinks and relaxed talk. Nadeshiko orders iced cocoa, Ema iced café au lait, Ena ginger ale. Their stop depends on a revised route via Sakudaira/Shinkansen that reaches Karuizawa about 40 minutes earlier for a higher fare. Both seniors acknowledge Ema guided them through their sleep and bow in gratitude. Ema's photographs preserve scenery they missed, but the manga does not pretend they experienced it directly. The return-view promise remains prospective and is immediately teased as another chance to sleep (s077–080;E16-06).

## 2.4 Ch91: a walk with jokes, light kit and bounded warnings

The café owner describes a driving hobby and photographs; an 11:00 alarm ends the stop. Nadeshiko thanks her and explains the Yokokawa walk. Ema and Nadeshiko share a liking for round headlights in a hypothetical future car. This preference supplies no acquired license or actual owned vehicle (s083–085;E16-07).

The path initially feels like forest rather than railway. The trio hypothesize what happened to the tracks. Ema privately finds the descent easier than expected and attributes part of the comfort to Ibigawa's light borrowed equipment. She compares her compact bag with the seniors' bulky sleeping bags, suspects the kit is expensive and resolves to handle it carefully. Its price is not established. The visible buried rail confirms their local hypothesis; greetings with other walkers and Ena's brief rest on luggage fill out unhurried travel (s086–090).

Nakasendō signage and the Usui-no-yu area introduce intersecting historical and leisure infrastructures. Nadeshiko explains a weekend/holiday trolley but chooses the walk they came for. The hot spring is also deferred because it would break the schedule; Ema notes she is not yet very tired. Ena proposes an imaginary bath, drawing on Ema's imaginative practice. Ema privately tries it and the page briefly overlays a fantastical bath/aura. The others' puzzled question returns the scene to actual walking. No bath was taken (s091–095).

The old Maruyama substation receives a historical caption and large building panels. Ema compares its appearance with school/library; Ena peers inside. Two monkeys are actually present on an overhead structure. As Nadeshiko approaches to photograph, Ema advises distance and says she does not know the details. The angry-monkey image illustrates the warning; no attack occurs. Ena experiments verbally with ways of looking, and the group passes while the animals groom. The ensuing 白い目 and other literal-eye jokes depict absent friends in comic form, not actual encounters (s096–101).

After a modern bridge selfie, the museum comes into view. A100m side sign prompts a Usui checkpoint detour and a false-goal joke; Ema corrects the seniors before all reach the actual trail endpoint. The narration gives 45 minutes along the trail before the final detour/arrival; the phone later gives 11:55, five minutes ahead of schedule. Ena reports satisfaction from walking on her own feet; Ema says the walk was enjoyable and easier than expected. Nadeshiko checks her fatigue before asking whether they may go straight into the museum. Both assent (s102–106;E16-07). This assent qualifies any reading of Nadeshiko simply carrying the others through her hobby.

## 2.5 Ch92: trains interpreted through pictures and previous experience

Nadeshiko enthusiastically photographs the first retro train but tells Ena she does not know the technical details. Ema reads the plaque identifying its checking function. A title image frames a large shed/train with Ema's observing face and camera foreground. Families at mini-trains prompt Ema's private, comically ominous fantasy of a facility manufacturing future train enthusiasts; she tells Nadeshiko nothing is wrong when asked. This thought is neither the museum's objective purpose nor spoken criticism of visitors (s109–112;E16-08).

Nadeshiko compares a train with a bird. Ena initially cannot see the resemblance; Ema draws the analogy on her tablet and the others understand. A second fugu comparison receives another drawing. Nadeshiko imitates the train's face and Ema photographs her; Ena finds that treating the visit like a zoo makes it interesting. Nadeshiko's imagined train animation then receives Ema's dramatic train image in response. The unfamiliar hobby becomes approachable through the newcomer’s existing skill, rather than illustration being abandoned in favor of trains (s113–116).

Cab inspection, simulator information and discussion of actual driving experiences follow. The course/exam/rank diagram is explanatory; the girls do not take the course or acquire a license. Repetition and money are noticed. Ema privately evaluates looking into an unknown genre as stimulating and not bad; she photographs the hardware and notes a steampunk resemblance. This is a bounded positive evaluation of a visit, not a permanent train-enthusiast identity (s117–120;E16-08).

Nadeshiko reaches the Abt locomotive she especially wanted to see and explains its rack/gear relation to steep climbing. When Ema notes her particular knowledge, Nadeshiko says riding the Oigawa Abt train sparked her interest in old trains. She shows photographs from that earlier journey: mountains, dam and bridge. Ema recognizes the bridge photograph. The shared conversational knowledge is current; the photographed trip is previous experience, not a Gunma detour by this trio. Nadeshiko's knowledge is thus narrow, experiential and emotionally situated even while her broader technical knowledge is limited (s121–124;E16-09).

Ena notices a museum autocamp event poster. Dates April 29, 30, May 1 show that it ended yesterday. Nadeshiko regrets not researching thoroughly enough. She declares a future wish to camp here and combine the full trail, trolley, bath and Clubman meal; Ema wants to revisit Clubman too. The nighttime tent image conveys the missed possibility; no museum camp occurs in V16 (s125–127;E16-10).

A mostly silent montage lets Ema photograph more engines and the trio continue through displays/shop. Nadeshiko holds a train T-shirt; payment is not depicted. The 12:50 alarm ends the visit. Ema notes it did not take long to make the circuit; Nadeshiko explains she likes the trains without deep knowledge and an enthusiast might spend the whole day. This qualifies the earlier worry about one hour: for these particular visitors, the chosen visit has worked. Nadeshiko apologizes for bringing them along on her hobby; Ena says retro trains were good and observes walking the old line may have created affinity. Ema assents. They now plan shopping and the campsite; kamameshi is deferred until tomorrow's train toward Nozori. The actual closing event is departing toward Takasaki (s128–132;E16-10).

## 2.6 Special: earlier spring break, separate movement and shared food

Rin arrives by scooter at Inagako Forest Park and checks where forest and lakeside sites lie. A soup-curry sign tempts her, but she prioritizes registration. A nature-display building is initially mistaken for administration, yielding a small exhibit-animal gag before she finds the correct building. She registers one lakeside night and buys a bundle of firewood. The bundle is an ordinary purchase here; it is not the literal V12 inherited firewood (s135–139;E16-11).

The episode's own flashback explicitly occurs a few days earlier and says spring break has a little time left. Nadeshiko found the camp on a map and proposes one final spring-break trip; Rin knew the lake from nearby roads but not its campground. Their agreement places the special before the main Golden Week chronology. In the actual present, Nadeshiko messages at10:05 from Kajikazawa and rides uphill with loaded bicycle. Rin expects around-noon arrival given distance and Nadeshiko's stamina. Nadeshiko stops for scenery but clouds obscure Fuji (s139–142).

Rin has already set up her site. Her subsequent solitary walk compares allocated camping space with crowded free sites, notes openness and gives extended near-silent forest/lake views. She photographs the carp fountain, carries her chair to the lakeside, wraps in a blanket and privately wonders whether the place is a remarkable hidden spot. The pages render solo attention as satisfying in its own right while she awaits a chosen companion (s142–146;E16-11).

Nadeshiko sends an 11:10 onsen discovery. Rin wonders what hot spring lies on the route. Nadeshiko descends a long way, eventually reaches Takashita's Diamond Fuji viewpoint and recognizes it from the others' New Year account. The panorama provides a real discovery, but also reveals that she missed the Hirabayashi turn. Later, near 4 o'clock at camp, Rin reconstructs the detour: Nadeshiko went to Takashita, turned back and crossed the mountain twice more. Nadeshiko admits maps should be checked frequently. Rin notes that a sustained descent should have been a clue when the campsite was uphill (s147–152;E16-12). No rescue or injury is shown, but a scenic reward does not turn the route error into adequate planning.

At the fire, Nadeshiko prepares a baked-apple dessert tortilla with butter and cinnamon. Rin returns on the scooter with ice cream obtained just before the resthouse closed. Nadeshiko thanks her and tops the tortilla generously. Both find it awkward to eat yet enjoy it; Rin's criticism of the overfilling is followed by her own participation. The earlier chicken tortilla is reported as an already eaten meal, not shown being cooked in this sequence. They discuss how wrapping food reduces washing and allows different group-camp dishes. Cream cheese, kiwi, mango sauce and whip remain recipe suggestions (s153–156;E16-13).

Nadeshiko searches for a campsite near the new viewpoint to watch Diamond Fuji at New Year. Finding none, the pair imagine what such a site might offer. Nadeshiko wants grass and pet access; Rin wants a quiet solo-use site and firewood sales even without a full shop. Rin can like this shared meal while explicitly wanting future solitude. Nadeshiko invites her next year; Rin's reply is bounded by remembering, not a reservation or firm completed plan. A broad night view of Kofu closes the special (s157–158;E16-14).
