# 1. Executive orientation

Volume 14 resolves one of the most important open questions generated by Volume 12 and deliberately left unresolved by Volume 13:

> **Can the Outclub actually persist into another cohort, and if it does, what kind of continuity will that be?**

The answer is now direct: **yes, actual institutional succession begins—but it does not take the form of replication.**

Nakatsugawa Mei (`中津川メイ`) joins the Outdoor Activities Circle. Her membership gives the group enough people to qualify for promotion from `サークル` to formal `部` status. Chiaki states the change explicitly:

> `これでついに野クルも晴れて部に昇格できる人数になった訳だ`
>
> (`V14 / 0129.jpg`)

This is the first genuine realization of the future-cohort horizon that Volume 12 could only imagine. Yet the details immediately prevent a simplistic “the juniors inherit the seniors' club” reading.

Mei does not enter as a blank recipient of an established camping tradition. Before joining, she has already been influenced by Aoi's roadside bicycle help; she independently rents a road bike rather than buying one immediately; she becomes interested in cycling as a practice; and once she joins, that interest begins altering what the Outclub plans to do. The next major trip is explicitly designed around a cycling pair and a support vehicle.

At the same time, Mizunami Ema (`瑞浪絵真`)—the other younger girl introduced in Volume 13—does **not** join the Outclub. She first solves her need for a quiet drawing space through the library, later takes a part-time job at Caribou, learns outdoor-product knowledge there, and participates in the cycling-camp departure by providing portable food while traveling separately in Toba's car.

The strongest volume-level proposition is therefore:

> **Institutional continuity in Yuru Camp△ is selective and adaptive. A next cohort can enter the same outdoor ecosystem without entering the same institution, and a newcomer who does join can change the institution by bringing a different practice with her.**

This substantially strengthens the checkpoint's **repertoire expansion under preserved difference** model. The principle now operates not only among established characters but across cohorts and institutions.

A second major result concerns **informal practical influence**. Aoi encounters Mei before either knows the other as club senior/junior. Mei's city bicycle has punctured during her rush to school. Aoi stops, asks `なにか困っとるん？`, considers whether her own road-bike tube can solve the problem, recognizes the mismatch and limits of her knowledge, and then goes to get a teacher rather than bluffing expertise (`V14 / 0075–0078.jpg`). Mei notices Aoi's sports bicycle. Later she chooses to rent and try a road bike.

The sequence does not justify saying “Aoi intentionally mentors Mei into cycling.” She does not. But it does establish something subtler and more useful:

> **Competent behavior can become socially influential before it becomes formal pedagogy.**

A third result is that **modular group travel receives its strongest post-checkpoint confirmation**. The cycling-camp plan in Chapter 81 is deliberately constructed from different participant roles:

- Aoi and Mei cycle;
- Chiaki and Toba travel ahead by car;
- the car carries camping luggage the novice riders cannot conveniently carry;
- Toba's vehicle provides a bailout if distance, fatigue, or mechanical trouble makes further riding unreasonable;
- Ema later travels with the support side rather than cycling.

This is no longer merely an Izu/Oigawa pattern. The manga has now reused the architecture in a new cohort, with a different activity and different safety problem.

The narrow concept should therefore be strengthened:

> **A shared trip can be intentionally built from unequal modes of movement and unequal logistical burdens, provided those differences are coordinated around a common destination and a credible reconvergence/support plan.**

A fourth result concerns the distinction between **specific material inheritance** and **institutional succession**. Volume 12's firewood sequence imagined current labor helping future juniors. Volume 14 now gives the Outclub an actual junior. But the volume does **not** show Mei using the seasoned V12 firewood or receiving that specific material resource.

The previous hypothesis therefore needs to split:

- `INSTITUTIONAL_SUCCESSION / INTER-COHORT_CONTINUITY` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN / PARTLY REALIZED**
- `SPECIFIC_MATERIAL_INHERITANCE (V12 FIREWOOD → JUNIORS)` → **OPEN**

That distinction matters. The future has arrived institutionally before the specific object-level handoff has been demonstrated.

Finally, Volume 14 gives Aoi one of her largest character-state expansions so far. Corrected V13 establishes her first substantial road-bike experiment. V14 shows the practice becoming habitual enough for commuting, practical enough to help another student, salient enough to shape recruitment-era conversation, and important enough that she participates in designing a cycling-camp plan. Aoi also explicitly says the road bike came from her grandmother. This makes cycling another family-enabled practice that becomes individually authored rather than a one-off gag.

The strongest Volume-14 freeze is:

> **Volume 14 turns imagined succession into selective, adaptive continuity. Mei joins the Outclub and makes formal club status possible, but she does not simply receive an unchanged club: her road-cycling interest becomes part of the group's next trip. Ema enters the same outdoor ecosystem through the library, Caribou, and support logistics rather than membership. Aoi's practical help crosses the cohort boundary before formal recruitment, while later planning turns novice limits into logistics rather than reasons for exclusion. The future cohort therefore arrives not as copies of the old one but as new participants whose different interests cause the existing social and institutional system to reorganize.**

# 2. Pass A — low-interpretation reconstruction

## 2.1 Chapter 76 — `思い出の風景`

Chapter 76 completes the blossom-tour material Volume 13 left open.

The chapter continues moving between specific trees, roads, photographs, food stops, and changing light. A very old weeping cherry receives attention not only as scenery but as an object with material support: the age, shape, and poles holding branches all become part of how the characters look at it (`V14 / 0005–0007.jpg`). The manga again avoids an abstract “sakura = transience” shortcut. These are particular trees in particular places, reached through particular roads and seasons.

A recurring device is **photo comparison**. Characters who spent portions of spring separately can later see what the others saw. Rin's separate solo-camp and blossom material becomes available to the group through images and conversation (`V14 / 0008.jpg`). That modestly strengthens the V10–V12 checkpoint's revised model of shared history: experience can become socially available after the fact without pretending everyone literally shared the original encounter.

Food remains mixed-function. A stop for homemade firm pudding (`自家製固焼きプリン`) supplies local specificity, sensory pleasure, and recovery rhythm rather than an automatic symbolic “care” function (`V14 / 0009.jpg`).

The latter part of the chapter gives sustained visual attention to illuminated blossoms and night scenery (`V14 / 0018–0028.jpg`). The important formal point is duration. The manga permits scenery to occupy narrative time after the day's procedural movement. Characters looking together matters, but the panels do not need to convert every shared view into a statement about friendship.

**Low-interpretation result:** V14 completes the spring-tour sequence by letting separately accumulated spring experiences become compared, narrated, photographed, and partially re-shared in later co-presence.

## 2.2 Chapter 77 — `ソロキャンの夜、犬山家の真実`

The chapter returns to Chiaki's first solo camp at night.

She hears movement outside the tent and explicitly reasons from prior camping experience:

> `このガサガサ音…今までキャンプをしてきた経験から分かる`
>
> `風でテントが揺れている音ではない`
>
> `明らかに動物が立てている音だ。`
>
> (`V14 / 0034.jpg`)

Her response is a mixture of legitimate wildlife caution and comedy. She remembers bear-response rules, tries not to run or turn her back, and is also desperate to reach the toilet (`V14 / 0035.jpg`). The threat turns out to be cats (`V14 / 0036.jpg`).

This is good competence evidence precisely because the danger is misidentified. Chiaki's reasoning is not omniscient. She notices a category of evidence, chooses a cautious response while uncertain, and revises once the source becomes visible.

Afterward, Rin messages her. Rin has sent a live-camera link because she assumes Chiaki may have time to spare on her first solo night:

> `ソロキャンで暇を持て余してるかと思って`
>
> (`V14 / 0038.jpg`)

The two compare their separate camps. Chiaki describes a quiet site with some blossoms and a good hot spring; Rin describes her own sparse blossom site and local meal. Neither needs the other physically present for the experiences to become mutually legible (`V14 / 0038–0040.jpg`).

Rin also says she had a Saratoga Cooler while viewing blossoms and promises to make one for Chiaki the next day because ingredients remain:

> `材料残ってるから明日作ってやるよ`
>
> (`V14 / 0040.jpg`)

Chiaki then reports:

> `そういえば夕方イヌ子がロードバイクでいきなり現れてさー`
>
> (`V14 / 0040.jpg`)

This line is one of the direct continuity anchors confirming Aoi as V13's rider.

The following reunion moves from separate camps into a group meal. Toba proposes Jingisukan (`ジンギスカン焼き肉`) (`V14 / 0046–0047.jpg`). Aoi's family apparently eats Jingisukan with striking frequency; Toba connects the dish to Hokkaido blossom-viewing practice. The meal then veers into specialty meats, including a frog-meat gag. A character remarks that the meat tastes much like ordinary chicken karaage but that knowing it is frog makes the experience harder to receive neutrally (`V14 / 0050.jpg`).

Food here simultaneously performs:

- reunion;
- regional/cultural reference;
- bodily recovery;
- adult convenience;
- novelty;
- disgust comedy.

It again resists a single universal “food = care” meaning.

**Low-interpretation result:** Chiaki's solo experience remains socially porous through messaging and next-day reunion, but the volume does not imply that solo camping is inherently porous for everyone. This is Chiaki's particular first solo night in a dense friendship network.

## 2.3 Chapter 78 — `新入部員獲得作戦会議`

The school year changes and the Outclub confronts recruitment as an institutional problem.

Its poster still reads `野外活動サークル` and advertises activities such as making fires and camping practice (`V14 / 0058.jpg`). Chiaki notes that first-years tend to settle club choices quickly even though the formal deadline is later. Recruitment is therefore not an abstract aspiration; it has a short practical window.

The first obstacle is comic but structurally revealing: **other school clubs are also advertising camping**. The biology club, canoe club, mountaineering club, bicycle club, Go/Shogi club, cooking club, and others all use camp-related activities in recruitment (`V14 / 0061.jpg`). Chiaki reacts:

> `ほとんどの部活がキャンプ推しになってやがるんだよー!!`

The camping boom has reached the school itself.

This is valuable institutional evidence. Camping is no longer the Outclub's exclusive identity marker. A practice can diffuse widely enough that the small specialist group must articulate what is distinct about *its* version.

The members negotiate with other clubs and then develop a recruitment proposition. Chiaki identifies Nadeshiko as the group's strongest “asset,” quickly clarified not as mascot objectification but as **camp food**:

> `正しくはなでしこが作るキャンプめしだ`
>
> `なでしこちゃんのキャンプごはんは野クルの売り`
>
> (`V14 / 0065.jpg`)

The recruitment plan becomes food distribution: small, inexpensive dishes that can be cooked and handed to students after school. Nadeshiko enthusiastically accepts the role.

The important boundary is that food is now functioning as **institutional communication**. It converts club practice into something a newcomer can immediately taste. That still does not make food universally equivalent to care.

The chapter then shifts into a morning bicycle problem. A first-year student's city bicycle has punctured while she is rushing to school. Aoi, now commuting by road bike, notices and stops.

She asks:

> `なにか困っとるん？`
>
> (`V14 / 0076.jpg`)

Aoi initially considers whether she can repair the puncture. She carries a spare tube because she was told to, but she recognizes uncertainty about compatibility between her road-bike equipment and a city bicycle. Instead of improvising past her knowledge, she goes to get a teacher and reports the stranded student (`V14 / 0077–0078.jpg`).

The first-year notices Aoi's sports bicycle after being helped.

This scene is one of the cleanest examples so far of **bounded practical competence**:

1. notice another person's problem;
2. offer help;
3. identify the likely technical issue;
4. inspect whether your tools/knowledge fit;
5. recognize a mismatch;
6. escalate to someone with appropriate authority/resources.

The helper does not need to solve the entire problem personally to count as competent.

## 2.4 Chapter 79 — `思い立ったが吉日`

The first-year helped by Aoi is later established as Nakatsugawa Mei. Rather than immediately buying a road bike after becoming interested, Mei and her friend Ema use a rental opportunity to test one (`V14 / 0086–0087.jpg`).

That choice is analytically important. New enthusiasm is not instantly converted into expensive ownership. **Try-before-buy** becomes another low-risk experiment structure.

Mei learns the embodied side of the machine: road cycling is fast and appealing, but also physically demanding. The story allows enthusiasm and fatigue to coexist.

Ema, meanwhile, has a different problem. She recently acquired a tablet and wants somewhere at school where she can draw quietly. She considers several spaces and rejects them for practical reasons:

> `パソコン室はIT部の部室でダメ`
>
> `屋上もダメか`
>
> `教室もなんだかんだで人が残ってて気になる`
>
> `ようやくタブレット買ったのに`
>
> `校内で落ち着いて絵が描ける場所って意外とないなぁ`
>
> (`V14 / 0100.jpg`)

This is a small but useful institutional problem: Ema needs quiet, power/permission, and freedom from unwanted observation. She does not initially need “a club.” She needs a usable place.

Nadeshiko encounters her during the Outclub recruitment effort and offers a fresh mini-pizza:

> `こんにちは 新入生の方ですか？`
>
> `今焼きたてのミニピザを配ってるんですけど1枚いかがですか？`
>
> `キャンプに興味があったら野外活動サークルにぜひ遊びに来てください！`
>
> (`V14 / 0102.jpg`)

Ema likes the food and considers the Outclub, but then finds the library and asks whether she may use her tablet there if she remains quiet. Permission is granted, and she is visibly relieved (`V14 / 0103–0104.jpg`).

This is significant **negative evidence** against a recruitment-as-destiny reading. Nadeshiko's food succeeds as an invitation, but invitation does not dictate outcome. Ema finds another institution that better solves her immediate need.

## 2.5 Chapter 80 — `新入部員さんいらっしゃい`

Chapter 80 delivers the institutional transition.

Ema sees a Caribou recruitment notice and later takes the job (`V14 / 0127.jpg`, `0132.jpg`). This gives her an outdoor-world route that is not Outclub membership.

Mei, by contrast, appears in the Outclub room and introduces herself directly:

> `中津川メイっス！`
>
> `よろしくおねがいします！`
>
> (`V14 / 0128.jpg`)

Aoi introduces herself again as `犬山あおい`, and Chiaki becomes comically deferential about being club leader.

Then comes the direct institutional statement:

> `これでついに野クルも晴れて部に昇格できる人数になった訳だ`
>
> (`V14 / 0129.jpg`)

The Outclub's future is no longer hypothetical. A new cohort has entered, and the new member changes the institution's formal status.

Aoi immediately jokes that if they are becoming a club, perhaps they should rename themselves `ゆる自転車部`. She explains that since receiving a road bike from her grandmother she has found cycling/commuting increasingly enjoyable. The gag should remain a gag; the club does not literally abandon camping. But it makes the underlying point clearly: **new practices can become salient enough to pressure the institution's self-description.**

Mei expresses direct interest in camping by road bike:

> `ロードでキャンプ行ってみたいッス！`
>
> (`V14 / 0131.jpg`)

At Caribou, Ema is shown wearing a `瑞浪` name badge and working the floor (`V14 / 0132.jpg`). Her presence in an outdoor retail institution creates a second next-cohort route.

The strongest low-interpretation distinction is therefore:

- Mei → formal school club membership;
- Ema → outdoor retail employment;
- both → continued proximity to outdoor practices;
- neither → simple copy of a senior character.

## 2.6 Chapter 81 — `野クルのサイクリング×キャンプ計画`

The new club's next plan immediately shows that succession changes practice.

Mei asks what the cycling camp will look like. The planned participants are Aoi, Chiaki, Mei, and Toba (`V14 / 0138.jpg`). The basic design is unequal by intention:

- Aoi and Mei cycle;
- Chiaki and Toba travel ahead by car;
- the car carries the camping luggage;
- the cyclists can enjoy the ride without requiring Mei to have a full load-carrying bicycle setup;
- everyone reconverges at the campsite.

Mei explicitly appreciates the luggage support because her road bike does not yet have a carrier.

This is a textbook extension of the project's narrow **modular group travel** category.

The planned route is initially ambitious: roughly 70 km one way along the Fuji River / Kōfu-basin direction and toward the Hirose Dam area (`V14 / 0139.jpg`). Mei reacts:

> `片道70キロ!?`

and then:

> `かかか片道70キロなんて絶対ムリっス!!`
>
> (`V14 / 0139–0140.jpg`)

The plan does not demand that she prove herself by accepting fear silently. The group discusses why the route/site changed—Golden Week campsite availability is a concrete constraint—and builds in an explicit support condition.

Toba has agreed that if mechanical trouble or physical limits make continued riding unreasonable, she will load the riders and bicycles into the car:

> `一応距離は70キロやけど`
>
> `何かしらのトラブルとか体力の限界で走れんようなった時は`
>
> `先生が自転車ごと車でキャンプ場に送ってくれるて言ってくれてな`
>
> (`V14 / 0141.jpg`)

The safety logic is important. The support vehicle does not cancel the challenge. It **makes challenge reversible**.

Mei's confidence is also partly comparative. She has heard about prior long bicycle journeys and reasons that a road bike may make her own attempt more manageable (`V14 / 0147.jpg`). This is another instance of experience moving socially through stories, but the inference remains bounded: hearing another person's route does not remove the need to test one's own capacity.

Ema's Caribou job now becomes part of the trip's material infrastructure. She reports that product knowledge is difficult, notices how easy it is to want equipment while surrounded by it, and describes a coworker whose wages disappear into camping gear (`V14 / 0147–0149.jpg`). Ema herself still dislikes insects and does not suddenly become a camper. Instead she imagines outdoor gear as interior/furnishing material because she likes its design. This is excellent counterevidence against treating possession of “camping gear” as equivalent to camping identity.

Price also remains visible. Road bikes and camping equipment are expensive enough that Mei starts thinking about finding a part-time job (`V14 / 0149.jpg`). Leisure possibility is again constrained by income and acquisition, not just desire.

Immediately before departure, Ema remembers advice from work that long-distance cyclists need water and nutrition. She has brought portable food as backup:

> `自転車で長距離走る時は水分と栄養補給が大事なんだろ？`
>
> `携行食持ってくるのすっかり忘れてました`
>
> `私は一応携行食持ってきとるけど 足りんくなるかもしれんしありがたく頂くわ`
>
> (`V14 / 0157.jpg`)

Ema then says she will go in the teacher's car:

> `んじゃ私は先生の車で行くから`

Aoi and Mei depart by bicycle. Ema warns them about cars; the chapter ends with the ride underway (`V14 / 0158.jpg`). The camp itself is not yet completed inside Volume 14.

This ending is analytically useful because it freezes the trip at **planned differentiation becoming actual differentiated departure**, not at successful arrival. Volume 15 must still determine how the plan works under real distance, fatigue, weather, roads, and group reconvergence.

# 3. Character-state updates

## 3.1 Inuyama Aoi — major V14 delta

Aoi receives the largest established-character update in the volume.

Corrected V13 showed her first substantial road-bike experiment. V14 demonstrates that the experiment has persisted into practice:

- she rides the road bike to school;
- she reports that her gear-change timing is becoming more natural (`V14 / 0075.jpg`);
- she carries a spare tube because adult/family instruction has made contingency planning habitual;
- she stops to help Mei;
- she recognizes when her tools or knowledge may not fit the problem;
- she recruits an appropriate adult rather than pretending expertise;
- she later describes cycling as increasingly enjoyable;
- she co-designs a cycling-camp plan;
- she prepares for a long ride with a novice junior.

This is a significant competence consolidation.

### Family transmission without destiny

Aoi says the road bike came from her grandmother (`おばあちゃんにロードバイクをもろてから…`, `V14 / 0129.jpg`). The bike is inherited, but the practice is not predetermined. Aoi chooses to commute, improve shifting, imagine bicycle camping, and invite the new member into a ride.

That fits the project's mature family-transmission model:

> **Family can provide material access and practical knowledge without determining the meaning the younger person must give them.**

### Emerging senior / mentor mode

Aoi's roadside help precedes club membership. Once Mei joins, Aoi becomes the established cyclist beside a novice junior. Her seniority is not authoritarian; she shares enthusiasm, information, and planned activity while leaving room for Mei's fear and uncertainty.

This is not yet a mature “Aoi as mentor” thesis. But **emerging practical seniority** is now directly supported.

## 3.2 Nakatsugawa Mei — new canonical baseline

Volume 14 establishes Mei as the actual new Outclub member.

Her direct traits include:

- energetic, action-first presentation;
- a high-amplitude first-person social style marked by `～っス`;
- willingness to ask for/receive practical help;
- rapid curiosity about road bikes after seeing one;
- sensible use of rental/testing before ownership;
- enthusiasm that does not erase fear;
- immediate willingness to join the club;
- interest in bicycle camping;
- awareness that equipment costs may require paid work.

### Voice

Mei's `～っス` register is frequent enough to be treated as an early voice marker. It gives her speech youthful casual respect: informal energy combined with junior/senior awareness. Examples include:

> `中津川メイっス！`
>
> `ロードでキャンプ行ってみたいッス！`

This voice should be tracked longitudinally rather than reduced to a generic tomboy label.

### Repertoire baseline

Mei does not enter as an experienced camper. Her early identity is better described as **newcomer with strong mobility curiosity**. That matters because the Outclub's next plan is already being shaped around what she wants to try.

## 3.3 Mizunami Ema — differentiated entry rather than failed recruitment

Volume 14 turns Ema from a prospective younger character into a distinct institutional participant.

She remains an illustrator first. Her initial school problem is finding a quiet place where she can use her tablet. The library solves that need more directly than the Outclub does.

She later takes a job at Caribou. This does not automatically convert her into a camper. In fact, she says she dislikes insects and imagines camping furniture/equipment as stylish room interior rather than as proof of a camping vocation (`V14 / 0148.jpg`).

This is strong character preservation.

Yet work produces new knowledge. By the cycling-camp departure, Ema has learned enough from store staff to know the importance of water and portable nutrition for long-distance riding. She translates employment knowledge into practical care for Aoi and Mei (`V14 / 0157.jpg`).

**State transition:** aspiring digital artist seeking quiet space → student with an independent outdoor-retail work route whose material knowledge can support friends without requiring camping identity.

## 3.4 Ōgaki Chiaki — circle leader becomes formal club leader

Chiaki's V14 state has two components.

First, Chapter 77 completes the emotional/practical aftermath of her first solo camp. She applies prior experience to ambiguous animal noise, communicates easily with Rin during solitude, and moves back into group participation without requiring the solo episode to become a permanent identity shift.

Second, recruitment makes her institutional role newly consequential. She is no longer merely the loud organizer of a tiny circle. Mei's arrival creates the conditions for the Outclub to become a formal `部`. Chiaki's exaggerated politeness toward the new member is comedic, but it exposes how much she understands the newcomer's importance to institutional survival.

Her leadership style remains unmistakably Chiaki-shaped: anxious, improvisational, persuasive, humorous, willing to bargain with other clubs, and willing to reorganize activities around recruitment realities.

## 3.5 Kagamihara Nadeshiko

Nadeshiko's largest V14 contribution is institutional rather than personal-arc dramatic.

Her camp cooking becomes the Outclub's recruitment offering. Chiaki explicitly identifies Nadeshiko's food as a distinctive club strength. Nadeshiko then performs direct outreach to Ema with a mini-pizza and an invitation.

The result is analytically important because Ema **does not join**. Nadeshiko's sociability can create a welcoming opening without controlling another person's choice.

This strengthens Nadeshiko's role as a social bridge while providing counterevidence against any theory that her warmth automatically absorbs people into her group.

## 3.6 Shima Rin

Rin's V14 role is lighter after her substantial V13 solo/touring material.

The strongest contribution is relational/retrospective. Her separate spring experiences are compared with others through photos and conversation. She also messages Chiaki during the latter's solo camp, not to rescue or supervise her, but because she imagines Chiaki may have idle time and sends something strange/funny to look at.

Rin and Chiaki's exchange is now easy enough that separate solo camps become conversation material rather than exceptional events.

There is no major new Rin thesis in V14, and the analysis should not invent one merely because she is a central character.

## 3.7 Toba Minami

Toba's adult role strengthens again through logistics rather than moral authority.

For the cycling camp, she becomes the support driver and emergency recovery option. The key is that the adult fallback does not preempt the students' ambitious plan. Toba helps make an uncertain challenge reversible:

- carries luggage;
- transports non-cycling participants;
- remains available for rider/bike pickup;
- permits the junior to attempt the ride without defining withdrawal as failure.

This is one of the cleanest examples of **adult support enlarging youth autonomy rather than replacing it**.

# 4. Relationship-state updates

## 4.1 Aoi ↔ Mei — practical influence becomes senior/junior continuity

This is the volume's most important new dyad.

Their relationship begins before club membership through roadside assistance. Aoi notices Mei's puncture and stops. She does not know Mei as “a future junior”; she simply sees someone with a problem.

Afterward, Mei notices the road bike. The later rental experiment means Aoi's behavior becomes part of Mei's practical horizon, but the causal claim must remain careful:

> Aoi **exposes** Mei to a new possibility; Mei subsequently chooses to explore it.

Once Mei joins the Outclub, the dyad changes category. Aoi is now both an established member and the more experienced road cyclist. The cycling-camp plan gives them a shared activity in which skill/experience are unequal but purpose is mutual.

**State transition:** unknown helper/first-year → club senior/junior → planned cycling pair.

## 4.2 Ema ↔ Mei — differentiated friendship

The volume establishes that the two younger girls can remain close while choosing different institutional routes.

Mei tests road cycling and joins the Outclub. Ema looks for a drawing space, uses the library, and gets a job at Caribou. Neither choice is framed as abandonment of the other.

Ema later supports Mei's cycling attempt with portable food while traveling separately.

This is a compact next-cohort version of the series' broader anti-homogenization pattern:

> **Friendship does not require shared institution, shared hobby, or shared travel mode.**

No romantic claim is supported at this boundary.

## 4.3 Chiaki ↔ Mei — leadership gains an actual successor relation

Chiaki's relation to “future juniors” was imaginary in Volume 12. V14 gives her an actual new member.

Her over-the-top gratitude is comic, but the institutional fact is serious: Mei's membership makes formal club promotion possible.

The relation should be tracked for whether Chiaki transmits norms, imposes old routines, adapts to Mei, or does all three. V14 already shows adaptation through the cycling-camp plan.

## 4.4 Nadeshiko ↔ Ema — invitation without capture

Nadeshiko's direct recruitment contact is warm, concrete, and low-pressure. She offers food, names the club, and invites Ema to visit if interested.

Ema likes the food but chooses another immediate solution for her needs.

This is very good evidence for the project's social-regulation model:

> **An invitation can succeed as welcome even when it fails as recruitment.**

The relationship remains weak at V14.

## 4.5 Rin ↔ Chiaki — experiential legibility continues

Chiaki's V13 solo camp made Rin's preference more experientially legible. V14 adds ordinary post-experience exchange: the two can compare camps, food, scenery, and quiet through messages while still physically separate.

This is a modest strengthening of presupposed familiarity, not a major relationship transformation.

## 4.6 Aoi ↔ existing Outclub

Aoi's cycling interest now has enough continuity to pressure group planning. Her joke about turning the organization into `ゆる自転車部` is not a real institutional proposal, but Chapter 81 shows the underlying mechanism in non-joke form: the club creates a camping trip that makes cycling a central participant mode.

This supports a broader inference:

> **The group's identity is stable enough to incorporate member-specific interests without defending a pure definition of camping.**

# 5. The V12 inheritance hypothesis splits: institution versus object

Volume 12's firewood sequence produced a strong but provisional idea: current labor could create material value for hypothetical future juniors.

Volume 13 introduced younger students but did not connect them to the Outclub.

Volume 14 now changes the status substantially.

## 5.1 Institutional succession is real

Mei joins. The group reaches the membership threshold for promotion from circle to club.

This is direct evidence that the organization can persist across cohort boundaries.

**Decision:** `INSTITUTIONAL_SUCCESSION / INTER-COHORT_CONTINUITY` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN / PARTLY REALIZED**.

“Partly” matters because succession is not complete turnover. The current members remain. What has begun is **overlap between cohorts**.

## 5.2 Specific material inheritance remains unproven

Nothing in Volume 14 shows Mei using the V12 firewood, being assigned it, or receiving a material stock accumulated by the seniors.

Therefore:

**Decision:** `V12 FIREWOOD AS SPECIFIC MATERIAL INHERITANCE` → **PRESERVE / OPEN**.

This prevents a common analytical error: treating later institutional continuity as retroactive proof of every earlier imagined handoff.

## 5.3 Succession is not replication

The most important interpretive gain is that Mei changes the club almost immediately.

The first major post-recruitment plan is not simply “teach the junior the same campsite routine.” It combines road cycling with camping because Mei and Aoi are interested in cycling.

This supports:

> **Healthy institutional continuity may require the inherited institution to become available for alteration by the newcomer.**

This is still an `IN`, not yet a normative series-level `TH`. Later volumes must test whether newcomer agency continues or whether the group recenters old norms.

# 6. Repertoire expansion under preserved difference — V14 stress test

V14 strongly supports the governing model.

The relevant examples are now distributed across two generations:

- Aoi's road biking persists beyond a one-off experiment;
- Mei sees a sports bicycle, rents one, and begins considering bicycle camping;
- Ema does **not** become a camper but enters outdoor-material culture through employment;
- Chiaki's solo-camp episode returns to group life without becoming permanent “solo-cam identity”;
- Nadeshiko uses cooking as recruitment without converting new students into copies of herself.

The strongest refinement is:

> **Repertoire expansion does not require every person to sample the same repertoire. The social world becomes richer because possibilities circulate, while uptake remains selective.**

That is stronger than a simple “everyone tries new things” thesis.

Ema is especially valuable counterevidence. She can work in an outdoor shop, learn product knowledge, support a cycling trip, appreciate the design of camping gear, and still say she dislikes bugs and does not want camping itself. Material participation and hobby identity are separable.

**Decision:** `REPERTOIRE_EXPANSION_UNDER_PRESERVED_DIFFERENCE` → **STRENGTHEN**.

# 7. Configuration flexibility and modular group travel

The V10–V12 checkpoint deliberately separated:

- broad **configuration flexibility**;
- narrow **modular group travel**.

Volume 14 validates that distinction and strengthens both at their correct scopes.

## 7.1 Configuration flexibility

The new-school-term chapters support different forms of participation:

- Mei joins the club;
- Ema uses the library;
- Ema works at Caribou;
- Aoi cycles;
- Nadeshiko recruits through cooking;
- Chiaki organizes;
- Toba supports logistics;
- Ema joins the cycling-camp party through the car rather than the bicycle pair.

No single form is treated as the only legitimate way to belong near the outdoor world.

**Decision:** `CONFIGURATION_FLEXIBILITY` → **STRENGTHEN**.

## 7.2 Modular group travel now has a third strong example

Izu established a group trip in which Rin could take a separate motorcycle route.

Oigawa strengthened the pattern by explicitly composing a Rin–Ayano touring module and a Nadeshiko solo-feeling interval before reconvergence.

V14 now creates a third architecture:

> **Aoi + Mei cycle / Chiaki + Toba carry the camping load by car / Ema joins the support side / common campsite destination / emergency bike-and-rider recovery available.**

This is not accidental separation. The difference is designed into the plan.

**Decision:** `MODULAR_GROUP_TRAVEL` → **STRENGTHEN** from cross-trip RP to **strong cross-arc recurrent pattern**.

The generalization must still remain bounded. Not every subgroup outing is modular group travel. The term applies when one shared trip is intentionally composed from distinct travel/experience modules that coordinate and reconverge.

# 8. Competence as situated uncertainty management — V14 stress test

V14 is exceptionally strong for the competence ledger.

## 8.1 Aoi's puncture assistance

Aoi's help to Mei demonstrates competence through **recognition of limits**.

She knows enough to identify a puncture and think in terms of tubes/repair. She also knows enough to question whether her road-bike spare fits a city bicycle. She does not improvise recklessly. She gets a teacher.

The competence is therefore not “Aoi can repair every bicycle.” It is:

> **Aoi can diagnose the problem category, inventory her available tools, recognize uncertainty, and route the problem to a more appropriate resource.**

## 8.2 Mei's rental experiment

Mei does not respond to enthusiasm by immediately buying a road bike. Rental allows her to test fit, fatigue, enjoyment, and practical desire at lower commitment.

This is the same bounded-experiment logic previously seen in safe cooking/gear experiments, now applied to expensive mobility equipment.

## 8.3 Cycling-camp route design

The initial 70-km route frightens Mei. The planning process treats the fear as information rather than shame.

The group accounts for:

- route length;
- campsite availability;
- Golden Week capacity;
- luggage constraints;
- Mei's lack of a bicycle carrier;
- mechanical trouble;
- physical exhaustion;
- vehicle recovery.

This is precisely **situated uncertainty management**.

## 8.4 Adult backup without task cancellation

Toba's car is a contingency, not a substitute for the ride. The students can attempt something difficult because failure is made recoverable.

The distinction is important:

> **Reducing the cost of failure can increase meaningful autonomy without pretending risk has disappeared.**

## 8.5 Ema's portable-food contribution

Ema acquires knowledge from Caribou staff and applies it to another person's activity. The information is not glamorous: water and nutrition matter on long rides. Yet this is exactly the kind of ordinary procedural knowledge the manga treats as competence.

## 8.6 Afterword safety

The cardboard-oven warning provides literal paratextual support for the established risk distinction. Low-cost DIY is welcome only within procedural limits; if the method can become an uncontrolled fire, research comes first.

**Decision:** `SITUATED_UNCERTAINTY_MANAGEMENT` → **STRONGLY STRENGTHEN**.

# 9. Supported autonomy — from individual trips to institutional design

Volume 14 expands supported autonomy from individual acts into **trip architecture**.

Mei's planned cycling is genuinely hers. Yet it is made workable by:

- Aoi's more developed cycling familiarity;
- car-based luggage transport;
- Toba's recovery capacity;
- route/site research;
- Ema's food support;
- the option to stop if body or machine reaches a limit.

This does not make Mei passive. It makes a difficult novice activity reversible.

Ema's route is equally important. Nadeshiko invites her to the Outclub; Ema instead uses the library and takes a job. The social environment permits her to remain connected without compelling membership.

The mature formulation through V14 is:

> **Support is most autonomy-preserving when it increases the number of choices a person can safely sustain, including the choice not to enter the offered institution.**

This is a strong `IN`. V07's covert monitoring remains counterevidence to any absolute “care never overrides” formulation.

# 10. Informal pedagogy, practice transmission, and inter-cohort influence

Volume 13 made informal pedagogy explicit through camping etiquette taught by grandfather/acquaintances.

Volume 14 extends practice transmission into the next cohort.

## 10.1 Teaching can precede formal roles

Aoi helps Mei before either knows the future club relationship. That assistance makes a sports bicycle visible as an ordinary practical object rather than a distant specialty.

Later, Mei chooses to try one.

The defensible claim is **influence**, not intentional mentorship.

## 10.2 Formal membership creates new pedagogical opportunity

Once Mei joins, Aoi has practical seniority in road cycling and the group has camping experience Mei lacks. The planned trip creates a situation where knowledge can circulate in both directions.

## 10.3 Newcomers also alter what seniors learn/do

Crucially, transmission is not one-way. Mei's interest helps produce the cycling-camp plan. The senior institution reorganizes around the junior's possible practice.

This suggests a strong emerging formulation:

> **Transmission in Yuru Camp△ is increasingly reciprocal: people inherit know-how, but the presence of a new learner can also change which know-how the group develops next.**

**Status:** NEW STRONG `IN`; hold for V15 checkpoint.

# 11. Material culture, work, and economic access

Volume 14 substantially strengthens the project's material-culture and access ledger.

## 11.1 Outdoor gear does not imply outdoor identity

Ema is the best counterexample so far. She works at Caribou and sees camping gear as aesthetically appealing. She can imagine using it as room furniture while still disliking bugs and not wanting to camp.

Therefore:

> **Objects can migrate between practice domains without carrying a fixed identity meaning.**

## 11.2 Leisure access requires money

Mei recognizes that road bikes are expensive and that camping equipment adds another layer of cost. She begins considering a part-time job.

Ema already works because work itself becomes a route into material knowledge and purchasing possibility.

The manga still does not become a labor-politics treatise. But wages, jobs, prices, ownership, borrowing, rental, and family provision remain indispensable constraints on leisure.

## 11.3 Rental as access technology

Rental lets Mei test a high-cost practice before committing to purchase. This deserves a place in the material-culture ledger alongside borrowing, inheritance, buying, making, and repurposing.

The updated practice list is now:

> **buying / borrowing / inheriting / renting / making / repairing / repurposing / receiving / learning through work**

No one mode is morally privileged.

# 21. Counterevidence and rejected shortcuts

## 21.1 REJECT — “Mei simply inherits the seniors' club unchanged”

Her arrival changes the club's formal status and her cycling interest immediately affects the next trip design. Succession is adaptive.

## 21.2 REJECT — “Ema fails to join the Outclub and therefore drops out of the outdoor story”

She takes a Caribou job, learns practical material knowledge, supplies cycling food, and joins the support side of the trip. Non-membership is not narrative exclusion.

## 21.3 REJECT — “Aoi's help proves she is now a bicycle expert”

The puncture scene shows the opposite. She recognizes a likely repair issue but doubts tube compatibility and gets a teacher. Her competence includes limits.

## 21.4 REJECT — “The 70-km plan valorizes pushing through at all costs”

The support vehicle is explicitly available if body or machine reaches a limit. The plan makes withdrawal/recovery possible.

## 21.5 REJECT — “Camping gear only matters to people who camp”

Ema likes the design and imagines indoor use while still disliking bugs/camping. Material culture crosses practice boundaries.

## 21.6 REJECT — “Nadeshiko's recruitment warmth automatically converts strangers into group members”

Ema enjoys the pizza and then chooses the library/work route. Invitation does not determine uptake.

## 21.7 DOWNGRADE — “Material inheritance is already fulfilled because a junior exists”

Institutional succession is fulfilled in part; the specific V12 firewood handoff is not.

## 21.8 REJECT — “Every new-cohort pair reproduces an old character dyad”

Mei and Ema have distinct interests and routes. Mapping them mechanically onto Rin/Nadeshiko, Chiaki/Aoi, or any other established pair would flatten the evidence.

## 21.9 REJECT — “Modular travel means any time characters separate”

The term remains reserved for a single shared trip deliberately designed with differentiated movement/experience modules and planned reconvergence. Chapter 81 qualifies; unrelated parallel days do not.

## 21.10 HOLD — “Yuru Camp△ is now centrally about legacy”

Succession is stronger, but “legacy” remains too broad and solemn if it erases comedy, choice, discontinuity, and the fact that the newcomer changes the inherited structure. `Inter-cohort continuity` is currently more precise.

# 26. Volume-14 final freeze

The strongest responsible reading of Volume 14 is:

> **Volume 14 turns imagined succession into selective, adaptive continuity. Mei joins the Outclub and makes formal club status possible, but she does not simply receive an unchanged club: her road-cycling interest becomes part of the group's next trip. Ema enters the same outdoor ecosystem through the library, Caribou, and support logistics rather than membership. Aoi's practical help crosses the cohort boundary before formal recruitment, while later planning turns novice limits into logistics rather than reasons for exclusion. The future cohort therefore arrives not as copies of the old one but as new participants whose different interests cause the existing social and institutional system to reorganize.**

A second freeze concerns competence:

> **Volume 14 strengthens competence as situated uncertainty management by repeatedly making limits actionable. Aoi knows enough about Mei's puncture to recognize when her own tube knowledge may not fit; Mei tests road cycling by rental before committing; the 70-km camping plan builds luggage support and vehicle recovery into the challenge; Ema converts retail knowledge into portable nutrition; and Afro's afterword explicitly warns that an easy cardboard oven can become a fire if procedure is ignored. Competence is increasingly the ability to identify which uncertainty matters, route it to the right resource, and preserve a safe exit without cancelling self-authored activity.**

A third freeze concerns inheritance:

> **The V12 inheritance hypothesis must now divide. Institutional succession is real: a new student joins, and her presence changes the Outclub's formal status. Specific material inheritance remains unproven: the volume does not show the V12 firewood passing to her. More importantly, succession is already non-identical. Mei brings cycling into the club, while Ema chooses another institutional route. The strongest current model is therefore inter-cohort continuity through adaptation, not reproduction.**

The most concise longitudinal update is:

> **The club survives by becoming capable of being changed by the people who arrive next.**
