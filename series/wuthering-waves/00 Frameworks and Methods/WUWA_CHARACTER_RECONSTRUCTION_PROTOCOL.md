# WUWA character reconstruction protocol

## Goal

Produce a source-grounded character model capable of literary explanation and bounded unfamiliar-situation prediction without collapsing the character into an archetype, a timeless trait card, one relationship, one dub, or one evidentiary modality.

The preferred reconstruction order is deliberately staged. Textual and machine-audio evidence establish the initial model before audiovisual evidence is used to test, extend, qualify, or contradict it. Human perceptual listening is an optional post-reconstruction addendum rather than a prerequisite for a complete integrated reconstruction.

## Phase 0 — authority and scope

1. Read the WUWA current-state map and source boundary.
2. Resolve the character through the Drive evidence bridge and identity crosswalk.
3. Record the pinned source generation, languages, materialized modalities, missing surfaces, and future-version exclusion.
4. Decide whether the operation is a new reconstruction, update, reread, or model-only compilation.
5. Record which audiovisual surfaces are already materialized and which remain references only.

## Phase 1 — identity and occurrence adjudication

Before interpretation:

- enumerate accepted technical IDs and alternate forms;
- preserve compound speakers as compound;
- review hidden/generic candidates at exact locators;
- distinguish speaking, mention/context, message, archive, gameplay-only, and audiovisual-only identities;
- record unresolved occurrences instead of forcing attribution.

A character model built on contaminated attribution is invalid regardless of prose quality.

## Phase 2 — chronological and state reconstruction

Build working state slices from demonstrated changes in memory, role, goals, relationships, institutions, or durable behavior. Type each slice:

- developmental state;
- persona/form;
- operational context;
- relationship state.

Do not use patch order as diegetic chronology without support. Do not promote every costume, mission, combat mode, or temporary mood into a state.

This phase establishes a working chronology. Later audiovisual evidence may revise scene identity, temporal ordering, state boundaries, or contextual distinctions. Such revisions must preserve provenance rather than silently rewriting the earlier basis.

## Phase 3 — complete textual reading

Read source material in bounded sequence, including where available:

- main/companion/event story context;
- favor/archive stories and words;
- profile, materials, skill and item descriptions when narratively relevant;
- WavesLine/messages;
- public retellings, plays, summaries, and institutional descriptions;
- multilingual witnesses for high-value claims.

Treat summaries and public myths as reception evidence unless they are independently confirmed.

## Phase 4 — ordinary-life extraction

Actively search for low-stakes evidence. Record facts and limits concerning:

- food and drink;
- rest, sleep, routine, sensory attention;
- hobbies, leisure, music, dance, art, games, travel, crowds, solitude;
- gifting and shared activity;
- practical skills, deficits, learning, money, shopping;
- humor, embarrassment, praise, boredom, frustration;
- casual social initiative and conflict.

Ask: **What does this person do when nothing important is happening?**

## Phase 5 — speech and interaction

Model speech by state, situation, and relationship:

- public/private/crisis registers;
- directness, hesitation, disclosure, questions, commands;
- reassurance, teasing, disagreement, apology, praise response;
- terms of address and relationship markers;
- recurring rhetorical structures;
- localization-stable features and witness-specific differences.

Separate textual-register findings from performed-voice interpretations. Do not infer emotion or acting intent merely from waveform measurements.

## Phase 6 — comprehensive machine voice analysis

When usable audio exists, process the complete feasible corpus according to `WUWA_MACHINE_VOICE_ANALYSIS_PROTOCOL.md`.

The output should establish reproducible measurements, distributions, variant coverage, outliers, and review cohorts. It must not invent emotion labels or convert acoustic measurements into unmarked claims about personality, motivation, or acting intent.

Machine voice analysis is part of the normal reconstruction workflow when audio is materially available. Human perceptual listening is not required at this stage.

## Phase 7 — provisional modality-local claim construction

After textual/contextual reading and machine voice processing, construct provisional claims that are explicit about their evidentiary basis.

For each material claim record:

- proposition;
- state/situation/relationship scope;
- positive evidence;
- counterevidence;
- rival explanation;
- confidence;
- extrapolation limit;
- modalities reviewed;
- materially relevant modalities not yet reviewed.

Claims produced before audiovisual review are not yet fully integrated claims when relevant audiovisual evidence remains materially available.

Prefer claims that constrain predictions and can be falsified.

## Phase 8 — audiovisual evidence hierarchy and nomination

When audiovisual material is materially available or can reasonably be collected, perform an evidence-hierarchy pass before bulk acquisition.

Classify candidate material by source type and analytical responsibility. Relevant categories may include:

- canonical story cutscenes;
- scripted in-engine story or dialogue sequences;
- companion/event sequences;
- character archive/profile imagery;
- official character showcases;
- official trailers and promotional videos;
- character splash art and key art;
- official promotional illustrations;
- marketing composites or other stylized promotional material.

For each category, distinguish at least:

- **diegetic authority** — whether the depicted event or behavior occurs within the story/world;
- **representational authority** — whether the material is an official depiction of appearance, movement, design, or presentation;
- **interpretive authority** — how strongly the artifact can support a claim about personality, behavior, relationships, or state.

Do not treat lower-diegetic promotional material as useless. Route it to the questions it can actually answer.

The output of this phase is a bounded audiovisual collection plan, not an indiscriminate media dump. Prioritize material that can test or materially extend the reconstruction, including:

- high-emotion and low-emotion states;
- ordinary/default conversational states;
- contrasting interlocutors and relationship states;
- deception, hesitation, conflict, intimacy, embarrassment, anger, fear, confidence, or restraint where relevant;
- identity or state-transition scenes;
- recurring gestures, posture, movement, or physical mannerisms;
- costume/design reveals and repeated visual motifs;
- scenes where staging, framing, or animation materially contributes to interpretation;
- showcase or promotional material that exposes relevant movement or visual language not represented in story footage.

## Phase 9 — targeted audiovisual acquisition and analysis

Acquire the material selected in Phase 8 and preserve its source identity, provenance, date/version where relevant, and relationship to the textual/quest evidence.

Analyze only what the material can support. Relevant domains may include:

- facial expression;
- gesture and posture;
- body language and physical mannerisms;
- blocking and interpersonal distance;
- staging and spatial relationships;
- costume and character-design evidence;
- repeated visual motifs and iconography;
- shot composition and framing;
- animation/performance choices;
- combat embodiment where narratively relevant;
- differences between diegetic behavior and promotional presentation.

Do not collapse promotional framing into diegetic behavior. A trailer, splash image, showcase, or marketing illustration may establish official representational framing without establishing that the character habitually behaves that way in-story.

Where practical, use audiovisual evidence as a structured second-pass test of provisional textual and machine-audio claims. Record whether it:

- preserves;
- strengthens;
- qualifies;
- scope-limits;
- contradicts;
- or leaves open

the earlier proposition.

## Phase 10 — integrated claim construction

After all materially available mandatory modalities have been reviewed, convert accepted provisional findings into integrated claims.

For each material integrated claim preserve:

- proposition;
- state/situation/relationship scope;
- textual/contextual support;
- machine-audio support where relevant;
- audiovisual support where relevant;
- counterevidence;
- rival explanation;
- confidence;
- extrapolation limit;
- unresolved modality conflicts.

A strong integrated claim does not require every modality to agree. It requires disagreement to be visible rather than averaged away.

If a relevant modality is unavailable, say so. Lack of materialized evidence is not evidence of absence.

## Phase 11 — integrated monograph and specialist profiles

Write the monograph as the interpretive center only after the mandatory available textual, machine-audio, and audiovisual passes have been integrated.

Split ordinary-life, relationship/state, speech/voice, design/visual, or other specialist material into standalone homes only when evidence density warrants repeated retrieval.

The monograph must include rival readings rather than merely presenting the most elegant thesis. It must distinguish source-grounded character conclusions from localization-specific or promotional presentation effects.

A character may still receive a bounded textual reconstruction when audiovisual material is genuinely unavailable. Such an artifact must state its modality limits and must not be mislabeled as audiovisually hardened.

## Phase 12 — compiled model

Compile the machine-readable model from accepted integrated analysis. The model must select a state before predicting and must carry mandatory abstentions.

Do not include generated scenes as evidence.

Do not encode a localization-specific or promotional-only performance feature as a universal character trait without support.

## Phase 13 — fidelity and prospective testing

Test the model on:

- ordinary unstructured time;
- praise, embarrassment, minor failure, boredom, shopping, meals, gifts;
- unfamiliar public social settings;
- relationship-sensitive disagreement;
- crisis choices that expose contradictions;
- ambiguous romance cues;
- language/dub transfer traps;
- visual-presentation traps where promotional framing could be mistaken for habitual behavior.

A good answer is not necessarily decisive. Correct abstention is a pass condition.

## Phase 14 — integrated revision and hardening

Revise the monograph, specialist profiles, and compiled model in response to fidelity failures, modality conflicts, newly materialized mandatory evidence, or demonstrated state/chronology errors.

Preserve earlier measurements and provenance. Revision should state whether a prior claim was preserved, strengthened, qualified, scope-limited, contradicted, rejected, or left open.

A reconstruction may be considered fully integrated across the materially available mandatory modalities after this phase even if no human perceptual listening addendum has been performed.

## Optional addendum — human listening and performance annotation

Human perceptual review is optional and occurs after the integrated reconstruction unless a specific research question explicitly requires it earlier.

Its purpose is to test the completed reconstruction against human perception of performed voice, especially where text and reproducible acoustic measurements cannot capture qualities such as perceived warmth, guardedness, playfulness, brittleness, intimacy, restraint, formality, embarrassment, or other performance impressions.

Use machine-selected exemplars, outliers, state transitions, major claim lines, counterexamples, and cross-language contrasts rather than requiring exhaustive listening by default.

Keep three levels separate:

1. **low-inference observation** — audible pauses, laughter, sighs, breathiness, clipping, prolongation, pitch/register shifts, tempo changes, tremor, loudness contrasts, and similar directly perceivable features;
2. **interpretive performance judgment** — perceived affection, irritation, embarrassment, teasing, guardedness, formality, vulnerability, detachment, or similar listener judgments;
3. **character-level inference** — broader claims such as defensive humor, relationship-specific softening, or one dub presenting a systematically different persona.

Human perceptual judgments must be labeled as such. They do not become objective measurements merely because they are recorded systematically.

Where multiple languages are reviewed, distinguish performance-specific characterization from localization-stable characterization. A feature found strongly in one dub but weakly or not at all in others should not silently overwrite the underlying character model.

The preferred output is a separate artifact, for example:

`<CHARACTER>_HUMAN_VOICE_PERFORMANCE_ADDENDUM.md`

For each reviewed finding, record whether human listening:

- preserves;
- strengthens;
- adds nuance;
- revises;
- downgrades;
- contradicts;
- rejects;
- or leaves open

the existing interpretation.

Human review may recommend reopening an integrated claim. It must not silently overwrite the canonical monograph or delete reproducible machine measurements.

**Human performance review is not a prerequisite for an integrated character reconstruction.** It is optional perceptual enrichment and audit. The main reconstruction must remain reproducible without undocumented dependence on the project owner's subjective listening impressions.

## Completion states

Core reconstruction states:

- `textually_reconstructed`
- `machine_voice_profiled`
- `audiovisual_hierarchy_completed`
- `audiovisually_hardened`
- `integrated_reconstruction_completed`
- `model_compiled`
- `fidelity_checked`

Optional human-performance enrichment states:

- `human_performance_partially_reviewed`
- `human_performance_hardened`

These are independent dimensions, not a single false "complete" flag.

A character can legitimately be `integrated_reconstruction_completed` without either human-performance state. Conversely, human-performance review does not substitute for missing textual, identity, or materially available audiovisual evidence.
