# Phoebe — action at the vault and limits of solitary recovery

This correction comes from client-derived quest-tree summaries, not a newly watched playthrough or heard line. The [evidence matrix](WUWA_PHOEBE_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md) had already placed Phoebe with Zani at the vault and read her direct choices about Echo safety, nonbelievers and expression. It had not adequately registered the synopsis's account of the final threat and whose action ended it. The distinction matters to a behavioral model: an acolyte willing to question an institution is also capable of decisive protective action when that institution attempts concealment [PHO-E20, PHO-E36, PHO-C33].

## A purported relic and a reported intervention

Quest-tree node `230301` links four quest IDs, `122000008` through `122000011`, under main node `210030`. `QuestTree_Summary_230301` says Rover, Phoebe and Zani carry what the Order calls a “relic” into the Averardo Vault. They discover that the Montelli family sheltered artists and scientists from the Order; those people have died, but their Reverberations remain trapped. The item carried into the vault proves to be a device intended to erase evidence of the Order's wrongdoing. The English summary calls it a *bomb*. The Chinese anchor says a device, Japanese says a device, and Korean likewise describes a device rather than requiring an explosive mechanism. A reconstruction may mention an apparent bomb **as the English witness's wording**, but cannot impose an identical mechanism on all four texts or infer technical disarmament steps from the synopsis alone [PHO-E36, C33].

The four summaries converge on the attribution that Phoebe uses her power to resolve the crisis; the trapped Reverberations are released and the vault opens. English explicitly says she defuses the crisis before anything happens. Chinese, Japanese and Korean state the successful intervention and release without the same exact timing claim. This is a retrospective narrator's outcome, not a transcript of Phoebe explaining her method. It is stronger than the selected earlier `flow#/5643/2/3` and `flow#/5692/6/12–15` excerpts alone for *the outcome*, but weaker for her reasoning, precise ability mechanics or performed delivery. It credits Phoebe's act without erasing Rover and Zani as co-participants in the investigation. Neither their presence nor her final action proves that any of them knew the relic's purpose at entry [PHO-E20, E36, C33].

The moral contrast is sharp but not tidy. The Order both formed Phoebe's vocation and entrusted the group with the dangerous object; the summary attributes the evidence-erasure intent to the Order. That does not establish which individual authorized it, whether every acolyte knew, or that Phoebe herself had been knowingly complicit. Her action prevents the immediate concealment attempt as reported. It does not by itself constitute an institution-wide confession, restitution to the dead, repaired relationships with surviving kin, or formal amendment of doctrine. Earlier `flow#/5606/3/11–12` limits her ability to apologize for the whole Order. The vault outcome should strengthen the model's willingness to act against institutional wrongdoing, not create a universal claim that Phoebe can settle every harm with light [PHO-E18, E20, E36, C12, C33].

## Rebuilding is work distributed across people

A different side-quest node, `240002`, links quest `189800000` beneath main node `212000`. `QuestTree_Summary_240002` reports that recovery tasks in Rinascita after the Dark Tide leave Phoebe unable to cover all the work, and Rover decides to take on some Septimont rebuilding. Chinese's `分身乏术` conveys insufficient capacity to be in many places at once; Japanese likewise says her hands are full, and Korean emphasizes how much work there is. English says “overwhelmed.” This is a workload and practical-support fact, not a clinical diagnosis, a personality failure, or proof that she never seeks help herself. It also does not show a specific conversation in which Phoebe asks Rover to take over [PHO-E21, PHO-E37, PHO-C34].

The late crisis dialogue already distinguishes what her light can do for bodily/frequency corruption from unresolved emotional wounds. The reconstruction summary adds a second limit: even a powerful, conscientious helper cannot be physically present for every civic repair task. Rover's decision to share the Septimont work is evidence of distributed labor, not a debt she owes personally or proof of exclusive intimacy. A model can let Phoebe continue serving and accept practical allocation of duties without turning her into a flawless tireless saint or claiming she has abandoned the Order. Conversely, it should not make the synopsis's one burden moment a permanent state of incapacitation [PHO-E21, E37, C11, C34].

## What remains to verify

These summaries are exact pinned four-language textual witnesses attached to quest nodes. The chosen packet has not reconstructed every reachability detail or watched the vault device operated in a client runtime. A direct graph/runtime pass should identify the scene in which the device's nature becomes known, the visible power action, who witnesses it and whether the localization “bomb” is backed by a depicted explosive mechanism. For the recovery node, direct quest dialogue could reveal whether Phoebe requested, welcomed, resisted or never discussed Rover's help. Until then, the model should preserve the **reported outcome** and **the limits of attribution**, rather than filling both gaps with an imagined conversation or audio performance [PHO-E36–E37, C33–C34].
