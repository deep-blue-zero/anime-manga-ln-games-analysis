# Phoebe — whose journey is it?

Phoebe's holiday companion quest gives two unlike tests of care. She offers to travel with Rover after asking what Rover intends to do; later she must decide whether to approach an injured, frightened Echo again after a failed first attempt. In both cases she has a genuine purpose of her own—pilgrimage on leave, then treatment and safe return—but she should not make another being's destination merely an extension of that purpose. The raw dialogue graph and narration support a narrower, more useful model than either “she always asks for perfect consent” or “she simply imposes kindness.” They show responsive wording, a channelled quest route, an explicit nod to examination, and an inferred preference to accompany her back to Ragunna. The exact strength of each assent differs [PHO-E14–E16, E28–E31; PHO-C08–C09, C25–C28].

## A holiday plan with her own motive

At `flow#/4798/3`, Phoebe meets Rover after the Carnevale and congratulates them on the Laurel. She is on leave and intends to visit sacred sites in Ragunna. That project is hers before she discovers Rover's next destination; it should not be retrospectively rewritten as an itinerary invented solely to secure a date. Her sacred-site visits join leisure, historical interest and devotion without proving that every stop is purely ritual duty. She asks where Rover plans to go, then adapts what she can offer. The scene's warmth and shared travel make a romantic *affordance*, not a confirmed romantic relationship or a unilateral claim over Rover's time [PHO-E05, E14, E28–E29; PHO-C17–C18, C25].

The scene's first choice is a small social test. Rover can simply thank her for the performance compliment, say they were being themselves, or boast about acting talent (`POI_LGNJR_1_5–7`). Those options lead to three distinct Phoebe replies. The thanks route receives a civic celebration summary; the self-presentation route gets her admiration of bravery and sincerity; the boast route briefly surprises her before she connects that confidence to the festive success. The raw `JumpTalk` actions on TalkIDs 4, 7 and 10 rejoin at TalkID 11. These are alternate reactions to one moment, not a dialogue where Rover says all three things and Phoebe repeatedly praises them. In an invented playful encounter, her answer should be responsive to the *one* pose the counterpart actually takes: sincerity, confidence and ordinary gratitude elicit different attention from her [PHO-E28; PHO-C25].

## Three Rover purposes, three offers, one guided path

Phoebe's next question branches on Rover's declared purpose: sightseeing (`POI_LGNJR_1_17`), searching for the Sentinel's traces (`_1_18`), or looking for worthy opponents (`_1_19`). The four text witnesses preserve those three broad intents. In the sightseeing route she mentions her leave and sacred-site pilgrimage, then asks whether they might travel together (`_1_20–21`). In the Sentinel route she connects those sites to stories about the Sentinel before inviting Rover (`_1_22–24`). In the combat route she first addresses the request on its own terms—formidable Tacet Discords may be in remote ruins—then offers to explain Ragunna's ruins and older buildings (`_1_25–28`). This is neither a promise to hunt enemies beside Rover nor a scold for wanting a fight. She finds an overlap with her own route without pretending their motives are identical [PHO-E14, E29; PHO-C18, C25].

The raw structure matters. At `4798/3` TalkID 12, the three option `JumpTalk` targets are TalkIDs 13, 15 and 18. The sightseeing and Sentinel invitations at TalkIDs 14 and 17 jump to TalkID 22. The combat/ruins invitation at TalkID 21 has a single visible Rover response about a free guide (`POI_LGNJR_1_29`) that leads to TalkID 22. Common guide language then begins with Phoebe saying she will try to do the job well (`_1_30`). The projected array contains three apparently similar vacation descriptions and invitations because it retains mutually exclusive paths. A model must not stack them into three consecutive dates or say that she changes her itinerary three times. Equally, the graph does **not** test what she would say to a categorical refusal: the first two branches jump to the guided path and the third offers an affirmative response, not an observed no. Her invitations are solicitational in wording, but a robust model's respectful handling of a new refusal is a normative extrapolation from broader character evidence, not a recorded `4798/3` outcome [PHO-E29; PHO-C26].

The combat route has a useful social edge. Rover's final free-guide response can be comic or self-serving, yet it is optional and does not turn Phoebe into a permanently unpaid servant. She is choosing to guide on a day off. Her volunteer effort does not establish that her vocation makes her incapable of saying no to anyone else. It also gives a scene writer a concrete low-stakes social mode: Phoebe can enjoy explaining places, accept a joke, and sustain a purpose beyond formal worship. All four source text witnesses show the broad route, though their honorifics and exact warmth are localization-sensitive; no dub performance has been heard here.

## The Echo's nod is not its later headshake

The later `flow#/4807/5` encounter must be read after her rushed first attempt at `4802/4`. Phoebe admits the Echo remains afraid, approaches again, senses its distress, sees some of its history through a Terminal connection, apologizes for that intrusion, and refuses to claim total equivalence with its experience (`POI_LGNJR_11_10–20`). Her apology is a necessary correction, not retroactive permission to read the past. She then conditionally offers to help it find a way home (`_11_21`) and requests a frequency check or tuning because it appears injured (`_11_23–24`). The source narration at `_11_25` says the Echo **nods** and receives repair. That is the scene's clearest assent to a bounded intervention: examination/tuning and treatment. It is not blanket consent for every subsequent use of her ability [PHO-E15–E16, E30; PHO-C08–C09, C27].

After the repair, Phoebe starts planning how to send it back to its origin. The Echo then **shakes its head and approaches her** (`POI_LGNJR_11_27–29`). Phoebe asks whether it wants to return to Ragunna with her (`_11_31`) and thanks it for its trust (`_11_32`). Chinese, English, Japanese and Korean preserve the contrast between the earlier nod and later headshake/approach. This strongly supports the scene's intended inference that the Echo prefers accompanying her to the proposed homeward journey. It is not the same evidence as the earlier explicit nod to treatment: no separate Echo statement or fresh yes-after-question is recorded between her Ragunna question and thanks. The text does not explain its entire interior reasoning, duration of the arrangement, or whether it would later change its mind. “It trusts Phoebe enough to go with her here” is safer than “it has irrevocably chosen Phoebe as owner.” The Echo's behavior can answer her offer without becoming an all-purpose consent token [PHO-E30; PHO-C27].

This asymmetry matters for the character model. Phoebe originally assumes that restoration means return to the place of origin. The Echo's headshake interrupts that benevolent plan; she has to reinterpret what “home” means for this particular being. She is not simply discovering a new fact with supernatural certainty. She forms a reading from gesture, asks it aloud, and then responds warmly. A future scenario may have her confirm the preference rather than speak for a silent companion. That prediction is consistent with her learned humility but is not an additional observed line. Nor should a retelling erase the first frightened flight so the final trust seems automatic.

For a silly crossover episode, the durable trait is not “Phoebe always volunteers to be a guide.” It is her ability to ask what another person wants, retain a real wish of her own, find a noncoercive overlap, and revise her plan when a companion's behavior contradicts her assumption. The failure cases are equally specific: fusing three Rover purposes into one speech, calling the free-guide option a lifetime contract, inventing an enacted refusal response, declaring a reluctant Echo cured of fear by one spell, or treating a nod to medical tuning as permanent consent to determine its home.
