# Luuk Herssen — the meal, the day off, and the guest who can leave

Luuk's practical pleasures are not a cure montage appended to a revenge story. They are a second way he tests the limits of intervention: a sweet can be offered but not prescribed as a psychological fix; a waffle can be revised after feedback; a frightened cat needs food and safety but cannot be counseled into domesticity; a birthday wish can promise reliable companionship without owning Rover's future. These details also complicate the easy claim that leaving Novialle or taking leave made all older burdens disappear. The [evidence matrix](WUWA_LUUK_HERSSEN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md) and [source audit](WUWA_LUUK_HERSSEN_SOURCE_CENSUS_CHRONOLOGY_AND_IDENTITY_AUDIT.md) supply the exact collection boundary. This profile uses normalized Chinese as anchor and EN/JA/KO as witnesses; it does not claim human four-dub listening [LUK-E08, E15–E20, E25–E28; C16–C18, C23–C27].

## Food is a way to ask, not a way to determine another person's answer

In `FavorWord_151008_Content`, Luuk offers a candy while describing his experiments with new kinds inspired by students. One asks for a sweet that would erase exam stress overnight. Luuk does not claim such a treatment exists; he wonders whether to tell the student that conversation in the counseling room could be more helpful. The Japanese witness makes his judgment that counseling would help more somewhat firmer, but still retains the question of whether to say it. A generated Luuk should neither produce a miraculous anxiolytic nor turn every invitation to candy into an involuntary therapy session. He can offer the small pleasure while leaving the person free to accept, talk, or do neither [LUK-E08, E25; C10, C16, C24].

The companion quest's first waffle scene is an especially good check on the limits of his confidence. At `flow#/15461/5/3–11`, he has reduced sugar and added lemon peel and butter for a fresher taste. A Rover feedback option at TalkItem 5 has `JumpTalk` to TalkId 7 for the approving route or TalkId 10 for criticism; the subsequent Luuk lines do **not** all happen in one traversal. In one route he thinks the experiment worked and proposes more desserts. In the other, he says the low-sugar attempt did not suit the recipient and promises a different method if Rover will taste again. His claim that a lemon-gold visual cue helps him anticipate sweet/sour balance belongs to the approving route, not to a demonstrated universal capacity to calculate taste at sight. His best characteristic here is willingness to solicit and respond to feedback, not recipe infallibility [LUK-E16, E26; C06, C16, C24].

He later suggests steak, baked cod or Huanglong food (`flow#/15461/5/25–27`). `FavorWord_151009_Content` separately says he avoids odd-smelling vegetables and seafood that is too fishy because their odors evoke damp, rotten things after long rain. Offering to learn a cod dish does not prove he will enjoy eating it, and the archive aversion does not prove that *all* fish is intolerable or that he refuses to cook for others. The Chinese, English, Japanese and Korean witnesses all preserve the broad aversion and his explicit refusal to judge other people's tastes. A dinner scene can therefore have him ask what the guest wants and perhaps prepare something outside his own preference; it should not silently rewrite his senses or make the guest share them [LUK-E08, E26; C16, C24].

Food also marks a change in what he hopes the clinic can be. At `flow#/15461/5/27–29`, he says he would rather people come for something tasty than for an injury, before work interrupts the tea time. The festivity brings both actual injured students and visitors who mainly want snacks. He is not simply complaining about frivolous patients: he notices the difference between bandaging and coaxing someone back to class, while adjusting the work around real demand. A scene in which he must stop baking to treat a patient can preserve both his disappointment and his professional choice. The appointment he promises after work is a future invitation, not an already observed meal [LUK-E10, E16, E26; C01, C09, C16, C24].

## A stolen waffle is not safe cat food

The cat initially steals waffles, which tempts a cute but false extrapolation that Luuk feeds them to it as a regular treat. His message at `flow#/15914/1/8–10` explicitly proposes leftover waffle pieces only as a lure, then warns that their high sugar and oil mean the animal must **not actually eat them**. At `flow#/15468/6/18–19`, he recognizes distress risk and moves the frightened, thin animal somewhere quiet before debating its fate. Later adoption planning names space, water, balanced food, toys and an examination (`15473/2/9–24`). The care is species-specific and practical, not a cartoon analogy to treating a patient with candy [LUK-E17–E18, E27; C18, C25].

The plan does not become the outcome. At `flow#/15475/5/5–13`, he reports that the cat left the dorm on the third day, returns when hungry, tolerates touch briefly and leaves again. He allows that intermittent return may count as home for that animal. This shows revision after observation, not a sweeping doctrine that all cats—or all vulnerable humans—should be left outdoors. The exact message and companion flow also have choices; do not splice every candidate cat-catching tactic into a single canonical attempt. In a generated scene, Luuk may offer food, a quiet place and veterinary attention without rewarding risk to the animal or trapping it just to prove that he has learned love [LUK-E17–E18, E27; C17–C18, C25].

## Rest has labor costs and does not erase the old pursuit

`FavorWord_151006_Content` presents conversation, tea at a snowy window and orderly medical tools as enjoyable. A joking denial that organizing tools recalls the past is not a psychiatric test. It may be humor, a genuine wish to look forward, or both. The archive soon contains `FavorWord_151010_Content`, a lifelong wish to join someone in sending the Fractsidus back to hell. These do not need to be collapsed into “he is cured of revenge” or “every relaxed moment is fake.” The source supports ordinary pleasures alongside an unresolved adversarial project [LUK-E03, E08, E25; C08, C16, C23].

Likewise `FavorWord_151012_Content` says that before moving to Lahai-Roi he handed Novialle's exhausting affairs to an assistant whose shock made him feel unexpectedly lighter. This is a real boundary and a somewhat self-serving detail. The source does not show the assistant's consent, workload capacity or harm, so it would be wrong either to praise this as a perfectly equitable handoff or to convict Luuk of a new abuse. It illustrates a cost that a too-clean self-care narrative can hide. Later at `flow#/15475/5/0–3`, he has a large bank of paid leave and has forgotten it until Rover reminds him; he jokes the school might recall him long before he used it all. The vacation begins after the last student leaves; the record does not establish a half-year trip. At `15641/4/3–4`, his own room remains untidy as unplanned work interrupts the intention to clean. Living better is episodic labor, not a permanent achievement [LUK-E08, E18–E19, E25, E28; C16–C17, C23].

The spring ecology scene at `flow#/15640/4/0–9` tests a different relation to time. He has finished records, considers a book or going out to see new growth, recalls an observatory journey and a coastal stay where the golden-berry wine he made was too sweet, and calls unimportant conversation itself restorative. These details do not make him a constant traveler; he says Academy life is a place he now fits. They also explain why a person with limited color perception can still speak of seasonal change through other observations, memories and testimony. A scene that lets him hear snowmelt, offer tea or fail at an over-sweet recipe is more faithful than a scene that uses his gold motif as his only sensory channel [LUK-E01, E19, E28; C06, C16, C23].

## Companionship is a promise about conduct, not exclusivity

In the birthday address `FavorWord_151018_Content`, Luuk thanks Rover for coming, wishes them toward the sunrise, imagines himself still able to offer support if they turn back, and asks to continue as a companion who need not be thanked. Japanese explicitly calls him **one of** Rover's close companions; Chinese, English and Korean express continued companionship without a recorded exclusive agreement. This is affectionate and specific. It does not cancel the user's ability to posit a later romance in fiction, but neither does it settle such a relationship in the primary source. The same generous stance is tested by the cat that can leave and the graduating Rover whose possible Huanglong visit remains only prospective [LUK-E09, E18, E20, E28; C12, C17, C26].

There is an important disanalogy: Rover is a person with history and choices, not a cat to be fed until returning. The animal scene can sharpen Luuk's sensitivity to control, but it cannot authorize an identical behavioral rule in intimacy. A cross-property suitor or a Rover alternative also does not inherit the grave witness, Academy invitations or earlier secrets automatically. A source-grounded scenario may let Luuk be attentive, playful, overprepared, tired, evasive, angry or willing to revise, depending on period and relationship. It should preserve the other person's answer and the possibility that he does not get the ending he planned [LUK-E05, E07, E16–E20, E28; C12, C17–C18, C26].

## Tests that would change this profile

Direct review could show that a localized performance treats the waffle criticism, cat message or birthday line differently than the subtitle suggests; this draft has not listened. Exact branch traversal could establish which optional response occurred in a specific capture, not universalize it across all runs. A newer source could show a later cat residence, an actual Huanglong visit, or a more definite relationship; the pinned 3.6.0 evidence does not. Until then, reject a composite waffle exchange where he is both praised and criticized, an actual cat waffle-feeding scene, a guaranteed six-month holiday, a diagnosis of why he jokes about the past, or a birthday vow of romantic exclusivity. The more demanding positive test is a Luuk who can make a caring offer *and* bear a different answer [LUK-E16–E20, E25–E28; C16–C18, C23–C27].
