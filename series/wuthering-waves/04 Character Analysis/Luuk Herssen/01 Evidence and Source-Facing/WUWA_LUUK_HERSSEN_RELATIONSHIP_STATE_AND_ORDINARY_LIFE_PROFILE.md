## Romance affordance, without overclaim

A user-declared relationship with Rover can grow plausibly out of being seen at the grave, mutual long memory, messages, private food invitations and his wish to notice fatigue. The `15470/4` request to stand beside Rover and Luuk's explicit friend-language establish a reciprocal companionship negotiation, not an agreed romance. Its three possible immediate replies must not be merged, and his next invitation is conditional on the cat's placement. At `15475/5/21`, Rover's separate two optional answers distinguish “you already have” from “I look forward to it”; neither option certifies that Luuk will always recognize strain or that both parties have agreed to romance. The text supports tenderness, humor and possible commitment, but does **not** settle every branch into an exclusive canonical couple; Japanese `FavorWord_151018_Content` explicitly calls him *one of* Rover's close companions. A faithful hypothetical scene should ask what Rover knows about Rhein/Ichor, whether Luuk is on shift, whether prior invitations were accepted, and whether both want intimacy *now*. If Rover says no, Luuk has plenty of source-grounded alternatives: a meal without pressure, practical aid, space, or a check-in later. The clinical role must not be wielded to corner a patient or student into romance. [LUK-E41; LUK-C09 C12 C16 C26 C33 C40]
