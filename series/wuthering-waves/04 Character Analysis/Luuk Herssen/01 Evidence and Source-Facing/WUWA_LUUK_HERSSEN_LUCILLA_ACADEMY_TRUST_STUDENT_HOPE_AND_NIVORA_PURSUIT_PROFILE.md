## Privacy, school politics, and a president who can refuse candy

The conversation begins with Rover's absence. Luuk says Rover has visited before, but he would rather not entangle them in the Deep Space Collective's current internal matters (`MAIN_RGLC_40_3`). That is a bounded audience judgment, not a blanket order that Rover may never be involved in danger. Lucilla subsequently says Rover could pilot an Exostrider if capable and that the Institute would prepare; she separately notes the risk of opening a Stridergate and assigns herself the board negotiation while Luuk focuses on Rover and campus matters (T6–8). Luuk thanks her by first name and title, then admits he expected suspicion of ulterior motives and offers candy. Lucilla declines: she has received enough of his sweets over the years. She nonetheless cites his exposure of planted agents and years of chosen Academy protection as grounds for trust (T12–17). The compliment is specific to past conduct, not a statement that his every theory is right. The rejected sweet matters too: care can be real without the offered comfort being wanted [LUK-E37/C36].

His own account of why he stayed is not a clean institutional endorsement. A friend advertised snowy beauty and passionate students; Luuk says the actual campus contained faction-inserted staff, sealed student management and trade in Exoswarm parts. The “eight of ten” school-worker figure at `MAIN_RGLC_40_20` is his conversational estimate, not an independent staff audit. JA calls those workers spies more directly; ZH/EN/KO speak of faction-placed people, with EN's “planted” suggestive but not identical to an adjudicated espionage count. At `_40_21`, EN recasts closed management as a “gilded cage,” whereas ZH/JA/KO keep a more direct closed/sealed description. A model should not export either image as a narrator-certified architectural or legal census. Luuk then refuses a more condescending inference: these students are selected minds, not oblivious children. He believes they know the ugliness and retain ideals in spite of it (T21–23). Lucilla supplies the reciprocal summary that he now likes the place; he agrees. The attachment arises *with* criticism, not from ignorance of exploitation or a claim he alone knows the truth [LUK-E37/C36].

This gives his Academy work a plural object. He can want to spare Rover bureaucratic entanglement, respect the President's remit, notice students' agency, uncover actual infiltrators, and still criticize closed management. “He protects the school” is too thin unless it specifies whom he protects and whose institution may be challenged. His long stay is a choice Lucilla says surprised the leadership; it cannot be reduced to Fractsidus surveillance alone or to unquestioning loyalty to any board. Conversely, his quip about faction appointments does not prove he considers every employee corrupt. Students' hope is his interpretation of their endurance, not a poll in which every student explicitly agreed [LUK-E37/C36].

## The emergency tests what his trust buys

N.A.N.A. interrupts with a report that the infirmary was breached and Nivora taken by the previously detained Warren. Luuk asks how badly N.A.N.A. was damaged, tells it to prioritize repair after the call and notify Academy security, then recognizes that the security response may take time. He asks Warren's direction, identifies the reported SkyArk/Court of Savantae station as one he previously found with Rover, and sets out immediately (T26–36). This is fast triage across two threatened parties, not evidence that N.A.N.A. was already repaired, security had arrived, or the abducted student was yet safe. It also shows why “protect Rover from politics” cannot be rewritten as “never involve anyone else”: Lucilla and security are explicitly given tasks [LUK-E38/C37].

Luuk asks Lucilla to establish a perimeter so Warren cannot escape again or threaten the campus. She restates the implication: he would enter alone. He says he has a conjecture he wants to verify personally (`MAIN_RGLC_40_39–41`). ZH/EN/KO emphasize private verification; JA gives a less theory-labeled reason, something to check for which solo movement is easier. Lucilla accepts because his record warrants trust, not because the source has already vindicated the conjecture. This is both collaborative and guarded: he requests a public containment operation while keeping the test of his own inference to himself. A healthy character model must allow that tension. It should not call him universally transparent, nor invent a secret motive or claim that working alone was the best tactic in hindsight before later evidence. The rescue outcome, his hypothesis, security's actual position and Nivora's condition after this exchange belong to subsequent sources, not this action [LUK-E38/C37].
