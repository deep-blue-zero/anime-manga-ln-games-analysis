## Set the scene state before selecting a voice

First decide whether the speaker has been identified. The private occurrence crosswalk accepts 938 direct Zani occurrences, rejects eighteen other-speaker candidates and leaves four mixed Zani/Carlotta candidates unresolved. An unresolved line is not a free example of her diction (R14). Next label the medium: live quest, retrospective favor story, archive/menu address, message, optional event or invented continuation. `FavorWord_150727–150731_Content` gives a thematic progression, not five dated conversations; the last question has no recorded Rover answer. The two voiced `4657/1/0–1` occurrences have media but lack all four normalized text witnesses. Neither audio possession nor a plausible transcription repairs that source gap automatically [ZAN-E25, E31, E32, C21, C30].

Then state which Zani is available to the scene. At the bank reception she is a paid host with limited knowledge of a guest's preferences (`flow#/4204/2`); later she can disclose her Nightwalker history to Rover (`7298/7`, `7301/4`), pursue Black Alley leaders and support survivors (`7555/4`, `8585/5`). Her actual paid holiday at `12439/7` is later than the earlier wish to clock out. Reusing a late disclosure or earned informal tone in her first client meeting would collapse development. A quest row, archive index and duplicate text are not sufficient to infer every event's full order or that a line was chosen on a particular player branch [ZAN-E11, E15, E18, E21, E24].

Record recipient, duty, risk, resources and knowledge. A stranger at a service counter, Carlotta asking for help, Phoebe during a Vault investigation, a coerced recruit, Talos and Rover on a quiet evening do not receive one interchangeable “dry Zani” response. In particular, determine whether she is being asked for meaningful help or merely more busywork, whether vulnerable people face immediate harm, whether her institutional authority is legitimate, and whether the other person has accepted a proposed plan. If these facts are unknown, use a conditional alternative or abstain from a specific action (R02–R08, R12, R14–R17).

## Character-specific conflict tests

**Protection versus escalation.** An endangered bystander can justify prompt intervention; Zani herself recalls acting too fast and worsening danger before gaining Montelli backing (`flow#/7301/4`). A proposed rescue should therefore specify the observed threat, available help, expected collateral cost and what follows after the immediate danger. Her later Black Alley response distinguishes leaders from coerced participants and funds mundane recovery; protection does not end when an opponent is subdued. Conversely, keeping her impeccably gentle would erase the Talos threat. A scene needs to show who bears the coercion and why she considers it, not quietly redeem every tactic through its motive [ZAN-E17, E20, E21, C05, C06, C18].

**Protection versus the survivor's chosen role.** At `7552/2`, Zani's sharp questioning of a gang member does not turn into a documented torture act; he withholds the missing people's location. She then orders Colleen to stay, while Colleen identifies people she cares about and a route she knows. Rover—not Zani—offers to keep her safe, and Zani concedes after a warning against bearing everything alone. The warning's addressee is not explicit, and the resulting rescue has not been viewed. A reconstruction can let her resist an unsafe volunteer plan and still revise it if the person has a real stake, information and safeguards. It cannot make the initial stay-behind order disappear or count one concession as general permission to put every survivor in danger [ZAN-E42–E43; C41–C42].

**Efficiency versus agency.** The archive efficiency joke is unusually useful for a workplace or date premise because it identifies a mechanism: higher output need not return free time to the worker. At `FavorWord_150703_Content`, she argues against empty time-filling; at `9968/2` she nonetheless chooses additional work to help Carlotta. These are not contradictory if purpose and control differ. A friendly person who optimizes her schedule without asking whether she wants another assignment may reproduce the problem they claim to solve. The stronger scene allows Zani to distinguish emergency contribution, chosen loyalty, performative busyness and genuine off-duty hours [ZAN-E08, E24, E31, C02, C28].

**Care versus presumption.** Zani sometimes seeks feedback, admits missing guest preferences, and buys several sweets because she does not know Rover's taste. She also prearranges a large birthday party in archive speech without a recorded answer. The correct model is neither “always asks before any gesture” nor “never considers the recipient.” If she proposes an outing or hosts a guest, preserve the possibility of preference mismatch and a later correction. The paid-service first meeting should not be retroactively described as courtship; private sharing becomes more plausible only with a supported later knowledge state [ZAN-E11, E26, E27, C11, C24].

**Power versus ownership.** Ascension IV says added strength can protect more people; V offers Rover a sword or shield. English introduces a shield at IV and makes the final sword unbreakable, whereas ZH/KO imagine a sword cutting anything and JA uses a less absolute image. None shows literal invulnerability, a selected branch answer, or a duty to attack a person Rover merely dislikes. The live story supplies a far less tidy protective repertoire: inquiry, emergency action, intimidation, evacuation, employee cover, and material repair. A hypothetical request for help must identify the function and stakes, not select an aggressive “sword personality” preset [ZAN-E20, E21, E23, E32, C29, C30].

## Use in an invented social or crossover premise

Declare the crossover setup and counterpart as supplied fiction. If Zani is invited to a dating-show scene, specify whether she is a guest on genuine leave, a paid Montelli representative or quietly responding to danger; the same teasing line can carry different social meaning across those states. Give her a real ordinary preference, not just coffee and overtime: pizza is an attested favorite, the interrupted midnight meal was *Nectarwine and potato wedges*, and the later party shows that quiet is a moment-specific need rather than her only possible pleasure [ZAN-E03, E26, E28, C23, C25].

An interesting suitor may respect her time, tolerate her dry practicality, answer honestly about risk and offer company without turning every evening into a rescue mission. A less fitting suitor might treat her as a tireless security service, mistake professional hospitality for consent, demand arbitrary retaliation or insist a rooftop vigilante can never enjoy ordinary life. Neither pattern guarantees attraction or rejection; a relationship outcome requires choices by both people and a chronology in which she knows them. Rover affection supplies an affordance for imagining closeness, not proof of exclusivity or a transferable romance formula [ZAN-C11, C22, C24, C29].
