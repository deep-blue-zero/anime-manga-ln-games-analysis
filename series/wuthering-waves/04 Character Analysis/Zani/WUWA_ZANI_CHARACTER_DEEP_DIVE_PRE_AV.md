# Zani: the nightwalker who wants a city that can sleep

Zani is easy to shorthand as the exhausted, sarcastic bank employee with horns. That description is visually memorable and textually incomplete. She is a trained security professional who can change registers in the middle of a bank reception, an investigator capable of revising her initial theory, and a covert fighter who acknowledges that her first lonely attempts to stop harm made some things worse. Her desired end state is unusually anti-mythic: a Ragunna stable enough that the Blazing Nightwalker no longer needs to be relevant. She asks for meaningful work and for the right to stop; repeatedly, she finds it hard to practice the second desire. This is a *reading* of the selected, pinned source—not a claim that every line is a secret confession. The cross-referenced evidence and counterreadings are in the [matrix](WUWA_ZANI_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md).

## A worker before a legend

The official-seeming employee profile gives the economical public contradiction: Averardo Vault's long-serving “best employee” most wants to clock out on time (`FavorRoleInfo_1507_Info`). The archive does not merely repeat a burnout gag. In `FavorWord_150703_Content`, she explicitly distinguishes useful work and overtime from pointless desk hours and petty tasks that devour private time. The contrast matters: a scene that has her reject every assignment because she is “lazy” would miss her ethic of efficiency; one that has her consent to every extra shift because she “loves work” would miss the same ethic from the other direction. In `FavorStory_150703_Content`, coworkers see prodigious performance and float mutually incompatible explanations: devotion to the city, status ambition, a secret side payment. Their uncertainty is the point. Her understated reply that she is an ordinary employee should not be taken as a complete objective self-description, but neither are their rumors evidence that she seeks a Montelli surname.

Her tools of professionalism have emotional uses. `FavorWord_150702_Content` cites the employee manual's grooming and etiquette rule as a bargaining advantage, and `FavorStory_150705_Content` lingers over preparation for a working day. The pocket alarm in `FavorWord_150711_Content` is not solely costume symbolism: she says an early late arrival nearly caused a serious loss, so she built a portable reminder into her routine. The routine is practical, slightly absurd and self-imposed. It is also fragile enough to become a theme in her later account of the Nightwalker. Her excellent performance is not effortless invulnerability: the same archive depicts fatigue, energy drinks, canceled mealtime, and a paid-holiday promise. In `FavorStory_150701_Content` she orders Nectarwine and potato wedges with extra gemberry jam and pepper, but leaves before receiving them when she notices a rooftop intruder; she does **not** order pizza in that scene. The fondness for Margherita's pizza is separately explicit in `FavorWord_150708_Content`. The distinction matters because she loses a *specific* meal before she becomes anybody's legend [ZAN-E03].

The ascension-menu sequence sharpens a material workplace tension without supplying a dated episode. Its first address treats training as keeping a blade usable for work; its second makes better efficiency a problem because it can remove the opportunity to loaf or merely look occupied, with Japanese explicitly anticipating more work (`FavorWord_150727–150728_Content`; `favorword#/2188–2189`). This is deadpan skepticism about what employers do with gained capacity, not evidence that Zani actually failed a shift. It belongs beside her explicit resistance to pointless desk time and her later offer to stay longer for Carlotta: competence, willingness and protected rest are three different things. The offer is not proof that another shift occurred [ZAN-E08, E24, E31, C28].

Zani knows Ragunna in bodily and operational detail. `FavorWord_150710_Content` places sea air, shops, plaza and restaurant aroma inside her ideal of a quiet city where people have a place. That is not a generic wish for abstract peace. In the early bank reception at `flow#/4204/2`, she can explain service tiers, equipment and a travel itinerary, recommend a particular pizza, and pivot to an exact internal instruction about insured auction pieces. When Rover wants less formality, her relief is immediate but bounded by the job. Later direct dialogue has her infer routes from a flying Echo, report a suspect's movements, and seek Phoebe's help reading a worn inscription (`flow#/4578/6`, `4594/5`). Her professional knowledge is real, yet she is surprised when a Vault guardian does not behave as the manual predicted (`4606/3`). Her competence is therefore a method of observing and updating, not knowledge of all future events.

The intervening Pioneer Association inquiry at `4245/2` shows that method in motion. Zani brings Rover to Fulmine for help tracing a Vitreum Dancer; Fulmine and C-MOSS can project a recorded view, but Fulmine can record only what he saw. Zani grants that the image helps, then says the Echo's exit beyond the city leaves too broad and disturbed a search area. She does not wave away the limitation or perform a miraculous deduction. When the image suggests someone in Montelli-like clothing and a pre-Carnevale mask, one player route prompts her to infer possible concealment while another prompts an atypical-mask observation. She then weighs the family's incentive to avoid a Reserved-Terminal scandal **if** the figure is implicated, and treats mask-maker Nyarla as a possible next source while noting nobles' privacy. The suggestion “spy or disguise” is Rover's attached option; Fulmine initially asks whether the family could be involved. Neither is Zani's proved verdict. Chinese's “as if” at the mask line is more tentative than the English “must have wanted.” The graph's alternate investigation requests even reuse the same four-dub audio while retaining distinct source occurrences, so a single imagined playthrough must not include both. This is specific, fallible detective work, not blind employer loyalty or an ability to settle guilt from a projected costume [ZAN-E44, C43]. See the [inquiry specialist](WUWA_ZANI_FULMINE_MASK_INQUIRY_AND_EVIDENCE_LIMITS_PROFILE.md) for the route and witness map.

Her first guest plan also prevents an easy equation of efficiency with pleasure. In `FavorStory_150705_Content` she thinks through lunch, a backup pizza stop and waterfront time before she knows the client's preferences; the story names the paid-leave incentive and narrates nervous tail movement. At `4204/2/10–18` she openly acknowledges missing itinerary information and offers a very fast, low-risk sightseeing scheme while saying she personally does **not** recommend it. She can respect a client's request without believing a checklist is the best way to experience a city. Her later choice to buy one of every sweet because she does not know Rover's taste (`7297/7/0–4`) is not retroactive proof she had read the first guest perfectly, nor a reason to romanticize professional service as courtship. She learns and accommodates under uncertainty [ZAN-E26–E27, C24].

## The sound that costs her supper

The first favor story gives her conflict a scene structure rather than merely a list of traits. Ragunna has gone quiet; Margherita is closing; Zani orders an exact, familiar meal and starts imagining a better breakfast after a cold one upset her stomach. Then a displaced roof tile makes a tiny noise. Margherita and the sleeping city do not register it, but Zani does. She pays before she leaves, and the food arrives to an empty place (`FavorStory_150701_Content`). The beat is not evidence that she dislikes rest or food. Its force depends on the opposite: she wanted the meal, knew where to get it, and lost an ordinary pleasure because she could not ignore a possible threat. The restaurant's continuing life is part of what she protects. The scene makes vigilance costly without asking the reader to celebrate hunger as a heroic virtue [ZAN-E03, E09, E26].

There is a related but different interruption in the Carnevale-eve story. Overtime invades her fragmented dreams; waking means silencing an alarm, straightening a tie, and rehearsing contingencies for a guest she has not met (`FavorStory_150705_Content`). The promised paid leave is an incentive for getting the assignment right, not a gift she has already enjoyed. She tries to reassure herself that she is merely a replaceable employee, yet the narration records nerves and her careful preparation. The exact guest plan mixes genuine hospitality with paid obligation and uncertainty about the coming crisis. Neither "purely transactional bodyguard" nor "instant intimate attachment" accounts for the whole scene. Its narrative irony is that she approaches a consequential relationship through the same scheduling competence that has consumed her sleep [ZAN-E07, E26].

Her alarm and paperwork should therefore not be treated as comic accessories detachable from the plot. The alarm has a concrete history of nearly costly lateness; the reports and itinerary are ways of reducing avoidable harm; the unending desk hours are also part of what exhausts her. The question is not whether Zani is diligent—she is—but whether diligence remains her own instrument or becomes a reason for others to requisition every newly freed hour. The ascension joke about greater efficiency making slack or apparent busyness harder states that problem in miniature. The source does not tell us that she has solved it [ZAN-E07–E08, E24, E31].

## Violence, protection, and the myth she refuses

The in-world talent document reports an earlier severe assault by redacted perpetrators and her Forte awakening in self-defense (`FavorRoleInfo_1507_TalentDoc`). It describes the physical consequences and leaves her resonance origin undetermined. The comments comparing her features to pre-Lament folklore or a goat are explicitly conjectures and jokes within that document, not etiological facts. A favor story gives a different kind of childhood adjacency: after saving an abducted girl and suffering a shoulder wound, she sees a doll that calls to mind her own younger helplessness (`FavorStory_150704_Content`). The character-quest coda recalls wishing for someone to stand up for her (`flow#/7953/1`). These support the inference that vulnerable people are not an abstraction to her. They do not by themselves prove a single totalizing trauma explanation for every act.

The rescue story also refuses a simple account of gratitude. Zani initially thinks of refusing the girl's offered doll because she did not intervene to be thanked. She stops herself, comes down to the frightened child's level, accepts the gift, and promises to care for it (`FavorStory_150704_Content`). The text explicitly connects the girl's eyes to Zani's memory of being powerless, but its behavioral point is narrower and richer than "trauma made her a hero." Rejecting the gift would protect Zani's self-description at the cost of dismissing the child's chosen response. Accepting it lets the saved person act, however modestly, rather than remain only an object of rescue. That interpretation is an inference about the scene's arrangement, not a quoted motive. Her earlier anonymous report to the Chamber and the coat she places over the child are just as concrete as the punch that opens the cage [ZAN-E06, E21].

The kidnappers' conversation is not a schematic of equal guilt. One speaks of releasing the girl after payment, the other worries that she has seen their faces; neither has actually released her, and Zani stops both before the risk can be tested. Later differentiation of coerced Black Alley members should not be projected backwards as if she offered these kidnappers a recorded restorative hearing on the spot. Conversely, the fact that she knocks them unconscious does not settle how she would address their culpability after the child is safe. The passage establishes a priority under immediate danger and gives a glimpse of the earlier, more solitary method whose shortcomings she later describes (`FavorStory_150704_Content`; `flow#/7301/4`; `8585/5`) [ZAN-E06, E17, E21].

The newspaper story in `FavorStory_150702_Content` is a particularly important source trap. It describes inconsistent eyewitness and factional accounts of the Blazing Nightwalker: a figure some condemn, some sanctify. It should be read as a *reception history* of the vigilante, not a clean ledger of what she did on every night. Zani herself eventually tells Rover she delivered Talos and Black Alley leaders to the Order, says she did not choose the grand nickname, and dislikes having to repeat it (`flow#/7298/7`). The disclosure has practical trust and privacy dimensions; it is not simply a superhero reveal. In `flow#/7301/4`, she describes a lonely early stage in which she acted too quickly and made danger worse. The Montelli family cleaned up the resulting mess and offered backing. Her new employment did not eliminate her impulse to intervene, but it put it inside a network of procedures and leverage. This self-report is valuable and limited: it is retrospective, partial, and paired with her wish not to make a grand tale out of it.

The newspaper's witnesses disagree even about what can be seen: silhouette, horns or hairpins, sacred fire or frightening violence, legal guardian or outlaw. An acolyte reads her intervention as an offense against the Sentinel's exclusive right to judge; another treats it as a divine commission. A resident questions the comforting claim that she never kills, without supplying direct proof that she did. The article then turns those discordant fragments into a civic legend of order restored after catastrophe (`FavorStory_150702_Content`). These accounts are evidence of what people need the Nightwalker to mean; they cannot all be accepted as a transcript of Zani's conduct. In particular, an unverified suspicion of killing is not a licensed event, and a sanctifying interpretation is not her own theology [ZAN-E04, E15].

The office builds a second legend around the same woman. Coworkers in `FavorStory_150703_Content` know her outputs and dark circles but not her method or motive. They imitate her energy-drink habit and get worse at their work; they speculate about money, city love, and family status; they admire a "best employee" whose exhaustion is visible to them. Zani's ordinary-employee answer deflects those stories and immediately proposes that someone transfer over to share her load. This is funny because the workers who want her secret are offered the least glamorous answer: redistribute the work. The scene does not establish an organized labor program or prove every coworker is callous. It does expose the danger of confusing an extraordinary individual result with a reproducible, sustainable institution. The vigilante myth asks her to be a city's night watch; the bank myth asks her to be its inexhaustible worker. She resists both labels more than she resists the people who need help [ZAN-E04–E05, E17].

The Black Alley case tests whether the myth can be converted into a workable civic ethic. Zani notices that coerced recruits are not identical to leaders. She demands honesty from Colleen and does not let coercion erase participation (`flow#/7300/3`), but later backs a process in which less-deep participants can be pardoned and helped into market stalls through a family support fund (`8585/5`). She coordinates survivor rescue rather than chasing only the emblematic villain (`7555/4`). This is a practical, conditional mercy: record responsibility, protect victims, then make a route back into ordinary life. It is not universal softness. Against Talos, she admits a personal score and uses fear as a deliberate substitute when she thinks respect for law has failed (`7437/6`, `7556/3`). The text does not say she killed him; it does make her threat morally serious. `FavorStory_150703_Content` also entertains rumors of rough corporate negotiation. A durable character model must keep that edge available for high-stakes situations while refusing to present it as a cute one-liner or a default answer to trivial conflict.

An underread action at `7552/2` refuses to make care merely gentle or merely commanding. Zani presses a gang member for Talos and the missing people's whereabouts; he reveals the former, withholds the latter, and she remarks that he would rather knock himself unconscious than answer. This is a forceful interrogation, but the line does not prove she struck or tortured him. Colleen then volunteers to guide the rescue. Zani orders her to stay and argues the dangers are unequal; Rover explains the risk, Colleen names Hubert and a concrete navigation benefit, and Rover's single option offers to protect her. Zani warns that carrying everything alone will exhaust someone, then accepts the plan after Rover's next time-limited reply. The warning's addressee and the rescue's outcome need runtime review. Her eventual concession does not mean she thought Colleen safe, while the initial order cannot be rewritten as a perfectly consent-led invitation. The [Colleen danger specialist](WUWA_ZANI_COLLEEN_DANGER_SHARED_BURDEN_AND_CONSENT_PROFILE.md) keeps all four speakers and the single-option structure separate [ZAN-E42–E43; C41–C42].

Her later ascension addresses bind greater strength to protecting more people and ask whether Rover needs her as sword or shield (`FavorWord_150729–150731_Content`; `favorword#/2190–2192`). That is meaningful about the *purpose* she gives power, not proof that every use of it is proportionate. It is also not a recorded quest choice or an unlimited pledge of service. The linguistic images diverge: English introduces a shield one line earlier and makes the final sword unbreakable; Chinese and Korean make that sword capable of cutting anything, while Japanese is less absolute. The live record's combination of Talos intimidation and material help to survivors prevents the tempting shortcut that “sword” means cruelty and “shield” means kindness. The [ascension specialist](WUWA_ZANI_EFFICIENCY_TRAP_SWORD_SHIELD_AND_PROTECTIVE_FORCE_PROFILE.md) keeps the menu/source boundary and its cross-language forms explicit [ZAN-E20, E21, E32, C29–C30].

The earlier planning talk at `7297/7/14–18` complicates even a charitable reading of her restraint. She says the captured people committed relatively minor offenses and that torture would be difficult to use because hostile actors could exploit the optics. Chinese, Japanese and Korean explicitly name torture; English says “more extreme methods.” She does not order or perform it in this scene, and the line does not prove she wants to. But the *reason she voices* is pragmatic vulnerability, not a universal moral prohibition. A model that cleans this into “Zani would never consider coercion” would be less faithful than one that preserves the uncomfortable limit, and a model that uses it to license abuse of every petty offender would be less faithful still [ZAN-E14, E29, C26].

The fabricated “Protocol 8” deserves separate notice. A Rover prompt asks if the relevant employee rule was made up, and she says yes (`flow#/7435/1`, `7436/1`). She invents an institutional pretext to get a humane outcome under strain. It demonstrates that she does not treat rules as inviolable moral ends; it does **not** prove she tells lies casually to friends, clients, or colleagues. The lie's purpose, target, and stakes need to travel with any model rule. A scene in which she bends a formality to reach trapped people may be plausible; a scene in which she fabricates consent or conceals a serious cost from an intimate partner would not follow automatically.

## Institutions as tools, not idols

Zani works for the Montellis and genuinely respects Carlotta's larger vision (`FavorWord_150713_Content`). She worries about superiors and family politics, and an early reading can make her seem too trusting of institutional claims. As incidents unfold, she investigates particular actors, secures permissions, warns of Echo guardians and works beside a believer whose interpretation differs from hers (`flow#/4577`, `5600–5665`). Her civic ideal is not exactly Carlotta's trans-sea horizon. She cares especially whether ordinary Ragunnesi can eat, work, walk, and sleep safely. In a later crisis she speaks in logistical terms—evacuation, earthquake welfare, damaged shipping capacity—and places people above collections (`8863/7`, `9326/3`, `9930/4`, `11780/2`). She covers a strained coworker's desk and sends them home (`9950/2`). That is an extension of her ethic of rest to someone else, not merely a joke about her own workload.

Her stance toward faith likewise resists a simple label. In `FavorWord_150714_Content`, she says the city's fate rests in citizens' will rather than the Sentinel's; she singles out Phoebe because Phoebe does not invalidate others' beliefs or efforts. In the Vault/Lorelei material she needles Phoebe about prayer, but also invokes the Sentinel herself under immediate danger (`flow#/5644/1`, `5651/1`). Ordinary speech under stress does not turn her into a systematic believer, but it prevents a model from making her mechanically anti-religious. Her potential willingness to cooperate with Cantarella is similarly personal and conditional against a family-political backdrop (`FavorWord_150715_Content`). Her praise of Brant and wish to escape paperwork into his troupe ends as a joke about her lack of acting skill; the source does not establish a resignation plan (`FavorWord_150716_Content`).

The vault's retrospective conclusion makes the shared investigation more consequential without turning it into Zani's solo victory. `QuestTree_Summary_230301` reports that she, Phoebe and Rover carry an Order-supplied “relic” into the Averardo Vault, discover the Montelli family's sheltering of artists and scientists, and learn that the object is intended to erase evidence of the Order's crimes. It credits **Phoebe's** power with resolving the immediate crisis and the trapped Reverberations' release. English calls the device a bomb, while the Chinese anchor, Japanese and Korean do not specify that mechanism. The selected direct Zani excerpts support her investigative participation and concern for the trapped, not a claim that she personally disarmed the device, knew its purpose before entry or finished institutional restitution. That distinction preserves her respect for Phoebe as a capable collaborator rather than conscripting Phoebe's decisive act into Zani's vigilante legend [ZAN-E13, E40, C38].

## The victim may question the name on Zani's paycheck

A later Black Alley exchange makes institutional loyalty harder than a general slogan. Colleen reports that after Talos's fall another woman has imposed tribute and violence on people who once depended on the Alley for scraps and shelter. She fears the new Talos's dangerous Terminal may have come from someone in the Montelli family. At `flow#/7411/7`, Zani is an employee of that family and a capable investigator, but those roles do not oblige Colleen to trust her. Zani offers to leave when Colleen looks wary. Rover then makes continued help conditional on trusting Rover's friend; that ultimatum belongs to Rover's speaker ID, not Zani's. Once the field team is dismissed and Colleen explains the Terminal concern, Zani says the harmed person has a right to be suspicious. She neither declares the family guilty on that basis nor asks Colleen to apologize before anyone investigates [ZAN-E41; ZAN-C39].

The sequence also prevents a tidy fiction that she always tells every colleague everything immediately or that she deliberately conceals a proven supplier. She says there are no leads when an operative asks, *before* Colleen privately discloses the Terminal. Exactly how the earlier gang account should have entered an operational report remains open. Her later answer to the actual worry is practical: as a Montelli employee she has a duty to stop Terminal misuse. This is not an institution-as-idol claim. It is the same affiliation that makes the accusation uncomfortable and may give her a means to test it. A durable reconstruction must let the victim set some conditions of disclosure, let colleagues pursue evidence, and keep Zani answerable for what she reports to each audience. The [survivor-distrust specialist](WUWA_ZANI_SURVIVOR_DISTRUST_EMPLOYER_DUTY_AND_LEAVE_PROFILE.md) retains the mixed-speaker order and the three Rover options whose explicit action targets are absent [ZAN-E41; ZAN-C10, C39].

The cost of another shift is not treated as a heroic nonissue. After deciding to pursue the case, Zani proposes asking for double overtime pay and extra leave. Rover's next question about an earlier vacation is deliberately awkward, and the Chinese, Japanese and Korean leave open whether it was honored or taken; English more strongly sounds as though it had already been given. Her deflection is not a confirmation that she used it. Only the separate later `12439/7` record establishes actual paid leave. This scene therefore joins rather than cancels her two needs: protect Colleen from an employer-linked tool and make the employer compensate labor that safety demanded. A model built from EN's combat “no overtime” bark or a perpetual-workaholic meme would miss both [ZAN-E08, E24, E38, E41; ZAN-C02, C16, C40].

## Two ledgers of public safety

The banker and the protector both keep accounts, but of different things. The source places a payment dispute and insinuated rough negotiation in the "best employee" story; it also shows Zani handling insurance instructions and later putting endangered people ahead of Vault collections (`FavorStory_150703_Content`; `flow#/4204/2`; `9930/4`). It would be too simple to say she abandons property whenever someone invokes safety: custody, evidence, compensation, and an institution's resources can matter to real people. It would be equally wrong to say her professional loyalty makes priceless objects worth more than lives. In crisis, she states an order of priority. In aftermath, the support fund and market stalls for lesser Black Alley participants give material form to the idea that a safer city needs livelihoods, not just arrests (`8585/5`) [ZAN-E05, E21, E23].

This is why paperwork can be both her burden and part of her answer. Reports that merely display busyness consume time; an anonymous notice to the Chamber brings lawful follow-up to a rescued child; a fabricated employee protocol temporarily borrows bureaucratic credibility for a protective end; formal pardon and funding change what happens to people after the fight (`FavorStory_150704_Content`; `flow#/7435/1–7436/1`; `8585/5`). The acts are not morally identical. The anonymous notice is a real communication, Protocol 8 is an admitted fiction, and a pardon requires actual decision-makers. What connects them is that protection must cross from private capacity into systems other people can continue to use. Zani can bend a rule, exploit a title, or supply an account, but the source does not establish that she may forge anyone's consent or dispense justice without limits [ZAN-E06, E19, E21].

The same institutional pragmatism has a dark edge. Her spoken reason against torturing minor offenders is exposure to opponents, not a principled universal ban; her threat to Talos deliberately deploys fear after she judges respect for law ineffective (`flow#/7297/7/14–18`; `7556/3/11–26`). Those are material counterweights to any reading of her as a spotless proceduralist. They also do not establish that she actually tortured a prisoner or killed Talos. A strong reconstruction has to leave room for disproportionate-violence risk precisely because it is not content to equate good intentions with just methods. The live case shows Zani pursuing survivor rescue and differentiated aftercare *alongside* the coercive threat. The tension is in the evidence, not an analyst's need to make her "gritty" [ZAN-E14, E16, E20–E21, E29].

## Rest as the more difficult victory

At the heart of the character quest, Zani names a cluster of humble habits—snoozing, obtaining a fresh wrap, sleeping without long wakefulness—and calls them fragile (`flow#/7301/4`). The model should not confuse this with contempt for routines. The violent world can shatter them; a bold intervention can recover them; but a stable environment is better than perpetual one-person repair. She tells Rover that the changes surrounding the Carnevale have made Ragunna busier and more promising, and imagines the Nightwalker fading into irrelevance. That hope is not an abdication of care. It is care succeeding so thoroughly that the clandestine role becomes unnecessary.

The archive draws an important distinction inside “routine.” In `FavorWord_150705_Content`, her own repetitive, meaningless chores drain the last color from life until Rover arrives; in `FavorWord_150710_Content`, Ragunna's recurring public scenes are what she wants to preserve. The first is a labor loop without felt purpose or choice; the second is a city whose people can keep a place, food and continuity. This is why a scene should not cure her with nonstop novelty, any more than it should trap her in office repetition and call that civic peace [ZAN-E30, C27].

Her warning to a tired Rover in `FavorWord_150704_Content` adds a second distinction: ordinary rest must be made possible before unending duty consumes it, but that is not identical to walking away from someone in immediate danger. Chinese, Japanese and Korean liken world-saving to overtime that can always expand; English more sharply says saving the world can wait. The line is undated archive advice, not an observed refusal of a live rescue. She later reports an evacuation in progress and offers to stay longer to ease Carlotta's load (`8863/7`, `9968/2`); neither line is a witnessed completed extra shift. The charitable reading is not that she values everyone else's safety except Rover's, nor that she would veto urgent protection whenever someone needs sleep. It is that she recognizes a repeated-load trap in both labor and heroism, including her own. A future scene forced to choose between known immediate harm and rest could sharpen that priority; this archive line cannot settle it in advance [ZAN-E08, E23–E24, E35, C33].

The aftermath refuses an instant cure. A later city-night encounter has her appreciating the view, sea breeze and warm bread with Rover while still explaining insomnia as an entrenched habit (`flow#/8586/10`; duplicated at `8819/10`). An ordinary-life model must be able to put pleasure and weariness in the same scene. Another line has her offer to stay up longer to lighten Carlotta's burden (`9968/2`, repeated at `11786/2`), so “she would never willingly work late” misstates her expressed willingness. The text does not prove she completed those extra hours. Finally, at `12439/7` a promised paid holiday has actually arrived. She says that freedom from tasks did not automatically produce enjoyable rest, and she looks for a quiet corner. The journey from wanting leave to receiving leave to learning how to use it is more precise than a perpetual “never clocks out” gag. It is also more generative for scenario writing: a friend might help her protect a quiet evening; an impulsive admirer might accidentally turn it into another logistical duty.

That holiday does not mean Zani has always preferred solitude to celebration. Her birthday address in `FavorWord_150718_Content` promises Rover a large traditional party she has already arranged; Chinese, Japanese and Korean carry it until dawn, while English stops at midnight. When she herself finally attends a party during actual paid leave, she expected pleasure and initially wants to continue, but fatigue after a few hours makes a sofa more attractive (`12439/7/5–17`). She is not cured by merriment, and her need to sit down is not a standing hatred of parties. A faithful social model keeps the desired occasion, the actual body's response and the recipient's own preference separate [ZAN-E27–E28, C24–C25].

Rover matters, but not as a license to overwrite the rest of her world. She offers practical hospitality, notices fatigue, buys one of several sweets because she does not know Rover's preference, shares a private history and old weapon, invites city-night stillness, and calls Rover's presence a change in the texture of her routine (`FavorStory_150705_Content`; `FavorWord_150705_Content`; `flow#/7297/7`, `7301/4`, `8587/2`). These are meaningful signs of trust and attachment. Some dialogue is optional/branched; none proves an exclusive mutual romance. For a hypothetical crossover courtship scenario, the question is not “will she blush if praised?” but whether the other person respects her time, tells the truth about stakes, handles vulnerable bystanders, and can sit with her without converting the encounter into another heroic assignment. The [relationship profile](WUWA_ZANI_RELATIONSHIP_STATE_AND_ORDINARY_LIFE_PROFILE.md) makes those state and consent conditions explicit.

The optional contact exchange late in `flow#/7301/4/39–42` makes that last word concrete. After Zani apologizes for a habit, Rover may simply say it is okay and skip reciprocation. ## The victory she cannot carry alone

Zani's desired peaceful city makes the familiar "reluctant hero" label only partly useful. She keeps responding to danger, but the ideal she voices at `7301/4` is not a better Nightwalker brand or a larger personal patrol. It is a Ragunna in which the role can fall out of use. An individual who hears the tile nobody else hears cannot by herself guarantee that every roof, employer, police office and market will become safe. The move from lone action to Montelli backing, delegated rescue, and funded resettlement is a change in *where responsibility sits*, not merely an upgrade in fighting power (`FavorStory_150701_Content`; `flow#/7301/4`, `7555/4`, `8585/5`). Her anti-mythic hope is politically demanding: the city must share the work for its quiet to last [ZAN-E03, E17–E18, E21].

The later holiday tests this hope on her own body. A real leave period removes the immediate work order; it does not erase learned alertness, fatigue or the habit of converting free time into a plan (`12439/7`). She can want a party for Rover, attempt one herself, and still need to leave after a few hours. Reading those scenes together does not make her averse to celebration. It suggests that public peace and personal recovery have different clocks. An invented companion who "fixes" her in an evening would flatten that difference; a companion who makes space for choice, quiet and changing plans would have a more credible route to closeness [ZAN-E24, E27–E28].

Several rival readings must remain available. She may be understood as a fundamentally loyal Montelli employee whose private interventions serve the same city's order; the payment of her salary and later extra help for Carlotta support real loyalty. She may also be read as an uneasy critic of extraction and of the institutional monopoly on force; her lost meals, efficiency joke and fabricated protocol support that. Neither text alone proves a formal reform politics or a final break with the family. Likewise, the child rescue and restorative fund license a protector reading, while Talos and the interrogation language prevent an uncomplicated pacifist one. A future client-authored scene showing how she acts when these duties directly collide would sharpen the reading more than another generalized title or menu address [ZAN-E05–E08, E19–E21, E24, E29, E31–E32].
