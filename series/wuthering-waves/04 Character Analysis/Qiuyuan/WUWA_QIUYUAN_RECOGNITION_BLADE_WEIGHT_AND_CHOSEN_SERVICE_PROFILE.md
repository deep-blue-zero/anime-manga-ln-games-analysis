# Qiuyuan — recognition, a heavier blade, and the limits of being useful

The archive's five ascension utterances are an important counterweight to a purely grim interpretation of Qiuyuan's sword metaphor. They do not simply praise a stronger weapon. They join being recognized, dangerous sharpening, a desire to test strength, and a late comparison between a once-light and now-heavy blade. The tension is not solved by choosing one line as the “real” Qiuyuan. This profile reads them beside the longer self-account, the short self-introduction, the harbor's sheath image and ordinary hospitality. The [matrix](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md) supplies QIU-E29–E31 and QIU-C26–C29; the [source audit](WUWA_QIUYUAN_SOURCE_CENSUS_CHRONOLOGY_AND_IDENTITY_AUDIT.md) fixes the selected corpus and source hierarchy.

## What the five entries are—and are not

`FavorWord_141127–131_Content` occupies `favorword#/2649–2653` at the pinned commit. Their labels are Ascension I–V and their event paths are distinct archive/menu voice triggers. The labels order game-progression speech; the retained source does not supply a dated quest-state graph saying I occurred after his apprenticeship, II after Liang's death, or V after the harbor departure. The sequence is interpretable as a designed thematic progression, but not as five witnessed, consecutive conversations. In particular, Ascension IV proposes seeking strong opponents to test a newly honed blade; it does not itself record that a new duel occurred. [QIU-E29](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#evidence-bundles) [QIU-C28](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#claims-counterreadings-and-revision-tests)

Ascension I recognizes clarity and skill in the addressee. Ascension II names the transformative force of being recognized. Ascension III insists on maintaining sharpness despite hard material and life-cost; IV wants a test against formidable opposition. V looks back at a younger fixation on edge alone and a present sword that feels heavier as people and affairs accumulate. Reading only III–IV would yield a fighter who values danger for its own sake; reading only V would yield a man already past fighting. Neither reading is sufficient. The archive can support both appetite for meaningful combat and awareness that fighting now bears relationships and consequences. It does not fix how often either disposition governs a later live choice. [QIU-E29](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#evidence-bundles) [QIU-E30](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#evidence-bundles)

## Recognition is not ownership

The Chinese Ascension II formula equates the grace of being *recognized/understood for one's value* with a kind of remaking. Japanese directly tells the addressee that their recognition feels like a second life, and Korean explicitly says “you” recognizing him has that force. English abstracts this into a chance and a rewritten future. These versions agree that recognition matters deeply, but they do not all specify the beneficiary–recipient relationship with the same directness. Nor does the line explain which exact deed earned the gratitude. Its voice-menu address makes Rover a plausible addressee, not a witnessed exchange establishing mutual vows, romantic exclusivity, or legal allegiance. [QIU-E29](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#evidence-bundles) [QIU-E31](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#evidence-bundles) [QIU-C26](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#claims-counterreadings-and-revision-tests)

`FavorWord_141105_Content` is the better context for *why* he offers a blade. Its Chinese self-account traces successive objects of service—himself, a benefactor, Mingting, then an unsettlement—and concludes that he senses a path or principle in Rover worth unsheathing for. JA and KO keep the worthiness of that principle; English renders it as a calling/faith. The short `FavorWord_141122_Content` says he will not ask many questions about how the blade is used, with English compressing the line into a particularly unqualified invitation. The source permits an apparent tension between humility and discernment; it does not require us to delete either half. His live investigation of a false capital charge, rejection of a disguised intruder and warning to Rover before the lantern handoff demonstrate judgment under stakes. A hypothetical cruel instruction from Rover is therefore not answered by mechanically applying the shortest menu line. Conversely, the longer line is not proof that he would always refuse an order an analyst dislikes. [QIU-E18](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#evidence-bundles) [QIU-E31](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#evidence-bundles) [QIU-C13](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#claims-counterreadings-and-revision-tests) [QIU-C29](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#claims-counterreadings-and-revision-tests)

There is a relational asymmetry worth preserving. Qiuyuan can feel remade by recognition without the other person having undertaken the permanent role of keeping him whole. His birthday line `FavorWord_141118_Content` even asks for temporary shelter in a journey rather than claiming a right to stay. At `9932/3/33–35`, Rover's people become an example of reciprocal home/sheath, while his own analogous future remains unfinished. A generated scene may explore trust and intimacy, but must say whether it is a counterfactual or a later hypothesized development. Archive gratitude and a harbor metaphor alone cannot certify an exclusive relationship. ## Sharpness and weight are not the same variable

Ascension III uses whetstone, armor, weapons and life to insist that a sword remain sharp even through breaking. The Chinese and Korean list these in a hard parallel; English makes the “fire of life” more figurative, while Japanese says a broken blade is honed anew. The line can reveal determination and danger tolerance. It does not identify a particular person killed, a specific training event, or a request for self-destruction. IV's proposal to seek opponents intensifies the martial register but remains a menu invitation, not a completed challenge. [QIU-E29](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#evidence-bundles)

Ascension V reinterprets rather than cancels that edge. The Chinese self-comparison says his youthful sword felt light when sharpening alone was central; now the people and matters attached to him make the sword heavier, though this is *not about technique*. Korean specifies people and affairs he must protect. Japanese emphasizes mounting things to carry, with less explicit relational wording. English specifies lives touched and choices carried, then says his skill never falters. The shared core is experienced weight from obligation. The English technical certainty is stronger than Chinese's narrower causal denial; a model should not say the four texts prove that injury, medication, fatigue and training can never affect his performance. Nor does “heavy” mean he has lost the ability or will to fight. [QIU-E30](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#evidence-bundles) [QIU-C27](WUWA_QIUYUAN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#claims-counterreadings-and-revision-tests)

This weight has a plausible relation to the harbor's sheath without being identical to it. A sheath is an imagined reciprocal place for a blade, not merely a heavier list of duties. The hut already shows practical care for patients, the agency years show public service, and Rover's network gives him a picture of people who stand with one another. None proves that every tie is a burden or that he has found the home he imagines. The reader can hold two readings open: increased obligation may make the sword less casually drawn, or it may make its necessary use more urgent. The selected text does not quantify his choice rate. His Scar threat and later survivor-priority line show that circumstance, not an abstract compassion score, determines which side becomes visible. ## An operational reading for hypothetical scenes

A scene should specify whether it uses a live quest state, a voiced archive/menu address, or a future scenario. It should then identify the recipient, what Qiuyuan knows of their cause, whether immediate people need protection, and whether his vow or a chosen service relationship is engaged. If someone praises his skill, Ascension I–II allow a marked response to being seen; neither dictates instant devotion. If a comrade urges a reckless test, III–IV permit appetite for challenge, but the later weight line and the live rescue priorities prevent automatic disregard of bystanders. If a companion notices fatigue or apprehension, V permits him to speak of a sword made heavier by ties, not a canonical admission that he has become technically weak. These are bounded generative affordances, not dialogue templates.
