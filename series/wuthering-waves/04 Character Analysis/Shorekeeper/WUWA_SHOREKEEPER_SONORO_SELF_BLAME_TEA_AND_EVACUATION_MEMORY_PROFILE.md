## “If I had understood”: testimony, causation and self-blame

At `3456/2/1` Shorekeeper apologizes for losing control of her surrounding Sonoro. A player option asks whether she personally experienced the scenes; she answers that she has **not** lived through those Laments, but others told her of them (items 2, 5; `Heihaian_main_1_3_51_9`). Their Lament data passes through her and becomes the underground stars through which Tethys can issue warnings (item 6; `_51_10`). This is neither pure detached computation nor firsthand survivor testimony. It describes a mediated bodily/data role and an obligation to remember. ZH/JA/KO preserve more of the “through me / stars” construction than EN's compression into her having encoded observable stars. The visual nature of the actual Sonoro and whether a runtime viewer saw every depicted image remain separate AV questions [SHK-E33, C32].

After an option asks why she apologizes, she says she did not protect the place well enough and wonders whether greater effort or understanding of people's emotions might have prevented the disaster (items 7–9; `_51_22`, `_51_28`). The conditional is a *self-attribution under grief*, not an independent systems audit establishing that emotional incomprehension caused a catastrophe. No four-language text here quantifies Tethys's warning latency, demonstrates a feasible intervention she knowingly withheld, or makes every loss her fault. That distinction matters because the later deep-shore plan also risks giving herself the entire burden: the two episodes form a plausible recurrent self-cost question, but they are different moments, with different information and not one continuous confession. A faithful model should allow her to feel responsible and be challenged about that responsibility without instantly replacing it with either total exoneration or factual culpability [SHK-E25, E33; C23, C32].

At items 10–12 she names Sonoro and body as things she is willing to spend to protect the starry memorial and Rover, and recalls a continuing promise (`_51_30–32`). The *time grammar* diverges: EN and KO render the body-cost language as prospective or possible, whereas JA says she has given all she could and has protected them through that effort; ZH is elliptical about `倾尽一切`. A cross-dub claim that she has already physically destroyed her Sonoro in this specific scene—or that all four versions make an identical future suicide vow—would be stronger than the text. Her later recorded bodily damage and deep-shore risk are separate evidence, not back-projected proof of what item 10 itself enacted [SHK-E05, E25, E33; C08, C32].

The closing turns distinguish resolve from fluency in emotion labels. At `_51_35–36` ZH/JA/KO first say she will *find a way* and then name Rover's binding Möbius fate; EN compresses the two-step resolve by already saying it will break fate in `_51_35`. At `_51_38` ZH's `不舍` and JA's `未練` suggest difficulty letting go or lingering attachment, KO's `아쉬움` can express regret/reluctance, while EN chooses “longing.” All four make her ask how to describe what she feels; this does not make EN “longing” an exclusive four-dub romance finding. At `_51_39` ZH/JA/KO ask what *face/expression* one shows, whereas EN asks what *emotion* to express. The scene therefore supports a person with felt conflict and uncertain expressive vocabulary, not a person devoid of emotion who waits for the correct facial program. Her later explicit love self-report `FavorWord_150531_Content` is stronger evidence for that distinct claim; it need not be smuggled back into every earlier ambiguous word [SHK-E10, E26, E33; C13, C16, C32].

The option attached to item 9 offers three Rover reassurances—she did well, things might have been worse without her, or she saved lives (`_51_29`, `_51_40–41`). All have empty option actions in the retained item and the `TalkSequence` continues through item 10. The source can show three available answers and a subsequent common Shorekeeper speech; it cannot show which reassurance any player selected, that all three were said together, or how she heard a particular one. These alternatives provide a useful counterweight to her self-accusation without proving that the player's reassurance objectively settled the causal question.

## Tea does not have to be a service-level agreement

The `3825/1` action is small-scale, but not disposable texture. Shorekeeper tastes a hot tea and finds it bitter (`Heihaian_main_1_3_5605_2–4`, `_5605_10`). She asks whether Rover likes tea; Rover can say it is not their thing or that they enjoy it (`_5605_5–6`). Her further speech notes tea seeds in the Bank of Life, suggests planting some in the Greenhouse, and offers to keep tea leaves in her Sonoro for Rover's travels (`_5605_8–11`). These are observations and *possible* acts. The source does not show that she planted tea, filled the Sonoro, acquired a durable taste for tea, or secured Rover's acceptance. The two later Rover options tell her the offer need not be worth her effort or that she has enough on her shoulders (`_5605_12`, `_5605_14`); they are options, not a universal rebuke or two cumulative sentences. Shorekeeper answers that Rover's enjoyment matters to her (`_5605_16`). This can be attentive hospitality without making her a perfectly accommodating machine: she has her own immediate sensory reaction, makes a practical proposal, and remains in a conversation in which the other person can decline [SHK-E34, C34].

The tea scene is a proportional test of the high-stakes theme. In the Sonoro exchange she considers spending body and vision to prevent disaster; here she considers spending time and storage to offer a drink. Those costs are not equivalent. The common question is whether Shorekeeper can recognize another's desire *and* regard her own effort as something to discuss rather than automatically erase. A model that turns a disliked bitter sip into “she hates all tea” ignores the uncertainty; one that takes a politely declined offer as proof of heartbreak or self-sacrifice imports crisis stakes into an ordinary conversation. Her later culinary experiments with Chisa's drink and Rover's tasting feedback have separate sources and a later context [SHK-E18, E31, E34; C19, C34].

## A warning was issued; people still died

At `4173/1/1–6` Shorekeeper tells Rover that they are nearing a depiction of the Guixu Port City Lament's beginning. She reports that Black Shores *did* issue a warning, Rover dispatched available personnel to help Huanglong evacuate, and the spread exceeded Tethys's prediction; some lives could not be saved (`Heihaian_main_1_3_5609_3–6`). This constrains two easy but incompatible simplifications. The source does not support “the organization simply did nothing” or “the prediction gave ample time and safety was guaranteed.” It also does not prove the warning was timely enough, that every possible evacuation measure was taken, or that the casualty count was unavoidable. Those questions require a fuller disaster chronology, not guilt or assurance abstracted from a single retrospective speaker [SHK-E35, C33].

Rover names a Bloom Bearer in an option; localization of the name differs: ZH 霍德, EN Hoda, JA フォード and KO 오르디 (`_5609_7`). Shorekeeper confirms she was among the casualties and was engaged in evacuation/rescue (items 7–9). Her grief and institutional respect do not make a frequency trace the same person alive. The following Rover option asks whether the woman would be reconstructed or replicated (`_5609_15`). Shorekeeper answers that she is unsure whether *seeing her* is right (`_5609_17`). This does not establish that a replica was already generated, that it is identical to the original person, that the encounter occurred, or that Shorekeeper had reached a settled policy on simulations of the dead. The exact moral question is open: whether such a meeting would comfort, counterfeit, or burden survivors and the remembered person. Delone's memorial star is a distinct earlier loss and a useful negative control, not proof of a uniform Black Shores resurrection technology [SHK-E04, E35; C15, C33].

Across these actions, Shorekeeper's language moves among technical explanation, causal regret, difficult expression, mundane offering and historical care for rescuers. It is not evidence of a simple “machine becomes human” switch at a single line. Nor do 156 integrity-valid four-dub objects let us report perceived tenderness, flatness, strain, or hesitation as heard facts. The [AV crosswalk](WUWA_SHOREKEEPER_AV_HUMAN_RETRIEVAL_CROSSWALK.md) nominates exact keys and two runtime/branch questions; the test is whether direct listening and scene viewing *change* any of the bounded claims, not whether they can decorate a thesis already considered certain [SHK-E33–E35; C32–C34].
