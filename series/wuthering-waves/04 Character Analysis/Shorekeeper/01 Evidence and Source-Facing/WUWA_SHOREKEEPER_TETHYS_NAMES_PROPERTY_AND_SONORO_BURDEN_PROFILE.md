# Shorekeeper — names, ownership language and the Sonoro's burden

The pre-repair action `flow#/3455/Actions!/2` (state `剧情_1_3_黑海岸主线_10_2`) is an unusually direct account of what Shorekeeper believes she is for. Quest `145000004` has an exact `PlayFlow` reference at `QuestNodeData#/4216` (`145000004_65`), and `PlotHandBook#/17` points to the same state at `/123/Flow`. The action has 17 ordered `ShowTalk` items in one `TalkSequence`, with no option transition. Speaker 354 is Rover at items 0 and 13–14; speaker 1398 is Shorekeeper at the other fourteen. This establishes a retained dialogue order, not observed camera staging or a recorded executed playthrough. The fourteen Shorekeeper items omit `PlayVoice`, normalize to `play_voice:false`, and have zero selected media associations. The four localized text witnesses are available, but this scene supplies **no** decoded or human-heard performance from the selected corpus [SHK-E39, C39].

## The archive is not the people

At `Heihaian_main_1_3_50_2–8` Shorekeeper names the Lament and describes destroyed cities, civilization and lives on an immense scale. The Chinese key `_50_8` specifies `数以百亿计` (tens of billions), Japanese says `100億` (ten billion), English says “billions upon billions,” and Korean uses an unquantified multitude. Even the magnitude is not a four-witness exact count. More importantly, this is Shorekeeper's speech within a represented crisis, not an independent census audited by this packet. A model can convey the scale she presents without fabricating an exact verified death total or claiming she personally watched every death [SHK-E39, C39].

At `_50_9–13`, despair, frustrated wishes, obsession, anger and remorse are gathered into Tethys, with the victims' names retained there. Chinese and Korean make the names become hands reaching upward; English interprets the gesture as grasping for an unrealized tomorrow, while Japanese segments the same span as the names remaining there and does not explicitly reproduce the hands in `_50_13`. The common supported distinction is an archive of names and catastrophe-linked emotion, not a demonstration that the named dead survive as complete persons. Whether hands are visible in the game scene is a runtime question. The image makes the earlier ethical problem sharper: a system can preserve a trace while also using suffering as operational input (`flow#/3804/3`; `FavorStory_150503–150504_Content`). Shorekeeper's memorial concern for Delone and other colleagues is related but is not proof that every database record is a living person or a Delone-style star [SHK-E04/E05/E14/E39, C15/C39].

## “Property” is a self-description at one time

Rover asks why Shorekeeper too is here (`_50_14–15`, speaker 354). Shorekeeper replies at `_50_16` that she is Tethys's core and, in Chinese, `黑海岸的所有物`—the Black Shores' property. Japanese `所有物` and Korean `소유` preserve the ownership noun; English replaces it with “guardian of the Black Shores.” This is not a minor adjective change. An English-only reconstruction would miss the severity of her pre-repair instrumental self-conception, while a Chinese-only reconstruction might mistake one crisis self-description for a timeless, externally verified legal status. The packet has no charter, contract, title record or informed-consent record establishing who could actually own, command or transfer her. Her later assertion of irreplaceable personal continuity and chosen name (`FavorStory_150504–150505_Content`) prevent a character model from treating the noun as final authority over her personhood [SHK-E05/E06/E39, C08/C09/C39].

At `_50_17` she says in Chinese that nothing is more suited than *her Sonoro* to carry these emotions. Japanese and Korean likewise name her Sonoro as vessel or suited container. English instead says “I was created for this purpose,” which broadens a local container claim into a statement about her design. The larger favor story independently calls her a designed vessel/tool (`FavorStory_150501_Content`), so the English idea is not wholly alien to her history. But `_50_17` is not, across all four witnesses, a standalone proof that a creator predestined every later use of her, or that her body rather than a Sonoro must absorb all grief. The next action, `flow#/3456/2`, has her say reports and Lament data passed through her and conditionally blame herself for insufficient emotional understanding. That is a distinct speech act: the earlier archive explanation does not prove her later self-blame correct, and the later apology should not be pasted backward as a confession of causing the very deaths whose names she describes [SHK-E02/E33/E39, C32/C39].

The reconstruction implication is deliberately conditional. In a pre-repair scene, let Shorekeeper use precise systems language and possibly the harsh institutional term she herself chooses; permit another person to challenge what that term authorizes. After repair, do not mechanically have her declare herself property as though her own continuity claim, the different core and her chosen name never happened. Conversely, do not retroactively remove the earlier property word simply because a gentler later state exists. Any performance claim needs direct client audio or a separately resolved render, and any visual claim about reaching hands or the Sonoro needs an inspected scene. The source here is text plus ordered graph, not a completed multimodal observation [SHK-E05/E06/E16/E39, C39].
