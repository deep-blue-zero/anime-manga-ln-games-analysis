## Method and acceptance gates

1. Retrieve by semantic voice occurrence ID and exact source locator, then runtime render variant ID, language, event/media identity, canonical PCM and FLAC hash. A friendly name is not sufficient.
2. Confirm four text witnesses and their localization differences before comparing four performances. The Chinese normalized record is the semantic anchor; client media proves actual bytes of each dub, not plot truth.
3. Inspect QC flags and channel layout. On flagged multichannel objects, listen to channels deliberately or abstain from speaker-specific labels if separation is not defensible. Record waveform-analysis method separately from human impression.
4. For a full performed-voice claim, process **all 845 selected semantic lines** and associated available language renders with explicit unreviewed, ambiguous, mixed and source-unvoiced statuses. The 23-case JSON and separate trigger/story nominations are retrieval samples only.
5. Review runtime frames around the first audience's two tea routes, Sea of Ghosts, former-head confrontation, Tyrvine handoff, three-route Bloodpact gift, staged return, tower light sparks and meteor wish (`7088/8`–`7089/6`), outside-city tea and mixed-energy refusal. Check whether the sparks' visible response supports more than the narration's initially uncertain emotion, without assigning a voice to source-unvoiced Cantarella turns. Up to 1080p with sound is permissible if requested and locally available, but media stays restricted. Capture source version, state/branch, timecodes, visual asset IDs or scene paths, and direct observations separately from interpretive design readings.
6. Ask a human reviewer to sign off each stronger performance or gesture claim, keeping contrary dub readings rather than averaging them into one “true voice.” A claim may remain open indefinitely without blocking the text-grounded model.
