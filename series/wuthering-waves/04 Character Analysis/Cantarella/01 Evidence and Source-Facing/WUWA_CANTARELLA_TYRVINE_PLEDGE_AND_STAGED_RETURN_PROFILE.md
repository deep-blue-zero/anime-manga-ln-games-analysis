# Cantarella — choice made visible, authority still mediated

Three substantial voiced actions complicate a simple story of the dangerous Fisalia head becoming a benevolent rescuer. In one mainline state she prepares Tyrvine for Cartethyia while insisting that the latter's choice, not a predestined title, gives the blade its meaning. In another she thanks Rover with a unique family-head gift, jokes along one optional route, admits scarcity and conflict inside Fisalia along another, and controls when a message of freedom reaches the rest of the house. Her character-quest finale reveals that returning the girls home will be presented through a staged “elixir of revival,” with Rover asked to be its credible public agent. These are **different scenes and source surfaces**, not one continuous conversation or a proof that she always chooses perfectly [CAN-E42–E44].

## Tyrvine and the difference between an instrument and a chosen name

The mainline `flow#/6811/2`, state `剧情_2_2_阿维纽林主线_11_24`, joins quest `145000005` through QuestNodeData `#/10698` and PlotHandBook `#/36`. Its 34-item `ShowTalk` sequence is ordered in a single retained `TalkSequence`. Cantarella is technical speaker `150009`; Cartethyia is `50074`. Cantarella explains Tyrvine as a Sentinel sword that might help withstand a Threnodian attack for a time. She says past Maiden candidates resonated through it, survivors lost memories and received fragments of truth, and displaced traces collected in the Sonoro can be gathered by the blade (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_128_7–13`, items 6–12). This is her **in-world account of a mechanism and history**; the dialogue and inventory alone do not independently verify every candidate's experience, the sword's final field effect or the exact physics of the Sonoro [CAN-E42/C39].

She then refuses to make the sword a decree of fate. In the Chinese anchor and other witnesses, its designation as a Maiden's sword depends on Cartethyia choosing to take it (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_128_14`, item 13). The utterance assigns a consequential choice to the person in front of her while still presenting a real material danger. It is not a claim that Cartethyia can wish every constraint away, nor that Cantarella's earlier assessment entitles her to dictate the choice. Cantarella reports that her own severed contribution has already been used up (`_128_16`, item 15), then recounts using separated Second-Awakening power to let the Sentinel temporarily regain control and Fleurdelys seize an opening (`_128_17–23`, items 16–22). That report should not be turned into a second currently available stock of her power or into independent proof of each unseen battle step [CAN-E42/C39].

The next title claim is deliberately destabilizing. Cantarella states that the “real” Blessed Maiden is dead, then asks why a supposedly “false” one cannot become true; she immediately rejects the idea that an unchanging blade defines a person (`_128_25–28`, items 24–27). These are Cantarella's character-and-role arguments addressed to Cartethyia, not an all-time fact about Cartethyia's later survival or an endorsement of replacing one person with another. Japanese `_128_26` frames the false-to-true passage more as a necessity than the Chinese/English/Korean rhetorical permission; preserve the witness when using the line. Her own earlier power choice and her refusal to appropriate the trial girls' mixed frequencies later make this autonomy rhetoric consequential, but they do not prove her every strategic action was selfless. An analytical model should distinguish *placing a tool within reach*, *describing its danger*, and *another person electing to use it* [CAN-E10/E23/E42, C12/C39].

## A gift, three optional conversations, and a delayed message

At mainline `flow#/5775/5`, state `剧情_2_2_阿维纽林主线_12_2`, quest `145000005` is joined by QuestNodeData `#/11062` and PlotHandBook `#/36`. This `ShowTalk` action has 32 items and no retained `TalkSequence` field; its raw `Options` and `JumpTalk` edges therefore matter. Cantarella asks after Cartethyia's adjustment to mortal life before giving Rover the unique Bloodpact's Pledge available to a Fisalia head (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_143_5–13`, items 2–9). This is a material and institutional gratitude act, not by itself a wedding promise, a transfer of headship or a settled exclusive romance [CAN-E43/C40].

At TalkItem ID 13 (zero-based position 12, key `_143_15`), Rover has three alternatives: ask for Fisalia headship (`_143_16`, jump to ID 14), ask for peace with the Montellis (`_143_17`, jump to ID 17), or defer a further request (`_143_18`, jump to ID 23). The headship route contains Cantarella's bloodline/consort joke and a later joke-withdrawal response before jumping to common ID 25. The mediation route has her describe limited shared “sunlight” between houses **and within** a house, then promise she has time to attend to it; the undecided route lets her invite later contact. These are selectable alternatives, not three requests Rover made in one witnessed meeting. Her sun/branch image does not prove an achieved peace settlement or egalitarian reform, but it makes internal allocation a named political problem rather than an offstage abstraction [CAN-E43/C40].

The consort branch is particularly easy to misuse. `Main_Linaxita_2_4_143_21` (item 14) has `{Male=...;Female=...}` text and **two distinct PCM-valid render variants in each of the four languages**. The female-address version shifts materially: Chinese turns to mutual willingness over formal detail, Japanese explicitly invokes love, while English uses a different self-description. Those are localized and player-gender-conditioned render variants **inside an optional headship joke**. Neither the more intimate Japanese phrasing nor the existence of two audio files makes consortship, a proposal, Rover's gender, or a reciprocal relationship canonical across all routes. The model should be able to play the joke *if that route is declared*, while abstaining from treating it as baseline history [CAN-E43/C40].

After the branches converge, Rover conveys Imperator's message that the family is free (`_143_35–39`, items 24–27) and asks whether Cantarella will tell the others. She says she has prepared for this day for a long time and can take a little longer (`_143_40`, item 29), then offers more tea. The Chinese anchor specifies two more days, Japanese says roughly two or three, English says a few more, and Korean leaves the extra interval unnumbered. The scene supports **deferred communication under her control**; it does not say why she waits, prove that everyone heard the message, or show a policy for trial-file disclosure. This is a different information decision from the earlier headship line about pursuit of the past and the later Harbinger-panic decision. Collapsing all three into either “she always lies” or “every delay is harmless protection” would erase the particular audience and stakes [CAN-E19/E32/E41/E43, C26/C40].

## A return that uses a public fiction without erasing Cheri's testimony

The character-quest `flow#/7315/7`, state `剧情_2_2_角色_坎特蕾拉线_副本_14_1`, has a PlotHandBook `#/37` association with quest `880000033`. In its 34-item `ShowTalk` action, Cheri (`50135`) first says Cantarella kept the girls hidden deep in the estate after taking headship, while Order members were still prying into the old events. Cheri misses home; the annual masked Night of Remembrance let them visit their village and look at family, but **visits under cover are not the same as living freely there** (`Character_Cantarella_123_1–5`, items 0–4). This is an identifiable girl's testimony, not Cantarella's self-exonerating narration and not a plebiscite of all trial survivors [CAN-E44/C41].

Cantarella announces the girls may now be with their families, then explains her planned public device. As host she chose a ghost-girls theme and intended to present herself as having solved the riddles and obtained an “elixir of revival” to send the girls home at the festival (`_123_6–17`, items 5–14). Across the four textual witnesses, the account of *claiming* to have acquired the elixir makes the revival story a staged explanation for the girls' reappearance, **not evidence that they had literally died and were resurrected by a demonstrated potion**. The action's final directive to pack and go is prospective within the scene; a separate character-quest synopsis and later participant report say the girls were returned, but this action alone does not show each arrival or independent welfare follow-up [CAN-E17/E20/E37/E44, C09/C41].

Her first plan was to take the burden alone. She says the letter drawing Rover in was not from her; Chinese, English and Korean remain somewhat tentative about who sent it, while Japanese names an unnamed “that child” more directly (`_123_21–23`, items 16–18). She says Rover was already affected by the Sea's frequencies and admits she had been too self-reliant, thanking Rover for help (`_123_24–28`, items 19–23). This is a real admission that the initial solo design proved inadequate, not a full confession of every hidden act or proof that the letter sender's identity is settled. At item 24 Cheri urges her not to bear everything alone; the independent objection matters before Cantarella asks Rover to take a public role [CAN-E31/E44, C41].

Cantarella's reasons for asking Rover to present the “elixir” are layered, not pure spectacle. She anticipates villagers distrusting the Fisalia name, expects Rover's Laureate credibility to help them accept the return, and hopes Rover's standing will deter later Order investigation (`_123_30–34`, items 25–29). Korean `_123_34` stresses completing a *story* and pouring it before people; Chinese and English emphasize Rover's contribution and fittingness, while Japanese retains the role's suitability. The common facts support a protective publicity tactic under hostile conditions. They also leave questions: what the girls and families were told about their own history, whether they consented to this cover story and how deterring Order scrutiny affects a survivor-led inquiry. The raw speech does not say Cantarella barred the girls from speaking, nor does it certify a transparent truth process. Neither “all staged kindness is manipulation” nor “success cancels the right to ask” follows [CAN-E31/E32/E44, C41].

At TalkItem ID 30 (zero-based position 29), Rover can accept further help (`_123_35`, jump to ID 31) or ask whether Cantarella wants her name cleared (`_123_36`, jump to ID 32). The latter route leads to her saying a wary public is not necessarily wrong and that entrenched family habits need gradual change (`_123_38–39`, items 31–32); the former gives a promise of family support. Both lead to the shared packing instruction at ID 34. The second route is powerful evidence of **acknowledged institutional fault**, but it is not dialogue every player necessarily heard or proof that her promised reforms were already enacted. If a model stages a private exchange about her reputation, it must declare this route, distinguish family suspicion from each survivor's testimony, and avoid making another person's trust automatic [CAN-E44/C41].

All sound remains local/restricted [CAN-E42–E44].

Most valuable next observation: review the actual Tyrvine sequence and what happens after it, rather than treating Cantarella's mechanics as omniscient; traverse each optional mainline reply and gender render without splicing them; and view the girls' public return, with their own responses and what families are told, before treating a protective fiction as either full justice or proven exploitation. Human four-dub listening could refine tenderness, teasing or reluctance, but it cannot make mutually exclusive replies co-occur or supply missing survivor consent. Until those tests are run, the source supports a strategist who has learned to cede *some* choices while still controlling consequential stories about a house she leads [CAN-C39–C41].
