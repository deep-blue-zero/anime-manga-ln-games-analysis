# Cantarella — who gets to contest a protective bargain?

Cantarella's decision to remain Fisalia head can look like a complete ethical answer: end the trials, let the girls go, bear confinement herself. The source gives that decision a challenger *inside* the survivor community. In the farewell at `flow#/6607/6/0–7`, Cheri thanks her for care, remembers a wish Cantarella once confided during the trials, and says Cantarella should not have to carry the burden alone. Cantarella answers with a headship bargain. Cheri is not a dream-table aspect or a line spoken by Cantarella: she is a named other speaker, 50135, in the same retained flow action. Her objection does not automatically defeat the choice, but it makes “the girls wanted her to sacrifice her freedom” an unsupported shortcut [CAN-E19, E31; CAN-C01, C27].

## A desire remembered by someone else

Cheri's `Character_Cantarella_124_4` recalls Cantarella wanting to go far from the manor, hide her name and live freely. Chinese, English, Japanese and Korean agree on the basic wish; Korean explicitly remembers the two speaking when they went through the trials together. This is an independent *testimony about Cantarella's earlier desire*, not a present statement that she must now abandon headship. It also gives the desire a social witness beyond the favor-story dream child: freedom is not only an allegory at the dream table. Cheri's next line objects to the cold manor and solitary burden (`flow#/6607/6/4`). The four language witnesses vary in force—Japanese says she need not carry all of it, while the others emphasize that she is or would be alone—but none grants Cheri legal authority to make Cantarella's decision for her [CAN-E06, E19, E31; CAN-C06, C27].

Cantarella replies at `flow#/6607/6/5–7` that remaining head makes recurrence of the trials and further innocent harm less likely, and casts one person's lost freedom as a favorable exchange for many. This is her own judgment. The reason can be admirable without making it a contract the former girls requested, or proof that Cheri's concern is naïve. It also need not mean lifelong literal confinement: later sources show Cantarella visiting Ragunna, buying ordinary tea and hosting a guest (`FavorStory_160705_Content`; `flow#/7344/6`; `flow#/7345/5`). The actual trade is of unencumbered departure from the office and its responsibility, not demonstrably every hour of movement or enjoyment. A scenario that has Cheri urge rest or shared responsibility can be source-consistent even if Cantarella initially resists. A scenario that makes Cheri simply applaud the bargain loses her recorded dissent [CAN-E07, E19, E22, E31; CAN-C16, C27].

## “The past” has an uncertain investigator

The same headship answer describes what happens to past trials. Chinese `Character_Cantarella_124_6` says the past becomes difficult to pursue or hold to account while she remains head; it leaves the pursuing party implicit. English says what happened stays buried. Japanese has Cantarella declare that no one will be allowed to dig into the past. Korean is more specific: the Order will have difficulty calling the past to account. Those are materially different scopes. The common fact is that headship closes or constrains *some* inquiry at the same time it blocks renewed trials. The pinned lines do not decide whether the constraint protects survivors from an Order investigation, shields Fisalia wrongdoers from public accountability, does both, or changes with audience. The preceding system of trials and her later strategic Order alignment make the question important, not answerable by the most convenient gloss [CAN-E19, E32; CAN-C01, C26, C28].

This distinction tightens two earlier packet readings. “No new trials” is not “every previous perpetrator was investigated.” Equally, “the past is hard to pursue” is not yet proof she destroyed records or forbade Cheri and other survivors to speak. A model should name who is asking—an Order official, a victim's family, a former trial girl, Rover, or a public commission—and what information they seek before predicting Cantarella's response. The Korean Order-specific witness supports one narrower motive; the Japanese broad prohibition supports a more troubling one. Both are witnesses to the same source key, not independent scenes that can be combined into a new event [CAN-E19, E20, E32; CAN-C25–C28].

## Claim, corroboration and the survivor's separate future

In the later postquest account Cantarella reports that the girls have returned home (`flow#/6620/3/18–20`). The farewell scene shows Cheri departing and thanking her for care; it provides *some* independent support for a released survivor and for a relationship between them. It does not independently verify every girl's destination, safety, recovery or satisfaction with the headship arrangement. Cantarella's retrospective account is meaningful but remains a participant's report. Cheri's words are not reducible to a prop in the head's redemption; the source gives her memory, a wish for Cantarella and a path out of the manor. A durable model should permit Cheri or another survivor to decline a reunion, ask hard questions or lead a life outside the family without casting that as betrayal [CAN-E17, E19, E20, E31; CAN-C09, C27].

The later `flow#/7472/6/1–11` refusal to reclaim a power mixture that includes the children's frequencies is an observable limit on Cantarella's appropriation. It supports ethical change more directly than a verbal promise alone. But it does not specify a full restitution mechanism, ownership of the records, or whether each girl wants the same outcome. When she tells Rover at `flow#/6620/3/1–6` that a necessary-sacrifice system trampled others' will, that moral rule should also constrain a well-intended decision to keep survivors silent for their own good. This is a source-bounded extrapolation, not proof that she has already put such a rule into practice in every setting [CAN-E20, E23, E32; CAN-C02, C09, C28].

## Private disclosure has two response routes, not a public resolution

At `flow#/6620/3/25`, her offer of Fisalia medicines and secrets to Rover is a *common* line before Rover's two options; the raw `TalkSequence` then routes to Cantarella's `/26` or `/27` response and rejoins at `/28`. A previous formulation that called the offer itself route-responsive was imprecise. On one path she calls sharing an honor; on the other she says they need not divide mine from yours, which English renders with especially domestic coloring. The common offer may be sincere, but a route-specific follow-up cannot be made universal. Neither path proves an Order inquiry, a public record release, a victim's access to the family archive, or a chosen household with Rover. It therefore does not resolve the headship question above [CAN-E20, E32; CAN-C11, C26, C28].

For hypothetical scenes, the decisive inputs are Cantarella's time state, who is asking, whether a dream is offered with an exit, who owns the risk or memory, what records actually exist, and which Rover response occurred. Cheri's testimony should make her willing to *hear an objection to lonely duty* in a well-grounded model; it does not require her to accept it instantly. A model that refuses all inquiry in the name of protection has outrun the evidence. So has a model that announces complete institutional justice because she ended the trials. The more faithful possibility is a guarded leader who has genuinely stopped an abuse, still owes survivors an account she cannot dictate for them, and may be moved by the person who remembers she once wanted another life. All performance and visual claims remain open until human four-dub listening and runtime observation.
