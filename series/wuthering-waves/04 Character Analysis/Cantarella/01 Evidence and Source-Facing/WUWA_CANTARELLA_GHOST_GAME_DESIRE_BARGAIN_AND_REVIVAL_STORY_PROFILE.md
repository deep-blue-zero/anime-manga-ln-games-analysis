# Cantarella — a bargain tailored to the hearer's desire

At `flow#/6701/6`, state `剧情_2_2_角色_坎特蕾拉线_1_14`, Cantarella describes a festival ghost-girl game before the later disclosed public return. Three elusive stalls hold riddles; the contestant who reconstructs the ghost girl's story may ask the host for an elixir (`Character_Cantarella_134_1`, `_134_5`). She rejects the demon rumor **within this encounter**, pointing instead to distorted frequencies and people disguised to obtain something (`_134_2`). She says the vendors want access to what others desire rather than money (`_134_3–4`). The phrasing gives her a characteristic double register: amused by an economy of hidden wants, yet sufficiently lucid about artifice to name the people behind a supernatural costume. It is not a world-wide proof that no other entity could exist [CAN-E46/C43].

The prize list raises the stakes: gold, endless youth or even reviving the dead (`_134_6`). This is an **advertised game promise**, not a witnessed potion effect. English's “this potion is here to make it real” is stronger than Chinese's invitation to request any secret medicine and Korean's hearsay-shaped final question. Later, at `flow#/7315/7`, Cantarella says she planned to *present* the protected girls' return through a festival “elixir of revival.” Cheri separately remembers years of hidden residence and masked visits. The later disclosure makes the earlier contest intelligible as part of her public-story architecture, but it does not prove that she had already told Rover the plan here, that every vendor knew it, that a literal resurrection medicine was available, or that each girl consented to the eventual account [CAN-E44 E46; CAN-C41 C43].

The relief route matters ethically. A model should not either sanitize this as cost-free flirting or convict it as a proven coercive curse. The exact message effect, whether retreat was possible, what Cantarella knew at this moment and which answer was played remain open. Her later willingness to have Rover judge her and her eventual protective return plan must be evaluated alongside, not substituted for, the earlier leverage [CAN-E13 E44 E46; CAN-C11 C17 C41 C43].
