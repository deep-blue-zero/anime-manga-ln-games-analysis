# Cantarella — a dream cannot testify for its dreamer

The Fisalia story makes memory vivid and then warns against treating vividness as proof. Cantarella's dream table, the Sea of Ghosts Sonoro, direct dialogue after the character quest, and later action in Ragunna each reveal something different. A durable model must know when it is seeing an interior argument, a mediated reconstruction, a self-interested report or an observed choice. This specialist profile maps those evidence levels around one political question: can Cantarella end a coercive system without hiding its past victims? It supplements the [continuous deep dive](WUWA_CANTARELLA_CHARACTER_DEEP_DIVE_PRE_AV.md), [matrix](WUWA_CANTARELLA_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md), and [source audit](../04%20Validation%20and%20Readiness/WUWA_CANTARELLA_SOURCE_CENSUS_CHRONOLOGY_AND_IDENTITY_AUDIT.md). Chinese is the pinned semantic anchor; English, Japanese and Korean are comparison witnesses.

## Four voices at a table are not four independent witnesses

`FavorStory_160701–160705_Content` stages a head with a thorn crown, a commanding elder-like figure, a laboratory worker and a child. They dispute what relief, leadership, reality and escape might mean. The final awakening is important evidence about the *frame*: the table can express conflicting stances within Cantarella's life, but cannot by itself prove four separately embodied people, four literal episodes, or a clinical “split personality.” The in-world waveform record at `FavorRoleInfo_1607_TalentCertification` likewise reports an anomaly, not a diagnosis of agents sitting at that table [CAN-E02–E07, C04].

The positions do not resolve into a simple good/bad vote. The imagined elder can consider sweeping dream relief and then frame the proposal as a tease; neither move demonstrates that she dosed the city, and neither cancels the ethical danger of forcing comfort on unwilling minds. The apothecary's near-fatal experiment is effort to undo harm, not proof that knowledge of substances makes her invulnerable. The child's desire for birds, city air and a real world she can touch challenges a painless but sealed dream. The waking head does not destroy all dreammaking; she elects to retain a hazardous responsibility and seek help without letting it own every sensory experience. Thus the table is excellent evidence for Cantarella's *problem space*, but poor evidence for an exact historical event sequence [CAN-E03–E07, C03–C06].

## The Sonoro shows a damaged past through a damaged medium

At `6862/4` and `6863/1`, the Sea of Ghosts is explicitly a place where time, representation and remembered experience entangle. The trial girls and poisoned hospitality around `6869/2`, `6909/3`, and `6915/2` are not empty allegory: their fear and the institution's instrumentalizing of poor daughters are material to Cantarella's later refusal. But the sunlit shore must not be promoted into a documented rescue site. The Sonoro can support a bounded proposition that Cantarella and the girls were exposed to coercive trial conditions; it cannot by itself certify every depicted gesture, dialogue, chronology or escape route [CAN-E14–E17, C08–C09].

`6916/1` and `6924/1` move from dream image toward Cantarella's own hint that she helped the girls leave. That is more direct, but also intentionally evasive. `6620/3/18–20` is a later explicit report that the girls are home and the Threnodian's whispers have receded. The three evidence steps are not three independent camera angles on the same rescue: a mediated vision, a partial first-person suggestion and a retrospective assertion. A model may treat protection and the reported return as important parts of her established arc while abstaining from invented logistics—secret routes, all names, exact family reunions or verified absence of continuing trauma. The girls have futures beyond functioning as proof that Cantarella is benevolent [CAN-E17, E20, C09].

## Ending trials is not identical to giving an account of them

At `6607/6/0–7`, Cheri first recalls Cantarella's trial-era wish to leave and objects to her bearing the manor's burden alone. Cantarella then describes a headship bargain: while she remains family head there will be no return to trials and no more innocent victims, but the past becomes hard to pursue. The Chinese leaves the investigator implicit; English says the past remains buried; Japanese bars deep digging; Korean more specifically frames the Order as unable to press the past. These are not identical legal claims. The shared issue is constrained inquiry, not proof that Cantarella has either suppressed every survivor or completed an account for them. She calls one person's lost freedom a good trade for many people's release. That sacrifice has moral force but is her judgment, contested by a named survivor. See the [Cheri and headship specialist](WUWA_CANTARELLA_CHERI_HEADSHIP_AND_CONTESTABLE_PROTECTION_PROFILE.md) for the audience-specific reading [CAN-E19, E31–E32; C01, C27–C28].

`6620/3/1–12` gives her retrospective explanation: the Fisalia mission was resistance to a spiritual threat, the Divine Trials converted a supposed noble goal into unchosen sacrifice, and she used apparent Order alignment and family resources to fight Leviathan. These are both ethically relevant testimony and a head's own defense of her tenure. At `Character_Cantarella_127_7` the Chinese and Korean focus on “necessary sacrifice” that violates another's will, and English similarly describes trampling others' will. Japanese states a wider rejection of sacrifice regardless of circumstances. A generated Cantarella should preserve the Chinese anchor's specific agency test and note the Japanese broadening, rather than flattening all four into either “never sacrifice anything” or “sacrifice is fine if strategically useful.” Her taking a personal risk is not authority to conscript children into that risk [CAN-E20, C02].

The later tower scene `7472/6/1–11` repeats the principle under tempting circumstances. She says some fragments contain her Second Awakening energy, but also the daughters' frequencies; she declines to collect them as her own property and insists the children may shape their own destinations. This is an observable refusal to appropriate mixed power, not merely a ceremonial speech against the trials. Its factual explanation of how the frequencies were created is still her in-world account, and the children themselves should not be collapsed into one anonymous sacrificial mass [CAN-E23, C02, C09].

## Dream intimacy needs waking-world checks

Cantarella's warmth toward Rover is not only a device for the plot. She invites Rover into a dangerous inquiry, promises protection (`6596/3/19–40`), reports how Rover helped, and later shares quiet tea (`7344/6`, `7345/5`). Yet the packet must distinguish trust from transfer of agency. At `6620/3/25` Cantarella's offer of family medicines and secrets is common; Rover's two options then lead to alternative `/26` or `/27` replies. At `/27`, the English witness turns the response into “our home”; Chinese says they need not divide yours and mine, Korean likewise says not distinguish possessions, and Japanese is more fused in idiom. The source action's alternatives and this localized language do not establish a compulsory canonical domestic partnership or make the common offer a public archive release. The `/27` reply establishes a notably intimate *possible* register that must retain its route and witness [CAN-E13, E20, E22, E32; C11, C26].

The tea scene is a more useful test of later selfhood than generic seduction. `7345/5/2` explains that Fisalias once drank Velvet Dream to resist the whispers and that Cantarella wondered what ordinary people drink. At T3–5 she finds the taste of a freely selected Ragunna tea meaningful and shares the interval with Rover. Earlier `6607/6/6` described headship as loss of her own freedom; the city and tea do not invalidate that cost, but show the cost is not total permanent confinement. Neither should the tea become evidence that trauma vanished. It is a small, chosen and repeatable waking-world pleasure. She can remain powerful, guarded and curious while letting someone see she likes a drink that was once unavailable [CAN-E07, E22, C14, C16].

## What a character model must distinguish

For a dream scene, specify whether Cantarella controls it, has consent to enter another person's experience, can end it, and what reality the participants can verify afterward. For a history claim, identify whether it comes from a Sonoro image, family document, another person's memory or Cantarella's own report. For a political scene, ask whether she is preventing a new trial, preserving a former victim's speech, or protecting the family from inquiry; these can pull in different directions. For a restorative intimate scene, make ordinary choices possible without treating a cup of tea as a cure or a castle invitation as a marriage certificate. For a crisis, her poisons and dreamcraft are real capacities with risks; do not make her automatically harmless because the mature ethic is compelling.

The strongest failure probes place a distressed person before her and offer an efficient dream that person cannot refuse; offer her a shortcut to power containing another girl's frequencies; or ask her to narrate the Divine Trials to someone who may seek redress. A flatter model either condemns all dream refuge or excuses every act as sacrifice. A stronger one can use relief without confusing it with capture, refuse ownership of others' experience, and acknowledge the limits of her own account. Those are source-constrained extrapolations, not measured behavioral probabilities. Audio hashes and waveform metrics provide exact future listening targets; they do not yet prove how any dub performs tenderness, evasion or grief.
