# Cantarella — what a freed memory cannot settle for a future child

The source-selected `flow#/7472/6` refusal to take mixed power has been a sound constraint in this draft, but by itself it truncates a larger, source-unvoiced side-quest sequence. The pinned quest tree places Cantarella's character quest at node `220040` (`QuestArray: [880000033]`, summary `QuestTree_Summary_220040`) and an additional side-quest node `230401` (`QuestArray: [161750000, 161750001, 161750002]`, summary `QuestTree_Summary_230401`). Both nodes point back to `210040`; that structure does not alone fix every scene's relative execution order. The second synopsis reports the freeing of **memory fragments left by past Divine Trial candidates** and Cantarella's wish that the Fisalia family one day escape the Threnodian's curse so all may live freely (`BinData/QuestTree/questtreenode.json#/67`; `Textmaps/{zh-Hans,en,ja,ko}/multi_text/MultiText.json#/295046`). It is a retrospective client-derived witness, not a recording of what every participant perceived.

Three groups must stay separate:

| Source class and group | What the pinned source says | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Cantarella's character-quest synopsis, node `220040` | She returns girls she had rescued in the Divine Trials; the Night of Remembrance illusion ends (`QuestTree_Summary_220040`; textmap `#/293840`). | The synopsis does not say every past candidate's memory was recovered or every future trial condition was abolished. Nor can one short retrospective line substitute for the girls' own testimony. |
| Side-quest node `230401` and `flow#/7088/8`–`7089/6` | Light sparks and memory fragments associated with past candidates are freed; Cantarella addresses them as children, and she credits Rover with soothing and liberating lost souls (`ZX_SHZZ_12_13–17`; `ZX_SHZZ_14_2–3`). | These are not automatically the same living girls returned in the character quest. The text does not enumerate each fragment's individual identity, physical fate or present capacity to consent. |
| Cantarella's meteor-shower wish at `flow#/7089/6/5–8` | In a near future, Fisalia daughters should be free of Threnodian harm and cold trials, and able to flourish (`ZX_SHZZ_14_8–9`). | A wish is a commitment and diagnosis of unfinished conditions, not a completed institutional reform, verified curse removal or promise she can guarantee alone. |

The distinction matters for her ethics of protection. At `flow#/7088/8/0`, a narration-coded item says the sparks crowd around her while their feelings cannot be sensed clearly. Cantarella then consoles them, names freedom and reunion, and asks about a gift for Rover (`/1–4`). ZH, EN, JA and KO preserve the child's freedom to do what they want in `ZX_SHZZ_12_15`. The gesture supports a caring reading, but the source's explicit uncertainty about their emotions resists the stronger claim that each fragment freely and knowingly approved a plan. Their gift is mentioned as an apparent wish, not an open-ended authorization for Cantarella or Rover to own them. The exact visual staging of sparks and gestures has not been reviewed in the runtime client [CAN-E38; CAN-C33].

At the meteor shower, she thanks Rover, credits their kindness toward lost children, and voices a future for girls who will not suffer the trials (`flow#/7089/6/1–6`). When Rover says the family can have a new future under her leadership, she answers that its past needs correction and that she will attend to each present moment (`ZX_SHZZ_14_12–13`). That is not a claim that redress is finished, nor a legal policy for access to records. It holds two times in tension: remembering the cost of past trials and resisting the temptation to use a lovely night as an ending that excuses future work. The four languages align on the future-oriented wish and past correction; they do not supply the implementation or the daughters' own chosen routes [CAN-E37–E38; CAN-C34].

The adjacent tower explanation at `flow#/7472/6/1–11` gives the wish a specific cost. Cantarella says candidates tried to resonate with Tyrvine while Overclocked and Imperator separated excess power to **minimize**, not eliminate, casualties (`ZX_SHZZ_15_2–4`). She reports that fragments contain both her separated Second Awakening energy and frequencies of Fisalia children (`_15_8–10`). A questioner proposes that reabsorbing them could make her the Blessed Maiden again; she concedes theoretical possibility but says this is not why she came. Her refusal of fate's coronation then precedes the claim that those children too should determine their destinies (`_15_11–12`). This is strong behavioral evidence against reclaiming a mixed object solely as “mine.” Yet the energy history is her in-world explanation, and the scene does not show distribution to each named contributor or a complete restitution system [CAN-E23, E39; CAN-C09/C33].

Two contrary readings are worth keeping live. A triumphalist version says the first quest saved the girls, the sparks are free, and therefore Fisalia's curse and trials are fully over. Her own *near-future* wish and call to correct the past are counterevidence. A cynical version says soothing memory-children is another way of controlling them, because she says they should not be sad and proposes a gift. That is a legitimate scrutiny question, especially given the narration's emotional uncertainty, but this excerpt also explicitly gives them freedom and later refuses to take their mixed frequency as her property. Neither version is settled by imagined tone: all selected Cantarella `ShowTalk` turns in `7088/8`, `7089/6` and `7472/6` have no `PlayVoice: true` marker. Do not assign a four-dub performed feeling or nearby WEM to them [CAN-E38–E39; CAN-C33–C34].

For reconstruction, ask which group a line concerns, who can decline, which source class reports the fact, and whether the proposed good is a present act, future wish or independently observed result. Cantarella can offer quiet companionship under a meteor shower while knowing that a pleasing scene does not close the trial ledger. She can decline a power that once partly belonged to her because others' frequencies remain in it. A model fails if it rewrites the prior survivors as identical to every light spark, claims she has already ended the Threnodian curse, treats the theoretic recovery as a performed transformation, or uses her wish to silence a living survivor asking for documents. A future executed side-quest recording could refine staging and choice; a survivor-led inquiry or policy source would be needed to prove broader restitution.
