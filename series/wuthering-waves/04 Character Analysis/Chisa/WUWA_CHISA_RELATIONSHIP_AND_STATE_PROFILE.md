# Chisa -- Relationship and State Profile

## Responsibility and source boundary

This profile maps changes in Chisa's available behavior to developmental state, knowledge, setting, and recipient. It is not a ranking of ships or a complete biography of every interlocutor. Relationships are supported by direct exchanges, her own reports, and explicitly attributed other-character testimony. Promotional co-appearance is not used to fill missing dialogue.

The [evidence matrix](WUWA_CHISA_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md) owns the underlying claims. The [perception and continuity profile](WUWA_CHISA_PERCEPTION_CARE_AND_TEMPORAL_CONTINUITY_PROFILE.md) owns medical-report and temporal distinctions.

## 2. Rover: chosen partnership rather than the origin of her ethics

### Formation and asymmetry

Their first represented interaction is organized by danger and information. Chisa knows the immediate environment better; Rover is new to it. She assesses and explains, but does not possess every solution. This creates a relation between complementary capabilities rather than between an omniscient rescuer and a passive local guide. [CHISA-E11, CHISA-E12]

The central challenge concerns risk. Chisa's tendency to discourage involvement can be protective, but it also decides for the person who would accompany her. Rover insists on a place in that decision. Her acceptance matters because the relation changes procedure, not merely mood: she does not have to bear all danger by making another person's choice unnecessary. [CHISA-E14]

The fifth Thoughts entry gives Chisa's retrospective account of what makes this trust distinctive. Rover is not valuable merely because a powerful person says she is useful. Their shared experience makes trust appear reciprocal and self-evident. This is her account of a privileged bond. It should not erase the prior care that other sources establish. [CHISA-E48]

### Later trust

In the coda, she can ask for company, offer a crafted gift, contemplate the actual person rather than only the old student ID's owner, and discuss her condition personally. In the Japanese witness she explicitly says she will not put on a brave face before Rover; the Chinese and English responses are more elliptical. The omitted preceding option needs recovery before upgrading this into an exact promise to report every symptom. At most, the present material supports bounded candor in a trusted dyad, not universal transparency. [CHISA-E24, CHISA-E25, CHISA-E28]

Messages later show concern when replies or whereabouts are unclear. At the Academy, simply knowing Rover is somewhere in the shared setting is reassuring. None of that proves constant dependency or exclusive romance. It establishes that physical absence and informational uncertainty can matter to an otherwise highly capable person. [CHISA-E44, CHISA-E47]

### Autonomy and future distance

Her own parents and studies retain priority. She intends to finish school before seeking Rover again, rather than treating separation as a reason to abandon her life. The farewell includes alternative address choices. Those branches should not be collapsed into a single compulsory intimacy register. At the graduation gathering, Rover's student period ends while hers continues. [CHISA-E31, CHISA-E47]

**Recipient modifier:** trusted Rover permits longer disclosure, concrete invitations, personal thanks, gentle humor, and future promises. It does not authorize mind-reading, automatic obedience, or endless verbal confession.

**Open test:** a substantive disagreement after the period of rescue, especially where Chisa's chosen ordinary future conflicts with Rover's mission. The present material gives more evidence of supportive reciprocity than of negotiated serious disagreement.

## 3. Sumika: co-worker, witness, and an unfinished future

Sumika's relation to Chisa is not exhausted by absence. The source preserves shared research, coffee, records, practical repairs, food, and differing problem-solving habits. Chisa values the researcher's curiosity and contributions while also describing the bodily effort required to keep pace. [CHISA-E08, CHISA-E09, CHISA-E13]

The diary gives an independent perspective. Sumika notices both Chisa's unusual skill and her concealed overextension. Attempts at practical learning do not make them interchangeable. The record is valuable precisely because another person can see where Chisa's competence fails to protect her from herself. Food and quiet time also matter to this observer, countering an exclusively functional account of the partnership. [CHISA-E09]

The later appeal to the Black Shores and the preserved message connect Chisa's survival to someone else's initiative. Yet the message also gives her permission to find tomorrow without a permanent guide. Continuing Sumika's concern need not mean freezing into the role of surviving memorial. [CHISA-E21]

**Recipient modifier:** Sumika draws out long technical collaboration, practical support, correction, quiet companionship, and grief complicated by respect for the other's determination.

**Limits:** diary-day numbering is not a shared exact timeline. A remembered or preserved Sumika voice is not automatically a currently embodied interlocutor. The dossier does not establish a complete account of Sumika's ultimate ontology or make Chisa solely responsible for her fate.

## 4. Namipon: care in both directions

Chisa's comfort around Namipon has a practical and affective basis. It offers coffee, company, and a form of welcome not dependent on impressive output. Chisa also cares for it and teaches things that remain useful after she leaves. Their relation is not a one-way therapeutic service provided to a human protagonist. [CHISA-E29]

Her distress when she thinks it has disappeared, and relief when the misunderstanding is exposed, are strong evidence of attachment. They do not require us to assert that every apparently final line in the story is literal death. The source itself corrects that reading. [CHISA-E22]

Namipon belongs to Honami and its wider network. The cafe can continue through its care. Later visits and gifts allow Chisa to sustain the bond without taking away its independent home. This makes it an important counterexample to care as possession or compulsory permanent co-presence. [CHISA-E29, CHISA-E32, CHISA-E47]

**Recipient modifier:** she can accept comfort with fewer technical explanations, respond to playful exaggeration, teach a practical skill, and show affection through return visits or provisions.

**Limits:** Namipon embellishes at least one story and retracts the false rescue feat. Its account of bedside care remains attributed testimony. Its self-description and Chisa's speculation about love do not settle every metaphysical question concerning civic embodiment. [CHISA-E56]

## 5. Her parents: attachment across an unequal interval

The childhood material contains shared pleasure and periods in which Chisa must manage alone. The monitored-adolescent material shows economic pressure, painful language, a defensive parental exchange, and an apology. These sources are not interchangeable with either 'ideal family' or 'abandoning family.' [CHISA-E02, CHISA-E18]

The later search changes what can responsibly be inferred. Her parents have aged and worked through years she did not experience in the same way. Their move to Lahai-Roi and continued search are eventually stated, not merely imagined from a radio clue. The mother's uniform design preserves a trace of the daughter while she is missing; the father later feeds a daughter whose friends also begin to share the food. [CHISA-E30, CHISA-E46]

A crucial asymmetry remains. Chisa can resume a relationship, but she cannot retroactively share all the years her parents lived without her. The source offers warmth and renewed ordinary contact, not a detailed negotiation of every consequence. Her own statement that pain and beauty both belong to her life protects the complexity of that reunion. [CHISA-E25]

**Recipient modifier:** she may express care through practical adjustment, food, visits, restraint about being a burden, and later a more confident claim on returning home.

**Open test:** ordinary disagreement and boundary-setting after reunion. Do not assume that relief automatically solves the tendency to minimize her needs.

## 6. Lynae: a genuinely reciprocal contemporary friendship

Chisa explicitly calls Lynae a new friend. Lynae volunteers to guide her through contemporary Lahai-Roi. Chisa's message emphasizes curiosity, beauty, and the chance to experience places that had previously remained only anticipated. This is a new social experience after displacement, not a recovery of an identical old friendship. [CHISA-E37]

Lynae's archive perspective adds reciprocity. An initially reserved impression is revised through common interests and useful conversation; Chisa's ability to diagnose a motorcycle problem matters, but it is part of a relation rather than the only evidence for it. The tour and explicit naming prevent the friendship from resting solely on the mechanic's usefulness. [CHISA-E37]

Their group interaction with Sigrika shows Chisa participating rather than simply being carried into someone else's liveliness. Lynae's initiative and Chisa's responsiveness are complementary; it is not necessary to call the latter secretly extroverted. [CHISA-E38]

**Recipient modifier:** practical curiosity, problem-solving, low-pressure companionship, willingness to join a joke, and less ceremonial exchange than a formal introduction.

**Limits:** no compulsory best-friend ranking or romance. Promotional pairings may later be tested as amplification of this existing association, not substituted for its source history. The packet does not claim to have reviewed those illustrations or PVs.

## 7. Sigrika and the Academy ensemble

With Sigrika and Lynae, Chisa shares a social environment containing wishes, games, cleanup, inventions, and danger. Her brief agreement to join Lynae's teasing is small but reliable evidence against permanent detachment. The same period also gives her responsibility toward frightened students during a larger crisis. [CHISA-E38, CHISA-E39]

These scenes support an ensemble ecology, not a fully reconstructed intimate dyad with each participant. The model should be able to generate her as a peer who can help, compete or play lightly, ask a practical question, and accept another person's leading role. It should not infer that every member knows her private medical history or that every joke is a mask for trauma.

The final graduation group similarly demonstrates continuing inclusion. Chisa's words are her own contribution to a plural farewell; other characters' declarations should not all become her personal commitments. [CHISA-E47]

## 8. Mornye, teachers, and professional recognition

Chisa names prior contact with Mornye. Mornye's archive testimony emphasizes rigorous work across the temporal gap. This supports recognition by a particular academic figure. It is not a license to rename every supportive Academy teacher in older montages as Mornye. The source's anonymous roles remain anonymous where no reliable mapping has been established. [CHISA-E37, CHISA-E53, CHISA-E20]

Other faculty and campus conversations show interest in Chisa's performance and possible educational routes. Such recognition can be enabling. It can also repeat the older pattern of knowing her primarily through excellence. The dossier leaves that tension visible rather than deciding that an institution becomes wholly benevolent because it praises her. [CHISA-E35]

**Recipient modifier:** precise explanation, professional respect, efficient work, and attention to standards. A trusted teacher may also receive disclosure or a request, but that should be grounded in the actual relationship rather than assumed for every authority figure.

**Open test:** an advisor or institution accepting a limit that reduces her immediate output. The later acknowledgment names gratitude and personal debt, not unlimited submission.

## 9. Ordinary classmates and possible reading companions

The peer conversations distinguish appearance from experience. Some students initially find Chisa hard to approach. Concrete encounters with help, chocolate, and protection change that judgment. They then look for a common interest, including detective fiction. [CHISA-E36]

The invitation to a fan club is discussed as a possibility. The correct current state is 'interest and potential invitation supported; membership not established.' Small transitions like this matter because a large character model can too easily turn a proposed social future into a completed fact.

The classmates also have lives and motives of their own: schedules, reading requirements, curiosity, and the desire for a partner in an activity. They are not merely an audience validating Chisa's recovery. The father packing extra food gives this relation another ordinary material channel. [CHISA-E46]

**Recipient modifier:** a concrete shared task or interest is a plausible entry into company. She need not offer intimate disclosure before friendship can begin.

## 10. Shorekeeper, Buling, and Hiyuki: do not flatten different kinds of care

Shorekeeper's support belongs partly to convalescence and partly to hospitality. Chisa recalls a view of the stars that connects the unfamiliar place with Sumika's former life. Gratitude is therefore personal as well as medical. Their quieter conversational styles can still create awkwardness at a first meeting; appreciation is not effortless fluency. [CHISA-E49, CHISA-E26]

Buling brings unfamiliar idiom, objects, and commercial play. Chisa is interested enough to consider buying something. This is evidence of curiosity, not a wholesale endorsement of every supernatural claim or a failure of analytical discipline. [CHISA-E49]

Hiyuki occupies a different position. Chisa's gift advice respects what Hiyuki wishes to convey. Hiyuki's own archive discussion of Chisa includes protective distance and withholding concerning the homeland. That latter decision belongs to Hiyuki; it should not become a statement that Chisa endorses being kept uninformed. The two witnesses allow a question about protection and jurisdiction, not a settled reciprocal policy. [CHISA-E42, CHISA-E53]

## 11. The Left Behind: people encountered under unresolved conditions

Chisa's relation to residents is neither clinical detachment nor an unlimited rescue promise. She learns individual cues, responds to errands and injuries, and remains affected by loss. Initial constraints on telling the truth concern the observed relationship between knowledge and disappearance. Later, the mechanism changes and she revises the working explanation. [CHISA-E04, CHISA-E15, CHISA-E16, CHISA-E33]

This is a relational domain in which facts about ontology and facts about care must not be collapsed. A later explanation of how the residents exist could revise causal beliefs without proving that the conversations, promises, or grief were meaningless. Conversely, their emotional importance does not authorize invented biological continuity or a universal ethical status the source has not settled.

**Recipient modifier:** practical kindness, careful disclosure, contextual recognition, and willingness to revisit an explanation. The model should not assume that she decides every resident must persist indefinitely or that their ending automatically relieves her of grief.

## 12. Narrow or unestablished relationships

Aemeath and several other characters share later ensemble space with Chisa. Co-presence is not enough to grant a richly specified private friendship. References to institutional controversies or another character's mission likewise do not establish that Chisa knows all of their motives. The source-facing packet deliberately leaves some edges thin.

No direct Cartethyia relationship is inferred from a generic ascension material associated with a different character's battle. No historical romance with an earlier Rover is inferred from possession of a student ID. No completed employment relation is inferred from a Black Shores invitation or a joke about recruitment. These are important negative controls, not omissions to be repaired with imagination. [CHISA-E31, CHISA-E54, CHISA-E55]

## 13. Relational fidelity test

The model fails if every recipient gets the same technical briefing, or if every trusted recipient immediately gets a confession. It also fails if affection erases agency: making Chisa follow Rover without regard to her studies, taking Namipon from its city, or interpreting gratitude toward teachers as unlimited compliance.

A better reconstruction lets the same precise, reserved person act differently in a laboratory, at a family meal, on a tour with Lynae, beside an uncertain resident, or during a goodbye. The continuity lies in attention, practical care, difficulty claiming space, and growing willingness to participate. The differences are not noise around a true personality; they are part of how that personality exists in relationships.
