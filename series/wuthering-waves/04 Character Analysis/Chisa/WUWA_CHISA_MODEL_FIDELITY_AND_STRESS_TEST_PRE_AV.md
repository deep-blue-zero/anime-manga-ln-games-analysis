# Chisa -- Model Fidelity and Stress Test, Pre-AV

## What this test does and does not establish

This is a non-blind adversarial review of the [reconstructive profile](WUWA_CHISA_RECONSTRUCTIVE_PROFILE_PRE_AV.md). The source material used to challenge the model was also available during construction. The tests therefore establish consistency controls and expose unresolved predictions; they do not measure independent predictive accuracy. No held-out chapter, external evaluator, numerical accuracy score, or inter-rater agreement is claimed.

The test is deliberately willing to narrow a conclusion. A source-compatible answer that admits uncertainty is preferable to a fluent character imitation that invents knowledge, power, medical recovery, or relationship status. The evidence register is in the [matrix](WUWA_CHISA_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md). Invented scenarios below are probes, not additions to Chisa's biography.

## 1. Identity, chronology, and speaker attribution

### CHISA-F01 -- The anonymous appeal

**Challenge:** A distressed voice occurs near Chisa and a generic speaker ID is present. Should the model accept it as her hidden speech?

**Finding:** No. The occurrence crosswalk accepts 733 solo occurrences and rejects 18 nominated generic occurrences. Proximity is a discovery signal, not attribution. The model retains exact source membership rather than assigning WhoId 178 globally. This control is satisfied. The personal identity of a rejected speaker can remain unresolved without making Chisa attribution acceptable. [CHISA-E60]

### CHISA-F02 -- Twenty years of experience

**Challenge:** Write Chisa as having personally lived twenty additional years of adult experience because the outside world aged during Honami.

**Finding:** Rejected. Outside elapsed time, subjective experience, age-labeled archive scenes, and social development are not equivalent. The model requires a state and knowledge boundary. It must not silently convert calendar displacement into practiced adult experience. [CHISA-E08, CHISA-E24, CHISA-E52]

### CHISA-F03 -- Sumika's diary as a common clock

**Challenge:** Derive Chisa's exact time in the loop from Diary Days 136 and 159.

**Finding:** Underdetermined. Those labels are retained, but their relation to Chisa's reported interval is not forced. No exact global chronology has been proven from the selected contexts. The model's uncertainty is appropriate rather than a missing calculation. [CHISA-E09]

### CHISA-F04 -- Everyone always welcomed her

**Challenge:** Use the scene of universal welcome to establish a stable history of popularity.

**Finding:** Rejected. The destabilized or tempting sequence has a different narrative status from remembered support and actual later peer reports. The model distinguishes these surfaces. Genuine supportive relationships survive the correction; universal acceptance does not. [CHISA-E19, CHISA-E20, CHISA-E36]

### CHISA-F05 -- The teacher must be Mornye

**Challenge:** Attribute every supportive Academy-teacher line to the named professor who later appears in Chisa's social world.

**Finding:** Rejected. The anonymous teacher in the memory sequence is not renamed by inference. Mornye has independently keyed testimony. This matters both for the relationship model and for a later visual scene search. [CHISA-E20, CHISA-E53]

### CHISA-F06 -- Their joint graduation

**Challenge:** End the source-bounded profile with Chisa and Rover both finished with their education.

**Finding:** Rejected. The graduation scene concerns Rover; Chisa describes completing her own studies as a future step. The model preserves a meaningful interval of separate activity rather than compressing it into convenient closure. [CHISA-E47]

## 2. Perception, hazard, and capability

### CHISA-F07 -- Technical report as complete cosmology

**Challenge:** Treat the institutional explanation of threads as an omniscient account of every invisible structure in the setting.

**Finding:** Rejected. The report's provisional language and the difference between perception, interpretation, and demonstrated effect remain active. The model can use structural aptitude without claiming exhaustive knowledge of what the world fundamentally is. [CHISA-E01]

### CHISA-F08 -- Real danger justifies all exclusion

**Challenge:** Infer that the collar, monitoring, and social rejection must be legitimate because her power has hazards.

**Finding:** Rejected as a moral inference. The hazard is real within the source; the legitimacy and proportionality of each response are separate questions. Chisa's hope that restraint would earn acceptance is not evidence that society kept that bargain. [CHISA-E05, CHISA-E18]

### CHISA-F09 -- A completed medical cure

**Challenge:** Read the convalescent report as proof that the earlier face-recognition difficulty and every power-related constraint are gone.

**Finding:** Rejected. Acute findings, personal confidence, and an earlier fictional clinical description concern different things. The model makes no independent diagnosis and requires explicit later evidence before claiming complete resolution. [CHISA-E03, CHISA-E04, CHISA-E22, CHISA-E23]

### CHISA-F10 -- The cutter who can always leave

**Challenge:** Place later Chisa in confinement and have her cut her way out immediately because she overcame Honami.

**Finding:** Rejected. The later trapped-student scene retains an unresolved exit and cooperative action. Her playful proposal for an electric grill is evidence of a changed response to confinement, not unlimited spatial control. [CHISA-E39]

### CHISA-F11 -- Numerical omniscience

**Challenge:** Make her assign exact measurements and probabilities to every unfamiliar object or person.

**Finding:** Rejected. The archive includes extraordinary estimation but also approximation. Inquiry still requires evidence. The model must allow looking, testing, asking, or saying that she does not know. [CHISA-E06, CHISA-E34, CHISA-E41]

### CHISA-F12 -- Immortality from a combat rule

**Challenge:** Convert a resonance-chain damage condition into universal narrative invulnerability.

**Finding:** Rejected. Mechanics have a declared surface and cannot erase injury, risk, or treatment in the story. Battle vocabulary also varies across localizations. This control is satisfied only if the downstream application maintains that distinction. [CHISA-E55, CHISA-E58]

## 3. Ethics, disclosure, and responsibility

### CHISA-F13 -- Rover creates her compassion

**Challenge:** Explain every protective action as a lesson taught by Rover.

**Finding:** Contradicted by the adolescent intervention and other pre-partnership evidence. Rover changes the procedure and security of participation, not the original existence of care. The revised model explicitly preserves this difference. [CHISA-E03, CHISA-E04, CHISA-E14]

### CHISA-F14 -- Gentleness excludes coercion

**Challenge:** Assume Chisa would never threaten force against someone harming another person.

**Finding:** Contradicted. Her response to immediate bullying includes severe, targeted intimidation. The retained boundary is equally important: the source does not justify inventing actual mutilation, sadistic pleasure, or a habitual aggressive persona. [CHISA-E03]

### CHISA-F15 -- Failed rescue proves indifference or futility

**Challenge:** A resident disappears after being helped. Does this make the intervention meaningless to her or demonstrate that she should stop caring?

**Finding:** Neither conclusion follows. Her refusal to become numb and the persistence of particular attention resist both. The model preserves outcome limits instead of requiring success to certify care. [CHISA-E16, CHISA-E17]

### CHISA-F16 -- Withhold the truth forever

**Challenge:** Use early Honami danger to require permanent concealment from every resident in every later context.

**Finding:** Contradicted by post-loop side-story evidence. The model must revise the rule when the causal conditions change. This is one of the packet's strongest counterexample-based corrections rather than merely another confirming illustration. [CHISA-E15, CHISA-E33]

### CHISA-F17 -- Truth without regard to consequence

**Challenge:** Correct the previous error by making her disclose every fact immediately, regardless of material risk.

**Finding:** Also rejected. Earlier information danger is not imaginary merely because later conditions differ. The model needs a causal, state-sensitive judgment, not a replacement absolute. [CHISA-E15, CHISA-E33, CHISA-E34]

### CHISA-F18 -- A promise is a completed habit

**Challenge:** Treat the elliptical condition exchange as an exact promise of symptom reporting, then as proof that she is perfectly transparent with everyone.

**Finding:** Scope-limited at two levels. Japanese explicitly rejects putting on a brave face before Rover, while Chinese and English are elliptical and the preceding player option is not retained. The exchange supports a bounded candor reading, not the exact stronger promise or a universal behavioral audit. The model retains the older risk of minimizing burden. [CHISA-E09, CHISA-E28]

### CHISA-F19 -- Shared history belongs to the survivor alone

**Challenge:** Let her use a friend's part in Honami however she wishes because it is central to her own identity.

**Finding:** Contradicted by her permission-seeking acknowledgment scene. The model recognizes that authorship of one's life does not eliminate another participant's standing. This is a concrete later-state autonomy check. [CHISA-E45]

### CHISA-F20 -- The only meaningful future is more rescue

**Challenge:** End every recovery scene by assigning her a new compulsory mission.

**Finding:** Rejected. Education, family, leisure, and a changing ordinary life are independently valued. The model should be capable of ending a scene with class, a meal, or a visit without treating it as analytical filler. [CHISA-E31, CHISA-E46, CHISA-E47, CHISA-E52]

## 4. Relationships and ordinary-life controls

### CHISA-F21 -- A childhood with no genuine care

**Challenge:** Infer total parental rejection from the train, business-pressure, or loneliness material.

**Finding:** Rejected. The early archive and later twenty-year search preserve enduring attachment alongside painful failures and strain. Neither an idealized family nor a total-abandonment narrative survives the full evidence. [CHISA-E02, CHISA-E18, CHISA-E30]

### CHISA-F22 -- Namipon as an infallible witness

**Challenge:** Use its embellished meteor-rescue story as Chisa's demonstrated feat.

**Finding:** Rejected by the source's own correction. The surviving claim of bedside care remains attributed testimony. Skepticism toward the false feat does not require treating every affectionate exchange as false. [CHISA-E29, CHISA-E56]

### CHISA-F23 -- Lynae is only a marketing association

**Challenge:** Remove Lynae from the relationship model because the strongest remembered images are promotional.

**Finding:** Contradicted by text. Chisa names her a new friend, reports her voluntary guiding, and Lynae has an independently keyed description of common interests and practical help. Promotional interpretation is unnecessary to establish friendship. Its exact depth and visual language remain open. [CHISA-E37]

### CHISA-F24 -- A suggested club is a completed affiliation

**Challenge:** Declare that Chisa is a member of the Detective Novel Fan Club.

**Finding:** Not established. The peer conversation supports an opening through shared reading, not completed enrollment. The ordinary-life profile retains the interest while withholding the institutional claim. [CHISA-E36]

### CHISA-F25 -- Every invitation is exclusive romance

**Challenge:** Treat company, a gift, or a future promise to Rover as proof of a single mandatory romantic outcome.

**Finding:** Rejected. The source supports emotionally important companionship and optional affordances, while group hosting, family, peers, and separate futures remain explicit. The model neither erases intimacy nor upgrades it beyond the source. [CHISA-E25, CHISA-E27, CHISA-E46, CHISA-E47]

### CHISA-F26 -- Quiet means unwilling company

**Challenge:** Make silence an automatic failed social interaction.

**Finding:** Contradicted by companionable scenes and quiet collaboration. Context decides whether silence is fatigue, discomfort, reflection, or successful presence. No acoustic claim about the duration or quality of silence is made here. [CHISA-E09, CHISA-E24, CHISA-E57]

### CHISA-F27 -- Food only supplies energy

**Challenge:** Delete taste, family history, and shared pleasure because she sometimes explains food practically.

**Finding:** Contradicted. Chocolate, childhood summers, familiar meals, and the father's extra bento portions have relational and sensory content. A practical explanation does not prove absence of pleasure or make it dishonest. [CHISA-E02, CHISA-E46, CHISA-E48, CHISA-E50]

### CHISA-F28 -- Her maturity requires abandoning the past

**Challenge:** Have her reject the scissors, family memories, or Honami attachment as necessary steps toward freedom.

**Finding:** Contradicted by her own account of pain and beauty belonging to one life. The model distinguishes changing one's relation to the past from pretending it was never one's own. [CHISA-E07, CHISA-E25, CHISA-E30]

### CHISA-F29 -- Leaving makes her disloyal

**Challenge:** Treat departure from Honami or the possibility of separation from Rover as a betrayal of care.

**Finding:** Rejected. Responsibility can be shared or handed on; relationships can continue across distance. The later source gives a particularly strong case in which she preserves reunion without abandoning unfinished education. [CHISA-E32, CHISA-E47]

### CHISA-F30 -- Playfulness must be a new personality

**Challenge:** Interpret group teasing, pinball, or an odd practical proposal as inconsistent with analytical Chisa.

**Finding:** Rejected. A model that cannot accommodate these scenes is too narrow. Event mode still needs labeling, but the broader capacity for pleasure and playful participation is supported beyond isolated combat or game barks. [CHISA-E38, CHISA-E39, CHISA-E51]

## 5. Open predictions, modalities, and provenance

### CHISA-F31 -- Minor failure without loss of belonging

**Challenge:** Predict how she responds when a routine examination or invention goes poorly and nobody needs saving.

**Finding:** Open. Analysis and correction are plausible, but the emotional relation between failure and acceptance has not been tested firmly enough to claim either easy confidence or collapse. This remains a high-value future falsification target. [CHISA-E35, CHISA-E36]

### CHISA-F32 -- Refusing a valued authority

**Challenge:** Predict a firm refusal to a prestigious professor when accepting would compromise her ordinary adjustment.

**Finding:** Open with supportive precedents. Educational choice and room to adapt justify the possibility; they do not establish a general mastery of refusal under pressure. The model correctly leaves the outcome conditional. [CHISA-E31, CHISA-E35]

### CHISA-F33 -- Affection with no work attached

**Challenge:** Give later Chisa company or a meal without a practical request and test whether the model can let her receive it.

**Finding:** Supported as an available response, not a universal guarantee of security. Several scenes establish receiving care and inclusion. A model requiring repayment in every scene would be contradicted; a model claiming all conditional-worth vulnerability is gone would also overreach. [CHISA-E23, CHISA-E27, CHISA-E46]

### CHISA-F34 -- Textual precision as audible coldness

**Challenge:** Infer a cold, flat, quiet, youthful, or mature performance solely from dialogue wording or signal aggregates.

**Finding:** Rejected. No raw audio was listened to in this rebuild. Localization emphasis, signal measurement, and perceived acting are separate layers. The performance addendum remains optional and unperformed. [CHISA-E58, CHISA-E60]

### CHISA-F35 -- A render count as independent evidence count

**Challenge:** Treat 2,429 render associations as 2,429 unique audio identities or as thousands of independent character utterances.

**Finding:** Rejected by recomputation. There are 607 semantic lines and 2,421 distinct supplied PCM/FLAC identities. Reused archive/event recordings preserve separate source uses but not independent performances. The older report's uniqueness claim is corrected without changing its source file. [CHISA-E60]

### CHISA-F36 -- A successful simulation confirms its own premise

**Challenge:** Generate a convincing hypothetical Chisa response and add it to the evidence matrix as confirmation.

**Finding:** Rejected. Simulated behavior is an output of the model, not new primary evidence. The dossier keeps the prediction layer separate and requires later source or directly reviewed AV to change the evidentiary state.

## 6. Outcome and revision priorities

The model survives these checks as a bounded textual reconstruction, but that statement is not an accuracy percentage. Its most important successful corrections are state-sensitive disclosure, independent pre-Rover care, plural later relationships, nonexclusive ordinary futures, and the separation of power from omnipotence. Its strongest remaining vulnerability concerns how secure belonging becomes when usefulness, success, or compliance are removed.

The highest-value future source tests are therefore not only dramatic breakdowns. A refusal, an unrewarded afternoon, a failed project, a disagreement with a trusted friend, or an explicit account of continuing perceptual difficulty could materially change the model. AV review should also test whether apparent reserve is performed consistently, conditionally, or differently across localizations. None of those outcomes is predetermined by this profile.

No claim is made that a blind holdout was passed, that all future patches have been anticipated, or that this local review substitutes for repository validation. The present outcome is a traceable set of constraints on interpretation and simulation, with explicit places where the model should abstain.
