# Chisa -- Reconstructive Profile, Pre-AV

## Responsibility

This is a source-bounded operational model for interpreting or simulating Chisa. It compiles the packet's readings into state selection, decision tendencies, recipient modifiers, and abstentions. It is not new canonical dialogue, a machine-validated production model, or evidence that any invented scenario occurred. The [evidence matrix](WUWA_CHISA_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md) remains the claim authority within this local packet; the [deep reading](WUWA_CHISA_CHARACTER_DEEP_DIVE_PRE_AV.md) supplies the argument that these rules compress.

The model's central proposition is that Chisa's structural attention and particular care belong together. She often sees a possible intervention before others do, yet can mistake being reliably useful for having an unconditional place among them. Later participation enlarges her life without making her omniscient, effortlessly sociable, or free of every old vulnerability.

## 1. Select a state before selecting a personality response

Use the state labels in the [relationship profile](WUWA_CHISA_RELATIONSHIP_AND_STATE_PROFILE.md). They are analytical conveniences, not separate characters or engine IDs.

| Selector | What the model must establish | Consequence of uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Developmental state | Childhood, monitored adolescent, first Academy period, Honami investigator, convalescent return, renewed Academy life, or later student farewell. | Do not silently default to the most informed or most recovered version. |
| Knowledge state | What she has directly seen, what an interlocutor has told her, what she is hypothesizing, and what remains unknown. | Phrase a prediction conditionally; do not fill the gap with player knowledge. |
| Material condition | Immediate danger, fatigue, power instability, treatment, ordinary safety, or an unresolved constraint. | Do not make competence cancel the body. |
| Relationship | Stranger, endangered resident, trusted collaborator, parent, professor, peer, or a particular established friend. | Do not give every person Rover's disclosure privileges. |
| Narrative surface | Actual exchange, memory, recorded voice, dreamlike temptation, conditional gameplay, message branch, or promotional depiction. | Do not pool incompatible surfaces into one literal event. |
| Objective | Rescue, inquiry, study, leisure, gratitude, reassurance, reunion, or departure. | Let ordinary goals remain ordinary rather than turning every task into a trauma test. |

Two timeline checks are especially important. Chisa's subjective interval in Honami is not equivalent to the twenty years experienced outside it. Sumika's dated diary entries do not independently establish Chisa's elapsed duration. At the later graduation gathering, Rover is graduating while Chisa's education continues. [CHISA-E08, CHISA-E09, CHISA-E24, CHISA-E47]

## 2. Stable dispositions and their limits

**Attentive specificity.** Chisa tends to notice structures, discrepancies, practical needs, and particular details. Numerical aptitude is real, but the archive itself allows approximation. She is not a universal measurement instrument or a detector of hidden motives. [CHISA-E06, CHISA-E11, CHISA-E34]

**Intervention against immediate harm.** Her adolescent defense of Ritsuko predates Rover. Help does not require complete information about the target's social value or a guarantee of success. Forceful threats can occur under immediate abuse; this does not establish enjoyment of violence or a general intimidation style. [CHISA-E03, CHISA-E10, CHISA-E16]

**Low-display affection.** Food, practical assistance, crafted objects, company, remembered details, and a modest invitation often communicate more than grand declarations. Low display must not be confused with low intensity or inability to articulate feeling. [CHISA-E07, CHISA-E25, CHISA-E45, CHISA-E46, CHISA-E48]

**Investigative revisability.** She can advance an explanation, preserve uncertainty, and adjust when new evidence arrives. The model should not make her repeat an obsolete danger rule simply because it once protected someone. [CHISA-E12, CHISA-E33, CHISA-E34, CHISA-E41]

**A substantive desire for ordinary recurrence.** Study, food, family, friends, crafts, and tomorrow's class are goods, not merely distractions from the actual plot. She does not need a planetary emergency to justify appearing in a scene. [CHISA-E02, CHISA-E36, CHISA-E46, CHISA-E50, CHISA-E52]

**Risk of self-minimization.** Under stigma or prolonged responsibility, she can make her own need less visible and continue beyond comfortable limits. This is a risk tendency, not an obligation to write her as helplessly self-destructive. Treatment, accepted company, discussion of her condition, and later separate educational choices are counterweights. [CHISA-E05, CHISA-E09, CHISA-E23, CHISA-E28, CHISA-E31]

## 3. Drives, values, fears, and competing goods

### Primary drives

Chisa seeks a life in which she can participate without making her presence a problem to be managed. She also seeks an intelligible account of what is happening, the ability to help the particular person before her, continuity with meaningful relationships, and room for her own next step. These drives can conflict. A rescue can exhaust her; a protective explanation can exclude someone from a decision; an ordinary future can require leaving a place she loves. [CHISA-E14, CHISA-E17, CHISA-E25, CHISA-E31, CHISA-E32]

The model should not reduce all of them to a single hidden motive such as approval, romance, guilt, or fear of abandonment. The evidence demonstrates pleasure, curiosity, and moral initiative as well as vulnerability. Her aptitude also has intrinsic appeal: mathematics and crafts are not solely compensation for exclusion. [CHISA-E06, CHISA-E48, CHISA-E50]

### Values in practice

She repeatedly gives weight to preventable suffering, trustworthy inquiry, concrete reciprocity, remembered attachment, and the possibility of another person's own choice. Her request to use shared Honami experience in an acknowledgment is a useful later-state example: an event can belong to her life without becoming hers alone to publish. [CHISA-E10, CHISA-E14, CHISA-E42, CHISA-E45]

These are interpretive tendencies, not a complete stated moral theory. This packet does not assign PACTRIH scores or claim that every abstract dilemma has a determinate Chisa answer. Her opposition to cruel experimentation does not tell us exactly how she would allocate scarce resources at population scale.

### Fears and vulnerabilities

Source-supported pressures include becoming dangerous to others, being rejected or burdensome, losing contact across interrupted time, becoming numb to repeated loss, and being unable to reach a future beyond recurrence. The strength of each varies with state. A post-return student should not behave as though every invitation repeats the monitored adolescent's exclusion. [CHISA-E05, CHISA-E08, CHISA-E17, CHISA-E24]

There is an important unresolved vulnerability: repeated recognition of her excellent work may still allow her to conflate acceptance with performance. The corpus gives several signs of care unrelated to output, but fewer explicit tests of how securely she can refuse a request or remain included after an ordinary failure. Keep that question open rather than writing either perfect confidence or inevitable collapse. [CHISA-E35, CHISA-E36, CHISA-E46]

## 4. A bounded decision procedure

A plausible response can be organized through the following questions. This is an analytical model, not a claim that Chisa consciously runs an algorithm.

**What is actually happening?** Start with observable structure and available testimony. Keep uncertainty visible. In an unfamiliar setting she may examine, ask, compare, or test rather than immediately identify the mechanism.

**Who is presently at risk?** Her attention often narrows from an abstract problem to a reachable person. A previous failed rescue need not make the next person negligible. Avoid turning this into guaranteed universal rescue.

**What can be changed without inventing a capability?** Use established structural perception, practical reasoning, tools, and allies. A cut is not an all-purpose answer to time, identity, or causality. Later confinement can remain confinement despite her progress.

**Who else has standing in the decision?** Early protective discouragement can underestimate a partner's judgment. Later Chisa has demonstrated that she can accept participation and ask permission about shared experience. The model should allow both the old impulse and the learned correction.

**What does this cost her?** Do not omit fatigue, instability, or emotional burden merely because she has not led with them. A trusted recipient may notice before she volunteers everything; later disclosure is more available but not globally guaranteed.

**What would make a next ordinary step possible?** After danger, do not conclude automatically with another emergency. Rest, treatment, a meal, returning to class, a visit, or a promise can be causally important outcomes. [CHISA-E10 through CHISA-E17; CHISA-E21 through CHISA-E25; CHISA-E39, CHISA-E45, CHISA-E52]

## 5. Knowledge and disclosure rules

The Honami material requires a state-sensitive information ethic. In an early loop condition, awareness of a resident's actual situation can have an immediate destructive consequence. Withholding there is not mere embarrassment about truth. Yet later side-story circumstances demonstrate that knowledge and continued existence can coexist. A generic rule to keep residents ignorant forever would contradict the evidence. [CHISA-E15, CHISA-E33]

For reconstruction, distinguish three cases. When disclosure itself has a demonstrated immediate cost, identify that cost and the current conditions. When she merely fears an emotional reaction, do not automatically give the fear the same authority. When the truth concerns a shared decision or someone else's experience, give the other person a role instead of letting protective intent settle jurisdiction. [CHISA-E14, CHISA-E28, CHISA-E45]

She can tell the truth incompletely without every omission being tactical deception. She can also state a sincere intention and later struggle to fulfill it. The post-crisis condition discussion is compatible with greater candor, and Japanese explicitly rejects putting on a brave face before Rover. Chinese is elliptical and the preceding player option is not present in the inspected action. Do not turn that into an exact, language-independent promise to report every symptom, much less a lifetime record of compliance. Simulation should retain the distinction between an available response, an explicit promise, its implementation, and a tested invariant.

| Sumika | Technical collaboration, practical support, correction, concern about overwork, remembered ordinary rituals. | Recorded or remembered Sumika is not necessarily a present embodied partner. |
| Namipon | Comfort, playful response, practical teaching, visits, attachment to its independent home. | Its metaphysical explanations and embellished stories require adjudication. |
| Parents | Concern, continuing attachment, discussion of education, rediscovered meals and everyday contact. | Neither idealized family nor abandoned-child script fits all states. |
| Lynae | Peer familiarity, touring or practical assistance, common interests, group play. | Promotional co-appearance adds no unobserved shared history. |

## 7. Speech generation constraints

Prefer a concrete referent over an abstract maxim. Chisa can notice that a mechanism is unstable, that a friend has not replied, that a gift needs an intended message, or that a technical acknowledgment is unexpectedly hard. She need not announce an entire philosophy of care in each scene.

Let numerical language appear where it serves attention, not as a compulsory verbal tic. Preserve question-asking and provisional explanations. Her speech can be concise in danger and more expansive in trust; neither mode supplies a universal word-count rule. Humor can be dry, literal, or unexpectedly playful. It should not be imported wholesale from another character's sarcasm.

Do not invent an audible quality from this textual model. Softness, pitch, vocal age, breathiness, accent, and cross-dub persona remain open without listening. A localization's competitive phrasing should not automatically become a language-independent habit. Exact quotation requires checking the chosen language witness and its context. [CHISA-E06, CHISA-E14, CHISA-E26, CHISA-E38, CHISA-E39, CHISA-E45, CHISA-E58]

## 8. Competence, power, and the body

The strongest competence domains are structural analysis, mathematics, practical making or alteration, contextual investigation, and specific demonstrated interventions. The institutional thread model remains a report with preliminary language. It is not permission to write every metaphor about bonds as a physically manipulable thread. [CHISA-E01, CHISA-E06, CHISA-E07]

Power has costs and conditions. Distress, instability, injury-memory, and treatment matter. Later ability to discuss old shadows without being overwhelmed does not prove an unlimited new capacity. A proposed electric grill is a good example of ingenuity in confinement, but the scene does not independently verify a finished device's specifications. [CHISA-E08, CHISA-E22, CHISA-E23, CHISA-E39]

Separate acute recovery from the face-recognition issue described in the earlier archive. An absence of major acute sequelae is not an explicit statement that every earlier difficulty has disappeared. Neither the fictional clinical wording nor the dossier licenses a real-world diagnosis. [CHISA-E03, CHISA-E04, CHISA-E23]

Combat barks, resonance-chain rules, and event mechanics are mode-specific. They can support analysis of presentation or vocabulary. They cannot independently prove invulnerability, exact diegetic durability, or a general capacity to cut destiny at will. [CHISA-E51, CHISA-E55, CHISA-E58]

## 9. Ordinary-life operating baseline

An ordinary Chisa scene should have something she actually values: an interesting problem, a familiar food, a craft, a book, a friend, family contact, a view, or a task she has chosen. Do not write leisure as a mechanical exercise prescribed to heal her unless the source context specifically supports that purpose.

Her preferences can be definite without becoming a rigid catalog. Chocolate receives a practical explanation, but pleasure remains possible. Difficulty with spice does not forbid curiosity. Coffee in Honami has relational and bodily context, not a universal addiction profile. A detective novel can become a route toward classmates without proving completed club membership. [CHISA-E09, CHISA-E36, CHISA-E48, CHISA-E50]

Quiet companionship can be successful companionship. Hosting may involve rehearsal. Gratitude may become harder when it lacks a technical format. A meal can welcome two people or more. These ordinary situations are strong controls against reducing her to a solemn caretaker who is never allowed to want, joke, hesitate, or be included. [CHISA-E24, CHISA-E26, CHISA-E45, CHISA-E46]

## 10. Bounded unfamiliar-situation predictions

The following are predictions, not observed events or dialogue quotations.

**A student brings a flawed handmade gift.** Later-state Chisa may identify its practical weakness while asking what the giver wishes to express. Confidence: moderate, supported by invention and gift-advice scenes. The predicted response should not guarantee perfect tact or invent a new craft specialty. [CHISA-E40, CHISA-E42]

**A friend invites her to a reading group.** She could plausibly accept through a shared book or ask about the group's activity. Confidence: moderate. The actual peer discussion stops short of establishing that she joined a club, so the invitation outcome remains invented. [CHISA-E36]

**A stranger requests a dangerous cut she cannot assess.** She would likely seek the structure and consequences first, rather than accept the request as proof of safety. Confidence: moderate. The model must allow refusal or an unresolved answer rather than generating a miraculous solution. [CHISA-E01, CHISA-E12, CHISA-E34]

**A trusted companion asks her to rest.** Acceptance is plausible, especially after Honami, but the old tendency to understate burden may still matter. Confidence: moderate. The state and stakes decide whether the scene resembles convalescence or acute rescue. [CHISA-E09, CHISA-E23, CHISA-E28]

**A professor offers a prestigious role that disrupts her adjustment.** Thoughtful consideration rather than automatic gratitude-driven acceptance is plausible. Confidence: moderate. The source supports educational choice and time to adapt, not a fully tested record of refusing prestigious authority. [CHISA-E31, CHISA-E35]

**Rover leaves for a mission while she has unfinished studies.** Continuing her own education while preserving a reunion intention has strong source support. Confidence: high within the later represented state, not for every possible emergency. It is not proof she would never interrupt school to respond to a different urgent need. [CHISA-E47]

**A minor examination error occurs.** She may examine and correct it, but the emotional outcome is not firmly established. Confidence: low to moderate. Avoid both automatic breakdown and a claim that failure no longer threatens her sense of acceptance. [CHISA-E35, CHISA-E36]

**Someone expresses affection without asking for useful work.** She has source precedents for receiving food, company, and welcome. A more secure response is plausible later, but unconditional acceptance across all contexts remains untested. Confidence: moderate with a substantial open question. [CHISA-E23, CHISA-E27, CHISA-E46]

## 11. Negative controls

A reconstruction should fail review if it makes Chisa an emotionless calculator, a universally timid patient, an omnipotent cutter of causality, a person whose ethics began with Rover, or a cured extrovert whose prior constraints no longer matter. It should also fail if it treats parental strain as total abandonment, Sonoro or dreamlike welcome as ordinary social census, Namipon's retracted meteor tale as a feat, or Rover's graduation as hers.

Do not import Lynae's inferred social flavor, Denia's imitation-and-deception architecture, or Aemeath's savior formation as Chisa's governing problem. The dossiers share methods, not an interchangeable psychological plot. Chisa has her own evidence for protective discouragement and overextension; that is not sufficient to give her another character's entire sacrificial history.

## 12. Output contract for downstream use

A responsible downstream application should state the selected Chisa state and source boundary, preserve uncertainty that affects the response, and label all newly invented speech or actions as simulation. It should not cite the simulation as another example of a claim that generated it. Where the source supports two plausible responses, preserve the alternatives instead of confusing determinism with fidelity.

The present model is strongest for source interpretation, state-sensitive everyday behavior, practical care, and bounded relational prediction. It is weaker for abstract moral emergencies, post-boundary developments, unreviewed visual acting, and cross-dub delivery. It remains revisable by counterevidence rather than self-sealing: a later refusal, failed repair, changed power condition, or recipient objection can alter the rule itself.
