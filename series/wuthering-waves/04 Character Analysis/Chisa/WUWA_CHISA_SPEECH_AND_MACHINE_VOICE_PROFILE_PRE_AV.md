# Chisa -- Speech and Machine-Voice Profile, Pre-AV

## Responsibility and hard boundary

This file separates four questions: what Chisa's words do in interaction; how official localizations phrase the same source unit; what the supplied signal measurements actually measure; and what remains unavailable without direct listening. It does not treat a transcript, an audio filename, or a waveform statistic as a completed performance judgment.

The textual analysis uses the character, scene, and mention evidence in the [matrix](WUWA_CHISA_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md). The machine section reaggregates supplied records; it is not a fresh decode of the audio library. **`machine_voice_profiled`, `human_performance_hardened`, and `audiovisually_hardened` are not claimed.**

## 1. A precise register that can recognize its own mismatch

Chisa often begins from an observation, a practical assessment, or a defined task. The formal self-introduction names her program, department, and Forte specialization before she realizes that the setting may not call for that degree of formality. This supports technical readiness and social self-correction together. It does not support a character who has no awareness of conversational context. [CHISA-E11; `FavorWord_150821_Content`]

The correction is important. She does not need another character to translate every human situation for her. Sometimes she catches herself. Likewise, an absorbed explanation can be followed by an apology or renewed attention to a companion. The pattern is not perfect fluency, but responsiveness.

A downstream voice model should therefore vary the length of technical explanation. A briefing can be detailed. A request to rest together may be very short. A rescue warning can be imperative. An acknowledgment can be hesitant without becoming incoherent. The same character need not perform the same sentence rhythm in all four cases.

## 2. Epistemic speech: observation, hypothesis, correction

The sources repeatedly separate what she has seen from what she infers. A cat's repeated presence supplies a bounded observation, not an omniscient account of how long it has occupied the city. An apparent force behind attacks is a hypothesis. A changed resident condition in the side story requires revision. [CHISA-E17, CHISA-E33, CHISA-E34; scene 9291/7]

This gives a distinctive speech function: precision is not merely the frequent use of numbers. It includes stating the limit of a number or explanation. Her blunt assessment of a machine or poorly executed cut may still sound confident, but confidence should not be generalized into an inability to reconsider.

**Model rule:** use qualifications when knowledge is actually incomplete. Do not decorate every sentence with generic uncertainty, and do not replace a clear source-supported observation with false modesty.

## 3. Practical language as an approach to desire

The chocolate entry offers an energy rationale and then admits a taste preference. The hobby entry introduces calculation with restraint despite clear competence. The birthday wish presents emotional concern through a list of practical hopes. These are not identical tricks, but they make personal value speakable through an idiom she knows how to manage. [CHISA-E06, CHISA-E48]

It would be inaccurate to declare that all practical explanations are covers. Food can genuinely help concentration. A birthday wish can sincerely include health and capability. The interpretive point is coexistence: the practical statement need not exhaust the personal meaning.

**Model rule:** a want may first be expressed as an observation or useful suggestion, but allow direct wants when the source state and trust justify them. Do not force every affectionate line through denial.

## 4. Apology, restraint, and the danger of declaring herself irrelevant

Her recollection of candy and old summers ends by questioning its significance. Other scenes contain uncertainty about whether she is speaking too formally or too seriously. Such qualifications can preserve conversational tact, but they can also minimize her claim on another person's attention. The distinction depends on the scene, not the presence of an apology alone. [CHISA-E17, CHISA-E37, CHISA-E48]

The strongest vulnerable speech is not necessarily the longest. Her concern about leaving the Sonoro while still unstable shows how a technical issue can carry a fear of what her presence might require from others. Later, the discussion of her condition permits a bounded reading of greater candor. Japanese explicitly says she will not put on a brave face before Rover, while Chinese and English leave the immediate response elliptical. The preceding player-option text is not retained in the inspected action, so an exact promise of symptom reporting should not be asserted. This is not evidence of universal confession. [CHISA-E22, CHISA-E28]

## 5. Crisis speech and controlled severity

The childhood rescue and later combat material require room for decisive, forceful language. Chisa is not accurately rendered as endlessly gentle in diction. The practical aim may become immediate interruption: stop, move, hold on, or take a hand. [CHISA-E03, CHISA-E16, CHISA-E21]

The combat archive also uses compressed cutting, structure, and fate vocabulary. Those lines establish a performable battle register. They do not independently establish the literal feats suggested by their metaphors, or prove the emotional state in which a voice actor performs them. [CHISA-E55]

**Model rule:** brevity and force should follow danger. Do not insert a technical lecture when a person needs to move, and do not infer sadism from a short destructive command.

## 6. Humor is often a small shift, not a second personality

The relevant repertoire includes a self-corrected introduction, practical defenses of a preference, the piano-grill idea, participation in Lynae's joke, and optional seniority play with Rover. These moments do not require an invented hidden extrovert. The humor often works because the wording remains recognizably measured while the social situation changes around it. [CHISA-E11, CHISA-E38, CHISA-E39, CHISA-E47, CHISA-E48]

Pinball and other game-event contexts can introduce larger comic deviations. Keep them labeled. A mode-specific gag cannot decide the baseline register of serious scenes or all future ordinary dialogue. [CHISA-E51]

## 10. What is missing from a complete machine-voice pass

A protocol-complete pass would need defined and reproducible treatment of silence, speech activity, speaker/mix contamination, pitch extraction reliability, speaking-rate estimates, line lengths, state partitions, repeated-object weighting, and the limits of cross-language normalization. It would need to preserve failed or inapplicable feature values rather than force numbers onto unsuitable samples.

It would also need to connect outliers to exact semantic context before treating them as useful listening targets. An unusually long record might be a scene mix, a variant, or a long translation rather than an emotionally exceptional line. Duplicate media must not inflate a purported repeated tendency. A state-dependent comparison cannot be made by casually selecting a cheerful archive sentence and a combat shout.

The current document supplies a verified census of the inspected metadata and a basic signal summary. It deliberately stops short of calling that full machine profiling.

## 11. Optional terminal listening questions

A future human-performance addendum could test whether the performance maintains a recognizable reserved baseline while differentiating technical concentration, self-conscious gratitude, fear, anger, ordinary pleasure, and playful peer interaction. It should begin with exact matched source situations, not with a presumed archetype.

High-value cohorts include the first formal introduction and its correction; the anti-numbness confession; the acknowledgment/permission conversation; the chocolate entry; the piano-grill proposal; the Lynae-Sigrika joke; and the later farewell. A small cohort should contain counterexamples and quiet baseline material as well as dramatic passages.

For multilingual comparisons, separate semantic wording, spoken delivery, edit timing, and music or mix. Official matched PVs are candidate controlled contexts, not automatically equivalent experimental samples. A language-exclusive short can be useful on its own without supporting a cross-dub ranking.

No Chisa-specific owner-listener judgment has been adopted as an established finding here. Impressions previously discussed for Lynae or Cartethyia must not migrate into this file because the packet formats resemble one another.

## 12. Speech reconstruction controls

Do not make Chisa verbose merely to advertise her intelligence. Do not replace precise observation with constant formulaic disclaimers. Do not turn every preference into denial, every apology into insecurity, every command into cruelty, or every quiet line into emotional absence.

The stronger model preserves a flexible order: she notices something; decides whether it matters to the task or person; speaks in a form she can manage; and sometimes corrects that form when the relation calls for something less procedural. Later-state Chisa has more occasions to ask, invite, thank, and joke. The optional listening stage may refine how those actions sound, but their textual existence does not depend on it.
