## 4. A low-friction collection order

Begin with an intact recording of families A and B. They should cover many central targets and supply scene context that short edits remove. Then add the paired disclosure test in C and the contrasting group-play/confinement material in D. The later ecology requests are worth collecting even though they may be less spectacular: the thesis acknowledgment and family lunch are especially useful controls on a crisis-only reconstruction.

A few missing ecology scenes need not block every AV conclusion. Record which claims remain dependent on text and which have direct visual corroboration. Conversely, collecting an attractive PV does not close a missing narrative target with a different analytical responsibility.

When a candidate is found, verify the script anchor and nearby speaker exchange, record the exact parent video, and mark a bounded span. The same scene can be represented by several uploads; those are multiple captures, not multiple diegetic occurrences. Record repeated footage and alternative player choices rather than silently accumulating them as independent evidence.
