## Major relationships

### Rover — witness, rescuer, chosen companion, and future counterforce

Evidence density: **dense, multi-arc, multiple source types**. Story, archive/favor, later civic material, and affinity-sensitive lines all contribute; romantic status remains state/choice sensitive.

The relationship begins in mutual identity uncertainty: Rover is “the Laureate”/Swordbearer and Cartethyia is unsure whose Resonator she is. Rover does more than reassure her. When Cartethyia asks for a merger followed by annihilation, Rover refuses the requested ending and saves both the person who wants to live and the self that has lost hope. This becomes Cartethyia's model of care: later she promises that if Rover ever reaches an analogous crisis, she will defy Rover's wishes, defeat them, and save them (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_141_20`).

Trust becomes reciprocal but not perfectly transparent. Cartethyia names herself Rover's blade, shares ordinary private wishes, and asks for companionship. Yet in the Leviathan plan she withholds the full strategy and accepts Rover's experience of her apparent death as a cost. That is counterevidence to an uncomplicated “total openness” model.

Dimensions: very high trust; high intimacy; mutual rescue; pronounced protective reciprocity; some asymmetry because Rover is repeatedly positioned as rescuer/Arbiter; later strategic secrecy; playful challenge after integration.

### Fleurdelys — not an alter ego to discard, but wounded continuity to integrate

Evidence density: **dense, identity-defining, concentrated in the Avinoleum arc plus archive/profile evidence**. This is a relation-to-self/form, not an ordinary interpersonal relationship.

Fleurdelys is simultaneously a name/form, a public role, and the despairing part of the same person. Cartethyia first distinguishes the part that wants continuation from the part that wants self-destruction, then asks to reunite them so “I” can become whole. The story rejects both total separation and simple restoration of the old role. After integration she chooses the name Cartethyia because it is how Rover first knew her, while retaining Fleurdelys' memories and controllable form.

The relationship-to-self therefore moves from uncertainty, through ethical negotiation between survival and release, to integration without institutional reinstatement.

### Imperator — genuine bond, misused faith, and inherited ideal

Evidence density: **moderate, multi-source but largely retrospective**. The ideals and grief are clear; ordinary reciprocal interaction is comparatively under-shown.

Cartethyia's faith is not exposed as merely fake. The Threnodian exploits institutional faith and the image of divinity, but Imperator's acts and ideals remain meaningful to her. After Divinity is gone, she tells Phoebe that faith was shaped by people's work and could unite them while also driving liberation (`Main_Rinascita_2_12_7401_5`–`7401_9`). She keeps memory of the Sentinel without preserving divine rule.

Dimensions: devotion, gratitude, grief, prior deference, later interpretive independence. This is a transformation of faith into human responsibility, not a conversion from belief to cynicism.

### Leviathan — creator, exploiter, and object of ethical reversal

Evidence density: **dense antagonist evidence across two major arcs**. Causal and symbolic relations are strong; interpersonal reciprocity is not applicable.

Leviathan intended her continued existence to threaten others. Cartethyia's answer is not to deny the origin but to reverse its purpose: the “puppet” becomes the prison of its maker. In the later arc she repeats the reversal at greater scale by making her humanity an anchor inside Leviathan. The enemy defines her conditions of birth, not the moral meaning of her life.

### Cantarella — debt, shame, kinship, and delayed repair

Evidence density: **moderate and materially unresolved**. Assistance, guilt, and institutional kinship are clear across the Avinoleum and later evidence, but direct repair is promised more often than shown.

Cartethyia regards Cantarella as the “true Blessed Maiden” harmed by the false institution built around Cartethyia. She repeatedly worries about apologizing and defers a direct meeting. Cantarella nevertheless aids the Avinoleum rescue and later safeguards Cartethyia's body. Their relationship is morally important precisely because it is not frictionless: gratitude and solidarity coexist with guilt and avoidance.

Evidence: `FavorWord_140913_Content`; `Main_Linaxita_2_4_29_*`; `…_140_39`–`140_41`; later `Main_Rinascita_2_11_16_31`.

### Galbrena — trusted co-conspirator whose violence is deliberately misread

Evidence density: **thin_but_material, one later-arc operation**. Extreme operational trust is explicit; everyday closeness and emotional intimacy are not established.

Galbrena appears to kill Cartethyia, but the primary dialogue reveals a joint plan. Cartethyia thanks Galbrena for choosing misunderstanding and hatred and offers herself as Galbrena's “first bullet.” After returning, Cartethyia turns the episode into potential Carnevale material. Trust is operational and severe: each accepts that public/relational appearances must be sacrificed to the plan.

The compound `WhoId` 1633 must remain compound evidence. It supports coordination, not automatic sole attribution.

### Lupa — energetic peer and uncomplicated acceptance

Evidence density: **single-arc, bounded but useful peer evidence**. It is sufficient to demonstrate a less hierarchical social register, not a broad lifetime relationship model.

Cartethyia values that Lupa does not recoil from her nature or power. Their Septimont partnership allows Cartethyia to act as a wandering knight rather than a sacred figure. Cartethyia playfully claims the older-sister role, while Lupa's directness prevents excessive solemnity. This relationship is a strong witness for peer behavior outside Rover-centered intimacy.

Evidence: `FavorWord_140917_Content`; `StoryReview_Lupa_BPart_3_Content`; `StoryReview_Rover_BPart_0_Content`.

### Phoebe — respected faith under conditions of dangerous truth

Evidence density: **thin_but_material, thematically clear**. The evidence strongly supports religious/institutional tension but not broad everyday intimacy.

Cartethyia respects Phoebe's sincere faith and initially hesitates to reveal truths that might destabilize her. After Leviathan, she reframes faith for Phoebe as confidence in deeds and ideals rather than obedience. The relationship shows Cartethyia's tension between honesty and protective withholding.

Evidence: `FavorWord_140914_Content`; `Main_Rinascita_2_12_7401_5`–`7401_9`.

### Haultir/Cadanto and the fallen defenders — loyalty, mourning, and unfinished duty

Evidence density: **moderate, concentrated in archive and one memory-heavy arc**. Grief and obligation are strong; the surviving record offers limited ordinary interaction.

Haultir is remembered as a companion whose heart, though born from lies, repeatedly chose rescue. Cadanto takes a mortal blow for Cartethyia and later returns corrupted. More broadly, the residual defenders in Avinoleum are not anonymous casualties to her: she sings farewell, worries she lacks the right to mourn them, then turns grief into a promise of real victory.

Evidence: `FavorWord_140911_Content`; `Main_Linaxita_2_4_106_5`–`106_30`; `…_10_14`.

### Rinascita and the Order — belonging without ownership

Evidence density: **dense across profile, archive, and multiple arcs**. This is a civic/institutional relationship and should not be scored as if it were interpersonal intimacy.

Cartethyia cannot simply leave Rinascita behind: her power is unstable at distance and her moral identity is bound to its people. But she refuses to equate responsibility with entitlement to rule. After Leviathan she declines the Primus role, admits she lacks patience for civic office, and nonetheless commits to correct the Order's wrongs (`Main_Rinascita_2_12_743_14`–`743_17`).

This relationship supplies the central contradiction of her freedom: she is “unfettered” only in the sense that service is chosen, not absent.

## Interaction rules supported by the evidence

- With strangers or dependents, she defaults to courteous aid, safety checks, and practical questions before self-disclosure.
- With trusted companions, formality loosens into teasing, requests for company, food/dance invitations, and visible embarrassment.
- With institutions, she distinguishes the value that people invested in a symbol from the authority claimed by the institution.
- Under existential threat, she becomes direct and operational, but her first solution is too often self-instrumentalization.
- When challenged playfully by a trusted peer, provocation can energize rather than alienate her (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_141_28`).
- When confronted with moral uncertainty, she prefers painful knowledge to protective ignorance.

## Unresolved relationship questions

- The exact long-term repair between Cartethyia and Cantarella is promised more often than shown.
- The later Galbrena plan proves extreme trust but not whether Cartethyia fully considered the relational harm of secrecy.
- Archive intimacy with Rover is affinity-gated and should not be treated as a mandatory romance state.
- There is insufficient WavesLine coverage to model her asynchronous-chat behavior independently from spoken dialogue.
