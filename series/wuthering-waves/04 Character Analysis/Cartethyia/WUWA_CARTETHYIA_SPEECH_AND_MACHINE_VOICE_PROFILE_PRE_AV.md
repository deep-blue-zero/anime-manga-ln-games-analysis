# Cartethyia: Speech and Bounded Machine-Voice Profile

## 1. What has and has not been reviewed

This artifact has three layers: source-facing speech analysis; selective comparison of the four official textual witnesses; and new aggregates computed from the supplied basic acoustic measurements. No raw audio was newly listened to, no actor intention is inferred, and no emotion detector was used. The machine layer is a reproducible reaggregation of retained measurements, not a new full signal-extraction run. (E47-E50, E59.)

The profile does **not** claim `machine_voice_profiled` under the full machine-voice protocol. Pitch distributions, pause segmentation, text-normalized rate, reliability-controlled spectral measures, state-conditioned normalization, and cluster stability analysis are not supplied by this pass. A source table labeled acoustic observations 'complete' is complete for its own basic schema, not automatically for the later broader protocol.

Human listening remains an optional terminal enrichment phase. Its absence does not invalidate the textual speech model. It does mean that words such as girlish, deep, wistful, flirtatious, or commanding cannot be presented as this analyst's verified observations of the recording.

## 2. Her speech is not one ceremonial register

Cartethyia can explain danger, ask a practical question, invite someone to eat, tease a trusted companion, hesitate before an apology, express irritation at institutional rules, or adopt elevated knightly language. The central pattern is not uniform formality. It is a changing relation among **role, exposure, purpose, and recipient**. (E14, E20-E24, E35, E41, E44-E49.)

In a public or operational context, sentences can become compact statements of mechanism and resolve. In an ordinary trusted context, the source often turns toward concrete things: taste, a place, an activity, a view. When she fears a person's judgment, speech can become hesitant or indirect. These claims concern textual construction and conversational sequence, not observed vocal timbre.

Her knightly idiom also extends into play. A low-stakes challenge can receive a grand response without becoming an actual life oath. This is an important negative control for simulation: do not strip the idiom from ordinary life, but do not interpret every use literally at maximum stakes.

## 4. Effortful composure is source-explicit

The archive acknowledges that maintaining Fleurdelys's composed manner and tone requires effort. This is unusually useful evidence because it locates performance within Cartethyia's own self-description. It licenses a hypothesis that the public form is more deliberately regulated. It does not license a claim that the actor's lower pitch has been heard or measured, nor that the form is fake. (E47.)

The appropriate analytical question is what becomes harder or easier in that register. Does it help her reassure others, issue commands, endure observation, or keep private uncertainty out of view? Text can nominate such functions. Actual clips are needed to characterize tempo, placement, articulation, breath, dynamic range, or the moment of a vocal transition.

A future listening pass should also test counterexamples: formally worded warmth, playful Fleurdelys, serious Cartethyia, and private speech that remains dignified. Without them, a Maiden/Knight dichotomy would simply reproduce the sampling design.

## 5. Multilingual textual checks that materially affect interpretation

### 5.1 The name choice

Chinese and Japanese explicitly connect the preferred name with the name she introduced at the first meeting. The English line emphasizes how Rover first knew her. None establishes a name invented by Rover. The archive removes residual ambiguity by using Cartethyia before the sacred naming. The corrected claim is not a dub preference; it is a cross-source identity finding. (E03, E07-E08, E20.)

### 5.2 Wanting gentleness versus offering an aphorism

At `katixiya-shengtai_3_7`, the Chinese moves from wanting to be treated gently toward treating others gently. Japanese similarly retains the paired personal wishes. English renders a more generalized proposition about those who seek and give kindness. That difference can shift whether the listener notices her own need. The dossier preserves the first-person desire recoverable in the primary witness rather than portraying her as an exclusively selfless giver. (E28.)

### 5.3 Happiness beside grief

At `MAIN_YGDX_37_19`, English says she is at peace; Chinese emphasizes feeling happy at this moment. The surrounding scene and narration retain loss. The packet therefore uses 'genuinely values this outcome' rather than 'has fully resolved her grief.' This is a scope correction, not a claim that one localization is globally less accurate. (E23.)

### 5.4 Suitability for civic office

At `Main_Rinascita_2_12_743_15`, English emphasizes never having had the patience for civic duties. Chinese states that handling city affairs is beyond what she can manage well and that others are better suited; Japanese and Korean also emphasize capacity or suitability. A broad personality claim that she is impatient with all administration would exceed the primary wording. The stable claim is self-limited competence and refusal of this office while retaining responsibility for repair. (E41.)

### 5.5 Effort is not automatically literal sacrifice

`FavorWord_140929_Content` has a stronger sacrificial surface in English than the Chinese effort-oriented wording. It is not counted as a separate literal self-sacrifice event. A simulation trained only on the English phrase could overproduce martyr-like language. The actual sacrifice argument rests on the explicit narrative proposals and their context, not on this localization shortcut. (E50.)

### 5.6 A line that needs witness-level reconciliation

At `Main_Linaxita_2_4_140_2`, the supplied Chinese and Japanese contrast the present gentle expression with the earlier fight, whereas the English wording points toward gentleness during the fight. This appears to reverse the temporal comparison in the materialized witnesses. The dossier does not use that English sentence to infer a distinctive emotional event during combat. A later runtime subtitle/clip check should adjudicate the discrepancy before it supports a facial-expression or relationship-timing claim. (E20.)

### 5.7 Cooking and agency

In the restored-meal scene, the Chinese explicitly offers to borrow the kitchen and prepare another serving, while one English line instead says she will ask the tavern keeper to prepare something. Nearby text and the broader cooking evidence support her own participation. The packet does not let that single translated line erase the practical competence established elsewhere. This is another reason to compare the local sequence rather than quote a line without its neighbors. (E06, E23.)

The below-threshold feature is the supplied `silence_fraction_below_minus_50_dbfs_20ms`. It is a thresholded signal measure, not a count of meaningful hesitations. RMS is not emotional intensity. Total duration is recording duration, not verified voiced duration or articulation time. Text length, language, mixing, non-speech content, and source class all affect these values.

The sum of within-language unique counts is 3,018, not 2,937. This is not a discrepancy: 27 PCM objects appear in all four language groupings, adding 81 cross-language memberships. Their matching bytes do not establish that four actors independently delivered the same sound. They are shared retained objects; their acoustic content has not been classified by listening here. They should not serve as independent cross-dub performance evidence.

Global deduplication gives approximately 300.06 minutes of retained recording duration. Again, this includes whatever is present in the stored objects and is not a claim of five hours of isolated Cartethyia speech.

## 8. Source-class and channel cautions

The archive includes short combat and ordinary barks alongside much longer reflective lines. Its median durations are around two seconds even though some entries approach or exceed a minute. Story-line medians are roughly five seconds. A comparison that treats the archive median as her conversational pace would be invalid.

The longest archive entries differ by language. The main long-form candidates include `FavorWord_140904_Content`, `140912_Content`, and `140918_Content`; English also places `140910_Content` among its longest. These are useful retrieval targets, not evidence that one performance is more emotionally complex.

Of the 2,937 unique objects, 2,861 are mono, 52 stereo, and 24 have five channels. The five-channel associations occur in the `MAIN_RYAM_63_1`, `64_1`, `65_1` and `Main_Linaxita_2_4_131_1`, `131_4` families, including runtime variants. This pass does not assert a spatial layout or isolated-speaker channel. Do not automatically downmix under an assumed layout. A single semantic association can point to a scene-like or mixed object, so equality of text keys does not guarantee equivalent acoustic isolation across languages.

## 9. Reproduction recipe and its boundary

The local audit used Python standard-library JSON parsing, grouping by canonical PCM hash, summing supplied duration, and calculating medians with `statistics.median`. Inputs are the exact eight source files identified and hashed in the packet README, especially the full voice-line file and coverage audit.

A reproducing analyst should flatten each line's `renders`, retain `voice_language`, `record_class`, `text_key`, `canonical_pcm_sha256`, and `machine_acoustic_observations`, then deduplicate within the requested grouping. Cross-language intersections must be reported separately. Before extending into pitch or rate, recover the raw objects through their exact provenance rather than inferring identity from filenames.

A full machine pass should add validated extraction with disclosed tool versions, per-channel quality assessment, voice/activity segmentation, robust within-language baselines, source-class and state partitions, matched-semantic comparisons, and explicit unreliable-feature exclusions. None of those future operations is represented as completed here.

## 11. Owner-listener hypotheses, retained without promotion

**H-JP-01, OPEN, source: owner report in this conversation.** Japanese Cartethyia is perceived as brighter and more girlish, with Fleurdelys shifting toward a deeper, controlled martial register. The Saber/warrior-general comparison is a listener's shorthand, not evidence of imitation or casting intention.

**H-EN-01, OPEN, source: owner report in this conversation.** English is perceived as maintaining a narrower state distance: both modes remain knightly, with one brighter and the other more elevated. This is not a verified ranking of quality or fidelity.

The useful test is within-dub separation across matched states, followed by between-dub comparison. Perceived age, formality, warmth, spontaneity, and command must be recorded separately rather than collapsed into one maturity score. Seriousness must be tested in Cartethyia form and warmth in Fleurdelys form. A result restricted to promotional editing should remain promotional and language-specific.

No human-performance addendum is instantiated as completed. These hypotheses are preserved so later review can test them rather than losing the owner's observation or silently treating it as canon.

## 12. Speech-model constraints

A faithful generated response should identify the concrete situation before reaching for solemnity. It should allow gratitude without submission, a question without helplessness, a joke without triviality, and a command without changing lore identity. It should not write all English as archaic court speech because an in-world play uses that style. It should not make Japanese social coding the language-independent character essence.

The strongest current result is textual: Cartethyia's speech provides multiple ways of negotiating what others may expect from her. The performed result remains open. That boundary preserves both the usefulness of this profile and the possibility that actual voice will materially alter it.
