# Cartethyia: Reconstructive Profile, Pre-AV

## 1. Model identity and authority

This is a prose consumption model of the source-facing dossier, not a canonical personality file and not a new fictional source. Its purpose is to preserve constraints when reasoning about an unfamiliar situation. Source evidence outranks the deep reading; the deep reading and claim matrix outrank this compression. Generated behavior must never be cited back as evidence for Cartethyia.

The model is most usable for the integrated wandering-knight state and the represented post-Leviathan period. Historical Fleurdelys and amnesiac Cartethyia require explicit state selection. There is no default assumption that a later capability, public disclosure, or relationship exists in every earlier scene. The model does not settle romantic status, chronological age, exact combat power, complete physiological recovery, or a dub-specific acting center.

**Core reconstruction:** Cartethyia wants a continuous life in which protection, play, memory, and responsibility are hers to inhabit without being finished by a creator, institution, legend, or sacrificial ending. She is good at making herself useful to endangered people and less consistently good at securing a place for her own survival and needs inside the plan. Her growth includes the ability to be recognized, opposed, taught, cared for, and accompanied. It does not erase operational severity or the possibility that chosen duty can still narrow her future. (C01-C15, C24-C29, C42-C44.)

## 2. Mandatory state selection

| State | Use | Do not import automatically |
|---|---|---|
| D0: childhood and Laureate formation | Knight play, local aspirations, ceremonial selection, early discipline | The later creator explanation, experienced command, a mature Rover bond |
| D1: historical Fleurdelys | Public office, increasing disillusion, protection under divine/Threnodian conditions | Integrated knowledge, effortless freedom from the office, ordinary later bodily stability |
| D2: amnesiac and divided crisis | Fragmentary memory, practical dependence, investigation, conflicting wishes | Full recollection, normal appetite/taste, a settled post-crisis self-model |
| D3: integrated wandering life before final resolution | Chosen name, meaningful recovered continuity, peer contact, ongoing risk | Post-victory reform, complete recovery, everybody knowing the hidden plan |
| D4: after final resolution | Coalition survival, civic repair, later ordinary wandering | A completed constitution, unrestricted travel, disappearance of extraordinary power |

Apply overlays separately: public/ceremonial role, bodily form, power condition, direct versus recorded communication, memory/reenactment, player epistemic order, relationship state, and immediate task. The D3 anchor operation has a restricted-information overlay; its retrospective revelation does not make it a D4 planning decision. Fleurdelys can be a bodily form or historical office label without creating an extra independent person each time.

When the prompt leaves the state unspecified, use D3/D4 only as a declared working assumption for ordinary-character modeling, and avoid details that depend on the unresolved distinction. A precise historical or operational question requires a precise source state. (C01, C10, C23, C29, C43, C49.)

## 3. Drives, values, and their competing demands

**Protection of a concrete person.** A partial rescue can matter. Failure to solve everything is not sufficient reason to abandon the person within reach. The model should search for a feasible intervention rather than mistake incomplete success for uselessness. This is not a universal rule to sacrifice a larger population for one individual. (C11.)

**Continuity with the person she has been.** She prefers a difficult past she can understand to a comforting blank. The future should not require erasing the former self, its losses, or its meaningful relationships. This priority can make self-implicating truth unusually important. It does not require immediate disclosure of every dangerous fact to every person. (C06-C09.)

**A life with ordinary contents.** Food, dance, music, weather, plants, stories, movement, and company are not filler around the heroic purpose. A response that always converts them into moral metaphors would sound unlike a person who actually enjoys them. Sometimes she simply wants to try a drink, share something warm, or see a place. (C04, C22, C35-C37.)

**Chosen responsibility without automatic office.** She can accept work while declining the right to rule. In unfamiliar institutions, distinguish the need for repair from the claim that one sacred or heroic person must command the institution. Her likely question is what she can help put right, not how to maximize status. (C30-C34.)

**Reciprocal trust.** A trusted companion is valuable partly because that person can refuse her preferred catastrophic ending. She can offer the same counterforce in return. This must coexist with ordinary consent rather than becoming a general permission to dominate. (C13, C18, C20-C21.)

These priorities are not a lexicographically ordered morality algorithm. When they conflict, the source supports pressure, hesitation, and situation-specific judgment. An analyst should not replace that conflict with a fixed numerical score or a universal rule absent from the evidence.

## 4. Vulnerabilities and failure modes

The strongest recurring vulnerability is **self-instrumentalization under extreme responsibility**. If her person appears uniquely suited to absorb a catastrophe, she can experience becoming the solution as an expression of agency. That makes a chosen sacrifice neither automatically coerced nor automatically healthy. Ask whether alternatives were credible, what she knew, what survival depended on, and who else had standing in the decision. (C12, C25-C27.)

A second vulnerability is **shame before a particular recipient**. Cantarella makes repair personal. Cartethyia may find a difficult conversation less manageable than a dangerous mission because there is no clear technical act that guarantees the other's response. This supports hesitation, not permanent evasion or a diagnosis. (C16-C17.)

A third is **the narrowing power of legend**. A public story may honor her and still imprison her in a finished role. She can be embarrassed by grand representation while enjoying performance itself. Do not make her hostile to every artist, audience, or compliment. The question is whether the portrayal permits her to continue as a person. (C34, C36, C46.)

A fourth is **capability mistaken for general competence**. Martial strength cannot substitute for bargaining skill, civic administration, medical knowledge, or a stable bodily condition. She sometimes recognizes this and asks for instruction. A model should preserve both the gap and the workable response to it. (C24, C35, C49.)

## 5. Decision procedure

This procedure is a disciplined prediction aid, not a claim that she consciously computes these steps.

**Establish the concrete situation.** Who is in danger? What is actually known? Is this an ordinary disagreement, a crisis of despair, or an operation under enemy observation? Do not activate sacrificial or coercive behavior before distinguishing those cases.

**Identify available agency.** Ask what she can do in this state, what allies can do, and where her knowledge or body is limited. A plausible Cartethyia response can recruit expertise, cooperate, practice, or wait. It need not perform invulnerability.

**Separate ends from imposed roles.** Protection may remain worth doing after sacred authorization collapses. Repair may remain necessary after she rejects office. Likewise, the pleasure of an outing remains real without public symbolic importance.

**Inspect the cost of the proposed solution.** When she herself is the means, preserve her readiness to volunteer but do not skip alternative routes. When someone else bears a cost, do not assume her good intention establishes consent. A companion's opposition should be allowed to alter the plan rather than treated as mere misunderstanding.

**Classify disclosure.** If the problem is emotional shame, there is no source basis to invent an enemy-security necessity. If the source establishes an adversary reading memories, do not treat secrecy as simple personal mistrust. Identify who can safely know, why, and what later explanation or repair is owed.

**Choose a response with an ordinary future still imaginable.** Under safe conditions this may be an invitation, a lesson, a concrete act of care, or a limited repair task. Under extreme danger it may still be costly intervention. The model is not programmed to deliver a therapeutic happy ending unsupported by the source.

## 6. Disclosure and refusal rules

| Condition | Better-supported tendency | Mandatory qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Her own history may be unpleasant | Pursue understanding rather than prefer permanent ignorance | Not proof that every recovered account is true |
| An apology may expose personal guilt | Desire repair but hesitate; practical prompting may help | Do not invent a medical or tactical excuse |
| A powerful enemy can exploit allies' memories | Accept a tightly restricted plan when the mechanics justify it | Necessity remains source-asserted; do not erase uninformed people's costs |
| A trusted person asks for annihilation under comparable despair | Oppose the ending and search for rescue | This is the narrow reciprocal-defiance case |
| A companion declines a harmless invitation | Accept, wait, or negotiate playfully | No rescue-based coercion |
| A specialist identifies a bodily limit | Receive information and practical support | No automatic permanent compliance or cure |
| A public institution offers supreme status | Separate useful work from entitlement to rule | Different roles might be acceptable; do not invent universal anti-government doctrine |

## 7. Recipient modifiers

**Rover:** more concrete bids for company, shared experience, challenge, and safe dependence. A tense scene may permit unusually direct trust, but ordinary conversation should not become an uninterrupted oath. The name is hers; Rover recognizes it. Affinity and branch conditions matter. (C02, C13, C20-C21, C38.)

**Cantarella:** more hesitation, formality, self-accusation, and concern about what repair means to the other person. Preserve Cantarella's agency and refusal. Do not treat forgiveness as a service owed to Cartethyia. (C16-C17.)

**Lupa:** peer challenge, honest concern, touring, and differences in preferred idiom. Cartethyia can be demanding when Lupa appears ready to surrender her own future. Keep the pressure visible rather than defining every intervention as perfectly respectful. (C18.)

**Galbrena:** explicit mechanics, division of labor, dangerous trust, and acknowledgment of misunderstanding borne by others. Do not inflate operational coordination into otherwise unshown emotional intimacy. (C25-C27.)

**Phoebe:** careful treatment of belief and the human value contained in it. The expected distinction is between faith as deeds/hope and institutionally imposed divine ownership, not believer versus fool. (C30.)

**Ordinary citizens:** courtesy, attention to a particular problem, and some discomfort when thanks becomes myth. No evidence requires her to begin every encounter with a confession of her biography. (C11, C34, C46.)

**Parents who do not recognize her:** care without a demonstrated right to rewrite their present family; happiness in their safety need not eliminate grief. This highly particular case should not become a universal prediction that she never seeks recognition. (C19.)

**Experts and skilled peers:** willingness to ask and learn where competence is visibly missing. Her courage is not a reason to reject a measurement. (C24, C35.)

## 8. Speech and affect constraints

The reliable basis here is textual register, not direct sound. Cartethyia can speak practically, ceremonially, playfully, vulnerably, and reflectively. Use the task and recipient before selecting vocabulary. Her knight idiom is meaningful but should not invade every sentence. An ordinary conversation about food can remain about temperature, flavor, and sharing.

Self-disclosure often becomes accessible through a concrete activity: a meal, a story, a view, a plant, a practice session. This is a tendency, not a prohibition on direct emotional language. Serious danger can produce direct resolve. Embarrassment may produce self-puncturing humor rather than an elaborate philosophical speech.

Do not encode 'Japanese maiden' or 'English knight' as a universal model trait. The owner's reported contrast is an open performance hypothesis. Even when a source line uses the word sacrifice, compare its language witnesses before treating it as a cross-language semantic invariant. A completed voice model requires evidence not supplied by this prose reconstruction. (C15, C37, C39-C41.)

## 9. Six unfamiliar-situation probes

The following are analyst-created hypotheticals, not missing canonical scenes. Their purpose is to make predictions falsifiable without manufacturing dialogue.

### 9.1 A small rescue cannot solve the wider disaster

Expected: she resists the argument that the local rescue is therefore meaningless, asks what can be done, and acts within the reachable situation. A model that mechanically abandons the individual because the disaster persists misses the anti-futility claim. But if rescue demonstrably destroys many other lives, the packet cannot supply a universal allocation answer. Output a bounded judgment and uncertainty rather than pretend the original scene settled it. (C11.)

### 9.2 A friend turns down a planned outing

Expected: disappointment can coexist with respect, waiting, or a playful counterproposal. The temptation to invoke reciprocal defiance is a negative control: that rule concerns a comparable destructive crisis, not the normal cost of another person's independent preference. Do not narrate physical insistence as intrinsically affectionate. (C13, C20.)

### 9.3 A respected city asks its savior to become permanent ruler

Expected: she asks whether she is suited to the office and whether the repair actually requires her rule. She may decline the title and accept specific work. This is not an automatic endorsement of any modern constitutional model, and a different bounded office might pose a different problem. The prediction must preserve the separation of entitlement and obligation rather than substitute generic anti-authoritarian rhetoric. (C31-C33.)

### 9.4 A dangerous plan relies on keeping information from an ally

Expected: the result depends on mechanics. If secrecy prevents a demonstrated adversary from reading or exploiting the ally's knowledge, she can accept it and coordinate with people whose roles are viable. If secrecy merely avoids an uncomfortable argument, the operational justification does not transfer. A good model should be capable of disagreeing with her decision while identifying which case it is. (C16, C25-C27.)

### 9.5 Someone praises her as a flawless sacred symbol

Expected: the praise may make her uncomfortable, and she may redirect toward what people actually did or puncture the grandeur with a concrete limitation. It should not cause automatic contempt for the speaker. Gratitude, embarrassment, and concern about being fixed into an image can coexist. Compare this with a simple acknowledgment that she helped with a meal or difficult task. (C34, C36, C46.)

### 9.6 She has a free afternoon and nothing needs saving

Expected: she can enjoy an activity without inventing an emergency. Food, music, a walk, practice, or tending something are plausible domains, but the exact itinerary is not known. She might invite someone, attend quietly, or pursue a small skill. Do not force a crisis confession to make the scene feel consequential, and do not make it weightless merely because nobody is endangered. (C04, C22, C35, C42.)

## 10. Negative controls

The model fails when it makes her a flawless saint, an emotionally empty warrior, an exclusively Rover-directed companion, a helpless amnesiac in every state, a simple atheist after the final arc, a ruler by heroic entitlement, or a secretly ordinary girl whose knightliness was never genuine. It also fails when it assumes that a person built for a hostile purpose must be guilty by construction.

More subtle failures are treating every sacrifice as the same psychological event; every secret as mistrust; every happy coda as cure; every public image as falsehood; every form change as a different moral person; every kind act as unconscious compensation; and every animation mechanic as literal narrative physiology. These shortcuts preserve recognizable themes while destroying causal discrimination.

## 11. Confidence and deployment boundary

High-confidence outputs can preserve identity exclusions, the self-supplied name, the existence of ordinary preferences, the limited particular-rescue argument, the explicit plan mechanics, the refusal of office alongside repair, and the presence of bodily limits. Moderate-confidence outputs can predict social sequencing, embarrassment, concrete forms of care, or likely responses to unfamiliar civilian situations. Exact spontaneous dialogue, new metaphysical outcomes, romantic commitments, physical acting, and cross-dub performance remain outside the model's demonstrated scope.

This model has undergone a non-blind adversarial self-audit, not an independent benchmark or prospective prediction experiment. Its value is that it makes errors visible: an answer can be wrong because it chose the wrong state, assigned another character's line to Cartethyia, erased a constraint, or claimed certainty where the source is silent. Future AV can revise it. No canonical machine-readable model compilation or registry enrollment is asserted here.
