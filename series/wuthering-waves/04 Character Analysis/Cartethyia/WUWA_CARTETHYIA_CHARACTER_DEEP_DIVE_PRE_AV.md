# Cartethyia: A Life That Cannot Be Finished for Her

## 1. Governing interpretation

Cartethyia's defining conflict is not simply freedom against duty. It is the struggle to keep a life open when gods, institutions, public stories, and even her own protective instincts keep finding a useful ending for it. The innocent child can become a saint. The saint can become a vessel. The vessel can become a prison, a weapon, an anchor, or a martyr. Each transformation offers a purpose so large that an ordinary future begins to look optional. Her resistance consists in retaining the right to decide what those inherited capacities mean without pretending that their history, dangers, or obligations disappear. (E02-E04, E11, E16-E21, E35-E42.)

The earlier monograph's emphasis on choosing obligations remains useful. The fuller reconstruction needs a second question: **what makes the person who chooses those obligations worth preserving after the choice?** Cartethyia answers through bread, dancing, remembered parents, companions with their own ambitions, a place to plant flowers, and the ability to ask someone to stay without immediately making herself useful. These are not rewards appended to the real story. They are the contents of the life that the sacrificial solution repeatedly threatens to consume. (E20, E23, E27-E31, E44-E48.)

Knightliness connects rather than separates her public and ordinary selves. Before the holy name Fleurdelys, she plays at defending others. After the sacred office loses its authority, she still wants to protect people and travel as a knight. A solemn register can be effortful without being fraudulent; a playful girl can be genuinely committed to martial responsibility. The task is not to excavate a harmless authentic girl beneath a false warrior. It is to understand how one person has learned several ways of carrying protection, pleasure, exposure, and authority. (E02-E04, E11, E21, E47.)

There are two indispensable qualifications. First, relationships enable her freedom, but do not manufacture her identity from nothing. She supplies the name Cartethyia at her first introduction; Rover later honors it. Second, her later secrecy cannot be explained only as a return to unhealthy self-sacrifice. The Galbrena plan has an explicit information-security premise: the enemy can exploit other people's cognition, while the two planners' memories are inaccessible. The plan remains ethically costly. Its cost, however, must be evaluated under its actual fictional constraints rather than a generic lesson that honest friends should always share everything. (E07-E08, E20, E34-E36.)

The strongest working thesis is therefore:

> Cartethyia reclaims a continuous, imperfect life from powers that would define its origin, role, and ending. She retains the knight's vocation while learning that protection can include being protected, that remembrance need not entail ownership, and that responsibility can continue without sacred office. Her freedom remains vulnerable wherever extraordinary necessity makes her own life appear to be the most efficient instrument available.

This is a pre-audiovisual reconstruction. It establishes what the reviewed text supports and prepares questions that actual performance, visual staging, and promotional framing could revise. It does not announce an integrated multimodal endpoint.

## 2. What this reading covers

The source boundary is the frozen semantic generation at commit `353f2eaed119bc9f680eab92807d20ac75a79b40`, not every future release of the game. The reading covers the Chinese text of all 245 materialized scene/action contexts, comprising 3,097 contextual talk items; the five favor stories and 99 favor-word entries; profile evidence; and the 440-row mention nomination surface. Contextual talk items include other characters, narration, repeated projections, and options. They are not 3,097 Cartethyia utterances. The occurrence authority accepts 878 solo story occurrences; 687 are source-voiced. Adding 99 archive lines produces the 786-line direct voice census. (E59-E60.)

Chinese is the governing textual witness. English was read alongside the archive and selected scene contexts; high-value passages were checked across Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and English. This is not an assertion of an exhaustive close reading of all four complete localizations. An English phrase is not allowed to decide a character claim when the Chinese says something materially narrower.

The scene ledger is character-centered. Its 13 quest wrappers do not establish complete graphs for every referenced quest. A scene's internal key is not a public patch number, and the order of records is not automatically diegetic chronology. Historical speech, reconstructed memories, public plays, optional ecology interactions, and stylized celebrations retain their separate status. References to Cartethyia in later antagonistic planning do not prove that she knows those plans.

Existing audiovisual evidence is not absent. Ten bounded English, audio-bearing playthrough derivatives have already been prepared in the evidence plane. Their current manifest still marks structured moving-image observations unannotated. This pass reviewed their routing and metadata, not their footage. The distinction matters particularly because two older clip timings were corrected in the later manifest. A prepared file and a remembered title are not a completed observation. (E58.)

## 3. The name belongs to the person who introduces herself

At the first relevant introduction, Cartethyia hesitates toward the beginning of Fleurdelys, stops, and gives Cartethyia as her name. Carlotta acknowledges that she will remember it. Later, after integration, Cartethyia chooses the name she supplied when she and Rover first met. Chinese, Japanese, and Korean make that self-introduction particularly clear. English compresses it into the name by which Rover first knew her, but does not establish that Rover invented it. (E07, E20.)

The archive supplies an earlier anchor: Cartethyia is already the unruly Laureate before receiving the public holy name Fleurdelys. The name choice after integration consequently has both personal continuity and relational significance. It retrieves a name from before the office and preserves the name under which the present relationship began. It is neither amnesiac innocence severed from history nor a new identity bestowed by a rescuer. (E03, E08.)

This corrects a causal overreach in the older monograph's claim that Rover supplies continuity by naming and remembering her. Remembering, recognizing, refusing her disappearance, and honoring her preferred address remain important. Naming as original authorship does not. The distinction is small at the level of plot summary and large at the level of personhood: **a witness can help someone keep her name without becoming the author of the self that name identifies.**

Nor is the preferred name evidence that Fleurdelys was never real. Cartethyia does not disown the public defender's memories, care, failures, or suffering. She chooses the address under which she will continue to bear them. The model must therefore retain the distinction among a former office, an embodied form, historical memory, and present self-identification. They intersect; they are not interchangeable switches.

## 4. Protection begins in play

The childhood archive places knightly imagination before sacred appointment. Stories of defenders, a child's improvised weapon, collective play, and the desire to stand between someone else and danger all precede the institution's elevated interpretation of her. The early knight is a role she enjoys inhabiting, not only a duty she is trained to perform. (E02.)

That matters because later ordinary behavior repeatedly revives the same connection. An errand can become an adventure; a small harvest can receive a grand title; a challenge can be answered as a knightly test. These gestures are humorous partly because the stakes are low, but their pleasure is not sarcastic. She likes the imaginative form. The game does not require her to become embarrassed by earnestness before she can become free. (E49.)

A purely trauma-centered reading would be too narrow. It would treat the knight as a defense against helplessness and every cheerful action as a cover for pain. The sources do show fear, shame, and costly adaptation. They also show enjoyment that has a history of its own. The childhood archive, puppet-making under amnesia, restored cooking, rail practice, and later festival participation make pleasure more than an absence of crisis. It is something she actively knows how to create. (E02, E05-E06, E23, E30, E44-E46.)

The continuity is not unlimited. Childhood mischief does not entitle a reconstruction model to reproduce the same conduct in every mature situation. Her adult rule-breaking has to be read in its actual context. The defensible invariant is a tendency to make shared activity vivid and playful, with occasional practical overreach. It is not a timeless license to steal wine, damage property, or ignore others' labor.

## 5. Appointment does not simply replace the child

The Laureate story emphasizes discomfort with the form imposed on her. Country habits, spontaneous movement, wine, dance, and unruly creativity do not fit smoothly into the institution's desired saintliness. Public reverence and contempt both become ways of telling her what the role requires. The holy name arrives at the edge of an altered life. (E03.)

Yet the subsequent training cannot be dismissed as wholly alien discipline. She studies, learns to fight, tends suffering, listens, and takes the lives behind the illuminated windows seriously. The qualities that make her usable to a sacred institution are also qualities through which she develops genuine competence. The institution does not invent compassion; it gives that compassion forms, resources, expectations, and a dangerous public ownership claim. (E04.)

This is why 'mask' is an insufficient master metaphor. A mask can imply a detachable false exterior. Fleurdelys's practiced composure is part of what Cartethyia has actually learned. Archive speech explicitly acknowledges the effort of maintaining a composed bearing and tone. That effort can become constraining, but it is also a skill. The person who later relaxes has not discovered that all prior courage was counterfeit. (E47.)

The more useful distinction is between **a capacity she can employ** and **a capacity others claim she must always embody**. Formality is not the enemy; permanent compulsory formality is. Protection is not the enemy; a system that makes her body the standing guarantee of everyone else's safety is. Public memory is not the enemy; an official story that determines which futures remain permissible is.

## 6. The child, the vessel, and the creator's explanation

The revelation of manufactured origins threatens to collapse every action into design. If Leviathan made the vessel, perhaps her life is simply a device carrying out a concealed purpose. The antagonist has an interest in that conclusion. It would turn origin into jurisdiction: because the creator produced the conditions of her existence, the creator would own the meaning of everything she has subsequently become. (E11, E16-E18.)

The narrative does not answer by proving that she has an uncontaminated origin. It gives her choices made from within contamination. Historical Fleurdelys resists the destruction of people. Present Cartethyia insists on knowing the past. After reunion, she rejects an option that would discard painful memories. These acts do not make her independent of every causal influence. They show that influence does not exhaust agency. (E09, E11, E18.)

Imperator's explanation must also remain ethically complicated. Benevolent purpose and later help do not erase the coercive structure of making a person bear fused powers, uncertain risks, and contingencies involving her destruction. The source permits gratitude without requiring the analyst to treat beneficial outcomes as retrospective consent. Rover's past role, Imperator's account, and what Cartethyia herself has actually heard must not be merged into one omniscient biography. (E17.)

The correct question is not whether she was made by good or evil hands. It is whether her subsequent life can make claims those hands must respect. Her insistence on imperfect continuity is the strongest textual answer. A perfect instrument would have no reason to insist that the inconvenient memories remain hers.

## 7. Amnesia is an epistemic and practical state

Amnesia changes what Cartethyia can know and do. She is uncertain about her allegiance, her history, the power in her, and her standing among the people whose lives were damaged. Some practical capacities are weakened; she needs guidance, reassurance, and help in traversing the abnormal environment. These are real constraints, not evidence that her personality is essentially childish. (E09-E10, E14-E16.)

Her insistence that an understood villain would be preferable to an unknowable self is not a wish to be guilty. It is a refusal of comfort bought through permanent disorientation. The feared outcome is that she will never become capable of answering for herself. The statement also sets a limit on an easy therapeutic reading in which all painful knowledge should be delayed for her wellbeing. She herself values intelligibility even when it may condemn her. (E09.)

But she is not uniformly competent about every inference. The sword's rejection becomes, for her, a possible moral verdict. Others have to challenge the slide from dangerous origin to personal blame. Cantarella's intervention is especially important because it does not merely comfort. It questions Cartethyia's presumption about other people's will and helps provoke an apology. Cartethyia's self-accusation can erase others' agency just as effectively as an arrogant claim to control them. (E12, E14.)

The amnesiac corpus also retains social intelligence. She notices how reassuring Abby and Rover can work; she tries to be useful; she can turn a puzzle or awkward movement into a shared task. Need does not erase contribution. A model that writes every amnesiac scene as helpless dependence would lose much of the interactional texture.

## 8. Hunger, remembered taste, and the recovered body

The camp conversation distinguishes remembered preference from present sensation. Cartethyia can remember liking salad and disliking fish while being unable, in that condition, to experience ordinary hunger or taste. A static 'favorite food' field would miss the dramatic point: knowing what she likes is not yet the same as possessing a body through which she can enjoy it. (E10.)

The later Septimont scene restores the connection. She has prepared salad again, can savor its texture, and identifies eating what she wants as one part of a future she intends to live. Cooking is both remembered skill and present accomplishment. The arc does not make bodily pleasure irrelevant because identity survives metaphysical disruption. It makes the recovered capacity to enjoy an ordinary meal one of the proofs that continued life has substance. (E23.)

Her fish aversion should not be converted into symbolic trauma without evidence. The archive frames it as a long-standing dislike. Leviathan's association with overwhelming waters does not authorize a causal explanation for every food preference. Nor does her enthusiasm for one salad imply nutritional asceticism. Her kitchen curiosity, interest in flavor, and later drink experimentation show a broader, concrete relationship with food. (E06, E44-E48.)

The same discipline applies to her two forms. The source certification describes exceptional frequency conditions and controlled transformation. It does not establish that one appearance is a morally inferior body, that the other is her only adult self, or that shifts can be diagnosed through a real-world psychiatric vocabulary. Form is evidence to be modeled, not a shortcut to an archetype.

## 9. Mourning without taking possession of the dead

Cadanto and Haultir are distinct relationships. The first Echo is not interchangeable with the sword-bearing human companion, and the people of Avinoleum do not exist merely to add weight to Cartethyia's guilt. Their own decisions and courage matter. Her difficulty is partly that she wants to answer for their losses without appropriating their sacrifices as proof that she alone owns the debt. (E13-E15.)

The farewell song is significant as an action: she participates in remembrance and worries whether she has the right to mourn. This pass does not infer the song's timbre, musical affect, or camera treatment. Textually, the scene binds grief to uncertainty about moral standing. She can fear that accepting the role of mourner would quietly excuse the part she believes she played in others' suffering. (E15.)

Later visits alter the practice of remembrance. She returns with news of a living world: people, food, music, changed streets. The dead are not erased when she continues, but neither do they require her to repeat the office in which they knew her. The living world becomes something she can bring back to them. (E27.)

This supports a more exact version of her continuity ethic. To remember is not always to preserve conditions unchanged. It can mean letting the dead remain part of a life that accumulates things they never saw. That reading is interpretive, but it explains why the memorial and ordinary-life scenes belong in the same dossier rather than separate tragic and comic folders.

## 10. The requested ending and the refusal of perfection

When Cartethyia asks for reunion followed by annihilation, the situation is not just a change in social tone. Desire to live and the exhausted Fleurdelys state have been materially divided under the story's metaphysics. Reunion carries a danger she takes seriously. The proposal therefore has a protective rationale as well as a despairing one. To reduce it to irrational sadness would discard the very conditions that make the choice difficult. (E16.)

Rover refuses the ending she proposes. That refusal is ethically tense. It overrides an articulated request, but the narrative also opens a possibility Cartethyia cannot yet secure by herself. Cantarella's contribution and the return of separated elements prevent the scene from being understood as one rescuer simply choosing which half deserves to exist. What is preserved is a life with both its wish to continue and its history of exhaustion. (E12, E16-E18.)

Her later rejection of memory erasure is crucial. A clean version of the future is offered at the cost of severing part of the past. She does not take it. The painful life is not defended because suffering is inherently ennobling, but because the people and experiences within it are hers. Eliminating pain by eliminating continuity would repeat the creator's logic in a gentler form. (E18.)

The working interpretation is that **imperfection is the condition of an unfinished life**. A perfected vessel has a function; a completed martyr has an ending. Cartethyia asks to remain someone who can still choose, revise, enjoy, and fail. That is not guaranteed psychological recovery. It is the refusal to let technical or narrative completion substitute for living.

## 11. What saving one person proves, and what it does not

The anti-futility exchange is one of her clearest ethical statements. A rescue need not solve the whole crisis to matter. The person saved before her is not made worthless by the persistence of danger elsewhere. This resists an argument that treats partial success as equivalent to no success at all. (E19.)

It does not establish a comprehensive doctrine against consequences, numbers, triage, or large-scale planning. Cartethyia later participates in a strategy whose outcome depends on a civilization-wide coalition. She is capable of thinking beyond the nearest person. The local rescue and the strategic campaign are both hers. An 'anti-utilitarian' label would be too blunt unless narrowed to the actual proposition being rejected.

The better model is a protective particularism that refuses to let abstract hopelessness cancel concrete obligation. It explains why lost children, distressed citizens, stranded explorers, sick companions, and threatened friends can all claim her attention. They do not first need to represent a grand historical cause. (E19, E44.)

Its danger is not indifference to others but asymmetric inclusion of herself. She can defend the worth of one other life more readily than treat her own life as something a plan must preserve. Yet the asymmetry should not be made absolute: the recovered preference for a future, acceptance of company, and willingness to hand the Gem to someone else are material counterexamples. (E20, E23, E40.)

## 12. Reciprocal defiance is not obedience in affectionate language

After rescue, Cartethyia imagines becoming strong enough to oppose Rover if a comparable crisis demands it. The promise is not merely 'I will always be your sword.' It authorizes judgment within the relationship. A sword that can only obey would repeat the structure of the vessel. A companion capable of refusal can protect a person rather than merely execute a command. (E21.)

The scope matters. The exchange concerns an extreme self-destructive ending, not every ordinary preference. It does not give Cartethyia standing to overrule Rover's friendships, travel, work, or benign refusals. The ecology invitation provides a useful contrast: when an ordinary shared experience is postponed, she can accept the delay. The crisis exception and everyday respect must coexist in the model. (E21, E28.)

Nor is romance necessary to the mechanism. The relationship is intimate and can support romance-coded readings in appropriate scenes, but the explicit ethical structure is trust, rescue, opposition, and reciprocity. A model should not convert affinity-gated archive warmth or a tender invitation into a mandatory canonical relationship category. It should also avoid the opposite mistake of treating all affection as analytically embarrassing and therefore irrelevant.

Her response to playful provocation is equally specific. She can be energized by challenge and wants to surpass Rover. That does not mean she enjoys humiliation. The productive interlocutor recognizes her competence and leaves her room to answer. The same competitive energy can later be reduced to the scale of who will grow a plant better. (E21, E31.)

## 13. She sometimes wants to receive, not perform, care

The post-integration scene includes an unusually important request: she does not want to decide the next objective immediately. She wants to remain beside Rover for a while. A character reduced to relentless usefulness would turn the moment into another offer of service. Cartethyia can instead make her current need the content of the exchange. (E20.)

The hidden-place conversation similarly joins wanting gentleness to treating others gently. The Chinese formulation begins with a desire to be treated kindly before moving toward giving kindness. English sounds more like a general maxim about reciprocity; Japanese makes the paired personal wishes particularly clear. This difference should stop the analysis from quietly converting her into a person who only gives and never asks. (E28.)

These scenes do not prove that she has solved every difficulty with dependence. Her guilt before Cantarella remains capable of delaying contact, and crisis can still make self-instrumentalization attractive. The evidence supports a repertoire, not a total transformation. She has ways of receiving care; they do not automatically activate in every threatening situation.

This is also where an audiovisual pass may matter most. A voice, pause, physical distance, or unhurried scene ending could strengthen or complicate the apparent ease of the request. Until inspected, the text supports the request itself and its contrast with service, not a detailed account of bodily relaxation.

## 14. Cantarella: shame can be more difficult than metaphysics

Cartethyia can demand the truth about catastrophic origins and still struggle to make a personal call. Her concern that she has taken the place or future belonging to Cantarella is not an objective accounting of a stolen destiny. It is her guilt-laden reconstruction of an institution that placed multiple girls within a violent selection system. Cantarella's own choices cannot be erased by Cartethyia's self-blame. (E12, E14, E22, E48.)

The call scene gives the difficulty an ordinary shape. She says she is not ready. Rover initiates contact anyway. Cartethyia's hesitation and awkward opening make accountability less majestic than facing a god. There is no guarantee that she will perform repair efficiently simply because she takes responsibility seriously. (E22.)

Cantarella is not only the recipient of an apology. She helps preserve the conditions of Cartethyia's survival, acts on judgments of her own, and later protects the body left after the apparent shooting. The relation contains intervention, disagreement, rescue, guilt, and incomplete ease. It should not be reduced to 'the other candidate forgives the heroine.' (E12, E33-E34.)

A bounded prediction follows: when responsibility involves an identifiable person whose response she cannot control, Cartethyia may postpone contact even while wanting to repair the harm. That is a different mechanism from the Galbrena plan's information restriction. The dossier needs both, precisely so 'she withholds' does not become a one-word explanation for unrelated behaviors.

## 15. Lupa makes room for a life outside rescue by Rover

Lupa's importance is not numerical screen time alone. She offers a relation in which Cartethyia can be capable, playful, imperfect, and occasionally mistaken without being worshiped or defined primarily as a rescue object. The archive values Lupa's lack of recoil and permits an older-sister joke despite their physical contrast. (E24-E26, E48, E51.)

Their narrative languages also differ. Cartethyia reaches for the knightly story; Lupa retains the gladiator's freedom and idiom. A faithful reconstruction lets that difference stand. Cartethyia does not need every friend to become a supporting character in her own preferred genre. At their best, the two can recognize one another as people belonging to Rinascita without treating belonging as ownership by a god. (E26.)

The relation is not frictionless. Lupa's own archive describes the discomfort of becoming a burden when exhausted. Cartethyia's protective competence can unintentionally expose another capable person's shame about needing help. Conversely, when Lupa tries to leave the city's future to her, Cartethyia insists that Lupa's own life and responsibility cannot simply be transferred. That insistence is supportive, but also demanding; the source does not make every refusal to accept resignation automatically free of pressure. (E25, E51.)

After the crisis, Cartethyia asks Lupa to show her the city. This request is analytically valuable because it is not a dramatic confession and not an attempt to save anyone. She wants company, local knowledge, and enjoyment. A dossier that retrieves only the life-and-death scenes would miss what makes their friendship capable of sustaining an ordinary future. (E24.)

## 16. The parents who cannot remember her

The Septimont meal combines sensory recovery with a much less reparable loss. Cartethyia identifies a couple and their child as her adoptive family, then explains that the memory of her as their daughter was fabricated by the Threnodian and disappeared when It was sealed. She has found people she wanted to see, but not the reciprocal recognition she might have hoped to receive. (E23.)

The scene supports neither an easy reunion nor the claim that all childhood experience was unreal. The stated fabrication concerns the daughter-memory. It does not provide a license to delete every event, affection, or habit in the childhood archive. The dossier preserves what the source specifies and leaves the larger mechanics unresolved.

Cartethyia watches from a distance and values the fact that they survived and are living with a child of their own. She does not insist that the present family be reorganized around her lost relationship. This is a strong instance of nonpossessive attachment. It is also an instance of managing grief through comparison with worse outcomes: their deaths or twenty years of bereavement would have been more painful. Her gratitude does not prove that the loss is emotionally costless.

The multilingual wording matters. English describes her as at peace; Chinese expresses happiness in the present moment; Japanese and Korean also emphasize happiness or satisfaction rather than providing an independent certificate of completed grief. The retained contextual text `MAIN_YGDX_37_27` preserves bitterness; it is present in the mention corpus rather than among the scene's solo talk items. The strongest reading is coexistence: she can genuinely welcome their wellbeing while retaining the pain of not being known. This is a textual characterization, not a claim to have inspected her gaze. (E23.)

This materially expands the earlier relational-authorship thesis. Relationships can hold identity, but Cartethyia also has to remain a person when a relationship no longer remembers her. Recognition is valuable; it cannot be the sole ontological foundation of her life.

## 17. Wandering needs somewhere to return

The Black Shores planting scenes give freedom a material counterweight. Cartethyia sees flowers as threads connecting travelers to a place: someone must return to tend what was planted. She wants to leave a trace and acquire another shore at which she can rest. The wandering knight is not an ideal of perfect detachment. (E31.)

There is humor in her impatience. She imagines borrowing temporal power to accelerate the flowers' growth, then accepts waiting and turns cultivation into a shared contest. Her repertoire includes both the extraordinary shortcut and the ordinary practice that makes duration meaningful. Neither 'impatient with everything' nor 'serenely patient' captures the scene.

The administrative detail is equally important. When a rule threatens the permanence of the planting, Cartethyia does not demand that affection suspend all regulation. Rover supplies the exception. A reconstruction that credits her with forcing the institution to recognize her would invent an assertion the scene does not show.

The plants connect several otherwise separate dimensions: existing relationships, future return, environmental knowledge, domestic maintenance, and competitive play. They are therefore stronger ordinary-life evidence than a generic statement that she likes flowers. They show one way she can organize a future not wholly measured by emergencies.

## 18. Competence is uneven, and she knows some of its limits

Cartethyia is capable of training, fighting, navigating unusual conditions, perceiving dangerous frequencies, planning, cooking a favored dish, and learning an activity on her own. She is not thereby competent at every administrative, commercial, medical, or social task. She can ask Carlotta to teach bargaining, ask Lupa for local guidance, and accept help in traversal. (E24, E30-E32, E48.)

The medical consultation is a useful corrective to heroic self-description. Cartethyia suggests overcoming difficulty through will; the specialist tells her to trust science. The source supplies an environmental explanation and a proposed recovery setting rather than treating resolve as a cure. Her earlier willingness to report worsening imbalance also contradicts an absolute rule that she always conceals weakness. (E24, E32.)

There is no need to ridicule her for not being a scientist. Her competence lies partly in taking the condition seriously enough to enter the conversation. At the same time, the dossier must not convert her determination into physiological authority. The specialist's explanation governs the medical proposition, while her response is evidence about how she approaches dependence and limitation.

After victory she still cannot safely absorb the Gem. Galbrena's explanation explicitly invokes balance within her. 'Human' therefore cannot mean that every extraordinary power and constraint has vanished. The physiological account is not fully closed by the thematic ending. (E40.)

## 19. The apparent death is a test of epistemic order

Before its explanation, the shooting presents a frightening and morally charged event. Companions react to what appears to have happened. Later exposition changes the causal interpretation: Cartethyia and Galbrena have designed a way to place her humanity inside Leviathan as an anchor. The earlier experience of danger and loss is not retroactively unreal, even though the reader's explanation changes. (E33-E36.)

The dossier keeps three layers separate: the event as witnessed, the actors' prior plan, and the audience's later understanding. Folding them into one timeless synopsis would either condemn Galbrena as a simple murderer or make the companions foolish for responding to information they actually had. Neither is adequate.

The plan exploits the similarity between the frequencies of Cartethyia's humanity and divinity. What seems like a reason for hopeless entanglement becomes a tactical opportunity. By drawing Leviathan toward what It cannot resist reclaiming, the planners give a formless enemy something that can be fixed and confronted. Cartethyia's body and history remain operationally useful, but she participates in defining that usefulness. (E35.)

Authorship does not make the risk disappear. Sending her humanity into the Dark Tide is not a harmless stage trick. Success depends on others reaching and defeating the enemy. Her confidence in them is a wager on a coalition, not proof that she secretly knows she cannot be harmed.

## 20. Necessary secrecy and the cost it does not cancel

The strongest correction to the earlier reading concerns why Rover and others are kept uninformed. Cartethyia says the Dark Tide cannot read the two planners' memories; Galbrena explains that the beliefs of those closest to the Maiden are needed to deceive Leviathan. The recipients' grief is not merely an unfortunate side effect of poor communication. Within the stated mechanism, genuine belief is part of the concealment. (E34-E35.)

That creates a constrained moral problem, not an automatic vindication. Cartethyia consents to her own extreme risk. Galbrena accepts danger, suspicion, and hostility. Their closest companions do not thereby consent to having their beliefs and emotions used as tactical material. The plan's outcome cannot erase that asymmetry.

But an analyst must not demand a solution that the source conditions exclude and then diagnose her failure to choose it. Complete disclosure to a readable mind would frustrate the described strategy. The strongest criticism therefore concerns whether alternatives existed, whether less severe deception could have worked, what residual duty of explanation or repair follows, and how necessity should be evaluated. The available text does not supply a comprehensive comparison of all alternatives.

This changes the psychological conclusion. The scene still shows how quickly Cartethyia can offer herself as a means. It does not independently prove that she distrusts friends, cannot accept help, or has learned nothing since Avinoleum. The operation is intensely collaborative and depends on confidence in those deliberately excluded from its initial knowledge. High operational trust and restricted disclosure coexist here.

The distinction is especially important when comparing her with Lynae, Aemeath, or Denia. Similar images of self-sacrifice do not establish identical causes. A reusable method should expose the difference between shame, protective paternalism, strategic compartmentalization, imposed obedience, and a genuinely unavoidable cost. It should not make the same therapeutic verdict the default endpoint of every character dossier.

## 21. Humanity as allegiance rather than purity

In the final confrontation, Cartethyia's human identity has collective force. It is bound to people's lives, wishes, memories, and the coalition that makes resistance possible. This is not a claim that one individual simply wills a god out of existence. Others supply actions and capacities she cannot substitute for. (E35, E38.)

The scene's humanism is also more complicated than biological purity. She carries extraordinary power and has an engineered history. Her insistence on humanity refuses a creator's right to decide her allegiance. It does not depend on proving that she was always untouched by nonhuman forces. The post-battle balance limit reinforces that distinction. (E01, E38, E40.)

This leaves an important question open. The narrative strongly supports Cartethyia's personhood and her rejection of being used against people. It does not provide a complete philosophical taxonomy of every Echo, god, construct, or frequency remnant. Her respect for individual companions and her objection to restrictions on Common Echo contact show a wider social concern, but the dossier should not fabricate a fully explicit universal theory of nonhuman rights.

The model can therefore predict resistance to origin-based disqualification more confidently than it can answer an unfamiliar metaphysical classification problem. That is not an analytical defect. It is an accurate statement of what the source has and has not tested.

## 22. Repairing the Order without becoming its new sacred ruler

The aftermath contains practical political work. Cartethyia reads changed doctrines, objects to banned theatre, exclusion of ordinary people from festival performance, and restrictions on closeness with Common Echoes. She asks about collective deliberation and learns how much authority has been concentrated in Fenrico. These details connect her ordinary attachments to institutional judgment. The freedom to perform, celebrate, and live with familiar beings is a real part of what she thinks the Order has damaged. (E41.)

She declines candidacy for leadership. Chinese combines her position or identity with an admission that she cannot manage city affairs well; English gives the second point a stronger patience-based coloring. The source does not establish that she thinks all office is corrupt or that competent government is beneath a wandering knight. She believes others are better suited and still promises to help correct the Order's errors. (E41.)

Her refusal is thus not abandonment of Rinascita. It limits the inference from extraordinary service to entitlement to rule. The person who helped save the city need not become the institution through which every future decision passes.

The announced reform is promising but incomplete. The Order, Montelli, and Fisalia are to deliberate with public opinion; Cartethyia says the future should belong to the people. Those statements do not supply constitutional safeguards, equal representation, enforceable refusal rights, or proof that elite mediation has ceased to dominate. The dossier records a pluralizing commitment, not a fully realized liberal democracy. (E42.)

## 23. Faith after divine appointment

Cartethyia's exchange with Phoebe refuses a crude choice between restoring the old sacred hierarchy and declaring all faith foolish. The office conferred by divinity can end while human deeds, hopes, and shared effort retain their value. Statues and inherited forms matter because people made and sustained them, not because their existence automatically grants a god or cleric jurisdiction over everyone else. (E39.)

This is compatible with gratitude toward Imperator and with mourning. Rejecting divine ownership need not mean erasing every relationship formed under it. It also permits Cartethyia to remain responsible for institutional harm without becoming permanently captive to the title through which it occurred.

The public response is not unanimous. Post-crisis NPC material preserves a difference between renewed reliance on a sacred figure and a more self-reliant interpretation of what happened. Cartethyia can reject the old office while other people continue trying to turn her into a guarantee. The social danger has not ended simply because her self-description changed. (E43.)

That unresolved tension belongs in any mature political reading: a person may relinquish sacred authority without being able to stop others from investing it in her. The next source generation would need to test how she responds if public reverence begins to constrain her freedom again.

## 24. Theatricality is not always someone else's lie

The public martyrdom play is a particularly clear warning against treating every official game text as transparent biography. It is an in-world literary work with incomplete knowledge and an unfinished ending. Its elevated language tells us something about the public Maiden, not necessarily how Cartethyia habitually speaks. (E54.)

Cartethyia can be embarrassed by dramatization and still love theatre. She can criticize a playwright, hide near a performance, or joke that the apparent death may furnish another production. She also narrativizes ordinary actions herself. The important variable is less truth versus performance than who chooses the form, what it claims, and whether the living person can answer back. (E29, E40, E49, E53.)

Her own performance can be morally complicated. A staged death that others must believe is not innocent merely because she has reclaimed the role of author. Conversely, a knightly boast in a game is not manipulative merely because it is theatrical. Authority and consequence have to be assessed at the scene level.

PVs and trailers will form another layer. They may foreground a liberated, solemn, playful, or embattled Cartethyia, but that is evidence of released framing before it is evidence of what she literally did. Matching multilingual versions can help separate script and vocal choices. It cannot turn editing into a direct record of private creative intention.

## 25. The ordinary future has work in it

Later conversations describe minor rescues, unfamiliar foods, songs, scenery, and communal celebration. Cartethyia identifies this as the life she wanted. The statement does not prove that every danger is over, but it does show that protection and pleasure can occupy the same day without a divine mandate organizing them. (E44.)

Her small failures remain. A drink can taste odd; she can give honest feedback while preserving appreciation for the person who made it. Festival enthusiasm can outrun logistical judgment. Kitchen curiosity can require apology. These moments protect the character from becoming a moral abstraction whose every ordinary response is flawlessly considerate. (E06, E45-E46.)

Nor is non-crisis life empty waiting between great sacrifices. Plant care requires return. Cooking requires practice. Friendship requires invitations and conversations without world-saving utility. Repairing doctrine requires reading and deliberation. The future becomes durable through repeatable actions, not simply through the announcement that she is now free. (E23-E24, E31, E41, E44.)

The phrase 'life beyond usefulness' would still be too absolute. Much of this life is useful, and she enjoys being capable. What changes is that usefulness no longer has to be the entire justification for remaining alive. A nap, a dance, or a moment of company need not apologize for failing to defeat Leviathan.

## 26. Rival readings and their limits

**The saint whose goodness survives corruption.** This explains her courage, care, and consistency across hostile conditions. It is weakened when it makes origin irrelevant, erases mischief, treats every self-accusation as moral truth, or makes her life valuable mainly because it serves others. The archive's ordinary texture and the request to receive care must remain inside the account.

**The ordinary girl beneath a constructed public mask.** This explains the effort of formal bearing and her discomfort with public myth. It fails if the knight becomes wholly fake. Protective imagination predates appointment; training creates real ability; mature errantry preserves rather than discards that inheritance.

**Relational authorship.** This remains the strongest broad explanation. Recognition, opposition, rescue, peer difference, and shared institutions sustain her ability to continue. It needs correction where it gives Rover original authorship of her name or treats reciprocal recognition as the only basis of identity. The adoptive-family scene shows that she also survives nonrecognition.

**Duty reinscribed as freedom.** This is the most important critical rival. Calling sacrifice chosen can conceal how readily she still treats her life as a resource. It illuminates her offers of self-use. It becomes reductive if it ignores actual strategic constraints, the Galbrena partnership, requests for care, medical disclosure, refusal of office, and everyday preferences. Not every dangerous chosen obligation is proof that freedom has failed.

**Institutional humanism.** This explains the displacement of sacred appointment by human deeds and collective will. It does not exhaust the private character, and it must not turn proposed plural deliberation into a fully demonstrated political settlement.

**Romance as the solution to fragmented personhood.** The affection and intimacy are real textual material. The explanatory claim is too narrow. Cantarella, Lupa, Echo companions, medical helpers, former family, and public action have independent roles. No mandatory romantic state is needed to explain her refusal of memory erasure or her commitment to ordinary life.

## 27. Reconstructive consequences

A usable model should first select temporal state and knowledge. Amnesiac Cartethyia cannot speak with post-integration certainty; historical Fleurdelys cannot cite later reparations; a post-victory conversation should not silently remove the balance problem. Next it should select context: ordinary companionship, public responsibility, medical dependence, shame before Cantarella, or adversarial information conditions.

Within those constraints, several predictions are strong. She will often turn companionship into an activity rather than an abstract discussion. Concrete praise will be easier to receive than mythic elevation. A trusted playful challenge can produce determination. Origin-based dismissal should meet resistance. Public responsibility can be accepted without appetite for office. She may hesitate before a painful apology even when she accepts the need for it.

Other predictions require caution. A necessary sacrifice is not automatically chosen merely because she has sacrificed before. A friend's refusal is not automatically overridden. A quieter tone does not establish a different form. A request to remain close does not establish exclusivity. Her ability to use extraordinary power does not establish that she can safely use it without limits.

The model is most faithful when it retains a tension rather than resolving it in advance: **she wants a future, and she remains very good at inventing a purpose that could consume it.** The sources also show why that future is becoming harder to treat as expendable. It now contains meals she can taste, people whose independent lives matter to her, work she can choose, flowers awaiting return, and a name she has decided to keep.

## 28. What would change this reading

The later audiovisual pass should test transitions rather than merely illustrate conclusions. Does formal bearing persist in intimate ordinary scenes? Does an apparent return to lightness coexist with residual hesitation? How is the parents' nonrecognition staged? Does the shooting sequence encourage audience endorsement, unease, or both? Are ordinary refusals given time and space? Does promotional editing make the knight and the girl appear more separate than narrative dialogue does?

These are open questions. The ten existing anchor clips provide useful access to the earlier arc, but do not cover the full medical, familial, civic, and later ordinary-life material identified here. The new nomination plan therefore reuses the corrected inherited anchors and requests additional targeted witnesses rather than pretending the archive begins from zero.

The dossier's endpoint is not a claim that Cartethyia has learned never to sacrifice herself. It is a more precise understanding of what is at stake when she considers doing so. A life once appropriated as an answer now contains questions, attachments, and pleasures that no god, public myth, rescuer, or analyst should be permitted to finish on her behalf.
