# Cartethyia: Freedom as the Right to Choose One's Obligations

Analytical status: active provisional  
Canon boundary: pinned Wuthering Waves 3.6.0/3.6.6 evidence at Arikatsu commit `353f2eaed119bc9f680eab92807d20ac75a79b40`  
Text authority: Chinese; Japanese, Korean, and English are official localization witnesses  
Audio boundary: twelve verified archive FLACs, measured but not yet human-annotated  

## Thesis

Cartethyia's story is not a simple liberation from duty. It is a struggle over who gets to give duty its meaning.

Nearly every public identity available to her has been authored by somebody else: a child is made Laureate, the Laureate becomes a Blessed Maiden, the Maiden becomes a vessel, the vessel becomes a weapon, and the weapon becomes a story through which a city understands its suffering. Even the name Fleurdelys arrives carrying institutional expectation. The striking continuity in Cartethyia is that she does not answer this appropriation by withdrawing from obligation. Instead, she repeatedly takes the forms meant to use her and turns them toward a purpose she can own. A vessel becomes a protector. A creature designed to threaten humanity becomes its captor's prison. An apparent death becomes a way to carry humanity into Leviathan. A sacred sword becomes not a servant but a companion who promises to disobey.

Her freedom is therefore neither innocence nor escape. It is the right to inherit a compromised past, choose a name, and accept responsibilities without granting the institutions that produced them permanent authority over her. This makes self-sacrifice both her most admirable instinct and her most persistent danger. She is extraordinarily good at converting herself into a solution. She becomes fully herself only through relationships with people prepared to say that saving her is also part of the moral problem.

This authorship is not solitary. Relationships hold her in continuity when memory fails, and Rinascita's people supply the human labor that lets her reinterpret faith. A more exact thesis is therefore relational as well as voluntarist: Cartethyia struggles over who may give duty its meaning, but the answer is produced with trusted witnesses and a surviving social world rather than by private will alone.

## The child beneath the public legend

The archive stories establish a beginning more unruly and ordinary than the saintly image that later covers it. The child from Egla loves knight stories, adventure, performance, and the prospect of protecting someone. These are not yet separate drives. Play and ethics occupy the same imaginative space: becoming a wandering knight promises both delight and usefulness. Her later attraction to bread, dancing, flying, stories, and shared festivals is not decorative softness added after the tragedy. It is the surviving substance of the life that the public role interrupted.

Rinascita converts this child into a sign. The public needs a Blessed Maiden, the Order needs a sacred narrative, and later cultural memory needs a martyr. `InfoDisplay_311000011_Text`, the unfinished play about her, is especially revealing because it is evidence about reception rather than transparent biography. It shows how public narrative can make a clean dramatic object from an unfinished person. Cartethyia is embarrassed by the play, yet she does not reject performance itself. She remembers Carnevale and dancing fondly; after her staged death she can joke that the spectacle may become another play. The difference is authorship. Performance forced upon her turns life into propaganda. Performance reclaimed with companions becomes play, memory, and control over the ending.

That distinction prevents a common flattening. Cartethyia is not secretly an ordinary carefree girl whose solemnity is entirely fake, nor is she essentially a serene saint whose warmth is fan-service ornament. Her formal register is learned, difficult, and purposeful. `FavorWord_140907_Content` explicitly says maintaining Fleurdelys' composed manners and tone takes effort. But effort does not make the register false. It is a real skill developed for public protection, just as private hesitation, laughter, and teasing are real parts of her. The person contains both.

## What an ordinary afternoon with Cartethyia feels like

The mundane evidence gives Cartethyia a more specific social rhythm than “warm in private.” She tends to make companionship concrete. She brings chestnut bread, warns that it is hot, insists it tastes best torn open immediately, and frames the advice as a small confidence shared because the relationship is close (`FavorWord_140901_Content`). The scene moves from food to homecoming memory and only then to the vulnerable invitation to watch her dance. An ordinary meeting with her is therefore likely to begin with something to eat, see, practice, or explore before it becomes an explicit conversation about feeling.

Her tastes are definite and slightly stubborn. She says she could eat Laurus Salad for three meals without tiring of it, praises both its sweetness and crunch, and takes modest pride in being able to prepare it (`FavorWord_140908_Content`). Fish produces an almost bodily refusal that has survived childhood pressure and the inconvenience of living by the sea (`FavorWord_140909_Content`). These preferences make her neither a gourmet nor an ascetic. She likes simple repeatable pleasures, can be picky, and does not always discipline an ordinary aversion out of herself merely because circumstances make it impractical.

She is socially drawn to collective joy. Carnevale, cheerful crowds, singing, dancing, laughter, and spinning hand in hand are things she wants to join rather than observe from ceremonial distance (`FavorWord_140906_Content`). Her everyday generosity often takes the form of arranging an experience: a loaf at the right moment, a dance, a view from above the sea, or a story. On a birthday she plans an extended flight from sunrise toward sunset and offers her hand as the means of seeing familiar scenery differently (`FavorWord_140918_Content`). She gives time and attention more readily than status.

This sociability does not mean constant chatter. After hearing Ciaccona from a distance, she stands beneath a tree listening until noon has become dusk (`FavorWord_140916_Content`). The evidence supports sustained quiet absorption in music and landscape, though not yet a general claim that she is comfortable with every kind of interpersonal silence. Her idle lines also permit uncomplicated rest: she notices a good smell, tells herself it is time for a small nap, and slips into dream language (`FavorWord_140920_Content`–`140921_Content`). Rest exists in her repertoire even if crisis repeatedly drives her away from it.

Play is often narrativized. She announces an imaginary battle in which “Cartethyia the Knight” wins, calls danger the knight's challenge, treats chests as storybook treasure, and invents “A Knight's Great Harvest” for gathered provisions. The knight ideal is serious ethics and a game she enjoys performing. That dual use keeps her from becoming an abstract moral emblem: she can make an errand feel like an episode in an adventure.

She is also easier to embarrass than her public bearing suggests. Hearing a dramatized version of her life makes her want to leap from her seat and flee. She punctures the poets' grandeur by mentioning her poor scripture grades (`FavorWord_140902_Content`). She asks “How was I? Was I helpful?” after reappearing as a capable companion (`FavorWord_140903_Content`), which reads as a bid for concrete reassurance about competence rather than a demand for worship. Praise is safest when it concerns something she actually did; grand mythologizing makes her restless.

Adult Cartethyia retains curiosity about skills she lacks. She admits she cannot haggle and wants Carlotta to teach her, revisits practical survival skills for wandering, and invites Rover to help design a more heroic entrance (`FavorWord_140910_Content`, `140915_Content`). This suggests she is not embarrassed by every incompetence. She can convert a deficit into a shared lesson when it does not touch her deepest guilt.

Her patience is contextual. She can listen to a song for hours and train persistently, but later refuses civic office partly because she lacks the temperament for its routine. A companion would likely find her attentive when an activity has human, aesthetic, or knightly meaning and impatient with ceremonial bureaucracy, inflated praise, or passive confinement. Childhood mischief—stealing sacramental wine, dancing on platforms, and turning detention walls into a canvas in `FavorStory_140902_Content`—should not be copied mechanically into adult conduct, but it establishes that rule-bending and exuberance predate the saintly role.

An ordinary afternoon with integrated Cartethyia would therefore not feel like an uninterrupted philosophical seminar. It would more likely alternate between a concrete invitation, enthusiastic sensory attention, a knight-story joke, a pocket of quiet observation, and a hesitant disclosure that becomes direct only once she has decided to trust the listener. She is pleasant company for people who can accept both earnestness and play. She may be difficult company when shame makes her postpone repair, when she decides secrecy is protective, or when danger causes her to turn herself into the plan.

## Amnesia and the ethics of continuity

*The Maiden, The Defier, The Death Crier* makes loss of memory an ethical test rather than merely a mystery device. In Avinoleum, Cartethyia cannot tell whether she belongs to Imperator or Leviathan, whether the power in her is sacred or catastrophic, or whether the self she might recover deserves sympathy. Her fear is not principally that the answer will condemn her. She says she is afraid she may never find an answer at all (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_31_5`). Later she sharpens the position into an antithesis: she would rather know herself as a villain than wander without understanding (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_1003_1`).

The four textual witnesses preserve the choice even when they shade it differently. Chinese gives the clearest “clear-eyed villain” against ignorant wandering. Japanese makes the desire to live while understanding herself somewhat more explicit. English emphasizes being lost in a world without understanding. The shared characterization is strong: moral personhood requires continuity with what one has done, not the comfort of starting from a blank record.

This is not a universal doctrine of immediate disclosure. Cartethyia sometimes withholds dangerous truths from others. She hesitates to destabilize Phoebe's faith and later conceals the full Galbrena plan. Her truth ethic is most absolute when the hidden truth concerns her own responsibility. She refuses an innocence purchased by amnesia, but she can still decide—sometimes paternalistically—that another person should be protected from knowledge. That exception matters because it stops “honest” from becoming a context-free trait. The deeper pattern is responsibility: she wants the information necessary to answer for herself.

Choosing the name Cartethyia after integration completes this movement. She does not erase Fleurdelys' memories or deny that the two forms belong to one life. Nor does she restore the old office unchanged. She keeps continuity while selecting the name given at her first meeting with Rover (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_140_5`). The choice does not say “that past was not me.” It says “that past does not get the last word on who I must be.”

## Protection at the scale of one person

Cartethyia's protective ethic is strikingly particularist and anti-futility. Against arguments that an action is meaningless because it cannot repair everything, she answers at the human scale. Saving the identifiable person before her is enough to make the action meaningful (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_134_3`–`134_4`). This rejects total-calculation arguments that erase the value of partial rescue; it does not establish her answer to abstract population-level allocation dilemmas. She also plans strategically at civilization scale.

This ethic explains her courtesy toward strangers and dependents. In uncertain company she checks safety, asks practical questions, explains danger, and postpones her own story. It also explains why fallen defenders remain individuals to her. Cadanto and Haultir are not interchangeable sacrifices in the history of Avinoleum. She sings farewell, doubts her right to mourn people she failed, and converts grief into an obligation to make their choice matter.

Yet the ethic has a blind spot: Cartethyia has difficulty including herself among the particular persons who deserve rescue. When her existence appears to be the source of danger, she proposes disappearance. When Fleurdelys' despair and Cartethyia's wish to live seem impossible to reconcile, she asks Rover first to merge them and then annihilate the whole. Much later, she volunteers her humanity as the anchor inside Leviathan and asks Galbrena to make her the first bullet. These are not identical situations—the later plan is strategic, coordinated, and reversible in conception—but both show how readily she treats herself as an instrument.

Self-sacrifice is therefore not a superficial contradiction to agency. It is the form her agency most naturally takes under pressure. She asserts authorship by choosing how she will be used. The story's challenge is to move her toward a harder form of freedom in which survival is not selfishness and other people may legitimately refuse the solution she offers.

## Rover and the invention of reciprocal defiance

Rover's decisive contribution is not simple reassurance. Cartethyia asks to be made whole and destroyed; Rover hears the request and refuses its ending. The rescue does not merely save the “good” part from the “bad” Fleurdelys. It preserves both the person who wants to continue and the self whose despair has become terminal. The episode is ethically uncomfortable because Rover overrides an articulated wish. The narrative justifies that intervention by framing the wish as self-erasure under hopelessness, but the tension should not be hidden.

Cartethyia later interprets the act as a model of care. She promises that if Rover ever reaches the same edge, she will oppose them, defeat them if necessary, and save them (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_141_20`). The former sacred sword has acquired a new meaning. To be someone's blade no longer means obedience; it means accepting responsibility for judgment, including the duty to disobey.

This is the relationship's deepest reciprocity. Rover first supplies continuity by naming and remembering Cartethyia when she cannot. Cartethyia then offers not submission but a future counterforce. Their intimacy grows through ordinary proposals—food, dance, flight, company—and through playful challenge. When Rover provokes her, she warns that provocation works too well and turns it into competitive resolve (`Main_Linaxita_2_4_141_28`). The safe conclusion is that trusted challenge energizes her. It is not evidence that she enjoys humiliation or flirts indiscriminately.

Nor does high trust mean perfect transparency. In the Leviathan plan, Cartethyia accepts Rover's experience of her apparent murder as an operational cost. The plan proves confidence in Rover's eventual role, but it also shows the survival of her old tendency to protect by withholding and self-instrumentalizing. Archive intimacy is additionally affinity-gated. The evidence supports a close, potentially romantic bond in applicable states; it does not establish one mandatory canonical romance outcome.

## Relationships that keep her from becoming a Rover satellite

Cantarella reveals guilt that cannot be solved by heroic action. Cartethyia considers her the “true Blessed Maiden” harmed by the false institution built around Cartethyia's image. Cantarella helps rescue her and later safeguards her body, yet Cartethyia repeatedly delays direct repair. This is an important counterexample to the idea that she always confronts responsibility immediately. She can face metaphysical guilt more readily than interpersonal shame.

Galbrena reveals operational trust at its harshest. Later summaries can make Galbrena's shot look like murder, but the primary dialogue shows a joint design: godhood and humanity are divided, public misunderstanding is accepted, and Cartethyia asks to be fired into Leviathan as an anchor (`Main_Rinascita_2_12_37_29`–`37_44`). She thanks Galbrena for accepting hatred and misunderstanding. The relation proves extreme coordination, not necessarily emotional intimacy. It also raises a real criticism of Cartethyia: she is willing to make relational trauma part of a plan without fully dramatizing whether everyone affected consented to that cost.

Lupa provides a less burdened peer relation. In Septimont, Cartethyia can be a wandering knight rather than a sacred object. She values Lupa's lack of recoil, jokes about being the older sister, and works alongside separate agency instead of occupying a hierarchy. Lupa's directness draws out competitiveness and warmth. This is some of the best evidence for how Cartethyia behaves when neither worship nor rescue organizes the interaction.

Phoebe tests the relation between faith and truth. Cartethyia respects Phoebe's devotion enough to fear what disclosure might do to it. After Leviathan, she does not tell Phoebe that faith was merely foolish. She says the divinely chosen office is gone, while the deeds and hopes of people remain capable of unity and liberation (`Main_Rinascita_2_12_7401_5`–`7401_9`). She preserves the human labor invested in faith while rejecting its claim to divine ownership.

Imperator occupies a similar transformed place. Devotion, gratitude, and grief are genuine. What changes is the inference from meaningful ideal to rightful rule. Cartethyia can retain the Sentinel's ideal without reconstructing the institution that spoke in its name.

Finally, Rinascita itself is a relationship rather than a backdrop. Cartethyia's freedom does not make departure morally or physically simple. Her power, history, and affections tie her to its people. She declines the Primus role because she lacks the temperament and desire for civic office, then immediately accepts responsibility for correcting the Order's wrongs (`Main_Rinascita_2_12_743_14`–`743_17`). The distinction is exact: she rejects entitlement to rule, not obligation to repair.

## Speech as a map of exposure

Cartethyia's speech changes more by situation than by a single ornamental verbal tic. Publicly, she favors formal courtesy, complete declarations, role names, and the vocabulary of duty. Fleurdelys intensifies this into elevated, terminal language fit for a sacred warrior. In private, the seams show: ellipses, self-correction, small interjections, laughter, concrete invitations, and embarrassment. In crisis, uncertainty drops away and syntax compresses into imperatives, vows, and ethical antitheses.

Hesitation therefore marks exposure rather than weak conviction. She stumbles when naming fear, identity, shame, or desire for company. Once she chooses, she becomes direct. A simulation that makes her perpetually tentative would miss this transition; one that makes her uniformly commanding would erase the cost of disclosure.

Her characteristic rhetorical move is reversal: not the role as imposed, but the role repurposed. The puppet imprisons its maker; death delivers humanity; faith demands liberation; the sword defies its bearer. This is both an intellectual habit and the formal signature of her arc. She rarely escapes a damaged symbol by abandoning it. She argues over what it will mean.

Private affection is often mediated through an activity or object. She offers hot bread, dance, flight, song, or shared observation before naming closeness directly. This makes her warmth embodied and situational. It also offers a useful boundary for unfamiliar scenarios: when interested or concerned, she is more likely to propose doing something together than to deliver an abstract emotional monologue at once.

## From divinity to human work

The later Leviathan arc repeats Cartethyia's old danger at a higher level and gives it a different political resolution. She again makes herself the instrument. The apparent death is deliberately staged so that her humanity can become an anchor inside Leviathan. When she returns, she calls herself returned from the “dead,” insists that the divinely chosen Maiden is gone, and jokes about the convincing performance (`Main_Rinascita_2_12_7401_3`; `Main_Rinascita_2_12_75_8`–`75_14`).

The distinction between humanity and godhood is not a claim that ordinary life has no obligations. Her humanity is powerful precisely because it carries attachments, memory, and collective labor. The people who believed were not passive dupes; their actions made solidarity real even when the institution's metaphysics were corrupted. Cartethyia's post-divine position is consequently humanistic rather than cynical. Faith becomes a name for confidence embodied in deeds, and liberation becomes the test of the symbol's value.

This also reframes the title of wandering knight. At childhood, it is fantasy. Under the Order, knighthood is absorbed by public role. After integration, it is a personal narrative of travel and protection. After Leviathan, it must coexist with civic repair. The wandering knight is not the person with no home. She is the person who can leave office behind while still returning to answer for what her institutions did.

## Thematic and narrative function

Cartethyia concentrates several of *Wuthering Waves*' larger tensions: memory and personhood, resonance and possession, divinity and collective belief, role and agency, destruction and renewal. Her two-form design makes an internal ethical problem visible. Fleurdelys is not a disposable evil double; she is despair, duty, memory, and public identity made confrontable. Integration says that freedom cannot be achieved by amputating the compromised past.

The recurring sword motif similarly changes ownership. A sword is an instrument, but Cartethyia turns instrumentality into a chosen relation. To offer oneself as a blade can reproduce subordination; to promise judgment and defiance makes the metaphor reciprocal. The same ambivalence governs the stage. A role can erase a person, yet a performance can also let her author a conclusion. Her symbols do not acquire one fixed meaning. Their transformations are the story.

There is nevertheless room for a less celebratory reading. The narrative repeatedly rewards spectacular self-endangerment. Rover's rescue may teach reciprocal defiance, but Cartethyia later returns to strategic self-sacrifice. Her civic return resolves death, faith, and office rapidly enough that the social cost of institutional deception—especially for Cantarella and Phoebe—can feel compressed. The story recognizes her guilt but has not yet fully dramatized repair. “Chosen obligation” may be freedom, but it can also become a noble vocabulary for never learning to rest.

That criticism strengthens rather than destroys the character thesis. Cartethyia's arc is compelling because the contradiction remains active. She has learned that she deserves rescue; she has not fully learned to design plans that do not require others to rescue her from herself.

## Rival readings and thesis revision

The governing interpretation should not survive merely because it organizes the evidence elegantly. Three rival readings explain substantial parts of the same record.

### Duty-reinscription

The strongest skeptical reading is that Cartethyia never becomes meaningfully free. Every release is converted back into service: after integration she becomes a wandering knight and Rover's sword; after apparent death she returns to institutional repair; even ordinary pleasures are narrated as brief intervals around obligation. “Chosen duty” may be the noble language of somebody who cannot imagine worth outside usefulness.

This rival handles the recurrence of self-instrumentalization better than a celebratory autonomy reading. The Leviathan plan is particularly damaging counterevidence: reciprocal defiance has not stopped her from making her own expendability operational. The governing thesis handles her rejection of the Primus title, chosen name, humor, appetite, and desire for nameless travel better. Those details show real discrimination among forms of service rather than automatic submission. Verdict: **STRENGTHEN as a standing counter-reading**, but do not replace the thesis.

### Continuity through attachment

A second reading makes recognition, not self-authorship, the center. Rover remembers and names Cartethyia when she cannot; Cantarella protects the body that permits return; Lupa's lack of recoil stabilizes peer identity; the people of Rinascita transform faith through shared action. On this account, the self persists because other people hold it in relation.

This rival explains why the name given at first meeting matters and why reciprocal rescue is more than an individual moral rule. It also corrects any fantasy of a sovereign self creating meaning alone. The authorship thesis better explains her willingness to accept condemning knowledge, distinguish title from responsibility, and reverse symbols imposed by enemies. Verdict: **REVISE the governing thesis** so that authorship is explicitly relational.

### Institutional reform rather than personal liberation

A third reading treats Cartethyia chiefly as the vehicle for transferring sacred legitimacy from Divinity and the Order to collective human work. Her personal integration matters because it models a political integration: ideals and faith survive while divine entitlement is rejected.

This reading best explains the Phoebe exchange, the refusal of Primus office, and the return to civic repair. It also sees why the story cannot end with private travel alone. It handles bread, embarrassment, peer play, shame toward Cantarella, and the intimate ethics of rescue less well. Verdict: **PRESERVE as a major narrative-function reading**, not the whole character.

The resulting thesis transition is **REVISE**, not rejection. Cartethyia's story remains a struggle over who may give duty its meaning, but “who” is neither the isolated individual nor the inherited institution. Meaning is negotiated among a morally continuous person, trusted relationships capable of opposition, and collective human action. The duty-reinscription reading remains the strongest warning that this freedom may still be incomplete.

## Bounded predictions

In an unfamiliar social setting, the selected state matters. S1 Fleurdelys is more formal, deferential to sacred duty, and willing to disappear into a mission. S5 Cartethyia is more playful, institutionally skeptical, and prepared to decline office while accepting concrete repair work.

For S5, the evidence supports these bounded expectations:

- If a stranger is publicly humiliated, she is likely to intervene courteously but firmly and attend first to the harmed person. Whether she immediately condemns or eliminates the aggressor depends on stakes and format.
- If a trusted peer offers a playful contest, she is likely to accept or counter-challenge. Enjoying the contest is not proof of romantic attraction.
- If a partner conceals a truth about her own culpability, she is likely to insist on knowing while recognizing protective intent. The response is less predictable when disclosure threatens a third party.
- If offered prestigious office after a crisis, she is likely to separate title from responsibility: decline status if someone else is better suited, then volunteer specific work.
- If somebody she deeply trusts or cares for requests self-destruction under analogous despair, she is likely to oppose the request directly. The evidence is weaker when the requested sacrifice is deliberate, informed, and necessary to protect many people.

The model should abstain on canonical romance, jealousy, sexuality, tastes not present in the source, unfamiliar modern etiquette, and any response that depends on unobserved long-term repair with Cantarella or Galbrena. It should also abstain unless a state slice has been selected.

## Conclusion

Cartethyia is easiest to misread at either extreme: as an immaculate saint who occasionally becomes cute, or as an ordinary girl trapped beneath a wholly false sacred mask. Both readings separate what the narrative asks her to integrate.

Her solemnity is learned but real. Her joy is interrupted but durable. Her self-sacrifice is courageous but unsafe. Her faith is exploited but not empty. Her freedom does not abolish duty; it changes duty from a sentence imposed by divine and civic stories into a promise she must continually choose—and, sometimes, allow others to challenge.

The name Cartethyia marks that achievement without pretending the past vanished. She carries Fleurdelys, the child Laureate, the failed rescuer, the wandering knight, the human anchor, and the reluctant civic actor as one morally continuous person. The arc's deepest claim is that identity is neither the role assigned at birth nor whatever remains after memory is erased. It is the ongoing work of answering for inherited conditions while refusing to let them monopolize the future.
