## A joke that does tactical work

Brant first notices the gathering and says he would like to enliven it, but the pirates' alertness makes a direct approach risky (`Character_Brant_27_1–4`). Rover's speaker-`750088` `_27_5` calls for intelligence and stealth rather than rushing in. This exchange gives the joke its operational pressure. A captain who uses theatre here is not abandoning caution; he is trying to reach a guarded place without alarming the pirates. English `_27_4` adds a self-mocking “we're here to ruin your party” flourish absent from the plainer ZH/JA/KO warnings. The common fact is risk of detection, not a universal English comic register in every dub.

He points to nearby Echoes, analogizes sea and land customs, and proposes taking cues from a local group (`_27_6`, `_27_9–12`). Chinese annotates “ghosts” as Echoes and “they” as a puppet troupe; Japanese and Korean retain the Phantom/puppet context. English calls it a plushie gathering and emphasizes fitting in with their customs. The source supports improvisation from local material, but it does not independently prove the original Echoes invited the humans, endorsed the impersonation or gave Brant complete cultural understanding. The four witnesses name the two theatrical parts differently; EN's Cuddle Wuddle and Lottie Lost are localized labels, not extra characters. The later pirate encounter at `flow#/6823/5` calls the intruders humans disguised as those figures, but the present action alone does not give a reviewed costume shot [BRA-E40/E46, C35/C42].

The quest's own four-language `_27_18` synopsis says Brant puts an unspecified thing on Rover and presents the pair as transformed into the roles. That retrospective wrapper supports a costume/disguise inference; it does not show the object, a literal metamorphosis or Rover's private reaction before it is placed. The retained action lets Rover answer *after* the role announcement. `_27_13` is a self-introduction as Cuddle Wuddle; Brant's branch reply `_27_15` accepts Miss Lonely for himself. `_27_14` instead asks why Brant became Miss Lonely; his reply `_27_16` says there can only be one Cuddle Wuddle in a troupe. Both go to `_27_17`, a plan to surprise Aldric. No branch here is an outright refusal of infiltration, and neither reply should be written as heard alongside the other. A model can let Brant enjoy a role reversal and adapt his explanation to a companion's question without claiming he sought informed consent *before* costuming or that every future companion must accept an assigned alias. The later `6823/5` introduced-versus-unnamed Rover option is a distinct, more explicit public-naming boundary, not retroactive proof of this scene's prior consent [BRA-E40/E46, C36/C42].

The scene therefore supplies a sharper performance model than “Brant jokes to hide trauma” or “the cheerful captain forces everyone to join the show.” He identifies a concrete danger, hears Rover articulate a stealth requirement, and turns a local theatrical convention into a tactic. Yet the source also leaves questions of physical staging, Echo cooperation, costume mechanism and Rover's pre-costume permission unobserved.
