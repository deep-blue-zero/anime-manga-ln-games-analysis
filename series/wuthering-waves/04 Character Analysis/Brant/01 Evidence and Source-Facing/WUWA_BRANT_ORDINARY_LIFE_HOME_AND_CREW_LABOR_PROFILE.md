# Brant — ordinary life, chosen home, and the work behind a show

The charismatic captain is also a person who presses costumes, manages a crowded supper, worries about funds, asks his first mate for a day off, remembers an unfinished music lesson and learns an instrument with companions. These details are not decorative relief from the freedom theme. They show what freedom costs and how he practices it when there is no audience. They also prevent the opposite reduction: offstage life is not simply a secret, uniformly sad “true Brant” behind a fake theatrical front. Source IDs resolve in the [matrix](WUWA_BRANT_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md). Chinese is the semantic anchor; EN/JA/KO are comparison witnesses, and no performed-voice claim follows from a measured audio file.

## Work before applause

`FavorStory_120603_Content` places him late at a campfire, cleaning masks, improvising through different roles, ironing each costume edge and sorting props. His dislike of the corrupt courtier and religious acolyte does not cancel the duty to portray them as characters; respect for the stage and audience is an enacted craft constraint. The polished mask is therefore neither proof of personal dishonesty nor an effortless extension of his personality. `FavorWord_120604_Content` gives a closely related but narrower stage-world contrast: on stage he inhabits a role's entire world; offstage he becomes one participant in a larger world's story. EN's “minor character” sharpens self-minimization beyond the Chinese anchor's simpler “walks into the world's story.” It would be unsafe to infer that he regards his real life as worthless.

The same favor story calls a carved tern box and an ammonoid his most important stage prop. They were left by his parents, carry a buried name and the memory of the Pilgrim's Sail, and direct his attention toward lives beneath the performance. The box should not be treated as a magical identity device or every-prop romance token. In practical model terms, touching or putting it away might be written as *inference* from his careful handling, but a fixed habitual gesture requires runtime or repeated textual observation. Even when the object evokes grief, his preparation remains work for an audience. This is why a scene in which he skips rehearsal because “improvisation means no planning” is not grounded.

`FavorStory_120602_Content` supplies the collective version. After a long rehearsal, named crew members fish, hunt, cook, compose, practice singing and teach breath control; Brant organizes the beach meal and shares reserved wine. The children interrupt his attempted definition of a Fool with candy. Rather than giving them a doctrine of belonging through public validation, he says the members pursue freedom and need only show themselves at Carnevale. The surrounding narrative still says they hope to rebut the city's accusation that they are mad. This is a useful, unresolved tension: self-worth need not depend on Ragunna's applause, while being publicly misrecognized has real consequences. A model should not resolve it by pretending he never cares what the audience thinks or that acceptance is the sole purpose of the troupe.

The meal is not a sentimental tableau of one captain sustaining everyone. Battier, Lavito, Tina, Roccia and the children contribute different labor and resist becoming a single interchangeable “crew.” Roccia's independent stage-device authority in `FavorWord_120613_Content` and Brant's concrete crew roll call at `flow#/5238/2/9–10` reinforce this distribution. His inclusive definition of Fools is an invitation to agency, not an ownership claim over every rescued exile.

## Satisfying appetite does not abolish logistics

The archive juxtaposes one of the briefest money admissions, `FavorWord_120607_Content`, with a crew-specific drink in `FavorWord_120608_Content`. His own brewed “Strength” is sweet, Battier's spicy and Tina's refreshing. The flavors are attributed to different makers' lives; the source does not prescribe a particular chemical recipe or prove Brant is habitually intoxicated. In `FavorWord_120609_Content`, the Chinese describes his willingness to eat available foods without overthinking preference, roughly “eat when you want”; EN turns the principle toward hunger making everything taste good. Preserve adaptable appetite rather than an invented favorite dish or physiological hunger threshold. `FavorStory_120602_Content` shows actual food procurement, not a captain living on rhetoric alone.

His money trouble is real but should not be pasted onto every chronology. The Carnevale rehearsal meal and the later duel/prize material at `flow#/8703–8705` belong to distinguishable situations; the near-duplicate 8703/8704 variants are not two separate deficits. A hypothetical companion can help with provisions or be told a voyage's cost, but should not be assigned automatic financial control over the troupe. Conversely, the freedom language in `FavorWord_120610_Content` cannot make supply, risk and payment disappear.

## Returning to a city without reclaiming a house

`FavorWord_120605_Content` gives the small threshold scene: he walks a familiar Ragunna alley, remembers exclusion, and finds that the immediate desire is to buy pizza at Margherita's. Chinese and JA explicitly retain that he still likes or loves the city's taste and place; EN recasts it more indirectly as the city remaining part of him. The robust reading is a bond with home despite the institution that expelled him, not an instant forgiveness of the Order. A dinner invitation at the restaurant can therefore be ordinary and loaded at once.

The later post-Carnevale `FavorStory_120605_Content` makes freedom operational in two directions. Some troupe members leave for former homes; he blesses them. Some stay to explore; he welcomes them. Those who cannot decide receive a break. He then asks his first mate for a day off, leaves mask, costume and captain's hat behind, buys pizza, jokes with pigeons and a Sentinel statue, and visits the site of his former house. It has been sold, and he does not enter; hearing an unfamiliar piano piece tells him another family lives there. The house is not recovered. Nor does its loss prove that Ragunna itself is no longer home to him. The story ends with his chosen home in shared songs and stories, and with an invitation to new travel that others may accept or decline.

His father's interrupted piano instruction is especially important because it is easy to miswrite. He remembers being urged to find music he loves, but says he had **not learned to play** before the Pilgrim's Sail. Now he has begun learning the hurdy-gurdy with the troupe. Do not make him an accomplished pianist for an intimate scene, or turn an unfinished lesson into proof his father abandoned him. The evidence supports a line of inheritance through a question—what music belongs to him—rather than a completed skill. The KO witness's final sentence can be read as saying he returned to his original house, unlike the Chinese/EN/JA ending that has him charting a new course; the earlier KO sentence still says he did not enter the sold house. For this material conclusion, keep the Chinese anchor and note the localization inconsistency rather than invent a property return.

For a crossover or hypothetical romance, the strongest test is not whether a suitor enjoys his theatrical charm. It is whether they can recognize crew labor, accept that he has a prior home and a chosen new one, ask for an honest accounting of danger, and decline his invitation without being cast as betraying freedom. He may admire someone taking a different route. This is a scene-writing affordance, not evidence of canonical partner selection. Runtime blocking, human four-dub listening and exact optional-route traversal could revise the weight of warmth or hesitation, but are not supplied by these textual witnesses alone.
