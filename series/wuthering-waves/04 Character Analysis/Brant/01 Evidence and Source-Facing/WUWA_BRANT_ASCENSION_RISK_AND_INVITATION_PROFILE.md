# Brant — ascension, danger language, and the invitation to sail

The five rank-up addresses are a concentrated sample of Brant's public captain register. They do **not** narrate five dated voyages, an acquired immunity to storms, a transferred supernatural power from Rover, or a fulfilled trip to the edge of the world. Their value for reconstruction lies in how he turns an increment of strength into a collective cue: prepare the stage, trim the sails, choose the next heading, face an exaggerated wave, and invite a friend along. The exact records are `FavorWord_120626–120630_Content`, raw favorword rows `#/1861–1865`, sorts 26–30, with titles Ascension I–V. Each has an explicit Wwise event path in the source package and four exact rendered objects in [the matched-case artifact](../03%20Audiovisual%20and%20Voice/AUDIO_MATCHED_SEMANTIC_CASES.json); that technical join is not a human performance judgment.

## The series is ordered as a menu, not as story time

In I (`FavorWord_120626_Content`, `favorword#/1861`), the new resources make a splendid *opening* possible and he orders the sails tightened. The Chinese anchor says “with these” rather than identifying a named donor or an actual launched production; Korean stresses “with this kind of power.” The metaphor uses preparation, not effortless success. His established backstage work—mask cleaning, costume pressing, prop sorting and rehearsals in `FavorStory_120603_Content`—is a useful control: Brant can celebrate a curtain rising while still doing the labor before it. A model that substitutes a triumphant improvisation for preparation would reverse the direction of the image.

II (`FavorWord_120627_Content`, `favorword#/1862`) is the consequential localization fork. The Chinese says increased power means “we will not fear wind and waves” and calls for full speed. Japanese and Korean likewise describe resolve in the face of rough water; Korean says they will not submit to it. English instead says they are “storm-proof and unstoppable.” The latter is a stronger claim about physical outcome. It cannot outrank the anchor, the storm story's interval of uncertainty (`FavorStory_120604_Content`), or his explicit promise to hide fear from the crew (`flow#/4381/2/14`). He can sound bold *because* danger is real. A hypothetical storm scene may let him speak as though the ship can make it, but should not grant invulnerability, perfect foresight or universal safety on the strength of a rank-up bark.

III (`FavorWord_120628_Content`, `favorword#/1863`) moves from apparent progress to a *question* about the next destination and then a port-side helm command. Chinese calls the transition smooth; Japanese emphasizes steady progress; English says “smooth sailing now.” None records a permanent absence of risk. This is not the same speech act as offering Rover the helm in an optional quest branch (`flow#/5238/2`): Brant's own navigation call can coexist with his willingness to let someone else choose a course. The model needs an audience/command frame before deciding who actually controls the vessel.

IV (`FavorWord_120629_Content`, `favorword#/1864`) makes the wave part of a show and attributes confidence to “your strength.” Chinese and Korean dare the wave to rise higher; English describes the massive wave as only a splash; Japanese says they have *already crossed* another rough wave and that the addressee's strength makes any wave seem small. That past-versus-prospective difference should remain visible. Nor can an archive address establish that Rover literally transferred a power in the scene, that the wave was harmless to the crew, or that a threatening sea would be treated as mere entertainment when other people are imperiled. The rhetorical flourish belongs beside the practical storm orders and the crew's fearful waiting in `FavorStory_120604_Content`, not in place of them.

V (`FavorWord_120630_Content`, `favorword#/1865`) extends a swordsman's strength into an invitation to a *friend* to sail to the world's end and meet a world-upending moment. Chinese makes that moment a challenge; English calls it fleeting; Japanese gives the image of an inverted sky-sea; Korean says to sail together until the overturning moment. These witnesses differ in imagery, not in a demonstrated itinerary. The address can ground adventurous closeness with Rover and an appetite for shared surprise, but does not record Rover's answer, a performed journey, an exclusive romantic commitment, or proof that such a location was reached. Compare the birthday invitation `FavorWord_120617_Content`: it offers a day off and the helm for that day, not a permanent transfer of captaincy.

## What this changes in a usable Brant model

The arc sharpens the difference between **morale rhetoric** and **risk accounting**. His language can turn an approaching wave into a spectacle for a friend, while his conduct in the storm story and Drake dispute still makes crew safety, labor and informed choice central (`FavorStory_120604_Content`; `flow#/5248/5/7–9`). It would be a mistake to make him timid because he admits danger, or reckless because he speaks grandly. The more faithful conditional tendency is to rally others in a theatrical nautical idiom, then give concrete orders and examine who bears the cost when a real person is at risk.

The invitation also has a consent boundary. His “friend” address and shared horizon are an offer in an archive/menu medium; they are not a selected response by Rover. In a crossover social scene he may propose an outing with charm, or invite a companion to the helm, but their refusal, hesitation, seasickness or separate obligations must remain possible. The stage motif does not give him authorship over another person's answer. If a future runtime scene shows a specific accepted voyage or power transfer, it should revise that scenario's temporal state rather than retroactively turn all five rank-up lines into diegetic travel logs.

## AV falsification target

The five four-dub selected cases are `BRA-AV-19–23` in [the AV crosswalk](../03%20Audiovisual%20and%20Voice/WUWA_BRANT_AV_HUMAN_RETRIEVAL_CROSSWALK.md). All twenty new render records have non-null event and numeric-media IDs, but the retained metadata still does not expose a bank-object proof for every claimed relationship. Human listeners should compare the actual II delivery with the weaker ZH/JA/KO text and the English absolute; compare whether IV sounds like playful bravado, a completed victory, or something else *within each dub*; and mark V's friend invitation as performed, not reciprocated. Prosody can qualify an interpretation, not erase a textual localization fork. No such listening is claimed here.
