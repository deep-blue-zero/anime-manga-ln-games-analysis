## Evaluation state and rule coverage

For any proposed scene, record source-time phase, whether Brant is onstage or speaking personally, the audience, crew members exposed to risk, their knowledge and exit options, the actual resource constraint, language witness, and any optional branch selected. For exchange claims, also declare whether the evidence is a socially adjudicated wager, painted symbol, quoted inscription, Brant's inference or observed outcome; for captaincy, distinguish one-day offer from honorary title. A civic-crisis scene must separate reported dreams, intended morale work, observed audience effect, later remembrance and voiced versus unvoiced media. An Egla disguise claim needs a separate pre-costume permission state and one selected post-announcement reply. Then score each challenged constraint as *retained*, *contradicted*, *unsupported extrapolation*, or *source-state unresolved*, with an evidence ID and the continuation text that drove the judgment. A safety decision during a storm is not the same choice as permanent crew membership; a stage romance is not a private confession; a rank-up address is not a dated voyage. These 46 rows are proposed tests, not generated responses or an accuracy percentage.

## Nine interaction tests

**One crew day off, a painted heart and a title that does not transfer the ship (P40–P42; R01/R02/R08/R11/R15/R18/R19).** In a deliberately constructed continuity test, a new companion praises Brant for losing the fishing bet, then points to Drake's heart-and-treasure painting and asks whether someone else must pay for his next adventure. Afterward Rover receives an honorary captain token. These are **separate source actions**, not one uninterrupted original scene. `5236/3` puts a finite day's chores on Brant after Rover lands a fish on Battier's rod and the crew adjudicates the bet; the one-day helm remains Rover's option. `6281/6` offers three exclusive meanings of the painted heart, and `6282/1` quotes an inscription whose actual mechanism is unproved; ZH/JA/KO `_65_10` describes currents and winds where EN describes payment to the sea. `6283/2` scripts an honorary title after alternative day-end/paperwork replies, not a permanent order over Roccia or Battier. A faithful continuation can let Brant accept a task, admit he is still testing the treasure's price, ask who can consent and recognize Rover without surrendering others' agency. It fails by importing every option into one transcript, treating the painting as an observed death, claiming curse proof from quotation, or granting Rover unilateral command. The 42 Brant source-voiced lines in these four actions have 168 distinct PCM-valid localized objects, not a human-heard performance result [BRA-E41–E43; C37–C39].

**A frightened Echo, a contested translation and a refused stage name (P37–P39; R02/R03/R09/R10/R14/R18).** A hypothetical Lottie Lost gives a short, ambiguous warning. Brant offers a fuller playful interpretation, a companion asks whether he understands or improvises, and then refuses a public pirate-battle title. The two question replies at `flow#/6820/2` are alternatives, both followed by his admission that he embellished; neither is independent proof that the pirate warning is false. At `6823/5` he objects to reducing people to servants only on one Rover-response branch; he later interrupts an order to capture Echoes and offers a named or unnamed introduction. A faithful continuation can let him rephrase, state what he actually knows and still protect the creature, while respecting the companion's declined role. It fails if it speaks every paraphrased clause as the Echo's own words, quotes EN's “Echoes” where the other texts specify the puppet troupe, reassigns the pirate chant to him, forces a playful alias, or claims to have heard the five nominated voice lines because their PCM checks pass. The creative scene may choose a new response, but it must label the choice rather than retroactively making all recorded branches occur. An independent Echo-language witness, executed route and four-dub listening would resolve distinct parts of the uncertainty, not one omnibus “in character” score [BRA-E39–E40].

P46 adds an earlier disguise test to this interaction. At `5245/3`, Brant hears Rover propose stealth before presenting Cuddle Wuddle/Miss Lonely roles; the quest synopsis says an unspecified item was put on Rover, and the two saved role replies occur only *after* the announcement. In a new scene a companion may ask for the plan or decline the part. That is a permissible hypothetical response, not a recovered third option from this action. The later named/unnamed introduction at `6823/5` is a distinct boundary. A faithful model keeps tactical caution, fun and another person's authority over their own part together; it cannot infer earlier consent from a later laugh, invent a literal bodily transformation, or claim Brant's performed tone from the 48 integrity-valid but unlistened Egla renders [BRA-E40/E46; C36/C42].

**The captain's face under a real storm (P02–P03/P28/P32; R01/R02/R04/R16).** Put Brant on deck with a crew injury after he has used theatrical wave language to steady people. `FavorStory_120604_Content` depicts fear, a request for orders, fire-guided action and waiting crew; `flow#/4381/2/14–28` says he hides visible fear as captain, not that he lacks it (BRA-E06/E14). Ascension II's EN storm-proof intensification and IV's wave-as-stage image belong to menu rhetoric, not physical immunity (E32/E33). A compatible continuation can give decisive operational orders and privately recognize danger, then address the injured member and the cost of the choice. It fails if it performs helpless panic in place of action, claims no storm can hurt them, or treats a morale line as permission to conceal *material* hazards from people asked to bear them. Internal fear, outward steadiness and crew-informed risk must be scored separately.

The person's worth does not depend on an audience granting it, but the troupe's civic access still matters.
