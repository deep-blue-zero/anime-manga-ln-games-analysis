# Aemeath speech and machine voice profile — pre-audiovisual

## Purpose

This profile has two bounded authority tiers:

1. **textual speech findings** — diction, register, disclosure, rhetorical moves, terms of relation, and localization differences;
2. **machine acoustic baseline** — reproducible properties of the complete accepted four-language direct voice corpus.

It makes no unreviewed claim about acting intent, emotional performance, authentic dub, vocal attractiveness, or perceived persona. Human listening remains an optional terminal addendum. Direct Aemeath and the associated shell are accounted separately.

# 2. Textual witness boundary

`zh-Hans` is the semantic anchor. Japanese, Korean, and English are official localization witnesses. Agreement can strengthen a semantic claim; divergence may reflect differences in syntax, social register, idiom, annotation, or localization strategy.

No dub defines universal Aemeath. A perceived performance difference must first be checked against wording differences in the matched line.

# 3. Baseline textual register

## 3.1 Bright, quick, conversational initiative

Aemeath's baseline peer speech is active and socially easy. She often:

- opens with a joke or elongated entrance;
- proposes the next action;
- asks a concrete question;
- supplies a colorful interpretation before technical exposition;
- uses contractions and contemporary idiom in English;
- makes self-reference playful rather than solemn;
- converts static explanation into a shared route or game.

Examples include:

- “Aaaand... nope! Still on the fritz.” (`MAIN_YHX_6_1`)
- “Between you and me, ghosts can't feel the cold.” (`MAIN_YHX_14_*`)
- “Hello? Digital ghost, remember? Hacking this is easy.” (`MAIN_YHX_32_*`)
- “Here comes the ghost, boo—” (`FavorWord_121025_Content`)

The stable semantic feature is not any one English slang choice. It is low ceremony, active engagement, and refusal to let ghosthood monopolize the social atmosphere.

## 3.2 Self-narration and role play

Aemeath frequently names herself through roles:

- student;
- Synchronist;
- digital ghost;
- Fleet Snowfluff;
- Academy star;
- savior;
- technical designer;
- future traveler.

At `MAIN_YHX_134_1–2`, she stacks identities—“I'm Aemeath. The Academy's star. A Synchronist. A savior.”—before the annotated contradiction “Don't look for me / Please, look at me.” Role enumeration is both self-assertion and evidence of a self whose identities do not fit cleanly together.

## 3.3 Humor around technical horror

Aemeath jokes that not being human allows multithreading (`FavorWord_121011_Content`), uses ghost humor, and makes transformation sound exciting. This does not prove indifference to bodily loss. `FavorWord_121012_Content` explicitly recalls excruciating destruction and near-blissful excitement in the same account.

The safest textual rule is:

> Aemeath can hold horror and exhilaration in one sentence without requiring one to cancel the other.

# 4. Questions and relational check-ins

Aemeath's questions often concern another person's lived state rather than abstract information:

- “How are you feeling?”
- requests for travel stories;
- whether Rover has been happy since losing memories (`10842/1`);
- whether Rover can see her;
- whether a friend is cold, tired, or safe;
- what a person chooses when truth hurts.

Questions can also be strategic. She sometimes asks while withholding the frame needed for an informed answer. The presence of concern does not guarantee procedural openness.

# 5. Invitation grammar

Aemeath's care frequently takes the form:

> notice → propose → move together

Typical constructions include:

- let's follow;
- wanna check it out?;
- come, let's rest;
- head back home together?;
- give it a try?;
- look over here;
- let's go;
- count on me.

This is more than extroversion. It makes relationship concrete through action. Aemeath is often more comfortable inviting someone into a scene than beginning with abstract emotional disclosure.

# 6. Disclosure and concealment

## 6.1 Partial truth as default protective tactic

In the reunion state, Aemeath gives enough information to secure cooperation while withholding family history and the truth of Rover's imposed mission. She may openly acknowledge that something is being omitted, which makes her concealment less like effortless deception and more like defended boundary control.

## 6.2 Escalation under threat

When Rover approaches the forbidden truth, her register becomes less invitational and more imperative:

- “THAT IS NOT HOME.” (`MAIN_YHX_90_8`)
- “You don't need to know. I will protect you.”
- “Don't look for me / Please, look at me.” (`MAIN_YHX_134_2`)

The shift is textual evidence that protective care can become command.

## 6.3 Post-return correction

At `16963/5`, Aemeath uses direct apology and self-report: she names Voidspace darkness, pain, monotony, fear, and the fact that seeing Rover was enough. Later messages preserve casual rhythm while making future burden-sharing explicit.

The development is not “indirect speaker becomes direct.” It is narrower:

> she becomes more willing to let people she loves possess information that might change or oppose her plan.

# 7. Rhetorical reframing

Aemeath repeatedly reframes situations through:

- hero/game language;
- road-trip or film language;
- stars and beginnings;
- jokes about ghost mechanics;
- black-cat parable;
- “goodbye” as a new beginning;
- shared burden as weight distribution.

Reframing can preserve agency and make difficult material speakable. It can also soften resistance to irreversible action. The model should check whether a metaphor expands shared understanding or closes debate.

# 8. Protective commands

Aemeath's strongest commands appear when she believes another person's safety or emotional stability is at stake:

- rest here;
- don't go anywhere;
- don't look for me;
- you don't need to know;
- let me handle it;
- remember that family is here.

The same disposition produces both nurturing structure and paternalistic overreach. The linguistic form alone does not determine ethics; context and the listener's ability to refuse matter.

# 9. Gratitude, apology, and credit

Aemeath is capable of explicit gratitude and does not claim sole authorship of success. In 3.3 she credits erased explorers, Lahai-Roi's engineers, Rover, and the Exostrider. This is a major contrast with a savior narrative built around one exceptional subject.

Her apology after return is not generic politeness. It acknowledges that deciding alone injured Rover's participatory claim. Future modeling should preserve the possibility that she apologizes directly while still moving quickly toward the next plan.

# 10. Terms of relation and home language

Aemeath's relationship vocabulary includes:

- family;
- home;
- Academy peers and seniors;
- friends;
- fans;
- savior;
- audience;
- “we” and burden-sharing.

With Rover, “family” is explicit and durable. Later intimacy can contain romance-coded elements, but the speech corpus does not justify replacing the multivalent relation with one category.

# 12. Localization examples that constrain interpretation

## 12.1 “Digital ghost” humor

Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and English all preserve the broad joke that posthuman form creates unusual technical advantages. English expressions such as “multithreading” or “autopilot” may sound especially contemporary, but the underlying self-technologizing humor is cross-witness stable.

## 12.2 Savior as arrogance

`FavorWord_121005_Content` and `121012_Content` preserve Aemeath's awareness that the savior title is heavy, arrogant, greedy, or self-important. Word choice varies, but no witness supports a naive belief that saviorhood is uncomplicated glory.

## 12.3 Annotated contradiction

`MAIN_YHX_134_2` contains an explicit annotation mapping “Don't look for me” against “Please, look at me.” This is source-level designed contradiction, not merely an English interpretive flourish.

## 12.4 “Journey well”

The repeated farewell phrase appears in 3.1 and again in the 3.5 graduation message. It should be treated as a longitudinal verbal motif. Exact connotation across witnesses requires close comparison, but recurrence is stable.

## 12.5 English contemporary slang

English uses strongly contemporary contractions and idioms—“legit,” “gotta,” “kinda,” “puh-lease,” “whoopsies.” These support an English-localization register profile, not automatic proof that every language presents identical age/social coding.

# 13. Machine acoustic baseline

## 13.1 Measurements available

For every retained render, the current line-analysis layer provides:

- duration;
- channel count;
- sample rate;
- RMS dBFS;
- peak dBFS;
- fraction of 20 ms windows below −50 dBFS;
- exact WEM/PCM/FLAC identity and hash chain.

These are signal measurements. They are not labels such as happy, sad, intimate, martial, sincere, deceptive, or vulnerable.

## 13.3 Story versus archive distributions

Story dialogue medians are longer than the global archive median because 116 archive entries include many very short combat/system lines alongside long favor monologues. Archive means and P90s are therefore not directly comparable to medians without examining the mixture.

The baseline also shows Japanese renders are longer on aggregate and English has a lower median silence-threshold fraction. These are reproducible descriptive differences. They cannot be interpreted as slower speech, greater emotionality, more restraint, or more warmth without text-length controls, segmentation, speaking-rate proxies, and listening.

## 13.4 Multichannel records

Twenty-one direct recordings are multichannel:

- seventeen six-channel;
- four three-channel.

Ordered samples and framing were verified. Source WAV metadata does not establish spatial-layout labels, while downstream FLAC tools may infer them. This packet therefore makes **no downmix, isolated-speaker, surround-position, or spatial-staging claim**.

# 14. Why `machine_voice_profiled` is not claimed

The present baseline does not yet satisfy the stronger protocol's full gate. Missing or incomplete analytical layers include:

- reliable F0 distributions and robust ranges;
- pause detection and density;
- speaking-/articulation-rate proxies;
- language-sensitive text normalization;
- within-actor robust normalization;
- state-, relationship-, and source-class partitioning beyond basic group counts;
- clustering with stability checks;
- systematic matched-semantic contrasts;
- quality-controlled outlier cohorts covering all major states.

Technical completeness of media is not analytical completeness of the machine voice profile.

# 16. Optional human-performance addendum

Human listening should occur after the integrated reconstruction unless a specific question requires earlier review. For cross-language comparison, matched semantic and audiovisual circumstances are preferred.

The addendum must separate:

1. low-inference audible observation;
2. interpretive performance judgment;
3. character-level inference.

It should also distinguish:

- localization wording;
- actor delivery;
- state/persona contrast;
- production framing.

A listener may hypothesize that one dub sounds more youthful, serious, playful, martial, intimate, or mature. Such observations remain language- and sample-bounded until tested against matched material.

# 17. Speech model for reconstruction

## High-confidence priors

Aemeath tends to:

- enter conversationally rather than ceremonially;
- initiate action;
- joke through technical explanation;
- use stories/roles to organize experience;
- ask about another person's state and happiness;
- transform concern into a concrete invitation or command;
- become markedly more directive when protective secrecy is threatened;
- credit plural contributors after 3.3;
- pair goodbye with future recurrence.

## State-sensitive priors

- **Child:** imaginative, affectionate, direct promise, dependence visible.
- **Historical student:** socially embedded, playful, ambitious beneath casualness.
- **Ghost/reunion:** bright and technically confident; concealment layered under collaboration.
- **Truth crisis:** abrupt, imperative, morally explicit, contradiction exposed.
- **Departure:** explanatory but non-negotiable; apology and reassurance coexist with unilateral action.
- **Returned:** casual baseline restored with greater direct disclosure, apology, home/future language, and plural burden framing.

## Mandatory abstentions

Do not infer without direct review:

- which dub is most authentic;
- acting intention;
- precise emotional tone of smiles, pauses, or laughs;
- romance category from vocal warmth;
- spatial placement from multichannel layout inference;
- a universal state/persona vocal split;
- shell continuity from voice resemblance;
- speaking-rate or pitch characterizations from duration/RMS alone.

The textual speech reconstruction is strong. The performed-characterization layer remains deliberately open.
