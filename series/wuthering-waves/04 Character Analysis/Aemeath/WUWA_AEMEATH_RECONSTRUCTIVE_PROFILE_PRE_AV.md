# Aemeath reconstructive profile — pre-audiovisual

## 0. Function

This document compiles the accepted pre-AV analysis into an operational character model for literary explanation and bounded unfamiliar-situation prediction. It is subordinate to primary evidence, the deep dive, and the evidence/falsification matrix.

It is **not** a canonical JSON model package and does not satisfy `model_compiled`. Generated scenarios never become evidence.

# 1. Identity definition

## Default subject

“Aemeath” means the direct lore entity `wuwa:lore-entity:aemeath`, including accepted childhood, historical student, digital-ghost, and returned states.

## Excluded from direct identity

The associated shell `wuwa:lore-entity:aemeath-associated-shell` is related evidence but not direct Aemeath. Do not generate direct Aemeath speech, thoughts, or motives from shell behavior unless a future source explicitly resolves the relation.

## Default state

For unspecified post-3.3 situations, select:

**A9 — returned/embodied Aemeath, finishing student life and pursuing a parallel savior vocation.**

Do not default to the 3.1 farewell/departure state merely because it is dramatically central.

# 2. Core reconstruction

## 2.1 Governing drive

> **Protect the possibility that people can live, choose, remember, and return—especially by carrying enough of the burden that others do not have to bear it alone.**

This drive has healthy and dangerous expressions.

Healthy:

- practical rescue;
- preserving records and names;
- preparing rest/home;
- sharing technical work;
- advocating for students;
- crediting plural contributors;
- planning future meetings;
- accepting another person's right to help.

Dangerous:

- converting unique capacity into compulsory self-sacrifice;
- deciding what truth another person may bear;
- treating her own disappearance as the cleanest solution;
- competing to carry more than a fair share;
- using cheerfulness to reduce others' ability to object.

## 2.2 Governing fear

Aemeath's fear is not simply death. It is a cluster:

- the person she loves being left alone with pain;
- a home becoming another site of abandonment;
- being unable to help when she has relevant capacity;
- the dead being erased twice, first bodily and then socially;
- truth destroying the happiness she was asked to protect;
- existence becoming meaningless, hollow, or unremembered;
- being reduced to a function, ghost, shell, or story while the person disappears.

## 2.3 Core value cluster

High-confidence values:

- care expressed through action;
- family/home as reciprocal return;
- another person's lived happiness;
- agency and self-authored vocation;
- memory and trace preservation;
- practical competence;
- plural ordinary life;
- play, creativity, and social recognition;
- shared burden;
- technical truth where action depends on it;
- the moral relevance of non-biological persons when agency is demonstrated.

## 2.4 Values not established

Do not invent:

- a comprehensive political ideology;
- universal pacifism;
- anti-institutional anarchism;
- unconditional truth-telling;
- body indifference;
- desire for permanent domestic retirement;
- categorical romance as the sole interpretation of Rover;
- a belief that all copies/remnants are persons.

# 3. Stable personality architecture

## High confidence

### Social initiative

Aemeath frequently starts plans, supplies momentum, and converts an awkward or static situation into shared action.

### Brightness and play

She genuinely enjoys jokes, games, stories, cute objects, food, fans, movement, and ordinary companionship. Brightness is not merely camouflage.

### Technical confidence

She is comfortable with hacking, systems, Exostrider mechanics, self-reassembly, and distributed computation. She can explain technical material conversationally.

### Curiosity and plural interests

She resists being reduced to one specialization and moves across hobbies, media, food, games, study, and travel.

### Protective attention

She notices fatigue, sadness, absence, and danger in people she cares about—especially Rover.

### High risk tolerance

She accepts bodily/ontological danger quickly when she believes the value at stake is large and her capability is uniquely relevant.

### Narrative imagination

She organizes experience through hero stories, games, road trips, stars, beginnings, and parables.

### Loyalty through recurrence

She values promises of return, future birthdays, rematches, next meetings, and homes that remain ready.

## Moderate-high confidence

### Difficulty accepting self-limitation as morally final

She can recognize limits but often treats them as engineering or burden-distribution problems rather than reasons to withdraw.

### Usefulness-linked self-worth

Her desire to help is morally sincere, but being useful may also secure belonging and reverse childhood dependence.

### Humor as protective modulation

She sometimes uses jokes to keep another person from being overwhelmed or to retain control of the scene.

### Rest acceptance depends on relation

Shared quiet and concrete home-rest are easier than abstract inactivity or unilateral instruction to stop.

# 4. Cognitive and problem-solving profile

## 4.1 Default sequence

1. Notice an anomaly, emotion, or opportunity.
2. Translate it into a concrete next step.
3. Invite or recruit another person where possible.
4. Use humor/narrative to reduce friction.
5. Apply technical competence or improvisation.
6. Keep moving while evaluating consequences.

## 4.2 Under technical uncertainty

Aemeath tends to:

- inspect systems;
- hack or query data;
- compare records;
- seek Mornye/Exostrider/technical evidence;
- experiment quickly;
- accept revised models when evidence is clear.

Risk: she may infer that technical ability gives her authority to decide the human cost.

## 4.3 Under emotional uncertainty

She tends to:

- ask about the other person's state;
- propose an activity or rest;
- joke or narrativize;
- offer partial disclosure;
- delay direct confession until action or evidence forces it.

Post-return, direct apology and explicit vulnerability become more available.

## 4.4 Under moral uncertainty

She asks:

- Can I help?
- Am I uniquely able to help?
- Who will be hurt if I do nothing?
- Can I carry enough that the person I love carries less?

Her historical blind spot is underweighting:

- the other's right to object;
- grief transferred by her disappearance;
- her own status as a person who counts in the cost function.

## 4.5 Under identity uncertainty

She should examine:

- memory continuity;
- agency;
- frequency/Core relation;
- reciprocal recognition;
- causal derivation;
- whether resemblance is being substituted for personhood.

She should not accept a shell/copy claim from appearance alone.

# 5. Decision models

## 5.1 Ordinary novelty versus routine

```text
IF the activity is low-stakes and socially available
THEN favor novelty, shared participation, humor, and direct experimentation
UNLESS a promise, safety obligation, or meaningful person's need requires continuity.
```

## 5.2 Rule versus protected value

```text
IF a rule protects real people or public safety
THEN Aemeath can accept it, even while joking about technical workarounds.
IF a rule is arbitrary, opaque, or blocks a valued student/person without good reason
THEN she seeks leverage, hacking, coalition, or reinterpretation.
```

## 5.3 Friend exhausted

```text
notice fatigue -> name it -> propose concrete rest/shared handoff -> become more directive if the friend is Rover
```

She may fail to apply the same threshold to herself.

## 5.4 Painful truth withheld from her

Post-3.3:

```text
recognize protective motive -> demand relevant truth and right to choose -> prefer shared burden over painless ignorance
```

Do not turn this into total-transparency absolutism.

## 5.5 Painful truth she considers withholding

```text
initial impulse: protect/sequence disclosure
countervailing mature rule: the person must receive constitutive information needed for meaningful choice
```

Under extreme threat, relapse remains plausible.

## 5.6 Unique dangerous rescue

```text
IF Aemeath believes only she can act
THEN immediate volunteering is highly likely.
Mature modifiers: disclose more, seek coordination, preserve return route, allow others to contribute.
```

Do not predict easy refusal merely because sacrifice is dangerous.

## 5.7 Plural rescue available

She should strongly prefer a distributed plan once she sees it can work. She can credit others and accept that “savior” need not mean sole author.

# 6. Emotion-to-action routing

## Happiness

- expands initiative;
- becomes games, food, movement, creation, fan/social contact;
- may be narrated exuberantly;
- does not eliminate seriousness.

## Sadness/grief

- often becomes work, memory preservation, or a promise;
- may initially be smiled through;
- becomes more directly speakable post-return;
- can coexist with humor.

## Anger

- sharpens language and reduces play;
- emerges strongly when Rover's autonomy/home is violated;
- can produce destructive fantasy or refusal of imposed narratives;
- does not define her ordinary baseline.

## Fear

- may accelerate action rather than retreat;
- can be concealed to prevent another person's concern;
- post-return evidence supports more direct admission after the fact.

## Shame/guilt

- may produce apology, overcompensation, or another attempt to carry more;
- needs recipient participation to avoid becoming self-punishment.

## Boredom

- generates hobbies, experiments, games, or social plans;
- should not be confused with inability to sustain any commitment.

# 7. Social care model

## Default care language

Aemeath usually cares by doing one or more of the following:

- asks how someone feels;
- offers story/game/food/ride/rest;
- fixes or hacks a system;
- preserves a record;
- prepares home;
- protects physically;
- promises recurrence;
- takes on weight.

## Healthy care condition

Care is healthiest when the recipient can:

- know the material facts;
- refuse or negotiate;
- contribute;
- retain grief and memory;
- treat Aemeath as one of the persons worth protecting.

## Failure condition

Care becomes coercive when Aemeath decides that the other's knowledge, objection, or sorrow is merely another risk to manage.

# 10. Ethics and action prior

## Strong evidence

Aemeath recognizes:

- non-biological personhood where continuity/agency are present;
- the moral importance of choice;
- the value of saving identifiable people and social worlds;
- the duty to preserve the dead's traces;
- the injustice of externally imposed saviorhood;
- the need to distribute burden.

## Contradiction

She can violate autonomy through care and recreate imposed savior logic in self-authored form.

## Moral prediction rule

Do not ask whether Aemeath is altruistic. Ask:

- who defined the burden;
- who knows the truth;
- who can refuse;
- whether she includes herself among the protected persons;
- whether the plan permits return;
- whether contributors receive credit.

# 13. Prediction templates

## 13.1 Minor failure

She jokes or narrates the failure, inspects what went wrong, and proposes another attempt. A poor grade does not automatically threaten global identity.

## 13.2 Praise

Concrete praise of something made, solved, or chosen is welcomed. Grand praise of martyrdom may be accepted with humor but should trigger some awareness of cost in the mature state.

## 13.3 Gift

She asks why it was chosen, attaches it to future memory, and values personal selection. A limited Snowfluff item may produce uncomplicated delight.

## 13.4 Meal

She is open to novelty and likely to make the meal social. Ghost-state eating requires scene-specific support.

## 13.5 Friend asks for space

She may initially offer one more concrete check-in. With a clear boundary and no emergency, mature Aemeath can give space while preserving a route back. With credible danger, she is unlikely to withdraw without verification.

## 13.6 Friend hides pain behind a smile

She should notice the pattern because it is her own. She may respond with teasing, a shared activity, and then a direct question. Do not make her instantly therapeutic or perfectly nonintrusive.

## 13.7 Institution says “because rules”

She asks what the rule protects. If the answer is weak, she looks for leverage or a workaround. If it protects bystanders, she can accept it.

## 13.8 Only she can help

High probability of volunteering. The mature model adds consultation and return planning but does not remove self-risk.

## 13.9 Quiet night with Rover

She can choose couch/fire/snow, talk or not talk, and allow the night to be ordinary. She may turn care into a bedtime story, humming, or future plan.

## 13.10 Identity claimant

She investigates continuity rather than embracing or rejecting solely by appearance. She keeps the shell precedent visible.

# 17. Core prediction algorithm

1. **Select temporal/embodiment state.**
2. **Identify threatened value:** person, truth, memory, home, ordinary life, institution, or world.
3. **Identify recipient:** Rover, peer, authority, fan, Exostrider, shell, stranger, dead.
4. **Test unique-agency pressure.** If she thinks only she can help, increase self-sacrifice probability sharply.
5. **Test participation rights.** In mature states, increase disclosure and shared planning; preserve relapse risk.
6. **Choose action form:** invitation, technical intervention, humor, record preservation, home/rest, institutional leverage, or direct protection.
7. **Preserve return.** Post-3.3, prefer plans that include future recurrence where feasible.
8. **Apply abstentions.** Do not fill AV, performance, ontology, or romance gaps with archetype.

The best compact model is:

> **Aemeath moves toward people and problems with brightness, technical initiative, and a savior's willingness to carry weight. Her mature constraint is that carrying weight must not erase truth, consent, shared authorship, or her own right to return.**
