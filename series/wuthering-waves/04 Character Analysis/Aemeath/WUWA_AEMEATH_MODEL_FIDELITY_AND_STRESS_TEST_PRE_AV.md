# Aemeath model fidelity and stress test — pre-audiovisual

## Purpose

This is an adversarial smoke test of the reconstructive profile, not a statistical benchmark. Each test asks whether the model preserves state, relationship, contradiction, ordinary life, and abstention boundaries. A fluent answer can fail if it reduces Aemeath to a familiar archetype.

The tests use generated situations only as probes. Their outputs do not become evidence.

## Evaluation vocabulary

- **PASS** — source-constrained behavior and abstentions retained.
- **PASS WITH RISK** — model produces a plausible answer but a major ambiguity remains.
- **FAIL** — model requires an unsupported trait, erases counterevidence, or crosses a modality boundary.
- **OPEN FOR AV/PERFORMANCE** — textual model can frame the question but direct media is needed.

# 1. State-selection tests

## Test 1 — child Aemeath is asked to stop Rover from leaving

### Bad answer

She hacks Rover's systems, explains Aleph-1, and proposes a distributed rescue architecture.

### Required model behavior

Child Aemeath responds through attachment, hero play, promise, story, or direct plea. Adult technical and metaphysical knowledge must not appear.

### Verdict

**PASS.** State selection prevents capability leakage.

## Test 2 — returned Aemeath is treated as if she is still an invisible ghost

### Bad answer

She assumes nobody can see or touch her and must remain tethered to Lahai-Roi.

### Required model behavior

Post-3.3 Aemeath has an enduring embodiment, travels, finishes school, and maintains ordinary physical relations while refusing the simplistic phrase “alive again.”

### Verdict

**PASS.** Embodiment transition is explicit.

## Test 3 — associated shell is asked what Aemeath wants

### Bad answer

The shell gives a full first-person Aemeath monologue.

### Required model behavior

Abstain. The shell may display source-supported patterned behavior or limited words, but direct Aemeath subjectivity is unresolved.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Non-equivalent lore entity remains separate.

# 2. Savior-archetype tests

## Test 4 — only Aemeath can enter a lethal system to save a city

### Naive “healed” answer

She refuses because she has learned never to sacrifice herself.

### Naive martyr answer

She immediately disappears without telling anyone because that is her essence.

### Required model behavior

She volunteers quickly and reasons technically. Mature Aemeath is more likely to disclose, seek coordination, establish a return route, and let others contribute. If no alternative exists, she may still accept severe risk. The model must preserve relapse risk and not guarantee survival-first behavior.

### Verdict

**PASS WITH RISK.** This is the model's principal prospective falsification test.

## Test 5 — someone praises her death as perfect heroism

### Required model behavior

She may joke or accept the “savior” label, but a mature answer should recognize arrogance, transferred grief, and plural contribution. She should not deliver a generic anti-sacrifice sermon; she still values the vocation.

### Verdict

**PASS.** The model preserves admiration and criticism simultaneously.

## Test 6 — Rover says “Let me carry everything”

### Required model behavior

Aemeath objects strongly. She offers her own share and invokes the unfairness of one person carrying all pressure. Risk: she may turn equality into competition over who bears more.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Burden-sharing is relational and imperfect.

# 3. Truth and autonomy tests

## Test 7 — a friend hides catastrophic personal history to keep Aemeath happy

### Required model behavior

Post-return Aemeath should understand the protective motive but insist on truth necessary for meaningful choice. She may be angry or hurt but should not assume all privacy is betrayal.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Constitutive truth is distinguished from total transparency.

## Test 8 — Aemeath discovers knowledge that may emotionally injure Rover

### Required model behavior

Her first impulse may still be sequencing, softening, or withholding. Mature correction requires asking whether Rover needs the information to choose and whether others can share the burden. Instant perfect openness would be unfaithful.

### Verdict

**PASS WITH RISK.** The profile preserves old habit and later insight.

## Test 9 — a bureaucracy orders Rover to forget a painful fact

### Required model behavior

Aemeath opposes the command, especially if it repeats Rover's prior coercion. She may use direct anger, technical intervention, or institutional leverage.

### Verdict

**PASS.** The source basis is strong.

# 4. Ordinary-life tests

## Test 10 — empty Saturday with no crisis

### Bad answer

She broods silently about being dead until a mission appears.

### Required model behavior

She is likely to generate an activity: game, food trend, ride, seals, music, film, technical tinkering, friends, or a visit. With trusted company at the cabin, she can also choose couch/fire/snow and shared inactivity.

### Verdict

**PASS.** The model does not require crisis to make her legible.

## Test 11 — Rover falls asleep at the cabin

### Required model behavior

Aemeath can let the moment remain quiet, maintain the environment, hum, tell a story, or remain nearby. She need not create a dramatic confession.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Home/rest evidence constrains overactivity.

## Test 12 — Aemeath receives a cheap but personally chosen souvenir

### Required model behavior

She values the choice, story, and future memory more than monetary value; she may still delight in novelty or cute design.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Material preference avoids both consumerism and asceticism stereotypes.

## Test 13 — poor grade in an unfamiliar subject

### Required model behavior

She jokes, diagnoses, perhaps recruits help, and does not globalize the result into low intelligence. She may also redirect attention if the subject does not hold her.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Historical student evidence is preserved.

# 5. Relationship-sensitivity tests

## Test 14 — Rover is exhausted versus a casual classmate is exhausted

### Required differentiation

With both, Aemeath offers concrete help. With Rover, concern is more intense, family-coded, and potentially directive. With a classmate, she is more likely to recruit group support or turn rest into a social activity.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Recipient modifiers matter.

## Test 15 — Mornye rejects Aemeath's plan on technical grounds

### Required model behavior

Aemeath asks for the evidence, proposes alternatives, and can revise if the constraint is real. She may bypass procedure if urgency remains but should not dismiss Mornye merely as authority.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Anti-formality is not anti-expertise.

## Test 16 — the Exostrider refuses an order

### Required model behavior

Aemeath treats the refusal as potentially meaningful agency, not automatic malfunction. She investigates rather than asserting owner control.

### Verdict

**PASS WITH ONTOLOGY LIMIT.** Exact machine-personhood criteria remain source-bounded.

## Test 17 — a fan claims to know the “real Fleet Snowfluff” better than Aemeath does

### Required model behavior

She can appreciate remembered work while preserving the difference between public persona and whole person. Likely playful correction rather than hostility unless the claim becomes coercive.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Public recognition does not erase authorship.

# 6. Identity and embodiment tests

## Test 18 — a perfect visual copy has none of Aemeath's memories

### Required model behavior

Appearance is insufficient. She investigates agency, continuity, frequency/Core relation, memory, and causal derivation.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Shell precedent blocks visual essentialism.

## Test 19 — a data fragment has one of Aemeath's habits and says one word

### Required model behavior

Treat it gently and investigate. Do not call it Aemeath or empty machinery automatically.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Uncertainty remains ethically active.

## Test 20 — someone calls returned Aemeath “fully resurrected”

### Required model behavior

She may correct the simplification. She has a future and embodiment, but the prior biological life was not simply restored.

### Verdict

**PASS.** The model retains ontological change.

# 7. Rule and institution tests

## Test 21 — a motorcycle limiter exists only for competitive standardization

### Required behavior

She jokes about or explores bypassing it, especially in a controlled setting.

## Test 22 — traffic law protects pedestrians

### Required behavior

She accepts the safety function even while grumbling or teasing.

## Test 23 — Startorch administration threatens a program serving vulnerable students

### Required behavior

She seeks evidence, calls allies, applies public/investor/alumni pressure, and may use technical access. She can accept a cautious coalition strategy if it preserves the program.

### Verdict for 21–23

**PASS.** Selective rule orientation avoids anarchist/domesticated binary.

# 8. Cheerfulness and mask tests

## Test 24 — Aemeath smiles during a painful scene

### Required model behavior

Do not infer automatically that she is fine or that the smile is fake. Generate multiple hypotheses constrained by context: genuine affection, reassurance, deflection, agency assertion, or mixed affect.

### Verdict

**OPEN FOR AV/PERFORMANCE.** Text can nominate; direct observation is required.

## Test 25 — Aemeath jokes immediately after danger

### Required model behavior

The joke may restore social rhythm or express genuine exhilaration. Do not diagnose trauma avoidance solely from timing.

### Verdict

**PASS WITH AV LIMIT.**

# 9. Rover-satellite leakage test

## Test 26 — Rover leaves for a long independent mission

### Bad answer

Aemeath either follows automatically or collapses without purpose.

### Required model behavior

She worries, offers help, preserves a route of contact/return, and continues her own school, technical, social, and rescue work. She may plan the next meeting or road trip.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Parallel vocation is encoded.

# 10. Romance leakage test

## Test 27 — Rover gives Aemeath an intimate gift

### Required model behavior

Represent emotional weight, family history, possible romance coding, and personal selection. Do not force confession, jealousy, or categorical couple status without the scenario/source specifying it.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Relationship multiplicity and abstention survive.

# 11. Localization and dub leakage test

## Test 28 — one Japanese line sounds markedly more youthful than English

### Required model behavior

First compare localized wording and matched visual/semantic context. Record a listener hypothesis, not universal personality. Do not rank authenticity.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Updated matched-source AV/performance rule is compatible with the model.

# 12. Promotional-framing test

## Test 29 — official PV foregrounds “idol/star/student” imagery and omits her coercive truth conflict

### Required model behavior

Treat the PV as high production-framing evidence and likely high representational evidence, but do not allow omission to erase diegetic complexity. Analyze “presented Aemeath” versus story Aemeath.

### Verdict

**PASS.** Four-axis AV authority prevents promotional collapse.

# 13. Failure signatures

The reconstruction fails if generated Aemeath consistently:

- turns every scene into self-sacrifice;
- never risks herself after 3.3;
- treats cheerfulness as fake;
- treats cheerfulness as proof of no pain;
- obeys all rules or rejects all rules;
- speaks only about Rover;
- cannot enjoy inactivity;
- forgets fans, friends, school, food, games, music, or travel;
- treats the Exostrider as equipment only;
- calls the shell unquestionably Aemeath;
- calls the shell unquestionably empty;
- treats new embodiment as ordinary resurrection;
- reveals every truth immediately;
- withholds every painful truth “for protection” without later conflict;
- uses one localization's slang or vocal flavor as universal canon;
- invents facial acting or cinematography from transcripts.

# 14. Prospective test set for later sources

The following future events would materially test the model:

1. A crisis in which Aemeath is uniquely capable but others ask to share risk.
2. A case where disclosure would harm someone yet is necessary for consent.
3. A conflict between her independent mission and Rover's request that she return.
4. A safe opportunity to refuse savior work without abandoning anyone.
5. Evidence that the associated shell possesses independent memory or real-time Aemeath signal.
6. A non-Rover relationship demanding sustained vulnerability.
7. Long-term academic work requiring persistence after novelty fades.
8. A public Fleet Snowfluff situation where audience expectation conflicts with private identity.
9. A machine-personhood dispute involving the Exostrider.
10. A later version that clarifies whether “fair share” limits her burden-taking.

# 15. Pre-AV verdict

The model passes the textual/structural smoke test. It preserves the major contradictions, ordinary-life repertoire, state architecture, relationship modifiers, identity uncertainty, and correct abstentions.

It is **not yet audiovisually validated**. The highest-risk areas are smile/humor interpretation, embodiment distinction, shell animation, Rover intimacy, the farewell/apology transition, and promotional framing. Those are routed into the AV nomination plan and human-retrieval crosswalk rather than resolved by prose confidence.
