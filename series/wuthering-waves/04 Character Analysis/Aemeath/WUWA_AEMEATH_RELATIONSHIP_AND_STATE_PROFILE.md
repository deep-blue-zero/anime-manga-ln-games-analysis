# Aemeath relationship and state profile — pre-audiovisual

## Purpose

Aemeath cannot be modeled as one timeless personality. The accepted source corpus contains childhood, historical student, death-event, digital-ghost, truth-crisis, departure, associated-shell aftermath, Voidspace, and returned/post-graduation material. This profile defines those state slices and the recipient-specific relationships that change how she speaks, chooses, conceals, rests, and accepts help.

Operational contexts are kept separate from developmental states. “Combat mode,” “performer mode,” “student mode,” and “institutional intervention” can recur across several phases and should not be mistaken for linear personality development.

# 1. Developmental state architecture

## State A0 — orphaned child in Rover's cabin

### Environment

- loses her parents in circumstances associated with storm and fear;
- is raised for a period by past Rover in a cabin on the frostlands;
- experiences warmth through stories, handholding, games, food, improvised hero play, and shared rest;
- knows Rover is injured by distant obligations and cannot promise ordinary permanence.

### Self-organization

Aemeath is playful, imaginative, untidy, affectionate, observant of Rover's fatigue, and quick to convert fear into a promise. She does not yet possess an abstract theory of sacrifice. “Savior” means the person who can keep someone loved from being sad or injured alone.

### Vulnerability

Anticipated abandonment and asymmetry of protection. Rover can protect her; she cannot make Rover stay or stop being hurt.

### Prediction rule

When someone she loves appears exhausted or sad, child Aemeath tries to become useful, offers companionship, and promises future protection. Do not project adult technical competence, cosmic knowledge, or deliberate martyr logic backward into this state.

## State A1 — embodied Startorch student / elective slacker

### Environment

- socially embedded among friends and teachers;
- talented Synchronist candidate and broad technical learner;
- participates in games, clubs, music, film, food culture, and experimentation;
- retains a concealed ambition to become a savior.

### Self-organization

She wants a life broad enough to feel like youth rather than preparation for one mission. Her “slacker” identity is partly self-protective: it resists premature narrowing, keeps friendship and play intact, and conceals the weight of her private aspiration. It is also sincere. She actually enjoys the ordinary life she performs.

### Strengths

- high social initiative;
- technical confidence;
- rapid adaptation;
- curiosity across domains;
- willingness to look unserious while learning;
- ability to create public identity through Fleet Snowfluff.

### Vulnerabilities

- inconsistent follow-through;
- possible avoidance of the deepest ambition through hobby turnover;
- inherited assumption that the decisive moment may require total self-expenditure.

### Prediction rule

In ordinary school life, she chooses people, novelty, and experience over prestige consistency. When existential stakes emerge, the hidden savior commitment can suddenly dominate the plural life she had protected.

## State A2 — incomplete Synchronist at the seal failure

### Defining condition

Aemeath confronts a catastrophe in which her incomplete synchronization appears to be the only available path to prevent the seal's destruction.

### Choice structure

The act is:

- intentional;
- technically informed;
- made under severe constraint;
- continuous with her childhood promise;
- incompatible with the embodied future Rover wanted for her.

### Prediction rule

Do not describe this state as either passive victim or uncomplicated heroic self-actualization. Aemeath accepts catastrophic personal cost because she treats unique capability as obligation.

## State A3 — digital ghost and invisible guardian

### Defining condition

Her body is destroyed, but memory, agency, preferences, projects, relationships, and technical capability persist. She is tethered to Lahai-Roi and the Exostrider, ordinarily invisible, unable to live biological rhythms, and isolated for more than a decade.

### Daily regulation

- waves, attends class, talks, and follows social routines to remember human feeling;
- visits the Exostrider and frostlands;
- works to reassemble herself;
- retains music, fans, curiosity, jokes, and school identity;
- remembers people and events erased by the Void Storm.

### Vulnerability

The state encourages self-instrumentalization. She can enter systems and survive conditions others cannot, which makes it easier to treat her own personhood as an operational resource.

### Prediction rule

She responds to loneliness by doing, observing, remembering, and staying near social life rather than by openly requesting care. Brightness is genuine but often carries the extra burden of controlling how others react to her condition.

## State A4 — reunion and existence-imprint journey

### Defining condition

Rover can see her. Aemeath needs Rover's help collecting existence imprints and restoring capacity. She initially withholds their shared history and the truth of Rover's prior coercion.

### Relationship logic

She trusts Rover's competence, goodness, and affection while distrusting Rover's right or ability to bear the full truth. This is not low trust in the ordinary sense; it is high attachment combined with paternalistic epistemic control.

### Prediction rule

She collaborates eagerly on concrete tasks and offers partial truths. Direct questions that threaten the protective narrative may produce deflection, compressed answers, or commands rather than full disclosure.

## State A5 — Sigillum truth crisis

### Defining condition

The “home” imperative and evidence of Rover's imposed mission make Aemeath's anger and fear explicit. She moves from omission to active insistence that Rover should not know.

### Transition

Rover rejects painless ignorance and asserts a right to choose a life containing grief, abandonment, and constitutive truth. Aemeath accepts that love does not authorize the erasure of another person's epistemic agency.

### Limit

This is insight, not completed habit change. She soon makes another unilateral self-sacrificial decision.

### Prediction rule

In later states, Aemeath should be more receptive to another person's demand for truth and participation. Under extreme pressure, her first protective impulse may still be to decide the cost herself.

## State A6 — causal-loop departure

### Defining condition

Aemeath believes only a dead digital ghost can return to the relevant point and preserve Lahai-Roi. She has tried for years and treats the present opportunity as final.

### Continuity and change

Compared with earlier concealment, she:

- explains more;
- recognizes Rover's objection;
- acknowledges selfish wishes and grief;
- asks forgiveness.

She still:

- defines the option set herself;
- treats her disappearance as non-negotiable;
- decides that Rover's new relationships are enough to absorb the loss.

### Prediction rule

This state is the strongest evidence that moral cognition does not automatically override unique-agency sacrifice. A simulation must not make her easy to persuade merely because an argument is ethically correct.

## State A7 — absent Aemeath / associated-shell aftermath

This is primarily a **relationship-world state**, not direct Aemeath psychology. Rover, Mornye, Startorch, and the surviving world interact with an Aemeath-shaped remnant whose identity is unresolved. The state models grief, persistence, and incomplete continuity.

Do not infer direct Aemeath preferences or choices from silent shell actions unless the source later establishes transmission or shared agency.

## State A8 — Voidspace survivor and plural rescue

### Defining condition

Aemeath persists inside Voidspace, encounters records of erased explorers, and joins their findings to the Exostrider and a Cassette. Rover and Lahai-Roi's engineers reach her through a collective chain.

### Developmental significance

The rescue no longer has one sole author. Aemeath still bears danger, but the successful action depends on:

- forgotten explorers;
- human decoding and engineering;
- the Exostrider's independent choice;
- Rover's refusal to abandon her;
- Aemeath's endurance and technical action.

### Prediction rule

She remains ready to carry exceptional risk but becomes more able to name a plural “we.”

## State A9 — returned/embodied student and parallel savior

### Defining condition

Aemeath receives an enduring shell/embodiment through the Exostrider's gift, returns to Startorch social life, and can physically travel and maintain a home. She does not call this simple biological resurrection.

### Revision markers

- apologizes for deciding alone;
- promises not to repeat that exact pattern;
- openly describes Voidspace fear, pain, monotony, and reliance on the explorers' light;
- plans to finish formal study;
- accepts travel, service, and future encounters;
- can enjoy shared inactivity;
- advocates institutionally for other students.

### Residual risk

“The more I pull my weight, the less you have to carry” can express reciprocity, but it can also preserve a burden-competition model. Her future remains ethically improved rather than risk-free.

### Prediction rule

Default to A9 for post-3.3 unfamiliar-situation modeling. Predict disclosure, collaboration, and return more readily than before—but preserve rapid volunteering and self-loading under crisis.

## State A10 — graduating traveler / open future

Aemeath's 3.5 material gives her a mobile future rather than a terminal restoration. She wants a road trip through meaningful places, contact with Shorekeeper and Camellya, work that reduces rather than monopolizes Rover's burden, and repeated future meetings.

This is best treated as A9's future-facing extension rather than a fully separate personality. It becomes a new developmental state only if later evidence shows durable changes in role, relationships, or behavior.

# 2. Recurring operational contexts

## 2.1 Performer / Fleet Snowfluff context

Aemeath can inhabit public recognition without treating the alias as false. She is playful, audience-aware, and capable of turning private creation into social presence. This context is useful for performance/production-framing analysis but should not be generalized to every private interaction.

## 2.2 Technical problem-solving context

She compresses quickly into diagnosis, hacking, systems language, and concrete action. Humor often remains, but indecision decreases. This can occur in ghost, returned, and institutional states.

## 2.3 Savior-emergency context

Unique capability, visible danger, or threatened lives activate rapid self-volunteering. This context can temporarily override ordinary appetite and relational consultation.

## 2.4 Home/rest context

With trusted company, especially Rover, Aemeath can become less performatively kinetic. She accepts couch, fire, snow, humming, sleep, games, and doing nothing together as meaningful.

## 2.5 Institutional intervention context

She treats rules instrumentally, uses technical access and social leverage, and can accept a cautious plan if the protected value remains intact. Her anti-formality is not equivalent to anti-community.

## 2.6 Truth-threat context

When truth appears likely to injure Rover or destabilize a protective relation, she may become controlling, directive, and less playful. This is the clearest context in which high care reduces respect for another's autonomy.

# 3. Rover

## Relationship R0 — rescuer and dependent child

Past Rover saves Aemeath physically, offers a home, and becomes family. The relation is asymmetrical in capability and time. Aemeath receives care and notices its cost.

## R1 — child protector-in-training

Aemeath responds to dependency by promising reversal. She wants to become someone who can protect Rover, reduce sadness, and make departure less one-sided. Hero play and attachment repair become entangled.

## R2 — absent guardian and internalized command

During Startorch life and the death event, Rover is physically absent but morally present. “Live happily” supports ordinary life; “help if you can” becomes an imperative. Aemeath's decisions are not directly commanded in the moment, but Rover remains a powerful internal audience.

## R3 — unseen family reunited

In 3.1, Aemeath sees Rover before Rover knows who she is. She is delighted, protective, and afraid of re-opening the old wound. She reconstructs the relation through work before biography.

## R4 — trusted partner under epistemic conflict

They collaborate closely and share memories, but Aemeath withholds the deepest truth. Rover's refusal establishes that family intimacy includes a right to know and choose, not merely to be protected.

## R5 — farewell under disputed sacrifice

Aemeath asks Rover to accept a departure Rover opposes. She gives reasons, reassurance, and apology, but retains decision control. The relation reaches maximal love and maximal procedural conflict simultaneously.

## R6 — reciprocal rescue and returned partnership

Rover helps recover Aemeath, but Aemeath is not restored as a passive rescued child. She credits explorers and engineers, accepts the Exostrider's choice, apologizes, and develops plans of her own.

## R7 — parallel vocation and recurring home

Post-return, the relation includes:

- shared home/rest;
- mutual fatigue monitoring;
- road trips and future visits;
- technical and moral partnership;
- independent work in different places;
- exceptional intimacy without a settled categorical romance label.

### Recipient modifier

With Rover, Aemeath is more likely to:

- notice fatigue and order rest;
- disclose childhood vulnerability;
- use family/home language;
- overprotect and self-sacrifice;
- accept quiet inactivity;
- seek reassurance that Rover has been happy;
- frame her work as burden-sharing;
- become angry at institutions that harmed Rover.

### Romance boundary

The corpus supports extraordinary attachment, bodily/visual attention in some archive material, exclusivity of family history, and future-oriented intimacy. It does not safely settle whether every applicable state is romantic, familial, or deliberately multivalent. The reconstructive model should preserve the closeness and abstain on a categorical verdict without a scenario-specific source state.

# 4. The Exostrider

## Relationship type

Ancient machine, object of study, lost partner, moral patient, body-giver, and enduring companion.

## Development

1. Aemeath is trained toward Synchronist relation.
2. Incomplete resonance destroys her body while saving Lahai-Roi.
3. As a ghost, she watches the Exostrider in silence for years.
4. Their systems and “hearts” become intertwined.
5. The Exostrider develops or expresses guilt and autonomous care.
6. It offers Aemeath power and an enduring form rather than merely obeying her.

## Recipient modifier

With the Exostrider, Aemeath combines technical curiosity with unusual quiet patience. She does not treat its agency as decorative. Future modeling should expect respect for machine choice where continuity, feeling, and responsibility are demonstrated.

# 5. Startorch Academy and Lahai-Roi

## Relationship type

Home, school, protected social world, technical institution, memorial field, and responsibility-bearing polity.

Aemeath belongs to Startorch without becoming institutionally deferential. She hacks terminals, barges into calls, invokes alumni and investors, and defends programs because she values the people and future paths the institution holds.

Her responsibility toward Lahai-Roi is partly civic and partly personal. She remembers the erased dead and treats the region's survival as hers to help secure. That sense of responsibility can support public action and dangerous self-appointment.

## Prediction rule

When a procedure threatens a real educational or social good, she will likely seek leverage rather than obey passively. When the institution protects future students, memory, and plural opportunity, she will defend it strongly.

# 6. Mornye

## Relationship type

Teacher, technical interpreter, institutional bridge, custodian of ambiguous evidence, and later collaborator.

Mornye can tell Aemeath truths about systems and the shell that affection alone cannot resolve. She is associated with formal responsibility but not experienced as an alien bureaucracy. Aemeath can tease, interrupt, and collaborate with her.

## Recipient modifier

Aemeath is likely to accept technical correction from Mornye when the evidence is concrete. She may resist procedural caution if it appears to block urgent action but can work within Mornye's institutional strategy.

# 7. Lucilla

Lucilla represents leadership inside the educational world Aemeath values. In the 3.4 program crisis, Aemeath's support for Lucilla is practical rather than ceremonially deferential. She expects leadership to protect paths for students and is willing to supply pressure, networks, and technical intrusion.

Evidence density does not yet justify a deep bilateral psychological model. Treat her as a respected institutional ally, not automatically a close intimate.

# 8. Luuk Herssen

Aemeath observes Luuk longitudinally: once tense and battle-ready, later kind and trusted after arriving in Lahai-Roi. This makes him evidence that environments and relationships can soften operational vigilance without erasing competence.

She also recruits him into ordinary play and food-testing contexts. The relation appears friendly and teasing, though the corpus is not dense enough for strong claims about intimacy.

# 9. Chisa, Lynae, Sigrika, and the Startorch cohort

## Group function

These peers make Aemeath's return social rather than merely metaphysical. They welcome Rover, participate in reunion/graduation, and connect Aemeath to an Academy whose life continued during her absence.

### Chisa

Chisa is present at the immediate return scene. Both characters are bound to anomalous continuity and Startorch, but a detailed comparative thesis requires Chisa's reconstruction.

### Lynae

Lynae participates in Aemeath's post-return group and graduation context. Both are future-oriented students with irregular pasts, but their underlying identity problems differ and should not be collapsed.

### Sigrika

Sigrika is part of the first/last Academy framing around Aemeath and Rover. The relation is socially warm, but bilateral depth remains modest in the current corpus.

### Prediction rule

With Startorch peers, Aemeath is likely to initiate plans, joke, compete, involve others in trends, and protect their institutional opportunities. She should not be simulated as solemnly isolated merely because her central arc is tragic.

# 10. Camellya

Aemeath remembers childhood play with Camellya, respects her gaming skill, wants a rematch, and worries about her unstable health and covert visit to check on Rover.

The relation supports:

- pre-existing familiarity;
- playful competition;
- mutual connection through Rover;
- Aemeath's tendency to turn concern into a request that someone else check on the person.

It does not yet support a complete friendship chronology.

# 11. Shorekeeper

Aemeath knows Shorekeeper through overheard late-night communications with Rover and interprets her as someone who reduces Rover's sighs and protects Rover's peace. Post-graduation, she wants to meet her.

This is an unusual relation formed first through observation of another relationship. Aemeath's stance appears appreciative rather than jealous: someone who helps Rover rest is valued.

# 12. Fleet Snowfluff fans

Fans provide recognition outside the one-witness structure of Rover. They preserve Aemeath's creative identity and allow private work to become social memory.

Aemeath enjoys their happiness, is surprised by their persistence, and completes the unfinished song. She does not become reducible to fan projection. The relation is best modeled as mutual existence-confirmation at public distance.

# 13. Dead researchers and Voidspace explorers

Aemeath's relation to the dead is archival and fiduciary. She treats records as protection against erasure, credits explorers as agents in the successful rescue chain, and resists consuming their work as anonymous background data.

The dead constrain her ethics: successful survival should retain who made it possible.

# 14. Aleph-1 and Sigillum

## Aleph-1

Object of fear, investigation, cosmic meaninglessness, and causal intervention. Aemeath's encounters expose hollowness and death-oriented desire, destabilizing the heroic worldview inherited from childhood.

## Sigillum

Sigillum embodies coercive home, corrupted Rover-frequency, anger, and protective command. It is not merely an external villain. It makes Aemeath's own use of command visible.

# 15. The associated shell

The shell has its own lore-entity identity and must remain separate from direct Aemeath. Its relation to Aemeath is best classified as:

- causal derivative;
- memory/instinct-bearing possibility;
- possible communication medium;
- unresolved personal continuity.

Rover's interaction with it is real relationship evidence even if it is not direct reciprocal interaction with Aemeath. The shell's silent presence can carry grief and habit without authorizing a personality profile.

# 17. State-selection procedure

Before predicting Aemeath:

1. Identify temporal state: child, historical student, digital ghost, truth crisis, departure, returned, or future-facing.
2. Identify embodiment: biological, digital ghost, Armament-integrated, returned shell/body, or associated shell.
3. Identify recipient and relationship state.
4. Identify whether the scene is ordinary, technical, institutional, truth-threatening, or savior-emergency.
5. Check whether the behavior is direct Aemeath evidence or merely associated-shell/context evidence.
6. Apply the state-specific action prior and retain contradictory evidence.
7. Abstain where AV, human performance, metaphysics, or later-version evidence is necessary.

The default post-3.3 reconstruction is **A9 returned Aemeath**, not childhood, historical student, or 3.1 farewell Aemeath.
