# Augusta — three ways an Ephor exercises authority

Augusta's title can make all her dialogue sound like one unvarying command. Three underused actions instead distribute authority differently. At the arena she responds to Lupa/Julia's contested allegation and Iuno's Temple verdict by ordering Julia away. At a later hunt she identifies Avidius's desire for power and invites him to pursue it beside her. In another crisis she answers Iuno's conditional question about predetermined fate with a ruler's purpose **and** a concrete evacuation. These are three source/time settings, not one continuous test of trust; their value is to show the kinds of decision she takes, the other speakers who constrain each decision, and the facts she does not know [AUG-E40–E42].

## A Temple verdict does not speak the Ephor's order

`flow#/6989/4`, raw state `剧情_2_4_七丘主线_上2_3_5`, has an exact quest-node reference `125000126_33` and PlotHandBook `#/43` reference. A single `ShowTalk` action has 31 TalkItems and a linear `TalkSequence`; Rover's arrival has two alternative menu reactions but not two distinct outcomes for the public judgment. Lupa (`250009`) alleges that her spear, originally a gift from House Silva, went out of control and almost harmed her teammate. Julia Silva (`850351`) asks for evidence, says the spear would pass scrutiny and contests Lupa's motive. The record presents conflicting testimony; it does not show the spear examined. Iuno (`250024`) invokes heroic spirits, declares guilt as the Temple's judgment and asks Augusta to expel the **Silvas**. These are Iuno's words, not a speech to assign to Augusta [AUG-E40/C36].

Augusta (`250022`) first asks Lupa to speak plainly (`MAIN_RYAM_44_3`, item 2). After Iuno's judgment, Augusta addresses Julia directly (`_44_23`, item 21), recalls how Silva ancestors helped build Septimont and sent warriors to defend it (`_44_25–26`, items 23–24), says Julia has disgraced them (`_44_27`, item 25), and orders her to leave and never return (`_44_28`, item 26). Julia submits to the Ephor (item 27). This is a definite sanction against the addressed Julia. Iuno requested family-wide expulsion, but Augusta's own singular imperative and Julia's reply do not prove every Silva family member was actually expelled. The historical praise does not absolve Julia; Augusta uses it to make the present betrayal more serious. Equally, the speech does not announce hereditary guilt as an independent rule for all descendants. A model should preserve the severity of her order without inflating its observed target [AUG-E40/C36].

The evidence context has an additional language fork. Chinese `_44_23` says Julia should know what she was doing in “these images,” and Japanese also mentions images/video; English says she knows the weight of her actions, while Korean speaks of knowing what she must do. Thus ZH/JA suggest a visual presentation in the addressed moment. The extracted `ShowTalk` text and sequence asset pointer do **not** reveal the images' content, origin, whether they show the spear, or how Iuno reached the Temple verdict. Nor does Julia's hesitant response supply a detailed confession. A runtime capture or decoded sequence might sharpen the finding. Until then, “Augusta acted on no evidence whatsoever” and “the images conclusively prove every sabotage detail” are both overclaims [AUG-E40/C36].

This is not a repudiation of her earlier nonlethal arena reform. That reform opposed forcing defeated fighters into fatal spectacles; it was not a promise that an Ephor would never impose a civic penalty for an alleged arena wrong. But expulsion is costly and the absence of an observed procedure remains relevant. A hypothetical hearing should not cite her justice rhetoric as automatic proof of due process, nor infer that the Temple's judgment gave Julia a meaningful opportunity to inspect the image. A verified procedural source could change that narrower assessment [AUG-E04/E10/E40, C02/C36].

## Bringing ambition close is not proof that it is safe

At `flow#/8381/3`, raw state `剧情_2_6_狄斯台地主线_上半_1_29_1`, exact quest-node `311000001_132`, Iuno describes the corpse-strewn Asphodel Barrens and the Dark Tide creatures' reciprocal hunt. Augusta then addresses Avidius by name and lineage (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_320_8`, item 6), says she sees a desire for power (`_320_9`, item 7), judges his resolve insufficient to stand as her opponent (`_320_10`, item 8), and tells him to step beside her and pursue his ambition (`_320_11–12`, items 9–10). Avidius (`1580`) accepts at item 11; Iuno later lets the two handle the matter while Rover rests. The source supports Augusta's awareness that his aspiration is **power**, not an innocent assumption that he wants only civic service [AUG-E41/C37].

The invitation is deliberately provocative, but its purpose cannot be read off the lines with certainty. It might draw an ambitious gladiator into a more accountable contest, encourage him to expose himself, or simply welcome a worthy challenge. The speech alone does not establish a secret surveillance plan, durable alliance, romantic trust, endorsement of his later choices or a fixed enmity. Her archive elsewhere values Avidius's capability while worrying about his ambition (`FavorWord_130614_Content`); the two source surfaces are compatible if her invitation is treated as an *action under known risk*, not evidence she has resolved the concern. Avidius's acceptance is his, not a promise that ambition will remain under her control [AUG-E11/E41, C12/C37].

## The “impossible” answer immediately becomes evacuation work

`flow#/10906/3`, raw state `剧情_2_6_狄斯台地主线_上半_1_85_2`, has an exact quest-node join to `311000001_227` and PlotHandBook `#/53`. In its voiced 19-item `ShowTalk` sequence, Abby (`1316`) describes a disturbing Dark Tide encounter. Augusta's early question (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_350_5`, item 3) asks how someone is holding up; this action does not make the addressee of that short question certain from the text alone. A separate technical speaker `350005` then praises a supposed divine gift and urges Augusta to Murmurstown. The speech belongs to that source speaker, not Augusta or Iuno, and its claims are not omniscient narration. Iuno (`250024`) calls Augusta and twice qualifies a question: *if* Septimont's fate were already decided, would she continue fighting? In Chinese, Japanese and Korean the second call prints a cough; English omits it. That is a localization/text observation, not human listening or a diagnosis [AUG-E42/C38].

Augusta answers first with the ruler's obligation to achieve what seems impossible for her people (`_350_18`, item 16). She immediately says the area is unsafe, that she will lead a team to move everyone to a temporary camp, and that she must check no one is left behind (`_350_19`, item 17). She directs Rover's group ahead and entrusts Iuno to them (`_350_20`, item 18). The phrase about impossible deeds is therefore not separable from a mundane safety operation. Her response rejects fatalistic passivity, but does not report a verified victory over the Dark Tide or show her knowing the hidden voice's true identity. Entrusting Iuno acknowledges another person's immediate need while reserving for Augusta the task of checking the civilians. Neither action proves she must always refuse to evacuate personally or always hand Iuno to Rover; these are choices made under this crisis [AUG-E42/C38].

The scene's contrast can be used in a character model without turning Augusta into an ideal machine. She may answer a friend's fear with a kingly sentence and still be tasked with a headcount, routes and transport. She does not need to solve the prophecy before getting people away from danger. Conversely, a model that cites the speech to make her abandon every civilian for a duel has severed her declaration from its next two sentences. The source also permits Iuno to be frightened or physically strained without determining the cause of her condition; Augusta's care is a reported action, not a clinical inference [AUG-E42/C38].
