## The Wedge sets the scale of what a champion can do

At `8371/3/22–35` Augusta first identifies the Corrosaurus as a Dark Tide creature, distinguishes the exceptional Tyrannos, and refuses the premature inference that the Threnodian's failed conquest means surrender. Her account makes the Earthrend Wedge, not her sword alone, the specific means of severing the Dark Tide's link to its creator (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_65_31`). Hunting disperses a creature's frequencies (`_65_32`); the Wedge's energy reaches only a limited region, so the desired “true death” must be delivered within the operative hunting area (`_65_33`, `_65_35`). These are Augusta's technical claims inside the story. They do not establish a universal metaphysical law of killing, a completed Tyrannos defeat or a limitless Imperator field. The source also lets her recognize adversarial cognition: the prey knows what the hunters intend and can behave as hunter (`_65_37`). Waiting for it to enter the proper terrain (`_65_39–40`) is controlled patience, not indecision or enjoyment of civilian risk [AUG-E37, C33].

Her earlier observation of Iuno at `8371/3/18–20` is a useful relationship boundary inside this same planning scene. Augusta notices Iuno being unusually restrained with Rover, speculates that Rover is “different” to her, suggests some similarity, and calls Iuno dependable. The source proves Augusta's affectionate observation and her confidence in Iuno; it does not prove what Iuno privately feels, confirm a romance, or license treating the two as interchangeable tacticians. Her admiration sits beside disagreement about prophecy elsewhere. A future hypothetical can make her notice a friend's change in manner without making her an omniscient interpreter of that friend's desire [AUG-E11, E37, C09].

At `8380/3/5–9` the Anchor Stone is an intermediate instrument: Tacetite formed near the Wedge retains a fraction of its force; Augusta plans three placements at preselected Maroonwood points so their field drives the creature toward the lower Asphodel Barrens near the Wedge's base. A plan to deploy is not evidence that all three were placed, that the field worked, or that the final battle occurred there. The four text witnesses agree on temporary repulsion of Dark Tide material but do not use equally absolute self-defense wording. EN says the stone “offers no defense capabilities”; ZH says it cannot be used for personal defense, JA says its force is insufficient to protect the holder, and KO says it cannot fully protect. The safe synthesis is that it is insufficient personal protection even while useful for temporary repulsion, not that every possible defensive effect is impossible (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_135_7–8`). When Augusta identifies an ambush against expected Griffrex flight, she switches the party to a walk (`_135_13–16`). She can rally fighters in ceremonial language *after* a tactical change; neither register cancels the other [AUG-E38, C33].

## Information changes hands, but not all minds know the same thing

The later `8388/4` scene is not Augusta single-handedly solving a failed plan. At items 5–8 she reports defensive preparation and an explicit exchange: Black Shores may use Septimont's Resonance Nexus to monitor and collect Dark Tide data during the hunt, while it owes technical support if complications arise (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_370_6–9`). She identifies Buling as the connected consultant at item 10; Rover offers contact and Augusta accepts at item 14. This is a negotiated institutional resource, neither a gift of unbounded surveillance rights nor proof that Black Shores was already exercising every access at all times. The local scene permits a more specific account of Augusta's leadership than “she distrusts all outside experts” or “she just wins duels”: she budgets outside expertise and makes contingencies before a crisis [AUG-E39, C34].

Buling, technical speaker `250004`, reads Tethys's reports and forecasts the emerging False Sovereign's appetite for frequencies and danger to the Wedge (items 17–22). An inserted narrator item 49, speaker `83`, explicitly sits between item 22 and item 23 in `TalkSequence`. It says Rover recalls Iuno's disclosure that she and Augusta had secretly planned a Sovereign hunt, that the plan failed, and that Rover conveys it to Buling with Augusta's permission (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_370_57`). This fixes a four-party information flow: Iuno and Augusta designed, Iuno earlier told Rover, Rover now briefs Buling, and Augusta authorizes. It does not make Buling the designer of the original plan or prove that every Gladiator was privy to it. Buling infers Corrosaurus was bait for a larger, still-forming Sovereign (items 24–25); Augusta confirms the inference at item 26. The inference is supported by a named participant's assent, not by a narrator declaring every detail of tactical intent [AUG-E39, C34].

Buling then describes a strange frequency that accelerated formation and disrupted the expected course (items 27–31). Her sense that someone might have hidden the signs is a question under impaired divination, not a proved identity or act. Augusta's response at item 32 is to find and stop the Sovereign before more fusion; ZH/EN/KO describe this as *possibly* or presently the only way, while JA says the goal remains consistent, without the same exclusivity assertion (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_370_35`). Do not turn a contingent crisis assessment into a timeless rule that she rejects all alternatives. Items 35–36 locate Murmurstown and state an intent to stop the threat, not a completed solo victory. The retrospective `QuestTree_Summary_210080` reports a later joint Augusta–Rover victory, but does not license splicing that result into this earlier briefing as already accomplished [AUG-E35, E39, C31, C34].

## Who volunteers, and what consent does not settle

After Augusta states her intention, a younger Gladiator—technical speaker `1580`, not Augusta—asks to accompany her (`8388/4/38–41`). He says he lacks the right to *require* others to go, while invoking the glory they have earned and ought to uphold (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_370_46`). JA explicitly frames danger as a reason he cannot compel them. An older Gladiator, `350006`, answers with the eagle image and offers collective support (items 42–43). Only then does Augusta, `250022`, praise their resolve, accept the wish to fight beside her, and rally them by Septimont and the heroes' name (items 44–46; `_370_51–53`). The distinction prevents two symmetric errors: claiming Augusta conscripted the group in the recorded scene, and claiming her command is free of honor pressure simply because someone volunteered. The younger speaker himself ties volunteerism to deserved glory; Augusta amplifies a martial civic ideal. The source does not record a dissenting Gladiator's fate, the legal right to refuse, or the later casualty count [AUG-E39, C35].

This scene can be read alongside the later Aeolus letter without flattening either. The letter literally refuses an individual's withdrawal request, though its timing and administrative effect remain unresolved. Here a group offers itself during an acute threat and Augusta accepts. These do not prove a stable, just policy of voluntary exit from all Gladiator service; nor does the letter prove she compelled these particular volunteers. A faithful reconstruction may let Augusta respect the courage of those who step forward, speak in the ceremonial language she uses, and still be questioned about whether honor makes stepping back socially costly. The two cases jointly test the difference between *expressed willingness*, *freedom from direct order*, and *freedom from institutional pressure* [AUG-E36, E39; C32, C35].
