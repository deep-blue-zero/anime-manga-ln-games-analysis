# Iuno — recognizing what a memory cannot certify

The Chaos remnant does not merely collect lost recollections as if each were an archival film. Two source actions let the incomplete Iuno notice that a place feels familiar while rejecting a scene of Sibylla as insufficiently witnessed. A separate, earlier crisis lets the embodied Iuno ask Augusta whether she would keep fighting *if* Septimont's fate were fixed. Read together but not fused into one timeline, these actions show a person who can desire knowledge, distrust an apparently vivid image and still seek another person's chosen resolve [IUN-E45–E46].

## The mother's words are presented, then epistemically challenged

At `flow#/7761/5`, raw state `剧情_2_6_狄斯台地主线_下半_混沌之间_1_11`, PlotHandBook `#/55` associates the state with quest `886000001`. Four turns by speaker `350029` answer in Sibylla's voice: the western defense is at stake, she wants to return and fights to win, yet will accept mourning if victory and death coincide. The speaking figures are embedded in a Chaos memory encounter. Without additional authentication, their exact words are not independent historical testimony from the mother or her companion [IUN-E45/C40].

The remnant Iuno `350026` has four accepted, source-voiced items at zero-based indices 6–9 (`Main_Linaxita_2_10_27_11`, `_12`, `_10`, `_13`). The source itself poses the evidentiary objection: is this how it was, or only an imagined version like other memories? She then says what happened cannot be changed and that someone who closed her eyes did not really see; this is not the real Sibylla. Chinese, English and Korean explicitly oppose an imagined scene to what was seen; Japanese at `_12` uses wording closer to being mistaken, then at `_13` explicitly says *she* closed her eyes. That person-specific wording reinforces the admission of limited first-hand access rather than proving every detail of the depicted argument false. The sound object is real, the recollection is a story event, but the *mother's exact speech in that image* remains unverified as a past recording [IUN-E03/E18/E45, C04/C40].

This distinction matters to Iuno's character reconstruction. She has lived under the accusation she directs at her younger self: perhaps she should have looked longer and could have prevented Sibylla's death. In this scene she does not gain an omniscient replay that settles the counterfactual. She instead recognizes the difference between a morally instructive representation and reliable witness of the whole past. A model that narrates Sibylla as deliberately choosing death or abandoning her daughter from these four presented lines overstates a scene Iuno herself questions. A model that dismisses Sibylla's courage altogether also goes beyond her narrower objection. Her own source records support a loved mother who encouraged freedom; the Chaos dialogue is an uncertain image of a specific last choice [IUN-E03/E45, C04/C40].

## An injured question to Augusta tests a choice, not fate's result

Earlier in story time, `flow#/10906/3`, raw state `剧情_2_6_狄斯台地主线_上半_1_85_2`, is linked through quest-node `311000001` and PlotHandBook `#/53`. Its 19-item voiced sequence contains Abby (`1316`) describing a troubling Dark Tide encounter; Augusta (`250022`) asks after someone; speaker `350005` praises a supposed “Gift of Divinity” and urges Augusta toward Murmurstown. The exact identity, reliability and metaphysical status of that speaker's claims are not settled by this one action. They must not be relabeled as Iuno's own beliefs or omniscient narration. Iuno's four direct turns are items 11–14 (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_350_13–16`): she calls Augusta twice, coughs in the Chinese/JA/KO text of the second call, and asks **if** Septimont's fate had already been determined whether Augusta would continue fighting. English omits the written cough from `_350_14`, so text-level physical detail must be language-qualified and not turned into an acoustic observation [IUN-E47/C42].

The source does not have Iuno assert that she has just seen an irrevocable future; the doubled “if” makes it a conditional test. Augusta's next voiced response first articulates a ruler's task of accomplishing the impossible for her subjects, then specifies immediate logistics: escort everyone to a temporary camp and check that no one is left behind. She asks Rover to take Iuno ahead. Her answer is a commitment expressed in **evacuation labor**, not simply a slogan or proof she can win every battle. It is also not Iuno's answer supplied by proxy. Iuno is presently vulnerable enough for Augusta to entrust her care, but the exact injury and its cause are not diagnosed here. A historically located model may let Iuno ask her closest ally for chosen resolve while receiving practical protection; it should not promote the question to prophecy, assume she has no agency because she is coughing, or have Augusta abandon endangered civilians to deliver a speech [IUN-E47/C42].

The two time planes can illuminate one another without erasing their order. The embodied Iuno asks Augusta how to act under a hypothetical fixed outcome; the later remnant doubts a constructed past and follows incomplete familiarity. Both resist letting an apparently certain account substitute for lived choice. That is an interpretation across sources, not a claim Iuno recalls the later Chaos scenes while posing the earlier question. It also leaves room for counterreadings: the battlefield question may register pain or fear as much as philosophical curiosity, and the remnant's sensed site may prove misleading. Neither is settled by a source text's confident syntax or by signal measurements [IUN-E45–E47/C40–C42].
