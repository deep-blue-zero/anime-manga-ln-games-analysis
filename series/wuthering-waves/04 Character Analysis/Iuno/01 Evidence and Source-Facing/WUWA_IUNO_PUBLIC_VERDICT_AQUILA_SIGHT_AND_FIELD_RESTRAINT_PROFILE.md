# Iuno — when to declare, when to look, and when to stand aside

Three previously thinly analyzed source actions reveal a public register that is neither the passive Temple oracle nor the privately hesitant friend. At the arena, Iuno uses the Temple's moral authority to announce a verdict and press Augusta to act. In the later hunt, she invokes Aquila and the sacred flames for sight, describes a lethal field, and then asks Rover to rest while Augusta addresses Avidius. These are **three distinct actions**, not one executed, uninterrupted scene. Their common analytical question is how she handles imperfect access to truth and other people's authority [IUN-E43–E44].

## The arena judgment is a consequential act, not a forensic transcript

At `flow#/6989/4`, raw state `剧情_2_4_七丘主线_上2_3_5`, exact quest-node reference `125000126_33`, a single `ShowTalk` action has 31 TalkItems and a linear `TalkSequence` of IDs 1–31. Rover's item 12 is a two-option reaction—ask who has appeared or remark on the dramatic entrance—without a `JumpTalk` branch to a different judgment. It is wrong to script both Rover responses as spoken. Lupa (`250009`) alleges that a spear given to her by House Silva behaved uncontrollably and nearly struck her teammate (items 0–5). Julia Silva (`850351`) asks for proof, asserts the spear would withstand scrutiny, and suggests Lupa has a motive to ruin her former patron (items 6–10). An unidentified speaker `849` objects to a public performance; this single line is not a census of audience opinion. Lupa then identifies Iuno as the Septimont Priestess and says most people trust her judgment, while noting that not everyone likes her aggressive manner (items 13, 17–18). These are Lupa's statements about Iuno's reputation, not independent proof of what Iuno saw or of the spear's mechanism [IUN-E43/C38].

Iuno (`250024`) addresses the moon-and-star-lit heroic spirits and says a lie is polluting the arena (`MAIN_RYAM_44_16`, item 14). She pronounces guilt (`_44_17`, item 15), asks Augusta to expel the Silvas (`_44_18`, item 16), and repeats that guilt is the Temple's judgment (`_44_22`, item 20). The language is a public institutional intervention, not a tentative hunch. Yet this action does not show her inspect a spear, disclose a divination method, produce an independent witness, or explain how she distinguished Lupa's allegation from Julia's denial. Augusta (`250022`) addresses Julia, invokes the Silva family's founding and defensive service, says Julia has disgraced that history, and orders **Julia** to leave forever (items 21, 23–26). Augusta's opening line `_44_23` refers to “these images” in ZH and JA; EN refers to the weight of Julia's actions and KO points to what Julia must do. The extracted dialogue does not show the images' contents, provenance or how Iuno used them. Julia's halting reply and submission to the Ephor (items 22, 27) establish the outcome in this action, not a complete forensic confession. Thus Iuno **declares and presses**; Augusta **issues the order to Julia**. The scope of Iuno's requested family-wide sanction should not be silently equated with Augusta's singular address. The two authorities collaborate, but their words and acts are not interchangeable [IUN-E43/C38].

Immediately afterward Iuno evaluates Rover and Lupa's arena performance, scorns fighters who retreat merely to preserve points, encourages the two competitors, and warns them not to disappoint her or Augusta in the final (`_44_30–32`, items 28–30). Her brusque praise is competitively sincere, not a continuous solemn oracle performance. It makes room in a character model for public bite and an institutional verdict alongside private fear, care and later criticism of Temple constraints. It does **not** allow an arbitrary accusation to become true because she can speak with conviction. Nor should a scenario remove her political role by treating her as only Augusta's supportive friend [IUN-E43/C38].

The source's strongest alternative reading is that the Temple verdict itself settles guilt: Lupa describes a Priestess with decisive standing, Julia does not continue contesting the matter, and Augusta acts. The narrower reading retained here accepts the verdict and banishment as **story events**, while leaving the *specific spear sabotage mechanism and Iuno's route to knowledge* unverified by this action. A later exact scene, inspection result, culprit admission or verified runtime presentation could resolve those questions. In the meantime, neither “Iuno proved every detail” nor “Julia was innocent” is justified. Her earlier disclosure that a famous Augusta “prophecy” was an ordinary personal judgment (`FavorWord_141013_Content`) is a reason to separate office, confidence and method, not to declare this different verdict fraudulent [IUN-E10/E43, C06/C38].

## Sight is requested before the hunt, not a demonstrated new future vision

`flow#/8378/3` is raw state `剧情_2_6_狄斯台地主线_上半_1_25_1`, joined to quest `311000001` through an exact quest-node reference (`311000001_43`). Its seven Iuno `250024` items are one linear voiced sequence. She notes the timing, says she should move, notices Rover talking with Lillibet but postpones that inquiry, addresses Aquila, calls to the heroic spirits, and petitions the sacred flames for a wide view (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_115_1–7`). That is an intentional observation or ritual request within a field operation; the raw text alone does not show *what* Aquila is, what visual channel was actually produced, or whether the invocation succeeded. English `_115_5` adds an explicit “get eyes on this beast” objective absent from the shorter ZH/JA/KO address to Aquila; this may reflect context but should not be quoted as a four-language literal. Japanese `_115_7` asks for an eye that sees all, while ZH/EN use expansive vision and KO asks to widen sight. None of these prayers is a dated demonstration of the precise fate-thread peering she later says she exchanged (`flow#/8386/4/19–20`) [IUN-E14/E44, C39].

`flow#/8381/3` is a separate raw state, `剧情_2_6_狄斯台地主线_上半_1_29_1`, with exact quest-node reference `311000001_132`. The opening Rover item is a single text option, not a branching conversation; eight subsequent Iuno turns are voiced (items 1–5 and 12–14). She calls the scene a frightening field of corpses, identifies it with Tyrannos's birthplace and brutal kingdom, and describes how gladiators drive Tide creatures toward the Asphodel Barrens while the creatures lure hunters in turn. Her hunter/prey phrase ends with survival deciding the designation (`Main_Linaxita_2_9_320_3–7`). This is a tactical and historical account, not an observed prediction of who will live. Korean explicitly calls the result unpredictable where ZH/EN stress a fated even contest; the common ending still withholds the winner. The extracted dialogue does not by itself adjudicate whether the corpses are directly present, vision-mediated, remembered or represented in the level sequence. That requires visual/runtime inspection [IUN-E44/C39].

Augusta next addresses Avidius, invites him to stand beside her and pursue ambition; Avidius answers (items 6–11). Iuno finds this less boring, invites Rover to remain beside her and rest, and says there is no need for them to interfere **yet** (items 12–14). Her line's ZH/JA/KO object is Augusta and a gladiator; English names Avidius. The immediately adjacent named exchange makes Avidius a strong contextual referent, but the translation's narrower naming should be recorded rather than smuggled into every quotation. “Beside me” establishes a local invitation to pause while two others act, not exclusive romantic status, permanent passivity, or a rule to abandon Augusta if danger develops. Her temporal qualifier leaves later intervention open. She can enjoy a contest and respect another leader's initiative at once [IUN-E44/C39].

Both hunt states belong to quest `311000001`, and PlotHandBook `#/53` references their flow IDs 25 and 29; the later ability-disclosure state has flow ID 36. These are **static source joins**. We have not observed the exact runtime passage from invocation to field image, selected any playthrough, or reviewed the visual sequence. A source-grounded hypothetical may connect the themes—seeking better sight, then speaking from a dangerous landscape—but must mark the linkage as a candidate, not treat `8378/3` as mechanically causing every claim in `8381/3` [IUN-E44/C39].
