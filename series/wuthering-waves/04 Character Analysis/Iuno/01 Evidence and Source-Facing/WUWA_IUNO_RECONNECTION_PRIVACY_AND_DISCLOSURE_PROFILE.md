# Iuno — a second beginning is not a restored first one

The returned Iuno has two simultaneous social facts to live with: she remembers the people she saved, and those people no longer necessarily remember their history with her. The return conversation at `flow#/7759/3` makes the imbalance explicit. She tells Rover that friends she met on the way did not recognize her (`flow#/7759/3/28–29`), while Rover's remembrance is an exceptional sufficiency for her (`flow#/7759/3/30`). She then expects to befriend Augusta and the others again (`flow#/7759/3/31`). The Chinese, English and Korean witnesses emphasize becoming friends *as before*; the Japanese makes the process more explicit as beginning friendship again from the start and creating new memories. Neither wording says that Augusta has already regained the original memory. Iuno's optimism is about a possible relationship, not an evidentiary shortcut past the other person's present experience [IUN-E20, E33; IUN-C06, C27].

The earlier archive address `FavorWord_141004_Content` makes Rover's unusual position costly as well as cherished. Iuno says a person who remembers may suffer more than one who forgets and calls her wish to be remembered selfish because she did not know she could return. Chinese, English and Korean condition the exceptional rememberer on *if anyone* retains a trace of her; Japanese more directly singles out the addressee and asks that her whole self be remembered. The latter is an intense localization choice, not a reciprocal promise Rover has made or a claim that Iuno wants no other friend. The later bracelet and renewed recognition give context to the wish; they do not let Iuno or a narrator impose perpetual memory work, public disclosure of Chaos or romance on Rover [IUN-E08, E16, E20, E37; IUN-C03, C32].

## Four different kinds of memory and consent

For a new scene, the social state cannot be represented by a single `remembers_Iuno` flag. At minimum distinguish (1) Iuno's own memory of the shared past, (2) the counterpart's memory, (3) the counterpart's belief about her account, and (4) their present willingness to build or resume trust. Rover can know more than Augusta without speaking for Augusta. Iuno can be confident in her charm without being entitled to a friend's immediate recognition. The bracelet is a private anchor between Iuno and Rover at `flow#/7759/3/32–34`, after an earlier practical and memorial use (`flow#/8386/4/42–46`; `flow#/7741/5/23–28`). It does not provide a portable certificate that every old relationship has been restored. Likewise, the paired toasts in `FavorStory_141003_Content` and `FavorStory_141005_Content` make the continuing social loss visible: return anchors Iuno, not all lost reciprocal memories [IUN-E05, E07, E20; IUN-C03, C07].

This distinction is consequential for Augusta. Before the disappearance she joined a tactical plan without the full personal cost; afterward Iuno admits that concealment to Rover (`flow#/7759/3/17–19`). The returned Iuno *hopes* to become Augusta's friend anew, but the source does not record Augusta hearing, accepting, forgiving, or even being told the full Chaos history in that scene. A crossover writer should leave Augusta's agency open: she may respond to new conduct, question the past, or accept friendship without being granted a false recovered memory. The same applies to a new suitor who meets returned Iuno; a compelling story can permit attraction or care, but cannot treat Rover's exceptional memory or an archive intimacy line as transferable consent [IUN-E10, E15, E20; IUN-C06, C07, C27].

## Disclosure repair has a named recipient and a conditional future

At `flow#/7759/3/23–26`, Iuno answers Rover's objection to self-sacrifice or uncertain improvisation. She concedes that the more she trusts someone, the harder her impulses can be to restrain; then she promises that **if there is a next time**, Rover will be the first to know and will hear the full story. All four language witnesses of `Main_Linaxita_2_10_63_51` and `_52` preserve that conditional, addressed promise. This is a stronger statement than a vague aspiration to be honest, but a narrower one than a guarantee that she will disclose every private fact to everyone or that she already informed Augusta. The promise is untested future behavior in the pinned source. Her past concealment remains an ethical issue, especially where she solicits cooperation from someone who will bear a material cost [IUN-E20, E32; IUN-C08, C21, C26].

Shortly afterward, she asks Rover not to tell a third person what they saw and experienced in Chaos (`flow#/7759/3/37`). This is not a contradiction that can be solved by deleting either line. The future promise concerns advance information to the trusted participant in a possible future high-risk plan; the privacy request concerns disclosure of her own startling return to outsiders *after* the fact. The second request does not erase the first, and the first is no warrant for a citywide explanation of her personhood. Conversely, her privacy request should not be used as a blanket rationale for withholding risk from collaborators in the next rescue [IUN-E32; IUN-C26].

The three response routes are materially different. After the shared request, Rover may tease her embarrassment, agree to a private secret, or say they will think about it. The raw `flow#/7759/3/37` item has three `JumpTalk` targets: talk IDs 30, 32 and 33 respectively. The teasing path includes `flow#/7759/3/38–39`, where she denies embarrassment and invokes the difficulty of explanation and acceptance; the other responses go through `flow#/7759/3/40` or `/41`. These are alternatives, not a single transcript in which Rover both agrees and withholds agreement. The Chinese and Korean `/38` lines explicitly mention others' acceptance, while English stresses the explanatory hassle; `/39` is rendered in English as her being an “anomaly,” whereas Japanese foregrounds that people do not know her past. Those choices can color an individual dub's reading without changing the common source fact that she requests privacy. If Rover says they will think about it, the scene does not prove a promise to keep silent [IUN-E32; IUN-C26].

## What a durable model may and may not infer

Iuno's wish for a second beginning has a hopeful, socially active texture: she teases Rover about her charm, offers the bracelet again, asks for discretion, and then turns toward the shared celebration (`flow#/7759/3/31–45`). It is neither a retreat into isolation nor a demand for public testimony. A credible model can make her reluctant to have her old life explained over her head, yet willing to tell a trusted collaborator a dangerous plan's cost. Those are different privacy and informed-choice interests. It should ask whose story is being told, whose risk is being incurred, which time state applies and whether the other person has assented. These are *scenario controls*, not a claim that every future Iuno choice will be ethically perfect [IUN-C08, C22, C26–C27].

Two opposite simplifications fail. “She promised transparency, so she must tell every Septimontian everything” ignores the explicit privacy request and the named recipient. “She requested secrecy, so Augusta and Rover may never question concealed costs” ignores the earlier harm and the promise of fuller future disclosure. The more difficult interpretation keeps both duties available for negotiation. The later ascension-menu warning of a possible shared unknown is another *address*, not proof that she disclosed the old erasure cost before the joint plan [IUN-E35, C29]. Human listening of the twenty-four exact four-dub cases, especially the promise, forgotten-friend, three-route privacy and ascension clusters, remains to be done; text and a measured FLAC do not establish the actor's emotional intention. Runtime scene traversal should also verify the response route before treating any branch-specific reaction as her invariant attitude.
