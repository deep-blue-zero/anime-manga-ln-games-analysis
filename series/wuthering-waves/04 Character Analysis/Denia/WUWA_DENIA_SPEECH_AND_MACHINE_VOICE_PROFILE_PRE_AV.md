# Denia speech and machine-voice profile — pre-audiovisual

## Purpose and authority boundary

This profile separates three layers that must not be collapsed:

1. **textual speech findings** — diction, interactional habits, disclosure, rhetorical structure, and recipient-conditioned register;
2. **reproducible signal observations** — duration, level, channel count, and silence measurements attached to all retained voice renders;
3. **human performance interpretation** — perceived acting qualities, which have not been reviewed and remain optional.

The current source package supports a strong textual speech model and a complete basic signal census. It does **not** support acting labels such as sincere, flirtatious, tender, ashamed, or deceptive merely from acoustic measurements.

# 2. Textual baseline: familiar, low-ceremony, and socially active

Denia's default language is conspicuously informal for someone whose interior life is organized around danger, metaphysical intrusion, and uncertain identity. She prefers ordinary address, short reactions, teasing questions, mock complaints, and conversational bargains over solemn declaration.

Common moves include:

- “Fine, fine... I'll help” (`FavorWord_121124_Content`);
- playful surprise and challenge;
- casual reframing of danger as inconvenience;
- questions that make the recipient reveal a position;
- laughter or self-puncturing immediately after a heavy claim;
- joking applications of transactional language;
- reluctance to let ceremonial language stand uncontested.

This does not make her emotionally simple. Informality is both a genuine social preference and an instrument for controlling distance. It makes other people comfortable, keeps her from sounding like an institutional operative, and allows serious content to be floated without committing to a vulnerable register.

# 3. The “student Denia” register

At Startorch, Denia presents herself through a contemporary student register rather than through the vocabulary of a zealot or metaphysical vessel. She talks about classes, clubs, games, events, food, teachers, photos, and being too tired to work. In 3.2 ordinary scenes (`12391/5`, `12392/2`, `12413/8`), her dialogue is socially legible and low-stakes even when the larger plot is dangerous.

Her student register has several functions:

- **cover:** it conceals affiliation and unusual power;
- **participation:** it lets her inhabit activities not organized around experiment or mission;
- **self-testing:** she observes whether borrowed social forms produce felt attachment;
- **continuity:** later references to the Academy as her golden age show that the register becomes attached to memory.

The Chinese corpus often uses peer-address and casual-particle patterns that reinforce proximity. Official localizations may map that informality into different culturally legible personas. A future listening pass should therefore distinguish semantic informality, localization register, and performed characterization.

# 4. Meta-performance language

Denia frequently speaks as someone aware that social behavior can be staged. She comments on smiling, modeling, being fooled, acting like a real person, practicing goodbyes, and performing a role convincingly.

This metalinguistic awareness appears in several forms:

- explaining that models smile regardless of what they eat (`12581/3`);
- treating gentleness as something that can be researched and imitated;
- calling lying the one thing she is good at (`12588/5`);
- judging whether a social scene “worked” by how completely others were fooled;
- referring to a memory as possibly a movie scene;
- coding affection through “hate,” “least favorite,” or ex-friend language.

The result is not that every line contains a hidden opposite. Denia is simply unusually conscious of the fact that speech produces an audience effect. She can observe herself speaking almost as if she were reviewing a performance.

# 5. Questions as control and invitation

Denia asks many questions. They do not all seek information.

## 5.1 Diagnostic questions

She tests what another person knows, whether they believe her, and how far they will go. The infirmary negotiation (`17400/3`) is a clear example: the exchange establishes motive, price, risk, and whether Rover will admit mixed reasons rather than perform pure trust.

## 5.2 Philosophical questions

She converts metaphysical positions into concrete provocations:

- if life is a gift, why do babies arrive crying?
- if the world is built from lies, why fear deception?
- where does Rover's suffering end?
- can a person with a name and birthday die as a real person?

These questions are not neutral seminars. They expose the pressure points in her worldview.

## 5.3 Relational invitations

Some questions give the other person a small opening: trust me once; celebrate with me; ride with me; play a game; tell me whether this ending satisfies you. Even when the invitation is disguised as teasing, it creates the possibility of refusal and response.

# 6. Transactional and utility vocabulary

Denia learned in an environment where value meant use. That grammar persists in her speech:

- trash versus useful material;
- gifts that conceal conditions;
- deals and fair exchange;
- help as something owed or purchased;
- data as a parting gift;
- friendship framed temporarily as a useful cover;
- power borrowed at unacceptable risk.

She can employ this vocabulary critically, ironically, or sincerely. The key modeling question is whether a particular transaction expands mutual agency or disguises ownership.

Her deal with Rover is revealing because she appears to prefer explicit mixed motives to moral theater. A declared exchange can feel safer than unbounded kindness: the terms are visible, and both parties can answer.

# 7. Humor near horror

Denia often places a joke beside grim content. She can move from death rehearsal to casual laughter, from existential meaninglessness to an irritated aside, or from a dangerous plan to regret about missing cake.

This humor should not be reduced to a generic trauma defense. It performs several possible functions:

- lowering the social cost of disclosure;
- retaining control over the emotional temperature;
- protecting the recipient from a demand for immediate consolation;
- preserving an ordinary self inside a crisis;
- testing whether the other person will take the underlying claim seriously;
- expressing genuine amusement.

A faithful model allows more than one function at once. Direct AV and human listening would be required to determine whether particular jokes are brittle, dry, playful, exhausted, or sincerely buoyant.

# 8. Disclosure architecture

Denia rarely moves directly from concealment to complete confession. Her disclosures tend to occur in layers.

## Layer 1 — harmless self-presentation

She offers preferences, jokes, laziness, and low-risk social information.

## Layer 2 — controlled abnormality

She mentions her Forte, Aleph-1's continued mumbling, poor sleep at “home,” or the unpleasant conditions of her past without necessarily explaining the whole structure.

## Layer 3 — recipient test

She asks whether Rover trusts her, acknowledges a deal, or gives the recipient an opportunity to display caution.

## Layer 4 — partial truth with retained jurisdiction

She supplies enough information to coordinate but withholds what would allow others to stop her final plan.

## Layer 5 — terminal or retrospective confession

The full account arrives when Denia believes the decision is already irreversible: the birthday confession, the farewell bubble, or the confrontation with the creator.

Her developmental challenge is not merely to speak more honestly. It is to disclose early enough that other people can act on the truth rather than merely witness the completed choice.

# 9. Crisis register

In danger, Denia's speech becomes shorter and more operational. She identifies obstacles, controls her breathing, searches for keys, comments on enemy behavior, and gives concise movement instructions (`13556–13576`). The teasing register does not disappear completely, but mission language takes precedence.

This switch demonstrates that apparent slacking is not incapacity. She can coordinate, infer mechanics, and continue while frightened. The repeated self-command to calm down is especially important: composure is maintained through active speech rather than simply possessed as a trait.

Crisis lines should not be generalized into her ordinary social register. Conversely, her ordinary jokes should not be used to deny the seriousness of her operational competence.

# 10. Recipient-conditioned speech

## 10.1 Rover

With Rover, Denia combines challenge, philosophical probing, bargaining, confession, inverted affection, and possible romance-coded teasing. Rover is one of the few people to whom she can expose both the architecture of her lies and the wish to be contradicted.

Prediction: she is more likely to pose a morally loaded question than to ask directly for reassurance. When she does ask for trust, the request may itself contain a test.

## 10.2 Sigrika

With Sigrika, Denia is socially lighter and more protective. Nicknames, dry jokes, invitations, and strategic redirection dominate. The relationship also elicits her most ethically consequential concealment: she wants Sigrika's day to remain ordinary and does not permit her to meaningfully confront the farewell plan.

Prediction: Denia will often soften danger through humor or apparent normality around Sigrika, then attempt the costly action elsewhere.

## 10.3 Nastasha and teachers

She can be teasing and evasive with institutional adults, but Nastasha's noninstrumental care produces embarrassment and avoidance rather than simple defiance. Mornye is described through affectionate evaluation of voice, teaching style, and the fact that she does not interrupt naps (`FavorWord_121116_Content`).

## 10.4 Nivora / the Grand Architect

Her register becomes more direct, angry, and accusatory. She attacks the creator's ownership premises rather than trying to charm her way into recognition. The profanity or curse at `16229/1` is a break from polished student performance and should remain state-specific.

## 10.5 Aleph-1

Aleph-1 is represented as an intrusive discourse of meaninglessness. Denia sometimes paraphrases it rather than speaking in its voice (`FavorWord_121131_Content`). The distinction matters: repetition of Aleph-1's thesis is not automatic endorsement.

## 10.6 Strangers and institutional counterparts

Denia can be personable, strategically vague, and quick to establish an exchange. She is likely to hide the highest-risk information until she knows what the other party values.

# 11. Affection through negation

Denia's “least favorite,” “hate,” and ex-friend formulations can encode attachment while preserving emotional cover. The pattern works because the recipient and surrounding scene supply the corrective meaning.

It should be modeled as a probabilistic, relationship-specific move:

> When direct affection would expose dependence or turn a farewell into a plea, Denia may choose a negative label whose implausibility invites the trusted recipient to hear the opposite.

Do not apply this rule to hostile or low-trust contexts without evidence.

# 12. Localization boundary

Chinese is the semantic anchor. Japanese, Korean, and English are official witnesses that may differ in:

- casualness and politeness;
- age coding;
- nickname texture;
- sarcasm;
- sentence-final softness or force;
- explicitness of affection;
- philosophical abstraction;
- idiomatic framing of lies, gifts, and meaninglessness.

A future multilingual pass should classify each proposed difference as:

1. semantic divergence;
2. register divergence;
3. performance divergence;
4. uncertain interaction among the three.

No dub defines universal Denia by itself.

# 14. Why `machine_voice_profiled` is not yet claimed

The governing machine-voice protocol asks for more than file-level signal accounting. The current package does not yet provide a complete validated layer for:

- F0 medians, ranges, and variability;
- pause count and internal-pause density;
- voiced-duration segmentation;
- speaking-rate or articulation-rate proxies;
- spectral/breathiness proxies with reliability disclosure;
- text-length normalization;
- within-actor robust normalization;
- state-, interlocutor-, and source-class-conditioned comparison;
- clustering with stability checks;
- systematically selected medoids, outliers, and counterexamples.

Accordingly, the current status is:

> **complete media coverage plus partial reproducible acoustic features; not a completed machine voice profile.**

# 15. Recommended future machine partitions

When the fuller analysis is run, the corpus should be partitioned at minimum by:

- favor/archive versus story dialogue;
- ordinary student life;
- infiltration and tactical concealment;
- infirmary/fatigue;
- birthday social day;
- personhood confession;
- crisis traversal;
- creator/Aleph confrontation;
- post-return ordinary ecology;
- Rover versus Sigrika versus authority/creator interlocutors;
- present Denia versus past-memory Denia.

The four past-memory lines are too few for broad distributional conclusions but may be used as individually reviewed state-transition exemplars.

# 17. Speech-generation constraints

A reconstruction producing Denia dialogue should follow these rules:

- default to informal, contemporary phrasing rather than ornate abstraction;
- use teasing or mock complaint to regulate proximity;
- permit heavy ideas to enter through questions, jokes, or concrete examples;
- make bargaining and explicit terms feel safer than vague benevolence;
- switch to concise operational language in immediate danger;
- let affection become indirect when dependence is frightening;
- preserve recipient differences;
- avoid constant cryptic nihilism;
- avoid turning every line into a lie;
- do not imitate any specific dub's vocal flavor in text unless the requested language witness is explicitly selected.

The strongest Denia dialogue sounds ordinary enough to be lived and dangerous enough to reveal that ordinariness is something she had to learn.
