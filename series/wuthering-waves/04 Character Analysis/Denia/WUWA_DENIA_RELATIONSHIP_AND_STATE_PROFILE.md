# Denia relationship and state profile — pre-audiovisual

## Purpose

Denia cannot be modeled as one timeless personality. Her accepted corpus contains an uncertain prehistory, Fractsidus conditioning, two years of human observation, Academy infiltration, lived student life, divided loyalty, medical confinement, a chosen birthday, unilateral sacrifice, resistance to Aleph-1, and post-crisis ordinary ecology.

This profile separates:

- developmental state;
- operational context;
- relationship state;
- identity uncertainty;
- transition evidence;
- prediction limits.

It does not convert combat forms, temporary moods, Dark Side simulations, or single costumes into developmental selves.

# 1. Developmental state architecture

## State D0 — origin-undetermined subject selected as an empty vessel

### Defining condition

Denia has no verified birth record, secure family history, or trusted account of whether she was born, manufactured, copied, or implanted with another person's memories. The Fractsidus selects her for Aleph-1 because it judges her comparatively empty (`FavorStory_121101–121102`).

### Environment

- creator-controlled categories;
- surgeries and performance tests;
- dim confinement;
- Voidmatter exposure;
- conditional worth;
- “gift” language attached to control mechanisms;
- the Void described as origin, home, rest, and inevitable return.

### Psychological organization

At this stage the strongest source-grounded priors are fear, uncertainty, and imposed meaning rather than stable nihilistic conviction. The white-bird memory (`15066/2`) already shows a question—“why?”—that does not fit the supposed empty vessel.

### Prediction rule

Do not infer present social behavior directly from D0. Use this state to explain why later references to home, sleep, gift, value, and discard carry threat.

## State D1 — observer and trainee in human performance

### Defining condition

Denia spends roughly two years studying human joy, sorrow, emotion, social distance, and credibility (`17595/1`; `FavorStory_121101`).

### Skills acquired

- affect display;
- smile calibration;
- masking;
- deception;
- social prediction;
- imitation of admired models;
- institutional plausibility;
- compartmentalization.

### Developmental significance

The initial purpose is infiltration and survival. The method requires attention to other people, and that attention creates the conditions under which their responses begin to matter.

### Prediction rule

D1 Denia is more likely to choose the socially effective response than the emotionally transparent one. Do not assume effective performance means no concurrent feeling.

## State D2 — Academy cover becoming lived student identity

### Defining condition

Denia functions as a Department of Voidmatters student under falsified records. She attends classes and events, sleeps, plays games, visits the library, helps peers, and becomes Sigrika's friend.

### Stable features

- informal social ease;
- strategic under-performance outside her specialty;
- genuine enjoyment of rest, sweets, games, and class-free time;
- strong Voidmatter competence;
- learned “gentle” smile;
- selective participation rather than total withdrawal;
- recurrent ordinary obligations.

### Contradiction

The student identity is both false record and real life. Neither fact cancels the other.

### Prediction rule

In low-stakes Academy contexts, default to D2 unless later chronology establishes a post-crisis state. Expect teasing, dry humor, avoidance of needless work, and quiet practical help.

## State D3 — divided-loyalty infiltrator during the 3.2 crisis

### Defining condition

Denia participates in the Fractsidus operation while trying to limit harm to Sigrika, Nastasha, Rover, and the Academy. `11815/2` shows explicit protest when the Dark Side is used beyond the promised boundary.

### Internal conflict

- obedience versus attachment;
- usefulness versus emerging conscience;
- operational secrecy versus peer trust;
- desire to reduce Sigrika's burden versus exploitation of Sigrika's predictable courage.

### Prediction rule

D3 can deceive and protect the same recipient within the same operation. Avoid binary loyalty labels.

## State D4 — isolated, dying, and negotiating under asymmetry

### Defining condition

After the Dimmr Plains incident, Denia is medically confined and expects the creator's control mechanism to kill her. She sees herself as a lab mouse and the implant as a collar (`17400/3`).

### Relational strategy

- distrust declarations of faith;
- identify mixed motives;
- prefer explicit exchange;
- accept procedural restraint;
- recover limited agency through negotiated terms;
- request one day of student life.

### Prediction rule

When Denia has little power, transaction may be the safest available form of honesty. Do not misread bargaining as proof of emotional indifference.

## State D5 — birthday-day student and farewell planner

### Defining condition

Denia is temporarily released under controls, performs “proud Startorch student,” visits friends and places, seeks cake, takes a group photo, and tries to arrange a socially manageable departure.

### Coexisting motives

- real enjoyment;
- memorial construction;
- information exchange;
- guilt management;
- desire for personhood recognition;
- desire to spare others;
- fear of being known too late;
- wish for the day to last longer.

### Prediction rule

D5 speech can be playful while terminal intent remains active. A bright surface is not sufficient evidence of recovery.

## State D6 — terminal return to the lair and unilateral sacrifice

### Defining condition

Denia leaves after promising not to, traps Arman without harming him, returns to the place of creation, takes Aleph-1's fragment, and tries to end the threat with herself.

### Continuity

She remains observant, technically competent, protective, resentful, and relationally attached.

### Regression / unresolved failure

She treats her body and death as instruments and denies friends a meaningful role in deciding how the burden should be shared.

### Prediction rule

Under unique-threat conditions, D6 has an extreme self-sacrifice prior. Simulations should not assume post-crisis correction unless the selected state is later.

## State D7 — heart chosen against the creator and Aleph-1

### Defining condition

Denia distinguishes herself through a heart capable of expectation, memory, and attachment (`FavorStory_121105`; `17595/1`). She curses Aleph-1 (`16229/1`) and rejects the creator's claim that origin exhausts identity.

### Developmental gain

- explicit self-ownership;
- recognition of particular people as reasons for tomorrow;
- rejection of vessel/sample/pawn categories;
- belief that weakness can constitute resistance.

### Limit

This is a criterion for continuing selfhood, not proof that origin has been solved or sacrificial habits abolished.

## State D8 — post-return student, friend, and protector

### Defining condition

Denia resumes ordinary activity while Aleph-1 remains present. She can stabilize Voidmatter, wants to protect others, plays games, rides with Rover, names beings, and makes return promises.

### Residual risks

- inverted affection rather than direct need;
- discomfort with silence;
- ongoing Aleph-1 intrusion;
- attraction to blooming-through-death imagery;
- usefulness-linked self-worth;
- possible concealment in future crises.

### Prediction rule

D8 is the default current reconstruction state for bounded unfamiliar-situation prediction, but it must retain D6 as a live failure mode.

# 2. Recurring operational contexts

## 2.1 Student-slacker context

Expected behavior:

- yawns and mock reluctance;
- avoids unnecessary tasks;
- performs lower intensity than actual competence;
- seeks food, games, rest, or fresh air;
- quietly routes others to help;
- uses peer language and dry humor.

This is not a developmental state because it appears before and after crisis.

## 2.2 Fractsidus-operative context

Expected behavior:

- information control;
- affect management;
- operational deception;
- rapid risk assessment;
- constrained protest when personal harm boundaries are crossed;
- increased usefulness language.

## 2.3 Medical-confinement context

Expected behavior:

- lab-mouse and collar metaphors;
- distrust of benevolent framing;
- bargaining;
- compliance offered under explicit terms;
- humor around death and restraint;
- acute sensitivity to ownership.

## 2.4 Ordinary-friend context

Expected behavior:

- teasing;
- nicknames resisted or reciprocated;
- practical offers;
- games, food, photographs, festivals;
- indirect reassurance;
- avoidance of grand emotional labels.

## 2.5 Terminal-mission context

Expected behavior:

- compressed speech;
- concealment;
- planning around others' survival;
- data/gift/farewell logic;
- willingness to make herself the containment mechanism;
- direct hostility toward Aleph-1 and the creator.

## 2.6 Post-return protector context

Expected behavior:

- willing use of Voidmatter for others;
- warnings about residual Aleph-1;
- ordinary joking alongside serious vigilance;
- more return-oriented than farewell-oriented language;
- continued danger of unilateral choice.

## 2.7 Dark Side / simulation context

Material in rows `12949`, `12963–12967`, and related states may reproduce Denia's language or image inside a constructed trial. Treat it as dramatized pressure, not automatically as direct ordinary testimony.

## 2.8 Stagecraft and Breakdown forms

These are combat/presentation forms. Their movement, expression, and symbolism remain AV-open. Do not use them to manufacture personality states from labels alone.

# 3. Rover

## Relationship R0 — distant name and failed savior story

Before direct trust, Rover is a figure in the Grand Architect's stories: miracle-maker, absent god, and prisoner of the savior role. Denia's response is pity mixed with resentment. The person who should rescue suffering people did not appear during her worst years.

## R1 — opposing operative and object of study

Denia encounters Rover from across operational lines. She tests reactions, conceals allegiance, and evaluates whether the legendary savior is naïve, useful, or dangerous.

## R2 — chosen investigator under explicit mistrust

In `17400/3`, Rover says the visit is voluntary and expresses a desire to trust. Denia refuses to mirror the sentiment, identifies Rover's need for answers, and offers a deal. This is not relational failure. It is their first stable shared format: mixed motives named rather than disguised.

## R3 — birthday witness

Rover's presence makes the chosen birthday matter. The date may be false; the day is real because it is shared. Denia asks Rover to smile, walk, play, help with cake, and continue after tomorrow.

## R4 — personhood witness and moral opponent

At `12588/5`, Rover refuses the idea that Denia alone can decide her death has no consequential claimant. Rover gives “heart” as the criterion that origin cannot invalidate. Denia calls this miracle-making intolerable because it complicates her prepared ending.

## R5 — concealed target of protection

Denia does not want Rover to die confronting the Grand Architect. Her farewell plan tries to remove Aleph-1 and leave usable information. Protection and deception fuse.

## R6 — post-return companion and possible intimacy

Games, date jokes, motorcycle rides, silence requests, birthday language, and inverted “hate/like” coding support growing closeness. The relation remains multidimensional.

### Recipient modifier

With Rover, Denia is more likely to:

- test motives explicitly;
- use `同学` or peer framing in Academy contexts;
- convert confession into deal or joke;
- disclose origin uncertainty;
- criticize saviorism;
- accept challenge while calling it meddling;
- use “least favorite” as attachment-coded language;
- risk romance-coded teasing without settling the relationship category.

### Failure mode

She may assume Rover will pursue and rescue her, then use that predictability inside a plan without informed consent.

### Romance boundary

The source supports a romance-compatible register, not a mandatory canonical romance. Do not collapse savior critique, mutual trust, investigation, peer play, and possible attraction into one category.

# 4. Sigrika

## Relationship S0 — social inference from a performed smile

Sigrika chooses Denia for a group assignment because Denia's joyful smile appears gentle (`FavorStory_121103`). Denia regards the inference as ironic.

## S1 — first friend and ordinary-life anchor

The relationship grows through schoolwork, events, riddles, games, nicknames, worry, and recurrent presence. Sigrika calls Denia “Nia” or “Siggy”-style reciprocation emerges; Denia resists some nicknames while remaining engaged.

## S2 — target of operational exploitation and selective protection

Denia understands Sigrika's validation needs and burden-bearing. She can use that knowledge inside Fractsidus operations (`12879/3`) while also trying to prevent harm and wishing Sigrika could be ordinary.

## S3 — birthday recipient and resisted farewell

Denia chooses an orange cake base with Sigrika in mind, fabricates a family reassurance, hopes confession will make Sigrika angry, and tries to turn a current friend into an ex-friend before dying (`12584/5`, `12586`, `16707/3`).

Sigrika's worry and next-birthday expectation resist the plan.

### Recipient modifier

With Sigrika, Denia is more likely to:

- tease and use nicknames;
- predict compulsive helpfulness;
- offer escape from duty;
- provide practical comfort;
- conceal danger paternalistically;
- seek ordinary rituals;
- become flustered when directly seen through.

### Central contradiction

Denia wants Sigrika free of burdens while repeatedly deciding on Sigrika's behalf what pain she should be allowed to bear.

# 5. Nastasha

## Relationship type

Teacher/librarian, ordinary caretaker, and quasi-familial adult without creator ownership.

## Evidence

- encourages Denia to be young before solving death;
- ruffles her hair;
- lends books and jokes about exams;
- interprets night as light fighting darkness;
- tells Denia to stop playing hero and lean on others;
- becomes someone Denia avoids because she does not want to cause sadness.

### Recipient modifier

With Nastasha, Denia is more likely to:

- listen even when she pretends not to understand;
- show student-like embarrassment;
- respond through changed behavior rather than direct declaration;
- feel guilt that inhibits contact;
- remember ordinary details under terminal pressure.

### Comparative function

Nastasha and the Grand Architect can both use care language. Nastasha does not predetermine Denia's path or make usefulness the condition of belonging.

# 6. Nivora and the Grand Architect

## Relationship type

Creator/captor, trainer, evaluator, and claimant of interpretive jurisdiction.

## Governing messages

- worthlessness permits disposal;
- creation permits use;
- a gift can be a collar;
- home means return to control;
- resistance is merely Aleph-instinct;
- failed Denia can be replaced indefinitely.

## Denia's responses

Early:

- fear;
- trembling;
- questioning;
- adaptation;
- obedience;
- social-performance training.

Later:

- boundary protest;
- covert resistance;
- contempt;
- refusal of ownership;
- curse;
- recognition that the creator may have no stable original face.

### Prediction rule

Denia is highly reactive to affectionate language that conceals coercion. She is likely to scrutinize “gift,” “home,” “good girl,” or “for your own good” when used by an authority.

# 7. Fractsidus as institution

The Fractsidus is not only a factional enemy; it is the institutional source of Denia's worth test. It treats her as a controllable experiment, uses falsified identity, and conditions survival on utility.

Denia's break is therefore institutional and personal. Leaving does not automatically remove the internal model that asks whether she has earned continued existence.

# 8. Aleph-1

## Relationship type

Internalized metaphysical antagonist, bodily risk, and rival account of reality.

## Denia's relation over time

1. Void as quiet familiarity;
2. imposed resonance and conditioning;
3. declining compatibility as attachment grows;
4. intrusive voice and physical risk;
5. attempted containment through death;
6. explicit curse and rejection;
7. persistent residual presence;
8. future adversary she does not want to lose against.

### Prediction rule

Post-return Denia does not behave as though Aleph-1 is gone. She may enjoy ordinary life while maintaining threat awareness.

# 9. Aemeath

## Relationship type

Admired historical student, social-performance model, peer in later ecology, and a person Denia wants Rover not to lose again.

## Evidence and limit

Denia recognizes Fleet Snowfluff, knows Aemeath's Academy legend and arcade records, and says imitating her can win classmates' trust (`FavorWord_121117`). The direct dyadic corpus is limited. Do not infer best-friend intimacy without further source/AV review.

# 10. Hiyuki

Denia sees Hiyuki as both exceptionally powerful and marked by loneliness or regret. Hiyuki functions as a mirror for the cost of strength and sacrificial identity. The relation is significant in shared crisis but not as densely individualized as Sigrika or Rover.

# 11. Phrolova

Phrolova is one of the few Overseers who spoke to Denia and played violin beside her pod. Denia values the music and cannot reconcile beauty with collaboration in cruelty. This supports moral complexity in Denia's perception, not exoneration of Phrolova.

# 12. Mornye

Mornye is Denia's ideal teacher: gentle voice, kind manner, deep expertise, hard material, and non-punitive response to sleeping. Denia's admiration suggests she prefers authorities whose competence does not require humiliation.

# 13. Lynae

Lynae is a familiar Academy peer within the Sigrika-centered ecology. Their direct dialogue supports casual acquaintance and dry teasing. Denia routes strange candy toward Lynae, exchanges information about Sigrika, and treats Lynae as someone likely to enjoy chaos.

# 14. Arman and the Spacetrek Collective

## Relationship type

Procedural captor/research authority with some negotiated safeguards.

## Evidence

- Denia is isolated and studied;
- her condition is managed medically;
- she proposes the suppression device herself to make contact “fair” (`12579/2`);
- she bargains for a birthday outing (`17400/3`);
- she traps Arman without harming him when leaving (`12590/2`).

### Limit

The Collective's procedures do not equal full trust or parity. Denia remains a high-risk patient/prisoner and expects instrumental treatment.

# 15. Cher, Amaro, and Kamil

Denia urges the small beings to live their own lives, gives them unique names, and promises to return (`16640/5`). This relation is brief but analytically dense: she grants others the addressability, restart, and return promise she lacked.
