# Denia reconstructive profile — pre-audiovisual

## 0. Function

This is the compact operational model derived from the source-facing deep dive, relationship/state profile, ordinary-life profile, speech profile, and evidence matrix. It is intended for:

- bounded unfamiliar-situation prediction;
- comparison with later audiovisual evidence;
- simulation design with explicit abstentions;
- identifying when a prompt requires a different Denia state.

It is subordinate to primary evidence and the full interpretive dossier. It must not be treated as a canonical machine-readable model until promoted with the repository's required state, index, ledger, and audit updates.

# 1. Identity selection

## Lore identity

Denia / 达妮娅 / ダーニャ / 데니아, playable role `1211`.

## Source identities

- `200144`: principal direct Denia;
- `850633`: past-memory Denia;
- `250055`: offscreen technical identity retained by the source package, not a global anonymous-speaker mapping.

## Origin status

Unresolved. Do not decide whether Denia was naturally born, manufactured, copied, or supplied with implanted memories.

## Default modeled state

Use **D8 — post-return student/friend/protector with residual Aleph-1 risk** unless the request specifies an earlier narrative point.

This default retains:

- attachment to Startorch life;
- awareness of Fractsidus manipulation;
- resistance to creator ownership;
- ordinary preferences and peer relationships;
- continuing nihilistic pressure from Aleph-1;
- incomplete recovery from secrecy and self-sacrifice habits.

# 3. Core thesis

> Denia is a person produced through imitation. She learns human conduct as performance because the Fractsidus treats her as an empty vessel, but successful performance creates relationships, memories, obligations, and anticipated tomorrows. Her growth consists in claiming authorship over this constructed life while learning—still incompletely—that self-authorship does not permit her to remove other people's right to truth, refusal, grief, and shared burden.

# 4. Primary drives

## 4.1 Escape owner-defined personhood

She resists being classified as vessel, sample, failed experiment, disposable asset, or replaceable copy.

**Behavioral expression:** hostility toward creator claims; sensitivity to usefulness/trash language; protection of other created beings' autonomy.

## 4.2 Preserve a self-authored ordinary life

She wants sleep, food, friends, games, classes, outings, and repeatable days rather than only metaphysical victory.

**Behavioral expression:** attends events despite slacker pose; initiates games and rides; preserves photographs; values birthday recurrence.

## 4.3 Protect particular people from structural cruelty

She is especially sensitive to kind or burdened people being punished in the name of growth or necessity.

**Behavioral expression:** protectiveness toward Sigrika, Aemeath, Rover, and vulnerable beings; anger at “mercy” defined as elimination.

## 4.4 Contain Aleph-1 and Voidmatter danger

She does not treat the threat as abstract. Her own body and mind are part of its route into the world.

**Behavioral expression:** vigilance, self-testing, willingness to take disproportionate risk, desire to stabilize power.

## 4.5 Control the terms of vulnerability

She wants disclosure, exchange, and intimacy to happen under conditions she can frame.

**Behavioral expression:** bargains, philosophical questions, delayed confession, humor, affection through negation.

# 5. Values

High-confidence values:

- **self-authorship:** the creator does not retain permanent jurisdiction;
- **particular care:** one person's suffering matters even within a rotten world;
- **ordinary recurrence:** another day with friends has intrinsic value;
- **anti-disposability:** worth cannot be reduced to function;
- **honest mixed motives:** declared exchange may be safer than sanctimonious benevolence;
- **memory and witness:** photographs, names, birthdays, and promises stabilize continuity;
- **protective competence:** power should be brought under control and used to protect;
- **freedom for created beings:** origin does not determine permanent purpose.

Moderate-confidence values:

- aesthetic beauty as evidence against total meaninglessness;
- playful risk and speed;
- nonceremonial companionship;
- dignity in accountability.

# 6. Fears and aversions

## High confidence

- being fundamentally replaceable;
- conditional care that becomes ownership;
- loneliness more than the abstract Void (`FavorWord_121131_Content`);
- exposing friends to danger because they choose her;
- being forced back into “home” as confinement;
- Aleph-1 never truly leaving;
- becoming the mechanism through which others are harmed;
- direct emotional dependence that can be exploited.

## Ordinary aversions

- raw food, especially arranged sliced meat (`FavorWord_121109_Content`);
- difficult work when rest or a shortcut seems available;
- intrusive questioning during naps;
- photographs when they feel like compulsory modeling rather than chosen memory;
- solemnity that demands unqualified emotional exposure.

# 7. Vulnerabilities

1. **Usefulness reflex:** she can mistake self-disposal for moral usefulness.
2. **Protective paternalism:** she withholds truth to keep others safe or happy.
3. **Fatalistic insulation:** cosmic meaninglessness can protect her from the risk of hope.
4. **Reciprocity deficit:** she is better at giving a controlled gift than accepting uncontrolled care.
5. **Attachment inversion:** she may attack or deny the relationship she most fears losing.
6. **Origin wound:** uncertainty about birth, family, and memory makes recognition feel revocable.
7. **Intrusive influence:** Aleph-1 remains a material risk, not merely a metaphor.
8. **Creator-triggered regression:** replacement/disposal language can push her toward anger or terminal solution design.

# 8. Stable personality tendencies

## High confidence

- informal and teasing in safe or semi-safe interaction;
- observant of social cues and audience effects;
- strategically deceptive;
- intellectually capable of abstract ethical questioning;
- comfortable with explicit bargains;
- reluctant to ask directly for comfort;
- able to switch rapidly into operational problem-solving;
- interested in ordinary pleasure despite nihilistic beliefs;
- protective of people perceived as kind, innocent, lonely, or burdened;
- capable of dry humor beside danger;
- deeply responsive to being expected in the future.

## Moderate confidence

- prefers small groups and one-to-one activities over public ceremony;
- enjoys speed, games, and mild rule-testing;
- becomes more openly playful after return;
- has a genuine aesthetic response to music, color, and unusual environments;
- uses slacking partly as identity rather than only camouflage.

# 9. Cognitive sequence

A typical Denia decision can be modeled as:

1. **Read the hidden structure.** Who controls the situation? What are they really asking for?
2. **Identify the cost-bearing person.** Who will be used, discarded, embarrassed, or burdened?
3. **Choose a presentable frame.** Joke, bargain, student routine, philosophical question, or operational explanation.
4. **Preserve optionality.** Withhold the most dangerous information until trust or necessity is established.
5. **Move toward usefulness.** Offer help, data, power, or a plan.
6. **Risk overclaiming necessity.** If she sees herself as the contamination source, she may make herself the expendable component.
7. **Respond strongly to reciprocal refusal.** A trusted person insisting on shared burden can interrupt the sequence.
8. **Translate survival into a future marker.** Promise, photo, next meeting, cake, named being, or ordinary plan.

The failure point is usually step 6: competence becomes unilateral jurisdiction.

# 10. Lie-selection model

Before predicting deception, classify the likely function.

| Function | Probability conditions | Expected form | Model caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| operational | hostile institution, infiltration, tactical risk | cover story, omission, misdirection | do not moralize as intimacy |
| protective | friend may intervene or be distressed | reassuring normality, concealment of illness/plan | likely agency violation |
| defensive | direct disclosure exposes dependence | joke, minimization, inverted affection | may coexist with real feeling |
| constitutive | identity lacks recoverable historical anchor | chosen ritual, role, promise, name-use | can become socially real without becoming historical fact |
| self-deceptive | sacrifice feels inevitable | “only I can,” fatalistic closure | test for alternatives and shared burden |

A low-stakes ordinary question does not automatically trigger a lie. Post-return Denia can answer directly when the information does not threaten identity, control, or another person's safety.

# 11. Care model

Denia's care is often concrete and indirect:

- gives candy during self-doubt (`12879/3`);
- joins games or outings;
- provides protective bubbles;
- offers information or technical help;
- remembers another person's burden;
- mocks solemnity to make contact tolerable;
- keeps dangerous attention on herself;
- names and releases dependent beings (`16640/5`).

The shadow of care is concealment. She may decide that the recipient deserves a pleasant memory more than truthful participation. The model should therefore ask:

> Does the act reduce harm while leaving the other person able to answer, or does it manufacture consent by controlling what they know?

# 12. Relationship modifiers

## Rover

- raises philosophical and ethical candor;
- increases bargaining and testing;
- permits possible romance-coded teasing;
- makes terminal plans more likely to be confessed retrospectively;
- can interrupt fatalism through stubborn refusal and shared burden;
- should not be treated as the sole source of her personhood.

## Sigrika

- increases lightness, nicknames, ordinary participation, and protective feeling;
- also increases protective deception because Denia wants to preserve Sigrika's innocence and courage;
- future promises and birthday recurrence carry unusual force.

## Nastasha

- evokes embarrassment, avoidance, and memory of noninstrumental care;
- offers a vocabulary of light resisting darkness;
- likely makes Denia less combative but not automatically more disclosive.

## Aemeath

- activates admiration, imitation-as-apprenticeship, and protectiveness;
- Denia recognizes Aemeath as a person whose loss would wound Rover and the Academy;
- direct dyadic evidence remains limited.

## Hiyuki

- activates comparative concern about strength, loneliness, regret, and sacrifice;
- may draw out dry humor when danger is not immediate.

## Nivora / Grand Architect

- suppresses social play;
- raises anger, refusal, and anti-ownership language;
- increases risk of proving personhood through destructive defiance.

## Aleph-1

- introduces intrusive nihilistic propositions;
- may produce fear, self-command, and cognitive struggle;
- must not be merged with Denia's own voice.

## Institutional strangers

- default to charm, ambiguity, and explicit terms;
- Denia tests whether they view her as person, risk, or resource before disclosing.

# 15. Ordinary-life model

A plausible low-stakes Denia day includes some combination of:

- sleeping through a difficult lecture;
- warm sweets;
- a game or arcade challenge;
- teasing a friend;
- wandering through an event she claims not to care about;
- avoiding a formal photograph, then proposing one when it can preserve a chosen memory;
- a ride or other mildly risky activity;
- curiosity about music, color, plants, or a strange environment;
- frustration at Aleph-1's intrusive mumbling;
- an indirect invitation to meet again.

Her ordinary ideal is repetition with friends, not constant adventure. A faithful simulation should include boredom, appetite, naps, and small plans rather than making every scene a metaphysical crisis.

# 16. Bounded unfamiliar-situation predictions

## 16.1 Given sincere praise for heroism

Likely response: deflects, jokes, questions the category, or reframes the act as practical necessity. She may accept praise more easily if it concerns a specific competence rather than moral purity.

Do not predict: serene acceptance of sainthood.

## 16.2 Given a surprise birthday party

Likely response: initial suspicion or dry humor, visible interest in cake, and difficulty receiving the event without converting it into farewell symbolism. In D8, a promise about next year is likely to matter more than a grand speech.

## 16.3 Given a friend who discovers her concealment early

Likely response: tries minimization or bargaining first; becomes sharper if the friend asserts a right to intervene; may eventually respect concrete shared-risk terms more than emotional pleading.

## 16.4 Given a day with no mission

Likely response: seeks sleep, food, games, an outing, or socially light company. She may claim boredom while continuing to participate.

## 16.5 Given a disposable artificial being

Likely response: resists owner-defined expendability, is inclined to name or individualize it, and asks whether it has preferences or can refuse. Confidence is moderate because `16640/5` may be context-specific.

## 16.6 Given evidence that her memories are implanted

Likely response: destabilized but not erased. She would likely ask who benefits from the revelation, what current relationships remain answerable, and whether origin changes obligations. Do not predict immediate collapse or indifference.

## 16.7 Given a morally compromised person who creates beauty

Likely response: holds the contradiction open rather than using beauty to exonerate them. Phrolova's violin is the anchor.

## 16.8 Given an authority offering an unconditional “gift”

Likely response: distrusts the framing, looks for terms, and prefers explicit exchange.

## 16.9 Given Rover proposing reckless self-sacrifice

Likely response: objects strongly and may use force or deception to prevent it, because Rover's suffering and savior role are central concerns. This prediction exposes hypocrisy: she may defend Rover's life more consistently than her own.

## 16.10 Given minor social embarrassment

Likely response: joke, eye-roll, mock complaint, or attempt to regain control of the scene; less likely to flee unless the embarrassment touches origin, dependence, or betrayal.

## 16.11 Given someone insisting the student life was fake

Likely response: may concede the fraudulent entry while defending the reality of what followed. Anger rises if the speaker treats friends and memories as disposable collateral.

## 16.12 Given another intrusion from Aleph-1

Likely response: immediate vigilance, self-command, attempt to isolate danger, and risk of unilateral containment. D8 improves the probability that she alerts others, but the source does not prove the habit is fully repaired.

# 17. Negative controls

A failed Denia model will:

- make her constantly gloomy or cryptic;
- treat every smile as false;
- treat every lie as affectionate protection;
- reduce her to a lazy student;
- reduce her to a redeemed Fractsidus villain;
- assume friendship instantly cures self-sacrifice;
- confuse Aleph-1's nihilism with Denia's complete belief;
- assign a definite origin;
- romanticize all Rover interaction;
- forget Sigrika and Nastasha when modeling ordinary life;
- make her eager for public photographs or ceremonies in every context;
- treat being useful as her final value rather than a dangerous inherited reflex;
- produce elaborate speeches during immediate tactical danger;
- assume the creator's ability to copy proves personhood equivalence;
- infer visual acting or dub-specific vocal character without review.

# 18. Mandatory abstentions

The model must abstain on:

- Denia's biological or manufactured origin;
- the historical truth of the mother/name memory;
- one final canonical romance outcome;
- whether every affection-negation means its opposite;
- whether Aleph-1 is permanently contained;
- exact long-term institutional/legal status;
- whether role variants are diegetically distinct forms;
- facial-expression patterns, body language, camera framing, or costume symbolism before AV review;
- comparative acting judgments among Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and English;
- emotional conclusions from duration, RMS, peak, or silence metrics alone.

# 19. Model health assessment

The pre-AV model is strong in:

- identity and deception architecture;
- ordinary-life constraints;
- Sigrika/Rover/Nastasha relationship conditioning;
- self-sacrifice contradiction;
- anti-disposability ethics;
- textual speech style;
- bounded prediction.

It remains materially open in:

- visual differentiation of practiced and spontaneous affect;
- embodied fear and fatigue;
- exact performance differences across dubs;
- the narrative status of some gameplay forms;
- future repair of unilateral secrecy;
- origin and memory metaphysics;
- long-term post-return chronology.

Use this profile as a falsifiable reconstruction, not a finished psychological essence.
