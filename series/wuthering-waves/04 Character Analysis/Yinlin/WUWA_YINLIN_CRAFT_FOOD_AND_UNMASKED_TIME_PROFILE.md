# Yinlin — a craft and a meal after the cover ends

Yinlin's undercover life makes ordinary conduct especially easy to misread. A meal can be an assignment. A puppet show can hide an investigation. A promise not to lie can be unusually meaningful precisely because concealment remains part of her job. The shortcut “everything she does is a mask” denies her ordinary preferences; the shortcut “Rover is the one person who makes all masks disappear forever” misstates their bounded, time-indexed relationship. This profile tests that tension through small repeatable activities rather than treating leisure as a decorative reward after the main quest. The [claim matrix](WUWA_YINLIN_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md) gives exact bundles, counterreadings and revision tests; the [source audit](WUWA_YINLIN_SOURCE_CENSUS_CHRONOLOGY_AND_IDENTITY_AUDIT.md) defines the pinned route and optional-state limits. All quoted source surfaces below are text witnesses, not human-listened performances.

## A puppet is an instrument; theater is a practiced social act

`FavorWord_130206_Content` (`favorword#/577`) says puppet theater is a Huanglong tradition, not especially fashionable in Jinzhou. Yinlin learned it from Dollmaker to improve her manipulation technique and to blend into crowds. She then offers to teach Rover. That offer is not evidence that every earlier lesson from her guardian was benevolent, but it gives the craft a transmissible, social form: she can demonstrate and teach it outside an immediate fight. The Japanese rendering explicitly says she likes the theater, a shade of positive affinity stronger than the Chinese anchor's neutral account; the Chinese festival lines later supply their own evidence of pleasure [YIN-E08, C03].

`FavorWord_130211_Content` (`favorword#/582`) gives Zapstring/悬丝 a mundane material history. The puppet was a common practice product, modified by Yinlin for combat. It is old but handles most comfortably for her. The source supports practiced attachment to a repeatedly used tool, not an independent consciousness, a bespoke childhood gift, or proof that she authored the original puppet. The English “novice” can imply more about the maker than the Chinese “ordinary practice item”; what matters across the witnesses is the subsequent adaptation and familiarity. An imagined scene where she adjusts strings, repairs worn joints, or teaches grip is plausible extrapolation from a working artifact. A scene claiming Zapstring makes autonomous moral decisions would need different evidence [YIN-E08, C14].

The five-story archive also refuses a single moral purpose for theater. In `FavorStory_130202_Content` a public performance helps draw out a kidnapped child and subdue an abductor. Its crowd-pleasing form enables protection. In the character quest, puppet likenesses and the Dollmaker's project create a much more troubling system of grief, coercion and uncertain personhood. The same physical vocabulary—strings, replicas, concealed control—cannot be stamped either “heroic art” or “evil manipulation” without identifying the subject, audience and consent. Yinlin learned the medium from someone who genuinely rescued her and later endangered others; choosing to keep the medium is not choosing his ends [YIN-E01, E04, E10, E17].

## The festival does not erase the work behind a free show

The Moonlit Festival begins with logistics, not a transcendent declaration. At `3987/6/1–9`, Rover asks her to replace an unavailable puppeteer. She says she has a temporary gap in assignments, can appear for several days if the fee is paid, considers festival-appropriate designs, invites Rover's ideas, and notes there is no time to fabricate an entire cast from scratch. Ready-made puppets therefore become a practical acquisition. Those details matter for modeling: she is a craft worker with preparation time, materials, compensation, and repertoire choices, not a magician who produces any desired spectacle without effort [YIN-E21, C03].

The apparently criminal cargo detour must be read to its conclusion. At `3991/6/2–17`, Yinlin inspects the goods, finds fresh meat without a hidden compartment or poison, accepts the men's explanation, and apologizes. In isolation this looks like a false suspicion honestly corrected. At `3992/7/6–16`, Rover's option indicates that they already recognized the truth, and she says that level of *performance* was unlikely to fool them. Alternative Rover prompts can cite her old informant's identity, the purchaser's appetite and the cargo, or intuition; they should not be merged into one witnessed chain of deductions. What she knew at the instant she first saw the informant is not explicit, and the source does not show her staging the actual transaction. Her later statement could also serve pride or face-saving. The durable reading is narrower: she allows an apparent mistaken investigation to play before one audience and later *claims* a performed component to another. A character model must not cite this as a proven detective blunder, nor inflate it into proof of omniscience [YIN-E21, C09, C17].

The ordinary pleasure survives that reveal. At `3992/7/17–23`, a branch-dependent exchange links the day's unusual happiness, feeling unmasked with Rover, and a request that Rover keep watching future performances. At `4231/1/3–5` she distinguishes former puppet shows that required vigilance toward targets from this chance to perform according to her own ideas; she says she is beginning to enjoy it, while also warning that unfinished work may take her away without notice. The future-show request is at `3992/7`, not `4231/1`. These are not proof of retirement, a standing schedule, or an exclusive audience. They show why a craft she mastered for danger can remain worth doing when she is not forced to watch a suspect [YIN-E22, C03, C18, C21].

## Undercover eating and food she actually remembers

`FavorWord_130208_Content` (`favorword#/579`) places expensive meals in undercover work and an unpretentious Aureate Fried Rice in her memory. The Chinese line praises the maker's skill over ingredient price. English adds “care,” while Japanese praises the cook's poured-in “soul”; those are sympathetic renderings, but the anchor does not establish that she knows the specific cook, has a signature recipe, or cooks the dish herself. Her taste is evaluative and craft-sensitive. It makes a small link to puppetry: she values what someone *does* with ordinary material more than the prestige of the material alone. That comparison is an interpretive analogy, not a source claim that she consciously explains her art through cooking [YIN-E29, C24].

`FavorWord_130209_Content` (`favorword#/580`) is not an answer of simple food indifference. With an investigative target she has to order to their taste and smile through food she finds repellent. Asked what she dislikes, the Chinese reply shifts emphasis to people with whom she would rather not share a table; she makes Rover an exception, saying that with them the menu matters little. English smooths this to no dishes she truly dislikes, despite the source's explicit revulsion. Korean initially broadens it to disliking meals with other people, then also makes a Rover exception. The stable four-language core is **performed compliance with targets versus voluntarily chosen company**, not absence of culinary preference or blanket social avoidance. A hypothetical quiet meal may let her drop the target's required smile and have an opinion; it should not force romance or invent a permanent domestic routine from one archive address [YIN-E09, E29, C24].

## A day offered is not a lifetime claimed

`FavorWord_130205_Content` (`favorword#/576`) says she could shed operational identities, yet values being remembered by Rover as Yinlin. English expresses this as being “myself,” whereas the Chinese anchor stresses being a singular Yinlin in Rover's memory. `FavorWord_130207_Content` (`favorword#/578`) adds a practical constraint: meetings with a high-profile Rover could expose her identity. She explicitly does not ask for the meetings to stop, only for more care. `FavorWord_130217_Content` (`favorword#/588`) similarly places a boundary around saying publicly what she does. Wanting a known name in one relationship is not a commitment to disclose classified work in all settings [YIN-E07, C18].

The birthday line `FavorWord_130213_Content` (`favorword#/584`) is a particularly sharp multilingual hinge. In Chinese she notices Rover's skepticism, says it truly is their birthday, pledges not to utter a lie **at least today**, and asks for all of today's time if they have made no plans. Japanese and Korean also express a birthday-day no-lie commitment. English says she is “not playing any games this time” and asks to have Rover “all to myself” for the day. The latter is more possessive; the former is less precise than the explicit no-lie vow. Neither difference can be flattened into a universal rule that she is honest forever with Rover, or a proof that she possesses their time. The question form matters: it is an invitation, not an observed acceptance. If a scenario has Rover decline, her reaction is not supplied by this archive line and should be written as an extrapolation [YIN-E30, C19, C25].

At `3992/7/19`, the Chinese says she can speak and act as she wishes without a mask when with Rover. English adds “You are... the only one.” Japanese and Korean retain the more bounded when-with-you condition. This supports a meaningful privileged intimacy in the scene, but does not by itself settle mutual exclusivity, all future secrecy, or the ethics of the earlier tracker and shock. The festival's staged case and subsequent “unmasked” claim can coexist if the state is carefully timed: a person can test or perform before later choosing a more candid interval. A model should specify *which* interval is being generated, rather than impose one permanent “real Yinlin” register on the entire archive [YIN-E12, E21, E30, C04, C25].

## Scenario tests and open checks

A source-respecting low-stakes scene can ask Yinlin to teach one puppet movement, choose a simple skilled meal over a prestigious one, negotiate a public performance's supplies, or invite someone to a bounded day away from an assignment. She may still check the room, protect her identity in public, or tease someone who notices a staged mistake. Those habits need not make the meal or show a hidden operation. Conversely, a fictional partner does not acquire consent to surveillance because they like her, and English intensification does not create a canon exclusive relationship. The [model probes](WUWA_YINLIN_MODEL_FIDELITY_AND_STRESS_TEST_PRE_AV.md) now test these failure modes rather than merely noting them.

Three observations remain open. First, the exact festival branch and on-screen staging could date when she knew the cargo was lawful. Second, four-dub listening of the archive birthday, food and identity lines could test delivery—without changing what the text says. Third, direct runtime footage could establish visible puppet handling, stage labor and reactions not inferable from static UI art. Until then, sensory mannerisms and acting intentions are hypotheses; text, conditional source graph, and measured media provenance are separate evidence planes.
