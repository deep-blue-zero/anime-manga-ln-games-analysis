## 1. Ordinary evidence is a constraint, not decoration

The ordinary-life corpus stops the reconstruction from becoming an account of nothing but pressure. Sigrika has tastes, amusements, objects she carries, uneven practical skills, people she wants to invite, and things she would like to see. Those details do not need to originate in the Dark Side ordeal to matter. The distinction throughout is between **source fact**, an **analytical implication**, and a limit on extrapolation. An activity offered through a branch is available conduct, not a record that all players chose it. ## 2. Food: precise tastes rather than a generic sweet tooth

**Source facts.** The archive gives specific attachments to Fruit Crackle associated with her mother and to Smoked Meat Stew, while Grilled Fern Spore is an explicit aversion. Her own version of a food and her mother's preparation must not be silently treated as identical. Birthday-cake dialogue includes an orange preference and practical advice within a shared project. [SIG-E35](WUWA_SIGRIKA_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#sig-e35) [SIG-E47](WUWA_SIGRIKA_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#sig-e47)

**Inference.** Food can join sensory pleasure to memory and care. A familiar taste makes home available in an ordinary form; helping with a cake lets care become an activity rather than a declaration. But neither observation warrants a complete nutritional profile, a universal preference for all sweet foods, or a claim that she cooks every dish competently.

A generic gameplay description that she is “not picky” cannot erase a named dislike. The defensible synthesis is a broadly accepting food stance with an explicit exception, and even that generalization should retain the difference between playful caption and direct archive preference. ## 4. Craft: an imperfect object can be a good gift

The folded black cat is a concrete thank-you/apology object. Its slightly crooked or imperfect quality belongs to the gift rather than disqualifying it. Sigrika expends attention on making something for a recipient; the recipient does not need it to certify expert craftsmanship. [SIG-E28](WUWA_SIGRIKA_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#sig-e28)

**Inference.** This offers a modest counterpoint to the fear that valued contribution requires flawless performance. The evidence does not prove she has consciously generalized that lesson. It shows that an imperfect contribution can function successfully within this relationship. That is a useful constraint on predictions about future gifts: effort and recipient relevance may matter more than expensive polish.

Do not upgrade the example into a general claim that she is skilled in every handicraft, that all handmade objects carry the same apology, or that accepting this gift establishes a romantic contract.

A missed fan event is a particular disappointment, not proof that she always sacrifices leisure or that her tastes are secretly superficial. Film invitations are branchable; the corpus preserves possible attendance and response rather than one compulsory itinerary. [SIG-E38](WUWA_SIGRIKA_EVIDENCE_AND_FALSIFICATION_MATRIX.md#sig-e38)

The source supports enjoyment and conversation about what has been watched or heard. Celebration can also make a future social commitment tangible. Denia's cake and next-birthday request are important because another ordinary event is being anticipated. The point is not that a cake solves concealed danger. It is that an ordinary ritual can contest the assumption that a person's current day must be their last shared one. ## 13. Everyday prediction boundaries

**Supported tendencies, not scripts:** she may offer a recipient-relevant small gift; enjoy a birdwatching or shared-play invitation; connect a familiar food with home; become apologetic about imperfect work; and, in a developed supported state, decline an unmanageable task or replace a nonurgent plan with rest. **Insufficient evidence:** exact spending habits, full sleep schedule, alcohol preferences, comprehensive dietary restrictions, every genre preference, medical explanations for fatigue, private romantic commitments, and the location or occupation of an adult future. These should remain unknown rather than filled from an archetype.

The governing constraint is simple: preserve both the person who can overburden an ordinary game with expectations and the person who can simply want to play it. Which response is likely depends on the state, stakes, recipient, and information—not on the analyst's need to make every detail repeat the thesis.
