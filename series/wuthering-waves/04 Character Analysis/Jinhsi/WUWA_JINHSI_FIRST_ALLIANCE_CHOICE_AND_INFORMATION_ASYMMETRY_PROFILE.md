# Jinhsi — an alliance offered inside a prepared encounter

Jinhsi says she does not want to exchange knowledge of Rover's lost past for service to Jinzhou. That is a genuine and important ethical statement. It is not the whole structure of their first meeting. Before Rover hears it, Jinhsi has arranged a citywide welcome, a distinctive token that can identify the guest and draw hidden threats into view, discreet Sanhua protection, and a three-day appointment she risks injury to keep (`FavorStory_130405_Content`). At `flow#/1191/3`, she explains some of that preparation, requests secrecy about Jué's absence, lays out a security case against Scar, and asks for operational assistance. Her anti-barter statement comes after Rover has chosen between *joining now* and *considering*, not before all her strategic framing. The tension is not hypocrisy proven by one line, nor flawless informed consent proven by another. It is the effort of a young Magistrate to protect an unknown guest and a city while retaining control of when each party learns the stakes [JIN-E06, E10, E25–E27; JIN-C03, C23–C26].

## The preparation makes hospitality consequential

The fifth favor story rejects her reputation for omniscience at the exact moment it explains her detailed preparations. She knows the historical record of someone able to absorb Tacet Discords, anticipates an unusual guest, and is frank in retrospective narration that much about this person remains unknown. Her announcement welcomes visitors broadly rather than naming one secret identity. The token has two functions in her account: it helps her identify the guest attuned to her plan and draws out hostile elements already threatening Rover and Jinzhou. She also has Sanhua guard Rover without obvious introduction. Changli later acknowledges Rover's inference that the tokens were her suggestion and apologizes for the earlier burden (`flow#/3296/4/6–9`); this cross-character witness makes the welcome collaborative without transferring every Jinhsi decision to her teacher. It is too simple to call this a trap designed to sacrifice Rover; the source gives a protective route. It is also too simple to call it wholly disclosed hospitality: Rover cannot consent in advance to a plan whose token, surveillance and strategic purpose are not yet explained [JIN-E06, E25].

The [actual public message](WUWA_JINHSI_PUBLIC_INVITATION_PRE_FIRMAMENT_AGENCY_AND_BATTLE_CHOICE_PROFILE.md) makes “broadly” precise. It mentions one guest of special importance but gives no identifying traits, asks conditionally to meet, and says in all four texts that the visitor is free to choose (`flow#/2948/1/8–15`). The freedom language precedes this private meeting, so it is not merely a postchoice rationalization. The broadcast still does not reveal the token's functions or Sanhua's assignment, and it contains no recorded guest response. Its public assurance therefore strengthens evidence for Jinhsi's stated principle without proving informed acceptance of her protective design [JIN-E31; JIN-C29].

The favor story is a retrospectively favorable narration. It makes the three-day return, Jinhsi's injuries and her rehearsal of the greeting emotionally vivid; it cannot verify every risk calculation or establish that her welcome was harmless to everyone. The live encounter's dry joke about whether she is another projection (`flow#/1191/3/3–8`) is likewise one optional opening, not a universal event in every route. Jinhsi is capable of personal wit within official ceremony; this does not dissolve the information gap. A useful model should ask which stage of the encounter the other person has reached: before the token is explained, after the Scar warning, after a choice, or after the later identity conversation.

## A choice to join or consider is not a refusal branch

The raw `TalkSequence` in `RAW_RELEVANT_FLOW_STATES.jsonl` row `1191`, action 3, is decisive. A common sequence ends with Jinhsi's conditional safety assurance at `Huanglong_main_1_5_79_32` (`flow#/1191/3/24`). Rover's alternatives are `..._79_33` (“join”) and `..._79_34` (“consider”). Their `JumpTalk` actions lead to TalkIDs 24 and 25: Jinhsi thanks Rover for trust in `..._79_35` or says she respects the decision in `..._79_36`. Both replies jump to TalkID 26, after which the long explanation continues. The two replies are mutually exclusive; the later explanation is shared. There is no observed “I refuse to help” option at this node. Her respect for a decision is textually real, but this local graph proves room to *defer*, not an enacted full refusal of cooperation or a divergent mission outcome [JIN-E10, E26; JIN-C03, C23].

This distinction improves rather than weakens the account of her ethics. The Chinese conditional invitation, the English “if willing,” the Japanese direct polite request, and the Korean conditional safety promise all make cooperation solicitational rather than an order. The “consider” route is explicitly accepted by all four localized replies. A reconstruction may have her listen to hesitation and allow thought, because the source shows exactly that. It should not claim a tested willingness to abandon the Scar operation or an observed consequence if Rover declines altogether. Conversely, treating the absence of a refusal button as proof that Jinhsi would compel service in-world would mistake game dialogue affordance for a demonstrated policy.

## Nonbarter is a stated norm, not retroactive transparency

After both response routes rejoin, Jinhsi says she has not waited for a hero to descend and save Jinzhou (`..._79_80`). She wants to help Rover understand their past because that uncertainty troubles them (`..._79_81`). At `..._79_82`, the Chinese anchor explicitly refuses to use that information as a reason to *make a demand*; EN frames help as appreciated but not obligated, JA rejects exploiting Rover's wish to know, and KO emphatically denies an imposed duty. These witnesses reinforce the same nonbarter norm without having identical rhetorical force. At `..._79_83–85` she returns responsibility to herself, the soldiers and diligent citizens, then trusts Rover to choose after learning more. Later she promises not to block departure from Jinzhou or Huanglong once the present crisis settles (`..._79_88`) and offers future help (`..._79_89`) [JIN-E10, E27; JIN-C03, C15, C24].

The timing matters. Her argument follows the threat description, her contingent protection offer and Rover's join/consider choice. It offers a moral explanation for why her intelligence-sharing should not be treated as an invoice. It does not make the earlier token and hidden guard retrospectively transparent. It also does not prove an unrestricted exit *during* an immediate city emergency; her promise has a postcrisis condition. A hypothetical Rover who asks for information but declines an assignment is strongly supported as a case where she should not declare the past “payment.” Whether she would accept a categorical refusal at the earlier node without disappointment or renegotiation is a prediction, not directly tested by this graph.

The final line of this common route, `..._79_90`, asks to keep the conversation between them. The Chinese and English end as a question; Japanese makes a polite request and Korean seeks assent. The retained TalkItem has no visible response option. We may say she requests confidentiality; we may not record Rover's agreement, a forever-binding pact, or a general ban on disclosing all information about Jué. Nor is this exactly the same issue as her public withholding of the Sentinel's absence. The source contains at least three audiences—Rover, Jinzhou's population, and the hostile Fractsidus—and the legitimate scope of withholding could differ across them. A model that labels every secret “deception” misses security context; one that labels every withheld fact “protection” cannot be falsified [JIN-E10, E27; JIN-C25].

## Later disclosure is partial and bounded by what she knows

At `flow#/2514/6/1–3`, Jinhsi tells Rover that she will not yet divulge Scar's allegation about Jué until she can verify it with Jué. She does not silently present the allegation as established truth, but she does refuse information to a person she earlier described as free to choose. In the same conversation she explains that Black Shores members contacted her about Rover on the day Rover awoke and helped bring about their meeting; she says they still watch Rover, have assisted with Lament analysis, and are not equivalent to Fractsidus. Crucially, she also says she does not know their deeper purpose (`flow#/2514/6/18–26`). The text does not specify every datum she supplied in response to their inquiry. Thus a scene in which Rover asks “what did you tell them about me?” should trigger a bounded answer or an admission that the packet cannot establish it, rather than an invented private-data leak or a blanket assurance of zero sharing [JIN-E11, E28; JIN-C06, C25].

This later admission preserves a social cost of her staged greeting. Rover may reasonably trust her intention to protect while objecting to being watched or having a dangerous clue withheld. Jinhsi's best-supported response is neither the omniscient ruler who dismisses the complaint nor an automatic confession of an unobserved act. She can give the reasons and limits she actually has, distinguish unverified claims from verified threat, and accept that her counterpart's consent is not wholly supplied by her own good aims. That is an analytical extrapolation from the tension among her words and conduct, not a recorded apology or a numerical behavior probability.

## Reconstruction boundary

A source-faithful first-alliance scene should declare the exact route: initial greeting option, join versus consider, and whether it occurs before or after the long common explanation. It should distinguish *request for help*, *postcrisis freedom to leave*, *request for secrecy*, and *a demonstrated refusal path*—the last is absent here. It should not project later fond favor address or optional fair intimacy back onto this guarded initial alliance. The six newly selected four-dub sound cases in the [AV crosswalk](WUWA_JINHSI_AV_HUMAN_RETRIEVAL_CROSSWALK.md) make these distinctions retrievable for future listening, not heard. Direct runtime traversal could establish performance, staging and exact player experience, but it would not turn a line's ethical aspiration into a proven universal policy. The most useful future test is an unfamiliar counterpart who wants the facts, needs protection, and declines the requested labor: which part of Jinhsi's stated nonbarter rule survives when she cannot control the outcome?
