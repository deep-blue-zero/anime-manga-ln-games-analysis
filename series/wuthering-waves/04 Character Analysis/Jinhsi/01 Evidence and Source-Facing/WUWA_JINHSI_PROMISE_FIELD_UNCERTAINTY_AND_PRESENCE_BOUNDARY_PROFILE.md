# Jinhsi — promised disclosure, field uncertainty, and the question of presence

Three source actions sharpen the difference between a leader's reliable intention and information she does not yet possess. The Chapter 1 Black Shores conversation (`flow#/2515/3`) contains a prospective promise of disclosure. A later Mt. Firmament approach (`3157/2`) explicitly requires closer observation before the next decision. A memory-handbook/side action (`7456/2`) **reprises** the original first-meeting opening at `1191/3`, but changes its branch presentation. These source states, choices and audio proofs must not be collapsed into one continuously witnessed encounter [JIN-E39–E41; JIN-C38–C40].

## A promise to report what can be learned

Jinhsi first checks Rover's condition. Rover has a single prompt about seeing themself wearing a Black Bloom; her ensuing inference is that the Black Shores have a deep relation to Rover's past (`flow#/2515/3/0–1`; `Huanglong_main_1_5_82_1–3`). That is *her inference from Rover's report*, not an independent recovery of Rover's memory or a full account of the organization. ZH's `一定` and KO's categorical language sound confident; JA says there seems to be some connection. The shared robust proposition is that she takes the vision seriously as a lead. A later contradiction about the flower's origin would revise the inference, not the fact that Rover reported it [JIN-E39; JIN-C38].

She warns that Fractsidus leaders may be monitoring Rover and suggests Black Shores members could be allies (`_82_4–5`). ZH and EN mark the surveillance as possible/likely; JA and KO sound firmer. The sentence does not show a camera, a known pursuer at that instant, or the Black Shores' actual protective capacity. In the preceding source discussion she had already admitted incomplete knowledge of that organization; this line does not retroactively resolve its aims. The advice is an option under threat, not proof that a later alliance is inevitable or that her earlier information choreography was harmless [JIN-E11, E28, E39].

Her pledge is precisely prospective: she will seek Jué and tell Rover what *they learn* (`_82_6–7`), after which Rover can answer “agreed” in a single option and she closes the exchange (`_82_8–9`). The option is not a multiple-route consent test. “Everything we learned” in EN and the equivalent ZH/JA wording do not mean she presently knows everything, can promise Jué will be found by a particular date, or has already told Rover everything her office knows. There is a real ethical commitment to share new knowledge; whether and when it was fulfilled requires later primary evidence. It matters that this promise follows a prior selective disclosure and a separately managed welcome: credibility is neither erased by those tensions nor proven by the promise alone [JIN-C25, C38].

The raw `ShowTalk` action has seven named, accepted, source-voiced Jinhsi items, a single linear `TalkSequence` and two single Rover prompts. The quest-node state match is `139000031_142`, with a handbook pointer at `PlotHandBook#/5`; neither proves a particular player consumed the line. The seven semantic voice rows each join four *different* key-matching external-source WEM/PCM/FLAC objects (28 distinct PCM identities), technically decoded and roundtrip-validated. That is a sound retrieval proof at the object level, not human listening or proof the promise was kept.

She uses the frozen surface as a route, yet from a distance cannot see what is happening inside and says more information is needed before choosing the next step (`_290_6–8`). This is neither indecision nor omniscience. It is an operationally useful move under causal uncertainty, with a declared observation threshold for further action [JIN-E40; JIN-C39].

The action has seven talk items: one non-Jinhsi ellipsis, six accepted and voiced Jinhsi items, and the one Rover option after the plan. The quest-node keys `125000122_134` and `121800003_134` and a handbook pointer are state-match leads, not proof both routes were played. A future scene review could test whether the team actually crossed, what became visible, and which water mechanism a later source established; the quoted action alone cannot settle those outcomes.

## A hand offered after a projection question

It is therefore a *retelling/reuse*, not an independent second face-to-face meeting. Crucially, the memory-handbook `TalkSequence` places Rover's “Hello” and “another projection?” lines at consecutive TalkItems 3–4. In the original `1191/3` raw `SequenceTransitions[0]`, those correspond to distinct choices `Huanglong_main_1_5_79_4` and `_79_7`; the latter alone enters the additional projection-response sequence. A linear reading of the side recap would splice two mutually exclusive first-meeting replies into one alleged executed route. Use the original graph to model first-meeting choice, while retaining the side action as evidence of how the game later re-presents it [JIN-E25–E26, E41; JIN-C23, C40].

In both presentations Jinhsi says another image would be discourteous, comments that Huanglong's terminals cannot produce a projection this lifelike, and offers physical verification. Narration says she extends a hand in a handshake posture. The source supports a *depicted and verbally asserted* face-to-face meeting and an offered handshake on the original projection-question route. It does not say Rover took her hand or prove that every later Jinhsi-like image is embodied. The side action's combined reply sequence is not a second witness to a completed greeting [JIN-E41; JIN-C40].

The four written witnesses differ. EN explicitly adds that Rover is speaking to a “real human in the flesh”; ZH, JA and KO instead emphasize discourtesy and the apparent limits of Huanglong terminal projections. Nor does a later Tethys image disprove the first meeting's depiction: Huanglong-terminal capability and Tethys's simulation are not one asserted technical system. ## Reconstructive boundary

A faithful Jinhsi can commit to share what she learns, recommend a possibly helpful ally, exploit a usable path while withholding a causal verdict, and offer verification when a guest asks whether she is physically present. Each act is conditional on what she knows and where the source places it. The model fails if it says she already knew Rover's full past, had proved surveillance, guaranteed a route through the chamber, combined both opening replies in one original first meeting, completed an unseen handshake, or personally made a later Tethys image's invitations. It also fails if it discards her genuine stated commitment and operational competence merely because the outcomes are unobserved. Future evidence should be attached to the exact promise, field state, original-route replay or simulated-image state it actually tests, rather than averaged into a single “trustworthy/untrustworthy” trait.
