# Jinhsi — the temporal bargain is not a two-person duel

The Mt. Firmament confrontation is easy to reconstruct as a young human breaking with a controlling god. That loses the scene's actual decision geometry. Jué is injured and holding a temporal disaster back; its proposed city-wide suspension is costly and uncertain, not a harmless status quo. Jinhsi's proposed Second Awakening could restore normal time but might destroy her body or leave her trapped in repeated temporal harm. Rover is asked to make a timed intervention that may improve her survival. Xinyi and the people of Hongzhen also have independent knowledge and responsibilities. The [deep dive](WUWA_JINHSI_CHARACTER_DEEP_DIVE_PRE_AV.md) gives the continuous reading; this profile keeps the alternatives, speakers, text witnesses and graph states separate so a future character model cannot turn risk into prophecy fulfillment or love into a rescue guarantee [JIN-E12–E14, E29–E30].

At `flow#/3153/4/18–24`, the assertion that Jué selected Jinhsi in order to restore the temporal order is made by Xinyi as an inference from earlier court discussion, not by Jué in that turn. Jinhsi's answer at `/29–32` is striking because she does **not** ground her choice only in appointment: she names the younger child who delighted in mountain snow and the possibility of that seed taking root. This is not evidence of a second Jinhsi body. It is a first-person answer to a community witness who had asked for candor. Xinyi's acceptance also means the scene cannot be reduced to a solitary child challenging a god; another person bears the safety of Hongzhen, even though the risk to Jinhsi remains her own [JIN-E12, E29].

## Two bad futures are different kinds of uncertainty

Jué describes a temporal cascade already under way: its old injury and the defense of Mt. Firmament have left time unstable; the recent Fractsidus injury removes the chamber's ability to keep it alive. Its forecast of uncontrolled time is hideous—people caught in repeated life, death, celebration and battle. The proposed countermeasure is not to *fix* time. Jué would spend its remaining life to stop Jinzhou's time, preserving a still city for an unknown future civilization to rescue (`flow#/3160/5/10–27`). It calls the plan imperfect yet, in its deduction, the most reliable available (`/29,34–37`). A model cannot tell Jinhsi that doing nothing is benign; nor can it claim the suspension itself guarantees that anyone wakes. The narrative presents a comparison between threatened immediate temporal catastrophe and suspended life with a speculative future rescuer.

Jinhsi's objection is political and temporal, not simply emotional refusal to obey: a city left still for decades or centuries might be forgotten, and later people might lack resources or will to recover it (`flow#/3160/5/30–33`). She asks about a prophesied confrontation and infers that a clash of resonant powers might trigger her Second Awakening, allowing her to correct the temporal flow (`/38–42`). Jué rebuts with a concrete mechanism of bodily danger: Overclocking could leave an empty husk, or a temporal fissure could repeatedly tear and re-form body and mind (`/43–46`). Those are Jué's warnings in a dialogue, not guaranteed outcomes of every route. Jinhsi answers by comparing her proposed action with Jué's own long defiance of fate for Jinzhou (`/47`). She does not deny the warning. Their conflict is over which uncertainty to impose on whom and when, inside a relationship of rescue, shared power and protective disagreement [JIN-C07/C09/C11/C27].

The follow-on `flow#/3161/3/0–17` is often paraphrased as a death speech. Its Chinese is more specific. She says a simple death is *not* what she wants, even amid possible dispersal or endless harm (`/10–12`), asks Jué to gamble on her (`/5–6`), and says she can safeguard Jué while winning a future the forecast did not contain (`/12`). `flow#/3223/3/20–25` explicitly says she is not planning to die and seeks even a slim chance of survival. Her willingness to lose life if necessary and her opposition to dying without trying belong in the same model state. Calling her either a risk-free miracle worker or a death-seeking saint would erase half the source.

## The third person's intervention has timing, permission and localization limits

Jué turns to Rover at `flow#/3160/5/50–54`. The Chinese says Rover's intervention may increase Jinhsi's chance by `多上一成` and asks Rover to *permit* Jué to hand over temporal power at the moment Jinhsi is exhausted and near lethal Overclocking. This is an increment stated in tenths, **not** an absolute “Jinhsi has a 10% survival rate,” and the source gives no baseline. EN generalizes the gain to “a fraction” and says “Command me”; KO preserves a one-tenth expression but its following line sounds more certain about survival than the ZH conditional. The JA witness across keys `_380_58–_62` compresses/resegments the sequence and does not retain the numerical increment at key `_380_59`; it still warns of an unforgiving missed moment. The safe cross-language proposition is conditional, time-sensitive help, not one universal probability or command relationship. A later witnessed battle performance might clarify tone, not manufacture the absent baseline [JIN-E30].

The later gate scene `flow#/3163/2` makes Rover's involvement more concrete. Jué says only Rover can open the domain gate; Rover outlines best and worst cases and the alternative if they remain inactive. The raw `TalkSequence` first presents common items 1–9, then an option from Rover to open when ready or a hesitant acknowledgment that opening has no return. Those lead to distinct Jinhsi responses at zero-based TalkItems `/9` and `/10`, and both `JumpTalk` to the common sequence beginning at `/11`. Jinhsi then speaks of lost future sunshine/snow, the city's intergenerational labor and her own identity as person, citizen and Magistrate (`/11–16`). Her statement of preparation is not evidence that the two route-specific lines were both heard in one playthrough. The scene talks about *not opening* as a counterfactual, but its retained option set at this node does not provide a categorical “refuse and leave” route. That absence should not be converted into a universal claim that Rover has no agency elsewhere [JIN-E30/C28].

## Reconstruction consequences and revision gate

A hypothetical scene can have Jinhsi disagree respectfully with Jué, explain why an indefinitely suspended city is unacceptable to her, ask Rover for help and still let each person's risk matter. It should give Xinyi and local people tasks rather than using them as a chorus, and it should not let Rover's potential intervention retroactively turn Jinhsi's decision into Rover's creation. Jinhsi is grateful to Jué and Xinyi, but the source lets her argue, choose and pursue survival. The temporal bargain tests her larger civic theory: human self-rule cannot mean refusing all aid from a Sentinel or ally; it means that such aid should not erase the humans who bear the consequences [JIN-C09/C11/C12/C27–C28].

This is a pinned text-and-graph reading, not a witnessed battle route, an empirical probability estimate, a human four-dub listening report or proof of a permanent postbattle power scale. Direct runtime traversal of both retained gate options and the battle, source-confirmed outcome boundaries, and line-timestamped four-language listening would refine branch realization and performed tone. A newer source showing a different plan or explicit numerical baseline would require a claim revision, not silent retrojection into `3160/5`.
