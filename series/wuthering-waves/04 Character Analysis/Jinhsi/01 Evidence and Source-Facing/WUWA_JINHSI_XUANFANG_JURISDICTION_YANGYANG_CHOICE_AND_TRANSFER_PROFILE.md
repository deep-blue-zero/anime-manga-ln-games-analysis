# Jinhsi — Xuanfang jurisdiction, Yangyang's choice, and a proposed transfer

Two later source actions test whether Jinhsi's preference for human agency survives a case involving people she cares about and an adjacent government's authority. `flow#/17181/2` records an emergency before the rescue; `flow#/17636/6` records a later conversation after the immediate case is said to be closed. Each `ShowTalk` has one retained `TalkSequence`, not the three mutually exclusive Rover answer routes found in the earlier Jinzhou defense. This is a text-and-source-graph reading, not a witnessed runtime traversal or a human-heard performance [JIN-E17, E34–E35; JIN-C12, C32–C33].

## Inquiry is not unrestricted intervention

Jinhsi reports disappearances leading toward Xuanfang, Chixia's grievous return, and Yangyang's lost contact (`17181/2/19`, `Main_HuangLong_SYYLFML_110_28`). She says she immediately sent messengers toward Mengzhou and *tried* to send elite Midnight Rangers to scout the hold again, but its gate was shut (`/20`, `_29`). The wording does not prove that scouts entered or learned what was inside. It also prevents an over-simple maxim that she never sends anyone in that direction.

Her next distinction is institutional. Xuanfang belongs to Mengzhou, and its hold is a military stronghold; as a neighboring state, Jinzhou is ordinarily not free to interfere in internal affairs (`/31`, `_31`). All four text witnesses retain the jurisdictional limit. She then says she cannot dispatch personnel but could go in her *personal* capacity as Jué's Resonator (`/32`, `_67`). Read together with the earlier scout attempt, the latter is a limit on deploying personnel into a neighboring jurisdiction as an intervention, not a claim that no inquiry or message could ever be sent. This is Jinhsi's account of a particular emergency, not an independently codified constitutional rule [JIN-C32].

An older agreement between Sentinels lets their Resonators retrieve an emergency token; she says she has already obtained a “spare key” (`/23–28`, `_34–40`). She even says the emergency determination is her own. That discretion complicates a flattering portrait of pure procedural restraint: she can interpret the exception, and she initially proposes that she and Rover go (`/30`, `_42`). But personal entitlement to enter is not the same as an assertion that she can command Jinzhou forces across the boundary. After Rover and Suisui argue that Jinzhou needs its Magistrate at home, she yields and promises as Magistrate to bear responsibility for their actions in Xuanfang (`/36–40`, `_48–53`). She asks them to bring Yangyang and Jinzhou's people home (`/40`, `_66`). The text shows a commitment and a change of plan, not Jinhsi's actual entry, a proved legal indemnity, or her ability to guarantee rescue. The earlier unnamed visitor's turns in this mixed action are not hers [JIN-E17, E19, E34].

## Yangyang initiates the later exception

In the later `17636/6` conversation, Jinhsi asks Yangyang what *she* wishes to do (`/6`, `Main_HuangLong_XLZHDLY_480_39`). Yangyang says a Midnight Ranger ordinarily should return to Jinzhou when the case is closed, but asks permission to remain temporarily with Rover because Xuanfang is unstable and people there still need help (`/7–10`, `_40–44`). Jinhsi first corrects Yangyang's self-deprecation and recognizes her connection to the land (`/11–12`, `_45–46`), then says she respects her decision and asks local Commander Qiuhong for a view (`/13`, `_47`). Qiuhong welcomes the help and asks Yangyang to serve temporarily as a Xuan Watcher; Yangyang accepts in conditional terms of helping people (`/14–16`, `_48–50`). These are separately voiced participants, not a single decree staged as consent [JIN-E35; JIN-C33].

Jinhsi then says she *will consult* General Jiyan and arrange a temporary transfer into Xuanfang's Ministry of War (`/17`, `_51`). ZH uses `之后会同…商议`, EN “will speak … and arrange,” JA `相談して`, and KO `상의하여`: the prospective consultation is common to the four texts. EN's introductory “Then it is settled” expresses assent to the plan, but the source does not show the personnel order already issued or the transfer completed. Her request that Rover look after Yangyang (`/18`, `_52`) and her promise to wait in Jinzhou for their return (`/20`, `_56`) show personal concern without cancelling Yangyang's volunteered aim or Qiuhong's local authority [JIN-C33].

## What the pair changes in the model

The two actions should not be collapsed into “Jinhsi could not send anyone, then sent Yangyang anyway.” Before rescue, she distinguishes attempted inquiry from entering another state's military affairs and ultimately stays to govern Jinzhou. After the case, Yangyang asks to stay, the local commander asks for her service, and Jinhsi describes a future consultation and formal temporary transfer. Those changed conditions can explain the different response; they do **not** establish the unshown administrative outcome. A later record of an executed transfer, its authority and end date could strengthen that conclusion. Evidence that Yangyang was ordered to remain against her stated wish, or that the local commander objected, would defeat this consensual reading. A runtime route could test delivery and staging but cannot make the recorded future-tense consultation a completed past act.

For a source-grounded fictional scene, specify whether it occurs before rescue, at the later request, or after a separately evidenced transfer. Jinhsi may seek a narrow emergency path, accept counsel to stay at her post, ask Yangyang's intention, and negotiate with the competent commanders. She should not be written as either a borderless commander whose kindness overrules everyone, or a passive official whose jurisdiction prevents even asking questions or arranging help. The tension is that she retains discretionary authority while trying to make other people's choices and institutions matter [JIN-C12, C32–C33].
