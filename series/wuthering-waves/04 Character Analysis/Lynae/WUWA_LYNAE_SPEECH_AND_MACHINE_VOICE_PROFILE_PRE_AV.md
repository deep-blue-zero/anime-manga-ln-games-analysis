# Lynae speech and machine voice profile — pre-AV

## Purpose

This artifact keeps three evidentiary levels separate:

1. **textual speech behavior** — what Lynae says and how the written witnesses structure interaction;
2. **machine acoustic observations** — reproducible signal measurements;
3. **human performance interpretation** — not performed here.

The distinction is mandatory. Duration, RMS level, peak level, or silence fraction cannot tell us that a line is affectionate, guarded, flirtatious, irritated, vulnerable, or sincere.

# 2. Textual witness boundary

Chinese is the semantic anchor.

Japanese, Korean, and English are official localizations. They can reveal production choices and register differences but should not silently override the Chinese source.

A later four-language human listening pass may produce performance-specific observations. Those should be labeled by language and should not become language-independent personality facts without cross-witness support.

# 3. Baseline textual speech architecture

## 3.1 Casual, low-formality peer register

Lynae's dominant peer-facing register is informal.

Evidence includes:

- preference for “just Lynae” rather than title-like address;
- frequent short invitations;
- teasing questions;
- contractions/colloquial particles in localization;
- direct “let's go” sequencing;
- low ceremonial distance with classmates.

English makes this especially visible through forms such as:

- “c'mon”;
- “gimme”;
- “whaddya”;
- “gotta”;
- “ya”;
- “hell”;
- elongated “Whaaat?”;
- playful mock-quotation.

Those exact lexical choices are EN localization evidence, not a universal source-language personality layer.

The Chinese primary witness is also colloquial and direct, so the higher-level low-formality claim is stable.

## 3.2 Anti-formality is not anti-respect

During the early campus introduction, Lynae jokes that Rover could call her “Miss Lynae,” then immediately backs off because she is not used to the formality.

With Mornye, she jokes and pushes for play while still treating the professor as competent and, in some contexts, as someone whose class she should not skip.

The speech model should therefore separate:

- **formal register preference** — low;
- **capacity to respect authority/person** — clearly present.

# 4. Invitation grammar

One of Lynae's most stable speech behaviors is converting social contact into a proposal.

Common structures are functionally equivalent to:

- want to go?
- let's eat;
- hop on;
- let's play;
- come check this;
- wanna see?
- let's visit;
- let's take a picture;
- when you're free, we'll do it;
- next time.

This matters because invitation is not merely friendliness. It is one of her default mechanisms for:

- care;
- affect regulation;
- intimacy;
- novelty;
- relationship continuation.

A future-scenario model should therefore prefer **concrete proposals** over generic emotional speeches unless the situation has already become explicitly confessional.

# 6. Humor and teasing

## 6.1 Functions

Lynae uses humor for at least four different purposes:

1. **play** — jokes with friends, games, class, food;
2. **anti-formality** — puncturing titles and bureaucratic stiffness;
3. **embarrassment management** — dancing posts, slogans, attention;
4. **deflection under strain** — forced brightness or quick subject shifts after difficult material.

The fourth function is real and should not absorb the first three.

## 6.2 Trauma-related example

“Lurking Shadows” shows that she can force a smile/joke after a stage scenario activates mercenary memories.

This supports **some defensive humor**.

It does not justify a trait statement such as “her humor is always a mask.”

# 7. Professional / operational register

When threat, systems, or field operations dominate, Lynae's speech becomes more compressed and technical.

Examples include:

- identifying abnormal frequency spikes;
- reasoning from surveillance systems;
- explaining Hyvatia/Exoswarm connections;
- decrypting and hardening device access;
- discussing security reset windows;
- designing fallback plans;
- tracking dangerous mechs;
- reporting crisis-zone progress.

The important reconstruction point is not exact jargon. It is that an efficient operational register remains available alongside the student register.

This is a **situational register**, not a separate personality.

---

# 8. Transactional framing under emotional exposure

The 3.0 confession is one of the best speech-state transition examples.

At row `10117`, Lynae first offers information under a condition: let her remain a student.

She then defines cooperation through “shared interests.”

After Rover challenges that framing, she pauses, jokes briefly about them sounding like a teacher, and rephrases:

- she likes the school;
- people there treat her as a friend;
- they are worth protecting.

### Inference

When an emotionally vulnerable motive is exposed, Lynae may initially choose a defensible operational explanation before giving the more personal one.

### Limit

This is one high-value scene. Do not turn it into a universal law for every confession.

---

# 9. Directness and concealment coexist

Lynae values directness in others.

After Rover asks about the suspicious device rather than letting the matter slide, she says:

> “Good... you're still you.”

Yet she herself can be indirect when stakes threaten identity or belonging.

This is not a contradiction that needs to be “solved.” It is a relationship asymmetry:

- direct truth from a trusted person can feel stabilizing;
- disclosure by Lynae can carry much higher material cost.

After re-entry, that asymmetry narrows but does not disappear.

---

# 10. Gratitude

Lynae's serious gratitude is more direct than her general casual style might suggest.

Examples:

- thanks Rover for speaking up;
- thanks them for trusting her;
- says “time after time” rather than reducing the support to one incident;
- credits Mornye's official email;
- recognizes seniors who helped her adapt;
- gives back through volunteering and practical support.

This supports a person who is capable of explicit appreciation, not merely indirect acts of service.

---

# 11. Concern routed through future continuity

During later crises, Lynae often expresses worry by insisting on another meeting.

Examples:

- “Catch you on the other side.”
- “You wouldn't bail on [our rides], would you?”
- “Don't let this be our last meeting.”
- “Better make it back in one piece.”

The rhetorical strategy is important.

Rather than dwelling only on fear, she constructs a concrete future event and places the endangered person inside it.

### Inference

Future plans can function as reassurance and relational anchoring.

### Limit

Do not read every “next time” as fear.

---

# 12. Seriousness followed by ordinary life

A recurrent textual rhythm is:

1. serious disclosure / crisis / difficult self-reflection;
2. immediate movement toward food, riding, games, or another concrete activity.

This should not be interpreted automatically as avoidance.

In mature states, reinserting the ordinary into serious life may itself be part of the character's value system: survival is not the endpoint; living is.

The AV/human-performance pass may help distinguish abrupt defensive deflection from warm re-grounding in specific scenes.

## 15.3 What these differences do not mean

It would be invalid to conclude from the table alone that:

- Japanese Lynae is more hesitant;
- English Lynae is louder/more assertive as acting;
- Chinese Lynae is more restrained;
- Korean Lynae is faster/slower emotionally.

Possible confounds include:

- translation length;
- phonotactics;
- acting direction;
- mastering;
- variant rows;
- pauses;
- silence segmentation behavior;
- line-local context.

The correct use is retrieval and matched comparison.

# 16. Why `machine_voice_profiled` is not claimed

The governing WUWA machine voice protocol calls for a fuller reproducible layer where feasible, including:

- robust F0/pitch range;
- speaking rate or voiced-rate proxies;
- pause counts/density;
- intensity dynamics;
- spectral/voice-quality proxies where reliable;
- within-language normalization;
- state/source partitions;
- clustering/outlier inspection.

The current Lynae Drive acoustic artifact is excellent for transport verification and first-stage retrieval but does not contain all of those.

Therefore:

- `machine_acoustic_analysis_status`: strong partial;
- `machine_voice_profiled`: **OPEN / not completed**.

This prevents the word “COMPLETE” in filenames from being mistaken for interpretive completeness.

# 18. Human-performance addendum boundary

If the owner later supplies subjective listening observations, they should be recorded as one of:

1. low-inference audible observation;
2. interpretive performance judgment;
3. character-level inference.

Example:

- **observation:** longer pause before the rephrasing;
- **performance judgment:** EN delivery reads more guarded;
- **character inference:** Lynae operationalizes emotion before disclosing it.

Only the third changes character interpretation, and only after comparison with text, context, other lines, and rival explanations.

No such judgments are made in this packet.

# 19. Speech-model summary for reconstruction

## High-confidence textual priors

Lynae tends to:

- speak casually with peers;
- resist unnecessary formality;
- initiate;
- ask questions;
- turn care into concrete proposals;
- tease;
- use humor flexibly;
- become efficient/technical under operational pressure;
- show direct gratitude after trust is established;
- use future plans to anchor dangerous separations;
- accept another person's “not now” more readily in mature ordinary contexts.

## State-sensitive priors

Under identity threat in L1/L2, expect more:

- defensive answers;
- bargaining;
- tactical omission;
- transactional framing.

In L3, expect more:

- self-disclosure;
- explicit relational language;
- willingness to name home/future;
- but not zero secrecy.

## Mandatory abstentions

Without human listening, do not specify:

- habitual pitch;
- warmth;
- breathiness as acting choice;
- flirtation in delivery;
- vocal age presentation;
- emotional restraint;
- dub-specific “maiden/knight” style analogies;
- comparative acting quality.

Those are future questions, not current findings.
