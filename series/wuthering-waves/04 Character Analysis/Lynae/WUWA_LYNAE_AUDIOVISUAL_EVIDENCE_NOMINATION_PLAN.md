# Lynae audiovisual evidence hierarchy and nomination plan

## Purpose

This document performs the planning portion of Phase 8 of the WUWA character reconstruction protocol. It does not claim that Lynae has been audiovisually analyzed. Its job is to determine which audiovisual sources should be collected, what analytical responsibility each source class can carry, and which pre-AV claims should be tested rather than merely illustrated.

The present Lynae packet is already textually reconstructive and has a partial machine-acoustic baseline. Direct visual evidence is the largest remaining mandatory modality before an integrated reconstruction can be hardened. The later AV pass should therefore function as an adversarial second reading of the current model.

The governing rule is simple:

> Collect audiovisual evidence because it can answer a defined analytical question, not because it contains Lynae.

## 1. Authority dimensions

Audiovisual sources should not be ranked on a single ladder. Each item should receive separate judgments for three forms of authority.

### 1.1 Diegetic authority

How strongly does the artifact establish that the depicted action, expression, spatial relation, or event occurs in the story world?

High-diegetic material includes canonical story cutscenes and scripted in-engine narrative sequences. Promotional montage, splash art, and stylized PV imagery can have low or ambiguous diegetic authority even when officially produced.

### 1.2 Representational authority

How strongly does the artifact establish official choices about Lynae's appearance, movement, costume, combat embodiment, visual motifs, or audience-facing presentation?

Official key art and character showcases can have very high representational authority even when they do not depict literal story events.

### 1.3 Interpretive authority

How strongly can this particular item support a claim about personality, behavior, relationship state, or development?

A long contextual cutscene can have high interpretive authority. A promotional pose can strongly support costume or iconographic analysis while having little authority over habitual behavior.

These axes must remain separate. Official production does not make every depiction equally biographical.

## 2. Source hierarchy by analytical responsibility

| Class | Typical examples | Diegetic authority | Representational authority | Primary analytical responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | canonical story cutscenes | very high | high | facial action, gesture, blocking, gaze, interpersonal distance, scene staging, performed reactions |
| B | scripted in-engine story/dialogue sequences | high | high | habitual posture, movement through space, physical initiative, practical action, low-stakes interaction |
| C | official character gameplay/showcase footage | variable | very high | combat embodiment, movement vocabulary, kinetic identity, official character presentation |
| D | official trailers, PVs, teaser films | variable/ambiguous | very high | symbolic framing, audience-facing characterization, recurring visual motifs; story claims only when independently anchored |
| E | official splash art, key art, profile art | low/none for events | very high | design, costume, props, color language, iconography, representational identity |
| F | official promotional illustrations/social graphics | usually low | high to moderate | promotional framing and visual motif comparison; weak evidence for ordinary conduct |
| G | edited marketing composites/reused footage | low/uncertain | moderate | reception/presentation evidence only unless exact source footage is recoverable |

Lower classes are not worthless. They simply answer narrower questions.

## 4. Pre-AV claims that must be attacked

The later AV pass should explicitly seek evidence against the following strong pre-AV propositions.

### AV-H01 - "life capable of carrying weight"

Current textual model: Lynae develops from a survival discipline based on disposability into a life that can accumulate possessions, trust, obligations, friends, and future plans.

Falsification/qualification targets:

- dorm staging that treats possessions as superficial decoration rather than emotionally meaningful accumulation;
- later scenes that repeatedly frame all attachments as expendable despite textual claims;
- visual behavior showing unchanged escape-readiness even after institutional security is established.

### AV-H02 - presentism changes causal meaning

Current model: early "live today" behavior is partly defensive because she assumes belonging may end; later immediacy survives but is no longer premised on inevitable departure.

Test with early and later matched scenes. Look for changes in watchfulness, spatial orientation, exit-monitoring, stillness, and willingness to remain after the immediate activity ends. These are hypotheses, not facts until observed.

### AV-H03 - rule-breaking is selective rather than anarchic

Current model: Lynae bends rules readily when she judges them obstructive or protective action necessary, but she can accept consequences, paperwork, safety constraints, and legitimate repair obligations.

Collect both transgressive and accountable scenes. If AV consistently stylizes all rule-breaking as carefree rebellion, record the representational divergence without allowing marketing framing to erase textual accountability.

### AV-H04 - usefulness is a residual vulnerability

Current model: overvolunteering and discomfort without tasks may preserve a learned association between usefulness and security/self-worth.

This is only a medium-confidence causal inference. AV should not be used to pathologize generic energetic animation. Support would require recurrent visible difficulty resting across multiple contexts, especially when no external urgency exists.

### AV-H05 - Rover trust permits quiet, not only stimulation

Current model: the Rover relationship is not reducible to banter, rides, danger, or activity. Later source material explicitly permits quiet co-presence.

The AV test should compare active scenes with the quiet `15686`-cluster scene and any other low-action Rover sequences. Physical closeness must not be converted automatically into romance.

### AV-H06 - color and light are both sensory preference and identity language

Current textual evidence strongly supports rare/complex color preference, paint customization, aversion to gray/bland associations, optical Resonance, and a self-description of life becoming colorful.

AV can test whether production design systematically reinforces this language. It must remain open to the possibility that colorful marketing is simply the game's general art direction rather than Lynae-specific symbolism.

### AV-H07 - high/open spaces change meaning across the arc

Textual evidence says Lynae once used elevated concealed places for tactical control and later experiences one such place as alive and real.

Direct visual comparison should determine whether camera, blocking, openness, skyline, light, or companion presence actually support that transformation.

### AV-H08 - identity integration is not domestication

Current model: later Lynae accepts social accountability and durable belonging without becoming conventionally obedient or losing speed, mischief, customization, tactical secrecy, or appetite for risk.

AV should look for continuity of kinetic and visual character across pre- and post-disclosure states rather than assuming a costume or school status equals personality replacement.

## 5. Observation protocol for collected media

For each selected clip or image, record low-inference observations before comparing them with the textual model.

### 5.1 Clip metadata

Record:

- official source URL or canonical evidence-file identity;
- release/version/date where known;
- quest/event/flow-state relationship;
- exact time range;
- language/audio track;
- whether the footage is direct capture, official upload, trailer edit, showcase, or still image;
- whether the sequence is diegetic, representational, promotional, or mixed;
- any editing, cropping, or recompression performed for analytical convenience.

### 5.2 Low-inference visual observation

Describe before interpreting:

- body orientation;
- interpersonal distance;
- locomotion and changes in position;
- hands/arms and object interaction;
- head/gaze direction where visible;
- facial changes that can be described without naming an emotion;
- pauses and stillness;
- entrance/exit behavior;
- camera distance and angle;
- composition and spatial hierarchy;
- lighting/color changes;
- editing rhythm;
- synchronized sound/music only when actually reviewed.

### 5.3 Interpretive comparison

Only after low-inference notes are recorded, compare the witness with the relevant pre-AV claim and assign one transition:

- `PRESERVE`
- `STRENGTHEN`
- `QUALIFY`
- `SCOPE_LIMIT`
- `REVISE`
- `REJECT`
- `OPEN`

Do not force every clip to change a claim. "No material new information" is a valid outcome.

## 6. Collection format

Preferred acquisition for story material:

1. retain the complete bounded scene or sequence whenever practical;
2. preserve original timing and the surrounding reaction context;
3. keep an unmodified master witness in the evidence plane;
4. derive shorter analytical clips only with reversible source/timecode routing;
5. derive screenshots/contact sheets from the retained video rather than using isolated social-media screenshots as the only witness;
6. retain the relevant audio track when gesture and voice timing interact;
7. avoid cropping away interlocutors when blocking or relationship distance is analytically relevant.

For official promotional images, preserve the highest-quality official source practical and record publication context/date. Fan edits, reposts, wiki crops, and thumbnails should be locators of last resort, not preferred evidence.

## 7. Bias control

The existing textual reconstruction creates useful hypotheses but also creates anchoring risk. The AV reviewer should therefore:

- annotate visible action before reading the relevant deep-dive section when practical;
- seek scenes expected to contradict the model, not only iconic confirming scenes;
- compare early and late matched contexts;
- distinguish production-wide visual conventions from Lynae-specific patterns;
- distinguish character animation from camera/editor framing;
- treat promotional sexuality, heroization, coolness, or vulnerability as audience-facing presentation unless narrative context independently supports habitual behavior;
- preserve mixed results rather than averaging them into one personality adjective.

## 9. Output expected from the later AV pass

The completed AV operation should not merely append a gallery. It should produce:

1. a source-routed AV observation ledger or equivalent evidence artifact;
2. claim-by-claim revision outcomes against the current evidence/falsification matrix;
3. explicit notes on visual motifs that are production-wide versus character-specific;
4. revisions to relationship/state and ordinary-life profiles where embodied evidence matters;
5. revisions to the reconstructive profile where AV exposes reliable behavioral constraints;
6. only then, an integrated Lynae monograph/reconstructive artifact eligible for `audiovisually_hardened` and, after fidelity testing, `integrated_reconstruction_completed`.

The optional human listening/performance addendum remains separate and may occur after that integrated reconstruction.
