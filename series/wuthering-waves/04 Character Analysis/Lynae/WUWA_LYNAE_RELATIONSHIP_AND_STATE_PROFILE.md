# Lynae relationship and state profile

## Purpose

This artifact isolates two things that should not be repeatedly reconstructed from the main essay:

1. Lynae's **developmental states**;
2. the way her behavior changes by **relationship and recipient**.

It is deliberately pre-AV. Facial expression, blocking, gaze, distance, and embodied intimacy remain open until direct audiovisual review.

---

# 1. Developmental state model

## State L0 — pre-Startorch mercenary

**Type:** developmental state  
**Temporal status:** retrospective, pre-playable-school identity  
**Primary evidence:** `FavorStory_150904_Content`, row 10117 confession, row 10722 dormitory reflection, archive thoughts/chat

### Environment

- New Federation, including Lawless Zone upbringing;
- frequent violence;
- off-record government-contractor dirty work;
- mobility without fixed home;
- contract/cash logic;
- low permission to question mission justification;
- skills valued instrumentally.

### Self-organization

The available evidence supports a person who has preferences and curiosity but does not organize them into a durable civilian biography.

Key constraints:

- do not settle;
- do not accumulate non-portable attachments;
- do not leave identifying traces;
- expect usefulness to determine role;
- expect departure.

### Behavioral strengths

- rapid situational assessment;
- weapons/security competence;
- field survival;
- technical adaptation;
- concealment;
- mobility;
- willingness to act under danger.

### Vulnerabilities

- attachment is structurally unsafe;
- non-instrumental self-worth is poorly scaffolded;
- future planning is thin;
- questioning authority can be excluded by role;
- violence and mission cues can retain involuntary affective force.

### Prediction rule

Do not back-project later school warmth as though she had a stable ordinary support network here.

Do not back-project later accountability as though she had institutional conditions that made disclosure safe.

## State L1 — stolen-name provisional student

**Type:** developmental state  
**Temporal status:** early Startorch through pre-exposure equilibrium  
**Primary evidence:** first-meeting material, 3.0 campus introduction, `FavorWord_150903`, early favor stories

### Defining condition

The name “Lynae” grants access to school life, but her right to remain depends on concealment.

### Core motive

Experience as much of the desired life as possible before the cost arrives.

### Temporal orientation

Strong present-focus.

This state is where “live in the moment” is most likely to contain a defensive component. `FavorWord_150903_Content` explicitly says she makes choices as though it may be her final day at Startorch.

### Social presentation

- bright;
- casual;
- highly invitational;
- jokes about class/rules;
- helps newcomers;
- rapidly forms peer relations;
- does not generally advertise the cost of the disguise.

### Concealment profile

High.

Identity-level truth is dangerous. When probing approaches family/origin questions, she can become guarded and professionally evasive.

### Relationship need

She wants the social life the identity gives her while still treating exposure as likely to end it.

### Prediction rule

Under identity threat, this state should be more likely than later states to:

- bargain;
- lie;
- provide half-truths;
- separate others from her problem;
- plan flight.

## State L2 — exposure / confession / attempted flight

**Type:** transition state  
**Temporal status:** 3.0 main-crisis threshold  
**Primary evidence:** rows 10720, 10117, 10722, 10825–10832

### Defining condition

The concealed past becomes inseparable from current danger.

### Key transition

Operational justification gives way to relational admission.

At row 10117 she first proposes cooperation through “shared interests,” then rephrases after challenge:

> the school contains people who treat her as a friend and are worth protecting.

### Contradiction

Even after this admission, when expulsion/repatriation appears inevitable, she plans to run.

This is not regression in the sense of “the earlier growth was fake.” It is evidence that attachment has developed faster than confidence that attachment can survive disclosure.

### Operational mode

Old mercenary competence returns strongly:

- device analysis;
- hacking;
- tracking;
- contingency design;
- risk calculation;
- counter-contact planning.

### Social mode

Rover becomes a unique trusted witness/advocate, but Lynae still tries to control exposure.

### Prediction rule

This state combines high relational stakes with high concealment reflex. Do not treat later “no point hiding anymore” as already fully available.

## State L3 — legitimate Startorch Lynae

**Type:** developmental state  
**Temporal status:** post-crisis re-admission through later campus life  
**Primary evidence:** row 10723, row 12047, ecology row 14805, 3.2 companion story and ordinary-life material

### Defining condition

Lynae is allowed to remain after investigation and re-enters through her own entrance examination/admission letter.

### Identity organization

The name remains Lynae, but its institutional basis is no longer the original person's letter.

Key markers:

- “My own letter.”
- “No more ghosts on my heels.”
- “I met everyone… as ‘Lynae.’”
- future now belongs to her;
- later self-introduction: “Just Lynae.”

### Temporal orientation

Present enjoyment remains strong, but the future becomes available.

The problem shifts from “will I be allowed to have one?” to “which of the many possible futures do I want?”

### Accountability

Higher.

She:

- discloses her past in counseling/investigation;
- accepts discipline;
- can recognize her old concealment pattern in another student;
- pushes that student toward full disclosure and repair.

### Rule profile

Still pragmatic.

Legitimate Lynae continues to skip, speed, evade, and bend restrictions, but can accept rules linked to concrete safety/repair.

### Rest/social safety

Trusted quiet becomes tolerable.

### Prediction rule

Use this as the default state for ordinary post-arc reconstruction unless a later source clearly indicates an additional state transition.

---

## State L3b — future-facing legitimate Lynae

**Type:** developmental extension, not a separate personality  
**Temporal status:** later 3.3–3.5 material  
**Primary evidence:** crisis volunteering, `Main_HuangLong_SYYLFML_91_*`

### Why this is not L4

The source does not demonstrate a new durable personality structure. The same core values persist.

What changes is the time horizon.

Rover's graduation and Lynae's own imagined graduation force her to recognize that Startorch itself is temporary. The next identity question becomes:

> What kind of person will future “Lynae” become?

### New pressure

The school that made durable identity possible cannot be the final answer forever.

### Prediction rule

Career/post-graduation scenarios require more abstention than ordinary campus scenarios. The source intentionally leaves the answer open.

# 2. Operational contexts that should not be mistaken for developmental states

## Mercenary-competence context

Triggered by:

- danger;
- pursuit;
- hacking/security systems;
- tracking;
- weapons;
- field risk;
- covert adversaries.

Expected changes:

- more compressed task speech;
- rapid planning;
- higher willingness to use deception;
- greater risk tolerance;
- reliance on technical competence.

This is a context mode available in L1–L3, not proof that “the real mercenary self” replaces the student.

## School-play context

Triggered by:

- peers;
- games;
- markets;
- food;
- bikes;
- festivals;
- ordinary classes;
- clubs.

Expected changes:

- invitation;
- teasing;
- experimentation;
- relaxed informal speech;
- mild rule-bending;
- novelty seeking.

## Protection/crisis context

Triggered by:

- classmates in danger;
- Startorch threatened;
- Rover entering high-risk operations;
- friend overburdened.

Expected changes:

- practical help;
- urgency;
- stronger commitment language;
- possible procedural shortcuts;
- difficulty accepting helpless observer status.

## High-shame identity context

Triggered by:

- original identity;
- false admission;
- return to New Federation;
- praise under incomplete truth;
- being judged as fraudulent.

Expected changes depend heavily on state:

- L1: conceal/deflect;
- L2: bargain/confess/possibly flee;
- L3: more direct accountability, but tactical secrecy remains possible.

# 3. Rover

## 3.1 Relationship state R0 — possible threat / accidental encounter

The first meeting begins with danger.

The retrospective favor story says Lynae initially worries Rover may have seen too much and reflexively tenses toward the trigger. The acceptance-letter context and realization that Rover is a student shift her out of that mode.

The relationship therefore begins at the boundary between mercenary threat assessment and the school identity she wants to inhabit.

## 3.2 R1 — classmate / debt / guided newcomer

Lynae hits Rover with the bike, checks injury, escorts them, explains the Academy, helps enrollment, and gives the campus tour.

Care is concrete before it is intimate.

This is a durable pattern.

## 3.3 R2 — secret-holder and direct questioner

Rover becomes someone who sees discrepancies.

Lynae values that they remain direct rather than politely accepting a cover story. `Main_LahaiRoi_3_1_38_32`—“Good... you're still you”—shows that consistency itself is reassuring.

## 3.4 R3 — advocate and trusted witness

Rover speaks for Lynae during scrutiny.

Lynae thanks them specifically for trust and says trust outside contracts is rare.

The relationship becomes a place where she can test whether disclosure necessarily destroys belonging.

## 3.5 R4 — ordinary intimate friend / recurring future

Post-crisis evidence includes:

- snacks;
- rides;
- films;
- music;
- games;
- shopping;
- shared photos;
- birthday plans;
- “I want to see you” messages;
- quiet sitting;
- de-stress outings;
- future ride promises;
- willingness to help in the New Federation.

The relation is high-trust and intimacy-coded.

### Romance boundary

Do not convert this automatically into a definitive canonical romance.

Some surfaces are affinity/choice-sensitive; friendship explains much of the observable behavior; no current source requires a formal category.

The safe model is:

> **Rover is Lynae's most structurally important individual witness in the current corpus and one of her closest chosen relationships.**

## 3.6 Recipient modifiers with Rover

Compared with strangers/authority, Lynae is more likely to:

- admit uncertainty;
- ask for secrecy;
- initiate leisure;
- prioritize one-on-one time;
- tolerate silence;
- state desire to meet;
- phrase crisis concern through future plans;
- accept challenge to her self-explanation.

Do not infer that Rover can always override her choices.

# 4. Startorch Academy

Treating Startorch as mere setting loses too much.

## Relationship status

**Home / institution / community / aspirational social order**

Lynae explicitly calls it home later and repeatedly identifies its people and ordinary life as worth protecting.

## What Startorch provides

- legal/educational identity;
- access to knowledge;
- peers;
- opportunities;
- hobbies;
- mistakes with consequences rather than disposal;
- institutional support;
- recognition of potential;
- a social world in which “dead weight” can accumulate.

## What Lynae provides

- practical labor;
- defense;
- volunteering;
- technical skill;
- social energy;
- tutoring/peer help;
- crisis competence;
- rule headaches.

## Conflict pattern

She can oppose Academy procedure while remaining loyal to the Academy as community.

This distinction is central.

### Example

Unlocking restricted bike settings during crisis is disobedient **because** she believes she can better protect Startorch students, not because the institution has become irrelevant.

## Institutional dependence warning

The mature self is strongly scaffolded by Startorch.

3.5 correctly reopens the question of who Lynae becomes when graduation ends that specific role.

Do not assume post-graduation identity stability is fully solved.

# 10. New Federation

## Relationship type

**origin institution / skill source / negative model**

Lynae's account is harsh:

- Resonators restricted;
- mercenaries work for cash;
- little loyalty or self-value;
- usefulness determines treatment;
- off-record dirty work;
- people can be designated threats and erased.

Later she says a New Federation pattern is “if useful, use it; if useless, throw it away.”

## Complication

She does not deny that New Federation experience produced useful abilities.

During crisis she is grateful that those days were “not all for nothing” because they let her take risks other students should not.

## Prediction

She is likely to approach New Federation institutions with suspicion and practical familiarity.

Do not infer hatred of every New Federation person.

# 12. Original identity-holder “Lynae”

## Relationship type

Not a conventional relationship. This is an **appropriated identity and unresolved moral predecessor**.

Facts:

- original Lynae was killed before playable Lynae took the letter;
- original Lynae appears connected to a Fractsidus mission;
- playable Lynae intentionally used the acceptance letter/name;
- playable Lynae felt guilt at the first explicit lie;
- later she receives her own letter under the chosen name.

## What is resolved

Playable Lynae's right to remain at Startorch under the name.

## What is not resolved

- moral debt to the dead person;
- whether anyone connected to original Lynae survives;
- what it means for playable Lynae to retain the name if confronted by such people;
- whether she would treat the name as wholly hers, shared historical burden, or something else.

The reconstructive model should abstain here.

# 13. The frightened engineering student as a mirror relation

The 3.2 companion-story student is not merely a side character.

He performs Lynae's former problem in a safer key:

- hides dangerous truth;
- believes he must carry the burden alone;
- fears disclosure will destroy belonging;
- covers one lie with another;
- acts from a legitimate protective motive through dangerous means.

Lynae recognizes the resemblance herself.

Her response demonstrates changed capacity:

- empathize without simply excusing;
- praise competence;
- widen choices;
- share burden;
- identify home/friends as continuing resources;
- insist on eventual full explanation;
- accept formal consequences;
- help convert unauthorized skill into legitimate future development.

This relationship is the best behavioral proof that her 3.0 identity arc has become a transferable model rather than a one-time catharsis.

# 14. Group-social profile

## In groups Lynae tends to

- initiate activities;
- translate seriousness into play;
- bridge people through shared tasks;
- notice practical problems;
- volunteer quickly;
- tease formality;
- value “the more, the merrier” for some games;
- permit people to participate at their own pace;
- become bored when forced into passive waiting.

## Risks in groups

- overcommitment;
- assuming people need activation;
- bypassing procedure because she sees a faster solution;
- treating her own greater competence as reason to absorb more danger.

## What she does not appear to need

She does not require dominance or center status.

The corpus supports social initiative, not social hierarchy-seeking.

# 16. State-selection rule for future reconstruction

Before predicting Lynae, determine:

1. Is the scenario pre-Startorch, stolen-name, crisis-transition, or legitimate-post-reentry?
2. Is identity exposure active?
3. Is the context ordinary, technical, crisis/protection, or shame-laden?
4. Who is the recipient?
5. Does the rule/constraint have an intelligible protective purpose?
6. Is Lynae able to act, or forced to watch helplessly?
7. Is the situation about today, or does it require a long-term future she has not yet chosen?

If those questions are unresolved, lower confidence rather than averaging all states into one personality.
