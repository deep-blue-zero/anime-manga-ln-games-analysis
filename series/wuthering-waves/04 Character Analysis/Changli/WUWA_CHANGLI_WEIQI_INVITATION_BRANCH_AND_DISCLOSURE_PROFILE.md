# Changli — a personal invitation through a shaped choice

Changli's Weiqi invitation is one of her best sources for the difference between strategic control and interpersonal care. She has arranged for Rover to come, presents a game of uncertain significance, apologizes for her earlier part in Jinzhou's token plan, and calls the mountain search a personal request (`flow#/3296/4`). These are real acts of disclosure and courtesy. They do not create an observed refusal outcome. The raw `TalkSequence` and `SequenceTransitions` offer three replies to the search request: accept at once, ask why Changli chose Rover, or ask what the reward will be. Each route reaches an acceptance and the same departure arrangement. A reconstruction may infer that a polite invitation *could* be declined in a new scenario; it cannot say that this source node tested how she responds when Rover does decline [CHL-E13, E25–E28; CHL-C08, C15, C23–C26].

## Hospitality after the earlier token plan

Rover asks whether the identifying tokens received in Jinzhou were Changli's suggestion. She acknowledges the guess, gives the city's simultaneous threats as context, says she had not wanted to burden a guest, and apologizes in person (`Character_ChangLi_8_10–11`, `_8_69–70`, `flow#/3296/4/6–9`). The apology is on the common sequence after two possible opening exchanges. It cannot be made into an alternate route where she either apologizes *or* privately desires Rover's company. Both the admission and the personal greeting occur in the same action. Conversely, the apology does not prove the token carried no added risk or that Rover learned its purposes when first given it. Jinhsi's favorable archive account of the planned welcome (`FavorStory_130405_Content`, cross-character primary evidence) and Changli's later admission place both women in the planning history without making one the puppet of the other. The text does not specify a complete allocation of their decisions.

Rover can respond to the apology by joking about another Tacet Discord fight, joking about another errand, or asking directly why they were called. The first two paths receive parallel reassurances that such treatment would ill suit a guest/benefactor (`Character_ChangLi_8_17–21`); the direct-question path moves to the next shared topic. Even a joking answer is evidence of Rover's ability to call attention to instrumental treatment. It is not evidence that every grievance has been resolved by Changli's one apology. A roleplay model that casts the token plan as secret romance or guiltless omniscience cannot explain why the text bothers to ask forgiveness.

## The optional opening is not a universal flirtation

The opening choice first separates Rover's simple reassurance about Changli's lateness from a suggestion that she had been watching Rover study the board. Only the latter reaches `Character_ChangLi_8_78`; the former has a shorter thank-you before both paths rejoin. In Chinese, Japanese and Korean, Changli says Rover looked intently at the game and she did not wish to interrupt. English adds an explicit “adorable” characterization. That adjective is a real English witness for an optional route, not a four-language invariant or a line every player necessarily hears. A generated English scene may draw on this specific playful register when its situation warrants it. It should not retroactively convert all Changli–Rover interactions into open flirting, nor discard the other witnesses' quieter attentiveness [CHL-E26; CHL-C24].

The next common admission is also locally uneven. Chinese and Korean frame Changli's present desire as private and her pleasure in finding someone who answers a move; English makes the wish to see Rover explicit; Japanese emphasizes Rover joining her in another troublesome affair (`Character_ChangLi_8_15–16`). All four then convey happiness that Rover came. These are meaningful differences in how play, burden and desire surface in speech. They do not license selecting the most intimate clause from each language and splicing them into a single canonical confession. Nor does a warm line erase the apology about having once used a difficult civic situation to choreograph Rover's route.

## Her uncertainty is shared before the request

The game she shows Rover is neither a verified immortality device nor an empty pretext. She asks whether the board evokes memory, apologizes when memory loss makes that intrusive, distinguishes the woodcutter tale from surviving records, and states that the game itself exists even if the tale's embellishments cannot be checked (`flow#/3296/4/14–29`). She then advances a conditional relation between the record and Jué's temporal wound. After the two alternative Sentinel-wound responses rejoin, she explicitly calls a cure a very slim possibility and allows that the record may only preserve Xuanmiao's memory (`flow#/3296/4/30–36`). Calling the trip personal is not a claim that it has no civic implications: healing Jué would matter to Jinzhou. It means she is not issuing it as a compulsory office assignment and has not represented a conjecture as certain [CHL-E13, E27; CHL-C08, C12].

There is an unresolved asymmetry even in this improved disclosure. Changli knows more of Xuanmiao's clue and her prior search than Rover does; she has selected the meeting and sequenced the explanation. Her apology and uncertainty markers are evidence against the caricature of an invulnerable manipulator. They are not a certificate of fully equal information or a universal policy of prior disclosure. A model should distinguish what she has *told* Rover by each stage from what she knows, suspects or has not yet answered.

## Three ways into the mountain, none a recorded refusal

The raw action's `SequenceTransitions` route the request after TalkSequence 14. Immediate acceptance (`Character_ChangLi_8_50`) enters sequence 15, where Changli thanks Rover and promises a reward (`_8_53`). Asking “why me?” (`_8_51`) enters sequence 16. There she cites their earlier mountain journey and Jué, calls Rover special both to herself and to the mountain, explains that the board might bear on Rover, and notes that a game needs two players (`_8_54–55`, `_8_72`, `_8_74`). Asking about compensation (`_8_52`) enters sequence 17. Changli then offers only a *speculative* memory clue and future help if they find the record (`_8_75`, `_8_56`). Sequences 15, 16 and 17 all transition into common sequence 18, which arranges the Hongzhen meeting (`_8_67`, `_8_57–58`). The reward path even presents two differently worded acceptance options after her answer; neither refuses [CHL-E28; CHL-C25–C26].

The branches must not be collapsed into an escalating single speech. “Special to me” is in the *why-me* route, not a preface to every reward bargain; the explicit promise of future service is in the *reward* route, not a universal term of her invitation. Immediate acceptance has its own thanks and reward assurance. The Chinese and Korean future-service wording can sound expansive, English promises aid within her capacity, and Japanese frames it as a favor owed. Those renderings share reciprocal obligation but not a precise unlimited contract. More importantly, the promise is conditional on returning with the record. No source here establishes that Changli becomes Rover's permanent servant or romantic property [CHL-E28; CHL-C26].

The option labels themselves have nuance. Chinese/Japanese/Korean's reward question allows or implies joining while requesting compensation; English says Rover will *consider* it. Yet every recorded route subsequently accepts the mountain plan. The English wording cannot create a decline outcome that the graph does not contain. Equally, the graph's lack of an explicit refusal option cannot prove that Changli would punish refusal in-world. It marks the limit of what this particular game interaction can demonstrate. An unfamiliar crossover visitor who asks for the facts and then says no is a valuable *counterfactual test* of her stated interpersonal ethic, not a rerun of an already observed line.

## Model and AV boundary

For this encounter, a reconstruction needs the opening option, the anti-errand response, the memory/sentinel path, and one of the three final invitation routes. It should ask whether the scene is before or after her apology, what uncertainty has been disclosed, and whether the counterpart wants a personal favor, a public justification, or a reward. A reasonable prediction is that she can answer with tact and revise a hypothesis; the exact response to categorical refusal remains open. Her later game invitation after the Sonoro (`flow#/3323/6`) is another time point and cannot be pasted into this first quest meeting. The four-language `QuestTree_Summary_120040` retrospectively says that later game is finished; it does not add a refusal branch to this earlier invitation, a voiced post-invitation reply, or a romance contract [CHL-E39, C35].

Six added exact four-language cases in the [AV crosswalk](WUWA_CHANGLI_AV_HUMAN_RETRIEVAL_CROSSWALK.md) expose the opening variant, apology, private desire and three route-specific replies for future listening. They join exact semantic occurrences to source text and measured local media, but nobody has listened to them for this revision. The 18 poetic text lines at `flow#/3688/0/0–17` still have unsupported runtime dispatch, and static UI art cannot establish how Changli looks or moves in this conversation. The most important subsequent observation is direct traversal of each branch alongside four-dub listening: which nuances belong to delivery, which to localization, and which were merely inferred from the projected text?
