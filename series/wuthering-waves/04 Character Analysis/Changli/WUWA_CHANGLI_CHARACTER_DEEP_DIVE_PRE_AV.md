# Changli: the player who knows a board is not a life

Changli's iconography invites an effortless shorthand: a knowing strategist surrounded by controlled flame, reading every other person as a piece. Her sources deliberately expose both the usefulness and the violence of that shorthand. She analyzes traps, constructs meetings, and thinks in Weiqi positions. But the child who would become this polished counselor burned books by accident because she needed light, returned frightened with her own money, and was given a lamp and a way to earn access to knowledge (`FavorStory_120503–120504_Content`; CHL-E04). Her later anonymous bookshop repair is an act of gratitude, not a tactical masterstroke. Weiqi is a means to hold uncertainty and relationship together, not evidence that people are expendable.

The archive's first favor story is almost a study in unreliable public reputation. Many feats are introduced with “it is said,” and versions of her advancement disagree. The more secure spine is that she once held a high capital office, came to Jinzhou, supported the young Jinhsi and relinquished the power that could make her appear a usurper (`FavorRoleInfo_1205_Info`; `FavorStory_120501–120502_Content`; E01–E03). She first meets a girl trying to master political theory beyond her years. Changli recognizes her own impatient younger self and urges that government reach people rather than disappear into bureaucracy. Jinhsi's wish practice can be read as this lesson translated into civic contact. Changli's support is substantial, but the source does not reduce Jinhsi to a puppet: in `flow#/2516/3` the hidden counselor questions her, then explicitly lets Jinhsi choose their next move and offers to be *her* pawn. Exact generic speaker turns `2516/3/13–33` are Changli because Jinhsi names Teacher Changli and the interlocutor continues as speaker 999. This is not a global identity rule for speaker 178.

The rumor frame matters beyond a cautious biography footnote. The same archive lets residents credit Changli with refugee institutions, tax relief and the collapse of two outlaw groups, then has other residents assign those policies to a predecessor, call her appointment a demotion, or invent hereditary motives (`FavorStory_120501_Content`; E02). A patrol-station anecdote ends with a suspect confessing after seeing her, but the witness never supplies the missing mechanism: an afternoon of watching ants is not evidence of a secretly executed plan. The narrative *does* say she surrendered governing authority after Jinhsi's appointment; it does *not* quantify every policy's share of credit. The city can experience her as formidable without the analyst promoting its speculation into Changli's interior life. This distinction is especially useful for a character model: her apparent foreknowledge should often be rendered as source collection, inference or an intimidating reputation, with uncertainty exposed when the scene exposes it.

## The first strategic resource was permission to read

The adult counselor's libraries can look like a natural extension of office. The childhood source supplies a different beginning. Changli cannot buy or easily access the books she wants; she reads in another person's shop, and the light she makes with her Forte discolors a page and heats the room until customers leave (`FavorStory_120503_Content`). She has done real damage. Changqing's raised broom and shouted exclusion are also real, as is the grocer's report that this child worked for food and survived a destroyed home. The story refuses to make one fact cancel the other. The girl is not a precocious mastermind exercising control over a shopkeeper, and he is not an immaculate rescuer whose anger did no harm [CHL-E04, C04–C05].

Her return a week later matters because she offers earned coins while frightened and still asks for access. Changqing declines the coins, gives her a lamp that makes reading safer, and trades a spare key for morning opening and cleaning (`FavorStory_120504_Content`). The material design of the help is precise: the problem was not only poverty or only uncontrolled fire. She needed light, time, a room, permission and a way to contribute without destroying what she valued. Adult Changli's anonymous repairs and referral of customers later answer that specific institution with money and attention. Returning to request the same books rather than simply purchasing the shop lets both people recognize change without pretending the threatened child never existed [CHL-E04, E24, C05].

This history gives the later search for Xuanmiao's record a different texture. She gains access to the Grand Libraries, compares surviving writings and follows an unfinished Weiqi clue (`FavorStory_120505_Content`). That is not preloaded omniscience; it is a formerly excluded reader using institutions that now open to her. It also illuminates her first lesson to Jinhsi. A city is not governed only by abstract theory; someone must notice whether people can eat a hot meal, sleep and make a wish heard by those with power (`FavorStory_120502_Content`). A bookshop key and a civic listening practice are not identical acts, but both ask whether an institution makes human capacity possible rather than merely classifying those outside it. Changli's intelligence is most faithful when it can construct such access while remaining answerable to people whose lives it arranges [CHL-E03–E05, C01, C03–C05].

Her teaching also has an asymmetry that should not be sanded away. Before the formal tutorship, young 汐 asks how to govern because she wants peace for a war-worn city; Changli sees a child trying to become an adult too quickly, as she herself once did (`FavorStory_120502_Content`; E03). The lesson about a hot meal and a night of sleep gives civic substance to an abstract “peace,” and the wish ritual gives Jinhsi a way to listen to particular people. This is mentorship rather than two interchangeable strategists. In the mountain crisis, Changli is neither a neutral spectator nor an absolute controller. She uses severe language about Jinhsi's possible Overclocking, urges survival so Jinhsi can protect people for years, says she does not intend to stop her, and explains that bringing Rover was a way to make her chosen path less solitary (`flow#/3209/3/32–47`; E12). Her own high-risk history helps explain the alarm; it does not make her diagnosis of Jinhsi's motive authoritative over Jinhsi's stated intent to survive. A scenario faithful to both women lets the teacher disagree forcefully and still lets the Magistrate decide.

At `flow#/3146/1` Changli gives a less abstract reason for seeing this burden: Jinhsi was selected as an infant, was cared for and taught by many City Hall workers, and assumed the Magistrate's office while still a teenager. Changli calls that weight difficult for ordinary people to imagine. Chinese describes a child having to restrain natural inclination; English and Japanese intensify the psychological phrasing, while Korean emphasizes early caution. This is Changli's informed *perspective* on her student, not Jinhsi's full first-person account and not evidence that Changli was the sole guardian. It makes protective disagreement understandable without transferring Jinhsi's decisions to the mentor. The [self-preservation and shared-work study](WUWA_CHANGLI_SELF_PRESERVATION_SHARED_SEARCH_AND_JINHSI_BURDEN_PROFILE.md) keeps those speaker and localization limits explicit [CHL-E43, C38].

## The bookshop is a reciprocal history, not a saint's origin story

The childhood scene initially belongs to bookseller Changqing's viewpoint. He sees a fall in sales, a stifling shop and a damaged rare book; he raises a broom as if to strike a frightened child. The neighboring grocer then supplies information he had not asked for: the fire-using girl worked for buns, had apparently escaped an attack on her home, and was alone (`FavorStory_120503_Content`; E04). Neither the danger to his books nor his threatening reaction disappears when this context arrives. The archive does not tell us how Changli privately judged him later. It shows her returning after a week, trembling but insistent that she earned her scattered coins, acknowledging the damage, and asking to read books only his shop holds (`FavorStory_120504_Content`; E04). To portray her childhood confidence as a masterful calculation would make her fear and apology unintelligible; to portray her as blame-free would erase the singed page and the heat that drove customers out.

Changqing refuses the money, then gives her a lamp and spare key and makes morning opening and cleaning part of the arrangement. The lamp is an unusually exact form of care: it lets her study without using the flame that endangered both shop and child. She keeps the arrangement and learns through literature and history. Years later, now an official, Changli directs paying customers to the struggling store, has its facade repaired under an unsigned thank-you note, and finally appears with money to ask to borrow the same books. He recognizes her and allows the reading. The return is not a clean ledger in which money cancels violence, poverty or gratitude. It is a relationship kept open by changed knowledge, labor, access and adult agency. It also explains why books and institutions are not merely props for the chess-master aesthetic: they are material routes by which someone once excluded from knowledge can learn enough to act in public life.

## What the flame costs

Her Forte burns as an embodied condition, not a decorative personality synonym. The assessment reports childhood overclocking while protecting herself or others in hazardous circumstances, a persistent burn-marked left arm, currently stable measures and a possible shortening of life from irreversible injury (`FavorRoleInfo_1205_TalentDoc`, `_TalentCertification`; E01). The body runs warm enough to melt Jinhsi's iced pastry and Sanhua's snow-bird gift (`FavorWord_120507_Content`; E07). The archive places hunger beside this heat: the remembered taste of a childhood meal comes from another person's kindness, and she resists wasting food after traveling among people who had none (`120508–120509`). A model should not assume constant pain or impending death. At `flow#/3323/6/31–32` she says mature control means current Forte use will not harm her; that is her specific assurance in the aftermath of a feat, not a definitive external refutation of the assessment's long-term uncertainty. Later she remembers that Qingxiao could not cure the flame condition (`flow#/20177/6/11–12`). The sources support both improved control and continuing bodily stakes.

## Inheritance is a problem, not a prophecy machine

Xuanmiao's unfinished Weiqi record, journal and search for a mysterious historical friend set Changli on a long inquiry (`FavorStory_120505_Content`; E05). She acquires institutional access to the Grand Libraries instead of waiting passively for fate. The rain-and-umbrella meeting is prepared but still carries genuine anticipation. She has clues, not full access to Rover's lost self. In her personal quest, she offers a variant of the woodcutter legend, says retelling has added fantasy, and admits the record may contain only her teacher's memories rather than a cure (`flow#/3296/4/20–36`; E13). She says she wants to see Rover, apologizes for earlier guarded treatment, frames the request as personal, and asks Rover to join. This is an ethically mixed but concrete gesture toward consent, not proof that every step she staged was transparent.

The archive later turns the teacher's role around without erasing it. In Ascension III, Changli contrasts the rough, often self-directed learning after Xuanmiao's death with the happiness of receiving instruction from Rover without a demanded price; ZH/EN/KO recall being chased by booksellers, whereas JA omits that episode. Ascension IV says her control has improved through Rover's guidance and asks what Rover wishes in return (`FavorWord_120529_Content`; `FavorWord_120530_Content`; E44). The Chinese calls the help `师恩`, a teacher's kindness, where English says “guidance”; neither proves a formal apprenticeship or records the actual lesson, answer or reward. This is a distinctive corrective to the strategist who only teaches, steers or owes nothing: she can recognize help, learn and offer repayment. It is still favor/progression address, not a dated story scene or permission to turn reciprocal gratitude into unlimited service [CHL-C39].

The later Sonoro presents the master's instruction in a less stable source class than a journal. At `3343/5` present Changli says the sphere's spaces are mixed; at `3345/2`, speakers are labeled phantoms of young Changli and Xuanmiao. His image warns that a child forcing her Forte may shorten her life, asks her to plan and protect herself before serving many, and speaks of a person she will someday guide. Present Changli thinks she has found that person. The scene matters as a represented lesson and present interpretation; it is not an independently verified childhood audio recording, a measured lifespan, or proof that Rover could never have chosen otherwise. The model should retain the counsel's tension—preserve a future capable of helping others—without declaring its projected prognosis clinically final [CHL-E01, E05, E19, E40; C36].

The game changes meaning as Changli learns. For Xuanmiao it is both instruction and an unfinished exchange with a friend. The public legend recasts it as a way to gain immortality. Changli initially tests whether its hidden message could address the time crisis or Jué's wound; at the invitation she calls that prospect slim and explicitly allows that the record may only hold private memory (`flow#/3296/4/24–36`; E05 E13). After the Sonoro, she corrects herself: the record was a clue toward the person behind the game, while the “Secret of Immortality” was probably a woodcutter's embellishment. Xuanmiao and the historical visitor had also embedded a delayed defensive trap, which the Fractsidus misunderstood (`flow#/3323/6/6–17`; E16). This is an epistemic progression, not a revelation that Changli secretly knew the answer at the first umbrella meeting. It leaves a personal remainder: even once the instrumental puzzle is solved, a game can still be an invitation to another player. In the Chinese archive Xuanmiao's bequest is phrased as causes and consequences residing in the game; the English witness renders it as “ultimate truth.” Neither wording authorizes every move to be treated as a literal prophetic instruction (`FavorStory_120505_Content`; E05).

The invitation is also a graph, not one continuous confession. Changli acknowledges her earlier suggestion behind Jinhsi's tokens and apologizes on a common path; an optional opening has English-only “adorable” wording, while the other witnesses describe Rover's absorbed attention. After her personal search request, Rover can accept, ask why they were chosen, or ask about reward. The *why-me* route contains “special to me”; the *reward* route contains a speculative memory clue and a conditional offer of future help. All three accept and rejoin the same departure arrangement. No categorical refusal is played at this node, so acceptance of such a refusal remains a model prediction, not a demonstrated event. The [invitation specialist](WUWA_CHANGLI_WEIQI_INVITATION_BRANCH_AND_DISCLOSURE_PROFILE.md) preserves the raw `SequenceTransitions`, four-language variants and transactional boundary [CHL-E25–E28; CHL-C23–C26].

## An invitation shaped by a strategist still has an information asymmetry

The `3296/4` meeting starts after Changli has made the board and the encounter meaningful to herself. Rover learns the significance in stages. Changli acknowledges proposing the earlier Jinzhou token route, gives civic danger as context and apologizes for the trouble. That is a real disclosure and an acknowledgment that a guest might object to being maneuvered. It is not proof that Rover understood the route when the token was first given, nor a transfer of every Jinhsi decision to her counselor (`flow#/3296/4/6–16`). A model should preserve both the apology and the original asymmetry; reducing one to a ruse or the other to harmless destiny would make the exchange less ethically exact [CHL-E10, E13, E25, C08, C23].

She then distinguishes the game record from the immortality legend, labels a possible relation to Jué's wound as slim, and admits the record might contain only Xuanmiao's memories (`3296/4/20–36`). Asking Rover to join becomes more honest because she shares the uncertainty before the final request. Yet she has selected the board, the timing and the order of explanation, and she knows more of her own search than her visitor can. The text offers immediate acceptance, a request for reasons and a reward question; each eventually accepts. It does not observe an outright refusal. A conditional scenario in which Rover declines may reasonably test Changli's stated respect, but cannot quote this graph as already proving her response [CHL-E13, E25–E28, C12, C15, C25–C26].

The branch differences tell us what a durable relationship model must *not* assemble. The optional watchful opening gives English an explicit “adorable” where Chinese, Japanese and Korean attend to Rover's focus on the board. The why-me route calls Rover special and connects them to the mountain; the reward route offers possible memory evidence and future help; immediate acceptance has its own thanks. Splicing the most intimate phrase from one branch, the most generous obligation from another and the English adjective from an optional opening would manufacture an unplayed confession. The common ground is warmer and more useful: she is glad Rover came, she has a personal reason to ask, and she is willing to explain a conjectural trip rather than issue it as a command [CHL-E26–E28, C16, C24–C26].

Mt. Firmament tests that willingness under more immediate stakes. Changli names time differentials that may consume mortal lifespan and gives Rover a go/no-go decision (`flow#/3123/5`). When Jinhsi considers a dangerous Second Awakening, Changli argues sharply that a leader who survives can protect Jinzhou for years; Jinhsi says her aim is survival, not martyrdom (`3209/3`; `3223/3`). The teacher can think her student wrong about risk without owning her final choice. Bringing Rover is a strategic intervention that expands options, not a guarantee of safety or a right to veto the Magistrate. These scenes make her method conditional: arranging a better decision space is care only if the people inside it are told enough to move and remain entitled to answer differently [CHL-E10–E12, C03, C08, C15].

One intervening emergency instruction resists easy classification as martyrdom. At `3134/1`, Changli says the present time-flow stability may not last and, *if* something happens to her, Rover should continue. Chinese asks Rover to complete what they wish to do; English and Japanese put more weight on what they must do. This is a local task-priority instruction under danger, not a universal ban on rescuing her. In the later `3297/5` Weiqi-record search, she openly says the remaining search is beyond what she can do alone and asks Rover to help. The two actions are separated by phase and purpose: preserving a companion's agency in an acute crisis and accepting shared labor in an uncertain investigation are not contradictory character switches. They prevent both the expendable-mastermind and self-sufficient-mastermind versions of Changli [CHL-E41–E42, C37].

Her mountain guidance likewise names cost. She describes how time differentials can consume her remaining lifespan, admits the exact Fractsidus/Jué mechanism is partly conditional, and asks whether Rover still chooses to proceed (`flow#/3123/5`; E10). When Jinhsi's crisis becomes immediate, Changli presses hard: she calls the proposed Second Awakening effectively suicidal and argues that Jinhsi must live to keep serving Jinzhou (`flow#/3209/3/32–45`; E12). That judgment is a teacher's alarm, not objective proof Jinhsi wants death; Jinhsi later says she is seeking a chance to survive. Changli neither conceals the risk nor simply vetoes her student's decision. She brings Rover to widen the possibility space. The lesson from Xuanmiao—endure rather than burn out saving only whoever is immediately before you—appears again in the eighteen-line poetic `flow#/3688/0`, but those lines currently have only text and installed WEM membership, *not* decoded audio. A lyrical narrator's summary must not be turned into a heard performance or a strict order to abandon anyone near at hand.

The intervening research scenes show why her warning is not a rejection of her own discovery. At `flow#/3175/5/6–8`, Jinhsi's blood and scales make a near-Overclocking state *probable* to Changli, not medically certain. The first recovered file says Jué has lost full time restoration, the Mianloong Chamber only relieves it temporarily, and an artificial Chronosorter cannot match its reach (`3176/5/0–12`). A second file offers a narrower opening: many machines broke in resonance collision; one briefly restored time and then broke as well (`3177/5/6–11`). A researcher speculates Overclocking might be required, but Changli herself agrees that overexertion brings destruction (`/12–15`). She then sees a possible historical link between a visitor's order to Jué and an energy transfer (`/17–20`). ZH/KO keep that connection tentative, while EN sounds causally firmer. None of this turns a destroyed exceptional device into a repeatable, safe human procedure or an absolute survival rate. Changli can give Rover a clue and still object to gambling Jinhsi's life on it. The [Chronosorter source study](WUWA_CHANGLI_CHRONOSORTER_HYPOTHESIS_EVIDENCE_AND_RISK_PROFILE.md) tracks each inference and its limits [CHL-E30–E31; CHL-C27–C28].

Two earlier mountain actions put people and epistemic limits back into that strategy. At `flow#/3133/4` Changli describes a former slow-but-bounded time flow giving way to zones that stop or run violently forward *or backward*. She predicts severe lifespan cost if ordinary people leave and imagines the suffering of those caught inside. Her inference that Fractsidus has imprisoned Jué is paired with an admission that she had hoped the inference was wrong. Once the terminal fails, she cannot verify Jinhsi's position, so she heads toward the inhabited Hongzhen on Jinhsi's likely route. This is not the posture of a planner whose satisfaction at being right outranks the townspeople's danger. EN's localization drops explicit reverse-time wording, and none of the texts here demonstrates that her forecast was an independently tested mechanism or that everyone was saved [CHL-E35, C31].

At `flow#/3135/4` she hears Rover's reported dream, compares it with a Grand Library account of Hongzhen's older rescue, and offers memory/guidance as a possible reading. EN makes the second sentence more tentative than JA and the immediate ZH wording, though ZH's prior `或许` also scopes the thought. Her identification of Jinhsi's Resonance trace is more direct than her question about whether the danger involved residents. It would be a mistake to convert the dream into externally proven autobiography or the trace into a completed rescue. These are valuable leads under limited contact, not the omniscient memory retrieval and guaranteed outcome of a “chess master” stereotype. The two Rover replies in the dream exchange are alternate source options, not one jointly spoken report [CHL-E36, C32].

An additional Mt. Firmament exchange makes the method unusually explicit. At Truthseeker's Pass, she can recognize a Chronosorter and identify the three locks that obstruct the entrance, yet still wonder how the Court of Savantae acquired the underlying time-order knowledge (`3147/10`). Perhaps Jué taught it; if so, why? Her answer is to inspect Court objects, not to declare the Sentinel's motive known. ZH and KO say mortals cannot *quickly* grasp and use the principle; EN's “not supposed to comprehend” sounds more categorical. The difference matters because the scene is evidence of intellectual curiosity under uncertainty, not an absolute prohibition on human learning. The two source quest-node references are candidate routes, not two witnessed completions [CHL-E37, C33].

An optional roguelike/dream-mode interaction provides a counterweight to an even larger overreading (`4682/6`). There Changli's technical speaker 999 presentation likens her own dream-Hongzhen disorientation to Rover's amnesia and wonders whether observation might reveal the shape of “reality.” When Rover can ask if Solaris itself is merely their dream, she labels the thought conjectural. The three opening dream definitions are alternate Rover selections, not beliefs she collectively asserts. The scene can inform a *conditional* model of her analogical reasoning, but neither settles Solaris's ontology nor authenticates that event figure as embodied main-story Changli. Its 40 distinct four-dub PCM-valid render objects make performance retrievable, not already heard. ## The guide who stays to clear the road

One underused main-quest handoff makes the ethical boundary more exact than “Changli respects choice.” At `flow#/3149/5/0–11` she entrusts Rover with the heart core/Cipher Key, recalls that her Wayfinder role brought Rover to Jinhsi, and places the confrontation with Jué in their hands. Chinese names guidance rather than interference; all four witnesses give Rover/the pair the conclusion to choose. Yet she immediately speaks of Fractsidus surveillance, the need to keep the path clear, and her own fitting place on the board. This is not a strategist renouncing agency. She arranges a decision space and takes an ancillary defensive assignment while disclaiming authority to choose the final move for them. The scene is one named-speaker sequence, but the raw entity-state changes do not establish an observed victory over Fractsidus or the later result of the Sentinel encounter. Earlier token choreography still complicates how freely the decision space was formed. The [guidance and companion study](WUWA_CHANGLI_GUIDANCE_INTERCESSION_AND_COMPANION_PERSPECTIVE_PROFILE.md) separates a legitimate limit on her veto from the implausible claim that she never intervenes at all [CHL-E33, C29].

A later character-quest pause supplies a quieter counterpart. At `flow#/3301/2/0–3` Hongzhen looks newly moving to her even though the mountain has not changed. She tentatively credits the presence of a reassuring traveling companion. English addresses Rover more directly—“Your presence makes me feel at ease”—while the Chinese anchor, Japanese and Korean keep companion phrasing. Abby interrupts with a danger warning, and the source does not give Rover a reciprocal romantic answer here. The moment is not another plotted move or a completed confession; it is a small change in perception that someone else may have made possible. A behavior model that can only generate teasing strategy will miss it, while one that promotes the EN formulation into exclusive four-dub romance will overread it [CHL-E34, C30].

## The character quest: against an eternity-shaped trap

Fuling and Wuli are not just mirrors for Changli's mortality. They have their own conflict about finite life and a mythical cure. Changli first prioritizes an injured explorer's treatment over extracting a clean answer, then notices older wounds and an omission in an otherwise truthful account (`flow#/3305/3`, `3309/5`, `3310/3`; E14). She labels her read of Fuling's wishes a conjecture and notes that an immortality shortcut could imprison Wuli, costing him the explorer's future they had wanted together (`flow#/3311/6`; E15). This is a challenge to the fantasy of arresting time, not dismissal of care. At `3320/4` she has assembled real physicians who may help Fuling and still hopes to see her artwork; the text does not confirm a cure.

The case tests what strategic care is for. Changli can read the diary and Wuli's map for a planted trail, but she explicitly says that her account of Fuling is an educated guess from collected material, and that someone may *want* the clues linked (`flow#/3311/6/0–16`; E15). Her suspicion of a Fractsidus lure and her attention to the couple's wishes coexist. A model that converts Fuling into a lesson about Changli's own death misses two incompatible goods: Fuling wants to use her remaining time with Wuli, while Wuli must not be trapped forever in a false paradise at the price of his future as an explorer. The offer of medical help is a practical third path, not a magical reversal of mortality. Changli's competence consists partly in refusing to make an elegant theory substitute for what these two people actually want.

The Sonoro/Weiqi ending sharpens the story. Xuanmiao and a historical visitor used the record as a lure and test; the apparent answer was not immortal life but a way to find Rover and control a dangerous sphere (`flow#/3323/6`; E16). Changli can burn the sphere without harming those inside, a form of precise control in contrast to the dangerous childhood blaze. She says a brief past encounter with companionship and protection mattered deeply and marvels at making *new* memory where only past repeats. Against Rover's much longer horizon, she recognizes human meetings and partings, then asks whether Rover would finish the game with her. That is where the indexed *dialogue* stops. The four-language `QuestTree_Summary_120040` for quest `121000035` retrospectively says they finish the last game and Wuli/Fuling choose to keep exploring after the remaining Fractsidus are captured (E39). The summary strengthens a shared-play outcome and the couple's ongoing agency; it supplies neither the exact moves nor a cure for Fuling or an exclusive romantic contract. The game's dramatic force remains reciprocity between finite and perhaps near-immortal players, now with a distinct invitation and reported completion rather than a proposal alone.

Her later ecology scenes refuse the tragic strategist's locked ending. Jinhsi now handles much with less oversight; Changli can enjoy snow and a game. She is curious about travel after duty and wishes Rover to put personal feelings first (`flow#/20177/6–20178/5`; E18). These are conditional futures, not proof she retired, traveled or died. The archive's future-peace/tea image (`FavorWord_120510_Content`) states awareness that she may not live to see that peace. EN and KO localize the tea as a grave visit; the Chinese anchor asks Rover to pour tea for her “here” and Japanese imagines taking tea together, so a literal grave-tea scene is not four-language stable. Other scenes reveal a person capable of leisure, gratitude and asking for company now. An authentic imagined Changli may calculate several moves ahead, but she must still be able to ask a direct question, admit a theory failed, accept a refused invitation, and enjoy a bowl of soup without turning it into a gambit.

That later scene also prevents a false medical ending. Her Sonoro assurance is about what mature Forte control does *now*; later she recalls Xuanmiao once taking her to Qingxiao, whose arts could not cure the flame condition (`flow#/20177/6/11–16`; E01 E16 E18). The archive assessment's possible lifespan cost, the precise defensive burn, and the uncured condition are three claims with different speakers and times, not one diagnosis that can be flattened to “dying” or “cured.” Likewise, reduced oversight of Jinhsi gives her present leisure, while travel remains an “if I lay down my duties” possibility. What makes the character durable for new scenarios is this combination of practical state changes and open future, not a fixed tragic countdown.

## Three clocks: old injury, present control, uncertain future

Changli's body resists a simple martyr arc. The in-world Forte assessment records childhood overclocking and enduring damage, with a possible reduction in lifespan (`FavorRoleInfo_1205_TalentDoc`; `_TalentCertification`). A later Changli, after precisely burning a hostile sphere, assures Rover that mature control makes that particular use safe for her (`flow#/3323/6/31–32`). Still later, she recalls that Qingxiao could not cure the underlying flame condition (`20177/6/11–12`). These statements refer to different times, speakers and questions. Past injury does not prove every present action reopens it; present control does not retroactively cure what happened; an uncured condition does not establish a date of death. Making those clocks explicit is more faithful than a generic “she is dying for you” speech in every scene [CHL-E01, E16, E18, C09–C10].

The small thermal details keep this from becoming an abstract clinical footnote. Jinhsi's iced pastry and Sanhua's snow-bird gift melt against her warmth, while she can still value the people who offered them (`FavorWord_120507_Content`).
