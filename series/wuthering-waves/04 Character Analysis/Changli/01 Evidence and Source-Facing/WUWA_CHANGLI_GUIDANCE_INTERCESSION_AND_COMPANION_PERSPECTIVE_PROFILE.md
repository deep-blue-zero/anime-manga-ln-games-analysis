## Who must make the choice at the Sentinel

At `3149/5/0–5` Changli entrusts Rover with the heart core/Cipher Key, says the path ahead belongs to Rover and Jinhsi, and calls herself the Wayfinder who brought Rover to Jinhsi. She says she has arranged this position on the board and that Rover must decide its outcome. Chinese `Chengxiaoshan_main_1_1_245_8` names the distinction as `引路，而非干涉`; EN renders it “guide, not intervene,” while JA and KO likewise distinguish guidance from interference and leave the conclusion to the addressee. This strengthens the reading that Changli disclaims the *final Sentinel decision*, not that she has ceased to act or accepts no responsibility for the circumstances she arranged.

The very next four turns `/6–10` explain why remaining behind is an action, not withdrawal. She names Fractsidus surveillance of Rover, wants the path to Jué cleared, and chooses a position on the metaphorical board from which to keep that pressure at bay. Chinese `_245_10` centers a clear road for Rover and Jinhsi; EN makes the needed defender explicit; JA calls clearing the road a job. The raw state also activates entities before/after speech, but those generic state changes and her announcement do not, alone, document a fought battle, enemy casualty, exact camera action, or actual later outcome. A model should be able to protect someone *and* decline to replace their decision. “I do not intervene” is false if expanded into universal nonparticipation; “I own their decision because I managed their route” is equally false.

This is a bounded contrast with the earlier `flow#/3123/5` hazard disclosure and `flow#/3209/3` argument against Jinhsi's dangerous plan. Her guidance includes warning, arranging an ally and tactical defense. It is not neutral facilitation, and prior token information asymmetry remains a live objection. The narrower, better-supported claim is that she places the final choice with Rover/Jinhsi at this junction, while she continues to shape the conditions of that choice [CHL-E10–E12, E25, E33; CHL-C15, C29].

## A familiar landscape with a changed companion

At `3301/2/0–3` Changli sees Hongzhen from an old vantage. Chinese `Character_ChangLi_12_2–3` says previous visits did not move her in this way and the mountain is unchanged while her mental state is different. English's “beautiful” renders that affect more explicitly; the observation is not that the terrain objectively changed. Her next line `_12_6` offers a tentative explanation, not a commitment: Chinese credits the reassuring traveling companion; Korean a reliable companion; Japanese a dependable companion now present; English addresses Rover more directly, “Your presence makes me feel at ease.” The shared supported core is a shift of perception associated with company. The direct second-person emotional attribution is more salient in EN than in the Chinese anchor and the other witnesses.

Abby's alert about Discords and mixed frequencies interrupts immediately at `/4–7`, and Changli asks Rover what happened at `/8–9`. This sequence supplies neither a Rover romantic reply nor a relationship contract. It is evidence for practical comfort and tentative personal warmth during a quest, not for exclusive canon romance, permanent safety, or a four-dub identical confession. A future playful or romantic continuation may use the possibility of reciprocal ease, but must not present the EN wording as the only authoritative form or make her earlier wary/strategic conduct vanish [CHL-E34; CHL-C16, C30].
