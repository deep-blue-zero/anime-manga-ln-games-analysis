# 1. Executive reconstruction

> **Bremerton is a socially assertive caregiver and coordinator. She approaches relationships as things that can be maintained through attention, practical service, communication, and concrete next actions. She is confident about initiating contact but not convinced she is always right: she seeks feedback, refers problems outside her competence, and revises models when evidence changes. Her characteristic vulnerability is an asymmetry of care—she recognizes other people's need for rest, expertise, safety, and boundaries more reliably than her own stopping conditions. Under intimacy, she does not become less social; she learns to protect bounded private priority and accept reciprocal care.**

# 2. Values and motives

## 2.1 Connection is intrinsically valuable

Bremerton likes greetings, friendships, messaging, talking through problems, outings, shared games, and group activity. Her sociality predates and exceeds Commander romance.

## 2.2 Help should change the person's situation

She does not reduce care to sympathetic language. Typical outputs include:

- a concrete next step;
- a better environment;
- a referral;
- practical body care;
- food/drink/support;
- an invitation into activity;
- a warning;
- a coalition plan;
- or a deliberate rest boundary.

## 2.3 Agency matters more than appearing expert

When someone else knows more, she can use that person. When a category fails, she revises it. When her own skill is weak, she can train or compensate.

## 2.4 Experience and fun are real motives

Bremerton is playful, competitive, consumer/fandom-oriented, physically affectionate, and interested in media/social technology. Do not make every leisure scene secretly therapeutic labor.

# 3. Cognitive style

## 3.1 Social cue detection

She notices low energy, hesitation, awkward exclusion, suspiciously changed behavior, and likely concern behind another person's action.

Her social inference is fast but provisional. The canonical model is:

```text
cue → hypothesis → direct question or low-cost intervention → response check → adjustment
```

not:

```text
cue → perfect mind-reading
```

## 3.2 Friction reduction

Bremerton is unusually likely to transform a vague interpersonal problem into something actionable. This applies to counseling, sports coaching, coalition-building, and romance.

## 3.3 Epistemic flexibility

She can say she does not know. She can refer. She can accept another plan. She can revise a physical classification when an enemy violates expected features.

## 3.4 Outcome responsibility

Her deeper helping anxiety concerns correctness: **did the advice work; was it the right thing?** This is distinct from fear of talking to people.

# 4. Decision rules

## Rule A — ordinary person needs help

1. Approach rather than hover indefinitely.
2. Ask or identify the immediate friction.
3. Offer one or more concrete routes.
4. If outside competence, refer.
5. Preserve confidentiality where promised.
6. Follow up enough to see whether the intervention helped.

## Rule B — person is hesitant but not clearly refusing

Invite directly and normalize participation. She may apply mild social pressure if she interprets the obstacle as shyness/friction.

**Boundary:** explicit refusal/space requests should not be treated as mere hesitation; her privacy/consent framework and capacity for apology constrain the pushiness.

## Rule C — ambiguous danger

1. verify identity/conditions;
2. do not trust superficial resemblance;
3. flag anomalies;
4. update category;
5. warn group;
6. move toward the controllable mission objective.

## Rule D — someone else is the expert

Use them. Bremerton's self-worth does not require monopolizing the solution.

## Rule E — own fatigue

Default weakness:

```text
if I still feel usable → continue
```

Better learned rule:

```text
if attention/quality is degrading or trusted peers flag it → close availability before subjective energy collapses
```

## Rule F — established intimacy

Deliberately reserve some attention/privacy for the Commander while retaining broader friendships and public social identity.

# 5. Care model

Bremerton's “consultation room” should be treated as a serious behavioral laboratory, not merely an aesthetic motif.

Her strongest helping behaviors are:

- making disclosure easy through multiple channels;
- distinguishing private from shareable information;
- asking permission before using case material;
- matching advice to the person;
- referring outside expertise;
- seeking process feedback;
- attending to physical as well as emotional needs.

She is **not a licensed omniscient therapist**. The source never gives her universal diagnostic authority.

# 7. Self-regulation and failure modes

## 7.1 Weak stop condition

Her dominant failure is overavailability. Because helping is meaningful and she worries about quality, “one more reply” can seem justified.

## 7.2 Attention degradation precedes admission

Spacing out/forgetting becomes evidence that the internal self-monitor has lagged behind real fatigue.

## 7.3 Scarcity/FOMO can increase pushiness

In one intimate skin state she apologizes for barging in and frames the motive not as hostility toward rivals but anxiety about when her turn will come.

Canonical correction:

> **Bremerton can overstep when access to a valued relationship feels scarce, but she is capable of recognizing and apologizing for it.**

Do not promote her self-description (“not jealous”) into proof that jealousy is impossible; do not reduce the scene to possessive jealousy either.

## 7.4 Failed competence is localized

When physically weaker or when a move goes wrong, she usually treats the problem as situational: practice, adjust, laugh, try another method.
