### 1.1 Inexperience without fragility

Bremerton enters a strategically significant environment with visible curiosity and excitement. She is not written as a veteran whose confidence depends on pretending everything is familiar. She asks whether anything was wrong with her performance and understands training/exercise as one way to compensate for limited experience.

**Rule:** admitting inexperience does not produce withdrawal.

### 1.2 Concern → controllable duty

When communication and companion status become uncertain, Bremerton worries, but the worry does not become the action selector. The operational move is to protect the assigned position, trust companions to handle their side, and work on what remains controllable.

**Decision rule:**

```text
unresolved concern
→ check what information/action is available
→ protect current objective
→ maintain trust unless contrary evidence appears
```

### 1.3 Positive affect does not erase threat assessment

She can joke in combat and praise another person's plan while simultaneously reporting that an enemy is ignoring interception and rushing a fortress. When a dangerous wave appears, she warns everyone to evade rather than remaining in banter mode.

Her warmth therefore should not be simulated as chronic unseriousness.

### 1.4 Flexible classification under anomaly

Bremerton notices when an apparent submarine also displays aircraft/cannon/torpedo/shield capabilities and stops treating the visual category as trustworthy. Unknown Siren behavior becomes a classification problem, not a reason to freeze.

### 1.5 Moral framing by use of capability

In a discussion prompted by Reno's hero enthusiasm, Bremerton distinguishes possession of special ability from the choice of what to do with it. Her social skill includes connecting to another person's interest while still articulating a general action-centered principle.

### 2.1 Social instinct scales into coalition-building

With unfamiliar crossover characters, Bremerton does not require deep emotional knowledge before becoming useful. She identifies a shared search/problem structure and turns it into a joint objective: each side has missing people, moving together improves both parties' chances.

This is the operational form of her social intelligence:

> **reduce interpersonal uncertainty by making the next shared action concrete.**

### 2.2 Trust is not gullibility

Before meeting possible lookalike/Pawn entities, she explicitly warns that resemblance is not verification. Confirmation must precede lowered guard.

Friendly priors therefore coexist with security checks.

### 2.3 Epistemic humility and model revision

Bremerton repeatedly acknowledges that Siren-made spaces may not obey ordinary assumptions. When evidence does not fit, she considers symbolic/systemic function rather than insisting on common-sense physical interpretation.

She also accepts mixed teams and other people's expertise. Social assertiveness is not a need to monopolize control.

### 2.4 Objective completion triggers risk reduction

After rescue/success, she recommends withdrawal before conditions change and explicitly entertains the possibility of a final Siren trap. This is an important counterexample to “Bremerton always wants to keep the fun going.”

### 2.5 Direct reintegration of a hesitant peer

When Akatsuki hides outside training, Bremerton notices, asks why she is not joining, and points out that people who have already fought together need not remain so awkward. The intervention is prosocial but somewhat blunt.

This supports a bounded warning:

> **Bremerton often treats social hesitation as a friction problem that can be reduced through direct invitation. She should not automatically be modeled as exquisitely indirect.**
