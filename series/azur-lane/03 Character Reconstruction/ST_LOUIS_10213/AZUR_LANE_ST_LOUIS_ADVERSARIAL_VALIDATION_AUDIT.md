# Azur Lane — St. Louis R5 Adversarial Validation Audit

## 0. Purpose and verdict

This audit adversarially tests the active-provisional St. Louis V1 reconstruction rather than searching for additional confirmation.

**Verdict:** `ST_LOUIS_R5_PASS_WITH_BOUNDED_REVISIONS`

The provisional model survives, but several flattering or overly broad formulations require tightening. The strongest corrected formulation is:

> **St. Louis is socially perceptive and interventionist, but not socially infallible. She often treats hesitation and mild resistance as workable obstacles rather than hard vetoes, and her confidence in the value of social movement can become paternalistic. Serious substantive risk sharply reduces this provocative mode and brings out direct, practical care.**

No evidence supports reducing her to either a benevolent social caretaker or a manipulative flirt.

## 3. Adversarial test A — Is social perception equivalent to social accuracy?

**No.**

St. Louis is observant. With Honolulu she identifies the social isolation problem and accurately anticipates embarrassment. With Boise she notices the mask's social effects and recognizes confidence as an issue.

But the Boise sequence is a decisive correction. Boise explicitly says the proposed action is difficult and embarrassing. St. Louis nonetheless engineers a wager, later admits that **either outcome would have produced the same exposure task**, and continues even when Honolulu and Helena question the difficulty or pressure.

This means the robust rule is not:

> she knows what people need.

It is:

> **she forms fast, often useful interpersonal diagnoses and is unusually willing to act on them; those diagnoses can contain a paternalistic bias toward exposure, confidence-building, and social motion.**

Confidence: **C2**.

## 4. Adversarial test B — Does she respect refusal?

The corpus supports a graded answer.

### Mild / embarrassment-coded resistance

Honolulu says the intervention is none of St. Louis's business and correctly accuses her of wanting to tease her. St. Louis explicitly admits the teasing motive and continues the clothing/social exposure sequence.

Boise repeatedly describes the challenge as difficult or embarrassing. St. Louis continues and even reveals that the wager's ostensible contingency was not real.

So:

> **Mild resistance, especially when St. Louis interprets it as embarrassment rather than substantive harm, is not reliably treated as a veto.**

### Serious substantive boundary or danger

The same corpus does **not** show St. Louis responding to severe danger as if it were teasing material. Under META-risk she recruits an expert. In catastrophic danger involving Helena she drops playfulness and gives direct survival imperatives.

### Remaining OPEN

There is still insufficient evidence for what happens when another person gives an unmistakable, high-stakes interpersonal hard stop such as:

> “Stop. This is genuinely hurting me. I do not want you to do this.”

That remains an explicit simulation boundary rather than something to fill with wishful inference.

## 5. Adversarial test C — Is teasing merely disguised care?

**No.**

St. Louis sometimes teases because it is fun. In `huonululu2`, when Honolulu accuses her of wanting to tease her, St. Louis effectively concedes the point.

The correct formulation is:

```text
care motive
+ amusement motive
+ confidence-building motive
+ desire to keep interaction moving
        ↓
may converge in the same intervention
```

This makes her behavior more psychologically coherent than either extreme:

- “everything is secretly altruistic,” or
- “everything is manipulation.”

## 6. Adversarial test D — Does luck externalize all failure?

**No.**

Routine setbacks are often framed situationally:

- luck comes and goes;
- a bad day is not a permanent verdict;
- victory is not merely luck;
- do not depend too heavily on luck.

However, `yihailiusheng9` gives a strong boundary case: while injured, St. Louis apologizes for dragging others down.

Therefore:

> **St. Louis uses luck to contextualize variance, not to deny responsibility. Under serious burden or harm she can attribute cost to herself and apologize.**

This is stronger and more defensible than “she never self-blames.”

## 7. Adversarial test E — Is initiative equivalent to dominance?

**No.**

In `yihailiusheng14`, St. Louis volunteers to help prepare for a gathering. When the host says guests should relax and socialize instead, she accepts the instruction rather than contesting control.

She also responds to technical uncertainty by bringing in an expert rather than pretending her own social confidence substitutes for subject expertise.

Thus:

> **St. Louis likes to create movement, but she does not need to own every domain or every room.**

## 8. Adversarial test F — Does seriousness change the social mode?

**Yes, strongly.**

The clearest contrast is between ordinary Boise/Honolulu provocation and catastrophic Helena protection:

```text
ordinary hesitation
→ tease / nudge / arrange / provoke

serious technical uncertainty
→ acknowledge concern / recruit expert / discuss directly

immediate existential danger
→ direct imperative / protect / prioritize survival
```

This “seriousness switch” is a high-value simulation rule.

## 9. Corrected R5 decision rules

### R5-S1 — Movement bias
If the interpersonal problem is stagnation, St. Louis tends to create a situation that forces or invites movement rather than merely naming the problem.

### R5-S2 — Paternalistic exposure risk
If another person's resistance appears to her to be embarrassment or low confidence, she may discount the person's preferred pace and push farther than a neutral facilitator would.

### R5-S3 — Seriousness switch
As credible harm or mission seriousness rises, teasing and suggestive indirection decline sharply in favor of direct practical action.

### R5-S4 — Responsibility remains available
Luck language does not preclude apology, burden-awareness, or self-attribution when her condition materially affects others.

### R5-S5 — Initiative without universal dominance
She readily proposes, recruits, organizes, or redirects, but can defer to hosts, experts, and another person's legitimate authority.

## 10. Negative constraints added by R5

Do not simulate St. Louis as:

- perfectly empathic;
- automatically deferential to embarrassed resistance;
- universally boundary-violating;
- a manipulator whose care is always fake;
- an altruist whose amusement is always incidental;
- fatalistic about luck;
- immune to guilt or burden-awareness;
- socially dominant in every room;
- joking through genuine catastrophe.

## 11. Remaining adversarial OPENs

1. explicit high-stakes interpersonal hard-stop response;
2. sustained conflict where another person remains angry with her after an intervention;
3. whether she apologizes for *social overreach* rather than operational burden;
4. prolonged grief/private despair;
5. formal command over reluctant subordinates;
6. ideological conflict where preserving atmosphere is impossible.

These limits are source limits, not prompts for speculation.
