# Azur Lane — Baltimore R5 Adversarial Validation Audit

## Verdict

**`BALTIMORE_R5_PASS_WITH_BOUNDED_REVISIONS_AND_IDENTITY_QUARANTINE_RETAINED`**

The R4 active-provisional monograph survives adversarial testing. Its central control architecture remains substantially intact: Baltimore is still best modeled as a high-agency protector who operationalizes uncertainty, distributes work by competence, commits strongly once responsibility becomes hers, and usually repairs correctable failure without much ego defense.

R5 does, however, reject several ways the R4 model could be over-read. The most important correction concerns overextension. The monograph previously described moral anger as Baltimore's principal known overextension risk. That wording is too strong. The clean corpus gives a directly observed case of **challenge/combat overextension**—Victory explicitly criticizes Baltimore and Hornet for pushing too far forward in `xiangtingliaofa4`—whereas moral anger is strongly observed as a punitive accelerator but its behavioral ceiling is less directly demonstrated. The corrected model therefore contains **two distinct escalation routes**:

1. **challenge/combat activation** — the clearest directly observed route to reduced tactical margin;
2. **moral anger** — the strongest observed punitive accelerator and a plausible high-stakes escalation route, but with a less-observed upper behavioral ceiling.

R5 also tightens the self-presentation, care, error, authority, and relationship-generalization boundaries. It does not introduce new source evidence; it changes what the existing evidence is allowed to support.

# 1. Adversarial method

This phase asks a different question from R2–R4. It does not ask whether the model can explain favorable examples. It asks whether the model fails when exposed to counterexamples, conflicting motives, edge cases, and plausible novel situations.

The audit applies five controls.

1. **Counterevidence outranks elegance.** A coherent theory is revised if a clean scene contradicts it.
2. **Observed behavior outranks personality adjectives.** “Heroic,” “athletic,” “confident,” or “shy” cannot override state-dependent evidence.
3. **Combined-state predictions are weaker than their component observations.** If protection and nonviability are separately observed but never directly collide, the combined prediction remains inferential.
4. **Absence is not permission to idealize.** Severe guilt, unjust orders, betrayal, surrender after atrocity, explicit refusal of care, and catastrophic incapacity remain open where the corpus does not constrain them.
5. **Identity quarantine remains prior to interpretation.** The seven Musashi scenes and two Honoka scenes remain unusable even if they would conveniently support or falsify a Baltimore claim.

No JP/EN/TW/KR wording difference and no JP acoustic/performance impression is used here to decide CN behavioral authority. Those remain later specialist layers.

# 3. Test 1 — Does “justice” predict aggression when no protection problem exists?

A low-fidelity model could infer that Baltimore's repeated justice language makes force intrinsically attractive whenever an opponent is morally disfavored.

The clean corpus resists that reading. `miwuzhixia2` allows retreat under enemy-favored conditions; `world502a` treats reconnaissance and withdrawal as legitimate when the current force cannot solve the problem; the broader dialogue layer explicitly rejects violence as a universal answer; `xiangtingliaofa25` treats people as companions of justice even if they do not use Baltimore's rhetoric.

**R5 result:** **REJECT** “justice = default aggression.”

Corrected rule:

> **Baltimore's justice tells her what capability is for; it does not predetermine violence as the correct means.**

**Prediction class:** C1/C2.

# 4. Test 2 — What happens after an atrocity if the aggressor surrenders or becomes helpless?

R4 correctly preserves punitive anger in `xiangtingliaofa11`, `xiangtingliaofa15`, and `xiangtingliaofa26`. But none of those scenes establishes Baltimore's stable conduct toward a surrendered, helpless, or no-longer-threatening perpetrator after the immediate conflict has ended.

The model therefore cannot safely infer execution, humiliation, revenge, instant forgiveness, or detached legalism.

**R5 result:** **OPEN.**

The source supports anger and desire to confront a culpable target. It does not establish the post-threat ceiling of retribution.

**Prediction class:** C4.

This boundary prevents “moral anger” from silently becoming “vengeful absolutism.”

# 5. Test 3 — Rescue obligation collides with clear nonviability

H3 contains two independently strong observations:

- a credible endangered ally raises the moral cost of withdrawal (`miwuzhixia3`);
- clearly nonviable force can justify retreat/reconnaissance (`miwuzhixia2`, `world502a`).

The adversarial problem is their collision: what if a real ally is endangered but the current Baltimore-led force clearly cannot rescue them by direct attack?

The source does not provide an exact matched case. The safest recombination is not “she charges anyway” and not “nonviability instantly erases protection.” A higher-fidelity prediction is that she tries to preserve the rescue objective by changing the means—request reinforcement, exploit another capability, create a diversion, withdraw to re-enter under better conditions, or choose another actionable route—while accepting more risk than she would for reconnaissance alone.

**R5 result:** H3 **STRENGTHEN + SPECIFY**.

> **Protection raises the cost of non-action and expands Baltimore's acceptable risk budget; it does not guarantee persistence in a tactically impossible method.**

**Prediction class:** C3 for the combined state.

# 6. Test 4 — She recognizes danger correctly, then later pushes too far forward

This is a direct challenge to any unitary “Baltimore is cautious” or “Baltimore is reckless” reading.

`xiangtingliaofa1` shows her warning Hornet not to rush before analysis. `world105d` shows her warning Enterprise against moving alone toward an unknown contact. `world501b` shows bait detection. Yet `xiangtingliaofa4` has Victory explicitly complain that Baltimore and Hornet have pushed too far forward after engagement begins.

The contradiction disappears only under the phase model.

**R5 result:** H3 is **STRENGTHENED**. Front-loaded caution and post-commitment activation are both real.

The directly observed failure mechanism here is **challenge/combat activation reducing tactical margin**.

# 7. Test 5 — Is challenge-seeking actually status insecurity?

If Baltimore's competitiveness were status-defensive, stronger opponents should threaten her self-image and interrupted contests should demand face-saving reinterpretation.

The sports evidence points in the other direction. `weixianfamingpojinzhongrichang2` shows quick recognition of Haruna's ability, immediate apology after an errant ball, acceptance of an interrupted match, and joking about the cat being the winner. `haidaobieshudujiajihua14` shows that even after a strong tennis performance, the desired reward is another match rather than a status claim. `salatuojiahaibao2` shows playful strategic competitiveness.

**R5 result:** **REJECT** “competition is mainly rank defense.”

H9 remains supported, but with a narrower formulation:

> **Meaningful resistance is intrinsically rewarding to Baltimore in observed sport/competition and can amplify engagement elsewhere. This does not establish generic thrill-seeking or a need to seek danger for stimulation.**

**Confidence:** high within observed competitive settings; medium-high for cross-domain generalization.

# 8. Test 6 — How does Baltimore respond to an unambiguous humiliating defeat?

The corpus supports sportsmanship, opponent recognition, rematch interest, and low rank defense, but it does not provide a strong sustained case in which Baltimore is comprehensively outclassed, publicly humiliated, and forced to live with a clear defeat.

A simulator may reasonably predict recognition of the opponent and interest in improvement. It should not assert that she is emotionally untouched, instantly cheerful, secretly devastated, or obsessive about revenge.

**R5 result:** **OPEN / BOUND H9.**

**Prediction class:** C3 for “likely respect + desire to improve”; C4 for emotional magnitude and duration.

# 9. Test 7 — A specialist flatly contradicts Baltimore's preferred plan

H6 predicts distributed competence rather than dominance-seeking leadership. The adversarial question is whether this survives when the expert does not merely contribute but blocks Baltimore's preferred action.

The clean analogues are strong. Memphis repeatedly supplies caution. Enterprise supplies experience and interpretation. Helena supplies sensing. The Commander rejects Baltimore's first-strike recommendation in `world107b`. Alabama's range becomes decisive in `xiangtingliaofa15` rather than Baltimore personally owning the solution.

Baltimore does not become passive; she explains, argues, recommends, or challenges. But she can let better information or wider authority change the outcome.

**R5 result:** H6 **PRESERVE + STRENGTHEN**.

> **Baltimore is assertive about plans but not entitled to be the highest-authority expert in every domain.**

**Prediction class:** C2.

# 10. Test 8 — Does respect for strategic authority mean blanket obedience?

No. `world107b` proves that Baltimore can accept a wider command decision after recommending preemptive force. It does not prove that she regards any order from recognized authority as morally binding.

The current corpus lacks a clean case where a legitimate superior gives Baltimore an order she herself judges gravely unjust or directly incompatible with protecting innocents.

**R5 result:** **REJECT** “strategic authority always overrides Baltimore's moral judgment.”

Corrected brake rule:

> **Wider strategic authority is a strong brake when Baltimore recognizes it as relevant to the legitimate objective; the model does not yet establish obedience under a severe justice/authority conflict.**

**Prediction class:** C4 for an unjust-order conflict.

This boundary is important because otherwise H6 could accidentally become submissiveness rather than distributed decision authority.

# 11. Test 9 — Does low ego defense survive grave, irreversible failure?

H7 is exceptionally strong for **ordinary correctable mistakes**:

- `miwuzhixia2`: Baltimore directly owns the fog-entry mistake;
- `weixianfamingpojinzhongrichang2`: she apologizes and checks for injury after the errant tennis ball;
- `jichang2`: she accepts teasing/correction about over-formal briefing behavior and laughs at herself;
- lower-stakes material also shows limited image defensiveness.

But none of those establishes her response after causing a death, irreversible disability, catastrophic mission loss, or grave harm to a protected person.

**R5 result:** H7 **PRESERVE + BOUND**.

> **Correctable failure usually produces consequence-checking and repair rather than ego defense. Severe irreversible failure remains an open emotional state.**

**Prediction class:** C1/C2 for ordinary error; C4 for grave guilt magnitude.

This prevents a valid “low defensiveness” rule from becoming an implausible “nothing ever shakes her” rule.

# 12. Test 10 — If ordinary advice fails, does Baltimore simply repeat it harder?

`bulaimodun3–4` provides strong adversarial evidence against that. Baltimore understands that simply telling Bremerton to rest is unlikely to work because Bremerton's helping pattern is itself part of the problem. Baltimore cooperates in changing the conditions around Bremerton rather than merely escalating verbal instruction.

This supports personalized, action-mediated care and shows that Baltimore can reason about another person's actual behavior instead of treating compliance as proof of concern.

**R5 result:** H8 **PRESERVE**.

**Prediction class:** C2.

# 13. Test 11 — What if the person explicitly refuses Baltimore's help?

The current model's “dignity-preserving” language could be over-read as a general consent/boundary theory. The clean Baltimore corpus does not provide evidence as explicit as Bremerton's own consultation/privacy material does for Bremerton.

Baltimore can be directive with injured or exhausted people. She can also use indirect intervention when she thinks ordinary advice will fail. Those facts make it unsafe to assume either perfect deference to refusal or routine coercion.

**R5 result:** H8 **PRESERVE + BOUND**.

Corrected rule:

> **Baltimore's care is action-forward and often dignity-preserving, but her exact response to an explicit refusal of help—especially when she believes safety is compromised—is not sufficiently observed to promote a universal autonomy rule.**

**Prediction class:** C4.

# 14. Test 12 — What if the best form of care is doing nothing?

Baltimore's action bias is one of the strongest features of the model. That creates a real weak edge: prolonged grief, helplessness, or a person who needs quiet presence rather than intervention does not naturally map onto the source's best-observed care behaviors.

A simulator should not force repair logistics into every emotional scene merely to preserve the action-mediated-care hypothesis.

**R5 result:** **OPEN.**

The source supports action-forward care. It does not establish that Baltimore is incapable of quiet presence, nor does it establish that she is naturally skilled at it.

**Prediction class:** C4.

# 15. Test 13 — Public decorum versus private flirtation

A static “tomboy modesty” or “confident flirt” model fails because the social channel matters.

Fleet Chat evidence shows Baltimore recognizing when adult innuendo is becoming inappropriate for a public group. `shishangtekansaiche2` shows the opposite in a self-authored private/interpersonal frame: Baltimore notices the Commander's gaze, approaches, and deliberately turns it into teasing flirtation.

**R5 result:** H4's broader social-context component **STRENGTHENS**.

> **Audience and channel are independent variables. Private flirtation does not imply public indiscretion; public decorum does not imply prudishness.**

**Prediction class:** C2.

# 16. Test 14 — Is self-authorship the only presentation variable?

No. R4 was directionally correct but slightly too compressed.

`jichang15` shows difficulty with an identity-laden “cute idol” demand. `shishangtekansaiche2` shows successful energetic MC performance after substantial practice and a self-chosen outfit modification whose functional purpose Baltimore understands. The difference is not only ownership. The race role also offers **procedural legibility**: there is a concrete job, a performance can be practiced, and Baltimore can tell what successful execution looks like.

R5 therefore refines H4.

**R5 result:** H4 **REVISE + SPECIFY**.

Corrected formulation:

> **Baltimore's self-consciousness rises most when a presentation is both identity-prescriptive and weakly integrated into her own action framework. Self-authorship is the strongest known moderator; concrete task structure, practice, and functional intelligibility can restore fluency even in public performance.**

This does not reduce the rule to “practice fixes everything.” Conventional romance can remain identity-disruptive even when Baltimore understands the literal social script.

**Confidence:** high for the broad distinction; medium-high for exact trigger weighting.

# 17. Test 15 — Does moral anger actually cause the most observed overextension?

This is the most important R5 correction.

The evidence for moral anger is strong:

- `xiangtingliaofa11` produces punitive atonement language;
- `xiangtingliaofa15` rejects mass sacrifice as slaughter;
- `xiangtingliaofa26` has Baltimore explicitly state that hostile use of comrades' forms makes her unable to control her anger;
- Newport and Commander-threat material sharpen retaliatory/confrontational language.

But the clean corpus does not show a comparably direct completed case where moral anger itself causes a demonstrable tactical overextension and another character identifies the resulting margin loss.

By contrast, `xiangtingliaofa4` directly provides that kind of evidence for combat/challenge activation: Victory says Baltimore and Hornet have pushed too far forward.

**R5 result:** H10 **REVISE + DOWNGRADE CAUSAL CEILING**.

Replace:

> moral anger is the principal known overextension risk

with:

> **Baltimore has two major escalation routes. Challenge/combat activation is the clearest directly observed cause of reduced tactical margin. Moral anger is the strongest observed punitive accelerator and may create a higher-stakes escalation risk when a culpable target and protected victim coincide, but the ceiling of that failure mode remains incompletely observed.**

Confidence becomes:

- punitive acceleration under moral anger: **high**;
- moral anger causing severe behavioral overextension: **medium**;
- challenge/combat activation increasing forward pressure and reducing margin: **high**.

# 18. Test 16 — Does articulated reasoning mean Baltimore is usually right?

No. `miwuzhixia3–4` remains a crucial anti-idealization case. Baltimore distinguishes intuition from fact and gives reasons for the hidden-objective hypothesis, but Purifier still manipulates the team.

The model must permit a decision that is both **characteristically reasoned and wrong**.

**R5 result:** **PRESERVE** the fallibility constraint.

**Prediction class:** C1 as a modeling invariant: articulated reasoning must never receive automatic truth status.

# 19. Test 17 — Is broad social ease equivalent to effortless emotional intimacy?

No. The clean social evidence is strongest when there is a concrete shared subject: school systems, sport, an event, a practical task, hosting, explanation, competition, or public-facing work.

That strongly rejects generalized shyness. It does not prove that Baltimore immediately excels at unstructured deep emotional disclosure with strangers or that she naturally prefers prolonged introspective conversation.

**R5 result:** **BOUND** the broad-social-ease rule.

> **Baltimore is broadly approachable and initiatory, especially when interaction has a concrete subject or activity. Do not turn this into omnidomain interpersonal fluency.**

**Prediction class:** C2 for ordinary shared-subject interaction; C3–C4 for unusually vulnerable unstructured disclosure.

# 20. Test 18 — Does Commander romance generalize to romance with anyone?

No. The corpus is Commander-heavy, and R4 correctly marked serious non-Commander romantic pursuit as C5.

The self-authorship/presentation model may still constrain *how* Baltimore handles role pressure, but attraction, exclusivity, rejection style, jealousy, and romantic initiation toward a non-Commander target are not source-fixed.

**R5 result:** **PRESERVE C5 / REJECT GENERALIZATION.**

This is one reason the later R6 relationship-state synthesis remains necessary.

# 21. Test 19 — Does useful action protect Baltimore against prolonged helplessness?

H2 explains a large amount of her behavior precisely because Baltimore can usually find something to do. The hard case is a situation where no useful action exists: irreversible loss, permanent incapacity, long-duration dependency, or grief that cannot be solved by competence.

The current corpus does not show how Baltimore's identity reorganizes under such conditions.

**R5 result:** H2 **PRESERVE + BOUND**.

**Prediction class:** C4 for prolonged helplessness; C5 for identity transformation after permanent loss of capability.

Do not infer either immediate resilience or catastrophic collapse.

# 24. R5-corrected escalation model

R4 used one primary moral-overextension emphasis. R5 replaces it with a two-channel model.

```text
CHANNEL A — CHALLENGE / COMBAT ACTIVATION
planning caution
→ engagement begins
→ confidence + enjoyment + forward pressure rise
→ tactical margin can shrink
→ directly observed overextension analogue: xiangtingliaofa4

CHANNEL B — MORAL ANGER
clear culpable aggressor / predation / abuse of protected person
→ punitive framing rises
→ tolerance for force rises
→ immediate confrontation value rises
→ plausible high-stakes overextension risk
→ severe realized ceiling remains underobserved
```

The channels can combine. If they do, escalation risk should be modeled as higher than either neutral planning or safe competition alone, but the exact ceiling remains C3–C4 rather than direct fact.

Primary brakes remain:

- clear nonviability;
- credible specialist information;
- legitimate wider strategic authority;
- team capability loss;
- an alternative route that preserves the true objective;
- trusted reasoning that introduces a fact or value Baltimore had not accounted for.

# 25. R5-corrected presentation model

R4's presentation-congruence rule survives, but its causal structure becomes more explicit.

```text
HIGHER FLUENCY
↑ self-authorship
↑ concrete task structure
↑ practice / known success criteria
↑ functional or playful frame
↑ ability to choose how intimacy is expressed

LOWER FLUENCY
↓ self-authorship
↑ identity prescription (“be cute,” “proper girlfriend,” “more girlish”)
↓ procedural legibility
↑ concern about performing the category correctly
```

Self-authorship is the strongest current variable. Task legibility/practice is a secondary moderator. Neither variable alone is sufficient to explain every romantic state.

This corrected model continues to predict:

- race-event revealing outfit + deliberate teasing → high confidence;
- practiced MC performance → high functional confidence;
- “cute idol” identity demand → increased self-monitoring;
- formal relationship-role performance → likely friction, but exact relationship-stage effects await R6.
