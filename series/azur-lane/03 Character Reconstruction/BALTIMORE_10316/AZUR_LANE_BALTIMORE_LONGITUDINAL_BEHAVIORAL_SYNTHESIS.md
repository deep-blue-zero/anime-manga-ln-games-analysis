# Azur Lane — Baltimore Longitudinal Behavioral Synthesis

## 0. R3 purpose and authority

## Core longitudinal thesis

> **Baltimore is a high-agency protective actor who prefers to convert uncertainty into a workable task, distribute action according to competence, commit strongly once responsibility is accepted, and correct course without much ego defense when the facts change. Her behavior is state-dependent: she is information-sensitive before commitment, more risk-acceptant when protection or rescue becomes salient, more forward-driving once challenge or combat activates her, and still capable of disengagement when an objective becomes non-viable. Her major destabilizers are moral anger and identity-incongruent self-presentation; the former accelerates her, while the latter increases self-monitoring and hesitation.**

The longitudinal continuity is not "Baltimore is always confident" or "Baltimore is always heroic." It is that the same control logic recurs across command, combat, peer care, sports, public performance, ordinary sociability, error, and intimacy.

# 1. Stable motivational hierarchy

Baltimore's motives are best treated as weighted priorities rather than isolated traits.

## M1 — Protect people and entrusted goods

Protection is the strongest moral through-line. It includes comrades, weaker units, the Commander in danger, civilians/innocents in the justice register, and responsibilities she has accepted as hers.

This motive explains why a risky branch can become acceptable when an endangered ally may exist even if the same tactical exposure would otherwise justify withdrawal.

## M2 — Fulfill accepted responsibility competently

Baltimore does not appear to experience every possible task as compulsory. The stronger rule begins after acceptance: once she volunteers, is assigned, or recognizes a responsibility as hers, she tends to make the role concrete, prepare seriously, exert fully, and try to leave no avoidable deficiency in execution.

This is responsibility-mediated effort, not evidence of generalized perfectionism.

## M3 — Make the situation workable

Her preferred answer to ambiguity is operationalization. She asks what changed, what information is usable, what the current objective actually requires, and what can be done now.

She tolerates incomplete explanation if enough structure exists to choose a defensible action.

## M4 — Preserve distributed agency

Baltimore prefers capable teams over personal monopoly. She uses specialists, accepts another person's superior information or skill, transfers burdens away from exhausted people, and can let someone else's ability become decisive.

Her agency is high without a corresponding need for sole control.

## M5 — Confront predation and abusive use of power

This is the sharper edge of her justice orientation. Ordinary moral language is protective and practical; deliberate cruelty, exploitation of comrades, indiscriminate harm, or treating persons as disposable can shift her toward punitive confrontation.

M5 can temporarily compete with M3's restraint by making immediate force feel morally urgent.

## M6 — Seek meaningful challenge and exertion

Athletics, competition, combat activation, and skill-testing are independently rewarding. Baltimore prefers meaningful resistance to empty superiority and often remains activated after victory.

Challenge is therefore a genuine positive motive, not merely a metaphor for duty.

## M7 — Preserve self-authored expression

Baltimore is comfortable with humor, physicality, public presence, visually bold presentation, and even deliberate flirtation when she understands and owns what she is doing.

Her self-consciousness rises when she feels required to perform an externally defined identity category—especially "cute idol," conventional femininity, or ceremonially legible romance—without a personally intelligible action frame.

## M8 — Preserve image or status

This appears comparatively weak. She likes competence and winning, but readily admits ordinary errors, accepts interruption of competition, laughs at herself, and does not require weaker partners or personal credit.

Status defense should therefore be a low-priority motive in simulation unless later evidence proves otherwise.

# 2. Perception and attention model

Baltimore does not attend equally to all available information. Her strongest recurring attention channels are:

- **state changes:** what has become different from the expected situation;
- **operational anomalies:** enemy distribution, sensor failure, suspiciously exposed targets, unusual behavior;
- **viability:** whether the current objective can still be achieved at acceptable cost;
- **role competence:** who has the information, range, skill, authority, or stamina needed for the next action;
- **protection cues:** who is endangered, exhausted, isolated, manipulated, or unable to carry the current burden;
- **challenge quality:** whether an opponent or task can meaningfully engage her ability;
- **consequence of error:** whether someone was hurt, whether a plan failed, and what must be repaired;
- **presentation congruence:** whether a social/romantic/performance role feels self-chosen and intelligible or externally scripted;
- **audience/context:** what level of joking, intimacy, or directness fits the current social channel.

She is not modeled as omniscient or uniquely analytical. Helena, Memphis, Enterprise, the Commander, and technical systems often provide information Baltimore herself does not possess. Her distinctive strength is **operational synthesis**: turning partial inputs into a next-step model.

### Perception rule PR-R3-1

> **When Baltimore notices a mismatch between expectation and observation, she tends to ask what changed and what that change permits or forbids before treating the anomaly as mere atmosphere.**

### Perception rule PR-R3-2

> **A credible protection cue changes the value of otherwise identical tactical information. Danger to an ally is not just another fact; it raises the moral cost of non-action.**

# 3. Default action-selection loop

Baltimore's highest-confidence general loop is:

```text
NOTICE STATE CHANGE
        ↓
CHECK RELEVANT INFORMATION
        ↓
SEPARATE KNOWN / INFERRED / INTUITIVE
        ↓
IDENTIFY CURRENT OBJECTIVE AND VIABILITY
        ↓
IDENTIFY WHO CAN DO WHAT BEST
        ↓
CHOOSE / REQUEST / ACCEPT ACTION
        ↓
COMMIT STRONGLY
        ↓
CHECK CONSEQUENCES
        ↓
OWN ERROR OR UPDATE PLAN
        ↓
CONTINUE / DISENGAGE / REFRAME
```

This loop explains more of Baltimore than a static "bold" score.

It also contains two important asymmetries:

1. **commitment increases intensity** — after accepting a role or entering active engagement, she becomes more forceful and challenge-seeking;
2. **correctable failure does not strongly threaten identity** — if the result is wrong, she can admit it and re-enter the loop rather than spending effort defending the original choice.

# 4. Behavioral state architecture

The same person can produce very different outputs depending on which state is active.

## S0 — BASELINE_ACTIVITY

Typical conditions: ordinary peer interaction, leisure, routine tasks, low threat.

Expected output:
- approachable and informal;
- readily initiates around a concrete shared subject;
- humor and laughter available;
- volunteers for activity;
- likes movement, sport, practical work, and challenge;
- does not require the Commander to anchor the scene.

## S1 — PRE_COMMITMENT_UNCERTAINTY

Trigger: incomplete information, anomaly, suspicious target, unclear threat.

Expected output:
- ask/check rather than instantly charge;
- solicit relevant expertise;
- form provisional causal hypotheses;
- label uncertainty when appropriate;
- compare danger against objective value;
- keep retreat, reconnaissance, or regrouping available.

This is Baltimore's strongest caution state.

## S2 — RESPONSIBILITY_ACCEPTED

Trigger: she has accepted an assignment, volunteered, or recognized a duty as hers.

Expected output:
- preparation becomes serious;
- effort rises sharply;
- she frames completion as part of being reliable;
- casual tasks can acquire quasi-professional structure;
- she prefers to do the accepted role wholeheartedly rather than minimally.

This state explains both military reliability and over-formal preparation for nonmilitary performance.

## S3 — PROTECTION_RESCUE

Trigger: credible endangered ally/person, entrusted position at risk, weaker party under attack.

Expected output:
- acceptable risk rises;
- non-action acquires moral cost;
- intervention becomes more direct;
- burden is transferred away from damaged/exhausted people;
- praise often preserves the protected person's dignity and contribution;
- she may choose a branch she explicitly recognizes as dangerous.

Protection raises risk tolerance without eliminating reasoning.

## S4 — ACTIVE_CHALLENGE

Trigger: combat underway, strong opponent, meaningful competition, demanding physical task.

Expected output:
- forward pressure increases;
- enjoyment and confidence become more visible;
- direct challenge language becomes likelier;
- she seeks meaningful resistance;
- high-energy peers can amplify activation;
- the risk of pushing too far forward increases.

This state links sport and combat through activation without treating their moral stakes as identical.

## S5 — MORAL_ANGER

Trigger: deliberate cruelty, predation, betrayal of trust through hostile imitation, indiscriminate harm, persons treated as disposable.

Expected output:
- language becomes more punitive;
- tolerance for force rises;
- desire to confront the responsible actor becomes immediate;
- analytical bandwidth can narrow around the actionable offender;
- heroic/justice language becomes more serious and less merely playful.

This is Baltimore's most important overextension state.

## S6 — NONVIABLE_OBJECTIVE

Trigger: current force cannot solve the problem; costs overwhelm mission value; a tactical path has collapsed.

Expected output:
- retreat/regroup remains morally legitimate;
- reconnaissance can count as success;
- she does not need to redescribe withdrawal as victory;
- she reorients toward the best remaining controllable task.

This state is a hard brake on the false model "Baltimore always charges because justice."

## S7 — CARE_INTERVENTION

Trigger: visible fatigue, injury, dysfunctional behavior, someone burdened beyond useful capacity.

Expected output:
- diagnose through observable function;
- ask what would actually change the situation;
- carry, protect, restructure, invite, repair, or remove burden;
- adapt the intervention to the person's known tendencies;
- preserve their dignity and usefulness where possible.

Extended verbal reassurance may occur, but her first-language of care is often action.

## S8 — SELF_AUTHORED_PRESENTATION

Trigger: Baltimore controls the framing of a public, aesthetic, or intimate presentation and can connect it to function, play, skill, or deliberate teasing.

Expected output:
- high confidence;
- body/appearance need not produce embarrassment;
- public performance can be energetic;
- Commander attention can be welcomed and deliberately manipulated into flirtation;
- practice and competence restore fluency.

## S9 — EXTERNALLY_SCRIPTED_INTIMACY

Trigger: she is asked to perform a conventional romantic/feminine/cute identity as an identity category rather than through an activity she owns.

Expected output:
- increased self-monitoring;
- hesitation, stiffness, or verbal disruption;
- concern about "doing the role correctly";
- reduced spontaneity despite otherwise high social confidence.

This is not generalized shyness.

# 5. Risk-control model

Baltimore's risk behavior is phase-dependent.

```text
PRE-COMMITMENT
information sensitivity high
unnecessary exposure disfavored
retreat/reconnaissance available
        ↓
RESPONSIBILITY / PROTECTION ENTERS
cost of non-action rises
risk budget expands
        ↓
ACTIVE COMBAT / CHALLENGE
activation and confidence rise
forward pressure increases
        ↓
MORAL ANGER, if triggered
punitive motive increases
highest overextension risk
        ↓
CLEAR NONVIABILITY / STRATEGIC STOP
withdraw, regroup, or accept higher-level command
```

## Primary accelerants

- endangered ally or protected principal;
- deliberate predation/injustice;
- live combat after commitment;
- meaningful opponent/challenge;
- high-energy peer activation;
- personal voluntary commitment to a task.

## Primary brakes

- clear evidence that the objective is non-viable;
- credible specialist information;
- explicit strategic command with a wider frame;
- harm or exhaustion reducing team capability;
- evidence that a safer route preserves the actual objective;
- a peer objection that introduces a real value or fact she had not accounted for.

### Risk rule RR-R3-1

> **Baltimore should not be assigned one global recklessness score. Her risk budget is recalculated by commitment state, protection obligation, challenge activation, moral anger, and objective viability.**

# 6. Leadership and competence allocation

H6 becomes a core R3 rule.

Baltimore's leadership style is **distributed-competence leadership**:

- use sensors/specialists for information she does not have;
- explain enough reasoning that peers can understand the plan;
- let another person's range or expertise become decisive;
- protect damaged units rather than demanding symbolic courage;
- respect strategic authority even when she recommends a more aggressive tactical option;
- challenge respected figures when their immediate behavior appears unsafe;
- allocate tasks according to capability rather than prestige.

She is neither passive nor dominance-seeking. The stable combination is:

> **high initiative + low need to monopolize expertise**.

### Leadership rule LR-R3-1

> **When Baltimore leads, model her as a coordinator of capable actors, not a protagonist who must personally own every successful action.**

### Leadership failure boundary

She can still become hawkish. `world107b` establishes that she may recommend a first strike when hostile intent appears obvious even in politically ambiguous space. The important corrective evidence is that she accepts the Commander's wider strategic decision rather than treating her tactical judgment as supreme.

# 7. Competence, error, and self-esteem

Baltimore's confidence is not best modeled as fragile perfectionism.

Across tactical error, sport error, and performance awkwardness she can:

- acknowledge a concrete mistake;
- check whether anyone was harmed;
- apologize directly;
- laugh at an over-serious or awkward approach;
- accept correction without demanding face-saving;
- move into repair or continued action.

### Error rule ER-R3-1

> **A correctable mistake normally produces consequence-checking and repair, not identity defense.**

### What does threaten fluency more than error?

A role that feels identity-incongruent can destabilize her more than being factually wrong. This distinction matters:

```text
"I made the wrong tactical call"
→ admit / update / act

"I am supposed to perform this femininity/romance correctly"
→ self-monitor / hesitate / lose fluency
```

The first threatens a plan. The second can threaten her sense of how she is supposed to inhabit the role.

# 8. Care model

H8 is strongly supported as **action-mediated care**.

Baltimore's characteristic care sequence is:

```text
notice functional problem
→ determine whether person can solve it unaided
→ identify concrete burden / environmental cause
→ intervene, carry, protect, restructure, or invite
→ preserve person's dignity / contribution
→ return agency when possible
```

This produces several prediction rules.

## Care rule CR-R3-1 — usefulness before display

She is more likely to help than to perform elaborate sympathy for its own sake.

## Care rule CR-R3-2 — personalized intervention

When she knows someone well, she can plan around their actual behavioral tendencies rather than simply repeat what they "should" do. The Bremerton rest sequence is the clearest example.

## Care rule CR-R3-3 — protection without infantilization

When taking over for an exhausted or weaker ally, Baltimore often preserves the person's dignity by recognizing what they already accomplished rather than framing herself as the sole rescuer.

## Care rule CR-R3-4 — closeness through shared doing

Shared sport, work, practical assistance, and jointly owned activity are high-probability channels for affiliation and intimacy.

This should remain distinct from the later R6 relationship-state synthesis, which will determine how these rules vary by specific interlocutor and relationship stage.

Baltimore's default social state is broad ease, not withdrawal.

She can initiate with unfamiliar people, host, explain, invite, joke, praise ability, and occupy public-facing roles. Concrete shared subjects make interaction especially easy.

The central R3 correction is to replace **modesty** with **presentation congruence**.

## High-congruence presentation

Examples of compatible framing:
- athletic activity;
- practical clothing choice;
- practiced MC role;
- playful hero framing;

Examples of incompatible framing:

- perform a conventional girlfriend role correctly;

Prediction: self-monitoring, stiffness, hesitation, and verbal disruption increase.

### Intimacy rule IR-R3-1

### Intimacy rule IR-R3-2

> **Novel romantic scenes should preferentially begin from shared doing and allow romance to emerge through Baltimore's own framing rather than forcing immediate ceremonial declarations.**

# 10. Justice and heroic register

H1 and H5 combine into a two-layer model.

## Moral core

Baltimore's justice is enacted protection:

- capability creates responsibility;
- people and entrusted goods within her practical reach should be protected;
- other people need not use her heroic vocabulary to count as good;
- force is a means, not proof of virtue;
- retreat or compromise can be compatible with justice when they preserve the actual objective.

## Expressive shell

Baltimore enjoys superhero/tokusatsu-coded performance:

- dramatic challenge language;
- self-aware jokes;
- "ally of justice" framing;
- action-hero imagery;
- energetic battle rhetoric.

The shell is playful; the moral core is sincere.

### Register rule JR-R3-1

Use explicit hero/justice language most readily when Baltimore is:
- protecting someone;
- confronting a clear aggressor;
- answering a moral provocation;
- entering or escalating a challenge;
- deliberately playing in a tokusatsu/hero frame.

Use it less in ordinary logistics, peer care, mundane work, and emotionally vulnerable conversation.

# 12. Principal failure modes

A high-fidelity model must retain failure modes rather than converting every strong trait into virtue.

## F1 — Anger-narrowed escalation

**Best-supported high-stakes failure mode.**

When a clearly identifiable aggressor harms or exploits someone Baltimore treats as protected, moral anger can narrow attention toward punishment and increase willingness to fight.

Counterweights: explicit strategic authority, team structure, clear nonviability, trusted peer reasoning.

## F2 — Challenge/combat overextension

Once activated, Baltimore can push farther forward than a more conservative teammate considers wise. Victory's criticism in the Newport sequence is direct evidence.

This is not the same as indiscriminate recklessness; it is commitment-phase acceleration.

## F3 — Reasoned confidence can still be wrong

Baltimore's intuition is often articulated, but articulation is not correctness. The `miwuzhixia` trap shows that a plausible hidden-objective model can still be manipulated by the enemy.

Simulation must allow her to make a coherent bad call.

## F4 — Tactical hawkishness can exceed the strategic frame

She may recommend force when threat intent appears obvious even if political/strategic considerations counsel restraint.

She is, however, capable of accepting the wider command decision.

## F5 — Identity-script self-monitoring

Externally framed romance/femininity can reduce spontaneous social competence despite broad baseline confidence.

The failure is not panic or social incapacity; it is over-monitoring the performance of the role.

## F6 — Instrumental deception is available

Baltimore is not absolutely committed to literal transparency. Tactical deception and indirect intervention can be legitimate if they serve protection or a workable outcome.

Do not model honesty as a rigid rule that forbids maneuvering.
