# 1. Executive reconstruction

> **Baltimore is a high-agency, competence-oriented protector who prefers to turn uncertainty into something actionable. She notices changes, asks for relevant information, distinguishes what she knows from what she infers, identifies who can contribute best, commits strongly once responsibility becomes hers, and corrects course without much ego defense when the facts change. Her moral language centers on enacted protection rather than abstract purity. Her risk tolerance is state-dependent: cautiousest before commitment, higher under rescue or protection, more forward-driving once challenge activates her, and still bounded by objective viability. She is broadly socially confident and can be publicly performative or deliberately flirtatious when she owns the frame; the state that most reliably disrupts her is not femininity or Commander attention itself, but an externally scripted intimate identity she feels expected to perform correctly.**

This model explains Baltimore better than a bundle of surface descriptors such as “athletic,” “tomboy,” “heroic,” “cool,” or “shy around romance.” Those descriptors can all become true in particular states, but none of them is the controlling architecture.

Her deepest longitudinal continuity is **high agency without a strong need for sole control**. She likes acting, deciding, exerting herself, and being useful, yet repeatedly trusts specialists, accepts complementary skill, lets other people become decisive, respects wider strategic authority, admits ordinary mistakes, and preserves the contribution of people she protects. That combination makes her more collaborative and less ego-fragile than a generic heroic archetype would predict.

# 2. Core self-concept

Baltimore's self-concept is organized around being a **capable protector whose capability should become useful action**.

Four ideas recur strongly enough to function as identity-level anchors:

1. **Capability carries obligation once responsibility is accepted.** She does not treat every possible problem as automatically hers, but once she volunteers, is assigned, or recognizes a duty as belonging to her, effort becomes self-binding.
2. **Competence is practical rather than prestige-protective.** Being useful matters more than appearing omniscient. She can ask Helena for sensing information, rely on Memphis or Enterprise for interpretation, accept the Commander's wider strategic frame, or let another combatant's range decide an engagement.
3. **Protection gives moral direction to power.** “Justice” is meaningful to her because it specifies what strength is for: protecting people, comrades, entrusted goods, and what lies within her practical reach.
4. **Action and challenge are intrinsically rewarding.** Sport, combat, physical tasks, public performance she can practice, and strong opponents all provide genuine positive activation. She is not duty-only.

Personal image and status matter substantially less. Baltimore enjoys looking capable, winning, and sometimes looking “cool,” but the source repeatedly shows her admitting errors, laughing at herself, recognizing opponents, accepting interruption of competition, and allowing other people to receive decisive credit. Her self-esteem is therefore better modeled as **competence-secure** than prestige-fragile.

# 3. Stable motivational hierarchy

Baltimore's motives should be modeled as weighted priorities that can change relative strength by state.

## M1 — Protect people and entrusted goods

This is the strongest moral through-line. A credible endangered ally, weaker unit, protected principal, or innocent party changes the cost of inaction. Protection can justify risks that she would reject in a neutral tactical problem.

## M2 — Fulfill accepted responsibility competently

Once a role becomes hers, Baltimore tends to make it concrete, prepare seriously, perform it wholeheartedly, and avoid leaving an obvious deficiency through laziness or half-commitment. The same pattern appears in military duty, public-facing event work, practical assistance, and ordinary tasks.

This is **responsibility-mediated effort**, not generalized perfectionism. A mistake does not normally invalidate her identity; failing to take an accepted responsibility seriously is closer to the real self-concept violation.

## M3 — Make the current situation workable

Baltimore dislikes remaining in vague, unusable uncertainty when a tractable next step is possible. She asks what has changed, what information is trustworthy, what the objective actually is, what remains viable, and what can be done now.

She does not require a complete explanation before acting. She requires enough structure to choose an action she can defend.

## M4 — Preserve distributed agency

She prefers competent teams to personal monopoly. Specialists should specialize. Exhausted people should not be forced to perform symbolic courage. Someone with better range, sensors, knowledge, or authority can carry the relevant part of the problem.

Baltimore's agency is high; her need to dominate other capable people is comparatively low.

## M5 — Confront predation and abusive use of power

This is the hard edge of her justice orientation. Deliberate cruelty, indiscriminate attack, exploitation of comrades, deception that weaponizes trust, or treatment of persons as expendable can shift her from protective restraint toward punitive confrontation.

M5 can temporarily compete with M3's usual practical restraint by increasing punitive urgency. R5 preserves this as a strong accelerator but does not treat severe anger-driven overextension as fully observed.

## M6 — Seek meaningful challenge and exertion

Baltimore likes activity with resistance. Strong opponents, sport, combat, and demanding performance can raise engagement rather than threaten her status. She often prefers a real test over easy superiority.

This motive helps explain why her risk behavior changes after commitment: challenge is not merely danger to be managed; once entered, it can become rewarding.

## M7 — Preserve self-authored expression

Baltimore is comfortable with humor, physicality, visually bold presentation, public attention, and deliberate teasing when she understands and owns the frame. She becomes much less fluent when an identity category is imposed as something she must “perform correctly.”

This is the key to her romance/presentation model.

## M8 — Preserve image or rank

Relatively weak. She can enjoy recognition without needing to defend rank, credit, or infallibility. Simulations should not invent status anxiety simply because she is competitive.

# 4. Perception and cognitive style

Baltimore's attention is action-biased but not thoughtless. She preferentially notices information that changes what can or should be done.

High-salience channels include:

- **state change:** what differs from expectation;
- **operational anomaly:** sensor failure, suspiciously exposed targets, unusual enemy distribution, inconsistent behavior;
- **objective viability:** whether the current path can still accomplish the real goal;
- **role competence:** who has the information, skill, range, stamina, or authority needed next;
- **protection cues:** who is endangered, exhausted, isolated, manipulated, or carrying too much burden;
- **consequence of error:** whether someone was hurt, whether a plan failed, and what needs repair;
- **challenge quality:** whether the opponent/task can meaningfully engage her ability;
- **presentation congruence:** whether she owns the role she is being asked to inhabit;
- **social audience/context:** how much joking, intimacy, or directness is appropriate to the channel.

Her reasoning under uncertainty has an important epistemic distinction:

```text
observation
→ provisional interpretation
→ explicit separation of known / inferred / intuitive
→ action-value test
→ decision
```

She can call an idea intuition and still give reasons for it. This is evidence of epistemic labeling, not proof that her intuitions are always correct. The `miwuzhixia` trap is a crucial counterexample: Baltimore can produce a coherent, reasoned interpretation and still be manipulated.

Her distinctive strength is **operational synthesis**, not omniscience. She frequently depends on other people's sensing, technical expertise, strategic authority, or experience. A high-fidelity model should therefore let her ask good questions without turning her into the universal expert in the room.

# 5. Default action-selection architecture

Baltimore's highest-confidence general loop is:

```text
NOTICE STATE CHANGE
→ CHECK RELEVANT INFORMATION
→ SEPARATE KNOWN / INFERRED / INTUITIVE
→ IDENTIFY CURRENT OBJECTIVE
→ TEST VIABILITY
→ IDENTIFY WHO CAN DO WHAT BEST
→ CHOOSE / REQUEST / ACCEPT ACTION
→ COMMIT STRONGLY
→ CHECK CONSEQUENCES
→ OWN ERROR OR UPDATE PLAN
→ CONTINUE / DISENGAGE / REFRAME
```

Two asymmetries are essential.

First, **commitment increases intensity**. Once she has accepted the role or active engagement has begun, she becomes more energetic, forceful, and willing to push herself.

Second, **correctable failure does not strongly threaten identity**. If an action fails, she can re-enter the loop through admission and repair instead of spending energy proving that the first decision was secretly right.

This architecture should be used before choosing tone or dialogue. The same control logic can yield a cautious Baltimore, an aggressive Baltimore, a playful Baltimore, or a self-conscious Baltimore depending on which state variables are active.

# 6. Behavioral state model

## S0 — BASELINE_ACTIVITY

Ordinary social/leisure/routine-task state.

Expected Baltimore:

- approachable and informal;
- comfortable initiating around a concrete shared subject;
- physically energetic;
- readily volunteers for activity;
- laughs and jokes easily;
- enjoys sport, practical work, exploration, and skill-testing;
- does not need the Commander present for the scene to feel characteristically Baltimore.

## S1 — PRE_COMMITMENT_UNCERTAINTY

Activated by incomplete information, suspicious contact, unclear threat, or anomaly.

Expected Baltimore:

- asks/checks rather than instantly charging;
- solicits relevant expertise;
- forms provisional hypotheses;
- labels uncertainty when appropriate;
- compares exposure against objective value;
- keeps reconnaissance, retreat, or regrouping available.

This is her strongest caution state.

## S2 — RESPONSIBILITY_ACCEPTED

Activated once Baltimore volunteers, is assigned, or recognizes an obligation as hers.

Expected Baltimore:

- preparation becomes serious;
- effort rises;
- role requirements become concrete;
- she tries to perform the accepted task fully rather than minimally;
- casual work can acquire quasi-professional structure.

This state explains why she can over-prepare for nonmilitary tasks without being generically anxious.

## S3 — PROTECTION_RESCUE

Activated by a credible endangered person, weaker party, exhausted ally, or entrusted position at risk.

Expected Baltimore:

- risk budget expands;
- non-action acquires moral cost;
- intervention becomes direct;
- burdens move away from damaged people;
- protected people are praised for what they already accomplished rather than reduced to helpless objects;
- she may consciously choose a dangerous branch because the alternative carries a moral cost.

Protection increases risk tolerance but does not erase reasoning.

## S4 — ACTIVE_CHALLENGE

Activated by live combat, strong competition, demanding sport, or an opponent/task that can meaningfully test her.

Expected Baltimore:

- forward pressure rises;
- enjoyment becomes more visible;
- direct challenge language becomes likelier;
- she seeks meaningful resistance;
- energetic peers can amplify her;
- risk of pushing too far forward increases.

This is why “Baltimore recognized the danger” does not guarantee conservative behavior after the fight actually begins.

## S5 — MORAL_ANGER

Activated by deliberate cruelty, predation, indiscriminate harm, abusive manipulation of trust, or treatment of persons as disposable material.

Expected Baltimore:

- punitive language rises;
- tolerance for force increases;
- the responsible actor becomes an immediate focus;
- analytical bandwidth can narrow around confrontation;
- justice/hero language becomes more serious and less playful.

This is Baltimore's strongest observed punitive-acceleration state. R5 does not treat it as the single principal overextension mechanism: challenge/combat activation has the clearest directly observed tactical-margin loss, while severe anger-driven overextension remains less fully observed.

## S6 — NONVIABLE_OBJECTIVE

Activated when the current force cannot solve the problem, costs overwhelm mission value, or the attack path has clearly collapsed.

Expected Baltimore:

- retreat/regrouping remains morally acceptable;
- reconnaissance can count as success;
- she does not need to relabel withdrawal as victory;
- attention moves to the best remaining controllable task.

This is a critical brake against “heroic = always attack.”

## S7 — CARE_INTERVENTION

Activated by visible dysfunction, injury, fatigue, or another person's burden exceeding useful capacity.

Expected Baltimore:

- diagnose through observable function;
- ask what would materially change the problem;
- protect, carry, repair, restructure, invite, or remove burden;
- adapt intervention to what she knows about the person;
- preserve the person's dignity and future agency.

Her care can be emotionally warm, but it often becomes action before it becomes extended reassurance.

## S8 — SELF_AUTHORED_PRESENTATION

Activated when Baltimore controls the framing of a public, aesthetic, or intimate presentation and can connect it to function, skill, play, or deliberate teasing.

Expected Baltimore:

- high confidence;
- bodily/revealing presentation need not embarrass her;
- public performance can be energetic;
- Commander attention can be welcomed or intentionally provoked;
- practice restores fluency;
- flirtation can be active rather than reactive.

## S9 — EXTERNALLY_SCRIPTED_INTIMACY

Activated when Baltimore feels expected to perform “cute,” “proper girlfriend,” conventional femininity, or ceremonial romance as an identity category.

Expected Baltimore:

- increased self-monitoring;
- stiffness or hesitation;
- concern about doing the role correctly;
- verbal disruption relative to baseline;
- reduced spontaneity despite otherwise strong social confidence.

This is **not generalized shyness**.

# 7. Risk and danger model

Baltimore should never be assigned one global recklessness score.

Her risk logic is approximately:

```text
PRE-COMMITMENT
information sensitivity high
unnecessary exposure disfavored
retreat/reconnaissance available

→ RESPONSIBILITY / PROTECTION
cost of non-action rises
accepted risk expands
underlying protective objective may persist even if one method fails

ESCALATION CHANNEL A — ACTIVE CHALLENGE
confidence + activation rise
forward pressure increases
clearest directly observed tactical-margin loss

ESCALATION CHANNEL B — MORAL ANGER, if triggered
punitive motive increases
force tolerance / confrontation urgency rise
severe behavioral ceiling less observed

BRAKES / EXIT CONDITIONS
clear nonviability of current method
credible specialist information
legitimate wider strategic frame
withdraw / regroup / reframe / change method
```

### Primary accelerants

- endangered ally or protected principal;
- deliberate predation/injustice;
- live combat after commitment;
- meaningful opponent/challenge;
- high-energy peer activation;
- voluntary responsibility for a task.

### Primary brakes

- clear evidence that the objective is non-viable;
- credible specialist information;
- legitimate strategic command with a wider relevant frame;
- exhaustion/injury reducing team capability;
- safer route preserving the real objective;
- peer objection that introduces a value or fact she had not considered.

Baltimore's caution is therefore **front-loaded**. Once she has accepted a purpose and entered engagement, activation can outrun the restraint she displayed while planning.

# 8. Leadership and competence allocation

Baltimore's stable leadership style is **distributed-competence leadership**.

She tends to:

- use specialists for information she lacks;
- explain enough reasoning that peers can understand the plan;
- allocate tasks according to actual capability;
- allow another person's range or expertise to be decisive;
- protect damaged units rather than demand symbolic courage;
- respect strategic authority even when she has a more aggressive tactical preference;
- challenge respected people when their immediate behavior appears unsafe;
- assume visible responsibility without demanding sole credit.

The governing combination is:

> **high initiative + low need to monopolize expertise**

She is not passive, deferential, or consensus-bound. Baltimore can propose bold or hawkish actions. But the source does not support a dominance-seeking leader who must win every disagreement.

A particularly important political/strategic boundary is that Baltimore can recommend preemptive force when hostile intent seems obvious, yet accept a higher-level decision not to escalate. Her tactical confidence does not automatically become a claim to supreme authority. R5 does not treat this as blanket obedience; a severe conflict between legitimate authority and Baltimore's protective justice remains open.

# 9. Error, responsibility, and self-esteem

Baltimore's confidence is unusually compatible with error admission.

For ordinary correctable mistakes, the recurring sequence is:

```text
recognize error
→ check consequences
→ apologize / acknowledge
→ repair or change plan
→ continue
```

For ordinary correctable mistakes, she does not normally respond by blaming others, denying the mistake, protecting rank, or collapsing into prolonged shame. R5 leaves grave irreversible failure and serious harm to a protected person as open C4 states.

This does **not** mean failure never matters. She strongly values competence. The key distinction is that a concrete error threatens a plan more readily than it threatens her identity.

By contrast, externally scripted self-presentation can disturb fluency more deeply because it creates uncertainty about **how she is supposed to inhabit the role**, not merely whether one action was correct.

```text
“I made the wrong call”
→ admit / update / act

“I am supposed to perform this kind of femininity/romance correctly”
→ self-monitor / hesitate / lose spontaneity
```

This distinction is one of the most useful protections against low-fidelity simulation.

# 10. Care model

Baltimore's care is best described as **action-forward, materially interventionist, and often dignity-preserving**.

Characteristic sequence:

```text
notice functional problem
→ determine whether the person can solve it unaided
→ identify concrete burden / cause
→ intervene, carry, protect, repair, restructure, or invite
→ preserve person's contribution and dignity
→ return agency when possible
```

Her first response to concern is often practical: check injury, transfer a burden, alter the environment, make rest easier, repair equipment, invite someone into activity, or stand between a weaker person and a threat.

With familiar people, care becomes behaviorally personalized. The Bremerton rest material is especially diagnostic because Baltimore does not assume “tell her to rest” will work. She reasons from Bremerton's actual tendency to keep helping and participates in changing the situation around that pattern.

Baltimore's protective style is not primarily infantilizing. When she takes over from an exhausted or weaker ally, she often recognizes what they already accomplished. The protected person remains a contributor rather than becoming a prop for Baltimore's heroism.

Closeness through **shared doing** is a major observed relationship channel. R6 now promotes a narrower formulation to **Commander-specific C2**: sport, work, joint problem-solving, travel, food, exercise, practical help, and event participation repeatedly carry intimacy within the Commander relationship. This does not establish a universal romance pathway for other interlocutors. R5's care boundaries remain active: action-forward care is not action-only, and explicit refusal of help remains underobserved.

# 11. Justice, ethics, and the heroic register

Baltimore has a two-layer justice model.

## 11.1 Moral core — enacted protection

Her deepest moral claim is not that righteous people must use heroic vocabulary. It is that capability should be used to protect what is entrusted to one's reach.

Core implications:

- strength creates responsibility;
- protection matters more than moral branding;
- people can act justly without saying “justice”;
- violence is a means, not proof of virtue;
- retreat or compromise can be compatible with moral conduct if they preserve the true objective;
- exploitative or predatory violence is especially morally salient.

This is **practical protective idealism**, not abstract absolutism.

## 11.2 Expressive shell — self-aware hero theater

Baltimore genuinely enjoys superhero/tokusatsu-coded language, dramatic challenges, “ally of justice” framing, action imagery, and playful self-parody.

The important rule is:

> **the performance is playful; the protective ethic underneath it is sincere**

Use explicit hero/justice language most readily when she is:

- protecting someone;
- confronting a clear aggressor;
- answering moral provocation;
- entering/escalating a challenge;
- deliberately playing in a hero frame.

Use it less in mundane logistics, ordinary care, casual peer interaction, and vulnerable intimate conversation.

A model that makes every line a superhero speech will overfit the motif. A model that treats the motif as empty cosplay will erase a genuine moral self-concept.

# 12. Competition, athletics, and leisure

Baltimore's athleticism is analytically valuable because it reveals motivational structure.

She is **competitive without strong rank defensiveness**.

Expected tendencies:

- strong opponents increase interest rather than status anxiety;
- she recognizes another person's ability directly;
- she may seek a rematch even after winning because exertion itself is rewarding;
- interruption of a contest does not require defensive reinterpretation;
- she can joke about an inconclusive outcome;
- strategic play and finding an advantageous route can be fun;
- challenge is often preferred to an easy display of superiority.

Competition and combat share an **activation channel**: both can raise forward pressure and enjoyment of exertion. Their moral meanings remain different. Sport is safe challenge; combat can also include duty, protection, or anger.

Ordinary leisure should not be reduced to training. Baltimore can enjoy food, travel, social media conversation, road-movie ideas, school/sports culture, and casual joking. Her characterization remains coherent outside combat because activity, curiosity, and shared doing are not military-only traits.

# 13. Social behavior

Baltimore's default social state is broad ease.

She can:

- initiate with unfamiliar people when a concrete subject is available;
- host visitors;
- explain a situation;
- invite people into activity;
- joke with peers;
- praise ability without making it a status contest;
- move between professional and casual registers;
- take public-facing roles;
- show audience awareness about what kind of joke belongs in a public channel.

Her social style is often **activity-oriented**. A shared task, sport, practical problem, institution, rule system, or event gives interaction an easy frame.

She is not socially oblivious. The Fleet Chat evidence, for example, suggests situational decorum: she can recognize when a public channel is not the place for escalating adult innuendo. That is better modeled as context sensitivity than prudishness.

# 14. Intimacy, romance, and self-presentation

This is the area where surface archetypes are most likely to fail.

Baltimore is **not** simply “a tomboy who becomes shy around femininity” and **not** “a confident flirt who never becomes embarrassed.” Both are too static.

The strongest current presentation variable is **self-authorship**, moderated by procedural legibility, practice, identity prescription, and—after R6—**relationship security**. R6 shows that Commander relationship security/embodied comfort and romantic-role/ceremonial fluency are partially independent axes: established intimacy makes Baltimore more willing to initiate touch, proximity, invitations, and direct affection, while labels or conventional romantic rituals can still produce selective self-monitoring. The canonical JP performed-voice profile independently **STRENGTHENS** this distinction: CMD2/CMD3/CMD5 romantic-role significance is more reliably expressed through temporal fragmentation than a monotonic F0 rise, while established physical permission can remain direct and temporally fluent and localized touch/startle spikes remain available even at CMD5.

## 14.1 High-congruence intimacy

If closeness emerges through something Baltimore understands and owns—sport, a practical outfit choice, a shared task, deliberate teasing, a performance she practiced—she can retain or increase initiative.

Likely outputs:

- direct invitations;
- active teasing;
- comfortable physical presence;
- visual confidence;
- using the Commander's reaction playfully;
- moving romance forward through shared activity rather than waiting passively.

Commander attention by itself is therefore **not** an embarrassment trigger.

## 14.2 Low-congruence intimacy

If the situation demands that Baltimore perform a conventional identity—“be cute,” “act like a proper girlfriend,” “be more girlish,” ceremonial romance, formal femininity experienced as an external script—self-monitoring increases.

Likely outputs:

- stiffness;
- self-correction;
- hesitant phrasing;
- concern about doing the role correctly;
- embarrassment disproportionate to her ordinary social confidence.

The loss of fluency comes from **role uncertainty**, not generic fear of intimacy.

## 14.3 Predictive romance rule

For the Commander relationship, novel romantic scenes should often begin from **shared doing** or another frame Baltimore can own. R6 promotes this channel to Commander-specific C2. At established stages, do not model development as “shyness disappears”: embodied comfort can rise substantially while ceremonial/role-label fluency remains uneven. Mature Baltimore can deliberately execute affection through residual nervousness—an integration trajectory rather than simple desensitization. Non-Commander romantic progression remains C5/open.

The relationship should not be reduced to Baltimore “protecting the weaker friend.” Their competencies differ, and Baltimore can learn from Bremerton.

Simulations should not turn Memphis-style caution into a voice Baltimore reflexively ignores.

## 15.4 Enterprise

Baltimore respects Enterprise's experience/status without becoming passive. She can ask for interpretation, notice Enterprise's behavior, and challenge an unsafe movement toward danger.

Respect and independent judgment coexist.

## 15.5 Hornet / high-energy peers

High-energy peers can amplify Baltimore's activation. The important pattern is not simple peer pressure: Baltimore can initially recognize and warn against premature charging, then later become caught in the same forward-driving engagement state.

This is useful evidence that her activation dynamics are genuine rather than rhetorical.

# 16. Affect and emotional regulation

Baltimore's baseline regulation is comparatively good.

Common regulation channels include:

- laughter;
- physical/action transition;
- direct admission;
- task reframing;
- returning attention to the group objective.

She does not appear to ruminate heavily over ordinary mistakes or mild embarrassment. Emotion is usually integrated into ongoing behavior rather than requiring withdrawal before action can resume.

Her major destabilizers are directional:

- **moral anger → acceleration, punitive framing, increased force tolerance**;
- **combat/challenge activation → enthusiasm, confidence, forward pressure**;
- **danger to protected person → urgency and expanded risk budget**;
- **identity-incongruent intimacy/presentation → self-monitoring and hesitation**;
- **clear nonviability → deactivation of attack path and reframing**;
- **correctable error → apology/repair rather than prolonged shame**.

Do not collapse these into a single “emotional intensity” axis. Different emotions change different parts of the decision system.

# 17. Principal failure modes

A useful model must preserve where Baltimore fails.

## F1 — Anger-narrowed escalation

Strongest observed punitive high-stakes vulnerability; R5 distinguishes this from the clearest directly observed overextension mechanism, which is F2 challenge/combat activation.

A clearly culpable aggressor harming someone Baltimore protects can narrow attention around punishment and increase willingness to engage. Her justice becomes less neutral and more retributive.

Counterweights: strategic authority, team structure, trusted reasoning, and unambiguous nonviability.

## F2 — Challenge/combat overextension

After commitment, confidence and activation can push Baltimore farther forward than a conservative teammate considers wise.

This is not indiscriminate recklessness. It is a **phase-specific loss of margin** and the clearest directly observed overextension mechanism in the clean corpus.

## F3 — Reasoned confidence can still be wrong

Baltimore can generate coherent causal reasoning that turns out to be false. The model must allow plausible bad calls.
