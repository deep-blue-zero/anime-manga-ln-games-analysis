# Azur Lane — Taihou Full CN Narrative Deep Reading

## 0. Purpose and evidence discipline

This artifact completes R2 over the **28-scene CN narrative surface**. It preserves the seven-part memory as the causal anchor, then uses the remaining 21 scenes primarily as **adversarial and cross-context tests**.

The additional scenes are not assumed to form one simple chronology. Event, μ-weapon, skin-like interaction, crossover, ensemble, and exercise premises are tagged by context and weighted according to causal depth. A striking line in a gimmick scene does not outrank sustained ordinary behavior merely because it is memorable.

**R2 verdict: `TAIHOU_R2_FULL_NARRATIVE_PASS_WITH_SELECTIVE_BOUNDARY_OVERRIDE_PEER_COMPETENCE_AND_DUAL_ATTACHMENT_THREAT_MODEL`.**

The anchor model survives, but several hypotheses become more precise.

# 1. Cross-context result at a glance

The full narrative supports five high-level conclusions strongly enough to carry into R3 as longitudinal candidates.

1. **Taihou's Commander attachment is highly salient but does not erase task competence or peer functioning.** She can accept team roles, take technical advice, participate in ensembles, perform artistically, wait for work to finish, and converse normally with peers.
2. **Boundary problems are selective and attachment-driven, not evidence of generalized boundary blindness.** She can recognize another person's work, privacy, authority, and role constraints; she sometimes chooses to override ordinary privacy specifically when closeness/service to the Commander is prioritized.
3. **Jealousy is real but literal exclusivity is negotiable.** Romantic competition raises possessive rhetoric and sometimes aggressive threat display, yet Taihou can cooperate, share activities, accept a three-person date compromise, and function in crowded Commander-attention contexts.
4. **There are at least two attachment-threat pathways.** External competitor cues tend toward claiming/rivalry; evidence that Taihou herself may be unwanted tends toward withdrawal, self-condemnation, and reassurance seeking.
5. **Taihou's expressiveness is actively constructed rather than merely impulsive.** She practices music and performance, plans clothing and presentation, engineers encounters, and uses dramatic/sexual/theatrical framing as deliberate relational action.

These conclusions do not yet constitute a full monograph. Social, character-text, relationship, and Dorm3D layers must still test their boundaries.

# 2. Rivalry is intense but not equivalent to absolute exclusion

## `gongmingdepassion17` — rivalry resolves into a shared date

Taihou and Akagi arrive to claim wish-fulfillment dates with the Commander and immediately argue over order. The important endpoint is not the argument. They return with a negotiated solution: **the three of them go together**.

This is a direct counterexample to the strongest literal-exclusivity reading.

Taihou clearly wants privileged attention. She will contest another claimant. But under at least some secure/playful conditions, the system can solve competition by **shared access rather than elimination**.

This requires a revision:

```text
old simplification:
competitor -> exclusion must be restored

better current model:
competitor -> territoriality / comparison / claim pressure
           -> may resolve through winning, negotiation, shared access,
              or continued rivalry depending on context
```

This does not make jealousy cosmetic. It makes the behavior more flexible than “one rival must disappear.”

## `jiarixintiaodayoulun21` — crowded competition without escalation

At the masquerade, many characters ask to dance with the Commander. Taihou adds a highly pressuring “of course you will dance with me” bid, but the scene resolves through taking turns. No special Taihou escalation follows.

Because this is a broad ensemble scene and her contribution is brief, it is moderate evidence only. It nonetheless supports the idea that **mere presence of many interested people is not sufficient by itself to trigger severe possessive action**.

# 3. Self-authored performance is a major attachment strategy

Several scenes show Taihou investing skill, practice, and planning into being seen by the Commander.

## `haidaobieshudujiajihua17` — cultivated musical competence

Taihou performs a skilled pipa piece she says was composed for the Commander. The narration treats the playing as genuinely accomplished rather than as empty seduction. She then compares the emotional impact of her solo with the other performers.

The scene strengthens two separate propositions:

- attachment motivates cultivated competence;
- competitive display does not require sabotaging others' actual performance.

Her confidence is proactive: she tries to secure an advantage by performing privately before the concert, then boasts that the outcome is already decided.

## `haidaobieshudujiajihua6` — attention as performance incentive

When an open concert is announced, Taihou immediately recognizes it as an opportunity to monopolize the Commander's gaze. This is a compact line, but in combination with the pipa scene and festival performances it supports a recurring strategy:

`desired attention -> prepare/display competence -> create memorable privileged impression`

## `hongbaorichang4` — learned public performance

Taihou performs at the Spring Festival using skills learned from Yat Sen and explicitly invites the Commander to watch. The scene shows no generalized performance shyness. She is willing to practice an unfamiliar cultural form and present publicly when the frame is self-authored and goal-congruent.

## `jichang15` — group rather than solo

Albacore asks why Taihou joined an idol group instead of going solo. Taihou answers that she wants the Commander to see the most perfect version of her and notes that Akagi also joined a group. Just as important, she and Albacore exchange practical performance information: Taihou spots an impossible stage design, and Albacore gives her choreography advice.

This is strong evidence that:

- Commander-directed presentation does not require solitary control;
- Taihou can accept peer technical correction;
- competition with Akagi can motivate **team participation** rather than refusal to collaborate.

## `jichang19` — theatrical extremity is not literal psychology

In the μ-performance premise, Taihou jokes that eliminating other people and the audience would solve the question of who shines most. Roon joins the joke, and Taihou's internal aside shows that Roon's escalation actually frightens her.

This is a critical reading control:

> extreme language inside competitive/theatrical framing cannot automatically be treated as literal homicidal intent.

The scene demonstrates Taihou's capacity for grotesque hyperbole while simultaneously giving a counter-signal about actual emotional reaction.

# 4. Secure Commander intimacy is often assertive, not fragile

## `haidaobieshudujiajihua21` — engineered encounter and direct sexual initiative

Taihou waits in the game room, interprets the Commander's arrival as evidence of connection, and turns an offer to teach billiards into increasingly intimate physical/sexual framing. She is not passively waiting to be pursued; she has created a setting in which the Commander is likely to find her and then escalates the interaction herself.

Because this scene is highly intimate and presentation-heavy, it should model a **high-security/high-permission Commander state**, not global baseline behavior.

What it establishes strongly:

- sexual initiative is available;
- Taihou can be teasing and controlling when she owns the frame;
- embarrassment is not a universal consequence of romantic salience;
- she uses ambiguity, double meaning, proximity, and staged circumstance deliberately.

## `haidaorichang9` — controlled “private date” frame

Taihou emphasizes that the beach is temporarily theirs alone, enjoys the Commander's flustered reaction, physically stops withdrawal, and tells him to leave everything to her.

This is consistent with the anchor secure-attachment state:

`assumed acceptance + self-authored intimate frame -> high initiative / low hesitation / strong proximity control`

It does **not** answer the hard-refusal question. The scene never provides an explicit, serious request to stop. Therefore it cannot be used to claim what Taihou would do after informed refusal.

## `renqidafeng` — self-as-gift performance

Taihou constructs an elaborate gift-box scenario in which she presents herself as a unique Commander-only gift and asks to be “unwrapped.” The scene is playful, highly sexualized, and self-authored. She repeatedly invites intimate contact and says she would not reject the Commander's choices.

Analytical use:

- evidence of active erotic self-presentation;
- evidence that “exclusive possession” language can be mutually romantic/theatrical rather than always coercive;
- evidence that she enjoys engineering situations that make the Commander participate physically.

Boundary:

Her “never refuse” rhetoric is **not evidence of universal consent under all future circumstances**. It is an utterance inside an invited erotic performance.

# 5. Taihou can voluntarily respect work, role, and command constraints

This is the strongest correction to any global “boundaries mean nothing to her” reading.

## `shanchenglifu2` — she chooses not to interrupt the Commander

Taihou tells Yamashiro that she had considered entering the Commander's office but abandoned the plan because he was working and she did not want to disturb him. She waits outside with a drink instead.

This scene proves three things directly:

1. Taihou recognizes a legitimate boundary created by the Commander's current task.
2. She can inhibit an attachment-driven access impulse without external force.
3. The same character who duplicates a private-room key can voluntarily remain outside an office when the Commander's work is salient.

Therefore H2 must be formulated as **selective priority weighting**, not conceptual ignorance.

A more precise decision structure is:

```text
attachment goal
+ assumed welcome
+ weak competing constraint
-> boundary override becomes plausible

attachment goal
+ Commander's clearly legible work/task interest
-> restraint can win
```

The exact weight of explicit refusal remains untested.

## `junheboss1–5` — professional command can override romantic impulse

In the joint exercise, Taihou initially asks to be placed where she can meet the Commander's fleet. The temporary Sakura commander insists that deployment must follow operational goals. Taihou accepts that answer.

During the exercise:

- she realistically judges the Commander's fleet as stronger;
- she worries about Sakura's face/morale despite saying she would personally enjoy losing to the Commander;
- she is assigned reconnaissance because she can reliably locate him;
- she responds competitively when challenged to prove that ability;
- when told to focus on reconnaissance rather than physically rushing the Commander, she complains but **complies**;
- the commander transfers flagship authority to her during the plan, implying trust in her operational capability;
- after the exercise Taihou is still lively and attachment-focused, but she has not sabotaged the team to obtain personal access.

This is substantial E1/E2 evidence that Taihou can function within **distributed professional authority**.

It also sharpens a useful distinction:

> attachment preference can remain verbally present while behavior follows the mission.

A simulator must not treat romantic thoughts as proof that she cannot execute professional roles.

# 6. Peer-social competence is broader than the memory alone suggested

## `hongbaorichang2` — shared practical work

Taihou enters the office expecting to present herself to the Commander and instead finds a group decorating while he is absent. Once told the work is for him, she immediately joins and takes responsibility for the doorway decoration.

Her motive remains Commander-linked, but the behavior is cooperative and task-oriented. She does not try to expel the other women from the space or refuse shared effort.

## `huihua3–5` — repeated Albacore relationship

These scenes are especially useful because they combine conflict and persistence.

Taihou says that she no longer cares much about the past and distinguishes past events from the present. When Albacore threatens to “mess with” the Commander, Taihou immediately reactivates severe threatening language and deploys her aircraft enough to frighten Albacore. Once Albacore withdraws the threat, their social interaction resumes and Taihou ultimately accompanies her to the festival.

This refines the earlier Albacore model:

- reconciliation is real but not unconditional;
- Commander-protection/competition cues can activate aggressive display toward a familiar peer;
- removal of the threat restores peer functionality rapidly;
- Taihou can be dragged into a broader social group even after insisting the Commander is her only goal.

In `huihua5`, Albacore effectively adds Essex to their group. Taihou objects to casually increasing the group but ultimately tolerates it. This is another example of **resistant permeability**, not isolation.

## `shanchenglifu2` — explicit social broadening

Taihou's conversation with Yamashiro is among the most valuable non-Commander scenes in the corpus.

She jokes normally, listens to Yamashiro's festival experiences, admits that she originally thought only the Commander mattered but now finds the port a comfortable place, says she may try more activities next time, and invites Yamashiro to sit and drink/chat while they wait.

This is stronger than “Taihou can tolerate peers.” It supports:

> Taihou is capable of genuine environmental and social integration beyond the attachment figure, even while the Commander remains her dominant preference.

The scene also suggests that her social world can **expand through lived familiarity** rather than requiring personality replacement.

# 7. Competition is domain-selective

Taihou is not globally competitive about everything.

In `huihua5`, Enterprise asks why Taihou did not participate in the martial competition; Taihou simply says she is not interested. By contrast, she invests heavily in music/performance contexts that can capture the Commander's attention and in direct romantic rivalry.

So the current evidence supports:

```text
competition value rises when:
- Commander attention is at stake;
- personal presentation is at stake;
- a meaningful relational rival is involved;
- task/role gives her a reason to prove capability.

competition is not automatically rewarding in every domain.
```

This is distinct from Baltimore's broad challenge activation and should not be imported from another character model.

# 8. Privacy and boundary behavior is asymmetric

## `weixianfamingpojinzhongrichang6` — private collection room

In this crossover scene, Momo accidentally enters Taihou's private collection room and apologizes. Taihou immediately demands to know how she found it and is suspicious that the entry was not accidental.

The room contains carefully collected objects associated with the Commander.

This scene is E5 because of the crossover premise, but it supplies an analytically useful asymmetry:

> Taihou understands the concept of **her own private space** quite clearly.

That further weakens any claim that she simply lacks a model of privacy. The problem is better located in how she weights the Commander's privacy against her attachment/service objective.

The same scene again activates Akagi/Roon rivalry and immediate suspicion that Momo might study the Commander to gain an advantage. Once Momo explicitly denies being a comparable rival and offers advice, Taihou becomes willing to listen.

Even inside a jealousy-heavy premise, threat classification matters.

# 9. Aggression and threat language require state/context separation

The corpus contains genuine threatening behavior and theatrical exaggeration. They should not be merged.

`huihua3`:

- Albacore proposes bothering the Commander;
- Taihou uses severe threat language;
- she deploys aircraft enough to frighten Albacore;

## Weaker / theatrical evidence

`jichang19`:

- extreme elimination rhetoric occurs in an idol/performance competition;
- Roon's literal-sounding escalation frightens Taihou herself;
- the scene frames the exchange as comic performance.

This should model rhetorical style/performance, not homicide propensity.

## Sparse combat evidence

`huapohaikongzhiyi5` gives Taihou only a minimal enemy-role line about at least taking down the opposing group. It is insufficient by itself for ethics, anger, or general aggression claims.

Current rule:

`threat wording must be interpreted through target, premise, relationship, and realized action—not lexical severity alone.`

# 10. Revised conditional architecture after all 28 scenes

The current R2 model is:

```text
1. DETERMINE COMMANDER / ATTACHMENT SALIENCE

2. IF COMMANDER SALIENCE LOW:
   Taihou can still perform professional, peer, group, leisure,
   artistic, and combat-role behavior.

3. IF COMMANDER SALIENCE HIGH:
   classify the current route:

   A. SECURE ACCESS / SELF-AUTHORED PRESENTATION
      -> service, preparation, teasing, erotic initiative,
         cultivated performance, proximity seeking

   B. LEGIBLE COMMANDER TASK / PROFESSIONAL CONSTRAINT
      -> attachment preference remains visible
      -> restraint/compliance can override access impulse

   C. EXTERNAL ROMANTIC COMPETITOR
      -> possessive language, comparison, claiming,
         possibly aggressive display
      -> but negotiation/shared access remains possible

   D. FAMILIAR PEER DISRUPTION WITHOUT ACCEPTANCE THREAT
      -> startle/irritation/resistance
      -> participation and relationship can persist

   E. TAIHOU-HERSELF-MAY-BE-UNWANTED
      -> self-reassessment
      -> negative reinterpretation / self-condemnation
      -> withdrawal
      -> repeated acceptance verification

4. IF EXPLICIT COMMANDER ACCEPTANCE RESOLVES ROUTE E:
   -> rapid return to approach/proximity
```

This architecture explains more of the narrative corpus than a fixed trait such as “jealous,” “possessive,” “seductive,” or “unstable.” Those remain possible outputs of particular states.

# 11. H1–H10 transitions after the full narrative pass

## H1 — attachment is enacted through useful service

**Transition: STRENGTHEN + EXPAND.**

The memory's secretary competence survives broader evidence. Taihou also invests in music, presentation, event preparation, and team roles to create value/attention. Professional exercise scenes demonstrate actual competence outside domestic secretary service.

Current formulation:

> Attachment strongly motivates Taihou to become useful, memorable, and uniquely salient to the Commander, but her underlying capacity for preparation and role execution is not confined to romance.

Confidence: high.

## H2 — attachment certainty can suppress explicit boundary checking

**Transition: REVISE + SPECIFY.**

`shanchenglifu2` is decisive counterevidence to generalized boundary blindness: Taihou voluntarily stays out because the Commander is working. The joint exercise shows legitimate operational authority can also constrain her behavior. The private collection room shows she recognizes privacy as a concept.

Current formulation:

> Taihou understands privacy, role, and situational boundaries but can selectively override them when Commander attachment/access is highly valued and she assumes her intervention is welcome or justified. Clear Commander task salience and legitimate operational structure can restore restraint.

Confidence: high for the distinction; hard-refusal behavior remains open.

## H3 — perceived unwantedness is a principal attachment destabilizer

**Transition: PRESERVE + BOUND.**

No other narrative scene produces a comparable inward collapse. That preserves `dafeng7` as the clearest observed attachment-security failure, but the remaining corpus does not supply a second clean rejection experiment.

Current formulation:

> Credible uncertainty that the Commander may reject Taihou because of her own devotion is a severe observed destabilizer; whether it is the single strongest possible destabilizer remains open.

Confidence: high for observed episode, medium for cross-context ranking.

## H4 — jealousy/exclusivity is strongly relationship-triggered

**Transition: STRENGTHEN + REVISE.**

Commander rivals repeatedly activate possession/claiming, but `gongmingdepassion17` disproves absolute exclusion as an invariant. Group work, public events, and shared attention are repeatedly tolerated.

Current formulation:

> Taihou shows strong Commander-centered territoriality and rivalry, but the operative motive is privileged attachment/access rather than a mechanically absolute requirement that all rivals be excluded. Negotiated/shared access can be accepted in some states.

Confidence: high.

## H5 — peer social competence exists beneath Commander-centric preference

**Transition: STRENGTHEN.**

Albacore, Shoukaku, Yamashiro, group decoration, μ-team interaction, and broader festival participation all support actual peer functioning. `shanchenglifu2` adds explicit evidence of growing interest in the port beyond the Commander.

Current formulation:

> Taihou is Commander-centric by preference and salience, not socially nonfunctional. Familiarity can broaden her peer engagement, and she can joke, advise, receive advice, cooperate, invite, and participate.

Confidence: high.

## H6 — rivalry does not erase competence recognition

**Transition: STRENGTHEN + EXPAND.**

She recognizes Akagi's experience, accepts another commander's tactical reasoning, judges the Commander's fleet realistically, receives Albacore's choreography advice, and works in teams.

Current formulation:

> Taihou can preserve domain competence judgments even when attachment rivalry or personal display is active. She is not epistemically required to diminish rivals or collaborators to protect self-image.

Confidence: high.

## H7 — care-giving motivation exceeds self-care motivation

**Transition: PRESERVE + BOUND.**

The 21 additional scenes add little clean self-care evidence. The memory asymmetry remains meaningful but under-tested.

Current formulation:

**Transition: STRENGTHEN + SPECIFY.**

Commander tracking, collection, encounter engineering, and privacy circumvention recur. Yet she voluntarily respects work, accepts command structure, and understands her own privacy.

Current formulation:

**Transition: STRENGTHEN BY CONTRAST + BOUND.**

`huihua3` provides the contrasting outward pathway when an external peer threatens Commander access. This makes the distinction more credible even though `dafeng7` remains the only full inward-collapse episode.

Current formulation:

> Taihou's threat response depends on attribution: an external competitor/harasser can trigger outward territorial escalation; self-as-unwanted-problem can trigger withdrawal and self-condemnation.

## H10 — Commander acceptance has unusually strong regulatory leverage

**Transition: PRESERVE + BOUND.**

The narrative does not repeat the exact reassurance experiment. High-security scenes are consistent with strong reliance on mutual acceptance but do not independently prove the same rapid regulatory mechanism.

Current formulation:

Confidence: high for `dafeng7`, medium longitudinally.
