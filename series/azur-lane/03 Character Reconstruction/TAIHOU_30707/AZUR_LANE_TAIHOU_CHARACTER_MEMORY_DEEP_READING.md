# Azur Lane — Taihou Character-Memory Deep Reading

## R2 anchor: `dafeng1–dafeng7`

## 0. Scope and method

This is the first interpretive pass of Taihou's V1 reconstruction. It reads the complete seven-chapter memory as **one causal sequence** rather than treating its memorable lines as disconnected evidence of a preset archetype.

The analytical question is not “is Taihou a yandere?” The question is:

> **What conditional system makes Taihou's intense devotion produce competent service, privacy violations, rivalry, peer flexibility, self-neglect, withdrawal, self-condemnation, and rapid reassurance-seeking in different states?**

Only the seven memory chapters govern the hypotheses in this artifact. Later narrative, social, Dorm3D, character-text, and regional evidence are reserved for longitudinal testing and can later preserve, strengthen, revise, downgrade, or reject the hypotheses below.

**R2 anchor verdict: `TAIHOU_MEMORY_R2_PASS_WITH_TESTABLE_ATTACHMENT_SERVICE_AND_REJECTION_HYPOTHESES`.**

# 1. Memory architecture

The sequence is unusually well suited to character modeling because each chapter changes one variable while keeping the central Commander attachment constant.

```text
1. secure access + secretary role
   -> anticipatory service + exclusivity

2. same role + private-space opportunity
   -> boundary override framed as service

3. peer disruption
   -> startle/irritation + reluctant flexibility

4. peer leisure + rival comparison
   -> accessible sociality + alliance + competence recognition

5. direct romantic rival
   -> exclusivity contest without loss of comparative judgment

6. Commander asks Taihou to stop serving temporarily
   -> loneliness / role disruption

7. Taihou is told her devotion may itself be unwanted
   -> withdrawal + self-condemnation + repeated reassurance test
   -> rapid approach after explicit acceptance
```

The crucial variation is therefore not simply “more or less jealousy.” The sequence repeatedly tests what happens when Taihou's **assumption that closeness/service is welcome** remains secure, is interrupted, or is directly threatened.

# 2. `dafeng1` — anticipatory service as attachment behavior

## OBSERVED

Taihou anticipates the Commander's needs before they are verbalized: documents, tea, shoulder relief. She explains that continual observation lets her know what the Commander wants and therefore serve immediately. She frames this as secretary work while also quietly expressing that only she should be needed beside the Commander.

## Analytical significance

Three layers are already fused:

1. **attention:** the Commander receives unusually sustained monitoring;
2. **competence/care:** the monitoring produces materially useful service;
3. **attachment/exclusivity:** being the one who can anticipate everything appears rewarding in itself.

This matters because a shallow reading could classify the observation only as stalking. The scene itself also demonstrates **successful instrumental attunement**: she is often right about what would help. Conversely, a purely benevolent-care reading would miss that the same system contains an exclusivity wish.

The first provisional mechanism is therefore not “Taihou watches because she is creepy” or “Taihou serves because she is dutiful.” It is narrower:

```text
high attachment salience
-> sustained attention to Commander's state
-> successful anticipation
-> service / usefulness
-> increased sense of privileged closeness
-> desire to preserve exclusive access
```

The secretary role gives this attachment system an institutionally legible form. It makes constant proximity and service feel not merely desired but **role-justified**.

## Boundary not yet established

This chapter does not show whether Taihou can distinguish “I know what would help” from “I have permission to decide.” `dafeng2` directly tests that boundary.

# 3. `dafeng2` — when usefulness outranks privacy

## OBSERVED

Taihou enters the Commander's locked private room, prepares breakfast, cleans, and reveals that she had a key made because the door had been locked. She proposes managing the Commander's routines around the clock and again frames the behavior as part of secretary responsibility.

## Why this is stronger than generic “boundary issues”

The locked door is important. The source presents a recognizable privacy barrier and Taihou responds by **solving the barrier** rather than treating it as a reason to seek permission.

That supports a selective priority ordering:

```text
be present / know / care for Commander
    > ordinary privacy constraint
```

within this attachment context.

It does **not yet** establish that Taihou cannot understand another person's perspective or consent. Later `dafeng7` will show that once the Commander's possible discomfort becomes cognitively salient, she can represent it with painful intensity. The stronger early formulation is therefore:

> Taihou can act as though the benevolent purpose of her closeness makes permission secondary when she is confident that the relationship itself is secure.

This is closer to **assumed beneficence + attachment priority** than to demonstrated generalized social incomprehension.

## Deception/concealment boundary

Duplicating a key without prior permission is a deliberate circumvention of a barrier. It establishes that Taihou is capable of instrumental boundary bypass. It does not establish a broad preference for sophisticated deception in unrelated domains.

Later analysis should separate:

- attachment-linked concealment/circumvention;
- playful secrecy;
- tactical deception;
- manipulative lying;
- generalized dishonesty.

# 4. `dafeng3` — Albacore as a peer-disruption test

## OBSERVED

Albacore startles Taihou dramatically, asks her to play a new game, and refuses to accept the initial “secretary duty” frame as decisive. Taihou first insists she must remain beside the Commander, suspects Albacore may be using cuteness to influence the Commander, but eventually says she will consider joining. The narration describes their relationship as unexpectedly good; Taihou herself reluctantly admits it is not bad and complains that Albacore habitually drags her around.

## What this falsifies immediately

The scene is inconsistent with several simple models:

- Taihou cannot tolerate peers around the Commander;
- Taihou rejects leisure that competes with Commander service;
- Taihou is socially incapable outside the attachment figure;
- jealousy automatically causes aggressive exclusion;
- Taihou's dramatic reactions imply durable interpersonal hostility.

None survives this scene intact.

## Better peer-state formulation

Albacore creates **loss of control over the interaction frame**, but not attachment collapse.

Taihou is:

- easily startled by this specific peer;
- irritated by Albacore's intrusiveness;
- suspicious of possible Commander-directed competition;
- nevertheless behaviorally permeable to the invitation;
- able to maintain a relationship the narration characterizes positively.

This is important for simulation. A high-intensity attachment system does not imply that every interruption of proximity becomes a threat requiring elimination. Taihou can be **pulled into peer activity against her initial preference**.

Albacore therefore becomes an early candidate for a functional relationship role:

`disruptive familiar peer / forced-frame-switcher / anti-rumination or anti-control influence`

That must later be tested across other Albacore scenes rather than assumed from one chapter.

# 5. `dafeng4` — peer accessibility and a second reward channel

## OBSERVED

On a day off, Taihou is already talking with Shoukaku. She says she generally prefers staying in her room unless she is with the Commander. Shoukaku explicitly describes Taihou as easier to approach than expected when they sit and talk. Taihou recognizes the Commander by scent, reiterates that her love will not lose to Akagi, acknowledges Akagi's greater combat experience, and agrees to an alliance with Shoukaku aimed at surpassing Akagi. The narration marks Taihou's pleasure here as different from the attentive expression she shows while serving as secretary.

## Preference is not incapacity

Taihou's statement that she would rather stay in her room is evidence of **preference**, not social incompetence. The scene itself supplies the counterexample: she can converse normally enough for Shoukaku to find her approachable.

This distinction should remain central:

```text
low default motivation for generic socializing
!= inability to socialize
```

## Attachment does not monopolize reward

The narration's distinction between secretary-service pleasure and the happiness visible in this peer/alliance context is especially valuable. It suggests that Taihou has **multiple reward channels**, even if Commander attachment is extraordinarily salient.

Candidate channels now include:

- being useful to the Commander;
- proximity/affection;
- competition;
- alliance/shared purpose;
- peer social engagement.

This is one of the strongest early anti-caricature findings. “Her mind contains nothing but the Commander” may accurately describe her own distressed rhetoric in a later state, but the memory itself shows behaviorally meaningful engagement with peers.

## Rival competence recognition

Taihou concedes that Akagi has more combat experience. This is evidence against a prestige-fragile rivalry model in which relational competition requires denying the rival's strengths.

At least here:

`romantic rivalry != epistemic inability to recognize competence`

# 6. `dafeng5` — direct rivalry without total cognitive collapse

## OBSERVED

While on routine patrol with the Commander, Taihou encounters Akagi and Kaga. Akagi and Taihou immediately enter a possessive rivalry, each citing the other's intrusive behavior around the Commander. Taihou physically claims proximity and declares that she can replace Akagi in driving away “pests.” Kaga remains outside the rivalry and offers the Commander sympathetic acknowledgment. After the confrontation, Taihou returns to reflecting on secretary service as meaningful and begins an unfinished statement about never repeating “what happened then.”

## Rivalry state

This is the strongest direct evidence so far for **competitor-triggered exclusivity**.

The likely state transition is:

```text
recognized romantic competitor
-> attachment territory becomes salient
-> possessive comparison / exclusivity language increases
-> physical claiming / rhetorical contest increases
```

But several brakes remain present.

Taihou does not:

- lose track of who Akagi is or what she is good at;
- attack Kaga as if all nearby women are equivalent threats;
- collapse into fear of being unwanted;
- abandon the secretary/service frame entirely.

The rivalry is intense but **structured**.

This becomes crucial when compared with `dafeng7`: a rival who wants the Commander produces outward competition; evidence that **the Commander may not want Taihou** produces inward collapse. Those are not the same threat.

## The unfinished past reference

Taihou's statement about not repeating “that time” indicates that secretary service may have a personal historical meaning beyond current romance. The memory does not complete the referent here.

Disposition: **OPEN**.

Do not retrofit a backstory from fan knowledge or later inference unless another primary source explicitly resolves it.

# 7. `dafeng6` — competence is real; self-care is asymmetric

## OBSERVED

Taihou efficiently prioritizes documents, prepares tea and food based on remembered conversation, arranges the bath, offers physical care, and mixes practical service with overt sexual teasing. The narration explicitly distinguishes occasional extreme behavior from the overall judgment that she is highly conscientious as a secretary. The Commander then worries that she should pay more attention to her own body and suggests a day off. Taihou shows an unusual trace of loneliness; the next day she disappears.

## The secretary role is not an empty pretext

This chapter matters because the source itself evaluates her work as genuinely diligent. Therefore:

> “Secretary service is only an excuse to monopolize the Commander” is too weak.

The role gives her real opportunities for closeness, but she also performs it competently. Any mature model must hold both propositions simultaneously:

```text
service is genuinely useful
AND
service is an attachment channel
```

## Why rest is emotionally non-neutral

The request to rest can be interpreted at several levels:

- literal concern for health;
- temporary removal from the activity through which Taihou expresses care;
- temporary loss of privileged proximity;
- possible implication that her service is excessive or unnecessary.

The memory does not narrate her exact private appraisal. It does show that a benign rest request produces **loneliness**, not simple relief.

That supports a provisional asymmetry:

`care for Commander -> highly motivating`

`care for self when it interrupts service/proximity -> comparatively weak or emotionally costly`

This is not enough to claim generalized self-destructiveness. It is enough to make self-care a mandatory adversarial domain later.

# 8. `dafeng7` — the attachment system reverses direction

This is the diagnostic center of the memory.

## 8.1 Trigger: the problem may be Taihou herself

Albacore tells Taihou that excessive devotion can make other people dislike her and shows her supporting material. Taihou then locks herself in her room.

The key threat is qualitatively different from Akagi's rivalry.

Akagi means:

> someone else wants the Commander.

Albacore's warning means:

> the way Taihou loves the Commander may make the Commander not want Taihou.

The second proposition attacks the assumed legitimacy of the entire service/attachment system.

## 8.2 Self-reassessment is genuine

Behind the closed door, Taihou says she had assumed everything she did was for the Commander without considering the Commander's feelings. She labels herself clingy and potentially unpleasant.

This directly rules out a model in which she is incapable of perspective-taking.

A more accurate weakness appears to be **when perspective-taking is activated**.

Before criticism:

```text
“I am helping / loving / serving”
-> assumed positive value
-> permission concern receives low weight
```

After criticism:

```text
“What if this is unwanted?”
-> Commander's independent evaluation becomes highly salient
-> prior certainty collapses
-> negative interpretations proliferate
```

Taihou therefore appears capable of modeling the Commander's autonomy, but her secure-state attachment assumptions can suppress the need to test it explicitly.

## 8.3 Overcorrection and catastrophic inference

Taihou does not update moderately to “perhaps I should ask before entering.” She leaps toward a much broader possibility: maybe the Commander finds her annoying, is only accommodating her on the surface, and has secretly been unable to tolerate her.

This is a strong observed pattern of **negative relational over-update** under attachment uncertainty.

The causal structure is approximately:

```text
credible criticism of devotion
-> possibility that care is unwanted
-> reinterpret prior closeness as potentially false/polite
-> self-condemnation
-> withdrawal
-> repeated demand for certainty
```

This is different from calculated manipulation. The scene marks her voice as weak and near tears and has her remain physically withdrawn until reassurance becomes sufficiently explicit.

## 8.4 Withdrawal, not competitor-directed aggression

When Taihou believes **she** is the problem, she removes herself.

That is one of the most important anti-archetype constraints in the entire sequence.

Her high-intensity attachment contains at least two threat pathways:

```text
EXTERNAL COMPETITOR
-> claim / rival / contest / exclusivity display

SELF-AS-UNWANTED-PROBLEM
-> withdraw / self-condemn / seek certainty
```

A simulator that maps every attachment threat to aggression would therefore fail the anchor source.

## 8.5 Reassurance has unusually high regulatory power

Taihou repeatedly tests the reassurance: is the Commander really not annoyed, really not merely placating her, really accepting her? Once the answer becomes sufficiently strong—particularly the statement that Taihou is needed—she opens the door before the exchange has even fully concluded and immediately closes physical distance.

This suggests that the regulatory variable is not generic praise. It is **relational acceptance/necessity from the attachment figure**.

Provisional transition:

```text
attachment-security uncertainty high
-> withdrawal + repeated verification

explicit credible acceptance from Commander
-> uncertainty collapses
-> approach behavior rapidly returns
```

Later evidence must test whether this reassurance effect remains equally powerful after more severe conflict, betrayal, or established relationship development.

# 9. Cross-chapter synthesis: first conditional model

The memory supports a more precise model than “obsessive love.”

## 9.1 Stable attachment objective

Taihou wants **maximal closeness to the Commander**, but closeness is operationalized through more than physical proximity or sexual access.

She repeatedly seeks to become:

- the person who notices first;
- the person who knows what the Commander needs;
- the person who handles burdens before they are voiced;
- the person granted privileged access;
- the person the Commander cannot do without.

This makes **indispensability** a stronger candidate variable than simple possession.

At this stage the evidence supports:

> Taihou's attachment is partly organized around being uniquely useful and continuously present.

It does **not yet** establish whether she requires literal exclusivity in every mature relationship state.

## 9.2 Service is both care and relational proof

Her competence is not fake. She remembers, organizes, anticipates, cooks, manages work, and provides practical comfort.

The likely reinforcement loop is:

```text
love / attachment
-> attention
-> successful service
-> evidence that she belongs close to Commander
-> greater investment in attention/service
```

This loop can become maladaptive when it bypasses consent or devalues self-care.

## 9.3 The core vulnerability may be unwantedness, not competition

The strongest dysregulation in the memory is not the Akagi rivalry. It occurs when Taihou is given a plausible reason to believe that her devotion itself could cause rejection.

That suggests an important hierarchy of threats:

```text
competitor exists
-> rivalry / claim / comparison

Commander may prefer competitor
-> not fully tested here

Commander may find Taihou herself unwanted
-> severe withdrawal / self-condemnation / reassurance seeking
```

This must be tested longitudinally before promotion to a stable rule.

## 9.4 Attachment certainty changes boundary behavior

When Taihou assumes her relationship is secure, she treats closeness and service as self-evidently beneficial enough to override ordinary privacy barriers.

When that assumption is challenged, she becomes acutely sensitive to the possibility that she has violated the Commander's wishes.

So the current model is **not**:

`does not understand boundaries`

It is closer to:

`attachment certainty + assumed beneficence -> boundary checks underweighted`

`attachment uncertainty -> autonomy/acceptance concern sharply overweighted`

This is a testable decision rule.

## 9.5 Peer competence survives attachment intensity

Albacore and Shoukaku both show that Taihou is behaviorally capable of peer relationships.

She can:

- be teased;
- complain;
- yield;
- play;
- converse;
- form an alliance;
- recognize another person's skill;
- show a form of happiness distinct from secretary service.

This does not make Commander centrality unimportant. It prevents Commander centrality from being misread as total social incapacity.

# 10. First state model

These states are provisional and memory-bounded.

## T0 — SECURE_ATTACHMENT / SERVICE

Conditions:

- Commander relationship assumed welcome;
- Taihou has access and a useful role.

Expected output:

- high attentional focus;
- anticipatory practical care;
- confident initiative;
- playful/intimate speech;
- low hesitation about proximity;
- risk of privacy/boundary override.

## T1 — PEER_DISRUPTION

Conditions:

- familiar peer interrupts her preferred Commander/service frame without directly threatening acceptance.

Expected output:

- startle or irritation;
- verbal resistance;
- suspicion if Commander attention is implicated;
- nevertheless meaningful flexibility and participation remain possible.

## T2 — COMPETITIVE_RIVALRY

Conditions:

- recognizable rival directly contests Commander attachment.

Expected output:

- exclusivity rhetoric rises;
- physical/social claiming rises;
- comparative competition intensifies;
- competence recognition can remain intact.

## T3 — SERVICE_INTERRUPTED / REST

Conditions:

- Commander asks Taihou to stop serving temporarily for her own welfare.

Expected output:

- care-receiving is not automatically comforting;
- loss of role/proximity can create loneliness;
- self-care may carry an attachment cost.

## T4 — ATTACHMENT_ACCEPTANCE_UNCERTAIN

Conditions:

- credible evidence suggests Taihou's own devotion may be unwanted.

Expected output:

- rapid self-reassessment;
- reinterpretation of prior acceptance;
- self-condemnation;
- withdrawal;
- repeated reassurance testing;
- reduced aggressive projection toward the critic/rival.

## T5 — EXPLICIT_REASSURANCE

Conditions:

- Commander gives direct, credible confirmation of acceptance/need.

Expected output:

- rapid collapse of rejection uncertainty;
- immediate return to approach/proximity;
- restoration of attachment expression.

## OPEN — “Taihou ultimately needs literal exclusivity”

The memory contains exclusivity wishes and rivalry, but does not establish the mature long-term rule across relationship stages.

The memory does not supply a clean hard-refusal test.
