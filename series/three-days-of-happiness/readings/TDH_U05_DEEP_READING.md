## 2. Entering context and exposure boundary

## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**A drunk vow.** With the light off, Kusunoki keeps drinking. He has managed, he says, to get peacefully drunk. The quick knack for recovering at such times is to plunge into the abyss and soak in tepid self-pity (P0004). The familiar room, blue with moonlight and with Miyagi sitting still in the corner like a 座敷童 (a child-like household spirit), feels like a different space. He feels as though he were in the wings of a stage, about to begin his performance (P0005–P0006). Suddenly he feels he could do anything; the narrator calls this a drunk's arrogance and a misapprehension that something was changing in him (P0007). He declares to Miyagi that with the ¥300,000 from his lifespan he will change something in his remaining three months (P0008–P0010). She glances up a few centimeters, says only 「そうですか」, and returns to her notebook (P0011–P0012). He presses on: "It may be only three hundred thousand, but it is my life. I'll make it worth more than thirty million or three billion. I'll strive like mad and strike back at this world" (P0013–P0015).

**Miyagi's reply.** "Everyone says the same thing," she says. She puts down her pen and hugs her knees, chin between them (P0016–P0018). She has heard similar lines about five times. As death nears, people's ideas grow extreme, especially among those whose lives were unfulfilled. Like gamblers chasing a one-shot reversal, people who keep losing at life come to want unrealistic happiness. Seeing life's glow relativized by death, they regain something like vigor and think, "I was hopeless before, but now that I've seen my error I can do anything." She thinks this a fatal misapprehension. They have only 〔傍点〕ようやくスタート地点に立った〔/傍点〕, *just reached the starting line* (P0017–P0019). "Your lifespan was valued so low because you would achieve nothing in your remaining thirty years. You know that, don't you? How could someone who achieves nothing in thirty years change anything in three months?" (P0019–P0021).

"You don't know until you try," he answers on the spur of the moment, sickened by how hollow it sounds (P0022–P0023). The next line says it was obvious before trying, and that she is entirely right (P0024). Whether that is the experiencing self's thought or the later narrator's verdict is undecidable. She suggests he seek ordinary, modest satisfaction. Three months is too short to change anything and too long to do nothing, so it would be wiser to pile up small but certain happiness. "You lose because you try to win. A way of life that finds wins inside losses leaves less disappointment" (P0025).

He concedes aloud: "All right, you're right. But I've had enough sound arguments." Had he been sober, he might have kept arguing (P0026). Then he asks her to tell him everything that will happen, correcting himself to how he *was going to* spend the lost thirty years, so that he might stop expecting too much of himself (P0026). After a silence she agrees, as if giving in, that perhaps he had better know it all. She reminds him that what she knows "might have happened" but now "can never happen," so he need not fall into self-abandonment. He calls it a kind of fortune-telling, and adds that self-abandonment is never necessary: one falls into it only because there is nothing else to do. She hopes he won't (P0027–P0030).

**Fireworks.** A sound like a giant tower falling turns out to be fireworks. He has not properly seen fireworks in years (P0033). He has only ever seen them through a window, never eating okonomiyaki from a stall, never holding hands and glancing between the sky and a lover's face (P0034). Since he can remember, he has avoided crowded places. As a child he went to parks, pools, the hill behind the school, shopping streets, summer festivals and fireworks displays only when forced. In high school he kept away from entertainment districts and main streets (P0035). The last time he saw fireworks was when he was very small, and he thinks Himeno was beside him then too (P0036–P0037). He realizes he knows almost nothing about fireworks up close (P0038).

He is tempted to look out of the window, but will not do anything so forlorn in front of Miyagi. He imagines her asking why he doesn't just go and watch them, and wonders why, with so little life left, he still cares about others' eyes (P0039–P0040). As if, he says, to mock his struggle, Miyagi crosses in front of him, opens the screen and leans out to watch. She seems to him not so much moved by something beautiful as impressed by something unusual, but she is apparently not uninterested in such things (P0041).

"Should you be watching that, Ms. Watcher? What if I ran off?" "Do you want to be watched?" she says ironically. He says he wants her gone soon, since being watched makes things awkward. "You must have something to feel guilty about." She then adds that if he runs off and gets beyond a certain distance from her, he will be deemed to intend to cause others trouble, and his lifespan will be extracted and he will die. The distance, she says, "isn't that strict, but roughly a hundred meters, I'd think" (P0042–P0047). He thinks she should have said so at the start (P0048). The fireworks reach their climax, and the party next door has gone quiet; perhaps the neighbors went to see them (P0050).

**The lost thirty years.** Miyagi then speaks of what might have happened (P0051).
- **University and work.** His university life passes in a flash: earning a living, reading, listening to music, sleeping. Empty, indistinguishable days fly by. He graduates with nothing solid and, ironically, takes the kind of job his hopeful younger self despised most. Unable to forget the "once special self," and feeling that "this isn't my real place," he never fits in. He commutes dead-eyed, works himself to the bone and comes to live for drink. His ambition fades, and he becomes far removed from his childhood ideals (P0052).
- **His interjection.** "Not a rare story," he puts in. "No, a perfectly ordinary despair," she agrees, "though the pain one takes from it differs from person to person" (P0053–P0054).
- **The broken pillar.** For him, she says, he had to be more excellent than anyone. With no one to rely on emotionally, he alone had to hold up his world, and when that single pillar broke, the pain was enough to send him toward ruin (P0054–P0055).
- **The crash.** In his late thirties his lonely hobby is riding a motorcycle aimlessly. In a solo crash, with no other car and no pedestrian involved, he loses half his face, the ability to walk and most of his fingers (P0056). He finds this easy to understand and hard to imagine (P0057–P0058).
- **The end.** His looks had been one of his supports, she says. He considers "the last resort" but cannot take the final step. A last drop of hope remains: "even so, something good might happen someday." No one can refute that, she says, but it is only a kind of devil's proof (悪魔の証明). With that hope he lives on to fifty, gains nothing, and dies alone in ruin, loved by no one and remembered by no one, lamenting to the last, "It wasn't supposed to be like this" (P0059).

Strangely, he accepts the account without resistance (P0060–P0061). Asked his impressions, he says he is truly glad he sold the thirty years. The narrator adds that it was not bravado, since what "might have happened" is now what "can never happen" (P0062–P0064). He says he should have kept only three days rather than three months. Miyagi says it is not too late, since he is still allowed two transactions (P0065–P0066). "When I'm down to three days, you'll leave my side too, won't you?" "Yes. If you really can't stand my presence, that is one option" (P0067–P0068). He says he'll remember (P0069).

In fact, he reflects, for someone with no particular hope for the three months, selling all but three days would be the smartest course. What stops him, again, is the devil's-proof hope that "even so, something good might happen someday." These three months are wholly different from the lost thirty years, and the future is not fixed. Something good might happen, something that would make enduring worthwhile. The possibility is not zero, so he cannot die yet (P0070–P0073).

**Night.** He wakes after three in the morning to rain spilling from a broken gutter (P0076). A sweet scent fills the room. He is slow to recognize it as women's shampoo, and concludes by elimination that it is Miyagi's and that she bathed while he slept (P0077–P0078). He finds this hard to accept, since he sleeps so lightly that newspaper deliveries and footsteps upstairs wake him; perhaps the rain covered the sound (P0079). He puts the question off, along with the odd feeling of a girl he has only just met showering in his home. He needs to sleep for tomorrow (P0080). Unable to sleep, he turns to music as usual and puts on one of the CDs he did not sell, 「プリーズ・ミスター・ロストマン」. By his private theory, anyone who listens to that on sleepless nights cannot lead a decent life. With such music, he says, he has too readily forgiven a self that could not fit the world and would not try (P0081). Perhaps he is paying for it now (P0082).

**Ending condition.** It is the small hours after the night of day one, in rain. Kusunoki has heard, at his own request, Miyagi's account of his forfeited life, and is glad to have sold. He has considered selling down to three days, the point at which the watcher leaves. The hope that something good might still happen holds him back. He knows Miyagi's hedged statement of a distance rule. He held back from watching fireworks in front of her while she leaned out to watch.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

1. **The vow and its puncture.** Drunk, Kusunoki vows to make his ¥300,000 "worth more than thirty million or three billion" (P0014). The narrator labels the feeling a drunk's arrogance and 勘違い (P0007). He concedes aloud (P0026). Whether P0024 (「彼女の言い分は、どこまでも正しい」) is his thought at the time or the later narrator's endorsement is undecidable, given its non-past のだ within past narration (B-U05, D1-U05). The figures are OBSERVATION. That the vow re-enacts U02's escalating self-valuations and is deflated by the same appraisal logic is INFERENCE.
2. **Miyagi's diagnosis coincides with the narrator's private analysis.** Several of Miyagi's phrases reproduce wording that had appeared in Kusunoki's *narration*:
   - 一発逆転 and the gambler's reversal (P0019) match Ch02 P0121.
   - 「かつて特別であった自分」 (P0052) matches Ch02 P0120.
   - 「それでも、いつかいいことがあるかもしれない」 (P0059) matches Ch03 P0033.
   - 「こんなはずじゃなかったんだ」 (P0059) matches Ch02 P0017.

   This lexical coincidence is OBSERVATION (D1-U05-30, C-U05-04). There is no evidence that he ever spoke these phrases to her. Three readings remain:
   - (a) her knowledge reaches his inner attitudes;
   - (b) the later narrator, who adopts her label (P0070), has taken over her terms in narrating his own past (B-U05-04);
   - (c) the type is common, and he had just voiced the attitude aloud in his vow (P0009, P0014).

   P0007's self-diagnosis anticipates her P0019 closely. Her claim to have heard "about five" such declarations (P0019) counts declarations she heard. That she has watched others is an INFERENCE (moderate).
3. **The low valuation, as testimony, with a tension.** Miyagi states that his lifespan's value was so low "because you would achieve nothing in your remaining thirty years" (P0019). This ties the price to the forfeited life she then narrates (P0052–P0059). It sits uneasily with U02. There the same clerk said she did not know the assessment in detail, stated that a separate advisory body performs it, and relayed four broader criteria as hearsay (「とは聞いています」, Ch02 P0170) (D1-U05-14/31).

   Her account of the forfeited life is unhedged. She tells it, inner states included, as plain narrative in the second person (あなた about fifteen times). That contrasts with U04's graded certainty, and with her hedging in this chapter of her opinions and of the distance rule. It appears to exceed the "especially important matters" scope she claimed (Ch03 P0108). This is INFERENCE; the rival is that "important matters" may include his psychology (B-U05-03, D1-U05-19/29).
4. **The forfeited life (`TDH-C021`).** Miyagi's narration supplies:
   - a hollow university;
   - a despised job and estrangement from it;
   - drink as the sole pleasure;
   - a psychology: he needed to be more excellent than anyone, and with no emotional support he held up his world alone;
   - a non-fatal solo motorcycle crash in his late thirties, costing half his face, walking and most fingers;
   - "the last resort" (最後の手段) contemplated and not taken, held back by hope (reading it as suicide is INFERENCE, strong);
   - death alone at fifty, unloved and unremembered.

   Death at about fifty is consistent with the assessed remaining thirty years and three months at twenty. That consistency is the appraisal's arithmetic, not a reason. The text gives no cause of death at fifty and no reason why his remaining life was short. The crash concerns the quality of the years, not their number. Ch02 P0173's question, "illness? accident?", is addressed but not answered (D-U05-03).

   The account names no person at all, not even Himeno, though in U03 Miyagi said his life's synopsis was full of her (Ch03 P0110) (B-U05-05, C-U05-06). Kusunoki asks nothing about how she knows (C-U05-04). Miyagi now says what she knows "can never happen" (P0028). That gives him a clear opening to ask whether Himeno's forecast suicide (Ch03 P0101) is likewise cancelled, and he does not take it (C-U05-06).

   **Present-tense character claims?** Miyagi's psychological claims are in the past tense: 誰よりも優秀でいる必要がある人物でした, 精神的に頼るべき相手がいない (P0054), 自身の容姿をよりどころの一つとしていた (P0059). Whether they describe the present Kusunoki is open. The diagnosis fits his own narration (Ch01 P0035; Ch02 P0019, P0120–P0122). 頼るべき相手がいない echoes 親には頼れない (Ch02 P0007). The looks claim is new, and it sits in tension with Ch04 P0070 (「兄の俺が勝っている要素は何一つなかった」) (B-U05-14, C-U05-13, D1-U05-41).

   **The cancelled future's habits.** The forecast routine of reading, music and drink as the only pleasure (P0052) appears in this very night: drinking in the dark (P0004) and a kept CD to get to sleep, 「いつものように」 (P0081). 〝絶対に起こり得ない〟 covers events, not dispositions. That bears on how far the sale "changed his future" (Ch03 P0108). This is INFERENCE; the rival is that the forecast extrapolates known habits (B-U05-06, C-U05-20).
5. **Hope as the hinge.** In the forfeited life, the hope that "something good might happen someday" keeps him alive in ruin to fifty (P0059). In U03 Kusunoki called that same hope both salvation and trap, and welcomed the verdict that removed it (Ch03 P0033–P0035). Here the same hope, named 悪魔の証明的希望, "devil's-proof hope," is what keeps him from selling down to three days (P0070–P0073). OBSERVATION of the recurrence. Two of his lines reverse earlier ones: 「可能性は、ゼロではない」 (P0072) reverses U04's ほぼゼロ (Ch04 P0072), and 「まだ死ぬわけにはいかなかった」 (P0073) reverses U03's 「安心して死ぬことができる」 (Ch03 P0035) (B-U05-02).

   INFERENCE (moderate-strong): the unit makes this hope the hinge between the forfeited life and the three months. Rival (B): it is Miyagi's rhetorical framing, not a design. Kusunoki grants that in the forfeited future it was only a devil's proof, yet lets it govern the present, on the ground that the three months are "wholly different" and "the future is not fixed" (P0071). Rival: he relocates the hope into the three months, which lie outside the appraised future, so it is not the same hope (D1-U05-22).

   His drunken project to "change something" by effort has shrunk to waiting for something good to happen *to* him. The hope is passive, and it is not Miyagi's advice to accumulate small, certain happiness (P0025) (C-U05-01, D1-U05-55). P0071's 「我慢して生きてきてよかったと思えるような出来事」 faintly echoes Himeno's prophecy, 「『生きててよかった』って心の底から思える」 (Ch01 P0070), which Ch02 P0015 judged failed "so far." The text draws no link (INFERENCE, low). `TDH-C017` → v2.
6. **From contest to request.** In U03 he protested that Miyagi's pre-emptive disclosure took away his "freedom to be hurt and disappointed" (Ch03 P0120). Here he *requests* pre-emptive deflation, explicitly to stop over-expecting (P0026), and concedes. His stance moves from contest (U03), through silence (U04), to deference and request. Rivals include drink (P0026), weariness (正論はもうたくさんだ) and the moot status of the thirty years (C-U05-05). Miyagi's handling of the request shows hesitation and care, or at least caution: a pause, then a giving-in (諦めたように, P0027), a caveat that he need not abandon himself (P0028), and a wish that he won't (P0030). Whether that is care or duty, the watcher system exists to prevent 自暴自棄, so it is undecided (B-U05-17, C-U05-10, D1-U05-64). The narrator does not comment on her tone while she tells the account.
7. **Selling down to three days.** Kusunoki says he should have kept only three days (P0065). He asks whether she will then leave his side too (P0067; the も, "you too," is ambiguous in tone). Miyagi confirms that two transactions remain, and calls selling down "one option" if he cannot stand her presence (P0066–P0068). INFERENCE (moderate-strong): the watcher leaves at three days even when a further sale produces them, which partly answers U03's open question about changed lifespans (C-U05-07). This makes the final three days a live, chooseable horizon for the first time. Miyagi frames the option as relief from her; his stated reason for declining is hope. The text supports neither "Miyagi tempts him" nor "he stays for Miyagi" (D1-U05-50/51).
8. **A further rule: the tether.** If he runs off beyond "a certain distance," he will be deemed (見なされ) to intend to cause others trouble (迷惑), and his lifespan will be extracted (抜かれて) (P0045–P0047). The rule is delivered as an aside, with agentless passives. No agent and no report to headquarters are named, unlike Ch03 P0067's mechanism (B-U05-09, C-U05-07). Miyagi hedges the threshold herself: 「そこまで厳密なものではありませんけど、大体、百メートルというところではないでしょうか」. Whether the rule is new, or was in the contract he ignored (Ch02 P0182) or the briefing he forgot (Ch03 P0052, P0065), is open. His complaint that he should have been told first may overlook his own inattention (D1-U05-32). It is Miyagi's statement and unverified (`TDH-C022`).
9. **Fireworks and the gaze of others.** Kusunoki narrates a lifelong avoidance of crowded places (P0035). The exception clause, 「誰かに強いられでもしない限り」, reconciles this with Ch01 P0049, where he and Himeno went out on festival days. He holds back from even looking out of the window because Miyagi is watching (P0039), at least through P0041; whether he looked later is not narrated. He asks why he still minds others' eyes with so little life left (P0040). The Miyagi line he imagines, 「そんなに花火が見たいなら、見にいけばいいんじゃないですか？」 (P0039), is his own construction.

   INFERENCE: the fireworks offer a costless opportunity for list items 3 ("don't resist desire") and 5 ("see beautiful things"; Ch03 P0088, P0090), and he does not take it. The text does not make the link. Rival: he may not count watching through a window as seeing beautiful things, since he calls it わびしい, "forlorn," a poor substitute for going (P0039) (C-U05-02, D1-U05-52). The inhibition before an observer qualifies `TDH-C020`'s "normalized values." It also sits against his U03 gloss that trust loses value once death is certain (Ch03 P0066).

   Miyagi leans out to watch. His reading, that she is "impressed by the unusual" rather than moved but "apparently not uninterested" (P0041), is a hedged qualification of his own. The analyst counts it, with the inferred bath, as counterevidence to his U03–U04 verdict that she is machine-like. His non-human figures persist (座敷童, P0005).
10. **The shampoo, and not inquiring.** He infers that Miyagi bathed while he slept (P0078). He finds it odd that he did not wake (P0079), offers the rain as cover, and defers the question: 「その結論を先送りにした」, 「考えないことにした」 (P0080). This is his second explicit decision not to pursue something about Miyagi, after Ch03 P0128 (C-U05-16). He leaves unraised the obvious explanation that he was heavily drunk (P0004). He accepts the biography readily (すんなり, P0061) but balks at the bath (受け入れがたかった, P0079) (B-U05-20).

**Not established.**
- What became of the people whose declarations Miyagi heard.
- The cause of death at fifty, and why his remaining life was short.
- Whether the forfeited life involved Himeno at all (`OPEN`; §11).
- Whether "can never happen" covers Himeno's forecast suicide.
- Whether Miyagi's past-tense character claims describe him now.
- Whether Miyagi actually bathed, and where she was at three in the morning.
- How strictly the distance rule applies, and whether it is new.
- Whether he looked at the fireworks after P0041.
- What Miyagi thinks of the fireworks.

## 5. Narration, focalization and time

**Retrospective labeling.** The narrator pre-judges his drunk exaltation as 勘違い (P0007). It is the word he used for his childhood sense of greatness (Ch01 P0035). The narrator also marks the vow's appeal as how it sounded "to my drunk head" (P0015). P0024's endorsement of Miyagi is voice-undecidable (§4.1).

**Miyagi's speech as set piece.** Two long direct-speech passages carry the unit: the diagnosis (P0019, P0025) and the forfeited life (P0052, P0054, P0056, P0059). His interjections are brief (P0053, P0055). Narrated reaction is sparse. He finds half a face easy to understand but hard to imagine (P0057–P0058), and the account strangely easy to accept (P0060–P0061).

**Tense in the forfeited life.** Miyagi begins in the non-past (終わります, 就きます, 馴染めません). She shifts into the past at P0054, right after his interjection (でした, しかなかったんです). She stays there through the late thirties (差し掛かっていました, 失いました) and returns to the non-past at the end (死んでいきます) (B-U05-03). The tense pattern is OBSERVATION. Its effect, a future told as already concluded, is INFERENCE.

**Night interior.** The closing section returns to Kusunoki's solitary sensibility: rain, the shampoo scent, his light sleep, a song. The aphorism about listeners on sleepless nights sits mid-paragraph (P0081). The chapter ends on a hedged ledger line, 「その付けを、今、払わされているのかもしれない」 (P0082). Whether 今 is that night or the time of narration is undecidable.

## 6. Japanese language and form

- **Sesame-dot emphasis.** 〔傍点〕ようやくスタート地点に立った〔/傍点〕 (P0019), "have *only just reached the starting line*," is the sixth emphasis. Like the fifth (Ch04 P0046), it falls inside Miyagi's speech and marks a verdict on where he stands (`TDH-F015`).
- **The title, and the modal pattern.** これから起こることすべて, "everything that will happen from now on," is Kusunoki's request (P0026), repeating his U03 question (Ch03 P0106). He self-corrects at once to 「どう過ごすことになっていた」, "how was I going to spend." The past form かもしれなかった ("might have") occurs only inside 〝〟 for Miyagi's term for the closed might-have-been (P0028, P0051, P0064). The hope formula moves from her non-past quotation (P0059) to his own (P0070–P0071). Non-past かもしれない is otherwise common in the chapter (P0026, P0028, P0039, P0050, P0082) (B-U05-01). INFERENCE (medium): the title names what no one can tell him, the open future of the three months. Rival (B): it simply labels Miyagi's account, which she presents as complete.
- **悪魔の証明, and her words in his mouth.** Miyagi's 「ある種の悪魔の証明に過ぎません」 (P0059) names the impossibility of disproving that "something good might happen." Kusunoki takes the phrase over as 「悪魔の証明的希望」 (P0070). It joins other terms of hers now in his narration: 自ら…飛び込んで (P0004; compare Ch03 P0124) and 馴染めず (P0081; compare P0052). He takes her label, conceding that the hope is unprovable, and refuses her conclusion (B-U05-04; `TDH-F016`).
- **Market and gambling vocabulary.** 三千万や三十億よりも価値のある三十万 (P0014); 賭け事, 一発逆転, 負け続けのギャンブル (P0019); 勝とうなんて思うから負けるんですよ。負けの中に勝ちを見いだす (P0025). Miyagi's advice inverts the win-or-lose frame without leaving it (`TDH-F005` recurrence).
- **幸せ in Miyagi's mouth.** Miyagi uses 幸せ twice. She sets 非現実的な幸せ, "unrealistic happiness" (P0019), against 小さくても確実な幸せ, "small, certain happiness" (P0025). The chapter also has 幸い (P0004) and 不幸中の幸い (P0056). The title's 幸福 does not occur.

## 12. Exiting state

No day break is marked. The unit continues the night of day one, through a nearby fireworks display, and ends in the small hours after it, past 3 a.m. by the clock, in rain (INFERENCE, strong; §10).

**The vow and Miyagi's reply.**
- Drunk, Kusunoki vowed to "change something" in his three months with the ¥300,000. He would make it "worth more than thirty million or three billion" and "strike back at this world." The narrator calls the feeling behind it a drunk's arrogance and a misapprehension (P0007).
- Miyagi said such vows are common: she has heard similar lines "about five times." He has, she said, only just reached the starting line. His lifespan was valued so low, she said, because he will achieve nothing in the remaining thirty years. That causal claim is her testimony. In U02 she disclaimed detailed knowledge of the assessment and relayed four broader criteria (Ch02 P0170).
- She advised piling up small, certain happiness. He conceded aloud, tired of "sound arguments" (P0026). Whether the narrator also endorses her (P0024) is undecidable.

**The lost thirty years (Miyagi's testimony, at his request).**
- He asked to be told "everything that will happen," then corrected himself: how he would have spent the lost thirty years. He wanted to stop expecting too much of himself.
- After a pause she agreed. She said what she knows "might have happened" but now "can never happen," and that he need not become 自暴自棄 (self-abandonment), the watcher system's word.
- Her account hedges no event and names no other person. It runs:
  - a hollow university life;
  - a job his younger self despised;
  - drink as his only pleasure;
  - a solo motorcycle crash in his late thirties that cost him half his face, his ability to walk and most of his fingers;
  - "the last resort" (suicide, by strong inference) contemplated and not taken, held back by the hope that "something good might happen someday," which she called a devil's proof;
  - death alone at fifty, unloved and unremembered.
- The crash is not fatal, and no cause of death at fifty is given. The account omits Himeno, though in U03 Miyagi said his life's synopsis was full of her.
- He asked nothing about how she knows, and did not ask whether "can never happen" also covers Himeno's forecast suicide. He found his ready acceptance "strange" and said he was glad he sold. The narrator says this was not bravado.

**The three-day option.**
- He said he should have kept only three days. Miyagi said it is not too late, since two transactions remain. She confirmed that the watcher leaves when three days remain, and called selling down "one option" if he cannot stand her presence. He said he would remember.
- He reasons that, with no particular hope for the three months, selling down would be the smartest course. What stops him is the hope that something good might happen to him, which he now calls, in her term, a "devil's-proof hope."
- He holds that the three months are wholly different from the lost thirty years and that his future is not fixed: "the possibility is not zero," so he "couldn't die yet."
- This reverses, or relocates into the three months, the relief from hope he reported in U03 (Ch03 P0033–P0035). The hope is for something to happen to him, not Miyagi's advice to build small, certain happiness.

**The distance rule (Miyagi's statement, hedged).** If he runs off and gets beyond a certain distance from her, he will be deemed to intend to cause others trouble (迷惑), and his lifespan will be extracted and he will die. On the distance she said it "isn't that strict, but roughly a hundred meters, I'd think." Whether he had been told before, in the contract he did not attend to or the briefing he forgot, is open.

**The fireworks.**
- He wanted to look out of the window but held back because Miyagi was watching, and asked himself why, with so little life left, he still minds others' eyes. Whether he looked later is not narrated.
- He recounted a lifelong avoidance of crowded places. He has a hedged memory that Himeno was beside him at the last fireworks he saw, in early childhood.
