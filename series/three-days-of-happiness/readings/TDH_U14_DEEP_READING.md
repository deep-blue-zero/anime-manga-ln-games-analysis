## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**The change.** The change took clear shape, the narrator says, when he had fewer than fifty days left (P0004). Many people disliked his conduct, he says; some whispered, some insulted him loudly, and he had no right to complain (P0005–P0006). One day three men in an izakaya harass him as he talks to an empty seat. He waits for them to tire, but they grow bolder (P0007–P0008). "This is a problem," Miyagi says, looking worried (P0009).

**Shinbashi.** A man's voice says, "Isn't it Kusunoki? With Miyagi again today?" (P0010–P0011). It is the neighbour who used to look disgusted when Kusunoki came and went talking to her; his name, Kusunoki recalls, was Shinbashi (P0012–P0015). Politely but with menace, Shinbashi asks one of the men for his seat; the men, it seems, lose interest and leave (P0016, P0021). Shinbashi introduces himself to Miyagi, and nods as if she had answered. They are not really conversing, so he cannot actually see her, Kusunoki gathers; perhaps he is pretending (P0017–P0020).

He does not really believe in this "Miyagi," Shinbashi says, nor was he simply helping. His view is that Kusunoki is performing, testing how far a perfect mime can make others feel an invisible girl is real; on him, he says, it has partly worked. He takes an interest in the change in himself: if he keeps accepting her, he wonders, he might even come to see her (P0022–P0028). Kusunoki describes her: not tall, fair, slight, cool eyes, an occasional modest smile, thin-framed glasses for small print, semi-long hair curling inward. Every feature, Shinbashi says, matches what he had imagined (P0029–P0030). Kusunoki asks him to hold out his right hand. Miyagi takes it in both of hers and shakes it, delighted, as Kusunoki narrates. Through Kusunoki she thanks him; "I somehow thought so," Shinbashi says (P0031–P0041). Before returning to his own table he says others probably sense her too, and tell themselves it is an illusion; with some trigger, such as learning that they are not alone, everyone might accept her. He has no grounds, he says, but hopes so (P0042–P0043).

**Acceptance.** Shinbashi was right, the narrator says. People began to accept Miyagi's existence, not as belief but as a shared convention, playing along with his nonsense (P0046–P0048). At town amusements, a school festival and local festivals he became, it seems, a minor celebrity, treated as "a pitiable, amusing person." Not a few watched him, holding hands with and embracing what to them was an imaginary lover, with indulgent eyes (P0049).

One night Shinbashi invites them to his room, where three friends are drinking (P0050–P0052). Suzumi touches Miyagi's arm: "Now you say it, I kind of feel it." Miyagi gently holds her hand, Kusunoki narrates (P0053–P0055). Asakura probes for contradictions; finding the story consistent, he gives Miyagi's place a cushion and a glass. "I like women like that," he says; "lucky for you I can't see her." "No use either way," Kusunoki says: "Miyagi likes me." "Don't say such things," Miyagi says, hitting him with a cushion (P0056–P0059). Riko asks him to prove how much he loves Miyagi (P0060). "Miyagi," he says. "Yes." He kisses her, and the drunks cheer. None of them truly believes she is there, he thinks; they take him for a mad, amusing fool. But what is wrong with that? That summer, he says, he was the town's greatest clown, for better or worse (P0061–P0066).

**The car keys.** Some days later Shinbashi tosses him car keys: he is going home and won't need the car; take Miyagi to the sea or the mountains (P0069–P0073). He cannot see Kusunoki as simply lying, he says; perhaps there is a world only Kusunoki can see (P0074–P0075). There is a faint smell of autumn; the cicadas are ending the summer (P0076–P0078).

**The sketchbook.** That night they share the futon; the boundary between them has gone. She sleeps facing him, and he finds her sleeping face dear (P0079–P0080). Getting up for water, he finds "the sketchbook," hers by strong inference, dropped by the changing-room door and opens it (P0081). It holds drawings of the places of their days: the station waiting room, the restaurant where he met Naruse, the time-capsule school, his and Himeno's secret base, the room strewn with cranes, the library, festival stalls, the riverside, the observatory, the community hall, the Cub, the sweet shop, vending machines, the public phone, the star lake, the old bookshop, the swan boat, the Ferris wheel. And his sleeping face (P0082–P0084).

In return he begins to draw her sleeping face. Only when he finishes does he realize it is the first drawing he has completed in years, the drawing he thought he had given up (P0085–P0087). He feels a faint unease he could easily ignore, but he feels certain (確信があった), and searches for tens of minutes until, by chance, he grasps it. Then, as if possessed, he draws all night (P0088–P0097).

**Fireworks.** A few days later they go to a fireworks display at a primary school (P0100). Children point and laugh, "It's Kusunoki!", kindly, as he reads it; high-school students tease, "Your girlfriend's lovely." He puts his arm around her: "Nice, isn't she? You can't have her" (P0101–P0102). He is glad: they seem to enjoy his nonsense, even without believing it. Better an enjoyed fiction, he says, than an ignored truth (P0103–P0104).

Lying behind the school building, he steals a glance at her and their eyes meet; she seemed, he says, to have had the same idea (P0105–P0109). She is off tomorrow, she says, but back the day after; only one day this time (P0110–P0114). He tells her he is now a minor favourite of the town, he says; half the smiles mock, half are goodwill, and he is proud of it (P0117). As a boy he hated a classmate who hid his cleverness and clowned to be liked; lately he has understood that he envied him. He always wanted to get on with everyone, and thanks to Miyagi he has: he has made peace with the world (P0119). Thank you for everything, he says (P0123). "And 'please go on,'" she says: there is more than a month left (P0124).

**His wish.** She wanted to know his wish, he says (P0125). Anything she can do, she says (P0127). His small wish: when he dies, forget him completely (P0128). "No," she says at once. Then, it seems, she guesses what he means to do tomorrow, and begs him not to do anything foolish (P0129–P0132). Who could have foreseen, he says, that a thirty-yen man would have such a remaining life? Then her future is unknown too: a more reliable man may appear and make her happy (P0134). "[Such a man] won't appear." "But for me, you were never supposed to appear either. So for you—" "[He] won't appear" (P0135–P0137; the subject is unexpressed). She pushes him down and buries her face in his chest. It is the first time he has heard her cry (P0138–P0141). At least for one more month, she begs, stay beside her. She endures everything else, she says: that he will die soon, that they cannot meet on her days off, that they cannot show others their joined hands, that after he dies she must live thirty years alone. So do not throw away the time they have together (P0142). He strokes her head as she sobs. They sleep in each other's arms; her tears, he says, did not stop to the very end (P0143–P0145).

**Farewell.** At midnight Miyagi leaves the apartment. At the door they embrace again; she draws away reluctantly, as he reads it, with a lonely-seeming smile. "Goodbye. I was happy" (the subject of 幸せでした is unexpressed). She bows and walks away in the moonlight (P0148–P0152).

**The sale.** Next morning, with the substitute, he goes to the old building where he and Miyagi first met. He sells thirty of his remaining thirty-three days; the last three days, he is told, are not bought (P0155–P0158). Seeing the appraisal, the substitute is surprised: "You came here knowing 〔傍点〕this would happen〔/傍点〕?" "Yes" (P0159–P0161). The appraiser, a woman in her thirties, says she does not recommend it. Money no longer matters, she says; if he went home now, he is set to (ことになってる) paint for the remaining thirty-three days, with the watcher girl beside him encouraging him, his name would stay in art history forever. What more could he want? (P0162–P0165). "Fame is as meaningless after death as money," he says. "Don't you want to be eternal?" "Becoming eternal in a world without me would not make me glad at all" (P0166–P0168).

**What was sold.** His paintings, it seems, would later have been called "the world's most vulgar paintings," stirred controversy, and won great acclaim. Having sold the thirty days, that is now something that could have happened but never can (P0171–P0173). He chooses to think, he says, that such a talent would normally have taken an immense time to bloom; that he was fated to lose the chance in a traffic accident first; and that selling his lifespan, and above all Miyagi's being beside him, shortened that time so that it bloomed before he died (P0174–P0176).

**His painting.** He was once good at drawing, he says, with a talent anyone who knew him then would grant (P0179–P0181). At seventeen, in winter, he stopped: he would not become the great person he had promised Himeno to become that way, and he needed a revolution. He would not draw again until everything meshed (P0182–P0185). At nineteen, in summer, out of impatience, he let himself paint, at the worst possible time, and lost the ability; he could not draw even an apple (P0186–P0187). He gave up and withdrew (P0188). The cause, he now thinks, was trying to make everyone accept his work. Universality lies not in pandering but in what is drawn up from the bottom of one's own well (P0189). To see this he needed to draw once purely for pleasure, 〔傍点〕for himself〔/傍点〕; Miyagi gave him that chance, through her sleeping face (P0190). That night he drew the scenes he had pictured every night before sleep since about five: the world he wished to live in, memories that never were, places he had never been. His technique was complete, though just before death (P0191).

According to the appraiser, the paintings of the lost thirty days would have been "like de Chirico made unbearably sweet," her interpretation, he says; it is the kind of thing he would paint (P0192). The right to paint them and leave a small name in history sold for an unbelievably high price. It was not quite enough to clear Miyagi's debt, but with about three more years of work, it seems, she will be free (P0193). "Thirty days worth more than thirty years," the substitute says with a laugh as they part (P0194–P0195). And so, the narrator says, he failed to become eternal (P0196).

**The prophecy.** The "summer ten years on" that Himeno once foretold is about to end. Her prophecy was half wrong: he never became great or rich. It was half right: "something very good" did happen, and he could think from the heart, "I'm glad I lived" (P0197–P0201; Ch01 P0070).

**Ending condition.** After the sale, on a late-summer day; by the shop's terms he has three days left (P0158). Miyagi has said goodbye.
- **Acceptance.** By the narrator's account, through Shinbashi the town has come to treat Miyagi's presence as a shared convention, not belief. Kusunoki is a known clown, mocked and liked. She laments that they still cannot show others their joined hands (P0142).
- **The sketchbook and the painting.** He found drawings of their days in the sketchbook (hers, by strong inference), drew her sleeping face, and, by his account, recovered his ability to draw.
- **His wish.** He asked her to forget him when he dies. She refused, wept, and begged him to stay beside her for the last month.
- **Farewell.** She said goodbye at midnight before her day off.
- **The sale.** He sold thirty of his remaining thirty-three days, against the appraiser's advice and against Miyagi's plea. The price was very high and fell just short of her debt (narrated; that it went to her debt is a strong inference); she will be free after about three more years of work, it seems (hearsay).
- **The prophecy.** He says Himeno's prophecy came half true: he is glad he lived.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

1. **The time marker** (P0004). 「俺の寿命が五十日を切った頃」. About day forty or later. The narrator reports the change retrospectively.
2. **The harassment** (P0005–P0009).
   - **His standing.** The narrator grants others' disgust: 「先に不快感を与えたのは俺の方なのだから」 (P0006). VALUE_JUDGMENT, his.
   - **The three men.** Their character is his reading (P0007, 退屈していたのだろう).
   - **His restraint.** 「以前の俺ならば…今の俺はもうそういうことにエネルギーを割こうとは思えなかった」 (P0008).
3. **Shinbashi's intervention** (P0010–P0021).
   - **Identity.** The neighbour who looked at him 「気味悪そうな」 (P0014; cf. Ch11 P0094). Name recalled with 確か…はずだ (P0015).
   - **Method.** Polite words, intimidating manner; his size and 「人を脅すのに慣れたような目付き」 (P0016), the narrator's reading.
   - **Speaking to Miyagi.** He addresses her and nods 「まるでミヤギが何かしらの返事をしたかのように」 (P0018); the "reply" he answers is his own invention (D1). His 「あなたの話はいつもクスノキさんから聞いてる」 (P0017) is overheard talk (B; cf. P0014) or part of the performance (C). 「会話は成立していない」, so he cannot see her (P0019); 〝ふり〟 is Kusunoki's guess (P0020). Miyagi is 呆気にとられた (P0018).
4. **Shinbashi's account** (P0022–P0043), testimony.
   - **His position.** Not belief: 「本気でその〝ミヤギ〟とかいう女の子がいることを信じているわけじゃありません」 (P0022). Not simple help (P0024).
   - **His theory.** A performance testing how far 「完全なパントマイム」 can unsettle others' perception; on him 「そこそこ成功しています」 (P0026). Compare the townspeople's "skilled mime" (Ch13 P0102). 錯覚 (P0026, P0043) recalls Kusunoki's own Ch04 sense of being like a couple (Ch04 P0061) (D1).
   - **The match.** Kusunoki's description of Miyagi (P0029) matches, Shinbashi says, his own imagined picture 「一から十まで」 (P0030). That is his testimony. No explanation is given.
   - **The handshake.** Miyagi shakes his hand with both hands (P0035). Shinbashi looks at his hand moving up and down (上下に揺さぶられる自分の手を見て, P0036); the passive belongs to Kusunoki's focalization, and what Shinbashi experiences is given only by Kusunoki's claim (P0037; cf. Ch06 P0060). Her delight is Kusunoki's narration (嬉しそうに, P0035) and speech (相当喜んでいる, P0037).
   - **His hope.** Others sense her too and dismiss it; a trigger could bring everyone to accept her (P0043). 「何の根拠もない話です」, by his own admission.
   - **Physical description.** P0029 gathers her appearance, repeating the thin-framed glasses first seen at the shop (Ch02 P0110; Ch03 P0041–P0049). INFERENCE: it is given at the point where her presence becomes socially real.
5. **Acceptance as convention** (P0046–P0049).
   - **The narrator's verdict.** 「シンバシの予想は正しかった」 (P0046). Not belief: 「俺のたわごとを、共通の約束事として扱い、話をあわせてくれるようになったというだけだ」; 「ミヤギの存在が仮説の域を出ることはなかった」 (P0048).
   - **Fame.** 「ちょっとした有名人になったようだった」, hedged; 「可哀想で面白い人」 (P0049). This is the narrated consequence of the U13 fame (F-U13-e).
6. **The party** (P0050–P0066).
   - **Next door.** In Ch04 he called the chance of a warm welcome home 「ほぼゼロ」, and sardonically rated being accepted into the neighbours' party 「まだ高い」 by comparison (Ch04 P0072). Here he is invited (C).
   - **Suzumi.** 「いわれてみれば、そんな気もするねえ」 (P0054). Miyagi holds her hand (P0055); the narrator hedges that some 存在感 may not be wholly erased (かもしれない).
   - **Asakura.** Tests for contradictions; finds them absent; makes room for her (P0056). His remark about liking such women (P0057).
   - **Kusunoki's claim.** 「ミヤギは俺のことが好きだから」 (P0058). Her protest, 「勝手なこといわないでください」, with the cushion (P0059), is not a denial of the substance. INFERENCE.
   - **The kiss.** 「ミヤギに、俺はキスをした」 (P0063). Here キス is used of the two of them for the first time, unlike Ch13 (Ch13 P0157–P0160). It is offered to onlookers as "proof" of his love (P0060). She blushes (顔をほんのり赤らめて), in his narration. He makes no spoken avowal of love to her (D1).
   - **His self-judgment.** 「この夏、俺はこの町で、一番の道化だった。／良くも、悪くも」 (P0065–P0066).
7. **Shinbashi's farewell** (P0069–P0078).
   - **The car.** He lends his car while he is away (P0072). Whether it is used is not narrated in U14.
   - **His last view.** 「あるいは実際に、あなたにしか見えていない世界があるのかもしれませんね」 (P0075). Testimony, hedged.
   - **Season.** 秋の匂い; つくつく法師 (P0077–P0078). Late summer.
8. **The sketchbook** (P0079–P0097).
   - **The futon.** 「境界線はいつの間にかなくなっていた」 (P0079), recalling the Ch11 arrangement in which he took the left edge of the futon himself and left the rest to her (Ch11 P0015–P0019). 境界 does not occur earlier (lexical check). Her sleeping face 「愛おしかった」 (P0080): his feeling, named.
   - **The object.** 例のスケッチブック (P0081) has no earlier lexical antecedent. Two candidates: (i) her drawing ノート, which she declined to show him (Ch11 P0106, P0112–P0114), moderate (B); (ii) the notebook left at his pillow (Ch11 P0215), which he called her 「観察記録」 (Ch12 P0045) and the substitute skimmed (Ch12 P0048), HYPOTHESIS (B). Whether (i) and (ii) are one object is itself unstated; C gives low confidence to any identity.
   - **Opening it.** He opens it while she sleeps, though in Ch11 she deflected his request to see her drawings in her ノート (C). INFERENCE: she was keeping memories, as she asked of him (Ch13 P0087).
   - **Contents.** Eighteen places of their days, in story order, from the station waiting room to the Ferris wheel (P0083), and 「俺の寝顔」 (P0084). OBSERVATION.
   - **Ch12 revisited.** If the sketchbook is the Ch11–Ch12 ノート, Kusunoki's Ch12 supposition that it was her 「観察記録」 of him (Ch12 P0045), and the substitute's two-minute skim, read differently. Not established (B). Several (the secret base, the time-capsule school) are places of his past with Himeno, visited with Miyagi in U07 (Ch07 P0057, P0078).
   - **His drawing.** 「仕返しに、ミヤギの寝顔を描きはじめた」 (P0085). The first drawing completed in years (P0086); 「挫折したはずだった、絵」 (P0087).
   - **The unease and the certainty.** A 違和感 easily ignored (P0088–P0089); 「しかし、確信があった」 (P0090); he grasps it after tens of minutes, 偶然 (P0093–P0095). What he understood is given only later, in P0189–P0191.
   - **Authorship.** That the drawings are hers is inferred from 「仕返しに」 (P0085) (D1). He carries 「スケッチブック」 to the sale (P0164). Whether she saw his drawings is not narrated.
9. **The fireworks** (P0100–P0124).
   - **Ch05 enacted.** In Ch05, fireworks were always seen through a window, never hand in hand (Ch05 P0034); here they are (B).
   - **The town.** Children's 「クスノキさんだー」 (P0101), 好意的な笑い, his reading. High-school students: 「恋人さん、素敵っすね」 (P0102).
   - **His maxim.** 「相手にされない真実より、楽しまれる虚構の方が、ずっといい」 (P0104). VALUE_JUDGMENT, his. INFERENCE: 虚構, like 架空の恋人 (P0049) and たわごと (P0048, P0103), voices the onlookers' frame; he does not retract her presence (D1, C).
   - **Her day off.** 「明日は休日ですけど、明後日には戻ってきますよ。前回と違って、いなくなるのはたった一日だけ」 (P0114). Testimony; the previous day off was two days (Ch12 P0007).
   - **The town's word, and his.** The hedged 有名人 (P0049) becomes 人気者 in his own mouth (P0117) (B).
   - **Peace with the world.** His self-report, in speech: the boy he hated and envied (P0119), by strong inference Ch01's 「どのクラスにも一人はいるような頭のいい道化」 (Ch01 P0031) (D1): 「世界と仲直りすることに、俺は成功した」. INFERENCE: it answers his Ch13 「自身の体たらくを世界のせいにする必要もない」 (Ch13 P0137), and his Ch10 bedtime habit as his only way to come to terms with the world (Ch10 P0242) (B). It stands against Ch04 P0065, 「これまで、こんな気持ちになったことはなかった」: there he said he had never envied before. C's rival: the Ch04 claim was a defensive misreading, as 「最近になって、わかってきた」 implies.
   - **Her reply.** 「まだ一か月以上あるんですよ」 (P0124). At the sale, 33 days remain (P0165).
10. **His wish** (P0125–P0145).
    - **The wish.** 「俺が死んだら、俺のことはきれいさっぱり忘れてくれ。それが俺の、ささやかな願いごと」 (P0128). It answers her Ch13 request to know his wish (Ch13 P0125). It reverses his Ch12 request, 「俺が死んだら、たくさん悲しんでくれ」 (Ch12 P0205), and answers her Ch12 「あなたのこと、忘れられないじゃないですか」 (Ch12 P0204). It inverts her Ch13 「借金のことは、もう忘れてください」 (Ch13 P0085) (D1). ささやか gives the Ch12 title's 「小さな願い」 a lexical neighbour, two chapters on. INFERENCE.
    - **Her inference.** 「明日、俺が何をしようとしているのか、感付いたらしかった」 (P0131): the narrator's reading of her (ようだった, らしかった, P0130–P0131). What he intends is not stated until P0157. He had planned an 「打ち明け話」 (P0113), but never names the act to her; his 「ところが、そうもいかないんだ」 (P0111) already presupposes a parting (C).
    - **His argument.** A thirty-yen man's unforeseeable happiness implies her future is open; 「もっと頼り甲斐のある男が現れて」 (P0134). It cites 「ミヤギも読んだっていう査定結果」. Yet next day he sells a future the appraisers foresee, and accepts their foresight (P0161) (C). C's own rival: no contradiction, since the old appraisal could not foresee a life changed by Miyagi, while the new one values an ability already realized.
    - **Her offer, his wish.** Her 「私にできることなら、何でもしますよ」 (P0127) meets the one wish she refuses (P0129). It also opposes her Ch13 request for memories (Ch13 P0087).

## 5. Narration, focalization and time

**The narrating present.** 「この記録」 is not named in U14 (D1). 「三十日を売り払ってしまった今」 (P0173), 「俺はそう考えることにしている」 (P0176) and 「〝十年後の夏〟が、いよいよ終わろうとしている」 (P0197) place the narrating instance after the sale, at summer's end. INFERENCE, strong: the record is being written within the three days the shop did not buy. That fits Ch13's 「俺がこの記録に割ける時間も少なくなってきた」 (Ch13 P0004). Other self-reference markers: 先述したように (P0005), この夏 (P0065), あの習慣 (P0191) (B). B's rival: the location of the present depends on two testimony-based counts, the thirty-three days (P0165) and the three-day term (そうだ, P0158). Ch02's 「外れた」 was framed by 「今のところ」 (Ch02 P0015): earlier chapters voiced their own time's knowledge, which bears on when passages were written (C).

**Disclosure order.** The night's understanding (P0095) is withheld for some eighty paragraphs and explained only after the sale (P0189–P0191). The sale itself is narrated before its rationale.

**Counterfactual knowledge.** What he would have painted, and how it would have been received, comes from the appraisal (らしい, P0172, P0192). The narrator states it as hearsay.

**Focalization.** Through Kusunoki. Others' perceptions and feelings are reported as their testimony (Shinbashi, Suzumi, Asakura, the appraiser) or as his readings (Miyagi's delight, her guess, her sad smile).

**Depicted time.** Assuming "three months" (Ch02 P0162) is about ninety days: from when fewer than fifty days remained (P0004) through some days (P0069), a few more days (P0100), the fireworks night, Miyagi's midnight departure, and the sale the next morning, with thirty-three days remaining (P0165). Late summer (P0077–P0078).

## 11. Competing interpretations, claims and forecasts

**Interpretation A: a candidate chain for Ch13's "wisest decision."** The narrator credits Miyagi's presence (P0175, P0190), not the "foolish" resolution itself (D1). The foolish life (Ch13 P0090) made Miyagi's presence socially real (P0046–P0049); drawing for himself, the chance for which Miyagi gave him through her sleeping face (P0190), raised the value of his remaining days; their sale nearly frees her (P0193). The narrator does not connect his public conduct to the drawing: the occasion was the sketchbook found at night (P0081–P0085). HYPOTHESIS, low to moderate (B, C, D1).
- Weakeners: the narrator never links P0193 to Ch13 P0090. The sale goes against her request (P0142) and against Ch13's apparent displacement of the debt goal.
- Rival: the "wisest decision" is shown by his own summation, 「生きててよかった」 (P0201), not by the sale. Or its reason lies later in the record.

**Interpretation B: value given and value refused.** The appraisal values his month by future fame (P0165). He refuses fame after death, 「俺がいない世界で俺が永遠になっても」 (P0168), and spends its price on her. INFERENCE, strong. It continues U12–U13's relational view of value. The irony, voiced by the substitute: thirty days worth more than thirty years (P0194).
- Rival: the sale is also self-protective, a refusal to watch her endure (HYPOTHESIS); the text gives only his stated reasons. His money/fame parallel (P0166) leaves Miyagi out (D1).

**Interpretation C: the unilateral, concealed gift.** She asked him not to throw away their remaining time (P0142). He sells it on the day she is away (P0155). INFERENCE, moderate: the act repeats the structure of her price lie, a unilateral, concealed gift, which she called 「ひとりよがりな利他行為」 and 「歪んだ厚意の押し付け」 (Ch12 P0170, P0172).
