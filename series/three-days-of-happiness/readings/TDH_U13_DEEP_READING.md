## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**The plan.** The story is nearing its end, the narrator says, and his time for this record is running short. He may not finish, and must thin the writing (P0004–P0005). He had resolved to devote his remaining life to her debt, he says, but the premise was impossible: her debt far exceeds the lifetime wages of a salaryman that Himeno once cited (P0006; Ch01 P0009). He weighs the means. Honest work could at most repay the ¥300,000 she gave him, and he does not think she would want his last months spent that way. Crime she would not want; gambling he rejects (P0007–P0010). He walks for days, eating little, thinking how to pay her debt, how to let her sleep safely, how not to leave her alone after he is gone (P0011–P0013). His last hope is the shop: he still has two uses left (P0014).

**Her objection.** He asks Miyagi what his lifespan would fetch now that he is happier. Value is fluid to a degree, she says, but subjective happiness does not affect it that much; what counts is objectively measurable "happiness," and social contribution and fame were, she thinks, heavily favoured (P0015–P0019). Don't think anything strange, she says, looking worried. She thinks she knows: he is trying to pay her debt. It makes her happy, she says, but she does not want him to spend his time on it; if it is for her happiness, it is misguided. What would make her happy? "Pay attention to me," she says, sulking: he has hardly talked to her lately (P0020–P0027). He grants that she is right, but has his pride. Becoming someone with recognizable value would, it seemed to him, raise his price; whether that or earning money is more realistic, he cannot tell (P0028–P0031).

**Two advisers.** He goes to the old bookseller, listening to baseball on the radio. He does not mention the shop: that would mean telling him that less than two months remain, and might make him feel responsible. Hiding Miyagi's presence for once, he talks of weather, baseball, books and festivals, and feels at peace; he supposes he liked the shop and the old man (P0034–P0038). While Miyagi looks at the shelves, he asks quietly how to raise his own worth. Do things steadily, one by one, the old man says; but more important, don't trust advice from a failure like him. Knowing failure is not knowing success, he says; fixing failure only brings you to a grey starting point (P0039–P0044). Kusunoki recalls Miyagi saying something similar (P0045; Ch05 P0019). At the end the old man asks: you're thinking of selling your lifespan 〔傍点〕again〔/傍点〕, aren't you? "What do you mean?" Kusunoki says, laughing it off (P0046–P0049).

At the CD shop the blond clerk, asked out of Miyagi's hearing how to achieve something in limited time, says: rely on others (P0050–P0055). He lends Kusunoki an umbrella against a sudden downpour: whatever you're doing, health comes first (P0056–P0057). Under the small umbrella both get soaked shoulders. Passersby stare; to them, the narrator supposes, he must look like a fool holding his umbrella in the wrong place (P0058). "I like this," Miyagi says: his wet left shoulder looks silly to others but has a warm meaning (P0059–P0061). He blushes. "Shameless, and shy," she says (P0062–P0063). He no longer minds being taken for a lunatic, the narrator says; he enjoys it, because it makes her laugh (P0064).

**"Miyagi is certainly here."** Sheltering under the eaves of a shop, he meets the acquaintance, who asks coldly where he has been (P0067–P0070). With his hand on Miyagi's shoulder, Kusunoki says he has been out with "this girl, Miyagi." "Not funny. Creepy," says the man (P0071–P0072). He would react the same way, Kusunoki says, but Miyagi is certainly here; he respects the man's disbelief, and asks him to respect his belief (P0073). You're crazy, the man says: you've shut yourself in your shell; look at the outside world. He leaves (P0074–P0075). On a bench after the rain, Miyagi thanks him for "what happened just now" and leans on his shoulder; he combs her hair with his fingers (P0076–P0079).

**Memories instead.** "Steadily," he murmurs, the old man's word (P0080–P0081). Perhaps, he thinks, he was too bound to the debt; there are things he can surely do for her happiness. She asked him to pay attention to her, and being taken for a suspicious man seems to please her a great deal. Why not do what is in front of him? (P0082–P0083). As if reading his change of mind, she speaks. His using his last life to save her makes her truly happy, she says, but there is no need: she is already saved. Decades after he is gone, she thinks, she will recall their days and cry and laugh alone, and the memories will make living easier. So forget her debt (P0084–P0085). Instead, leaning on him, she asks for memories, as many as possible, to warm her when she is lonely after he is gone (P0086–P0087).

**The foolish decision.** So, the narrator says, he resolved to live out his life as a person more foolish than anyone he had ever met. Ironically, he says, that was the wisest decision of his life, as anyone who reads this record to the end will understand (P0090).

They take a bus to a park with a large pond, and he hires a swan boat. The attendant seems puzzled by a lone man; when Kusunoki smiles at Miyagi, invisible to him, and says "Come on, let's go," the attendant flinches and retreats (P0091–P0094). Miyagi laughs throughout: to others, she says, he looks like a grown man alone in a swan boat (P0095–P0097). She whistles "Stand by Me" (P0098). The cherry trees make him think of spring and winter, which he will never see; seeing her laugh, he stops minding (P0099–P0100).

Day after day, he does "everything you shouldn't do alone": a Ferris wheel, a merry-go-round, a picnic, an aquarium, a zoo, a pool, an izakaya, a barbecue. Each time he calls her name, holds her hand, meets her eyes, and asserts her presence. When money runs low he does a few days' casual labour (P0101). He did not notice at the time, the narrator says, that he was becoming known in the small town. Reactions, it seems, varied widely: some mocked him, some looked away; some took him for a skilled mime or a political protest; some were even cheered by him. Bad and good impressions were, surprisingly, about equal (P0102–P0103). Perhaps, he says, it was simply because he looked happy to the core (P0104–P0107).

**Her wish.** One morning Miyagi asks whether there is anything he would like her to do: she feels she only receives, and wants for once to give (P0110–P0113). He asks the same of her. Her wish, she says, is to know his wish; his, he says, is to know hers. After four rounds she gives in (P0114–P0118). He lists her three wishes, "star lake, my grave, childhood friend," using the later label for the friend, and guesses she wants to see him; she nods. She too does not know when she will die, she says; better to go while she still knows where he is. Not to meet him, only to look at him one-sidedly, unseen. Will he come? Of course, he says. "Tell me your wish someday, too." "When I think of one" (P0119–P0126).

On the bus to her home town she says she expects to be disappointed: a wish that nothing change has never once been granted. But even if her memories are spoiled, she says, she can bear it now, because he is there (P0127–P0129). "The best consolation for a loser is a more miserable loser," he says. "That's not what I meant. Are you stupid?" He strokes her head: "This, then?" "That's it" (P0130–P0133).

**Enishi.** It is a small, ordinary town, but now everything looks beautiful to him: he no longer needs to perceive the world efficiently, or blame it for his own failings (P0136–P0138). Miyagi leads. She knows Enishi lives here but not where; she tries the places he might be (P0139). When they find him she hides behind Kusunoki, then edges up until she stands beside him (P0140). Enishi sits reading at a small station. His face has a composure that Kusunoki, the narrator says, is lately beginning to understand: probably only someone sure of loving and being loved has it (P0141–P0142). Kusunoki senses that Enishi is waiting for someone off the train, and does not want Miyagi to see who. He suggests leaving; she refuses: she wants to see whom he loves now (P0143–P0145). A pleasant woman in her mid-twenties gets off. Her smile is natural to the point of seeming unnatural. Their joy at seeing each other, the narrator says, was enough to show that they are happy (P0146–P0148). Enishi was doing well without Miyagi, he concludes from those few seconds (P0149).

Miyagi watches them without expression. Perhaps, the narrator says, he was the more shaken: he saw himself and Himeno in the pair, and for a moment imagined a calm, happy future, one in which he might not have had to die (P0150–P0151).

When the two have gone, Miyagi says she had meant to do all sorts of things, since he could not see her, but stopped, she says. Such as? A forced hug, she says. "In your place I'd have done more," he says. Before she can ask "Such as?", he pulls her to him by the waist and does "more" (P0152–P0157). It lasts about two minutes. She stiffens at first; then, as he narrates it, she responds (P0158–P0159). When their lips part he says: if no one is going to reproach it anyway, he will be this selfish (P0160–P0161). "…Yes. No one will reproach it," she manages, looking down; in both lines the object of 咎める is unexpressed (P0162–P0163).

**Ending condition.** It is at the small station in Miyagi's home town, after the kiss. The day is unstated: under two months remain (P0037), and an unstated run of days has passed since U12 (P0012, P0101).
- **The debt goal.** He weighed means, including the shop's two remaining uses. Miyagi guessed his aim, objected, said she is already saved, and asked for memories instead. By strong inference he set the goal aside (こうして, P0090); no reply to her is narrated, and the transactions are not renounced.
- **The foolish decision.** He resolved to live out his life more foolishly than anyone he had met. The narrator calls it the wisest decision of his life, its reason withheld.
- **The outings.** Day after day he did things meant for two, alone to others' eyes, asserting her presence. He became known in the town, which he did not notice then.
- **The advisers.** The old bookseller asked whether he means to sell his lifespan "again." He evaded with feigned ignorance.
- **Her wish.** She watched Enishi, unseen, meeting a woman; by the narrator's reading they are happy. She showed no expression. He saw himself and Himeno in them.
- **The kiss.** He kissed her unasked; by his account she responded. "No one will reproach it," she said, looking down.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

1. **The narrating present** (P0004–P0005).
   - **The statement.** 「俺がこの記録に割ける時間も少なくなってきた」 (P0004). The story nears its end; he may not finish, and must lower the 密度 of the prose.
   - **INFERENCE, strong.** The record is being written within his remaining life, with his writing time running out. It is again called 「この記録」 (`TDH-C010`; F-U12-f).
   - **The density claim tested** (B). Ch13 is about as long as Ch12 (≈10,100 against ≈10,300 characters). The thinning shows as compressed story time (P0012, P0101, P0118) and a lower share of dialogue paragraphs (about 40% against 55%). INFERENCE, moderate.
   - **No cause given.** Why his writing time is short is not stated. 割ける implies time divided among uses, not only time remaining. Candidates: his approaching death; time he would rather give Miyagi (B).
2. **The impossible premise** (P0006–P0013).
   - **The debt's size.** Her debt 「かつてヒメノのいっていたサラリーマンの生涯賃金を遙かに上回る額だった」 (P0006). Himeno's figure was ¥200–300 million (Ch01 P0009; recalled as hers at Ch02 P0138). The money amount has no narrated source: Miyagi gave the debt only as repayable by selling 「三十年ちょっと」 of her time (Ch08 P0074). OBSERVATION of his statement (D1).
   - **Means rejected.** Steady work (焼け石に水); repaying the ¥300,000 is possible but she would not want it; crime she would not want; gambling (P0007–P0010). The criterion is her wishes: 「彼女のために稼ぐのだからこそ、彼女が望まない方法で稼ぐわけにはいかなかった」 (P0007).
   - **His aims.** Pay her debt; let her sleep safely every day; not leave her alone after he is gone (P0011). INFERENCE: the second answers the danger of her job and her Ch12 fatalism (Ch08 P0072; Ch12 P0163); the third answers his Ch11 thought that every subject who perceives her dies within a year (Ch11 P0196) (C). 一人ぼっち contrasts with 二人ぼっち (Ch11 P0136) (B). It is an unspoken question, which her later request for memories meets without her hearing it (P0087; INFERENCE, D1).
   - **His method.** Walking and fasting for intuition (P0012–P0013). Nothing addresses how a lifespan of under two months would be priced (D1). His gambling verdict adopts Miyagi's Ch05 gambling analogy (Ch05 P0019) and reverses his Ch02 longing for a single big swing (Ch02 P0121) (C).
   - **His self-judgment.** 「見当違いばかりする愚かさ」 (P0006), with a self-exoneration for what follows.
3. **The shop as last hope** (P0014). 「まだ二回利用する権利が残っている」. He returns in thought to the shop that 「かつて俺を絶望の淵に叩き込んだ」. By lexical check, the first raising of the remaining transactions since Ch05 P0066–P0073, where Miyagi reminded him of them and he deferred selling down on "hope" (F-U10-e; F-U02-e).
4. **Her valuation testimony** (P0015–P0019).
   - **His question.** He is 遙かに幸福 now; what would his lifespan fetch?
   - **Her answer, testimony.** 「人の価値というのは、ある程度、流動的なもの」; subjective happiness 「そこまで寿命の価値に影響を及ぼさない」 ("doesn't affect it that much"); 「客観的に計測可能な、根拠ありきの『幸福』」 counts; social contribution and fame 「やたらと優遇されていたと思います」, past tense and hedged (P0016–P0018). Her own view: 「そういうのって、どうかと思いますけど」 (P0016).
   - **Standing.** She is a self-declared liar (Ch12 P0193), and she hedges (思います, P0018). This is testimony, unverified. As the clerk in Ch02 she said she did not know the appraisal's details, which another advisory body made (Ch02 P0170). Her new specificity sits against that disclaimer. It adds fame (知名度) to the Ch02 criteria, and discounts subjectively felt happiness.
5. **Her objection** (P0020–P0027).
   - **How she learns.** She infers his aim, hedged: 「大体、わかっているつもりです」; 「大方、私の借金を返す方法を考えてくれているんでしょう？」 (P0025). He has not told her, and neither confirms nor denies it (P0024, P0028). OBSERVATION.
   - **Her position.** Happy, but she does not want his time spent so; if for her happiness, 見当違い (P0025).
   - **Her wish.** 「……構ってくださいよ」, sulking: he has not talked to her lately (P0027). INFERENCE: his days of walking and thinking left her neglected.
   - **His response.** He grants it, but 「こちらにも意地があった」 (P0029). He now considers becoming famous enough to sell high: 「誰もが名前を知っているような偉い人間になればいい」 (P0030–P0031). It echoes his pre-sale dream, 「誰もがその名前を知る人物となること」 (Ch02 P0122; D2), and her 「どこかの偉い人たち」 who set his price (Ch12 P0174) (C). 「それは確からしかった」 (P0030): it seemed certain to him, an inference from her testimony.
6. **The old bookseller** (P0034–P0049).
   - **Identity.** The old man who first referred him to the shop (Ch02 P0025–P0063). Kusunoki withholds the shop and his remaining time 「彼に責任を感じさせてしまうことになるかもしれない」 (P0037). INFERENCE: he credits the old man with conscience about the referral.
   - **Concealment, twice.** He hides Miyagi's presence 「このときばかりは」 (P0038), and asks his question when Miyagi is looking at the shelves (P0039). The second hides his consultations from her, after she has guessed the plan (P0025). Motive: to avoid her veto, or to spare her worry (C); not stated.
   - **Liking.** 「多分俺は、この店が、そしてこの老人のことが好きだったんだろう」 (P0038), hedged. In U13 the old man is the only one he says he liked (C).
   - **The advice.** 「堅実にやる」, one achievable thing at a time (P0042); then 「『俺みたいな奴のアドバイスを信用しない』」. Knowing failure is not knowing success; fixing failure leaves 「灰色の出発点」 (P0044).
   - **The echo.** Kusunoki recalls Miyagi's words, first spoken inside the price lie (Ch05 P0019, which goes on to explain his low value), quoted in 〝…〟: 〝彼らは、ようやくスタート地点に立ったというだけなんです…〟 (P0045). The source is Ch05 P0019. The recollection is excerpted verbatim and drops its emphasis (〔傍点〕ようやくスタート地点に立った〔/傍点〕), as Ch08 P0077 also did; Ch12 P0154 instead added emphasis to an unemphasized Ch01 line. HYPOTHESIS (B): emphasis is a device of the narration, not a record of delivery.
   - **"Again."** 「なあ、あんた、〔傍点〕また〔/傍点〕寿命を売ろうと考えているんだろう？」 (P0047). It replays his own Ch02 proposal, 「寿命を売る気はねえか？」 (Ch02 P0048), with お前 now あんた. Two translations: "selling *again*" (he presumes the first sale) or "*again* thinking of selling" (he recalls the offer). INFERENCE, moderate, for the first (B). How he would know is not stated. Kusunoki evades: 「何のことでしょう？」, 白々しく笑った (P0048–P0049).
   - **The evasion echoed.** His 「何のことでしょう？」 recalls Miyagi's feigned 「何のことをいっているのか、よくわかりませんね」 (Ch12 P0140; also P0158). INFERENCE, lexical.
   - **The certainty he once sold.** In Ch02 the old man said of lifespan 「高く売れるのは確かだ」 (Ch02 P0051), a certainty the thirty yen belied (B).
7. **The CD clerk** (P0050–P0058).
   - **Identity.** The clerk who also referred him to the shop and seemed to have sold himself (Ch02 P0078–P0090).
   - **The advice.** 「人を頼るしか、ないんじゃないっすかね」 (P0054). Kusunoki finds it of uncertain use (P0055). INFERENCE: it chimes with his own 「そろそろ他者の想像力にも頼る必要があった」 (P0034).
   - **The umbrella.** 「何かを成し遂げたいなら、まず健康は欠かせませんからね」 (P0057), said to a man who has been fasting (P0013). Whether the clerk's own sale bears on this is not stated.
   - **The onlookers.** 「傍目には、見当違いな位置に傘を差している馬鹿に映ったに違いない」 (P0058): the narrator's inference about passersby.
8. **Her reading of the umbrella** (P0059–P0064).
   - **Her words.** 「あなたが左肩を濡らしていることには、とっても温かい意味がある」 (P0061). INFERENCE: he tilts the umbrella toward her.
   - **His motive, now stated.** 「周りから異常者扱いされるのが楽しかった。そうすることで、ミヤギは笑ってくれるから」 (P0064). Here the narrator gives her pleasure as the reason for his public conduct. The motives he stated earlier were partly self-regarding (Ch09 P0084; Ch10 P0057).
9. **The acquaintance** (P0067–P0079).
   - **The exchange.** Kusunoki names her, with his hand on her shoulder: 「この子と遊び回ってたんだよ」 (P0071). In Ch11 he had told the photo-shop owner that she kept him company 「仕事でね」 (Ch11 P0087). The man: 「笑えねえよ、気持ち悪い」 (P0072). He once avoided acquaintances (Ch05 P0035; Ch07 P0036) (C).
   - **His statement.** 「ミヤギは確かにここにいる」 (P0073), followed by a request that each respect the other's belief. He frames her presence as a belief to be respected, and asserts it as certain.
   - **The verdict.** 「お前、頭がおかしいんだよ…ずっと自分の殻に閉じこもってるんだろう？」 (P0074). VALUE_JUDGMENT, the acquaintance's. INFERENCE: dramatic irony; he is now more outward than ever (P0137–P0138). 殻に閉じこもる repeats Miyagi's Ch11 words for what she had expected of her friend after she vanished: 「自分の殻に固く閉じこもって、私の帰りを待ち望んでいるだろう」 (Ch11 P0203). INFERENCE, lexical (C).
   - **Her thanks.** 「あの、さっきの……ありがとうございます」 (P0077). INFERENCE: for being named as present before a witness; compare Ch12 P0183.
10. **The turn** (P0080–P0090).
    - **"Steadily."** He had once scorned the steady course for the big swing (Ch02 P0121). Now he applies the old man's word, though told not to trust it, to her: 「考えてみれば俺には、ミヤギの幸せのためにしてあげられることが、確かにあるのだ」 (P0082). 「目の前にやるべきことがあるのに、どうしてそれをやらない？」 (P0083).
    - **Timing.** 「俺の思考の変化を読み取ったかのようなタイミングで」 (P0084) is a simile. It does not credit her with reading his mind or his record (D1).
    - **Her release, testimony.** 「私はとっくに、救われているんですから」; 「私の借金のことは、もう忘れてください」 (P0085). She asks instead for 「思い出」 (P0087).
    - **A tension in her testimony.** She speaks of living decades after him (「何十年経とうと」, P0085). Later in the chapter she says she does not know when she will die (P0123), and in Ch12 that she will die like her mother before repaying (Ch12 P0163). The text does not reconcile these. Rivals: 「何十年経とうと」 is concessive, not a forecast (B); honest uncertainty given her job's danger (C); each statement fits its purpose (D1).
    - **His decision.** 「これまでに出会ったどんな人間よりも愚かしい人間として一生を終えようと決意した」 (P0090): to live out his life (一生を終える, not "end his life") more foolishly than anyone he had met. It is the unit's second 決意 (P0006), named only in retrospect. The narrator adds a prolepsis: 「皮肉にもそれこそが俺の一生でもっとも賢い判断であったということは、この記録を最後まで読めばわかるだろう」. The reason is withheld. The record presupposes, hypothetically, a reader who reads it to the end, possibly as a rhetorical convention (B); 最後まで読めば answers P0004's 最後まで書き切れるか (B). In Ch12, asked whether he would let anyone read it, he said 「さあな」 (Ch12 P0058–P0059).
    - **Whether the debt goal is dropped.** INFERENCE, strong, from 「こうして」 (P0090) and the unit's silence on the debt after P0085: the "foolish" life replaces it, at her request. He makes no narrated reply to her, and does not renounce the two remaining transactions.
11. **The swan boat** (P0091–P0100).
    - **The attendant.** He seemed puzzled (不思議に思ったようだった); on 「さあ、いこう」 he 「顔をひきつらせ」 and left (P0093–P0094). Narrator's readings.
    - **Her laughter**, and 「スタンド・バイ・ミー」 whistled (P0098). In U10 she whistled a different song (Ch10 P0260).
    - **Spring and winter.** 「自分が二度と春も冬も迎えられない人間である」 (P0100). INFERENCE: consistent with death before winter; autumn is unmentioned, and the text gives no date.
12. **"Everything you shouldn't do alone"** (P0101–P0107).
    - **The list.** Eight outings, each with her name called, hands held, eyes met, 「彼女の存在を周りに主張するようにした」 (P0101). INFERENCE: it carries out his Ch12 offer, 「俺が死ぬまでは、いつでもやってやるよ」 (Ch12 P0186), against the list of things she said delighted her (Ch12 P0183) (C).
    - **Money.** A few days' 日雇いのアルバイト when funds ran low (P0101). Amounts are not given.
    - **Fame.** 「自分が段々と有名人になってきていることに、俺はその時点では気付いていなかった」 (P0102). The reactions are reported with らしい and ようなのだ: knowledge acquired later, source unstated. The ratio is 「そう違わなかった」 (P0103).

## 5. Narration, focalization and time

**The narrating present comes forward.** P0004–P0005 is the most direct statement yet of the narrator writing now: the story nears its end, his writing time is short, he must thin the prose. Other markers: 「最近の俺は…理解し始めている」 (P0141; a story-time historical present is the rival reading, B) and the hypothetical reader of 「この記録を最後まで読めばわかるだろう」 (P0090). INFERENCE, strong: the record is being written during his remaining life, from after the chapter's events, since he knows of his fame (P0102) and judges the decision (P0090).

**Prolepsis with withheld content.** P0090 claims the "foolish" decision was the wisest of his life, and defers the proof to the end of the record. It is another withheld, evaluative prolepsis (cf. Ch11 P0154–P0158, the "betrayal"; Ch11 P0213, the regret).

**Summary mode.** Long stretches are iterative or summary: days of walking (P0012), the outings (P0101), the town's reactions (P0102–P0103). Scenes are few and short: the advisers, the acquaintance, the boat, the bus, the station.

**Knowledge after the fact.** The town's reactions are hearsay (らしい, ようなのだ, P0102). He did not notice then (その時点では, P0102). The route of his later knowledge is unstated.

**Focalization.** Through Kusunoki throughout. Others' feelings are his readings: the attendant (P0093), the passersby (P0058), Enishi and the woman (P0141–P0148), Miyagi's response (P0159).

**Depicted time.** From the night after U12 (about day thirty-two) through an unstated span: several days of walking (P0012), the bookseller visit with under two months left (P0037), day-after-day outings (P0101), a morning (P0110), the trip to her home town. Summer throughout (P0056, P0098).

## 6. Japanese language and form

- **Sesame-dot emphasis.** One span: 〔傍点〕また〔/傍点〕 (P0047), the old man's "again." By span count it is the twenty-fourth; on U11's alternative count, the twenty-second. It marks his presumption of a prior sale, or of a renewed thought of selling (`TDH-F034`).
- **〝…〟 recollection.** Miyagi's Ch05 P0019 lines are recalled in 〝…〟 (P0045), abridged, with their emphasis dropped. Other 〝〟: 〝堅実に〟 (P0080), 〝誰か〟 (P0143), 〝それ以上のこと〟 (P0157).
- **確か cluster (title).** 確か proper five times, plus 確かめたい (P0037) and 確信 (P0142):
  - 「それは確からしかった」 (P0030);
  - 確かに in the old man's speech (P0044);
  - 「ミヤギは確かにここにいる」 (P0073);
  - 「確かにあるのだ」 (P0082);
  - 「この町に彼女の幼馴染がいるのは確かだが」 (P0139);
  - 「確信のある者にしかできない表情」 (P0142).

  INFERENCE: the title has no single referent. P0073 and P0142 carry the most weight: her presence asserted, and the certainty of loving and being loved. The cluster answers Miyagi's Ch05 forecast of his lost thirty years: 「何一つ確かなものは身に付けられないまま」 (Ch05 P0052; INFERENCE, lexical, C).
- **見当違い chain.** Five occurrences: 「見当違いばかりする愚かさ」 and 「俺の見当違い」 (P0006); her 「それは見当違いだ」 (P0025); his 「俺のしていることは、どこまでも見当違いだ」 (P0028); 「見当違いな位置に傘を差している馬鹿」 (P0058). The word passes from his plan, to her verdict, to his concession, to the onlookers' view of his tenderness (`TDH-F033`). Compare Ch10 P0257, 「見当違いな期待」.
- **いる / いない.** 確かにここにいる (P0073); そこにいるから (P0129); いなくとも (P0149); いなくなる three times for his death. It extends Ch03 P0062 and Ch12's 〝いる〟 (B).
- **構う.** 構わない (P0064) against 構ってくださいよ (P0027, P0082): one verb, lost in English (B).
- **堅実 trajectory.** Rejected (Ch02 P0121), ironic (「堅実に間違いを犯した」, Ch10 P0114), adopted (P0042, P0080) (B).
- **愚か / 賢い.** 「愚かしい人間」 against 「もっとも賢い判断」 (P0090), an explicit paradox.
- **Lists.** 「一人観覧車。一人メリーゴーランド。…」 (P0101), verbless noun phrases.
- **Registers.**
  - The old man: rough, rambling (〜ねえ, 〜さな, 〜だな).
  - The CD clerk: casual っす (P0054).
  - The acquaintance: blunt (笑えねえよ, 頭がおかしい).
  - Miyagi: playful (恥知らずの、照れ屋さん, P0063; 馬鹿じゃないですか, P0131).
- **Song.** 「スタンド・バイ・ミー」 (P0098). Its function is HYPOTHESIS.
- **Names.** エニシ is given in katakana with らしかった; whether surname or given name is unknown. It may evoke 縁, "bond" (HYPOTHESIS, B).
- **Ending.** The chapter closes on Miyagi's line, without narratorial evaluation (B).

## 11. Competing interpretations, claims and forecasts

**Interpretation A: from repaying to giving memories.** The goal of U12, money for her debt, is weighed and found impossible (P0006–P0031). At her request it becomes a giving of attention and memories (P0085–P0087). The narrator frames the change as a paradox, the foolish life as the wisest choice (P0090). INFERENCE, strong. It continues U12's relational view of value (U12 Interpretation B): her own testimony that subjective happiness matters little to the buyers (P0016) sets their scale aside.
- Weakener: whether he gives up the transactions is not stated, and P0090 withholds its reason.
- Rivals: (i) the change is overdetermined by the impossibility he had already conceded (P0006, P0031), not only by her request; (ii) her release may be a kindness to free him rather than a statement of need (C); (iii) P0090's "wisest" may not concern memories at all.

**Interpretation B: the title's certainties.** 確か recurs five times, with 確かめ and 確信 beside it. It answers Miyagi's Ch05 forecast that he would gain 「何一つ確かなもの」 (Ch05 P0052). The weightiest are 「ミヤギは確かにここにいる」 (P0073), a certainty asserted against a witness who cannot see, and 「確信」 of loving and being loved (P0142), which the narrator says he is lately coming to understand. INFERENCE, moderate.
- Rivals: the title points to the narrator's certainty about his decision (P0090), or to her "already saved" (P0085). The text does not decide.

**Interpretation C: the Enishi scene as mirror.** Miyagi sees the friend she says she was a fetter to (Ch11 P0205) happy without her, by the narrator's reading. Kusunoki sees himself and Himeno, and a future in which he need not have died. INFERENCE, moderate. The kiss follows directly. Readings of the kiss:
- (i) A response to both their losses, a claim on the present. INFERENCE, moderate.
- (ii) His own desire, as he frames it, 「自分勝手」, licensed by invisibility (P0161). OBSERVATION of his frame.
- (iii) Her logic turned: her renounced てやる toward one who cannot see her becomes his toward one who can (P0153–P0161; B, C). INFERENCE, moderate.
- (iv) An unasked act on impulse that repeats, in a gentler key, his Ch08 indifference to consequence: his 自分勝手; her 身を固く (P0159) against 身体を強張らせた (Ch08 P0024); Ch08 P0009 (B, C, D1). INFERENCE, moderate. Kusunoki narrates surprise followed by response; neither that sequence nor Miyagi’s conditional offer before a separate embrace (Ch12 P0193–P0199) establishes her wishes or permission before this kiss. He calls his initiative 自分勝手.

  Her downcast answer (P0162–P0163) leaves her feeling unstated. Her response is his narration (P0159).
