## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**Home, and a shared futon.** After a four-hour walk Kusunoki reaches his apartment, sweaty and blistered (P0004–P0005). He wonders whether to let Miyagi shower first, but fears that too much consideration would break the distance she has built. He has noticed that she seems to bathe and eat only while he sleeps, so he lies down to sleep quickly (P0005–P0006). Pretending to sleep, he hears her set off and come back (P0007). She comes to his pillow and whispers. He looks to her as if he is feigning sleep, she says; she would like it if he were doing so out of consideration for her. She says goodnight and goes to shower (P0008–P0011).

He tries her corner and her posture and cannot sleep. She tells him he should sleep on the futon; he says the same to her (P0011–P0014). He lies at the futon's left edge. The right side, he says, is an ideal place from which to watch him; hers to use or not, and he will not look (P0015). It is a compromise; she would never accept the futon outright, he supposes (P0016). After about twenty minutes she slips in, and soon he hears her faint breathing. They share the futon back to back (P0017–P0019). He knows the offer is self-satisfaction, he thinks, and that he is troubling her again. A dying man's fickle kindness can hurt rather than help. Yet she accepted it, perhaps respecting his goodwill, perhaps simply exhausted (P0019–P0020). They wake at sunset. Their eyes meet and both look away. She looks, to him, terribly unguarded. From tomorrow she will sleep in her old place, she says, "but thank you" (P0021–P0023).

**What now.** That same evening she seems to him a little more distant (P0024–P0025). At a convenience store he finds this month's part-time pay deposited alongside his small savings. This will probably be his last war chest, he supposes, to be used carefully (P0026–P0028). Over a beef-bowl meal, for which she buys her own ticket, he says he has done everything on his list. What is left (P0029–P0030)? Music and books were means to endure a hopeless life, he says; now they feel made for people who will go on living (P0031–P0034). A man of about fifty frowns at him talking about death alone (P0035).

She asks whether he has simpler pleasures, and recounts past subjects' last pastimes. One spent his last month lying in the bed of a light truck, watching the sky, having paid an old man his money to keep driving (P0036–P0040). He asks for more such stories. On the moped, in light clothes, he feels her body against him, gets distracted and nearly runs a red light; braking brings them closer (P0045). They share canned coffee overlooking town (P0046). At home after the outing, she tells him harmless snippets of past subjects’ lives like a picture book (P0047).

**Vending machines.** The next day they fold cranes together and shower each other with them (P0048). Buying cigarettes and a canned coffee, he realizes that there is something he truly loves: vending machines. As a child he wanted to be one (P0049–P0056). She tries to follow, then puts it in her own words: vending machines are always there, give something warm whenever you pay, and suggest a clear-cut relationship, constancy, permanence. He is moved (P0057–P0067). For watchers too, she says, they matter: unlike shop clerks, they do not ignore watchers (P0068). Recalling Paul Auster's *Smoke*, he resolves to photograph ordinary vending machines, persistently. She says she likes that kind of thing too (P0069–P0071).

**The rounds.** He buys a second-hand film camera and photographs every vending machine he sees, with its surroundings (P0074–P0076). Others do this better, he knows, but this is the way that suits him (P0077). Each morning he has film developed; each night he and Miyagi go through the prints and put them in an album (P0078). The photo-shop owner asks whether someone is there. "Yes, a girl called Miyagi; her job is to watch me," Kusunoki says. The owner seems to accept it, though he cannot follow why they photograph machines (P0079–P0088). Outside, Kusunoki takes one photograph of Miyagi beside the moped (P0089–P0093). A tall neighbour eyes him with distaste for talking to no one (P0094).

**The home town and the old woman.** Lost again, he finds himself in his and Himeno's home town (P0097–P0098). At the old candy shop, long closed, the broken vending machine and the phone box remain (P0099–P0100). In an overgrown park two cats retreat as Miyagi approaches: dogs and cats can see watchers, she says, though that does not make them like her (P0101–P0105). She sketches a cat in pencil in her notebook. He had not known of this hobby, and wonders whether her "observation records" have sometimes been drawing. He tells her it is not very good; she is practising, she says, oddly proud. Asked to show more, she changes the subject (P0106–P0114).

Passing the shop again, he sees on the bench an old woman he knows well, over ninety by his estimate (P0117–P0122). She moves her eyes to him but says nothing; he takes her silence to mean she does not remember him (P0122–P0127). He talks to her anyway, about how people forget even their best and worst memories (P0128–P0130). At eight or nine, after a fight with his mother, he ran away. She took him in, and when his parents phoned she said, "He's here, but I'll say he isn't for another hour." It is because of that, he thinks, that he can still expect anything of others (P0131–P0133). He tells her of Himeno: a princess-like girl, shunned, he says, for being too unlike the others, so that the two were "alone together." She transferred in the summer of fourth grade because of a parent's job transfer. He recalls her promise, and says he has learned that she hated him enough to want to die in front of him (P0134–P0139). At Himeno's name the old woman seems to him to react a little (P0137).

Then the narrator writes 〔傍点〕答えあわせといこう〔/傍点〕, *time for the answer check* (P0140–P0141). Himeno's capsule letter was missing, he tells her. The teacher would never have buried the capsule without a pupil's letter, so someone must have dug it up first and taken it, and that could only be Himeno (P0142). Everything connects for him (P0143–P0144). Himeno, he says, never wrote to anyone, yet at seventeen she sent him a letter with her return address. 〔傍点〕ヒメノなりのＳＯＳでした〔/傍点〕, *it was Himeno's SOS*. Like him, he says, she was cornered, clung to the past, dug up the capsule and wrote to her only childhood friend. He failed to see it, and lost her. Himeno will soon kill herself, he says, and his own life will soon end (P0145–P0148). The narration endorses that he should have noticed sooner (P0146–P0147). The old woman says only "goodbye" (P0149–P0151). Being forgotten by her, as he takes it, hardly hurts him. He was, the narrator supposes, getting used to being betrayed by his memories (P0152–P0153). But he had wholly overlooked one possibility, the narrator says: that Miyagi would betray him (P0154–P0158).

**The star lake.** Miyagi, who now hugs him without hesitation while riding pillion, taps his side. She says she remembers this road from long ago, before she became a watcher; it leads to the star lake (P0161–P0165). They refuel and climb a mountain road for an hour and a half (P0169–P0171). She tells him not to look up, then leads him by the hand with his eyes closed. He hears the insects as four distinct voices (P0172–P0176). What if she were deceiving him, she asks, leading him toward a cliff or a bridge? He has never thought of it, he says; he sees no reason why she would. "I see," she says, as if bored (P0177–P0183). On a pier he lies down and opens his eyes (P0184–P0185). That day, he says, he first knew a starry sky, far vaster than he had imagined (P0186–P0191). He feels he somehow understands why she wanted to see it again (P0192–P0193). He decides he would wish to die peacefully, as if asleep. He supposes that she would wish to stop being a watcher (P0194–P0196).

He asks: she lied for his sake, didn't she, about Himeno barely remembering him? Instead of answering, she says she too had a childhood friend, the "once-important person" (P0197–P0201). They were two misfits, she says, in a co-dependent world of two. On her first day off as a watcher she went to see him, expecting him to be grieving. Within a month he had adapted to the world, even in the way of those who had shunned them. She realized, she says, that she had been a shackle to him (P0203–P0205). Honestly, she wanted him to be unhappy and to wait for her. She has not gone to see him since, but before she dies she would want to see him: there is nowhere else to cling to. "So I understand how you feel, very well," she says, "though you may not want me to." "No," he says at once. "Thank you for understanding" (P0205–P0209).

They photograph a vending machine near the lake and go home. Saying she is very tired, she gets into his futon. He tries to steal a glance; she seems to have been doing the same, and both turn away (P0210–P0212). He should have wished on the shooting star for those days to continue, the narrator says (P0213). When he next wakes, Miyagi is gone. Only a notebook is left by his pillow (P0214–P0215).

**Ending condition.** He next wakes in his apartment, some days into the rounds; the day count is unstated. Miyagi is gone, and a notebook, hers by strong inference, lies by his pillow.
- They have twice slept in the same futon: by day (P0018–P0021) and on the last night (P0211).
- He has begun photographing vending machines as "the way that suits me."
- A photo-shop owner seemed to accept her existence. Cats can see her, she says.
- She sketches in her notebook.
- To an old benefactor he set out his conclusions: Himeno took her own capsule letter, and her letter at seventeen was an SOS. The narration endorses the SOS conclusion. He believes Himeno will soon die.
- At the star lake Miyagi told of her childhood friend, who adapted without her. She said she understands his feelings very well. She did not answer whether she lied for his sake.
- The narrator foreshadows that Miyagi would "betray" him.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

1. **Distance and care.**
   - **His restraint.** He weighs letting her shower first against "breaking the distance she has built" (P0005).
   - **Her routine.** He has inferred that she bathes and eats only while he sleeps (のみのようだった, P0006).
   - **Her words.** She names his apparent feigned sleep and says she would like it if it were for her (P0010). That is the first time either names a feigned sleep aloud. The U10 night's feigning (Ch10 P0233, P0249) is not mentioned.
   - **Her corner.** He tries her corner posture and cannot sleep (P0012). That is his first trial of her conditions, an INFERENCE about its intent.
2. **The shared futon.**
   - **The arrangement.** His left-edge offer is framed as a compromise she could accept (P0015–P0016); that is his reasoning. She joins after twenty minutes, and they lie back to back (P0018–P0019).
   - **His self-judgment.** It is self-satisfaction, a dying man's unstable kindness that may hurt, and it endangers her hard-won toughness (P0019). That is his judgment. It records a breach of his U10 resolve (「結果的に、俺はまたミヤギのことを困らせてしまっている」) while keeping its principle (Ch10 P0256–P0257). It also inverts U10: now *his* kindness is the dying man's unreliable whim.
   - **A safe framing.** His offer frames the right side as 「俺を近くで監視するのにうってつけの場所」 (P0015), so she can accept within her role, and he promises not to look. INFERENCE: after the Ch08 near-assault, this reads as a deliberately safe framing. His promise 「見もしない」 is broken at P0212, as hers to return to her place is broken at P0211.
   - **Her acceptance.** It is read two ways: as respect for his goodwill, or as exhaustion (かもしれない, P0020).
   - **Waking.** They look away together (P0021). She looks unguarded to him. She says she will return to her place, and thanks him (P0022–P0023). That same evening, after the daytime sleep, she seems, in his reading, a little more distant (P0024–P0025).
   - **The last night.** She enters the futon herself, "very tired" (P0211). He tries to glance at her; she seems to have been doing the same (らしく, his inference), and both turn away (P0212).
3. **Money.** Alongside his small savings (僅かばかりの預金), this month's part-time pay has arrived. He supposes it will be his last war chest: 「最後の軍資金になるのだろう」 (P0026–P0028). That answers his cashless state after U10 (Ch10 P0212–P0215), which had already cost him the four-hour walk home (P0004). The two remaining transactions are not mentioned.
4. **"The list is done."**
   - **His claim.** He says he has done everything on his list (P0030). That is his claim.
     - Item 6, 「遺書を書く」, was drafted, not completed or sent (Ch07 P0010–P0014).
     - Item 8, 「ヒメノと会って想いを打ち明ける」, was not done as written: he confessed the sale instead (Ch10 P0137–P0146), and struck it through for an unstated reason (Ch10 P0016).
     - Item 5, 「綺麗なものを見る」, is arguably met afterwards at the lake, unremarked.
   - **Hobbies.** He says music and reading were means to endure life, and now feel made for those who will live on (P0032–P0034).
   - **An onlooker.** A man at the counter frowns at him (P0035). This is a public misperception, as in U09 and U10.
5. **Miyagi's past subjects** (testimony). She gives examples of their last pastimes. One sold his life, paid an old man his money, and spent his last month lying in a light truck's bed (P0036–P0040). That night she tells more, only "the harmless parts" (害のない部分, P0047). Her stories show that she remembers at least some subjects (cf. Ch06 P0119 「覚えていません」, where the question was whether past subjects grew lonely; F-U08-C4). Her stories console him more than fine literature (P0047).
6. **The pillion ride.** In light clothes he feels her body against him, gets distracted and nearly runs a red light; braking presses them closer. He hopes she cannot feel his heartbeat (P0045). That is his attraction, narrated as his. Later she hugs him without hesitation, but only while riding (P0162).
7. **Vending machines and the "way that suits me"** (`TDH-C052`).
   - **The confession.** He loves vending machines and as a child wanted to be one (P0054–P0056). He calls it a worthless hobby, a symbol of a worthless life (P0063).
   - **Her articulation.** Machines 「いつでもそこにいてくれます」, give something warm for money, and suggest 「割り切った関係とか、不変性とか、永遠性とか」 (P0066). He is moved (P0067).
   - **Watchers and machines.** For watchers, vending machines matter because, unlike clerks, they do not ignore them (P0068). INFERENCE: this is consistent with the nullification of her effects on people (Ch06 P0060), and with her vending-machine purchases (Ch09 P0080).
   - **The project.** Auster's *Smoke*, Auggie Wren's daily photograph, suggests the project, a "seemingly meaningless" act that defies cheap meaning (P0069).
   - **"The way that suits me."** He accepts that others do it better: 「誰が何といおうと、これが俺に向いたやり方なのだ」 (P0077). That echoes Miyagi's Ch06 「もっとあなたに向いたやり方がある気がする」 (Ch06 P0015), which he never asked her to explain. INFERENCE, moderate: the echo answers her remark from his side. The text does not say she meant this.
   - **The prints.** The album and the machine as a "measuring stick" make the machine a constant against which surroundings differ (P0078).
8. **Third parties and Miyagi.**
   - **The photo-shop owner.** He accepts Miyagi's existence without seeing her: 「なるほど」, and "such people exist sometimes" (P0083). That is the second person told of her, after Himeno (Ch10 P0146). As narrated, he is the first to accept it plainly, but how sincerely is undecidable.
   - **The photograph.** He photographs Miyagi beside the moped (P0089). Whether she will appear in the print is not stated; she says others would see "just a bike" (P0092).
   - **The neighbour.** He reacts with distaste (P0094).
9. **Animals see watchers** (testimony plus a demonstration). The cats retreat as she approaches. 「犬や猫には見えるんですよ」 (P0105). This adds a category to the visibility rules (`TDH-C024`), alongside the watched person and the off-duty exception.
10. **Miyagi's drawing.**
    - **The sketch.** She sketches a cat in pencil in her notebook (P0106).
    - **His speculation.** Perhaps her "observation records" have sometimes been drawing (かもしれない, P0107).
    - **Her reply.** He says it is not very good. She says she is practising, and refuses to show more (P0110–P0114).
    - **Other drawing.** INFERENCE: drawing returns, but as hers. His own painting stopped at seventeen (Ch10 P0119). He judges her skill but says nothing of his own painting, an absence with real opportunity. Her own name for the notebook was 「行動観察記録です。あなたの」 (Ch04 P0077). He speculates that it may hold more than records; its content is unknown.
11. **The old woman.**
    - **Who she is.** She is over ninety in his estimate (P0122). She moves her eyes to look at him (目だけを動かしてこちらを見た, P0122), but does not speak. He takes her silence to mean that she does not remember him (無言は、肯定のサイン, P0125); that is his inference. The narrator's 「かつての恩人に忘れられてしまっていたこと」 (P0152) presupposes it. Against it, she seems to react a little at Himeno's name (P0137), and says 「さようなら」 at the end (P0149). Whether she remembers him is undecidable.
    - **Was the shop closed?** His 「もう十年くらい前のことですからね、俺が最後にここを訪れたのは」 (P0126) sits against Ch07 P0055, where he passed the closed shop this summer.
    - **His monologue.** It covers forgetting (P0129); the runaway night at eight or nine and her kindness, "the reason I can still expect anything of others" (P0131–P0133); and Himeno as princess and fellow outcast (P0136).
    - **New facts.**
      - Himeno's transfer was due to a parent's job transfer (P0139).
      - His mother is identified (not named) as the party in the childhood fight.
      - He calls it the mistake of an 「可愛げのない子供」 to have believed adults' kindness was only duty (P0131). That is exactly how he explained Miyagi's kindness in U09–U10 (Ch09 P0017; Ch10 P0256). Yet he still tells the photo-shop owner she accompanies him 「仕事でね」 (P0087); the rival is social shorthand to a stranger. INFERENCE: the U10 resolve is under strain.
        - **Breached in conduct.** P0015 and P0019.
        - **Contradicted in hedged speech.** P0133, 「少なくとも俺は、勝手にそう思い込んでます」, which may name a capacity, not conduct.
        - **Kept.** P0005, P0087 and P0195.

        Whether it is "broken" is undecidable.
    - **Her one word.** It is 「さようなら」 (P0149–P0150).
12. **His answer check** (P0139–P0148; his conclusions, not established). Miyagi is unnarrated throughout the old-woman scene (P0117–P0153). Whether she heard his reasoning, or 「もうすぐ自殺します」, is inference, and no knowledge of his conclusions is assigned to her.
    - **Retelling.** His account of Himeno revises earlier ones (OBSERVATION of the discrepancies; INFERENCE on their bearing).
      - She was shunned, he now says, for being 「あまりに異質」, with a 「特異な美しさ」 (P0136). Ch01 P0047 had her disliked 「俺と同じような理由で」.
      - The promise is set in 『』 at P0139 but paraphrased. It drops 売れ残り同士 and her retraction, 「……冗談だよ。忘れてちょうだい」 (Ch01 P0087–P0092).
      - "She never wrote to anyone" (P0145) is a new premise. Ch09 found only the letter's tone odd (Ch09 P0055–P0060).
      - He supplies the diagnosis himself: 「神格化されてしまいました」 (P0139).

      These revisions bear on how far his SOS thesis is shaped by idealization. They are the main counterweight to the narrator's endorsement.
    - **What he says of Himeno.** That Himeno hated him 「殺したいほど」, "enough to want to kill me" (P0139). That overstates the letter, which spoke of dying before him, not killing him (Ch10 P0191–P0195).
    - **The capsule.** The teacher would not have buried the capsule without a pupil's letter, so someone took Himeno's letter, and only Himeno would (P0142). This adopts the removal rival recorded in U07 (`TDH-C032`). His premise, the teacher's character, is his characterization. He ignores a rival: burial and transfer fall in the same summer (Ch07 P0016; P0139), so she may never have written a capsule letter.
    - **The SOS.** Her letter at seventeen was 〔傍点〕ヒメノなりのＳＯＳ〔/傍点〕 (P0148). He says she never wrote letters, yet she gave her return address (P0145). INFERENCE, his: she too dug up the capsule and wrote to her only childhood friend. This aligns with U10's inference that her "call for help" was that letter (`TDH-C042`). It is his explicit conclusion. The narration endorses only that he should have noticed sooner: 「そう。／俺はもっと早くに気付くべきだったのだ」 (P0146–P0147). Himeno has not confirmed it. His decoding also repeats, almost word for word, his own plan for a disguised farewell letter (Ch07 P0013): what matters is that the letter is from him and to her. Whether that is insight or projection is undecidable.
    - **Self-blame.** He takes the blame on himself: 資格 is lost, and 報い, retribution, follows (P0148).
    - **Her fate.** 「ヒメノはもうすぐ自殺します」 (P0148). It compresses Ch03's "two years later" into "soon." That is his belief, resting on Miyagi's original-course testimony, and nothing in the frontier supports it. Her letter dropped only the revenge (Ch10 P0196–P0198). Miyagi has called the original course information now without meaning (Ch03 P0108; cf. Ch05 P0028). Her fate in the altered course is not established, and he does not consider intervening, though he has her address and time.
13. **The betrayal prolepsis** (P0154–P0158).
    - **What it says.** The narrator says he wholly overlooked the possibility that Miyagi would betray him (裏切る). It is framed by three lines praising her: always beside him, sharing his despair but choosing time, "kind, kind."
    - **What it does not say.** What the "betrayal" is, when it happens, and whether it harms him are withheld. In this text 裏切る also means defeating an expectation: being betrayed by his memories (P0153); the clerk's look 「手酷い裏切りを受けでも」 (Ch02 P0078). With a personal object, 俺を裏切る reads more naturally as a negative "betray" (B). The narrator, who knows what it was, still calls her 優しい優しい女の子. It echoes his U09 self-warning that forgetting she is there for work would invite 「手酷い裏切り」 (Ch09 P0018). That warning sits awkwardly with 「完全に見落としていた」.
    - **Status.** It is a prolepsis about Miyagi with withheld content. It is not to be resolved here, and P0214's disappearance is not to be equated with it (INFERENCE caution).
14. **The star lake** (`TDH-C055`).
    - **Her guidance.** She says she remembers the road from before she was a watcher (P0163; testimony). That makes her lake wish (Ch09 P0090) a shared outing. She guides him with his eyes closed, holding his hand (P0176).
    - **Her hypothetical.** She asks what if she were deceiving him, leading him to a cliff or a bridge (P0177–P0179). He trusts her because he sees no motive for her to deceive him: 「ミヤギがそんなことをする理由が見当たらないからさ」 (P0182). Her 「そうですか」 is つまらなそうに, as if bored, which is his reading (P0183).
    - **Two echoes.** INFERENCE: her hypothetical echoes Himeno's planned fall (Ch10 P0192) and stands beside the betrayal prolepsis. That is his and the reader's juxtaposition, not hers.
    - **The sky.** "That day I first knew a starry sky" (P0187), vast beyond imagining (P0190). 知った answers her Ch09 『知っている』 (Ch09 P0090). Her grave wish goes unmentioned as they pass a hillside cemetery earlier (P0044).
    - **His wish.** To die peacefully, as if asleep (P0195).
    - **Her wish.** His guess is that she wants to stop being a watcher, to be invisible no longer (P0196). That is his inference.
15. **The lie and her childhood friend.**
    - **His question.** He asks whether she lied for his sake (P0197). She does not answer it.
    - **Her story** (testimony). The "once-important person" was her childhood friend, a "he" (P0199–P0203). They were two misfits in a co-dependent world. On her first day off as a watcher she saw him adapted within a month, fitting the world as those who excluded them had (P0203). She felt herself a shackle, and wanted him unhappy and waiting (P0205). She has not seen him since. She would still see him before dying, having nowhere else to cling to (すがれる, P0207).

## 11. Competing interpretations, claims and forecasts

**Interpretation A: a life on his own terms.** With the list "done" and Himeno lost, he finds a modest, constant pleasure, photographing vending machines, and calls it "the way that suits me" (P0077). It echoes Miyagi's unasked "a way more suited to you" (Ch06 P0015). Support: P0054–P0078. Weakener: the echo is lexical; she never says this is what she meant.

**Interpretation B: two people of the same kind.** Several details pair them as alike (INFERENCE):
- her account of her childhood friend matches his Himeno story;
- she says she understands him "very well" (P0207);
- she shares his futon, his album and his lake;
- vending machines are valued by both;
- watchers are not ignored by them (P0068).

Weakener: her story is testimony, and he tells himself her kindness is work (Ch10 P0256).

**Interpretation C: the gathering of foreshadowing.** The betrayal prolepsis (P0158), her deceiving hypothetical (P0177–P0179), "I should have wished" (P0213) and her disappearance (P0214) gather. The chapter ends on an absence whose meaning is withheld. HYPOTHESIS, with co-equal rivals: the "betrayal" concerns her disappearance; or something her notebook discloses; or a defeated expectation of another kind; or a matter not yet in view. None is established; to be tested.
