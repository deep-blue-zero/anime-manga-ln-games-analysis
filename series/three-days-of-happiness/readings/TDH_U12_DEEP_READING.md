## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**The substitute.** In Miyagi's place there is a watcher of exactly the kind Kusunoki once wished for, the narrator says: short, balding, red-faced and greasy (P0004–P0005; Ch06 P0010). Where is the usual one, Kusunoki asks. She is on a day off, the man says; he is the substitute today and tomorrow. Kusunoki is relieved: in two days she should be back (P0006–P0012). The man looks through his album of vending machines and calls it rubbish, and sneers at the cranes and at how he spends his remaining time (P0013–P0019). Kusunoki finds this bluntness easier than being watched with meaningful looks (P0020–P0023).

After lunch the man asks whether he has troubled "that girl" (P0024–P0028). So you're on her side too, Kusunoki thinks, and warms to him (P0029–P0031). They are not close, the man says: watchers cannot see one another, and they have only exchanged a few written messages. But he says he was the one who bought her time, and he has read her records (P0032–P0033). "A pitiable child," he says, and Kusunoki takes it as sincere. She sold the one thing that should not be sold, he says, at ten, unable to judge, and must go on dealing with people like Kusunoki (P0034–P0040). Kusunoki answers honestly. He says he has troubled her a great deal, said things that hurt her, nearly injured her, and nearly pushed her down by force (P0041–P0042).

As the man flares up, Kusunoki hands him the notebook Miyagi left. He supposes it is her observation record of him, which a subject should not read, and he would not want her punished for leaving it (P0043–P0047). The man skims it in about two minutes and says only, "I see." What it held, Kusunoki does not know. The man hardly needles him after that, and Kusunoki supposes she wrote of him favourably (P0048–P0049).

**His notebook.** Had he not thought to buy a notebook of his own, the narrator says, this record would never have been written (P0052). He buys a B5 Tsubame notebook and a cheap fountain pen. He will spend these two days doing, in a wholesome sense, what he would not want Miyagi to see (P0052–P0053). He writes, from the moral lesson in elementary school onward: the price of a life, the promise with Himeno, the shop, meeting Miyagi (P0054). The words flow (P0055). It is a way of sorting, he tells the man, "like defragging" (P0056–P0059).

He writes until after ten at night, then walks out with the man (P0060–P0062). Has the man too sold his time, he asks. He says he sold nothing and does this by choice: it is like visiting other people's graves, a way of getting used to death before his own. "An old man's idea," says Kusunoki. "I am old," says the man (P0063–P0070). The neighbour's room always seems to have visitors; his own has only ever held watchers (P0071). He sleeps eleven hours (P0073). By the next evening the record has caught up with the present. Retelling seems to him to make good memories easier to savour and bad ones easier to accept (P0074–P0075).

**"Did you really believe it?"** On the last night the man asks what Kusunoki spent the money on. He gave it away a note at a time, Kusunoki says, after the person it was meant for ran off (P0079–P0085). The man laughs strangely; it is not that part he finds funny (P0086–P0091). "Did you really believe, without a doubt, that your lifespan was worth three hundred thousand?" (P0094). Kusunoki says he thought it too cheap at first. The man will say no more. Tomorrow, he suggests, Kusunoki should ask her directly (P0095–P0101). Kusunoki lies awake thinking (P0102–P0103).

**Miyagi returns; fireflies.** In the morning Miyagi greets him with what he takes for an intimate smile. She is telling him a lie, the narrator says (P0106–P0108). He decides to pretend to know nothing rather than trouble her (P0109–P0113). They ride all day; she seems glad it is the vending-machine rounds (P0114–P0115). At night, at a small dam, he asks: what if he were deceiving her, leading her somewhere outrageous? Somewhere beautiful, then, she says (P0116–P0120). It is fireflies. "Fireflies really do glow," she says; she may never have seen them before. He thinks he understands. As with his starry sky, some beauty is unknown until seen (P0121–P0127). He had come here once last year, he says, and remembered it only yesterday (P0128–P0129). May she take this as thanks for the lake, she asks. However she likes, he says. "Then I'll take it freely. Very much" (P0131–P0134).

**The lie.** At home, with the light out, he calls her name and asks why she lied (P0135–P0138). She says she does not know what he means. Was his lifespan really ¥300,000, he asks. "Of course," she says: that is his worth (P0139–P0143). She asks whether the substitute said something. He says the man only told him to check again (P0144–P0148). She keeps denying it.

At first, he says, when he "heard Miyagi was lying," he thought she had pocketed money owed to him: thirty million or three billion, perhaps. But he could not believe, he says, that she had been deceiving him from the start, behind her smiles. Overnight, he says, he understood that his premise was wrong (P0150–P0152). The narrator recalls the teacher's words ten years earlier: 〔傍点〕ここでは〔/傍点〕、〔傍点〕一度そういう考え方を捨ててほしいんです〔/傍点〕, *here, I want you to set that way of thinking aside* (P0153–P0154; Ch01 P0013). Why had he believed ¥10,000 a year was the minimum, he asks, or that a life ought to sell for millions? (P0155). "Why did 〔傍点〕you〔/傍点〕 decide to give a stranger like me three hundred thousand?" (P0157). She looks away and says she has no idea what he means (P0158).

He moves to the corner opposite hers and sits as she does, knees drawn up. She smiles a little. Even if she pretends not to know, he says, thank you (P0159–P0161). It doesn't matter, she says. In this job she will die like her mother anyway before paying off her debt, and even freed she has no happy life promised; better to spend the money that way (P0162–P0163). What was he really worth? "…Thirty yen," she says quietly. "A three-minute phone call," he laughs. He apologizes for wasting her ¥300,000, and she wishes he had used it for himself, in a voice angry in form but gentle (P0164–P0169).

**Her reasons.** She understands him, she says: her giving and his giving away share a root. Both were lonely, sad, empty and desperate, and turned to self-righteous altruism. Had she not lied, she adds, he might not have sold his lifespan at all, and might have lived longer. She is sorry (P0170–P0171). Perhaps, she says, she wanted for once to be the one who gives, and to save herself by doing for someone like her what no one did for her. It was a warped kindness forced on him, she says, and apologizes (P0172).

He disagrees. Told "thirty yen" at the start, he says, he would have sold everything, not even three days left; her lie let him do the rounds, fold cranes, see stars and fireflies (P0173). Thirty yen is a price some important people set, she says; to her he is now worth thirty million or three billion. He asks her not to console him. She tells him to be quiet and be consoled: it is not kindness, she says, only what she wants to say (P0174–P0181).

At first, she says, she thought him worth thirty yen, and gave the money for her own satisfaction; anyone would have done. That changed after the station night. He listened to her seriously and sympathized, and from then he was no longer just a subject (P0183). Worse, she says, she was happy that he talked to her, even in front of others. She has always been invisible, ignored as her job. Eating out while talking, shopping, walking, holding hands by the river felt like a dream. He was the first, she says, to treat her always as "there" (P0183). He does not know what to say. He had never thought anyone grateful to him (P0184–P0185). He offers, half joking, to do it any time until he dies. "I thought so. That's why I love you," she says. It is pointless to love someone who will disappear, she adds, smiling sadly (P0186–P0190). He cannot speak (P0191–P0192).

**More lies.** She has lied to him about many things, she says, her voice wet: the price, Himeno, and, for example, the rule that his life would be ended if he tried to trouble others. That was a lie, she says. So was the rule that he would die beyond a hundred metres from her. Both, she says, were expedients to protect herself (P0193). If he is angry, she says, he may do anything to her, even something cruel (P0195–P0197). "Then I won't hold back," he says, and pulls her up and holds her tight (P0198–P0199). He tries to commit every detail of her to memory, so that he can recall it any time and never forget (P0200–P0202). That's cruel, she says, sniffing; now she won't be able to forget him. "When I die, grieve a lot," he says. She will do that as long as she lives, she says, and laughs (P0203–P0207).

**A goal.** At this point, the narrator says, his short, meaningless remaining life finally gains a goal. In the two months left, he wants somehow to pay off all her debt: he, whose whole life is worth less than a can of juice. He calls it presumptuous (P0210–P0215).

**Ending condition.** It is night, about a month after the sale ("この一か月", P0057; "残り二か月", P0212), in his apartment, after the embrace.
- **The absence.** By the substitute's account, Miyagi's absence was a two-day day off; her return bears it out. The substitute skimmed her notebook, which Kusunoki did not read. Its contents are not narrated.
- **His notebook.** Kusunoki has written his own record from the moral lesson to the present. The narrator calls it "this record."
- **The price.** By her eventual admission, after his deduction from the substitute's hint, his lifespan was worth thirty yen in all, and she gave him the ¥300,000 herself.
- **The rules.** She says the misconduct rule and the hundred-metre rule were lies, told to protect herself.
- **Her declaration.** She has said she loves him, and he has held her.
- **His goal.** He wants to pay off her debt in two months.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

1. **The substitute and the hypothetical.** The narrator asserts that the substitute is 「まさにそのような男」, the 「醜く太った不潔な中年」 Kusunoki once wished for in Miyagi's place (P0004–P0005; Ch06 P0010). He describes him as short, balding, red-faced and greasy; fatness is not described. Even with the wished-for watcher, he still governs himself by Miyagi's imagined regard (P0053), so the Ch06 premise fails. He is relieved that watchers are not rotated: 監視員が交代制でない (P0008).
2. **The absence explained** (testimony): 「休みだ…今日と明日は、俺が代理だ」 (P0007), with 「一応、そういう予定にはなってる」 (P0012). Days off were mentioned by Miyagi at Ch11 P0203. Her return (P0106) bears out the account. This answers U11's open question (`TDH-C056`). Whether she left the notebook on purpose or forgot it (Kusunoki calls it a 忘れ物, P0047) is open.
3. **The substitute's testimony.**
   - **Watchers.** Watchers cannot see each other: 「俺たちも互いに姿は見えねえ」 (P0033).
   - **Miyagi.** He bought her time, and has read her records. She sold "the one thing that should not be sold" at ten, unable to judge (P0033, P0040).
   - **Himself.** He sold nothing and is a watcher by choice, to accustom himself to death; he is old (P0066–P0070).
   - **New claims.** By his testimony, these add to the watcher system (`TDH-C024`, `TDH-C035`): a buyer who is also a watcher; a watcher who sold nothing; watchers mutually invisible. The "at ten" matches her own account (Ch08 P0072–P0074). Since watchers cannot see each other, Miyagi cannot have seen the substitute; her 「代理の監視員に、何かいわれたんですね？」 (P0146) is an inference from his certainty.
   - **Status.** His regard for her is "apparently sincere" (本心からの言葉であるようだった, P0036), which is Kusunoki's reading.
4. **The confession to a third party.** Kusunoki tells the substitute that he troubled Miyagi, said wounding things, nearly injured her, and 「あとちょっとで、無理矢理押し倒すところだった」 (P0042). That is the first narrated acknowledgement of the U08 near-assault to a third party; he had acknowledged it to Miyagi herself in Ch08 (Ch08 P0033, P0036, P0045: 「さっきみたいな行動」). INFERENCE: "wounding things" may include the grooming remark (Ch07) and the Ch10 rebuff, and "nearly injured" the glass (Ch06). It bears on F-U10-C2's "fault he could not admit" (Ch10 P0182), but which fault was meant there is unstated.
5. **The notebook handed over.**
   - **His reason.** He gives Miyagi's notebook to the substitute so she will not be punished for leaving it, supposing it to be her observation record (P0045–P0047). His own term 観察記録 follows hers, 行動観察記録 (Ch04 P0077).
   - **The reading.** The substitute skims it in about two minutes: 「なるほどな」 (P0048).
   - **What is known.** The contents are not narrated: 「そこに何が書いてあったのかは、俺もわからない」 (P0049). Kusunoki infers she wrote favourably of him from the man's changed manner (だろう). A rival cause is his own act of protecting her. The substitute only skimmed it, and later says he has 「あまり詳しくは読んでねえ」 (P0082). Ownership is asserted as hers by him (P0045), not otherwise confirmed.
6. **His notebook and the narrating instance** (`TDH-C010`).
   - **The claim.** 「このとき俺が自分のノートを購入することを思い付かなかったら、この記録が書かれることもなかっただろう」 (P0052). Strong INFERENCE from the deictic この and the content match: the narration presents itself as continuous with the record begun in this notebook. B's rival is that the printed text is a later version drawing on the notebook.
   - **The writing.** He writes from the moral lesson onward (P0054), and by the next evening the record "had caught up with the present" (P0074). Its first page, the moral lesson, repeats the opening of Ch01 P0004 almost verbatim. Its listed topics run in the order of Ch01–Ch02 (B).
   - **Dating.** Its first pages were written from about day thirty. Ch12 itself is narrated from after its events: the nonpast わからない (P0049, P0200), the evaluative のだろう (P0215) and the retrospective 「このとき」 (P0052). That is the secure evidence that writing continued past P0074. Which earlier passages were written or revised after P0074 is not fixed. Ch02 P0146–P0147, a prolepsis about her lying, is compatible with day-thirty composition, since the Himeno lie was known from Ch10.
   - **Open.** How the later parts were written, and when the record ends, are open.
   - **What writing did.** He reports that rewriting made good memories easier to savour and bad ones easier to accept (P0075).
   - **A reader.** The man's question 「誰かに読ませるのか？」 goes unanswered: 「さあな」 (P0058–P0059). It is not presented as a farewell letter. It is written as something he would not want Miyagi to see (P0053), but his answer leaves any eventual reader open. By contrast, the Ch07 farewell letter could not begin without one (Ch07 P0004).
   - **A conditional dating problem** (C). If a prolepsis presupposes knowledge acquired after P0074, it was revised in or written later. That holds, for instance, if the "betrayal" (Ch11 P0154–P0158) is the price lie. The text does not fix which prolepses do.
   - **Not established.** That the record is identical to the printed text; when any given passage was written, so the Ch11 prolepses cannot be dated to these days; and whom it is for.
7. **The price lie** (`TDH-C001`, `TDH-C012`, `TDH-C057`).
   - **The hint.** The substitute laughs at his belief: 「本当に、自分の寿命が三十万だっていわれて、疑いもせずに信じちまったのか？」 (P0094). He suggests asking Miyagi (P0100).
   - **Her denials.** Miyagi denies repeatedly (P0140, P0143, P0148, P0158; three or four, by count). She concedes only after his overnight reasoning.
   - **The appraisal scene.** INFERENCE, moderate: at the Ch02 appraisal, the clerk's 決まりの悪そうな顔 and 人差し指で頬をかいた (Ch02 P0150) may now read as the moment of the lie. C grades it strong; B and D1 call it a HYPOTHESIS. U12 does not narrate the substitution.
   - **His reasoning.**
     - He first suspected embezzlement (P0150–P0152), but rejected it because he did not want to believe it. That was a motivated inference, which proves correct, unlike U11's unconfirmed SOS thesis.
     - His two reports of the substitute's words disagree. One is 「もう一回確認してみろっていわれただけ…具体的な事実は何も知らされていない」 (P0147). The other is 「ミヤギが嘘をついていると聞いたとき」 (P0150). The man never said 嘘.
     - Then the narration recalls the teacher's 「ここでは、一度そういう考え方を捨ててほしいんです」 (Ch01 P0013; emphasized, P0154). P0153–P0154 is narration interposed in his speech, and nothing shows that he told Miyagi.
     - He reversed his premise: ¥10,000 a year was not a minimum, and a life need not be worth millions (P0155).
     - So the money came from her (P0157).
   - **Her admission.** That the money was hers is first his deduction, carried by the emphasized 〔傍点〕あんたが〔/傍点〕 (P0157). She does not confirm this directly (P0158), but then speaks as if conceding. She says it doesn't matter, gives her reasons, and names the true value, 「……三十円です」 (P0162–P0166). She says 「私があなたに三十万円を与えた理由」 (P0170).
   - **What is overturned.** The clerk's Ch02 statement, 「最低買取価格の一万円」 (Ch02 P0162), was false (her admission). His whole remaining lifespan was valued at thirty yen. The thirty yen and the gift rest on her testimony alone (P0163, P0166, P0170, P0183). The substitute only hinted, and refused to say more (P0094–P0101). It falsifies his first-meeting intuition, 「あの女が関わっているところに、嘘はない」 (Ch02 P0146–P0147).
   - **Her own price.** Her Ch08 testimony, 「一年につき、一万円」, "the same as yours" (Ch08 P0080–P0082), is now doubtful: if his was false, hers may have been too. Unresolved (`TDH-C038`).
   - **His Ch05 vow.** Her 「三千万とか、三十億の価値がある人間」 (P0174) answers it: 「三千万や三十億よりも価値のある三十万にしてやる」 (Ch05 P0014). INFERENCE, a lexical echo. The vow was spoken to her (Ch05 P0008–P0017).
8. **Her reasons** (testimony, hedged: たぶん, かもしれません).
   - **A shared root.** Her giving and his giving away come from loneliness and despair, as self-righteous altruism (P0170).
   - **Her outlook.** It is reversed from Ch08, where she hoped to live 〝ちゃんと〟 after repayment (Ch08 P0074). Now she says she will 「どうせ」 die like her mother first, with no happy life promised (P0163).
   - **Re-reading.** It was her money that he gave away, and that she tried to stop (Ch10 P0210). His 「使い道は俺の勝手だろう？」 (Ch09 P0039) takes on an irony.
   - **An apology.** If she had not lied, he might not have sold at all (P0170).
   - **Wanting to give.** Perhaps she wanted once to be the giver, saving herself by doing for someone like her what no one did for her (P0172). That echoes the old woman's kindness to him (Ch11 P0133), and her own childhood (Ch08 P0069); INFERENCE.
   - **His reply.** Without the lie he would have sold everything, and the lie gave him his remaining pleasures (P0173). His 「嘘をついてくれなかったら」 treats her lie as a favour received.
   - **His Ch05 hope.** That counters his Ch05 hope that restrained selling down (Ch05 P0070–P0073; `TDH-C017`). His account now credits her lie with the three months' content.
    - **A rival for Ch09.** Her 「クスノキさんの気持ちは、よくわかるんですよ…根っこは一緒ですから」 (P0170) is a strong rival to U11's childhood-friend reading of the Ch09 「わからなくはない」 prolepsis. This chapter, too, narrates no moment of recalled understanding.
9. **Her confession** (testimony).
   - **Her view of him.** At first she held him to be worth thirty yen and anyone would have done; after the station night (Ch08) he was "no longer just a subject" (P0183).
   - **What made her happy.** That he talked to her even in public; that he ate, shopped, walked and held hands with her by the river.
   - **"Present."** 「どんなときも一貫して私のことを〝いる〟ものとして扱ってくれたのは、クスノキさん、あなたが初めてだったんです」 (P0183). That reframes his public "madness" (U06, U09–U11) as the gift she valued. Being treated as 〝いる〟 inverts her first-day request, 「私のことは、いないと思っていただいて結構ですから」 (Ch03 P0062).
   - **The confession itself.** 「だから、好きなんです。あなたのこと」 (P0188). The unbracketed 「いなくなる人のこと、好きになっても、しかたないんですけどね」 (P0189) is her speech, as P0190's 「そういって」 shows, not narration. This is the first avowal of love in the text by Miyagi. He names no feeling in return.
   - **The "never again" verdict.** It stands against Miyagi's own Ch04 verdict, 「以後、あなたを好きになろうとしてくれる人は、〔傍点〕二度と現れません〔/傍点〕」 (Ch04 P0046). The text does not reconcile them. INFERENCE: the verdict, too, may have been among her lies, or may have described the original course.
10. **The other lies** (testimony; P0193).
    - **What she disowns.** The misconduct rule, 「あなたが他人に迷惑をかけようとしたら寿命を尽きさせられる」, and the hundred-metre rule are lies, "expedients to protect myself."
    - **What this overturns.**
      - Ch03's relayed rule (`TDH-C014`). Two inferences follow, either of which may hold. The rule was a lie, which would explain why no report followed the U08 touch. Or she simply did not report it (Ch08; `TDH-C014` v2). On her testimony, her Ch08 P0019 statement of timing was part of the fiction.
      - The distance rule (`TDH-C022`; Ch05 P0045–P0047; Ch09 P0070–P0071), which he recalled himself and she confirmed.
    - **What it implies.** INFERENCE: the rules protected her against subjects like the one who killed her mother (Ch08 P0072). On her testimony, his U08 impulse met only a fictional deterrent: at the Ch08 station she was bluffing. The Ch09 pillion rides came out of the hundred-metre lie (Ch09 P0070–P0075).
    - **Scope.** 「たとえば」 makes the list non-exhaustive, and 「どれも…方便」 most naturally covers the rule lies only. She retracts only two rules: death for misconduct (Ch03 P0067; the twenty minutes at Ch08 P0019) and the hundred-metre limit. The three-day withdrawal (Ch03) is not retracted. She calls 「ヒメノさんのこと」 a lie without saying which statement; that does not retract Himeno's forecast suicide. She says 「嘘ばっかりなんです」, but which other statements were lies is not specified. Her testimony throughout (Ch03 onward) is now of uncertain standing, item by item.
11. **The embrace.**
    - **Her offer.** She offers, 「私に何をしても構いませんよ」, even something cruel (P0195–P0197). He holds her tight, and tries to memorize her, 「二度と忘れないように」 (P0199–P0202).
    - **Two touches.** INFERENCE: it answers the U08 scene, where "cruelty" was nearly an assault; now 「ひどいこと」 is an embrace. Her 「ひどいことしますね」 (P0203) names the inversion. Unbracketed speech recurs (Ch10 P0102–P0105; Ch11 P0048, P0211; P0189, P0203), and it includes his own lines. Its function, perhaps murmured or tender speech, is HYPOTHESIS (B).
    - **The exchange of promises.** His 「俺が死んだら、たくさん悲しんでくれ」 and her 「私が死ぬまでは、いつまでもやってあげますよ」 (P0205–P0206) mirror his earlier 「俺が死ぬまでは、いつでもやってやるよ」 (P0186).
    - **Memory.** It also reverses his U10 resolve to die unnoticed, 「猫のように人知れず」 (Ch10 P0258), into a request to be grieved. It asks of her what she wanted of her childhood friend, 「たっぷり悲しんでもらって」 (Ch11 P0205; INFERENCE, lexical). ## 11. Competing interpretations, claims and forecasts

**Interpretation A: the price lie as the betrayal.** U11's prolepsis said he had overlooked the possibility that Miyagi would "betray" him (Ch11 P0154–P0158). U12 reveals the price lie, which dates from their first meeting (Ch02 P0162) and is, by her account, a kind one. The rule lies came later (Ch03 P0067; Ch05 P0045; Ch08 P0019) and were, by her testimony, self-protective (P0193); the Himeno lie's motive is not given in U12.
- INFERENCE, low to moderate: the "betrayal" refers to the price lie. That fits the narrator's simultaneous praise of her as 優しい優しい女の子 (Ch11 P0157), which fits a benevolent lie but not the self-protective ones.
- Weakeners: Ch12 never uses 裏切る, and the text does not link them. U12's deception lexicon, 騙す (P0118, P0152), links to Ch11 P0177, not to 裏切る. He himself rejects the reading that she deceived him from the start (「信じたくなかった」, P0152).
- Rivals:
  - (i) Her love as a defeated expectation, low to moderate. In this text 裏切る means a defeated expectation: 「自分の思い出に裏切られる」 (Ch11 P0153) immediately precedes the prolepsis, and Ch09 P0018 forecast a 「手酷い裏切り」 if he forgot she was there only for work. Her love (P0188) defeats exactly the expectation of Ch10 P0256.
  - (ii) Her unannounced absence, low.
  - (iii) Something not yet in view, open.
- The §4.6 dating problem holds only if A holds.

**Interpretation B: a value set by others, and a value given.** Thirty yen is "a price some important people set" (P0174). Against it stand Miyagi's "thirty million or three billion" (P0174) and his old vow (Ch05 P0014).

His stated motives were partly self-regarding: being remembered even as a suspicious man (Ch09 P0084), finding deliberate eccentricity easier (Ch10 P0057).
