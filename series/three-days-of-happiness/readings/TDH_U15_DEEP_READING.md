## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**The first morning.** It is the first morning of the last three days. From here, the narrator says, there are no watchers' eyes: Miyagi is gone (P0004–P0006). He had long decided how to spend them. He spends the morning writing in the notebook, finishes the events up to yesterday, puts down the fountain pen and sleeps for some hours. He smokes outside and buys a cider from a machine (P0007). His wallet holds ¥187, sixty of it in one-yen coins; he counts three times. Noticing a curious coincidence, he smiles (P0008–P0009). He reviews the notebook, adds needed details, and rides the Cub alone to the places he once visited with Miyagi, seeking what he calls her lingering scent (P0010–P0011).

**Prayers.** She is probably watching someone else now, he supposes. He prays that no one attacks her in despair, that she works on, repays her debt, and lives days happy enough to forget him, and that someone appears whom she values more than him and who values her more than he does (P0012).

**Absence.** In a park, children wave at him. On a whim he pretends Miyagi is there and takes her imaginary hand (P0013–P0014). To others it must look as usual, he supposes, "Kusunoki the fool with his imaginary girlfriend again" (P0015). To him it is utterly different. He is overcome with grief and feels her absence as never before (P0016–P0019). Perhaps, he wonders, it was all a hallucination from the start. He is certain his life ends in three days; that feeling, at least, cannot be false. But did Miyagi really exist? Was she, or even the shop, a convenient fantasy of a man who sensed his death? There is now no way to confirm it (P0020–P0024).

**The crowd.** As he sits slumped at a fountain, a middle-school boy asks innocently: "With Miyagi again today?" "Miyagi's gone," he says. "Did you fight?" the girl asks. "Something like that. Don't you two fight." They shake their heads: if even Kusunoki and Miyagi fight, how could they not? (P0025–P0032). He means to agree, but cannot speak, and finds himself crying uncontrollably. The more he tries to console himself by imagining her beside him, the more the tears flow. The two sit on either side of him and comfort him (P0033–P0037).

When he looks up, a crowd has gathered: more people knew him than he had imagined, it seems (P0038–P0039). Suzumi and Asakura are among them. Asked what happened, he makes up a story: Miyagi gave up on him and left (P0040). A sharp-eyed high-school girl asks angrily what Miyagi disliked about him, as if Miyagi had really existed (P0041–P0043). A man he recognizes, the photo-shop manager, whom the narrator calls the first to affirm Miyagi's existence, says there must have been reasons; she did not seem the kind to do something so cruel (P0044–P0046). "But she did disappear," Suzumi says. A short-haired man out running says that Miyagi must be a no-good woman to leave such a good man, and pats his shoulder (P0047–P0049). He tries to speak, and chokes (P0050).

**Her voice.** Then a voice comes from behind: "Yes, and he's such a good man" (P0053–P0054). He knows the voice; it would take him three hundred, no, three thousand years to forget it (P0055–P0057). He is sure, but cannot believe it until he sees (P0058–P0061). She is laughing (笑っていた; or smiling). "That Miyagi is a no-good woman," she says, and throws her arms around his neck (P0062–P0064). "I'm home, Kusunoki-san. … I looked for you" (P0067). He holds her and smells her hair; it matches the sense of "Miyagi" in his body. She was certainly there (P0068–P0070).

**Seen.** The people around are as confused as he is; probably, he supposes, they were thinking that Miyagi was not supposed to exist. Everyone can see her clearly (P0071–P0076). A man in a tracksuit asks, "Are you Miyagi?" "Yes, the no-good Miyagi," she says. He slaps Kusunoki's shoulder: so she was real, and a beauty; he envies him (P0077–P0079). Kusunoki still cannot grasp why she is here, or why others see her (P0080–P0082). A high-school girl (perhaps the sharp-eyed one; not stated) says Miyagi is just as she imagined (P0083). Asakura suggests they leave the two alone; the crowd disperses with teasing and blessings. "She's just my type, as I imagined. Be happy," Asakura says (P0084–P0088).

**Her account.** She takes his hand and explains: the answer is simple; she did the same as he did (P0089–P0091). How much did she sell? he asks, understanding. "The same as you. I sold it all. I have only three days left" (P0092–P0094). His mind goes blank (P0095). Right after his sale, she says, the substitute contacted her and told her that Kusunoki had cut his lifespan still further, to the limit, and paid off most of her debt; by the time he finished she had decided, and the substitute handled the procedures (P0096). What she sold is unexpressed in her words; lifespan, by strong inference.

**The betrayal.** He ought to grieve, he says: the one he sacrificed everything to protect had betrayed his feelings and thrown away her life again (P0097–P0098). Yet he was happy. Her betrayal, her foolishness, were dearer to him now than anything (P0099–P0100). She leans on him. He is amazing, she says: in thirty days he bought back most of her life. And she is sorry to have thrown away the life he regained. "I'm an idiot, aren't I?" (P0101–P0102). "No," he says; he is the idiot: it seemed he could not live even three days without her, and he did not know what to do (P0103). She laughs and rubs her cheek on his shoulder (P0104). Thanks to him, she says, her life's value seems (みたい) to have risen a little: even after paying off all her debt, money is left over, more than three days could spend (P0105). "Rich, then," he says, hugging her. "Yes. Rich," she says (P0106–P0107). They cry again, both of them (P0108).

**The end.** He will die leaving nothing, he says. Perhaps someone curious will remember the fool he was, but more likely he will be forgotten. He does not mind. He no longer needs the eternity he once dreamed of, or anyone's memory, because she is beside him, laughing. With just that, he could forgive everything (P0111–P0118). "Now, Kusunoki-san," she says, turning to him with a lovely smile. "How shall we spend these three days?" (P0119–P0121). Those three days, he says, will probably be worth more than the miserable thirty years he was to have lived, and more than the meaningful thirty days (P0124–P0127).

**Ending condition.** The first day of his last three days, in a park, after Miyagi's return. By the shop's terms he has three days; by her account so does she; whether the spans align is unstated.
- **The record.** On the first morning he finished writing the notebook up to the day before, then added details.
- **Absence and doubt.** Alone, he grieved her absence and wondered whether she and the shop had been a fantasy; he says he could not confirm it.
- **Her return.** Miyagi returned, seen by everyone, saying she had looked for him.
- **Her sale, by her account.** On learning of his sale from the substitute, she sold all but three days (her words; lifespan by strong inference). She says the price paid off her debt with money left over.
- **The betrayal.** The narrator names her act as her betrayal of his feelings, and says it was dearer to him than anything.
- **The close.** He says he no longer needs to be remembered, and that their three days will probably be worth more than the thirty years and the thirty days he was to have lived.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

1. **The terms of the last three days** (P0004–P0006).
   - **The narrator's statement.** 「ここからは、監視員の目は一切ない」 / 「ミヤギは、もういないということだ」. Stated as fact. It applies the three-day withdrawal rule known from Miyagi's testimony (Ch03 P0068–P0069; Ch05 P0067–P0068; `TDH-C059`). The narration treats the rule as operative; no one confirms it independently.
2. **The record** (P0007, P0010).
   - **Writing.** 「午前中は、ノートへの書き込みに費やした。昨日までの出来事を書き終えると、万年筆を置いて」 (P0007). Then 「ノートを見直して必要な細部を付け足すと」 (P0010).
   - **INFERENCE, strong.** The notebook and fountain pen are those bought in Ch12 (Ch12 P0052). The record reaches the day before, the sale, on the first morning of the last three days. That places U14's narrating present (Ch14 P0173, P0197) on this morning (`TDH-C010`).
   - **Ch14 before the reunion.** The last narrated revision (P0010) precedes the reunion. So Ch14, including 「生きててよかった」 (Ch14 P0201), was written before Miyagi came back (D1).
   - **The narration of Ch15 itself.** It continues past the writing: the ride, the park, her return, the prospect of three days (P0010–P0127). It is narrated from after the reunion, looking ahead: 「多分、その三日間は…なるのだろう」 (P0124–P0127). Its staged beliefs (P0006, P0012, P0021–P0024) are the experiencing self's, left uncorrected (D1). How or when Ch15 was written, or whether it was written at all, and whether P0111–P0121 belong to the narrating present or to the scene, are not stated. 「この記録」 does not appear in Ch15 (B).
   - **Earlier prolepses.** The writing times of the Ch11 "betrayal" and the Ch13 "wisest decision" cannot be fixed; the reunion need not be what they meant when written (D1).
3. **¥187** (P0008–P0009). 「百八十七円…そのうち六十円はすべて一円玉」, counted three times. 「奇妙な偶然に気付き」: the narrator does not name the coincidence. In Ch09 the two read O. Henry's 『賢者の贈り物』 together (Ch09 P0111). INFERENCE, strong, resting on public knowledge of that story rather than on this source: the sum, the sixty in one-yen coins and the triple count adapt the story's opening (D1). No character is said to model an act on the story.
4. **The ride** (P0010–P0011). Alone on the Cub to 「かつてミヤギと一緒にめぐった場所」, seeking 「彼女の残り香のようなもの」.
5. **His prayers** (P0012).
   - **His supposition.** 「ミヤギは今頃、どこかで俺とは別の誰かを監視しているのだろう」: his inference.
   - **The prayers.** Safety from a subject's violence (cf. Ch08 P0072); repayment; happiness enough to forget him; someone she values more and who values her more. INFERENCE: the last two restate his Ch14 wish (Ch14 P0128, P0134).
6. **Pretending and grief** (P0013–P0019).
   - **The act.** For the first time his performance with Miyagi is a pretence, 「ミヤギがいるふり」 (P0013) (D1). He takes 「空想上のミヤギ」's hand (P0014). The onlookers' frame is his supposition (P0015, 架空の恋人).
   - **The grief.** 「まともに立っていられないほどの悲しみ」 (P0018); 「ミヤギの不在を、これ以上ないほど強烈に実感してしまった」 (P0019).
7. **The doubt** (P0020–P0024).
   - **His certainty.** 「自分の一生があと三日で終わるという確信はある」 (P0022).
   - **The question.** Whether Miyagi, and even the shop, were 「都合のいい幻想」 (P0023). 「今となっては、それを確かめる術はなかった」 (P0024); 今となっては is scene time. It turns on himself his Ch02 verdict on the old bookseller's tale, 「そんなに都合のいい話があるわけないじゃないか」 (Ch02 P0066–P0067) (D1).
   - **INFERENCE.** The doubt is narrated as his state then. The following scene answers it: others see her (P0076). The narrator does not comment on the doubt afterwards.
8. **The middle-schoolers** (P0025–P0037).
   - **His answer.** 「ミヤギはさ、もう、いないんだ」 (P0027); 「そんなところだな」 to "a fight?" (P0029).
   - **Their view.** 「あんなに仲良しの二人でさえ」 (P0032): the convention of U14 (Ch14 P0048).
   - **His tears.** 「栓が抜けたように、ぼろぼろ泣いていた」 (P0035). The children comfort him (P0037).
9. **The crowd** (P0038–P0050).
   - **Knowledge of him.** 「自分で想像していた以上に、俺のことを知っている人は大勢いたらしい」 (P0039), hedged.
   - **His fabrication.** 「向こうが俺を見限って、捨てたのだという話をでっちあげた」 (P0040). Perhaps to protect the convention, or her; the motive is not stated. He tells no one in the crowd that he has three days left (C).
   - **The high-school girl.** Angry at Miyagi, 「まるで、本当にミヤギという女の子が存在したかのような口ぶりで」 (P0043).
   - **The photo-shop manager.** 「初めてミヤギの存在を肯定してくれた人」 (P0045), the narrator's identification, consistent with Ch11 P0079–P0094 (店主 there, 店長 here). The "first" holds only if Himeno's Ch10 affirmation, that she could not think his story a lie (Ch10 P0149), is discounted by her letter (D1). He defends her (P0044, P0046).
   - **The runner.** 「そのミヤギってやつは、本当にろくでもない女だな」 (P0048).
10. **Her return** (P0053–P0070).
    - **The voice.** 「そうですよ、こんなにいい人なのに」 (P0054). Hyperbole: 「三百年、いや三千年」 (P0057).
    - **Certainty before sight.** 「俺は確信していた」 / 「でも実際に見るまでは、どうしても信じられなかった」 (P0059, P0061).
    - **Her words.** 「そのミヤギって人は、ろくでもない女ですね」 (P0063), taking up the runner's phrase. 「ただいま、クスノキさん。……探しましたよ」 (P0067).
    - **The embrace.** She embraces him (P0064). The 〝ミヤギ〟 sense in his body matches (P0069). 「確かに彼女は、そこにいた」 (P0070).
11. **Seen by all** (P0071–P0088).
    - **The narrator's statement.** 「ミヤギの姿は、誰の目にもはっきりと映っていた」 (P0076). OBSERVATION, from their reactions (P0075).
    - **The crowd.** The tracksuit man asks and congratulates (P0077–P0079). The high-school girl: 「私が想像してた通り。ほんとにそのまんま」 (P0083). Asakura: 「想像した通り、俺好みの子でしたよ」 (P0087). Testimony; compare Shinbashi's match (Ch14 P0030).
    - **Kusunoki's incomprehension.** P0080–P0082.
12. **Her account** (P0089–P0096), testimony.
    - **The answer.** 「私も、あなたと同じことをしたんです」 (P0090). 「全部売っちゃいました。あと三日しか、残ってません」 (P0094). What she sold is unexpressed in P0094; 寿命 is used only of his sale (P0096). P0093's 「どれくらい……売ったんだ？」 and 「あなたと同じ」 make it lifespan, by strong inference (B).
    - **Why she is visible.** She says the same answer explains both her presence and her visibility (P0090). INFERENCE, moderate to strong (C moderate, B moderate, D1 strong): having sold down to three days, with her debt cleared, she is no longer a watcher, and the invisibility of time-sellers no longer applies (`TDH-C024`). Her own testimony links time-selling to invisibility through her mother: 「母の姿は透明になって誰からも見えなくなってしまう」 (Ch08 P0069). Ch08 P0060's 「今後数十年間、透明人間であり続ける」 is Kusunoki's inference. The rule is not stated. INFERENCE: it carries out his Ch11 guess that her wish was to stop being a watcher (Ch11 P0196); the text draws no link. Her 「ここにいられる」 (P0090) presupposes the withdrawal (D1).
    - **The substitute's role.** 「クスノキさんが寿命を売った直後、あの代理監視員の人が連絡をくれたんです…手続きも、彼がしてくれました」 (P0096). By her testimony, he told her the sale and its effect: two relays, substitute to Miyagi to Kusunoki (C). By his own account in Ch12, the substitute had bought her time (Ch12 P0033), which bears on his handling her procedures (C). That he handled the procedures is her testimony. Whether this was within his duties is not stated.
    - **Her decision.** 「話を聞き終える頃には、私はもう、決めていました」 (P0096). Her motive is not stated. She sold all, more than the debt needed (P0105), and plans the three days with him (P0121): "to share his end" is an inference (D1). By her hedged account (P0105), the condition she gave in Ch08 is met: 「私の寿命に、人並の価値があったら、そうしていたでしょうね」 (Ch08 P0078). Her Ch08 valuation is itself doubtful (`TDH-C038`). When she sold, and whether her three days coincide with his, are unstated.
    - **Her return day.** By day count it matches her promised 「明後日には戻ってきますよ」 (Ch14 P0114), moderate (D1).
13. **The betrayal** (P0097–P0100).
    - **His purpose, now stated.** 「すべてを犠牲にして守ろうとした相手」 (P0098) is the narrator's first statement of the second sale's purpose: to protect her. The debt goal itself was narrated at Ch13 P0006. U14 had the sale's purpose as a strong inference (`TDH-C061`) (D1). P0098 does not settle U14's two readings (revived or never renounced) (D1).
    - **The narrator's naming.** 「俺の気持ちを裏切り、再びその生を投げ打ってしまった」 (P0098; 投げ打つ connotes devotion more than waste, B); 「彼女の裏切りが、彼女の愚かさが、今は、何よりも愛おしかった」 (P0100).
    - **The prolepsis answered.** Ch11 said he had overlooked 「ミヤギが、俺を裏切るという可能性を」 (Ch11 P0154–P0158). U15's 裏切り, the same word with the same roles, applied to her act, gives that prolepsis its content. OBSERVATION of the lexical match; INFERENCE, moderate to strong, that this is the referent: the match is exact in verb and roles, but no explicit link is made. Further ties: Ch11's 「常に隣にいて」 (Ch11 P0155) against 「隣にこの子が、いてくれるから」 (P0116); Ch11's 「寿命ではなく時間を売ることを選んだ」 (Ch11 P0156) against her 「あなたと同じ」 (P0094). Grades: B moderate; C moderate to high, with the rival live; D1 open, leaning to this candidate.

14. **The exchange** (P0101–P0108).
    - **Her words.** 「たった三十日で、私の人生の大半を買い戻しちゃった」 and 「馬鹿ですよね、私」 (P0102). Her Ch14 plea, 「自分から捨てるようなことは、しないでください」 (Ch14 P0142), returns as her own confession (B).
    - **His words.** 「たったの三日でさえ、俺はミヤギなしでは生きられそうになかった」 (P0103; そう: "it seemed"). It stands against P0007's 「とっくに決めていた」: he had long decided how to spend the days, yet was at a loss (P0103) (B, C). It reverses Miyagi's Ch05 picture of a world he could only hold up alone (Ch05 P0054) (C).

    - **Her return.** 「ただいま」 (P0067) answers, by inference, the return she once expected someone to be awaiting: 「私の帰りを待ち望んでいるだろう」 (Ch11 P0203) (C).
    - **Her valuation, testimony.** 「あなたのおかげで、私の人生の価値も、ちょっとは上がっていたみたいなんです」 (P0105). Hedged (みたい); the payment of the debt that follows is unhedged testimony. INFERENCE: it mirrors U14, where his talent bloomed sooner through her presence (Ch14 P0175), here her value raised by him.
    - **"Rich."** 「お金持ちか」 / 「ええ。お金持ちです」 (P0106–P0107). INFERENCE, lexical: it answers Himeno's prophecy of becoming 「お金持ち」 (Ch01 P0070), which Ch14, written before the reunion, judged unfulfilled 「最後まで」 (Ch14 P0199) (B, C).
15. **His summation** (P0111–P0118).
    - **Being forgotten.** Rival sense: 何も残さず may mean no works and no fame, the record being private (Ch12 P0058–P0059) (B, C). His conduct cuts against it: that morning he was finishing a record that addresses a reader (Ch13 P0090) (C, D1). 「俺は、何も残さずに死ぬ」; 「忘れられる可能性の方が大きい」; 「かつて夢見た永遠に、今なら期待しなくて済む」 (P0111–P0115). It completes the Ch02 → Ch14 永遠 chain (Ch02 P0122; Ch14 P0168, P0196).
    - **The reason.** 「隣にこの子が、いてくれるから」 / 「隣でこの子が、笑っていてくれるから」 (P0116–P0117).
    - **Forgiveness.** 「たったそれだけのことで、俺は今、すべてを許せた」 (P0118). The object of 許す is 「すべて」: unspecified.
16. **The last lines** (P0119–P0127).
    - **Her question.** 「これから三日間、どんな風に過ごしましょう？」 (P0121), echoing her Ch12 morning question, 「今日は、どんな風に過ごすんですか？」 (Ch12 P0109) (D1).

## 11. Competing interpretations, claims and forecasts

**Interpretation A: the crossed gifts.** He sold his last days to free her (P0098). She sold hers, by inference to be with him (P0094–P0096). Each gift defeats the other's purpose and answers it: her freedom is spent on his last days; his days are given back as hers. The title's allusion frames this. INFERENCE, strong.
- Two relays carry the account (C). Rival: her act is not a gift for him but a choice for herself, 「たったの三日でさえ」 being his words, not hers; her stated reason is not given beyond apology (P0102). INFERENCE, low to moderate.
- Limit: it is not a mutual cancellation as in O. Henry; his payment funded hers, and money is left over (D1).

**Interpretation B: the betrayal fulfilled as love.** The Ch11 prolepsis promised a betrayal by 優しい優しい女の子 (Ch11 P0157–P0158). It arrives, by inference, as her defeat of his sacrifice, which he finds dearest (P0100). INFERENCE, moderate to strong.
- Rivals: the price lie (weakened); her love as a defeated expectation (low to moderate); her absence (very low). Composition caveat: the identification requires an unnarrated revision of Ch11 after the reunion (§5).

**Interpretation C: "the wisest decision."** Ch13 called his foolish life the wisest decision, to be understood by reading to the end (Ch13 P0090). At the end: the town that knew his foolishness recognizes her (P0038–P0088); her foolishness answers his (P0100, P0102–P0103); the three days are prospected as worth more than thirty years or thirty days (P0124–P0127); and the title supplies 賢 against the body's 愚か. HYPOTHESIS, moderate for the lexical bridge (愚かしい/賢い with 賢者 and 愚か), low to moderate for the chains (B, D1). No sentence links them, and by the narrated chronology Ch13 P0090 was written before the reunion (P0007), so the reunion cannot have been its content at writing time without an unnarrated revision.
- Rival: P0090's wisdom lies only in his own summation (P0111–P0118), independent of her return. Low to moderate.

**Interpretation D: presence confirmed.** The book's question of whether she is there (Ch03 P0062; Ch13 P0073; Ch14 P0104) ends with 「確かに彼女は、そこにいた」 and others' sight (P0070, P0076). His doubt (P0020–P0024) is answered by the scene, not by argument. INFERENCE, strong.
- Limits: the scene proves her presence and visibility, not the narrator's reliability in general (D1); the shop's reality is answered only by testimony (P0096) (B).

## 12. Exiting state (terminal)

It is the first day of the last three days, in a park, after Miyagi's return. By the shop's terms he has three days; by her account so does she; whether the spans align is unstated. The record ends here, with a prospect.

**Alone.**
- On the first morning he finished writing the notebook to the day before. With ¥187 he rode alone to their places, praying that she would be safe, repay her debt, forget him, and find someone better.
- Pretending she was there, he was overcome by her absence, and wondered whether she and the shop had been a fantasy; he says he could not confirm it.
- Children asked after her; he wept; a crowd gathered. He invented a story that she had left him.

**Her return.**
- Miyagi's voice came from behind. She embraced him: "I'm home. I looked for you." Everyone could see her; two people said she looked as they had imagined.
- By her account, the substitute told her of his sale, and she sold all but three days (lifespan by strong inference); her value seems to have risen, and her debt is paid with money left over.
- He calls her act her betrayal of his feelings, and says it was dearer to him than anything. She apologized; he said it seemed he could not have lived even three days without her.

**The close.**
- He says he will die leaving nothing and does not mind; he no longer needs eternity or memory, because she is beside him. He could forgive everything.
- She asked how they should spend the three days. He says they will probably be worth more than the thirty years and the thirty days he was to have lived.
