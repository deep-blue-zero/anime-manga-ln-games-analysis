## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**Collapse and poverty.** It is summer, and the narrator is twenty. At his part-time job at a beer garden he bows deeply while saying 「申し訳ございません」 for the nineteenth time that day, then, by his own report, faints, hits his head and loses consciousness (「らしかった」, "apparently", P0004). The cause, he says, was obvious: working in blazing heat while hardly eating. A taxi to the emergency room makes his finances worse, and the manager tells him to stay off work for a while (P0005–P0006). He cannot remember when he last ate meat. He has not had a haircut in four months, and has bought no clothes since a coat two winters ago. Since entering university he has never gone out with anyone (P0006). For reasons he does not give, he cannot turn to his parents (P0007).

The only saleable things in his room, which has no computer or television, are his used CDs and books, each chosen with care. He lies on the tatami with headphones and a recycled blue-bladed fan and spends three days listening through most of the CDs before parting with them. It is his first absence from university, and he assumes no one will notice (P0008–P0011, P0022).

**Himeno's prophecy, judged so far.** At twenty, invoking Paul Nizan, the narrator reflects that twenty is not necessarily the most beautiful age (P0014). He quotes Himeno's prophecy of a good summer ten years on and rules that it "missed," at least on his side and so far: nothing good has happened to him, nor seems likely to (P0015). He has not seen her since she transferred schools in the summer of fourth grade (P0016). He is partly glad she has not watched him become ordinary and dull through middle school, high school and university (P0018). He also suspects that if she had come to the same middle school, her presence would have kept him at his best. She would have laughed at his failures and been vexed, to his benefit, at his successes (P0019). He has spent recent years in such regret (P0020).

**Two referrals.** He carries the books to an old secondhand bookshop. He first found it a year earlier while lost. For months he could reach it only by getting lost, as if the road to it appeared and vanished at whim, but this year he has learned the way (P0025–P0026). The elderly owner never smiles and mumbles prices without looking up. This time he looks the narrator in the eye, apparently surprised, and asks whether he is moving. The narrator jokes that paper tastes bad and has no nutrition, and the owner concludes: short of money (P0028–P0036). After a thirty-minute appraisal the owner pays about two-thirds of what the narrator expected (P0040). Then he asks: 「寿命を売る気はねえか？」, "Would you sell your lifespan?" He would not buy it himself, he says. A shop on the fourth floor of a nearby building buys lifespan, and also time and health. The price depends on how fulfilling a life one was going to live (P0048–P0063). The old man judges that the narrator seems a decent sort with passable taste in books and might fetch a fair price (P0059). The narrator thinks of the parable from his childhood moral lesson (P0060). He suspects fear of aging has addled the old man (P0054, P0066), but accepts a hand-drawn map and phone number (P0064).

At a CD shop, the blond clerk reacts to the narrator's selling his CDs with the same air of betrayal. The clerk tells him, as a secret, about the same shop. The narrator gathers that the clerk has sold lifespan himself, but the clerk evades the price, and he draws the same map (P0076–P0090). Outside, the narrator agonizes over and then allows himself a can of cider, his first carbonated drink in a long time, savoring it to the last drop (P0091–P0092). Then he decides the whole thing is absurd, clicks his tongue, crumples the map and throws it away (P0093–P0096).

**The shop.** "In the end" he stands before the building anyway (P0099). It is old, with blackened walls and a dead elevator. He climbs to the fourth floor, telling himself the two men may have meant some dangerous but well-paid job (P0100–P0102). The door is unmarked, but he is sure it is the place (P0103–P0104). Inside, the space is far cleaner than the building, with empty showcases and empty shelves: "a jeweler with no jewels" (P0106–P0107). A young woman in a suit and thin-framed glasses, who looks at him as if appraising him, asks: 「時間ですか？　健康ですか？　寿命ですか？」 (P0110–P0112). Tired of thinking, he answers 「寿命です」 at once. He has little left to lose (P0113–P0116).

He had privately valued his remaining sixty or so years at about six hundred million yen, roughly ten million a year. His confidence, he admits, was lower than in elementary school, but he still could not escape the sense that he alone was special. He dreamed ever larger of a single, legendary success (P0119–P0122). Looking back, he says he perhaps needed to be crushed completely, so going to sell his lifespan was probably the right thing: there his past and future life would be denied entirely (P0123–P0125).

The appraisal needs three hours and no name (P0129). While he waits he wanders, broke and hungry. He finds a pack of Seven Stars and a lighter on a bench, looks around for an owner and, seeing none, casually pockets them. He smokes in an alley for the first time in a long time (P0130–P0132). At the station he watches a woman feed pigeons and is disgusted to find their bread appetizing (P0133). To avoid disappointment he lowers his estimate to three hundred million. That is Himeno's lifetime-earnings figure, and a tenth of his childhood three billion. The narrator adds that he still underestimated how low his worth was. He remembered Himeno's figure, but had forgotten his own childhood thought that a classmate's life would come with a disposal fee (P0134–P0138).

**The price.** Back at the shop, the female clerk wakes him with 「クスノキさん」. This is the narrator's first naming, and he does not remember giving the name or showing any identification (P0141–P0143). By the time he returned, he says, he was already willing to believe the story, mainly because of her: he sensed that wherever she is involved there are no lies. He adds that, looking back later, he can see how unreliable his intuition was (P0145–P0147). When she says "thirty," his face apparently lights up with the old three-billion hope. Awkwardly, she scratches her cheek and prints the result rather than saying it (P0149–P0151). He first takes the 三十万 on the sheet as a yearly figure: twenty-four million over an eighty-year life. Even that crushes him, but his senses tell him she is right (P0152–P0160). Then she tells him what the narrator calls a still more merciless fact (P0161). The price per year is the minimum purchase price, ten thousand yen. His remaining life, she reports, is thirty years and three months, so he can leave with about three hundred thousand yen (P0162). He laughs, probably, he says, because he found himself objectively comical. The result is, literally, *off by orders of magnitude* (〔傍点〕桁違い〔/傍点〕, P0163–P0164).

The female clerk says the figure reflects only their standard, not universal value (P0167). The detailed assessment is done by a separate advisory body. The criteria, she has heard, are how far his remaining life would have brought happiness to himself and others, realized his dreams and contributed to society (P0170). The fairness crushes him: he fails on every count (P0171–P0172). Thirty years is also short for a twenty-year-old. When he asks why, she says such information is disclosed only to customers who have sold (P0173–P0175). He asks for time to think (P0177).

**The sale and after.** After a section break that skips the decision, the narrator reports the outcome. He sold all thirty years, keeping three months, and explains that his work-filled life and the day's sales had removed his resistance to selling what belonged to him cheaply (P0181). He nods through the contract, asking nothing, wanting to be out "of this shop. Of this life" (P0182–P0184). The female clerk tells him that up to three transactions are allowed in all, so two more transactions in lifespan, health or time remain (P0185). He leaves with an envelope of three hundred thousand yen (P0186). He feels the loss physically: about ninety percent of something in his core has been drawn out, like a headless chicken still running. His body, now almost certain not to reach twenty-one, feels each second heavier, and he is seized by the urge to do something (P0187–P0188). On the way home a beaming young man walking alone angers him, unaccountably (P0190–P0191). He buys four cans of beer and five skewers of yakitori, eats and drinks on the way, and vomits within thirty minutes of getting home (P0192–P0194). "So my last three months begin. As starts go, close to the worst" (P0195–P0196).

**Ending condition.** Kusunoki, twenty, has about three months to live by the shop's account, and three hundred thousand yen. The valuation and his remaining-life figure are the shop's, accepted on bodily sense. Unsold, his remaining thirty years and three months would have ended at about fifty. Two transactions remain. He does not know why his remaining life was assessed so short, and he asked nothing at contract. Himeno transferred away in fourth-grade summer, and they have not met since.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

1. **Conditions.** The background is poverty, with parents he says he cannot rely on for unstated reasons (P0007). Malnutrition, overwork, a collapse at work, medical costs and forced leave from his job create acute need (P0004–P0006). Social isolation sets the emotional background: no outings since entering university, and "no one would notice" his absence (P0006, P0010). So does his provisional verdict against Himeno's prophecy (P0015–P0020) (OBSERVATION).
2. **The route to the shop.** Two separate intermediaries, the bookshop owner and the CD clerk, react to his selling with dismay. Both refer him to the same shop, citing his taste, with the same map and number (P0048–P0090). He notices the repetition (P0085). Whether the referrals were coordinated is unsupported either way. The clerk says he is "not really supposed to tell" (P0081), and C-U02-10's HYPOTHESIS of systematic referral remains open. Right after the second referral the narrator dismisses the whole thing and throws the map away (P0093–P0096). His going "in the end" (P0099) is elided. The only stated thought on the way is that 「あの二人」, "those two," may have meant a risky, well-paid job they could not name outright (P0102). INFERENCE: the doubled referral matters only as reframed by that rationalization. A rival reading (C-U02-11) is that the referrals failed to persuade him, and that what moved him is not given.
3. **The narrator links the frame.** On hearing the old man, the narrator thinks of the childhood moral lesson (P0060). U01's frame trigger reads: on hearing that lifespan could be bought, "the first thing I remembered" was that lesson (Ch01 P0004). INFERENCE (strong): the U01 frame is this moment in the bookshop. There is a slight tension. U01's 真っ先に ("first of all") sits against U02's order, in which his first conclusion is that the old man is senile (P0054) and the lesson comes after the explanation (P0060) (D1-U02-54).
4. **Why he accepts the shop as real.** By the time he returned he was already "willing to believe," for reasons "complexly entangled." The strongest he names is the female clerk: 「あの女が関わっているところに、嘘はない」 (P0145–P0146). He also states a creed: before the irrational, trust bodily sense over common sense (P0158). The clerk's knowledge of his name comes after that readiness. やはり (P0144) marks it as confirming a suspicion already formed, not founding it (D-U02-06). He then undercuts his intuition: 「後になって振り返ってみると、俺の勘がいかに当てにならないか、よくわかる」 (P0147). What it proved wrong about is withheld (`OPEN`).
5. **The appraisal against the parable.** Several elements recall the parable (Ch01 P0016–P0021): the per-year unit (一年辺りの値段, P0162), the narrator's assumed sixty remaining years (P0119) and the figure 三十年. That the figure answers U01's 「正解らしきもの」 (Ch01 P0032) is INFERENCE (strong). But the proportion is reversed. The parable sold thirty of sixty years; Kusunoki sells thirty of thirty years and three months. He is not shown noticing the difference (C-U02-30). The parable's "buyer" has no confirmed counterpart: there is a shop, a clerk and an unnamed advisory body (B-U02-29, D1-U02-35). `TDH-C002` → **REVISE**.

   The criteria (happiness, others' happiness, realized dreams, contribution; P0170) value a life by its future yield, not by lifetime earnings, the frame Himeno supplied in U01. The future is phrased as a fixed, now-cancelled past twice. One is the narrator's paraphrase of the old man, 送るはずであった (P0058, marked らしい). The other is the clerk's することになっていた (P0170) (B-U02-04). The narrator calls the standard fair, and its fairness the worst part (P0171; VALUE_JUDGMENT, his).

   **The appraisal is testimony.** The figures and criteria are the shop's claims. The clerk relays the criteria as hearsay (「とは聞いています」, P0170) and disclaims universality (P0167). Kusunoki accepts them on bodily sense (P0158), the faculty he later calls unreliable (P0147). The narrating self confirms that the shop existed (P0073) and that he felt the loss (P0187), but not that the forecast was accurate (C-U02-12, D1-U02-32; `TDH-C012`).
6. **Why he sells everything.** The decision itself is skipped by a section break (P0179–P0181; B-U02-13). Afterwards the narrator gives two reasons. One is causal: habituation to selling cheaply (his labor, then his books and CDs) had removed his resistance to selling what belonged to him (P0181). The other is a wish to exit: 「この店からも。この人生からも。」 (P0183–P0184), where 人生 can mean a way of living as well as life itself (B-U02-14). Context: nothing left to lose (P0116). **Money is not cited for the final decision**, and why he kept three months is never stated. At ¥10,000 a year, three months are worth about ¥2,500, so the reserve is not monetary (C-U02-02, D1-U02-18).

   Rivals recorded by the specialists:
   - Despair produced by the verdict itself, which sits immediately before the decision (「俺はどこに救いを見いだせばいいのか」, P0172; INFERENCE).
   - An unstated quasi-logic that years judged worthless are no loss. HYPOTHESIS, not to be put in his mouth.
   - Face-saving in the habituation account (HYPOTHESIS).
   - Impatience to leave an embarrassing scene (P0183; INFERENCE).

   P0123–P0125 frame the sale retrospectively as salutary (正解だったのだろう). This is the narrating self's hedged evaluation, which states no later reform. It is not the chooser's motive (`TDH-C009`).
7. **Consequences.** Physical: hollowing, "a corpse" (P0187). Temporal: urgency, each second weightier (P0188). Social: unaccountable anger at a beaming stranger (P0191). Behavioral: reckless spending on beer and yakitori, then vomiting (P0192–P0194). The chapter ends on the start of his "last three months" (P0195).
8. **Chronology of the separation (resolves a U01 gap).** Himeno transferred schools in the summer of fourth grade, and the two have not met since (P0016; `TDH-C008`). Both U01 scenes "at ten" include her. So the shrine prophecy and proposal (Ch01 P0057, 十歳の夏) probably fall in her transfer summer, before she left. The age-ten lesson falls in fourth grade, which strengthens the identity of the lesson's homeroom teacher with the grades-four-to-six teacher (Ch01 P0051). Confidence varies across participants:
   - the reviewer's baseline: strong;
   - role C: strong for the same summer, moderate-strong for the teacher;
   - role B: medium, because being "ten" in a fourth-grade summer needs a birthday between April and that summer.

   The inference also assumes approximate age labels and the same homeroom within the year, and U01's B-U01-31 counterevidence stands (P0051 introduces the teacher as if new). Whether either child knew of the move at the shrine is unknown. U01's low-confidence "before or during middle school" is superseded (`TDH-C013`).

**Not established.**

- Why Kusunoki cannot rely on his parents.
- Why his remaining life was assessed at only thirty years and three months; he speculates illness or accident (P0173).
- How the shop knew his name.
- What the shop is, who the advisory body is, and what "time" and "health" sales are (the old man does not know, P0063).
- Whether the CD clerk really sold, and on what terms.
- Why he kept three months.
- Whether selling everything, which in the parable means death (Ch01 P0019), is permitted.
- Whether his two remaining 売買 could include *buying*, since 売買 is two-directional (B-U02-23).
- Whether, when invited to ask questions at contract (P0182), the sale had already taken effect, so that he was eligible under the clerk's rule (P0175) (C-U02-18).

## 5. Narration, focalization and time

**The story present is placed; the narrating instance stays later.** U02 places the story's present in the summer when the narrator is twenty, before and through the sale (P0014). The narrating instance lies after the sale. 「俺の予想は、半分当たっている…半分外れている」 (P0070–P0073) uses the present tense of retrospective judgment, and so do 「そう考えると、寿命を売りにいって、正解だったのだろう」 (P0124), 「まだまだ自分の価値の低さを甘く見ている」 (P0138) and 「後になって振り返ってみると」 (P0147). Where that instance sits relative to the "last three months" is **open**. A placement within the three months would depend on the shop's unverified figure and on the narrator narrating while alive (D-U02-01; `TDH-C010`). U02 supports, without proving, the U01 model in which chapter 1's closing present is the pre-sale story present. P0018's 「それはそれでよかったのかもしれない」 continues chapter 1's closing posture, 「あれはあれで…のかもしれない」 (Ch01 P0098). Both models remain open (B-U02-34, C-U02-29, D1-U02-54).

**Prolepsis.** The chapter announces outcomes before scenes four times:

1. The four-sentence block 「俺の予想は、半分当たっている。都合のいい話は、確かになかった。しかし、俺の予想は、半分外れている。寿命を買い取る店は、確かにあった。」 (P0070–P0073) answers his doubt before he reaches the shop.
2. P0123–P0125 announce that he will be completely denied.
3. P0138's present tense, 甘く見ている ("I am still underestimating"), anticipates the floor price.
4. P0147 discredits his intuition about the female clerk before the result.

Each time the chapter keeps suspense of meaning while giving away facts. The narrator also addresses the reader: 「……話を査定の方に戻そう」 (P0148).

**Self-exposure in the narration.** The narrator shows his self-valuation and then punctures it:

- the six-hundred-million estimate (P0119), with its admission of reduced confidence (「小学校時代ほどの自信はないにせよ」);
- the confession that he "still could not escape" feeling special (P0120);
- the dream of eternity (P0122);
- his face apparently lighting up at "thirty" (P0149);
- the reminder of his own forgotten disposal-fee thought (P0138).

P0138 makes pointed, by juxtaposition, the rebound irony that U01's language specialist judged merely available (B-U01-30). It does not say "I am like her." It also settles a U01 voice question. The disposal-fee thought was the child's: 「小学生時代…考えたことは、忘れてしまっていた」 (B-U02-16), where 彼女 refers to the gloomy classmate, not Himeno. P0138 requotes Himeno's figure with a small shift, 平均的なサラリーマン for her original サラリーマンの平均的な. Because the twenty-year-old still held 「自分だけは特別」 (P0120), U01's self-critique (勘違い, Ch01 P0035) belongs to the post-appraisal narrator (C-U02-03). U01's other voice-undecidable line, 「低能なクラスメイト」 (Ch01 P0049), remains `OPEN`.

**The prophecy judged early.** 「ヒメノの予言は、外れた」 (P0015) is anchored by 今のところ ("so far") to the story present, in the very summer she named and before the sale. It is limited by 少なくとも俺の方には ("at least on my side"). It quotes her selectively: the good thing and being glad to have lived, but not greatness, wealth or 『何か』. It is a premature, one-sided verdict, not the narrating instance's final one (D1-U02-20, B-U02-08). Her phrase 私たちにとってもいいこと parses two ways: "something very good for us" or "something good for us too." 外れる/当たる also grade the narrator's own 予想 at P0070–P0072, so prophecy and forecast are scored in one vocabulary.

**A thought that anticipates the script.** Kusunoki's reflection at P0129 ("not universal… but a standard") anticipates the clerk's disclaimer at P0167 in four lexical items. B-U02-18 offers three readings: a retrospective seam; the disclaimer voicing what anyone would think; or a designed echo. Retrospective composition is already established, so the echo does not locate the narrating instance.

**Free indirect thought and historical present.** Thought often enters without quotation marks: 「だって、そうだろう？　そんなに都合のいい話があるわけないじゃないか」 (P0067); 「貧乏人をからかうのが流行っているんだろうか？」 (P0087); 「いくら何でも、それは安すぎるんじゃないか？」 (P0156). These are the twenty-year-old's thoughts in the moment. The closing 「こうして俺の最後の三か月が始まる」 (P0195) is a threshold historical present that literalizes the chapter title.

**He knows himself from outside.** He knows his collapse (P0004) and his face (P0149) only by らしかった, and explains his own laughter with だろう (P0163) (B-U02-26). The self is partly reported to himself.

**Naturalizations raised and exhausted.** The text offers and sets aside a series of rational explanations: heat, the head blow, tinnitus (P0022, P0052), the old man's senility (P0054), mockery (P0087), a scam script (P0082), a risky job (P0102), a TV stunt, a psychology experiment and a prank (P0157). Uncanny markers accrue along the way: the elusive bookshop, certainty at an unmarked door, the empty yet "natural" room, the unspoken name. A residual hallucination reading has only the narrator against it. The text resists it through his bodily sense of loss (P0187) and the narrating self's assertion (P0073) (B-U02-15).

**Focalization of others.** Everyone is seen through Kusunoki. He reads the old man's surprise as a reader's disapproval (P0030), the CD clerk's face as betrayal (P0078), the female clerk's sighs as weariness at a question asked a thousand times (P0169), her awkwardness as reluctance to deliver bad news (P0150) and her tone as wanting him to hurry (P0178). Each reading is marked as his (のかもしれない, らしい, からいって).

## 6. Japanese language and form

- **Apology as labor.** The chapter opens on 「その日十九回目の『申し訳ございません』」 (P0004). Humble apology is counted as a unit of work, and the bow that ends in collapse is literal. It closes its central scene with the female clerk's 「申し訳ありませんが」 and small bow (P0175) (B-U02-12).
- **Registers.** Three people buy from him: the bookshop owner (books), the CD clerk (CDs) and the shop (lifespan). The old man speaks a rough, elderly masculine register: お前, ねえ for ない, 「寿命を売る気はねえか？」 (P0048), 「呆けちまってる」 (P0056), 知らん (P0063). The CD clerk uses youthful casual-polite すよ and すげえ mixed with humble お教えします (P0081), which the narrator hears as a con artist's script (P0082). The female clerk uses formal service keigo: 「いらっしゃいませ」 (P0109), 「お時間をいただきます」 (P0129), 「いかがいたしましょう？」 (P0151). She addresses him as あなた and クスノキさん.

  The narrator answers the old man politely (P0032, P0044). To the female clerk he is polite once (「寿命です」, P0115) and then plain, even curt: 「その基準というのを、詳しく知りたい」 (P0169), 「なぜ俺はこんなに余命が短いんだ？」 (P0174), 「少し、考えさせてくれ」 (P0177). The drop follows his noticing her youth (P0128) and the result (B-U02-10, C-U02-14). Even before either, his unasked question at P0111 is phrased in plain form. That slightly weakens an age-based explanation. The asymmetry may index age-deference toward the old man, resentment at the result, or a power inversion once he is the injured party (`OPEN`).
- **Sesame-dot emphasis.** The only emphasized word is 〔傍点〕桁違い〔/傍点〕 (P0164), "off by orders of magnitude." 予想とは桁違い ("orders of magnitude off from the forecast") is neutral in direction, and 文字通り ("literally") insists on the arithmetic. U01's emphasized ずれて marked the pair's misalignment with their classmates. 桁違い marks the narrator's misalignment with a scale of value.
- **A numeral pun.** In the Japanese 万/億 system, 三十億 (three billion) and 三十万 (three hundred thousand) both begin 三十 ("thirty"). So hearing 〝三十〟 lets him believe for an instant that his childhood three billion was right (P0149). His second misreading, of 〝三十万〟 as a per-year sum (P0152), concerns scope rather than a missing unit. Across ¥3 billion, ¥300 million and ¥300,000 the digit stays 3 while its place changes (B-U02-02, D1-U02-16). English cannot carry this without a gloss. His multiplication uses an eighty-year 一生 rather than the sixty remaining years he had assumed (P0119, P0153). That may be a slip, the character's or the narrator's, or a deliberate whole-life frame (`OPEN`; B-U02-38, C-U02-31).
- **The appraiser appraised.** He bought his CDs and books after 厳正な品定め, "strict appraisal" (P0008). The female clerk looks at him 品定めするように, "as if appraising" (P0110). 査定 covers both the books (P0037) and his life (P0129). The construction 〜だけ残して links the few CDs he keeps (P0022) to the three months he keeps (P0181). 売り払う covers books and years, and P0181 states the analogy (B-U02-05, D1-U02-61). The books fetched two-thirds of his expectation (P0040), a small rehearsal of the life appraisal.
- **The sale as a favor, from both sides.** The seller's side says 買い取ってもらえる/くれる (P0058, P0084, P0094). The shop says お売りいただいた (P0175). Neutral 寿命を売る/買い取る appears at P0048, P0073 and P0124. The narrator casts his needed negation as a service, 否定してもらう (P0123) (B-U02-06; continues `TDH-F001`). 寿命を売る is also flagged as a collocational anomaly (「不自然な単語の組みあわせ」, P0049) and rationalized back into 寿命を縮める ("to shorten one's life") (P0102).
- **Himeno as internalized audience.** At P0019 her laughter takes an adversative passive, 彼女に笑われる ("I would suffer her laughing at me"). Her chagrin takes a benefactive, 悔しがってくれる ("she would be vexed at being outdone, to my benefit"). His relief that his decline was 「彼女に見られずに済んだ」 (P0018) inverts U01's wish to be seen as still "unsold" (Ch01 P0095). 幼馴染 now appears without hedging (P0019), updating `TDH-F006` (B-U02-07, D1-U02-62).
- **Echoes of the lesson.** 「かけがえのないものといわれる人生の価値」 (P0129) echoes the teacher's 「かけがえのないものだといわれたり」 (Ch01 P0005). 「一つの基準」 (P0129) and 正解 (P0124) recur. The empty showcases, 「宝石のない宝石屋。眼鏡のない眼鏡屋。本のない本屋」 (P0107), make the shop's goods invisible. That this makes the parable's unseen 『得体の知れないなにか』 concrete is HYPOTHESIS. No buyer appears on the page (B-U02-29).
- **何か shifts.** 何か moves from an awaited event (Ch01 P0060, P0070) to an extracted substance, 「身体の芯に詰まっていた何か」 (P0187), to an obligation, 「何かしなければならない」 (P0188) (B-U02-19; `TDH-F002`).
- **Three days and the 三 cluster.** He spends 「三日間かけて」 listening to his CDs (P0022), the first occurrence of the title's 三日間 in the narrative. It is mundane here, and it bears on no forecast. Threes cluster: three days, three hours, ¥300,000, thirty years, three months, three transactions. 「三時間ほどお時間をいただきます」 is said in a shop that buys 時間 (P0129). At P0181 時間 has his everyday sense, wage labor as selling hours (B-U02-24). Any thematic weight is HYPOTHESIS (`TDH-F008`).
- **The vanishing road.** The bookshop can be found only by getting lost: 「まるでその古書店に通じる道が、気まぐれに姿を出したり消したりしているかのように」 (P0026). Read now, it prepares the uncanny shop. A naturalistic rival stands: he was new to the town (町の地理が頭に入っていなかった, P0025) and learned the way this year (P0026). INFERENCE: the novel signals its threshold through ordinary topography (getting lost, an unmarked door, a dead elevator) rather than spectacle.
- **Seasonal texture.** Summer markers are dense: 炎天下 (P0005), 蝉 (P0022), 朝顔 (P0027), posters for a summer festival, firefly viewing, stargazing and a reading club (P0038), 風鈴 (P0039), the smell of incense and tatami (P0038), with あの懐かしい匂い presuming a memory shared with the reader. They set the "summer ten years later" that Himeno prophesied. The festival poster quietly recalls the shrine festival of U01.
- **The boy's list.** At ten he listed the rights of twenty: drink, smoke, marry (Ch01 P0078). On this one day at twenty he smokes found cigarettes (P0131) and drinks until he vomits (P0192–P0194). Both are painful 久しぶり pleasures. Marriage, the subject of Himeno's proposal, is untouched. The echo is HYPOTHESIS (B-U02-20, C-U02-20).
- **Euphemism and imagery.** 「俺がいなくなったところで」 (P0010) uses いなくなる, "to be gone," which can euphemize death; here it means missing class (B-U02-40). The headless chicken (P0187) is followed by yakitori (P0192). 「もはや死体と呼んでもいいような状態」 renders the body continuing after its life has gone. The body is personified as impatient (P0188).
- **Literary reference.** Paul Nizan's line (P0014) is the first explicit citation, and the narrator hedges its categorical original (「必ずしも…とは限らない」).

## 7. Characters

**Kusunoki (the narrator; named at P0143).** Twenty, a university student about a year into university (P0025), living alone in an apartment (P0005). He has no PC or TV, and owns used CDs and books chosen with care (P0008). He is desperately poor: meals skipped, no meat, no haircut, no new clothes (P0006). He cannot rely on his parents (P0007), and he is isolated (P0006, P0010). He works a summer beer-garden job (P0004).

His dispositions in this unit:

- **Self-valuation.** Inflated, though less confident than in childhood (P0119–P0122), and punctured in the narration (P0120, P0123, P0138).
- **Regret centered on Himeno**, imagined as the spur that kept him sharp (P0019).

**The CD clerk (blond).** Late twenties, thin, droopy-eyed, in a rock-band T-shirt and faded jeans, with nervous fingers. He is always at the counter (P0078–P0079). **Minor figures.** The beer-garden manager, who orders leave (P0006); a middle-aged woman feeding pigeons (P0133); a beaming young man walking alone (P0190).

## 11. Competing interpretations, claims and forecasts

**Interpretation A: deflation as cure (the narrator's frame).** The narrator frames the appraisal as the total denial he perhaps needed in order to correct his course (P0123–P0125). If the novel bears this out, its movement runs from inflated self-worth through zero toward a different source of value. That is a conditional reading, recorded as `PRIOR_EXPOSURE_POSSIBLE` because it shades into forecast. Weakener: P0123–P0125 are retrospective and hedged, and the chooser sells to exit (P0184).

**Interpretation B: habituation and exit.** The sale is the endpoint of a gradual cheapening: labor, belongings, then time, then life. He sold because he no longer resisted (P0181) and wanted out (P0184). Support: the narrator's explicit causal statement. This reading treats A's salutary frame as hindsight.

**Interpretation C: a fair market for persons.** The shop's criteria value a life by future happiness, others' happiness, realized dreams and contribution, and the narrator calls that fair (P0171). U01's market idiom (`TDH-F005`) becomes an institution. Whether the novel endorses the shop's standard or exposes it is open. The female clerk insists it is "not universal value" (P0167), and the figures remain testimony (`TDH-C012`).
