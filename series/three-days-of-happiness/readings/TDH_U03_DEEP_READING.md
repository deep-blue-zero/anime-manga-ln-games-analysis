## 2. Entering context and exposure boundary

The entering frontier is U02, accepted in commit `18e5d7b4`. Kusunoki, twenty, has sold all but three months of an assessed remaining thirty years and three months, at ¥10,000 a year, for about ¥300,000. Two more transactions are allowed. The shop's female clerk is unnamed, and she says the detailed assessment is done by a separate advisory body. Himeno transferred away in fourth-grade summer.

Open claims entering: `TDH-C001`–`C013`. Pending forecasts: F-U01-a, -c, -f, -g, -C4; F-U02-e, -f, -g; F-U02-C1–C5. Before exposure the principal froze two forecasts (F-U03-a, -b). It made no forecast about the watcher's identity, because it cannot separate one from prior exposure. Prior parametric exposure is declared and quarantined.

**Terms.** "The female clerk" (女店員) is the shop's clerk of U02. She is the young woman at Kusunoki's door, recognized at P0043 and named ミヤギ (Miyagi) at P0050; this reading uses "Miyagi" from P0050 on. Calling the U02 clerk "Miyagi" follows the narrator's own back-dating at P0052 (「昨日のミヤギとの会話」). She is not called "the appraiser": the text assigns the assessment to an advisory body (Ch02 P0170). "The watcher" translates 監視員. Naruse (ナルセ) is a name first written in Kusunoki's list (P0092) and is not otherwise identified. "The system" covers the shop, its advisory body and "headquarters" (本部), whose relations are unstated.

## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**A dream.** After a hot, sleepless night Kusunoki wakes from a vivid, happy dream, which he calls all the crueler for being happy (P0004–P0005). In it he is a high-school student standing outside a park, watching his former elementary classmates hold a reunion with fireworks (P0006–P0007). Himeno appears beside him. Her face is blurred, probably, he says, because he could not imagine her beyond ten, but in the dream he finds it beautiful and is proud to know her (P0008–P0010).

The dream dialogue has no quotation brackets. She asks how high school is. He answers honestly that he isn't enjoying it, though it's not the worst. She says she is much the same, and he is secretly glad she too has a miserable youth (P0011–P0013). She says that looking back, "that time" must have been fun. When he asks which time, she crouches, looks up at him and asks, 「クスノキは、まだ〔傍点〕売れ残ってる〔/傍点〕の？」: is he still *unsold*? (P0014–P0017). "More or less," he says, watching her reaction. She gives an exasperated laugh, admits she is too, then adds shyly, "Good. Right on track." He agrees (P0018–P0022).

He despises himself for so childish a dream at twenty, yet tries hard to memorize it (P0024). At ten, he says, he certainly did not like Himeno much; his goodwill toward her was slight. The problem is that he could not feel even that slight goodwill for anyone he met afterward. Long after she was gone, he realized that this seemingly small goodwill may have been the greatest of his life (P0025–P0027).

**Relief.** Still in bed, he thinks over the sale. It feels real, not like a daydream. He does not regret it; rather, he feels unburdened (P0030–P0032). What had tied him to life, he reflects, was a shallow hope that "something good might happen someday," a hope that was both salvation and trap. To have been told, as he paraphrases the verdict, that nothing good would happen in his life was in a way a mercy: 「これで、安心して死ぬことができる」, "Now I can die at ease" (P0033–P0035). He wants at least to enjoy the remaining three months, so that he can think at the end that his dull life was "fairly happy" for those three months. He decides to go to a bookstore, read magazines and plan (P0036–P0037).

**The visitor.** The doorbell rings, though no one has visited in years (P0037–P0038). Hung over and nauseous, he opens the door to an unfamiliar girl with a carry case (P0039). When he asks who she is, she looks exasperated, puts on glasses and looks at him as if that should settle it. He asks whether she is the one who assessed his lifespan yesterday, and she confirms it (P0040–P0044).

Out of her suit she looks like a different person. She wears a cotton blouse and a saxe-blue dungaree skirt, and her shoulder-length black hair curls slightly inward. Her eyes behind the glasses seem faintly melancholy. A large bandage on her right knee covers what looks like a deep wound (P0045). He now judges her to be about his own age, nineteen or twenty (P0046). His first hope is that she has come to apologize for an error in the assessment, a misplaced digit perhaps (P0048). She removes her glasses, puts them neatly in their case and announces, without expression, 「今日から監視員を務めさせていただく、ミヤギです」: she is Miyagi, his watcher from today (P0049–P0051). He had completely forgotten about the watcher. Recalling the previous day's conversation, he runs to the toilet and vomits (P0052).

**The watcher.** When he comes out, Miyagi is standing right at the door (P0055). She begins to explain that, since his remaining life is under a year, he will be watched at all times from today. He irritably asks her to wait, since he feels ill (P0056–P0058). She takes her case to a corner, sits with her back to the wall, knees drawn up and hugged (三角座り), and watches him (P0059–P0061). She tells him to think of her as absent and to live as usual (P0062). Stealing a glance, he sees her writing in a notebook, perhaps a watch record. He feels the side of his body facing her burn under her gaze (P0063–P0064).

The narrator now gives the explanation Miyagi had given him the day before, as hearsay (「ミヤギの話によると」, 「〜のだそうだ」, 「〜という」, 「〜らしい」; P0065–P0068). Many sellers, she said, become reckless once less than a year remains, and commit "problem behavior." Kusunoki glosses why: people keep rules because trust matters for going on living, and trust cannot be taken to the next world (P0065–P0066). The watcher system, he was told, exists to stop sellers harming others. Anyone with under a year left is watched. If inappropriate behavior is observed, the watcher contacts headquarters and the seller's life is ended on the spot, regardless of remaining lifespan. He draws the conclusion himself: the girl in the corner could take his life with a single phone call (P0067). He was also told that, as a statistically significant finding, people with only a few days left lose the wish to trouble others. So when three days remain, the watcher is withdrawn. 「最後の三日間だけは、一人になれるのだ」: for the last three days alone, one (or he) can be alone (P0068–P0069).

**Evening.** He sleeps again and wakes at seven in the evening, headache and nausea gone. The first day of his precious three months has gone almost as badly as possible (P0072). Miyagi is still in the corner (P0073). Trying to act as usual, he changes into faded jeans and a frayed T-shirt and walks out for supper, with Miyagi about five steps behind (P0074). There is a yellow sunset, evening cicadas, and a single-car train on the line beside the road (P0075). At a run-down vending-machine diner on the old national road, he buys tempura soba from a machine for ¥300 and smokes while it cooks. Miyagi sits on a round stool looking up at a flickering fluorescent light (P0076–P0077). He wonders how she will eat while watching him. She seems so machine-like, so lacking in human warmth, that he could believe she does not eat at all (P0077). He finishes the soba and a sweet iced coffee from a drinks machine (P0078). He came to this place, he admits, only because he knew nothing else. Years of poverty seem to have stripped him of most of his imagination (P0079).

**The list.** Back home he writes in his pocket notebook a list headed 死ぬ前にやりたいこと, "Things to do before I die." It has eight items: not go to university; not work; not resist desire; eat delicious things; see beautiful things; write a will; meet and talk with Naruse; meet Himeno and confess his feelings (P0082–P0093). Miyagi, suddenly behind him, points at the last line: 「それ、やめておいた方がいいですよ」, "You'd better not do that" (P0095–P0097). She ignores his question whether watchers must pry like this too (P0098–P0099).

Instead, Miyagi tells him that "a lot happened" to Himeno. She gave birth at seventeen, left high school, married at eighteen and divorced a year later. Now twenty, she is raising her child at her parents' home. In two years she "is set to" jump to her death, leaving a grim note. Meeting her now would come to no good. Besides, Himeno barely remembers him, and certainly not 〔傍点〕例の約束〔/傍点〕, "that promise," exchanged at ten (P0100–P0101).

He cannot find his voice (P0102–P0103). Hiding his shock, he asks whether she knows everything that will happen (P0104–P0106). Miyagi says no. She knows what "might have happened" in his life and around it, and only the especially important things. That information is now meaningless, she says, since selling his lifespan has changed his future greatly (P0107–P0108). Himeno seems to have mattered greatly to him: the "synopsis" (あらすじ) of his life was full of her (P0109–P0110). He reflexively protests that this is only relative, since nothing else mattered much (P0111). Miyagi says perhaps so, but meeting Himeno would waste his time and ruin his memories. He answers that they are ruined already, and she replies that at least it saved him time (P0112–P0114).

When he asks whether it is all right to tell someone his future, she asks why it shouldn't be. He realizes that any misuse would only mean a report to headquarters and the end of his life (P0115–P0117). "We," Miyagi says, basically want him to live out his remaining time in peace, and give advice and warnings based on his future. She does not specify whether "we" means the watchers or the organization (P0118).

Wanting to answer back, he argues that by sparing him hurt and disappointment she has taken away his "freedom to be hurt and disappointed." What if, hypothetically, he had wanted to be wounded by hearing it from Himeno herself? (P0119–P0120). Miyagi sighs. She says she meant well, concedes that *if* so the remark may have been rash, and apologizes with a quick bow (P0121–P0123). Then she warns him not to expect fairness or consistency in what happens from now on. By selling his lifespan he has jumped into an irrational world without logic, and it is nearly useless to insist on freedom or rights there, since he jumped in himself (P0124). Back in her corner, she says that at least this time she will respect his "freedom to be hurt" and not interfere with the *other* items on the list, provided he troubles no one else (P0125–P0126). "I'd do that without being told," he thinks (P0127). The narrator adds that he did not miss the faintly sad look on Miyagi's face at that moment, but he did not try to think deeply about what it meant (P0128).

**Ending condition.** Day one of the three months has ended. Miyagi now watches Kusunoki. By the rules as she explained them, misconduct reported to headquarters would end his life, and the watcher is withdrawn when three days of a seller's remaining life remain. Two transactions remain (Ch02 P0185). He has listed eight aims and taken no step toward any of the persons or pleasures on it. Miyagi has said she will not interfere, this time, with the items other than Himeno. She has reported, as testimony, a troubled life for Himeno and a suicide "set" for two years hence. Whether that still holds after his sale is unclear.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

1. **The dream and its aftermath.** The dream is Kusunoki's construction, and it rewrites U01's retraction. In U01 Himeno disowned the proposal and said she could hardly be left over, and the boy said "good for her." In the dream she admits she is "unsold" too and herself says 「よかった。順調だね」 (P0017–P0021; B-U03-01). Her line 「あの頃は、きっと楽しかったんだね」 (P0015) repeats the narrator's own closing thought of Ch01 P0098 (D1-U03-21). Nothing in the dream is evidence of what Himeno feels.

   On waking, the narrator names a feeling for Himeno for the first time. He uses the weak word 好意 ("goodwill, fondness"), minimizes it (ほんの僅か), and then says it was perhaps 「最大のもの」 of his life (P0025–P0027). This answers U01's absent-trait test in hedged form. The realization is narrated as coming "long after she was gone," a point the text leaves unplaced. It adds to U01's account of his abstention. Chapter 1 told the abstention as refusal of others (はっきりと拒否した, Ch01 P0094). Chapter 3 adds that he could not feel even slight goodwill for anyone at all thereafter (誰に対しても抱くことができなかった, P0026). This bears on the misanthropy rival in `TDH-C003` and raises a new incapacity reading distinct from U01's incapacity for conformist performance. Rival (B): his "could not" may simply be the effect of fidelity (`TDH-C003` v2).
2. **Relief, and its undercutting.** Kusunoki reports relief rather than regret: the verdict removed a hope that was both salvation and trap (P0032–P0035). The quoted verdict 「今後、お前の人生にいいことは起こらない」 (P0034) was said by no one; it is his own paraphrase of the appraisal (D1-U03-22). The other title word, 幸福, appears in his wish for the three months (P0036; B-U03-08). That wish is grounded in his situation, 「こうなってしまったからには」 ("now that it's come to this"), rather than in the verdict alone. Within hours the relief is undercut. At Miyagi's arrival he "cannot help hoping" the assessment had a misplaced digit (期待せずにはいられなかった, P0048; C-U03-07, D1-U03-16). By Miyagi's own later statement that his future has changed (P0108), the comforting verdict no longer describes his remaining time. He does not register this (C-U03-21, D1-U03-35; `TDH-C017`).
3. **The watcher rules, as relayed.** All rules come through the narrator's report of what Miyagi said the day before (P0065–P0068). They are: constant watching once less than a year remains; summary death for inappropriate behavior, triggered by the watcher's contact (連絡) with headquarters; and withdrawal of the watcher when three days remain, justified by a statistical finding. These are the system's rules as told, not independently verified (OBSERVATION of report; `TDH-C014`). The rationale about trust (P0066) is unmarked. It is probably Kusunoki's gloss, possibly an unmarked paraphrase of Miyagi. "One phone call" (P0067) is his conclusion. Whether the three-day withdrawal tracks his current remaining life if it changes, through further transactions or termination, is unstated.
4. **A narrative gap closed.** 監視 never appears in chapters 1–2, yet Kusunoki says he received a detailed explanation "yesterday" and forgot it (P0052, P0065). INFERENCE, medium: the explanation was part of the contract he nodded through without attention (Ch02 P0182). Neither chapter says when the briefing occurred. The narration of chapter 2 followed the experiencing self's inattention (B-U03-05, C-U03-02).
5. **The list.** The list moves from refusals to wants (P0082). The negatives come first: no university, no work, no resistance to desire. Then pleasures (food, beauty), a will, and two persons: Naruse, unidentified, and Himeno, last. 想いを打ち明ける is conventionally romantic, so that it states a romantic aim is a strong INFERENCE (C-U03-05 rival). The first two items formalize his U02 situation, since he has already missed university and been sent home from work.
6. **Miyagi's disclosure and its status.** Miyagi's account of Himeno has three layers: past facts (birth at seventeen, dropping out, marriage, divorce, child-rearing at her parents' home); a future event, phrased 「飛び降り自殺することになっています」; and a claim about Himeno's memory (P0101). All three are single-source testimony. Its stated source is his life's "synopsis" of what "might have happened" in his life and its surroundings, now changed, and only "especially important" items (P0108–P0110). The claims also exceed that scope: the past promise, Himeno's present state, what Himeno remembers (D1-U03-12/13). Her verb 交わした ("exchanged") frames the promise as mutual, which U01 never showed (B-U03-02).

   Miyagi calls her future information meaningless (P0108), then says her side advises "based on your future" (P0118). Three readings are possible. "Future" may mean the forfeited, recorded one. Information about others around him, such as Himeno, may remain valid. Or this is loose speech (D-U03-24). Whether Himeno's suicide is still on course is `OPEN` (`TDH-C015`).

   Kusunoki's only stated reason in chapter 2 to trust this clerk was an intuition the narrator later called unreliable (Ch02 P0146–P0147). She is now the single source on Himeno's life, and P0128 is the narrator's second flag that his reading of her was limited (B-U03-12, C-U03-03, D1-U03-26). HYPOTHESIS (C-U03-03): she could be shading facts to deter a risky encounter. Support: P0118 only; undecidable.

   Kusunoki does not challenge the information. He calls it 「その事実」 (P0120). His response is shock, which he hides (P0102–P0105), then 「とっくに台なしだ」 (P0113). He turns his questions to Miyagi's knowledge and the rules. He voices no concern about Himeno's predicted death or her child, though the moment gives him a real opportunity to (an absent trait, unobserved rather than denied; C-U03-09). Rivals: the shock may itself be the concern; he may take the information as moot (P0108); the event lies beyond his three months.

   His 「それはあくまで相対的な話だ」 (P0111) is marked つい, "reflexively." It can be read as denial of what the narration admitted (P0025–P0027). It can also be read as literally accurate and consistent with P0026–P0027 (B-U03-04, C-U03-05, D1-U03-55).
7. **Freedom versus the absurd.** Kusunoki's protest about the "freedom to be hurt" (P0120) is the first value claim he argues aloud to another person in the present-day story. His stated motive is to answer back (この女の子に何かいい返してやりたくなった, P0119), and the claim is framed as a hypothetical (「仮に…思っていたとしたら」). INFERENCE: what it protects is the hope attached to Himeno (C-U03-08). Miyagi concedes rashness on that hypothetical, not error (「仮にそうだったとしたら」, P0122). Her non-interference is limited: "at least this time" (少なくとも今回は), the *other* items only, within the bounds of not troubling others (P0126). Her objection to the Himeno item stands (D1-U03-19). Her counter-warning (P0124) declares the world he has entered irrational. It contrasts with U02, where Kusunoki, not the shop, called the valuation fair (Ch02 P0171), after the clerk had disclaimed universal value (Ch02 P0167). Rival readings of P0124 (B, C) include a rationale for withholding redress, a weary truth, and a warning that may itself reflect her own experience. Undecided. Her motives for intervening at all may be the good intention she claims (P0122), risk management on the system's behalf, or personal feeling (C). The text does not decide.

**Not established.**

- Who Naruse is.
- How Miyagi found his apartment, how she eats, and what her knee wound is.
- Why watching begins "today" rather than at the sale (P0056).
- Whom 私たち denotes (P0118).
- What "inappropriate behavior" covers (P0065). He did not ask.
- Who headquarters is.
- Whether the three-day withdrawal tracks a changed remaining life.
- Whether Himeno's forecast suicide is still her future.
- Whether Miyagi's information about Himeno is accurate.
- What the faintly sad look (P0128) meant.

## 5. Narration, focalization and time

**Dream speech without brackets.** The dream dialogue (P0008–P0022) is rendered without 「」. Some lines are carried by narration (と俺に訊いた, とヒメノはうなずく, と彼女はいう); others stand bare (あの頃は、きっと楽しかったんだね). Waking speech uses brackets normally. The formal difference marks dream speech as remembered and constructed. In the dream Himeno addresses him as bare クスノキ. That is a dream construction, not evidence of how the real Himeno addressed him.

**Retrospective hints.** Two narrator interventions point beyond the moment. P0027's 「それに気付いたのは、彼女がいなくなった、ずっと後だった」 places the realization "long after she was gone," a point the text leaves unplaced. P0128's 「…俺は見逃してはいなかった。しかし、その表情が意味するところまでは、深く考えてみようとはしなかった」 records an observation and a failure to pursue its meaning. Together with Ch02 P0147, it is the second narrator flag that his reading of the clerk was limited (B-U03-12). Both are hints, not disclosures. The meaning of her expression is `OPEN`.

**A gap in U02 disclosed, and reference to Miyagi.** Chapter 2's silence on the watcher followed the experiencing self's inattention (§4.4). Person-reference for Miyagi shifts. Chapter 2 used 女 and 女店員. Chapter 3 uses 女の子 ("girl") nine times (P0039–P0119). The word persists alongside ミヤギ after the naming at P0050, tracking his revised sense of her age (P0046) (B-U03-13, C-U03-01).

**Reported exposition.** The watcher rules (P0065–P0069) are delayed exposition, reported as hearsay. The closing line 「最後の三日間だけは、一人になれるのだ」 (P0069) is the narrator's own conclusion, with explanatory のだ, isolated before a section break. Its subject is open: generic "one" or "I." Its affect frames solitude as relief (B-U03-08, C-U03-02, D1-U03-37).

**Kusunoki's perception of Miyagi.** All of her interior comes through his readings. He sees exasperation (呆れたような顔, P0041), melancholy eyes (心なしか憂いを帯びているようにも見える, P0045), expressionless eyes (感情のない目, P0049) and machine-likeness (P0077). He hears a weary sigh (面倒くさそうに, P0121) and sees a faintly sad face (心なしか悲しそうな顔, P0128). He hedges two readings with 心なしか, the melancholy and the sadness, and leaves "emotionless" and "machine-like" unhedged (B-U03-12).

## 6. Japanese language and form

- **Sesame-dot emphasis, twice.** The chapter's only two emphases both fall on the terms of the U01 proposal: dream-Himeno's 〔傍点〕売れ残ってる〔/傍点〕 (P0017) and Miyagi's 〔傍点〕例の約束〔/傍点〕 (P0101). The first puts U01's market term for remaining unmarried in Himeno's imagined mouth. The second is Miyagi's label for the proposal, echoing U01's narrator-construed 約束. The typography ties the dream's fantasy of shared "unsold" fidelity to the report that Himeno has forgotten "that promise" (`TDH-F011`; B-U03-02).
- **三角座り.** The title phrase, sitting with knees drawn up and hugged, a posture taught to schoolchildren, is enacted literally at P0059 and P0125. A childlike, self-enclosing posture belongs to an official whose reports could end his life. The contrast of posture and role is OBSERVATION. Its characterizing weight is INFERENCE.
- **Registers.** Miyagi is always polite and never plain. She drifts from business-formal (務めさせていただく, つきましては) to colloquial-polite (覚えてません, させてもらいます), with a recurring sentence-final から (いないと思っていただいて結構ですから; 私は止めませんから). She addresses him as あなた and クスノキさん. Kusunoki's あんた (P0106) is his first second-person pronoun in present-day speech. He speaks plain and curt: 「その話、もう少し後にしてくれないか？」 (P0057); 「お気遣いありがとう。しかし、とっくに台なしだ」 (P0113). This continues U02's asymmetry (`TDH-F009`; `TDH-F012`; B-U03-15).
- **することになっています.** Miyagi's 「二年後、彼女は飛び降り自殺することになっています」 (P0101) uses the scheduled-future construction of U02's clerk (することになっていた, Ch02 P0170), here in the non-past, presenting Himeno's death as still on schedule. That sits uneasily with Miyagi's statement that his future, at least, has changed (P0108). The modal status is undecidable (B-U03-09, B-U03-23; `TDH-F010`).
- **あらすじ.** Miyagi says his life's 「あらすじ」, its "synopsis," was full of Himeno (P0110). The system's knowledge of a life takes the form of a plot summary (OBSERVATION). The metafictional echo is HYPOTHESIS.
- **Three days.** 最後の三日間 (P0069) is the first consequential use of 三日間: the final three days without a watcher. U02's 三日間 of CD listening was incidental (`TDH-F008`).
- **Imagery.** A yellow sunset, ひぐらし and a single-car train (P0075) recall U01's shrine-scene markers. The diner's decay (faded signs, a single flickering fluorescent light, decade-old arcade cabinets; P0076–P0077) extends U02's world of used and cheapened things. Besides watching him, Miyagi is shown attending to her notebook (P0063), his list (P0096, P0109), her glasses and case (P0041, P0049) and the flickering light (P0077).
- **公正さ returns.** Miyagi's 「公正さとか整合性とかを期待しない方がいいですよ」 (P0124) answers Kusunoki's own U02 verdict, 「その公正さが、さらに俺を打ちのめす」 (Ch02 P0171), and his reading of her as someone who 不正を嫌う (Ch02 P0146).

## 7. Characters

**Kusunoki.** On day one he is hung over. He wakes from a dream of Himeno and holds onto it while calling it childish (P0024). He names a slight goodwill toward her as perhaps the greatest of his life (P0027). He feels relief at a verdict he paraphrases as "nothing good will happen" and wants a "fairly happy" three months (P0034–P0036). His first hope at Miyagi's arrival is a clerical error in his favor (P0048). He is irritable toward her (P0057) and uncomfortable under observation (P0064), and he finds her machine-like (P0077). He eats the only cheap food he knows and admits poverty seems to have stripped his imagination (P0079). He drafts a list of things to do before dying, ending with confessing to Himeno (P0093). Shocked by Miyagi's disclosure, he hides it (P0105). He argues, as a retort, for a "freedom to be hurt" (P0119–P0120) and privately resolves to do as he likes (P0127, unspoken). Ordinary detail: he smokes while waiting for vending-machine soba, wears faded jeans and a frayed T-shirt, drinks a sweet iced coffee, and writes with a ballpoint in a pocket notebook.

**Miyagi (ミヤギ; the female clerk).** Named at P0050, in katakana like Kusunoki and Himeno.
