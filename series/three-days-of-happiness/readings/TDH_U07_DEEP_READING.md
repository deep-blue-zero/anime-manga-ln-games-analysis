## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**The farewell letter.** Setting out to write his farewell letter (遺書), Kusunoki realizes he cannot begin writing anything without an intended reader (P0004). He sits a long time before stationery bought at a local shop. A cicada on the utility pole outside is as loud as if it were in the room. He can blame the cicada for his not writing, but after it flies off he still writes nothing (P0005). Whom does he expect to read it? Words are a means of conveying something invisible inside him to someone (P0006). Asking himself whom he wants to tell what, the first person who comes to mind is, after all (やはり), his childhood friend Himeno. Should he then write her thanks, or a confession of love? (P0007).

As a trial he spends about an hour writing her a letter, which he gives only in summary (P0008). In it he tells her:
- whatever she now thinks of him, he has loved her since "that day" at ten;
- he lived to twenty on the memory of her beside him, and dies because he cannot bear a world without her;
- in a sense he has been dead since they parted;
- he hopes the ten-year-old he was will live on a little longer in her (P0010).

Rereading it, he thinks it will never be posted. Somewhere it commits a fatal mistake; this is not what he wants to say. What he wants to say cannot be set down exactly; put into words, it would die (P0012). His wish comes down to the last sentence, that the boy he was should live on a little longer in Himeno. If the letter is to serve that end, perhaps it should say nothing at all. Anything would do, so long as it is addressed to Himeno and comes from him. If a blank letter would be eerie, he could write only 「手紙が出したかった」, "I wanted to send a letter." Or he could write about trivial everyday things and say nothing of his death (P0013).

He tosses down the pen, folds the letter so that Miyagi cannot read it, and lies looking at the ceiling. When did he last write a letter? He has never had a pen pal, and since elementary school he has had no one to send New Year's or summer cards to. In his whole life it amounts to only a few letters (P0014). Leaving aside 〔傍点〕あれ〔/傍点〕, "*that one*," at seventeen, the last letter he wrote was in the summer of fourth grade (P0015).

**The time capsule.** In the summer when he was ten, the class buried a time capsule, behind the gym as he first recalls it (P0016; he corrects the place at P0071). It was proposed by the homeroom teacher, the one who, through that moral lesson, first gave him the chance to think about the value of a life (P0016). The capsule, a sphere, held the pupils' letters (P0017). The teacher asked them to write to themselves ten years on, with questions to the future self (had their dreams come true, were they happy, did they remember) or requests to it (P0018). She could hardly have failed to foresee, the narrator thinks, that ten years later half of them would have given up their dreams, become unhappy and forgotten much; perhaps the letters were really for the selves writing them (P0019–P0020). She also asked them to write at the end the name of the person they considered their best friend, without worrying how that person felt about them, and promised that no one, not even she, would see it (P0021–P0022).

He cannot remember what he wrote to himself. Whose name he wrote, he has no need to try to remember (P0023–P0024). The capsule was to be dug up after ten years, which is this year. He has had no word of it. Perhaps he simply was not called; perhaps whoever was in charge forgot (P0025). He wants to read that letter before he dies, alone, without meeting any of his former classmates (P0026–P0027). "How will you spend today?" Miyagi asks as he gets up. "Time-capsule raiding," he says (P0028–P0029).

**Home town.** He returns to his home town for the first time in a year. Outside a shabby, prefab-like station spreads the familiar landscape, a green town of slopes, with the sounds of insects and the smell of plants far denser than where he lives now (P0032). Surely he is not going to sneak into an elementary school in broad daylight and dig, Miyagi asks; of course he will wait till night (P0033–P0034). But he has not thought how to kill time in a town with almost no amusements or places to eat, and no convenience store within walking distance. He might have come by moped (P0035). He has no intention of going to his family home, and none of meeting acquaintances (P0036).

Miyagi suggests, as if seeing through him, that he go round places with memories, places he went often as a child and has not visited in years (P0037). The town is full of bad memories, he says. "Except those connected with Himeno, surely?" Miyagi says. He asks her not to bring up that name so lightly, especially not from her mouth. She says she will be careful from now on, but, perhaps meddling, advises him not to go and see anyone. He does not intend to, he says. "Good," she says, with a detached look (P0038–P0043).

At a bench outside the station he notices Miyagi applying what looks like sunscreen. He has thought her pale since they first met. That she works at it surprises him, since he took her for an earnest person indifferent to appearance (P0044–P0045). He asks whether no one but he can see her. "Basically, yes," she says. Always? No one but the person she watches can see her, she answers, but as he knows there are exceptions: when she is off watching duty, as when he visited the shop, only people intending to sell lifespan, time or health can see her. Why does he ask? (P0046–P0049). No reason, he says; he just wondered why she bothers with her looks when no one can see her (P0050). This remark, it seems to him, lands as a blow. "It's a matter of my own mood," she says, as if offended. "You take a shower even when you're not meeting anyone, don't you?" (P0051–P0052). She seems hurt, he thinks. With another girl he would have apologized in a hurry; with Miyagi, he is glad to have scored a hit, and would like to praise himself for his careless remark (P0053).

**The secret base.** Walking while he thinks where to go, his feet take him to the woods near the houses he and Himeno grew up in, where the two often played. He is annoyed to have done just what Miyagi suggested, as though proving how ordinary he is (P0054). He takes a long detour to avoid passing near his family home. The candy shop where he used to hang out has closed, its sign removed (P0055). Five minutes off a woodland path stands an abandoned bus, his and Himeno's "secret base." Its red paint survives faintly. It is rusty outside but, apart from thick dust, surprisingly clean inside, with few insects (P0056–P0057).

He walks through it looking for traces of the two of them, finds none, and is about to leave when he glances at the driver's seat. Beside it is a small arrow in blue permanent marker; following its direction he finds another (P0058–P0059). Six arrows lead to the back of a seat, where there seems to be a 相合傘: the umbrella drawing with two names under it that elementary pupils use to pair other people's names as a joke, or secretly their own with a beloved's (P0060). The names are of course his and Himeno's. He has no memory of drawing it, and only he and Himeno knew the place, so he concludes it must be Himeno's (P0061). She didn't seem the type for such girlish things, he thinks, and smiles (P0062). He looks at it a while. Miyagi looks at it too from behind him, and shows no sign, to him, of saying anything sarcastic (P0063). Then he climbs onto the roof of the bus by a fallen tree, as in childhood, brushes off leaves and twigs, and lies on his back until dusk, when the evening cicadas begin (P0064–P0065).

**Night: the capsule.** By the time he has visited his grandfather's grave and reached the school, it is night. Helping himself to (拝借) a shovel from a shed, he digs where he guesses behind the gym, in the dim green light of an emergency-exit sign (P0068). He digs for about an hour, drenched in sweat, and finds nothing. Either his memory is wrong or the capsule was dug up long ago (P0069). His throat is parched, and after yesterday's batting center his hands are in ruins. Miyagi watches and writes in her notebook (P0070). Smoking on a break, he remembers: they had first meant to bury it by a tree behind the gym, but because a new tree might be planted there, they buried it elsewhere (P0071). Behind the backstop of the schoolyard he strikes something hard in under ten minutes. He digs the sphere out carefully and carries it to the light. He had expected it to be locked, but it slides open (P0072).

He had meant to take only his own letter and put the capsule back. Since it has cost him so much trouble, he decides to read every letter. That should be permitted, he thinks, to someone who will die in a few months (P0073). He reads each pupil's "message to the future self" and "best friend." In his notebook he writes each letter-writer's name with an arrow to the "best friend." Name by name the arrows grow into a kind of relationship chart: who likes whom, who is liked by whom, where it is mutual and where one-sided (P0074–P0075). As he expected, when he has read them all, his is the only isolated name. No one chose him as "best friend" (P0076). And Himeno's letter is nowhere in the capsule. Perhaps she happened to be absent the day it was buried (P0077). If she had been there, he thinks, she would have written his name: a girl who would secretly draw a 相合傘 in the secret base would surely have written it, and added a heart or two. If only Himeno's letter had been there (P0078–P0079).

He stuffs his own letter, found a little earlier, into his jeans pocket and reburies the capsule. He returns the shovel to the shed, washes his hands and face carefully at a nearby tap, and leaves the school (P0080). Dragging his tired body along the night road, he hears Miyagi behind him (P0081–P0082). Surely he understands by now, she says: he should not cling to past relationships, having neglected them all along. After Himeno transferred, did he ever once send her a letter on his own initiative (一度でも、あなたの方から手紙を出しましたか)? After high school, did he ever once contact Naruse? Why did Wakana give up on him? Did he go to reunions? Clinging to the past now, she says, is too convenient (虫がよすぎる) (P0083). He is angry, but has no words to answer (P0084). Perhaps she is right. What he is doing is like someone who believes in no god turning to every shrine, temple and church only when in trouble (P0085). But then, with past and future both closed, what is he to cling to? (P0086).

At the station he finds that the last train has long gone. When he lived here he rarely used the trains, and did not know the last one left so absurdly early (P0087). He could call a taxi, and it was not that he couldn't fall back on his family home, but he chooses to spend the night at the station. Physical pain outweighing mental pain is better, he thinks, than the reverse. He wants to hurt himself moderately, so that his attention goes there (P0088). He sits on a hard bench and closes his eyes. Insects keep striking the fluorescent lights, the station is strangely bright, and insects crawl around his feet (P0089). From the bench behind comes the sound of Miyagi's pen. Tough, he thinks. From the past few days, she apparently hardly ever sleeps properly, sleeping a minute and waking for five through the night. For a watcher there may be no other way, but it is too harsh a job for a young woman (P0090). Still, he does not pity her; he is only glad the role is not his (P0091).

**Ending condition.** It is the night of day three, at his home-town station, after the last train.
- Kusunoki has drafted a letter to Himeno declaring lifelong love, and judged that it commits a "fatal mistake" somewhere and is "not what I want to say." He wishes instead that the boy he was might live on in her.
- He has found a 相合傘 with their two names in their secret base, which he attributes to Himeno.
- He has read every time-capsule letter and found that no classmate named him "best friend." Himeno's letter is missing, and he imagines it would have named him.
- He has pocketed his own letter. The trip's stated aim was to read it, yet the narration reports neither its content nor whether he read it.
- Miyagi has told him he neglected his past relationships and should not cling to them now. He makes no reply, allows that she may be right, and asks what he can cling to.
- Miyagi has told him, as her testimony, of the off-duty exception to her imperceptibility.
- No report, consequence or rule enforcement follows the night's trespass in what is narrated; he is alive at the end.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

1. **The farewell letter turns into a letter to Himeno (`TDH-C029`).** The search for a reader (P0004–P0006) leads at once to Himeno, the first, and the only named, reader he considers (P0007). Family, Wakana and Naruse are not mentioned, an absence with real opportunity. P0007 first raises 愛の告白, a confession of love, as possible content. The trial letter (P0010) is a *summary* the narrator provides: 「要約すると、こんなところだ」 (P0008). It is not a verbatim document. It contains the strongest statements of feeling so far (OBSERVATION):
   - 「十歳のあの日からずっと、お前のことを愛し続けてきた」, the first declarative 愛 in his narration for anyone but himself (compare Ch06 P0100's self-love);
   - survival to twenty owed to her memory.

   It also gives a new account of why he is dying: he cannot bear a world without her. Set beside the accounts already recorded under `TDH-C009`, it is a third account of his motive, and a disowned one.

   **The disowning.** He judges at once that the draft 「どこかで致命的な間違いを犯している」, commits a fatal mistake somewhere, and that 「俺がいいたいのはこんなことではない」, this is not what he wants to say (P0012). P0012 continues: what he wants to say cannot be set down exactly, and put into words it would die. INFERENCE: the narration thereby discloses its most explicit declaration and disowns it. Its claims (love since ten, survival owed to her, death because of her absence, "already dead") are not adopted here as the narrator's beliefs. Where the "fatal mistake" lies is left open. It may lie in the content, or in verbalization: B's rival is that the declarations are sincere but inadequate to what he means. Its "since that day at ten" also stands in tension with Ch03 P0025, where his liking for her at ten was "slight" (ほんの僅か). P0010 has two 「あの日」, "that day at ten" and the day they parted, which may be two different days.

   **The reduced wish.** His wish, that "the ten-year-old I was may live on in her," keeps only the letter's last line (P0013). It replaces a claim about his feeling with a wish about her memory. In what is voiced, the wish takes no account of her forecast death (Ch03 P0101). The "barely remembers" testimony is arguably absorbed by the letter's defiant opening (今更お前が俺のことをどう思おうと) and by 「少しでも長く」, "a little longer." Its alternatives are:
   - a blank letter;
   - 「手紙が出したかった」, whose past tense may be posthumous ("I wanted to") or a softened present ("I just felt like it");
   - trivial everyday talk without his death.

   None is written in this unit. List item 6 is attempted and not completed. INFERENCE: it merges with item 8 (Himeno) in a letter without confession.
2. **The letter at seventeen.** 「十七歳のときの〔傍点〕あれ〔/傍点〕を除くと」 (P0015) withholds something written at seventeen, emphasized. In context it is letter-like, since he is counting letters, and the age is most naturally his own. Its recipient, its content and whether it was sent are not stated. The text separates writing a letter from sending it (投函, P0012). Miyagi's question is whether he ever *sent* (出しましたか) Himeno a letter on his own initiative (あなたの方から, P0083). If "that one" was a letter sent to Himeno, her question would be false or loaded. If not, P0014–P0015 confirm her charge from the fourth-grade summer to the present. The text does not decide (`OPEN`; `TDH-C030`). Seventeen is also Miyagi's age for Himeno's childbirth (Ch03 P0101). That is a coincidence of numbers only; nothing in the text links them.
3. **The capsule and the teacher.** The capsule was buried in "the summer when I was ten" (P0016), at the moral-lesson teacher's proposal. P0016 names that teacher explicitly: 「例の、道徳の授業を通じて…学級担任の提案だった」. P0016 states the burial place as "behind the gym." P0071 corrects it to behind the backstop, so P0016 is not evidence of the place.

   **Teacher identity.** P0015 dates his last letter, apart from the one at seventeen, to the summer of fourth grade. The sequence with P0016 makes that letter the capsule letter (strong INFERENCE), so the capsule summer is the fourth-grade summer. P0016 calls its proposer the 学級担任, the homeroom teacher. Barring a change of homeroom within fourth grade, the homeroom teacher then is the woman who taught him from grades four to six (Ch01 P0051). P0016 thus makes the lesson teacher's identity with the grades four to six teacher strong (`TDH-C013` → annotated; conditions in §11). B-U01-31 remains as a stylistic counterweight: Ch01 P0051 introduces her as if she were new.

   **The narrator's gloss.** His reflection that the teacher "could hardly have failed to foresee" that half would fail their wishes, and that the letters were perhaps "for the present self" (P0019–P0020), is retrospective imputation, then hedged. The failures he lists fit his own case (INFERENCE).

   **The same summer.** The capsule summer is also the summer of Himeno's transfer (Ch02 P0016). The order of the two events is unstated (see §4.8).
4. **Whose name he wrote; his own letter.** 「誰の名前を書いたかは、思い出すまでもない」 (P0024) implies, without stating, that he wrote Himeno's name as best friend. That is a strong INFERENCE from context. He does not remember what he wrote to himself (P0023). The trip's stated aim was to read "that letter" (P0026). He finds his own letter "a little earlier" and pockets it (P0080). The narration reports neither its content nor whether he read it, though it had a real opportunity. The capsule now lacks his letter and holds none from her (INFERENCE: if hers was ever there, two are now absent).

   **No dig yet.** The intact, still-buried capsule shows that no official dig has happened (P0072). His fear that he "simply wasn't called" (P0025) is therefore likely unfounded (INFERENCE). He does not draw this conclusion.
5. **The scope of imperceptibility, specified by testimony.** Asked whether no one but he can see her, Miyagi first says 「基本的にはそうですね」 ("basically, yes," P0047). Asked "always?" (P0048), she states her rule: no one but the person watched can see her. Then, by way of example (たとえば), when off watching duty, as when he visited the shop, only people intending to sell lifespan, time or health can see her (姿を目にする; P0049). This narrows U06's open scope question on her word alone. Points to note:
   - 「あなたもご存じの通り」 presupposes that he already knows the exception.
   - 「限って」 excludes ordinary people even off duty.
   - たとえば presents the off-duty case as one example, so further exceptions are implied.
   - The exception is stated for sight only. The vocabulary shifts from U06's 知覚 ("perceive") and its nullification of effects to 見える ("be seen").

   INFERENCE: by her rule, his seeing her at the shop implies the requisite 意思, intention, when he met her. That is compatible with his U02 account. He did not believe the literal story, but came expecting a life-shortening risky job (Ch02 P0102), and answered 「寿命です」 at once (Ch02 P0115). Whether 意思 requires belief in the literal sale is open. He does not register the implication (`TDH-C024` → v2 REVISE).
6. **The careless remark and his relish.** His remark that her grooming is pointless since no one can see her lands as a blow, in his thrice-hedged reading (らしかった, ように, ようだった; P0051–P0053). The remark erases his own gaze a day after he admitted her looks are exactly his type (Ch06 P0008). He calls it 不用意, careless. Only afterward is he glad, as of scoring a hit (一矢報いる), and he would praise himself (P0053). That gladness rests on his reading that she was hurt. His claim that with another girl he would have apologized is unsupported by any case. 「この一言」 (P0051) may echo Ch06's 〔傍点〕その一言〔/傍点〕 (Ch06 P0072), Naruse's wounding remark; in his reading, he is now the one who wounds. The rival is that この一言 is an ordinary phrase.

   Her reply, 「私の気分の問題ですよ」, asserts self-regard independent of any viewer. She likens it to showering when no one is coming (OBSERVATION). The sunscreen (P0045) and her apparent offense (P0052) add to the human detail that U06 set against his "machine-like" verdict. The verdict is not retracted.
7. **The secret base and the 相合傘.** Six blue arrows lead to a 相合傘 with his and Himeno's names (P0059–P0061). He attributes it to Himeno: he has no memory of drawing it, and only the two of them knew the place (P0061). That is his INFERENCE, and both premises weaken within the chapter:
   - his memory fails twice the same day (the burial place, P0016 against P0071; his own message, P0023);
   - the bus has stood abandoned and reachable for ten years.

   His certainty rises across successive propositions:
   - what the drawing is: どうやら…らしかった (P0060);
   - whose names: もちろん (P0061);
   - who drew it: やはり…どう考えても (P0061);
   - the counterfactual about her letter: 必ずや (P0078).

   P0060 names two uses of such drawings. The joke use pairs *other people's* names (ふざけて他人の名前を書いたり); the secret use pairs one's own with a beloved's. The signposted six-arrow trail implies someone expected a finder, which sits awkwardly with his "secretly" (密かに, P0078). The rivals the text leaves open:
   - a third party pairing the two of them as a tease, the use the text names;
   - he drew it himself and forgot;
   - Himeno drew it, as a secret wish (his reading) or as a joke the text does not name.

   When it was drawn is unknown. He smiles (P0062); Miyagi says nothing sarcastic, in his reading (P0063). This is the first physical trace bearing on Himeno in the present narrative. If hers, it is evidence of the child Himeno's pairing of herself with him, not of her present memory, which Miyagi testifies is faint (Ch03 P0101). INFERENCE: the scene partly revises Ch01's account of bored, reluctant visits to each other's homes (Ch01 P0048), since the wood was a place "the two of us often played" (P0054). The rival is that the wood was neutral ground, distinct from the homes.
8. **Reading all the letters.** He reads every pupil's private "best friend" entry and charts them, against the teacher's promise of confidentiality (P0022). That breach is OBSERVATION; any moral verdict on it must name its standard. He folds his own letter so that Miyagi cannot read it (P0014), then reads everyone else's.

   **The license.** He licenses the reading by sunk effort (苦労させられた) and by his impending death: 「あと数か月で死ぬ人間には、それくらいのことは許されるはずだ」 (P0073). That reasserts a dying man's license the day after he concluded that the world does not grow kind to the dying (Ch06 P0123). Rivals:
   - P0073 may be wry;
   - Ch06 P0123 concerns the world's kindness, not his own permissions;
   - Ch06 P0123 itself confesses 「甘えた考えが抜けきらない俺は」, so P0073 also fits as a continuation of that admitted habit, not only a reversal.

   **The chart.** His chart recasts ten-year-olds' single best-friend nominations in the vocabulary of love (両想い, 片想い; P0075). His isolation in it was expected (予想はしていた, P0076); no emotional reaction is narrated. 孤立 can only mean that no classmate nominated him at ten. It says nothing of his later standing, and does not show that nobody liked him (Wakana's 好意, Ch04 P0014).

   **Himeno's missing letter.** Himeno's absence from the capsule (P0077) leaves her choice unknown. His explanation is absence that day. The text leaves rivals open:
   - she had already transferred (same summer, Ch02 P0016);
   - she submitted no letter;
   - her letter was removed. The capsule was unlocked (P0072), and his own pocketing and reburial show that removal leaves no trace.

   His 「もし彼女がいたら」 (P0078) is itself ambiguous between "present that day" and "still at the school," which bears on the transfer rival.

   **The counterfactual.** He fills the gap with a confident counterfactual: she would surely have written his name, with a heart or two (P0078). It rests on the 相合傘 he has just attributed to her (INFERENCE built on INFERENCE). Its benefactive form, 書いていてくれただろうに, may answer the teacher's 書いてあげてください (P0022): a name given as a gift, which no classmate gave him. The rival is that 〜てあげて is a routine softener in a teacher's speech.
9. **Miyagi's verdict on his past.** Miyagi's reproach is unsolicited and concerns his actual past, not a forecast (P0083). It continues the series of unsolicited judgments she has passed on his relationships: the "answer checks" of U04 and U06, though she does not call this one that. Its four rhetorical questions, two of them with 一度でも, presuppose that:
   - he never sent Himeno a letter on his own initiative after her transfer;
   - he never contacted Naruse after high school;
   - Wakana gave up on him (見限った) for a reason;
   - he did not go to reunions.

## 5. Narration, focalization and time

**A summarized document.** P0010 is set off by blank paragraphs as a letter. It is introduced as a summary (要約すると), so its wording is the narrator's condensation, not the letter's text. Even so, it is the most direct declaration of love the narration has offered, and it is disowned two paragraphs later (P0012). The novel stages a confession and its retraction inside one act of writing.

**Withholding.** 「十七歳のときの〔傍点〕あれ〔/傍点〕」 (P0015) is a new withheld item, marked with emphasis. It follows the pattern of delayed disclosure seen with the watcher rules (U03), imperceptibility (U06) and drawing (U06).

**Retrospective reading of the teacher.** The narrator's inference about the teacher's foresight (P0019–P0020) is retrospective commentary by the twenty-year-old on an act at ten.

**Memory-bound narration.** P0016 states the burial place as fact, and P0071 corrects it. The narration stays inside the experiencing self's memory at the time and does not pre-correct it. Unhedged statements of remembered fact in this unit are therefore no stronger than his memory.

**Withheld fulfilment.** The trip's stated aim, reading his own letter (P0026), is never reported as fulfilled. The letter is "found a little earlier" and pocketed (P0080).

**Tense and narratee.** Historical present marks the staging points, and free indirect discourse carries P0012–P0014 and P0086. There is no prolepsis. 「要約すると、こんなところだ」 (P0008) and 「例の」 (P0016) address a narratee who shares the earlier chapters.

**Miyagi's speech.** Miyagi's suggestion (P0037), her answer about the exception (P0047–P0049) and her closing verdict (P0083) are reported directly. After P0050 he speaks no line in the chapter. His readings of her are marked as his: that she sees through him (見抜いたように, P0037), that she is hurt (P0051–P0053), detached (醒めた顔, P0043), without sarcasm (P0063), sleeping in snatches (P0090).

## 6. Japanese language and form

- **Sesame-dot emphasis.** 〔傍点〕あれ〔/傍点〕 (P0015), "*that one*," is the eleventh emphasis and the unit's only one. It is the first emphasized placeholder whose referent the reader cannot recover; the earlier ones, such as 例の約束 and 〔傍点〕その一言〔/傍点〕, had referents the reader could recover, earlier in the text or within the scene (その一言, Ch06 P0072, points forward to Naruse's question). It marks a withheld referent, not a verdict or an affect (`TDH-F019`).
- **遺書 and 手紙.** 遺書 (P0004–P0007) is a farewell letter or suicide note. It shares its word with Himeno's forecast 「陰惨な遺書」 (Ch03 P0101), and turns into 手紙 from P0008. The opening principle, that nothing can be written without an envisaged reader, is stated generally (どんなことを書くにも, P0004). It may bear on the narration's own unnamed addressee (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Words that kill.** 「言葉にすると…死んでしまう」 and 生き延びる (P0012–P0013) set words against survival: the letter's words would kill what they mean, while the boy he was should live on.
- **The letter's address.** In the summarized letter he addresses Himeno as お前 (P0010), the familiar, masculine "you," and frames her present view with 「今更お前が俺のことをどう思おうと知ったことじゃないが」. A defiant opening and a declaration of love sit together.
- **Names written in pairs.** The chapter's writing reduces to names and arrows:
  - addressee and sender suffice for the letter (P0013);
  - a best friend's name ends each capsule letter (P0022);
  - six 矢印, arrows, lead to the 相合傘 pairing his name with Himeno's (P0059–P0061);
  - arrows in his notebook build a 相関図, a relationship chart, in which only his name is 孤立, isolated (P0074–P0076).

  Paired at the bus, isolated in the chart: Himeno's missing letter is the hinge, filled by a counterfactual (P0078).
- **The benefactive economy of names.** The teacher's 書いてあげてください (P0022) makes the name a gift to the friend. His 書いていてくれただろうに (P0078) imagines Himeno giving it to him. No one gave it (P0076). INFERENCE; the rival is that 〜てあげて is a routine softener in a teacher's speech.
- **The teacher's examples.** 「夢は叶いましたか」, 「幸せですか」, 「このことを覚えていますか」 (P0018) bring 幸せ into the frame of a letter to the future self. P0019 inverts the three point for point: dreams given up, happiness lost, things forgotten. The capsule letter spans the same ten years as the childhood promise and the prophecy (Ch01; Ch02 P0015).
- **相合傘.** The umbrella-and-names drawing is a conventional schoolchild sign, used as a joke about others or secretly about oneself and a beloved (P0060). The narrator glosses it himself, naming both uses.
- **The relationship chart.** 相関図 (P0075) turns the class into a directed graph of nominations, recast in the vocabulary of love (好いて／好かれて, 両想い, 片想い). His isolated node (孤立している, P0076) literalizes Ch01's pariah status.
- **Miyagi's words in his narration** (`TDH-F021`, recurring `TDH-F016`).
  - 巡る passes from her suggestion (P0037) into his 神頼み simile (P0085).
  - すがる passes from her 「過去の人間関係にすがるべきではない」 (P0083) into his 「一体何にすがればいいのだろう」 (P0086).
  - His 致命的な間違いを犯している (P0012) echoes her 「思うに、そうした人たちは致命的な勘違いを犯して」 (Ch05 P0019), and his 思うに recurs at P0088. Mundane rivals, ordinary idiom among them, are open.
  - The 神頼み simile also recalls the children praying at the shrine under the higurashi (Ch01 P0061). The higurashi return at dusk (P0065).
- **Agentless closure.** 「過去も未来も閉ざされた」 (P0086) uses an agentless participle (INFERENCE that it hides his own part; rival: a conventional phrase).
- **The remark.** 「この一言」 (P0051) may echo Ch06's emphasized 〔傍点〕その一言〔/傍点〕 (P0072); the rival is an ordinary phrase. 気分の問題 and 気分を害した (P0052) play on 気分.
- **Registers.** Kusunoki to Miyagi: 「あんた」, plain, curt (P0040, P0046). Miyagi: polite, with cool edges (P0043, P0052, P0083). Her 「お節介かもしれませんが」 (P0041) recalls U03. His request not to bring up the name lightly, 「特に、あんたの口からは」 (P0040), guards Himeno's name against Miyagi in particular.

## 7. Characters

**Kusunoki.**
- **The farewell letter.** He cannot write without a reader, and Himeno is the reader who comes to mind (P0004–P0007). In the narrator's summary, he writes and disowns a letter declaring lifelong love (P0010–P0012). He keeps only the wish that the boy he was live on in her (P0013), and folds the draft away from Miyagi (P0014).
- **The past.** A letter-like 〔傍点〕あれ〔/傍点〕 at seventeen is withheld (P0015). He wants to read his capsule letter alone, without classmates (P0026–P0027). On his first trip home in a year he avoids his family home and acquaintances (P0032, P0036, P0055), yet visits his grandfather's grave, a first mention in one subordinate clause (P0068). He asks Miyagi not to name Himeno lightly, "especially from your mouth" (P0040).
- **Miyagi.** He resents following her suggestion because it "proves" him ordinary (P0054); "my feet naturally" softens the compliance. After a remark he calls careless, he relishes having hurt her about her grooming, in his reading of her (P0053).
- **The bus.** He smiles at the 相合傘 he attributes to Himeno (P0062), and lies on the bus roof as in childhood (P0064).
- **The capsule.** He digs for over an hour with hands ruined from the day before (P0068–P0072). He reads all the letters against the promise of secrecy, and charts them (P0073–P0076). He imagines Himeno's missing letter naming him (P0078).

He chooses the station bench to hurt himself moderately (P0088). He infers her sleeplessness, admires her toughness, and disclaims pity (P0090–P0091).
- **Ordinary detail.** Stationery from a local shop; a cicada on the pole; a moped he might have brought; a shovel helped to (拝借); a cigarette break; washing at a tap.

**Miyagi.**
- **Daily care.** She asks again how he will spend the day (P0028). She applies sunscreen and cares about her looks for "my own mood" (P0045, P0052).
- **Toward his past.** She suggests memory places, yet warns him against going to see anyone, naming no one (P0037, P0041). Her suggestion is generic; nothing shows she knew of the bus. Her knowledge of Himeno plausibly comes from the "synopsis" (Ch03 P0110) (INFERENCE). She names Himeno, promises to be careful, and names her again in the verdict (P0039, P0041, P0083).

She neither warns against nor stops the night trespass, the shovel or the reading of the letters; she writes in her notebook (P0033, P0070). Its content is unknown.
- **The verdict on his past.** Unsolicited, she lists his neglect of past relationships (P0083). She frames her interventions with personal-opinion hedges (お節介かもしれませんが, P0041; こういっては何ですけど, P0083). She holds back at the 相合傘 (P0063) and speaks after the capsule result (P0083).
- **Sleep.** By his inference she sleeps in brief snatches, writing through the night (P0090).

**Himeno (absent; traces).** By his inference, the child Himeno drew a 相合傘 of their names in the secret base (P0061). Her letter is missing from the capsule (P0077); why is unknown. In his imagination she would have named him with hearts (P0078). The disowned trial letter names her as the one he has "loved since that day." Her present whereabouts are not given.

**The teacher (past).** She proposed the time capsule, framed letters to the future self and asked each pupil to name a best friend, promising confidentiality (P0016–P0022).

**Classmates (past).** Their capsule letters, read by him, name their best friends. None names him (P0076).

## 11. Competing interpretations, claims and forecasts

**Interpretation A: Himeno as the organizing attachment.** The farewell letter's reader, the 相合傘 and the missing letter all point to Himeno as the organizing attachment of his life. That is consistent with Miyagi's "synopsis full of Himeno" (Ch03 P0110). Support: P0007–P0013, P0061, P0078. Weakeners:
- the attribution of the 相合傘 is his inference, and its premises weaken within the chapter;
- the declaration is disowned;
- his liking at ten was "slight" (Ch03 P0025);
- Miyagi testifies to Himeno's forgetting (Ch03 P0101).

**Interpretation B: clinging to the past as avoidance.** Miyagi's verdict is that he neglected his relationships when they were live and clings to them only now (P0083). His hedged allowance (P0085), the letter count (P0014–P0015) and the pattern of U04–U06 support it. The time capsule, the secret base and the letter to Himeno all look backward. Weakeners: her charges rest on unstated sources and are partly countered (the Naruse call, Ch06 P0020; Wakana's redial, Ch04 P0048; the letter at seventeen). His question, what to cling to with past and future closed (P0086), is left unanswered, and this reading does not answer it for him.

**Interpretation C: writing and its failure.** The farewell letter cannot be written without a reader (P0004). The letter cannot say what he means (P0012). He considers a blank letter the truest (P0013). The capsule letters were perhaps "for the present self" (P0020). His own letter, the trip's goal, is withheld (P0080). The unit makes writing a site where his feelings both appear and fail. HYPOTHESIS, to be tested against later writing.
