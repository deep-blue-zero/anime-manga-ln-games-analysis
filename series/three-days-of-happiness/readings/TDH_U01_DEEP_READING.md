## 3. Current-unit story synopsis

**Front matter.** The cover shows a summer townscape under a towering cumulonimbus: rooftops, a railway line with a train, a distant crane. In the foreground, at a railing, a dark-haired young man in a raglan T-shirt stands with his back to the viewer, hands in pockets, head turned to his right. Beside him a young woman in a short-sleeved white blouse and dark skirt leans on the rail, holding a drink can and a green folder. Her gaze is directed out of the frame, slightly to one side, and her expression is hard to read (`cover.jpeg@S01`). The next image repeats the scene and composition, with title, author and the credit イラスト／E9L, but **without the young woman**; only the young man stands at the rail (`images/00001.jpeg@S02`). An image of a printed contents page follows, giving print page numbers for the fifteen chapters (chapter 1 at p. 004, chapter 15 at p. 277) and no afterword entry (`images/00002.jpeg@S03`). A publisher's rights notice (`text/part0002.html` P0001–P0002) and the text contents page come next. The text contents page lists the fifteen chapters and an afterword (`text/part0003.html`).

**Chapter 1.** An unnamed first-person narrator (俺) opens at an unstated moment: when he heard it said that one could have one's lifespan bought, the first thing he remembered was a moral-education lesson at age ten (P0004). The homeroom teacher, a woman in her late twenties, asked what a human life is worth in money (P0005). A pupil the narrator calls only "a clever one" (お利口さん) cited a book and answered with average lifetime earnings of two to three hundred million yen (P0008–P0009). The teacher accepted this as one valid view and asked the class to set it aside. She drew an unidentifiable figure in blue chalk, which looked like a person or like gum stuck to a road, and posed a parable (P0013–P0016). An unknowable "something" with unlimited money longs for a human way of living and wants to buy your whole life. Refused, it asks to buy thirty of your sixty remaining years. What price per year would it pay? (P0016–P0021). The boy listened, cheek in hand, and thought he could sell that much, since a short, intense life is surely better than a long, thin one (P0020). He could not join the class discussion; he first says he "didn't," then corrects it to "couldn't," since he was as much a pariah as the clever pupil. He pretended not to care, and privately reasoned that if others were worth three hundred million, he could well be worth about three billion (P0023–P0028). He does not remember the outcome, only that the discussion was fruitless. He recalls a girl who looked headed for a bleak future insisting that a life cannot be priced; he thought the right to live her life might come with a disposal fee. A clever class clown joked that no one would pay three hundred yen to live his life (P0029–P0031). The teacher had said there was no correct answer, but something like one existed: ten years later, at twenty, the narrator actually sold his lifespan and received payment (P0032).

The second section turns to the narrator's childhood. He believed he would become a great man and looked down on the other children, who ostracized him and hid his things (P0035–P0036). He scored full marks on every test, but so did one other pupil. Only now does the narrator identify the "clever one" of the lesson: a girl named Himeno (P0037–P0038). Neither could be truly first because of the other, so on the surface they were rivals. Yet each was the only person the other could talk to without being misunderstood (for her side he says "probably"), and so they always ended up together (P0039–P0041). Their houses stood almost opposite each other and their parents were friends. Before elementary school, each child was left at the other's house when the parents were busy (P0042). Before their parents they acted like close childhood friends while kicking each other's shins under the table. The narrator allows that perhaps they really were what they acted (P0043–P0044).

The third section describes school. Himeno was disliked for the same reasons. Their houses near the hilltop were far from classmates', which gave the two an excuse to stay home. They visited each other only when bored, and went out together on festival days and at Christmas so their parents would not worry (P0047–P0049). Class meetings were repeatedly held over their harassment. The woman who was their homeroom teacher from fourth to sixth grade kept it from their parents unless it became serious. She understood, the narrator says, that they needed one place where they could forget they were bullied (P0050–P0051). Their deepest problem was that they could not laugh along when everyone laughed. For him, forcing it felt like something essential being scraped away; he assumes Himeno felt similarly. He concedes that they were haughty, but says that more fundamentally they were *out of step*, like flowers that mistook their season (P0052–P0054).

The fourth section is a scene. One summer evening when they were ten, Himeno and the boy sat at sunset on the stone steps of a shrine above a summer festival. Himeno's bag had been thrown in the trash dozens of times; the boy's shoes had been slit with scissors. They could not go down among the cheerful crowd. They kept silent because speaking would make their voices blur, and waited for a "something" that would affirm their existence (P0057–P0061). As the sun set, Himeno stood, brushed off her skirt and declared in her clear voice that they would become very great in future. The boy asked how far off; "maybe ten years," she said, and he worked out that they would then be twenty (P0062–P0068). She went on: that "something" would surely happen in a summer ten years on, and then they would at last be glad to have lived. Great and rich, they would look back and condemn their school. They must become great enough to give their classmates heart attacks from envy, "otherwise it wouldn't balance out" (P0070–P0074). The boy agreed, escalated the image, and felt her words as prophecy rather than consolation (P0071–P0076).

As they talked about being twenty, the boy listed what adults may do, including marrying, and said he felt he would never be able to marry because he hated too many things. Himeno answered, "Then maybe me too" (P0077–P0084). She looked down. Then, briefly meeting his eyes and looking away, she began haltingly in plain speech: when they were twenty and had become great, if neither had found anyone to marry… After a small cough she switched to polite speech: the two "leftovers" should marry each other. The narrator says he understood even then that the change of tone meant she was embarrassed. He replied in the same polite register with a question, "What is that supposed to be?" She laughed it off: it was a joke, he should forget it, she could hardly be left over. "Good for her," he answered, or thought, and laughed too; the line is unbracketed (P0084–P0093).

The narrator then discloses that he and Himeno later became separated, without saying when or why. He nonetheless remembered "that promise" (約束, his word). In middle school, in high school and at university he firmly refused girls who showed interest in him, so that if he someday met her again he could show her he had properly stayed "unsold." He calls this utterly foolish, twice (P0094–P0096). The chapter ends: ten years have passed since then, and looking back, he thinks that time may, in its own way, have been a shining one (P0097–P0098).

**Ending condition.** The reader knows that the narrator sold some of his lifespan for money at twenty, and that he and Himeno separated at an unstated point after age ten; no reunion is narrated. The text leaves two time models open. On one, the closing present at about twenty (P0097–P0098) is the same as the narrating instance, which already knows of the sale. On the other, it is the story's present, possibly just before the sale. Unrevealed are the narrator's name, the price, quantity, buyer and motive of the sale, the cause of the separation, whether the two have met since, Himeno's present life, and the present-day setting.

## 4. Factual and causal reconstruction

The chapter builds a retrospective causal chain, but most of its links are the narrator's claims rather than demonstrated connections.

1. **The frame trigger.** The opening section is a memory prompted when the narrator "heard the story" (話を聞いた) that lifespan can be bought (P0004). The source and timing of the report, and whether he believed it, are not given. That it came before the sale, near twenty, is an INFERENCE. Only the first section is explicitly tied to the trigger; the later sections are general retrospect (OBSERVATION).
2. **The lesson as template.** The parable (P0016–P0021) supplies a buyer, a price per year of remaining life and a partial sale. P0032 asserts only that the narrator sold lifespan for payment, and that "something like a correct answer existed." The narrator draws the link (OBSERVATION). That the real sale shares the parable's per-year pricing and partial scope is an INFERENCE, likely but not stated. The parable's vocabulary matters. The teacher moves from 命 (life as such) to 人生 (the life one will lead) to 余命 (years remaining, priced 一年につき, per year). What the narrator sells is 寿命 (lifespan as a quantity), and the buyer longs for 生活, a way of living (B-U01-05). HYPOTHESIS: the actual price will correct the boy's self-valuation (P0028). P0032 gives no figure, buyer, quantity, mechanism or motive.
3. **Himeno's estimate frames the room.** That the front-row group's 三億円 (P0026) derives from Himeno's answer (P0009) is a strong INFERENCE, since the teacher also restated the idea (P0013). The boy's private self-valuation (P0027–P0028) builds on the same lifetime-earnings frame, which the teacher had asked the class to discard (P0013; C-U01-03). That Himeno was excluded from the discussion is implied by P0024, not stated (INFERENCE, strong). So Himeno, though apparently shut out, supplied the terms of the room. The boy's three billion stayed private.
4. **Rivalry and pairing.** The narrator's stated grounds differ by relation. Shared rank grounds the *rivalry* (「だから俺たちは…いがみあっていた」, P0039). Mutual intelligibility grounds the *pairing*: 「そういうわけで、最終的には…いつも一緒にいることになる」 (P0041) follows directly from P0040, "she was the only one who took what I said without misunderstanding" (D-U01-04). Proximity and parental childcare came first in time (P0042, 「幼い頃から」). That ostracism also pushed the two together is the analyst's INFERENCE from P0036 and P0047, not the narrator's stated cause.
5. **Performing for adults.** The narrator says the performance of friendship before parents had "no particular reason; it just seemed better" (P0043). Its stated purpose on festival and Christmas outings was to spare the parents needless worry (P0049). The teacher's discretion kept reports of bullying from the parents (P0051). INFERENCE: these practices together kept home free of the fact of bullying. What the parents actually knew is unknown, and slit shoes are hard to hide (C-U01-10). The shrine scene is plausibly one such outing, spent apart above the festival rather than in it (INFERENCE; C-U01-12, D1).
6. **Injury, consolation, prophecy.** The prophecy follows concrete humiliations (P0057) and a silent wait for affirmation (P0060). Its content is rank, wealth and envy. Its logic is accounting: present suffering must be repaid (「そうじゃないと、わりにあわないもんね」, P0074). Himeno's 「ようやく『生きててよかった』って…思える」 (P0070) implies she is not glad to be alive at ten (INFERENCE; C-U01-14). Her own modality moves from assertion through a hedge (たぶん) and confidence (きっと) to necessity (必要がある) and compensation. The boy receives all of it as settled prophecy (確定した未来, P0075; B-U01-23).
7. **Proposal, non-answer, retraction.** The boy first raises marriage, listing it among adult rights he expects never to be able to exercise (P0078–P0082). Himeno's 「じゃあ、私もそうかも」 (P0083) is ambiguous. It may mean that she shares his aversion, or that her prospects depend on his. Her offer is set at twenty, after both have "become great" (「二十歳になって、私たちが偉くなって」, P0087), and is conditional on neither having found a partner (もし…としたら). Whether "having become great" is a further condition or a backdrop presupposed from the prophecy is a parsing choice (D-U01-18). The boy replies with a non-committal question in mirrored register (P0091). She retracts it explicitly (P0092). Nobody accepts within the scene (OBSERVATION; B-U01-26/27, C-U01-19, D1-U01-27).
8. **A retracted proposal kept as a "promise".** Only the narrator calls the exchange 約束 (P0094). The chapter title 十年後の約束 may refer to it, or to the ten-year prophecy, or to both (B-U01-27, D1-U01-49). He reports firmly refusing romantic interest at each stage from middle school through university. His stated purpose is to *show* Himeno he stayed "unsold" (P0095). He remembered the proposal for about ten years. His childhood forecast, 「いつまでも結婚できない気がする」 (P0080), is so far borne out, but by his account through refusal rather than inability. Rival explanations remain live: his professed hatred of everything (P0082) or incapacity for conformist social performance (P0053) could produce the same abstention, with the promise as a dignified story (D1-U01-29). The text gives no independent check (`TDH-C003`). The refusals also show that he received romantic interest, so he was not wholly isolated after childhood (C-U01-20; self-report).

**Not established.** When and why the two separated; whether they have met since; the narrator's life from ten onward beyond schooling and refusals (he did attend university, P0094); the season and place of the narrating present; whether he is currently a student. Whether the lesson's homeroom teacher (P0004) and the teacher of grades four to six (P0051) are one person is unknown. Both are women and both were homeroom teachers in the right years, but P0051 introduces its teacher with a full description, as if new (B-U01-31). Identity is a moderate INFERENCE at most.

## 5. Narration, focalization and time

**Time layers.** There are three. (1) The narrating instance, which knows of the sale: P0032's 〜ことになる ("would come to") presupposes the outcome. It does not imply intention, which an English "was going to" would (B-U01-08). (2) The time when he "heard the story" (P0004), unplaced. (3) Childhood at ten. The closing 「あの頃から、十年が過ぎた。…俺は思う」 (P0097–P0098) gives a present at about twenty. It may be the narrating instance itself, or the story's present just before the sale, from which a following chapter could continue (D1-U01-25; D-U01-01). The text does not decide.

Present-tense evaluations in the narrator's commentary (覚えていない P0029; ように思う P0039; 馬鹿げた話だと思う P0096) belong to the narrating self. They differ from historical-present tags (話を止める P0017; と俺は考える P0027; と俺は繰り返す P0068; うつむく P0084; 彼女はいう P0088), which cluster at narrative hinges and do not mark the narrating present (B-U01-09). The narrator also marks his claimed authority over his past self with 当時の俺 ("I at the time"; P0020, P0071, P0090; B-U01-10). The most consequential instance is his claim to have recognized Himeno's embarrassment then.

**Early disclosure.** The sale (P0032), the separation (P0094) and the ten elapsed years (P0097) are disclosed early and without their causes. Suspense shifts from *whether* to *how* and *what it means*.

**A partial self-revision.** The narrator calls his childhood belief in his own greatness a 勘違い (P0035) and admits arrogance (驕り P0036; 高飛車, 気取り屋 P0054). He twice corrects choice into incapacity: 「参加しなかった。正確にいうと、できなかった」 (P0023) and 「眉一つ動かさなかった。動かせなかった、といってもいい」 (P0053). This face-saving-then-correction habit is characteristic (B-U01-17, C-U01-04). Yet in his own assertion at P0035 he describes his neighborhood as 「どうしようもなく冴えない親の生んだどうしようもなく冴えない子供」 ("hopelessly dull children born to hopelessly dull parents"). He has revised his self-estimate but not clearly his low estimate of others (`TDH-C004`). Two further contemptuous lines are voice-undecidable. The disposal fee at P0030 follows 「そう俺は思った」, and 「低能なクラスメイト」 at P0049 sits inside the past-tense 「…と思ったのだ」. Either may be the boy's view or the narrator's wording (D1-U01-18/19). His 勘違い is open to the charge he levels at the class clown: self-deprecation by someone who privately ranks himself high (P0031; C-U01-07). That reflexive irony is available, not established. So is a second one. His label for the girl, 「いかにも将来が暗そうな」 ("obviously headed for a bleak future", P0030), could rebound on a narrator who sells his lifespan at twenty (B-U01-30).

**Child thought versus narrating thought.** Unbracketed thoughts (P0028, P0076) are the boy's. Non-past passages after a past-tense frame can belong to either self (B-U01-11). The pointed case is 「太く短い人生の方がいいに決まっているのだ」 (P0020). It may be the boy's view in free indirect form, or the present narrator's endorsement. The reviewer argues for the boy's view: the frame 「当時の俺は…と思った」, the feeling verb 気がする and the child-scaled hedge 限度はあるにせよ. The language and character specialists see a live ambiguity. This reading keeps it `OPEN`. Nothing in U01 gives the motive for the sale (D1-U01-17).

**Concealment and naming.** At P0008–P0011 the lifetime-earnings pupil is an anonymous, gender-neutral お利口さん whom "most pupils hated." At P0024 the narrator groups himself with this pupil. At P0038 he names her, setting his own earlier word in 〝〟: 「例の〝お利口さん〟の女の子、ヒメノだ」. The label is the narrator's. 例の points back to his own P0008, and no classmate is quoted using it (D-U01-03). It may echo how others saw her (INFERENCE, weak), or carry fond irony (C-U01-02 rival). The reader meets Himeno first as a pupil the class hated, and the disclosure order replays the surface rivalry before admitting intimacy (C-U01-02, B-U01-12). The narrator is never named. Between the two children there are no second-person pronouns or vocatives: their dialogue runs on 私たち, 俺たち and お互い, even where a vocative would be natural (「……ねえ」, P0087) (B-U01-13).

**Access to other minds.** Himeno's interior comes only through the narrator's markers: おそらく (P0040), のだろう (P0053), the asserted shared knowledge (P0059), and his claim to have recognized her embarrassment at the time (P0090). Her stated position ("joke", P0092) stands against his reading, and this reading keeps both attributed. The teacher's and pupils' motives are likewise his attributions (P0006, P0007, P0051; D1-U01-21).

**External views and doubled perspective.** At the emotional peak the narrator calls himself and Himeno 二人 rather than 俺たち, and hedges: 「二人は少なからず、神に祈っていたのかもしれない」 (P0061). It is the only point where he looks at the pair from outside (B-U01-14). 神 at a shrine is not a monotheistic God. The adult, technical simile 滑走路灯 ("runway lights", P0058) inside a child-focalized scene doubles the perspective in the same way (B-U01-35).

**A seam or an intimacy.** Himeno says 「その『何か』」 (P0070), although nobody has said 何か in their dialogue; the word has appeared only in narration (P0057, P0060). Either she names an unspoken shared waiting, or the narration at P0060 borrows her later word (B-U01-25). `OPEN`.

## 6. Japanese language and form

- **Benefactives in a sale.** 「寿命を買い取ってもらえる」 (P0004) casts being bought as receiving a service: "having someone buy one's lifespan off one." The boy's 「売ってやってもいい」 (P0020) casts selling as a favor granted downward. A plain English "sell" erases both stances (B-U01-04). Rival: 買い取ってもらう is also the everyday idiom of trade-in services, so the favor nuance may be bleached. The stance is a candidate motif, not an established one (`TDH-F001`).
- **Market language for persons.** 生涯賃金 (P0009), 三十億円 (P0028), 処分料金 (P0030), お金持ち (P0070, P0072), わりにあわない (P0074) and 売れ残り (P0089) form a shared idiom of worth-as-price, and Himeno supplies most of its key terms (C-U01-24). The narrator frames his years of abstention in *her* word, 〝売れ残っている〟 (P0095). The verb 売る links staying "unsold" for Himeno (P0095) with selling lifespan (P0032). OBSERVATION; the irony's significance is a HYPOTHESIS (`TDH-F005`).
- **Two "somethings".** The teacher's buyer is 『なにか』, in hiragana and double brackets (P0016–P0021). It speaks softly and pleads: ぼく, きみ, 「それがどうしても必要なんだよ」 (P0019; B-U01-31). What the children await is 「何か」 in kanji (P0060), and in Himeno's speech 『何か』 (P0070). The orthography keeps buyer and awaited event apart; the echo invites a connection. Himeno dates her 何か to a summer ten years on. P0032 dates the sale to "ten years later, at twenty" (B-U01-07). Any identification of the two is HYPOTHESIS (`TDH-F002`).
- **Sesame-dot emphasis.** The chapter's only emphasized word is 〔傍点〕ずれて〔/傍点〕いた (P0054), followed by 咲く季節を間違えた花のように. The narrator elevates being out of step, not intelligence or arrogance, to the root cause. "Different" loses the offset and lag; "out of step" keeps it (`TDH-F003`). The simile implies mistiming rather than defect, and so a season of their own. That links to Himeno's prophecy (HYPOTHESIS; C-U01-11).
- **Mirror syntax.** The pair are written symmetrically: 「ヒメノのせいで俺は…、俺のせいでヒメノは…」 (P0039); こちら/向こう houses facing each other (P0042); 「鞄を背負ったヒメノと、…靴を履いた俺」 (P0057). Reciprocal 〜あう verbs operate between them (いがみあう, 蹴りあう, つねりあう, 目をあわせる). Their social failure is to あわせる with others: 「周りにあわせて上手く笑えなかった」 (P0054) (B-U01-15).
- **Height.** The pair live near the hilltop and sit above the festival. 見下ろす ("look down at", P0058) shares 見下 with 見下す ("look down on", P0036, P0047). They cannot "go down" to the cheerful crowd, precisely because it is cheerful (P0058). 偉くなる carries rank and elevation, which "become successful" flattens (B-U01-21).
- **Voice.** Tears are never named. The children fear their voices will 滲む ("blur, bleed", P0059). Himeno breaks the silence in 「あの透き通った声」 (P0064), with あの as a distal memory deictic. P0064–P0065 stand as detached fragments, each its own paragraph (B-U01-22).
- **Register shift as the emotional signal.** Himeno moves from halting plain speech (P0087) to polite 「一緒になりませんか？」 (P0089). 一緒になる is the idiom for marrying. The boy mirrors her: 「なんですか、それは」 (P0091). The reply can be read as defensive, playful or teasing, and the choice decides how he received the proposal (`OPEN`). Her retreat returns to plain speech with 「忘れてちょうだい」, a familiar soft request, perhaps mock-mature (P0092). English needs a gloss to carry the switch (`TDH-F004`).
- **Certainty versus hedging.** 確 vocabulary (確かに P0020/P0030/P0054; 確かだ P0029; 確かだった P0040; 確定 P0065/P0075) contrasts with the closing のかもしれない (P0098) (B-U01-23).
- **Two retrospects.** Himeno scripts a future look back with 振り返って (P0070): at twenty, great and rich, they will condemn the school. At twenty the narrator looks back with the same verb and judges *the period*: 「あれはあれで、輝かしい時代だったのかもしれない」 (P0098). He does not revise the verdict on the school, but his evaluation of the time diverges from the tone she scripted (OBSERVATION of the echo). INFERENCE, medium: the present at twenty is not the vindication she foresaw. Rival: あれはあれで concedes the misery, and "shining" may mean only that they still had hope (B-U01-24).
- **Brackets around the unsaid.** 「」 here also encloses faces and attitudes never spoken: 「『好きでここにいるわけじゃない』といった顔で」 (P0048) and 「とでもいうふうに振る舞った」 (P0049). These are performances, not utterances, and must not be logged as speech (B-U01-20). The performance vocabulary is dense: ふり, 振る舞う, みたいにしていた, 素振り, 茶化すように.
- **Distanced 幼馴染.** 「いわゆる『幼馴染』というものに当たるのだろう」 (P0042) hedges the word four ways (いわゆる, brackets, というもの, だろう). Elsewhere it is paired with performance (P0043) and with 憎たらしい (P0049). The chapter-10 title uses it warmly: 私の、たった一人の幼馴染へ. That contrast is a cue to track (B-U01-37). 気のおけない (P0043) means "at ease," not "untrustworthy."
- **Rhythm.** Double blank paragraphs divide four sections: frame, self-portrait, social diagnosis, scene (P0033–P0034, P0045–P0046, P0055–P0056). Each ends on a short final turn (the last sentence of P0032; P0044; the simile closing P0054; P0098). ## 7. Characters

**The narrator (俺; unnamed in U01).** About twenty at the closing present (P0032, P0097); attended university (P0094). As a boy he thought himself exceptional, possibly worth ten times his peers (P0028, P0035). He was ostracized and bullied (P0036, P0057). In the lesson he listened cheek in hand, could not join the discussion and feigned indifference. No public answer of his is reported, while Himeno answered aloud (P0009, P0020, P0023–P0025). He could not laugh along with the group (P0053). He values authenticity, the refusal to scrape away an "essential part" (P0053), and despises false modesty (P0031). He thinks about people in money and in types. He gives Himeno epistemic authority: what she says becomes prophecy because she said it (P0075). He is grandiose and hopeless within minutes, vowing revenge (P0076) and then declaring 「世の中に起こることが何もかも嫌なんだ。そんなんで、やってけるわけがないよ」 (P0082). His narration habitually hedges and self-corrects (§5). He never names a feeling for Himeno, though he has room to (P0085–P0086, P0094–P0096). He reports refusing romantic interest at each stage from middle school through university, framed as fidelity and dismissed as foolish (P0094–P0096). Ordinary detail: cheek on hand in class (P0020); slit shoes still worn (P0057); a deadpan, competitive humor (P0073, P0093).

**Himeno (ヒメノ).** Named only in katakana; whether it is a surname or a given name is unknown. Equal top scorer (P0037). In class she answers in polite です/ます and cites a book (P0009). In private her speech is declarative and cutting (P0070). She was disliked for openly looking down on others (P0047). In the shrine scene she makes most of the major moves: she stands, breaks the silence, sets the ten-year horizon and the summer, supplies the idea that a school can be judged bad (P0071), and proposes (P0062–P0089). She speaks of their future as 私たち throughout (P0063, P0070, P0072, P0087). The prophecy shows her values: rank, wealth, others' envy and a ledger of repayment (P0070–P0074). Her exposed moment is the proposal, preceded by looking down, averted eyes, halting speech and a cough, and covered at once by a boast (P0084–P0092). The narrator sees her profile as both older and more easily hurt (P0086). Whether she believed the prophecy, or meant the proposal, is unknown from her side (C-U01-14, C-U01-18).

**The lesson's homeroom teacher (P0004–P0021).** A woman in her late twenties. The narrator says most pupils liked her as "young and pretty" (P0007; his attribution). She pauses about twenty seconds with her back to the class, which the narrator takes as her rethinking the question (P0006). She mocks her own habit of "hard-to-follow parables" (P0013), states calmly that the seller would die (P0019) and insists there is no correct answer (P0021). She handles Himeno's answer with a wry smile and a redirect (P0012–P0013).

**The teacher of grades four to six (P0051).** A woman who, unless matters grew serious, did not report the bullying to the two children's parents. The narrator credits her with understanding their need for one refuge. Whether she is the lesson's teacher is open (§4).

The narrator presents both teachers charitably, and the lesson's teacher he corrects only from experience (P0032; C-U01-23). Toward adults generally (teachers, parents) U01 shows no contempt; his contempt is directed at classmates.

**Classmates.** All unnamed types: an earnest boy who asks what happens after selling (P0018); a girl who looked headed for a bleak future and alone objects to pricing life (P0030); a clever clown (P0031); a front-row group who reason from "three hundred million" (P0026); the class as harassers (P0036, P0047, P0050, P0057). Their views reach us only as the narrator's labels.

**Parents.** Both families are friendly, and they swapped childcare before elementary school (P0042). They are the audience of the children's performance (P0043, P0049), and the school did not inform them of the bullying (P0051). They are offstage.

## 11. Competing interpretations, claims and forecasts

**Interpretation A: the irony of valuation.** The boy who priced himself at about three billion yen will learn what a buyer actually pays. The prophecy of greatness at twenty will meet an adult who sells his lifespan. Support: P0028, P0032, P0072–P0076, P0098. Weakener: later units treat the price neutrally, or self-worth is not at issue.

**Interpretation B: fidelity as the subject.** The economic frame is the occasion; the chapter's weight lies in a one-sided loyalty to a retracted proposal that has organized the narrator's emotional life. Support: the longest scene; P0094–P0095; perhaps the title. Weakener: Himeno proves peripheral later.

**Interpretation C: the unsold and the sold.** A and B are one design. The same market idiom prices a life (P0005–P0032) and a marriageable person (P0089, P0095). The man who stays "unsold" for one person is the man who sells his years. Status: HYPOTHESIS until later units show whether the two transactions interact.

The three readings are compatible; the chapter weights all three. This reading keeps them open.

**Claims registered:**

- `TDH-C001` (OBSERVATION): The narrator sold some lifespan for payment at twenty. The chapter discloses this early, with no price, quantity, buyer, mechanism or motive (P0032).
- `TDH-C002` (INFERENCE): The teacher's parable (a buyer; a price per year of remaining life; a partial sale) is offered by the narrator as the template for his sale. P0032 links the two through 「正解らしきもの」. That the real sale follows the parable's per-year pricing and partial scope is likely but unstated (P0016–P0021, P0032).
- `TDH-C003` (INFERENCE): The narrator's reported refusals of romantic interest, at each stage from middle school through university, are self-attributed to Himeno's proposal.
