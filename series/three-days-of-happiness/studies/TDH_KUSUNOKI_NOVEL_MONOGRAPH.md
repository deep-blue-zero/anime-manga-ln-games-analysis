# Three Days of Happiness — Kusunoki (クスノキ)

## Orientation

Kusunoki is the first-person narrator of 三秋縋『三日間の幸福』 and its only consistently focalizing consciousness. He appears as a boy of ten in chapter 1. For most of the novel he is a poor, isolated university student of twenty who, in one summer, sells all but three months of his lifespan, and later all but three days. He is also the man who writes the record we read. He is named only once his story reaches the shop: the female clerk, later known as Miyagi, wakes him with 「クスノキさん」 (Ch02 P0143). He does not remember giving the name (`TDH-C011`). He has no given name in the text.

The novel turns on his worth: what a life is worth in money, what his is worth to others, and what he thinks it is worth. Kusunoki begins as a boy who privately prices himself at three billion yen (Ch01 P0028). He becomes a twenty-year-old who still cannot shake the feeling that he alone is special (Ch02 P0120). He is appraised at what the shop calls its minimum price (Ch02 P0162), and later learns, by his deduction and Miyagi's implicit admission, that even that figure was her gift (Ch12 P0157, P0170). He ends, with three days left, saying he no longer needs eternity or anyone's memory, because Miyagi is beside him (Ch15 P0114–P0116). Between those points he is contemptuous and self-contemptuous, lonely and avoidant, witty, impulsive, sometimes cruel, ashamed, grateful, secretive and, at the end, publicly and foolishly happy, on his own account, including his account of how the town sees him.

**At a glance**

- **Ages shown.** Ten (Ch01, and recollections); seventeen and nineteen (recounted); twenty throughout the story present (Ch02–Ch15).
- **Situation.** A university student of about a year's standing, living alone in a four-and-a-half-mat apartment (Ch02 P0025; Ch09 P0004). He is desperately poor, cannot rely on his parents (Ch02 P0007), and has been estranged from his talent for painting since seventeen or nineteen (the accounts conflict; §11).
- **Principal relationships.** Miyagi, the shop clerk who becomes his watcher, companion and beloved (Ch02–Ch15). Himeno, the childhood rival whose retracted proposal he kept as a "promise" (Ch01–Ch11, Ch14). His family, whom he avoids (Ch04, Ch07, Ch10–Ch11). Two shopkeepers who refer him to the shop and later advise him (Ch02, Ch13). His neighbour Shinbashi and the town that comes to treat his invisible companion as a shared convention (Ch13–Ch15).
- **Arc in one line.** From pricing himself above others, through accepting that he is worth almost nothing, to valuing three days with one person above both the thirty years he forfeited and the thirty days he sold (Ch15 P0124–P0127).
- **Evidence condition.** Everything about Kusunoki comes through Kusunoki. His interior is richly self-reported; his self-reports are frequently contradicted or qualified by what his own narration shows. Every other character's feelings reach us as his readings or as their testimony.

## 1. Identity, circumstances and boundaries

### 1.1 Name and reference

He calls himself 俺 throughout narration and speech. As a boy writing to his future self he wrote 僕 (Ch09 P0031–P0035), a register the adult re-voices as 俺 when he speaks of 〔傍点〕十年前の俺の頼み〔/傍点〕, "the request of me ten years ago" (Ch09 P0039; `TDH-F024`). クスノキ is written in katakana, like ミヤギ and ヒメノ. That it is a surname is an inference from usage (`TDH-S012`).

Others address him as follows:

- 「クスノキさん」: Miyagi, Wakana, Shinbashi and the townspeople.
- Bare 「クスノキ」: Himeno, Naruse and the hostile acquaintance (Ch10 P0098; Ch06 P0073; Ch13 P0074), and some townspeople on the last day (Ch15 P0039, imagined; P0041).
- お前: the old bookseller, Naruse, the substitute watcher (Ch02 P0043; Ch06 P0073; Ch12 P0025).

The town's shorthand, as he imagines it, is 「クスノキの馬鹿」 (Ch15 P0015).

### 1.2 Circumstances at twenty

When the story present opens he is twenty, in summer (Ch02 P0014). He works at a beer garden and collapses there from heat and hunger after his nineteenth 「申し訳ございません」 of the day (Ch02 P0004). He has not eaten meat in memory, has not had a haircut in four months, and has bought no clothes since a coat two winters earlier. Since entering university he has never gone out with anyone (Ch02 P0006). He "cannot rely on" his parents, for reasons the chapter does not give (Ch02 P0007).

He owns no computer or television. His saleable possessions are used CDs and books, each bought after 「厳正な品定め」, "strict appraisal" (Ch02 P0008). His university is, by his own account, 「呆れるほど平凡」. He talked his parents round to paying the entrance fee and covers tuition and living costs himself (Ch10 P0119). He moved to this town for university and learned its streets only slowly (Ch02 P0025–P0026).

His home town is a green, sloping place a train ride away. It has his family home, the candy shop of his childhood, the elementary school, the abandoned bus that was his and Himeno's "secret base," the shrine of the childhood prophecy, and his grandfather's grave (Ch07 P0032–P0068; Ch09 P0120–P0134; Ch11 P0097–P0100).

### 1.3 Boundaries of this portrait

- **Source.** Chapters 1–15 of the Japanese electronic edition (witness `TDH-JP-01`), read through the accepted readings U01–U15 and the normalized text. The afterword and end matter are excluded as character evidence. No translation, adaptation or outside summary is used. Any training-derived familiarity with the novel is quarantined and supplies nothing here.
- **Continuity.** One continuity. Miyagi's accounts of his "original course" (the forfeited thirty years, Ch05 P0052–P0059; Naruse and Wakana in that course, Ch04 P0044–P0046, Ch06 P0089) are testimony about a counterfactual, not a second continuity.
- **Time.** Childhood from about age four (first meeting with Himeno, Ch09 P0141) to the first of his last three days (Ch15). The novel narrates neither those three days nor his death; nothing after Ch15 P0127 is known.
- **Spoilers.** Whole novel, including the ending.

## 2. The boy, the man and the writer

Kusunoki exists in three positions, and the portrait depends on keeping them apart.

### 2.1 Three positions

1. **The boy of ten.** He is seen almost entirely in chapter 1 and in fragments (the capsule letter; memories of fireworks, the candy shop, the observation-deck railing). The narration marks the boy's views as 当時の俺, "me at the time" (Ch01 P0020, P0071, P0090). Unbracketed thoughts such as 「そうだよな、俺たちが偉くなれないわけがないじゃないか」 belong to the boy (Ch01 P0076). Some non-past lines are undecidable between the boy and the later narrator. The best-known case is 「太く短い人生の方がいいに決まっているのだ」 (Ch01 P0020), kept OPEN in U01.
2. **The experiencing twenty-year-old.** He acts in the story present. Much of the narration follows his knowledge at the time and does not pre-correct it. For example, he states the capsule's burial place wrongly and corrects it only when he remembers (Ch07 P0016, P0071). Chapter 2's 「ヒメノの予言は、外れた」 is anchored by 今のところ, "so far," to the moment of the story (Ch02 P0015).
3. **The writer.** The narrating instance comments, judges and withholds. It declares that "from here my foolishness accelerates" (Ch04 P0004). It discloses that he learned "later" how unreliable his intuition about the clerk was (Ch02 P0147). It calls the foolish resolve 「俺の一生でもっとも賢い判断」, the wisest decision of his life (Ch13 P0090).

### 2.2 The record and its chronology (`TDH-C010`)

The narration presents itself, by strong inference, as continuous with a notebook Kusunoki begins writing about thirty days into his three months: 「このとき俺が自分のノートを購入することを思い付かなかったら、この記録が書かれることもなかっただろう」 (Ch12 P0052). He writes 「この一か月にあったこと」, "what has happened this month," starting from the moral lesson in elementary school (Ch12 P0054, P0057). He calls the writing a re-ordering, 「デフラグみたいなもの」 (Ch12 P0059). By the next evening the record has caught up with the present (Ch12 P0074). He reports a side effect: retelling made good memories easier to savour and bad ones easier to accept (Ch12 P0075).

In chapter 13 his time for the record is running short and he must thin the prose (Ch13 P0004–P0005). On the first morning of his last three days he finishes writing "the events up to yesterday," sleeps, then reviews the notebook and adds necessary details (Ch15 P0007, P0010). Chapter 15 itself is narrated from after the reunion and ends in prospect. When, or whether, it was written is not stated.

The identity of the printed text with this notebook is an inference, strong but not explicit. A recorded rival (U12, role B) holds that the printed text may be a later redaction (`TDH-C010` v2–v5).

Three consequences follow for the portrait:

- **Most of chapters 1–11 were first written about day 30.** The man who writes the scathing self-portrait of chapter 2, and calls his childhood greatness a 勘違い, "a misapprehension" (Ch01 P0035), has already met Himeno, read her letter and lived a month with Miyagi. He has not yet learned of the price lie. The substitute's hint comes that night, and Miyagi's admission the next (Ch12 P0079–P0170). The text does not fix which earlier passages were revised later.
- **The prolepses are the writer's.** The possibility he says he overlooked, 「ミヤギが、俺を裏切るという可能性」 (Ch11 P0154–P0158), and "the wisest decision" (Ch13 P0090) are evaluations from a later vantage. Their content is withheld, and by the record's own chronology they were written before the reunion. Identifying them with the ending would require an unnarrated revision (`TDH-C056` v2, `TDH-C064` v2; §14).
- **The record is an act of his character.** He began it because he wanted, "in a wholesome sense," to do something he would not want Miyagi to see (Ch12 P0053). When the substitute asks whether he will let someone read it, he says he does not care: he writes to sort his head (Ch12 P0058–P0059). Chapter 13 nonetheless presupposes a reader who reads "to the end" (Ch13 P0090), and the closing section of chapter 15 opens with 「俺は、何も残さずに死ぬ」, "I will die leaving nothing" (Ch15 P0111). That tension is unresolved.

### 2.3 Habits of the narrating voice

Several habits bear directly on how far his self-account can be trusted.

- **Correction from choice to incapacity.** 「参加しなかった。正確にいうと、できなかった」 ("I didn't take part. Strictly, I couldn't," Ch01 P0023). 「眉一つ動かさなかった。動かせなかった、といってもいい」 (Ch01 P0053). Face-saving comes first, then the correction.
- **Explanation by denied motives.** He lists four reasons he did *not* lose his temper at Miyagi, then admits he does not know why (Ch06 P0090–P0091). The denied motives stay in view.
- **Stacked hedges at the worst moments.** The station impulse is explained with 多分…と思う and a string of かもしれない alternatives, then 何でもいい, "whatever the reason" (Ch08 P0007–P0008).
- **Knowing himself from outside.** His collapse and his own lit-up face are known only by らしかった, "apparently" (Ch02 P0004, P0149). He explains his own laughter with だろう (Ch02 P0163).
- **Withholding and delayed disclosure.** He releases facts at the point of consequence: his drawing (Ch06 P0073), his attraction to Miyagi since their first meeting (Ch06 P0007–P0010), the letter-like 〔傍点〕あれ〔/傍点〕 at seventeen (Ch07 P0015, explained in Ch09 P0054–P0066), the bedtime habit (Ch10 P0236), and the full painting history (Ch14 P0179–P0191).
- **Narratee management.** 「さて、手紙の話だ」 (Ch09 P0024). 「さて、答えあわせといこう」 (Ch10 P0112; emphasized at Ch11 P0141). The second borrows Miyagi's institutional phrase for checking answers (`TDH-F029`).

### 2.5 His readings of others

Every other person's interior reaches us through Kusunoki, and he marks many of his readings as readings: 心なしか, ように見えた, らしかった, のかもしれない. Several readings are later contradicted or left unconfirmed:

- **Miyagi as "machine-like."** Overtaken by conduct, as in §2.4.
- **Miyagi's kindness as role-bound.** He judges it "only work" (Ch10 P0256). Miyagi's later testimony says she valued him after the station night (Ch12 P0183), but that testimony comes from an admitted liar (§8.1).
- **Wakana's voice.** He hears reproach and mockery in her callback and flags that this may be because of what Miyagi had just told him (Ch04 P0050, P0053).
- **Himeno's warmth at dinner.** It is undecidable given the plan in her letter (Ch10 P0190–P0200).
- **The old woman.** He reads her silence as "she has forgotten me" (Ch11 P0125); whether she remembers him is undecidable.
- **Enishi and his companion.** He judges them happy from a few seconds' sight (Ch13 P0148).

He also notices more than he pursues. Of Miyagi's faintly sad look he writes: 「俺は見逃してはいなかった。しかし…深く考えてみようとはしなかった」 ("I hadn't missed it. But I didn't try to think deeply about it," Ch03 P0128). The same holds for the shampoo scent (Ch05 P0080) and her knee scar (Ch08 P0040).

The rule for using this monograph: treat any statement about what Miyagi, Himeno or anyone else felt as Kusunoki's reading or as their testimony, never as narrated fact.

## 3. Personal history and its bearing on the present

Most of Kusunoki's past is disclosed late, by the writer, at the moment it becomes useful. For each formative experience below, three questions matter: when it happened, when the reader learns it, and what the experiencing self knew or believed at the time.

**Childhood with Himeno.** He and Himeno lived almost opposite each other near a hilltop. Their parents were friends, and before elementary school each child was left at the other's house (Ch01 P0042). They were equal top scorers and rivals. By his account each was the only person the other could talk to without being misunderstood; for her side he says "probably" (Ch01 P0039–P0041). Both were disliked and bullied: her bag was binned dozens of times, his shoes were slit with scissors (Ch01 P0036, P0057). For their parents they performed close friendship while kicking each other's shins under the table (Ch01 P0043–P0044, P0049).

He describes their deepest problem as an inability to laugh along with everyone: forcing it felt like something essential being 「がりがりと削られる」, "scraped away" (Ch01 P0053). He then elevates it with sesame dots: they were 〔傍点〕ずれて〔/傍点〕いた, "out of step," like flowers that mistook their season (Ch01 P0054; `TDH-F003`).

*Bearing.* This is the root of three lifelong patterns, each later visible in the adult:

- the refusal of conformist performance, coexisting with performances staged for an audience;
- a sense of mistiming, which he later calls being 救いがたく間の悪い, "hopelessly ill-timed" (Ch04 P0029);
- the habit of taking Himeno's words as authoritative frames (Ch01 P0075).

**The moral lesson at ten.** The teacher asks what a life is worth. Himeno answers with average lifetime earnings. The teacher then poses a parable of an unknowable "something" that buys thirty of your sixty remaining years and asks the price per year (Ch01 P0008–P0021). The boy thinks he would sell that much, since a short intense life must be better (Ch01 P0020, voice OPEN). He privately reckons himself at about three billion yen if others are worth three hundred million (Ch01 P0028). He also thinks that a gloomy classmate's life might come with a disposal fee (Ch01 P0030).

The teacher says there is no correct answer. The narrator adds that something like one existed, since ten years later he sold his lifespan (Ch01 P0032).

*Bearing.* The lesson is the frame through which he meets the shop (Ch02 P0060). It is also the key by which he later decodes the price lie: the teacher's 〔傍点〕ここでは〔/傍点〕、〔傍点〕一度そういう考え方を捨ててほしいんです〔/傍点〕, "here, I want you to set that way of thinking aside," returns emphasized in Ch12 P0154 (`TDH-F031`). At twenty he has forgotten his disposal-fee thought, and the narrator recalls it against himself (Ch02 P0138).

**The shrine, the prophecy and the proposal (summer at ten).** On the shrine steps above a festival, Himeno prophesies that "something very good" will happen in a summer ten years on. Then they will at last be glad to have lived, great and rich enough to make their classmates sick with envy (Ch01 P0070–P0074). Haltingly, she proposes that if neither has found a partner by twenty the two "leftovers" marry. The boy answers in her mirrored polite register, 「なんですか、それは」 ("What's that supposed to be?"). She retracts it as a joke, and he laughs: そいつはよかった, "good for you," given unbracketed as reported speech (Ch01 P0087–P0093).

The narrator calls it 「その約束」, "that promise," and says that through middle school, high school and university he firmly refused girls' interest so as to be able to show her he had stayed 〝売れ残っている〟, "unsold" (Ch01 P0094–P0095). He calls this 「まったくもって、馬鹿げた話」 (Ch01 P0096).

*Bearing.* The construal as a promise is his alone. Nobody accepted anything in the scene (`TDH-C006`). His stated motive for the refusals is partly display: to *show* her (`TDH-C003`). The "promise" organizes his self-account for ten years, and he invokes it at each crisis: the Wakana refusal (Ch04 P0028), the capsule trip (Ch07), the visit (Ch09 P0039), and his reason for giving up painting (Ch14 P0182).

**The capsule letter at ten.** He wrote to himself at twenty: if still "unsold," go and see Himeno, because each seems unable to manage without the other (Ch09 P0031–P0035). The adult reads it once before chapter 9 and again there (`TDH-C033` v2).

*Bearing.* It supplies the explicit instruction he honours in Ch09.

**Himeno's transfer (fourth-grade summer).** They have not met since (Ch02 P0016; `TDH-C008`).

*Bearing.* She becomes an imagined audience. At twenty he is partly glad she has not seen him become 「平凡で退屈」, "ordinary and dull" (Ch02 P0018). He also imagines that her presence would have kept him sharp: she would have laughed at his failures and been vexed, to his benefit, at his successes (Ch02 P0019).

**The runaway (age eight or nine).** By his account to the old woman (Ch11 P0131–P0133), after a night quarrel with his mother he ran to the candy shop. The old woman there sheltered him and told his parents on the phone that he was there "but I'll say he isn't for another hour" (Ch11 P0131–P0133). He tells her that, as a child, he believed adults were kind only because their position required it, and that this night is why he can still expect anything of others (Ch11 P0131–P0133).

*Bearing.* This is disclosed only in Ch11. It names, in his own words, a childhood frame that recurs in the adult: kindness as role-bound. That is how he reads Miyagi's gentleness in Ch09 P0017–P0018 and Ch10 P0256. The old woman's runaway kindness is the counter-memory (U11 §4.11, INFERENCE).

**Decline from middle school.** From the second year of middle school his grades and once-perfect memory declined. He went to the top local high school, fell behind, and ended at an ordinary university (Ch10 P0119).

*Bearing.* This is the history behind his 「過去の栄光」, "past glory," and his clinging to specialness at twenty (Ch02 P0120).

**Painting.** He had, he says, an exceptional talent: 「当時の俺を知る者なら誰もが認める」 (Ch14 P0181). Himeno's testimony supports it; she took his pictures home and envied them (Ch10 P0121–P0125).

In the winter at seventeen he stopped. He would not become the great person he had promised Himeno to become by painting as before, so he forbade himself to paint until 「すべてが噛みあったとき」, "when everything meshed" (Ch14 P0182–P0184). In the summer at nineteen, from impatience, he let himself paint and lost the ability. He could not draw even an apple (Ch14 P0186–P0188). He told Naruse he stopped on entering university (Ch06 P0074), and Himeno that he had not held a brush since seventeen (Ch10 P0119).

*Bearing.* This is the 「かつての夢」, "former dream" (Ch06 P0090), whose mockery ends the Naruse friendship. Its recovery in Ch14 is the novel's clearest case of regained capacity. The conflict between the accounts is unresolved (§11).

**The letter exchange at seventeen.** Himeno wrote first, a trivial letter in a round hand, 「彼女らしくない」, "unlike her" (Ch09 P0055–P0060). He replied in the same vein, adding that he would be glad if she visited. He believes he wrote honestly, 「相当な無理をして」, "at great effort," that he wanted to see her (Ch09 P0061–P0064). No answer came.

*Bearing.* Himeno's later letter names an unanswered call for help (Ch10 P0194). Kusunoki concludes, emphatically, that her letter at seventeen was 〔傍点〕ヒメノなりのＳＯＳでした〔/傍点〕, "Himeno's SOS" (Ch11 P0148). That conclusion is his inference; the narration endorses only that he should have noticed sooner (`TDH-C042`).

**Family.** His account of his brother and his parents' preference is given once, on the first night of the three months (Ch04 P0068–P0072; `TDH-C019`). He has a younger brother of nineteen who surpasses him in character, height, looks, romance, university and sport (he pitched at Kōshien). His parents' love shifted to the brother. Kusunoki, treated as a 失敗作, "failure," says he did not find this unfair. In the same breath he uses the grievance verb 奪われ続けてきた, "kept being robbed of," for his parents' love (Ch04 P0070).

*Bearing.* He refuses all family contact for his remaining time (Ch04 P0069). On his home-town visit he detours around the family home (Ch07 P0036, P0055), yet visits his grandfather's grave (Ch07 P0068). The grave is mentioned in one subordinate clause; no reason is given. His family never learns of his sale or his death within the text.

**Wakana (spring at twenty).** A first-year who mistook his glare in the bookshop for recognition, lunched with him, talked books and music, and entered her number in his phone (Ch04 P0014–P0027). He kept her messages and calls brief 「あまり期待させてはいけない」, "so as not to raise her hopes," because he was still keeping the promise (Ch04 P0028).

*Bearing.* This is the first datable case of conduct he attributes to fidelity (`TDH-C003` v3). It qualifies chapter 2's picture of total isolation as partly self-maintained.

## 4. Personality and psychology

### 4.1 Worth: the central axis

Kusunoki thinks about people, himself included, in prices. This is the most stable feature of his mind, and the novel tests it more than any other. His self-valuation can be traced from the moral lesson to the last page.

| Point | Valuation | Whose, and how held | Locator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age ten | About ¥3 billion, ten times a classmate | The boy's private reckoning | Ch01 P0028 |
| Twenty, before the appraisal | About ¥600 million for sixty years; "less confident than in elementary school"; still dreaming of 「永遠」 and a legendary success | His; punctured by the narrator | Ch02 P0119–P0122 |
| Waiting for the result | Lowered to ¥300 million, Himeno's lifetime-earnings figure, "to avoid disappointment" | His; the narrator adds 「まだまだ自分の価値の低さを甘く見ている」 | Ch02 P0134–P0138 |
| The appraisal | ¥10,000 a year, "the minimum"; thirty years sold for about ¥300,000 | The shop's claim, accepted on bodily sense; he calls it fair and is crushed by its fairness | Ch02 P0158–P0172 |
| Drunk vow | "I'll make it worth more than thirty million or three billion" | His, abandoned within the scene | Ch05 P0014, P0026 |
| After the station night | 「なるほど、三十万円の価値も納得だ」, "fair after all" | His | Ch08 P0035 |
| Rehearsal | 「一年につき一万円程度の価値しかない人間」, said as a joke about his new look | His, ironic | Ch10 P0044 |
| Price lie revealed | Thirty yen in all, 「電話三分程度の価値」 ("worth a three-minute phone call"); the ¥300,000 was hers | Her testimony after his deduction; he laughs | Ch12 P0164–P0167 |
| Her valuation of him | "To me you're worth thirty million or three billion" | Her testimony; he asks her not to console him | Ch12 P0174–P0177 |
| The goal | 「一生が缶ジュース一本の値段に満たないこの俺が」, "I, whose whole life is worth less than a can of juice," wants to pay her debt | His; 「身のほど知らず」 | Ch12 P0214–P0215 |

| Second sale | Thirty of his thirty-three remaining days sell 「目を疑うくらい高く」, "unbelievably high," for a painting future of fame; the substitute: 「三十年より価値のある三十日」 | The shop's appraisal (hearsay for the future) | Ch14 P0163–P0195 |
| Refusal | 「俺がいない世界で俺が永遠になっても、何にも嬉しくありませんよ」 | His | Ch14 P0168 |
| End | No need for eternity or memory; the three days will "probably" be worth more than the thirty years and the thirty days | His, hedged | Ch15 P0114–P0118, P0124–P0127 |

Three things in this chain matter for the portrait.

**Price, value and worth are different things to him.** The shop's figures are the shop's claims, never independently verified (`TDH-C012`). His own valuations follow them closely: he calls the first verdict 「公正」 and its fairness the worst part (Ch02 P0171). He finds his parents' preference for his brother not unfair (Ch04 P0071). After the station night he endorses the ¥300,000 as deserved (Ch08 P0035). He accepts low valuations of himself readily (U04 §9), with two clear exceptions. He argues back once, about the "freedom to be hurt," when Himeno-hope is at stake (Ch03 P0120). And in Ch12 he questions the premise itself: 「なぜ寿命一年につき一万円という値段が、最低買取価格だなんて信じていたんだろう？」, "why did I believe ten thousand yen a year was the minimum?" (Ch12 P0155). The re-reading is deflationary, not consoling. In the same speech he calls the idea that life is precious above all a 「たわごと」, "nonsense," which he may still have half believed (Ch12 P0155). After that he questions the market figure's authority, though he still measures himself by it at times (「一生が缶ジュース一本の値段に満たないこの俺が」, Ch12 P0214; plans to raise his market value, Ch13 P0030–P0031; 「三十円の俺」, Ch14 P0134). Only at the end does value detach from price.

**The market idiom shapes even his love.** He frames family love as investment (愛し甲斐 / 投資し甲斐, Ch04 P0071) and his long fidelity in the word 売れ残り, "unsold" (Ch01 P0095). He calls Himeno 中古品, "second-hand," and himself 非売品, "not for sale" (Ch09 P0130–P0131; `TDH-F025`, a VALUE_JUDGMENT needing a named standard). By the end the idiom has not vanished; 「価値」 survives in his last sentence. But it has detached from price: the most valuable thing is three days with Miyagi (Ch15 P0124–P0127).

**The appraisal did not reform him overnight.** The narrator says he perhaps needed to be denied completely, and that going to sell his lifespan was therefore probably 「正解」, "the right answer" (Ch02 P0123–P0124). That is a hedged retrospective judgment, not a description of the man who walked out of the shop. He walked out furious at a smiling stranger and vomited his beer (Ch02 P0191–P0194; `TDH-C009`).

### 4.2 Specialness, contempt and their revision

As a boy he looked down on other children and was ostracized for it. He concedes he was 「驕りを隠しきれるほど器用でも謙虚でもなかった」, "neither deft nor modest enough to hide my arrogance" (Ch01 P0036). The adult narrator revises the self-estimate but, in the same breath, still calls the neighbourhood children hopelessly dull (Ch01 P0035; `TDH-C004`).

At twenty his contempt extends in three directions:

- **To himself.** Disgust at his hunger for pigeons' bread (Ch02 P0133).
- **To strangers.** A middle-aged pigeon-feeder judged by her clothes (Ch02 P0133). A beaming young man who angers him 「無性に」, "unaccountably" (Ch02 P0191). Restaurant customers reduced to noise (Ch06 P0091).
- **To intermediaries.** The old bookseller diagnosed as addled by fear of ageing (Ch02 P0054, P0066). The CD clerk's tip heard as a con artist's script (Ch02 P0082).

The habit of uncharitable typing persists into the second day of the three months. He imagines 「醜く太った不潔な中年」, "an ugly, fat, unclean middle-aged" watcher, as the foil to Miyagi (Ch06 P0010; `TDH-C004` v3a).

The counterexamples matter as much:

- **Charity toward adults.** He presents his teachers charitably (Ch01 P0051).
- **Gratitude toward a benefactor.** He credits the old woman's kindness (Ch11 P0133).
- **Apology.** He apologizes sincerely for the glass (Ch06 P0106).

By Ch14 he says he envied the clever clown he hated as a boy, wanted from the first to get on with everyone, and, thanks to Miyagi, has 「世界と仲直りすることに、俺は成功した」, "succeeded in making peace with the world" (Ch14 P0119). His conduct in that phase supports the self-report in part:

- He waits out harassers without retaliating (Ch14 P0008).
- He respects the disbelief of the acquaintance who calls him crazy (Ch13 P0073).
- He takes pride in being half mocked and half liked (Ch14 P0117).

The final 「すべてを許せた」, "I could forgive everything" (Ch15 P0118), is self-report; what "everything" includes is unstated.

### 4.3 Authenticity and performance

He values not doing what he does not feel. The boy could not laugh along without something essential being scraped away (Ch01 P0053). Yet performance runs through his life:

- **Childhood.** The staged friendship for parents (Ch01 P0049).
- **The drunk vow.** The stage metaphor of 舞台袖 and 「俺の演技が始まる」, "my performance begins" (Ch05 P0006).
- **The first honest want.** His first clear want after resolving on honesty is to be misread by Naruse (Ch06 P0046–P0047).
- **A lie to Wakana.** The cheerful lie that he misdialled (Ch04 P0051).
- **The town.** In the late chapters, the public performance of a relationship that others cannot see (Ch13 P0101; Ch14 P0005).

For most of the novel he is acutely conscious of observers. He will not look out at the fireworks in front of Miyagi and asks himself why, so near death, he still minds others' eyes (Ch05 P0039–P0040). In the late chapters he turns that self-consciousness inside out. He welcomes suspicious looks in the library lounge, perhaps preferring to be remembered as suspicious than not at all (Ch09 P0084). He enjoys being taken for a lunatic because it makes Miyagi laugh (Ch13 P0064). He coins 傍若無人かつ傍若有人, "as if no one were by, and as if someone were by," for his own conduct (Ch14 P0005). And he states the maxim 「相手にされない真実より、楽しまれる虚構の方が、ずっといい」, "an enjoyed fiction is far better than an ignored truth" (Ch14 P0104). His VALUE_JUDGMENT there voices the onlookers' frame; he never retracts her presence.

### 4.4 Loneliness, non-initiation and the fear of misplaced hope

"Since childhood," he says, he has never invited anyone or sought conversation, which cost him possibilities and spared him bother (Ch04 P0008). Once his end is fixed, the first change he names is 「人恋しさ」, a longing for company unthinkable for his former self (Ch04 P0013). He concedes 「人並みに、人恋しかった」, "like anyone, I longed for people" (Ch04 P0067).

The longing is real, and at first indiscriminate:

- he wanted Wakana "by my side until my life ran out" (Ch04 P0012);
- he invites Miyagi to drink with "anyone would do" (Ch04 P0074);
- to her he says only 「寂しいから」, "because I'm lonely" (Ch06 P0114).

Miyagi's charge that he regards others as tools to fill his loneliness (Ch04 P0046) is partly supported by these lines and partly contested. He named the one person who had shown him goodwill, had real rapport with Wakana, and avoided raising her hopes (U04 §4.3).

Against the longing stands a fear of expecting too much. He tells himself not to make Miyagi his support, since it seems unfair (Ch09 P0015). He suspects her softening is instructed and warns himself that forgetting her role "would invite a harsh betrayal" (Ch09 P0017–P0018). After Himeno's letter, the fear becomes a resolve: 「これ以上、見当違いな期待をしてはならない」, "I must not expect anything misplaced any more." He will die quietly, 「猫のように人知れず」, "unnoticed, like a cat" (Ch10 P0257–P0258). The resolve is under strain within days (Ch11 P0015–P0019) and is reversed by Miyagi's confession (Ch12 P0205, P0210–P0215).

### 4.5 Hope as a "devil's proof"

On the first morning he feels relief rather than regret. A shallow hope that "something good might happen someday" had been both salvation and trap, so being told, as he paraphrases it, that nothing good would happen means 「これで、安心して死ぬことができる」, "now I can die at ease" (Ch03 P0033–P0035). Within hours he cannot help hoping the assessment had a misplaced digit (Ch03 P0048).

That night he declines to sell down to three days, though he judges it the smartest course. What stops him is the 「悪魔の証明的希望」, "devil's-proof hope," a label he takes from Miyagi: the possibility of something good is 「ゼロではない」, "not zero" (Ch05 P0070–P0073; `TDH-C017` v2; `TDH-F016`). He takes her label and refuses her conclusion.

The hope is passive: something good may *happen to* him (Ch05 P0071). It is discharged in Ch14, when he judges Himeno's prophecy half true: 〝とってもいいこと〟は、確かに起きた, "'something very good' did happen," and he could think from the heart that he was glad to have lived (Ch14 P0201).

### 4.6 Fidelity to Himeno as a self-account

For ten years, by his account, he refused other attachments to keep a promise that was never made as a promise (`TDH-C003`, `TDH-C006`). The text never confirms the motive independently. Rivals remain live:

- the ten-year-old's professed hatred of 「世の中に起こることが何もかも」, "everything that happens" (Ch01 P0082);
- an incapacity for conformist performance (Ch01 P0053);
- an incapacity, which he himself states, to feel even slight goodwill for anyone later (Ch03 P0026);
- a display motive: to *show* her he stayed unsold (Ch01 P0095).

The one datable case, Wakana, is overdetermined by his lifelong non-initiation (Ch04 P0008).

What can be said is that Himeno functions as his measure and audience. The prophecy is the standard by which he judges his life (Ch02 P0015; Ch14 P0197–P0201). The capsule letter is an instruction he chooses to honour, 「尊重してやりたい」, "I want to respect it," a favour granted downward to his past self (Ch09 P0039). His feeling for her is first named only in Ch03, as a 「ほんの僅かな好意」, "the slightest goodwill," that may have been the greatest of his life (Ch03 P0025–P0027; `TDH-C016`).

### 4.7 Coping repertoire

His ways of enduring are consistent and largely solitary:

- **Tepid self-pity.** He soaks in it as a "quick knack for recovering" (Ch05 P0004).
- **Music on sleepless nights.** The kept CD 「プリーズ・ミスター・ロストマン」, with his own verdict that such music let him forgive too readily a self that could not and would not fit the world (Ch05 P0081–P0082).
- **The bedtime habit.** Since about five he has pictured 「俺が本来住みたかった世界」, "the world I really wanted to live in," and memories that never were. He thinks it may be why he never fitted the world, and also his only way of coming to terms with it (Ch10 P0236–P0242).
- **Physical pain to displace mental pain.** Three hundred balls at a batting centre until his hands bleed (Ch06 P0098). A night on a station bench chosen 「自分をほどよく痛めつける」, "to hurt myself moderately" (Ch07 P0088).
- **Meaningless, beautiful work.** Five days of folding a thousand cranes, 「無意味で美しい行為ほど、純粋な祈りがあるだろうか？」 ("Is there any prayer purer than an act meaningless and beautiful?", Ch09 P0014).
- **Walking and fasting to think.** He relies on hunger to sharpen intuition (Ch13 P0012–P0013).
- **Writing as re-ordering.** "Like defragging" (Ch12 P0059).
- **Retreat into solitude after hurt.** 「こんな思いをしないために、これまで一人で生きてきたのに」, "I lived alone till now precisely to avoid feeling like this" (Ch10 P0203).
- **Jokes at his own expense.** §7.5.

### 4.8 Self-conception in his own words

He describes himself as:

- 〔傍点〕ずれて〔/傍点〕いた, "out of step" (Ch01 P0054);
- hopelessly ill-timed (Ch04 P0029);
- a failure beside his brother (Ch04 P0071);
- a 「クズ」, "scum" (Ch08 P0033);
- a fool, 馬鹿だなあ、俺は (Ch10 P0202, in narration);
- one who commits mistakes 「堅実に」, "steadily" (Ch10 P0114);
- a man more foolish than anyone he has met, by choice (Ch13 P0090);
- 「この町で、一番の道化」, "the town's greatest clown" (Ch14 P0065);
- at the end, the idiot who could not live even three days without her (Ch15 P0103).

The terms move from defect toward chosen foolishness. The early self-contempt describes what he is. The late self-mockery describes what he does, and it is said with satisfaction.

## 5. Cognition and decision-making

### 5.1 What he notices

He is a close observer of surfaces, especially visual ones, and his narration makes much of colour and light:

- a rainbow in which he counts only five colours, finding orange and indigo missing on an imaginary palette (Ch06 P0029);
- bluish tints under batting-centre lights (Ch06 P0099);
- outlines that look sharper yet hollow under a cumulonimbus (Ch09 P0078);
- four distinct insect voices where he had heard one (Ch11 P0176).

That this is a painter's attention is an INFERENCE (moderate), made after the drawing is disclosed (U06 §6).

He notices other people's states and frequently declines to pursue them. Examples are Miyagi's sad look (Ch03 P0128), her shampoo (Ch05 P0080), her scar (Ch08 P0040), her "more suited way" (Ch06 P0015–P0016), and whom she once loved (Ch09 P0095). His stated reasons, where given, are restraint or deference: "my sudden concern would only annoy her" (Ch08 P0040).

He notices his own body's verdicts and trusts them. He states the creed outright: before the irrational, trust bodily sense over common sense (Ch02 P0158). He feels the sale as something drawn out of his core (Ch02 P0187), and is certain of his coming death by feel even when he doubts everything else (Ch15 P0022).

### 5.2 How he reasons

- **In ledgers and prices.** He reasons through worth, return, fairness and repayment (§4.1). Even his drunk vow of effort is a price: 「三千万や三十億よりも価値のある三十万にしてやる」, "I'll make it a three hundred thousand worth more than thirty million or three billion" (Ch05 P0014). Himeno's childhood frame was the same: suffering must be repaid, 「そうじゃないと、わりにあわないもんね」 (Ch01 P0074).
- **By elimination and pattern-completion.** He concludes that the 相合傘 in the bus was drawn by Himeno because only the two of them knew the place and he does not remember drawing it (Ch07 P0061). His premises are weakened by the same day's memory lapses (Ch07 P0016, P0071; `TDH-C031`). He infers by elimination that the shampoo scent means Miyagi bathed (Ch05 P0078).
- **Escalating certainty from weak premises.** The 相合傘 becomes the certain counterfactual that Himeno's missing capsule letter would have named him, "with a heart or two" (Ch07 P0078; `TDH-C032`). Before the old woman, the missing letter becomes proof that Himeno dug up the capsule. Her letter at seventeen becomes an SOS. He adds the flat 「ヒメノはもうすぐ自殺します」, "Himeno will soon kill herself," a belief the frontier does not support (Ch11 P0142–P0148; `TDH-C054`). He marks the moment of insight in the historical present, 「俺の中ですべてが繋がる」, "everything connects in me" (Ch11 P0144).
- **By counterfactual.** If he had known Himeno's state, he would have courted Wakana (Ch04 P0028). If he had been told "thirty yen," he would have sold everything, not even keeping three days (Ch12 P0173). If Himeno had been at the capsule burial, she would have named him (Ch07 P0078). He reasons forward as well as back. A thirty-yen man had an unforeseeable happy remainder, so Miyagi's future too is open (Ch14 P0134).
- **Through his own childhood frames.** Himeno's prophecy is the yardstick of his life (Ch02 P0015; Ch14 P0197–P0201). The teacher's lesson is the key to the price lie (Ch12 P0153–P0155). His boyhood belief that adults are kind only because their position requires it reappears in his readings of Miyagi (Ch09 P0017; Ch10 P0256; INFERENCE, U11 §4.11).
- **Aphorism.** He generalizes in compact sentences: 「死ぬというのは、死に続ける以外のことすべて、二度とできなくなるということなのだ」 (Ch06 P0037); 「世界が優しいのは、おそらく、既に死んでしまった人に対してのみだ」 (Ch06 P0123); the enjoyed-fiction maxim (Ch14 P0104). These are VALUE_JUDGMENTs, his, often revised by later events.

### 5.3 Uncertainty and testimony

Faced with claims he cannot check, he mostly accepts them.

- **The shop.** He accepts the shop's valuation on bodily sense: before the irrational, 「信じるべきは常識ではなく肌の感覚」, "trust the feel of your skin, not common sense," is one of his creeds (Ch02 P0158). Asked at contract whether he has questions, he says no; he wants only to leave (Ch02 P0182–P0184).
- **Miyagi's Himeno report.** He calls it 「その事実」, "that fact" (Ch03 P0120). In retelling he hardens her 「ほとんど覚えてません」 ("barely remembers") into 「忘れてしまっている」 ("has forgotten") (Ch04 P0028).
- **Wakana.** He does not challenge Miyagi's account of Wakana (Ch04 P0044–P0046).
- **The forfeited life.** 「奇妙なものだ」, "strange": he finds he can accept her account 「すんなりと」, "without resistance" (Ch05 P0060–P0061). Much later he adopts its motorcycle accident as his fate (Ch14 P0174, 「俺はそう考えることにしている」, "that is how I choose to think," P0176).

The exceptions are telling. He argues back when the testimony touches his hope of Himeno, defending a "freedom to be hurt and disappointed" (Ch03 P0119–P0120). His stated motive was simply to answer back: 「何かいい返してやりたくなった」 (Ch03 P0119). And in Ch12 he deduces the price lie from a hint, refusing the first explanation (embezzlement) because he did not want to believe it. Her admission bore him out (Ch12 P0150–P0157; U12 §9).

He disowns his own intuition once, explicitly: 「後になって振り返ってみると、俺の勘がいかに当てにならないか、よくわかる」, "looking back later, I can see how unreliable my intuition was" (Ch02 P0147). The judgment it disowns was that where the clerk is involved 「嘘はない」, "there are no lies" (Ch02 P0146). His later discoveries about her concealments (Ch10, Ch12) pay it off.

### 5.4 The major decisions

A recurring structural fact: the decisive moment is often elided, and reasons arrive afterwards, from the writer.

**The first sale (Ch02 P0179–P0184).** A section break skips the decision itself.

- *Stated reasons.* Habituation to "selling cheaply what belongs to me" through wage labour and the day's sales (Ch02 P0181). A wish to leave 「この店からも。この人生からも」, "this shop, and this life" (Ch02 P0183–P0184).
- *Alternatives and rivals.* Money is not cited. Why he kept three months is never stated; at ¥10,000 a year three months are worth about ¥2,500, so the reserve is not monetary. Rivals include despair at the verdict, impatience to leave and face-saving (`TDH-C009`).

**Declining to sell down to three days (Ch05 P0065–P0073).** He raises it himself and asks whether Miyagi would then leave him. He declines on the "devil's-proof hope" that something good might still happen (`TDH-C023`).

**The list (Ch03 P0082–P0093).** Eight items, from refusals (no university, no work, no resisting desire) to pleasures to two people: Naruse, and last, confessing to Himeno. He accepts Miyagi's critique that it reads like "things someone other than you would do" and admits he is imitating someone because he cannot do nothing (Ch06 P0011–P0014).

**Reading every capsule letter (Ch07 P0073).** He means to take only his own. Having worked so hard, he reads them all, against the teacher's promise of confidentiality, on the licence that 「あと数か月で死ぬ人間には、それくらいのことは許されるはずだ」, "a man who will die in a few months should be allowed that much."

**The station impulse and its stop (Ch08 P0007–P0035).** The impulse is explained under hedge: confusion, disappointments, fatigue, "or that I liked her looks more than I realized" (Ch08 P0007). It is licensed by list item 3, "don't resist desire," and by the arithmetic that it would bring his death only a few months forward (Ch08 P0009). As narrated, he stops when he imagines the harm and sees what looks to him like her sadness. No rule, resistance or third party is narrated as stopping him (U08 §9); unnarrated fear of death and exhaustion or pain remain rivals (U08 §4.4).

**Going to see Himeno (Ch09 P0039).** He resolves to honour the boy's request, talk to her once more and give her the remaining ¥300,000 「俺に人生を与えてくれた恩返しに」, "in return for her giving me a life." The list's confession is absent from the plan.

**Confessing the sale at dinner (Ch10 P0137–P0146).** He narrates this as a loss of composure he could see and could not stop: 「しかし、止められなかった」 (Ch10 P0140). He speaks 「同情を引くような調子で」, "in a tone fishing for sympathy" (Ch10 P0145). The writer calls it a grave mistake that would nonetheless 「俺の身を救う」, "save me" (Ch10 P0113–P0115).

**Giving the money away (Ch10 P0207–P0215).** After Himeno's letter he sends the letter into the river as a paper aeroplane, then hands the envelope meant for her, and the cash in his wallet, to passers-by, one note at a time. Shaking off Miyagi's sleeve, he says 「別に人に迷惑はかけてないだろ」, "I'm not bothering anyone" (Ch10 P0211).

**The resolve to expect nothing (Ch10 P0253–P0259).** Made in the night, after he feels, or dreams, Miyagi stroking his head.

**The debt goal (Ch12 P0210–P0215; Ch13 P0006–P0031).** Formed silently the night of her confession; Miyagi does not know of it then. He weighs the means:

- honest work, rejected because it could at most repay the ¥300,000 and she would not want his last months spent so;
- crime, rejected because she would not want it;
- gambling, rejected;
- the shop's two remaining uses, and raising his own value, considered.

When Miyagi guesses his aim and asks him instead to pay attention to her and give her memories, he concedes she is right, 「しかし…こちらにも意地があった」, "but I had my pride too" (Ch13 P0028–P0029). He hides his consultations with the old bookseller and the CD clerk from her (Ch13 P0039, P0051). He then resolves on the "foolish" life (Ch13 P0090). That the goal was set aside there is a strong inference that U14 weakened (`TDH-C061` v2–v3).

**The second sale (Ch14 P0155–P0168).** No decision scene is narrated; the plan exists by Ch14 P0111. He sells on Miyagi's day off, against her plea of the night before (Ch14 P0142) and against the appraiser's advice. He refuses the posthumous fame offered as the alternative. Whether the debt goal was revived when a means appeared by chance or never given up is undecided (`TDH-C061` v3). He states its purpose only afterwards: to protect her, at the cost of everything (Ch15 P0098; `TDH-C061` v4).

### 5.5 Conflicting obligations

When his wishes conflict with someone else's expressed wishes about his own life, he tends to act on his own judgment, alone, and explain later. This holds for Miyagi's warning about Himeno (Ch03 P0095–P0097; he goes anyway, Ch09), for her request to forget the debt (Ch13 P0085), and for her plea to stay a month (Ch14 P0142). He frames it as pride or as protection, not as defiance.

Toward the old bookseller the conflict runs the other way. He keeps silent about the shop so that the old man will not feel responsible (Ch13 P0037), and laughs off his 〔傍点〕また〔/傍点〕, "*again*," with 「何のことでしょう？」, "What do you mean?". The narrator calls that laugh 白々しく, "transparent" (Ch13 P0047–P0049).

The rule that emerges, as INFERENCE: he protects others by concealment and acts for them without consent, and he is prepared to be the one who is hurt.

## 6. Behavioural patterns

**Ordinary conduct.**

- *Frugal and rationed.* He agonizes over a can of cider before allowing himself it, and savours it to the last drop (Ch02 P0091–P0092). He pockets a found pack of cigarettes after looking round for an owner (Ch02 P0131).
- *Habit-bound.* He eats at the only cheap place he knows because poverty seems to have stripped his imagination (Ch03 P0079), and cannot imagine what luxury would be (Ch04 P0059).
- *Crowd-avoidant.* Since childhood he has gone to parks, festivals and fireworks only when forced (Ch05 P0035).
- *A light sleeper.* Newspaper deliveries and footsteps upstairs wake him (Ch05 P0079).
- *A walker.* He walks for days to think (Ch13 P0012) and walks four hours home after giving away his fare (Ch11 P0004).

**Cooperation.** He follows advice while resenting it.

- He goes round places of memory exactly as Miyagi suggested and is annoyed to prove himself ordinary (Ch07 P0054).
- He buys clothes and has his hair cut on her advice (Ch10 P0035–P0041).
- He picks up his cigarette ends at her reproachful look (Ch09 P0117).
- He accepts Shinbashi's help and car (Ch14 P0016–P0021, P0069–P0073).
- He works casual labour when money runs low (Ch13 P0101).

**Conflict.** His conflicts escalate in a characteristic order: retort, silent reproach, displaced explosion, then flight or self-punishment.

- *Retort.* The "freedom to be hurt" (Ch03 P0120).
- *Silent reproach.* At Naruse: 〔傍点〕それだけは〔/傍点〕、笑っちゃいけなかったんだ, "*that one thing* you mustn't laugh at," addressed only inwardly (Ch06 P0080–P0084).
- *Displaced explosion.* The glass thrown at the wall on Miyagi's side. It does not hit her, and he weighs no risk (Ch06 P0094; `TDH-C027`). He flees, leaving money on the table (Ch06 P0097).
- *A careless wound relished.* He remarks that she bothers with her looks though no one can see her. It seems to him to hurt her, and he is glad, wanting to "praise myself" for the remark (Ch07 P0053).
- *Restraint.* After Miyagi's verdict that he neglected everyone and should not cling to the past now, he is angry, says nothing, and allows that she may be right (Ch07 P0084–P0085). There is no outburst this time.
- *The aborted assault.* §5.4; §10.
- *Late non-retaliation.* With three harassers in an izakaya he waits patiently for them to tire, though "before, I might have answered back out of stubbornness" (Ch14 P0008).

**Stress responses.**

- *Bodily.* He vomits after the sale and on Miyagi's arrival (Ch02 P0194; Ch03 P0052).
- *Flight.* From the restaurant (Ch06 P0097).
- *Self-punishment.* The batting centre and the station bench (Ch06 P0098; Ch07 P0088).
- *Dispersal.* The letter thrown into the river; the money given to strangers (Ch10 P0207–P0215).
- *Withdrawal.* The resolve to die unnoticed (Ch10 P0258).
- *Tears.* Late in the novel, uncontrolled tears before children (Ch15 P0035).

**Mistakes and repair.**

- *The glass.* He apologizes sincerely and explains that he sometimes flares up (Ch06 P0106).
- *The station.* After the aborted assault he calls himself scum and judges his valuation deserved, but does not apologize in so many words (Ch08 P0033–P0035; `TDH-C036`). He first acknowledges the act to a third party a month later, to the substitute: 「あとちょっとで、無理矢理押し倒すところだったな」, "I very nearly forced her down" (Ch12 P0042; `TDH-C060`).
- *The money.* He apologizes for wasting Miyagi's ¥300,000 (Ch12 P0167).
- *Memory errors.* He corrects his memory of the capsule site when it fails (Ch07 P0071).
- *Himeno.* He reproaches himself for her but never tries to contact her again.

**Meaningful exceptions.**

- An out-of-character 「ありがとう」 to Miyagi, marked 柄にもなく, "unlike me" (Ch09 P0050).
- Restraint from leaning on her (Ch09 P0015).
- Silence instead of an outburst (Ch07 P0084).
- Concern for her safety in his prayers, the first thing he prays for (Ch15 P0012).

## 7. Speech and communication

### 7.1 Medium limits

This is a printed novel. Everything is written register: no performed voice, intonation or accent is available.

His speech reaches us only as the narrator, himself, chose to quote it. Some lines are bracketed. Others are given without brackets in historical present, as in the dream (Ch03 P0011–P0022) and the walk with Himeno (Ch10 P0102–P0105). Some, he says, he does not remember at all (Ch06 P0070; Ch10 P0004–P0005).

The only description of his voice is his own. On the date with Himeno it rises about half an octave, which "apparently was its natural range" and sounded surprisingly pleasant to him (Ch10 P0092).

### 7.2 Self-reference

He uses 俺 in all present-day speech and narration. The ten-year-old's letter to his future self uses 僕 and polite です/ます to an あなた, the genre the teacher modelled (Ch09 P0031–P0035; Ch07 P0018). At ten, the shrine dialogue with Himeno runs on 俺たち and 私たち with no second-person pronouns or vocatives (Ch01; U01 §5).

The pattern is consistent. He is polite to elders, to shopkeepers and to people in an official or service role. He is plain to peers, to men he meets as equals or opponents (the substitute, Shinbashi, the acquaintance), and to Miyagi from the moment she becomes his watcher. He stays plain with Shinbashi even though Shinbashi addresses him in formal です/ます (U14 §6). After the shop he never uses keigo to Miyagi, even when grateful, apologetic or in love.

### 7.4 The arc of his address to Miyagi

The changes in how he names her track the relationship, though no single form should be read as a timeless label.

1. **Generic labels.** The 女/女店員 of the shop (Ch02) and the 女の子 of the first days (Ch03), used nine times in Ch03, track his revised sense of her age (U03 §5).
2. **Roles, not names.** The teasing 「監視員さん」 (Ch05–Ch06) addresses her by role; through Ch08 he never uses her name in speech (U08 §6).
3. **The name enters.** It first appears in his speech when he announces he will visit Himeno and expects her to object (Ch09 P0039). It becomes a vocative when he asks a favour (Ch10 P0024).
4. **The name in key requests.** It carries the most consequential lines that follow: 「なあミヤギ、率直に答えてくれ」 (Ch10 P0167); 「ミヤギ。俺が死んだら、俺のことはきれいさっぱり忘れてくれ」 (Ch14 P0128).
5. **The last あんた.** It comes in the price-lie scene, at its most tender: 「あんたが優しいことは十分に知ってる」, "I know very well you're kind" (Ch12 P0177).
6. **Name only.** Afterwards he addresses her only by name. In public he asserts her by name: 「ミヤギは確かにここにいる」, "Miyagi is certainly here" (Ch13 P0073).

That the dropping of あんた marks a change of footing is an INFERENCE (moderate); it is not remarked on in the text.

### 7.5 Rhetorical habits and humour

- **Deadpan wit under pressure.** Selling his books, he tells the bookseller 「紙は食感も悪いし、栄養もありませんから」, "paper has a bad texture and no nutrition" (Ch02 P0034). Privately he hears the CD clerk's tip as a line fit for 詐欺の教科書, a con artist's textbook (Ch02 P0082). The first day closes on 「スタートの切り方としては、最悪に近い」, "as starts go, close to the worst" (Ch02 P0196).
- **Self-deprecating jokes about his worth.** 「そういい続けて、五十年間何一つ得られないまま死んでいく予定だった男のことを、俺は一人知ってるぜ」, "I know a man who kept saying that and was due to die at fifty with nothing" (Ch08 P0085), which makes Miyagi smile at him (Ch08 P0086), their first shared joke; her first narrated smiles all fall in this scene (P0059, P0081, P0086; U08). 「電話三分程度の価値か」, "worth a three-minute phone call" (Ch12 P0167). The proxy-date joke about looking like a man worth ¥10,000 a year (Ch10 P0044).
- **Argument by hypothetical.** The "freedom to be hurt" (Ch03 P0120). Returning her own trust test: 「もし俺が今、あんたを騙して、とんでもない場所に向かっていたらどうする？」 (Ch12 P0118).
- **Courtship under cover of a joke.** On the proxy date he tells Miyagi, ostensibly as practice for Himeno but with あんた, that the best thing in his life was her appearing and the worst her disappearing, and that her answer could swap them. Then: 「冗談だよ」 (Ch10 P0059, P0065). The line is double-addressed (`TDH-C046`).
- **Uptake of Miyagi's words.** Her terms pass into his narration and speech: 悪魔の証明 (Ch05 P0070), すがる (Ch07 P0086), 答えあわせ (Ch10 P0112; Ch11 P0141), 「向いたやり方」 (Ch11 P0077, echoing Ch06 P0015), and her formula for foreclosed futures (Ch14 P0173). He takes her labels while contesting her conclusions (`TDH-F016`, `TDH-F021`, `TDH-F029`).
- **Playful in the late phase.** On the bus to her home town he answers her "Are you stupid?" by stroking her head: "This, then?" (Ch13 P0130–P0133). To the teasing high-schoolers: 「いいだろう？　渡さないぞ」, "Nice, isn't she? You can't have her" (Ch14 P0102).

### 7.6 Silence, inner speech and the unsaid

Much of what matters is never spoken to its object.

- **Inner address.** His reproach to Naruse is entirely silent (Ch06 P0080–P0084).
- **Unspoken resolves.** To be remembered as the watched person easiest to be near (Ch08 P0091). To die unnoticed (Ch10 P0258). The debt goal, unknown to Miyagi (Ch12 P0212).
- **No apology in words.** After the station touch (Ch08).
- **No reply narrated.** To Miyagi's request to forget the debt (Ch13 P0085–P0090), and to her plea to stay; he only strokes her head, and acts against it next morning (Ch14 P0143).
- **No word of his own death to the town.** He invents instead a story that Miyagi left him (Ch15 P0040).
- **No declaration to Miyagi.** He never tells her in words that he loves her. His quoted speech never applies 好き or 愛 to his feeling for her (OBSERVATION, full search). In his speech 好き occurs only for vending machines (Ch11 P0052–P0061; Ch12 P0017), for Himeno's hoped liking (Ch10 P0028), in a question about what Miyagi likes (Ch13 P0060) and for her feeling for him (Ch14 P0058); 愛 does not occur in his speech. He says she likes *him*, to others (Ch14 P0058). Asked to prove how much he loves her, he kisses her (Ch14 P0060–P0063). His feeling is named only in narration: her sleeping face 「愛おしかった」 (Ch14 P0080); her betrayal and foolishness 「何よりも愛おしかった」 (Ch15 P0100).

The direction of disclosure changes late. In Ch13–Ch14 he speaks openly to strangers about Miyagi's presence and her love, while still keeping his plans from her.

### 7.7 Written voice: letters and the record

**The trial farewell letter.** Given only in the narrator's summary, it addresses Himeno as お前 and pairs defiance (「今更お前が俺のことをどう思おうと知ったことじゃないが」, "I don't care what you think of me now") with the only declaration of love in his words in the novel: 「俺は十歳のあの日からずっと、お前のことを愛し続けてきた」 (Ch07 P0010). He disowns it at once: it 「どこかで致命的な間違いを犯している」, "commits a fatal mistake somewhere." What he wants to say would die if put into words (Ch07 P0012). His wish shrinks to "that the ten-year-old me live on a little longer in her," and to a letter that might say only 「手紙が出したかった」, "I wanted to send a letter" (Ch07 P0013).

**The reply at seventeen.** Given only in summary: trivial news, and that he would be glad if she visited. He meant to write honestly, with great effort, that he wanted to see her (Ch09 P0061–P0064).

**The record.** It is written in plain だ/である narration with explanatory のだ, dense hedging, aphorism and rhetorical questions. Single-line paragraphs mark turns (そう。, Ch11 P0146; それなのに、俺は幸せだった。, Ch15 P0099). Historical present appears at hinges. Twenty-six sesame-dot emphases occur across Ch01–Ch14 and none in Ch15 (`TDH-F037`). In Ch06 and Ch11 the emphases fall on his own affect and conclusions (`TDH-F017`, `TDH-F029`).

The written self is more candid about feeling than the speaking self. It admits attraction, envy, shame and love that his speech withholds. Yet it distrusts its own medium (Ch07 P0012) and openly reshapes memory (Ch12 P0075).

## 8. Relationships

Relationship evidence is directional. What follows is Kusunoki's conduct, trust, attachment and disclosure toward each person, with the other side given only as narrated conduct, testimony or his reading.

### 8.1 Miyagi

Miyagi is the relationship of the novel, and Kusunoki's conduct toward her changes more than toward anyone else. Her statements are testimony throughout. After Ch12 P0193, where she says she has lied to him about "many things," her later statements carry an additional uncertainty that the text never resolves: her love, her reasons, her being "already saved," her account of her own sale.

**The clerk (Ch02).** He trusts her instantly, on a smell of honesty. Where she is involved, he feels, there are no lies, and she seems to him a person who dislikes injustice for its own sake (Ch02 P0146). The writer disowns this at once (Ch02 P0147). His speech to her drops from polite to curt as the result arrives (Ch02 P0169–P0177). In his readings she is awkward at the result and impatient for a decision (Ch02 P0150, P0178).

**The watcher, days one to three (Ch03–Ch07).**

- *Irritation and discomfort.* He is irritated by her, and the side of his body facing her "burns" under her gaze (Ch03 P0057, P0064).
- *Machine-like.* He finds her so lacking in human warmth he can believe she does not eat (Ch03 P0077).
- *The one argument.* He argues back on Himeno (Ch03 P0120).
- *Pretend domesticity.* He takes 「むなしい幸せ」, "empty happiness," in shopping with her at night like a cohabiting couple, while insisting her existence is a nuisance (Ch04 P0061–P0063).
- *Concession and request.* Drunk, he concedes her argument and asks her to tell him his forfeited life (Ch05 P0026).
- *Teasing.* He calls her 「監視員さん」 and says he wants her gone (Ch05 P0042–P0044).
- *Attraction, disclosed late.* Since their first meeting she has been exactly his type, and he has censored himself before her (Ch06 P0007–P0010; `TDH-C028`).
- *The glass, then apology.* He throws a glass at the wall on her side (Ch06 P0094), then apologizes sincerely and says he is lonely (Ch06 P0106–P0114).
- *The relished wound.* He relishes having hurt her with a careless remark (Ch07 P0053).
- *The verdict.* He takes her verdict on his neglect of others in angry silence (Ch07 P0083–P0086).
- *A watch kept on her.* He notices that she seems to sleep in snatches through the night and calls the job too harsh for a young woman, while disclaiming pity (Ch07 P0090–P0091).

**The station night (Ch08).** §5.4 and §10 give the act. What follows it changes his view of her. When she tells him her history (testimony: an absent father, a mother who disliked her and was killed by a subject, an inherited debt, thirty years of time sold), he revises his assumption that she was born cold. Those traits 「彼女が生き残るために、必死の思いで身に付けたものだったのかもしれない」, "may have been acquired desperately, to survive" (Ch08 P0057). He calls her life a tragedy (Ch08 P0076).

They share a joke about his forfeited self, and she lights her cigarette from his, the contact initiated by her (Ch08 P0085–P0089). Seeing her relax for the first time, he resolves, unspoken: 「せめて、彼女にとって、一番、傍にいて気が楽だった監視対象者として記憶されよう」, "at least let me be remembered as the subject easiest to be near" (Ch08 P0091; `TDH-C039`).

**Companion (Ch09–Ch10).**

- *Restraint and suspicion.* He restrains himself from leaning on her and suspects her softening may be instructed (Ch09 P0015–P0018).
- *Showing and thanking.* He shows her his capsule letter (Ch09 P0037) and thanks her, unlike himself (Ch09 P0050).
- *Moving at her pace.* He rides with her pillion at her proposal and reads beside her (Ch09 P0070–P0116). He asks her how she would spend her last months and hears her three wishes (Ch09 P0081–P0099).
- *The proxy date.* He asks her by name to stand in for Himeno and accepts her advice on clothes. He compliments her openly, 「図書館の妖精」, "a library fairy," insisting he means her, not Himeno (Ch10 P0046, P0052). Tipsy, he swings her hand; she lets herself be led, looking troubled (Ch10 P0057).
- *Her concealment accepted.* When he learns she knew Himeno's purpose and hid it, he forgives at once: 「謝ることはないさ。いい夢を見させてもらったよ」, "No need to apologize. You let me have a good dream." The narrator adds that the apology was really his to make, but he had no strength left to admit his fault (Ch10 P0178–P0182).
- *The rain.* He refuses her umbrella; she closes it and walks soaked behind him, mirroring his line (Ch10 P0218–P0223). He pities her asleep at the bus stop and carries her to a community hall, inferring that she feigned sleep (Ch10 P0229–P0233).
- *Saved, and forbidding hope.* That night he feels, or dreams, her stroking his head. He asks himself how much her existence saves him, then forbids himself to hope: she is there for work, kind because he is dying, 「決して…好意を向けているわけではない」, "not out of any feeling for me" (Ch10 P0254–P0256). The reading is his, and her later testimony contradicts it (Ch12 P0183).

**Intimacy (Ch11–Ch12).**

- *The futon.* He feigns sleep so she can bathe; she names his feigned sleep and says she would like it if it were for her (Ch11 P0008–P0010). He offers half his futon, framed as a convenient post for watching him, then judges the offer self-satisfaction that troubles her (Ch11 P0015–P0019).
- *Sharing what he loves.* He discovers his love of vending machines in her presence. She articulates it better than he can, and he is moved (Ch11 P0054–P0067). They do the rounds and fill the album together (Ch11 P0078).
- *Trust at the lake.* Led eyes closed, he says he never thought she might deceive him: 「ミヤギがそんなことをする理由が見当たらないからさ」, "I can't see any reason why Miyagi would" (Ch11 P0182). He thanks her for understanding him (Ch11 P0208).
- *Protecting her in absence.* While she is away he defends her to the substitute and hands over her notebook unread so that she will not be punished (Ch12 P0047).
- *The price lie, met gently.* He pretends to know nothing rather than trouble her (Ch12 P0109–P0113), then confronts her gently and thanks her even if she denies it (Ch12 P0161). He insists her lie gave him his three months (Ch12 P0173).
- *Her confession.* When she says she loves him, he cannot speak. He offers, before it, to go on treating her as present 「俺が死ぬまでは、いつでも」 (Ch12 P0186). He names no feeling in return (`TDH-S069`). Invited to do anything to her if angry, he pulls her up and holds her, memorizing her so as never to forget (Ch12 P0198–P0202). He asks: 「俺が死んだら、たくさん悲しんでくれ」, "When I die, grieve a lot" (Ch12 P0205).

**The foolish summer (Ch13–Ch14).**

- *Hiding to protect.* He hides his debt plans and consultations from her (Ch13 P0039, P0051).
- *Asserting her presence.* He asserts her presence to a sneering acquaintance (Ch13 P0073). He enjoys being thought mad because she laughs (Ch13 P0064).
- *Doing what she asked.* He takes her request to "pay attention to me" as the thing in front of him to do (Ch13 P0082–P0083). He accompanies her to watch Enishi one-sidedly (Ch13 P0124–P0151).
- *The kiss.* He kisses her unasked and calls it selfish (Ch13 P0157–P0161; §10).
- *Before others.* He interprets for her before Shinbashi (Ch14 P0039) and kisses her before Shinbashi's friends on request (Ch14 P0063).
- *The sketchbook.* He finds drawings of their days and of his own sleeping face in a sketchbook, hers by strong inference. 「仕返しに」, "in return," he draws her sleeping face, and his painting comes back (Ch14 P0081–P0097; `TDH-C067`).
- *Thanks and the wish.* He thanks her for everything (Ch14 P0123) and asks, as his one small wish, that she forget him completely when he dies (Ch14 P0128), reversing his request of Ch12. When she refuses, weeps, and begs him to stay a month, he strokes her head (Ch14 P0129–P0143). Next morning, on her day off, he sells (Ch14 P0155–P0157).

**The end (Ch15).** Alone, he prays first that no subject attacks her in despair, then that she repays her debt and is happy enough to forget him, and that someone appears whom she values more than him and who values her more than he does (Ch15 P0012). When she returns, visible to all, and tells him she has sold all but three days, he names her act 「裏切り」, a betrayal of his feelings by the one he sacrificed everything to protect. It is nonetheless, now, 「何よりも愛おしかった」, "dearer than anything" (Ch15 P0098–P0100). What she sold is unexpressed; lifespan by strong inference (`TDH-C024` v3; return and testimony `TDH-C071`). He tells her he is the idiot: 「たったの三日でさえ、俺はミヤギなしでは生きられそうになかった」, "I couldn't have lived even three days without Miyagi" (Ch15 P0103).

### 8.2 Himeno

**At ten.** She is his rival and, by his statement, his only real interlocutor (Ch01 P0039–P0041). He receives her words as prophecy because she said them (Ch01 P0075). Given a real chance to accept her proposal he does not; given room to name a feeling, the narrator does not (U01 §9, absent-trait tests). His later account adds that his goodwill at ten was slight, though perhaps the greatest of his life (Ch03 P0025–P0027). The capsule letter has the boy say "I seem to need Himeno" (Ch09 P0034–P0035).

**The ten years.** She is absent and imagined. She is the measure of his failure and the spur who would have kept him sharp, and he is relieved she cannot see his decline (Ch02 P0015–P0021). In his dream she is still "unsold" too (Ch03 P0011–P0022), which is his construction, not evidence of her. He keeps the "promise" (Ch01 P0094–P0095). At seventeen he answers her one letter without saying what he meant to say (Ch09 P0061–P0064). He gives up painting so as to become the great man he promised her he would be (Ch14 P0182). He never goes to a reunion, expecting she would not come (Ch10 P0131).

**The three months.**

- *Before meeting.* His feeling becomes a list item, "meet Himeno and confess my feelings" (Ch03 P0093), then a farewell letter declaring love that he disowns (Ch07 P0010–P0013), then a plan to talk once and give her money in thanks for "a life," with the confession dropped (Ch09 P0039).
- *The festival.* He is relieved when she does not answer the door (Ch09 P0119). At the festival he recognizes her with joy in 「体中の細胞」, "every cell of my body" (Ch09 P0140), and crosses the item off his list (Ch10 P0016).
- *The proxy date.* Rehearsing with Miyagi, he says he does not care what anyone else thinks so long as Himeno likes him even a little (Ch10 P0028).
- *The dinner.* He is charmed and believes it is going well (Ch10 P0106–P0108). Then he confesses not his love but his sale, pleading (Ch10 P0137–P0146). He is overjoyed by her professed belief (Ch10 P0149–P0150).
- *Her letter.* It is testimony: lifelong resentment for an unanswered call for help, a planned revenge suicide before his eyes, abandoned because he seems "stranger" than she, and a closing hope that at least his short life is true (Ch10 P0190–P0200; `TDH-C048`). He responds without anger at her. In narration he writes 馬鹿だなあ、俺は, "what a fool I am," and "I should have trusted my own way to the end" (Ch10 P0202–P0204).

**Afterwards.** He reconstructs her for the old woman. She was a girl shunned for being too unlike the others; she took her own capsule letter; her letter at seventeen was her SOS; she will soon die (Ch11 P0136–P0148). He uses the word himself: the transfer made her, in him, 「いよいよ神格化されて」, "all the more deified" (Ch11 P0139). The narration endorses only that he should have noticed sooner (Ch11 P0146–P0147). The SOS is his inference, unconfirmed by Himeno (`TDH-C042`, `TDH-C054`).

At Enishi's station he briefly sees himself and Himeno in the happy couple, imagining a future in which he need not have died (Ch13 P0150–P0151). His final verdict on her prophecy: half wrong (he never became great or rich), half right ("something very good" did happen; he was glad he lived) (Ch14 P0197–P0201). He judges it only for himself, though it was promised to 私たち, "us" (U14 §4.16).

**What he never does.** After her letter he makes no attempt to contact her and gives her none of the money; it goes to strangers (Ch10 P0208–P0215). He does not ask Miyagi whether Himeno's forecast suicide still stands after his sale (U05 §8). In chapter 15 Himeno is not mentioned; her fate is never narrated.

**The other side.** Himeno's view of him reaches us through her childhood speech, her dinner conversation (admiration of his paintings; professed belief) and her letter. These conflict, and the conflict is hers to carry. For his portrait, the point is that he never learns which was true.

### 8.3 Family

**Parents.** He cannot rely on them (Ch02 P0007). He quarrelled with them over university; they insisted only a famous university was worth attending and paid the entrance fee after he persuaded them (Ch10 P0119). He reports being treated as a failure beside his brother, calls it fair in investment terms, and uses a verb of grievance (Ch04 P0070–P0071). He refuses all contact for his remaining time, because 「自ら不快な思いをしにいくこと」, "going out of my way to feel bad," must be avoided (Ch04 P0069). He detours around the family home (Ch07 P0055) and chooses a station bench over it, though he "could" have gone (Ch07 P0088).

As a boy he staged friendship with Himeno partly so as not to worry his parents (Ch01 P0049), and ran away once after a quarrel with his mother (Ch11 P0131). Chapter 1's 「親同士の仲がよく」 describes the two families' friendship, not warmth toward him (U04 §4.7).

**Brother.** He is known only through Kusunoki's account. He is never named, never seen, and his knowledge of his brother's present life is not explained (U04 §4.7).

**Grandfather.** A grave visited on the home-town trip, in one clause (Ch07 P0068). It is the only family member he seeks out.

**Limitation.** Within the text his family never learns of the sale or his death. The family dimension of his portrait is thin, and what exists is his testimony.

### 8.4 Shopkeepers and benefactors

**The old bookseller.** Kusunoki found the shop by getting lost in his first year and could reach it only by getting lost again for months (Ch02 P0025–P0026). The old man never smiles. On the sale day he asks whether Kusunoki is moving, judges him decent with passable taste, and refers him to the shop (Ch02 P0028–P0064). Kusunoki first thinks him addled by fear of death, then follows his map (Ch02 P0054, P0066, P0099).

A month later he returns to consult him and finds peace in talk of weather and baseball: 「多分俺は、この店が、そしてこの老人のことが好きだったんだろう」, "I suppose I liked this shop, and this old man" (Ch13 P0038). He keeps the shop out of the conversation so the old man will not feel responsible (Ch13 P0037). He follows the old man's advice to proceed 「堅実に」, "steadily," while being told not to trust advice from a failure (Ch13 P0042–P0044, P0080). He laughs off the old man's 〔傍点〕また〔/傍点〕, "*again*" (Ch13 P0047–P0049), which his second sale bears out (`TDH-C062` v1a). He speaks to him politely throughout. Going to a bookshop when troubled is, he says, a habit (Ch13 P0035).

**The blond CD clerk.** Kusunoki first meets his familiarity with sarcasm and perfunctory thanks (Ch02 P0082, P0091). Later he asks him how to achieve something in limited time; the clerk says rely on others and lends him an umbrella, with "health comes first" (Ch13 P0050–P0057).

**The photo-shop owner.** Kusunoki tells him plainly that a girl called Miyagi is there and that her job is to watch him (Ch11 P0082). The narrator later calls the owner the first to affirm her existence (Ch15 P0045). Kusunoki is polite with him.

**The old woman at the candy shop.** He addresses her in a long, polite monologue on forgetting, on her kindness when he ran away, and on Himeno (Ch11 P0128–P0148). Being forgotten by her, as he takes it, hardly hurts; he was, the narrator supposes, getting used to being betrayed by his memories (Ch11 P0152–P0153). Whether she remembers him is undecidable.

**Pattern.** With shopkeepers Kusunoki is a reticent regular. He is polite and suspicious at first, comes back when troubled, protects them from his troubles, and takes their advice more than he admits. Apart from peers (Naruse, Wakana, Himeno), these are the only adults he seeks out voluntarily.

### 8.5 Shinbashi and the town

**Shinbashi.** A neighbour who used to look at Kusunoki with distaste when he came and went talking with Miyagi (Ch14 P0014), that is, to no one visible (Ch14 P0012–P0015; perhaps the tall neighbour of Ch11 P0094, which is not stated). He rescues Kusunoki from harassers while denying that he believes or that he was helping (Ch14 P0016–P0026). Kusunoki answers him plainly and describes Miyagi feature by feature. He has Miyagi shake Shinbashi's hand, which he narrates as her delight: 「そして今、ミヤギはあんたの目の前にいる」, "and now Miyagi is right in front of you" (Ch14 P0029–P0037). He relays her thanks (Ch14 P0039), goes to Shinbashi's party, and accepts his car keys (Ch14 P0050–P0073). Shinbashi's partial sense of her is testimony (`TDH-C066`). Kusunoki does not argue with Shinbashi's theory that he is performing a mime. He offers a description and a handshake, a demonstration rather than a proof.

**The town.** At first Kusunoki is the target of whispers, loud insults and harassment, and says he has no right to complain, since he gave offence first (Ch14 P0005–P0006). By his account, the town comes to treat Miyagi's presence as a shared convention, not belief. He becomes a minor celebrity, 「可哀想で面白い人」, "a pitiable, amusing person" (Ch14 P0046–P0049). The town's reactions reach him later, as hearsay (Ch13 P0102). He explains the half that liked him: 「俺が芯から幸せそうにしていたから」, "because I looked happy to the core" (Ch13 P0106). He calls himself the town's greatest clown, 「良くも、悪くも」, "for better or worse" (Ch14 P0065–P0066), and is proud that half the smiles are goodwill (Ch14 P0117).

On the last day, the town's children, students and acquaintances gather to comfort him when he breaks down. He invents a story that Miyagi left him (Ch15 P0040); why is not stated. He tells no one of his death. When she returns, everyone sees her (Ch15 P0071–P0088).

**Change.** He begins as a man who avoids crowds and hears customers as noise. He ends as a known public figure, and the crowd is the one that witnesses Miyagi's reality. The change in his conduct is real. The town's attitudes are his account.

### 8.6 Peers

**Naruse.** A high-school friend of three years with whom he shared the view that most things can be laughed off (Ch06 P0041–P0042). He wants to meet him to laugh like that again, and to be misread as Miyagi's companion (Ch06 P0043–P0047). He hides his remaining life to keep the laughter (Ch06 P0071).

His goodwill ends in under ten seconds when Naruse laughs at the thought that he might still be drawing (Ch06 P0073–P0078). Afterwards he doubts he ever liked Naruse rather than himself through Naruse (Ch06 P0100). He concludes that between Naruse who has changed and himself who could not, 「正しいのは、多分、ナルセの方なのだろう」, "the one in the right is probably Naruse" (Ch06 P0101–P0102). The voice of that verdict is undecidable. Miyagi's testimony that Naruse liked him less than he thought is unverified (`TDH-C026`).

**Wakana.** Covered in §3. On the first night he calls her out of loneliness, reads her bounced email as a deliberate cut, hears reproach in her callback, and lies that he misdialled (Ch04 P0009–P0055). Her own intent is not given.

**The acquaintance.** A man who calls him crazy and shut in his shell. Kusunoki answers with a measured statement of mutual respect for belief and disbelief (Ch13 P0067–P0075).

**Classmates.** At ten he despised them and was isolated; no capsule letter named him best friend (Ch07 P0076). He reads all their letters against the teacher's promise (Ch07 P0073). Of the clever clown he hated, he says late that he envied him and always wanted to get on with everyone (Ch14 P0119; the clown is Ch01 P0031, by strong inference).

### 8.7 The substitute, the appraiser, Enishi

**The substitute.** He is the "ugly, fat" watcher Kusunoki once wished for, the narrator says (Ch12 P0004–P0005; Ch06 P0010); the description given omits fatness (`TDH-C060`). Kusunoki finds his bluntness easier than meaningful looks. He warms to him as Miyagi's ally, confesses to him his conduct toward her, and entrusts him with her notebook (Ch12 P0020–P0047). From him he receives the hint about the price (Ch12 P0094). He accompanies Kusunoki to the second sale (Ch14 P0155–P0195).

**The appraiser.** She advises against the second sale and offers him eternity in art history. He refuses politely (Ch14 P0162–P0168).

**Enishi.** Miyagi's childhood friend, seen once. Kusunoki reads his composed face as that of someone 「誰かを愛し、誰かに愛されているという確信のある者」, "sure of loving and being loved," a look he says he is 「最近」, "lately," beginning to understand (Ch13 P0141–P0142). Kusunoki does not want Miyagi to see whom Enishi is waiting for and suggests leaving (Ch13 P0143–P0144).

## 9. Preferences and ordinary life

Ordinary life is one of the best-evidenced dimensions of Kusunoki, and it is consistent: a man of narrow means and exacting taste, whose pleasures are small, solitary and repeated.

**Money.**

- *Before the sale.* Desperately poor. No meat, no haircut, no clothes; a beer-garden job; an emergency-room taxi makes it worse (Ch02 P0004–P0006).
- *His first spending of the ¥300,000.* At a night supermarket he cannot imagine what luxury would be (Ch04 P0059). The night of the sale he had already bought beer and yakitori recklessly, a minor inconsistency (U04 §4.8).
- *Where the money goes.* Most goes to strangers on a bridge (Ch10 P0208–P0215).
- *What is left.* Small savings and a month's pay, "probably my last war chest," to be used carefully (Ch11 P0026–P0028). Casual labour when it runs low (Ch13 P0101).
- *The end.* He ends with ¥187, sixty of it in one-yen coins, counted three times. He smiles at 「奇妙な偶然」, "a curious coincidence" (Ch15 P0008–P0009). By inference this is the sum in the O. Henry story the two read together (Ch09 P0111); the text does not name it; the identification rests on public knowledge of O. Henry's story, not on this text (U15 §4.3).

**Drink and food.**

- *Cider.* Cider bookends his story: the agonized, savoured can of chapter 2 (Ch02 P0091–P0092) and the cider from a machine on his last morning (Ch15 P0007).
- *Other drinks.* A sweet iced coffee after vending-machine soba (Ch03 P0076–P0078). Cola in three gulps (Ch06 P0026). Pocari Sweat after the batting cages (Ch06 P0099). An energy drink at dawn (Ch08 P0004). Canned coffee (Ch11 P0046, P0049).
- *Beer.* He makes it taste better by counting how many litres he has left to drink (Ch06 P0105).
- *Food.* Cup ramen and fried chicken (Ch04 P0059); canned yakiniku warmed with green onion (Ch06 P0105); beef bowl (Ch11 P0029). One item from every festival stall, "a boy's dream," shared with Miyagi (Ch09 P0123–P0126). As a child he ate mostly sweets from the candy shop (Ch11 P0099).

**Cigarettes.** He smokes throughout: a found pack of Seven Stars after a long gap (Ch02 P0131), while waiting for soba (Ch03 P0077), outside after the Wakana call (Ch04 P0059), during the capsule dig (Ch07 P0071). He buys Short Hope (Ch11 P0049). He carries a portable ashtray but leaves butts at Himeno's house until Miyagi's look makes him pick them up (Ch09 P0117). The cigarette is also the medium of the shared light (Ch08 P0087–P0089).

**Vending machines.** The one thing he says he can love 「心の底から」, "from the bottom of my heart" (Ch11 P0052), rediscovered after being 「あまりにも近過ぎて、逆に見えなかった」, "too close to see" (Ch11 P0050): 「自動販売機が大好きなんだよ」, and as a child 「俺は自動販売機になりたかったんだ」, "I wanted to be a vending machine" (Ch11 P0054–P0056). Miyagi's gloss, which moves him, is that machines are always there, give something warm whenever you pay, and offer a clear-cut, constant relationship (Ch11 P0066). Recalling Paul Auster's *Smoke*, he buys a second-hand film camera and photographs every vending machine he passes, persistently, knowing others do it better: 「誰が何といおうと、これが俺に向いたやり方なのだ」, "whatever anyone says, this is the way that suits me" (Ch11 P0069–P0077; `TDH-C052`). He tells the substitute 「撮りたいから撮ってる。俺は自販機が好きなんだ」 (Ch12 P0017).

**The Cub.** A second-hand Cub 110 with a tandem seat (Ch09 P0072). He rides Miyagi pillion to the university, the lake and the rounds. On his last morning he rides alone to the places they visited, 「彼女の残り香のようなもの」, seeking "something like her lingering scent" (Ch15 P0010–P0011).

**Books.** Used, carefully chosen (Ch02 P0008). In the library he reads Auster, Miyazawa Kenji, O. Henry and Hemingway (Ch09 P0110). He quotes Paul Nizan on twenty (Ch02 P0014). He goes to bookshops when troubled (Ch13 P0035). He describes his reading as soup poured into a dish too small, so nothing stays; Wakana answers that what one reads remains somewhere (Ch04 P0023–P0024). Reading beside Miyagi makes the reading denser (Ch09 P0115–P0116).

**Music.** Used CDs, listened through for three days before most are sold (Ch02 P0022). He keeps 「プリーズ・ミスター・ロストマン」 for sleepless nights (Ch05 P0081). His hobbies feel to him, after the list, as if made for people who will go on living (Ch11 P0034).

**Habits.**

- Bargain shelves out of habit (Ch02 P0027).
- The bedtime world-building since about five (Ch10 P0236–P0242).
- Walking to think (Ch13 P0012).
- Folding cranes (Ch09).
- Writing in a B5 Tsubame notebook with a cheap fountain pen (Ch12 P0052).
- Light sleep (Ch05 P0079).

**Dress.** Faded jeans and a frayed T-shirt (Ch03 P0074). After the rehearsal he wears a Fred Perry polo, chinos and desert boots, chosen on Miyagi's advice, with a haircut that transforms him (Ch10 P0035–P0042). In his account he had long envied couples who go out in loungewear to buy drinks (Ch04 P0062).

**Aversions.** Crowds and their places (Ch05 P0035); being watched (Ch03 P0064; Ch05 P0044); his family home (Ch07); beaming strangers (Ch02 P0191); his own hunger (Ch02 P0133).

## 10. Romantic and intimate psychology

**Himeno.** At ten, what the text shows of the boy is limited to what it depicts: rivalry, mutual intelligibility, a mirrored non-answer to a proposal that was then retracted, and the capsule letter's "I seem to need Himeno" (Ch01 P0087–P0093; Ch09 P0034–P0035).

The adult's account of that feeling shifts:

- "slight goodwill," perhaps the greatest of his life (Ch03 P0025–P0027);
- a written declaration of love since "that day," disowned at once (Ch07 P0010–P0012);
- the hope that she might like him "even a little" (Ch10 P0028);
- joy at her sight (Ch09 P0140);
- charm at dinner (Ch10 P0086–P0108).

He never confesses love to her. The item is crossed out, and he confesses the sale instead (Ch10 P0016, P0141). His ten-year fidelity is self-reported and over-determined (§4.6). After her letter his attachment becomes retrospective idealization and remorse, which he names himself as 神格化, deification (Ch11 P0139).

**Wakana.** He kept her at a distance for the "promise" (Ch04 P0028). His call in loneliness states no feeling for her; he wanted company "until the end" (Ch04 P0012). By his own account he had never properly gone out with a woman in twenty years (Ch10 P0021).

**Miyagi.** Attraction comes first and is physical and aesthetic. She was 〔傍点〕そのもの〔/傍点〕, exactly his type, from the first meeting: cool eyes, melancholy brows, nervous fingers, slender white thighs. He became self-conscious before her, unable even to yawn carelessly (Ch06 P0007–P0010).

That attraction is named among the hedged causes of the worst thing he does. Before dawn on day four he wants, "to put it more directly," 「ミヤギを押し倒したくなった」, "to push Miyagi down," and to use her as an outlet for every feeling (Ch08 P0008). The intention is an intended sexual assault, by inference from the euphemisms (U08 §4; `TDH-C036`). He invokes his list item, asks how long he would have before the rule killed him, and touches her bare shoulder. As narrated he stops when he imagines the harm and sees what looks to him like her sadness and resignation. He withdraws in shame and calls himself scum (Ch08 P0007–P0035). Why she did not flee is undecidable; fear is among the rivals (U08 §4.2). He offers his attraction to her afterwards as the reason for the act (Ch08 P0045). He does not apologize in words, and he names it to a third party a month later (Ch12 P0042).

The episode belongs in the portrait unsoftened. It shows that he was capable of it. As narrated, the stop came from imagination and borrowed sadness; the rule plays no narrated part, though he had just asked whether it left him "more than ten minutes," which Miyagi did not confirm (Ch08 P0020). Unnarrated fear of death, and exhaustion or pain, remain rivals (U08 §4.4). It also stands at the turn of the relationship, not after it.

After the station night, physical approaches in the companion phase are mostly hers (U09 §8). His are hesitant and self-checked: the hand-swinging she allows (Ch10 P0057), the futon offer at the left edge with a promise not to look (Ch11 P0015), his body awareness on the bike which nearly makes him run a red light (Ch11 P0045).

After her confession his intimacy becomes open:

- the embrace in which he memorizes her body 「二度と忘れないように」, "so as never to forget" (Ch12 P0199–P0202);
- the request to be grieved (Ch12 P0205);
- the unasked kiss at Enishi's station, about two minutes, narrated as returned after she first stiffened, followed by 「どうせ誰にも咎められないんだったら、これくらい自分勝手なことをしてやるさ」, "if no one's going to reproach it anyway, I'll be this selfish" (Ch13 P0157–P0161). Consent beyond his narration is not claimed (`TDH-C065`);
- the public kiss on request (Ch14 P0063);
- the shared futon in which 「境界線はいつの間にかなくなっていた」, "the boundary had gone" (Ch14 P0079);
- her sleeping face, 「愛おしかった」 (Ch14 P0080).

No sexual relationship is narrated beyond kisses, embraces and a shared futon; whether there was one is unknown.

His characteristic intimate stance is to want to be remembered, then to want to be forgotten for the other's sake. He asks to be grieved (Ch12 P0205). He asks to be forgotten (Ch14 P0128). He prays she will be happy enough to forget him and find someone better (Ch15 P0012). In the end he needs neither memory nor eternity because she is beside him (Ch15 P0114–P0117).

**Jealousy.** Little evidence. He prefers that Miyagi not see whom Enishi is waiting for (Ch13 P0143–P0144). When Asakura says he would court her if he could see her, Kusunoki answers 「ミヤギは俺のことが好きだから」, "Miyagi likes me" (Ch14 P0058). Possessive teasing (「渡さないぞ」, Ch14 P0102) is play.

**Boundaries.** His own boundary is a refusal to make her his support (Ch09 P0015) and to hope (Ch10 P0256–P0258). Both give way. He never says he loves her in direct speech (§7.6).

## 11. Capabilities and limitations

**Intellect and learning.** Perfect test scores at ten (Ch01 P0037). A memory he calls once perfect, declining from middle school (Ch10 P0119). Wide reading, quotation, and fluent writing: 「言葉は淀みなく溢れてきた」, "the words flowed" (Ch12 P0055). His reasoning is sharp when deducing the price lie (Ch12 P0150–P0157) and over-confident in his Himeno answer check (Ch11 P0142–P0148).

**Painting.** A talent he claims and Himeno corroborates (Ch14 P0179–P0181; Ch10 P0121–P0125). Three accounts of its loss conflict:

- to Naruse, he stopped "after entering university" (Ch06 P0074);
- to Himeno, he had not held a brush since the winter at seventeen (Ch10 P0119);
- the narration says he stopped at seventeen by resolve, let himself paint at nineteen, and lost the ability completely (Ch14 P0182–P0188).

The conflict is unresolved. U14 §4.14 holds four rivals open without ranking them: concealment of the failure, a simplification, an attempt so brief he discounted it, and the narrator's later framing (`TDH-C067`).

His diagnosis of the loss is his: trying to make everyone accept his work. Universality lies instead in what is drawn up 「自分の井戸の底」, "from the bottom of one's own well" (Ch14 P0189). His recovery happens by his account: drawing Miyagi's sleeping face 〔傍点〕自分のために〔/傍点〕, *for himself*, then drawing all night the bedtime worlds he had pictured since five, 「俺の技法はそこで完成した」, "my technique was complete" (Ch14 P0190–P0191).

The worth of that capacity is known only through the shop's appraisal, as hearsay. The paintings of the lost thirty days would later have been called 「世界一通俗的な絵」, "the world's most vulgar paintings," stirred controversy and won great acclaim (Ch14 P0171–P0172). The appraiser described them as 「デ・キリコを極限まで甘ったるくしたような絵」, "like de Chirico made unbearably sweet," which he calls her interpretation (Ch14 P0192). He chooses to think that the talent would normally have taken a lifetime to bloom, that an accident was fated to take the chance first, and that the sale and Miyagi shortened the time (Ch14 P0174–P0176). That is his chosen theory, built on Miyagi's unverified Ch05 account.

**Capacity and willingness.** His central limitations are often willingness, not capacity:

- He refuses to draw until "everything meshes" (Ch14 P0183).
- He could sell down to three days and does not (Ch05 P0070–P0073).
- He could go home or call a taxi and sleeps on a bench (Ch07 P0088).
- He could have courted Wakana and did not (Ch04 P0028).
- He is capable of violence and stops (Ch06, Ch08).
- He refuses eternity when it is offered (Ch14 P0168).

**Social capacity.** He is poor at initiating (Ch04 P0008), able to be polite and charming when it matters (with elders; at the Himeno dinner, Ch10 P0091–P0099), and able to sustain a public performance under ridicule for weeks (Ch13–Ch14).

**Body.** Thin and malnourished at the start; he collapses from heat and hunger (Ch02 P0004–P0006). Capable of three hundred swings and an hour's digging with ruined hands (Ch06 P0098; Ch07 P0070), and of a four-hour walk (Ch11 P0004). A light sleeper (Ch05 P0079). After the second sale he feels his life almost burnt out (Ch15 P0022).

**The supernatural economy.** He has no special ability. He perceives Miyagi because he is the watched subject. By her testimony he saw her at the shop because he intended to sell (Ch07 P0046–P0049; `TDH-C024` v2; INFERENCE). Everything he knows of the shop's rules, valuations and the watcher system is testimony or hearsay, much of it later admitted by Miyagi to have been lies (`TDH-C059`).

## 12. Longitudinal development

This section separates what changes in Kusunoki from what is revealed late about a trait he already had. Each phase states what changed, for whom, and what the text gives as cause.

**Phase 1: deflation (the sale day, Ch02).**

- *State.* He enters with an inflated, attenuated self-valuation and a dream of eternity (Ch02 P0119–P0122). He leaves 「桁違い」, off by orders of magnitude, crushed by the fairness of the criteria (Ch02 P0164, P0171).
- *Conduct.* Angry at strangers, careless with money, sick (Ch02 P0191–P0194).
- *Cause.* The appraisal, as testimony, plus poverty and habituation (Ch02 P0181).
- *Limit.* The narrator's claim that the sale was "probably right" is hindsight. No change of conduct follows that day (`TDH-C009`).

**Phase 2: oscillation (days 1–3, Ch03–Ch07).** Relief at hopelessness turns within hours into hope (Ch03 P0035, P0048). Then come:

- loneliness and the first initiated call in years (Ch04 P0006–P0013);
- the drunk vow and its concession (Ch05 P0008–P0026);
- the choice to keep his three months on hope (Ch05 P0070–P0073);
- a resolution to honesty that yields performance (Ch06 P0018, P0046);
- the end of a friendship and a thrown glass (Ch06);
- the capsule and isolation confirmed (Ch07 P0076);
- Miyagi's verdict accepted in silence (Ch07 P0084–P0086).

*What changes.* Mostly mood, which is local and shifting (`TDH-C020`). Two durable shifts begin:

- *Loneliness acknowledged.* He admits ordinary loneliness, 「人並み」 (Ch04 P0067).
- *Restraint begins.* He moves from retort and violence toward silence (Ch06 to Ch07).

*Late revelations, not development.* His attraction to Miyagi is dated to the first meeting (Ch06 P0007). His drawing and its loss (Ch06 P0073–P0074). The letter-like あれ at seventeen (Ch07 P0015).

**Phase 3: the turn (the station night, Ch08).**

- *Change.* He acts on the worst impulse he has, stops, and is ashamed (Ch08 P0007–P0035). Then, for the first time, he asks about Miyagi's life with offers not to answer (Ch08 P0041–P0063). He revises his view of her nature (Ch08 P0057), laughs with her, and resolves to be the subject she found easiest to be near (Ch08 P0091).
- *Cause, as narrated.* Imagining the harm and her sadness (Ch08 P0025–P0030); her history (testimony); her identification with him (Ch08 P0082).
- *Status.* INFERENCE: the first real change of his orientation toward another person in the story present. U09 confirms a change of conduct (`TDH-C039`; F-U08-d confirmed in part). The causal link between the resolve and the later conduct is not narrated.

**Phase 4: the Himeno test (Ch09–Ch10).**

- *Change.* He honours the boy's request and goes to see her (Ch09 P0039). He discovers that the woman he kept faith with planned to die before his eyes in revenge (Ch10 P0190–P0200). He disperses the money and resolves to expect nothing and die unnoticed (Ch10 P0207–P0259).
- *Cause.* The collapse of the organizing story of his life.
- *Status.* A real loss of a belief. The resolve to withdraw is soon reversed, so it is a temporary state. His later reading of the old woman and of Himeno's SOS (Ch11) begins to undo the frame (U11 §4.11).

**Phase 5: companionship (Ch11).**

- *Change.* He finds something he loves without irony, vending machines, and a way of living that suits him: modest, persistent, unconcerned with being outdone (Ch11 P0054–P0077). He trusts Miyagi without reservation (Ch11 P0182) and thanks her for understanding him (Ch11 P0208).
- *Cause.* Her presence and her articulation of his love (Ch11 P0066); the answer to her Ch06 suggestion of 「もっとあなたに向いたやり方」, "a way more suited to you" (Ch06 P0015; lexical echo, `TDH-F030`).
- *Late revelation.* His love of machines is old, a childhood wish, rediscovered (Ch11 P0056).

**Phase 6: revelation and purpose (Ch12).**

- *Change.* He learns that his life was valued at thirty yen and that the ¥300,000 was her gift (her testimony). He re-reads his own valuation through the teacher's lesson (Ch12 P0153–P0155). He thanks her (Ch12 P0161, P0173), hears her love (Ch12 P0188), asks to be grieved (Ch12 P0205), and forms a goal of giving: to pay her debt (Ch12 P0210–P0215).
- *The writer.* He begins the record (Ch12 P0052).
- *Cause.* Her confession reverses his "kindness is only work" reading (Ch10 P0256). In his words, her words 「すさまじい変革をもたらした」, "brought about a tremendous change" (Ch12 P0211).
- *Status.* Real development, in priorities: from receiving to giving.

**Phase 7: the foolish summer (Ch13–Ch14).**

- *Change.* He turns, for the time, from the debt to the thing in front of him (Ch13 P0083) and resolves to live 「どんな人間よりも愚かしい人間として」, "as the most foolish person" (Ch13 P0090). He asserts her presence in public, becomes a known figure, bears hostility without retaliation (Ch14 P0008), and states that he has made peace with the world (Ch14 P0119). He recovers his painting (Ch14 P0085–P0097, P0190–P0191). He wishes to be forgotten for her sake (Ch14 P0128). He sells his last thirty days for her, against her plea (Ch14 P0157). He refuses eternity (Ch14 P0168) and declares himself glad to have lived (Ch14 P0201).
- *Cause.* Her request for memories and for attention (Ch13 P0027, P0087); the old man's "steadily" (Ch13 P0080–P0083); Shinbashi's intervention (Ch14); her sleeping face and her drawings (Ch14 P0084–P0085).
- *Status.* The largest observable change of conduct: from avoidance of others' eyes to public, cheerful foolishness. Some of it is self-report ("peace with the world"; "glad I lived"); his non-retaliation and acceptance of ridicule corroborate it in part.
- *Late revelations, not development.* His envy of the class clown is presented as an old feeling newly understood (Ch14 P0119). The painting history (Ch14 P0179–P0188). The bedtime habit, disclosed in Ch10, turns out to be the content of the paintings he draws all night (Ch14 P0191).

**Phase 8: the end (Ch15).**

- *Change.* Alone, he finds the pretence of her presence unbearable and doubts whether she or the shop was real (Ch15 P0013–P0024). He weeps before strangers (Ch15 P0035). When she returns, her "betrayal" of his sacrifice is dearer to him than anything (Ch15 P0100). He no longer needs to be remembered (Ch15 P0115). He 「すべてを許せた」, "could forgive everything" (Ch15 P0118). The three days will "probably" be worth more than any span he might have lived (Ch15 P0124–P0127).
- *Status.* The closing statements are self-report at the moment of writing, hedged in the last sentence by 多分 and のだろう.

## 13. Conditional behavioural profile

**Worked predictions** (within the story's world, conditional, not evidence).

1. *If, in the late phase, a stranger loudly mocked his "imaginary girlfriend,"* he would most likely not retaliate. He would wait it out or answer with a calm statement that he respects their disbelief and asks respect for his belief (Ch13 P0073; Ch14 P0008). Abstain on whether he would leave; the izakaya scene ends with outside help.
2. *If, early in the summer, a near-stranger offered him an unrequested kindness,* he would most likely accept, privately doubt its motive, and thank perfunctorily (the CD clerk, Ch02 P0091). Whether he would return depends on later need (Ch13).
3. *If Miyagi had asked him directly, late in the summer, whether he loved her,* the text supports only that he would answer with an act, a joke or a statement about her feelings. It does not support a confident prediction of the words; abstain.
4. *If given a further chance to sell to help someone,* the profile predicts concealment and unilateral action, *provided* the beneficiary is Miyagi or someone in a comparable place in his life. For anyone else, abstain.

**Abstention zones.**

- The three last days themselves, his death, and anything after Ch15 P0127.
- Life before ten beyond the few recounted incidents. Adolescence beyond the letter and the painting.
- Any sexual conduct beyond what is narrated.
- His conduct toward his parents or brother in person (never shown).
- Any further meeting with Himeno; the text gives none after her letter.
- Contexts outside the novel's supernatural economy, or a Kusunoki not under a death sentence. His habitual dispositions (frugality, reading, avoidance) are evidenced, but his late openness is bound to his situation and to Miyagi.
- Spoken voice, accent and intonation; the medium gives none.

## 14. Interpretive uncertainty

**Alternative explanations held open.**

1. *Why he sold.* His stated reasons are habituation and a wish to exit. Rivals: despair produced by the verdict, impatience to leave, face-saving. Why he kept three months is never stated (`TDH-C009`).
2. *Why he kept faith with Himeno.* The promise, by his account. Rivals: misanthropy, incapacity for conformist performance, an incapacity to feel goodwill, and display (`TDH-C003`).
3. *Whether his contempt was revised.* His self-assessment changed early. His contempt for others persisted at least through Ch06 and gives way only late, largely by self-report (`TDH-C004`). The "peace with the world" may be development or the summation of a happy man.
4. *Why he stopped at the station.* As narrated, through imagining harm and sharing her sadness. No other cause is narrated; rivals held open are unnarrated fear of death and exhaustion or pain (U08 §4.4). The stop is narrated as affective, not reasoned (U08 §5).
5. *Why he sold again.* For her debt, by the narrator's later statement (Ch15 P0098). Whether the goal was revived by chance or never given up is undecided (`TDH-C061` v3–v4).
6. *Why he invented the break-up story* (Ch15 P0040). Not stated. HYPOTHESES: to spare himself explaining an impossibility; to keep her reality intact in others' eyes. Neither is supported.

**Unresolved contradictions.**

- *The painting history.* The accounts at Ch06 P0074, Ch10 P0119 and Ch14 P0182–P0188 conflict. Rivals, held open and unranked (U14 §4.14): concealment of the failure, simplification, a brief attempt he discounted, and the narrator's later framing. Not resolved.
- *Never envied before* (Ch04 P0065) against Ch04 P0062 and Ch14 P0119.
- *No goodwill for anyone after Himeno* (Ch03 P0026) against three years of goodwill for Naruse (Ch06 P0077).
- *His two reports of the substitute's hint.* "Only told to check again" (Ch12 P0147) against "when I heard Miyagi was lying" (Ch12 P0150) (`TDH-C057`).
- *First spending of the money* (Ch04 P0059) against the sale-night beer and yakitori (Ch02 P0192–P0193). Minor.
- *「何も残さずに死ぬ」* (Ch15 P0111) against a record that presupposes a reader (Ch13 P0090).

**Hard points, handled as the readings handle them.**

- *The narrating record and its chronology* (`TDH-C010` v5). The record is his notebook by strong inference. Its identity with the printed text is itself an inference; a later redaction is a recorded rival. Which earlier passages were revised, and when Ch15 was written, are unknown.
- *The Ch11 "betrayal"* (`TDH-C056` v2). Given content by inference, moderate to strong, as her sale, which he names 裏切り with the same roles (Ch15 P0098–P0100). It is not explicitly linked. Rivals: the price lie (weakened), her love as a defeated expectation, her absence. By the record's chronology, the identification requires an unnarrated revision after the reunion.
- *The Ch13 "wisest decision"* (`TDH-C064` v2). Not resolved. Candidates at the record's end (the title's 賢 against the body's 愚か; her foolishness answering his; the three days valued) are HYPOTHESES, moderate for the lexical bridge and low to moderate for the chains. No sentence links them.
- *Miyagi's statements after Ch12 P0193.* Her love, reasons, being "already saved," her horizon and her account of her own sale all come from a self-declared liar. Kusunoki accepts them; the text does not verify them.
- *What Miyagi sold in Ch15.* Unexpressed in her words; lifespan by strong inference (Ch15 P0092–P0096).
- *The ending.* The novel does not narrate the three days or any death. His closing valuation is a prospect, hedged.

**Evidence gaps.**

- The real contents of Miyagi's notebook, and whether it is the sketchbook (Ch12 P0048; Ch14 P0081).
- The text of his capsule letter beyond the excerpt, and of his reply at seventeen.
- His parents' side, and his brother's existence beyond his account.
- The cause of his death at about fifty in the "original course" (never given).
- Whether his Ch05 forfeited future, which he adopts as fate (Ch14 P0174), was true.
- Himeno's fate after her letter.
- Whether the old woman remembered him.
- Whether the head-stroking in the community hall happened (Ch10 P0245–P0247; he himself cannot say).
- What "everything" he forgives (Ch15 P0118).

**Limits of extrapolation.** His late openness, gratitude and public foolishness are bound to a man with weeks to live and to one companion. The text gives no basis for projecting them onto a Kusunoki with a future, or into the unnarrated three days. His readings of others are not evidence of their feelings. Training-derived familiarity with the novel is quarantined and contributes nothing here.
