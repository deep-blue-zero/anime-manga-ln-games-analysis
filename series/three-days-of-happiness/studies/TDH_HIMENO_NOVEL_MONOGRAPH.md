# Three Days of Happiness — Himeno (ヒメノ)

## Orientation

Himeno is Kusunoki's childhood friend, neighbor and rival. At ten, on the stone steps of a shrine above a summer festival, she foretold that the two of them would become great and rich in "the summer ten years on." She then proposed, haltingly, that if neither had found a partner by twenty, the two "leftovers" should marry, and at once retracted it as a joke (Ch01 P0062–P0092). She moved away that summer (Ch02 P0016). For the next ten years she exists in the novel only as Kusunoki's memory, dream, imagined reader and interpreter, as a few physical traces, and as the testimony of Miyagi, his watcher. She reappears in person for one festival-night encounter and one rainy dinner (Ch09 P0136–P0148; Ch10 P0004–P0154). Then she leaves him a letter headed 「私の、たった一人の幼馴染へ」 ("To my one and only childhood friend"). In it she says she had resented him all along for not answering her call for help, and had meant to make herself indispensable to him and then die in front of him. She drops the plan because he seems to have become far stranger than she is (Ch10 P0190–P0200). She never appears again, and her fate is never narrated.

**The portrait in brief.** At ten, Himeno is clever, proud and ostracized. She supplies the frames others think in: the lifetime-earnings price of a life, the prophecy, the verdict on their school, and the market word 売れ残り ("left over, unsold"). Once, at the shrine, she exposes a wish for attachment, and she covers it at once with a joke and a boast. At twenty she is quick, teasing and socially adept. Her warmth at dinner cannot be separated from a concealed plan of spiteful revenge. She professes belief in Kusunoki's story but judges him privately, leaves rather than confronts, and puts her decisive message in writing after she has gone. Her self-diagnosis at dinner is that she has not changed since ten and cannot tolerate imperfection. That fits the all-or-nothing grievance her letter discloses (INFERENCE).

**The limits.** This portrait is bounded and mostly mediated. Almost everything about Himeno reaches the reader through Kusunoki, as narrator, as the record's writer, and as an interested party. Her own words are her dialogue as he records it (Ch01; Ch09 P0147; Ch10) and one written document, the letter at twenty. Her letter at seventeen survives only in his summary. Miyagi's account of Himeno's life is single-source testimony, partly corroborated, partly shown false and otherwise unverified. Miyagi later says she lied about "Himeno" without saying what (Ch12 P0193). Kusunoki's conclusions in chapter 11 are his inferences, not established facts: that Himeno's letter at seventeen was an SOS, that she took her own time-capsule letter, and that she "will soon kill herself." The novel never narrates her interior, her life between ten and twenty, her child (if there is one), her work, or what becomes of her.

## 2. Identity and circumstances

**Name.** ヒメノ, in katakana throughout the narration and dialogue. It is a surname: the nameplate on her family's house reads 姫野 (Ch09 P0103). Her given name is never given. Kusunoki's narration links the 姫 ("princess") of the name to her: the fine Japanese-style house of her childhood seemed to him to suit the name (Ch09 P0104), and he later tells the old woman she was "like a fairy-tale princess, as the name says" (Ch11 P0136). The narration and Kusunoki call her ヒメノ; Miyagi calls her ヒメノさん. She calls him クスノキ, plain surname.

**Age and position.** She is Kusunoki's classmate and the same age: ten in the fourth-grade summer, twenty at the reunion. Her family's house stood almost opposite his, near the top of a hill and far from other classmates (Ch01 P0042, P0048). The two families were friendly, and before elementary school each child was left at the other's house when the parents were busy (Ch01 P0042). They first met at four (Ch09 P0141).

**In the story.** She has four functions in the story, and they need to be kept apart:

1. She is a character in the childhood chapter. There she supplies the prophecy and the "promise" that give the novel's present its ten-year horizon and chapter 1 its title, 十年後の約束 (Ch01 P0002, P0070, P0094).
2. In Kusunoki's present she is an absent figure. She is the measure of his failure, the reader he imagines for a farewell letter, and the object of item 8 on his list, "meet Himeno and tell her my feelings" (Ch03 P0093).
3. She is a living adult who meets him twice.
4. She is the author of the letter that gives chapter 10 its title.

**Circumstances at twenty, with sources.**

- *Married young and divorced.* Her own account (Ch10 P0128, P0133) corroborates Miyagi's testimony (Ch03 P0101).
- *A child born at seventeen.* Miyagi's testimony only. Himeno omits it (Ch10 P0133).
- *Relying on her family home.* Miyagi, hedged with みたい at Ch09 P0048. The 姫野 house was shabby, and no one answered on two visits (Ch09 P0104–P0105, P0117).
- *In the area for work, with a car parked at the shrine.* Her account, reported by him as hearsay (らしい, のだそうだ; Ch10 P0006). She evades the question of her job except to say it is "work dealing with people."

**How the history bears on her conduct.** Only her own two statements connect her past to her present. At dinner she dates her arrested development to the summer she moved away and was separated from Kusunoki (Ch10 P0134). In her letter she grounds her hatred in an unanswered call for help (Ch10 P0194). Everything else is analyst inference. The ostracism at school and the pair's isolation make Kusunoki her only interlocutor at ten. That is stated for his side and only "probably" for hers (Ch01 P0040). The pattern makes plausible her claim that he was her "one and only" childhood friend, and the weight she puts on his failure to answer.

## 4. Two absent Himenos: his and Miyagi's

From chapter 2 until the festival night in chapter 9, Himeno is present only in Kusunoki's memories and constructions, in a few traces, and in Miyagi's testimony. His version and Miyagi's both continue after she reappears. Before the real woman can be assessed, those versions have to be set aside.

### 4.1 Kusunoki's Himeno

- **Measure and spur (Ch02 P0015–P0021).** At twenty he judges her prophecy "missed, on my side, so far." He is relieved she cannot see him becoming ordinary, and imagines that her presence would have kept him at his best, since she would laugh at his failures and be vexed by his successes. This tells us how he used her, not how she was.
- **The dream (Ch03 P0006–P0022).** He dreams that she too is still 〔傍点〕売れ残ってる〔/傍点〕 ("*still unsold*") and that she says the two of them are "on track." The dream reverses her Ch01 retraction. It is his construction, and the narration concedes that her face was a blur because he did not know her after ten (P0009). On waking he names his feeling for her, a "very slight goodwill" that may have been the greatest of his life (P0025–P0027; `TDH-C016`).
- **The farewell letter (Ch07 P0004–P0014).** She is the first and only reader he imagines for his 遺書. His summarized trial letter declares lifelong love and is disowned at once. What survives is the wish that "the ten-year-old I" live on in her (P0013; `TDH-C029`).
- **The traces (Ch07 P0058–P0079).** He attributes the 相合傘 to her by elimination, though his memory fails twice that day. He imagines that her missing capsule letter would have named him, with hearts (P0078). The confidence of his attributions rises from one step to the next (`TDH-C031`, `TDH-C032`).
- **The capsule letter (Ch09 P0031–P0035).** His own letter at ten asks his future self, if still unsold, to go and see Himeno, because "Himeno seems to need me, and I seem to need Himeno." It is the boy's view of her need, not hers.
- **Commodity terms (Ch09 P0130).** On Miyagi's testimony he calls her 中古品, "second-hand," and himself 非売品, "not for sale." That is a VALUE_JUDGMENT in the shop's idiom (`TDH-F025`).
- **The retelling (Ch11 P0136–P0148).** He tells the old woman at the closed candy shop that Himeno had a "singular beauty" and was shunned, he thinks, for being "too different" (あまりに異質). Ch01 P0047 said she was disliked for the same reasons he was. He paraphrases the proposal without its retraction, says she "never" wrote to anyone, and names his own process: she had been 神格化, "deified," in his mind (P0139). On these premises he concludes that she dug up the capsule and took her letter, that her letter at seventeen was 〔傍点〕ヒメノなりのＳＯＳ〔/傍点〕 ("*Himeno's own kind of SOS*"), and that she "will soon kill herself" (P0142–P0148; `TDH-C054`, `TDH-C042` v1b, `TDH-C015` v2a).
- **Afterwards.** To the substitute watcher he refers to her, unnamed, as the person who ran off on him (Ch12 P0083). Yet his record lists "the promise I exchanged (交わした) with Himeno" (Ch12 P0054); 交わした is the verb of mutuality Miyagi had used (Ch03 P0101), and Ch01 never showed the proposal accepted. Seeing Enishi with his partner, he imagines himself and Himeno in a happy future in which he need not have died (Ch13 P0150–P0151; `TDH-C065`). He says he stopped painting at seventeen to keep his promise to her to become great (Ch14 P0182), and judges her prophecy "half right" because he came to be glad he had lived (Ch14 P0197–P0201). In Ch15 she is not named. At the reunion, Kusunoki's 「お金持ちか」 and Miyagi's 「ええ。お金持ちです」 ("rich") answer her prophecy's word by lexical echo only (INFERENCE; Ch15 P0106–P0107; `TDH-C071`).

This Himeno is central to the novel's design. But it is a portrait of Kusunoki's attachment, and none of it is evidence of her feelings.

### 4.2 Miyagi's Himeno

On day one Miyagi tells Kusunoki a three-layer account (Ch03 P0101):

- a past: childbirth at seventeen, leaving school, marriage at eighteen, divorce a year later, child-rearing at her parents' home at twenty;
- a scheduled future: a jump "two years from now," leaving a grim farewell note;
- a claim about her memory: she barely remembers him, or 〔傍点〕例の約束〔/傍点〕 ("*that promise*").

Miyagi gives its source as his life's "synopsis" of what "might have happened," which she says was full of Himeno (Ch03 P0108–P0110). By the novel's end the standing of each item differs (`TDH-C015` v2):

| Item | Standing at the end |
|---|---|
| Dropout, marriage, divorce | Corroborated by Himeno (Ch10 P0128, P0133) |
| Childbirth at seventeen; the child | Miyagi only; Himeno omits it. Kusunoki and the narration accept it (Ch04 P0028; Ch09 P0065, P0130) |
| Dependence on her family home | Miyagi only, hedged (Ch09 P0048) |
| "Barely remembers you" | Shown false by strong inference. Her letter speaks of resentment "all along," and at dinner she recalls the umbrella, his paintings and their rivalry (Ch10 P0098, P0121–P0127, P0193) |
| "…or the promise" | Untested; neither raises it at dinner |
| In the original course she would jump before his eyes | Miyagi alone, restated after she has been shown concealing (Ch10 P0184); consistent with the plan Himeno's letter states |
| "Two years later," the grim note | Uncorroborated |
| Her fate in the altered course | Never raised |

Two further facts lower the account's standing. Miyagi's Ch05 narration of his forfeited thirty years names no one, Himeno included, although the synopsis was said to be full of her (`TDH-C021`). And at Ch12 P0193 Miyagi says she lied about 「ヒメノさんのこと」 ("the matter of Himeno") without specifying the statement (`TDH-C050` v1a, `TDH-C059`). Earlier, at the star lake, Kusunoki had named the lie himself as "barely remembers me," and Miyagi had not answered (Ch11 P0197–P0199). Her Himeno testimony therefore stands item by item, with only the corroborated items secure.

## 5. The girl at ten

**Social position.** Himeno and Kusunoki both scored full marks; each kept the other from being truly first, and "on the surface" they were rivals who thought only of getting the upper hand (Ch01 P0037–P0039). The class disliked her for the same reasons it disliked him: she believed herself clever, looked down on others, and did so too openly (P0047). Her bag was thrown in the trash dozens of times (P0057). By Kusunoki's account the two could not laugh along when the class laughed; he assumes, with のだろう, that she felt as he did (P0053). With each other they smile and laugh (P0074, P0092–P0093), so the inability is group-specific, not emotional flatness (U01 §9).

**The classroom.** She is the お利口さん, the "clever one," who answers the teacher's question about the price of a life by citing a book: average lifetime earnings of ¥200–300 million. She answers in polite です/ます (P0009). Half the class looks impressed and half sour; most of them, the narrator says, disliked this pupil (P0010–P0011). The teacher smiles wryly, grants that it is one correct answer, and asks the class to set that way of thinking aside (P0012–P0013). The narrator conceals her identity until P0038, so the reader first meets her as a disliked pupil. Her figure outlasts the lesson. Kusunoki still measures his own deflated worth by it at twenty (Ch02 P0138), and later Miyagi's debt (Ch13 P0006).

**Before the parents.** The two kept a tacit agreement to act like close friends in front of their parents, while kicking shins and pinching thighs under the table. On festival days and at Christmas they went out together so their parents would not worry (P0043, P0048–P0049). Kusunoki adds that perhaps they really were what they acted (P0044). Her part in the performance is reported only as a shared practice; whether she shared his reasons is not given.

**The shrine.** This is the one extended scene of her childhood, and in it she makes most of the moves (`TDH-C007`):

- She stands, brushes off her skirt, and breaks a silence both had kept because their voices would blur (P0059–P0062).
- In 「あの透き通った声」 ("that clear voice of hers") she declares that 私たち, "we," will become very great (P0063–P0064).
- She sets the horizon with graded caution: "not that near, not that far, maybe about ten years" (P0067).
- She fixes the season, a summer, and promises the moment when they will "at last" (ようやく) be glad to have lived (P0070). Her ようやく implies she is not glad to be alive now (INFERENCE; U01 §4.6).
- She scripts their future retrospect: great and rich, they will condemn the school as one that taught them nothing, "not even a bad example" (P0070).
- She supplies the condition of envy: their classmates must have heart attacks from it. She closes with 「わりにあわないもんね」 ("otherwise it wouldn't balance out"), and smiles (P0072–P0074).

Her values here are rank, wealth, the envy of others, and a ledger in which present suffering must be repaid. Her modality runs from assertion through たぶん and きっと to 必要がある ("we need to"). The boy takes the whole as settled prophecy, because she said it (P0075; `TDH-S003`). She also knows the legal age of marriage for girls, sixteen (P0079), and she adopts the frames he introduces: the arithmetic of twenty (P0068) and marriage (P0078).

**The proposal.** The boy says he will never be able to marry, because he hates too many things. She answers 「そっか。じゃあ、私もそうかも」 ("I see. Then maybe me too") and looks down (P0082–P0084). The narrator sees her profile at sunset as "like another person," more grown-up and more easily hurt (P0085–P0086, his perception). Then she makes the proposal (P0087–P0092; `TDH-C005`). She meets his eyes briefly, looks away, and begins in halting plain speech. She calls the prospect of being unpartnered at twenty 情けないことに ("pathetically"). A small cough follows, and then a switch to polite speech: 売れ残り同士、一緒になりませんか？ ("Shall we two leftovers get together?"). The narrator says he knew even then that the change of tone meant she was embarrassed (P0090). The boy answers in the same polite register with a non-committal question (P0091). She laughs it off, 「……冗談だよ。忘れてちょうだい」 ("…It's a joke. Forget it"), and adds that she could hardly be left over (P0092). Nobody accepts. Only the narrator calls it a 約束, a promise (P0094; `TDH-C006`).

Three things are observable about Himeno here.

1. She can name a wish for attachment, but only conditionally, in a deferred future, and in a register that distances it.
2. Exposure is followed at once by retraction and a compensating boast about her own desirability. U01 §9 lists that boast as a counterexample to her pride: it comes straight after looking down, averted eyes and a cough.
3. She keeps the 私たち frame to the end: the proposal speaks of 私たち becoming great (P0087).

Whether she believed the prophecy, or meant the proposal, cannot be decided from her side. Her "joke" stands against his reading of embarrassment (`TDH-S005`, `TDH-S007`).

**Smaller childhood traces.** Each comes with its source:

- *The railing.* He tells of pulling her back from a gap in an observation-deck railing she had tried to climb. He calls her intellectual on the surface but "surprisingly careless," someone he "couldn't leave alone," and says she was unusually kind all that day (Ch10 P0072). It is his anecdote, told while anxious (P0073).
- *The umbrella.* She always had one; he sheltered under hers "reluctantly." This is her recollection, and he assents (Ch10 P0098–P0099).
- *His drawings.* By her own account at twenty, she took them home and gazed at them. She never told him, because she could not concede him a talent beyond study, being someone with "no gift except study" herself (Ch10 P0123–P0125).
- *The 相合傘 and the capsule.* His attribution, and an unexplained absence (Ch07 P0061, P0077).
- *Childhood haunts.* The candy shop she visited with him (Ch11 P0136), and fireworks watched beside her, he "thinks," when very young (Ch05 P0037).

## 6. Seventeen: a letter, a reply, a silence

In the summer they were seventeen, Himeno wrote to Kusunoki first, from her new address (Ch09 P0054). The letter itself is lost to the reader. By his summary it was trivial:

- exam study left no time even to read books;
- she was writing it in several sittings between studying;
- the university she was aiming for;
- she might visit in the winter holidays.

It was written in "the rounded hand a seventeen-year-old girl would use" (P0057–P0058). He found it oddly unlike her, with no sarcasm and no abuse from a girl as twisted as he was. He wondered whether she had changed, or whether in writing she behaved like an ordinary girl (P0059–P0060). That question implies he had no earlier letter of hers to compare (INFERENCE).

Two weeks later he replied in kind: his own exams, his target university, and that he would be glad if she visited. He says he forced himself to put his wish to see her honestly into it (P0061, P0064). She never answered and did not come (P0062–P0063).

**What the episode shows of Himeno (OBSERVATION).** She took the initiative in renewing contact, seven years after the move. She gave him her address. She held out a possible visit and did not make it. She did not answer his reply.

**What is inferred.**

- Kusunoki infers, from Miyagi's testimony, that she was by then already pregnant (Ch09 P0065). He also assumes the child was her future husband's.
- Her letter at twenty names an unanswered 助けの求め. That this was the letter at seventeen is a moderate-to-strong INFERENCE (`TDH-C042` v1a). Its anticipatory clause, 身に覚えがないと言うかもしれませんが ("you may say you don't recall it"), fits an appeal that was never made explicitly (INFERENCE, moderate). A rival reading is that she expected denial.
- Kusunoki's later decoding is emphatic: the letter's content did not matter, only that it was addressed to him and sent by her; she, like him, dug up the past and remembered her 「たった一人の幼馴染」, "one and only childhood friend" (Ch11 P0145–P0148). That phrase is borrowed from her own letter's salutation (OBSERVATION). The narration endorses only that he should have noticed sooner (P0146–P0147). Several weaknesses stand against the decoding:
  - It rests on a new premise, that she never wrote to anyone.
  - It retraces almost word for word his own plan for a disguised farewell letter (Ch07 P0013).
  - It projects onto her his own course in the present summer at twenty: despair, the capsule, a letter.
  - It is unconfirmed by Himeno. Whether it is insight or projection cannot be decided (`TDH-C042` v1b).
- If the inference holds, Himeno experienced a reply that matched her surface and missed her need as a failure to answer. She did not write again. The letter's content (a university, a visit) also stands against her later course (dropout, marriage). Either her plans changed, or the content was a front (INFERENCE; both open).

## 7. The adult at twenty

### 7.1 The encounter (Ch09 P0136–P0148; Ch10 P0004–P0009)

A figure climbs the shrine steps where the prophecy was spoken. Kusunoki recognizes her at once. She looks at him with what he calls 「うつろな目」 ("hollow eyes"), then with what seems astonishment, and says 「……クスノキ？」 in the same clear voice (P0145–P0148). She recognizes him, though only after he says her name; that recognition is at most partial counterevidence to "barely remembers" (`TDH-C015` v1c). He remembers almost nothing of what they said, or what she wore (Ch10 P0004). She was not there for the festival. By her account, which he reports as hearsay, she was in the area for work and had parked at the shrine (Ch10 P0006). She evades the question of her job: "work dealing with people." She signals politely that she wants to leave, because she must be up early. She agrees to meet again, but for a meal rather than drinks (「アルコールは駄目だけど」, "alcohol's no good"; P0007–P0008). No reason for the abstention is given.

### 7.2 The dinner (Ch10 P0080–P0154)

Her conduct, in order:

- **Arrival.** She arrives twenty-seven minutes late, blaming a bus delayed by rain. She apologizes and offers to treat him (P0085, P0090).
- **Banter.** When he declines her treat as a loan, since he did the inviting this time, she catches the implication of a "next time" with 「ふうん」 and a prim look, and looks him over (P0093). His frank answer gets 「正直でよろしい」 ("Honest. Good"), with a giggle (P0095). He hears the girl he knew: sarcastic, but with a warmth in how she spoke (P0096).
- **The umbrella.** She takes his umbrella and holds it between them, recalling that he always forgot his and sheltered under hers. He takes it back and holds it toward her (P0097–P0099).
- **The walk.** She asks what he was doing at the shrine. He says he was looking for her, which is true. She calls him 「うそつき」 ("liar") and bumps his shoulder (P0102–P0104).
- **Steering.** At the restaurant she insists he tell his ten years first (「まずはクスノキから」; P0118).
- **His paintings.** She says she loved them and envied him (P0121–P0125). He tells her he has not held a brush since the winter at seventeen. His record later says he let himself paint at nineteen and lost the ability (Ch14 P0186–P0187), and he had told Naruse he stopped after entering university (Ch06 P0074). U14 leaves the conflict unresolved (rivals: concealment of the failure, simplification, a brief attempt discounted, the narrator's later framing). On most of these she was answering a partial account.
- **Her ten years.** She raises her dropout herself, expecting it was reunion gossip, and smiles with lowered brows when he feigns surprise (P0128–P0130). Her account, which he gives in quoted gist, is that she married a senior who had graduated, left school "carried away," and divorced through "a misunderstanding." It omits the childbirth Miyagi described (P0133).
- **Self-diagnosis.** She says she was a child who could not take things as they are and move on. She cannot bear the slightest imperfection, and ruins things at the root. Her head has not changed since she moved away at ten and was separated from Kusunoki. Early cleverness bred the arrogance of thinking she need not grow, so she is still "a ten-year-old girl who dreams" while everyone else changes (P0134). The narrator gives her "the eyes of a hurt girl" (P0135, his reading). She asks whether he has changed too (P0136).
- **His confession.** He loses composure and tells her everything about the sale and the watcher, in "a tone to draw sympathy," and points to Miyagi, whom she cannot see (P0137–P0146). She first asks, apologetically, whether he realizes how unreal it sounds. Then she says she cannot think it a lie: after so long an acquaintance she would know at once if he were lying or deceiving her, and she can believe he has sold his lifespan (P0147–P0149).
- **Exit.** She says she too has been hiding something, coughs into a handkerchief, rises, and suggests continuing "after the meal." She walks toward the restroom and 〔傍点〕二度と戻ってこなかった〔/傍点〕 ("*never came back*"; P0151–P0154). Per U10's adopted rule, no illness is to be inferred from the cough.

**What can and cannot be concluded.** Her letter reveals a plan to make herself indispensable to him first (P0195). Every warm act at the dinner is therefore undecidable between sincerity and strategy (`TDH-S054`; U10 §4.9): the banter, the umbrella, the admiration, the self-disclosure, the professed belief. It does not follow that they were false. Some of her lines refer to a shared past only she could supply: the umbrella, the drawings taken home. The self-diagnosis is consistent with the grievance the letter discloses (INFERENCE). Her professed belief stands against her letter: there she calls him 「おかしく」 ("strange, deranged") and hopes "only" that his short life is true (P0196, P0200). U10 keeps a rival that reconciles the two: she thought him sincere but deluded.

**Analyst observations on the exit** (with their limits):

- (a) The letter was written during the dinner, on a page torn from a pocket notebook (手帳を破り取って書いたらしい, P0175, his reading), and it refers to 「さっきの話」, "what you said just now" (P0200). So the decision it records was made within minutes of his confession (INFERENCE, strong).
- (b) The announced secret corresponds to what the letter discloses (INFERENCE, moderate). A rival is that she meant something else and changed course.
- (c) A small cough also preceded her proposal at ten (Ch01 P0088). This is a lexical recurrence at two moments of disclosure, noted as OBSERVATION only.

### 7.3 Her letter (Ch10 P0190–P0200; testimony)

The salutation is the chapter title, 「私の、たった一人の幼馴染へ」. The letter is in 私 and polite です/ます, with lines set like verse. In gist (`TDH-C048`):

- She had meant to die in front of him: at 「あの展望台」 ("that observation deck") she would have had him wait below and fallen right beside him.
- She had resented him "all along," whatever he might say about not recalling it.
- She hated him for failing to answer her call for help and then turning up now as if nothing had happened (今更のうのうと).
- So she meant to die once she had become 欠かせない存在, "indispensable," to him.
- But he seems to have become 「私よりもずっと、おかしく」, "far stranger than me," in these ten years. Revenge on him as he is now seems pointless.
- She will vanish from his life without a word, and says goodbye.
- She hopes that "what you said just now" is true at least in one respect: that he has little time left.

**Reading the letter.**

- **Spite.** She casts her own death three times in the malefactive 〜てやる: 死んでやる, 落っこちてやる, 死んでやろう (the language specialist's point, U10 §4.12). The death is aimed at him as a punishment he must witness. The unattributed lines that follow Miyagi's confirmation and precede the letter's text, most naturally his gloss, put it as making him watch and settling a long grudge (P0185–P0186).
- **The logic of the ledger.** An unanswered plea is a debt, to be repaid by a loss he must witness. That matches the ten-year-old's "otherwise it wouldn't balance out" (Ch01 P0074; INFERENCE, moderate). In childhood the repayment was to come from the classmates' envy. In the letter he is the one who owes.
- **The deck.** If 「あの展望台」 is the deck of the railing memory (strong INFERENCE; U10 §4.3), her plan inverts that scene. As a child she nearly fell and he pulled her back. In the plan she falls and he stands below, unable to stop it (INFERENCE, conditional). He draws no such connection.
- **Self-implication.** 「私よりも」 ("than me") counts herself among the おかしい, the "strange." It concedes, at least comparatively, the disorder she described at dinner (OBSERVATION of the comparative; INFERENCE on its weight).
- **Address.** 「私の、たった一人の幼馴染へ」 is warm in form: possessive, exclusive, and unhedged, against the narrator's fourfold-hedged 「いわゆる『幼馴染』」 in chapter 1 (`TDH-F006`, `TDH-F028`). Whether it is sincere, ironic or both cannot be decided. Either way, the grievance presupposes the bond: only "the one and only" could owe her an answer.
- **Abandonment.** She does not forgive him. She judges revenge pointless against a man already "stranger" than she, and withdraws in silence. The last line is a barb, not goodwill (U10's adopted correction).
- **Timing.** "Hated" (憎くてたまりませんでした) is tied to his reappearance, and the plan follows with 「だから」. So the plan as stated responds to his turning up (INFERENCE, moderate). Miyagi's original-course testimony implies that a reunion, and the plan, belonged to that course too. When the plan formed is not stated.

Kusunoki shows no anger. He calls himself a fool for leaving his solitary way, folds her letter into a paper plane and sends it into the river, and gives the envelope of money meant for her to strangers (Ch10 P0202–P0212; `TDH-C049`).

## 8. Personality and psychology

**Values.** At ten her values are rank (偉くなる), wealth (お金持ち), the envy of those who scorned her, and a balanced ledger (わりにあわない) (Ch01 P0070–P0074). Judgment matters to her: the school is to be condemned in hindsight. Her adult self-account values growth and acceptance, which she says she lacks (Ch10 P0134). Her letter values answerability: a plea creates an obligation, and failing it earns punishment (INFERENCE from Ch10 P0194–P0195). Her admiration of beauty made without effort, his drawings, comes through as envy (Ch10 P0123).

**Goals.** At ten she wanted future vindication, shared with Kusunoki (私たち). The conditional proposal points to a fallback partnership at twenty. At seventeen her goal is unknown: on Kusunoki's reading she sought help, which is unconfirmed. At twenty she pursued revenge by becoming indispensable and then dying before him, and she abandoned it. She stated no other goal.

**Fears and vulnerabilities** (INFERENCE unless noted):

- At ten she is not glad to be alive (ようやく, Ch01 P0070). She is humiliated (the binned bag), and she is exposed when she asks for attachment (the proposal's markers; `TDH-C005`).
- At twenty she names her own vulnerability: intolerance of imperfection, arrested growth, arrogance (Ch10 P0134; self-report).
- Miyagi's testimony that in the original course she would kill herself is the gravest vulnerability ascribed to her. It is testimony about a counterfactual.

**Contradictions.**

- She is a rival who, by his account, was always in his company, and for whom he was "probably" the only person who understood her (Ch01 P0039–P0041); that she needed him is the boy's view (Ch09 P0034).
- She is contemptuous of others yet ashamed to be left over (P0047, P0087–P0092).
- She proposes and retracts.
- She shows warmth at dinner while planning revenge.
- She professes belief and privately judges.
- She says she cannot bear imperfection, yet she drops a planned revenge when circumstances make it pointless. When the plan formed is not stated; as written it answers his reappearance (§7.3), while the resentment behind it is "all along". The abandonment is an adaptation her self-diagnosis would not predict, and a meaningful exception, though its weight depends on how settled the plan was.

**Coping.**

- At ten: imagining a future of vindication; contempt for the group; staying home; performing friendship for parents; humor as retreat ("a joke," "I could hardly be left over").
- At seventeen: by her own initiative, writing.
- At twenty, by her own account: impetuous moves (marriage, dropout "carried away"). By her letter: a plan of retaliation, then silent withdrawal.

**Self-conception.** At ten she is exceptional and destined (「私が売れ残るはずがない」, "I could never be left over"; 私たち…偉くなる, "we… will become great"). At twenty she calls herself a girl with "no gift except study" (Ch10 P0125), a child who never grew past ten (P0134), and, comparatively, おかしい (P0196). Between the two self-images lies the whole of her decade, and the novel never narrates it.

## 9. Cognition and decision-making

**What she notices.** She took in his drawings closely enough to take them home and gaze at them (Ch10 P0125). She remembers his habits, such as the umbrella (P0098). She claims she can tell at once when he lies (P0149). She registers implication: the "next time" in his offer (P0093), and the oddity of his story (P0147). At ten she knows a legal fact (P0079) and cites a book in class (Ch01 P0009).

**How she reasons.** She thinks in frames and verdicts. At ten she reframes their misery as a debt the future will pay (Ch01 P0070–P0074). The boy found her view that a school could be judged bad strikingly new (P0071). She reasons in terms of exchange: the classroom price of a life, わりにあわない, the "unsold" pair. Her revenge plan is strategic and long in horizon: first become indispensable, then act (Ch10 P0195). It is also contingent. When she judges its target already broken, she abandons it (P0196–P0197). That shows reassessment under new information, not mere impulse.

**Under uncertainty.** She hedges and then hardens: たぶん → きっと → 必要がある (Ch01 P0067–P0072). Faced with Kusunoki's incredible story, she first names its implausibility gently (気を悪くしないでほしいんだけどさ, "don't take this the wrong way, but"). She then professes belief on the strength of their long acquaintance (P0147–P0149). Whatever she concludes, she acts by leaving and settles the matter in writing. She does not argue it out.

**Conflicting obligations.** None is depicted from her side. A possible tension is that at dinner she was at once a polite companion and an avenger. The text never shows her weighing the two.

## 10. Behavioral patterns

- **Ordinary conduct.** She is proud and openly superior at school (Ch01 P0047). She is lively with him in private (Ch01 P0062–P0092), and banters and teases at twenty (Ch10 P0093–P0104). She takes small initiatives: breaking silences, proposing, taking the umbrella, steering the conversation, writing first at seventeen.
- **Cooperation.** At ten she joins the tacit performance for parents (Ch01 P0043, P0049). At twenty she accepts a dinner, arrives late with an apology and an offer to pay, and engages with his account.
- **Conflict.** As children: covert, physical, rivalrous (shins kicked, thighs pinched; P0043). As an adult: indirect. She does not confront him at dinner. Her hostility is planned and concealed, then stated in writing after she has left.
- **Stress.** In the shrine scene: silence, then a declaration (P0059–P0063); embarrassment, then retraction (P0084–P0092). Her stress at the dinner is not narrated. The "hurt girl" eyes are his reading (Ch10 P0135).
- **Mistakes and corrections.** She retracts the proposal (Ch01 P0092). She abandons the revenge plan (Ch10 P0196–P0198). The narration records no repair she made toward Kusunoki after seventeen. Her not answering his reply is, from his side, a silence. From hers, if the inference holds, his reply was the failure.
- **Exits.** Her departures are not narrated as her choices except the last. The move at ten was a parent's job transfer (Ch11 P0139). At seventeen she withdrew by silence. At twenty she chose to vanish without a word (黙って…消えます, Ch10 P0198), communicating through a third party, the waiter.

## 11. Speech and communication

**Medium.** All of her speech is written dialogue inside Kusunoki's record. There is no voice, only Japanese register as rendered. The narration twice calls her voice 透き通った, "clear" (Ch01 P0064; Ch09 P0148). That is his description, and in Ch10 P0248 the same word passes to Miyagi's smell (`TDH-F028`).

**Registers and their shifts:**

| Context | Register | Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Classroom, age ten | Polite です/ます, citing a book | Ch01 P0009 |
| Private with him, age ten | Plain, declarative, soft endings (〜んだよ, 〜の, 〜もんね, 〜ね); 私 for herself; 私たち for the pair | Ch01 P0063, P0070, P0074 |
| The proposal | Halting plain speech, then a sudden switch to polite 〜ませんか, then plain 忘れてちょうだい | Ch01 P0087–P0092 (`TDH-F004`) |
| Adult with him | Casual, quick and teasing; クスノキ and 君 for him; mock-teacherly 正直でよろしい; ふうん; なるほど | Ch10 P0090–P0149 |
| Letter at twenty | Written polite 私/あなた, verse-like lines, malefactive てやる, colloquial 落っこちてやる inside polite frames; 言う in kanji where the narration writes いう | Ch10 P0190–P0200 |

**Pattern** (OBSERVATION with HYPOTHESIS). Her two most consequential addresses to him, the proposal at ten and the letter at twenty, are in polite register. Her ordinary talk with him is plain. Polite register may work for her as distance at moments of exposure. The rival is strong for the letter, since written letters conventionally take です/ます. The proposal's switch, by contrast, is marked in the text as embarrassment (Ch01 P0090).

**Address.** At ten their dialogue uses no names or second-person pronouns at all. It runs on 私たち, 俺たち and お互い (U01 §5). At twenty she uses クスノキ and 君. In the letter he is あなた. The adult never says 私たち: Ch01 has it seven times across four of her utterances (P0063, P0070, P0072, P0087), Ch10 not once (OBSERVATION). The shift from a shared "we" at ten to 私 against あなた in the letter is the plainest grammatical trace of how her relation to him changed (INFERENCE).

**Vocabulary and rhetorical habits.**

- *A market idiom for persons.* 生涯賃金 ("lifetime earnings"), お金持ち ("rich"), わりにあわない ("doesn't balance out"), 売れ残り ("left over, unsold"). Kusunoki takes up her terms (〝売れ残っている〟, Ch01 P0095; `TDH-F005`).
- *Prophetic declaratives and scripted future speech* (Ch01 P0070).
- *Graded hedges* (そんなに近い話じゃない…たぶん, "not that near… maybe").
- *Self-cover.* 冗談だよ, いってみただけ, and the boast 私が売れ残るはずがないからね.
- *Teasing reversal.* うそつき in answer to the truth.
- *Apologetic preambles before frank content.* 気を悪くしないでほしいんだけどさ; 後出しになって悪いんだけど ("sorry to bring this up after the fact, but").
- *そっか* ("I see"), a recurrent acknowledgement: Ch01 P0083; Ch10 P0121, P0130, P0132. The dream-Himeno also says it (Ch03 P0019), but that line is Kusunoki's construction.

**Humor.** Dry and competitive at ten: the envy heart attacks, answered by his escalation. Teasing and evaluative at twenty. In the letter, humor turns into a barb (P0200).

**Silence and what goes unsaid.** At ten she keeps silent with him rather than let her voice blur (Ch01 P0059). At dinner she omits the child, if there is one. She never raises the promise, and neither does he. She evades the question of her work. She writes the decisive truth only after leaving.

**Kusunoki on her speech.** 皮肉屋ではあるものの、どこか温かみのある喋り方, "sarcastic, but with a certain warmth in how she spoke" (Ch10 P0096). He applies it, silently, to both the girl and the woman. At the moment he thought it, the woman was concealing her plan.

**Interior against outward speech.** Her interior is never narrated. The only interior text is the letter, and it reveals a gap between what she showed at dinner and what she intended. How wide that gap was, and whether her warmth was partly real, cannot be measured.

## 12. Relationships

**Toward Kusunoki** (Himeno → Kusunoki, as recorded). The relationship moves through four stages.

- **At ten.** She is a rival for first place and, by his account, "probably" found him, as he found her, the only person who understood her without misunderstanding (Ch01 P0040). She includes him in every future she imagines (私たち). She offers him a conditional fallback partnership and retracts it. If the 相合傘 is hers, she paired their names in secret (his inference). By her own later account she admired his talent in secret and took his drawings home. She was unusually kind to him for a day after he saved her (his anecdote).
- **At seventeen.** She writes first, and receives a reply that by inference did not answer what she asked. She falls silent.
- **At twenty, at dinner.** She is warm, teasing and curious about his decade. She admires his art, and confides an account of herself that leaves things out. She professes belief in his story and then leaves. All of this is undecidable between sincerity and strategy.
- **At twenty, in the letter.** She has resented him all along. She planned to punish him through her own death. She abandons the plan because he seems already broken. She still addresses him as her "one and only childhood friend."

**Asymmetry.** What she knew of his side was limited. She did not know that he kept the proposal as a promise and refused others for ten years (Ch01 P0094–P0096; Ch04 P0028), that his capsule letter asked him to go and see her, that he meant to give her his ¥300,000 "in thanks for giving me my life" (Ch09 P0039), or that "tell her my feelings" was on his list. What she knew of him at dinner was a decline, a story of selling his life, and an invisible watcher. For his part, he did not know of her admiration, her grievance, or her plan. Each was shaping the evening on a picture of the other the other did not share (INFERENCE). Her attitude toward him after the letter is unknown.

**Toward classmates.** At ten, mutual contempt and exclusion (Ch01 P0047, P0050). She wanted them to have heart attacks from envy (P0072).

**Toward parents and family.** At ten she kept up the friendship performance for both families (P0043, P0049). Her family moved for a parent's job (Ch11 P0139). At twenty, by Miyagi's hedged testimony, she relies on her family home (Ch09 P0048). No family member appears.

**Toward the teacher.** Only the classroom exchange: her answer, met with a wry smile and a request to think otherwise (Ch01 P0012–P0013).

**Toward her husband and child.** Her husband was "a senior who had graduated." She married him, left school "carried away," and divorced after "a misunderstanding" (her gist, Ch10 P0133). The child is Miyagi's testimony only. The novel shows nothing of her as a wife or a mother.

**Toward Miyagi.** She cannot perceive her (Ch10 P0146; watcher invisibility, `TDH-C024`). Through Kusunoki she knows only that a "watcher girl" is said to be present. Miyagi, for her part:

- knew Himeno's original course, by her own account;
- discouraged his attempt to meet her (Ch03 P0095, P0112; Ch07 P0041);
- frames Himeno as his "once-important person," like her own (Ch09 P0088);
- notes his fixation on Himeno (Ch08 P0044; Ch10 P0029);
- at the rehearsal predicts that Himeno would laugh off a courtship line (Ch10 P0062);
- hid the plan "for my sake," as the narrator attributes it (Ch10 P0172);
- later compares her own childhood friend to "what Himeno is to you" (Ch11 P0203).

None of this is evidence of Himeno's feelings. The readings hold any identification of Miyagi with Himeno as a non-claim (U13 §13.2).

**Others.**

- The waiter carries her letter (Ch10 P0174).
- The old woman at the candy shop "seems" to react a little to Himeno's name (Ch11 P0137). That is Kusunoki's perception, and whether she remembers either of them cannot be decided.

## 13. Preferences and ordinary life

The supported preferences and habits are few, and each is situated:

- *His drawings.* She admired them and took them home (Ch10 P0121–P0125; her testimony). This is a stable admiration by her account, concealed out of rivalry.
- *Umbrellas.* She carried one; he did not (Ch10 P0098). A childhood habit, by her recollection.
- *Books.* At ten she cites a book (Ch01 P0009). At seventeen she complains that exams leave no time "even to read" (Ch09 P0057), which presupposes reading as a habit (INFERENCE, weak).
- *Alcohol.* She declines it at twenty, "no good" (Ch10 P0008). The reason is unstated: preference, health, work or circumstance cannot be distinguished.
- *Home and outings.* At ten she stays home, visits Kusunoki only when bored (as he tells it), and goes out on festival days and at Christmas for the parents' sake (Ch01 P0048–P0049). The two also played often in the wood and at the bus (Ch07 P0054–P0057) and at the candy shop (Ch11 P0136).
- *At twenty.* A car and work in the area (Ch10 P0006); the early start next day (P0007).

Food, clothing, leisure, music and daily routine are unknown for both ages. Kusunoki cannot even recall what she wore at the shrine (Ch10 P0004).

## 14. Romantic and intimate psychology

At ten she is a minor, and the source depicts only this:

- a conditional, deferred proposal of marriage between "leftovers," marked by embarrassment and retracted as a joke (Ch01 P0087–P0092);
- if Kusunoki's attribution holds, a 相合傘 pairing their names (Ch07 P0061). In his imagination, not in evidence, she would have named him in the capsule with hearts (Ch07 P0078).

Whether she was attracted to him is not stated by her.

At seventeen to twenty, by Miyagi's testimony, she became pregnant and had a child. By her own account she married a senior and divorced. Nothing of her attachments in those years is shown.

At twenty, toward Kusunoki, her banter can read as flirtation (the "next time," 正直でよろしい, the shared umbrella). He believes his goodwill was returned (Ch10 P0106–P0108). Her letter shows that becoming "indispensable" to him was instrumental to revenge. Any romantic reading of her conduct at the dinner is therefore undecidable, and none is adopted here. Her intimate preferences are unknown.

## 15. Capabilities and limitations

- **Academic ability.** At ten she has perfect test scores, equal to his (Ch01 P0037). She calls herself someone with "no gift except study" (Ch10 P0125). She did not finish high school (Ch10 P0128).
- **Verbal and social skill.** She is articulate and frames situations for others at ten (`TDH-C007`). At twenty she manages a dinner conversation with ease and steers it.
- **Strategic patience.** She planned a revenge with a long horizon, one that depended on first becoming indispensable to him (Ch10 P0195). That is a capacity her letter claims, never shown being carried out.
- **Physical.** Only the railing climb (his anecdote), and a car at twenty, from which driving is a natural inference, not stated.
- **Capacity and willingness.** Her letter separates them. She judged revenge possible in principle and declined it as "pointless" (Ch10 P0197). Withdrawal is her choice. It is not presented as inability.

## 16. Longitudinal development

Himeno's development cannot be followed continuously. Ten years fall between her two narrated periods, and only one letter at seventeen and Miyagi's testimony fill the gap.

**What changed** (OBSERVATION and INFERENCE):

- **Orientation to Kusunoki.** At ten he is the partner in a shared future (私たち); at twenty, in her letter, he is the target of a grievance (私 against あなた). The cause she gives is the unanswered call for help, so the change has a stated reason. That this was the letter at seventeen is inference.
- **Fortune.** From top scorer, with a fine house and a prophecy of greatness and wealth, to a high-school dropout, divorced, possibly a mother, whose family house is shabby. The decline is in her circumstances (corroborated in part). That it is a decline in self-regard is supported by her own words: no gift but study, arrogance, おかしい.
- **Self-understanding.** At ten she claims a destiny. At twenty she offers a diagnosis of herself. Whether the diagnosis is sincere self-knowledge or part of a performance at dinner is undecidable. Her letter's 「私よりも」 suggests the self-assessment was not wholly performed.

**What persisted.** She says it herself: "my head hasn't changed since ten" (Ch10 P0134). The textual continuities support her in part:

- the ledger logic of repayment, from わりにあわない to revenge;
- pride and self-cover;
- framing others' situations;
- the clear voice and the quick, dry, competitive talk, both as Kusunoki hears them.

The adult who abandons a plan on reassessment is not the rigid figure her self-diagnosis describes.

**Late revelation versus development.** Two things were revealed late but belong to her childhood: her secret admiration of his drawings, and, if the attribution holds, the 相合傘. They are not development. They correct his childhood picture of a pure rival. Her resentment is, on her own causal account, a development: she grounds it in an unanswered plea, which she does not date.

**Within the dinner.** The one change narrated in real time is the abandonment of her plan. On strong inference it happened within minutes of his confession (§7.2).

## 17. Conditional behavioral profile

This compact, literary profile is drawn from the readings. It does not claim that a reconstruction model exists or has been tested, and it assigns no capability grade. Its validity is confined to three periods:

- **V10.** Age ten: chapter 1 and memories of childhood.
- **V17.** The letter at seventeen, known only through Kusunoki's summary.
- **V20.** The festival night and the dinner day at twenty.

Outside these periods it should abstain.

| V10, alone with Kusunoki after humiliation | Breaks the silence with a confident frame about a shared future (私たち), an evaluative verdict on others, and a demand for repayment; speaks plainly and declaratively | Shared injury; the festival crowd below; his receptiveness | She follows his frames when he supplies them (the age of twenty, marriage) | Moderate (one extended scene plus summary) |
| V10, voicing a wish for attachment | Conditional, deferred phrasing; looking down, averted eyes, a cough; a switch to polite register; then retraction as a joke with a boast | Talk that touches her own prospects (「私もそうかも」) | His mirrored non-answer gives no opening; acceptance is untested | Moderate (one instance, strongly marked) |

**Abstention zones.** The profile should not be used, and the source gives no basis for answers, in these areas:

- Her conduct as a mother, a wife, at work, or with anyone other than Kusunoki after ten.
- Her life between ten and twenty beyond the summarized facts.
- Whether she would have carried out the original-course suicide, or will die in the altered course. Kusunoki's "soon" is his belief.
- Her response to learning of his death, or to anything after the dinner.
- Her view of the childhood "promise."
- Her feelings toward Miyagi, whom she cannot see.
- Her inner state at any moment. The letter is testimony about past intention, not narrated interiority.
- Her humor, speech or choices outside the few situations shown.
- Any romantic reading of the dinner.

A novel situation resembling the shrine or the dinner may be approached by analogy, with the support levels above. Anything else should be marked as extrapolation.

## 18. Interpretive uncertainty

**Rival explanations.**

- **The dinner.** Sincere warmth with a plan in reserve, or performance in service of the plan, or a mixture that shifted as the evening went on. Undecidable (`TDH-S054`).
- **Her professed belief.** Strategic accommodation, belief in his sincerity but not his story, or a moment of belief overturned on reflection. Her letter's 「おかしく」 and 「本当であることを願います」 ("I hope it is true") cut against literal belief (U10 §4.9).
- **The call for help.** Her letter at seventeen (moderate to strong), another unnarrated appeal, or her sense of the whole relationship. A further rival, with no textual support, is that she never received his reply.
- **The missing capsule letter.**
  - She was absent that day: his first guess (Ch07 P0077).
  - She never wrote one, because she transferred in the same summer.
  - She removed it herself: his adopted theory (Ch11 P0142), which rests on his characterization of the teacher.
  - Someone else removed it.
  None is established (`TDH-C032` v1a).
- **The 相合傘.** Drawn by her, by a third party in jest, or by him and forgotten (`TDH-C031`). If hers, it shows a child's pairing, not adult memory.
- **The omission of the child.** Privacy, shame, strategy, or Miyagi's testimony is false. Miyagi has admitted an unspecified Himeno lie, so even the child's existence is not secure.
- **Her self-diagnosis.** Candid, performed, or both. Her letter's 「私よりも」 suggests a candid core (INFERENCE, low to moderate).

**Counterexamples to a simple portrait.**

- **Against "pure rival":** the secret admiration, the 相合傘 (if hers), the proposal, and her day of kindness after the railing.
- **Against "the faithful childhood sweetheart"** (his dream): her retraction, her early marriage, and her letter.
- **Against "rigid, unable to accept imperfection":** her abandonment of a stated revenge plan on reassessment (when the plan formed is not stated; the grievance behind it she calls lifelong).
- **Against "she forgot him"** (Miyagi): her memories and her grievance.

**Unresolved contradictions.**

- Her account and Miyagi's differ in order (her marriage before her dropout; Miyagi's childbirth, dropout, marriage) and in content (the child). The narrator's own list at Ch04 P0028 gives a third order (pregnancy, marriage, childbirth, divorce), a hardened retelling of Miyagi's report (U04).
- The exam-study letter at seventeen sits awkwardly with childbirth at seventeen (`TDH-C015` v1c).
- Miyagi's Ch05 account of his forfeited course omits Himeno, while her Ch03 and Ch10 accounts center Himeno (`TDH-C021`).
- Kusunoki's retelling (shunned for being "too different") contradicts his own earlier narration (Ch01 P0047); his premise that she "never wrote" is new, absent from his earlier account, where only the letter's tone seemed odd (Ch09 P0055–P0060; U11 §4.12).

**Composition and filtering.** The whole portrait passes through a narrator who loved, idealized and finally decoded her. He also writes his record after her letter (`TDH-C010`). Chapter 1's touches of her vulnerability (P0086) and of his insight into her embarrassment "even then" (P0090) cannot be checked for retrospective coloring. His Ch11 decoding of her is the most elaborated account of her motives in the novel, and it is his inference, emphasized by the narration's sesame dots (`TDH-F029`), not confirmed by her.

**Known evidence gaps.** The three largest:

1. **Her decade between ten and twenty.** Pregnancy, marriage, divorce and the circumstances of the letter at seventeen are known only as summary facts from two sources that disagree in part. One is Miyagi, who later admits lying about "Himeno" without saying what. The other is Himeno herself, at a dinner where her purpose was concealed. Her inner life in those years, and the "call for help," are never shown.
2. **Her motives at twenty.** The only direct access is her letter, written, by strong inference, within minutes of his confession (§7.2). It states a plan and its abandonment, but it does not settle whether her warmth at dinner was real, whether she believed him, or whether she remembered the "promise."
3. **Her fate.** Miyagi's original-course forecast (a suicide two years on, before his eyes) is uncorroborated except as a plan Himeno says she abandoned. Kusunoki's "she will soon kill herself" is his belief. Himeno is absent from chapters 12–15 except as memory, and the novel ends without addressing her (the readings' forecasts that her fate would be addressed, F-U14-f and F-U05-C5, were not met).

**Limits of extrapolation.** In her own person Himeno is a character of two concentrated sequences, one classroom answer and one document. A fuller portrait would have to invent her decade, her motherhood, her work and her interior, and this monograph declines to.
