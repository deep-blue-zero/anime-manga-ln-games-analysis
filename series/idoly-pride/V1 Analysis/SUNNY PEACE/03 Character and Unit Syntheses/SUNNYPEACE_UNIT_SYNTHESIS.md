# SUNNY PEACE: Member Personality, Voice, Characterization, and Visual Reference

## IDOLY PRIDE Comparative Analysis Document

## Purpose of this document

This document provides a comprehensive reference for the five members of **SUNNY PEACE** from *IDOLY PRIDE*: **Kawasaki Sakura**, **Ichinose Rei**, **Saeki Haruko**, **Shiraishi Chisa**, and **Hyōdō Shizuku**.

It is intended to function as an authoritative source for future comparative analysis of character design, personality, voice, performance identity, narrative role, visual storytelling, unit philosophy, and interpersonal dynamics.

The focus is not merely “what each girl is like.” The goal is to define how each member contributes a distinct form of light to SUNNY PEACE, how the unit’s shared philosophy depends on those differences, and how the group matures from a bright Hoshimi unit into one of *IDOLY PRIDE*’s clearest answers to the problem of inheritance after miracle.

SUNNY PEACE can be summarized as:

> **A unit of girls who could not shine alone, but learn to shine by becoming one another’s proof.**

Their collective image is bright, cheerful, accessible, and life-affirming, but the group is not shallowly optimistic. SUNNY PEACE is built from illness, failure, loneliness, delayed youth, fear, admiration, and the need to be supported. Their smiles matter because they are not effortless. They are chosen, practiced, protected, and returned to fans as warmth.

At the broadest level:

-   **Sakura** is life overflowing outward.

-   **Rei** is discipline becoming grace.

-   **Haruko** is warmth that waited too long to bloom.

-   **Chisa** is small courage made visible.

-   **Shizuku** is fandom, memory, and admiration returned from the stage.


Together, they form a unit whose idolhood is not based on overwhelming individual talent, elite perfection, ideological aggression, or mythic destiny, but on **reciprocal emotional support**: each girl is able to stand because the others help her stand, and each girl’s weakness becomes part of the group’s communicable light.

# I. SUNNY PEACE as a unit

## 1\. Core unit thesis

SUNNY PEACE is the “sun” unit of Hoshimi Production, but their sunlight should not be mistaken for simple cheerfulness. Their brightness is not the brightness of girls who have never suffered. It is the brightness of girls who have suffered, been delayed, frightened, overlooked, made dependent, or pushed into self-erasure — and who gradually learn to turn those experiences into warmth for others.

Their cleanest unit thesis is:

> **SUNNY PEACE are girls who were supported into standing, and who become idols by returning that support as shared warmth.**

A second formulation:

> **SUNNY PEACE is not the unit of effortless smiles. It is the unit that proves a smile can be survival, gratitude, courage, care, memory, and chosen performance.**

The group’s emotional grammar is:

> **I was supported, so now I want to support someone else.**

This is true for all five members.

Sakura was supported through illness, family love, Mana’s inherited miracle, her friend Tomo, Makino, Hoshimi, and the ordinary world she was almost denied. Her smile becomes gratitude made performative.

Rei was supported by the rediscovery of dance as joy rather than proof, by people who recognized her dream outside her father’s expectations, and by a unit that taught her disciplined movement could carry happiness instead of only self-justification.

Haruko was supported by an idol dream that waited for her across time, by Mana’s friendship, by Makino’s recognition, and by SUNNY PEACE as the place where delayed blooming became possible.

Chisa was supported by Saki, by the group, by fans, and by small acts of encouragement. Her fear becomes empathy, and her dependence becomes a basis for supporting others.

Shizuku was supported by idols from the audience side. Her fandom becomes a bridge between fan and performer, and her quiet attention becomes one of SUNNY PEACE’s mature creative powers.

The unit’s name is therefore almost literal. “SUNNY” is not only Sakura’s sun. “PEACE” is not only gentleness. The group’s peace is an emotionally survivable space — a place where girls who might otherwise have withdrawn, overtrained, hidden, delayed themselves, or remained observers can stand together and shine.

## 2\. From formation unit to mature working unit

SUNNY PEACE begins as a unit associated with warmth, emotional support, and Sakura’s central sunlight. But later event material deepens that identity considerably. The mature SUNNY PEACE is not simply “Sakura plus four girls who support her.” It is a five-member organism that has to learn how to carry sunlight collectively.

The group’s growth can be mapped in three broad stages.

First, **formation and recovery**. Each member brings a vulnerability into the unit: Sakura’s illness and Mana inheritance, Rei’s proof-driven dance wound, Haruko’s delayed idolhood, Chisa’s fear and dependence, and Shizuku’s fan-side quietness. SUNNY PEACE becomes the place where those vulnerabilities do not disqualify them.

Second, **fan-facing reciprocity**. As the group becomes more prominent, they are no longer only girls who need support. They become idols whose work is to receive fans’ letters, wishes, dreams, gratitude, and encouragement, then return those feelings through touring, songs, special projects, lives, and direct contact. Their mature keyword becomes **繋ぐ / tsunagu**, “to connect.”

Third, **shared sunlight**. Later material sharpens the danger of making Sakura alone the sun. Sakura’s “every stage matters because life is precious” philosophy is beautiful, but it can become self-consuming. SUNNY PEACE must become a group where Sakura is still the emotional ignition, but not the only bearer of radiance. The other four members must become real carriers of the sun.

This makes SUNNY PEACE more complex than an ordinary genki idol unit. Their final form is not merely “bright girls who encourage people.” It is:

> **a group that converts support into performance, performance into connection, and connection into a form of shared life.**

## 3\. Performance philosophy: returning received feeling

SUNNY PEACE’s performance philosophy is based on **returning received feeling**. They do not simply project charisma downward from stage to audience. They receive encouragement, fan support, friendship, memory, and gratitude, then return it as song, smile, presence, and shared experience.

Their idolhood is therefore circular rather than vertical.

A purely vertical idol model says:

> The idol shines.
> The audience receives.

SUNNY PEACE’s model says:

> The audience supports the idol.
> The idol receives that feeling.
> The idol returns it as warmth.
> The audience is encouraged in turn.

This is why their image depends so heavily on openness, accessibility, food, festivals, casual intimacy, emotional vulnerability, flowers, pastel colors, public smiles, fan letters, local travel, and shared activities. SUNNY PEACE are not meant to feel unreachable. They are meant to feel like an emotional place the fan can enter.

This also explains why their events often involve apparently ordinary things: touring, fishing, teaching, shopping, cooking, Valentine chocolate, local festivals, survival games, fan dreams, and handwritten wishes. SUNNY PEACE’s miracle is rarely abstract. It is made out of social and bodily life.

## 4\. The five-member emotional structure

Each SUNNY PEACE member contributes a different kind of emotional light.

**Sakura** provides solar immediacy. She is the person whose joy pulls others forward.

**Rei** provides disciplined form. She turns the group’s warmth into polished performance.

**Haruko** provides mature warmth. She makes the group feel safe, social, and emotionally inhabited.

**Chisa** provides visible effort. She lets fans see fear becoming courage.

**Shizuku** provides fan-memory. She remembers what idol light feels like from below and brings that memory onto the stage.

The unit fails if any one of these functions is removed. Without Sakura, SUNNY PEACE loses its central emotional ignition. Without Rei, that emotion lacks form. Without Haruko, the warmth lacks hearth. Without Chisa, the group loses its small, effortful courage. Without Shizuku, it loses its memory of fandom itself.

The mature unit is therefore not a single sun with satellites. It is a solar system whose warmth is distributed through different forms of light.

# II. Kawasaki Sakura

## 1\. Core thesis

**Kawasaki Sakura is SUNNY PEACE’s survivor-sun: a life-affirming idol whose brightness is inseparable from ordinary embodiment, gratitude, appetite, and the fact that she is still alive.**

Sakura is the emotional center of SUNNY PEACE and one of the central figures of *IDOLY PRIDE* as a whole. Her story cannot be reduced to “cheerful protagonist,” “genki center,” or “Mana’s successor.” She is a girl who survived serious illness, received a heart transplant, inherited a connection to Nagase Mana, and had to fight the danger of being treated as Mana’s vessel rather than Kawasaki Sakura.

Her defining movement is:

> **from miracle-receiver to living person;
> from Mana’s possible replacement to Sakura herself;
> from girl saved by others to idol who wants to make others feel alive.**

Sakura’s happiness matters because it is embodied. She eats, laughs, reaches, visits local places, loves mascots, enjoys festivals, responds intensely to food, and throws herself into the world. Her joy is not abstract. It is the joy of someone who knows ordinary life is not guaranteed.

## 2\. Personality

Sakura is warm, energetic, emotionally direct, curious, affectionate, and intensely responsive. She is a classic **元気 / genki** personality on the surface: lively, bright, enthusiastic, and easy to read. But her genki quality has depth because it grows from survival rather than innocence.

She is not cheerful because she lacks pain. She is cheerful because being able to experience joy feels precious to her.

Her personality is marked by several recurring traits.

First, **immediacy**. Sakura reacts quickly and openly. If she is excited, hungry, moved, embarrassed, or happy, everyone can tell. She is not a character who habitually conceals emotion behind analysis. She feels first, then thinks. This makes her socially powerful: people do not have to decode her.

Second, **appetite**. This is not a trivial gag. Sakura’s love of food is one of her deepest proof-of-life motifs. Eating means having a body that can enjoy the world. Food scenes, local treats, restaurants, and festival meals all support her characterization as someone whose miracle is ordinary life restored.

Third, **social warmth**. Sakura naturally pulls people into shared experience. She wants others to enjoy things with her. She does not simply say, “I am happy.” Her instinct is closer to, “Isn’t this wonderful? Let’s enjoy it together.”

Fourth, **survivor urgency**. Sakura’s desire to perform can become dangerous because she treats stages as precious, possibly unrepeatable moments. This gives her idolhood extraordinary sincerity, but it also creates the risk that she will overextend herself. Her brightness has a cost.

Fifth, **relational fearlessness**. Sakura approaches people who intimidate others. She can reach toward rivals, outsiders, fans, and emotionally guarded people with startling sincerity. This trait makes her powerful, but also risky: she sometimes moves into emotional territory without fully understanding the pain she is touching.

## 3\. Voice and speech style

Sakura’s voice is open, bright, emotionally transparent, and highly reactive. She tends to speak with warmth and immediacy rather than calculation. Her speech often feels like it arrives directly from feeling.

She uses enthusiastic reactions, direct praise, and emotionally simple but sincere statements. She is not unintelligent, but she is not verbally ornate in the way a more self-conscious performer might be. Her language is accessible. She says what she feels in a way that makes others feel invited rather than judged.

Her vocal identity can be summarized as:

> **warm immediacy.**

She speaks as someone who wants to close distance. Even when she is anxious or burdened, she tends to express emotion in a way that calls others toward her. Her idol voice is therefore a natural extension of her ordinary voice: bright, direct, life-affirming, and relational.

Sakura’s speech can also become morally clarifying because she often says the simple thing that more guarded characters cannot say. In a story full of grief, performance pressure, rivalry, and public memory, Sakura’s ability to say “I want to do this,” “I want everyone to smile,” or “Let’s go together” is not shallow. It is structurally important.

## 4\. Characterization and story role

Sakura’s central narrative function is to humanize miracle.

Nagase Mana represents miracle as myth: the legendary idol who died and left behind an afterimage so powerful that everyone must respond to it. Sakura is connected to that miracle through her heart and voice, but the story’s ethical task is to prevent her from becoming merely “Mana returned.”

Sakura’s character arc insists:

> **Inheritance is not replacement.**

She may carry something from Mana, but she is not Mana. She has her own body, appetite, family memories, local attachments, habits, friendships, weaknesses, and desires. The more the story risks turning her into a vessel, the more important her ordinary traits become.

This is why Sakura’s food, mascots, festivals, family memories, school friendships, wish lists, and local pleasures matter so much. They prove that she is not a shrine to Mana. She is a living girl.

Sakura also functions as SUNNY PEACE’s emotional ignition. She is the one who turns “let’s do our job” into “let’s make this meaningful.” In fan-facing events, she often gives the group its forward motion: let’s connect with fans, let’s go to them, let’s return what they gave us, let’s make people smile.

But later material complicates this. Sakura’s “every stage is precious” philosophy can become self-destructive when it turns into refusal to protect her own body. If she alone becomes “the sun,” she risks becoming another impossible miracle whose body burns too hard for others. SUNNY PEACE’s mature responsibility is to let Sakura remain Sakura without forcing her to become the sole source of light.

So Sakura is both inspiration and danger:

> She teaches the unit how to shine.
> The unit must teach her that she does not have to shine alone.

## 5\. Visual appearance

Sakura’s visual design is built around **sunlit approachability**.

Her hair is warm honey-blonde to light brown, usually soft, fluffy, and mobile. It is styled in ways that keep her silhouette buoyant and youthful. Her hair does not create a severe or elegant line like Rei’s; it creates warmth and motion.

Her eyes are large, amber-brown to reddish-brown, and very expressive. They are almost always emotionally legible. Sakura’s gaze invites. It does not hide.

Her silhouette is compact, rounded, and energetic. She often wears puffy sleeves, ribbons, short idol skirts, floral ornaments, food-themed or festival-themed costumes, and bright accessories. She is visually constructed as a burst of warmth at the center of the image.

Her color language centers on sunrise and festival colors: orange, yellow, coral, pink, white, sky blue, warm gold, and red accents. She looks natural among flowers, balloons, food, confetti, mascots, fireworks, and stage light.

Her body language is overwhelmingly open. She reaches toward the viewer, points outward, leans forward, sings with arms extended, laughs with her mouth open, and pulls people into the scene. She is not visually guarded.

The major motifs of Sakura’s design are:

-   sun;

-   flowers;

-   food;

-   festivals;

-   local life;

-   mascots;

-   reaching hands;

-   open smiles;

-   embodied joy.


Her design thesis is:

> **This girl is not a shrine to someone else’s miracle. She is alive.**

## 6\. Relationships and unit function

Sakura’s relationship to SUNNY PEACE is central but must be read carefully. She is the emotional sun, but not the whole sky. The danger of over-centering Sakura is precisely what later material corrects.

With **Rei**, Sakura forms the contrast between emotional immediacy and disciplined form. Sakura moves first; Rei shapes movement. Sakura helps Rei relax into joy; Rei helps Sakura’s warmth become stageworthy.

With **Haruko**, Sakura forms a warmth contrast. Sakura is sunlight: immediate, public, forward-moving. Haruko is hearth: mature, domestic, patient, inhabitable. Haruko is one of the members best suited to notice when Sakura’s brightness is becoming burden.

With **Chisa**, Sakura represents abundance while Chisa represents effort. Sakura pulls Chisa forward, but Chisa reminds Sakura that not everyone can move at Sakura’s speed.

With **Shizuku**, Sakura is the idol who shines outward, while Shizuku is the former fan who remembers what that shine feels like from below. Sakura gives warmth; Shizuku gives reception.

Sakura’s member statement is:

> **I am alive, and I want to share this joy with you.**

Within SUNNY PEACE, she is the survivor-sun: the girl whose joy is proof of life, but whose life must not be sacrificed to the demand that she keep shining forever.

# III. Ichinose Rei

## 1\. Core thesis

**Ichinose Rei is SUNNY PEACE’s disciplined grace: a former competitive dancer whose arc transforms performance from proof into joy.**

Rei is often legible at first as the serious dancer, the elegant one, or the strict senpai-like member. Those readings are not wrong, but they are incomplete. Her deeper character lies in the tension between bodily discipline and emotional softness.

Rei learned to understand her body as something that must perform correctly, win, satisfy expectations, and justify her dream. Idolhood teaches her that movement can also be shared happiness, fan encouragement, gratitude, and self-expression.

Her core movement is:

> **from controlled proof to shared joy.**

Rei does not become less disciplined. She learns that discipline can serve tenderness.

A refined version of her thesis after later event material is:

> **Rei begins by needing performance to prove that her dream is real. Over time, SUNNY PEACE, Haruko, fans, Makino, and even unofficial idol spaces teach her that performance becomes most real when it creates connection, courage, heat, gratitude, and shared life.**

## 2\. Personality

Rei is serious, diligent, physically aware, polite, responsible, and somewhat self-conscious. She often gives the impression of someone trying to do things properly. Compared with Sakura’s emotional immediacy, Rei’s personality is more measured. She thinks about form, training, standards, and whether she is fulfilling her role correctly.

Her core traits include:

First, **discipline**. Rei understands practice, correction, bodily control, and repeated effort. She is not casual about performance. She respects the stage as a place where preparation matters.

Second, **earnestness**. Rei is not cynical or detached. Her seriousness comes from caring too much, not too little. She wants to meet expectations and can become embarrassed when she feels she has failed to inhabit a role naturally.

Third, **latent softness**. Underneath the dancer’s composure is a girl who can be awkward, bashful, vulnerable, and surprisingly tender. She is not cold; she is controlled.

Fourth, **growth through relaxation**. Rei’s development often involves learning to smile naturally, accept praise, rest, rely on others, and enjoy being seen. Her greatest challenge is not effort. It is allowing effort to become pleasure.

Fifth, **legitimacy anxiety**. Rei repeatedly encounters the question of what makes performance meaningful. Is dance legitimate if her father does not acknowledge it? Is SUNNY PEACE legitimate if they become comfortable after BIG4 status? Is underground idol performance legitimate if it is unofficial or rough? Rei’s arc keeps forcing her to expand her definition of value beyond approval, rankings, institutions, and technical standards.

## 3\. Voice and speech style

Rei’s voice is polite, composed, and serious, with a tendency toward careful phrasing. She sounds like someone used to respecting structure and responsibility. She is less impulsive than Sakura, less domestic and gently teasing than Haruko, less timid than Chisa, and less fanlike/dreamy than Shizuku.

Her speech often has the quality of someone trying to understand the correct emotional or social response. When she is in familiar territory — dance, practice, performance, improvement — she can sound confident. When placed in softer, romantic, comedic, or socially playful situations, she becomes more flustered.

Her vocal identity can be summarized as:

> **composure learning warmth.**

She is not verbally cold. Rather, her emotional warmth often comes through after a layer of discipline, hesitation, or self-consciousness.

Rei’s strongest performance voice is more forceful than her private voice. Onstage, she can call to the audience, drive heat, and demand energy. This matters because she is not merely an elegant dancer; she is a performer capable of turning discipline into live electricity.

## 4\. Characterization and story role

Rei gives SUNNY PEACE its physical and technical credibility. She is the member who makes the group’s warmth feel stageworthy. Sakura supplies emotional ignition; Rei gives that ignition form.

Her story is one of the clearest examples of *IDOLY PRIDE*’s belief that idolhood transforms prior pain rather than erasing it. Rei’s dancer past is not discarded. The body that once existed to prove itself becomes a body that can encourage others.

Her father conflict is central. Rei’s father represents a worldview in which serious social usefulness — especially medicine, respectability, and adult practicality — is treated as superior to performance. Rei’s answer is not anti-medicine or childish rebellion. Her mature answer is that idols can help people too. They do not heal bodies in the way doctors do, but they can give people courage, hope, excitement, and tomorrow.

This makes Rei’s arc philosophical:

> **What makes performance meaningful if it cannot be measured only by practical utility, paternal approval, or technical excellence?**

The answer is reception. Performance is meaningful when it reaches people.

Her later ambition material complicates her further. After SUNNY PEACE reaches greater visibility, Rei worries about stagnation. She fears the group becoming comfortable. This is a real strength — she does not want SUNNY PEACE to coast — but it also risks turning her own dream into a new form of pressure. Haruko’s role as humane brake becomes essential here.

Rei’s mature lesson is not “stop trying so hard.” It is:

> **Do not let the dream you chose become another authority that crushes you.**

## 5\. Visual appearance

Rei’s visual design is built around **elegant athleticism**.

Her most striking feature is her long pale-blonde hair, often flowing behind her or arranged with braids, ribbons, or refined ornaments. Her hair creates an elongated, graceful silhouette and often acts as an extension of movement.

Her eyes are green or teal, calmer and sharper than Sakura’s. They often communicate focus, poise, or composed vulnerability.

Her silhouette is tall, long-limbed, and dancer-coded. She is frequently posed with controlled hands, extended legs, balanced posture, and elegant body lines. Even when seated or relaxed, her body tends to create a shaped composition.

Her color language centers on blue, white, aqua, gold, navy, cream, and occasionally black or red. Blue and white give her polish and cool elegance. Gold and blonde hair warm the image from within. Darker costumes reveal a more mature, glamorous, or severe Rei.

Her visual motifs include:

-   dance lines;

-   flowing hair;

-   blue-white stage costumes;

-   water and sky;

-   sunflowers;

-   elegant dresses;

-   practice wear;

-   controlled hands;

-   soft smiles emerging from composure.


The sunflower imagery is especially important. Sunflowers belong naturally to SUNNY PEACE’s sunny language, but Rei among sunflowers suggests that she has internalized the unit’s warmth. The disciplined dancer has learned to bloom.

Her design thesis is:

> **The body that once existed to prove itself can become a body that dances for others and smiles with friends.**

## 6\. Relationships and unit function

Rei’s most important SUNNY PEACE function is form. She turns the group’s warmth into something physically credible, repeatable, and beautiful.

With **Sakura**, she forms the body/sun axis: Sakura gives emotional ignition, Rei gives shape.

With **Haruko**, she forms the mature axis. Rei brings ambition, high standards, and urgency; Haruko brings pacing, care, and emotional habitability. Rei needs Haruko because unchecked ambition can become self-pressure. Haruko needs Rei because Rei’s discipline validates warmth as something worthy of the stage.

With **Chisa**, Rei often becomes a model of effort and technical improvement. Chisa’s trembling courage, in turn, reminds Rei that visible imperfection can also reach fans.

With **Shizuku**, Rei is the watched performer and Shizuku is the former watcher. Rei’s trained body gains deeper meaning when Shizuku reminds us how performance is received, remembered, and treasured by fans.

Rei’s member statement is:

> **I have trained myself into form, and now I am learning to let that form carry joy.**

Within SUNNY PEACE, Rei is disciplined grace. Without her, the unit’s warmth risks becoming emotionally sincere but physically under-formed. Rei makes SUNNY PEACE capable.

# IV. Saeki Haruko

## 1\. Core thesis

**Saeki Haruko is SUNNY PEACE’s delayed-blooming warmth: a soft, mature, emotionally generous idol whose kindness becomes radiance, but who also longs to be seen as cute, special, romantic, and chosen.**

After later event material, her thesis should be expanded:

> **Saeki Haruko is SUNNY PEACE’s delayed-blooming hearth and Hoshimi’s living bridge: a former solo idol whose disappointments made her doubt her own radiance, Mana’s friend who carries an unfinished promise, and a mature idol whose care turns memory, gratitude, failure, and ambition into warmth others can inherit.**

Haruko is often readable as the older-sister member, but that is only the surface. Her deeper characterization lies in the tension between **being the one who supports others** and **wanting to bloom herself**.

She is not simply mature. She is someone who worries that maturity might mean lateness: late to youth, late to romance, late to idolhood, late to being chosen first. SUNNY PEACE allows her to convert that fear into a different answer:

> **A girl who blooms later is still blooming.**

## 2\. Personality

Haruko is warm, kind, socially aware, nurturing, and emotionally steady. She is the member most naturally associated with comfort, hospitality, and interpersonal care. She often notices other people’s moods and tries to create a softer atmosphere.

Her core traits include:

First, **care**. Haruko has a strong instinct to support, feed, comfort, celebrate, and gently guide others. She makes emotional spaces livable.

Second, **self-conscious maturity**. She is aware of age, timing, ordinary adulthood, job hunting, marriage, romance, and the fear of being left behind. This gives her a more adult emotional texture than some of the other members.

Third, **delayed desire**. Haruko wants to be cute, glamorous, special, and personally cherished. She does not want to be reduced to a reliable caretaker. Her longing is soft but real.

Fourth, **persistence without bitterness**. Haruko’s idolhood is not based on being chosen early. It is based on continuing even after watching others shine first. This makes her warmth morally significant: she could have become resentful, but instead becomes generous.

Fifth, **memory-bearing**. Haruko is one of the most important living bridges to pre-SUNNY Hoshimi. She knew Mana not only as legend but as friend. This gives her warmth historical depth. She is not merely a comforting member in the present; she is someone who carries old promises forward.

## 3\. Voice and speech style

Haruko’s voice is gentle, warm, and socially smoothing. She often sounds like someone trying to make the other person comfortable. Her speech carries an older-sister softness: reassuring, mildly teasing, emotionally attentive, and careful.

But her voice also has moments of bashfulness and longing. When her own desires come into focus — wanting to be cute, wanting to be seen, wanting romance, wanting to be someone’s first choice — she can become more vulnerable.

Her vocal identity can be summarized as:

> **gentle care with delayed confession underneath.**

She is good at speaking for others’ comfort, but her most revealing moments are when she speaks from her own need.

This makes Haruko especially important in scenes where SUNNY PEACE must slow down. Sakura rushes forward, Rei intensifies, Chisa shrinks, Shizuku absorbs. Haruko often translates the emotional state of the room into something survivable.

## 4\. Characterization and story role

Haruko gives SUNNY PEACE its hearth. If Sakura is sunlight, Haruko is lamplight: less explosive, more inhabitable.

Her story role is crucial because SUNNY PEACE cannot be only youthful brightness. Haruko adds time, memory, patience, and adult tenderness. She represents the person who did not bloom immediately but still has beauty to offer.

Her presence reframes idolhood away from youthful destiny and toward cultivated warmth. She is not the prodigy, the miracle successor, the genius dancer, or the tiny anxious girl. She is the person who keeps going, who makes the table ready, who creates a room people want to return to.

Her later event material deepens this in two directions.

First, Haruko’s solo-era disappointments matter. Her rain/weather trauma and failed outdoor lives show that her warmth is not effortless. She has known humiliation, bad luck, empty audiences, and the fear that her presence ruins the stage. SUNNY PEACE does not make her cheerful from nothing; it gives her a group that says she is not bad luck, not too late, not merely failed.

Second, Haruko’s Mana connection matters. She is not only a successor to Mana’s era; she was Mana’s peer, friend, and incomplete duet partner. This makes her one of Hoshimi’s key transmission figures. She carries Mana not as a mythic idol to imitate, but as a living friend whose unfinished feelings can still move through song.

Haruko’s mature function is therefore:

> **to turn old memory into present warmth without freezing it as nostalgia.**

## 5\. Visual appearance

Haruko’s visual design is built around **soft maturity and delayed blooming**.

Her hair is dark brown, shoulder-length, gently wavy or softly curled. It feels natural and touchable rather than graphically iconic. Her eyes are warm amber or golden-brown, often soft, slightly drooping, and reassuring.

Her silhouette is rounded, feminine, and gentle. She is less associated with speed than Sakura and less associated with line than Rei. Her body language often emphasizes leaning, sitting, offering, hosting, reaching gently, and occupying shared spaces.

Her color language centers on warm earth tones, creams, greens, yellows, floral whites, muted blues, pinks, and romantic seasonal lighting. She looks especially natural in gardens, cafés, kitchens, parties, warm interiors, and flower-filled spaces.

Her visual motifs include:

-   cafés;

-   food;

-   flowers;

-   soft dresses;

-   aprons or domestic cues;

-   birthday cakes;

-   seasonal celebrations;

-   Christmas, Valentine, wedding, and pajama imagery;

-   warm indoor light;

-   gentle blushes;

-   mature but cute styling.


Haruko’s flower imagery is different from Sakura’s. Sakura’s flowers burst outward as celebration and life. Haruko’s flowers feel tended, cultivated, and waited for. They represent delayed blooming.

Her romantic and mature visual cards are especially important because they resist reducing her to “motherly caretaker.” Haruko’s design repeatedly insists that she wants to be seen as feminine, cute, desirable, glamorous, and personally cherished. Her warmth is not sexless utility. It is a living part of her womanhood and idol identity.

Her design thesis is:

> **Kindness can be radiance, ordinary warmth can become idol light, and a girl who thought she bloomed too late can still become someone’s most beautiful flower.**

## 6\. Relationships and unit function

Haruko’s unit role is emotional habitability. She makes SUNNY PEACE feel like somewhere one can stay.

With **Sakura**, she forms the sun/hearth contrast. Sakura’s joy can rush forward; Haruko’s warmth slows the room down enough for people to breathe.

With **Rei**, she becomes the humane counterweight to ambition. Rei asks how to keep rising; Haruko asks how to keep rising without breaking the people climbing.

With **Chisa**, she offers safe older-sister softness without trapping Chisa in dependency. Chisa reflects Haruko’s own desire to be allowed softness rather than always performing maturity.

With **Shizuku**, she shares memory orientation. Haruko carries delayed dreams and old Hoshimi; Shizuku carries fan memories and admired idols. Together they give SUNNY PEACE depth of time.

Haruko’s member statement is:

> **I have been waiting to bloom, and I want the warmth I give others to become my own light too.**

Within SUNNY PEACE, Haruko is hearth and flower. Without her, the unit’s cheerfulness would be brighter but less emotionally habitable.

# V. Shiraishi Chisa

## 1\. Core thesis

**Shiraishi Chisa is SUNNY PEACE’s handmade courage: a timid, soft, horror-loving, craft-oriented idol whose fear becomes empathy and whose visible effort helps others keep trying.**

After her major event material, the sharper formulation is:

> **Shiraishi Chisa is SUNNY PEACE’s handmade relational courage: a timid girl who once defined love as yielding, depending, or not becoming a burden, but who gradually learns that love can also mean stepping forward, confronting someone gently, saying what she wants, and letting others help her go where she truly wants to go.**

Chisa is not simply the shy little-sister figure. She is one of SUNNY PEACE’s clearest embodiments of effort as idol value. Her power is not command, overwhelming talent, mature stability, or solar charisma. Her power is that fans can see her being scared and trying anyway.

Her core movement is:

> **from someone who needs support to someone whose need for support becomes the basis for supporting others.**

Chisa does not become compelling by ceasing to be timid. She becomes compelling by letting a timid girl stand onstage.

## 2\. Personality

Chisa is shy, gentle, anxious, earnest, affectionate, easily overwhelmed, and deeply effortful. She is sensitive to evaluation and often unsure of herself, but she has a stubborn capacity to continue.

Her personality contains several important strands.

First, **timidity**. Chisa often begins from fear or hesitation. She struggles with directness, visibility, and self-assertion.

Second, **effort**. Her defining virtue is not fearlessness but continuation. She does one difficult thing at a time: speaking, performing, asking for help, finishing a task, trying a new role, accepting attention.

Third, **empathy**. Because Chisa knows fear and inadequacy from the inside, she can stand beside others who feel the same. Her idolhood is based on **寄り添う力 / yorisou chikara** — the power to stand beside or accompany someone.

Fourth, **craft and imagination**. Chisa’s love of costumes, handmade objects, sewing, design, plushes, and aesthetic details is not incidental. Craft is how she makes feelings tangible.

Fifth, **fear-as-play**. Her love of horror is one of her most interesting traits. It creates a paradox: the timid girl loves frightening things. This is not contradiction for contradiction’s sake. Horror lets fear become shaped, staged, aesthetic, and shareable. Chisa turns fear into experience.

Sixth, **relational courage**. Her best growth moments are not only when she becomes brave for herself, but when she becomes brave inside a relationship: confronting Saki, admitting she wants to go with SUNNY PEACE, accepting that being afraid does not mean she should be left behind.

## 3\. Voice and speech style

Chisa’s voice is soft, hesitant, polite, and emotionally careful. She often sounds like someone trying not to impose. Her speech likely includes pauses, uncertainty, and self-correction.

Her vocal identity can be summarized as:

> **small honesty becoming courage.**

Chisa’s voice matters because it does not need to become loud to become strong. When she says what she wants, the emotional force comes from the fact that saying it is difficult. Her quiet statements can be more powerful than louder declarations because they require visible effort.

Her speech is also often shaped by consideration. Chisa worries about inconveniencing others, being a burden, or overstepping. This makes her vulnerable to self-erasure, but it also gives her deep interpersonal sensitivity.

The mature Chisa voice is not fearless confidence. It is a small voice that has learned it still has the right to speak.

## 4\. Characterization and story role

Chisa gives SUNNY PEACE its most accessible form of courage.

Sakura’s joy is inspiring, but not everyone can be Sakura. Rei’s discipline is impressive, but not everyone can be Rei. Haruko’s warmth is comforting, but not everyone has her maturity. Shizuku’s fandom is tender, but not everyone shares her particular intensity.

Chisa is the member who says:

> You can be scared and still stand here.

This makes her extremely important for fans. Her idolhood does not ask the viewer to identify with genius or miracle. It asks them to identify with effort.

Her relationship with Saki is one of her core structures. Chisa begins as the younger sister who admires, depends on, and follows Saki. But the stronger reading is not simply “Chisa must become independent.” Her story asks something subtler:

> Can the protected person become someone who protects others without denying that she still needs support?

Her duet/rivalry/support material with Saki shows that both sisters must stop disappearing for each other. Chisa must not yield forever; Saki must not reduce herself to support. Chisa’s growth reaches a high point when she can ask Saki what Saki truly wants.

Her sea trauma and survival material give this theme physical form. Chisa’s old fear of water leads her to assume that if she cannot keep up, she should stay behind. Her growth is not “I am no longer afraid of the sea.” It is:

> **I am afraid, but I want to go with everyone.**

That is Chisa’s essence. She does not become strong by denying fear. She becomes strong by stating her wish through fear.

## 5\. Visual appearance

Chisa’s visual design is built around **soft handmade courage**.

Her hair is pale blonde to light brown, often styled in soft twin tails, braids, ribbons, or fluffy side sections. The design emphasizes delicacy and cuteness rather than sleekness or maturity.

Her eyes are large, gentle, and often slightly anxious or watery. Her gaze tends to read as vulnerable, earnest, or surprised. She often looks like someone who is asking whether it is okay to be there.

Her silhouette is petite, rounded, and doll-like. She is frequently framed with frills, bows, plushes, pajamas, handmade accessories, soft fabrics, and decorative objects. She often looks touchable, safe, and sheltered.

Her color language centers on lavender, pink, mint, pale yellow, white, cream, and soft blue. These colors give her a handmade, candy-like, bedroom-craft softness.

Her visual motifs include:

-   plushes;

-   sewing;

-   costumes;

-   frills and ribbons;

-   soft pajamas;

-   horror-cute props;

-   haunted-house imagery;

-   small handmade accessories;

-   shy smiles;

-   protective indoor spaces;

-   seaside and shell imagery.


The horror-cute motif is essential. It prevents Chisa from being merely soft. Her visual language often pairs cuteness with fear, making her design embody her psychology: the frightened girl who is fascinated by fear because fear can be shaped into play.

Her design thesis is:

> **A small, frightened, handmade thing can still become public light.**

## 6\. Relationships and unit function

Chisa’s unit role is visible effort. She is the member whose growth fans can most easily map onto their own fear.

With **Sakura**, she forms the joy/fear axis. Sakura says, “Let’s go.” Chisa says, “I am scared, but I want to go too.”

With **Rei**, she forms the effort contrast. Rei’s effort is polished, disciplined, and physically trained. Chisa’s effort is trembling, handmade, and emotionally legible.

With **Haruko**, she shares softness, but at different life stages. Haruko is mature hearth; Chisa is protected room. Haruko can care for Chisa without making her smaller.

With **Shizuku**, she forms the unit’s gentlest axis. Chisa is the timid girl learning to be seen; Shizuku is the fan learning to be watched. Their softnesses are different but mutually resonant.

Chisa’s member statement is:

> **I am scared, but I will keep trying — and maybe watching me try can help you keep trying too.**

Within SUNNY PEACE, Chisa is handmade courage. Without her, the unit would lose its most identifiable form of small, persistent bravery.

# VI. Hyōdō Shizuku

## 1\. Core thesis

**Hyōdō Shizuku is SUNNY PEACE’s fan-translator and attention-translator idol: a quiet former idol fan whose deepest strength is noticing what others overlook and turning it into communicable feeling.**

After her later event material, the stronger formulation is:

> **Hyōdō Shizuku is SUNNY PEACE’s fan-translator, attention-archivist, and hidden-protagonist idol: a quiet former idol fan whose love of idols becomes not only empathy, but authorship. She reads fan letters and designs projects around them, turns private hobbies into idol work, preserves off-shot intimacy, condenses feelings into memorable phrases, recruits collaborators through fan knowledge, competes for the lead role, and gradually learns that admiration does not require staying behind the people she loves.**

Shizuku is often easy to understate because she is quiet. She does not have Sakura’s solar immediacy, Rei’s elegant discipline, Haruko’s mature warmth, or Chisa’s visible trembling. Her power is subtler: she remembers what it feels like to love idols from below.

Her core movement is:

> **from watcher to performer;
> from fan to idol;
> from receiver of light to returner of light;
> from observer of stories to someone who can say she wants to be part of the story.**

## 2\. Personality

Shizuku is quiet, observant, dreamy, devoted, emotionally receptive, and surprisingly intense. She often appears passive on the surface, but this is misleading. Her interior life is extremely active.

Her personality contains several major strands.

First, **receptivity**. Shizuku notices. She notices fan letters, objects, gestures, songs, stage details, fishing conditions, local idols, offshots, goods, and emotional atmosphere. She absorbs more than she says.

Second, **idol fandom**. Shizuku is not merely someone who “likes idols.” Her fandom is one of her core ways of understanding the world. She thinks through fan letters, oshi culture, live etiquette, cheki, goods, penlights, magazines, costumes, local idol scenes, and the emotional rituals of support.

Third, **memory preservation**. Shizuku treats objects as emotional vessels: goods, photos, tickets, plushes, handwritten letters, costumes, signatures, and private shots. She understands that idol moments live after the event through things people keep.

Fourth, **quiet intensity**. Shizuku may speak softly, but when activated by idol topics, fishing, stagecraft, or an admired person, her energy can become startling. She can move from near-silence to torrent-like enthusiasm.

Fifth, **hidden ambition**. Shizuku’s later Alice/theater material reveals that her quietness should not be mistaken for absence of desire. She wants to stand at the center too. She wants to be Alice. She wants to compete. She wants to sing with Rui. She wants to make projects happen.

Sixth, **attention as care**. Shizuku loves by noticing. Her care is not always loud or direct; it is archival, specific, and attentive.

## 3\. Voice and speech style

Shizuku’s voice is gentle, dreamy, and emotionally receptive. She speaks softly and carefully, with a quality of wonder or quiet sincerity. Her speech can feel as though she is preserving the emotional weight of what she says.

Unlike Sakura, she does not close distance through energetic exclamation. Unlike Rei, she does not primarily speak from discipline. Unlike Haruko, she does not primarily speak as caretaker. Unlike Chisa, she is not defined by trembling effort. Shizuku speaks as someone who has looked carefully, loved quietly, and wants to express what that love means.

Her vocal identity can be summarized as:

> **quiet admiration becoming expression.**

She sounds most like herself when articulating what something meant to her.

Shizuku’s speech also has a compressed, phrase-making quality. Because she often says little, her words can land with unusual weight. She can produce odd, memorable, almost poetic lines: a missing phrase is a “lost piece,” an origin sleeps in a place, everyone’s pieces gather into one. Her language is not rhetorically polished like a speechwriter’s; it is image-based, inward, and emotionally condensed.

Her weakness is delayed expression. Her feelings are often larger than her immediate ability to articulate them. But given time, safety, and structure, she can express with great precision.

## 4\. Characterization and story role

Shizuku’s story role is to bring fandom onto the stage without betraying fandom.

Many idol works treat the fan and idol positions as separate: the fan looks up; the idol shines. Shizuku complicates that division. She begins from the audience side, from admiration and memory. Her idolhood is not a rejection of that origin. It is its continuation.

Her character says:

> The person holding the penlight can become the person standing under the penlights.

This makes her indispensable to SUNNY PEACE’s emotional philosophy. SUNNY PEACE is a unit built on receiving and returning feeling, and Shizuku understands that process from the fan side. She knows that an idol moment can become someone’s memory, comfort, courage, or treasured object.

The national tour/fan-dream material is one of her most important mature contributions. Sakura gives the tour its keyword of connection, but Shizuku proposes the mechanism: read fan letters, find the dreams inside them, and help those dreams become real. This is Shizuku’s fan-translator identity operating at unit scale.

Her theater/Alice material expands her beyond receptivity. Shizuku organizes, recruits, chooses, competes, and learns that wanting the lead role is not selfish. She initially tries to yield when she thinks Suzu is more suitable, but Suzu’s refusal forces her to understand that stepping aside can also be an insult to someone who is taking her seriously as a rival.

This is one of Shizuku’s most important lessons:

> **Admiration does not require disappearance.**

She can admire Rui and still sing beside her.
She can admire Suzu and still compete with her.
She can love idols and still become one.
She can understand fans and still be the person fans came to see.

## 5\. Visual appearance

Shizuku’s visual design is built around **soft receptivity and fan-goods dreaminess**.

Her hair is pale aqua-blue, often styled in twin buns, side tails, ribbons, or curled sections. It creates a fluffy, rounded, cloudlike silhouette. Her eyes are pinkish-red to purple-red, large, soft, and often slightly overwhelmed by wonder.

Her silhouette is petite, rounded, and decorative. She is often surrounded by ribbons, plushes, pastel objects, magazines, cards, penlights, mascots, and fan-adjacent goods.

Her color language centers on aqua, mint, lavender, pink, white, pale yellow, and candy-like accents. These colors make her feel like idol merchandise, pastel stationery, acrylic stands, plush packaging, magazine pages, and fan-event decoration.

Her visual motifs include:

-   magazines;

-   glowsticks;

-   idol goods;

-   plushes;

-   mascots;

-   rabbits or bears;

-   glasses;

-   cards and hobby-room objects;

-   angelic white-blue costumes;

-   dreamy stage light;

-   soft seated poses;

-   emotional upward gazes;

-   fishing gear;

-   cameras and preserved images;

-   theatrical fantasy imagery.


Her body language is usually gentle and low-intensity. She sits, leans, holds objects close, looks upward or sideways, and appears caught in moments of wonder. Even when performing, she often feels emotionally receptive rather than commanding.

But this softness should not be mistaken for lack of agency. Her visual corpus also includes moments where she becomes angelic, theatrical, centered, or intensely focused. Those images reveal the hidden-protagonist Shizuku: the girl whose quiet gaze can become stage authorship.

Her design thesis is:

> **Idolhood is not only the act of shining. It is remembering what it felt like to be reached by someone else’s light, then stepping onto the stage so that feeling can be returned.**

## 6\. Relationships and unit function

Shizuku’s unit role is audience memory. She is the member who understands idolhood from both sides of the stage.

With **Sakura**, she forms the shining/receiving axis. Sakura shines outward; Shizuku remembers what that light means when received.

With **Rei**, she pairs the watched body with the watching heart. Rei’s disciplined performance becomes more meaningful when Shizuku helps us understand how fans internalize performance.

With **Haruko**, she shares memory orientation. Haruko carries old Hoshimi and delayed dreams; Shizuku carries fan devotion and accumulated objects. Both understand that idolhood becomes memory in people’s lives.

With **Chisa**, she forms the gentle observer axis. Chisa is the timid person learning to be seen; Shizuku is the fan learning to be watched. Together they represent the unit’s softest but most emotionally precise light.

Shizuku’s member statement is:

> **I loved idols first, and now I want to return that love from the stage.**

Within SUNNY PEACE, Shizuku is fan-memory. Without her, the unit loses the audience-side emotional intelligence that makes its warmth reciprocal.

# VII. SUNNY PEACE internal dynamics

## 1\. Sakura and Rei

Sakura and Rei form the contrast between emotional immediacy and disciplined form.

Sakura moves outward first. Rei shapes movement. Sakura’s energy says, “Come with me.” Rei’s discipline says, “Let’s make this beautiful enough to reach.”

Their dynamic is not opposition but completion:

> Sakura gives Rei warmth.
> Rei gives Sakura form.

Sakura helps Rei relax into joy. Rei helps Sakura’s radiance become stable performance.

## 2\. Sakura and Haruko

Sakura and Haruko are two different kinds of warmth.

Sakura is sunlight: immediate, public, life-affirming, forward-moving. Haruko is hearth: gentle, domestic, mature, patient, and inhabitable.

Sakura’s joy can be urgent. Haruko’s warmth can slow the room down enough for people to breathe. Haruko is one of the members best suited to notice when Sakura’s brightness is becoming burden. Sakura, in turn, helps Haruko feel that blooming is still possible.

## 3\. Sakura and Chisa

Sakura and Chisa embody two routes to encouragement.

Sakura encourages through abundance: “Life is wonderful; let’s go.” Chisa encourages through effort: “I am scared too, but let’s try.”

Sakura’s energy can pull Chisa forward, but Chisa’s vulnerability reminds Sakura that not everyone can run at the same speed.

Their combined message to fans is powerful:

> Joy matters.
> So does fear.
> You can move with either.

## 4\. Sakura and Shizuku

Sakura and Shizuku are the idol-light circuit made visible.

Sakura is the girl who shines because life was restored to her. Shizuku is the girl who understands what that shine can become in the heart of a fan.

Sakura’s warmth gives Shizuku something to receive. Shizuku’s fan-memory gives Sakura’s warmth a destination.

## 5\. Rei and Haruko

Rei and Haruko form the mature support axis of SUNNY PEACE.

Rei asks how the unit can keep improving. Haruko asks how improvement can remain humane. Rei brings technique, standards, and urgency. Haruko brings pacing, care, and emotional wisdom.

This is one of the unit’s most important adult dynamics:

> Rei keeps SUNNY PEACE from becoming complacent.
> Haruko keeps Rei from turning ambition into self-harm.

## 6\. Rei and Chisa

Rei and Chisa are both effort characters, but their effort differs.

Rei’s effort is polished, physical, trained, and form-oriented. Chisa’s effort is trembling, visible, handmade, and emotional.

Rei shows Chisa that practice can build capability. Chisa shows Rei that vulnerability can also reach people.

## 7\. Rei and Shizuku

Rei and Shizuku pair performance execution with performance reception.

Rei’s body is trained to be seen. Shizuku’s heart remembers what being seen from the audience side means. Together, they show that idol performance is not complete until it reaches someone’s memory.

## 8\. Haruko and Chisa

Haruko and Chisa share domestic softness, but at different life stages.

Haruko cares with mature warmth. Chisa seeks comfort through handmade, plush, and safe-room imagery. Haruko can provide a reassuring older-sister atmosphere without trapping Chisa in dependency. Chisa, in turn, reflects Haruko’s own desire to be allowed softness rather than always performing maturity.

## 9\. Haruko and Shizuku

Haruko and Shizuku are both memory-oriented characters.

Haruko carries delayed dreams, Mana’s living memory, and the feeling of having waited to bloom. Shizuku carries fan memories and the feeling of having looked up from below.

Together, they give SUNNY PEACE depth of time.

## 10\. Chisa and Shizuku

Chisa and Shizuku are the two softest visual presences in the unit, but they differ sharply.

Chisa’s softness is fear becoming courage. Shizuku’s softness is admiration becoming expression. Chisa is the timid girl learning to be seen. Shizuku is the fan learning to be watched.

Together, they represent SUNNY PEACE’s gentlest axis:

> The scared person can stand on stage.
> The person in the audience can become the idol.
> The small feeling can become public light.

# IX. SUNNY PEACE’s broader significance in IDOLY PRIDE

SUNNY PEACE are central to *IDOLY PRIDE* because they embody the franchise’s most life-affirming answer to the problem of inheritance after miracle.

Nagase Mana’s miracle creates a shadow. It would be easy for Hoshimi Production to become a shrine, for Sakura to become a vessel, for Kotono to remain a mourner, and for Makino to remain trapped as witness to the dead. SUNNY PEACE answers that danger by making idolhood living, bodily, ordinary, social, and reciprocal.

Their light is not Mana’s light repeated. It is a new light made from five girls who each needed help standing.

This matters because SUNNY PEACE are not the franchise’s most severe unit, most elite unit, most glamorous unit, or most ideologically aggressive unit. TRINITYAiLE, LizNoir, IIIX, Tsuki no Tempest, and DoriKyun all challenge idolhood from other angles: perfection, pride, proof, vow, revolution, moonlight, institutional ambition, and adult damage.

SUNNY PEACE’s answer is simpler but not lesser:

> **To be an idol is to receive someone’s feeling, stand with others, and return warmth in a form that helps people live.**

This is why their cheerfulness should be taken seriously. In *IDOLY PRIDE*, a smile is not necessarily shallow. A smile can be an act of survival, gratitude, courage, support, professionalism, memory, or love.

SUNNY PEACE are the unit that most clearly turns the smile into an ethic.

## 1\. SUNNY PEACE versus Tsuki no Tempest

Tsuki no Tempest is moonlight: vow, grief, seriousness, attachment, rivalry, and inherited emotional weight. SUNNY PEACE is sunlight: warmth, support, ordinary life, visible effort, and fan-facing reciprocity.

Tsuki asks:

> How do attachments become vows strong enough to illuminate darkness?

SUNNY PEACE asks:

> How do supported girls become warmth strong enough to help others live?

The two units are not opposites in a simplistic sun/moon binary. They are complementary answers to Mana’s afterimage. Tsuki wrestles with memory through grief and vow. SUNNY PEACE wrestles with memory through life and continuation.

## 2\. SUNNY PEACE versus TRINITYAiLE

TRINITYAiLE represents elite polish, wings, perfection, and the challenge of reaching the top without cutting away human attachment. SUNNY PEACE represents accessible warmth, fan reciprocity, and emotional habitability.

TRINITYAiLE’s challenge is:

> Can perfection become gratitude?

SUNNY PEACE’s challenge is:

> Can fragility become warmth?

TRINITYAiLE descends from the sky into connection. SUNNY PEACE rises from ordinary support into light.

## 3\. SUNNY PEACE versus LizNoir

LizNoir is pride, seniority, perfection, and the recovery of heat after professional stagnation. SUNNY PEACE is vulnerability, mutual support, and smiles as survival.

LizNoir asks:

> Can skill and pride recover heart without losing severity?

SUNNY PEACE asks:

> Can fear, delay, illness, and admiration become a light others can inhabit?

LizNoir is about rekindling passion inside professional excellence. SUNNY PEACE is about making support itself into performance.

## 4\. SUNNY PEACE versus IIIX

IIIX are idols after innocence: adult-coded, media-literate, damaged professionals who use performance to fight erasure. SUNNY PEACE are idols of reciprocal warmth: girls who were supported and want to support.

IIIX says:

> We will not be reduced to what wounded us.

SUNNY PEACE says:

> We were supported, and now we will return that warmth.

IIIX weaponizes image. SUNNY PEACE shares feeling. IIIX proves survival through visibility, beauty, memory, and hostile intimacy. SUNNY PEACE proves survival through openness, care, effort, and collective sunlight.

This is why Sakura’s encounters with IIIX, especially miho, are so charged. Sakura’s sunlight is not merely cute sincerity. It touches the memory economy that miho is fighting. Sakura reminds miho of the kind of warmth miho has been grieving, resenting, and trying not to need.

## 5\. SUNNY PEACE versus DoriKyun

DoriKyun treats idolhood as rupture, combat, and revolutionary entertainment. SUNNY PEACE treats idolhood as connection, care, and returned warmth.

DoriKyun asks:

> If your dream cannot survive being crushed, was it strong enough?

SUNNY PEACE asks:

> What if the dream survives because others hold it with you?

DoriKyun is not wholly wrong: idolhood requires force, spectacle, and seriousness. But SUNNY PEACE reveals what DoriKyun’s worldview lacks: not all strength is domination. Some strength is the ability to make a frightened person say, “I want to go too.”

# X. Final synthesis

SUNNY PEACE are not merely the cheerful unit of *IDOLY PRIDE*. They are the unit that proves brightness can be built from fragility.

Sakura teaches that ordinary life is a miracle worth singing.

Rei teaches that disciplined beauty becomes complete only when it carries joy.

Haruko teaches that late blooming is still blooming.

Chisa teaches that fear can become empathy and courage.

Shizuku teaches that fandom itself can become idolhood.

Their shared philosophy is not naïve optimism. It is reciprocal radiance:

> **We were supported.
> We learned to stand.
> We shine together.
> We return that light to you.**

That is SUNNY PEACE’s place in *IDOLY PRIDE*: the group that transforms survival, discipline, delay, fear, and admiration into warmth fans can inhabit.

Their final comparative statement is:

> **SUNNY PEACE are the idols of life after being helped: girls who prove that support can become song, weakness can become warmth, and a smile can be not an escape from pain, but the form pain takes after it has been held by others and returned as light.**
