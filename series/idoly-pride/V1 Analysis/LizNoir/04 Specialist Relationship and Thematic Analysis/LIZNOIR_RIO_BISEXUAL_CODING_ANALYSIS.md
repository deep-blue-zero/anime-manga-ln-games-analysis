I would not call it **canon-confirmed bisexuality**, but I do think **“Rio is bi-coded” is probably the cleanest read if we take all her material together**.

More precisely:

**Rio is written with clear male-directed romantic coding around the manager, while her relationship with Aoi is written with strong female-female partner/yuri coding. So if I had to assign a sexuality read, I’d say Rio is plausibly bisexual or biromantic — with the caveat that the text keeps it in idol-franchise ambiguity rather than explicit orientation language.**

The reason I would not call her simply lesbian-coded is that the manager material is too direct to ignore. Rio’s wedding-card material frames her ideal partner through bride/groom language, asks for male perspective, and gives her very naked line: **“私を一番好きでいてほしい”** — she wants her partner to love her most. She also imagines a husband’s cooking, mutually supportive marriage, and lifelong exclusivity. card\_rio\_017\_st-card-rio-05-wed… Her messages also have explicitly hetero-framed lines like telling the manager he is at an age where he might have **“気になる異性”** — someone of the opposite sex he is interested in — while fussing over making him look better. message\_message\_group\_rio\_001\_m…

But the reason I would not reduce Aoi to “just best friend / work partner” is that Aoi’s material repeatedly exceeds ordinary unitmate framing. Rio tells Aoi that LizNoir is **“貴方と私 / 井川葵と神崎莉央のグループ”** — a group of Aoi and Rio — says she came this far because Aoi was there, thanks her, and says she was happy to be Aoi’s partner. Aoi then says that if Rio is not there, she has no lingering attachment to the entertainment world. That is not casual coworker language. origin\_liz\_004\_love\_heart

The domestic scenes intensify that. Rio cooks for Aoi, Aoi wants Rio’s food every night, Rio says she is not Aoi’s mother, and Aoi answers that she does not think of her that way. The text actively refuses the easy maternal explanation while preserving the daily-life intimacy. origin\_liz\_004\_love\_heart Then the “open two hearts” message adds hotel stay, old sleepover memories, bath/hair-drying intimacy, and Aoi being more spoiled when the two of them are alone. message\_message\_group\_rio\_001\_m…

So my best read is:

**Rio is not written as “confused” or as switching between mutually exclusive readings. She is written as someone whose capacity for attachment has at least two major romantic-coded channels: Aoi as her foundational female life/stage partner, and the manager as her later romantic-comedy / future-partner fantasy axis.**

The Aoi side is deeper, older, and more existential. It is about **identity, body, dance, waiting, return, domestic care, and the original closed world of LizNoir**.

The manager side is more overtly romantic in ordinary genre terms. It is about **bride imagery, jealousy, wanting to be loved first, family introduction, being seen as desirable, and imagining adult partnership**.

That combination is exactly why **bisexual/biromantic Rio** feels like a stronger interpretive label than trying to force the evidence into either “only Aoi” or “only manager.” But I would phrase it as:

**Rio is canonically unlabeled, but strongly bi-coded: romantically legible toward the manager and yuri-coded toward Aoi, with Aoi occupying the deeper stage-marriage/life-partner position.**
