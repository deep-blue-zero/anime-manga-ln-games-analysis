# IDOLY PRIDE V2 ANIME AND CROSS-MEDIA SEQUENCING PROTOCOL

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines where the *IDOLY PRIDE* television anime enters the V2 analytical sequence and how later game material may recontextualize it without erasing the anime's original dramatic state.

The governing principle is:

> **The anime is a bounded foundational work and should be reviewed comprehensively before the much larger game corpus is allowed to teach the analysis what to see in it. Later game evidence may recontextualize the anime, but it must not retroactively erase what the anime itself established prospectively.**

The game and anime are therefore not treated as two interchangeable transcript repositories.

The anime is a complete twelve-episode audiovisual drama with its own:

- narrative pacing;
- dramatic architecture;
- performance order;
- music placement;
- vocal acting;
- shot design;
- silence;
- blocking;
- visual motif system;
- audience framing;
- episode-level construction.

The game is a continuing live-service corpus that massively expands character histories, professional life, relationships, industry structures, and later development.

V2 must preserve both scales.

---

# 2. Why the anime requires a dedicated sequencing rule

The game corpus is so large that, if read first, it can unintentionally colonize interpretation of the anime.

For example, a character may acquire years of later material that makes an early gesture or line seem obvious in retrospect. That does not mean the original episode made the same proposition explicit.

The analytical danger is retrospective inevitability:

> "Because later material explains X, Episode 3 was always transparently about X."

V2 rejects that assumption.

The anime must first be read under the information conditions available within the anime itself.

Only after that baseline is frozen should later game material be permitted to revise, deepen, or destabilize the reading.

---

# 3. Two-pass anime model

The anime receives two analytically distinct passes.

## PASS A — prospective complete anime deep reading

This occurs immediately after Phase 0 source lock.

All twelve episodes should be reviewed comprehensively.

The purpose is to reconstruct the anime as a work in its own right.

For each episode, distinguish:

- what is explicitly established;
- what is strongly inferable at that point;
- what remains ambiguous;
- what is withheld;
- what character state exists by the end of the episode;
- what performance/music/visual form contributes beyond dialogue.

Do not import later game revelations unless explicitly marked as retrospective commentary outside the prospective reading.

The prospective anime pass should produce a frozen **anime-endpoint state** for:

- Makino;
- Nagase Mana;
- Nagase Kotono;
- Kawasaki Sakura;
- Ibuki Nagisa;
- SUNNY PEACE;
- Tsuki no Tempest;
- LizNoir where relevant;
- Hoshimi Production;
- the meaning of idolhood;
- audience recognition;
- miracle, inheritance, grief, and continuity;
- producer/idol relations;
- performance philosophy;
- the original anime's relationship architecture.

This baseline becomes the reference point against which the game is later compared.

### Phase-1 Hoshimi expansion bridge

Before the project leaves the anime-era Hoshimi material behind, Phase 1 performs a dedicated comparison of every `st-original-cmn` block against the relevant frozen anime episode state. This audit asks **how the game's telling expands the anime**, not which medium is the one true transcript.

At minimum, record:

- additional Makino first-person interiority;
- explicit professional reasoning that was only implicit or audiovisual in the anime;
- added scenes, dialogue, or ordinary-life context;
- player-selectable Makino phrasing and its branch status;
- changes of emphasis or narrative framing;
- material that remains anime-only because its meaning depends on acting, music, blocking, editing, or image composition;
- any genuine continuity tension.

The game manager in this bridge is treated as **Makino Kouhei continuity**. Custom naming and `{user}` are interface parameterization; branch choices are handled as mutually exclusive Makino-compatible expressions rather than as evidence for a different protagonist.

This Phase-1 bridge may make anime propositions newly explicit, but it must not rewrite the Episode 1–12 prospective ledger as though those explicitations were available during the original anime-only pass.

### Phase-1B unit-origin bridge and disclosure chronology

After the direct `st-original-cmn` audit, the project must inspect the origin stories of **SUNNY PEACE, Tsuki no Tempest, LizNoir, and TRINITYAiLE before entering the Tokyo arc**. These histories can be prequel, concurrent, or post-Hoshimi depending on the unit and scene. Their purpose at this stage is twofold:

1. establish each unit's origin/history with both diegetic and disclosure chronology preserved;
2. test whether the existing Hoshimi anime/game expansion audit requires an addendum because an apparent main-story expansion, motive, relationship fact, or chronology point is better sourced to a dedicated origin story.

The audit order is:

`SUNNY PEACE -> Tsuki no Tempest -> LizNoir -> TRINITYAiLE -> conditional Hoshimi addendum -> IIIX -> Tokyo`.

The **IIIX origin sequence is mandatory before Tokyo**. Tokyo features IIIX prominently, so a prospective Tokyo reading may inherit the frozen IIIX-origin baseline but must not use Tokyo outcomes to reconstruct IIIX's prior state.

For every origin sequence preserve:

- when the depicted events occur;
- when the game discloses them;
- what earlier anime/Hoshimi claims they retrospectively affect;
- the exact point after which later main-story analysis may treat the information as inherited state.

Later disclosure may recontextualize an earlier reading, but it must be represented as a revision delta rather than silently backfilled into the earlier information frontier.

## PASS B — retrospective targeted anime audit

This occurs after the major game longitudinal ledgers and specialist syntheses have stabilized.

It is not a second equal-depth reread of all twelve episodes.

The game corpus generates specific questions that route us back to selected anime scenes.

Examples:

- later Makino material changes the significance of an early interaction with Mana;
- later Kotono material reframes her early relationship to Mana's legacy;
- later Sakura material complicates what the anime initially presented as resemblance or inheritance;
- later unit material changes how an early performance is understood;
- later relationship material makes a small early exchange newly important;
- later professional/industry material reveals institutional assumptions that the anime leaves implicit.

The retrospective pass asks:

> **What has later canon made newly legible here?**

It must still preserve the distinction between:

- meaning available in the original episode;
- meaning created by later retrospective context.

# 5. Anime source hierarchy

For the prospective anime reading, use the following evidence roles.

## 5.1 Japanese audio

Authoritative for:

- spoken delivery;
- timing;
- pauses;
- hesitation;
- overlapping speech;
- breath;
- laughter;
- vocal strain;
- emotional leakage;
- performance contrast.

## 5.2 Japanese subtitle/dialogue layer

Authoritative for recoverable wording where it faithfully represents the audio.

Use it for:

- lexical analysis;
- particles;
- forms of address;
- sentence endings;
- repeated phrases;
- explicit statements.

For quotation-sensitive analysis, reconcile subtitle text with the audio when necessary.

## 5.3 Video/frame evidence

Authoritative for:

- composition;
- blocking;
- expression;
- gesture;
- camera relation;
- focus;
- visual rhyme;
- costume;
- lighting;
- audience position;
- physical distance;
- recurring objects and spaces.

## 5.4 Music and sound

Treat score, insert songs, performance music, transitions, silence, and sound design as independent formal evidence.

Dialogue is not sufficient to describe the emotional architecture of a scene if music materially shapes that architecture.

# 6. Anime episode output standard

The prospective pass should not necessarily produce twelve giant reader-facing essays.

The working episode analysis may remain in the source-audit layer.

Each episode should nevertheless record:

1. episode dramatic function;
2. prospective character-state changes;
3. relationship changes;
4. explicit thematic propositions;
5. unresolved questions;
6. Japanese voice/register findings;
7. vocal-performance findings;
8. important music/sound cues;
9. important visual/formal cues;
10. performance-stage analysis where relevant;
11. evidence locators;
12. later-retrospective hooks left blank or marked pending.

This provides enough structure to support later synthesis without prematurely forcing every episode into a polished standalone essay.

---

# 7. Anime endpoint ledger

After Episode 12, freeze an `ANIME_ENDPOINT_STATE_LEDGER`.

For each major character/unit, record:

- what the anime has established;
- what remains unresolved;
- stable relationships;
- stable wounds/desires;
- professional status;
- performance identity;
- speech/vocal identity;
- relation to Mana;
- relation to Makino;
- relation to audience;
- mature or provisional state at anime end.

This ledger is analytically important because the game later continues development from that state.

Do not rewrite the anime endpoint simply because later game material changes the character.

---

# 8. Cross-media chronology

V2 should maintain three different notions of chronology.

## Release chronology

When the audience actually received anime/game material.

## In-universe chronology

When events occur within the fictional timeline.

## Analytical chronology

When a source became available to the V2 interpretation.

These are not interchangeable.

A later-released flashback may occur earlier in-universe but still function as retrospective information.

The synthesis should therefore avoid language such as:

> "we always knew"

when the knowledge was actually supplied years later.

---

# 9. Retrospective recontextualization labels

When later game material changes an anime reading, use explicit labels.

## CONFIRMS

Later material independently supports the anime-era reading.

## DEEPENS

Later material adds specificity without changing the original structure.

## RECONTEXTUALIZES

The original scene remains factually intact but carries different meaning later.

## QUALIFIES

The original reading survives only with narrower wording.

## COMPLICATES

Later evidence introduces meaningful tension without defeating the earlier reading.

## OVERTURNS

Later canon directly displaces the earlier interpretation.

## REMAINS ANIME-SPECIFIC

The anime makes a formal or dramatic proposition that later game text neither repeats nor supersedes.

These labels should route into the broader V2 claim-revision ledger.

---

# 10. Cross-media disagreement

If anime and game presentation appear to conflict:

1. determine whether the disagreement is factual or tonal;
2. check whether the game is extending rather than contradicting;
3. distinguish dialogue content from performed/visual meaning;
4. check chronology;
5. check whether one medium compresses events;
6. preserve genuine contradiction if unresolved.

Do not automatically privilege the later game simply because it contains more text.

For a question about audiovisual characterization in the anime, the anime remains the governing source.

For later game-specific development, the game governs.

---

# 11. Music in the prospective anime pass

The anime pass should identify music that performs one or more of the following functions:

- establishes a unit identity;
- transforms a character's emotional state;
- marks a major narrative threshold;
- creates a Mana/Makino/Sakura/Kotono association;
- turns private feeling into public performance;
- frames audience recognition;
- creates a thematic return;
- changes the meaning of a scene through entrance timing;
- carries an episode ending or transition.

The prospective pass should record the function first.

Detailed musical close reading may occur immediately for indispensable cues or be escalated to the later audiovisual protocol.

---

# 12. Cross-media synthesis rule

The final V2 synthesis should not tell one flattened chronology in which every later fact is treated as though it was always present.

Where useful, distinguish:

> **anime proposition → game continuation → later recontextualization**

This is especially valuable for a live franchise because character identity is not only an in-universe object. It is also an evolving authored construction.

---

# 13. Relationship to the live-corpus model

The anime itself is frozen.

The game is not.

Therefore:

- the prospective anime baseline needs to be established once;
- later releases do not require redoing the complete prospective anime reading;
- Class-2 or Class-3 game updates may trigger targeted retrospective anime review;
- new game material may advance a character's game-state ledger without changing the frozen anime-endpoint ledger.

This gives the project a stable historical anchor even while the game continues.

---

# 14. Governing rule

The anime is not preliminary material to be discarded once the game becomes larger.

It is the franchise's bounded foundational audiovisual drama.

V2 therefore preserves two truths simultaneously:

> **The anime must be understood on its own terms.**

and

> **The game may later make parts of the anime newly legible.**

The method fails if either truth erases the other.
