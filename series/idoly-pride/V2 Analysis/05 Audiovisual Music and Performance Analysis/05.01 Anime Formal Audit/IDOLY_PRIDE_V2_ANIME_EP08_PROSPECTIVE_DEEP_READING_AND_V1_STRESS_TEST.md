# STEP 2 — PROSPECTIVE FINDING SET AND FREEZE

## Executive thesis

Episode 08 turns the franchise's inheritance problem into an explicit crisis of **authorship**.

Until now, Kotono has gradually learned to live, work, lead, and even enjoy idolhood while still carrying the governing proposition that her career exists because she is Nagase Mana's younger sister and must complete the summit Mana did not reach. Sakura, meanwhile, has accumulated a different involuntary resemblance to Mana: voice, atmosphere, chest-centered intuition, medical history, and public comparison, none of which she intentionally chose.

Episode 08 makes both positions unstable.

Rio tells Kotono that she is merely a degraded copy of Mana. Public spectators compare Kotono to Mana because of sisterhood and appearance, but then compare Sakura to Mana even more strongly because of voice and performance atmosphere. Sakura becomes uncomfortable enough to alter her natural singing style. Kotono, confronted with the fact that she cannot reproduce Mana's voice or effect no matter how hard she tries, proposes that Sakura sing the song Mana would have performed in the Grand Prix final.

That proposal reveals the contradiction at the center of Kotono's inherited purpose. If Sakura can perform the unfinished Mana-role better than Kotono can, then what exactly is Kotono herself entering the Grand Prix to do?

The answer comes not from Mana and not from management. It comes from Tsuki no Tempest.

Saki asks whether, if Kotono has lost her old goal, she could act **for them**. Nagisa then states the new principle directly:

> **「私たち月のテンペストが自分たちのために歌う」**
>
> Tsuki no Tempest sings for ourselves.

The group tells Kotono that they like **her** voice, want to sing with **her**, and can aim for the summit **for themselves**. Kotono finally says:

> **「私　優勝を目指す」**
>
> **「月のテンペストで　このみんなで」**

The strongest prospective formulation is therefore:

> **Episode 08 does not solve inheritance by erasing Mana. It moves Kotono's reason for singing from delegated completion of a dead person's unfinished life toward collective self-authorship among the living.**

The title and ending song, **「君は君のままでいい」**, reinforce the same argument: identity cannot be ethically secured by becoming the correct copy of someone else, nor by deforming oneself to avoid resemblance. The more sustainable answer is to become legible as oneself inside relationships capable of recognizing that self.

---

# 1. Qualification makes the Grand Prix immediate rather than hypothetical

The episode opens after three months of preliminary competition. SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest have accumulated enough points to wait for main-tournament qualification.

Both advance.

This matters because the Grand Prix is no longer the distant object around which everyone can project ideals. It is now an approaching professional event with a finite bracket, named opponents, public press encounters, and concrete consequences.

Kotono's inherited goal has therefore reached the point where it must survive actual proximity to fulfillment.

That is exactly when its internal contradiction becomes impossible to ignore.

---

# 2. The press encounter makes Mana's legacy a contested professional language

The episode shows LizNoir and TRINITYAiLE publicly declaring their intentions toward the tournament. The exchange is openly competitive, but still professional: victory claims are made, the rivals acknowledge one another, and the stage is accepted as the place where the claims will be tested.

This is important context for what follows with Kotono.

The professional field is capable of rivalry without requiring identity collapse.

Kotono's problem is not simply that competition exists. It is that the person against whom she has internally measured herself is dead and therefore cannot participate in reciprocal rivalry.

---

# 3. Rio's "degraded copy" accusation attacks the exact instability Kotono has not solved

Rio congratulates Tsuki no Tempest and acknowledges the group's potential. Then she isolates Kotono:

> **「あなたは長瀬麻奈によく似ているだけ」**
>
> **「今のあなたは麻奈の劣化コピーでしかない」**

The cruelty of **劣化コピー** is not merely that Kotono is inferior to Mana. It denies Kotono an independent category of evaluation. A degraded copy can only be judged according to the original.

Nagisa immediately defends Kotono, asking what Rio can possibly know about Kotono's feelings.

Kotono stops her.

Then Kotono says something unusually perceptive:

> **「たぶん　私も神崎さんも一緒だから」**
>
> **「ずっとお姉ちゃんだけを見てた　そうですよね」**

Kotono recognizes the fixation because she shares it.

This is significant. Earlier episodes showed Kotono learning to compare herself with the living present. Episode 08 now lets her diagnose another person's Mana-centered orientation because she has sufficient reflective distance to see the structure from outside.

Rio rejects the interpretation, but the exchange has already changed the analytical frame:

> **Mana can trap both the person trying to inherit her and the person trying to defeat or measure her.**

The problem is not admiration. It is allowing the dead idol to monopolize the coordinate system in which one's own professional identity becomes meaningful.

---

# 4. "As long as you are Nagase Mana's sister" externalizes Kotono's original self-definition

Before leaving, the LizNoir side tells Kotono:

> **「長瀬麻奈の妹であるかぎり　あなたはこの先へは上がれない」**

The sentence is especially sharp because Episode 02 Kotono had used almost the same identity category as sufficient justification for becoming an idol:

> I am Nagase Mana's younger sister.

What began as Kotono's answer to "why become an idol?" is now presented as a possible ceiling.

The issue is not that sisterhood must be rejected.

It is that a relational fact cannot function as a complete professional self.

---

# 5. Sakura's medical care becomes ordinary professional governance

Sakura asks Makino to accompany her regular hospital examination because the preliminary Live Battles have increased her activity level and her physician wants to discuss exercise volume with the responsible adult before the main tournament.

Makino agrees immediately:

> **「こういうのはちゃんとしておいたほうがいい」**

This extends Episode 07's management-ethics evidence. Sakura's health history is not treated as either melodramatic fragility or something the production can ignore because she appears energetic.

It becomes ordinary duty-of-care infrastructure:

- periodic examination;
- exercise-load review;
- responsible-manager involvement;
- continuing clearance before increased competition.

The franchise is therefore placing bodily limits *inside* professional management rather than outside the idol narrative.

---

# 6. Mana's response to Kotono reveals a limit in ghostly care

While waiting at the hospital, Makino notices the contrast between Mana's obvious concern for Sakura and her apparent lack of engagement with the recent Kotono/Rio confrontation.

He asks whether Mana is not worried about her sister.

Mana answers:

> **「そんなの意味ないじゃん」**
>
> **「私にはどうすることもできないんだから」**

This should not yet be converted into a complete psychological theory of Mana.

But it establishes a meaningful limitation in her posthumous agency.

Mana can:

- speak with Makino;
- socialize with Mei;
- observe Kotono;

but she cannot simply walk up to Kotono and repair the injury created by her own death and legacy.

Her apparent distance may therefore contain helplessness rather than indifference.

The episode does not yet tell us whether this helplessness also contains guilt, avoidance, or fear. Those remain open hypotheses.

---

# 7. Makino excludes even an invisible ghost from Sakura's private consultation

When Sakura's examination is complete, Makino is called into the consultation.

He asks Mana to leave:

> **「さくらのプライベートだしな」**

Mana initially questions this but complies.

This is a small but valuable management-ethics detail.

Makino treats privacy as a principle, not merely as control over who can physically hear something. The fact that Mana is invisible to Sakura does not make Sakura's medical information ethically ownerless.

That also contrasts usefully with Episode 07's Mana-video archive, where Makino had violated Mana's own deletion request. The anime therefore does not present him as perfectly consistent; it gives us evidence of both principled privacy behavior and memorial overreach.

---

# 8. Episode 08 crosses the transplant threshold—but donor identity remains unconfirmed

The physician says Sakura is currently doing well and explicitly states:

> **「移植してから時間も経っていますし」**

This is the first prospective confirmation that Sakura's heart surgery involved a **transplant**.

Makino then asks:

> **「ドナーってどんな方だったんですか」**

The physician explains that donor information is confidential.

Makino then articulates the hypothesis the anime has been building toward:

- Hoshimi lost an idol in an accident three years earlier;
- that idol was Nagase Mana;
- Sakura's singing voice is extremely similar to Mana's;
- when Makino first met Sakura, it felt as though Mana had guided him to her.

But the doctor does **not** confirm the connection.

The prospective evidence state after Episode 08 is therefore:

### TEXTUAL FACT

- Sakura received a heart transplant approximately three years earlier.
- donor identity is confidential.
- Makino explicitly suspects a relationship between Sakura's transplant and Mana.

### NOT YET TEXTUAL FACT

- Mana was Sakura's donor.
- Mana's heart causes Sakura's voice.
- Mana's heart causes Sakura's intuition.
- the apparent temporal fit is causal.

The anime has moved the donor hypothesis from audience inference into character-conscious speculation, but has deliberately withheld confirmation.

---

# 9. Makino's "guided by Mana" interpretation remains interpretation

Makino tells the doctor that meeting Sakura felt as though Mana had guided him.

This is important because even the manager who can literally see Mana is constructing meaning from coincidence, timing, voice, and feeling.

His supernatural access does not make every interpretation he forms authoritative.

The episode therefore preserves an epistemic distinction that will remain important later:

> **a character may possess privileged evidence without possessing omniscient causal knowledge.**

---

# 10. The celebration restores bodily and economic ordinariness immediately after the transplant scene

The episode cuts from the medically charged hospital sequence into celebration.

The party includes deliberately mundane forms of embodiment and materiality:

- Haruko drinks wine because it is a celebration and eventually falls asleep;
- Rei tries to regulate how much she drinks because practice continues the next day;
- Suzu has again exhausted her money and has had family card access cut off;
- Makino pays for the celebration;
- the girls joke about increasingly expensive rewards if they continue winning.

This tonal movement matters.

The transplant revelation does not convert Sakura into a solemn medical object. The narrative immediately returns the whole cast to money, food, alcohol, embarrassment, and ordinary group life.

---

# 11. Social media becomes an external identity-authoring machine

Shizuku performs ego-searching.

The resulting posts reproduce the central Episode 08 problem from outside the characters.

Kotono is praised through Mana:

- she is Mana's younger sister;
- her standing/appearance evokes Mana;
- therefore her value is described through resemblance.

Then the comments turn to Sakura.

Visible posts explicitly suggest that Sakura may resemble Mana even more strongly in performance:

- her singing style;
- the atmosphere around her while singing;
- recollection of Mana's own stage presence.

This is important because the successor problem is no longer privately generated only by Kotono.

The audience is actively narrating both girls into Mana-shaped categories.

Public recognition can therefore produce identity pressure even when the praise is positive.

A compliment can still function as an act of misrecognition if it makes the person valuable only insofar as she is legible as someone else.

---

# 12. Sakura's voice is natural to her even if its origin is mysterious

Sakura later explains that she is not consciously imitating Mana:

> **「別に麻奈さんの声に合わせようとか考えてるわけじゃなくて」**
>
> **「自然と歌っているというか」**

Then she gives a crucial chronological fact:

> **「手術の後から　急にこんなふうに声が出るようになって」**

After the operation, she suddenly became able to produce this kind of voice.

This does not establish the transplant as the causal mechanism.

But it does establish that Sakura experiences the voice as:

- post-surgical in chronology;
- involuntary in origin;
- natural in present use;
- not a deliberate Mana impression.

That distinction is essential to the ethics of the next scene.

If Sakura's current voice is simply the voice her body naturally produces, then changing it in order to avoid resemblance to Mana risks making **public comparison control her authentic practice**.

---

# 13. Sakura attempts self-distortion to escape an inherited label

During rehearsal, Sakura experiments with another way of singing.

The others immediately notice.

When asked why, she eventually admits that she researched Mana after the social-media discussion. She looked at where Mana came from, how she became an idol, how famous she was, and old performance footage.

Her response to Mana's greatness is not competitive pride.

It is discomfort:

> **「なんか　似てるなんてのが申し訳なくて」**

She feels almost apologetic for being compared to Mana.

Sakura's solution is therefore to alter her own singing so she will be less Mana-like.

This is the mirror image of Kotono's problem.

### Kotono

tries to become sufficiently like Mana.

### Sakura

tries to become sufficiently unlike Mana.

Both allow Mana to determine the permitted shape of their own identity.

That yields one of the episode's strongest conceptual findings:

> **Imitation and defensive anti-imitation can be two forms of the same dependence.**

The problem is not resemblance itself. The problem is allowing resemblance to dictate what one is allowed to become.

---

# 14. Sakura's concern is specifically relational: she fears harming Kotono

Sakura says that if she continues singing naturally, Shizuku believes people may call her Mana's successor.

Her immediate question is not whether the label helps her career.

It is whether Kotono would hate that.

This is consistent with Sakura's established leadership and relational style. She interprets identity problems through the effect her presence may have on another person.

But that care creates its own danger: Sakura becomes willing to deform her natural singing in order to protect Kotono from a reaction Kotono has not actually requested.

Care can therefore become over-accommodation when it attempts to solve another person's feelings by erasing part of oneself.

---

# 15. Kotono's confession destroys the fantasy that effort can make her Mana

Sakura demands the truth twice:

> **「本当のこと言ってよ」**

Kotono finally answers in unusually simple, declarative clauses:

> **「私には　お姉ちゃんの声は出ない」**
>
> **「お姉ちゃんほどの歌唱力はないし」**
>
> **「お姉ちゃんほど　お客さんを感動させられない」**

Sakura protests.

Kotono insists:

> **「そんなことある」**

and:

> **「何度練習してもダメだったの」**

This is not merely low self-esteem.

It is the collapse of an empirical project.

Kotono has repeatedly tried to reproduce Mana's performance capacity and has concluded from experience that she cannot.

The significance lies less in whether Kotono objectively undervalues herself than in the fact that **the Mana-copying project has failed by its own criteria**.

---

# 16. Kotono delegates the Mana-role to Sakura

Kotono then says:

> **「私がお姉ちゃんの代わりに歌っても」**
>
> **「お姉ちゃんの心残りは晴らせない」**

But:

> **「でも　さくらは違う」**
>
> **「さくらならできる」**

And finally:

> **「さくらなら　お姉ちゃんが決勝で歌うはずだった歌も…」**
>
> **「私は　さくらに歌ってほしい」**

This is one of the most important inheritance moments in the anime.

Kotono does not yet abandon the project of completing Mana.

Instead, she **delegates it** to the person who appears better equipped to perform it.

That is psychologically generous toward Sakura in one sense: Kotono is not jealously demanding exclusive possession of Mana's likeness.

But it is devastating to Kotono's own identity structure.

If her vocation was justified by completing what Mana could not, and Sakura can apparently complete that task better, then Kotono has outsourced the reason she became an idol.

The episode immediately recognizes the resulting void.

---

# 17. Kotono's real crisis is not inferiority; it is loss of reason

Later Kotono tells Nagisa:

> **「私はお姉ちゃんの代わりに　トップアイドルにならなきゃって思ってきた」**

She could not sing like Mana.

Then Sakura appeared.

If Sakura can sing like Mana and potentially perform the song Mana should have sung, Kotono asks:

> **「私がグランプリに出る理由って何だろうって」**

This is the episode's central crisis.

The question is not:

> Am I talented enough?

It is:

> **Why should I continue if the inherited function that justified my existence here can be performed by someone else?**

That exposes how precarious a borrowed reason for living can become.

If one's identity is organized around completing another person's task, the appearance of a more suitable substitute threatens the entire self-structure.

---

# 18. Kotono's ethical development makes the identity crisis harder, not easier

Kotono immediately says she knows this state is unacceptable because the other girls are seriously committed to the Grand Prix:

> **「みんなグランプリに懸けているんだから」**
>
> **「私も本気で挑まなきゃ失礼だし」**

This is important longitudinally.

Earlier Kotono could treat group idolhood instrumentally as the route to Mana's summit.

Now the group has become morally real enough that she recognizes an obligation not to participate half-heartedly.

Her development has therefore created a new ethical problem:

> the old reason has collapsed, but the living people around her now deserve a genuine reason.

That is progress even before she finds the answer.

---

# 19. Nagisa does not supply Kotono's identity; she points toward permission

Nagisa tells Kotono:

> **「琴乃ちゃんがしたいようにすればいいと思うよ」**

and says that everyone already understands enough of Kotono's feelings.

This is consistent with Nagisa's established role as relational translator.

She does not tell Kotono what her new reason must be.

She makes it socially permissible for Kotono to discover one that is not reducible to Mana.

That distinction matters. Self-authorship cannot be authored *for* Kotono by the friend who understands her best.

---

# 20. The group has learned Kotono well enough to perceive small changes

Kotono is surprised that the others have noticed something is wrong.

Nagisa explains:

> **「ずっと一緒にやってきたから」**
>
> **「ちょっとの変化でも　みんな気づくようになったんだよ」**

This provides one of the strongest retrospective validations of Episodes 03–05.

The group is no longer merely coordinated labor.

It has become a perceptual community.

People know Kotono's baseline well enough that subtle deviations become visible.

The same girl who once disappeared alone into a dark bedroom is now surrounded by people capable of noticing when she is only slightly different.

That is a profound change in the social conditions around her.

---

# 21. Tsuki tells Kotono that her voice has value without Mana

The other members join the conversation.

Saki says that if Kotono no longer knows what goal should organize her, perhaps she can think **for them**.

Mei says she does not really understand Mana, but would be happy if she could sing with Kotono and achieve a good Grand Prix result.

Nagisa states:

> **「みんなね　琴乃ちゃんの歌声が好きなんだよ」**
>
> **「麻奈さんのことは関係なく　琴乃ちゃんと一緒に歌いたい」**

This is precisely the recognition Kotono has never been able to give herself.

She believes her voice has value only relative to Mana's superior voice.

The group values the voice because it belongs to **Kotono**, the person with whom they have actually been living and singing.

That is the relational answer to Rio's "degraded copy" accusation.

A copy is evaluated by resemblance to an original.

A person is evaluated through relationships that could not exist without that particular person.

---

# 22. Suzu is the most important person to reject the Mana scale

Kotono still says her singing is inferior compared with her sister.

Suzu—one of the strongest Mana devotees in the cast—responds:

> **「麻奈様と比べることなんて　できるわけありませんわ」**

and then, somewhat awkwardly:

> **「ただ　あなたにはあなたのよさがあるのも事実ですわ」**

This is especially valuable because the argument does not come from somebody who thinks Mana is overrated.

Suzu can preserve intense admiration for Mana while refusing to make Mana the only scale on which Kotono can possess value.

The anime therefore does not solve inheritance through iconoclasm.

Mana does not need to become less great for Kotono to become more herself.

---

# 23. "For ourselves" is the decisive new professional grammar

The most important linguistic shift is from inherited obligation to collective first-person agency.

Nagisa says:

> **「私たち月のテンペストが自分たちのために歌う」**
>
> **「自分たちのためにグランプリの頂点を目指す」**

The repetition of **私たち / 自分たち** matters.

The summit does not disappear.

Ambition does not disappear.

The Grand Prix does not disappear.

What changes is the **possessive structure of the goal**.

It is no longer primarily:

> Mana's unfinished summit, inherited by Kotono.

It becomes:

> **our summit, pursued because we have chosen to pursue it together.**

That is collective self-authorship.

---

# 24. Kotono finally notices how much of the living world has accumulated around her

Kotono answers:

> **「ありがとう」**
>
> **「私　お姉ちゃんのことばかりで　全然気づいてなかった」**

She had not noticed how much everyone cared about her and about the group.

This does not mean she suddenly stops loving or grieving Mana.

It means the attentional field has widened.

Earlier:

> only sister -> goal.

Now:

> sister **and** group -> self-chosen future.

The living do not replace the dead.

They prevent the dead from occupying the entire horizon.

---

# 25. The moon becomes an environment of recognition rather than isolation

After the group reaches Kotono, moonlight becomes visually prominent and someone remarks:

> **「月の祝福だね」**

The anime has spent multiple episodes transforming Kotono's relationship to nocturnal imagery.

Episode 02:

> cool/dark domestic space -> isolation -> immobilization.

Episodes 03–06:

> darkness gradually becomes a space she can move through and eventually perform within.

Episode 08:

> moonlight falls on a Kotono who is surrounded by people capable of recognizing her as herself.

The moon therefore no longer functions merely as a symbol of derivative or borrowed identity.

At minimum, the unit has now **socially appropriated the lunar image as its own shared language**.

One should avoid making astronomical reflection itself into a deterministic metaphor unless later evidence supports it. The stronger formal fact is that lunar visual grammar has moved from enclosure toward belonging and chosen collective identity.

---

# 26. Kotono rewrites the goal in first person

Kotono says:

> **「私　優勝を目指す」**
>
> **「月のテンペストで　このみんなで」**

This is perhaps the cleanest statement of her development so far.

Compare the earlier justification:

> I am Mana's younger sister.

with the new one:

> **I will aim to win—with Tsuki no Tempest, with these people.**

The grammatical subject is still **私**, but the action is constituted through **このみんなで**.

Her new selfhood is neither solitary individualism nor inherited self-erasure.

It is a self capable of choosing a collective relation.

---

# 27. "Our summit" is the performance-level rewrite of inherited purpose

The first-round stage introduction includes:

> **「私たちの頂上目指して」**
>
> **「月の光を手に」**
>
> **「月のテンペスト」**

The phrase **私たちの頂上** is the public-performance counterpart to the night conversation's **自分たちのため**.

Earlier the summit primarily belonged to Mana's interrupted history.

Now Tsuki publicly names a summit as **ours**.

This is not simply motivational language. It is the professional articulation of the episode's identity shift.

---

# 28. The post-performance group embrace makes the new reason bodily

After the battle, Kotono is physically surrounded by her Tsuki members.

She initially responds with characteristic awkward resistance as Nagisa, Suzu, Mei, and then Saki join the celebration.

The importance lies in where the episode places this image.

Kotono has just chosen:

> **with these people.**

The ending then makes "with these people" physically unavoidable.

The girl whose grief once curled her body into solitary enclosure is now almost comically enclosed by living peers through affection rather than despair.

That does not erase her discomfort with exuberant contact. It demonstrates that the social environment around the same reserved person has radically changed.

---

# 29. 「君は君のままでいい」 functions as an explicit anti-copy thesis

The ending song contains unusually direct language:

> **「もう難しくしなくていい　君は君のままでいい」**
>
> **「君が君でいるから好きだと言えるんだ」**
>
> **「もっと胸の中にある光　信じて行こう」**

and later:

> **「青い空　白く浮かぶ月見上げ」**
>
> **「夜の月と違う　やわらかいもの」**
>
> **「まだまだ見つけるよ　新しい自分」**

As formal reinforcement, this is extraordinarily close to the episode's actual argument.

Both central girls have been overcomplicating identity through Mana:

- Kotono by trying to become the correct successor;
- Sakura by trying to stop sounding like the wrong successor.

The song's answer is not "ignore relationships."

It explicitly says that another person can love **you because you are you**, and that a new self can continue to be discovered rather than copied from a finished model.

The image of a moon appearing differently under a blue sky is also a useful formal parallel to the episode's treatment of identity: the same object need not possess only one legitimate mode of appearance.

V2 should classify this as **FORMAL REINFORCEMENT**, not as proof that every lyric describes Kotono and Sakura exclusively.

---

# 30. Episode 08 gives a two-sided critique of succession

The episode's inheritance problem can now be stated more precisely.

### Succession pressure on Kotono

> You are Mana's sister; therefore become sufficiently like Mana.

### Succession pressure on Sakura

> You sound like Mana; therefore you may be classified as Mana's successor whether or not you want the role.

Kotono responds through imitation.

Sakura responds through defensive alteration.

Both responses surrender authorship to the same absent center.

The episode's proposed alternative is neither pure independence nor denial of influence.

It is **relational self-authorship**:

> acknowledge the past;
> refuse to let resemblance determine worth;
> let living relationships recognize qualities the comparison scale cannot;
> choose a future whose goal belongs to the people pursuing it.

# Prospective claim set

1. **TEXTUAL FACT:** both SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest qualify for the Grand Prix main tournament.

No prospective answer yet exists.

# 3. Tsuki no Tempest after Episode 08

The unit has crossed an important threshold.

It is no longer simply:

> Kotono's group.

It can turn around and become a source of direction **for Kotono**.

That reciprocal movement is the strongest anime-native foundation so far for Tsuki as a genuinely relational system.

But V2 preserves the distinction between:

- this clear Episode-08 fact;
- and the richer mature mutual-illumination doctrine developed by later game material.

# 4. Mana after Episode 08

Mana's absence exerts pressure through multiple channels:

- Rio's stopped rivalry;
- Kotono's failed copying project;
- Sakura's involuntary resemblance;
- public comparison;
- Makino's donor suspicion.

Yet Mana herself says she cannot simply repair what her death did to Kotono.

The ghost remains a person with limited agency, not an omnipotent author of the living.

That limitation is becoming thematically important.

# 5. Makino after Episode 08

His managerial evidence ledger gains:

- routine medical oversight;
- respect for confidential health information;
- awareness that his own suspicion remains speculation;
- willingness to involve medical expertise rather than treating intuition as sufficient.

This sits productively beside his continuing supernatural access and his emotionally driven interpretation that Mana may have "guided" Sakura to him.

Makino's professional strength increasingly lies in **holding intuition and procedure together without pretending they are the same kind of knowledge**.

# 7. Frozen Episode 08 endpoint entering Episode 09

The following is the only anime state Episode 09 may inherit prospectively:

1. Sakura is explicitly a **heart-transplant recipient**.
2. The donor remains confidential and unconfirmed.
3. Makino suspects Mana but has no confirmation.
4. Sakura's Mana-like voice emerged after surgery and is natural rather than consciously imitated.
5. Public comparison makes Sakura consider changing that natural voice.
6. Kotono has admitted she cannot reproduce Mana despite repeated effort.
7. Kotono asks Sakura to sing the song Mana would have performed in the cancelled Grand Prix final.
8. That delegation collapses Kotono's old reason for competing.
9. Tsuki tells Kotono that her voice has value independently of Mana.
10. Kotono chooses to aim for victory **with these people**.
11. `私たちの頂上` is now the operative public grammar of her ambition.
12. Kotono's relationship to the diary/night/room has changed through agency and re-signification rather than abandonment.
13. The successor problem is **not finished** because Sakura now carries the role Kotono attempted to transfer.
14. Later donor confirmation, later Mana statements about song ownership, and later Sakura individuation remain unavailable until the anime itself supplies them.

The episode's frozen final question is therefore:

> **How can people profoundly shaped by Mana acknowledge resemblance and inheritance without allowing either to become an assigned identity?**

Episode 08 gives Kotono a first working answer.

Episode 09 must now test whether Sakura can find one too.

# Historical-analysis provenance note

The stress test therefore preserves both outcomes:

> **V1 was often interpretively right.**

and:

> **V2 is stricter about when the anime had actually earned the right to say it.**
