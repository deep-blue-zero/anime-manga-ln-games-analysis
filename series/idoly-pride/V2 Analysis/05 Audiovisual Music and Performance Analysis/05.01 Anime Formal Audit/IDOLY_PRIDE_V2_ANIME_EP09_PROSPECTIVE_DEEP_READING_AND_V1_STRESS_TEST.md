## Executive thesis

Episode 09 resolves the inheritance crisis opened in Episode 08 by making an explicit distinction between **receiving something from the dead** and **becoming the dead person’s successor/replacement**.

The episode confirms within its narrative frame that Sakura’s transplanted heart is Mana’s, while preserving the real-world institutional rule that donor identity cannot be publicly verified or disclosed. That revelation initially seems to strengthen the successor model: Sakura now has Mana’s heart, a Mana-like singing voice that appeared after surgery, chest-centered intuition, and possession of the one body part literally transferred from Mana into the future. Sakura herself briefly interprets these facts as obligation: because the heart was Mana’s, she should responsibly sing Mana’s unreleased Grand Prix song.

Mana rejects that conclusion.

Her most important statement is:

> **「私の歌は 私だけのものだから」**
>
> My song is mine alone.

Even a girl with the same voice cannot sing *Mana’s* song in the existential sense that matters. The composition may survive. The vocal resemblance may survive. The organ may survive. But the performed act that was Nagase Mana belonged to the person who stood onstage with her own feelings, audience orientation, pride, rivalry, and lived history.

Mana therefore changes the meaning of her one remaining wish. She does not want Sakura or Kotono to complete her interrupted performance. She wants SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest to become capable of reaching the summit **as themselves**. Her unfinished desire changes from substitution to futurity.

Sakura reaches the same conclusion from another direction. She admits that her idol career initially began because she followed the mysterious guidance of her heart, but says:

> **「最初はそうでした。でも 今は違います」**

Now she wants idolhood because living relationships, shared goals, and experience have made it *her* desire. The heart helped her find a life; it does not own the life that followed.

The strongest prospective formulation is therefore:

> **Episode 09 argues that ethical inheritance preserves what was received without making the recipient a replacement. The dead may give life, courage, memory, material, technique, or opportunity; the living become worthy of that inheritance only when they author a future that the dead could not pre-write.**

The episode title, **「もらった勇気を抱きしめて」** — roughly, “holding close the courage I received” — is exact. Received courage is neither discarded nor obeyed as destiny. It is embraced, incorporated, and carried forward.

---

# 1. The opening press conference shows Sakura’s identity being publicly authored by comparison

Episode 09 opens not in private uncertainty but in public media space.

Sakura is congratulated for advancing and immediately asked about being called:

> **「長瀬麻奈さんの再来」**
>
> the return/reappearance of Nagase Mana.

Sakura answers carefully that she does not really know herself; when she produces the voice that feels best to her, people sometimes tell her it resembles Mana. She adds that she is happy because Mana is a senior she respects.

This is an important refinement of Episode 08.

Sakura is not yet publicly resisting the comparison. Nor does she claim it. She receives it politely while locating the voice inside her own bodily experience:

> **「自分で一番気持ちいい声を出すと」**

When she produces the voice that feels best/most comfortable to herself.

That language makes the later identity problem sharper. The Mana-like voice is not primarily a consciously assumed role. It is the voice Sakura experiences as naturally satisfying to produce.

Formally, the press environment turns her into a framed object of interpretation: backdrop logos, cameras, questioners, frontal composition. The episode will later return Sakura to a public stage where she chooses the terms of disclosure herself. The movement is from **being questioned about what she is** toward **telling the public what she herself is prepared to say**.

---

# 2. Kotono finally reveals the unfinished last night with Mana

After the Grand Prix conflict of Episode 08, Kotono tells Sakura what happened on the final night before Mana died.

Kotono had said something cruel enough to wound Mana, then isolated herself in guilt. Mana still came to her room. When Kotono said she could not attend the final, Mana responded with a lightly self-deprecating line about how watching a family member sing all the time probably was not fun anyway.

Then she offered Kotono something instead:

> **「じゃあ せめてこの歌聴いて」**
>
> **「明日の決勝で初披露する歌なんだ」**
>
> **「琴乃が最初に聴くんだよ」**

At least listen to this song. I’m going to debut it in tomorrow’s final. You’ll be the first person to hear it.

This substantially deepens the meaning of the unreleased Grand Prix song.

It is not merely:

- the song Mana failed to perform publicly;
- a lost professional artifact;
- or an unfinished piece of idol history.

It was also part of Mana’s **last attempted relational repair with Kotono**.

Kotono remembers hearing it only once, but remembers it clearly and describes it as a song that made her chest feel warm.

That means her request to Sakura is not emotionally neutral delegation. Kotono is handing over something that was given first to her in one of the final private exchanges with her sister.

---

# 3. Sakura accepts the song only after recognizing that Kotono has become “Tsuki no Tempest’s Nagase Kotono”

Sakura tells Kotono something that matters enormously after Episode 08.

A little earlier, she would have refused the request. She understood that performing Mana’s unreleased song had been Kotono’s goal and feared that taking it would deprive Kotono of the reason she had used to continue.

But Episode 08 changed her interpretation:

> **「月のテンペストの長瀬琴乃って感じだったから」**

Kotono looked/felt like **Nagase Kotono of Tsuki no Tempest**.

This is the outside recognition that corresponds to Kotono’s own `私たちの頂上` shift.

Sakura no longer sees Kotono primarily as the girl tasked with finishing Mana.

She sees her as a person embedded in a living unit whose identity has become independently legible.

Only then does Sakura say:

> **「だから今は 麻奈さんの歌 私が預かるよ」**

The verb **預かる** is important. Sakura will *hold/take custody of* the song. She does not say she inherits Mana’s identity or becomes its rightful owner.

Her chest again tells her to sing it.

At this point, the episode deliberately makes the successor temptation as strong as possible before dismantling it.

---

# 4. The donor rumor converts private bodily history into public commodity

A media report appears suggesting that Sakura’s transplanted heart may have come from Mana.

Reporters pursue Sakura directly and ask whether she really had a heart transplant and whether she knows rumors that Mana was the donor. Management intervenes and asks for written questions through the agency.

This is not simply plot exposition. The episode shows a collision between:

- legitimate public interest in a famous idol;
- Sakura’s private medical history;
- Mana’s posthumous privacy;
- the sensational value of a miraculous succession narrative;
- and the legal/ethical secrecy built into transplant systems.

Episode 08 showed social media authoring identity through resemblance.

Episode 09 escalates that process into **biographical ownership**. The public no longer merely says Sakura *sounds* like Mana; it begins trying to explain Sakura’s body as Mana’s continuation.

Sakura is shaken enough that she struggles to rehearse. Even then, she initially tries to continue because the upcoming song requires her participation.

Makino sends her back to rest.

This extends the anime’s management ethics: professional obligation does not cancel the right to be psychologically or physically unable to work.

---

# 5. Mana was a registered donor, but institutional confidentiality blocks public proof

Kotono learns from her mother that Mana had indeed registered as an organ donor and that the family was spoken to by doctors after the accident.

Makino reminds Sakura that Japan’s transplant system keeps donor information confidential even from relatives and that there is no ordinary way to verify the donor identity.

This is an important epistemic distinction.

The narrative is about to present Mana as the donor as an internal truth, but the social world cannot legitimately transform that truth into publicly verified information.

There are therefore two different categories of knowledge in Episode 09:

1. **narrative/inner-circle certainty**;
2. **publicly provable institutional fact**.

The episode does not collapse them.

That separation becomes crucial in Sakura’s later public statement.

---

# 6. Episode 09 narratively confirms the donor while refusing to make the confirmation publicly usable

Sakura privately tells Makino:

> **「私の心臓…麻奈さんの心臓なんです」**

At first she thought the idea was impossible. Then, thinking through the accumulated evidence, she says she can feel that the heart is Mana’s.

She links:

- suddenly being able to sing after surgery;
- being guided into idolhood by the heart;
- being told she sounds like Mana;
- and the uncanny fit she feels with Mana’s unreleased song.

Makino then reveals that during the recent hospital examination he himself had already realized the transplanted heart was Mana’s. The chronology matches: Mana’s death and Sakura’s surgery occurred on the same day.

Mana herself, after resisting the idea, eventually acknowledges:

> **「本当に私の心臓なのね」**

Within the anime’s narrative grammar, this functions as the donor reveal.

However, it remains different from a legally documented disclosure. The doctor never violates confidentiality, and Sakura later refuses to publicly validate the rumor.

The correct prospective classification is therefore:

> **TEXTUAL/NARRATIVE FACT:** the anime now presents Mana’s heart as Sakura’s transplanted heart.
>
> **TEXTUAL FACT:** the donor cannot be publicly verified through ordinary transplant disclosure rules.

This distinction is more sophisticated than a simple mystery reveal.

---

# 7. The episode strongly links ghost Mana to the transplanted heart without fully explaining the mechanism

Makino says:

> **「たぶん 麻奈が存在しているのも 心臓と何か関係があるんだ」**

He hypothesizes that Mana’s continued ghostly presence may somehow be related to her heart.

This is not yet omniscient explanation. It is Makino’s theory.

But the episode supplies formal evidence in the same direction.

Later, shortly after Kotono listens to Sakura’s heartbeat and before the performance, Mana suddenly says her body feels heavy and she has no strength. The editing then cuts to Sakura, who also says she feels physically off.

The juxtaposition is conspicuous enough to record as:

> **FORMAL-DEPENDENT STRONG HYPOTHESIS:** Mana’s ghostly condition and Sakura’s embodied condition are linked through the transplanted heart.

The exact causal mechanism remains unresolved at the Episode 09 boundary.

---

# 8. Mana’s choice to remain hidden from Kotono is care and paternalism at the same time

Sakura discovers that Mei can see Mana and asks why Kotono has never been told.

Mana says Kotono should *not* be happy to learn she is still present.

Her reasoning is:

> **「琴乃は私がいないから ここまで頑張ってこられたと思うの」**
>
> **「私がいると分かったら きっと張り詰めたものが切れてしまう」**

Mana believes Kotono has been able to continue because Mana is absent. If Kotono learns the dead sister is still available, the tension holding her forward motion together might break.

This is psychologically plausible.

It is also paternalistic.

Mana is deciding what Kotono is allowed to know about her own grief without Kotono’s participation in the decision.

The episode does not provide enough evidence to declare Mana correct.

The prospective judgment should therefore preserve both aspects:

- **care:** Mana is trying not to destabilize Kotono;
- **control:** Mana is unilaterally managing Kotono’s emotional reality.

This is an important complication in a character often remembered only as warm, confident, and miraculous.

---

# 9. Mana’s performance philosophy explicitly rejects substitution

When Sakura says she will sing Mana’s song, Mana urgently says no.

Her first reason is professional rather than metaphysical:

> **「さくらちゃん自身の歌じゃないから」**

It is not Sakura’s own song.

Mana explains that audiences perceive what is happening inside the performer:

> who she is singing toward;
> what she is thinking on stage;
> whether she is desperate, sad, or happy.

Then:

> **「本当に心の底からあなたしか歌えない歌を歌わなきゃ トップアイドルにはなれない」**

Unless you sing, from the bottom of your heart, a song that only *you* can sing, you cannot become a top idol.

And:

> **「あなたが向かい合わなきゃいけないのは 琴乃でも私でもない」**
>
> **「応援してくれる人たちよ」**

The person Sakura must face is not Kotono and not Mana. It is the people supporting her.

This is one of the anime’s clearest statements of performance ethics so far.

Performance is not primarily fidelity to predecessor, biography, or private symbolic obligation.

It is a present-tense communicative relationship between a performer and an audience.

The audience is not a passive consumer. It is the relational endpoint that makes idol performance meaningful.

---

# 10. Sakura’s key self-authorship statement: “At first, yes. But now it’s different.”

Sakura cannot hear Mana directly, but after Mana’s speech she feels that the heart is telling her something.

Mana challenges her through Mei/Makino:

Did Sakura start idolhood only because the heart guided her? Did she have no will of her own?

Sakura answers:

> **「最初はそうでした」**
>
> **「でも 今は違います」**

This may be Sakura’s most important agency statement in the anime so far.

She says that after becoming companions with Kotono and everyone else, and after beginning to pursue a large shared goal, she realized:

> **「私のやりたかったことはこれなんだって」**

This is what *I* wanted to do.

The heart created an opening.

Experience produced commitment.

Relation turned an unexplained impulse into authored desire.

That is a much stronger solution than either of the following extremes:

- “Sakura was always purely self-determined”; or
- “Mana’s heart has been controlling Sakura all along.”

The anime permits a mixed origin.

A desire can begin through something not fully chosen and become genuinely one’s own through living it.

---

# 11. The transplanted heart becomes part of Sakura rather than a foreign claim on her

Makino tells her:

> **「この心臓もさくらの一部ってことなんだよ」**
>
> **「麻奈の心臓も含めてさくらなんだ」**

This is the episode’s cleanest ontology of bodily inheritance.

The heart is historically Mana’s.

The heart is presently Sakura’s body.

Those propositions are not mutually exclusive.

That means the anime rejects a model in which Sakura is merely carrying a sacred object that still properly belongs to Mana.

The donor history matters.

But transplantation has created a new embodied person whose life cannot be morally reduced to the donor’s unfinished identity.

This is a particularly strong personhood claim:

> **origin does not erase incorporation.**

---

# 12. Sakura initially interprets the donor revelation as responsibility to perform Mana correctly

After accepting that the heart is part of her, Sakura nevertheless reaches a dangerous conclusion.

She says that knowing it is Mana’s heart has clarified the issue:

> **「あの歌は私が歌わなきゃって」**

She feels she must sing the unreleased song.

And she plans to tell Kotono:

> **「この心臓は麻奈さんのものだから 私が責任持ってちゃんと歌うって」**

This is important because it shows that accepting bodily inheritance does not automatically solve symbolic succession.

Sakura converts biological provenance into **representational duty**.

If this organ came from Mana, then perhaps Sakura owes Mana a correct continuation.

Mana immediately rejects that logic.

---

# 13. Mana finally abolishes the successor category

Mana says she truly regrets never getting to perform the song. She wanted people to hear it.

But:

> **「でも誰かに歌ってもらいたいとは思ってない」**

She does not want someone else to perform it *for her* in order to erase her regret.

If the regret were to be fulfilled in that sense:

> **「私がステージで歌うしかない」**

Only Mana herself could do it.

Then comes the decisive pride statement:

> **「これでもアイドルとしてのプライドはあるの」**
>
> **「たとえ私と同じ歌声を持っている子にだって 私よりうまく歌えるとは思わない」**
>
> **「私の歌は 私だけのものだから」**

This is not merely arrogance.

It is a defense of artistic personhood.

No amount of physical resemblance, voice resemblance, donor continuity, or technical ability can make one performer ontologically interchangeable with another.

Episode 08 had shown the successor role becoming uninhabitable for Kotono and dangerously attractive for Sakura.

Episode 09 supplies the missing answer:

> **the successor role itself is a category error if it means replacing the performer.**

Inheritance is possible.

Replacement is not.

---

# 14. Mana redirects her unfinished wish from “perform my song” to “show me your future”

Mana says she wants Kotono and Sakura to reach the top.

She believes both have that potential.

But:

> **「2人とも 他の誰でもない 自分だけの歌を歌えるようにならなきゃ」**

They must learn to sing songs that belong to themselves and nobody else.

Then:

> **「私のことなんてどうでもいいから 仲間と一緒に自分の歌を探して」**

Forget about me; find your own song with your companions.

And:

> **「お互いにライバルとして切磋琢磨して 頂点を目指してほしい」**

She wants them to sharpen one another as rivals and aim for the summit.

Mana then finally admits a truth about her own past:

> **「私もLizNoirの莉央がいたから あそこまで頑張ることができたんだし」**
>
> **「当時の私は それを認めるのが嫌だったけど」**

Rio helped make Mana as strong as she became, even if Mana did not want to admit it then.

This is important because it de-mythologizes solitary miracle.

Even Mana’s legendary excellence had a relational condition.

Her final unfinished wish becomes:

> **「私は月のテンペストとサニーピースが頂点に立つところを見たいの」**

and she says seeing that would fulfill her one remaining unfinished wish.

The unfinished wish is no longer:

> complete my interrupted performance.

It is:

> **let the people who came after me become fully themselves.**

---

# 15. Episode 09 makes a productive distinction between the physical heart and `心残り`

The episode repeatedly plays with different meanings of “heart.”

There is the literal transplanted **心臓**.

There is Sakura’s chest-centered feeling and `胸の高鳴り`.

There is Mana’s **心残り** — lingering unfinished desire/regret.

A central point emerges:

> transferring the physical heart does not transfer the dead person’s unfinished life as an obligation.

Sakura receives the organ.

She does not thereby become responsible for completing Mana’s exact performance history.

Mana’s `心残り` can only be transformed into something ethical when it becomes a wish for the living to author futures of their own.

The episode therefore distinguishes **biological continuity** from **vocational destiny**.

---

# 16. Kotono listening to Sakura’s heartbeat transforms the donor revelation into sisterly grief rather than successor mythology

After Sakura tells Kotono the heart really is Mana’s, Kotono makes a strange, intimate request:

> **「心臓の鼓動 聞かせてほしいの」**

Sakura allows her to place her head against her chest.

The scene becomes exceptionally quiet. The dialogue drops away for a long interval, and direct audio inspection shows the sound field reducing enough for repeated low-frequency pulses to become the dominant audible structure during the silent stretch.

Visually, Kotono closes her eyes against Sakura’s chest and begins to cry.

Then:

> **「お姉ちゃん」**
>
> **「ごめんね」**

This scene could easily have become grotesquely substitutional — Kotono treating Sakura as though she were literally Mana.

The direction instead makes it about **contact with a trace**.

Sakura does not disappear from the scene. She actively allows the contact, watches Kotono, and remains herself.

Kotono is not given her sister back.

She is given a heartbeat carried by another living person.

That distinction is crucial.

The transplanted heart becomes neither sacred relic nor successor certificate.

It becomes a material site through which grief can finally touch something real.

---

# 17. The heartbeat scene completes a long movement from enclosure to embodied relation

Kotono’s grief has repeatedly been staged through space:

- Episode 02: curled, enclosed, alone;
- Episode 03: leaving the cool room;
- Episode 08: reading and closing the diary, then walking into shared night;
- Episode 09: placing her body in direct contact with another living body carrying part of Mana’s material history.

This is a major progression.

Earlier, memory lived in:

- empty rooms;
- the diary;
- private recordings;
- inherited songs.

Now memory is physically encountered through another person who cannot be collapsed into the remembered person.

That is one of the anime’s most concrete representations of grief becoming relational without becoming substitutional.

---

# 18. Mana and Sakura’s simultaneous physical weakness is a major formal clue

Soon after the heartbeat encounter, Mana suddenly loses strength and says:

> **「ちょっと力が入らないだけ」**
>
> **「なんか体が重くて」**

The episode then cuts to Sakura, who also says:

> **「あれ なんか調子が…」**

The cross-cut is conspicuous.

At the same time, fragments of `song for you` enter the soundtrack.

This provides stronger formal evidence for the proposed link among:

- Sakura’s transplanted heart;
- Sakura’s bodily state;
- Mana’s ghostly persistence;
- and the approaching performance of Mana’s unreleased song.

However, the exact mechanics remain unresolved.

The prospective claim should remain:

> **STRONG FORMAL HYPOTHESIS, not yet explanatory fact.**

---

# 19. Sakura resolves Episode 08’s remaining successor burden by choosing a final Mana-voice performance

Sakura tells Kotono:

> **「私ね 麻奈さんの歌声で歌うのは 次のステージで最後にしようと思う」**

This is the prospective resolution Episode 08 had not yet earned.

She is not claiming that the Mana-like voice was fake.

Nor is she pretending the transplanted history can be removed.

She makes a professional choice about how that resemblance will function publicly.

The voice may be part of her body and history.

But she does not want her future idol identity to remain organized around performing **as Mana’s continuation**.

The significant point is not vocal rejection.

It is the boundary she places around representational use.

---

# 20. Sakura’s public statement is an act of self-authorship under conditions of private certainty

Before the performance, Sakura asks to speak publicly.

She tells the audience that she had a heart transplant three years ago and that the transplanted heart saved her life.

She also says she believes it helped bring her to the stage.

Then she directly addresses the rumors.

But instead of publicly asserting the private conclusion that Mana was the donor, she explains the transplant system’s confidentiality and says:

> **「なので うわさの真偽についてはお答えできません」**

She cannot answer whether the rumor is true.

This is ethically important.

Sakura knows what she privately believes.

Makino knows what the narrative is presenting as true.

The public does not become entitled to the donor identity simply because the information would create a compelling idol story.

Sakura protects:

- donor privacy;
- recipient privacy;
- institutional fairness;
- and her own right not to convert a medical history into public spectacle.

Then she says she has imagined many different possibilities about who the donor may have been, but every imagined donor produced the same feeling:

> gratitude.

The public speech therefore refuses **biographical possession** and chooses **ethical gratitude**.

---

# 21. `song for you` becomes a different act when Sakura sings it

Sakura says:

> **「今日のステージは 今の私のすべての思いを込めて 歌わせてください」**

That line changes the status of the unreleased song.

The composition originated with Mana.

Kotono received its first private hearing.

Sakura performs it publicly.

But Sakura does not claim to be completing Mana’s exact performance.

She loads the song with **her present feelings**.

This resolves the apparent contradiction with Mana’s declaration that `私の歌は私だけのもの`.

A composition can be transmitted.

A performed song as lived communicative act cannot be duplicated.

Mana’s unperformed version remains unrecoverable.

Sakura’s version is not the performance Mana would have given.

It is a new performance created from inherited material.

That is exactly the distinction between inheritance and replacement.

---

# 22. The song title becomes radically plural

The song is `song for you`.

The Episode 09 performance gives “you” several possible referents at once:

- the unknown donor as Sakura publicly frames it;
- Mana as private truth;
- Kotono, who first heard the song;
- the companions who made Sakura’s idol desire her own;
- the audience Mana says Sakura must face;
- and potentially all those from whom Sakura has received courage.

The episode does not force a single exclusive referent.

That plurality is appropriate to its inheritance ethics.

A song originally attached to one relationship can accrue new relational meaning without erasing the prior one.

---

# 23. The staging moves Sakura from object of the camera to author before the audience

The episode’s opening and climax form a useful visual opposition.

### Opening

Sakura sits in front of press branding and cameras while other people ask what her resemblance to Mana means.

### Climax

Sakura stands before the audience and chooses:

- what medical facts to disclose;
- what she will not disclose;
- how to describe her gratitude;
- and what feelings she will put into the song.

The stage therefore becomes a site of authorship rather than merely exposure.

This is especially important in an idol story, where visibility can easily be mistaken for agency.

Episode 09 distinguishes them.

Being visible because the press wants your story is not the same as speaking because you have chosen your story’s terms.

---

# 24. SUNNY PEACE’s group presence prevents the performance from becoming a Sakura/Mana séance

The performance ultimately belongs to SUNNY PEACE, not to Sakura standing alone as Mana’s vessel.

The visual grammar repeatedly restores the five-member formation, collective lighting, and group embrace after the result.

That matters because the whole Episode 09 argument would collapse if the climax isolated Sakura as a mystical successor.

Instead, inherited material enters a unit identity that already exists.

Sakura is the focal point of the donor story.

She is not the entire social body of the performance.

The final result — SUNNY PEACE’s victory — therefore belongs to a living collective.

---

# 25. The episode title names inheritance as incorporation rather than obedience

The closing card reads:

> **「もらった勇気を抱きしめて」**

The important verbs are **もらう** and **抱きしめる**.

Something is received.

Then it is held close.

The title does not say:

- return the courage;
- repay it by living the donor’s life;
- reproduce its source;
- or prove oneself worthy by imitation.

The courage becomes intimate material that the recipient carries forward.

This is the episode’s cleanest compact statement of inheritance.

- Episode 09’s governing distinction is inheritance versus succession/replacement.
- Public donor rumor represents an attempt to convert medical history into a marketable succession narrative.

- The episode distinguishes physical heart (`心臓`), bodily feeling (`胸の高鳴り`), and unfinished desire (`心残り`) to argue that biological continuity does not create vocational destiny.

## Claim audit 02 — “The heart can be inherited; a self cannot”

### Historical claim

V1's best compact statement was that the heart, song, and courage can pass onward, but a self cannot.

### V2 disposition

**CONFIRMED / STRENGTHENED.**

The Japanese dialogue supplies an unusually explicit textual foundation for that proposition.

Makino says:

> **「この心臓もさくらの一部ってことなんだよ」**
>
> **「麻奈の心臓も含めてさくらなんだ」**

while Mana later says:

> **「私の歌は 私だけのものだから」**

Those statements solve different halves of the same problem.

Makino prevents the heart from remaining foreign property inside Sakura.

Mana prevents Sakura from becoming foreign property of the heart's history.

The combination yields a stronger V2 formulation:

> **The received organ belongs to Sakura's embodied present; the donor's personhood remains irreducibly Mana's.**

## Claim audit 03 — Donor revelation

### Historical claim

V1 treated Episode 09 as the point at which Sakura's heart is revealed to be Mana's.

### V2 disposition

**CONFIRMED WITH EPISTEMIC QUALIFICATION.**

The anime's inner narrative now presents the donor relation as true. Sakura states it, Makino says he realized it during the hospital visit, the chronology aligns, and Mana herself accepts the conclusion.

But the episode simultaneously insists that ordinary transplant confidentiality prevents public verification.

The distinction should remain visible in all later ledgers:

- **narrative/private certainty:** Mana -> Sakura donor relation confirmed;
- **publicly verifiable institutional information:** donor identity undisclosed.

This makes Sakura's public refusal to confirm the rumor an ethical act rather than evasion.

## Claim audit 04 — Public discourse authors Sakura's body

### Historical claim

V1 argued that the transplant rumor escalates the earlier resemblance discourse into a public attempt to read Sakura's body as biography.

### V2 disposition

**CONFIRMED / STRENGTHENED.**

The visual architecture makes the claim particularly strong.

Episode 09 begins with Sakura seated before media branding and cameras while reporters ask what her resemblance to Mana means. Later, reporters pursue the donor rumor directly. Her body becomes evidence within a story others want to complete.

The climax reverses the arrangement. Sakura stands before the audience and chooses:

- what medical history to disclose;
- what donor information she will not disclose;
- how to name her gratitude;
- and what feelings she will put into the song.

The progression is therefore:

> **public interpretation of body -> private knowledge -> chosen public testimony.**

V2 would describe this not merely as privacy protection but as **narrative self-authorship under conditions of unavoidable visibility**.

## Claim audit 05 — Sakura converts gratitude into debt

### Historical claim

V1 identified Sakura's dangerous trait as not passivity, but a tendency to convert gratitude into obligation. Because she received life, she feels she must repay the donor through continuation.

### V2 disposition

**CONFIRMED / STRENGTHENED.**

This is one of the episode's clearest psychological structures.

After Makino tells Sakura that Mana's heart has become part of Sakura herself, Sakura does not immediately conclude that she is free from obligation. She instead says that now she understands she **must** sing the song and plans to tell Kotono that because the heart was Mana's, she will take responsibility and perform properly.

That is the precise point where bodily incorporation still fails to produce symbolic autonomy.

The episode must therefore solve two problems separately:

1. **Is the heart part of Sakura?** Yes.
2. **Does carrying it make Sakura responsible for becoming Mana's continuation?** No.

Mana supplies the second answer.

## Claim audit 06 — Mana's anti-copy philosophy

### Historical claim

V1 treated Mana as the strongest defender of Sakura's individuation and emphasized `私の歌は私だけのもの` as artistic ownership.

### V2 disposition

**CONFIRMED / STRENGTHENED.**

The prospective V2 reading places even more weight on the possessive grammar.

Mana does not deliver a generic sermon about authenticity. She says:

- this is **my** regret;
- this would require **me** onstage;
- **my** song belongs to **me**;
- Sakura must find **her** song;
- Kotono must find **her** song.

The point is not purity from influence. It is non-interchangeability of persons.

That makes Mana's pride ethically productive:

> **her refusal to be replaceable protects Sakura from being consumed by resemblance.**

This is one of the most interesting paradoxes in the anime: Mana's ego is part of her generosity.

This is the point at which the anime earns the strong no-successor formulation.

# STEP 5 — EPISODE 09 DELTA AND CUMULATIVE ANIME STATE

## Episode 09 dramatic function

Episode 09 completes the two-episode inheritance crisis begun in Episode 08.

Episode 08 destroys Kotono's belief that succession can ground her own career.

Episode 09 destroys the remaining belief that Sakura should therefore become the more successful successor.

The resolution is not amnesia, purification, or independence from influence.

It is:

> **inheritance without replacement.**

That principle now governs the anime's treatment of:

- transplanted body;
- voice resemblance;
- artistic repertoire;
- grief;
- professional legacy;
- rivalry;
- and received courage.

---

## Character-state delta — Sakura

### Before Episode 09

- confirmed heart-transplant recipient;
- donor unconfirmed;
- natural singing voice resembles Mana;
- voice emerged after surgery;
- heart-guided impulses repeatedly influence major decisions;
- public comparison has already caused her to distort her natural singing;
- Kotono has asked her to carry Mana's unperformed song.

### After Episode 09

- Mana's donor relation is privately/narratively confirmed;
- Sakura explicitly says that idolhood is now her own desire, not merely something the heart directed her toward;
- the transplanted heart is conceptually incorporated into Sakura rather than treated as foreign sacred property;
- Sakura recognizes the danger of successor-oriented performance;
- she chooses a boundary around the Mana-like singing mode;
- she publicly owns her transplant history while refusing to disclose donor identity;
- she performs inherited material through her own gratitude and present feelings;
- her professional voice expands to include sustained serious public testimony.

### Governing delta

> **Sakura moves from being interpreted as a possible continuation of Mana toward becoming the author of what Mana's inheritance means inside Sakura's life.**

---

## Character-state delta — Kotono

### Before Episode 09

- has abandoned the need to become Mana;
- has chosen Tsuki's `our summit`;
- still asks Sakura to perform the lost Mana song;
- does not know Mana's ghost exists.

### After Episode 09

- learns that Sakura carries Mana's heart;
- hears the heartbeat directly;
- finally gives bodily, uncontrolled expression to grief in contact with a living person;
- does not reclaim Sakura as Mana or abandon the Sakura relationship;
- remains unaware that Mana's conscious ghost is physically nearby.

### Governing delta

> **Kotono gains material continuity without relational resurrection. The heart is real; the sister relationship is still absent.**

That unresolved difference is likely to matter more than the donor reveal itself.

---

## Character-state delta — Mana

Episode 09 gives Mana her clearest artistic and ethical philosophy so far.

She is now established as someone who:

- fiercely believes in the non-interchangeability of performers;
- refuses to make Sakura the instrument of her unfinished regret;
- wants Sakura and Kotono to find their own songs;
- understands rivalry as a productive relation because Rio helped sharpen her;
- reformulates her remaining wish around the future of SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest;
- continues to decide unilaterally that Kotono should not know Mana's ghost exists;
- exhibits new physical weakness apparently linked to Sakura.

### Governing delta

> **Mana's artistic ethics become more generous at the same moment her interpersonal paternalism becomes more visible.**

That contradiction should not be resolved prematurely.

---

## Character-state delta — Makino

Makino now:

- recognizes the donor connection;
- suspects the heart sustains Mana's ghost;
- continues to enforce donor/privacy boundaries;
- offers the strongest incorporation formulation for Sakura's body;
- mediates between Mana and Sakura without deciding Sakura's final artistic meaning;
- nevertheless participates in withholding Mana's ghost from Kotono.

The managerial ethics ledger therefore receives both a positive and a cautionary entry:

> **positive:** bodily safety, confidentiality, and performer authorship;
>
> **caution:** participation in unilateral emotional-information control.

---

## Relationship-state delta — Sakura / Kotono

The relationship has now crossed several thresholds:

1. accidental encounter;
2. mutual training;
3. rivalry;
4. direct concern;
5. radical honesty;
6. embodied grief trust.

Episode 09 makes Sakura the person against whom Kotono can physically break without losing control of the relationship.

It also makes Kotono a person whose request Sakura can reinterpret rather than merely obey.

Their connection remains deeply Mana-mediated in historical origin, but increasingly cannot be explained by Mana alone.

---

## Relationship-state delta — Sakura / Mana

The relationship becomes direct in ethical consequence without becoming unmediated mystical possession.

Sakura cannot ordinarily hear Mana; Mana's position moves through Mei and Makino. This is important because it prevents the dead donor from simply entering Sakura's consciousness and dictating meaning.

Mana's central gift to Sakura is paradoxically a refusal:

> **Do not become me for my sake.**

That refusal is the condition under which gratitude can remain compatible with selfhood.

## Relationship-state delta — Mana / Kotono

This remains the episode's most important unresolved relationship.

Kotono now has:

- the last-song memory;
- the donor truth;
- the heartbeat;
- renewed grief.

But she still lacks:

- direct communication with Mana;
- explanation;
- apology from or to Mana in ordinary reciprocal form;
- knowledge that the person she mourns is still consciously present.

Mana's professional wish for Kotono is generous.

Mana's control over Kotono's access to her is not obviously so.

Episode 10 inherits that contradiction intact.

# Japanese-language findings

## 1. `預かる`

Sakura says she will **預かる** Mana's song.

This is an unusually good verb for the episode's ethics because it implies temporary custody without ontological ownership.

The later drama then asks what responsible custody should mean.

## 2. `私の歌`

Mana's repeated first-person possession gives artistic individuality its strongest language.

`私の歌` does not merely mean copyright or repertoire.

It means the inseparability of performer, feeling, address, and lived history.

## 3. `自分だけの歌`

Mana's wish for Kotono and Sakura is not generic originality.

`自分だけの歌` means a song whose performed meaning cannot be exhausted by another person's identity.

## 4. `心臓` / `胸` / `心残り`

Episode 09 deliberately places literal organ, embodied feeling, and unresolved desire into proximity without treating them as identical.

The physical heart can transfer.

The dead person's `心残り` cannot simply be transplanted as duty.

## 5. `抱きしめる`

The title's verb is incorporation rather than repayment.

Courage is held close, not returned as debt.

# Audiovisual and formal findings

## 1. Cameras: objectification -> self-address

Opening press space frames Sakura as an object about whom meaning is requested.

Climactic stage space lets Sakura choose the terms on which she becomes publicly legible.

## 2. Heartbeat isolation

The heartbeat scene's reduced dialogue and low-frequency pulse foregrounding make bodily continuity literally audible.

Because the visual image does not turn Sakura into Mana, the scene holds trace and difference together.

## 3. Kotono's body

The longitudinal recovery grammar is now sufficiently repeated to classify as a high-confidence formal pattern:

> **recovery is tracked through what Kotono's body can do in relation to space and people, not through a simplistic warm/cool color binary.**

## 4. Mana/Sakura cross-cut weakness

This is the strongest current formal clue for a heart/ghost energetic relation, but it remains explanatory-open.

## 5. Group-stage grammar

SUNNY PEACE's five-member staging prevents Sakura's donor revelation from being aesthetically converted into a solo resurrection spectacle.

## 6. Ending-credit continuation

The episode extends `song for you` through the credits and integrates hospital/recovery imagery, making the ending space a biographical epilogue rather than detachable credits decoration.

# Genuinely new or more strongly foregrounded V2 claims

1. **Mana's last song was also a last attempted sisterly repair.** This makes Kotono's later transfer of the song to Sakura more relationally loaded than a simple inherited professional task.
2. **`預かる` is a useful custody metaphor for ethical inheritance.** Sakura initially takes custody of material before learning she cannot take custody of Mana's personhood.
3. **Makino's incorporation sentence is one of the anime's most important personhood statements.** The heart's donor history survives without leaving the organ morally foreign to Sakura.
4. **Mana's anti-possession ethic and her paternalism toward Kotono coexist.** Artistic self-authorship does not automatically make Mana fully respectful of relational autonomy.
5. **The climax is an authorship reversal of the episode's opening media arrangement.** Sakura moves from being asked what she *is* to choosing what she will *say*.
6. **The donor plot distinguishes private truth from public proof rather than treating revelation as automatic entitlement to disclosure.**
7. **The heartbeat sequence completes a bodily grief progression from self-enclosure to consented interdependence.**

# Episode 09 final synthesis

Episode 09 could very easily have collapsed the franchise into sentimental replacement metaphysics.

It possesses every ingredient:

- a legendary dead idol;
- a surviving recipient;
- a transplanted heart;
- a similar voice;
- bodily intuition;
- an unfinished song;
- a grieving younger sister;
- a literal ghost.

The easy answer would be:

> **Mana lives on through Sakura.**

The anime chooses the harder answer.

Something of Mana does live forward.

Her heart beats.

Her song survives.

Her courage survives.

Her memory reorganizes other people's lives.

Her rivalry with Rio helped produce an excellence that later idols inherit as professional history.

But none of those facts makes Sakura into Mana.

The decisive ethical movement of Episode 09 is not purification from inheritance. It is **authorship of inheritance**.

Sakura does not need to deny that the heart came from Mana.

Kotono does not need to deny that hearing it matters.

Mana does not need to pretend that the unperformed song ceased to be hers.

SUNNY PEACE does not need to erase the song's provenance in order to perform it.

The characters instead learn to keep provenance and personhood separate.

That is why Makino's formulation and Mana's formulation belong together:

> **Mana's heart is now part of Sakura.**
>
> **Mana's song remains Mana's in the sense that no one can reproduce Mana's performed self.**

The two principles look contradictory only if identity is understood as property that must belong wholly to one origin.

Episode 09 proposes something more relational.

A person is made partly from what she receives.

But receiving does not surrender authorship.

That is why Sakura's most important line may not be the donor revelation. It is:

> **「最初はそうでした。でも 今は違います」**

At first, yes.

Now, no.

Her career may have begun in an impulse she did not fully understand.

Living it with other people changed the status of that impulse.

It became her desire.

And that gives us the strongest Phase-0.5 formulation of Sakura so far:

> **Sakura does not become herself by proving Mana has nothing to do with her. She becomes herself by taking what came from Mana so fully into her own life that Mana can no longer exhaust the explanation of who Sakura is.**

Kotono receives a parallel lesson.

The heart allows her to touch a material remainder of her sister.

She cries against Sakura's body.

But the heartbeat does not restore ordinary sisterhood.

The living person holding her remains Sakura.

Memory has become relational without becoming resurrection.

And Mana herself supplies the final paradox.

Her greatest act of generosity is saying:

> **Do not become me.**

Her greatest unresolved act of control is saying, in effect:

> **Kotono still cannot be allowed to know that I am here.**

Episode 09 therefore does not complete the grief story.

It completes the **successor** story.

The next problem is no longer:

> Who will become Nagase Mana?

The anime has answered:

> **No one.**

The harder question remains:

> **What do the living owe the dead—and what do the dead owe the living—once replacement is finally off the table?**

That is the state Episode 10 inherits.
