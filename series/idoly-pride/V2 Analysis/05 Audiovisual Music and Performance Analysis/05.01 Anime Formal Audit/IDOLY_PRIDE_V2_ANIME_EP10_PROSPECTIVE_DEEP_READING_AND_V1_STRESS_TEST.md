# Executive thesis

Episode 10 takes the anti-substitution conclusion of Episode 09 and turns it into a professional test.

Sakura and SUNNY PEACE no longer merely say that Sakura should not become Nagase Mana. They voluntarily give up the most obvious competitive advantage tied to that resemblance: Sakura decides that the Mana-like singing voice will no longer define their future performances, and the unit decides it must stop relying on Sakura's exceptional resemblance as the principal source of its light. They will find **SUNNY PEACE's song**.

At the same time, the episode gives TRINITYAiLE a parallel problem. Tendo Rui has pursued an image of the **absolute idol** since adolescence, but the episode reveals that her drive is entangled with Asakura: the father who, according to Rui's mother, chose work over family and whose agency Rui deliberately entered. In the present, Rui remains preoccupied with whether Asakura recognizes her and concludes that she must surpass Mana to receive the recognition she seeks. Her two unitmates answer by telling her not to carry that burden alone and to entrust her back to them: that is what TRINITYAiLE exists for.

The semifinal therefore places two groups onstage whose central figures are both tempted by solitary inheritance/recognition structures and whose units answer by making ambition collective.

SUNNY PEACE's new song makes the argument explicit:

> **「1人きりじゃ 叶えられない / 夢だってあるでしょ？」**
>
> There are dreams that cannot be fulfilled alone.

and:

> **「乗り越えてく勇気を / みんなからもらえたよ」**
>
> I received from everyone the courage to overcome it.

and:

> **「みんなで良かった そう思えるよね」**
>
> We can think: I'm glad it was all of us.

The most important proof arrives after SUNNY PEACE defeats TRINITYAiLE. Rui tells Sakura that she had assumed Sakura's voice struck her because it resembled Mana's, but now realizes she was wrong.

The strongest prospective formulation is therefore:

> **Episode 10 argues that self-authorship becomes real only when it survives comparison. Sakura stops using Mana as the organizing explanation of her voice, SUNNY PEACE stops using Sakura's resemblance as the organizing explanation of the group, and Rui—the rival most invested in Mana as a standard—recognizes Sakura's performance as independently moving.**

A second formulation applies to both groups:

> **An idol may carry an intensely personal reason to reach the summit, but a group becomes artistically real when that reason is redistributed into mutual trust rather than borne as one person's private burden.**

# 1. Rui's dream predates the father revelation, but her professional path becomes entangled with him

The cold open begins with Rui's own narration:

> **「昔からアイドルになるのが夢だった」**

She dreamed of becoming an idol from long before the present conflict.

Her aspirational image is already maximal:

> more radiant than any picture-book world or night sky.

Then the episode places her at the Ban Production audition. Rui explains that when she decided to apply, her mother finally told her who her father was:

> **「この人が私の父親だと」**
>
> **「私と母より仕事を選んだ人だと」**

The source therefore does **not** support reducing Rui's idol dream to a simple attempt to win paternal approval. The dream existed beforehand.

But it does establish that Rui knowingly enters the professional institution run by the father who was absent from her family life. From that moment, idol achievement and the problem of paternal/professional recognition become structurally entangled.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Rui dreamed of being an idol before applying to BanPro; her mother told her before the audition that Asakura was her father and had chosen work over Rui and her mother.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Entering his agency transforms idolhood into one of the only available arenas in which Rui can force herself to become professionally legible to the absent father.

**GUARDRAIL:** Episode 10 does not establish that paternal recognition is Rui's only or original motive.

# 2. Rui defines the desired self as an "absolute idol"

Asked what kind of idol she wants to become, Rui answers immediately:

> **「トップアイドルです」**

Then defines it:

> **「人々に夢を与え まぶしく輝き」**
>
> **「常に目が離せない」**
>
> **「そんな絶対的アイドルに 私はなります」**

She wants to give people dreams, shine dazzlingly, and become an **absolute idol** from whom people cannot look away.

The word **絶対的** matters. Rui's desired identity is not merely competent, successful, or popular. It is formulated in singularizing language: indisputable, commanding, impossible to ignore.

The audition visuals sharpen the tension. Rui is positioned alone before the evaluators; the adult professional world sits across the room from her. Her resolve is communicated through frontal close-up and then an extreme close-up of the eyes.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Rui's early idol ideal is structured around becoming impossible to overlook. Given the immediately preceding father disclosure, this creates a powerful recognition subtext even though the text does not say, "I became an idol so my father would notice me."

# 3. SUNNY PEACE chooses to stop making Sakura's Mana resemblance the unit's competitive center

After the prior round, Sakura tells Makino that they want to change songs.

She says:

> **「麻奈さんの歌声で歌うのは 今日で最後って決めていたんです」**

and explains that the others agreed.

The decisive unit-level line is:

> **「ここからはサニーピースだけの歌を見つけようって」**

Then another member articulates why:

> **「さくらちゃんの歌声に頼ってばかりじゃなくて」**
>
> **「私たちも輝かなきゃダメですよね」**

This is more than Sakura's personal individuation.

SUNNY PEACE recognizes that a group can reproduce the same substitution problem collectively. If Sakura's miraculous resemblance to Mana becomes the unit's principal competitive resource, the other four risk becoming supporting infrastructure for a successor narrative.

Their answer is not to suppress Sakura. It is to demand a form in which **all five** have to generate the light.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Sakura decides to stop using the Mana-like singing voice as SUNNY PEACE's future default; the unit explicitly wants a song belonging to SUNNY PEACE and says the other members must shine rather than rely on Sakura's voice.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Episode 10 shifts the anti-copy argument from individual identity to group authorship.

# 4. Sakura and Rui represent two different relationships to Mana as a professional standard

Rui asks Sakura about `song for you`, recognizes its emotional power, and asks:

> **「あなたに勝てば 麻奈さんを超えることになるかしら」**

Sakura apologizes and tells her she will not sing that song going forward:

> **「私は私たちの歌で…」**
>
> **「サニーピースの歌で 頂点を目指します」**

Rui asks whether that song will let Sakura surpass Mana.

Sakura answers:

> **「分かりません」**
>
> **「でも もう決めたから」**

That reply is one of the clearest results of Episode 09's self-authorship argument.

Sakura no longer needs to prove that her chosen path will surpass Mana before she is permitted to choose it.

She does not know whether it will.

She chooses it anyway.

Rui then accepts the terms:

> **「なら やることは変わらないわね」**
>
> **「お互い頂点を懸けて全力を尽くしましょう」**

This is important. Rui remains Mana-centered, but she does not demand that Sakura accept Rui's framing in order to count as a legitimate rival.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Sakura has moved from inherited comparison toward chosen uncertainty. Rui has not yet left comparison behind, but she is capable of respecting a rival whose evaluative framework differs from hers.

# 7. Rui names the real wound: Asakura recognizes Mana, not her

While watching elite performance material and discussing Asakura, Rui says:

> **「ただあの人は私じゃなくて 長瀬麻奈を認めてる」**
>
> **「私にはまだ何かが足りない」**

The other members notice that Asakura becomes more emotional when speaking about Mana.

> **「あの人にトップアイドルと認めてもらうには」**

> **「長瀬麻奈を超える存在にならないといけないんだから」**

This is the strongest textual evidence for the recognition structure introduced in the cold open.

Rui is not chasing Mana's record merely because records are fun. She is trying to solve a recognition problem whose judge is Asakura.

The members respond not by telling her the desire is childish, but by collectively adopting the challenge:

> **「それなら私たちも 歴史を変えるぐらいのアイドルになればいい」**

and eventually:

>
> **「この3人で」**

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Rui explicitly wants Asakura to recognize her as a top idol and believes she must surpass Mana to achieve that recognition.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** The father-daughter wound has become professionalized. Rui is trying to obtain through elite performance a form of recognition that her family history denied her.

**GUARDRAIL:** The episode still does not show Rui asking Asakura to behave as a father; her demand remains translated into professional terms.

# 8. SUNNY PEACE replaces Sakura's inherited bodily certainty with chosen interpersonal trust

Before the semifinal, the girls discuss TRINITYAiLE's overwhelming record and performance quality.

Sakura reassures them:

> **「私たちだってTRINITYAiLEに負けないいいステージできるよ 絶対」**

Someone asks the crucial question:

> **「それってまた胸のドキドキが教えてくれてるの」**

Sakura answers:

> **「ううん」**
>
> **「でも私は信じてる」**

Then she names each teammate individually:

> Chisa.
> Haruko.
> Shizuku.
> Rei.

This is one of Episode 10's most important lines and a major cumulative Sakura development.

Earlier, Sakura's body frequently supplied directional certainty before conscious explanation. Episode 09 established that idolhood had become Sakura's own desire rather than mere obedience to that heart.

Episode 10 now gives her a major professional prediction **without** heart-guidance.

She believes because she trusts the people with whom she has trained.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Sakura explicitly denies that her `ドキドキ` is telling her they can win; she grounds her confidence in belief in the four SUNNY PEACE members.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Sakura's epistemology is maturing from unexplained bodily certainty toward relationally earned judgment. The heart remains part of her history, but it is no longer required to authorize every consequential choice.

# 10. Sakura calls the semifinal "in a sense, my debut"

Immediately before the stage, Sakura says:

> **「私にとってはある意味 今日がデビューなんだなって」**

Why?

> **「麻奈さんの歌声を借りずに」**
>
> **「自分の歌でステージに上がるんだもん」**

A teammate answers:

> **「今のさくらちゃんの歌声 私好きだよ」**
>
> **「みんなも きっと好きになる」**

This language slightly complicates Episode 09's embodiment argument. Sakura's Mana-like voice had emerged naturally after surgery and was experienced as a voice comfortable to produce, yet Sakura now retrospectively describes that performance mode as **borrowing Mana's singing voice**.

The safest interpretation is not that the prior voice was biologically "not Sakura." Episode 09 explicitly rejected that model.

Rather, Episode 10 shows Sakura reorganizing the *meaning* and use of vocal resemblance. She chooses a current singing register/style she experiences as more directly authored and labels the public debut of that choice as a second debut.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Sakura herself calls this performance a debut-like event because she will sing without using the Mana-like vocal mode and will perform "my song."

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Professional identity can contain more than one debut: first entry into the industry, and later entry into a publicly self-authored form.

# 16. Episode-level synthesis

Episode 10 is not primarily a tournament upset.

The semifinal result matters because it stress-tests two theories of idol identity.

Rui begins from an **absolute-idol** ideal and a recognition problem organized around Asakura and Mana. Yet TRINITYAiLE's actual strength depends on mutual trust, shared choreography, and the willingness to distribute Rui's burden across three people.

Sakura begins the series with a body that points her toward idolhood and a voice that other people interpret through Mana. By Episode 10 she makes a consequential prediction without consulting the heart, names trust in four living teammates as her evidence, changes her performance voice, and calls the stage a new debut.

SUNNY PEACE then wins with a song whose core proposition is explicitly that some dreams cannot be reached alone.

The victory would be thematically weaker if it simply proved Sakura was secretly stronger than Mana.

Instead, Rui supplies the crucial recognition:

> Sakura moved me for a reason other than Mana.

The strongest synthesis is therefore:

> **Episode 10 converts individuation from an internal ethical claim into public artistic evidence. The girls do not become themselves by purging inheritance; they become themselves when inherited history stops being the only explanation capable of making their performances legible.**

And at unit scale:

> **The route beyond the singular miracle is not lesser individual talent. It is a form of excellence in which several people become conditions of one another's highest performance.**

# Frozen cumulative state entering the historical comparison

# 1. V1 was right to center Rui's recognition wound—but V2 corrects its causal origin

The historical pass strongly associates Rui's pursuit of absolute top-idol status with two figures in front of her future:

- Nagase Mana;
- Asakura, her father.

That is correct **for the present Rui of Episode 10**.

The frozen source-native pass, however, preserves an important chronological fact that an elegant father-recognition thesis can easily flatten:

> **「昔からアイドルになるのが夢だった」**

Rui dreamed of becoming an idol **before** her mother told her that Asakura was her father and had chosen his work over Rui and her mother.

The sequence matters.

Rui's career is therefore not best described as:

> abandoned daughter becomes idol in order to force father to recognize her.

A stronger formulation is:

> **A pre-existing idol dream becomes entangled with an unresolved paternal-recognition problem when Rui knowingly chooses the professional institution run by the father who left.**

That is psychologically richer because it preserves the legitimacy of Rui's vocation.

She does not need to discover at the end that she "never really wanted to be an idol." She did.

The problem is that one of the most important evaluative relationships in her career becomes fused to a family wound, and Mana becomes the measuring instrument through which she thinks that wound can be resolved.

### Disposition

**QUALIFIED / STRENGTHENED**

The V1 recognition thesis survives. Its causal formulation should be tightened so paternal recognition is understood as a later entanglement within a genuine earlier dream, not the complete origin of that dream.

# 4. Sakura's second "debut" is real—but her first debut was not false

# STEP 5 — CANONICAL EPISODE-10 SYNTHESIS

## Final V2 thesis

Episode 10 is the episode where **self-authorship becomes answerable to other people**.

Episode 9 established a philosophical proposition:

> Sakura can receive Mana's heart without becoming Mana.

Episode 10 asks whether that proposition can survive the stage.

Sakura answers by relinquishing the safest inherited competitive explanation she has. She will no longer organize SUNNY PEACE around the Mana-like vocal mode. More importantly, the other four members refuse to organize SUNNY PEACE around Sakura alone. Their answer is not to make the miracle smaller. It is to make authorship wider.

The new song therefore begins from a proposition that would have been impossible for Mana's solo-idol model to express in the same form:

> **some dreams cannot be fulfilled alone.**

The result is not merely friendship winning a tournament.

The five women turn accumulated relation into professional advantage:

- Chisa's courage;
- Haruko's experience and pacing;
- Shizuku's audience-side knowledge;
- Rei's technical discipline;
- Sakura's emotional ignition.

The group becomes difficult to explain through any single member.

That is precisely why it becomes an ethical answer to the successor problem.

TRINITYAiLE supplies the more difficult mirror.

They are not a false collective.

Their stage is probably the strongest evidence yet that the three members have built an extraordinary embodied system. Their song is relational. Their movement is relational. Yu's care is relational. Sumire's physical affection is relational. Rui explicitly knows they have climbed together.

Yet Rui's **destination** remains partially authored by people outside that relation.

She wants Asakura to recognize her.

She thinks surpassing Mana is the way to make that happen.

She wants the undefeated record to become proof.

The unit is therefore already capable of a healthier future than the criterion Rui is still using to measure the present.

SUNNY PEACE's victory does not punish TRINITYAiLE for this contradiction.

That would be too easy.

TRINITYAiLE gives its best performance and loses.

The loss instead removes one of Rui's instruments of certainty.

The record cannot save her.

And Asakura immediately tells her why:

> records are broken.

The thing he values is `物語`—something narratable through a shining stage.

Then Rui's own perception changes.

She had assumed Sakura moved her because Sakura sounded like Mana.

The new performance forces her to say:

> **No. I was hearing Sakura.**

That recognition is one of the series' cleanest demonstrations of why performance matters in *IDOLY PRIDE*.

The stage is where a claim about identity becomes falsifiable.

Sakura says:

> I will sing as myself.

Rui watches.

Rui updates.

The stage has made Sakura's selfhood communicable.

The episode then refuses to let Rui metabolize defeat solely in articulate professional language. She says all the right things—and means them. Then she cries.

The special `realiser` ending begins precisely there and gives the unit a vocabulary Rui cannot yet fully speak:

> stop chasing only the back in front of you;
> trust the warmth you have physically received;
> make it real **together**;
> with you, the horizon remains open.

That is why the title belongs to both rivals:

> **「自分だけじゃ辿り着けない場所」**

SUNNY PEACE discovers a place made by five-person authorship.

TRINITYAiLE discovers, through loss, that its own three-person structure may matter more than the solitary proof Rui expected the summit to provide.

The two units are not converging aesthetically.

They are converging ethically on a shared recognition:

> **excellence is not diminished when another person becomes part of the conditions that make it possible.**
