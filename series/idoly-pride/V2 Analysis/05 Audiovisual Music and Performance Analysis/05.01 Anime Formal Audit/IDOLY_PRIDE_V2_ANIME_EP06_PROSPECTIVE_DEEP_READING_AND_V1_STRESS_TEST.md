# STEP 1 — INDEPENDENT PROSPECTIVE DEEP READING

# 1. Executive thesis: debut makes difference public

Episode 06 is the point at which Hoshimi's internal differentiations become **public artistic identities**.

Episodes 03-05 built the social machinery required for this moment. The original trainees learned reciprocal correction, productive friction, differentiated leadership, and finally how two professional groups could exist without destroying a larger Hoshimi community.

Episode 06 asks what those distinctions mean once an audience can actually see and hear them.

SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest therefore do not merely receive names. Their differences become public through:

- costume;
- song language;
- visual palette;
- stage lighting;
- facial affect;
- choreography/blocking;
- public speech;
- leadership behavior;
- and the explicit institutional language of rivalry.

The strongest prospective formulation is:

> **Debut makes difference public: two groups formed from one Hoshimi community become legible as different artistic worlds, while rivalry becomes a shared method of ascent rather than a social rupture.**

This is also the episode where “debut” stops functioning as a distant dream and becomes professional accountability.

---

# 2. The dream becomes a deadline

Makino's announcement at approximately **00:06-00:36** is almost comically abrupt:

> **「デビューライブが決まった」**

The girls need a moment to process it.

Kotono then supplies the characteristic operational question:

> **「いつですか」**

The debut will take place on the special Hoshimi Festival stage where Mana once debuted.

That sounds, initially, like another act of inheritance.

But the episode immediately surrounds that symbolic location with concrete professional infrastructure:

- choreography and audio materials;
- a serious Tokyo studio;
- an external instructor;
- media interviews;
- radio appearances;
- publicity;
- VENUS entry;
- AI-mediated ranking;
- professional rivals;
- and public judgment.

The girls are leaving a protected developmental environment.

Makino states the threshold explicitly:

> **「デビューすれば経歴もキャリアも関係ない」**

Once they debut, prior career and biography do not function as exemptions.

Their aspirations are becoming public claims.

# 3. Mana's old professional world is inherited as infrastructure, not shrine

At approximately **02:07-03:40**, Suzu calls the rehearsal location the legendary studio that trained Mana.

Haruko immediately moderates the statement: “legendary” is excessive, although LizNoir and TRINITYAiLE also work there.

That distinction matters.

The studio has history, but its present meaning is labor.

The instructor recognizes Kotono as Mana's younger sister, tells her to work hard, and then proceeds to stretching and time discipline.

The past is acknowledged without consuming the professional encounter.

This continues the anime's increasingly healthy handling of Mana's legacy:

> **history provides context and infrastructure; it does not automatically provide identity.**

---

# 4. Sakura's warmth does not automatically become media fluency

The formal interview sequence at approximately **03:44-04:16** reveals an important distinction.

Sakura has already become one of Hoshimi's best interpersonal bridge-builders. She notices anxiety, asks questions, and lowers relational barriers.

But formal media speech makes her stumble:

> **「は…はい」**
>
> **「よ…よろしくお願いちま…」**
>
> **「ます」**

She later admits that she repeatedly tripped over her words and barely remembers what she said.

So Episode 06 differentiates:

> **interpersonal openness**

from

> **professional self-presentation.**

Sakura is warm because she is immediate, not because she has already learned to package “Kawasaki Sakura, idol” for public consumption.

This is a useful voice baseline for later development.

---

# 5. Haruko's prior career becomes transferable competence

Haruko answers the same interview smoothly:

> she previously worked solo;
> the group is a fresh start;
> she intends to think of herself as a newcomer again.

The others immediately react to how fluently she speaks.

Episode 05 established Haruko as living institutional continuity. Episode 06 shows what that continuity can **do**.

She has a professional register.

Her experience gives the group:

- public phrasing;
- context for press work;
- confidence that unfamiliar situations can be survived;
- and an example of how to acknowledge the past without being immobilized by it.

Haruko and Sakura therefore already represent two different forms of warmth:

- Sakura reduces distance through immediacy.
- Haruko reduces friction through competence and social smoothing.

The distinction is prospectively visible even before later unit material elaborates it.

# 6. Kotono can finally call improvement 「楽しい」

The most important small character beat of the early episode occurs around **04:21-05:03**.

The instructor is strict. Suzu is visibly rattled.

Kotono says the teacher is demanding but careful.

Then:

> **「それに　楽しいよ」**

Why?

> **「自分たちがどんどんうまくなっていくのが分かるから」**

She can feel them getting better.

The other girls are startled precisely because Kotono has used the word **fun**.

Kotono herself does not understand why the remark seems surprising.

This is a major longitudinal change because Episode 02 had framed training as pain she was willing to **endure**:

> any severe lesson was acceptable if it moved her toward Mana's summit.

Episode 06 gives the activity a present-tense reward.

Kotono's pleasure is not identical to Mei's.

Mei experiences movement as spontaneous sensory play.

Kotono experiences pleasure through **visible improvement produced by discipline**.

But the larger change is unmistakable:

> **idol labor can now feel good while it is happening.**

The road is beginning to belong to Kotono rather than existing only as debt payment toward a dead sister's destination.

V2 does not attribute this change solely to Mei. Reciprocal review, group belonging, leadership, professional instruction, and accumulated improvement all plausibly contribute.

# 7. Rei converts media uncertainty into trainable form

When asked whether she handled the interview well, Rei explains that dance competitions already gave her some interview experience.

Then:

> **「ダンスと同じで　練習と慣れね」**

Like dance, it is practice and habituation.

This extends the Episode 05 Rei model.

Rei handles uncertainty by finding the form hidden inside it.

Now the method applies not only to:

- posture;
- rhythm;
- movement;
- stamina;

but also to:

- speech;
- media interaction;
- public presentation.

Her discipline is becoming a general professional epistemology:

> **identify the demanded form -> repeat it -> reduce uncertainty through familiarity.**

The legacy analysis additionally emphasized that Rei's plain conversational grammar after Episode 05 confirms that the reduction in keigo-distance was not a one-scene resolution. V2 accepts that observation after source reinspection, but because it was not separately foregrounded in the prospective freeze it is recorded as a source-supported historical refinement rather than blinded convergence.

# 8. Hoshimi remains a larger identity than either unit

>

Yet the girls still possess a larger institutional “we.”

Episode 05's final all-Hoshimi photograph therefore becomes ritual behavior in Episode 06.

Professional differentiation does not erase the community that preceded it.

# 9. TRINITYAiLE makes Mana's legacy a problem outside Hoshimi

The episode's middle section shifts to TRINITYAiLE at approximately **08:51-10:46**.

They win another live battle.

The interviewer immediately frames Rui against Mana's undefeated-from-debut record.

Rui says she has no specific opponent she wishes to face and is confident the group can defeat any idol because they have aimed for the top from the beginning.

Then Asakura sharply interrupts the record logic.

When Rui begins talking about closing in on Mana's streak, he says:

> **「それがどうした」**

and:

> **「くだらないことにとらわれているうちは　長瀬麻奈には追いつけない」**

As long as she remains caught by something so trivial, she cannot catch Mana.

The exact Rui/Mana/Asakura history remains unresolved at this prospective boundary.

But Episode 06 clearly establishes:

> **Mana's legacy exerts competitive gravity beyond Hoshimi.**

The dead idol has become a metric in the public professional field.

Asakura's warning introduces an important paradox:

> treating Mana entirely as a metric may be precisely what prevents someone from moving beyond her.

This is structurally parallel to Kotono's earlier problem without requiring the claim that Rui and Kotono have identical psychology.

# 10. The summit keeps moving because the living keep moving

Episode 04 had already forced Kotono to ask whether working harder than past Mana would be enough to defeat the **current** LizNoir and TRINITYAiLE.

Episode 06 supplies the answer in dramatic form:

TRINITYAiLE is still winning.

Its record is still changing.

Its members still aim higher.

Asakura expects an eventual collision with Hoshimi if the new groups prove strong enough.

The professional field is not a frozen monument to Mana.

The summit is moving because other living people are climbing it at the same time.

# 11. The night before debut differentiates anxiety by method

From approximately **10:55-12:24**, the episode becomes unusually domestic again.

That is important because professional pressure does not make everyone anxious in the same way.

### Nagisa — recursive relational anxiety

Nagisa worries about singing incorrectly, missing choreography, and most importantly becoming a burden to everyone else.

Her anxiety is interpersonal even when the event is professional.

Haruko responds with environmental care: better tea, calm, lowered activation.

### Saki — procedure as self-directed care

Saki builds a checklist of everything that must still be done.

Her explanation is revealing:

> **「大事な時こそ　いつもどおりにしたいから」**

and she wants to be able to tell herself that the preparation is complete and everything presently possible has been done.

Saki's organization is not only something she imposes outwardly.

It is how she grants **herself** permission to stop fearing unperformed obligations.

### Chisa and Shizuku — one more repetition

They ask to practice once more.

### Rei — disciplined pacing

Rei responds:

> **「あまり根を詰めると　明日に響くわ」**

If they push too hard, tomorrow will suffer.

She allows one more run, then requires thorough stretching.

This is an important Episode-05-to-06 development.

Rei has not become less disciplined.

She has expanded the optimization target.

Rest, recovery, and next-day performance have become part of professional rigor.

The relational lesson changes the **model of discipline**, not the personality that values it.

# 12. Sakura and Kotono become rivals without ceasing to be connected

The nighttime conversation around **12:32-13:57** is one of the episode's central relational scenes.

Sakura asks whether Kotono is nervous.

Sakura says:

> **「ドキドキしてる」**

The heartbeat language now carries ordinary anticipatory ambiguity—fear and excitement—rather than functioning only as unexplained directional intuition.

Kotono says she does not know exactly what she feels. She wanted debut quickly, but now that it is tomorrow she cannot settle.

Sakura then names the consequence of VENUS entry:

> **「そしたら私たちはライバル」**

Kotono answers:

> **「トップの席は1つしかない」**

If both keep rising, they will eventually stand on the same stage.

Then:

> **「その時は　負けないから」**

Sakura answers that she will work hard enough for them to stand together:

> **「私も頑張るね　一緒のステージに立てるように」**

The exchange closes on the same competitive promise.

This is not a friendship breakdown.

Rivalry gives the relationship a future.

> **Affiliative rivalry becomes a shared horizon: each wants the other to become strong enough to meet again under conditions where victory matters.**

The historical analysis sharpened this further by arguing that the rivalry is becoming **direct rather than Mana-mediated**. V2 accepts that formulation after reinspection. The conversation is conspicuous precisely because the girls speak about **each other** and the future stage, not about which one better represents Mana.

# 13. The crescent moon reinforces—but does not dictate—the rivalry scene

The scene is staged beneath a prominent crescent moon.

One episode after Makino's explicit sun/moon metaphor, this is difficult to treat as meaningless.

But V2 retains a formal guardrail:

> moon imagery is **reinforcement**, not deterministic character coding.

Sakura is physically present in the lunar scene too.

That matters.

The sun/moon division does not create separate metaphysical universes. The girls remain capable of sharing one visual and relational space while their artistic identities diverge.

# 14. Makino finally states a positive vocational desire

At approximately **14:12-15:19**, Makino and Mana remember Mana's own debut.

Mana insists she was not nervous.

Makino remembers:

- putting clothing on incorrectly;
- repeatedly checking the mirror;
- last-minute refusal;
- the ordinary chaos hidden underneath a later legend.

The scene continues the anime's anti-mythic humanization of Mana.

Then Makino explains what the memory means to him.

He remembers running around preparing, practicing with Mana, exchanging ideas, and even arguing.

When she finally stood onstage:

> **「そんな苦労も全部　いっぺんに吹き飛んだ」**

Then:

> **「俺はあの時のあの瞬間をもう一度見たくて」**
>
> **「今もこの仕事を続けてるのかもしれないな」**

This is the clearest anime-native description so far of Makino's **positive** managerial vocation.

He is not only continuing because of grief, guilt, or institutional inertia.

He wants to witness the moment when preparation becomes public transformation.

This reconnects directly to Episode 01's stage threshold:

> Makino prepares, evaluates, travels, argues, helps—and then stops where the performer must act for herself.

The ethical risk is worth tracking. Wanting to “see that moment again” could become repetition-seeking if Makino began forcing new idols into one remembered pattern.

But Episodes 03-05 already provide strong counterevidence: he explicitly rejects Mana as a reproducible solo model and differentiates the girls around their own strengths.

So the present judgment is:

> **Makino wants the transformation of emergence to recur; he does not presently appear to want Mana herself to recur.**

The historical Episode 06 analysis reached the same distinction independently of this V2 pass. This claim is therefore strongly confirmed.

# 15. One physical stage becomes two different artistic worlds

Episode 06's central formal achievement occurs between roughly **15:33 and 19:34**.

SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest perform on the same Hoshimi festival stage.

Chronology naturally contributes to the visual change—the performances progress from late-day warmth toward deeper night.

But natural time does not explain the whole distinction.

Costume, controlled stage lighting, song language, facial affect, pose vocabulary, and blocking intensify the separation.

The same architecture becomes two different artistic spaces.

This advances a formal idea developed across earlier episodes:

### Episode 01

Mana crosses the stage threshold; Makino must stop and watch.

### Episode 02

An ordinary outdoor location becomes a stage because Sakura and Kotono sing there.

### Episode 03

LizNoir's elite performance makes an ordinary rehearsal room perceptually function as stage space.

### Episode 06

The same literal stage becomes two distinct worlds because two groups perform it differently.

The emerging strong inference is:

> **A stage in IDOLY PRIDE is not merely architecture. It is a relation among performer, form, audience, and the meaning created in the moment.**

# 16. SUNNY PEACE: Sakura's private heartbeat becomes public unit language

Before entering, Sakura says:

> **「大丈夫」**
>
> **「きっとうまくいくから」**
>
> **「いくよ　サニーピース」**

The stage is dominated by warm late-day light, cream/red/gold costumes, open smiles, outward-facing gesture, and an inviting visual field.

Representative retained-frame diagnostics across the lyric-bearing performance give:

- average normalized luminance: approximately **0.708**;
- mean saturation: approximately **0.369**;
- mean RGB balance: approximately **0.841 / 0.680 / 0.595**.

These values support the obvious warm/high-luminance construction, but they are diagnostics rather than governing evidence.

The lyrics provide the stronger analytical basis.

They move from ordinary life becoming newly radiant:

> **「見慣れたはずの帰り道」**
>
> **「昨日よりも輝いて見える」**

toward bodily self-knowledge:

> **「胸の鼓動が教えてくれる」**

and:

> **「ちゃんと自分の気持ち　向き合えたら」**

Then:

> **「私は私って　もっと強くなれるね」**

And outward relation:

> **「明るく君を照らしたい」**

Finally:

> **「5つのピース　重ねれば　星になるんだ」**

This is the first strong public artistic formulation for SUNNY PEACE.

Most importantly, Sakura's private language has become a collective song.

From Episode 02 onward, `胸` and `ドキドキ` described a bodily certainty Sakura herself could not explain.

Episode 06 transforms that private epistemology into public artistic language:

> heartbeat -> face one's own feeling -> become more strongly oneself -> illuminate another person -> combine five different pieces into one star.

Nothing in this prospective reading requires the later transplant explanation.

The song belongs analytically to the living Sakura we have actually met so far.

# 17. SUNNY PEACE's brightness is sonic as well as visual, but not simply “louder”

Mixed-program audio diagnostics over the lyric-bearing Sunny section produce an average spectral centroid of roughly **2936 Hz**.

The comparable Tsuki section is roughly **2472 Hz**.

This supports the perceptual impression that Sunny's arrangement carries a brighter upper-frequency balance.

However, broad loudness and lyric-section dynamic ranges are not radically different enough to support a simplistic “Sunny is louder/faster, Tsuki is quieter/slower” theory.

The more defensible contrast is:

- timbral brightness;
- palette;
- gesture;
- lyrical orientation;
- and relational affect.

All audio measurements come from mixed episode audio, not isolated music/vocal stems, and are supporting diagnostics only.

# 18. Tsuki no Tempest: the dark becomes something the group can author

Kotono's pre-performance call is concise:

> **「走り抜けましょう」**
>
> **「月のテンペスト」**

The visual world shifts sharply.

The unit appears in deep cobalt/navy and silver, with lower-key lighting, stronger blue saturation, silhouettes, beams, sharper pose geometry, and more restrained/intense facial affect.

Representative retained-frame diagnostics across the lyric-bearing Tsuki section give:

- average normalized luminance: approximately **0.476**;
- mean saturation: approximately **0.510**;
- mean RGB balance: approximately **0.363 / 0.491 / 0.653**.

So the visual distinction from Sunny is materially large as well as perceptually obvious.

But the lyrics are the real center of the unit's first public identity.

They begin:

> **「夜に咲く花一輪　儚く美しいその姿」**

Then tears blur focus.

A strong breath is taken.

The singer looked straight ahead but:

> **「だけども少し震えてた」**

Then:

> **「本当の暗闇じゃ　何も見えなかったんだと」**

and:

> **「『輝くこと』意味を知る」**

The song then makes light explicitly relational:

> **「月明かり照らす光　君の光」**

and:

> **「初めて感じた真の願い」**

It refuses permanent emotional stability:

> **「満ち欠けのたび揺れてく　心の影」**

The answer is not elimination:

> **「大切に受け入れ　もう迷わない」**

Finally:

> **「君が照らすから　輝いてるんだ」**

This is an extraordinarily strong fit with the Kotono arc already visible by Episode 06.

Her development has never required becoming sunny.

Episodes 02-05 progressively showed that she could:

- leave dark space;
- use other people's eyes;
- affect and be affected by peers;
- lead;
- admit uncertainty;
- and experience enjoyment;

without surrendering her restrained, nocturnal emotional grammar.

Now the group makes that structure public.

> **Darkness remains. Trembling remains. Heart-shadow remains. Another person's light makes shining possible anyway.**

The historical analysis captured this as the transformation from blue darkness as enclosure into blue darkness as authored stage space. That conclusion is independently recovered and strongly confirmed.

# 19. Tsuki's song is explicitly anti-self-sufficient

The key line:

> **「君が照らすから　輝いてるんだ」**

is particularly important because early Kotono imagined legitimacy through a solo model.

The debut song does not celebrate solitary brilliance.

Its central theory of light is received and relational.

One shines **because another person illuminates**.

That makes the song a meaningful public contradiction of the earliest Kotono architecture even before Kotono has consciously articulated the full implication.

This is why V2 prefers to call the songs **aspirational constitutions**.

They state a way of being that the girls may not yet fully inhabit.

The remaining anime can test whether biography catches up with performance language.

# 20. Direct light and relational light: a useful but provisional contrast

By the end of the two performances, Episode 05's sun/moon hypothesis has become substantially more concrete.

### SUNNY PEACE

The song's light tends toward:

- warmth;
- bodily immediacy;
- self-recognition;
- outward illumination;
- pieces combining into a collective star.

### Tsuki no Tempest

The song's light tends toward:

- darkness acknowledged rather than erased;
- trembling and uncertainty;
- phases/shadow accepted;
- light received from another person;
- reciprocal/relational illumination.

Both units are relational.

The difference is not:

> one values others, one values self.

It is the **direction and texture** through which relation becomes light.

The historical analysis called this “direct light” versus “relational/reflected light.” V2 accepts that as a strong interpretive shorthand, while refusing to import the later game corpus as proof that the mature unit philosophies are already complete.

The prospective freeze independently recovered that one inherited stage becomes two new artistic worlds.

> Episode 02 used a Mana-associated location **and Mana's `First Step`**.
>
> Episode 06 returns to Mana's debut location but gives the stage **new songs belonging to SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest**.

The result is one of the cleanest images of inheritance in the anime so far:

> **the location can be inherited without the living being required to reproduce the dead person's artistic voice.**

Mana's history creates the conditions.

The living determine what fills them.

# 22. Makino's confession receives two different answers—not “two Manas”

Immediately before the performances, Makino admits that he remains in management partly because he wants to witness the debut transformation again.

He then receives two aesthetically incompatible answers on the same stage.

The historical analysis called them “two different miracles.”

V2 qualifies that phrase.

“Miracle” is already a loaded category in the anime, especially around Mana, and the Episode 06 ending will explicitly say that taking a first step is **not** a miracle.

The stronger formulation is:

> **Makino receives two different transformations.**

That is exactly what protects his vocational desire from becoming replication.

The moment he wants can recur without the person or style recurring.

# 23. Rivalry becomes Hoshimi's public developmental principle

After both songs, the groups state:

> **「私たちは今日　2組同時にデビューしました」**

and:

> **「私たちは共に切磋琢磨し」**
>
> **「刺激し合いながら　成長していきたいと思っています」**

The private Sakura/Kotono rivalry conversation has now become institutional language.

This is not merely “we happen to compete.”

The units publicly define mutual competition as part of how they intend to grow.

So rivalry functions as a **relationship technology**:

- a future encounter;
- a comparison point;
- pressure;
- recognition;
- and mutual sharpening.

The anime is therefore refusing a false opposition between care and competition.

# 24. The debut MC preserves personality rather than producing one generic “idol voice”

The post-performance greeting around **20:26-21:40** is deliberately imperfect.

Suzu turns her introduction into something like an election campaign:

> **「星見市が誇る　星見プロの成宮すずを」**
>
> **「どうぞよろしくお願いいたしますわ」**

She has to be told this is not an election.

Haruko considers deploying her established self-introduction gag and is discouraged.

Mei casually says:

> **「サニピと月スト　どっちもよろしく」**

compressing the two units back into one friendly Hoshimi sentence.

Rei notices a supporter from her supermarket job and has to be prompted to wave.

Shizuku, likewise pushed to address the audience, produces:

> **「頑張る…　ます」**

The malformed phrase is excellent characterization.

Professionalization has increased their public obligations.

It has not erased the linguistic strategies that made them distinct people.

The stage adds registers rather than replacing personalities.

# 25. Three different meanings of “being seen” are emerging

The historical analysis isolated a useful framework that was not explicitly separated in the prospective freeze.

Episode 06 contains at least three forms of visibility:

### Private recognition

A friend understands something another person cannot easily say.

### Professional observation

Trainer, interviewer, manager, VENUS system, and competitor evaluate ability or presentation.

### Audience reception

Fans respond to the actual live performance and public persona.

These are not interchangeable.

Sakura can be excellent at private recognition and poor at formal interview speech.

Rei can be professionally composed and still awkward when an ordinary acquaintance becomes audience.

Shizuku can understand idols deeply as a spectator and still struggle to speak from the stage side of the relationship.

V2 retains this framework as a useful post-comparison addition, but does not count it as blinded V1/V2 convergence.

# 26. Mana's concluding line preserves uncertainty

After the debut, Mana says:

> **「今はまだ小さな輝きだけど」**

They are still only a small light.

Then:

> **「琴乃たちはきっと…」**

The sentence remains incomplete.

That incompletion should remain intact.

Mana can perceive possibility without becoming an omniscient narrator who guarantees what the future must be.

This continues the anime's pattern of granting the dead character perspective without giving her interpretive sovereignty over everyone else.

# 27. The ending explicitly says that beginning is not miracle

The ED contains:

> **「踏み出す一歩は　奇跡じゃない」**

The first step is not a miracle.

That line is especially striking after the franchise's repeated language of Mana as miraculous.

The current girls still carry:

> **「戸惑いも　迷いも」**

confusion and hesitation.

# STEP 5 — CANONICAL EPISODE-06 SYNTHESIS

Episode 06 is best understood as the anime's first public test of whether the Hoshimi project has actually produced anything new.

That question matters because Mana is everywhere.

The debut occurs in her city and on the stage where she began. The girls train in a studio associated with her. Her old instructor recognizes Kotono. TRINITYAiLE is publicly measured against her undefeated record. Kotono is still climbing toward the summit associated with her. Mana's ghost stands beside Makino as he remembers the frighteningly ordinary girl behind the legend.

The episode could therefore have become an elaborate memorial reenactment.

It does not.

The crucial development is that history increasingly provides **conditions rather than answers**.

The studio belongs to the living professional ecosystem. The instructor treats Kotono as a trainee who must work. Makino has learned that Mana cannot become a reproducible management doctrine. TRINITYAiLE demonstrates that even a spectacular living group can become trapped if Mana is reduced to the metric that defines progress.

And when the Hoshimi festival stage finally fills, the girls do not merely replay `First Step`.

They make new worlds.

SUNNY PEACE takes Sakura's peculiar bodily grammar—heartbeat, immediate feeling, movement toward what matters—and converts it into something collective. The song asks its singers to face their feelings, become more strongly themselves, illuminate another person, and combine five pieces into a star. Its warmth is not simple optimism. It is a theory that self-recognition can become outwardly shareable.

Tsuki no Tempest does something complementary rather than opposite. It does not deny darkness, fear, tears, trembling, shadow, or fluctuation. It makes those conditions the reason shining acquires meaning. Its strongest line is relational rather than heroic: one shines because another person illuminates. Kotono's original solo logic is therefore publicly surrounded by a song whose very theory of light depends on other people.

The girls do not yet necessarily **live up to** everything their songs say.

That is why the songs are best treated as founding constitutions.

They announce what kind of communities these units might become.

Later episodes can ask whether their biographies ever match the artistic language they already perform.

For Kotono, Episode 06 is especially important because the developments that began quietly in Episodes 03-05 become visible at once.

She can call improvement fun.

She can feel unsettled without interpreting uncertainty as failure.

She can recognize Sakura as a rival in her own right.

And the blue-black world that once overwhelmed her now becomes a performance world she authors with four other people.

The night has not disappeared.

Her relation to it has changed.

Makino's confession places the entire debut in another frame. His managerial desire is not only to prevent tragedy or discharge an obligation to Mana. He wants to see preparation become radiance again.

Episode 06 then protects that desire ethically by showing him two radically different versions of the transformation.

The repeatable thing is not Mana.

It is the event in which a person or group crosses from preparation into public presence and becomes capable of affecting an audience in a way that cannot be fully predicted beforehand.

Finally, the joint MC refuses to let professional identity erase ordinary personality.

Suzu sounds like Suzu with a bigger microphone. Mei compresses two unit identities back into one friendly sentence. Rei is awkward when someone from her ordinary working life becomes audience. Shizuku can understand idol spectatorship far better than she can yet speak from the idol side of the exchange.

Debut therefore adds registers rather than replacing selves.

The strongest final formulation is:

> **Episode 06 is the moment Hoshimi proves that inheritance can become authorship: the living can accept the dead person's places, tools, history, and professional world without allowing those inheritances to dictate the only song they are permitted to sing.**

And the episode's final formal counterpoint is the ED:

> **「踏み出す一歩は　奇跡じゃない」**

The first step is not a miracle.

That may be the most important distinction between Mana's historical gravity and the girls' future.

They do not have to become miraculous before they are allowed to begin.

They have to begin, work, relate, fail, differentiate, and keep moving long enough for their own light to acquire form.
