# 0. Responsibility and authority

This is the third evidence-bearing SUNNY PEACE character ledger in Phase 2. Its responsibility is to reconstruct **Haruko as a changing adult professional across aspiration, repeated rejection, talent and suitability, kindness, institutional labor, Mana comparison, grief, age, ordinary adult life, competitive ethics, plural performance work, succession, and eventual career finitude** rather than to freeze her as the unit's veteran caretaker.

P2-B1 Haruko retains eight analytical boundaries.

1. **Mana is not the origin of Haruko's vocation.** Haruko wants the stage before Mana enters Hoshimi. Mana later becomes friend, rival, witness, and source of belief, but not the author of Haruko's desire.
2. **Suitability is neither meaningless nor sovereign.** The source repeatedly tells Haruko she lacks conventional idol aptitude; mature Haruko herself accepts real aptitude differences. The correction is not that talent is unreal. It is that aptitude alone does not possess jurisdiction over whether a person may choose a finite vocation.
3. **Support is professional value, but support can become self-erasure.** Haruko's care, organization, pedagogy, and boundary-setting are real strengths. The same disposition becomes maladaptive when she decides that stronger performers should take the stage while she disappears backstage.
4. **Persistence is longitudinally revised.** The childhood imperative never to give up remains important, but mature Haruko can acknowledge finite careers, credible alternatives, and eventual limits. Continued idolhood is therefore a renewed present choice, not an eternal command.
5. **Age awareness is not retirement authority.** Haruko is self-conscious about being older than many peers and explicitly recognizes the finite youth structure of idol work. Neither fact establishes that she should already leave.
6. **Mana succession is not restoration.** Unfinished songs, roles, and promises can survive through successors and transformed use without requiring literal copresence, impersonation, or Haruko's retirement into heritage maintenance.
7. **Ordinary adult life remains live.** University, friendship, leisure, household competence, adult sociality, and counterfactual ordinary futures remain part of Haruko's personhood rather than evidence against idol vocation.
8. **Material career viability remains open.** The snapshot establishes meaningful professional pluralization and later success, but not Haruko's terminal career arrangement, lifetime income security, physical sustainability, or final exit path.

The frozen substantive inheritance remains `IDOLY_PRIDE_V2_PHASE1_FINAL_BASELINE.md`. The completed Sakura and Rei ledgers are current dependencies where Haruko intersects SUNNY PEACE reciprocal care, health, burden-sharing, ambition, and adult authority. Dedicated Haruko/Mana, Haruko/Rei, Hoshimi legacy, and SUNNY PEACE unit ledgers remain later canonical homes for claims that outgrow individual-character responsibility.

# 2. Three clocks: chronology, disclosure, validation

## 2.1 Fictional chronology

The most useful causal chronology for Haruko is:

1. as a child at the first NEXT VENUS Grand Prix, Haruko receives live idol radiance and wants to become a performer who shines likewise;
2. Hoshimura Megumi helps the lost child and supplies the vocabulary of an idol needing a heart that does not lose, break, or give up;
3. Haruko spends years auditioning and failing, repeatedly hearing that she lacks conventional presence, glamour, memorability, and specialness;
4. Megumi challenges the inference that being unsuited removes Haruko's right to aspire;
5. Haruko reaches newly forming Hoshimi, where Saegusa initially calls her too kind for a ranked industry but ultimately selects that same combination of kindness and persistence as institutionally valuable;
6. Haruko becomes Hoshimi's first idol;
7. Mana later enters Hoshimi, quickly reveals the charisma Haruko has been told she lacks, becomes Haruko's close friend, and rapidly overtakes her professionally;
8. Haruko experiences painful comparison and envy without resentment, while Mana's belief and companionship help Haruko continue;
9. Haruko's own market results remain weak; Saegusa later acknowledges agency failure to convert her potential into work while Haruko also accepts responsibility for her then-current ability;
10. Haruko and Mana make several stage-facing promises, including future shared performance and a joint gratitude song;
11. Mana dies before some of those promises can be literally fulfilled;
12. Hoshimi enters crisis; Haruko's schedule empties, she performs office/support labor, loses the live benchmark she wanted to catch, and seriously considers how to arrange her future;
13. new Hoshimi trainees arrive as both a material threat to scarce work and a possible source of renewed change;
14. Haruko wants into the Sakura-centered new unit but cannot straightforwardly ask; Makino reveals he already considers her structurally necessary because the younger members need her experience, care, persistence, and idol commitment;
15. Haruko joins SUNNY PEACE expecting to make others shine, then recognizes herself as a member who also shines through shared relation;
16. after the NVGP, she can finally reintroduce herself to Megumi as Hoshimi Production's idol and SUNNY PEACE member;
17. adult bond material establishes university, friends, leisure, household competence, age awareness, and ordinary-life option-space alongside idol labor;
18. Haruko receives a credible musical-theater recruitment offer based on singing and dance, seriously considers it, and confronts the possibility that musical work may suit her better than idol work;
19. she admits that effort cannot erase aptitude differences and that Mana possessed unusually strong idol suitability;
20. with Makino and the group's care, Haruko rechooses idolhood while acknowledging the musical chance may not return and idolhood itself cannot last forever;
21. post-BIG4 Rei overtrains; Haruko uses rest, monitoring, strategic relational space, and eventually withdrawal from competition to protect Rei's body;
22. Haruko ignores insults against herself but becomes forceful when rivals demean Rei, articulating a norm of competition without degradation;
23. Haruko later organizes the old unfinished Mana-Haruko gratitude song for current Hoshimi, first trying to finish it alone and then opening authorship to the wider group;
24. she entrusts the song to current successors while explicitly refusing self-retirement, saying she will continue shining as an idol with them;
25. a revival of the musical Mana once led gives Haruko another performance opportunity: she is cast not in the old friend role she once sought, but as the protagonist in Mana's former role;
26. Haruko imagines impossible copresence with Mana, explicitly marks it as imagination, and understands the achieved continuity as delayed role/stage inheritance;
27. when unsure how to lead the musical cast, Haruko asks about Mana's precedent, then recognizes that hospitality and care are already her own leadership competencies and resolves to lead in her own way;
28. the snapshot therefore ends not with Haruko's retirement, but with a plural professional horizon in which idol work, musical performance, support competence, adult everyday life, and eventual career finitude remain simultaneously real.

## 2.2 Disclosure chronology

The anime first presents Haruko prospectively as the older, experienced, warm SUNNY PEACE member whose patience and practical judgment stabilize younger performers. That surface is accurate but incomplete. The later SUNNY origin discloses that the veteran role is the product of years of rejection, early Hoshimi founding history, painful Mana comparison, agency stagnation, and a near-exit period after Mana's death.

Later Hoshimi expansion then revises the origin's apparently absolute persistence slogan. The adult Haruko can say aptitude is real, an alternative vocation may fit her better, and idol careers are finite. The 2023 Rei-Haruko event tests whether kindness is competitively serious; the Mana-promise event tests whether legacy can be transmitted without restoration; and the admitted musical card tests whether succession can fulfill a promise without pretending the dead predecessor is literally present.

P2-B1 therefore does not backfill adult nuance into the child who heard 'never give up,' and it does not leave the child slogan as the final authority over adult life. Disclosure order matters because the later text is structured as a revision of what persistence means.

# 3. Longitudinal argument

Haruko's story is easiest to flatten when persistence is treated as the whole character. The primary source instead makes persistence the beginning of a problem that Haruko spends years learning to govern.

As a child she receives the idol system as wonder. Megumi's lesson is simple enough for a child: an idol needs a heart that does not lose, break, or give up. Haruko takes that lesson seriously because the industry immediately gives her reasons to need it. She auditions for years and repeatedly receives the same market judgment: not enough presence, not enough specialness, not memorable enough. When the girls who passed auditions Haruko failed are already standing on major stages, the external verdict starts to become self-description.

Megumi's intervention is not 'you are secretly the most talented.' It is more radical and more limited: even if Haruko is unsuited, why should only suited people be allowed to aspire? That question becomes the ethical seed of the later career. It preserves the right to choose under unequal aptitude without requiring Haruko to deny the inequality.

Saegusa's founding judgment adds the institutional dimension. He sees Haruko's kindness as potentially incompatible with a competitive system, yet also sees that a future agency will need idols capable of caring for other idols without abandoning themselves. Haruko therefore enters Hoshimi not despite possessing only non-performance virtues, but because the agency imagines those relational capacities as part of professional value.

Mana then makes the aptitude problem concrete. Haruko sees in Mana exactly the immediate charisma and specialness that audition panels said Haruko lacked. Mana debuts later and surpasses her quickly. Haruko is jealous; the text permits the jealousy. But Mana is also a friend who celebrates Haruko, needs Haruko's presence, and tells her that persistence has relational value. The bond works because comparison and affection are both real.

After Mana's death, the system that sustained Haruko fails on several layers at once. Hoshimi is in crisis, work disappears, office labor replaces performance time, and the nearby performer Haruko most wanted to catch can no longer answer her. That is why the pre-SUNNY near-exit is not proof that Haruko never really wanted idolhood. It is proof that vocation has material and relational infrastructure.

SUNNY PEACE reconstructs that infrastructure without simply replacing Mana. Haruko enters expecting to support a group of younger, less settled performers. She discovers that membership means allowing those performers to change and support her too. The adult who has spent years being told she lacks light can finally stand before Megumi and name the transformed form her childhood promise has taken: not imagined solo stardom, but the current identity she actually built.

The later musical offer is the decisive maturation test. Haruko is not trapped; a credible director wants her, names transferable strengths, and offers a path Haruko can respect. Haruko herself can now say something the adolescent needed not to say: effort sometimes does not close every gap, Mana really did possess more natural idol aptitude, and musical performance may even fit Haruko better. Yet the conclusion is not exit. It is also not denial. Haruko chooses idolhood now because there are people with whom she still wants to do it, because others' belief has become part of the relational world she values, and because a finite dream can be more precious precisely because it cannot continue forever.

This produces P2-B1's central distinction: **finite vocation without disposability**. Haruko accepts that the career has an ending without accepting that age, comparative talent, or a plausible alternative career can declare that ending on her behalf in advance.

The Rei-Haruko event shows the same maturity in ethical form. Haruko's kindness is not incapacity to compete. She can aim to win, accept that Rei wants the summit, and work hard. What she refuses is the conversion of competition into bodily self-destruction or interpersonal degradation. She will ignore an insult to herself and still stop a live after Rei is hurt; she will speak softly and still order an apology when another person is demeaned. Restraint is a chosen boundary, not an absence of force.

Legacy work supplies the final test. Haruko has more reason than most characters to turn Hoshimi into a memorial institution: she predates Mana there, lived beside Mana, and carries unfinished promises. Instead, the later text repeatedly makes her loosen ownership. The unfinished gratitude song becomes current Hoshimi's song. The appropriate singers can be successors. The old musical can return with Haruko in Mana's former role, but Haruko explicitly distinguishes imagined copresence from reality and then leads the new cast in her own way.

The mature Haruko therefore does not solve loss by restoring the past and does not solve finitude by denying it. She survives through **translation**: support becomes reciprocal membership; rivalry becomes ethical competition; promise becomes succession; a former idol role becomes plural performance work; persistence becomes a choice that can one day be chosen differently.

# 8. Voice, behavior, and worldview reconstruction notes

P2-B1 is a longitudinal ledger rather than the final Haruko monograph, but it now supports a substantially richer character model.

## 8.1 Voice and interaction style

Haruko's default speech is warm, socially cushioning, and practical. She frequently softens directives with questions, inclusive phrasing, humor, or self-deprecation. That softness should not be confused with indecision. When a boundary becomes morally important, especially another person's bodily safety or dignity, Haruko can switch rapidly into direct imperatives.

Her humor has several recurring functions: making age anxiety manageable, reducing hierarchy with younger members, masking embarrassment, and keeping vocational uncertainty from overwhelming ordinary interaction. A good Haruko voice model should therefore permit the same person to joke about being seventeen, casually discuss cooking or ruins, confess serious insecurity about professional aptitude, and sharply order someone to apologize.

## 8.2 Behavioral defaults

Haruko tends to notice logistics and interpersonal friction early. She brings food or drink, monitors rest, organizes schedules, negotiates venues, remembers other people's needs, and looks for indirect ways to create conversational space. She is comfortable doing low-status enabling work when needed. The risk is that this same competence can make self-erasure feel virtuous: if someone else is more talented, Haruko can decide the generous thing is to move herself offstage.

The mature corpus repeatedly corrects that risk by making other people answer Haruko in return. Mana wants Haruko beside her. Makino insists SUNNY PEACE cannot exist without her. The group worries about Haruko's career decision. A musical company gives her a lead role. Successors accept inherited material while telling Haruko she must continue leading too.

## 8.3 Worldview

Haruko's mature worldview is neither pure meritocracy nor anti-merit egalitarianism. She believes effort matters, talent matters, timing matters, management matters, relationships matter, and none of those facts alone owns the final decision about what a person may attempt. She accepts competition but rejects degradation. She values care but learns that care must be reciprocal. She preserves promises but lets their form change. She treats finite things as capable of greater value because they end.

That worldview makes her especially important to later series synthesis around **answerable interdependence**, **role continuity without role captivity**, and **legacy continuity without restoration**.

# 10. P2-B1 Haruko master thesis

Within `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`, Haruko is a self-authoring veteran whose persistence becomes more reflective rather than more absolute. She begins by learning that an allegedly unsuited person may still choose the stage, is institutionally selected because kindness and persistence can themselves be professional infrastructure, and spends years beside a star whose obvious charisma seems to confirm Haruko's own deficits. The later corpus does not solve this history by declaring talent meaningless. Instead, Haruko learns to distinguish **aptitude from jurisdiction, support from self-erasure, continuity from imitation, and endurance from denial of finitude**.

SUNNY PEACE turns the external caretaker into a supported member. The musical career fork becomes a real alternative rather than a threat she must pretend not to want. Competition becomes compatible with bodily limits and moral boundaries. Mana's unfinished promises survive through successors, shared authorship, inherited roles, and Haruko's own leadership rather than impossible restoration. Her university life and ordinary adult interests remain active around idol labor rather than being consumed by it.

The strongest current formulation is therefore **FINITE_VOCATION_WITHOUT_DISPOSABILITY**: Haruko accepts that idolhood may end or transform, but neither age, slower market recognition, comparative aptitude, nor inherited legacy gets to decide that endpoint for her in advance. Her career remains finite; her present-tense claim to choose and inhabit it remains her own.

This is a Phase-2 ledger formulation, not the final Phase-3 Haruko monograph.
