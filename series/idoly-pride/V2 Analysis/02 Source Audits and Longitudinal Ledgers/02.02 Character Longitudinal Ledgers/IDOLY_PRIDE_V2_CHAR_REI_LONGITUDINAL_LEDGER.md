# 0. Responsibility and authority

This is the second evidence-bearing SUNNY PEACE character ledger in Phase 2. Its responsibility is to reconstruct **Rei as a changing professional person across dance, family authority, proof, vocation, discipline, pedagogy, group membership, bodily limits, competitive ambition, audience relation, and later family contact** rather than to freeze her as either the “strict perfectionist” or the “serious dancer” of SUNNY PEACE.

P2-B1 Rei retains six analytical boundaries.

1. **The original dream precedes the proof problem.** Rei first loves dance because performance can enchant an audience and make another person want to move. Her later obsession with measurable achievement is therefore not the origin of her vocation; it is what desire becomes after subjective testimony is repeatedly denied legitimacy.
2. **Paternal concern and paternal jurisdiction are not identical.** Later source gives Rei's father intelligible fears about economic precarity, safety, and a meaningful career. Those concerns do not retroactively make his earlier role-imposition or refusal to hear Rei legitimate. The mature text can recognize care without restoring paternal sovereignty over Rei's future.
3. **The medical path must not be misclassified as a freely authored vocation.** Rei once studied toward medicine and later casually remembers that path. The origin establishes that this trajectory was imposed after her brother rejected the same paternal succession plan. It remains evidence of academic capacity and a real counterfactual life, but not evidence that Rei independently wanted to become a doctor.
4. **Discipline is not the problem; proof captivity is.** Rei's work ethic, technique, persistence, and result orientation are genuine strengths. They become dangerous when every uncertainty is converted into more private labor, when measurable achievement becomes the sole language in which a choice is allowed to count, or when her own coping system is universalized onto other bodies.
5. **Development is recurrent rather than linear.** Rei learns to match SUNNY PEACE's pace early, then later again fears being left behind in Tokyo and again overtrains under BIG4 pressure. The recurrence is not failed characterization. It shows that the same achievement grammar reappears when professional conditions change.
6. **Optional romance must remain optional.** The explicit alternate/bad route in which Makino confesses to Rei has `PHASE1_BRANCH_CAVEATED_NO_CONTINUITY_AUTHORITY`. It can demonstrate authored possibility-space. It cannot establish mainline Rei-to-Makino attraction, Makino-to-Rei attraction, confession, dating, exclusivity, or changed relationship status.

The frozen substantive inheritance remains `IDOLY_PRIDE_V2_PHASE1_FINAL_BASELINE.md`. The completed Sakura ledger is a current dependency where Rei's development intersects SUNNY PEACE burden-sharing, health, stage enjoyment, and reciprocal care. Dedicated Rei/family, Rei/Haruko, Rei/Chisa, and SUNNY PEACE unit ledgers remain later canonical homes if their evidence grows beyond individual-character responsibility.

# 2. The three clocks: chronology, disclosure, validation

## 2.1 Fictional chronology

The most useful causal chronology for Rei is:

1. childhood encounter with theme-park performance; Rei experiences dance as audience-facing wonder and wants to `ダンスでみんなに夢を届けたい`;
2. close admiration of her guitarist older brother, for whom Rei is an enthusiastic first fan;
3. father demands the brother abandon music and follow medicine; brother declares `俺は父さんの人形じゃない！　俺の人生は俺が決める！` and eventually leaves home;
4. paternal succession pressure transfers to Rei; she initially obeys the medical/academic path because she thinks she must meet parental expectations;
5. dance is stopped, then secretly resumed; embodied enjoyment and a younger girl's admiration help Rei recover the statement `私はダンスが好きなんだ`;
6. direct confrontation with father: Rei says medicine is not what she wants and defines happiness as dancing; father calls dance escape and says he no longer expects anything from her;
7. Rei chooses a dancer path and initially believes competition victory can make her father recognize it;
8. coach and rival Erika become important scaffolding; retrospective Rei says paternal recognition alone would not have sustained that amount of effort;
9. Rei wins the dance tournament; father dismisses the result as `くだらない` / `無駄なこと`;
10. Rei concludes that a person who refuses to hear subjective desire may only respond to `無視出来ないくらいの成果`; proof escalates from award to economic viability;
11. Rei works multiple jobs to finance training and prepare material independence from family;
12. Hoshimi becomes attractive because idolhood can turn dance into paid work; Rei enters idolhood partly as proof technology rather than childhood idol aspiration;
13. early Hoshimi/SUNNY PEACE professional pressure keeps paternal proof logic active: LizNoir must be beaten, results must be produced, family recognition still matters;
14. Rei initially drags the unit toward her own practice standard, then recognizes she was racing ahead and begins adapting guidance to the five-person group;
15. Rei experiences motive-purity shame because she originally used idolhood instrumentally;
16. Chisa redirects the question from origin purity to present desire;
17. at SUNNY PEACE's handshake event, the same girl who once watched Rei dance returns as a fan and tells her Rei's dancing has remained meaningful;
18. Rei recognizes that she now occupies the performer position that once gave her dreams; idolhood becomes a self-authored vocation independent of defeating her father's judgment;
19. anime development continues the same professional correction: high standards remain, but Rei learns that pacing, recovery, and pedagogy belong inside rigor rather than outside it;
20. 2021 family event reopens direct contact: Rei admits she wants to see her parents and wants them to hear her live;
21. she acknowledges her father's concern as concern without accepting his definition of happiness; critically, she says `成功してもしなくても、私は続ける` and `私の幸せは、私が決めたい`;
22. father attends after emergency medical work, recognizes that Rei is fulfilling real work among good colleagues, and says idolhood may be worth staking a life on, though he still cannot directly say he approves;
23. later bond/contact evidence keeps the family relation imperfect but alive: study worries, complaints about trust, mother's contact, indirect messages, and eventual invitations to come home;
24. Tokyo professional differentiation triggers another recurrence of growth anxiety; Rei fears the others are getting work and improving while she is being left behind;
25. group communication allows Rei to integrate her innovation impulse with SUNNY PEACE's continuity rather than forcing novelty as proof of growth;
26. after BIG4 status, Rei again converts uncertainty into private optimization: later practice, off-day work, comparison, bodily overreach, and impatience with Haruko;
27. Haruko's injury-first boundary and public defense of Rei demonstrate that kindness can be competitively serious and that one result does not outrank a person's body;
28. Rei recognizes `一人で焦り過ぎてた`, returns improvement proposals to the five-person group, and keeps top-idol ambition while making strategy answerable to others;
29. Sakura's later collapse activates Rei's own memory of being forced to rest; the care she once resisted becomes competence she can extend to Sakura;
30. Stellar preserves Rei's ambition and willingness to work hard while placing it inside managerial monitoring, mutual support, and explicit recognition that strain cannot simply be forbidden by slogan;
31. Rei says stage enjoyment is something the other members may have learned by watching Sakura, integrating joy into her mature professional vocabulary rather than treating it as the opposite of technique;
32. 2026 underground-idol event tests Rei's mature criteria: she initially discounts unregistered amateurs, becomes fascinated by their performances, loses to an 88-year-old group, and accepts that the relevant contest was not `どちらがうまいか` but who could better delight the audience;
33. she immediately returns to the goal of the idol summit with that lesson intact: audience meaning pluralizes excellence without abolishing ambition.

## 2.2 Disclosure chronology

The anime establishes Rei prospectively as disciplined, exacting, dance-first, and under family legitimacy pressure. It shows her learning to make discipline relationally adaptable, especially around Chisa, before later game-origin material discloses the full older-brother succession history and the exact **proof bargain** that structures her psychology.

The SUNNY PEACE origin therefore explains rather than replaces the anime surface. It reveals why Rei repeatedly reaches for measurable improvement when anxious: technique and result are not merely preferences; they became the language through which she once hoped to make a denied desire impossible to dismiss.

Later family, Tokyo, BIG4, Stellar, and 2026 material then stress-test that mechanism under new conditions. P2-B1 preserves these recurrences instead of pretending the Hoshimi five-person lesson solved the pattern permanently.

# 3. Longitudinal argument

Rei begins neither with paternal rebellion nor with competitive obsession. She begins as an audience member.

A theme-park dancer makes her understand that movement can enchant strangers. The first formulation of the dream is remarkably outward-facing: `ダンスでみんなに夢を届けたい`. Her professional life therefore starts with an intuition about transmission. Performance is meaningful because it can make another person feel what Rei has just felt.

The family conflict does not create the desire. It changes the **language in which Rei thinks desire must defend itself**.

Her older brother provides the first explicit model of self-authorship. When their father orders him away from guitar and toward medicine, he says he is not his father's doll and will decide his own life. Rei admires him as his first fan. But when he leaves, the same succession expectation transfers to her. Young Rei does not immediately rebel. She obeys. The medical path is therefore important as a historical role but analytically dangerous if described as a second freely chosen vocation. At this point Rei is trying to be the child who can carry the expectation the brother refused.

Secret return to dance restores embodied self-knowledge. The decisive recognition is not a ranking: it is that dancing is fun, that she loves it, and that a younger observer is moved enough to want to dance too. Rei's original audience relation reappears before she has any professional standing.

Her father nonetheless refuses the testimony. When Rei says dancing is what makes her happiest and that she wants to be a dancer, he treats the choice as avoidance. This is why the later tournament matters psychologically beyond competition. Rei believes success can translate subjective desire into evidence. If he will not accept `私は踊りたい`, perhaps he will accept a win.

He does not.

After the tournament, Rei reaches the dangerous conclusion that the problem was not the structure of the bargain but the **magnitude of the evidence**. She needs `無視出来ないくらいの成果`; if an award is dismissible, economic self-sufficiency may not be. She will make dance a profession and prove she can live on it. This is the origin of what P2-B1 calls **proof captivity**: the conversion of a self-authored value into an endless evidentiary burden before an authority that has not agreed to fair adjudication.

The origin itself prevents a monocausal reading. Rei later says that wanting to show her father was not enough to sustain all that effort. Coach, rival, coworkers, and other relationships mattered. Her discipline is therefore not fake, merely reactive, or externally caused. The paternal conflict shapes its defensive grammar; it does not create all of its energy.

Hoshimi intensifies the proof problem before it resolves it. Rei joins because idolhood can turn dance into recognized paid labor. That is an intelligent practical move, but Rei experiences it as morally impure when she compares herself to girls who always wanted to be idols. Chisa's answer is crucial: beginning for one reason does not invalidate what a person comes to want later.

The returning fan supplies the conversion mechanism. The girl who once watched Rei dance now appears at a handshake event and says she has loved Rei's dancing ever since. Rei suddenly occupies the position of the theme-park dancer from her own childhood. The circuit closes:

- Rei receives wonder from a performer;
- Rei transmits wonder to another child;
- the child returns years later as a fan;
- Rei receives proof of relational transmission from the audience rather than legitimacy judgment from the father.

This is why Rei can later say she wants to continue because `アイドルが好きだから`. The audience does not merely give her a more flattering external judge. It returns her to the reason she cared about dance **before** judgment captured it.

Yet the proof grammar is not gone. Early Hoshimi has Rei imposing her own pace because she equates stopping with failure. Tokyo later has her again fearing she is being left behind as teammates receive different opportunities. BIG4 status produces the sharpest recurrence: now paternal legitimacy has been displaced, but professional status itself can become the new judge. Rei interprets uncertainty as a demand for more training, pushes herself beyond healthy boundaries, and initially reads Haruko's slower pace as insufficient seriousness.

The `REI♡HARU` event is therefore not a story in which the disciplined girl learns that hard work is bad. Haruko herself is serious, competitive, and willing to confront people who demean Rei. Her boundary is more exact: one contest does not outrank Rei's body, and competitive seriousness does not require contempt. Rei's revelation is that she was treating **self-exhaustion as evidence of sincerity**. Once that is exposed, she does not abandon top-idol ambition; she returns concrete improvement proposals to the whole group.

This is the mature transformation: private optimization becomes **answerable strategy**.

The same movement governs family relations. Rei eventually admits that she still wants her parents to see her. That desire does not mean her earlier rebellion was false. It means autonomy is not emotional severance. She can tell her father that his concern is not inherently wrong, thank him for worrying, and still reject his authority to define her happiness. The decisive sentence is `成功してもしなくても、私は続ける`. The proof bargain is now broken. Success may matter professionally, but it no longer conditions whether her choice deserves to exist.

Her father changes too, though incompletely. He attends the live after emergency work, recognizes that Rei is doing real work among good people, and admits idolhood may be worth staking a life on. He still cannot simply say `認める`. Later he worries about study and asks her to come home. Rei complains that he could trust her a little more. The mature family relation is therefore **partial recognition plus continuing friction**, not restored obedience and not total estrangement.

Stellar converts Rei's own failure history into care competence. When Sakura collapses, Rei understands the desire to keep performing but also remembers how being forced to rest once felt and how seeing the others continue sustained her. She can now be the person who says the group will cover Sakura. This is not proof that Rei has transcended intensity: she later refuses to promise that they will never push themselves and asks Makino to help them keep striving. The stronger development is that ambition is now situated inside **mutual support and managerial answerability**.

The late 2026 underground-idol event gives the cleanest endpoint test because it directly challenges Rei's metric instincts. She initially assumes an unofficial performance outside the VENUS system cannot have much value. Experience changes her mind. After losing to technically less imposing elderly amateurs, she explicitly says the contest was not about who was better at technique; it was about who could make the audience enjoy themselves more. She accepts defeat as correct.

And then she says she will take that lesson back to their own stage and aim for the top.

That final conjunction matters. Rei has not become anti-ranking, anti-technique, anti-professional, or anti-ambition. She has learned that **excellence has more than one legitimate measure**, and that the measure must answer to the human purpose of performance.

The strongest current model is therefore:

> **DISCIPLINED SELF-AUTHORSHIP WITHOUT PROOF CAPTIVITY.** Rei retains exacting standards, bodily craft, competitiveness, and desire for the summit, but increasingly refuses to let one father, one result, one workload metric, one pace, or one technical hierarchy monopolize what makes a vocation legitimate.

# 20. Counterevidence and unresolved questions

## 20.4 Rest is not absolute
