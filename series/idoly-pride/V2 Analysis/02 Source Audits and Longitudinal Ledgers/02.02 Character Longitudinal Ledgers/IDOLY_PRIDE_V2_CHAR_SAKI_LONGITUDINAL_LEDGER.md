# 0. Responsibility and authority

This ledger is the current Phase-2 character authority for **Shiraishi Saki / character code `ski`** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`. It reconstructs Saki across chronology rather than reducing her to one familiar surface role: Chisa's competent older sister, the diligent student-council president, Tsuki no Tempest's balancing adult-like presence, or the member who converts crises into schedules and procedures.

Saki's central longitudinal problem is not that she is too responsible and must become less responsible. The source repeatedly treats responsibility, preparation, care, intellectual curiosity, and operational competence as real strengths. The problem is **jurisdiction**: Saki learns early to interpret being a good daughter, excellent student, and older sister as roles that decide what she is permitted to want. She then becomes so practiced at supporting other people that support itself can masquerade as destiny.

Her development therefore proceeds through repeated re-authoring rather than one clean emancipation. In the origin material she first discovers that remaining close to Chisa and abandoning idolhood are not the same proposition. She does not reject family, education, or sisterhood; she constructs a dual-track future in which university, idol work, and family support can coexist. In Hoshimi she confronts a second problem: loving Chisa can become overprotection, and help can preserve the very dependence she wants Chisa to outgrow. She experiments with changing the form of care, though the staged-withdrawal method itself has relational costs.

The 2022 sister-unit event then proves that the origin lesson was not permanently solved. When Saki and Chisa perform as a duo, both sisters instinctively optimize the other. Saki again treats her ability to support as evidence that she ought to disappear behind Chisa. Chisa explicitly tells her that the current Saki is not shining, asks to support Saki in return, and forces a second-order admission: Saki herself wants to be visible, wants to stand at center, and wants to compete for attention. Their repaired form is not separation but **mutual ambition**.

BIG4 moves the same mechanism from family psychology into group governance. After Kotono leaves, Saki initially self-blames, then allows Chisa to care for her, helps the four-person Tsuki pause, deliberate, vote, operationalize a new formation, and continue. When Kotono returns, Saki becomes the clearest procedural conscience in the unit. She does not merely forgive or condemn. She identifies the specific failure: Kotono made a group-level decision without sharing the process with the people whose futures were affected. Saki conditions renewed membership on more direct discussion and later stops Kotono from once again trying to absorb public consequences alone.

The mature Saki model is therefore:

> **Responsibility is legitimate when it is chosen, reciprocal, contestable, and answerable to the people whose lives it structures. It becomes captivity when Saki uses responsibility to erase her own desire, monopolize care, or decide for others what support must look like.**

This makes Saki one of IDOLY PRIDE's clearest bridges between personal ethics and institutional ethics. The same girl who once silently organized her life around expectations later becomes the person who insists that even sincere care and center responsibility must pass through disclosure, consultation, and shared authorship.

Analytical boundaries:

- Saki's parents are not reduced to coercive villains. Their expectations mattered, but the origin explicitly shows that Saki internalized and amplified them before receiving any categorical prohibition on idolhood.

- Chisa's dependence and Saki's overprotection are mutually reinforcing, not reducible to one guilty party.
- The Hoshimi staged-absence strategy produces real growth but also causes real anxiety and anger; it is not treated as an ideal universal model of care.
- Saki's support competence does not establish support as her natural or permanent preferred role.
- Her explicit center desire is real, but it does not prove that she must or will become Tsuki's permanent center.
- Saki's planning is both competence and potential defense. It should not be diagnosed as pathology merely because she likes structure.

# 3. Longitudinal argument

Saki's story begins with an unusually important distinction: **the role that later constrains her begins in love**. She does not remember Chisa as an unwanted burden. Being an older sister is described as special and joyful. Their parents' work situation gives that identity practical content, and the childhood accident gives it moral urgency. The resulting rule—`私がお姉ちゃんなんだから、しっかりしなきゃ！`—therefore feels to Saki less like external oppression than like the obvious meaning of being herself.

This is why later parental expectations become so powerful. Her father praises academic excellence; her mother asks her to support Chisa. Saki translates those requests into categorical internal rules: `優等生でないといけない`; `千紗を支え続けないといけない`. The source does not require an authoritarian parent because Saki's own conscientiousness supplies the enforcement mechanism. She becomes the administrator of expectations that have already entered her self-concept.

The hidden idol desire reveals the cost. Saki has not stopped loving idols. She practices, watches, remembers the experience of making Chisa happy, and imagines making many people smile. Yet she increasingly treats that desire as structurally incompatible with the life she believes she owes other people. Her crisis when her parents later tell her she may choose freely is psychologically important because the problem is no longer current permission. It is the accumulated meaning of past sacrifice. `散々期待したくせに、今更好きにしろだなんて……` expresses anger at the discovery that the walls she obeyed were less absolute than she had made them.

Chisa's intervention is therefore not simply encouragement to follow a dream. It forces Saki to separate propositions she had fused: **staying close to Chisa** and **giving up idolhood**. Saki explicitly recognizes the mistake. This is the first decisive act of self-authorship because it changes the structure of the problem rather than choosing one side of a false binary.

Her answer is characteristic. Saki does not run away from responsibility. She researches. She builds a presentation titled `アイドルと大学生を両立するには`, considers geography, schedule, school choice, risk, and family logistics, and argues that study and idolhood can strengthen one another. This is why “planning” must not be read simply as anxiety. Once expectation stops owning the conclusion, the same planning faculty that enforced captivity becomes an instrument of agency.

The family resolution similarly rejects sovereign individualism. Saki recognizes that she had been binding herself, but she does not conclude that adulthood means needing nobody. She asks her parents to let her rely on them. Her audition formulation makes the same point: she has important family beside her, and their encouragement expands how far she can run. Self-authorship here is **not isolation from support; it is jurisdiction over what support means**.

The Hoshimi sister arc then reveals the persistence of old relational grammar. Saki can choose idolhood and still believe that a good older sister must anticipate Chisa's needs. She understands Chisa's brave face because she has watched her for years; that observational competence is real. Yet the same intimacy can produce excessive intervention. When Kotono and others point out that constant help may make separation harder, Saki accepts the principle and engineers an opportunity for Chisa to participate in costume work without her.

The result is deliberately mixed. Chisa succeeds, which proves the developmental hypothesis was not empty. But Saki and Makino manipulate the conditions and conceal the plan; Chisa experiences serious anxiety and later anger when she learns the truth. The correct lesson is not “Saki learns to abandon Chisa.” It is more demanding: **care must change form, and even developmentally useful care remains answerable to the person being helped**.

The limited sister-unit event is the strongest proof that this development remains vulnerable to regression. Once the sisters are paired alone, the old dyadic grammar reappears almost immediately. Each takes a step back so the other can shine. Because Saki is practiced at balancing ensembles, she especially interprets support competence as a reason to subordinate herself. She hears criticism that their unit lacks clarity and responds by trying to optimize Chisa even more. This produces `RECIPROCAL_SELF_ERASURE`: two loving people each make themselves smaller for the other's sake until the performance contains less of either person.

Chisa's confrontation closes a loop that the origin could not. Earlier, Saki gave Chisa permission to want. Now Chisa returns that permission upward. `私にも……お姉ちゃんを支えさせて？` is a transfer of standing. Chisa does not ask Saki to stop being an older sister; she asks Saki to stop using that fact to monopolize care. Her accusation that Saki is not `キラキラ` is especially powerful because it recalls the pre-role childhood Saki who openly loved performing.

Saki then admits what her support competence had hidden: `私だって、本当は……月ストのセンターをやりたい`; `このユニットでも……もっともっと目立ちたい！`. This is not selfishness replacing care. The repaired sister unit becomes stronger when both try to be visible and tell one another exactly what they want. Their affection gains **conflict tolerance**.

BIG4 generalizes the same lesson into institutional behavior. Saki first responds to Kotono's departure with the familiar reflex—if only she had been more useful. Chisa interrupts that pattern simply by being available to hear her. Saki can say `ありがとう……千紗がいてくれて良かった……っ` without losing status. Receiving care has become compatible with her older-sister identity.

The next steps show Saki's planning faculty in mature form. She recognizes that the four members are too emotionally activated to decide immediately and proposes a pause. Later she supports a shared procedure, a simultaneous decision, and then produces a concrete four-person requirements list covering announcement, line distribution, choreography, and promotion. Her competence is no longer proof that she must privately carry everything. It becomes **shared infrastructure for other people's choices**.

At Kotono's return, this reaches its clearest ethical articulation. Saki does not deny Kotono's reasons. She says she understands them, then identifies the governance failure: the process was not shared. Kotono's belief that departure would make her stronger did not give her authority to decide what the separation meant for the others. Saki calls the behavior `自分勝手`, invokes the obligations of a professional idol to supporters, and makes honest discussion a condition of renewed group life.

This is the mature reversal of Saki's childhood problem. The younger Saki silently allowed expectations to decide her own future. The mature Saki insists that **nobody gets to decide other people's futures silently—not parents by implication, not an older sister through overprotection, not a center through unilateral sacrifice, and not Saki herself through benevolent optimization**.

# 4. Character mechanics

## 4.1 Responsibility is an identity before it is a behavior

Saki does not merely perform tasks reliably. She historically interprets reliability as evidence of moral adequacy. This explains why failure, uncertainty, or dependence can initially feel disproportionately threatening.

The longitudinal correction is not to remove responsibility from the identity but to change its constitutional status. Responsibility becomes one value among others rather than the office that determines every permissible desire.

## 4.2 Planning can be either captivity or agency

Saki repeatedly responds to uncertainty with structure:

- schedules;
- research;
- presentations;
- scenario comparison;
- operational lists;
- preparation before unfamiliar work;
- attention to logistical details.

The same trait behaves differently under different governing purposes.

When expectation owns the goal, planning efficiently enforces self-denial. When Saki owns the goal, planning expands option space. In BIG4, planning becomes collective infrastructure that helps four people act.

The correct model is therefore not `SAKI_IS_ANXIOUS_PLANNER`. It is:

> **Saki's planning is a general-purpose agency technology whose ethical value depends on who authors the goal and who is allowed to contest the plan.**

## 4.3 Care is anticipatory and observational

Saki often notices needs before they are spoken. This is a genuine relational skill. She reads Chisa's brave face, thinks about fatigue, prepares non-caffeinated tea, researches work locations in advance, and converts abstract concern into concrete action.

This strength creates its own failure mode: she can move from noticing another person's need to presuming authority over how that need should be met.

Mature care therefore adds consultation to anticipation.

## 4.4 Competence does not imply natural support destiny

Saki is good at balance, mediation, and support. Limited-unit material nevertheless proves that these competencies do not exhaust what she wants. She can want center, visibility, and competitive attention while remaining good at helping others.

This distinction is essential for later Tsuki synthesis:

> **role aptitude is evidence about capacity, not sovereign evidence about desired identity.**

## 4.5 Receiving care is a learned competence

The early Saki model privileges giving. Later material repeatedly lets her occupy the other position:

- Chisa makes time for her after Kotono's departure;
- Saki cries and says she is glad Chisa is there;
- Chisa asks to support her in the sister unit;
- the bond story reverses the ear-cleaning/knee-pillow care direction, which embarrasses Saki precisely because receiving is unfamiliar.

These scenes are not comic side notes. They show that reciprocity must become embodied and ordinary, not only philosophically accepted.

## 4.6 Conflict becomes a form of care

Saki's mature conflict style is neither explosive nor evasive. When she decides a disagreement must be surfaced, she can become very direct while preserving procedural fairness.

At Kotono's return she:

1. acknowledges Kotono's explanation;
2. states that she remains dissatisfied;
3. proposes that each member say what she felt then and feels now;
4. goes first rather than making others absorb the risk;
5. criticizes behavior rather than declaring Kotono worthless;
6. creates a condition for future group life rather than demanding submission.

This is a high-value model for `ANSWERABLE_CONSTITUTIONALISM` at interpersonal scale.

## 4.7 Saki is not generic perfection

Private material usefully prevents the competence profile from flattening into “perfect onee-san.” Saki has eccentric and intensely specific interests, including geography/terrain, transit-route talk, and stamp rallies. She can overprepare trivial leisure with `Myインク`. Her fashion sense can be sufficiently odd that others hesitate to tell her. She can misapply idol-stage delivery to a school assembly.

These details matter for reconstructing personality. Saki is earnest enough to be funny because she often attacks ordinary interests with professional-grade seriousness.

# 5. Shiraishi sisterhood: longitudinal reconstruction

## 5.1 Formation — older sisterhood as joy

The relationship begins before Saki has a developed independent memory of life without Chisa. She treats being an older sister as special and desirable. This blocks a simplistic “parentification caused resentment” model.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** responsibility grows in a household where the parents work and the sisters spend substantial time together.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** because care is fused with affection so early, later self-sacrifice can feel like authentic love rather than like a cost imposed from outside.

## 5.2 Accident — care acquires moral force

The childhood accident intensifies the older-sister rule. Saki does not merely want to help; she comes to believe she **must** be the reliable one.

This becomes one root of later role captivity, but it should not be overdiagnosed. The source supports an internalized protective rule, not a clinical conclusion.

## 5.3 Reciprocal sacrifice loop

The origin material reveals that both sisters misread love in complementary directions.

- Saki: if I love Chisa, I should remain available and suppress incompatible desire.
- Chisa: if I love Saki, I should avoid becoming the burden that blocks Saki's future.

Both are acting for the other. Both thereby deprive the other of information and agency.

The relationship problem is therefore not one-way dependency but a **reciprocal sacrifice loop**.

## 5.4 First detotalization — proximity is not renunciation

Saki's key origin realization is explicit:

`私……千紗のそばにいることと`

`アイドルの夢を諦めることを一緒に考えてたみたい`

This is the first `SIBLING_ROLE_DETOTALIZATION`: older sisterhood remains meaningful but loses the right to decide the whole future.

## 5.5 Post-debut overprotection — cognition changes before habit

Saki can intellectually accept Chisa's independence while still acting from old habit. `Dear My Sister` shows this clearly. She knows Chisa needs to stand alone and still finds herself waiting, watching, checking messages, creating action plans, and nearly intervening.

This is important because it makes growth behavioral rather than declarative.

## 5.6 Staged absence — useful intervention with an answerability defect

Saki's costume-meeting plan is causally productive: Chisa is forced to use her own expertise and discovers she can function without Saki at her side.

But the method is ethically mixed:

- Saki and Makino arrange the selection;
- Saki deliberately withholds presence;
- Chisa experiences intense anxiety;
- the plan is revealed only afterward;
- Chisa becomes angry and describes how worried she was.

The mature takeaway is not that benevolent deception is ideal. It is:

> **Autonomy-supporting intervention can still fail an answerability test when the supported person is denied standing in the method.**

## 5.7 Sister-unit relapse — growth is not linear

`event_2022_005_st-eve-2205-race` is a decisive longitudinal source because it recreates the original relational geometry after both sisters have already developed elsewhere.

Their first choreography repeatedly shows both stepping backward for the other. Saki's interpretation of criticism then makes the pattern worse: `私が……もっと千紗を引き立てないと`.

The event proves that old role schemas can reactivate under context even after genuine growth.

## 5.8 Chisa supports upward

Chisa's confrontation reverses the historical direction of permission.

`私にも……お姉ちゃんを支えさせて？`

This is not merely comfort. Chisa claims standing as someone capable of evaluating what is happening to Saki and interrupting it.

Saki initially answers with the old rule: `でも、私は……お姉ちゃんだから / あなたを、支えないと……`.

The old role is therefore still active enough to articulate itself directly.

## 5.9 Visibility and center desire become speakable

Chisa asks what Saki really wants and identifies the contradiction between the girl who loved dazzling idol performance and the woman now deleting herself from the duo.

Saki answers:

`私だって、本当は……月ストのセンターをやりたい`

`このユニットでも……もっともっと目立ちたい！`

This is one of the strongest Saki lines in the entire locked corpus. It prohibits any mature model in which she is naturally fulfilled only as support.

## 5.10 Mutual ambition stabilizes affection

The repaired duo does not alternate sacrifice. They compete to be visible, give direct corrections, and enjoy disagreement. Chisa narrates that they now say everything they think; the very interaction that once felt dangerous becomes fun.

# 8. Voice, behavior, and personality reconstruction notes

Saki does not become slangy or chaotic under serious conflict. Her language generally becomes **more precise**.

The Kotono confrontation is the model:

- acknowledge understanding;
- state unresolved objection;
- specify conduct;
- specify who was affected;
- invoke professional rule;
- invite response;
- establish future process.

This makes her an unusually useful voice for principled disagreement scenes.

## 8.4 Emotional rupture

When Saki's composure breaks, her language shortens and pauses multiply. The BIG4 scene with Chisa—`ありがとう……千紗がいてくれて良かった……っ`—should inform voice reconstruction. She does not normally dramatize herself, so direct dependence lands strongly when it appears.

## 8.5 Enthusiasm and hobby register

Saki becomes noticeably more animated around favored intellectual or collecting interests. She can discuss:

- transit-route structures;
- geography and terrain;
- plant facts after research;
- stamp-rally technique and ink preparation.

Her enthusiasm often retains the same explanatory precision as her professional register, which can make mundane interests comically intense.

## 8.6 Humor profile

Saki is not a constant joke generator, but she is highly compatible with humor based on:

- earnest overpreparation;
- literal execution of advice;
- professional intensity applied to leisure;
- blind spots in taste;
- embarrassment when placed in the care-recipient or “younger sister” position.

Do not model her as humorless.

## 8.7 Behavioral defaults

High-confidence behavioral tendencies:

- prepare before unfamiliar tasks;
- research before speaking with authority;
- notice fatigue and logistical needs;
- convert vague concern into a plan;
- seek a workable compromise before abandoning a commitment;
- prefer explicit procedures in group crises;
- self-blame when she believes she failed to support someone;
- initially suppress personal desire if it conflicts with a role she experiences as duty;
- become surprisingly direct when a fairness/process boundary is crossed;
- accept revised information and rebuild plans rather than defend a bad plan for pride alone.

## 8.8 Worldview

Saki's mature worldview is practical rather than libertarian in the narrow sense. She does not treat freedom as the absence of obligations. She treats a good life as one in which obligations, desires, relationships, education, and professional goals can be **organized without any single role acquiring veto power over the person**.

Her ethical intuition increasingly becomes:

> care must leave the cared-for person with standing;
> responsibility must be shareable;
> decisions affecting a group must be discussable;
> competence should expand options, not erase desire.
