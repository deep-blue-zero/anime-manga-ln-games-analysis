# IDOLY PRIDE V2 - HAYASAKA MEI LONGITUDINAL LEDGER

## P2-B2 - TSUKI NO TEMPEST - Hayasaka Mei

# 0. Responsibility and authority

This ledger is the current Phase-2 character authority for **Hayasaka Mei / character code `mei`** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`.

It reconstructs Mei across chronology rather than reducing her to any one familiar surface label: cheerful chaos agent, athletic natural, supernatural-sensitive girl, donut lover, social butterfly, Suzu's closest friend, Tsuki no Tempest's intuitive member, comic relief, or the person who keeps a serious unit from becoming too severe.

Those descriptions capture real parts of her. None is sufficient.

Mei's longitudinal problem begins with **permeability**. As a child she experiences social reality differently from many classmates. She speaks about companions that her mother and grandmother can perceive while her father cannot, talks extensively with cats, and is mocked by peers. The origin bundle does not itself explicitly classify those childhood companions as ghosts, so this ledger preserves that local ambiguity even though the later Hoshimi story explicitly establishes that Mei can perceive Mana's ghost and sometimes other apparitions. The important developmental point is that Mei does not remain socially uncalibrated. Her family teaches her selective disclosure and conventional manners; by adolescence she has many friends and can move fluently between social contexts.

That adaptation creates a second problem. Mei becomes extraordinarily easy to use in the benign sense: athletic, friendly, flexible, curious, willing to help, and inclined to answer requests with `いいよ`. Many clubs borrow her. Many people like her. She enjoys the activity. Yet she does not initially choose one durable project or one especially close relationship around which to organize continuity. The origin's strongest structural formulation is therefore not that Mei lacks connection. It is that she has **breadth before depth**: socially connected, repeatedly useful, but not deeply located.

Cheerleading gives the first sustained test. Mei already believes she is serious because she does things wholeheartedly and enjoys them fully. As nationals approach, the team increasingly associates seriousness with endurance, repetition, fatigue, and exclusion of competing commitments. When cheer and idol activity become impossible to sustain at the same level simultaneously, Mei cannot solve the problem by simply saying both are fun. She has to discover that commitment sometimes imposes limits and choices.

The source does not resolve this by teaching Mei that suffering is the real proof of seriousness. Instead she integrates two truths. Suzu and the other performers show her why people endure difficult practice for an outcome they deeply want. Mei accepts that form of `本気`. She then turns back toward the cheer team and insists that wholehearted enjoyment also belongs inside serious practice. The team remains strict, recovers a brighter atmosphere, and wins nationals. Mei's mature early philosophy is therefore:

> **Serious effort and serious enjoyment are not enemies. Discipline is healthiest when it remains connected to the lived reason people wanted to perform in the first place.**

The third transformation is from generalized usefulness to **particular belonging**. Mei explicitly says that although she had many friends, she had never become especially close to one person. Suzu's casual promise that they would keep walking together matters disproportionately because it tells Mei that somebody wants not merely her usefulness but her continued presence. Mei later says directly that Suzu is why she is now an idol. This does not mean Suzu creates Mei's entire self. It means Suzu supplies the first durable relational future strong enough to organize Mei's choice among many enjoyable possibilities.

A separate causal strand comes from Mana. In the Hoshimi retelling Mei becomes perhaps Mana's most ordinary post-death friend: joking with her, playing games, seeing her frustration and affection, and treating the legendary idol as a person. Mei's permeability also creates an ethical temptation. Because she can see Mana and knows Mana wants to speak to Kotono, she proposes becoming the bridge through which the sisters communicate. Mana refuses. Mei initially does not understand, but after hearing Mana's reasons she withdraws the intervention and calls her own impulse `お節介`. This is an early example of a major mature Mei lesson: **kindness does not automatically authorize action on another person's behalf**.

Mana then contributes something different from Suzu. Suzu gives Mei **particular belonging**. Mana gives Mei a model of **audience-facing vocation**. Mei sees stage footage and realizes what it feels like to be made to `わくわく`; she decides she wants to become an idol capable of giving that experience to other people. These causes must remain distinct. Mei does not join Tsuki simply because she worships Mana, and she does not remain an idol only because Suzu wants her nearby.

The Suzu/Mei limited-unit event provides the strongest later correction to any model of Mei as effortless intuition. Mei remembers production requirements, initiates scheduling, thinks about costume and choreography fit, considers what fans can imitate, and wants to create a concept that represents both members. The event also exposes the shadow side of her kindness. Mei can accommodate so habitually that frustration accumulates without being named. When Suzu's unilateral attempt to optimize the unit around Mei becomes unbearable, Mei deliberately returns to argue. The relationship becomes stronger not because conflict disappears but because both girls learn that disagreement, jealousy, hurt, loneliness, and affection can be spoken without ending the bond.

BIG4 then generalizes this relational lesson into a theory of group resilience. Mei grieves Kotono's departure and says she relied on and loved her, but she does not treat Kotono's absence as proof that the remaining Tsuki is meaningless. She is relieved that Nagisa, Saki, and Suzu want to continue. When Kotono returns, Mei insists that repair must include the fans who were hurt by the rupture. Before the final DoriKyun battle, she gives the arc's clearest language of recoverability: even if the new Tsuki loses, it is supple enough not to break; the five can begin again.

The mature model is therefore:

> **Mei becomes serious not by abandoning joy, spontaneity, permeability, or kindness, but by giving them durable form. She learns to choose particular people and projects, accept limits, tolerate conflict, work through difficult goals, respect other people's boundaries, and understand defeat as recoverable rather than annihilating. Her joy is not the opposite of commitment. It is one of the values commitment is supposed to protect.**

This is `RESILIENT_COMMITMENT_WITHOUT_JOY_ERASURE_MEI`.

# 3. Longitudinal argument

Mei's arc can be described as a sequence of transformations in the meaning of openness.

### Stage 1 — permeability without social protection

Young Mei experiences more than her peers are prepared to validate. Her response is not permanent withdrawal. She learns how much of herself can be disclosed in which context.

### Stage 2 — social fluency without particular location

The adaptation succeeds almost too well. Mei can enter many groups, help many people, and enjoy many activities. Her openness becomes horizontally expansive, but she has little reason to privilege one path over another.

### Stage 3 — enjoyment meets finite commitment

Cheer is the first context in which "I enjoy this" is insufficient because two serious projects compete for finite time and energy. Mei has to distinguish appetite from commitment.

### Stage 4 — serious joy

She does not discard her original philosophy. She revises it. Hard work can be meaningful, but enjoyment is not childish residue to be burned away by professionalism. The mature task is to keep the two answerable to each other.

### Stage 5 — particular belonging

Suzu's `ずっと一緒に` makes continuity itself emotionally meaningful. Mei chooses not only something fun but a person/group with whom she wants a future.

### Stage 6 — permeability becomes ethical relation

Mana tests the moral limit of Mei's connective instinct. Mei can see what Kotono cannot and wants to bridge the gap. Yet access does not create jurisdiction. She learns to remain close without forcing the connection she thinks would help.

### Stage 7 — vocation gains an audience-facing object

Stage-Mana gives Mei a positive answer to what idolhood should do: cause another person to feel wonder, excitement, and bodily anticipation.

### Stage 8 — kindness becomes contestable

The Suzu/Mei event proves that perpetual accommodation is not the same as care. Mei's anger is not a failure of the relationship; naming it becomes necessary for a more truthful relationship.

### Stage 9 — belonging becomes resilient rather than fragile

BIG4 proves that particular belonging need not become captivity. Mei can love Kotono, grieve her absence, continue with others, demand repair when she returns, recognize Suzu's independent authority, and imagine defeat without imagining annihilation.

The endpoint therefore preserves Mei's founding openness while adding **selection, limits, boundary respect, coauthorship, and recoverability**.

# 4. Character mechanics

## 4.1 Social permeability is real, but not naive social blankness

Mei rapidly approaches people, accepts unfamiliar situations, forms nicknames, asks direct questions, and treats unusual phenomena as ordinary sooner than most characters would. This can read as pure natural innocence.

The origin complicates that reading. She learned selective disclosure after being mocked, studied manners, and can perform conventional politeness when context calls for it.

Preferred model:

> **Mei's openness survives social learning; it is not evidence that social learning never occurred.**

## 4.2 Generalized usefulness is both strength and developmental risk

Mei's `いいよ` reflex makes her easy to recruit and pleasant to work with. It also allows other people's needs to determine where her energy goes without requiring her to ask which needs she wants to make durable.

This is not exploitation as a universal claim. Most club requests are benign, and Mei genuinely enjoys helping. The developmental risk is **diffusion**, not victimhood.

## 4.3 Joy is an evaluative instrument

Mei often asks, implicitly or explicitly, whether people still look alive inside the work they claim matters to them.

This appears in:

- cheer practice becoming severe but affectively depleted;
- concern when Suzu or others are exhausted;
- admiration for Rei when effort and joy align;
- later texture material where she prefers Rei's ordinary enjoyable dance to a dangerously grim mode;
- her own final description of the Suzu/Mei project as difficult and far more fun because of the difficulty, thought, conversation, and practice involved.

Joy therefore functions less as "avoid discomfort" and more as **evidence that effort has not lost contact with purpose**.

## 4.4 Mei is physically intelligent

Her broad club usefulness, cheer success, dance attraction, rapid adaptation, and later performance competence support a body-first intelligence that can be obscured by her verbal silliness.

She often understands movement by doing, watching, imitating, or feeling rather than by presenting an explicit technical theory first.

That does not make her anti-analytical. `event_2023_010` proves she can also reason concretely about design, choreography, schedule, audience imitation, and unit fit.

## 4.5 Observation hides under expressive chaos

Mei is noisy and distractible, but she notices:

- Rei's repeated private practice;
- the emotional difference between cheerful and grim performance;
- Mana's unspoken desire to talk to Kotono;
- Suzu's effort and later center burden;
- fans' reaction to four-person versus five-person Tsuki;
- whether another person appears tired, lonely, or emotionally blocked.

Her observation is often relational/embodied rather than abstractly diagnostic.

## 4.6 Kindness can accumulate unspoken cost

Mana's retrospective advice in the Suzu/Mei event gives this mechanism its clearest language: a kind person can still accumulate feelings she has not named.

Mei's own history makes this plausible. She is used to saying yes, helping, adapting, and keeping the atmosphere light.

Mature Mei therefore needs not less kindness, but **kindness capable of speaking its own limits**.

## 4.7 Humor often regulates affect rather than erasing it

Mei's comic impulses frequently arrive inside genuine stress:

- food and games after difficult practice;
- a bath/cold-water proposal during overheated BIG4 discussion;
- playful nicknames inside serious attachment;
- sound effects and exaggerated phrasing during logistical tasks.

The humor can deflect, but it also often keeps a difficult environment socially breathable. Do not assume every joke means she is avoiding the issue; she often returns to the issue afterward.

## 4.8 Recoverability is Mei's mature strength concept

Before the DoriKyun final she imagines losing and concludes that the current Tsuki would not break. Five people who can survive rupture, disagreement, and reconstitution can begin again.

This is not anti-competitive resignation. She immediately says she does not intend to lose.

The structure is:

> **commit fully -> accept that failure remains possible -> preserve the capacity for future action.**

# 5. Origin: social adaptation, serious joy, and particular belonging

## 5.1 Childhood companions remain ontologically open at the origin layer

`st-group-moon-01-03-01` establishes companions visible to Mei's mother and grandmother but apparently not her father. Their locations and Suzu's reaction create supernatural implication, but the origin does not itself call them ghosts.

Classification:

- **TEXTUAL FACT:** differential perception among family members.
- **STRONG INFERENCE:** the source deliberately invites a supernatural reading.
- **OPEN at this local source layer:** exact ontology of those specific childhood companions.

Later Hoshimi evidence establishes Mei's general supernatural perception but does not retroactively convert every childhood referent into a fully specified ghost identity.

## 5.2 Selective disclosure is learned self-protection, not shame-based self-erasure

After peer ridicule, Mei and her family decide that the unusual companions should be discussed with family or highly trusted people. She also learns manners to reduce avoidable social friction.

The result is not a repressed, false Mei. Her later voice remains highly eccentric.

The stronger reading is:

> **Mei learns contextual jurisdiction over disclosure while preserving the underlying self that needed protection.**

## 5.3 Broad friendship does not solve location

By middle school Mei has many friends and recurring invitations into other people's projects. She is happy, active, and useful.

She nevertheless says none of the clubs quite `ピンとこなかった` and later explains that she had not become especially close to one person.

This distinction prevents two opposite errors:

- "Mei was lonely and friendless until Tsuki" — false;
- "Mei already had everything relationally, so Suzu adds nothing structurally" — also false.

## 5.4 Cheer makes effort visible from inside

Cheerleading fits Mei unusually well because it combines athleticism, group timing, outward affect, and performance.

As nationals approach, Mei sees people exhausted yet fulfilled. She begins to understand why effort can itself be meaningful when attached to a goal.

## 5.5 Mei does not outsource the choice

When the captain makes the incompatibility explicit, Mei considers consulting Makino or Tsuki and then says:

`これは芽衣の問題なんだから / 自分でなんとかしないと`

This is a quiet but important authorship moment. The character most comfortable moving through other people's requests recognizes that this decision cannot simply be assigned back to her social network.

# 6. Hoshimi: kinetic attraction, Mana friendship, and audience-facing vocation

# 7. Suzu and Mei: kindness, conflict, and coauthorship

## 7.1 Mei begins the limited unit more organized than Suzu expects

In `st-eve-2310-race-001/002`, Mei tracks costume and choreography meetings, remembers prior limited-unit experience, and approaches the new project as something requiring production work.

`KINDNESS_ACCUMULATION_RISK`.

## 7.6 Mei deliberately chooses conflict

`芽衣ね、すずにゃんとケンカしに来たよ`

This is not loss of character consistency. It is development **within** her kindness.

Mei tells Suzu what hurt, calls out her failure to listen, and also says:

`全部ひっくるめて……すずにゃんのこと、大好きなんだ`

The event refuses the idea that affection cancels grievance or grievance cancels affection.

## 7.8 Vulnerability becomes reciprocal

Mei admits concern that her weirdness or conversational style may burden Suzu. Suzu admits hurt and loneliness. Both confess that the separate trip home felt lonely.

This is `RECIPROCAL_VULNERABILITY_DISCLOSURE`.

## 7.9 Coauthorship replaces optimization

Mei's mature unit principle is:

`二人のいいところといいところをくっつけたら`

The final concept becomes `かっこ可愛い`: neither Suzu disappearing into Mei's strengths nor Mei simply accepting Suzu's labor.

The unit is authored by the relation.

## 7.10 Difficulty and fun become mutually reinforcing

After the project Mei describes thinking, struggling, talking, practicing, and nevertheless finding the experience many times more fun.

This is one of the clearest later proofs that the origin philosophy survives maturation:

> **Fun is no longer the absence of difficulty. It can be the affective result of meaningful difficulty shared truthfully.**

# 8. BIG4: grief, answerability, and recoverability

## 8.1 Kotono's departure hurts without totalizing Mei's world

In `big4_006_choice_of_destiny`, Mei says she wanted Kotono to consult them because they had worked together as one group.

She also self-criticizes:

- she noticed Kotono was troubled;
- she wishes she had asked more;

This keeps Mei from occupying a purely accusatory position.

## 8.2 Mei's attachment to Kotono is real

Therefore her continuity with the remaining four is not evidence that Kotono never mattered.

## 8.3 Plural belonging makes continuation possible

Mei is deeply sad that Kotono is gone, but she also says she loves Nagisa, Saki, and Suzu and is genuinely happy they want to continue Tsuki.

This is an important maturation beyond the origin's first particularity.

## 8.4 Repair must include fans

In `big4_010`, Mei tells Kotono that the four-member unit had supporters but many fans were sad that the five were no longer together.

This establishes Mei as a carrier of audience answerability.

## 8.5 Mei blocks renewed solitary responsibility

When Kotono begins to frame public explanation as her responsibility alone, Mei immediately interrupts alongside the others.

The group has learned that responsibility can be real without requiring solitary ownership of every consequence.

## 8.6 Mei recognizes Suzu's center labor

Before the final battle, Mei follows Suzu out and tells her plainly that she worked hard, kept four-person Tsuki alive, and was central to why the others could continue.

This is not empty consolation after Suzu delegates center. Mei identifies concrete prior work.

## 8.7 Recoverability rejects victory absolutism

Immediately before the DoriKyun final Mei says:

`負けちゃっても大丈夫かな`

because current Tsuki feels `すっごくしなやか`.

She continues:

`もし負けたとしても / 折れることはないんじゃないかなーって思うんだ`

`五人でいれば、きっとまたやり直せるよ`

Then she adds that she has no intention of losing.

This yields Mei's strongest mature competitive proposition:

> **Failure is serious without being ontological. A loss can matter without exhausting the right or capacity to continue.**

## 8.8 Mei's serious joy has become high-stakes resilience

The origin asked whether fun could coexist with hard practice.

BIG4 asks a more difficult question: can commitment survive possible defeat?

Mei's answer is yes, because the group has become supple rather than brittle.

This is why `RESILIENT_COMMITMENT_WITHOUT_JOY_ERASURE_MEI` is the correct capstone rather than merely `SERIOUS_JOY_MEI`.

# 9. Voice, behavior, and personality reconstruction notes

## 9.1 Surface register

Mei's default Japanese voice is highly informal, emotionally transparent, and rhythmically playful.

Common features include:

- self-reference by name: `芽衣`;
- frequent `～だよ / ～だよね / ～かな / ～しよ / ～しようよ`;
- elongated vowels and orthographic stretching: `すっごく`, `ず～っと`, `えへへ～`, `う～ん`;
- sound effects and mimetic compression: `ビシバシー`, `どっかーーん`, playful chants and improvised phrases;
- high-frequency direct invitations: eat, play, go, watch, try, talk;
- easy `ちゃん` address across peers and seniors where relationship permits;
- nicknames, most notably `すずにゃん`;
- quick verbalized affect: surprise, hunger, delight, worry, loneliness, admiration;
- a strong preference for concrete sensory/relational description over abstract exposition in ordinary speech.

## 9.2 Name-self-reference is a stable personal marker

Mei commonly says `芽衣` rather than maintaining a consistently formal first-person pronoun.

The origin contains a notable serious reflective `私は` in the line about what she had understood `本気` to mean. This should not be overformalized into a rigid register rule, but it suggests that conceptual self-analysis can pull her toward more conventional reflective grammar.

## 9.3 Nicknames express relational domestication

`すずにゃん` is not merely a gag. It turns Suzu's ornate self-presentation into a familiar, touchable relationship without denying Suzu's theatricality.

Mei similarly uses warm `ちゃん` forms broadly and calls post-death Mana `麻奈ちゃん`, contributing to Mana's rehumanization outside public legend.

## 9.4 Emotional directness is high, but not complete

Mei readily says she likes people, misses them, wants to play, is excited, or is worried.

The Suzu/Mei event proves an important exception: repeated accommodation can hide more conflictual feelings even from Mei herself.

Therefore:

> **Mei is affectively expressive but not perfectly self-transparent.**

## 9.5 Thought often begins concrete and becomes conceptual

She may start with:

- someone's tired face;
- a dance she wants to imitate;
- fans copying choreography;
- whether a unit feels like both members;
- whether a person is smiling;
- whether practice remains enjoyable.

From those observations she can reach surprisingly rigorous principles about seriousness, mutuality, boundary, or resilience.

Do not mistake a non-academic speech surface for lack of conceptual capacity.

## 9.6 Play and appetite are persistent, not developmental debris

B4 bond material reproduces:

- donut fixation;
- food sharing;
- games and invented rules;
- cats;
- physical challenges;
- spontaneous outings;
- improvised solutions;
- ordinary forgetfulness;
- rapid recovery of enthusiasm.

Growth does not remove these. A reconstruction that makes late Mei solemn, restrained, or managerial by default would be wrong.

## 9.7 She is more practically competent than her comic mistakes imply

Mei can forget lunch, break bowls, become distracted by food, or generate absurd solutions. Yet stronger sources show:

- high athletic competence;
- rapid performance learning;
- schedule awareness;
- creative planning;
- audience observation;
- relational diagnosis;
- willingness to act independently;
- ability to carry difficult conversation.

Her incompetence is domain-specific and comic, not global.

## 9.8 Care is active and sometimes intrusive

Mei does not only comfort. She:

- recruits people into activities;
- intervenes in moods;
- proposes mediation;
- confronts Suzu;
- pushes Rei-family interaction in lower-tier material;
- sets repair expectations for Kotono.

This energy can help people and can cross boundaries. Mature Mei is best modeled as **care that increasingly learns answerability**, not as passive warmth.

## 9.9 Behavioral defaults

When reconstructing Mei in new dialogue or simulation, default to:

- entering social space quickly rather than waiting for formal invitation;
- noticing bodily mood and atmosphere before formal status distinctions;
- proposing a concrete fun activity when tension rises;
- using playful language even while caring seriously;
- voicing admiration directly;
- assuming connection is possible until given a reason otherwise;
- responding well to explicit boundaries once she understands them;
- preferring shared doing over prolonged abstract deliberation;
- becoming surprisingly firm when someone she trusts is making a relationship unilateral;
- treating failure as something to recover from rather than proof that the future is closed.

Avoid defaulting her to:

- stupidity;
- total social obliviousness;
- permanent cheerfulness with no anger;
- mystical oracle speech;
- passive support;
- unbounded people-pleasing;
- anti-discipline hedonism.

## 9.10 Worldview

Mei's mature worldview is not explicitly philosophical in vocabulary, but the sources support a coherent practical ethic:

1. people should be able to feel alive inside what they work hard for;
2. effort matters when it serves something genuinely wanted;
3. fun can be serious;
4. connection matters more when it becomes chosen continuity rather than generic availability;
5. kindness needs permission and honest feedback;
6. closeness should survive disagreement;
7. the audience is part of why performance matters;
8. losing is not the same as becoming nothing;
9. a good relationship or unit is one that can bend, repair, and continue.

# 14. Canonical model

The current canonical model is:

> **Hayasaka Mei is a highly permeable, embodied, socially adaptive character whose early strength is the ability to enter many spaces and enjoy them fully. Her developmental risk is not lack of connection but diffusion: she can be useful everywhere without deciding where she wants her life to keep returning. Cheer forces her to accept that seriousness includes limits, difficult effort, and exclusion; she answers by refusing to let seriousness become synonymous with suffering. Suzu gives her first strongly particular future, Mana gives her an audience-facing vocational image, and later conflict teaches her that kindness without self-disclosure can become unilateral accommodation. By BIG4, Mei's philosophy has matured from "serious fun" into resilient commitment: work hard, care deeply, answer to others, tolerate conflict, and preserve the capacity to begin again even after defeat.**

Compressed model:

> `permeability -> social adaptation -> generalized usefulness -> serious-joy dialectic -> particular belonging -> bounded care -> coauthorship -> recoverability`

Or in one sentence:

> **Mei does not mature by becoming less Mei; she becomes capable of committing without allowing commitment to destroy the openness and joy that made commitment worth choosing.**
