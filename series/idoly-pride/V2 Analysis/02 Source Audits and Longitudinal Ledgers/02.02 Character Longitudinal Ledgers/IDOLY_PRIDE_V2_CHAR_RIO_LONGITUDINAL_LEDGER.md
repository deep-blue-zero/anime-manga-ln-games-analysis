# 0. Responsibility and authority

This ledger is the current Phase-2 character authority for **Kanzaki Rio / 神崎莉央 / character code `rio`** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`.

It reconstructs Rio across chronology rather than flattening her into the familiar surface cluster of **proud elite idol, perfectionist, strict LizNoir leader, Mana rival, intimidating senior, or hyper-competitive professional**. All of those descriptions point toward genuine aspects of Rio. None by itself explains how those traits acquired their meaning, why they repeatedly become dangerous, or why they persist after she matures.

Rio's longitudinal problem begins not with vanity but with **responsibility under material pressure**. She enters entertainment because success appears capable of protecting her mother and the family cafe. Her earliest professional ambition is therefore instrumental: earn enough, succeed hard enough, make failure materially impossible. Saegusa then deliberately destroys the comfort of local superiority by telling her that the real benchmark is the professional field. This helps establish a recurring Rio need for **undeniability**: a result matters most when it cannot be dismissed as too small, too easy, or unearned.

That structure makes Aoi's earliest criticism decisive. Aoi does not tell Rio that technique is bad. She tells her that correct technique is failing to reach people because Rio's body and heart are separated from one another. Rio can execute; she has not yet fully authored what execution is for. Original LizNoir is consequently founded through complementarity: Rio supplies weight, discipline, structure, and forward pressure; Aoi supplies bodily freedom and an unusually direct sensor for whether performance remains alive. Their partnership is not merely emotional support after the fact. It is built into Rio's acquisition of an expressive professional self.

Mana is the second major transformation. Rio initially encounters her as an opponent who threatens a future she desperately needs. Yet Rio immediately establishes a fair-play floor: if she defeats Mana through scandal or extra-performance sabotage, the result will not answer the question Mana poses. Rio therefore wants to beat her **onstage, directly, under conditions that preserve the meaning of victory**. Rivalry becomes more than a ranking mechanism. It becomes a medium through which Rio discovers that idolhood itself has become personally non-substitutable.

Mana's death then turns the ideal form of rivalry into its pathological opposite. A living rival can change, answer, disappoint, improve, lose, win, and return. A dead rival can no longer participate in the future of the relation. Rio's year away from singing demonstrates that the profession can no longer be reduced to money: losing Mana makes the stage itself temporarily impossible. Mana becomes a frozen benchmark, and Rio's later development repeatedly asks how to remember that benchmark without allowing an interrupted contest to govern every living future.

The answer does not come through forgetting. The 2022 Mana-song event makes this explicit. Rio admits that a Mana song repeatedly encouraged her back toward singing and that she remains an idol in part because she encountered Mana and that song. She first refuses the cover project because she imagines singing Mana's songs as appropriation, then eventually understands performance as a way to say thank you. At Mana's grave she abandons the idea that she must forget in order to move. Memory becomes compatible with gratitude, surpassing, and continued self-authorship.

Defeat undergoes a parallel transformation. In the post-Mana two-person LizNoir, loss initially carries existential pressure: unit survival, Aoi's injury, accumulated secrecy, and Rio's unresolved history are all concentrated into competition. Yet after losing to Tsuki, Rio can still say that Aoi made her career possible and that being Aoi's partner was happiness. Later Tokyo and Stellar material repeat this at larger scale. Loss can hurt, teach, and create a rematch without acquiring sovereign power to declare that the previous career, relationship, or unit was false.

Leadership recreates Rio's original danger at a higher level. Once Ai and Kokoro join, Rio can again bury her own desire beneath responsibility—only now the duty is not protecting her mother but carrying LizNoir to the summit. `The Road of Battle` forces the leader mask open. Asked what **Kanzaki Rio herself** wants, she answers that she wants to stand onstage and reach the top for herself. This is not selfish regression. It is a correction to self-erasing responsibility: leadership is safer when the leader admits that she is also a participant with desire rather than pretending to be only the unit's instrument.

The successors then change what Rio's authority means. Her harsh criticism of Ai in `芽吹く黒ユリの蕾` contains a correct performance diagnosis but overbroad language. Rio later apologizes, explicitly says emotional expression is one of Ai's strengths, and invites Ai to keep speaking without restraint. When Ai proposes modifying inherited staging, Rio does not demand fidelity to a frozen original; she asks for a concrete proposal and makes Ai a co-designer. Mature succession therefore means more than permitting juniors to stand beside the founders. It means giving them the authority to reinterpret inherited form.

The 2024 professional-adaptation event provides Rio's strongest later self-correction. Rio is willing to accept an off-brand commercial job because growth can require change and because professional work sometimes requires expressing another person's intention. But she does not finally endorse obedience. She argues that client and performer must understand one another and negotiate a mutually intelligible landing point. At the same time, Aoi correctly notices that Rio's urgency toward BIG4 can make opportunity and name recognition outrun self-recognition. Rio explicitly wants Aoi to keep dissenting, and the event ends not with a solved slogan but with Rio admitting that she does not yet know for whom or why the summit matters. The text therefore refuses both static authenticity and metric capture.

Later four-person material stabilizes the endpoint. Rio can call the present unit a miracle requiring exactly these four; mature Aoi can define LizNoir-style dance as Rio dancing like Rio, Aoi like Aoi, Ai like Ai, and Kokoro like Kokoro; creative authority becomes distributed. Rio/Aoi remain a privileged founding dyad, but the mature unit is not two real members plus two attachments. Tokyo restores living rivalry as futurity, while Stellar lets Rio imagine the ideal idol as something perhaps decades away. The summit becomes a moving horizon rather than a trophy capable of closing the profession.

The mature model is therefore:

> **Rio does not mature by becoming less proud, less severe, less competitive, less technically demanding, or less interested in reaching the top. She matures by making excellence increasingly reciprocal and answerable. Technique must answer to expression. Victory must answer to fair contest. Leadership must answer to the leader's own desire and to juniors capable of correcting her. Adaptation must answer to negotiated purpose rather than external imposition. Memory must answer to the living future rather than freeze it. Pride must be able to coexist with dependence. A result can matter enormously without acquiring sovereign power to decide whether Rio, her relationships, or LizNoir were worth having.**

# 3. Longitudinal argument

### Stage 1 — duty before vocation
Rio initially treats professional success as a material solution to family vulnerability. Ambition is real, but its object is protection rather than idol self-realization.

### Stage 2 — undeniability becomes a professional need
Saegusa makes local superiority insufficient. Rio learns to seek standards whose difficulty gives success evidentiary force.

### Stage 3 — expression interrupts correctness
Aoi identifies that Rio can perform correctly without fully inhabiting the performance. Complementarity makes desire and bodily expression part of excellence.

### Stage 4 — rivalry turns work into vocation
Mana supplies an opponent Rio cannot reduce to a paycheck or external benchmark. Rio wants to win fairly because the encounter itself has become meaningful.

### Stage 5 — death freezes proof
Mana's death removes the other participant from the future. Rio's year away reveals that the stage has become personal enough to be wounded existentially.

### Stage 6 — defeat becomes survivable
Tsuki's victory hurts without erasing Rio/Aoi's shared past or future. Result remains important while losing its monopoly on meaning.

### Stage 7 — leadership repeats duty capture
As leader of four, Rio can again disappear into responsibility. The old family-duty structure returns at a more prestigious scale.

### Stage 8 — desire is re-admitted
Rio explicitly claims the summit for herself. Leadership stops pretending that the leader has no personal stake.

### Stage 9 — authority becomes contestable
Ai can receive correction and correct Rio; successor performance can reinterpret inherited form; Aoi can dissent; juniors can co-author LizNoir.

### Stage 10 — professionalism becomes negotiated
Rio learns to distinguish adaptive work from externally authored identity. Good professional work requires mutual understanding, not either rigid authenticity or passive compliance.

### Stage 11 — rivalry becomes future-facing
Living rivals allow loss, response, change, rematch, and reciprocal development. The structure that Mana's death froze becomes temporal again.

### Stage 12 — excellence becomes long-horizon
The ideal idol recedes as Rio approaches it. A title cannot complete the vocation because excellence is no longer one sovereign result.

# 4. Character mechanics

## 4.1 Rio converts anxiety into standards

Rio often reacts to vulnerability by making the problem measurable: earn more, train harder, win a more undeniable contest, reach BIG4, increase recognition, eliminate ambiguity. This is one reason she can look innately severe. Standards give her a way to turn uncertainty into something actionable.

The strength of this mechanism is obvious: Rio is disciplined, decisive, coachable at an elite level, and unusually resistant to vague self-excuse. Its failure mode is equally important: when the metric becomes the purpose, Rio can continue optimizing after she has lost contact with why the result matters.

## 4.2 Pride is defensive, aspirational, and relational

Rio's pride is not a single trait. It operates as:

- defense against helplessness;
- refusal to accept pity;
- commitment to fair conditions that make achievement meaningful;
- protection of LizNoir's accumulated labor;
- refusal to give a rival a compromised version of herself;
- aspiration toward standards she considers worthy of respect.

Mature pride therefore does not require invulnerability. Rio can need Aoi, take advice from Saki, apologize to Ai, accept creative input, and still remain recognizably proud.

## 4.3 Fairness protects the meaning of competition

Rio's refusal to sabotage Mana outside performance is not generic niceness. It is structurally tied to why she values winning. If the rival is weakened by scandal rather than performance, victory no longer proves what Rio wants it to prove.

This same logic later supports respect for rivals and rejection of pity. Competition is useful because it can generate evidence and transformation under conditions both sides recognize.

## 4.4 Rio tends to over-own responsibility

Across family duty, seniority toward Aoi, quartet leadership, and commercial self-production, Rio repeatedly assumes that being the responsible person means she should carry more of the burden herself.

The mature correction is not irresponsibility. It is **shared ownership**:

- Aoi can care for her;
- juniors can make decisions;
- managers can be trusted;
- clients must explain their purpose;
- teammates can bear creative authority;
- the leader may state personal desire instead of hiding it beneath duty.

## 4.5 She is capable of strong diagnosis and excessive delivery

Rio often sees the real problem quickly. With Ai, she correctly notices a breakdown in synchronization and stage awareness. With professional work, she sees that both client and performer intent matter. With rivals, she sees that pity can be disrespectful.

Her failure mode is compression: she can turn a precise diagnosis into a categorical judgment such as `LizNoir失格`. P2-B3 therefore distinguishes **diagnostic competence** from **optimal interpersonal delivery**.

## 4.6 Rio is more adaptive than her traditionalist image suggests

She can seem like the member most likely to defend inherited LizNoir form, yet the later corpus repeatedly shows conditional openness to change:

- partner adjustment in the original duo;
- admitting Ai/Kokoro into the unit;
- allowing Ai to reinterpret inherited staging;
- accepting commercial experimentation when negotiation can preserve meaning;
- recognizing natural/off-guard presentation as professionally usable;
- accepting the mature quartet as a form whose identity emerges from four different members.

What she resists is not change itself but change that seems to lack an intelligible relation to excellence, purpose, or LizNoir's self-recognition.

## 4.7 Emotional containment is strategic, not emotional absence

Rio says that a LizNoir leader cannot be a crybaby, and Aoi notes that Rio tends to cry alone. This is direct evidence of **private emotionalization under public control**.

The mature corpus does not fully abolish that pattern. Instead it makes its costs visible and increases the number of people before whom Rio can admit uncertainty, dependence, embarrassment, gratitude, or desire.

## 4.8 Rivalry is one of Rio's intimacy languages

Mana is the clearest case, but not the only one. Rio often communicates respect by wanting the other person fully present as an opponent. A diminished opponent produces a diminished result. Later living rivals restore a form of relationship in which recognition occurs through challenge, loss, and anticipated rematch.

This does not mean all Rio affection is competitive. The bond material shows domestic care and ordinary dependence. It means competition is one of the places where Rio most readily grants another person consequential standing.

## 4.9 Her ordinary self contains visible softness and comic stubbornness

Lower-pressure material matters because public Rio can otherwise be over-modeled as permanently composed. Stable ordinary traits include:

- strong cooking competence from the family-cafe background;
- intense food/pancake interest;
- stubborn persistence over trivial goals such as a crane-game prize;
- embarrassment when affection or old footage becomes too explicit;
- awkwardness in unstructured silence;
- suppressible laughter;
- a tendency to scold and then help;
- prideful resistance to asking for help followed by genuine correction.

These are not a hidden “real Rio” opposed to the elite idol. They are the same person under lower stakes.

## 4.10 Rio learns that dependence can be chosen rather than imposed

Early dependence feels dangerous because Rio's self-story is organized around being the one who protects, earns, teaches, or leads. Yet Aoi repeatedly proves useful before Rio is ready to admit it. Later Rio explicitly tells Saki to rely on others, recognizes the hypocrisy when she herself needs practical help, and decides to ask more honestly.

Dependence becomes compatible with pride when it is reciprocal and does not erase competence.

# 5. Origin: duty, complementarity, and vocation

## 5.1 Idolhood begins as a family-protection project

`origin_liz_001_a_budding_lily`, `st-group-liz-01-01-01` gives the earliest Rio motive with unusual clarity:

- `一杯稼ぐわ`
- `お母さんとあの店を守るために……`

This matters because later elite ambition cannot safely be projected backward as an innate desire to dominate the idol industry. Rio enters because professional success appears economically useful to people she loves.

The structural consequence is stronger than “Rio cares about family.” It means **failure begins as a problem with material consequences**. Her later attraction to hard, visible benchmarks therefore has a plausible developmental origin: if success is supposed to protect a future, ambiguous success feels unsafe.

## 5.2 Saegusa makes local excellence insufficient

Rio is already capable relative to the trainee environment. Saegusa refuses to let that local superiority become a terminal identity and points her toward the professional field.

P2-B3 interprets this as the first major installation of Rio's **undeniability drive**. She wants a benchmark that can answer back. A victory over a weak or compromised standard does not settle the question.

## 5.3 Aoi diagnoses a teleological problem, not a technical one

In `st-group-liz-01-01-04`, Aoi tells Rio:

- `莉央のダンスはすごくちゃんとしてる`
- `でも、それだけじゃ響かないんだ`
- `心と体がバラバラなんだ`
- `莉央、踊ってても楽しくないんじゃない？`

Aoi explicitly grants Rio technical correctness. The missing term is not skill but **owned expressive end**. Rio's body is solving the task while her desire remains external to it.

This is the earliest form of a problem that will recur much later in commercial adaptation and BIG4 pursuit: Rio can become extremely competent at an objective before asking whether she still owns the objective.

## 5.4 Founding complementarity is productive incompleteness

Rio's early line in `st-group-liz-01-01-05`—`私と貴方を足して、ようやく一人前なのね……`—captures the founding logic.

Neither member is positioned as the complete idol plus assistant. The duo is built because each exposes a deficit in the other. This gives Aoi unusually deep standing to challenge Rio later: Aoi's dissent is historically entangled with the conditions under which Rio first became a performer capable of reaching others.

## 5.5 Mana is accepted only as a rival whose answer cannot be faked

`origin_liz_002_one_more_dream`, `st-group-liz-01-01-07` makes Rio's fair-play floor explicit:

- `長瀬麻奈には何もしないでください`
- `LizNoirは正々堂々と、彼女と戦って勝ちます`

Rio is competitive before she is ethically softened by defeat. The important distinction is therefore not early ruthless Rio versus later kind Rio. Even early Rio rejects a victory whose causal basis would destroy its evidentiary meaning.

## 5.6 Mana transforms the object of ambition

Mana's performance is destabilizing because Rio encounters a rival who seems to integrate qualities Rio has had to build through duty and complementarity. Rio eventually calls her `運命の相手`.

The label matters less as romance than as vocation. A rival has become one of the places where Rio discovers what she wants from the stage.

## 5.7 The year away proves idolhood has become non-substitutable

After Mana's death, the source marks roughly a year of withdrawal before Rio returns to live performance (`st-group-liz-01-01-09` → `10`).

If idol work were still merely the fastest income strategy, another performative route could in principle replace it. Instead the profession becomes difficult to inhabit at all. This is why P2-B3 preserves the Phase-1 formulation:

> **Rio's idol vocation is born when instrumental work becomes personally non-substitutable.**

# 6. Mana, memory, and the frozen benchmark

## 6.1 Rio's Saegusa grievance is psychologically real and historically wrong

`origin_liz_009_the_beginning_venus`, `st-group-liz-01-02-11` establishes the chronology:

- Saegusa had already become disillusioned with the VENUS system;
- he had already decided to leave;
- Rio's intensity delayed departure long enough for him to produce LizNoir;
- he left BanPro and later founded Hoshimi;
- only after that did he encounter Mana.

Thus Mana did **not** steal Saegusa from Rio.

This does not make Rio's earlier grievance meaningless. Mana becomes the narrative point onto which mentor abandonment, rival challenge, records, interrupted recognition, and death are fused. P2-B3 therefore distinguishes factual causation from psychologically operative self-story.

## 6.2 Death changes rivalry's temporal structure

A living opponent can answer. A dead opponent cannot. That difference is why “frozen benchmark” is more precise than simply saying Rio cannot get over Mana.

The problem is not that Rio remembers. It is that the rivalry's normal future operations—response, rematch, mutual change—have been removed.

## 6.3 The Mana-song event rejects forgetting as the price of adulthood

`event_2022_010_st-eve-2210-race` retests Rio's mature relation to Mana.

At first Rio says she and LizNoir cannot sing Mana's song (`st-eve-2210-race-002`). Her refusal is not evidence of hatred. It is a boundary problem: she fears taking possession of something she associates with Mana.

In `st-eve-2210-race-004`, Rio admits that the song repeatedly encouraged her and that she remains able to sing because she encountered Mana and the song. At the grave in `st-eve-2210-race-005`, her internal movement becomes explicit:

- she had tried to forget;
- she thought she had to forget;
- she realizes she never could;
- then realizes she **did not need to**.

Memory therefore ceases to be a binary choice between captivity and erasure.

## 6.4 Gratitude does not end ambition

Rio's mature relation to Mana is not peaceful memorial closure in the sense of ceasing to compete. She wants to thank Mana, have Mana see who she has become, and continue trying to surpass her.

The combination is important:

> **gratitude + rivalry + future movement**

rather than:

> **forgetting + release from ambition**.

## 6.5 Hidden crying corrects the invulnerable-leader model

Aoi says Rio cries alone when she wants to cry. Rio answers in `st-eve-2210-race-004`:

`LizNoirのリーダーは泣き虫じゃ務まらないの`

This is not proof that tears disappear. It is proof that Rio has attached emotional containment to the office of leader. The longitudinal question becomes whether leadership can remain competent without requiring private affect to be entirely self-sequestered.

# 7. Rio and Aoi: constitutive witness, dependence, and dissent

## 7.1 Seniority begins as unilateral duty

`bond_rio_001_rio`, `st-company-bond-rio-01-01` shows pre-debut Rio insisting that she must manage the younger Aoi because:

`貴方よりずっと年上なんだから！`

The age relation becomes a moral job description in Rio's head. She is older, therefore she should be the competent caretaker.

## 7.2 Aoi's care precedes role equality

The same material shows Aoi noticing Rio's exhaustion and trying to help. Reciprocity therefore does not begin only after Rio consciously learns it. Aoi is already caring back while Rio still narrates the relation as one-way senior responsibility.

## 7.3 Dependence becomes speakable inside pride

The crane-game episode (`st-company-bond-rio-01-03`) provides a compact ordinary-life model. Rio becomes stubborn, Aoi solves the problem quickly, and Rio privately admits:

`悔しいけど頼もしいのよね`

The formulation is characterologically useful because it does not require pride to vanish. Reliance can be irritating to her pride and still be recognized as real competence in the other person.

## 7.4 The founding dyad stays special without becoming exclusive ontology

In the dinner episode (`st-company-bond-rio-01-04`), Aoi calls Rio special; Rio becomes visibly flustered, and both acknowledge the continuity of their bond even as the four-person unit has changed their daily life.

This supports a mature formulation already established by the origin audit:

> **Rio/Aoi remain LizNoir's constitutive and privileged original dyad; they are not LizNoir's exclusive mature ontology.**

## 7.5 Direct conflict becomes a relationship condition

The C2 card `card_rio_016_st-card-rio-05-past-00`, especially `st-card-rio-05-past-00-02` and `03`, retrospectively describes an early Rio/Aoi conflict around performance choices. Rio admits she had been too harsh and that they did not yet understand one another. The eventual live succeeds because **both adjust**: Rio incorporates Aoi's preferences while Aoi incorporates the original staging intent.

Rio later says that Aoi's complaints and resistance were themselves forms of communication and that she learned to face Aoi seriously and directly.

This strengthens the idea that Aoi's dissent is not an obstacle Rio eventually outgrows. Dissent becomes one of the conditions by which their partnership remains self-correcting.

## 7.6 Rio can teach reliance only after being taught by dependence

In `st-company-bond-rio-01-07` and `08`, Rio tells overloaded Saki:

`時には周りを頼るのも、必要なことよ`

Then ordinary errands expose Rio's own inability to do everything herself. Embarrassed by repeated calls for help, she eventually says:

`私も、もっと素直に頼ることにするわ`

The comedy performs a serious longitudinal correction: **care competence does not create immunity from needing care**.

# 8. Quartet leadership: authority becomes contestable

## 8.1 Leader-duty repeats Rio's oldest structure

Family protection taught Rio to turn responsibility into self-subordination. Quartet leadership gives that habit a prestigious new form: “take everyone to the summit,” “be the leader,” “carry the standard.”

The danger is not that she cares about the unit. It is that Rio can make herself disappear into the role and then treat the role's objective as self-justifying.

## 8.2 Rio must answer as Rio, not only as LizNoir's leader

`origin_liz_008_the_road_of_battle`, `st-group-liz-01-02-09` forces the distinction. Rio eventually says:

`私は、神崎莉央は……誰のためでもなく、自分のために`

`舞台に立ちたい。そして、アイドルの頂点を目指したい`

The repeated naming—`私は、神崎莉央は`—is structurally important. She extracts a first-person desire from beneath institutional role.

## 8.3 Admitted self-interest improves leadership

The scene does not punish Rio for wanting the summit. Aoi recognizes something more authentically Rio-like in the admission. Ai and Kokoro then confront their own tendency to define commitment as serving the seniors' dream.

Mature leadership therefore requires **plural self-interest made answerable**, not a saintly leader with no personal desire.

## 8.4 Four-person responsibility is not founder delegation

Ai's `私達は四人でLizNoirだから` in `st-group-liz-01-02-10` states the constitutional correction. The juniors cannot merely admire, obey, or carry out Rio/Aoi's design. If they are members, they must accept responsibility when the founders cannot carry everything.

# 9. Mentorship and succession: Ai may alter the inheritance

## 9.1 Rio's harsh critique contains a real technical claim

In `event_2021_003_st-eve-2109-backside`, Rio criticizes Ai after Ai's enthusiasm disrupts synchronization, stage awareness, and the relation to the rest of the group.

P2-B3 does not flatten this into “Rio is too strict.” The performance diagnosis is meaningful.

## 9.2 The source explicitly corrects Rio's delivery

In `st-eve-2109-backside-004`, Rio apologizes for having been too harsh and says:

- `感情を表に出して踊るのが悪いと言ってる訳じゃない`
- Ai's ability to express emotion directly is a strength;
- `あなたに期待しているからよ`.

Thus the story refuses the easy inference that emotional suppression is LizNoir's standard. Rio's mature standard is **controlled expressivity**, not affective absence.

## 9.3 Expectation is not permission to speak categorically

The arc is useful for modeling Rio because it separates three things she can conflate under pressure:

1. high expectation;
2. accurate diagnosis;
3. legitimate wording/interpersonal method.

The first two do not automatically validate the third.

## 9.4 Successor authority includes reinterpretation

When Ai proposes altering inherited `The Last Chance` staging, Rio says in `st-eve-2109-backside-005`:

`いいわ、愛のアイデアを検討しましょう`

and later:

`これからも思ったことは遠慮無く言いなさい`

This is not ceremonial openness. Rio makes Ai part of the design process. The successor is legitimate precisely because she is allowed to produce a version the predecessor did not already specify.

## 9.5 Rio's authority becomes strongest when it can be answered

This event supplies one of the clearest local forms of the capstone model. Rio remains leader, critic, and standard-bearer. But the relation becomes healthier when the person being led can contribute a distinct strength, challenge inherited form, and speak back without being treated as disloyal.

# 10. Professional adaptation: change without external authorship

## 10.1 Rio is not a simple traditionalist

`event_2024_005_st-eve-2405-race` begins with Rio already dissatisfied after a successful tour because BIG4 remains distant. An unusual commercial opportunity appears. Rio accepts quickly and even tells the manager:

`いい加減、他人を信頼して任せることを覚えなさい`

The irony is productive: Rio can articulate delegation before she fully practices it in relation to her own leader role.

## 10.2 External intention may legitimately enter the work

When the client asks LizNoir to perform in an intentionally off-brand cheerful/sparkly mode, Rio does not treat any foreign intention as contamination. She argues that growth sometimes requires change and that professional performers must sometimes attempt to express intentions not originally their own.

Her explicit position includes:

`変化を避けていては、私達はこれ以上成長出来ないと思う`

This is genuine development beyond a static-authenticity model.

## 10.3 Aoi correctly detects metric capture risk

Aoi's objection is not simply “never change.” She asks why the opportunity, name recognition, and BIG4 ascent have become so urgent that Rio may be losing contact with LizNoir's own meaningful standard.

Rio's answer—`理想論だけじゃ、BIG4にはなれない`—contains real professional truth, but it does not settle the argument.

## 10.4 Rio preserves dissent as an intended function

When Aoi says she will not compromise, Rio answers:

`貴方はそれでいいわ、葵`

This is unusually important for Rio's authority model. She does not merely tolerate dissent after the fact; she recognizes Aoi's uncompromising stance as something Aoi should continue to provide.

## 10.5 Professionalism becomes negotiated authorship

In the client meeting (`st-eve-2405-race-004`), Rio argues that good work cannot be produced through one-way imposition. Client intent must be understood, performer intent must be articulated, and both sides must keep adjusting toward a shared solution.

Her summary:

`それがプロの仕事でしょう？`

Mature professionalism therefore sits between two failures:

- **rigid self-enclosure**: refusing any outside intention;
- **external capture**: treating the client's request as sufficient justification.

The middle term is **negotiated professional authorship**.

## 10.6 Natural self-presentation can become professional material

The resulting CM incorporates playful, less-guarded footage. Rio recognizes that an unplanned natural self can have professional value without invalidating the constructed LizNoir image.

This is a subtle extension of the original Aoi lesson: authenticity is not the absence of craft, but craft can become sterile if every valuable expression must be pre-authorized by the formal image.

## 10.7 The event ends by reopening, not solving, the summit question

After damaged Rio/Aoi coordination nearly costs them a live battle, Aoi asks what they are willing to discard to reach the top and for whom the pursuit is happening.

Rio answers in `st-eve-2405-race-005`:

`今はまだ、分からないわ`

This is a mature admission of uncertainty, not a failure of characterization. P2-B3 therefore does **not** convert “Rio wants the top for herself” into a timeless self-justifying answer. Desire must still be periodically re-authored as circumstances change.

# 11. Competition, living rivals, and long-horizon vocation

## 11.1 Rio never becomes anti-competition

`origin_liz_009_the_beginning_venus` gives Rio one of the franchise's strongest defenses of competition after Saegusa describes the harms of VENUS ranking.

She refuses pity and argues that there are things sharpened or obtained only through competition. She intends to keep fighting on the stage Saegusa helped create and become an idol who makes him glad VENUS exists.

The source therefore requires a dual institutional conclusion:

> **Competition can sharpen vocation, create narrative, and make excellence visible; danger begins when measurement colonizes the ends for which performance exists.**

Rio is the strongest textual reason not to turn the series into an anti-ranking argument.

## 11.2 Defeat becomes a carrier of knowledge rather than a verdict of worth

The Tsuki defeat already begins this shift. Tokyo makes it more explicit. Rio's competitive ethic distinguishes respect from pity and treats winners and losers as participants who must carry the result forward.

The result remains real. The interpretation changes.

## 11.3 Living rivals restore time

Tokyo's mature Rio can recognize SUNNY PEACE as a rival who remains in front of her **now**. This is the exact temporal capacity Mana cannot provide after death.

A living rival can:

- answer back;
- improve;
- be defeated later;
- defeat Rio now;
- reveal a new standard;
- become a future encounter rather than a permanent counterfactual.

This is why P2-B3 treats rivalry as a form of reciprocal futurity rather than merely aggression.

## 11.4 Four-person LizNoir survives narrow defeat as four

Stellar's later LizNoir material explicitly identifies the present unit as a miracle requiring these exact four people. After a high-level loss, Rio and the unit can still understand the performance as excellent and identify further growth.

This is the opposite of result sovereignty: loss is information and motivation, not ontological cancellation.

## 11.5 The ideal idol becomes a moving horizon

The Stellar ending lets Rio imagine the ideal idol as perhaps twenty or thirty years away. This should not be turned into a literal career timetable. Its significance is horizon structure.

The summit no longer functions as a point after which the self has been proven once and for all. Excellence can remain unfinished across adulthood.

# 12. Mature quartet: identity without fixed form

## 12.1 The quartet is not Rio/Aoi plus apprentices

Later material consistently refuses to leave Ai and Kokoro at disciple status. They contribute ideas, correction, choreography, formation changes, and responsibility.

The original dyad remains historically and emotionally privileged, but mature LizNoir is an exact-four-person present.

## 12.2 `LizNoir-like` becomes emergent rather than prescriptive

`event_2025_010_st-eve-2511-race` provides a particularly clear mature formulation. Rio reflects that the real four-person LizNoir may have begun at the point when the quartet started constructing identity together. Aoi eventually says that LizNoir-like dance is not one fixed form; it is Rio dancing like Rio, Aoi like Aoi, Ai like Ai, and Kokoro like Kokoro.

This does **not** mean anything any member does is automatically LizNoir. It means identity is produced through coordinated plurality rather than obedience to one frozen choreography or founder image.

## 12.3 Rio remains a leader inside distributed authority

Distributed authorship does not erase Rio's seniority, status, or leadership tendency. It changes what those roles authorize. The leader may coordinate, demand, judge, and decide—but must remain answerable to other members who possess genuine standing as co-authors.

# 13. Voice, behavior, and personality reconstruction notes

This section is **character-ledger support**, not the final P2-F Japanese voice/register authority. It records Rio features already stable enough to constrain later modeling.

## 13.1 Surface register

Rio's default Japanese voice is controlled, declarative, and strongly adult-coded relative to many younger idols. Recurring tendencies include:

- first-person `私`;
- direct second-person address, often `貴方` in formal/assertive interpersonal contexts;
- feminine declarative endings such as `～わ`, `～よ`, and explanatory `～の`/`～でしょう` patterns;
- imperative/admonitory forms such as `～なさい` when occupying senior/leader role;
- relatively low tolerance for vague euphemism when she thinks a performance or professional issue must be named;
- ability to shift into polite professional language with clients, managers, or institutional seniors without losing firmness.

The effect is not simply “ojou-sama” speech. Rio's register communicates **controlled senior authority and precision**, with warmth or embarrassment appearing as deviations within that stable grammar.

## 13.2 Criticism is usually proposition-first

Rio often states what is wrong before cushioning how the recipient feels about hearing it. This makes her speech efficient under professional pressure and risky under emotional pressure.

A simulation that makes her constantly tactful would be wrong. A simulation that makes her incapable of apology would also be wrong.

## 13.3 She uses categorical language when anxious

Statements such as `LizNoir失格` illustrate a broader pattern: under pressure Rio can compress a local failure into a large category judgment. Later correction often involves reopening the category and specifying the actual problem more precisely.

## 13.4 Aoi receives a uniquely contestable register

With Aoi, Rio can scold, argue, become flustered, accept mockery, and admit dependence while still maintaining senior/proud posture. Aoi is one of the few people whose contradiction can be treated as continuity of the relationship rather than a threat to authority.

This makes Rio/Aoi simulation especially dependent on relationship-state evidence rather than generic Rio speech rules.

## 13.5 Junior speech combines expectation and command

With Ai/Kokoro and younger peers, Rio naturally occupies senpai/leader position. She gives direct instructions and evaluates performance. Mature Rio, however, increasingly pairs this with explicit invitation to speak back and contribute ideas.

The operational model is therefore:

> **high expectation + explicit hierarchy + increasing co-authorship**

rather than either authoritarian one-way command or flattened equality.

## 13.6 Rival speech is recognition through challenge

Rio's strongest respectful rival language often sounds severe because respect is expressed through taking the opponent seriously enough to demand full contest. Pity, strategic sabotage, or easy victory can be more insulting to her than harsh competitive language.

## 13.7 Embarrassment breaks composure quickly when intimacy becomes explicit

Bond/card material repeatedly shows rapid fluster when:

- Aoi calls the relationship special;
- old footage exposes earlier immaturity;
- affectionate physical demonstration becomes too direct;
- Rio's competence is undercut by an ordinary practical gap.

This is not a tsundere master key. It is a reliable contrast between **publicly managed authority** and **private exposure she did not script**.

## 13.8 Food and domestic competence are stable ordinary anchors

Rio cooks well because of the family cafe background and treats food quality seriously. Pancakes in particular repeatedly produce strong enthusiasm. These motifs matter for simulation because they give her ordinary desire that is not subordinate to idol philosophy.

## 13.9 Behavioral defaults

When confronted with a problem, Rio is likely to:

1. identify an actionable standard;
2. assume responsibility quickly;
3. ask whether the result can withstand external scrutiny;
4. push toward a concrete solution;
5. become impatient with vague emotional evasion;
6. underweight her own need for help or rest;
7. accept correction more readily from people who demonstrate competence and commitment;
8. preserve hierarchy while allowing reciprocal contribution once trust is established;
9. respond to defeat by analyzing what it means for the next attempt rather than seeking consolation;
10. become visibly flustered when the issue shifts from professional competence to exposed private affection or dependence.

## 13.10 Worldview

Rio's mature worldview can be summarized as follows:

- **effort should be capable of producing real differences;**
- **standards matter because achievement should mean something;**
- **competition is legitimate when the contest preserves the relation between performance and result;**
- **professionalism requires understanding and negotiation, not passive obedience;**
- **emotion belongs in elite performance, but must be integrated with craft and ensemble awareness;**
- **leadership is responsibility, but responsibility cannot safely erase the leader's own desire;**
- **a strong unit must be able to change without losing the capacity to recognize itself;**
- **memory should sustain future action rather than freeze it;**
- **dependence is not humiliation when it is reciprocal;**
- **defeat is consequential but not sovereign.**

# 18. Canonical model

## `RECIPROCAL_EXCELLENCE_WITHOUT_RESULT_SOVEREIGNTY_RIO`

Rio's character is best understood as a long conversion in **what excellence is allowed to answer to**.

At first, excellence answers to family survival. Then it answers to Saegusa's professional benchmark. Aoi forces it to answer to lived expression. Mana forces victory to answer to a rival worthy of fair contest. Death reveals what happens when the rival can no longer answer at all. Defeat teaches Rio that a result can be real without owning the meaning of an entire career. Quartet leadership forces her to answer as a person rather than hide behind office. Ai and Kokoro force authority to answer to successors who can reinterpret inherited work. Commercial production forces professional adaptation to answer to mutual understanding. Aoi keeps self-recognition inside the system by refusing to become a compliant extension of Rio's urgency. Living rivals restore the back-and-forth temporality of contest. Stellar extends the horizon so far that no single trophy can complete it.

The mature Rio is therefore neither anti-proof nor proof-captive.

She still wants evidence. She still wants opponents strong enough to matter. She still wants the summit. She still speaks sharply, trains hard, expects professionalism, and dislikes excuses. The change is constitutional:

> **no one result, rank, leader role, inherited form, dead rival, client demand, or image standard is permitted to become the sole sovereign that decides what Rio's work is for.**

Excellence becomes **reciprocal** because it is produced through answerable relations:

- performer ↔ audience;
- Rio ↔ Aoi;
- founder ↔ successor;
- LizNoir ↔ rival;
- professional ↔ client;
- present Rio ↔ remembered Mana;
- leader ↔ member;
- pride ↔ dependence;
- victory ↔ possibility of future loss and rematch.

Short retrieval formulation:

> **Rio learns to pursue undeniable excellence without letting proof own her: she can compete, lead, adapt, remember, lose, rely, and change while preserving both the seriousness of standards and the right of living people to keep reauthoring what those standards are for.**
