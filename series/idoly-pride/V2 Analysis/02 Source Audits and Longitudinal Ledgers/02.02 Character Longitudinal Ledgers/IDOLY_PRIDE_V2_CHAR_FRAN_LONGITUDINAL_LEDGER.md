# 0. Responsibility and authority

This ledger is the current Phase-2 character authority for **fran / character code `kor`** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`.

fran is especially vulnerable to interpretive flattening because the source makes her motives unusually explicit and unusually mixed.

One flattening is to take the early statement that idol work is only a `通過点` at face value forever and reduce her to a mercenary fashion professional who happens to sing because visibility and money are useful. That description is correct about her **entry state**, but not her endpoint. It cannot explain why she voluntarily re-selects IIIX after the original institutional bargain collapses, why she continues to develop idol-specific expertise, why she spends future-brand capital on group-specific costumes, why she accepts local challenges to her own center placement, why she transfers idol competencies back into fashion, or why the later card has her state that she intends to reach the top in **both** idol and designer work.

The opposite flattening is more sentimental: fran was secretly meant to be an idol all along, learns friendship, discovers that fashion was a defense, and eventually chooses IIIX as her real dream. The source rejects this too. Fashion is not a false dream. It is one of the deepest structures of her self-authorship. `ERFOLG` remains important. She rebuilds industry contacts, invests, studies markets, restarts the brand, absorbs humiliation in a domain where idol prestige does not automatically transfer, and continues to identify design as serious professional work. Mature fran is not converted away from fashion.

The more precise problem is **instrumental disposability**.

fran's childhood teaches her that an imposed appearance can become authored form. Fashion turns a body she once experienced as socially stigmatizing into something she can style, present, and eventually build a profession around. Modeling, design, and the first `ERFOLG` project therefore teach her a powerful lesson: identity can be made rather than merely received.

But the first brand collapse teaches another lesson only incompletely at first. Authorship without sufficient attention to market, trust, capital, and audience can become self-defeating. In response, fran becomes intensely concerned with optionality: money, contacts, visibility, transferable fame, contract terms, reputation, and the ability to convert one professional role into another. Idol work initially enters this system as an instrument—a high-visibility bridge back toward fashion.

That is not fake. It is rational professional strategy.

The developmental risk is that **optionality can become a theory of disposability**. If a role is real only while it advances the next role, if a group exists only while it wins, if collaboration matters only while it increases bargaining power, then the future is preserved by keeping the present permanently revocable.

fran's development does not abolish transactional reasoning. It teaches her that transactions, market adaptation, and investment can coexist with durable authorship. She can choose a present commitment without surrendering future alternatives. She can let idol work become intrinsically interesting without making fashion unreal. She can care through business without pretending business was only a cover. She can alter designs for customers without selling off aesthetic jurisdiction. She can accept that a center decision is locally contestable without losing center identity. She can strategically curate public representation without claiming that all strategic construction is truth. She can remain hostile toward miho and kana while behaving in ways that make them specifically non-disposable.

The capstone is therefore:

> **`PLURAL_AUTHORSHIP_WITHOUT_INSTRUMENTAL_DISPOSABILITY_FRAN`: fran matures from treating idol work, publicity, IIIX, and professional visibility primarily as exchangeable capital for recovering a fashion future into a plural professional author who can pursue fashion and idolhood as concurrent serious vocations, adapt to markets without surrendering aesthetic jurisdiction, and invest in specific collaborators and shared forms without requiring them to become conventional friends or pretending that commercial motive has disappeared.**

Short form:

> **fran does not become less transactional; she becomes more selective about what she is willing to treat as disposable.**

This formulation preserves the strongest source tensions:

- fashion remains real;
- idolhood becomes real too;
- money remains real;
- aesthetic pleasure remains real;
- IIIX remains abrasive;
- business reasons for continuation remain real;
- relational commitment does not require friendship-label conversion;
- market adaptation does not require artistic surrender;
- public curation does not automatically equal authenticity;
- private cynicism does not monopolize truth.

The artifact also preserves the v2.5 scope boundary. fran has unusually rich ordinary-life texture—finance, thrift, anime/manga fandom, solitary moviegoing, health/beauty routines, shopping habits, strong memory, adult industry networking, sarcastic humor, and private/public register switching. Those are excellent Phase-8.5 modeling evidence. They enter this ledger only when they explain vocational optionality, authenticity, professional behavior, or a longitudinal relation.

# 4. Close reading I — fashion as formative self-authorship

## 4.1 Childhood difference is the prehistory of the fashion vocation

fran's fashion ambition is not adequately explained by vanity or professional success alone.

`origin_thrx_002` gives the formative structure directly. She resembles her German mother, with blond hair and blue eyes, and as a child dislikes those features intensely. She interprets social exclusion through them: perhaps nobody wants to play with her because she looks strange.

The source does not permit a universal causal claim that every peer rejected her **because** of those traits. It establishes the child's perception and its emotional consequence.

A cute dress then changes the field. fran is afraid to wear it where familiar school peers can see, so she goes somewhere nobody knows her. There she is scouted.

The decisive thought is not merely “I am pretty.” It is that fashion can make someone like her cute. The appearance she could not choose becomes material for chosen presentation.

This is the origin of:

> **`AESTHETIC_SELF_AUTHORSHIP`**

Fashion gives fran a way to convert imposed visibility into selected form.

## 4.2 Modeling validates the mechanism

The modeling career turns that revelation into durable practice:

- her previously hated hair becomes professional advantage;
- she advances rapidly;
- she reaches major runway/Paris Collection-level work;
- she learns high-level bodily presentation;
- she becomes professionally legible through visual authorship rather than despite visual difference.

This is why later aesthetic boundaries matter so much. They are not simply taste preferences. They protect a mode of agency through which fran first learned that the body others see does not have to remain something others define.

## 4.3 The fashion vocation becomes generative rather than merely representational

Modeling is not enough indefinitely. fran wants to move from presenting form to creating it.

She launches `ERFOLG`—named from German “success”—and explicitly says she designed the clothes herself. The dream is not just fame in fashion but authored production and brand ownership.

This creates the first stable vocational proposition:

> **fran wants not only to be seen; she wants jurisdiction over the form through which she is seen.**

# 5. Close reading II — `ERFOLG` failure, debt, and the birth of optionality discipline

## 5.1 Failure does not falsify the vocation

The first `ERFOLG` attempt fails badly enough to leave fran with roughly one hundred million yen in debt.

The source later clarifies the failure mechanism more precisely than “she was unlucky.” fran responds to dismissal by trying to prove herself through expensive, spectacular commitment. Her self-confidence and aesthetic ambition outrun business discipline.

This matters because the narrative does not punish fashion desire. It punishes a specific professional theory:

> **if my form is good enough and spectacular enough, recognition should follow.**

That theory is revised across the corpus.

## 5.2 Debt turns optionality into a daily professional problem

Money becomes unusually salient for fran because money is not merely consumption. It is future jurisdiction.

Debt, investment, savings, networking, contracts, venue economics, brand publicity, and status conversion all matter because they determine whether she can attempt authored fashion work again.

This is why recurring financial behavior should not be reduced to a greed gag.

The B1-A bond material establishes:

> **`FINANCIAL_OPTIONALITY_AS_PRIVATE_PRACTICE`**

The secure claim is not “fran is poor.” It is that she persistently treats financial resources as future option capacity.

## 5.3 Optionality is active rather than hypothetical

`bond_kor_001_kor` makes this especially clear.

fran attends a fashion-industry party she dislikes because she wants contacts for brand revival. She studies business and markets. She designs IIIX costumes. She tells Chisa that current popularity can serve as advertising leverage and that present action is investment in the future.

This gives the stronger term:

> **`OPTIONALITY_IS_AN_ACTIVE_PRACTICE`**

A non-totalizing vocation is not simply the belief that “I could quit someday.” It is the active construction of alternatives through skills, networks, capital, reputation, and cross-role transfer.

This is a real strength. It becomes dangerous only if future optionality requires treating every present commitment as ontologically temporary.

# 6. Close reading III — early IIIX: idolhood as instrument and serious labor

## 6.1 The `通過点` statement is true at its own temporal frontier

In `origin_thrx_001`, fran states her reason plainly:

- increase visibility;
- gain better-paying work such as commercials;
- use idoldom as a waypoint;
- pursue other things beyond it.

The V2 ledger should **preserve** this rather than psychologize it away.

fran does not enter IIIX because idol performance is already her deepest vocation.

## 6.2 Instrumental motive does not mean unserious execution

At the same time, fran trains individually before required lessons, performs assigned work, understands modeling-level physical discipline, and pushes for efficient use of finite rehearsal time.

She effectively tells miho: I did what the job required; what more do you want?

This yields an important distinction:

> **`INSTRUMENTAL_ENTRY_WITH_PROFESSIONAL_SERIOUSNESS`**

A role can be instrumentally chosen and still be executed at elite professional standards.

This protects fran from two opposite misreadings:

- “she did not care, so her performance was fake”;
- “she worked hard, so she secretly loved idolhood already.”

Neither follows.

## 6.3 Center is initially institutional design, not self-evident essence

Preta Porte selects fran as IIIX center.

That choice is legible through the project's luxury/visual strategy: a charismatic high-fashion model with strong public prestige is useful as the group's central image.

fran accepts the role and later grows into real aesthetic/performative centerhood. But the founding assignment should not be rewritten as proof that the unit spontaneously recognized her as metaphysically central.

This distinction becomes important later when song-local center staging is contested.

# 7. Close reading IV — first defeat and the instrumental constitution

## 7.1 Victory initially supplies what shared vocation does not

Early IIIX is not founded through affection or one shared artistic philosophy.

The members have separate private ends. Victory is the common mechanism that advances all of them.

For fran, continued visibility and money advance debt repayment and fashion reconstruction.

For the triad, winning can temporarily substitute for the unanswered question of why these three should remain together.

## 7.2 First serious defeat forces motive disclosure

`origin_thrx_002` is structurally important because it makes fran's private fashion history legible inside the unit.

The defeat matters not merely because fran dislikes losing. It threatens the instrumental theory supporting her current detour: she cannot afford to stall because idol work was supposed to accelerate another future.

At this stage, defeat exposes the compact rather than transcending it.

## 7.3 The brand disclosure is not humiliation out of the story

When another idol recognizes `ERFOLG`, fran reacts defensively. She gives technically non-false euphemisms about restructuring/preparation.

This is not proof that she has abandoned the brand. It is evidence that failed authorship has become an exposed wound inside a public competence identity.

The later bond and C1-A material are necessary to understand the direction of growth: she eventually becomes willing to work openly at rebuilding the vocation that once embarrassed her.

# 8. Close reading V — Himeno and `AESTHETIC_SELF_JURISDICTION`

## 8.1 fran is not an innocent bystander to institutional contamination

fran is commercially intelligent enough to notice manipulation and power.

She understands that Himeno's access can be useful. She detects bad incentives and suspect tactics. She considers how to use other institutions/actors against an entrenched powerful figure.

The right ethical description is **motivated toleration**, not innocence.

She remains in the environment while the opportunity is useful.

## 8.2 She nevertheless distinguishes competitive excellence from engineered ugliness

The key hinge comes in `origin_thrx_004`.

fran's internal logic is unusually explicit:

- winning matters;
- winning increases work;
- work pays debt;
- idolhood is business;
- but she is still a designer;
- she will not sell off her aesthetic sense.

This establishes:

> **`AESTHETIC_SELF_JURISDICTION`**

The line is not “money is corrupt and art is pure.” fran continues to care about money.

The line is:

> **market participation may discipline the work, but it does not automatically acquire unlimited jurisdiction over the form she is willing to sign her identity to.**

## 8.3 This is not yet mature market theory

At this stage, the boundary is mainly defensive: I will not let your ugly methods define my work.

Later fashion material adds the complementary direction: aesthetic jurisdiction is not permission to ignore customers, trust, recognition, or market reality.

Mature fran therefore emerges between two failures:

- **artistic surrender** — letting market/institutional power author the work completely;
- **artistic absolutism** — assuming personal vision deserves adoption without earned recognition or adaptation.

# 9. Close reading VI — I-UNITY: judgment without self-dissolution

## 9.1 fran can acknowledge a singular superior performance

After Sunny Peace's contingent I-UNITY breakthrough, fran does not claim that IIIX's technical competence was fake.

She instead distinguishes repeatable superiority from what happened **in that moment**. She can believe IIIX would usually win and still say Sunny Peace was above them on that stage.

This is important evidence of professional judgment.

## 9.2 Audience capture enters the evaluative field without replacing technique

fran recognizes that the performance will remain in viewers' memories and become legend.

This is not a conversion to “heart beats skill.” It is an expansion of what skill/performance success can mean.

A stage may matter because of a contingent relation among performers, crisis, audience, and moment—something not exhausted by a repeatable optimized template.

## 9.3 But togetherness remains terminally result-conditioned at this frontier

At the Tokyo/I-UNITY endpoint, fran still says that if the three cannot win, there is no reason for them to remain together.

Do not back-project the later U.S. re-authorization here.

The chronology matters because later development consists precisely in crossing this boundary.

# 10. Close reading VII — collapse and the second founding

## 10.1 Dissolution is initially rational under the first constitution

When Preta Porte's idol project collapses and IIIX loses its institutional platform, fran sees dissolution as reasonable.

The members are hostile; the founding organization has failed; they did not achieve the intended result; the next step can be separated again.

Under the initial contract, this is coherent.

## 10.2 The small live produces a new fact: idol performance was not merely tolerated

The post-collapse live lets fran admit something narrower than a “true calling” revelation:

> she did not dislike being an idol.

She can even place it near fashion in preference while still insisting fashion matters.

The key is that idol performance has acquired **non-instrumental residue**. There are performance possibilities she would regret leaving unrealized.

When she imagines quitting immediately, what bothers her is not simply lost money. It feels uncool; she would remember that she could have done better.

This is the first durable revision of the `通過点` model.

## 10.3 fran re-selects the partners without friendship conversion

fran says she does not like miho/kana, but trusts them as the center's companions.

That sentence is a compact declaration of the new relation.

The members are no longer together **only because Preta Porte put them together**. They voluntarily choose continuation after the founding institution has ceased to compel it.

This produces:

# 29. P2-B5 fran conclusion

The strongest current fran model is not “fashion girl who learns to love being an idol.”

It is a model of **plural authorship**.

Her earliest formative experience teaches that external appearance can become authored form. Modeling makes that principle professionally effective. Design expands it into creation. The first brand collapse demonstrates that aesthetic conviction alone cannot produce a sustainable institution. Debt and failure then make optionality—money, visibility, contacts, transferable status, parallel skills—central to how she protects the future.

Idol work enters that system as an instrument.

That entry-state remains real.

But the corpus repeatedly makes the instrument generate values that the original plan did not contain. fran becomes interested in performance itself. She acquires irreplaceable professional antagonists. She becomes a material author inside IIIX. Her idol expertise feeds fashion, and fashion expertise feeds idol performance. She spends future-brand capital on a three-person visual body. She learns that market legitimacy must sometimes be earned from zero. She accepts customer trust as a constraint without letting the customer become total author. She learns that public construction can correspond to truth or detach from it. She keeps profit while losing the assumption that profit exhausts meaning.

The decisive development is therefore not a change from selfishness to selflessness.

It is a change in **jurisdiction**:

- the market may constrain but not own her aesthetic self;
- future options may remain open but do not make the present unreal;
- business motives may remain real but do not exhaust specific commitment;
- centerhood may remain real but does not monopolize every formal choice;
- private self may remain protected but does not make professional self false;
- fashion may remain foundational but does not make idolhood permanently disposable;
- hostile relations may remain hostile in vocabulary while becoming durable in practice.

The mature formulation is therefore:

> **`PLURAL_AUTHORSHIP_WITHOUT_INSTRUMENTAL_DISPOSABILITY_FRAN`**

and not:

> “fran learns that friendship matters more than business.”

That latter sentence would erase almost everything distinctive about the source.

Mature fran remains the person who can look at a relationship, a costume, a contract, a market, and a public image and ask what it costs, what it returns, how it looks, and whether it can be made to work.

What changes is that she also increasingly asks a quieter question:

> **is this something I have chosen deeply enough that I am no longer willing to treat it as replaceable merely because another option exists?**

For fashion, idolhood, and IIIX, the frozen snapshot increasingly answers yes.
