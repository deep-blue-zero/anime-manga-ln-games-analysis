# 0. Responsibility and authority

This ledger is the current Phase-2 character authority for **Ibuki Nagisa / character code `ngs`** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`. It reconstructs Nagisa across chronology rather than reducing her to any one familiar role: Kotono's cheerful childhood friend, grief caretaker, comic admirer, romantic/yuri-coded attachment figure, or Tsuki no Tempest's relational stabilizer.

The longitudinal problem is not whether Nagisa must stop caring about Kotono. The source repeatedly rejects that solution. Nagisa's maturation concerns **the form that durable attachment should take when support can become identity captivity**.

Nagisa begins by choosing Kotono before Kotono is socially easy to choose. She notices loneliness where classmates see aloofness, persists through rejection, accepts social cost, and becomes a friend whose interest is in Kotono herself rather than in Mana through Kotono. Mana later asks Nagisa to remain Kotono's ally, but the relationship is already self-authored; Nagisa does not become a delegated guardian whose authority derives from Mana. After Mana's death she provides continuity when Kotono is most withdrawn, and idolhood initially becomes an extension of that proximity: Nagisa wants to remain beside and support Kotono.

The crucial correction arrives only later. Nagisa's support has been real, good, and often indispensable, but BIG4 exposes its asymmetry. When Kotono leaves Tsuki and invites Nagisa to follow, Nagisa receives almost exactly what an earlier version of herself would have wanted: a privileged two-person future beside Kotono. She refuses it. Her reason is not weakened attachment. She has developed an idol vocation of her own, recognizes obligations to the four-person Tsuki, and concludes that merely following Kotono would reproduce a hierarchy in which she stands behind and supports rather than **beside and mutually illuminates**.

The late C1-B card makes this self-understanding explicit. Nagisa retrospectively distinguishes three stages: she initially thought only about supporting Kotono; during the four-member period she decided to support Tsuki; and now she also wants to shine herself. Yet she immediately insists that Kotono remains precious to her as both teammate and `親友`. Her mature goal is not `Kotono離れ`. It is to shine enough to stand proudly beside Kotono and become a relationship in which they illuminate one another from the closest position.

This makes Nagisa a particularly clear IDOLY PRIDE case of **differentiation without relational evacuation**. The self becomes larger so that relation can become more reciprocal.

Analytical boundaries:

- Nagisa's Kotono attachment is not reduced to generic kindness; it is unusually intense, persistent, embodied, and imaginatively saturated.

# 3. Longitudinal argument

Nagisa's arc is easiest to misunderstand if “dependence” and “love” are treated as synonyms. The source does something more careful. It gives Nagisa an attachment that is both profoundly generative and structurally risky.

Her earliest attention to Kotono already contains the features that will define her later personality. She is socially perceptive in a selective rather than detached way. Other students interpret Kotono's guardedness as bad attitude; Nagisa notices the lonely profile underneath it. She is also boundary-crossing and persistent. Finding Kotono's notebook begins with an intrusion; her subsequent effort to repair that bad first encounter becomes relentless enough that Kotono describes her as following her between breaks, lunch, the bathroom, and the walk home. Nagisa's warmth is therefore not passive agreeableness. It can become stubborn pursuit when she believes a person is worth reaching.

The relationship then acquires moral weight because Nagisa does not choose Kotono only when the choice is easy. Other classmates threaten social punishment. Nagisa nevertheless continues. Kotono, in turn, eventually refuses to preserve her own standing by disowning Nagisa. This matters to the later grief arc. Nagisa is not a caretaker assigned after Mana's death. She is a peer relation that Kotono has already learned to trust because both have paid something for it.

Mana's intervention must therefore be read in the correct causal order. Mana does ask Nagisa to remain Kotono's ally. The request is emotionally important and confirms Mana's recognition of Nagisa. But it cannot be the foundation of Nagisa's loyalty. Nagisa has already chosen Kotono. This protects the character from a surprisingly damaging interpretation: that her life becomes a posthumous assignment from Mana. The later V2 architecture repeatedly distinguishes being moved by another person from allowing that person to author one's future, and Nagisa belongs inside that distinction.

Bereavement raises the stakes. Nagisa cannot cure Kotono's grief and does not replace Mana. What she can do is preserve a bridge to the living. That role is so important that it begins to structure Nagisa's own vocational choice. She does not enter idolhood from a pure pre-existing idol dream. Being beside Kotono is a major causal reason. This is genuine agency—Nagisa chooses it—but it also creates a latent problem. A vocation organized around another person's need can become difficult to distinguish from a role obligation.

Tsuki gradually expands her. Caring for Kotono introduces Nagisa to idolhood; living as an idol gives her relationships, audience responsibility, competitive experience, interests, and competence that are not reducible to Kotono. The four other members matter. So do the ordinary acts by which Nagisa cooks, notices fatigue, jokes, mediates, comforts, and participates in the group's life. “Support” becomes less dyadic and more constitutional.

BIG4 then performs the decisive test. Kotono's proposed separation is not merely an external obstacle. It offers Nagisa the most seductive possible shortcut: leave with me. Kotono even frames the two of them as capable of walking alongside one another, correcting one another, and growing together. The invitation grants Nagisa exceptional status inside Kotono's attempt at severance.

Nagisa refuses.

The refusal is one of the strongest acts of self-authorship in her corpus because it does not depend on devaluing the relationship. She explicitly says she is now serious about herself as an idol and wants to shine more. She also says the invitation makes her genuinely, deeply happy. She chooses Tsuki because the four-person unit is now something she herself must sustain and because she understands that simply following Kotono would freeze the relational structure she needs to outgrow.

Her later internal formulation is exact: if she wants to remain with Kotono, then right now she cannot remain with Kotono. Otherwise she will only support Kotono from behind. To stand side by side, she must shine just as strongly.

This is the master transition. Nagisa does not choose autonomy **over** relation. She chooses differentiation **for the sake of a better relation**.

That same principle gives her unusual moral authority during the DoriKyun crisis. Nagisa asks Kotono whether the destructive competitive model before them is truly the strength she wanted. If strength requires defeating others by destroying their dream and future, Nagisa says she does not need victory. This is not competitive naivete. Nagisa has accepted loss and wants Tsuki to improve. Her objection is to making annihilation the definition of legitimacy. Because she is the person Kotono most wanted to exempt from separation, her challenge exposes the contradiction in Kotono's model with particular force.

After reconstitution, Nagisa's development does not culminate in becoming less attached. The C1-B card instead gives a retrospective taxonomy of her growth. She says that when she first became an idol she thought only about supporting Kotono; when Tsuki became four, she decided she would support Tsuki; now she wants herself to shine too. In the same breath she retains Kotono as precious teammate and `親友`, discusses rupture and repair through a romance-novel analogy, and later states the mature goal: shine herself, stand proudly beside Kotono, support one another, and illuminate one another from the closest position.

This is why `DIFFERENTIATED_ADJACENCY_WITHOUT_SUPPORT_CAPTIVITY_NAGISA` is preferable to either “independence” or “devotion.” Independence alone underdescribes the relational telos. Devotion alone underdescribes the achieved selfhood. Nagisa wants a life in which she can remain intensely attached without making her value derivative of the person she loves most.

# 4. Character mechanics

## 4.1 Warmth is active, observant, and sometimes invasive

Nagisa's dominant social surface is soft: friendly cadence, easy laughter, food-sharing, concern, teasing, emotional expressiveness. But her warmth is not merely low-conflict temperament.

The origin shows three stronger mechanisms:

1. **she notices affective states others ignore** — Kotono's lonely expression matters to her before she knows Kotono well;
2. **she persists across explicit rejection** — coldness does not easily deter her once she believes the relationship matters;
3. **she crosses ordinary boundaries in the name of closeness** — the notebook incident, relentless following, observation habits, and later Kotono archiving all show that curiosity and affection can become intrusive.

This should be preserved in any later character model. Nagisa is kind, but she is not neutral, passive, or infinitely deferential.

## 4.2 Cute-recognition is a stable Nagisa relational language

Nagisa repeatedly responds to Kotono through `可愛い` recognition: blushing, mistakes, excitement, gestures, eating, embarrassment, and ordinary physical proximity become objects of intense attention. The B3 bond even literalizes this as a `毎日琴乃ちゃん観察日記`.

This has several functions:

- it punctures Kotono's severe self-presentation;
- it allows Nagisa to preserve a private/ordinary Kotono against public idol abstraction;
- it is comic characterization;
- it contributes substantially to Nagisa-side yuri/romance coding;
- it also reveals a general Nagisa pleasure in attending to small affective details.

Guardrail: “Nagisa thinks Kotono is cute” is fact. “Every cute comment is a romantic declaration” is not.

## 4.3 Persistence is her hidden hard trait

Nagisa's gentle surface can obscure how difficult she is to move once she has chosen a commitment. Middle-school rejection does not stop her. Peer ostracism threat does not stop her. Mana's death does not stop her from maintaining contact with Kotono. Kotono's departure does not make her abandon Tsuki. DoriKyun's pressure does not make her accept an ethic she considers wrong.

Her persistence is therefore not only interpersonal. It is a core volitional trait.

## 4.4 Caregiving is broad, not Kotono-exclusive

B3 bond material deliberately supplies counterevidence to a one-person ontology. With Mei, Nagisa shares food, plays along with imaginative jokes, notices fatigue, and gently corrects bad habits. With Makino, even a Kotono-related playful possessive exchange can turn into an offer of Nagisa's own support if he is exhausted. In Tsuki, she participates in the practical maintenance of group morale and continuity.

Kotono is exceptional in intensity. Care itself is not exclusive to Kotono.

## 4.5 Romance literacy provides metaphor before category closure

Nagisa openly likes romance novels and responds strongly to conventional romantic scenes. C1-B then uses a breakup/reconciliation/marriage novel as an analogy for her relationship with Kotono. The text simultaneously supplies a category brake: Makino explicitly notes the difference between lovers and friends.

This combination matters. Nagisa thinks through romance because romantic narrative gives her concepts for rupture, repair, longing, proximity, jealousy, and future imagination. The text invites yuri-adjacent legibility while refusing to make analogy identical to declared relationship status.

# 5. Kotono relationship: longitudinal reconstruction

## 5.1 Formation — attraction to vulnerability beneath severity

Nagisa's first meaningful orientation toward Kotono contains both aesthetic and ethical attention. She sees a mysterious beautiful girl, but the decisive feature is that she keeps noticing loneliness where peers see unpleasantness.

The notebook intensifies this. Nagisa describes the illustration of two smiling girls holding hands as cute, gentle, warm, and full of the artist's affection. She wonders whether the sensation of seeing it is what `一目惚れ` feels like. The wording is affectively charged and yuri-adjacent, while the immediate grammatical object is the illustration rather than a direct confession to Kotono.

Transition result:

> **STRENGTHEN** early-affective-intensity model, with category guardrail.

## 5.2 Mutual costly recognition

Nagisa's persistence would be analytically weaker if only she paid a cost. The origin gives Kotono a reciprocal moment: Kotono discovers she already thinks of Nagisa as a friend and ultimately names that bond despite the hostile peer environment.

Later Kotono can explicitly say she is glad she became Nagisa's `親友`.

This makes their foundation mutual without requiring symmetrical personalities or symmetrical modes of expression.

Transition result:

> **PRESERVE + STRENGTHEN** `MUTUAL_COSTLY_RECOGNITION_KOTONO_NAGISA`.

## 5.3 Mana's entrustment — recognition, not authorship

Mana's private conversation with Nagisa recognizes what Nagisa already is becoming for Kotono. The key interpretive safeguard is chronological: the relationship precedes the request.

Therefore:

- Mana may validate and emotionally deepen Nagisa's sense of responsibility;
- Mana does not create Nagisa's love, friendship, or loyalty;
- Nagisa's later life is not ethically best understood as fulfilling a dead sister's assignment.

Transition result:

> **REVISE** any model that treats Nagisa as Mana-delegated caretaker.

Preferred formulation:

> `SELF_CHOSEN_LOYALTY_PRECEDES_ENTRUSTMENT_NAGISA`.

## 5.4 Bereavement — presence without replacement

After Mana dies, Nagisa's role becomes structurally important because she is one of the few living relations whose authority Kotono earned before bereavement. Nagisa can remain present through withdrawal without pretending to be the person Kotono lost.

The correct mechanism is not “Nagisa replaces Mana as Kotono's emotional center.” It is:

> **Nagisa preserves continuity with living relation while Kotono's relationship to Mana is being painfully reorganized.**

This remains complementary to the Kotono ledger's anti-replacement boundary.

## 5.5 Idol entry — proximity becomes vocation

The origins establish that Nagisa initially makes remaining beside Kotono the explicit reason she becomes an idol. This is more dependent than a generic vocation story but should not be treated as non-agency. Nagisa chooses the path herself.

The unresolved question at this stage is whether support will remain a **reason** for idolhood or become the **jurisdictional owner** of Nagisa's identity.

Phase 2 answer: it begins near the latter risk and later becomes one valued commitment among several self-authored commitments.

## 5.6 Group expansion — from Kotono support to Tsuki support

Tsuki gives Nagisa a relational network with independent value. Saki, Suzu, Mei, Makino, audiences, and the practical work of being an idol create commitments that cannot be collapsed back into “things Nagisa does for Kotono.”

This is why the BIG4 refusal becomes possible. By then, leaving Kotono's group does not mean choosing between Kotono and nothing. Nagisa has a unit that she herself belongs to and helps constitute.

## 5.7 BIG4 invitation — the decisive counterfactual

Kotono explicitly invites Nagisa to come with her. The offer is analytically invaluable because it removes ambiguity about whether Nagisa could preserve maximum proximity by following.

Nagisa could.

She does not.

She says she now wants to work hard **as an idol herself** and shine more. She says Kotono's invitation makes her genuinely happy. She nevertheless promises that the four will work so that Kotono has somewhere to return and tells her she cannot go with her.

This is not a forced separation. It is chosen differentiation.

Transition result:

> **STRENGTHEN** `INDEPENDENT_SELF_EXPANSION_WITHIN_RELATIONAL_COMMITMENT_NAGISA`.

## 5.8 Behind-support becomes unacceptable

Nagisa's most explicit self-correction is:

- if she wants them to remain together, right now she must not simply stay with Kotono;
- otherwise she will only be supporting Kotono from behind;
- if she wants to stand side by side, she must shine just as strongly.

This is the core evidence for the master model.

Transition result:

> **STRENGTHEN** `EQUAL_ADJACENCY_WITHOUT_SUPPORT_ERASURE_NAGISA`.

## 5.9 Normative challenge — relation becomes contestatory, not merely soothing

During the DoriKyun crisis Nagisa does not comfort Kotono into returning. She challenges her. She asks whether the destructive strength on display is truly what Kotono wanted and states that if winning requires crushing another's dream and future, she would rather not win.

This is mature support because it includes the right to oppose.

Nagisa therefore moves from:

> “I will stay beside you and help you”

through:

> “I will not follow you when following would make both of us worse”

into:

> “I can stand close enough to challenge the ethical terms of the path you chose.”

That is reciprocal standing rather than support servitude.

## 5.10 C1-B endpoint — explicit equal illumination

The late card `二人の関係` provides unusually clean self-commentary.

Nagisa says:

- when she became an idol, she thought only about supporting Kotono;
- when Tsuki became four, she decided to support Tsuki;
- now she also wants herself to shine;
- Kotono remains precious as teammate and `親友`;
- after conflict and repair they understand things they could not understand before;
- she wants to shine herself, stand proudly beside Kotono, support one another, and become a relationship that can `お互いを一番近くで照らし合える`.

This is the Phase-2 endpoint formulation.

# 7. Idol vocation

## 7.1 Borrowed direction is not false direction

Nagisa's initial idol motive is relational. That does not make it fake. The V2 distinction is between a cause of action and permanent jurisdiction over identity.

Kotono is a real cause of Nagisa becoming an idol. Later Nagisa chooses reasons that exceed Kotono without needing to repudiate that origin.

## 7.2 The self becomes an independent object of aspiration

The decisive new statement in BIG4 is `私自身もアイドルとして頑張りたい / もっと輝きたい`.

This is not merely “I should become stronger so I can help Kotono better.” The self's own luminosity becomes valuable.

That distinction is essential. Otherwise all Nagisa growth would remain instrumentalized toward Kotono.

## 7.3 Audience and professional-world expansion

By the late C1-B period, Nagisa has individual work linked to her interest in romance literature, feels her world expanding, thinks about her own public goals, and wants to deliver Tsuki's songs to more people. Her profession now has an audience and cultural field beyond the original dyadic motive.

Kotono remains constitutive. She is no longer exhaustive.

## 7.4 Competition without annihilation

Nagisa wants Tsuki to become stronger and accepts the seriousness of competition. But `big4_010` establishes a boundary: if strength means destroying another's dream and future as the condition of victory, she rejects that definition of winning.

Nagisa therefore contributes to IDOLY PRIDE's competition ethic:

> professional ambition can be intense without making another person's obliteration the proof of one's seriousness.
