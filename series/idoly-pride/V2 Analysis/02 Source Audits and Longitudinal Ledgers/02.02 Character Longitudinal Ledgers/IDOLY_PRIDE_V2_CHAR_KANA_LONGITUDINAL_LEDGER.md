# 0. Responsibility and authority

This ledger is the current Phase-2 character authority for **kana / character code `kan`** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`.

Kana is unusually easy to flatten because several superficially contradictory descriptions are simultaneously true.

She is vain, attention-seeking, competitive, manipulative, professionally polished, observant, distrustful, materially helpful, retaliatory, funny, affectionate in ways she often refuses to name as affection, and intensely sensitive to who is permitted to define or access her. Her public sweetness is constructed. Her private abrasiveness is also constructed in the sense that it is a stable self-presentation with its own pleasures and defenses. Neither register monopolizes authenticity.

The childhood/family axis creates the most dangerous simplification. Early sources make fame and ubiquity legible as an attachment-search strategy: if Kana is everywhere on television and the internet, perhaps her father will see her and find her. Later sources do **not** simply reveal that this belief was false because he had actually abandoned her, nor do they restore a hidden normal family. They establish mediated contact, gifts, media reactions, affection, long physical separation, an incomplete reunion, a father's new household, and Kana's own judgment that the shared family life she had worked toward did not materialize.

The relevant wound is therefore not best described as total paternal disappearance.

It is **failed family reconstitution under conditions of partial contact**.

This matters because Kana's development is not a linear movement from invisibility to visibility or abandonment to security. It is a movement toward separating several things she once had reason to collapse:

- being visible from being known;
- being known from being granted access;
- kinship from entitlement;
- a public story from exhaustive truth;
- an original motive from the later meaning of the work it produced;
- attention metrics from embodied audience response;
- affection from sentimental vocabulary;
- professional usefulness from moral innocence.

The capstone is therefore:

> **`SELECTIVE_VISIBILITY_WITHOUT_RELATIONAL_CAPTURE_KANA`: Kana develops from using visibility partly as a defensive and attachment-seeking technology—be seen, become findable, control the narrative before others control it—toward a more differentiated authorship in which she can remain highly public while deciding who receives private access, acknowledge that paternal contact did not recreate the family she wanted, preserve positive memories without making them proof of relational success, and allow idol work to acquire audience-facing and relational meaning beyond the childhood purpose that helped initiate it.**

Short form:

> **Kana learns that being seen can matter without giving the person who sees her ownership of her story, access, or future.**

This does **not** make her gentle. The mature Kana still weaponizes information, enjoys leverage, holds grudges, mocks people, manipulates presentation, and may pursue retaliation. The development is not moral purification. It is greater jurisdictional precision and broader vocational meaning.

## 2.2 Disclosure chronology

Kana's development is especially sensitive to **when the audience learns what**.

Early Kana can be read as a narcissistic child-star archetype: high follower consciousness, aggressive self-promotion, status sensitivity, and irritation at compulsory group intimacy.

The origins disclose a hidden but not contradictory cause: attention is also attachment search. She wants to be seen by the father who disappeared from ordinary life.

Later sources then complicate the word “disappeared.” Paternal contact existed in mediated forms. This does not invalidate the early attachment search. It changes what that search meant. The missing object was not merely information about whether her father existed. It was **physical presence, recognition, and shared family life**.

The U.S./C1-A material then breaks another possible simplification. Even getting the father physically into the audience does not retroactively complete the childhood project. The desired family does not reconstitute.

Finally, the fan-letter material opens a second axis of meaning. Kana's work can matter to people she never imagined when she began pursuing visibility.

The disclosure sequence therefore moves:

> **find me → see me → know my version → you still do not own me → the original person can see me and the original wish can still fail → the work can nevertheless matter to somebody else.**

# 3. Formative structure — unsafe kinship and the birth of jurisdiction

## 3.1 Mother absence is origin fact, not a complete psychological explanation

Kana's mother dies giving birth. This removes one potential parental relationship before Kana can exercise any agency toward it.

The ledger does not infer guilt, resentment, or a specific mother-centered wound beyond what later evidence supports. The more textually active family axis is father/grandfather.

**Transition:** `PRESERVE`.

## 3.2 The father is both loved and structurally unavailable

Childhood memories establish real positive attachment. His work absences matter because the good periods are intermittent rather than nonexistent.

The later desire to be found is therefore not longing for an entirely unknown parent. It is longing for return and continuity with somebody whose presence once felt good.

**Transition:** `STRENGTHEN`.

## 3.3 The grandfather teaches that family can be unsafe

The grandfather does not function merely as a strict guardian. He is tied to exploitative use of Kana's entertainment labor/name and to a pattern from which she eventually protects herself through separation and address control.

This gives unusually clear causal support to Kana's distrust of automatic intimacy.

Her origin statement that even actual family can be untrustworthy is not posturing. It is a learned rule.

**Canonical claim:** `KINSHIP_DOES_NOT_CREATE_TRUST_BY_ITSELF_KANA`.

## 3.4 Visibility and access become separable survival problems

The same girl who wants her father to see her later does not want the grandfather to know where she lives.

This is one of the most important structural facts in her characterization.

> **Wanted visibility to one relative can coexist with strict concealment from another.**

The correct model is selective jurisdiction, not generalized fear of being known or generalized hunger for exposure.

**Canonical claim:** `SELECTIVE_FAMILY_VISIBILITY_LONGING`.

**Canonical claim:** `KINSHIP_WITHOUT_CONTACT_ENTITLEMENT`.

**Transition:** `PRESERVE`.

## 4.2 Visibility is also attachment-search infrastructure

The origin states the instrumental logic directly: if Kana appears everywhere in mass media and online, perhaps her father will see and contact her.

This supports:

> **`VISIBILITY_AS_ATTACHMENT_SEARCH_KANA`**

The claim must remain bounded. It is a major origin motive, not a theory that every post, job, or competitive impulse is secretly about her father.

Kana's media literacy should therefore not be treated as an accidental gimmick. It is one of the domains in which her personality, childhood problem, and professional environment mutually reinforce one another.

**Strong inference:** `MEASURABLE_VISIBILITY_AS_CONTROLLED_RECOGNITION`.

## 4.4 The small venue expands visibility from metric to embodied relation

The reduced post-I-UNITY venue matters because audience response becomes physically local and personally legible.

Kana still interprets attention through her characteristic grandiosity, but she experiences direct audience response as intrinsically pleasurable and recognizes its professional value.

This is not humility.

It is an expansion of what counts as meaningful visibility.

**Transition:** `STRENGTHEN + REVISE`.

**Canonical claim:** `EMBODIED_AUDIENCE_RESPONSE_EXPANDS_VISIBILITY_MODEL`.

**Canonical claim:** `AUTHORED_PUBLIC_NARRATIVE_REMAINS_ANSWERABLE`.

# 16. Master longitudinal synthesis

Kana begins from a childhood in which the people who should make access safe do not provide a stable rule for access.

Her father can be loving and unavailable. Her grandfather can be family and exploitative. Entertainment can be coercive labor and a tool she learns to master. Visibility can expose a child to adult control and also become the mechanism by which she tries to regain control over who can find her.

This produces a character for whom **publicity and privacy are not opposites**.

Publicity is something she can operate.

Privacy is something she can allocate.

Her most characteristic social behaviors follow from that distinction. She is extraordinarily good at managing impressions because impressions are controllable surfaces. She is skeptical of compulsory closeness because relationship labels do not guarantee safety. She enjoys followers because metrics turn diffuse recognition into legible proof. She uses leverage because informal power is often how the entertainment world actually moves. She is suspicious of adults whose benevolence arrives bundled with control. She can cooperate intensely with people she refuses to call friends because negotiated professional relation feels more trustworthy than compulsory intimacy.

IIIX is therefore not incidental to her development.

The group's first compact is psychologically compatible with Kana precisely because it does not require emotional surrender. Each member can keep her private motive. What they owe each other is performance and victory.

But the first IIIX is too narrow because victory becomes the only admitted shared object. Himeno exposes the danger of that narrowness: an exploitative adult can align herself with the group's desired outcome and make manipulation look like rational cooperation. Kana detects the pattern before the others fully accept it, yet detection does not immediately produce refusal. She is implicated in the same motivated toleration.

The I-UNITY collapse and reduced-venue live then create a different kind of evidence. Audience response is no longer only a number proving reach. It is bodies, faces, immediate affect, and an experience Kana finds genuinely pleasurable. IIIX itself undergoes the same transformation. The group no longer exists because a corporation has assembled three high-value assets and promised upward return. The three choose one another again after that guarantee disappears.

Kana does not need to call this friendship for it to matter.

Her family arc later sharpens the same principle. Paternal recognition is not nothing. The U.S. live can fulfill a genuine childhood wish. Contact can be real. Gifts and messages can be real. Affection can be real.

And the family she wanted can still fail to exist.

The mature source therefore refuses both melodramatic abandonment and sentimental restoration. Kana's father is neither pure void nor recovered home. He is a relation whose contact and emotional meaning do not equal the shared family structure Kana once used her work to pursue.

That failure produces one of her strongest moments of vocational reauthorization. If the original private purpose failed, was the work wasted?

The fan letter answers without denying the loss. Somebody else, living through a painful home situation, has been helped by Kana's broadcasts. The work has acquired consequences beyond the person it was initially trying to reach.

This is the deepest change in Kana's visibility system.

Early visibility says:

> **If I become visible enough, the person I want may finally see me.**

Mature visibility can say:

> **People I never knew I was addressing may see me too, and that can matter without giving any of them ownership over me.**

The amusement-park material completes the same logic at the scale of memory. Kana can retain a genuinely good memory from an incomplete father relationship. She can pass the structure of that memory onward by helping another child have one good day. She does not need to pretend the institution is fair or that she can rescue the child completely.

A good memory does not prove the past was good as a whole.

A failed relationship does not make every good memory false.

A failed original vocation-motive does not make every later consequence of the vocation meaningless.

A constructed public persona does not make every public feeling false.

A private harsh voice does not make every private judgment truer.

A hostile relationship does not make every act of care unreal.

A family tie does not create access entitlement.

A person being able to see Kana does not grant the right to define Kana.

That is the mature longitudinal architecture.

> **`SELECTIVE_VISIBILITY_WITHOUT_RELATIONAL_CAPTURE_KANA`**

Kana remains a publicity expert, competitor, provocateur, and occasionally coercive actor. What changes is the sophistication of the boundaries around visibility and relation. She increasingly distinguishes recognition from possession, contact from family, authored narrative from exhaustive truth, audience metrics from lived response, and founding purpose from later vocational value.

Her growth is therefore best described not as becoming less artificial or less difficult, but as becoming more capable of deciding **what her visibility is for, who it is for, and what nobody gets merely because they can see her.**
