It reconstructs Chisa across chronology rather than treating her anime-era surface archetype, her relationship with Saki, or any single card/event as a complete character model.

Its central problem is causal and ethical: Chisa's reserve is not merely a fixed 'shy younger sister' trait. The origin material establishes an active pre-accident child and then a near-drowning that teaches her a damaging moral equation:

> **initiative / desire / separation → possible danger to Saki → being a burden → goodness requires self-suppression.**

The longitudinal question is therefore not whether Chisa becomes 'independent' in the abstract. It is how she learns to preserve love, reliance, sisterhood, and group belonging **without granting those relationships total jurisdiction over whether she may want, act, disagree, be visible, or impose legitimate needs.**

# 3. Longitudinal argument

Chisa's central developmental conflict is not confidence in the abstract. It is **moral permission to have a self in relationship**.

The near-drowning creates a causal shortcut: Chisa acts independently; Saki is terrified; therefore Chisa learns that action itself can be an ethical injury. Her response is not simply fear but **self-regulation on behalf of another person**. She calls this being good. She gives up space before anyone asks, hides desire before it becomes conflict, and assumes that the safest way to love Saki is to minimize the amount of Chisa that Saki must accommodate.

The tragedy is that Saki is doing something structurally similar. Saki converts being an older sister into a duty to remain near and protect Chisa, even at the expense of her own idol dream. Their love is real; the problem is that each lets a care role acquire excessive jurisdiction over the other's and her own future.

Idolhood first appears as a shared dream. The decisive early test is therefore the failed audition. Chisa tries to say the dream is over because 'with Saki' seems impossible, but her grief reveals a remainder: **she wants to be an idol even when the original relational condition is removed**. Makino then tests that proposition directly before scouting her.

From there, separation does not mean severance. Different groups create room for Chisa to learn with peers, especially Shizuku, and to discover competencies—fashion, craft, careful observation, peer care—that are not merely weaker copies of Saki's strengths. `Dear My Sister` then turns the metaphor explicit: Chisa must stop living behind Saki and learn to stand beside her. The culmination is not 'I can run alone, goodbye,' but **'I can run on my own feet; therefore let's run together.'**

The sister duo later proves that the old pattern can return whenever the sisters share one professional frame. Both try to disappear for the other's sake. Repair requires Chisa to do something early Chisa would have considered dangerous: criticize Saki, demand Saki admit that she wants center/visibility, ask to support Saki back, and compete for attention herself. Love becomes more robust when it no longer requires either sister to yield her selfhood in advance.

Chisa's professional maturation follows the same logic. Her first costume designs are technically good but asymmetrical because she designs for Saki rather than for two protagonists. Later she refuses a compromised production result even though a remake burdens budget and schedule. She learns that **having a legitimate need is not the same as irresponsibly burdening others**. Her adult-image anxiety repeats the old proof problem in comic form: she wants externally visible evidence that she has grown. Fan recognition helps her accept growth as a process rather than a borrowed persona.

By Stellar, Chisa can say to Sakura what she once could not believe about herself: the person who struggles does not have to withdraw to protect the group. SUNNY PEACE is five people; burdens can be distributed; the absent center can rest while the others keep growing. The old equation has not vanished—Chisa still apologizes, compares, and worries—but it no longer monopolizes her decisions.
