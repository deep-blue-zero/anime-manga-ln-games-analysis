# 0. Responsibility and authority

This ledger is the current Phase-2 character authority for **Nagase Kotono / character code `ktn`** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`. It reconstructs Kotono across chronology rather than treating the anime-era "Mana's sister" conflict, her bond with Nagisa, the BIG4 rupture, or the late memorial material as a complete model by itself.

The central longitudinal question is **jurisdiction over a life after bereavement**. Kotono begins with a sister who is not merely admired but forms most of her early social world. Mana's idol career creates real scarcity and grievance; death freezes that conflict before ordinary repair can finish. The diary then proves that Mana loved Kotono, but Kotono converts those facts into a rule Mana did not give her: finish the dead sister's unfinished future. The resulting vocation is causally real and morally serious, but its first governing purpose is memorial substitution.

The source progressively dismantles that substitution without demanding emotional detachment. Kotono learns to love idol work for herself, refuses even a possible ghost answer so that her future judgment remains hers, turns toward fans, discovers that comparison can migrate from Mana to Sakura and rank, and then makes a second error by treating relation itself as the enemy of growth. BIG4 corrects that error through distributed agency, conditional reintegration, answerability to fans, contestable center authority, and reciprocal illumination. The 2026 answer-song material then closes the loop most clearly: Kotono can actively seek more knowledge of Mana, cry openly that she loves her, share Mana's spaces and objects with living companions, and write back to Mana **because** differentiation has made memory less jurisdictional, not because she has returned to proxy life.

# 3. Longitudinal argument

Kotono begins with a problem older than idolhood: **her self is built in a relationship so dense that differentiation can initially feel like abandonment**. Mana is the person who leads her through the world. When Mana becomes an idol, the material fact of reduced time is filtered through that prior dependence. Kotono does not merely dislike the profession. She experiences the profession as evidence that the person who constituted her world has chosen a competing world instead.

Celebrity makes the problem worse. Schoolmates demonstrate that even people who approach Kotono may actually be approaching Mana through her. Nagisa matters because she is the first peer who disproves that equation. By the time Mana dies, Nagisa is already the person Kotono trusts with ugly grievance and loneliness. That is why post-death support can work: Nagisa is not an emergency replacement invented after the loss; she is an independently earned relationship that survives the loss.

The diary creates the first major epistemic correction. Kotono learns that she had been wrong to interpret work scarcity as evidence that Mana loved her less. Yet Kotono immediately converts that repair into a new burden. If Mana loved her, wanted to deliver the song, and died before the final, then Kotono concludes she **must** finish it. This is the key distinction: the source gives Kotono excellent evidence about Mana's feelings and bad evidence for a mandate over Kotono's future. Kotono supplies the mandate herself.

Hoshimi then progressively removes that jurisdiction. Sakura's Mana-like voice lets Kotono externalize the burden, but also makes the substitution problem visible. The first farewell is incomplete. The decisive move comes later when Kotono has a chance to obtain what grief ordinarily craves most: an answer from the dead. She refuses it. Her reason is not disbelief in Mana but authorship: this question must be settled by Kotono. Only after that refusal does she announce that she will no longer chase Mana, no longer seek the final in Mana's place, and will instead discover the idol she herself wants to become.

Tokyo proves this was not one emotional scene. Kotono can say publicly that being Mana's sister is fact while still intending to become a Kotono-specific idol. Later she goes further: present idol activity now has little to do with Mana as governing purpose. The object of answerability is increasingly the audience. Defeat then widens this audience ethic toward opponents: losing teaches Kotono what previous losers may have carried.

BIG4 is crucial because it prevents self-authorship from becoming a simple cult of independence. Kotono correctly sees that old Tsuki overconcentrated initiative and symbolic load in her. She incorrectly concludes that care itself causes weakness. Her request that Nagisa alone come with her exposes the incompleteness of that theory immediately. The subsequent cut-off under the language of `amae` turns a useful individuation experiment into self-punishment.

The correction does not come from proving separation produced nothing. It produced real skill. The correction comes from the four-person Tsuki producing equally real growth **without** severing one another. When Kotono returns, the group does not restore the old center. It makes her answer to each member, to fans, to the changed center role, and to the fact that everyone else now possesses more agency. Kotono can therefore say the path was wrong without calling it meaningless.

This produces her mature idol ontology. The moon is no longer a solitary central light with four satellites. Kotono realizes she was illuminated by Tsuki, Mana, fans, staff, Makino, rivals, and opponents. Her answer is not to become self-sufficient but to return that light without deciding the path another person must take. This is why an idol can help one person leave the profession and another return to it. Illumination is successful when it expands another person's ability to author a road.

The late memorial material is therefore not regression. It is the strongest evidence of successful differentiation. Kotono can reopen Mana's room, let Suzu play Mana's piano, ask other people what *their* Mana was like, discover that sisterly proximity created blind spots, cry openly over the paired pendant, and write an answer song in her own words. The dead sister remains beloved, legendary, and aspirational. What disappears is the rule that love requires living as her substitute.

# 8. Voice, behavior, and worldview reconstruction notes

## 8.1 Voice and interaction style

Kotono's default public register is **precise, serious, controlled, and somewhat defensive**, but the longitudinal corpus shows that this is a register rather than a total personality. She often speaks in short declarative units, corrects imprecision quickly, and uses questions to test responsibility, intent, or logistics. Around professional matters she prefers concrete criteria and can sound severe when she believes someone is evading responsibility. Around people she trusts, especially Nagisa, the surface becomes softer, more embarrassed, more easily flustered, and more playful.

Stable voice tendencies:

- direct corrective language when responsibility or preparation matters;
- a strong distinction between what she thinks privately and what she can comfortably avow aloud;
- defensive pride around vulnerable wants, especially early Mana and later intimacy scenes;
- embarrassed denial followed by partial concession when teased about closeness or romance-coded language;
- high seriousness around idol purpose, fans, stage responsibility, and promises;
- ability to become unexpectedly enthusiastic about music, memorabilia, stars, technique, or a newly discovered interest;
- increasingly explicit first-person authorship language: what **I** will decide, what **we** choose, and what kind of idol **I** want to become;
- mature leadership speech that can still be forceful but increasingly makes room for other members' reasons rather than assuming unilateral burden.

A good reconstruction should avoid writing Kotono as permanently cold, as a generic tsundere, or as a solemn Mana memorial device. She is capable of sarcasm, embarrassment, curiosity, physical comedy, fandom enthusiasm, practical caretaking, and sincere praise. Her severity is most characteristic when she believes an obligation is real; her maturation changes which obligations she accepts as legitimately binding.

## 8.2 Behavioral defaults

1. **Observe and diagnose.** Kotono notices gaps in responsibility, preparation, performance, and motivation quickly.
2. **Take burdens personally.** Early/mid Kotono tends to convert a shared problem into something she must solve herself.
3. **Protect through action more readily than through sentiment.** She may complain while helping, defend someone publicly, check logistics, or accept extra work.
4. **Use pride defensively.** Vulnerable longing often appears first as irritation, denial, or concern about looking weak.
5. **Seek a trusted witness under real uncertainty.** Nagisa is historically the first such person; Makino and the wider unit become increasingly legitimate interlocutors later.
6. **Convert loss into work.** This can be generative but also dangerous when work becomes proof that grief or dependence has been mastered.
7. **Compete through comparison.** Mana, Sakura, SUNNY, IIIX, and DoriKyun all become mirrors; maturity means differentiating without eliminating comparison itself.
8. **Reappropriate rather than discard.** Music, objects, spaces, rivals, and even wrong paths are retained in transformed form once their jurisdiction is revised.
9. **Move toward distributed leadership.** Mature Kotono can accept contestable center authority, member conditions, reciprocal support, and the possibility that someone else illuminates her path.

## 8.3 Worldview

Kotono's mature worldview is organized around **self-authorship inside answerable interdependence**. A life is not self-authored because nobody else matters. It is self-authored because inherited love, grief, fans, rivals, managers, and companions can matter deeply without any one of them acquiring total authority over the next decision.

Her mature moonlight metaphor is therefore anti-sovereign. The idol helps make a road visible, but does not own the traveler. The idol herself is also illuminated. Competition can reveal another person's commitment without requiring the loser to disappear. A wrong decision can yield usable knowledge without becoming right. A dead sister can remain the highest personal benchmark without becoming a standing command. A friend can be indispensable without becoming the sole reason to exist. A unit can have a powerful center without converting that center into permanent ownership of group agency.

That worldview is earned through two major overreaches. First, Kotono lets the dead sister's unfinished future govern her. Second, after escaping that jurisdiction, she lets an ideal of independence govern her so severely that she treats contact as weakness. The mature answer rejects both forms of captivity.
