# 0. Responsibility and authority

This ledger is the current Phase-2 character authority for **miho / 武田美帆 / character code `mhk`** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`.

miho is easy to flatten in two opposite directions.

The first flattening is to call her the cold, hyper-competitive IIIX tactician whose life is organized by score optimization and the desire to erase Nagase Mana. That description catches a real phase of her life, but mistakes a grief-generated strategy for her original vocation and then mistakes that strategy for her whole personality.

The second flattening is to say that she eventually “heals,” remembers why she became an idol, accepts new friends, and leaves revenge behind. That description is too clean. The source does not authorize a terminal grief cure. miho continues to prize victory, technical exactness, risk management, and professional severity. Her language about friendship remains resistant. Her relationship to Yō remains singular. Her anti-Mana project is destabilized and recontextualized across the corpus, but the frozen snapshot does not warrant a claim that every retaliatory impulse has disappeared.

The more precise longitudinal problem is **memorial jurisdiction**.

miho begins idolhood with Yō through an audience-facing wish: to stand on stage and make someone feel happy. Yō's death then produces several layers of injury at once: private bereavement, guilt over professional absence during Yō's illness, the collapse of Sundance, and a public-memory asymmetry in which Mana's later death receives enormous collective mourning while Yō seems to vanish from the industry's story. miho answers that asymmetry by giving herself an impossible memorial office. If public culture has erased Yō, she will use victory to erase Mana. If the record book has made one death historically sovereign, she will rewrite the record book.

The crucial fact is that **Yō never assigns this office to her**. Yō's explicit final direction is the opposite: do not remain trapped by me; find new companions and reach the top. The escalating anti-Mana project is therefore a survivor-authored interpretation of fidelity, not a dead friend's command.

That distinction matters because miho's development does not require abandoning Yō. It requires learning that preserving Yō does not give memorial grief unlimited jurisdiction over:

- which other dead person may remain remembered;
- what winning means;
- what scores are for;
- whether IIIX deserves a future after defeat;
- whether living collaborators may enter a promise that began with Yō;
- whether a Sundance song must remain formally frozen;
- whether a value first recognized by Yō can become miho's own embodied self-valuing;
- whether public memory is created by private enclosure or by circulation;
- whether new companionship necessarily replaces the dead.

The capstone is therefore:

> **`MEMORIAL_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_ERASURE_JURISDICTION_MIHO`: miho matures from treating competitive supremacy, archival overwrite, and technical control as compulsory proof that Yō's life mattered into a person increasingly able to preserve Yō through living circulation—audience happiness, self-authored bodily value, transformed performance, disclosed provenance, and chosen collaboration—without requiring Yō's memory to erase Mana, freeze art, terminate relationships after defeat, or monopolize the living present.**

Short form:

> **miho does not honor Yō less when she stops making remembrance a zero-sum contest. She honors Yō more durably when memory can circulate through a life that continues.**

This is not a claim of closure. The frozen corpus deliberately leaves the miho/Yō endpoint open. The stable claim is narrower: by the mature material, miho possesses modes of fidelity that do not depend on erasure, isolation, replication, or victory as a terminal verdict.

The artifact also preserves the v2.5 architecture boundary. miho has abundant ordinary-life evidence—hair care, beauty practice, alcohol, exercise, adult-professional routines, shopping, social embarrassment, mentorship, message style, sharp humor, and domestic preferences. Those remain source-grounded material for Phase 8.5 modeling. This ledger promotes them only when they alter the causal model.

# 4. Close reading I — Yō and the original audience-facing vocation

## 4.1 miho does not enter idolhood as a ranking machine

The opening retrospective in `origin_thrx_001_diamond_mining` is decisive. Adolescent miho attends an idol live because Yō wants to go. She remembers performers delivering smiles and making the audience ecstatic. Yō then asks her to audition together.

Yō's formulation is simple: like magical girls, perhaps they too can stand on stage and make someone feel happy.

That statement supplies the earliest secure telos of miho's idol vocation.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the original goal is audience-facing and relational.

It contains neither Mana nor VENUS rank nor archival revenge.

## 4.2 Yō is not merely “the dead friend who motivates revenge”

The origins establish an unusually specific pre-loss social history:

- miho had few friends;
- she experienced serious bullying;
- Yō became her singular close friend;
- they entered idolhood together;
- they discussed performances for hours after lives;
- they experienced competitive success together.

This matters because Yō is not an abstract moral symbol. She is the person through whom miho first experiences durable peer recognition, shared vocation, and a non-hostile social dyad.

The later fear of being left alone cannot be reduced to bereavement in the narrow sense. Yō's death removes both a person and miho's first reliable structure of mutual recognition.

## 4.3 Technique already belongs to the healthy past

miho's knowledge of VENUS scoring is not created by grief. In the origin material she remembers studying idol footage with Yō and Yō noticing timing details. Yō did not especially like “small tricks,” but the technical observational capacity predates IIIX.

This prevents a moralizing opposition:

> Yō = authentic feeling / miho = corrupted technique.

The stronger model is:

> **technique is a legitimate craft capacity whose jurisdiction later expands beyond its proper domain.**

miho's development can therefore retain technical intelligence without retaining score sovereignty.

# 5. Close reading II — death, guilt, and unequal public mourning

## 5.1 Yō's death creates private grief before it creates public politics

Yō becomes ill. Later card material shows that miho continues some professional work while expecting recovery, including an overseas tour. Yō sends reassuring photographs. miho returns to find her severely deteriorated.

The later question—why did I leave my friend behind?—is genuine retrospective guilt.

The ledger classifies:

- **professional absence during irreversible loss:** TEXTUAL FACT;
- **miho's retrospective guilt:** TEXTUAL FACT;
- **miho knowingly chose career over a known deathbed:** NOT ESTABLISHED;
- **management knowingly coerced her away despite terminal knowledge:** NOT ESTABLISHED.

This distinction prevents grief from being converted into invented culpability.

## 5.2 Yō's final direction is anti-captivity

Yō's remembered instruction is among the most important constraints in the entire character model:

- miho can become a great idol;
- do not remain trapped by me;
- find new companions;
- reach the top.

The exact wording matters less here than its direction. Yō authorizes continuation.

Therefore the later anti-Mana erasure project cannot be treated as simple obedience to the dead.

## 5.3 Mana's death converts grief into historical grievance

The trigger is not that miho believes Mana personally murdered or intentionally displaced Yō. The injury is symbolic and institutional.

Yō dies with relatively little public recognition. Mana later dies and receives overwhelming public grief. miho's protest is not simply “why do people love Mana?” It is closer to:

> **why does one death become nationally legible while the other almost disappears?**

That is a grievance about unequal memorial distribution.

The grievance is morally intelligible even though the chosen remedy becomes destructive.

## 5.4 Retaliation turns recognition into a zero-sum field

miho's response is to make historical correction competitive:

- surpass Mana;
- overwrite records;
- remove songs and traces when institutional means become available;
- crowd Mana out as Yō was crowded out.

This is the first form of **erasure jurisdiction**.

miho takes a real injustice—unequal public remembrance—and assumes that fidelity entitles her to decide whose memory must lose space.

The mature corpus never needs to say that the original grievance was imaginary. It revises the remedy.

# 6. Close reading III — early IIIX: corporate construction and victory-conditioned relation

## 6.1 IIIX is not founded by friendship

Preta Porte constructs IIIX as a prestige project: three already-marketable adults assembled under a “triple excellent” concept. miho meets fran and kana as impressive professional assets before she experiences them as chosen companions.

The internal contrast with Sundance is immediate. miho wants people who actually like idolhood; fran and kana have other motives and different professional histories.

miho's first instinct is conventional group coordination: train together, research, align, become strong enough to reach the top.

## 6.2 miho initially tries to supply the missing shared vocation through method

When the others do not share her idol enthusiasm, miho compensates by becoming a systems reader:

- study prior lives;
- explain VENUS scoring;
- identify timing/AI-sensitive details;
- stress preparation;
- seek group coordination.

Her frustration is not simply control-freak temperament. She is trying to solve a real coordination problem in a group whose members do not share a common intrinsic reason for being idols.

## 6.3 Success prevents the group from having to answer why it exists

The debut is enormously successful. Their raw talent, marketing power, and miho's technical expertise produce overwhelming score performance.

miho recognizes the paradox: perhaps these people will not become the kind of idol group she knew with Yō, but IIIX is strong enough to take her upward.

The relation therefore gains an instrumental constitution:

> **we do not need to agree on what idolhood means if victory keeps proving that the arrangement works.**

`origin_thrx_002_house_of_cards` later makes the latent premise explicit after defeat: if they cannot keep winning, what meaning is there in remaining together?

This becomes a major Phase-2 causal claim. The later U.S. dissolution crisis is not sudden. It was built into the first IIIX constitution.

# 7. Close reading IV — metric expertise and the danger of epistemic sovereignty

## 7.1 miho actually understands the scoring system

The source never treats her as a fraud who merely games numbers. She understands:

- performance mechanics;
- AI analysis;
- audience reaction data;
- precision and synchronization;
- repeatability;
- risk;
- opponent tendencies;
- how to train performers toward measurable improvement.

Her later BIG4 coaching confirms that these competencies generalize beyond self-serving score exploitation. She can teach performance economy and repeated-performance durability to others.

## 7.2 The problem is not measurement; it is metric colonization

The dangerous move is conceptual:

> what can be measured and controlled begins to look like what is most real.

miho's grief makes this attractive. Public mourning is uncontrollable. Death is irreversible. Historical memory is unstable. Scores, records, rank, and choreography can be attacked directly.

The system offers something grief does not: apparent causal legibility.

The ledger therefore distinguishes:

- **technical optimization:** legitimate professional competence;
- **score as useful evidence:** legitimate;
- **score as total definition of idol value or fidelity:** overreach;
- **rank as one professional goal:** legitimate;
- **rank as sovereign proof that Yō mattered:** unstable.

## 7.3 The audience is never actually absent from the metric system

Even VENUS incorporates audience response. miho knows this. Her error is subtler than “she forgets audiences exist.”

She tries to model audience relation as another controllable performance variable. I-UNITY becomes important because Sunny Peace produces a contingent audience capture that exceeds miho's repeatable model while remaining visible to the AI score.

The machine does not rescue IIIX from meaning. It records an encounter they did not know how to reproduce.

# 8. Close reading V — Himeno, motivated toleration, and the ethical floor of winning

## 8.1 miho does not ask for fixed results

This distinction is explicit in the origin material. miho insists she did not ask Himeno to rig a victory. Her position is closer to: there is no absolute certainty in competition, so institutional leverage can secure surrounding conditions.

That is not equivalent to innocence.

## 8.2 The danger is motivated toleration

IIIX sees warning signs and continues because Himeno's power aligns with their interests, including miho's anti-Mana goal.

The corpus therefore does not need a binary of:

- pure IIIX manipulated by evil executive; or
- IIIX knowingly masterminding every corrupt act.

The more accurate middle is **motivated toleration under incomplete knowledge**.

miho's memorial project lowers her threshold for accepting institutional power that promises archival control.

## 8.3 IIIX still preserves a professional pride line

When corruption becomes direct interference with the performance contest, IIIX rejects the implication that it needs such help. fran and kana are especially explicit, but miho agrees: their work should win because it is strong.

This is important evidence that victory is not purely instrumental. miho wants **earned** supremacy.

The ethical flaw is not that she has no distinction between real and fake performance. It is that she allows too much surrounding power to become acceptable while the result still appears technically attributable.

# 9. Close reading VI — I-UNITY defeat and the failure of erasure-by-victory

## 9.1 Sunny Peace does not win by proving technique irrelevant

The final is not a morality play in which sincere amateurs defeat cynical professionals. IIIX remains technically formidable and posts an extraordinary performance.

Sunny Peace's disruption response creates an unusual audience event. The audience is seized by the performance in a way IIIX's optimized routine cannot simply cancel.

miho understands what happened.

## 9.2 The historical irony is explicit

miho wanted to overwrite Mana's legend. Instead, Sunny Peace generates a new legend by performing.

This distinction is central:

> **historical supersession is not the same as archival deletion.**

The living do not have to destroy the old symbol in order to exceed its horizon.

That proposition later becomes important for miho's own Yō problem.

## 9.3 The defeat initially confirms the old constitution's crisis

At this phase, IIIX still says its shared relation is victory. If victory disappears, the reason to remain together disappears.

This is not yet the mature non-terminal victory model. The chronology correction requires that later reauthorization not be imported backward.

# 10. Close reading VII — collapse, the remembered fan, and the first return of purpose

## 10.1 Institutional collapse removes the success machinery

After I-UNITY and Himeno/Preta Porte collapse, IIIX loses work, prestige infrastructure, and institutional guarantee. The members can plausibly dissolve without claiming they failed to honor a friendship covenant, because friendship was never the formal basis of the group.

miho says she can start again; she has done so before.

At first she can even interpret dissolution as emotionally clean.

## 10.2 The Sundance fan falsifies a totalizing belief

At a reduced live, an ordinary fan recognizes miho, says he followed Sundance, and remembers Yō.

The encounter is small. That is precisely why it matters.

miho's grievance had grown toward a total proposition: Yō has been forgotten. One person remembering is sufficient to falsify the universal claim, even though it cannot solve bereavement or repair the unequal scale of mourning.

Correct transition:

- **“Yō was forgotten by everyone” → REJECT / falsified as total claim**;
- **“Yō received far less public memorial recognition than Mana” → PRESERVE**;
- **“miho still wants larger public recognition for Yō” → PRESERVE**;
- **“therefore Mana must be erased” → increasingly destabilized, not yet fully textually extinguished.**

## 10.3 The first promise returns

The encounter recalls the earliest dream: make people who come to the stage happy.

miho explicitly realizes that she has been carrying a later promise while forgetting the first.

This is not a retreat from ambition. She still wants the top. It changes the ordering of ends.

Top becomes a route through which a communicative vocation can operate rather than the only proof that the vocation is real.

# 11. Close reading VIII — second founding: reauthorization without sentimental conversion

## 11.1 IIIX could end here

The institutional project has failed. The original utility compact has been damaged. The source gives the trio a real exit.

That matters because continuation is only evidence of choice if non-continuation is possible.

## 11.2 miho re-selects fran and kana without calling them Yō's replacements

miho continues to deny friendship language. She can say Yō was her one friend while also admitting that after years together fran and kana have become a `腐れ縁`—a durable, irritating bond she elects to continue.

This is not linguistic cowardice that the text asks us to translate automatically into “best friends.” The refusal itself is part of miho's social grammar.

But behavior now exceeds pure utility.

A mature relation can therefore be:

> **non-sentimental, adversarial, mutually useful, affectively specific, and voluntarily retained.**

## 11.3 Non-replacement is the key memorial safeguard

Yō remains singular because the new relationships are not required to occupy Yō's old role.

This is structurally healthier than either extreme:

- permanent isolation to prove Yō was irreplaceable;
- or narrative substitution in which fran/kana become “the new Yō.”

The mature architecture permits **new companionship without role replacement**.

# 12. Close reading IX — Hoshimi integration: contribution without domestication

## 12.1 Hoshimi does not require personality assimilation

Early Hoshimi material makes IIIX's terms explicit. They join or cooperate for professional leverage, not because they suddenly desire Hoshimi-style belonging.

This becomes one of the strongest institutional tests in the project:

> can an organization integrate adults who remain abrasive, competitive, transactional, and private?

For miho, the answer is increasingly yes.

## 12.2 Sakura is a resemblance trigger, not a replacement cure

fran notices that Sakura reminds miho of Yō.

# 13. Close reading X — creative authorship: from optimizer to performance architect

# 14. Close reading XI — Stellar: hostile intimacy becomes developmentally generative

# 16. Close reading XIII — the U.S. archive: private fidelity fails as public preservation

## 16.1 The archival edit is the cruelest falsification

In the United States, miho encounters media/history that edits Yō out so that miho can be presented as a cleaner precursor to “perfect IIIX.”

This makes the memorial problem literal.

miho has spent years pursuing victory partly to stop Yō from disappearing. Yet the successful public image created by that path supplies an incentive to remove Yō from the story.

The result is a devastating contradiction:

> **private fidelity + professional success can coexist with public erasure.**

## 16.2 Memory requires circulation

fran supplies the missing distinction. Remembering someone privately is not the same as making that person publicly rememberable.

This becomes `MEMORY_REQUIRES_CIRCULATION`.

It revises miho's prior strategy in two ways:

1. memorial success cannot be measured only by miho's private intensity;
2. public memory requires forms other people can encounter, repeat, reinterpret, and carry forward.

## 16.3 Public circulation is incompatible with total enclosure

If Yō is to remain publicly present, miho cannot also insist that only she may decide what Yō's memory can become.

This does not mean surrendering provenance.

The mature formula is:

> **share consequences, preserve origin.**

fran and kana can participate in the future of Yō's promise without becoming its authors of origin or substitutes for Yō.

# 17. Close reading XIV — the vow hierarchy: top is not the final purpose

U.S. criticism forces miho to articulate the vow more precisely.

Her hierarchy contains at least two ends:

1. reach the top;
2. become an idol who can make someone happy.

The old metric doctrine tended to collapse the second into the first: if top rank proves fidelity, then score optimization becomes the most reliable route to all meaning.

The critique exposes **goal displacement**.

miho does not abandon score. She immediately returns to choreography and optimization. That is exactly why the scene is useful.

The revision is not ideological conversion to anti-competition.

It is:

> **score is evidence and means inside a communicative vocation; it is not the vocation's sovereign purpose.**

This is one of the strongest stable Phase-2 claims.

# 18. Close reading XV — non-terminal absolute victory

The mature IIIX still speaks in the grammar of absolute victory.

What changes is the consequence of loss.

Earlier:

> if we cannot win, being together has no meaning.

Later, confronted with the possibility of losing to the reigning No. 1, miho's answer is effectively:

> then we figure out how to beat her next time.

This creates `NON_TERMINAL_ABSOLUTE_VICTORY`.

The phrase sounds paradoxical but is precise.

- intention can remain absolute;
- standards can remain severe;
- competition can remain central;
- defeat can hurt;
- **but defeat no longer has jurisdiction to decide whether the relation or vocation deserves another future.**

This is perhaps the most important institutional change in miho's competitive ethic.

It also reworks grief. If loss is not terminal, then failure no longer needs to be immediately converted into a memorial verdict.

# 19. Close reading XVI — `Friend Glass`: memorial reperformance without replication

## 19.1 miho first confronts the impossibility of exact return

miho proposes covering Sundance's signature friendship song `Friend Glass` with IIIX.

Practice fails because fran and kana do not inhabit the song as Yō did. miho initially treats the mismatch as a production problem that can be corrected through adjustment.

Then she states the harder truth:

> the original song was miho and Yō; Yō is gone; it cannot simply be sung as before.

This is not artistic defeat. It is a correct recognition of non-substitutability.

## 19.2 The solution is not to preserve the song untouched

IIIX substantially rearranges the piece into its own abrasive, competitive relation.

miho can introduce it publicly as a precious Sundance song while also saying that the audience will now hear **their current friendship song**.

This is the strongest single demonstration of the capstone.

Preservation does not require formal stasis.

## 19.3 Transformation increases rather than destroys provenance

The response shows:

- old fans hear something new;
- new listeners become interested in the Sundance original;
- the original remains available rather than superseded;
- miho explicitly hopes Yō gains new fans.

The correct model is therefore:

> **memorial reperformance can increase circulation precisely by refusing replication.**

## 19.4 New companionship does not replace Yō

Earlier in the card miho privately admits that after nearly losing IIIX she does not want to be left alone again.

At the end, asked what she would tell Yō, her answer is essentially: **I am not alone anymore, so do not worry.**

This does not mean fran and kana have become Yō.

It means Yō's uniqueness no longer requires miho's permanent social isolation as proof.

That is `NEW_COMPANIONSHIP_WITHOUT_REPLACEMENT`.

# 20. Close reading XVII — hair: relational recognition becomes self-authored embodied value

The hair-care card supplies a small but theoretically powerful parallel to the memorial arc.

Early miho did not especially care about her hair. A long-time friend praised it as beautiful. miho says the praise made her happy, led her to like her own hair, and made care enjoyable and pride-producing.

The value therefore has a relational origin.

But it is not permanently owned by the dead recognizer.

miho now:

- enjoys hair care;
- treats it as idol professionalism;
- experiments with products and salons;
- uses seasonal product differences;
- accepts commercial work connected to it;
- feels proud of the maintained result.

The resulting pattern is:

> **RELATIONAL_RECOGNITION_BECOMES_SELF_AUTHORED_EMBODIED_VALUE.**

This is a miniature of the larger arc.

Yō can remain causally present without retaining exclusive jurisdiction over what miho may do with the value afterward.

The card does not establish that changing hairstyle would betray Yō. Treating the hair as a frozen relic would repeat precisely the memorial error the larger corpus is revising.

# 21. Mature personality architecture

## 21.1 miho is severe, but severity is usually organized around standards rather than indiscriminate cruelty

She is impatient with incompetence, sloppy preparation, naive management, and avoidable inefficiency. Her insults can be sharp. She often assumes others should read instructions, prepare properly, or stop creating preventable problems.

Yet the same source repeatedly shows practical intervention:

- she fixes problems;
- introduces resources;
- gives technical advice;
- mentors younger idols;
- watches professional risk;
- protects confidentiality;
- notices when others are unprepared or endangered.

Severity is therefore often a **care-adjacent professional register**, though it should not be romanticized into universal benevolence.

## 21.2 miho is socially defensive rather than socially indifferent

Her adolescent history and Yō relationship matter here. miho can present as if she needs very little relationally, but later evidence repeatedly exposes otherwise:

- she was deeply shaped by singular peer recognition;
- she fears being left alone again;
- she values long-term fans;
- she retains specific industry relationships;
- she becomes attached to IIIX without adopting sentimental vocabulary;
- she notices younger colleagues and gives advice.

The stable model is not “cold person secretly soft.”

It is:

> **a socially selective adult who dislikes cheap intimacy labels but forms highly consequential attachments.**

## 21.3 Professional competence is a real source of identity

miho has long industry experience. She knows performance preparation, image labor, scoring systems, choreography, audience mechanics, career risk, etiquette, and practical maintenance.

This expertise is not merely armor. She genuinely values doing work well.

## 21.4 Control becomes most dangerous under grief or uncertainty

When causal uncertainty rises, miho tends toward domains she can structure:

- score;
- rank;
- training;
- choreography;
- risk management;
- schedules;
- public image;
- archival strategy.

That tendency is productive until it becomes a substitute for answering the underlying question.

The anti-Mana project is the clearest example: an uncontrollable grief problem is translated into a technically actionable competitive program.

## 21.5 She can revise without renouncing prior competence

miho's maturation is unusually non-conversional.

She does not have to become:

- anti-score;
- anti-rank;
- anti-business;
- anti-publicity;
- openly sentimental;
- generically friendly;
- unambitious.

Instead she changes the **ordering and jurisdiction** of those systems.

## 21.6 She tolerates contradiction better at maturity

Mature miho can increasingly hold simultaneously:

- Yō is singular **and** fran/kana matter;
- victory is vital **and** defeat need not end the future;
- score matters **and** audience enjoyment can make a loss non-empty;
- public image is constructed **and** construction can answer to real trust;
- technical optimization is valuable **and** excessive synchronization can suppress the group's strength;
- memory should persist **and** memorial art may change;
- recognition may begin in another person **and** become one's own value.

This capacity to hold non-zero-sum propositions is the mature architecture underlying the capstone.

## 23.5 Adult social texture

Bond material establishes adult-professional domains unavailable to younger characters, including alcohol and long career memory. miho can become nostalgic when mildly disinhibited, but even then catches herself and restores control.

This is modeling-relevant later; it is not the core literary arc.

# 29. P2-B5 miho conclusion

miho's story is not fundamentally the story of a cold competitor learning that feelings matter.

She begins with feeling.

The first secure vocation is made possible by Yō: two socially connected girls see idols make an audience happy and imagine doing the same. Technical analysis, competition, reflection, and ambition all exist inside that earlier living relationship.

Death destroys the dyad but does not by itself create the central distortion. The distortion emerges when private grief becomes a public-history problem. Mana's death receives a scale of mourning Yō never received. miho experiences that imbalance as proof that the world has granted one dead idol historical existence while another has been allowed to disappear.

Her answer is understandable precisely because it is actionable. A bereaved person cannot reverse death, but an elite idol can attack records. She can study the scoring system, optimize performance, take the top, manipulate the archive's future, and—when institutional power offers the possibility—remove Mana's traces. Grief is converted into a technical program.

That conversion produces extraordinary competence. It also creates a category error.

Winning can establish performance strength. It cannot prove that Yō's life mattered. Erasing Mana cannot make Yō more alive. Private remembering cannot guarantee public circulation. A perfect public persona can even become the mechanism by which Yō is edited out. And a group held together only because it wins has no answer to the first defeat that cannot be reversed immediately.

The franchise does not correct miho by humiliating her technical worldview. It repeatedly repurposes it.

Her risk analysis becomes mentoring. Her choreography becomes creative authorship. Her obsession with repeatability becomes knowledge about where repeatability fails. Her competitive hostility becomes a way IIIX members develop one another. Her standards remain high enough that loss hurts, but loss becomes information rather than an existential verdict. She can still want absolute victory without allowing a single defeat to terminate the future.

The same revision occurs in memory.

The Sundance fan proves Yō was not literally forgotten by everyone. The U.S. archive proves that being privately remembered by miho is still not enough. The solution is not stronger enclosure. It is circulation.

That is why `Friend Glass` is so important. miho first reaches the correct impossibility: fran and kana cannot reproduce the song's old relational truth because they are not Yō. Instead of abandoning the song or forcing them into the missing person's role, IIIX changes it. They make the friendship song sound like the abrasive relation they actually have. The present performance points listeners backward to Sundance rather than overwriting it. Yō can gain new listeners because the work changed.

The hair card expresses the same logic at the scale of one body. Yō once tells miho that her hair is beautiful. The praise matters. But miho does not merely preserve an object for a dead observer. She learns to like the hair herself, enjoys caring for it, turns that care into professional discipline, experiments, and accepts commercial recognition. A value can originate in love and become genuinely one's own.

The mature miho is therefore not a person who escapes the dead.

She is a person learning how the dead can remain causally present **without becoming sovereign over the living**.

Yō can remain singular without requiring isolation. A promise can retain provenance while living collaborators participate in its future. A song can remain memorial while being rearranged. Competition can remain fierce while defeat ceases to be terminal. Technical measurement can remain useful while audience happiness recovers priority. Public image can remain constructed while becoming answerable to real trust. Mana can remain part of history without Yō disappearing.

This is the meaning of:

> **`MEMORIAL_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_ERASURE_JURISDICTION_MIHO`**

miho's mature achievement is not that she finally “moves on” from Yō. It is that continuation itself becomes a legitimate form of fidelity.
