# 0. Responsibility and authority

This ledger is the current Phase-2 character authority for **Komiyama Ai / 小美山愛 / character code `ai`** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`.

Ai is easy to compress into a cluster of accurate but incomplete labels: cheerful junior, LizNoir's smile, Kokoro's earnest best friend, hardworking underdog, comparatively ordinary trainee surrounded by elite dancers, or the emotionally open member who softens the severe founders. P2-B3 preserves those visible traits while rejecting them as a sufficient longitudinal explanation.

The central problem is **worthiness contingency**. Ai repeatedly converts admiration, gratitude, failure, and care into a question about whether she deserves the role or relationship she already occupies. She can be explicitly selected for LizNoir and still ask why someone like her was chosen. She can make a real technical mistake and interpret it as evidence that she may be unfit for LizNoir or idolhood. She can fail to help Kokoro immediately and feel that she has failed the role of closest friend. She can perform on what the founders themselves call LizNoir's best stage, lose, and move directly to `私のせいです` and the counterfactual that more effort from her might have prevented defeat.

This is why Ai should **not** be modeled through a clean “insecure girl becomes confident” curve. Her objective competence, institutional legitimacy, creative authority, and relationship reciprocity all rise markedly. Her subjective certainty about her own worth rises much less smoothly. Mature Ai can propose choreography, disagree with founders, tell them to rely on the juniors, claim LizNoir even under hypothetical founder absence, lead a cross-unit creative team, and supply the concept that becomes a tour song's guiding principle. Yet the old self-blame reflex remains available under stress.

Her arc is therefore the development of **authorial capacity despite insecurity**.

Ai's earliest idol motive is modest and concrete. Her father suggests idolhood, and Ai translates that suggestion into a wish to make people smile the way his ramen does and perhaps help the family shop. This matters because Ai's first value model is not prestige. It is **work that reaches another person and produces visible happiness**. Later material returns to her family and shows that filial contribution remains part of her identity rather than a disposable pre-debut anecdote.

Trainee-era Ai also admits something more complicated: she wanted to debut without seriously asking what kind of idol she wanted to become. The first LizNoir live supplies that missing form. Seeing Rio and Aoi produces `私も、あんな風になりたい`, followed by the realization that she has finally decided what kind of idol she wants to be. LizNoir is therefore not merely a famous group she joins later. It participates in the construction of Ai's vocational ideal.

That admiration is productive but dangerous. It gives Ai direction and persistence; it also creates an asymmetrical mental model in which Rio/Aoi possess everything and Ai has little to give them. At quartet formation the technical asymmetry is real. Ai has trained for about two years, repeatedly failed auditions, and has no comparable record. She responds not with entitlement but with work, apology, service, and an intense desire not to be a burden. Even after Kokoro tells her Asakura independently required both juniors, Ai's instinct remains `なんで、私なんか……`.

America begins the revision. Performing before an audience that may not understand the Japanese words, Ai feels rhythm, voice, and crowd become a shared field: `今、私達は感情を共有してるんだと`; `LizNoirの叫びが、海を越えたんだと`. Her difference from old LizNoir — visible emotion — begins to look like something the institution needs rather than merely a defect the junior must overcome.

The 2021 *The Last Chance* event makes this explicit. Ai becomes overexcited and falls out of coordination. The mistake is real. Rio's harsh response is also real. But Ai converts a performance error into existential rejection: perhaps she is unworthy, perhaps she should not be an idol, perhaps Rio is disappointed enough to abandon her. Under that fear, instruction becomes mimicry. Ai tries to suppress the very emotional visibility that makes her distinctive because she assumes being “LizNoir-like” means reproducing the founders.

Rio's repair changes the terms. She apologizes for excessive harshness and explicitly tells Ai that expressing feeling directly is one of Ai's strengths; high technique should **amplify** it. Ai then does something the frightened version of herself had avoided: she disagrees with inherited choreography. She argues that old member Rio and new member Ai should move forward together because that staging better expresses what *The Last Chance* now means. She gives up her own solo showcase to do it. Rio accepts and tells her to keep speaking her mind.

Ai then names the transition herself: she has spent her time following Rio's back, but she now wants to stand beside Rio and sometimes be capable of pulling her forward. This is the first clear conversion of successor legitimacy from **obedience** into **responsible disagreement**.

Kokoro supplies a parallel relationship school. Ai is the one who approaches the prickly trainee because she wants to be friends. She stays through repeated failure, remembers fine-grained changes in Kokoro's treatment of her, and ordinarily permits non-disclosure when Kokoro is not ready to explain painful history. Yet in the 2022 guilt crisis, Ai's care itself becomes worthiness-laden: if she is Kokoro's closest friend, why can she not help? Once she learns the truth, she becomes much more forceful.

The repair matters because Ai does not ultimately demand transparency for its own sake. She rejects the model in which Kokoro privately pays forever for an old injury. `過ぎたことを、一人で悔やむんじゃなくて / 未来のことを、みんなで話し合おうよ`. Care matures toward **shared burden and shared futurity**.

BIG4 scales that lesson from friendship to institutional succession. With Aoi absent and Rio strained, Ai/Kokoro initially frame their work as being for the founders. They then identify the limitation themselves. Ai says `莉央さんの夢を叶えるためとか、お二人のために / 頑張るとか――ただの言い訳だったね`. Sincere altruism can still hide unwillingness to claim authorship.

Ai's counterfactual standard becomes severe: even if Rio disappeared too, she wants to be able to say `私達が、私達こそがLizNoirだって`. This is not rejection of Rio/Aoi. It is **reciprocal inheritance**: the heir proves fidelity by becoming able to answer, carry, revise, and contribute rather than by maintaining permanent distance from the founder.

When Aoi returns, Ai makes the reciprocal burden model explicit. She wants the seniors to cause the juniors trouble too: `だって……私達は四人でLizNoirだから`. Mature hierarchy no longer means founders protect while juniors repay them through obedience. Being relied upon becomes evidence that the juniors are trusted to carry part of the shared institution.

Later material proves this was not crisis rhetoric. Ai/Kokoro draft choreography proactively. Ai tells Aoi that being relied upon makes them happy. In the 2025 Hoshimi national-tour project, Ai accepts songwriting-team leadership despite being visibly frightened. Asked what she herself wants the song to express, she supplies the theme that becomes the project's guide: **`感謝`** — gratitude toward Rio/Aoi, Kokoro, songs, Hoshimi Production, and especially the fans. Gratitude, once liable to become deference, becomes a creative principle authored forward.

Stellar then gives the strongest explicit four-person ownership and the strongest counterevidence to a clean confidence arc in immediate succession. Ai says none of the four could be absent or substituted and still produce this LizNoir; the four-person present is a miracle and `私達四人が最高のアイドルグループです！`. After defeat she nevertheless says `サニピにスコアが届かなかったのは、私のせいです` and imagines more personal effort might have changed the result. The source itself rejects her isolated causal reading.

The mature model is therefore:

> **Ai learns that admiration becomes mature inheritance only when the admirer is willing to answer back, contribute something the admired person did not already contain, accept being relied upon, and claim the shared institution as partly hers. Her old reflex to prove worth through endless effort and self-blame never fully disappears, so maturity is not permanent self-confidence; it is the growing capacity to act authorially despite worthiness anxiety.**

Short form:

> **Ai does not mature by outgrowing reverence, gratitude, hard work, or emotional openness. She matures by refusing to let reverence decide that her rightful place is always behind someone else.**

- The family/ramen motive is real but is not an exhaustive explanation of idolhood.
- Continuing reverence for Rio/Aoi is not developmental failure.
- Mature coauthorship does not imply stable high self-esteem in every context.
- Ai/Kokoro is highly intimate and strongly yuri-readable; explicit dating/romantic fact is not established in the locked corpus.

# 4. Character mechanics

## 4.1 Core desires

Ai wants to make people happy through work and performance; become an idol she can sincerely admire; move forward with Kokoro; deserve and then co-own LizNoir; reach audiences emotionally; help people she loves; and improve until effort becomes visible contribution. These motives overlap but should not be reduced to rank pursuit.

## 4.2 Core pressure: worthiness contingency

The recurrent pressure can be summarized as:

> *If I am not good enough, useful enough, hardworking enough, or emotionally helpful enough, perhaps I do not deserve the role or relationship I have been given.*

This is a behavioral reconstruction, not a diagnostic claim. It is supported by selection disbelief, service-as-compensation, rejection fear, friend-role shame, low-claiming center behavior, leadership anxiety, and post-defeat self-blame.

## 4.3 Effort has two functions

Healthy Ai effort develops capacity toward an owned goal. Worthiness-driven effort attempts to neutralize the possibility that she never deserved the role. The same visible behavior can therefore have different meanings depending on context.

## 4.4 Emotional expressivity is a resource, not an exemption

Ai is unusually legible in excitement, admiration, gratitude, anxiety, concern, frustration, and affection. This does not make her exempt from coordination or craft. The mature LizNoir model is that technique should enlarge communicative affect rather than replace it.

## 4.5 Public/private continuity

Unlike Kokoro, Ai is not principally organized around a sharply constructed public persona. Register changes by hierarchy and context, but her earnestness and emotional visibility are comparatively continuous across public/private settings.

## 4.6 Mature professional philosophy

By the endpoint Ai's practice implies: effort matters; technique matters; audience relation matters; feeling should be carried rather than erased; inherited excellence may be reinterpreted; fair competition can coexist with affection; burden should be shareable; gratitude should become contribution; and a nervous person can still be responsible for leading.

# 5. Origin: family work, trainee persistence, and the discovery of an idol telos

## 5.1 Family work as the earliest value model

`origin_liz_010`, `st-group-liz-01-03-13`, gives Ai an unusually ordinary point of entry into idolhood. Her father suggests she might try because she is cute. Ai then supplies her own reason:

> `アイドルになってお父さんの作ったラーメン / みたいにみんなを笑顔にしたい`

She also hopes greater visibility could bring customers to the shop and count as `親孝行`.

The point is not the comic recurrence of ramen by itself. The longitudinally important structure is that Ai understands value through **work offered outward**. Her father's food makes people happy; she wants performance to do something analogous. The later C2 birthday card preserves this motive after professionalization. Faced with pressure on the family shop, Ai wants to use her own work opportunities to help and tells her father `私も / お父さんの力になりたい。頼ってほしいな`.

This continuity matters because later reciprocal-burden language is not invented from nowhere. Ai's mature ethics repeatedly ask people she loves to let her contribute.

## 5.2 Desire for admission precedes a defined professional ideal

In `st-group-liz-01-02-08`, a coach tells Ai to show more of herself and asks whether she truly wants to become an idol. The question is not answered by saying she has not worked. She has. The problem is that her desire is still under-specified.

After attending LizNoir's live, Ai says:

> `……アイドルって、こんなに素敵なものだったんだ`
>
> `私も、あんな風になりたい`

and then diagnoses her earlier state:

> `アイドルになりたいとかデビューしたいばっかりで / どんなアイドルになりたいのか、全然考えてなかった`

before concluding:

> `でも、どんなアイドルになりたいのかは、今日決まった`

The first LizNoir live therefore functions as a **telos event**. Ai acquires not merely inspiration but a standard against which she can imagine a professional self.

## 5.3 Repeated failure produces persistence rather than entitlement

Ai and Kokoro experience repeated audition defeat, including seeing later trainees move ahead. Ai's response is that she must practice more. This stable respect for effort later informs both her admiration for the founders and her distaste for contemptuous or unfair competition.

Yet accumulated failure also matters psychologically. When selection finally arrives, success does not feel self-authenticating. Ai has learned how to continue after being told “not yet”; she has not necessarily learned how to inhabit “you were chosen.”

## 5.4 Ai chooses Kokoro before either succeeds

The Ai/Kokoro bond begins before professional status can make the relationship advantageous. Ai wants to be friends because she wants to be friends. Later, when Kokoro contemplates being disappointed by or disappointing Ai, Ai answers that she will not be disappointed and that she intends to debut together with Kokoro even if it requires extraordinary effort.

This is an important basis for later co-ownership: Ai's durable loyalties are not only upward toward admired seniors. She also forms horizontal commitments before success validates them.

# 6. Entry into LizNoir: admiration becomes hierarchy

## 6.1 The selection scene preserves the real asymmetry

In `origin_liz_005`, Ai introduces herself with `LizNoirにはずっと憧れてました！`. The founders then establish why the situation feels impossible. Ai has trained for roughly two years, lacks notable professional experience, and has repeatedly failed auditions. Rio/Aoi initially reject a formation that includes the juniors.

P2-B3 does not flatten this into “mean seniors fail to see hidden equality.” Ai is objectively entering an institution whose standards exceed her current level.

## 6.2 A performance gap becomes an existential legitimacy problem

When public reaction turns hostile, Ai does not stop at “we violated the old group's image rules.” She begins asking whether she and Kokoro were ever fit to join. This is the same cognitive movement later visible after her 2021 mistake and Stellar defeat:

> **local insufficiency -> global worthiness question**.

## 6.3 Independent selection is still hard to internalize

Ai eventually wonders whether Kokoro got her included. She points out that she was not the best trainee and lacks Kokoro's obvious character concept. Kokoro corrects her: Asakura explicitly wanted the pair, including Ai. Ai's reaction remains `なんで、私なんか……`.

This is among the cleanest demonstrations that external proof alone cannot solve Ai's internal legitimacy problem.

## 6.4 Service carries a compensatory logic

In `origin_liz_006`, Ai tries to take care of the seniors and explains:

> `私とこころはダンスが下手で`
>
> `いつも莉央さん達の足を引っ張って、迷惑を掛けてます`
>
> `せめて、これくらいは……`

The care is genuine. The `せめて` exposes the second function: if she cannot yet contribute enough onstage, she can reduce her cost elsewhere.

Rio gives a more calibrated evaluation. Ai/Kokoro are not bad by ordinary idol standards; LizNoir simply demands more. Rio also describes her own improvement as a product of sustained effort. Ai hears the encouragement but again translates it into whether effort will make the founders **recognize** them as real LizNoir.

The junior's question is still vertically organized.

# 7. America and 2021: difference becomes legitimate contribution

## 7.1 Ai experiences performance as shared affect

The America street-performance sequence in `origin_liz_006` is a key Ai source. The audience may not understand the Japanese words, but they respond bodily through rhythm and voice. Ai narrates:

> `私は感じました。今、私達は感情を共有してるんだと`
>
> `LizNoirの叫びが、海を越えたんだと`

The sequence returns Rio/Aoi to an older sense of heat while simultaneously making Ai's own affective orientation part of the quartet's reconstruction.

## 7.2 “Smile or perfection” resolves into integration, not replacement

The origin title invites a binary, but the actual resolution refuses it. Rio/Aoi do not abandon technical standards for conventional smile performance. Ai/Kokoro do not get excused from craft because they are emotionally accessible.

Instead:

> **technique serves expression; expression gives technique a communicative destination.**

This is why Ai's function cannot be reduced to “warmth.” She participates in redefining what professional excellence is for.

## 7.3 The 2021 mistake is genuinely Ai's mistake

Ai becomes so happy during a live that she gets ahead of herself and falls out of alignment. Her later apology is explicit. A rigorous model must preserve this: the source is not simply exposing Rio as unfair.

The developmental problem comes from Ai's interpretation. Rio's severe language causes Ai to move toward `やっぱり私はリズノワにふさわしくないのかな`. She fears not just technical correction but abandonment and global unfitness for idolhood.

## 7.4 Fear makes Ai suppress the wrong thing

Because *The Last Chance* is deeply important to Ai, she becomes afraid to sing it. She assumes someone unworthy could fail the song itself. Her solution is to follow Rio as literally as possible and suppress her own emotional style.

The more sacred the inherited work becomes, the more tempted Ai is to remove herself from it.

## 7.5 Rio explicitly validates Ai's difference

The repair in `st-eve-2109-backside-004` is unusually clear:

# 9. BIG4: from gratitude to ownership

## 9.1 Aoi's absence makes junior authorship unavoidable

With Aoi withdrawn and Rio overburdened, “support the founders” ceases to be a complete role specification. Ai and Kokoro have to decide what LizNoir is if the founders cannot presently carry it for them.

The claim needs precision. Ai does **not** discover that she never cared about Rio/Aoi. She discovers that sincere care can still protect her from the frightening sentence “this is mine too.”

## 9.3 Founder-independent ownership is the test

Ai wants to become able to say:

> `もし、莉央さんまでいなくなって二人きりになっても`
>
> `私達が、私達こそがLizNoirだって……`
>
> `胸を張って言える私になりたい`

The counterfactual is not a wish for Rio's disappearance. It is a test of whether the junior's identity depends on continuous founder validation.

## 9.4 The junior can now pull the senior

Ai/Kokoro increasingly tell Rio what she needs: stop carrying only the leader role, admit personal desire, let others share the burden. This is the mature realization of Ai's 2021 wish to sometimes pull Rio rather than always follow.

## 9.5 Exact-four settlement makes burden reciprocal

After Aoi returns, Ai tells the founders:

> `私達ももっと先輩達に迷惑を掛けられたいです`

because:

> `私達は四人でLizNoirだから`

Being burdened is no longer automatically evidence that Ai is a defective junior. It can be evidence that she is trusted as a member capable of carrying weight.

# 10. Later professionalism: reverence survives without restoring subordination

Ai remains visibly reverential toward Rio/Aoi in later material and can still describe them as `永遠の憧れ`. The 2023 center-contest event even lets her volunteer herself for the edge while stronger personalities contest center.

# 11. 2025 cross-unit leadership: `感謝` as authored principle

## 11.1 Ai participates in production design

# 14. Voice, behavior, and personality reconstruction notes

This section records voice/personality features needed for current P2 interpretation. The exhaustive “what is it like to spend a day with Ai?” profile is deferred to Phase 8.5.

## 14.1 First person and hierarchy-sensitive register

Ai uses **`私`**. With seniors and Makino she commonly uses polite `です/ます`, `莉央さん`, `葵さん`, respectful requests, and frequent apologies. The politeness is sincere rather than ironic.

## 14.2 Kokoro unlocks a much more casual register

With Kokoro, Ai becomes immediate and emotionally exposed: scolding, whining, teasing, direct affection, repetition, and under crisis even `このバカ!!` intensity. This forcefulness is relationship-conditioned and should not be generalized into a combative public voice.

## 14.3 Ai verbalizes affect directly

Ai often says what she feels in simple, emphatic forms: `凄く嬉しい！`, `凄く楽しい！`, `もっと歌いたい！`, `もっと踊りたい！`. Gratitude, admiration, anxiety, concern, and affection are similarly explicit.

This continuity between ordinary speech and stage feeling supports the performance model: emotional legibility is not an artificial persona she only puts on under spotlights.

## 14.4 Anxiety increases apology and ceremony

Under status pressure Ai tends toward:

- repeated `すみません`;
- `恐れ多い`-type self-lowering;
- elaborate formal introductions;
- visible qualification of her own right to speak;
- asking whether someone else is better suited.

The 2025 leader introduction is especially useful future voice evidence.

## 14.5 Earnest does not mean naïve

Ai can detect strain, separate suspicion from proof, distinguish reassurance from structural repair, propose practical organization, interpret performance, mediate strong personalities, and formulate a creative theme. She is emotionally plainspoken, not intellectually simple.

## 14.6 Fairness follows from her respect for effort

Ai dislikes denigrating people who are seriously working and dislikes unfair or ugly competition. She can simultaneously want to win intensely. Fairness is not noncompetitiveness.

## 14.7 Self-deprecation coexists with capability

A common Ai sequence is:

1. doubt whether she is the right person;
2. receive confidence from someone she trusts;
3. accept the role anyway;
4. remain visibly nervous;
5. work extremely hard;
6. make a genuine contribution;
7. distribute credit.

This is behaviorally more accurate than either “Ai is timid” or “Ai is secretly confident.”

## 14.8 Care is practical and active

Ai notices fatigue, asks what is wrong, offers help, changes practical conditions, recruits others, and moves conversation toward future action. Under high stakes, care can become intrusive because she experiences usefulness as part of relational worth.

## 14.9 Effort/fitness/ramen associations are real but belong downstream

The sources contain recurring ordinary-life material in which Ai translates problems through exercise or ramen, becomes highly animated about ramen, and can infodump when the topic appears. These are excellent Phase-8.5 experiential-profile inputs. P2-B3 preserves them only as routing notes because they are not necessary to the core longitudinal capstone.

## 14.10 Mature worldview

Operationally, mature Ai behaves as if:

- effort matters even when talent differs;
- admiration should give direction, not erase the admirer;
- technique and emotional communication should strengthen one another;
- a successor eventually has to answer back;
- care should make burdens shareable rather than privately heroic;
- gratitude should be returned through contribution;
- fair competition can coexist with affection and respect;
- being relied upon can be evidence of trust rather than proof of being a burden;
- a living institution belongs partly to the people who inherit and change it;
- self-doubt may return, but it does not have to decide whether one acts.
