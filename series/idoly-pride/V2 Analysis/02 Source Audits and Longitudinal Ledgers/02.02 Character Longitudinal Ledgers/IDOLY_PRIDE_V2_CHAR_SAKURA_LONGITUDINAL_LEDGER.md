# 0. Responsibility and authority

This is the first evidence-bearing SUNNY PEACE character ledger in Phase 2. Its responsibility is to reconstruct **Sakura as a changing person across survival, inherited bodily history, idol vocation, leadership, rivalry, audience relation, professional pressure, and reciprocal care** rather than to freeze her as the uncomplicated “sunny” center of SUNNY PEACE.

The ledger retains four boundaries that are especially important for Sakura.

1. **Chronology and disclosure are not the same clock.** Sakura's childhood illness, Tomo relationship, transplant recovery, pre-Hoshimi heartbeat practice, and early Haruko encounter are disclosed later in the game. They may explain Sakura's earlier conduct after disclosure, but they may not be projected backward into the anime viewer's prospective knowledge state.
2. **Bodily provenance is not personal jurisdiction.** Mana's heart is a literal historical fact of Sakura's body. That fact does not authorize the dead donor, the public, Kotono, Makino, or Sakura herself to treat Mana's former desires as commands that determine Sakura's identity.
3. **Brightness is neither pure temperament nor pure mask.** Childhood evidence establishes smiling partly as protective emotional labor and a “good child” burden-management strategy. Later evidence establishes spontaneous joy, chosen audience-facing generosity, and reciprocal radiance. A mature model must preserve all of these layers rather than selecting one as the “real” Sakura.
4. **Present-tense intensity is not automatically self-destruction.** Sakura's mortality history makes “do what I can while I can” a genuine self-authored value. Stellar also shows that this value can become dangerous when combined with public obligation, competition, and a body under load. The corpus therefore requires ethical tension rather than a simple verdict that either risk-taking or restraint is always right.

The frozen substantive inheritance remains `IDOLY_PRIDE_V2_PHASE1_FINAL_BASELINE.md`. The Mana and Makino Phase-2 ledgers are current dependencies where Sakura's story intersects inheritance, management, audience duty, and non-replacement. Dedicated Sakura/Kotono and SUNNY PEACE relationship/unit ledgers remain later canonical homes for relational and institutional conclusions that exceed Sakura's individual responsibility here.

# 2. The three clocks: chronology, disclosure, validation

## 2.1 Fictional chronology

The most useful causal chronology for Sakura is:

1. childhood physical frailty and diagnosis with severe heart disease;
2. observation of her mother's guilt and crying;
3. adoption of smiling and “being a good child” partly to reduce parental distress and burden;
4. overhearing that she may have only a few years to live and first explicit terror of death/nonexistence;
5. meeting Tomo in hospital and receiving permission to cry rather than continuously perform reassurance;
6. friendship with Tomo, discovery of ordinary-world desires, Hoshimi Festival, and the `やってみたかったリスト` practice;
7. Tomo's decline/death and Sakura's initial vow to accomplish desires “for Tomo”;
8. successful heart transplant, difficult rehabilitation, and formation of a heartbeat-as-encouragement/intuition practice before Sakura knows Mana's donor identity;
9. visit to Tomo's grave and renewed commitment to living broadly;
10. heartbeat-led encounter with Haruko at Mana's grave, where Sakura asks Haruko not to quit;
11. receipt of Tomo's notebook, which explicitly anticipates and rejects Sakura's impulse to complete Tomo's wishes by proxy, telling Sakura to pursue her own desires;
12. heartbeat-led arrival at Hoshimi and entry into the idol project;
13. rapid professional rise alongside a Mana-like voice whose origin Sakura does not yet understand;
14. formation of SUNNY PEACE and developing living bonds with Haruko, Rei, Chisa, Shizuku, Makino, and Kotono;
15. public discourse increasingly identifies Sakura as “Mana's return/reappearance,” creating gratitude, pride, discomfort, and identity anxiety;
16. Sakura questions whether the post-transplant voice, impulses, and idol life are truly “hers” or signs that the person before surgery has been replaced;
17. discovery that the transplanted heart belonged to Mana;
18. initial tendency to convert provenance into responsibility: if it is Mana's heart, Sakura reasons that she should sing Mana's unfinished song and carry the obligation correctly;
19. Mana explicitly rejects that proxy logic and says her song belongs to her alone; Sakura must find a song only Sakura can sing;
20. Sakura reframes idolhood as something the heart may have helped her discover but which is now **her own desired life**;
21. anime endpoint consolidates this into `私自身のドキドキ`: inherited history remains present without possessing the present self;
22. living rivalry with Kotono and unit differentiation displace the earlier successor/reappearance frame;
23. Tokyo-era SUNNY PEACE develops an explicit reciprocal audience philosophy: receive fans' feelings, return them through song, then receive again;
24. BIG4 recognition increases obligation and turns Sakura's public brightness into pressure to meet expectations continuously;
25. overwork collapse exposes the danger of a one-center responsibility model;
26. SUNNY PEACE redistributes responsibility across five members and teaches Sakura to receive support rather than only provide it;
27. Sakura nevertheless later faces continuing health strain during the VENUS Grand Prix and refuses simple withdrawal because mortality-origin urgency makes lost opportunity morally salient to her;
28. Rui challenges that present urgency by insisting Sakura also owes her future self the possibility of continued idolhood;
29. Makino, SUNNY PEACE, physician, and staff construct a risk-aware supported-performance arrangement rather than treating either total prohibition or unrestricted choice as sufficient;
30. Sakura enters the semifinal understanding the unit as five mutually supporting pieces and the audience as a source of strength;
31. SUNNY PEACE loses narrowly, but Sakura can experience the loss as painful **and** meaningful because she fully performed the chosen stage;
32. recovery time follows; loss is not narratively converted into personal invalidation;
33. mature horizon becomes continued idolhood for as long as `ドキドキ` and `ワクワク` remain—an open-ended future rather than a single succession task inherited from Mana.

## 2.2 Disclosure order

The audience receives this causal history in a different sequence.

The anime deliberately begins with mystery:

- unexplained heartbeat/intuition;
- Mana-like vocal resemblance;
- surgical scar;
- heart surgery;
- transplant;
- donor identity;
- then the ethical question of what provenance means.

The game Hoshimi retelling front-loads transplant information relative to the anime. The later SUNNY PEACE origin goes further backward still, revealing Tomo, childhood mortality, family burden, the origin of the wish list, and heartbeat trust before donor knowledge.

Therefore the later origin may **recontextualize** Sakura's smile and heartbeat, but it cannot be used to claim that an early anime viewer should already have interpreted those signs as transplant-related or mortality-shaped.

The later origin also produces a crucial retrospective result: Sakura's anti-substitution problem begins before she encounters Mana as a donor identity. Tomo has already made Sakura confront whether loving the dead means living the dead person's deferred life. This does not erase the Mana arc; it reveals that the Mana arc is a second, larger iteration of an already formative moral problem.

# 3. Executive longitudinal model

The strongest P2-B1 formulation is:

> **Sakura is a survivor who repeatedly has to convert inherited life into self-authored life.**

Her defining problem is not simply “Who am I if I have Mana's heart?” It begins much earlier. Childhood illness teaches Sakura that the continued happiness of people she loves may depend on how well she manages her own fear. She sees her mother cry and decides to smile; she sees her parents labor and decides to be an undemanding “good child.” Smiling therefore begins partly as a care strategy with a hidden cost: it can make Sakura's distress less legible precisely because she is using brightness to protect other people from it.

Tomo alters that structure. She recognizes that Sakura is about to cry even while Sakura insists she is smiling, and tells her she is allowed to cry. This is the first major correction to Sakura's self-conception: **care does not require emotional self-erasure**. Yet Tomo's death creates another danger. Sakura initially interprets survival through substitution—she will fulfill Tomo's desires for her. The later-delivered notebook directly anticipates that impulse and tells Sakura not to do it: these were Tomo's desires; Sakura must fulfill her own. The friend she loses therefore gives Sakura not merely a bucket list, but an anti-proxy ethic.

The heart transplant adds a second inheritance layer. During painful rehabilitation Sakura hears the heartbeat as encouragement and begins to trust `胸のドキドキ` before she knows anything about Mana as donor. This means the heartbeat is not reducible to conscious Mana imitation. It functions first as a survivor's embodied interpretive practice: a way to let the living body authorize continued action when fear and exhaustion would otherwise close the future.

That practice becomes more complicated once Sakura becomes an idol. She acquires a Mana-like voice, follows impulses that produce startlingly successful outcomes, and is publicly named the dead star's return. What initially feels like rebirth starts to threaten self-continuity: if the voice came after transplant, if the heart points her toward Hoshimi, and if the public hears Mana, is the current Sakura really Sakura? The answer the story builds is not bodily purification. Sakura never needs to deny that Mana's heart materially shapes her present. Instead, she has to distinguish **provenance from jurisdiction**.

The decisive Hoshimi movement makes this distinction explicit. Sakura initially tries to turn donor provenance into responsibility: if the heart is Mana's, perhaps she should responsibly sing Mana's unfinished song. Mana rejects the premise. Her regret cannot be repaired by another person reproducing her act. `私の歌は……私だけのものだから`. Sakura is required to find `あなたしか歌えない歌`. This reproduces, at larger scale, Tomo's earlier instruction: **the dead may remain loved, remembered, and causally important without becoming authors of the survivor's future**.

Sakura's “sun” identity is then best understood as a re-authored form of the same childhood trait. Smiling is no longer merely “I must look okay so my mother will not cry.” Tomo tells Sakura her smile genuinely helped another person; Sakura's mother tells her Sakura's healthy smile is already a source of happiness. Hoshimi then turns that interpersonal power into a vocation. But the mature text refuses one-way radiance. Tokyo SUNNY PEACE defines idolhood as a loop: receive fans' feelings, return them in song, receive again. Stellar goes further: Sakura calls the audience **her sun**. She gives light because she is also illuminated.

This reciprocal model is threatened by success. BIG4 status teaches Sakura to experience recognition as obligation: top idols must keep winning, meet expectations, and remain available. Her overwork collapse shows how easily the childhood “good child” logic can return in professional form. Once again Sakura tries to make her own burden invisible so other people will not be disappointed.

SUNNY PEACE's answer is constitutional rather than merely emotional. The group refuses the premise that Sakura alone must carry the audience. The other four can cover, rest can be legitimate, and `サニーピースは五人で一つ`. When the four themselves begin overworking to compensate, Makino extends the same rule to them. The mature Sunny relation is therefore **distributed answerability**: everyone can support and be supported; no one person's brightness creates unlimited bodily debt.

Stellar deliberately refuses to make that lesson perfectly linear. Sakura later risks performance while still medically vulnerable. Her defense is not shallow competitiveness. It is the adult continuation of the mortality-origin wish-list ethic: `未来がどうなるかなんて誰にも分からない`, so she wants to live fully now and avoid discovering later that she surrendered an irreplaceable opportunity. Rui's objection is equally serious: destroying future capacity in the name of present authorship can become another form of self-dispossession. The story's negotiated answer—medical clearance, physician availability, informed staff, member support, Makino's monitoring—does not prove Sakura's decision universally right. It shows the work moving from **unbounded heroic choice** toward **supported risk under answerable conditions**.

The semifinal loss completes an important transition. Sakura still hates losing, but the loss does not invalidate the stage. She can say she is frustrated and simultaneously feel clear because she gave everything she meant to give. Meaningful success becomes separable from rank. This is crucial for a character whose childhood survival, public resemblance to Mana, and BIG4 status repeatedly tempt her to turn outcomes into proof that her continued life is being used correctly.

Her mature horizon is therefore neither “complete Mana's unfinished work” nor “burn as brightly as possible before time runs out.” It is more difficult:

> **Live as Sakura, desire expansively, accept that the body and other people have claims that must be answered rather than erased, and keep transforming inherited life into relationships and performances whose meaning belongs to the living present.**

# 6. Smile/radiance longitudinal ledger inside Sakura

## 6.1 Stage 1 — smile as reassurance

Child Sakura smiles because another person's distress feels causally connected to her own illness. The smile is an attempt to manage the emotional environment.

## 6.2 Stage 2 — vulnerability authorized

Tomo teaches her that smiling is not mandatory. Sakura may cry without becoming cruel or burdensome.

## 6.3 Stage 3 — smile discovered as genuine relational power

Tomo later writes that Sakura's smile genuinely helped her and that friendship made her happy. Sakura's mother likewise says Sakura's healthy smile is already a great happiness.

The smile therefore stops being only concealment. Sakura learns that an authentic expression of joy can actually alter another person's experience.

## 6.4 Stage 4 — smile as vocation

Sakura converts that interpersonal discovery into idol aspiration: if her smile can make people happy, performance can scale the relation.

## 6.5 Stage 5 — smile threatened by status obligation

BIG4 status makes the relation coercive again. Sakura starts reasoning that top idols must keep meeting expectations and cannot merely enjoy one another's company. Her old logic returns in professional form:

> if I fail to remain visibly strong, other people will be disappointed because of me.

Her collapse is therefore not only a labor-management incident. It is a recurrence of the childhood pattern under commercial/public conditions.

## 6.6 Stage 6 — reciprocal radiance

SUNNY PEACE refuses to let Sakura remain the only source of light. Members tell her they can cover her, make her smile, and share the burden. Sakura realizes that with them she smiles naturally.

By the Stellar semifinal she formulates the relation directly: all five pieces support and influence one another. Haruko tells Sakura that the others support her and Sakura's smile supports them.

Then Sakura turns to the audience:

`応援してくれるみんなは、私の太陽だよ！`

This is the mature reversal. The “sun” can be illuminated.

### P2-B1 formulation

> **Sakura's radiance is mature only when it is reciprocal. A smile ceases to be a healthy gift when the smiling person is denied the right to receive care, rest, grief, or light in return.**

This strongly supports the series-level `DIFFERENTIATED_RECIPROCITY` / `answerable interdependence` architecture without making Sakura identical to every other case.

# 7. Audience philosophy and professional burden

## `IP-CHAR-SKR-021` — TOKYO RECIPROCAL AUDIENCE MODEL

In `st-main-cmn-01-02-36`, SUNNY PEACE collectively articulates what idolhood means to them.

Chisa begins from full enjoyment of the stage. Haruko rejects two one-way models: it is neither merely the idols enjoying themselves nor merely the idols entertaining fans. Shizuku supplies the circulation mechanism:

- receive fan feeling;
- put it into song and return it;
- receive the feeling that comes back;
- sing again.

Rei describes the pieces of the five members and each individual audience member joining into expanding bonds. Sakura names that as their answer to what idolhood is and what they want to accomplish.

The mature SUNNY PEACE audience ethic is therefore **reciprocal circulation**, not service-provider unilateralism.

This matters for Sakura because her childhood care grammar tends toward one-way responsibility. The unit's philosophy supplies an explicit correction: the audience is not only a population Sakura owes happiness. Audience response is one of the things that sustains Sakura.

## `IP-CHAR-SKR-022` — BIG4 AND RECOGNITION-AS-DEBT

Stellar reveals that status can corrupt this reciprocal relation.

After BIG4 recognition Sakura says she had pursued idol work wanting to make everyone happy, but now believes that is not enough. BIG4 idols must remain aspirational, continue meeting expectations, and keep winning against other hard-working performers.

The important word is not merely “pressure.” It is **debt** in the psychological sense: recognition starts to feel like a claim that must continuously be repaid through availability and success.

That belief explains why Sakura experiences rest as morally suspicious even after physical collapse. A day not used to become more worthy of BIG4 status feels like a day taken from people who trusted her.

This should not be confused with cynicism. Sakura genuinely values the audience and competition. The danger comes from turning that value into unlimited jurisdiction over the body.

## `IP-CHAR-SKR-024` — MANA'S `一期一会` ETHIC REAPPEARS

The strongest textual inheritance from Mana in Stellar is not voice or heart.

After Sakura collapses from overwork, she argues that she should still appear because:

# 9. Stellar risk conflict: present authorship versus future authorship

## 9.1 The medical fact pattern

By `stellar_010`, Sakura has experienced continuing poor condition following the earlier collapse. The physician says the situation is not immediately catastrophic if she rests and avoids overexertion, but warns that another collapse could, in the worst case, force her to stop idol activity.

The risk is therefore serious but should not be inflated.

P2-B1 **rejects** historical wording that the semifinal literally placed Sakura on the verge of death. The primary source supports potential career loss and bodily harm, not demonstrated imminent mortality.

## 9.2 Sakura's claim

Sakura refuses simple postponement. Rui proposes that they could compete at the next Grand Prix. Sakura answers that nobody knows what the future will bring; perhaps she will no longer be an idol. Therefore she wants to live fully now, do what she wants to do, and avoid later regret.

The origin makes this claim much harder to dismiss as teenage impatience. It is the same value structure encoded in the wish list.

Sakura's position is:

> **a future preserved by surrendering every irreplaceable present may cease to be the future I was trying to preserve.**

## 9.3 Rui's claim

Rui's objection is equally serious:

> **a present chosen by destroying the capacity to choose future presents may cease to be self-authorship and become self-cancellation.**

Rui wants Sakura to remain an extraordinary idol. Her intervention is not an attempt to win by default; it is a refusal to let Sakura's current desire monopolize the value of Sakura's future person.

## 9.4 Why this remains ethically unresolved

Neither side possesses a complete answer.

- Sakura is correct that opportunities are nonfungible.
- Rui is correct that future agency has moral standing.
- Makino is correct that professional care cannot simply abandon the decision to Sakura when material risk is known.
- Makino would also be wrong to confiscate all agency merely because risk exists.

This is one of the series' clearest cases of **answerable autonomy under risk**.

# 10. `IP-CHAR-SKR-029` — RISK-AWARE CO-AUTHORSHIP, NOT SIMPLE PERMISSION

The eventual semifinal decision is important because the source does not represent it as “Sakura insists, therefore she performs.”

Before the stage:

- Makino speaks with the treating physician;
- Sakura receives current-condition clearance;
- emergency response from the physician is arranged;
- staff are informed;
- SUNNY PEACE members commit to support Sakura;
- Makino explicitly promises to support her performance;
- prior unit history establishes the request that he **watch and support** so their own commitment does not become uncontrolled overwork.

This does not prove the choice was optimally safe. It changes the ethical category.

The best current classification is:

> **RISK-AWARE CO-AUTHORSHIP** — a person retains meaningful authorship of a risky goal while care institutions make the risk legible, add safeguards, retain monitoring, and preserve intervention capacity.

This model should not be universalized mechanically. Different degrees of risk, impairment, age, available alternatives, and decision capacity can require different intervention thresholds.

# 11. `IP-CHAR-SKR-030` — “EVERY STAGE IS MY FINAL” AS AN OPEN MAXIM

Sakura tells Rui:

`全てのステージが、私にとってのファイナルだから`.

The line is emotionally continuous with childhood mortality and professionally continuous with Mana's `一期一会` ethic. It gives Sakura a powerful resistance to complacency: she treats each encounter as nonrepeatable rather than assuming a future audience can substitute for the present one.

But the maxim contains a sustainability paradox.

If every stage is literally entitled to the resource expenditure of a career finale, no long career can survive the ethic. A performer with finite recovery, time, and health cannot spend the future in full at every present.

P2-B1 therefore leaves the proposition **OPEN** at normative level.

A durable mature translation may be:

> **Treat every stage as nonfungible in attention and sincerity, not as an unlimited claim on bodily sacrifice.**

The corpus strongly moves in this direction through group support, rest, medical safeguards, and the later long-horizon continuation language, but Sakura never explicitly rewrites the maxim into that exact formula within the locked snapshot.

The Phase-1 E2-C1 event freeze identifies a later Sakura/Kotono sequence in which Haruko's earlier experience with Mana transmits a care technique to Sakura. Sakura creates a tonkatsu outing with Kotono without making confession/disclosure the price of the outing's value.

> **care may create room for disclosure without treating disclosure as the caregiver's entitlement.**

This suits Sakura's own history. Tomo's first successful care did not force Sakura to provide an account of herself before receiving comfort; Tomo noticed, gave permission, and offered a place to cry.

Sakura later reproduces a softer version of the same grammar for Kotono: ordinary companionship can itself be care.

P2-B1 routes exact relationship-state implications to the future Sakura/Kotono ledger rather than inflating one event into a universal Sakura technique.

# 18. Long-horizon vocation after mortality urgency

The end of Stellar is important because Sakura does not say “I will keep going until I burn out” or “I completed what Mana/Tomo left.”

She says:

`胸のドキドキとワクワクがある限り これからもアイドルを続けていくよ`.

This is open-ended continuation.

The phrase preserves the body and excitement that have always animated Sakura while removing the dead person's unfinished task as endpoint. It is compatible with decades of vocation rather than a single sacrificial climax.

This does not resolve the risk paradox. Sakura still needs institutions and people capable of distinguishing intensity from self-cancellation. But the horizon has changed:

- from “I survived and therefore should accomplish the dead friend's wishes”;
- to “I was led into this path by inherited/embodied history”;
- to “this is what I want”;
- to “I will keep choosing it while the living relation of heartbeat and excitement remains.”

This is **ROLE CONTINUITY WITHOUT ROLE CAPTIVITY** in Sakura-specific form.

# 20. Revisions, downgrades, and rejected simplifications

## REJECT — “Sakura is just naturally cheerful”

Too shallow. She is genuinely exuberant, but childhood smiling also develops as protective labor and burden management.

## REJECT — “Sakura's smile is a fake mask”

Equally wrong. Tomo, Sakura's mother, SUNNY PEACE, and later audience relations establish authentic reciprocal joy. The smile has multiple genealogies/functions.

## REVISE — “Mana causes Sakura to become an idol”

Too strong. The heart/heartbeat participates in discovery and route entry, but Sakura later explicitly authors idolhood as her own desire. The origin also shows heartbeat trust before donor identity.

## REJECT — “Mana's heart means Sakura is partly Mana in the sense of inherited will”

The corpus does not establish a stable donor-will transfer mechanism. Bodily provenance is factual; metaphysical control remains open.

## REJECT — “Sakura should finish Mana's unfinished song because she has Mana's heart”

Directly contradicted by Mana, who says her song is hers alone and Sakura must sing Sakura's own.

## REVISE — “The Mana inheritance problem begins with Mana”

The later origin shows a precursor in Tomo's death and explicit anti-proxy notebook message.

## REJECT — “Tomo simply asked Sakura to fulfill her dreams”

Incomplete. Tomo's spoken late request supports proxy impulse, but the later-delivered written message specifically anticipates and rejects Sakura fulfilling Tomo's list instead of Sakura's own desires.

## REJECT — “Sakura's audience ethic is one-way altruism”

Tokyo and Stellar explicitly make it reciprocal. Fan feelings are received and returned; fans become Sakura's `太陽`.

## REVISE — “SUNNY PEACE protects Sakura by restraining her”

Too unilateral. The unit develops mutual support, and Makino's later role is to watch/support under safeguards. Protection is increasingly co-authored rather than simply imposed.

## DOWNGRADE — “Sakura's semifinal defeat saved her from death”

Primary text supports serious career/health risk, current medical clearance, and later recovery time. It does not establish imminent death.

## REJECT — “Because she had medical clearance, Sakura's semifinal choice is proven ethically correct”

Clearance and safeguards change the risk structure; they do not make the normative conflict disappear.

## REJECT — “Because another collapse could end her career, Makino/Rui should simply override Sakura”

The story gives future authorship standing, but it also gives Sakura's present irreplaceable opportunity real value and constructs supported choice rather than pure confiscation.

## OPEN — `全てのステージが、私にとってのファイナル` as durable professional doctrine

The maxim is narratively powerful but requires institutional translation if it is not to consume the career it motivates.

## OPEN — exact heart / ghost / miracle mechanism

Narrative associations are strong. Mechanism remains deliberately or evidentially underdetermined.

# 21. Longitudinal synthesis by analytical axis

## 21.1 Selfhood

Sakura's selfhood develops through incorporation rather than purification.

She does not become autonomous by proving that nothing in her came from other people. Quite the opposite:

- parents materially sustain her;
- Tomo changes her emotional grammar;
- another person's heart sustains her body;
- Mana's history changes the meaning of that organ;
- Haruko helps carry her into Hoshimi history;
- Makino and Hoshimi create professional conditions;
- SUNNY PEACE changes how she can bear responsibility;
- rivals give form to goals;
- audiences give strength.

The question is never whether Sakura is causally independent. The question is whether those causes become **inputs to self-authorship or claims of ownership over her**.

## 21.2 Survival

Survival begins as emergency and becomes ethical temptation.

A survivor can feel that being alive “instead of” someone else creates a duty to justify the allocation. Sakura experiences versions of this with Tomo and Mana. The series repeatedly rejects the strongest form of that debt.

Tomo does not want Sakura to become her executor. Mana does not want Sakura to become her replacement. The gift/debt distinction becomes one of Sakura's deepest themes.

## 21.3 Desire

The wish list is Sakura's recurring mechanism for naming desire before obligation captures it.

This makes her later `やりたいことは全部やりたい` more than impulsiveness. Desire itself is a survival achievement for a child who once structured behavior around being easy for adults to care for.

But self-authored desire is not automatically self-justifying. Stellar forces Sakura to answer how desire relates to bodily limits and future agency.

## 21.4 Care

Sakura begins by caring through concealment: smile so mother does not worry.

Tomo teaches care through permission: you may cry.

SUNNY PEACE teaches care through redistribution: you may stop; we can carry; then we also must stop when carrying becomes self-destructive.

Makino/Sakura bond evidence shows care through concrete maintenance: eat real food.

Later peer evidence shows care through non-extractive companionship: disclosure is possible but not owed as payment.

The longitudinal movement is from **self-erasing care** toward **reciprocal, bounded care**.

## 21.5 Audience

The audience begins as a population Sakura wants to make happy. Status then turns that desire into pressure. Tokyo makes the relation reciprocal; Stellar makes the bodily stakes explicit.

The mature audience question is therefore not “do idols owe fans?” It is:

> **What kind of audience obligation can remain meaningful without converting gratitude and one-time encounter into unlimited bodily availability?**

Sakura is one of the series' strongest test cases for that question.

## 21.6 Competition

Sakura is not anti-ranking. She wants to win, wants SUNNY PEACE at the top, and wants strong rivals.

Her maturation lies in learning that:

- rivalry can be relationship rather than enemy elimination;
- defeat can preserve value;
- rank can motivate without becoming a total verdict on self;
- competition becomes richer when the opponent is preserved as someone worth meeting again.

## 21.7 The body

Sakura's body is not a transparent vessel.

It contains:

- congenital illness;
- transplanted organ;
- recovered capability;
- voice change;
- heartbeat sensation;
- exhaustion;
- professional training;
- risk;
- pleasure and excitement.

The body can carry history without containing a simple textual instruction. This is why “provenance without jurisdiction” remains the best formula.

# 24. P2-B1 master thesis

> **Kawasaki Sakura's development is best understood as the repeated conversion of survival into self-authored reciprocity. Childhood heart disease first teaches her to hide fear so loved ones will not suffer, while Tomo teaches her that she may cry, gives her a mortality-shaped practice of naming desires, and ultimately rejects the idea that Sakura should live Tomo's deferred life. The transplant then makes inheritance literal: Sakura learns to trust her heartbeat before knowing Mana's identity, later confronts a public and private world eager to read Mana's voice, will, and unfinished work through her, and initially mistakes gratitude for mandate. Mana herself refuses that substitution. Sakura therefore does not mature by purging the inherited heart, but by making its history part of a life whose desire is now hers. Her smile undergoes the same transformation: from protective emotional labor, to witnessed vulnerability, to chosen relational gift, to idol vocation, and finally to reciprocal radiance in which companions and fans can illuminate the 'sun' in return. Success threatens this balance when BIG4 recognition and Mana's one-chance audience ethic convert care into bodily debt. SUNNY PEACE answers by distributing burden across five people, while Stellar refuses an easy endpoint and forces Sakura's mortality-born present urgency to answer the future self Rui and Makino are trying to preserve. Supported medical risk, meaningful defeat, and the later promise to continue idolhood while `ドキドキ` and `ワクワク` remain leave Sakura neither a sacrificial successor nor a protected child. She is a living person whose inherited body, dead relationships, audience, rivals, manager, and unit all matter profoundly without any of them acquiring final jurisdiction over who she must become.**

This is the **Phase-2 ledger formulation**, not the final polished Phase-3 Sakura monograph.
