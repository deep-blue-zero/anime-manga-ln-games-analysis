The strongest longitudinal formulation is:

> **`PRIDEFUL_COAUTHORSHIP_WITHOUT_FORM_CAPTIVITY_LIZNOIR`: LizNoir begins when two highly capable but differently incomplete performers choose one another. Rio brings discipline, weight, economic urgency, benchmark hunger, and the need to make professional worth undeniable; Aoi brings embodied freedom and the ability to detect when correct movement has stopped carrying a lived person. Their original partnership creates a powerful elite unit because difference makes each member's unrecognized self legible. Mana then gives their excellence a worthy external test and turns competition into vocation, but her death freezes the test and helps convert result pressure into a structure that can overtake the living relationship. Protective secrecy, injury concealment, and the dissolution threat reveal that even devoted partners can damage one another when care controls information and a result is allowed to monopolize futurity. The loss to Tsuki breaks that monopoly without reducing the desire to win. The founders nevertheless commit a second category error when they conclude that preserving LizNoir means preserving the dyad itself. Ai and Kokoro enter with real technical deficits and founder reverence; they do not become legitimate because the unit lowers its standard. New York instead reveals that the founders' own optimized precision has become insufficiently human: correct execution is no longer reliably transmitting desire, risk, or affect. The quartet becomes real when technical excellence and communicability are recombined, and later when the juniors can criticize, propose, carry, and be relied upon. BIG4 then turns the identity problem inward. Rio risks making rank and leader-duty sovereign over self-recognition; Aoi risks making fidelity sovereign over change. Neither position survives intact. Rio must say that she personally wants the stage and summit; Aoi must admit that the self she is trying to preserve has already changed through attachment; Ai and Kokoro must stop treating service to the founders as a sufficient reason to stand inside LizNoir. The mature invariant is therefore not a two-person roster, one choreographic vocabulary, a no-smile rule, or one ranking position. It is the four members' prideful authored participation in continuing struggle. From there the unit can preserve founder specialness without founder monopoly, keep hierarchy without unilateral jurisdiction, make center a contestable office, negotiate client work without surrendering self-recognition, let juniors initiate choreography, let Aoi lead creation and then ask the others for help, and accept that the best performance of their history may still lose. By the locked endpoint, LizNoir is exact-four because these four particular people have jointly accumulated the authority to define and revise what LizNoir is. Pride is more stable than form precisely because pride belongs to living coauthors rather than to an inherited silhouette.**

> **LizNoir survives by learning that its standard can remain severe while its form remains revisable.**

The deepest paradox is:

> **The founders preserve what was singular about original LizNoir only by giving up exclusive ownership of LizNoir's future.**

A second paradox is:

# 4. Founding constitution — excellence begins as productive difference

The first LizNoir is not created by selecting two people who already embody one finished doctrine. Saegusa places two very different relations to performance into contact.

Rio has technical discipline, urgency, and a material reason to make success undeniable. Aoi has extraordinary bodily fluency but comparatively little interest in professional status for its own sake. When Aoi watches Rio dance, she does not identify a missing technical skill. She identifies a failure of lived transmission: Rio looks as if she is not enjoying dance.

That diagnosis matters because Aoi's later role is sometimes reduced to “the free one” who softens Rio's severity. The origin is stronger. Aoi identifies a professional truth that the unit will repeatedly have to rediscover:

> **correct execution is not yet successful expression if no recognizable living desire passes through it.**

Rio then deliberately brackets debut pressure and dances to enjoy movement. Aoi joins. Aoi subsequently asks to dance with Rio specifically. Their partnership is therefore partly institutional in origin but becomes mutually selected.

This establishes the first LizNoir constitution:

- **Rio** — structure, ambition, weight, explicit professional horizon;
- **Aoi** — bodily self-recognition, expressive freedom, direct correction of deadened form;
- **the dyad** — a system in which difference can return a person to something she cannot see alone.

The Rio/Aoi P2-C ledger later names this `CHOSEN_PARTNERSHIP_THROUGH_ANSWERABLE_DIFFERENCE_RIO_AOI`. P2-D preserves that as founding truth while rejecting the inference that founding truth grants permanent exclusive ownership of unit ontology.

# 5. Mana rivalry — excellence acquires an ethical and vocational object

Mana is not merely a higher score. She fuses qualities on which the founders have been building LizNoir: skill, freedom, presence, and communicability.

For Rio, the contest becomes proof worth wanting because Mana is genuinely difficult to beat. For Aoi, who had previously cared little about rank, Mana produces the first explicit desire to win. Competition becomes relational rather than abstract.

The ethical floor matters just as much as the ambition. When sabotage through scandal is proposed, Rio refuses:

> `LizNoirは正々堂々と、彼女と戦って勝ちます`

This does not make LizNoir gentle. It makes the result meaningful. Victory must still answer the question posed by the opponent's actual performance.

That principle survives later sources. The unit can be proud, provocative, severe, and tactically opportunistic without treating offstage sabotage as an acceptable substitute for performance.

The 2023 event provides a useful stress test. When another production side attempts to sabotage LizNoir's shoes with lubricant, the quartet recognizes the tactic as malicious and physically dangerous. Rio declines to weaponize the discovery for retaliation; Aoi returns the dropped evidence with an explicit request to compete `正々堂々`. LizNoir answers the challenge onstage.

The unit's competitive aggression therefore has a boundary:

> **the opponent should remain capable of answering back as an opponent.**

# 6. Mana's death — rivalry becomes frozen time

A living rival can change the relation. She can improve, lose, win, answer, disappoint, or demand another rematch. Mana's death removes those possibilities immediately before the decisive contest.

Rio's later fixation therefore does not simply increase the intensity of healthy rivalry. It changes its temporal structure. The benchmark can no longer answer.

Rio leaves the stage. Aoi waits. When candidate replacements are offered, Aoi refuses them. That refusal is not evidence that LizNoir must forever be only two people. At this point in chronology it establishes something narrower and more important: Aoi's freedom has become **particular fidelity**. She will not force Rio to return, but she will not rewrite her own attachment merely because Rio is absent.

Rio eventually returns and the future grammar shifts from isolated obligation toward a shared future. Yet the dead-rival problem is not fully solved. The pair now organizes enormous pressure around winning enough to defeat an opponent who can no longer actually enter the rematch.

This is the first place where LizNoir's strength becomes vulnerable to **futurity capture**: one contest threatens to monopolize what all subsequent contests mean.

# 7. Protective secrecy — partnership fails when care controls reality

The dissolve-if-lose condition and Aoi's injury expose a constitutional weakness inside the original dyad.

Aoi genuinely wants to protect Rio. Rio is already carrying Mana, family responsibility, and extreme performance pressure. Aoi therefore withholds additional information. She also withholds her own injury.

The motive is care. The method is unilateral jurisdiction.

Rio's anger is grounded in partnership: if they are partners, one member should not decide which burdens the other is permitted to know exist. Aoi's protective logic also produces a dangerous causal fantasy—if she can hide enough, endure enough, and perform well enough, she can personally secure Rio's future.

The source does not resolve this by saying all privacy is wrong. It resolves the narrower problem:

> **a shared professional reality cannot be safely managed when one member privately edits the other member's decision environment.**

This becomes a precursor to mature exact-four burden-sharing. Later, being relied upon is treated as a sign of trusted standing rather than a failure of the senior to protect the junior.

# 8. Tsuki defeat — result loses its monopoly over meaning

The original dyad loses to Tsuki no Tempest.

The result matters. Aoi grieves. Rio grieves. The dissolution condition appears to make the loss terminal. Yet the emotional conclusion directly contradicts result sovereignty.

Aoi tries to isolate responsibility in her injury. Rio refuses. Rio says Aoi is part of why she became the idol she became and that being Aoi's partner was happiness.

This is a foundational result-theory transition:

> **a loss can be professionally decisive without retroactively declaring that the partnership, work, or career leading to it was false.**

Rio and Aoi do not become less competitive. What breaks is the assumption that one result has jurisdiction over all prior meaning.

That principle later scales from dyad to quartet.

# 9. Founder exclusivity — a historically real but superseded ontology

After the loss, Rio and Aoi sincerely treat LizNoir as the two of them. The proposed solution is not “find new members.” It is dissolution.

This cannot be erased as mere stubbornness because mature analysis prefers the quartet. Founder exclusivity is part of LizNoir's actual history.

When Asakura proposes Ai and Kokoro, the founders' objection has at least three layers:

1. the juniors are not yet at the founders' technical level;
2. the founders understand the existing dyad as the unit's identity;
3. they fear that changing membership would turn LizNoir into something else rather than repair it.

Rio's `LizNoirに笑顔はいりません` belongs to that moment. It should not be promoted into an eternal unit doctrine.

The deeper developmental error is not strictness. It is **identifying inherited form with invariant identity**.

---

# 10. Successor probation — real asymmetry without permanent inferiority

Ai and Kokoro do not enter as equals in every professional dimension.

They have real technical gaps. Founder criticism is therefore not wholly prejudicial. Ai's insecurity and Kokoro's swagger also produce different distortions: Ai interprets correction as possible proof of unworthiness; Kokoro sometimes assumes charisma, smiles, or audience instinct can compensate for too much.

The mature quartet is not earned by pretending these differences never existed.

Instead, succession works because the juniors eventually contribute functions the founders do not already contain in sufficient form:

- Ai's visible emotional transmission;
- Kokoro's audience accessibility, publicity instinct, and aggressive self-authorship;
- both juniors' willingness to articulate care and belonging in ways the founders often leave implicit;
- later, their willingness to propose choreography, carry center pressure, question leader motives, and accept responsibility for LizNoir's future.

Their legitimacy is therefore developmental and productive—not charitable admission into a founder-owned institution.

---

# 11. New York — machine perfection fails as communication

The New York material is the decisive correction to the simplistic “smiles versus perfection” reading.

The four can execute. The problem is that the founders' model has become overoptimized enough to resemble a machine. The source explicitly reaches:

> `私達はロボットじゃない`

The corrective is not to abandon technique. It is to remember what technique is for.

Performance must carry recognizable human intention across a boundary. The American setting makes that problem unusually visible because linguistic familiarity cannot do all the work. Rhythm, movement, emotional exposure, and the willingness to risk imperfect transmission become central.

Aoi's later formulation is equally important:

> `失敗したっていい。無様でもいい。僕達は挑戦者だ`

For a unit previously organized around proving superiority, accepting the possibility of visible failure is constitutional change. It restores risk to an elite system that had begun treating flawlessness as identity.

Rio then names a real restart:

> `今から、本当の意味での新生LizNoirよ`

The point is not that four members are automatically better than two. It is that these four can now build a system in which excellence has more channels through which to transmit a living person.

# 12. Exact-four reconstruction — succession becomes coauthorship

Four-person LizNoir becomes mature only after “successor” ceases to mean permanent follower.

> `ただの言い訳だったね`

The issue is not that gratitude is false. The issue is that gratitude can become a shelter from authorship.

The juniors' mature demand is stronger:

# 13. Institutional rupture — BanPro can disappear without taking LizNoir with it

- **agency matters materially** — contracts, management, bookings, protection, money, infrastructure;

# 15. BIG4 fracture — two legitimate values become absolutized

The BIG4 crisis is not a morality play in which Rio becomes “wrongly ambitious” and Aoi becomes “authentic.”

Aoi sees the unit adapting to popularity/support mechanics and fears that `LizNoirらしさ` will be discarded. Her error is not caring about self-recognition. It is beginning to equate fidelity with preserving a recognizable prior form.

The conflict is therefore constitutional:

- **Rio risks metric capture**;
- **Aoi risks form capture**.

The resolution must revise both sides.

# 18. Mature invariant — pride is more stable than form

The origin tranche gives the strongest explicit definition of mature LizNoir-ness.

Ai says:

> `私達は四人でLizNoirだから`

Kokoro then offers the unit invariant:

> `私達四人それぞれが誇りを抱いて進み、戦い続けること`

and identifies this as the strongest `リズノワらしさ`.

This formulation is analytically powerful because it does not erase difference.

It does not say all four must dance the same way, smile the same amount, want the same thing, possess equal experience, or occupy identical authority. It says each carries pride and continues the struggle.

“Pride” here means more than confidence. It includes:

- professional standards;
- refusal to become a passive instrument;
- willingness to answer for one's own performance;
- willingness to challenge another member;
- willingness to remain inside the unit after conflict;
- willingness to let unit form change without treating change as identity death.

That is why the mature unit can be exact-four without making fixed form sovereign.

# 19. Founder specialness without founder monopoly

Rio/Aoi do not become ordinary interchangeable members.

The founding pair remains historically and relationally privileged. Each can identify when the other has become estranged from her own desire. Their private history, bodily knowledge, and ability to return one another to suppressed selves remain distinctive.

But the exact-four corpus rejects the inference that specialness must equal monopoly.

Ai/Kokoro can interrupt founder nostalgia and demand inclusion. They can ask the founders to rely on them. They can carry the unit during founder fracture. They can propose choreography. Kokoro can be given contingent center authority. Ai can formulate the quartet's exact-four miracle. Aoi can lead creation and ask all three for help.

The mature constitutional formula is therefore:

> **privileged founding partnership + nonexclusive unit belonging.**

This directly inherits the Rio/Aoi P2-C category while scaling it to unit ontology.

This does **not** abolish hierarchy. Rio remains the established leader and a highly proven center-capable performer. Aoi remains an experienced founder. The juniors remain later entrants with distinct experience levels.

What changes is jurisdiction:

> **role authority must remain answerable to performance and circumstance rather than lineage.**

Center is therefore a contestable professional office, not founder property.

# 21. Fair competition — hostility without sabotage sovereignty

The unit then performs.

LizNoir's pride prefers a victory that still answers the opponent's actual capacity. A sabotaged opponent cannot supply the same proof.

# 22. Work, rest, and non-work attachment

The resulting experience then feeds creative work.

This does not turn LizNoir into a leisure-first unit. It establishes a healthier professional proposition:

> **experience that is not instrumentally scheduled as work can still sustain the people whose work depends on having something alive to express.**

This is consistent with New York's deeper lesson: craft becomes hollow when lived desire is treated as expendable.

# 23. Professional adaptation — neither obedience nor purity

`event_2024_005_st-eve-2405-race` stages LizNoir against an external creative brief that feels profoundly unlike its normal image.

The mature solution rejects both poles.

Rio explicitly argues that a one-way imposition cannot make good work. The performers must understand the client's intention. The client must understand what the performers can meaningfully express. They need a discussion until they can find an acceptable landing point.

Aoi accepts that change is sometimes necessary. Rio accepts that adaptation without self-recognition is insufficient. The final result combines client purpose with LizNoir's own participation.

The event therefore stabilizes:

> **`NEGOTIATED_ADAPTATION_WITHOUT_EXTERNAL_CAPTURE_LIZNOIR`.**

Professionalism is not obedience. Authenticity is not refusal. Authored adaptation requires negotiation.

Importantly, the event does not pretend all summit pressure is resolved. Aoi again asks how much of `僕達らしさ` they are prepared to discard for the top. Rio answers that she does not yet know.

The text leaves metric capture as a live maintenance problem.

She specifies the four-person differentiation: Rio like Rio, Aoi like Aoi, Ai like Ai, Kokoro like Kokoro.

Then, crucially, she asks the others for help.

Ai says being relied upon makes her happy. Rio tells Aoi not to hold back from relying on them. The target becomes:

> **finish the best LizNoir-like choreography together.**

This is mature coauthorship in practice.

# 27. Rivalry — worthy opponents must preserve futurity

LizNoir's strongest rivalries generate more future rather than less.

Healthy form:

- Mana before death — a living opponent who makes winning meaningful;
- TRINITYAiLE — a challenger whose full strength is desired because partial victory would be less meaningful;
- Tsuki and SUNNY PEACE — opponents whose victories generate rematch desire and higher standards;
- later world ambition — an open horizon rather than a single terminal proof.

Pathological form:

- Mana after death when the impossible rematch is allowed to monopolize Rio's future;
- any rule that treats one defeat as retroactive proof that the unit or career was meaningless;
- offstage sabotage that destroys the opponent's ability to answer.

The unit-level principle is therefore:

> **rivalry is healthiest when it preserves the rival as a future-bearing other rather than converting the rival into a sovereign judge or disposable obstacle.**
