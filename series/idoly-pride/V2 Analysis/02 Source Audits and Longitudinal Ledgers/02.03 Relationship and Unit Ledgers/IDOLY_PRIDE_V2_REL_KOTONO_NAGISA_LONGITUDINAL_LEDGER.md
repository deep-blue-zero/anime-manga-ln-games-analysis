# 0. Responsibility, authority, and the central problem

This ledger is the current Phase-2 relationship authority for **Nagase Kotono / Ibuki Nagisa** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`.

Its responsibility is narrower than either character ledger and broader than any single relationship card. It asks what the **relationship itself** becomes across time: how it forms, what each person risks for it, how Mana's fame and death change its function, how support becomes structurally asymmetric, why BIG4 separation is a developmental necessity rather than simple relational collapse, what is actually repaired, what remains unequal, what the late relationship language establishes, and exactly where romantic/yuri interpretation must stop.

The relationship is easy to flatten in several opposite directions:

- into “childhood best friends” whose closeness is static;
- into “Nagisa takes care of Kotono” as though the dyad is permanently caregiver/dependent;
- into a romance reading that converts strong Nagisa-side coding into mutual textual romance;
- into an anti-romance reading that treats explicit `親友` language as proof that romantic/yuri coding is absent;
- into a BIG4 “breakup and reunion” analogy that forgets the actual professional and constitutional stakes;
- into generic unit friendship that erases how exceptional the dyad is for each character;
- or into an exclusive two-person bond that erases the mature five-member Tsuki structure.

The complete frozen record supports none of those simplifications.

The strongest longitudinal formulation is:

> **`EARNED_ADJACENCY_THROUGH_DIFFERENTIATION_KOTONO_NAGISA`: Kotono and Nagisa begin as a relationship created through costly non-instrumental recognition; become highly asymmetric when Nagisa supplies continuity through Kotono's bereavement and initially organizes idolhood around supporting her; then survive a rupture in which Nagisa refuses privileged proximity precisely because remaining permanently behind Kotono would make their future less reciprocal; and mature into an explicit best-friend / teammate relation in which independent capacity, contestation, chosen reliance, and continued intense attachment can coexist.**

The relationship's deepest developmental paradox is:

> **they become more capable of remaining close when they become more capable of standing apart.**

That is not equivalent to romantic adjudication.

At the locked frontier:

- **TEXTUAL FACT:** they are teammates and explicitly `親友`;
- **TEXTUAL FACT:** Kotono grants Nagisa exceptional trust, private access, and repeated first-confidante status;
- **TEXTUAL FACT:** Nagisa's original idol motive substantially centers on supporting/remaining beside Kotono;
- **TEXTUAL FACT:** Kotono asks Nagisa alone to accompany her when she leaves Tsuki;
- **TEXTUAL FACT:** Nagisa refuses because she has obligations and an idol self beyond following Kotono, and because she wants eventually to stand beside rather than behind her;
- **TEXTUAL FACT:** Nagisa later defines her mature goal as mutual support and mutual illumination from the closest position;
- **TEXTUAL FACT:** fans publicly recognize the dyad as `ことなぎ`;
- **STRONG INFERENCE:** Nagisa's attachment is strongly yuri/romance-coded and exceeds generic friendship in affective and imaginative intensity;
- **OPEN / NOT ESTABLISHED:** Kotono-side reciprocal romantic attraction, mutual confession, dating, exclusivity, or a mutually agreed romantic future.

The governing relationship category therefore remains:

> **explicit best friendship + exceptional dyadic attachment + differentiated reciprocity + strong Nagisa-side romantic/yuri coding + mutual romance OPEN.**

## 2.2 Disclosure chronology

The audience does not learn the relationship in the same order that the relationship develops.

Early presentation makes Nagisa look like the naturally cheerful childhood friend attached to a severe and grieving Kotono. The later origin stories reverse that apparent simplicity:

- Nagisa actively **creates** the friendship through persistence;
- Kotono initially mistrusts her because previous “friends” were Mana-seeking intermediaries;
- friendship becomes credible because Nagisa wants Kotono herself;
- both accept social cost before Mana dies;
- Mana's request therefore recognizes an existing relation rather than creating a caretaker mandate.

The bereavement disclosure then deepens the pair's importance. Nagisa is not a replacement sister; she is a living relation that proves continued attachment does not betray the dead.

Later, BIG4 discloses that the very support structure that once helped Kotono survive can become developmentally constraining **for Nagisa** if it is allowed to remain her whole vocational identity. This does not retroactively make the earlier support unhealthy or false. It makes it historically insufficient as a permanent constitution.

C1-B then reveals the mature self-description only after the rupture has already happened. Nagisa can finally articulate that the answer is not distance from Kotono, but a different **position** relative to Kotono: not permanently behind, but beside.

# 3. Formation — non-instrumental recognition under conditions of distrust

## 3.1 Nagisa's first movement is fascination, not delegated care

The first direct formation source is unusually revealing. Nagisa notices that Kotono appears socially distant but also lonely. She is affected by Kotono's notebook illustration and internally compares the feeling it produces to `一目惚れ` before the story has established any friendship.

This does **not** by itself establish literal romantic love at first sight. The object of the immediate comparison is also the illustration and the warmth/love Nagisa senses inside it.

It does establish something stronger than neutral altruism:

> Nagisa's approach begins in aesthetic, emotional, and person-specific fascination.

The later `可愛い` fixation is already present at formation.

**Transition:** `STRENGTHEN`.

## 3.2 Kotono's initial rejection is evidence-bearing

Kotono's hostility is not just tsundere surface texture.

She has learned that peers may approach her as a route to Mana. When Nagisa persists, Kotono explicitly tests the instrumental hypothesis: does Nagisa want tickets, signatures, or access?

Nagisa does not.

That matters because the relationship's first achievement is not intimacy. It is **credibility**.

> Nagisa becomes trustworthy by remaining interested when Mana-mediated rewards are removed from the interaction.

Admit:

> **`NON_INSTRUMENTAL_RECOGNITION_KOTONO_NAGISA`**

This is one reason the bond remains unusually durable after Mana's death: its legitimacy was established against celebrity instrumentalization before bereavement made it high-stakes.

## 3.3 Friendship becomes costly before it becomes verbally settled

Classmates threaten Nagisa with ostracism for choosing Kotono. Nagisa continues anyway.

Kotono then hears the threat and intervenes. The crucial point is that Kotono's action comes before a neat mutual declaration. She tells the classmates, in substance:

> `私の友達を傷つけたら許さないから`

Kotono later recognizes that she had already come to think of Nagisa as a friend before Nagisa formally asked.

This gives the relationship a reciprocal founding condition:

- Nagisa pays a social cost by continuing to approach Kotono;
- Kotono pays a vulnerability/status cost by publicly claiming Nagisa.

Admit:

> **`COSTLY_MUTUAL_RECOGNITION_KOTONO_NAGISA`**

## 3.4 Mutuality exists before symmetry

Even in the formation period, roles are not identical.

Nagisa is more initiating, expressive, and socially mobile. Kotono is more guarded and grants access incrementally. The relationship becomes mutual without becoming temperamentally symmetrical.

This is the first version of the later `DIFFERENTIATED_RECIPROCITY` rule.

## 4.1 Mana recognizes the bond after it already exists

Mana meets Nagisa and is pleased that Kotono has a friend. She asks Nagisa to look after Kotono in the time Mana's career keeps her away.

This request can matter emotionally. It may intensify Nagisa's sense of responsibility.

> **Mana does not create the Kotono/Nagisa relationship and does not install Nagisa as Kotono's posthumous guardian.**

The friendship already contains:

- person-specific fascination;
- persistence;
- social cost;
- Kotono's public defense;
- reciprocal trust.

Admit:

> **`MANA_RECOGNITION_WITHOUT_RELATIONAL_DELEGATION`**

## 4.2 Nagisa is not a Mana replacement

The relationship must not be modeled as a substitution chain:

> Mana disappears -> Nagisa occupies Mana's place.

The source rejects this in several ways.

Nagisa does not share Mana's sister role, professional legacy role, or posthumous symbolic authority. Her power is different: she is present, living, peer-aged, contestable, and capable of being refused or refusing in return.

That distinction becomes crucial after bereavement.

# 5. Bereavement — presence without replacement

## 5.1 Daily persistence is high-stakes continuity work

After Mana's death, Kotono stops attending school and shuts herself in her room. Nagisa comes every day even when Kotono will not open the door.

The form of care matters:

- Nagisa does not force physical entry;
- she does not demand immediate disclosure;
- she continues ordinary narration from outside the room;
- she announces she will come back;
- contact is persistent without being framed as entitlement to instant recovery.

Kotono later states directly that Nagisa helped her recover and that she might have remained shut away without her.

Admit:

> **`PERSISTENT_PRESENCE_WITHOUT_FORCED_RECOVERY`**

## 5.2 The notebook intervention changes the ontology of “not alone”

The sister notebook originally contains a visual grammar of Mana and Kotono: one girl is joined by another, turning isolation into hand-holding continuity.

After Mana's death, Kotono keeps looking at the lonely figure while knowing Mana can no longer add the second girl.

Nagisa eventually adds **herself** beside Kotono's figure and writes:

> `いつまでも一緒だよ`

The scene is powerful because it does not delete the prior sister meaning. The notebook carries both histories.

Kotono's response is not “Nagisa replaces Mana.” It is:

> she had believed Mana's death meant she would be alone forever; Nagisa's intervention helps her realize that continuing relation with the living still exists.

Admit:

> **`LIVING_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_REPLACEMENT_KOTONO_NAGISA`**

This relationship therefore becomes one of the clearest local mechanisms for `LEGACY_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_RESTORATION`.

## 5.3 Care includes tolerance for misdirected anger

Kotono lashes out because feeling better can seem equivalent to forgetting Mana. She recognizes that she is using Nagisa's kindness as a safe place onto which to throw anger and despair.

This should not be romanticized.

The relationship's durability makes the behavior understandable; it does not make every use of the other person ethically neutral.

The important longitudinal point is that Kotono becomes afraid Nagisa will eventually leave—evidence that the living relationship has already become independently valuable.

# 6. From best friend to support-vocation — the first structural asymmetry

## 6.1 Nagisa's idol entry is substantially relational

Nagisa says during her idol interview that she wants to support Kotono from beside her. The later C1-B card retrospectively confirms the early state:

> when she became an idol, she thought primarily about supporting Kotono.

This is not a fandom inference. It is explicit self-description.

Admit:

> **`DYADIC_SUPPORT_VOCATION_NAGISA`**

## 6.2 The support-vocation is real and good — but insufficient as a permanent identity

It would be analytically convenient to call the support motive “codependence” and treat later individuation as correction of an unhealthy beginning.

The source does not support that simplification.

Nagisa's support:

- helps Kotono survive acute grief;
- supplies continuity without deciding Kotono's ultimate answer;
- enables practical idol functioning;
- is repeatedly welcomed by Kotono;
- sits inside a self-chosen friendship rather than a coercive duty.

The problem appears only when a role that was historically valuable threatens to become Nagisa's **entire** professional self-definition.

The correct transition is:

> **support is preserved; support captivity is rejected.**

## 6.3 Kotono's trust is unusually concentrated in Nagisa

Kotono's own origin gives the reciprocal side.

She says:

- she shows weak/embarrassing states to Nagisa that she would not show the others;
- she expects Nagisa to remain her friend regardless of what she sees;
- when she does not know why she is still an idol, Nagisa is the first person she wants to consult;
- retrospectively, `私の一番の理解者はいつだって渚だった`.

Admit:

> **`FIRST_CONFIDANTE_AND_FOREMOST_UNDERSTANDING_KOTONO_TO_NAGISA`**

This is stronger than ordinary teammate trust.

It is still not a textual romantic confession.

# 8. BIG4 departure — the dyad is both exception and test

## 8.1 Kotono's independence theory immediately makes Nagisa an exception

Kotono leaves because she comes to believe connection itself encourages `甘え` and blocks individual strength.

Yet before she can make the theory coherent, she invites Nagisa alone to come with her.

She says, in substance, that with Nagisa she can imagine:

- continuing together as two;
- criticizing/challenging one another;
- walking side by side.

This is extremely important relationship evidence.

Admit:

> **`NAGISA_AS_EXCEPTION_TO_KOTONO_ISOLATION_THEORY`**

The invitation establishes exceptional dyadic status.

It does **not** by itself establish romance. A best friend / foremost confidante can be uniquely invited into a professional break without that invitation being a confession.

## 8.2 The invitation is emotionally close but structurally regressive for Nagisa

Nagisa is genuinely delighted to be invited. The source does not portray refusal as easy.

But she now possesses claims on her own life that did not exist at idol entry:

- responsibility to four-person Tsuki;
- professional identity as an idol;
- an independent desire to shine;
- the wish eventually to stand **beside** Kotono rather than remain structurally behind her.

The invitation therefore offers emotional proximity at the price of preserving an old asymmetry.

## 8.3 Nagisa refuses proximity in order to preserve future adjacency

Nagisa's internal logic is unusually explicit:

- she wants to continue as an idol with Kotono;
- she wants them to remain together;
- but “if we want to stay together,” then **right now they cannot simply stay together**;
- in the existing structure she would only keep supporting Kotono from behind;
- if she wants to stand alongside Kotono, she must grow independently.

This is the relationship's decisive developmental act.

Admit:

> **`RELATION_PRESERVING_DIFFERENTIATION_KOTONO_NAGISA`**

and:

> **`PROXIMITY_REFUSAL_WITHOUT_ATTACHMENT_REFUSAL_NAGISA`**

## 8.4 The refusal corrects two people at once

For Nagisa, refusal prevents the dyad from swallowing her broader self.

For Kotono, refusal falsifies the assumption that loving/supportive relation must either be carried along as an exception or cut away as weakness. Nagisa becomes evidence that another person can remain deeply attached **and still refuse to follow**.

That is a more mature kind of support than compliance.

# 9. Separation — differentiation without relational evacuation

## 9.1 Separation produces real growth

The ledger must not claim that rupture was useless simply because Kotono's underlying theory was wrong.

Kotono develops genuine skills under DoriKyun. Nagisa and the other four develop genuine agency without Kotono.

The mature relation can only emerge because both sides return with more capacity.

## 9.2 Nagisa's attachment does not fade when autonomy increases

Nagisa continues to want a future with Kotono. Her independent growth is therefore not `Kotono離れ` in the sense of emotional evacuation.

This matters for relationship theory:

> **differentiation is not measured by how little another person matters.**

A person can become less role-captive while remaining intensely attached.

Admit:

> **`ATTACHMENT_PERSISTS_THROUGH_SELF_EXPANSION_NAGISA`**

# 10. Contestatory care — support acquires the right to oppose

## 10.1 Nagisa becomes able to challenge the person she most wants beside her

At the BIG4 climax, Nagisa asks Kotono whether DoriKyun's destructive victory model is truly what she wanted.

Her line:

> `私は、勝たなくてもいい`

is not generic pacifism. It is conditional: if winning requires crushing another idol's dream/future, Nagisa rejects that definition of strength.

The relationship significance is equally important.

Early support often takes the form:

> stay near -> soothe -> help Kotono continue.

Mature support can take the form:

> **tell Kotono that her chosen model is wrong even when doing so risks conflict.**

Admit:

> **`CONTESTATORY_CARE_KOTONO_NAGISA`**

## 10.2 Kotono can hear the challenge because Nagisa is not merely a follower anymore

Kotono's reversal is not produced by Nagisa alone; the four-person Tsuki and DoriKyun outcome supply broader evidence.

But Nagisa occupies a special position: she is the person who has known Kotono before idolhood, through grief, through proxy vocation, and through the departure.

When she challenges Kotono, the statement carries the authority of history without becoming jurisdiction over Kotono's life.

# 11. Reconstitution — repair without restoring the old relationship constitution

## 11.1 “Return” is not reset

Kotono apologizes and reconstitutes five-person Tsuki.

The relationship does not return to:

- Kotono as unquestioned center;
- Nagisa as rear-position supporter;
