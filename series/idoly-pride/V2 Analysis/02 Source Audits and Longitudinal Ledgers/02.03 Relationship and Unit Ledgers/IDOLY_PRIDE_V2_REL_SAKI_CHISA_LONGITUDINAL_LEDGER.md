# 0. Responsibility and warrant adjudication

This document performs two tasks in one canonical topical home:

1. adjudicate whether **Shiraishi Saki / Shiraishi Chisa** warrant a dedicated P2-C relationship ledger; and
2. because the answer is affirmative, serve as that ledger.

A separate warrant-only artifact would duplicate the same semantic responsibility and is therefore not created.

## 0.1 Warrant decision — WARRANTED

The pair passes the dedicated-ledger threshold for five independent reasons.

### A. The relationship is causally constitutive of both characters

The childhood near-drowning does not produce only one sister's trauma. It produces a **reciprocal care loop**:

- Saki interprets the accident as evidence that an older sister must never fail to protect Chisa;
- Chisa interprets Saki's fear and guilt as evidence that Chisa must stop creating danger, work, or pain for Saki;
- Saki therefore protects more;
- Chisa therefore asks for less and hides more desire;
- each sister can sincerely describe her own self-limitation as an act of love for the other.

This is a dyadic causal system. Neither character ledger can fully own it without repeating the other.

### B. The relationship changes state longitudinally

The pair does not remain a static protector/dependent bond. Its governing states change:

1. joyful early sister closeness;
2. accident-intensified reciprocal protective self-binding;
3. first mutual disclosure and release of the false choice between sisterhood and vocation;
4. institutional differentiation through separate regular units;
5. Hoshimi overprotection crisis and flawed developmental withdrawal;
6. self-propelled togetherness rather than dependence-versus-separation;
7. limited-unit relapse into reciprocal self-erasure;
8. explicit role reversal and bidirectional support;
9. conflict-tolerant mutual ambition;
10. mature affection across differentiated careers and units.

That is relationship development, not simply parallel character development.

### C. The relationship contains its own ethical problem

The pair repeatedly asks a proposition that belongs neither to Saki nor Chisa alone:

> **When does care preserve another person's agency, and when does loving care quietly become jurisdiction over what the other is allowed to want or do?**

The answer evolves. The mature relation does not abolish care. It makes care reciprocal, contestable, and compatible with distinct trajectories.

### D. The relationship has cross-unit architectural consequences

Saki and Chisa are placed in different regular groups. The source later lets Chisa interpret that distance as developmentally necessary: had she remained in the same group as Saki, she might have continued relying on her. Yet different groups never mean relational severance.

The pair therefore helps explain why IDOLY PRIDE's unit architecture sometimes uses **distance as autonomy infrastructure rather than as emotional rejection**.

### E. The relationship owns propositions not safely reducible to either character ledger

Examples include:

- `RECIPROCAL_PROTECTIVE_SELF_BINDING_SAKI_CHISA`;
- `LOVE_WITH_EXCESSIVE_CARE_JURISDICTION_SAKI_CHISA`;
- `SIBLING_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_COLOCATION_REQUIREMENT`;
- `DEVELOPMENTAL_SPACING_WITHOUT_RELATIONAL_SEVERANCE`;
- `AUTONOMY_SUPPORT_REQUIRES_ANSWERABILITY`;
- `RECIPROCAL_SELF_ERASURE_SISTER_UNIT`;
- `BIDIRECTIONAL_SUPPORT_STANDING_SAKI_CHISA`;
- `CONFLICT_TOLERANT_SISTERHOOD`;
- `MUTUAL_AMBITION_WITHOUT_AFFECTION_LOSS`;
- `SELF_PROPELLED_TOGETHERNESS_SAKI_CHISA`.

These are relationship objects with downstream relevance to both unit ledgers and the series-wide ethics of interdependence.

# 1. Governing relationship model

The strongest longitudinal formulation is:

> **`MUTUAL_PROTECTION_RELEASED_INTO_DIFFERENTIATED_SIBLING_RECIPROCITY_SAKI_CHISA`: Saki and Chisa begin from genuine, joyful sister attachment. A childhood near-drowning then reorganizes that love into complementary self-binding rules: Saki believes protecting Chisa requires constant reliability and proximity, while Chisa believes protecting Saki requires becoming harmless, undemanding, and small. Their first major repair occurs when each discovers that the other has been sacrificing desire in the other's name. They separate sisterhood from self-erasure and enter idolhood, but professional change does not automatically dissolve the old grammar. Different-unit placement gives Chisa developmental space; Saki's later attempt to reduce overprotection correctly identifies the need for autonomy but uses a deceptive withdrawal that causes real fear and must itself be answered for. The sisters locally reformulate closeness as self-propelled togetherness: Chisa can move on her own feet and therefore run beside Saki, while Saki can remain caring without making constant intervention the test of sisterly adequacy. A later two-person unit demonstrates that the old pattern can relapse: both yield visibility to the other until neither shines. Chisa then reverses the care direction, demands standing to support Saki, and restores Saki's suppressed desire for center and visibility. Their mature relation is therefore neither protector/dependent fusion nor independence through distance. It is differentiated sibling reciprocity: affection, admiration, advice, ordinary care, professional competition, direct disagreement, separate unit belonging, and mutual ambition can coexist because neither sister must disappear for the other to thrive.**

Short form:

> **They stop proving love by becoming smaller for each other.**

The deepest relational paradox is:

> **The sisters become more securely able to remain together when togetherness stops requiring either of them to surrender an independent trajectory.**

# 4. Relationship mechanics

## 4.1 The accident creates reciprocal protective self-binding

The childhood accident is not best modeled as “Saki becomes overprotective and Chisa becomes dependent.” That shorthand hides the symmetry of the causal logic.

Saki's rule:

> **If I love Chisa, I must prevent danger, remain reliable, and stay available.**

Chisa's rule:

> **If I love Saki, I must not make danger, work, fear, or disappointment for her.**

Each rule is intelligible as care. Together they create a closed system in which Saki's intervention confirms Chisa's sense of burden and Chisa's self-suppression confirms Saki's sense that intervention is necessary.

Canonical concept:

> **`RECIPROCAL_PROTECTIVE_SELF_BINDING_SAKI_CHISA`**

## 4.2 Love is real; the jurisdiction is the problem

The relationship is not secretly resentful.

Saki explicitly says she enjoys being beside Chisa and has never experienced supporting her as an unwanted burden. Chisa explicitly loves and admires Saki. Their childhood pleasures, idol fandom, domestic familiarity, and later leisure remain genuine.

The problem is therefore not attachment intensity. It is that both sisters allow attachment roles to decide what desires are permissible.

Canonical distinction:

> **`LOVE_WITH_EXCESSIVE_CARE_JURISDICTION_SAKI_CHISA`**

## 4.3 First repair: sisterhood and vocation are separable propositions

The origin's first major breakthrough comes when Chisa recognizes Saki's suppressed idol desire and Saki recognizes that she has fused “stay with Chisa” with “give up idolhood.”

The relationship does not solve this by choosing vocation over family. Saki retains sisterhood, family support, study, and idolhood.

This matters for later series ethics:

> **self-authorship is not proven by relational severance.**

## 4.4 Desire authorization is initially reciprocal but still asymmetrical

Each sister helps authorize a desire the other has made difficult to state:

- Chisa helps Saki admit idolhood;
- Saki helps Chisa admit idolhood.

This is reciprocal in function but not yet fully equal in structure. Chisa still grants Saki substantial authority over whether Chisa's desire is “too burdensome.” Later development must therefore reduce dependence on permission itself.

## 4.5 Separate units create developmental spacing

Different regular units are not a punishment or breakup.

They allow:

- Chisa to acquire peer competence without Saki automatically taking the protector position;
- Saki to acquire professional identity beyond being Chisa's support structure;
- each sister to encounter other models of care, leadership, weakness, competition, and reliance;
- eventual sister collaboration to occur between more differentiated people.

Canonical concept:

> **`DEVELOPMENTAL_SPACING_WITHOUT_RELATIONAL_SEVERANCE_SAKI_CHISA`**

## 4.6 Saki's observational care is both strength and risk

Saki really does know Chisa unusually well. She can identify Chisa's brave face and anticipate needs accurately.

The error occurs when accurate observation is silently converted into unilateral authority over the solution.

Mature care therefore requires a second step:

> **notice -> ask/answer -> act**, not simply **notice -> optimize**.

## 4.7 The staged absence is useful but ethically mixed

The fashion meeting proves two propositions simultaneously:

1. Chisa was capable of more independent action than the old relationship grammar allowed her to believe;
2. Saki and Makino's deceptive method frightened Chisa and denied her standing in the intervention design.

The successful outcome does not retroactively erase the relational cost.

Canonical rule:

> **`AUTONOMY_SUPPORT_REQUIRES_ANSWERABILITY_SAKI_CHISA`**

## 4.8 “Self-propelled togetherness” is not autarky

Chisa's running line is the relationship's strongest local Hoshimi formulation:

> she can run forward on her own feet, **therefore** she can ask Saki to run together.

Independence and attachment are not opposites here.

Canonical concept:

> **`SELF_PROPELLED_TOGETHERNESS_SAKI_CHISA`**

## 4.9 Dyadic concentration can reactivate old roles

The limited-unit event is essential because it prevents a falsely linear arc.

Once the sisters are paired alone:

- Saki's support aptitude becomes self-erasure;
- Chisa's admiration becomes self-erasure;
- both interpret giving the other more stage space as care;
- the relationship becomes less professionally expressive precisely because both are trying to be generous.

Canonical concept:

> **`RECIPROCAL_SELF_ERASURE_SISTER_UNIT`**

## 4.10 Chisa's decisive growth is not merely “becoming independent”

The 2022 confrontation shows a more demanding competence: Chisa risks relational friction to defend Saki's personhood against Saki's own role captivity.

`私にも……お姉ちゃんを支えさせて？` is not simply an offer of help. It is a claim to **standing** in the relationship.

Chisa says, in effect:

- you do not own the giving position because you are older;
- I do not own the receiving position because I am younger;
- your desire is not a threat to me;
- I want the version of you who visibly wants things.

Canonical concept:

> **`BIDIRECTIONAL_SUPPORT_STANDING_SAKI_CHISA`**

## 4.11 Mutual ambition is an attachment achievement

The repaired limited unit does not ask the sisters to stop competing. It asks them to compete without making ambition evidence of disloyalty.

Both can want audience attention. Both can want to shine more. Saki can want center. Chisa can threaten, playfully and sincerely, to outshine her.

Canonical concept:

> **`MUTUAL_AMBITION_WITHOUT_AFFECTION_LOSS_SAKI_CHISA`**

## 4.12 Conflict tolerance replaces protective mind-reading

Earlier, both sisters infer what the other needs and self-remove before asking.

Later, they state opinions strongly enough that outsiders can mistake the exchange for a fight. The sisters themselves experience it as enjoyable because direct disagreement no longer implies that the bond is endangered.

Canonical concept:

> **`CONFLICT_TOLERANT_SISTERHOOD_SAKI_CHISA`**

## 4.13 Care reversibility is mature evidence, not role inversion

Saki does not cease being a caring older sister. Chisa does not become “the protector now.”

The mature gain is reversibility:

- Chisa can comfort Saki;
- Saki can accept comfort;
- Chisa can give practical advice;
- Saki can follow it;
- ordinary physical/domestic care can reverse direction;
- neither reversal threatens their identities.

Reciprocity therefore means **bidirectional access to care**, not identical personalities or equal competence in every domain.

# 7. Rejected simplifications and overclaims

## “Saki sacrificed her life because Chisa was a burden” — REJECT

Chisa is not experienced by Saki as unwanted. Saki's love is genuine. The problem is Saki's belief that love authorizes a categorical duty structure.

## “Chisa is naturally timid and dependent” — REJECT

The origin explicitly gives a more active pre-accident Chisa and connects later inhibition to the near-drowning/burden-avoidance system.

## “Saki causes Chisa's dependence” — REJECT AS ONE-WAY MODEL

Saki's overprotection reinforces dependence, but Chisa also actively self-minimizes to protect Saki from burden. The system is reciprocal.

## “Chisa causes Saki to abandon her dream” — REJECT

Saki herself internalizes and administers the rule. Chisa later becomes the first person who helps Saki state the dream and separate sisterhood from sacrifice.

## “The parents simply forbid Saki from being an idol” — REJECT

Their expectations matter, but the source specifically shows Saki internalizing expectations more categorically than the parents later enforce them.

## “Different units mean the sisters were intentionally separated because their relationship was unhealthy” — REJECT AS OVERCLAIM

Different-unit placement does become developmentally useful. The exact degree to which every later benefit was prospectively designed as therapy is not established.

## “Saki's staged disappearance was ideal tough love” — REJECT

It produces genuine growth and genuine unnecessary fear. The outcome does not cancel the method's accountability problem.

## “Chisa becoming independent means she no longer needs or admires Saki” — REJECT

The mature formula is self-propelled togetherness, not autarky.

## “The 2022 limited unit proves earlier growth failed” — REJECT

It proves development is state-sensitive and nonlinear. The sisters recognize and correct the relapse using capacities acquired earlier.

## “Saki is naturally meant to be support rather than center” — REJECT

She is excellent at support and explicitly wants center/visibility.

## “Chisa must stop being deferential to become mature” — REJECT

Chisa remains soft-spoken and often anxious. The gain is the ability to speak when something important requires it, not personality inversion.

## “Reciprocity means the sisters become symmetrical” — REJECT

Age, temperament, skills, and habitual care direction remain uneven. Reciprocity means both possess standing to want, disagree, support, receive, and compete.

## “Competition damages their sisterly bond” — REJECT

The decisive limited-unit source shows the opposite: professional competition becomes healthier once it no longer requires self-erasure.

## “Close sister affection implies romantic coding” — REJECT

The relationship is explicitly familial/sibling. No romantic category is relevant or admitted.

# 11. Capstone

The Shiraishi sisters are a particularly clean IDOLY PRIDE case of why **love and healthy relational structure are not synonyms**.

Saki and Chisa never need to discover that they love one another. They know that from the beginning. Their problem is that love becomes encoded as complementary self-denial:

- Saki proves love by becoming more responsible, more available, and less free to pursue a path that might take her away;
- Chisa proves love by becoming easier to protect, asking for less, and withholding desires that might create risk or work.

The series does not solve that by declaring dependence bad and separation good.

Instead, it repeatedly asks the sisters to **change the form of togetherness**.

Saki can become an idol without abandoning Chisa. Chisa can become an idol even if she is not guaranteed to stand in Saki's group. Saki can worry without automatically intervening. Chisa can act without proving that she needs nobody. Saki can be supported. Chisa can challenge her. Both can want the center of attention. Both can disagree. Both can remain sisters.

The running image condenses the relationship better than a generic independence vocabulary:

> **Chisa does not say she can run alone and therefore no longer needs Saki. She says she can run on her own feet—and therefore asks Saki to run with her.**

The later limited-unit event then adds the necessary correction: even a repaired relationship can relapse when an old role structure becomes salient. Their maturity is not the absence of regression. It is that they now possess the communicative and ethical resources to identify the regression and renegotiate it.

That produces the relationship's final Phase-2 formulation:

> **`MUTUAL_PROTECTION_RELEASED_INTO_DIFFERENTIATED_SIBLING_RECIPROCITY_SAKI_CHISA`**

and its practical endpoint:

> **they stop proving love by becoming smaller for each other.**
