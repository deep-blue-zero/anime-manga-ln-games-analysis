# 1. Governing unit model

The strongest longitudinal formulation is:

> **`ATTACHMENT_TO_AUTHORSHIP_THROUGH_CONSTITUTIONAL_REFOUNDATION_TSUKI_NO_TEMPEST`: Tsuki no Tempest begins from five different problems of externally structured direction. Saki has learned to equate responsibility with self-limitation; Suzu initially frames aspiration through prestige, family authorization, and Mana-shaped idealization; Mei is socially expansive and useful but insufficiently located in a durable chosen commitment; Nagisa chooses devotion to Kotono so strongly that support risks becoming self-definition; Kotono turns Mana's love and death into an imagined commission over her future. Formation does not cure these problems by teaching independence. Instead, Tsuki gives each member a relationship in which attachment can become chosen direction. The early group nevertheless stabilizes an unequal constitution: Kotono is the center, the symbolic moon, the main directional force, and the member everyone expects to solve the hardest crises, while the other four are publicly and privately legible primarily through supporting her. That arrangement produces genuine excellence and genuine care, but it overconcentrates initiative and responsibility. BIG4 makes the contradiction unavoidable. Kotono correctly perceives that the unit has become too dependent on her, but falsely concludes that connection itself causes weakness; she leaves, cuts relational contact, and pursues self-punitive independence. The separation produces real gains on both sides, which must not be erased: Kotono acquires adversarial independence and sharper discipline, while Nagisa refuses to become her satellite, Suzu learns the embodied responsibility of centerhood, Saki converts care toward more explicit process and critique, Mei learns that belonging can survive rupture, and the four-person unit develops a visible identity capable of continuing without Kotono. Because those gains are real, reunion cannot mean restoration. Kotono returns only after the old center/periphery structure has been broken and after the members can answer her rather than merely support her. Suzu remains center at the comeback; Kotono receives one-song center authority as a communicative/professional instrument, not as automatic restoration. Fan injury must be answered publicly, not only repaired privately. The five then rearticulate the unit through reciprocal illumination: the four learned to produce their own light, Kotono learned to receive light, and each member can become a source of direction for the others. DoriKyun supplies the external stress test. Tsuki rejects the proposition that strength requires erasing another idol's future, while still taking winning seriously. Suzu later entrusts the decisive battle center to Kotono because the tested configuration performs better, explicitly retaining her own future center claim. Mei supplies the mature defeat rule: the current Tsuki is supple enough that even loss would not break it; the five could begin again. By 2026 that constitutional settlement is no longer an emergency accommodation. Tsuki has two established center-capable members, openly invites all five to contest center position, treats solo careers as additions to rather than defections from unit identity, explicitly frames members as illuminating one another, and has Kotono say she will rely on the others when needed. Defeat is no longer ontological collapse: if they lose, they try again. Mature Tsuki is therefore not strongest because dependence disappeared. It is strongest because attachment, centerhood, memory, ambition, criticism, and support have become contestable and answerable rather than proprietary.**

Short form:

> **Tsuki becomes a mature unit when being important to one another stops meaning having the right to author one another.**

Its decisive constitutional transition is:

> **one moon + four supporters -> separation and distributed agency -> five lights capable of illuminating one another -> centerhood as a contestable professional office.**

The central paradox is:

> **Tsuki becomes more securely relational only after proving that the relationship can survive members acting against one another's wishes.**

A second paradox is:

> **Kotono becomes a stronger center only after Tsuki becomes able to remain Tsuki without her at center.**

# 4. Formation constitution — attachment is not yet authorship

The five origin stories do not begin from a common temperament. They begin from five different ways a life can be directed by something one genuinely values.

Saki's responsibility is not fake. Suzu's admiration is not fake. Mei's pleasure in helping and joining things is not fake. Nagisa's love/care for Kotono is not fake. Kotono's love for Mana is not fake.

The problem is therefore not “false motives.” It is whether a valued attachment has become sufficiently reflective that the person can distinguish:

- **I choose this because it matters to me**

from:

- **this matters to me, therefore it has the right to decide my future for me.**

Tsuki is the unit in which that distinction becomes repeatedly testable.

The origin freeze's formulation remains the best starting point:

> **borrowed, inherited, or externally structured direction -> chosen commitment**

with the crucial qualifier:

> **attachment remains.**

This becomes especially important later because BIG4 can otherwise be misread as a simple independence arc. It is not. BIG4 demonstrates that severing attachment can create new skill while still being a mistaken theory of personhood and unit strength.

# 5. The first Tsuki constitution — real excellence with overconcentrated direction

The original five-person Tsuki works.

That point must be preserved because BIG4 is not exposing a completely false unit.

The first constitution has genuine strengths:

- Kotono's precision and directional intensity;
- Nagisa's stabilizing adjacency and social care;
- Saki's planning/structuring competence;
- Suzu's declared ambition and responsiveness to standards;
- Mei's kinetic joy, flexibility, and social permeability;
- unusually strong coordination;
- disciplined collective practice;
- a coherent “moon” identity.

But the model also overconcentrates three things in Kotono:

1. **symbolic centrality** — she is the moon most readily understood as the unit's defining light;
2. **initiative** — others often expect her to know how the unit should move;
3. **burden** — failure becomes especially legible to Kotono as evidence that she must change herself more radically.

The later BIG4 diagnosis should therefore be read carefully:

> **The old Tsuki is not wrong because the members support Kotono. It is unstable because support and direction are too unequal.**

That distinction is the foundation of the refoundation argument.

# 6. Mana inheritance — founding cause without permanent unit jurisdiction

Mana is structurally central to Tsuki because she is central to Kotono's entry into idolhood and to the broader Hoshimi inheritance system.

But Tsuki does not mature by becoming a five-person instrument for completing Mana's unfinished life.

The origin material already establishes that Kotono's later stated destination becomes:

> **reach the top with Tsuki no Tempest**

rather than merely complete Mana's song or become Mana's continuation.

This is an authorship change even when the surface ambition remains “top idol.” The subject of the dream has changed from a dead sister's imagined commission to a living collective that Kotono chooses.

BIG4 later generalizes the same principle from Mana -> Kotono to Kotono -> Tsuki:

> **A beloved or exceptional person can matter enormously without acquiring permanent sovereignty over everyone else's future.**

The unit's mature anti-proxy logic therefore operates at two levels:

- Kotono is not Mana's replacement/unfinished life;
- Nagisa/Saki/Suzu/Mei are not Kotono's supporting apparatus.

# 7. Pre-BIG4 maturation — high discipline is not yet constitutional maturity

Tokyo-era Tsuki is already highly professional.

`tokyo_011_moonlight_in_our_hands` shows Kotono directing the group toward higher-quality practice before an elite opponent and the others participating seriously in that preparation. The unit can discuss performance, rest, strategy, and opponent strength without being merely a novice ensemble.

The danger is precisely that this can look like maturity from the outside.

Technical competence and cohesion do not by themselves answer:

- who may oppose the center;
- who can carry direction when the center fails;
- whether support has become self-authored;
- whether the unit can survive a member's refusal;
- whether loss is a result or a verdict on the right to continue.

BIG4 tests those questions directly.

# 8. The BIG4 diagnosis — Kotono sees a real problem and chooses the wrong cure

After repeated failure, Kotono does not invent the old unit's asymmetry from nothing.

In `big4_005_goodbye_to_the_moon`, she names Tsuki as a genuinely wonderful group—kind, hardworking, coordinated—while also concluding that being surrounded by everyone's kindness makes her “甘えてしまう” and causes her to stop.

The diagnosis contains truth:

- the group has come to rely excessively on Kotono's initiative;
- Kotono has learned to experience receiving support as something suspiciously close to weakness;
- the others have underdeveloped some forms of agency because the center has carried too much directional load.

The error is causal:

> **Kotono turns “our dependence is structured too unequally” into “connection itself prevents strength.”**

She therefore chooses severance rather than redistribution.

That is why this arc cannot be summarized as “Kotono needs independence.”

Independence supplies real skills. It does not supply the correct relational theory.

# 9. Nagisa's refusal — attachment becomes contestatory

One of the most constitutionally important moments occurs when Kotono asks Nagisa to come with her.

Nagisa refuses.

This is decisive because Nagisa's origin motive really was strongly Kotono-centered. Her refusal is not evidence that Kotono no longer matters. It is evidence that attachment has become sufficiently authored that Nagisa can oppose the person she most wants to support.

The transition is:

> **support as following -> support as standing beside -> support as the right to say no.**

That right to refusal is one of the clearest markers that the mature unit cannot be organized by emotional sovereignty.

Nagisa does not leave Kotono behind emotionally. She refuses to let Kotono define the only legitimate meaning of staying close.

---

# 10. Four-person Tsuki — not a waiting room

`big4_006_choice_of_destiny` makes the structural question explicit.

With Kotono gone, management does not pretend that “Tsuki” automatically continues unchanged. The four are offered a real choice: continue in a new form, reorganize, or allow the prior group to end.

Their decision to continue matters because it creates an actual alternate unit constitution.

The four-person Tsuki is therefore not analytically reducible to:

- “holding the fort until Kotono returns”;
- “proving how much they miss Kotono”;
- “an intentionally inferior version of the real group.”

It develops capacities the original five-person unit underweighted:

- collective initiative without waiting for Kotono;
- increased individual visibility;
- explicit public self-definition;
- distributed problem solving;
- greater willingness to speak disagreement;
- center labor carried by someone who had previously idealized centerhood more than she had inhabited it.

Those capacities must survive reunion or the arc would be structurally circular.

---

# 11. Suzu's center — aspiration becomes burden-bearing office

Suzu's center development is the strongest proof that Tsuki's new constitution cannot be summarized as “Kotono learns to come home.”

The four-person group makes Suzu center. That position exposes centerhood as work:

- being publicly answerable for the group's continuity;
- becoming a focal point during a legitimacy crisis;
- carrying pressure previously projected onto Kotono;
- helping articulate the four-person unit's new identity;
- sustaining morale while still being allowed to feel fear and inadequacy.

This revises Suzu's earlier relation to prestige.

Center is no longer merely a glamorous sign that the “best” idol has been recognized. It becomes an office with responsibility, vulnerability, and temporary functional assignment.

That is why later delegation does not erase Suzu's center legitimacy.

# 12. Public legitimacy and fan jurisdiction

Kotono's departure is not merely a private conflict among five friends/coworkers.

`big4_007_new_moon_new_bright` makes public legitimacy unavoidable:

- reporters ask whether the departure is irresponsible;
- fans worry about the unit;
- the remaining four must publicly explain that they are continuing;
- the new configuration needs a visible identity rather than a private promise.

This produces a crucial P2-D distinction:

> **The members own their personal choices, but they do not own the consequences those choices create for a public-facing professional unit.**

That does not mean fans have veto power over membership decisions.

It means that a professional idol unit creates relationships of answerability beyond its internal emotional circle. When a member breaks the unit publicly, private reconciliation cannot be the sole repair surface.

This becomes even more explicit when Mei later insists that Kotono must face the fans who were hurt by the rupture.

# 13. Kotono outside Tsuki — real gains without validating severance

Kotono's independent period must not be narratively punished into meaninglessness.

She gains:

- sharper individual discipline;
- greater adversarial independence;
- experience performing without the old support structure;
- clearer awareness of her own agency;
- stronger ability to confront powerful rivals directly.

Those gains explain why the mature five-person unit is not merely the old five-person unit restored.

But the same period also intensifies failure modes:

- contact becomes equated with softness;
- needing others becomes morally suspect;
- accountability becomes self-punishment;
- independence risks becoming a purity test;
- relational severance is treated as proof of seriousness.

The P2-D conclusion is therefore:

> **The skills are retained. The relational theory is rejected.**

This is one of the cleanest examples in the series of a “wrong path” producing genuine capacities that later become usable once its governing interpretation is revised.

# 14. `big4_010` — Tsuki rejects elimination as the meaning of strength

DoriKyun's wager produces an extreme proposition:

> if you lose, the unit should disappear.

Nagisa's answer is constitutionally important:

> if winning requires crushing another person's dream and future, then she does not need that kind of victory.

This must not be flattened into “Tsuki stops caring about winning.”

They continue to want victory intensely. They train to win. They later choose center configurations by score and performance probability.

What they reject is **winner sovereignty**:

> **A result can decide who won this contest without deciding who deserves a future.**

That distinction becomes the basis of the mature recoverability model.

# 15. Reunion — accountability rather than sentimental reset

Kotono's return becomes meaningful only because the others can answer her.

The refounded unit requires:

- recognition that Kotono's unilateral departure caused real injury;
- Nagisa's refusal to collapse back into pure support;
- Saki's demand for more honest process;
- Suzu's center legitimacy;
- Mei's insistence on fan-facing repair;
- Kotono's acceptance that she cannot control the terms of everyone's care for her.

The decisive negative claim is:

> **love is insufficient as a reunion mechanism.**

The members already cared before the rupture.

What changes is the constitution under which care operates.

# 16. Comeback center — Suzu remains center when Kotono returns

`big4_011_back_of_entertainment` is the most important anti-restoration source.

At the comeback:

> `すず、今日はセンターをよろしく頼むぞ`

and Suzu explicitly introduces herself as the center of the newly restarted five-person Tsuki.

Kotono has returned to the unit.

Kotono has **not** automatically returned to center.

That one fact blocks a large family of simplistic interpretations.

Suzu then authorizes Kotono to center a particular song because the audience is unsettled and Kotono's ordinary apology is not reaching them. Kotono accepts the role as a communicative act:

> `これから五人で進む意志を――次の一曲に込める！`

The performance succeeds.

But afterward Suzu explicitly warns Kotono not to interpret that success as automatic restoration, and Kotono agrees:

> `センターに戻ろうなんて、思ってない`

This is **temporary role authorization**, not role repossession.

# 17. Center becomes a contestable professional office

The comeback scene then goes further than merely preserving Suzu's position.

Suzu says that if someone wants center, they should challenge for it. Nagisa says she needs to work harder. Mei jokes that she may aim for center. Saki accepts the idea of competition. Kotono says she will not lose.

This is the constitutional innovation:

> **center is no longer a personal essence.**

It is:

- meaningful;
- demanding;
- professionally consequential;
- contestable;
- temporarily assignable;
- open to performance evidence;
- compatible with multiple members having legitimate claims.

This does not make all members equally good at center in every song or moment.

It makes **the right to be considered** structurally plural.

# 18. Reciprocal illumination — Kotono learns to receive light

`big4_012_illuminate` gives the arc's most explicit retrospective interpretation.

Kotono recognizes that after she left, the other four did not merely wander through darkness. They learned to illuminate one another:

> `お互いを、『照らし合ってきたんだ』`

> `四人それぞれが光を放つようになって`

and she says their light illuminated her road as well.

This overturns the old one-way moon metaphor.

Kotono also explicitly acknowledges that her own path was never found alone. It was shaped by:

- Tsuki;
- Mana;
- fans;
- staff;
- Makino;
- other people who supported her.

Her answer to “what is an idol?” becomes:

> `みんなの道を照らすこと`

The important change is not that Kotono stops illuminating others.

It is that **illumination becomes reciprocal**.

Mature Tsuki therefore does not erase the moon metaphor. It pluralizes it.

# 19. Five lights, five storms — distributed personhood

The Big4 frozen findings capture the mature group ontology well:

> **Each member can be a moon for someone.**

> **Each member can be a storm that clears someone's hesitation.**

That should not be read as “all five are identical.”

Their contribution types remain differentiated:

- Kotono: concentrated performance direction, discipline, and later reflective illumination;
- Nagisa: relational persistence that becomes capable of opposition rather than trailing;
- Saki: process, critique, planning, and responsibility increasingly freed from self-erasure;
- Suzu: explicit ambition translated into tested center labor and role contestability;
- Mei: joy, permeability, and the unit's strongest explicit theory of recoverability.

Plural personhood is therefore not role flattening.

It is the end of **one-member monopoly over person-defining unit functions**.

# 20. Functional center delegation before DoriKyun

`big4_013_the_moonlight` makes the mature center principle unusually explicit.

Suzu has prepared to center the final battle. She nevertheless compares tested configurations and observes that Kotono-center produced the higher score and stronger shared response.

She therefore entrusts the position to Kotono.

Her reasoning is not:

- “Kotono was always the true center”;
- “I failed to become a real center”;
- “the old Tsuki has been restored.”

It is professional:

> winning is not everything, but if one configuration has a higher probability of victory, refusing it can disrespect the work already done and the opponent being faced.

She immediately preserves the temporary nature of the assignment:

> `すぐにセンターの座を奪い取りますわよ？`

Kotono replies:

> `私がセンターを預かる`

The verb choice matters conceptually even without over-literalizing it: center is treated as something **entrusted for the battle**, not metaphysically repossessed.

# 21. Suzu's four-person labor remains part of mature five-person Tsuki

Mei explicitly tells Suzu that the four-person unit survived because Suzu remained at its center and supported it.

This acknowledgment protects the arc from retrospective erasure.

The mature five-person group contains historical layers:

1. original Kotono-centered Tsuki;
2. four-person Suzu-centered Tsuki;
3. refounded five-person Tsuki with contestable center authority.

The third does not cancel the second.

It inherits its gains.

That is why Suzu's later willingness to delegate center is an act of professional judgment rather than surrender.

# 22. Recoverability — defeat ceases to be ontological

Before the final DoriKyun battle, Mei makes the strongest mature statement about failure:

> `今の月ストって...すっごくしなやか`

> `もし負けたとしても 折れることはない`

> `五人でいれば、きっとまたやり直せるよ`

She immediately adds that she has no intention of losing.

This is the exact balance the unit has been moving toward:

- **victory matters**;
- **defeat hurts**;
- **results produce real consequences**;
- **failure does not decide the unit's right to exist**.

Recoverability is not complacency.

It is the capacity to keep failure from becoming an identity verdict.

# 23. Competitive recognition without loser erasure

Tsuki's DoriKyun arc moves competition from existential sorting toward reciprocal testing.

The mature unit can use rivals to discover:

- weaknesses in its internal structure;
- performance configurations it had not considered;
- the difference between discipline and relational purity;
- the difference between winning a contest and owning another person's future.

This is why the DoriKyun conflict remains valuable even after Tsuki rejects the wager's elimination ethic.

The rival is not merely a villainous obstacle. It is an external force that exposes whether Tsuki's constitution can survive pressure.

The mature answer is yes—not because Tsuki becomes invulnerable, but because it becomes recoverable.

# 24. Fan-facing answerability exceeds internal affection

The comeback succeeds partly because performance provides a language of accountability ordinary explanation cannot fully supply.

The fans' uncertainty is not treated as illegitimate interference.

Nor are fans granted ownership over member choices.

The mature relation is narrower and more demanding:

> **if a public-facing unit breaks trust publicly, internal reconciliation does not exhaust the repair.**

Kotono's comeback performance works because it translates:

- failure;
- solitude;
- regret;
- renewed five-person intention;

into an act the audience can evaluate.

The unit therefore becomes answerable both inwardly and outwardly.

# 25. 2025 continuity — mature cohesion survives ordinary differentiation

The 2025 contest/event material is not another constitutional rupture, which is itself informative.

Saki's planning, Nagisa's collaborative suggestions, Suzu/Mei energy, and Kotono's serious review habits remain differentiated without becoming centrifugal.

# 26. 2026 endpoint — the refoundation becomes ordinary unit law

`event_2026_002_st-eve-2602-dice` is the strongest locked-snapshot endpoint because it restates Big4's constitutional gains without needing the original rupture as immediate context.

Recoverability has moved from Mei's pre-battle intuition into Kotono's own operational philosophy.

This is not an unstable contradiction waiting to be solved by choosing the “real” center.

The mature rule is therefore stronger than “Kotono and Suzu are co-centers.”

> **center authority is open to evidence-based contest among the whole unit.**

## 26.4 Individual careers do not threaten the group

Kotono has a solo live tour.

Suzu is creating a solo song.

The others explicitly respond not with abandonment anxiety but with motivation to grow as individual idols too.

Differentiation has become compatible with belonging.

Kotono says:

This is a direct reversal of the BIG4-era belief that relying on Tsuki meant stagnating through `甘え`.

The mature change is not that Kotono never needs support.

It is that she can receive support **without interpreting receipt as authorship loss or weakness**.

## 26.6 The five explicitly retain reciprocal illumination

Suzu closes the group-facing aspiration with:

> `これからも全員で照らしあって 嵐を巻き起こし続けますわよ`

The reciprocal-light model is therefore not only a retrospective reading of BIG4.

# 28. Counterevidence and guardrails

Kotono is a foundational center and remains exceptionally important.

The old dependency structure had real problems.

The source rejects Kotono's conclusion that severing connection is the correct cure. Mature Tsuki preserves attachment while redistributing authority.

The four acquire independent performance identity, center experience, public legitimacy work, and increased individual visibility. Those gains survive Kotono's return.

Suzu is the center of the four-person unit, remains center when Kotono returns, authorizes a temporary Kotono-centered song, and later delegates the decisive battle based on tested performance evidence while retaining an explicit future claim.

The comeback text explicitly denies this. Kotono says she is not assuming she has returned to center.

## 28.6 “All five are equally suited to center in every context” — REJECT

Contestability is not equivalence. `big4_013` explicitly uses performance evidence to prefer Kotono for a specific battle.

## 28.7 “Mature Tsuki is anti-competition” — REJECT

The unit strongly wants to win, uses performance metrics strategically, becomes BIG4, and continues to treat internal center selection competitively.

## 28.8 “Winning is all that matters” — REJECT

Tsuki rejects elimination of another idol's future as the price of strength and develops an explicit recovery model for defeat.

## 28.9 “Recoverability means defeat no longer matters” — REJECT

Mei says the unit can survive defeat and immediately says she has no intention of losing. Kotono likewise frames loss as a reason to try again, not as irrelevant.

## 28.10 “Nagisa's care must become emotional detachment for equality” — REJECT

Nagisa's mature equality is demonstrated by refusal/opposition and broadened motive, not by ceasing to care intensely.

## 28.11 “Mana no longer matters once Kotono self-authors” — REJECT

Mana remains loved, formative, and symbolically important. What changes is posthumous jurisdiction over Kotono/Tsuki's future.

## 28.12 “Individual solo work threatens mature Tsuki cohesion” — DOWNGRADE

It can create scheduling/workload pressure, but the locked 2026 endpoint treats Kotono's solo tour, Suzu's solo song, and others' individual challenges as compatible with group growth.

## 28.13 “The generated `tsuki_no_tempest.md` index defines the complete unit corpus” — REJECT

It uses `smr`/Sumire in 73 rows and `mei` in zero rows. It cannot govern unit membership or counts.
