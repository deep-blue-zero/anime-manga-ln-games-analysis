# 4. First founding — a prestige product before a shared vocation

IIIX begins with unusually high competence but unusually weak shared purpose.

The members are not novices discovering what they can do. Each arrives with preexisting value:

- Miho has technical knowledge, competitive severity, and a memorial/political grievance tied to Yō and Mana;
- Fran has modeling/fashion capital, visual authority, and a clear strategy for converting idol exposure into professional optionality;
- Kana has long entertainment-industry literacy, enormous publicity competence, and a strong need for visible recognition shaped partly by attachment history.

Preta Porte solves the coordination problem externally.

It supplies:

- the organizational frame;
- professional infrastructure;
- prestige positioning;
- songs and production;
- a center decision;
- upward commercial reward;
- a reason that does not require the members to agree on ultimate private purpose.

This is not a false unit. It is a **thin constitution**.

The first IIIX is real because three professionals genuinely perform, train, optimize, and win together. But the unit's reason for existence is substantially authored outside the members. It asks relatively little of them beyond excellence and shared result.

That arrangement is stable while the result keeps arriving.

# 5. Early success — product validation can conceal constitutional weakness

IIIX's early wins do more than establish competence. They allow the members to avoid answering a harder question.

If the unit keeps producing the result each member needs, motive heterogeneity is cheap.

Miho can treat victory as historical correction.

Fran can treat exposure and compensation as a bridge toward fashion.

Kana can treat visibility, recognition, and professional excitement as sufficient return.

The unit does not need one deep shared telos because the outcome satisfies three different private calculations at once.

This yields a general P2-D proposition:

> **A unit can be highly functional before it is deeply self-authored.**

For IIIX, winning initially acts as a coordination technology.

It is only when winning fails that the unit must discover whether it has any constitutional object more durable than successful exchange.

# 6. The first defeat — motive heterogeneity becomes public to the unit itself

The first serious defeat does not merely hurt confidence. It reveals that the members had been standing inside the same successful machine for different reasons.

That discovery creates a genuine problem:

- if one member's private project changes, should IIIX end?
- if the unit cannot guarantee the result, what remains shared?
- if the members do not want conventional friendship, what kind of bond can support continued professional risk?

The answer they first produce is deliberately narrow:

> **victory.**

This is development, not failure.

For the first time the unit has an explicitly shared constitutional object that is not simply supplied by the corporation. But victory remains too thin to carry every future shock.

It creates authored commonality while leaving the group vulnerable to result captivity.

# 7. Absolute victory — a real constitution with dangerous jurisdiction

IIIX's absolute-victory orientation should not be caricatured as childish obsession or morally defective ambition.

It performs several legitimate functions:

- creates a common objective across heterogeneous motives;
- rewards measurable craft;
- disciplines preparation;
- gives internal conflict an external professional object;
- permits members who reject sentimental fusion to coordinate intensely;
- protects the group from vague appeals to harmony that would not fit its actual social grammar.

Miho's score knowledge in particular is real expertise. Rule-aware optimization is part of professional craft.

The problem emerges when result becomes **sovereign**.

If victory alone validates the unit, then:

- defeat threatens existence rather than revising strategy;
- external actors can align manipulation with the unit's own desired outcome;
- audience meaning not captured by the score can be discounted;
- members can tolerate ethically suspect structures because the shared constitutional language is too narrow to object until authorship itself is threatened.

The mature IIIX does not abolish absolute victory.

It removes its monopoly over futurity.

# 8. Himeno — institutional capture exploits, rather than creates, instrumental logic

Himeno is important at unit level because she tests whether IIIX knows the difference between **optimization authored by the performers** and **outcomes contaminated by an external operator**.

She does not create IIIX's ambition, transactionality, or result focus.

Those already exist.

Her danger is that she makes external capture look like an extension of the unit's own rational strategy.

Kana detects exploitative-adult patterns early.

Miho has particularly strong motivated toleration because the result aligns with her historical grievance.

Fran's aesthetic/professional floor limits how far pure instrumental reasoning can go.

The unit does not cleanly refuse the structure at first.

That matters ethically. IIIX is not merely a passive victim of manipulation.

But the later refusal establishes a professional floor:

> **Winning only proves IIIX if IIIX remains causally answerable for the performance and result.**

This distinction becomes a precursor to the mature authenticity model. Authorship is not purity from institutions; it is the ability to distinguish assistance from capture and strategy from falsified causality.

# 9. I-UNITY defeat — technical excellence is real, but cannot guarantee the event

Sunny Peace's victory does not establish that technical excellence, metrics, or preparation were meaningless.

The source is more demanding.

IIIX can be technically formidable and still lose because live performance also contains:

- audience relation;
- singular event energy;
- response not fully controllable in advance;
- meanings that cannot be reduced to a repeatable optimization recipe.

For early IIIX, this is destabilizing because the unit has given victory too much constitutional authority.

The defeat therefore creates two different facts at once:

1. **IIIX remains excellent.**
2. **Excellence does not entitle IIIX to a guaranteed result.**

The mature unit eventually learns to hold both without collapsing either into anti-competition rhetoric or existential panic.

At the immediate post-I-UNITY frontier, however, that mature model is not yet available.

# 10. Institutional collapse — continuing as idols is not continuing as IIIX

Preta Porte's collapse is the unit's most important institutional test.

The practical facts matter:

- normal work acquisition weakens;
- already-contracted obligations remain;
- professional standing deteriorates;
- the office no longer supplies the stable infrastructure that made the first IIIX possible;
- the members can continue being idols individually without continuing this unit.

This distinction prevents a sentimental shortcut.

IIIX does not survive because the members discover that they were always inseparable.

It survives because the institutional structure that once made the choice cheap disappears and the members eventually make the choice anyway.

The unit's constitutional location changes.

Before collapse:

> institution -> unit -> members.

After voluntary continuation:

> members -> unit -> chosen institutional relations.

That reversal is the core P2-D transformation.

- they leave Preta Porte's stable base;
- they take on freelance/independent operation;

Self-authorship therefore has a material cost.

It means not only choosing the unit, but also taking responsibility for:

- work acquisition;
- logistics;
- professional negotiation;
- creative decisions;
- risk;
- continuity when prestige falls.

The mature IIIX should not be romanticized as freer because it has less infrastructure.

The point is narrower:

> **the unit's legitimacy no longer depends on the institution having assembled it.**

# 13. Hoshimi affiliation — institutional support without relational domestication

Hoshimi presents IIIX with a second institutional problem.

The question is no longer whether an institution can exploit the unit. It is whether a self-authored unit can accept support without interpreting dependence as surrender.

The BIG4 endpoint gives a strong answer.

IIIX accepts a reversible/trial professional affiliation with Hoshimi.

The trio:

- continues living separately;
- remains abrasive;
- does not imitate Hoshimi's warmer internal style;
- preserves self-interest and rivalry;
- can function as an internal high-level rival and professional resource;
- does not receive blanket emotional absolution for every prior grievance or tactic.

Hoshimi's legitimacy therefore comes partly from restraint.

It does not require IIIX to prove belonging through personality assimilation.

The preferred unit formulation remains:

> **`AFFILIATION_WITHOUT_DOMESTICATION_IIIX`: IIIX can enter a professional ecology, accept resources and mutual benefit, and remain recognizably IIIX because institutional membership does not claim jurisdiction over the unit's relational style or self-authored competitive identity.**

This matters beyond IIIX. It supplies evidence for later P2-E work on autonomy, management, intervention, and professional care.

Fran's center status is historically real.

She develops:

# 16. Conflict as production technology — useful only under answerable conditions

IIIX fights.

- expose weak assumptions;
- force a proposal to become more specific;
- prevent center entitlement;
- sharpen contrast between performers;
- make rivalry itself legible on stage;

# 18. `ABSOLUTE_VICTORY_WITH_NON_TERMINAL_DEFEAT_IIIX`

The mature competition model needs precision.

IIIX does not learn that winning is unimportant.

It does not become a participation-first unit.

It does not abandon rankings, elite status, BIG4 ambition, or the desire to stand at the top.

Early model:

> loss -> evidence the unit's reason for existence failed -> possible dissolution.

Mature model:

> loss -> real professional failure or missed objective -> evidence about audience, strategy, self, rival, or craft -> next authored attempt remains available.

This is recoverability without complacency.

A loss may still hurt, expose weakness, cost status, or require revision.

It simply no longer possesses automatic constitutional veto power.

The unit can therefore remain more intensely competitive because it is less dependent on pretending that every defeat is existential.
