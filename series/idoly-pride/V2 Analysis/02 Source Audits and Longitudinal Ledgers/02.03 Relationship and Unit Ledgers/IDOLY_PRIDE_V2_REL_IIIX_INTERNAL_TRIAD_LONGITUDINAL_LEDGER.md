# 0. Analytical responsibility and architecture boundary

This ledger is the canonical Phase-2 relationship authority for the **internal three-person relationship system of IIIX**: miho, fran, and kana.

It does **not** treat the triad as three pairwise relationships pasted together. Pairwise relations matter, but the central object is a three-person constitution: what has to be true for these particular three people to keep becoming `IIIX` after the institutions, goals, victories, and public stories that once justified the grouping repeatedly fail.

This ledger therefore owns propositions about:

- the difference between externally assembled proximity and chosen continuation;
- how three incompatible founding motives first acquire a common object;
- why victory can function as a shared constitutional object without being a complete shared vocation;
- how defeat, institutional collapse, renewed individual opportunity, and explicit dissolution repeatedly test the relation;
- why repeated re-selection is stronger evidence of durable relation than friendly vocabulary would be;
- how leadership and center status become locally contestable without eliminating role differentiation;
- how creative authorship is distributed among three specialists rather than concentrated in one leader;
- why dissent, insult, competitive aggression, and refusal of generic friendship can become productive only after sufficient mutual literacy accumulates;
- how Yō's memory can enter IIIX's future without fran or kana replacing Yō or acquiring retrospective ownership of the original promise;
- how individual motives remain plural even when a present shared telos becomes possible;
- how the triad moves from generic friendship performance toward a public image that is selective but answerable to its actual relational grammar;
- why privacy and selective withholding remain compatible with authenticity;
- why `信頼`, `腐れ縁`, repeated voluntary contact, and shared authorship must not be flattened either into conventional friendship or into pure transaction.

The governing architectural boundary is strict:

> **This is a P2-C relationship ledger, not the P2-D IIIX unit ledger.**

Accordingly, this document may stabilize the **internal relational constitution** of IIIX, but it does **not** advance IIIX unit freshness. P2-D still owns unit-level synthesis of performance identity, institutional history, market position, public image, creative governance, external rivalry, and full cross-surface unit state.

# 1. Governing relationship model

The strongest longitudinal formulation is:

> **`REPEATED_REAUTHORIZATION_OF_ADVERSARIAL_INTERDEPENDENCE_IIIX`: IIIX begins as an externally assembled coalition of three already marketable women whose private motives do not coincide. The corporation supplies concept, center, songs, infrastructure, and upward reward; victory allows the members to postpone the question of why these three should remain together. Their first serious defeat forces a provisional answer: they make victory itself the common object. That compact is real but still conditional, and Himeno can exploit precisely that result-centered instrumentalism. I-UNITY defeat and institutional collapse then remove the original bargain entirely. In the first decisive reauthorization, a reduced-venue performance reconnects all three to dimensions of idol work that exceed prestige or utility, and they voluntarily choose to continue without friendship conversion. Subsequent work deepens the constitution rather than sentimentalizing it: creative authority becomes modular and contestable; fran's default center can be challenged locally by kana and miho when the song demands it; miho, fran, and kana increasingly rely on one another's distinct specialist competence; conflict becomes a production method rather than proof of imminent collapse. Yet the old conditionality is not magically cured. In the 2025 U.S. crisis all three original personal projects again make dissolution rational, and the group explicitly agrees to end. The third reauthorization is therefore the strongest: after individual utility has already failed as a sufficient reason to stay, fran, kana, and miho each find a different reason to continue, while miho's recovered audience-happiness promise becomes a shared present-facing telos without requiring motive uniformity or making fran/kana heirs to Yō's original relational position. `Friend Glass` then proves that the same logic can govern memory: the living triad cannot recreate Sundance's relational truth, so it transforms the work into an honestly IIIX-shaped present while preserving provenance. By 2026, the members can explicitly say they trust one another while denying that conventional friendship exists. Their public self-presentation matures accordingly: generic friend branding gives way to selectively curated competitive hostility that viewers recognize as real because it is answerable to how the three actually behave. Mature IIIX is therefore durable not because three opportunists become sentimental friends, but because the same difficult partners repeatedly become non-disposable through chosen work, reciprocal challenge, differentiated authorship, bounded care, shared history, and the demonstrated capacity to survive the collapse of any single justification for staying together.**

Short form:

> **IIIX becomes real each time the reason it was supposed to exist stops being enough—and the same three people choose to author another reason.**

The core paradox is:

> **The triad becomes most durable when no member has to pretend the others satisfy the conventional idol-group role called “friends.”**

A second paradox is equally important:

> **The more IIIX distributes authorship and permits internal difference, the less its cohesion depends on any one member's motive, hierarchy, or inherited memory.**

# 4. Relationship phases

## Phase A — externally authored coalition

At origin, the answer to “why these three?” is primarily institutional:

- they are already marketable;
- their combined prestige is useful;
- the corporation can sell a “triple excellent” concept;
- fran is assigned the center;
- the system supplies songs, costumes, promotion, and competitive opportunity.

This is real work, but not yet a self-authored relationship.

The members do not enter with the same telos:

- miho sees IIIX as a route upward through grief-distorted competitive purpose;
- fran sees idol work as visibility, income, and a route back toward fashion entrepreneurship;
- kana sees visibility as status, self-branding, and a possible signal to an absent father.

The first constitutional mistake would be to call this “three friends with different dreams.”

They are not friends at foundation, and the separate dreams are not merely cosmetic diversity inside an already shared vocation.

## Phase B — victory substitutes for common vocation

Immediate success is relationally stabilizing because it prevents a harder question from becoming urgent.

As long as IIIX wins, the group does not need to decide whether its members actually want the same future.

The first serious defeat changes that.

miho asks whether being with these two has meaning if the unit cannot keep winning. Publicly performed friendship is exposed as public performance: kana can speak of `素敵なお友達`, then privately say `kanaとあんた達がお友達な訳ないじゃん`.

That scene is important because two things become true at once:

1. friendship is not the private constitutional basis;
2. failure still forces actual private disclosure and a stronger relationship than the public fiction.

The first mature-ish common object becomes victory itself.

This is a **real compact**, not fake because it is instrumental.

But it remains a narrow compact.

## Phase C — instrumental solidarity meets institutional capture

Himeno does not invent IIIX's instrumentalism. She finds a group already willing to organize itself around measurable outcome and offers institutional power aligned with that desire.

Kana recognizes the exploitative-adult pattern earlier than the others. Fran increasingly objects to ugliness, liability, and external contamination. miho remains exposed longer because Himeno can serve her uniquely personal anti-Mana project.

The crucial group-level correction is:

> recognizing manipulation is not the same as refusing benefits from manipulation.

IIIX is implicated in motivated toleration.

At the same time, the group preserves an ethical/professional floor: it does not want external sabotage to make its victory causally dependent on someone else's cheating.

This is not moral purification. It is defense of authorship.

## Phase D — I-UNITY and the collapse of result-conditioned meaning

The first constitution says, in effect:

> if we are not the strongest, our being together loses its justification.

I-UNITY makes that proposition dangerous because IIIX loses to a performance event it cannot reduce to incompetence.

When the institution then collapses, the group loses the corporate machine that once answered why IIIX exists.

The members can rationally end.

They nearly do.

## Phase E — second founding: voluntary continuation without friendship conversion

The reduced-venue live is the first major reauthorization.

It returns each member to a dimension of idol work that the prestige machine had obscured:

- miho remembers the audience-facing promise made with Yō;
- fran admits performance itself is not merely unpleasant instrumentality;
- kana experiences immediate spectator response as something affectively exhilarating rather than only measurable reach.

The members then choose continuation despite the absence of corporate necessity.

The vocabulary is deliberately non-sentimental.

Fran can say she trusts the others as the center's companions. miho can say Yō remains her one friend while fran and kana have become a `腐れ縁` after more than two years. Kana does not need a friendship label at all.

The correct transition is:

> **from external assembly to voluntary re-selection, not from dislike to ordinary friendship.**

## Phase F — durable but still conditional: Las Vegas

The second founding does not create unconditional permanence.

In Las Vegas, miho can still say:

> `今勝たなくては、私達が一緒にいる意味はないんだから`

This matters because it prevents a retrospective fantasy in which post-I-UNITY choice permanently resolves every earlier conditionality.

The group temporarily fragments under failure and anonymity.

Yet each member independently finds footing through her own competence, and the trio reconstitutes strategic action.

The relationship can survive temporary spatial/strategic separation because its members no longer require constant interpersonal fusion to function as a collective.

But Las Vegas also demonstrates a less mature form of public construction: IIIX is willing to sell an inflated “Japanese top” claim because the local audience cannot easily verify it.

That is strategically effective, but not yet the later answerable form of authenticity.

## Phase G — differentiated coauthorship

The 2024 new-song project is one of the strongest sources in the locked corpus because it answers **how three antagonistic professionals can create one object without a friendship-based harmony narrative**.

They divide work according to specialist competence:

### kana — verbal and communicative architecture

Kana identifies common keywords, builds lyric structure, and reasons through what will communicate the song's concept clearly.

### miho — choreographic and analytical architecture

miho handles choreography, reference analysis, performance structure, spatial visibility, and optimization.

### fran — material and aesthetic architecture

fran owns costume and stage-art design, connecting bodily presentation to the song's visual world.

This is not a leader assigning subordinate tasks.

It is a **distributed constitution of authority**.

Fran's center role remains real, but it is no longer sovereign over every local choice. When kana argues that the song's meaning and balance require a different center configuration, fran first appeals to inherited status: `スリクスのセンターは私よ`. Kana answers that someone who does not respect the song is not suited to center that section, and miho sides with kana locally.

The crucial mature rule becomes:

> **center status is a role, not unlimited jurisdiction.**

The group can preserve stable differentiation while allowing the work itself to challenge inherited hierarchy.

This is `DIFFERENTIATED_COAUTHORSHIP_WITHOUT_FUSION_IIIX`.

## Phase H — dissent becomes productive infrastructure

IIIX's arguments are not automatically healthy.

At origin, antagonism can produce cruelty, motivated denial, and ethical blind spots.

What changes is that accumulated mutual literacy makes some forms of disagreement useful because members know:

- which competence the other actually possesses;
- which insult is ordinary grammar and which signals real injury;
- when a professional objection should override status;
- when to intervene and when not to sentimentalize;
- which vulnerabilities can be addressed directly;
- which private materials should not be publicly exploited.

Thus “they fight a lot” is not itself the mature insight.

The mature insight is:

> **conflict becomes a usable production and correction mechanism because the members become answerable to shared objects and increasingly legible to one another.**

## Phase I — the 2025 dissolution: second founding is not enough

The U.S. arc is the decisive longitudinal stress test because it occurs **after** IIIX has already voluntarily reselected itself.

Each member's original personal architecture changes:

- kana's father-search project reaches a devastating/deflationary point;
- fran receives a serious fashion reopening that will consume time and alter incentives;
- miho is told her route cannot reach the top and confronts evidence that the “perfect IIIX” story is helping erase Yō.

The three explicitly decide to dissolve.

This proves that voluntary second founding created durable relation, but not an unconditional sacred covenant.

The mature ledger must therefore reject:

> “Once they chose each other after I-UNITY, the group could no longer become instrumental again.”

It could.

## Phase J — third reauthorization: plural motives, common future

The reversal of dissolution is stronger precisely because it does **not** occur through one sentimental revelation.

### Fran does not become altruistically converted

Her first reason to stop the dissolution is openly entangled with the investor's expectation that IIIX continue.

This must remain visible.

Later actions become more relationship-specific—especially spending brand-revival savings on three-person stagewear—but business motive never disappears.

### Fran uses antagonistic intimacy as intervention

She confronts miho over Yō-memory and kana over father-centered self-worth in ways a gentle outsider probably could not.

Her method can be cruel. It is also highly informed.

She knows exactly where their self-narratives are weak.

### miho refuses terminal judgment

miho independently creates a high-profile new contest rather than accepting the claim that IIIX cannot reach the top.

The key change is her defeat grammar: future loss no longer necessarily means the group lacks reason to exist. It can mean “think about how to beat them next time.”

### Fran invests in a three-person body

Her new costumes are neither pure sacrifice nor mere abstract business investment.

They are designed for these three specific performers.

She is embarrassed enough by the personal dimension to conceal the spending, and miho/kana are angry that she made the decision unilaterally.

This is commitment plus governance failure, not one or the other.

### miho's Yō promise becomes present-facing without provenance transfer

miho states the deeper promise:

- reach the top;
- become an idol who can make someone happy.

# 5. Constitutional principles of mature IIIX

## 5.1 Reauthorization outranks origin purity

IIIX does not become legitimate because its founders secretly possessed pure motives.

It becomes legitimate because motives and institutions repeatedly fail to sustain the group—and the members repeatedly choose another basis for continuing.

## 5.2 Trust does not require friendship conversion

The locked source directly permits:

- `腐れ縁`;
- durable chosen continuation;
- privacy protection;

- practical care;
- mutual correction;

while also permitting explicit denial that `友情` exists.

Do not “resolve” that tension by overwriting the source with conventional friendship vocabulary.

## 5.3 Differentiation is a cohesion mechanism

The group works best when each member's jurisdiction is real but bounded.

- fran need not stop being center to lose universal center sovereignty;
- kana need not stop being publicity-minded to become a serious lyric/communication author;
- miho need not stop optimizing to become a choreographic and audience-facing architect.

The audience-happiness formulation can become a common present-facing object while:

- miho remains tied to Yō and top-idol ambition;
- fran remains serious about fashion and commercial leverage;
- kana remains serious about visibility and still searches for a next self-authored goal.

Consensus of action is not identity of interior motive.

## 5.5 Care must remain answerable

The mature group repeatedly exposes a limit of unilateral “help.”

- Himeno demonstrates the extreme case of result-oriented power without accountable authorship.
- Fran's costume investment is materially generous but still legitimately angers the others because it was unilateral.

## 5.6 Adversarial grammar is local, not universally healthy

IIIX's insult-rich relation works because these specific members have years of mutual literacy and demonstrable competence.

It should not be generalized into “hostility is healthier than warmth.”

The same speech from a stranger, superior, or exploitative adult would have different meaning and risk.
