# 1. Governing relationship model

The strongest longitudinal formulation is:

> **`SINGULAR_FORMATIVE_FRIENDSHIP_WITH_ANTI_CAPTIVE_MEMORIAL_CONTINUITY_MIHO_YO`: Yō enters miho's life when miho has few friends and a history of bullying, becoming the one person miho explicitly remembers as her `たった一人の親友`. Yō does not merely comfort her socially; she opens the vocational horizon that becomes miho's adult life, inviting her to an idol live, asking her to audition together, and co-authoring an audience-facing ideal in which the two might stand on stage and make someone happy. Sundance turns that recognition into shared craft, competition, reflection, celebration, and ordinary adulthood. Yō's illness and death therefore destroy both a beloved person and miho's first durable social-vocational world. Yō's final direction is explicitly anti-captive: miho can become a great idol, should not remain trapped by Yō, should find new companions, and should reach the top. miho later narrows that open future into a survivor-authored memorial office in which winning, surpassing or erasing Mana, technical control, and archival supremacy are treated as proof that Yō mattered. The corpus repeatedly separates that interpretation from Yō's actual request. Mature development does not require miho to diminish Yō's singularity. It requires changing the jurisdiction of memory. A remembered Sundance fan falsifies total forgetting; the U.S. archive then proves that private remembrance alone cannot prevent public deletion. miho gradually lets Yō's name, promise, and art circulate through living relationships without surrendering provenance. `Friend Glass` becomes the decisive test: fran and kana cannot recreate the old Yō/miho relational truth, so IIIX transforms the song into its own present relation while publicly identifying it as a precious Sundance work and sending new listeners back to the original. Yō remains the deceased singular best friend and vocational origin partner; the living are not replacements. Continuation becomes a form of fidelity when Yō's causal presence can remain real without functioning as a permanent veto over miho's friendships, body, art, competition, memory of Mana, or future.**

Short form:

> **Yō remains singular most faithfully when miho stops treating singularity as a command to remain captive.**

The deepest relational paradox is:

> **The more miho allows Yō's memory to circulate through changed living forms, the less she needs to erase other people or freeze herself in order to prove that Yō existed.**

A second capstone is useful for downstream synthesis:

> **`PROVENANCE_WITHOUT_POSTHUMOUS_JURISDICTION_MIHO_YO`: a value, promise, work, or self-perception may genuinely originate in a dead relationship without giving that relationship permanent authority over every later form the value is allowed to take.**

# 5. Close readings

## 5.1 Formative recognition: Yō is a person before she is a memorial object

`origin_thrx_001_diamond_mining` blocks the most reductive reading of the relationship immediately.

Before Yō, miho remembers few friends, bullying, and an expectation that social isolation would continue. She then identifies Yō as:

> `私の、たった一人の親友`

Yō's first major function is therefore not “dead motivation.” It is **recognition**.

Yō asks miho to come to an idol live because she is afraid to go alone. She later asks miho to audition together. The sequence matters:

- Yō wants idolhood first;
- Yō trusts miho enough to invite her into the desire;
- miho initially participates because Yō asks;
- the shared project becomes genuinely miho's as well.

The relationship thus precedes and causally produces the vocation later distorted by grief.

### Governing claim

> **`FORMATIVE_RECOGNITION_PRECEDES_MEMORIALIZATION_MIHO_YO`**

Yō must remain reconstructable as a living friend with her own desires, not merely as the retrospective reason miho hates Mana.

---

## 5.2 Shared vocation: their first idol ethic is audience happiness

Yō's “magical girl” analogy establishes the founding telos. The fantasy is not ranking sovereignty. It is transformation plus audience effect: perhaps they can stand on stage and make someone happy.

miho answers that the two of them can do it together.

This is one of the most important chronology constraints in the entire relationship.

The later vocabulary of:

- scores;
- records;
- BIG4 status;
- “the top”;
- beating or erasing Mana;
- technical optimization;

must not be back-projected as the original reason Sundance exists.

The first secure vocation is relational and audience-facing.

### Governing claim

> **`AUDIENCE_FACING_VOCATION_AS_SHARED_ORIGIN_MIHO_YO`**

The relationship does not oppose excellence. Sundance wins, reviews its performances, and aims upward. The correction is narrower: **excellence is originally in service of a living shared performance world, not a substitute for it.**

---

## 5.3 Technical craft belongs to the healthy relationship too

The origins remember Sundance as a place of long post-live reflection and detailed discussion. Later evidence recalls Yō as capable of noticing performance specifics while resisting overreliance on gimmick-like “small tricks.”

This prevents a false moral binary:

> Yō / heart / authenticity **versus** miho / technique / corruption.

miho's technical intelligence is partly genealogical to Sundance itself.

What changes after grief is not that technique suddenly becomes bad. Its **jurisdiction expands**. Measurement and repeatability become tools for solving a memorial injury they cannot actually solve.

### Governing claim

> **`SHARED_CRAFT_WITHOUT_TECHNIQUE_PATHOLOGIZATION_MIHO_YO`**

A later synthesis should preserve the distinction between **capacity** and **sovereign use of capacity**.

---

## 5.4 Ordinary adulthood: the relationship exceeds vocation

The bond story supplies a small but valuable correction to tragedy-dominated memory.

miho remembers her twentieth birthday. Yō's birthday came earlier, but Yō waited until miho could legally drink so the two could try alcohol together. They barely drink, spend the time arguing over whether it tastes sweet or bitter, and the venue closes while they talk.

The point is not alcohol. The point is temporal texture.

Yō belongs to:

- waiting;
- birthdays;
- first adult experiences;
- idle argument;
- shared memory with no grand competitive function.

### Governing claim

> **`ORDINARY_LIFE_PROVENANCE_WITHIN_SINGULAR_FRIENDSHIP_MIHO_YO`**

The relationship should not be modeled only through death scenes and promises.

---

## 5.5 Illness and absence: guilt is real, knowing abandonment is not

`Friend Glass` provides the strongest information-state correction.

During Yō's hospitalization, miho is sent to Taiwan alone because her career cannot simply be paused. At the time:

- miho believes Yō will recover after hospitalization;
- she expects them to stand on stage together again;
- Yō sends photographs showing a healthy face so miho will not worry;
- miho returns to find Yō dramatically thinner and more ill than expected.

miho later asks why she left her friend behind and says that if she had known what would happen she would not have left Yō's side.

The moral emotion is textual: **survivor guilt**.

But guilt does not manufacture retrospective knowledge.

### Governing claim

> **`SURVIVOR_GUILT_WITHOUT_ESTABLISHED_KNOWING_ABANDONMENT_MIHO_YO`**

The relationship ledger therefore rejects any formulation in which miho knowingly chooses career over a friend she already knows is dying.

Yō's reassuring photographs also matter. They are a small final act of care: Yō manages miho's worry by concealing deterioration. The source does not make that deception the central cause of later guilt, but it establishes asymmetrical information clearly enough that culpability must remain bounded.

## 5.6 Yō's final request: anti-captivity is text, not inference

`origin_thrx_002_house_of_cards` preserves Yō's final words with unusual clarity.

Yō acknowledges one regret—she would have liked to beat Mana once—but immediately says that this is about all she regrets because she had genuine fun being paired with miho. She then tells miho:

> `あたしに囚われないで、新しい仲間とトップを取って`

Three propositions are inseparable:

1. **continuation** — miho has a future after Yō;
2. **new companionship** — that future may include people Yō never knew;
3. **ambition** — reaching the top remains legitimate.

This is not a command to erase another dead idol.

### Governing claim

> **`ANTI_CAPTIVITY_TESTAMENT_YO_TO_MIHO`**

The later anti-Mana project cannot be attributed to Yō without reversing the explicit direction of the text.

---

## 5.7 Death transforms reciprocity into interpretation

Yō's death creates an ontological break that this ledger must preserve.

Before death, the relationship is reciprocal:

- Yō can ask;
- miho can answer;
- they can revise each other;
- they can disagree;
- they can make new plans;
- Yō can surprise miho with information or change her mind.

After death, Yō cannot participate in new negotiations inside the locked corpus.

miho can remember Yō, interpret Yō, preserve Yō's words, address Yō imaginatively, perform Yō-associated work, and hope Yō somehow hears. She cannot receive a new ordinary answer from Yō.

That distinction makes **survivor-authored interpretation** ethically central.

### Governing claim

> **`POSTHUMOUS_RELATION_WITHOUT_NEW_RECIPROCAL_AUTHORIZATION_MIHO_YO`**

When miho later says “this is what I must do for Yō,” the statement describes miho's fidelity model unless a surviving fixed Yō utterance actually supports it.

---

## 5.8 Unequal mourning creates a real public injury

`origin_thrx_003_gears_go_awry` stages Yō's death next to the sudden public catastrophe surrounding Mana.

miho watches people grieve Mana everywhere while Yō's death receives almost no coverage. She asks why nobody will say anything for Yō and why one person in the surrounding crowd cannot mourn her too.

The scene establishes a real injury:

> **public memorial distribution is unequal.**

That inequality is not erased merely because miho's later remedy becomes destructive.

### Governing claim

> **`UNEQUAL_PUBLIC_MEMORY_AS_REAL_RELATIONAL_INJURY_MIHO_YO`**

This matters because the relationship is not only private. Yō was also an idol with an audience and a public history. miho is grieving both a person and the disappearance of that person's social trace.

---

## 5.9 The erasure project is survivor-authored memorial jurisdiction

In the same origin, grief becomes a technical program.

miho decides that if Mana has swallowed public memory, she will erase Mana's traces in return. Competitive achievement becomes memorial proof.

The move is psychologically intelligible because records are actionable. Death is not.

But it creates a category error:

- winning can establish competitive strength;
- records can establish historical achievement;
- neither can make Yō's life more real;
- erasing Mana cannot restore Yō's reciprocal presence.

Most importantly, Yō did not ask for it.

### Governing claim

> **`SURVIVOR_AUTHORED_MEMORIAL_OFFICE_MIHO_YO`**

The concept names miho's attempt to appoint herself administrator of what history is allowed to remember. It does **not** describe Yō's will.
