# 0. Responsibility, authority, and central problem

This ledger is the current Phase-2 relationship authority for **Nagase Mana / Nagase Kotono** through `IP-V2-SNAPSHOT-2026-08-13-A`.

Its task is not to decide whether the sisters loved one another. That is textually clear. The difficult problem is how to model a relationship in which **love, deprivation, guilt, professional vocation, death, memory, posthumous agency, imitation, and later self-authorship all remain simultaneously true without one being allowed to erase the others**.

Several reductive accounts fail against the full record:

- **“Mana abandoned Kotono for fame.”** This preserves Kotono's deprivation but incorrectly converts scarce presence into absence of love.
- **“Kotono merely misunderstood; Mana was always a perfect sister.”** This preserves Mana's love but erases real lost time, fame spillover, occupational danger, and unresolved conflict.
- **“Kotono hated Mana's idol career because she was selfish or jealous.”** This ignores ordinary attachment loss, instrumental treatment by peers, and explicit fear that overworked Mana would be injured or killed.
- **“Mana's diary tells Kotono to finish Mana's career.”** The diary records love, unfinished intention, and a song meant for Kotono. Kotono authors the proxy command herself.
- **“Ghost Mana completes the reconciliation by telling Kotono what to do.”** The Hoshimi sequence does the opposite: Mana accepts Kotono's right not to hear her answer, while Kotono settles her own vocation and only then addresses Mana.
- **“Moving on means leaving Mana behind.”** Late material instead lets Kotono love Mana more openly, study her more seriously, admire her professionally, write to her musically, and continue speaking to her after proxy obligation has been rejected.
- **“The late answer song restores the old sisterhood.”** Death remains irreversible. What survives is transformed continuity, not restored living reciprocity.

The strongest relationship model is therefore:

> **`TRANSFORMED_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_PROXY_SUBSTITUTION_MANA_KOTONO`: Mana and Kotono begin in a dense, mutually formative sisterhood whose shared time is materially damaged by Mana's idol career. Kotono mistakes scarcity for reduced love while Mana underestimates how little ordinary sisterhood her profession leaves available. Death freezes the conflict before normal repair is possible. The diary later corrects Kotono's belief that Mana stopped loving her, but Kotono turns that correction into a self-authored mandate to complete Mana's unfinished path. Ghost Mana and Kotono's own later choices dismantle that substitution. Their mature bond survives by changing form—memory, drawings, songs, objects, testimony, professional admiration, grief, rivalry, and ongoing address—without requiring Kotono to live as Mana's continuation.**

The most source-native emblem of this structure appears in the 2026 answer-song event. When Kotono loses one of a matching pair of necklaces, Mana does not recreate the original. She transforms Mana's remaining necklace into two new star pendants and says, in effect:

> **the form can change without changing that the two still match.**

The sisters' relationship later follows the same logic. Its form changes radically. Its significance does not disappear.

# 3. Longitudinal argument

## 3.1 Before idolhood, sisterhood is infrastructure rather than theme

Kotono does not begin from an abstract admiration of Mana. Mana is embedded in the practical construction of Kotono's world. Kotono describes herself as poor at social interaction and Mana as the person who took her by the hand and showed her things. Their family is musical. Shared playing, singing, outings, meals, shopping, and drawings are not merely charming background details; they establish the baseline against which later scarcity becomes legible.

This distinction matters. If Mana were only an admired celebrity sister, Kotono's later anger could be explained largely through status comparison. The source instead gives Kotono a pre-professional attachment system in which Mana is ordinary, available, and constitutive. Idolhood does not create the relationship. It reorganizes the conditions under which the relationship can exist.

The shared notebook is the first important object in this ledger. When Kotono draws a lonely girl, Mana adds a second girl. The sisters continue adding paired figures until the notebook functions like an exchange diary. The object already demonstrates a principle that will govern the mature relationship: **relation can survive changes in medium when direct presence is constrained**.

At this stage, however, changed medium is still compensatory. Kotono wants actual time with Mana.

## 3.2 “I love both” is true and insufficient

Mana's early-career origin gives the conflict its clearest non-sentimental form. Kotono does not complain in abstractions. She names what has disappeared: dinners, long conversations, shopping, time together. She says Mana now comes home around midnight and that shared time is almost nonexistent.

When Kotono asks whether Mana loves her or idol activity more, Mana refuses the zero-sum premise and says she loves both.

This is a truthful answer. It is also not a resource-allocation solution.

Mana cannot simultaneously preserve the previous quantity of ordinary sister time and accept an accelerating professional schedule. The text itself recognizes this. Mana apologizes for making Kotono lonely. Her private diary records guilt. The manager offers to reduce work. Mana considers whether quitting might stop the damage.

The relationship therefore cannot be modeled as a simple misunderstanding in which clear communication would have solved everything. There is a **material conflict of goods**:

- Mana's self-authored vocation is real and valuable;
- Kotono's need for sisterly presence is real and not childish merely because it conflicts with that vocation;
- Mana's audience commitments are real;
- Mana's body and time are finite;
- Kotono's social world is also being altered by Mana's fame whether Mana intends it or not.

The correct longitudinal formulation is not “Mana chose work over Kotono” or “Mana never chose between them.” It is:

> **Mana refuses to rank love and vocation, but her schedule repeatedly allocates time toward vocation in ways that materially cost the relationship.**

## 3.3 Kotono's anti-idol stance contains care as well as protest

The accident material prevents a shallow reading of Kotono as merely possessive. Kotono sees Mana as exhausted and explicitly worries that someone must notice because Mana will not complain for herself. She asks Mana to quit partly because she wants the old two-person life back, but also because she is afraid Mana will be hurt.

That mixture is important. Attachment protest and protective care are not cleanly separable here.

Kotono's fear is also retrospectively tragic. The story later makes an accident the mechanism that permanently removes Mana. This does not make Kotono prophetically “right” that idolhood must have ended in death. It does make her safety anxiety emotionally and causally serious rather than petulant foreshadowing.

Mana, meanwhile, does not dismiss Kotono's concern as ignorance. She privately considers it. She still concludes that she does not want to quit.

This is one of the relationship's foundational ethical asymmetries: **loving a person does not automatically grant authority to veto that person's vocation, even when the vocation imposes real relational costs and risk.** Conversely, autonomy does not make those costs unreal.

## 3.4 Both sisters attempt repair, but pride and scarcity keep missing the window

The later pre-death material is full of near-repair rather than total estrangement.

Kotono wants Mana to ask again after she refuses an amusement-park invitation. Mana does not. Kotono regrets it. When Mana later invites her to a movie, Kotono accepts and experiences the outing primarily as precious time with her sister. The sisters briefly recover the feeling of ordinary closeness.

This makes the final conflict more painful, not less. They are not people who have ceased loving one another. They are people who have demonstrated that reconnection remains possible but do not secure enough ordinary time to stabilize it.

As the NVGP approaches, Mana's professional horizon expands again. Kotono interprets the final as Mana moving farther away. Mana instead imagines the final as a place where her profession might finally communicate something she has been unable to say effectively at home.

The sisters therefore assign opposite meanings to the same stage:

- for Mana, the stage can become a **bridge back to Kotono**;
- for Kotono, the stage appears as the **institution that keeps taking Mana away**.

That is the structural tragedy of `song for you` before death. Mana tries to repair sisterhood through the professional medium that Kotono experiences as the source of rupture.

## 3.5 The last words are attachment protest, not literal indifference

Hoshimi later restores the final living argument in especially useful form. Kotono complains about the way Mana's fame has colonized her school life: classmates want signatures and access; Kotono feels constantly pulled around by Mana's career.

Her internal narration then supplies the key correction to her own speech. What she wants is simple: she wants Mana nearby **as an ordinary older sister**.

The spoken line that follows—wishing Mana were not there—is therefore not a stable proposition about Kotono's desires. It is an attachment-protest utterance generated by a wish for the opposite kind of presence.

That does not make the words harmless. They become the final words before death, and Kotono cannot revise them through an ordinary later conversation. The relationship is frozen with a mismatch between what Kotono meant, what she said, and what she will later wish she had said.

This is why posthumous interpretation matters so much. Death removes the possibility of easy conversational repair and makes objects, records, remembered acts, and eventually ghost dialogue carry burdens they were never designed to carry.

## 3.6 The diary repairs knowledge, then becomes dangerous because Kotono overauthorizes it

The diary is the first posthumous source with privileged access to Mana's private side. It proves several things Kotono did not know in life:

- Mana repeatedly thought about Kotono;
- Mana felt guilty about the relationship;
- Mana continued to love Kotono throughout her career;
- Mana was writing a song specifically for Kotono;
- the NVGP final mattered partly because Mana wanted to deliver that song to her sister.

This corrects Kotono's earlier epistemic model. Work scarcity did **not** mean Mana had ceased loving her.

But the repair immediately generates a new error. Kotono reads Mana's unfinished intention and says, in substance, **I must do what she could not do in her place**.

The logical transition is authored by Kotono. It is not written in Mana's diary.

This is the central Phase-2 revision:

> **Evidence of what the dead person loved and intended is not automatically a command governing the survivor's future.**

The relationship becomes most dangerous when Kotono treats documentary access to Mana's private love as if it grants a complete posthumous constitution for Kotono's life.

## 3.7 Ghost Mana is a direct agent—and one of the strongest witnesses against proxy inheritance

IDOLY PRIDE's diegesis complicates ordinary grief analysis because Mana is not merely metaphorically present. Ghost Mana can directly speak, know, worry, and choose.

That does not produce unlimited jurisdiction. On the contrary, ghost Mana explicitly rejects the most tempting substitution model.

In `hoshimi_018_idoly_pride`, Mana says her song is hers. She regrets not singing it, but someone else performing it cannot resolve *her* regret because it was Mana's act to perform. Sakura must find Sakura's own song. Kotono likewise must sing a song only Kotono can sing.

This is relationship evidence because it directly answers Kotono's diary-generated proxy theory. The person whom Kotono is supposedly honoring does **not** ask her to become a substitute.

Ghost Mana thereby distinguishes:

- **memory** from replication;
- **influence** from commission;
- **unfinished intention** from transferable obligation;
- **love** from jurisdiction.

## 3.8 The first farewell is not the endpoint

Kotono's `Still Live` movement forward matters, but the source refuses a one-scene cure. She thinks that she will move ahead rather than chase Mana and produces a spontaneous goodbye onstage.

Soon afterward she is still watching Mana's image and asking whether it is really acceptable to search for her own song. She worries that as Mana's sister she may still be misunderstanding what Mana wanted.

This residual doubt is crucial. It prevents the relationship from being modeled as “Kotono lets go of Mana.” The actual problem is **who has authority to determine what fidelity to Mana requires**.

As long as Kotono thinks she needs the correct answer from Mana before she may become herself, the posthumous relationship remains jurisdictionally unstable.

## 3.9 The one-sided Hoshimi conversation is the relationship's constitutional hinge

`hoshimi_020_on_my_way` provides the cleanest direct settlement.

Kotono has wanted to speak to Mana. She has questions and apologies. She wants to know what Mana thought in life and thinks now.

Then Kotono chooses a different form of dialogue:

- she will speak;
- she does not want Mana's voice mediated back to her;
- she will not ask Mana to decide the vocational question;
- she must settle it herself.

Before Kotono enters, Mana is told these terms and accepts them: if that is what Kotono wants, Mana agrees.

Kotono then tells Mana that she finally understands why idol work could matter so much. Crucially, she does **not** conclude that she must therefore become Mana.

She states instead:

- she will no longer chase Mana;
- she no longer wants to stand in the final in Mana's place;
- she will become a better idol for audiences;
- she will find the idol she herself wants to become;
- she will walk toward that future herself.

The scene preserves address. Kotono still wants Mana to know. She still hopes Mana is watching. What disappears is the requirement that Mana **adjudicate** Kotono's future.

This is the mature relationship's first constitutional form:

> **I can speak to you without asking you to govern me.**

The text then gives Kotono a subjective sensation of warmth/embrace and the feeling that she is being told that her choice is acceptable. Because Kotono cannot directly see or hear Mana, this should not be converted into a precise audiovisual or metaphysical claim. What is secure is the asymmetry of choice: Kotono's settlement is declared before any felt approval.

## 3.10 Later Kotono can know Mana more, not less, because she no longer needs one totalizing answer

The 2026 answer-song event is not a regression into Mana obsession. It is possible because the Hoshimi anti-proxy settlement has already occurred.

Kotono recognizes an epistemic limitation that an earlier version of her did not: **being Mana's sister gives privileged knowledge of family Mana, but it does not make Kotono the sole or complete historian of idol Mana**.

This is a major relationship maturation. Grief often encourages possession of the dead through privileged memory: *I knew her best, therefore my version is the real one*. Kotono instead pluralizes the record.

She asks people who knew Mana in different roles:

- Suzu as admirer/witness;
- Haruko as older professional acquaintance;
- Rio as rival;
- Sakura through the extraordinary heart/voice inheritance history.

The result is not the discovery of a secret “real Mana” behind a false family version. It is a more complete person built from non-identical viewpoints.

This protects Mana from being reduced to either:

- Kotono's beloved older sister;
- or the public legendary idol.

She was both, and more.

## 3.11 The pendant supplies the relationship's most precise object grammar

The matching pendant history is unusually important because it gives the narrative an explicit material formulation of transformed continuity.

Kotono once makes Mana a matching beaded necklace. Kotono later loses hers. Mana responds by dismantling and remaking Mana's necklace into two new matching star pendants. She acknowledges that they are not restored to the original form, then states that **changed form does not change the fact that they match**.

This is almost a miniature theory of the entire sisterhood.

The mature relationship cannot return to:

- childhood;
- ordinary co-residence;
- pre-idol abundance of time;
- a living chance to revise the last argument;
- symmetrical present-tense reciprocity.

But those losses do not require either substitution or erasure.

The relationship instead changes medium:

- drawings;
- diary;
- songs;
- pendant;
- testimony;
- remembered places;
- inherited professional standards;
- grief rituals;
- direct address.

The correct model is therefore not restoration. It is **continuity through transformed form**.

## 3.12 Kotono's discovery that she supported Mana revises the relationship's moral accounting

For much of Kotono's grief, the sisterhood is morally narrated from one direction:

- Mana cared for Kotono;
- Kotono rejected Mana;
- Mana died;
- Kotono failed to understand her;
- Kotono therefore owes the dead sister something.

The late pendant discovery materially revises that accounting.

Kotono finds Mana's matching pendant preserved in the bag Mana carried on the day of the NVGP final. In conjunction with testimony that Mana looked at the pendant when discouraged, Kotono reaches a new conclusion: **she was also a source of support to Mana**.

The significance is not that all guilt was baseless. Kotono did say cruel things, and the living relationship did remain unresolved. The revision is that the relationship was never simply benefactor/debtor.

Kotono mattered to Mana even during conflict.

This discovery permits a different kind of grief expression. Kotono breaks into open crying and says directly that she loves Mana. Because proxy duty has already been rejected, explicit love no longer functions as evidence that Kotono must surrender self-authorship.

## 3.13 `With your songs` is an answer, not a completion

The late song's title and construction matter.

Mana wrote `song for you` as an intended address to Kotono. Kotono later writes `With your songs` as an **answer song**.

An answer differs structurally from completion:

- it assumes another voice already exists;
- it responds from a different position;
- it need not reproduce the original speaker;
- it can incorporate changed knowledge;
- it preserves relation through difference.

Kotono's song is also pluralized. People who knew Mana from different positions contribute to its completion and performance. The sisterhood therefore becomes less possessive as it becomes more articulate.

At the late frontier Kotono can describe Mana simultaneously as:

- beloved older sister;
- legendary idol;
- highest professional goal.

She wants to shine strongly enough for competitive Mana to notice her and asks Mana to continue watching.

This is not proxy life. It is relational ambition.

Mana is no longer the route Kotono must repeat. Mana is a person Kotono can answer, love, study, admire, and compete with symbolically while remaining the author of Kotono's route.

# 7. Countermodels and rejection audit

## 7.1 “Mana abandoned Kotono for idolhood” — REJECT AS OVERSTRONG

What it gets right:

- Mana's career genuinely removes time;
- Kotono experiences repeated absence;
- Mana repeatedly returns late and has little ordinary availability;
- Kotono's social world is affected by Mana's fame.

Why it fails:

- Mana keeps trying to contact Kotono;
- Mana apologizes and privately records guilt;
- Mana continues thinking about Kotono in her diary;
- Mana wants Kotono at the final;
- Mana writes a song specifically to convey her feelings to Kotono;
- the late pendant evidence indicates Kotono herself was a sustaining attachment for Mana.

Correct replacement:

> **attachment under profession-driven scarcity.**

## 7.2 “Kotono's pain was based on a misunderstanding and therefore not justified” — REJECT

The later diary does correct a belief: Mana's reduced presence did not mean reduced love.

It does **not** retroactively restore:

- missed meals;
- lost outings;
- late returns;
- school celebrity spillover;
- danger/overwork;
- the unresolved final conflict.

An epistemic mistake can coexist with a materially accurate complaint.

## 7.3 “Kotono wanted Mana to fail because she hated her success” — REJECT AS REDUCTIVE

Kotono sometimes does wish Mana would stop or not reach the final. But the source identifies the underlying structure:

- Mana feels farther away as success grows;
- Kotono wants ordinary sister presence;
- she fears Mana's physical deterioration;
- she regrets missed chances for contact.

Professional success is threatening because of what it does to attachment conditions, not merely because Kotono resents status.

The diary supplies facts about Mana.

Kotono supplies the rule about Kotono.

The difference is textually visible in the sequence of inference and is later confirmed by ghost Mana's anti-replacement position.

## 7.5 “Ghost Mana validates the proxy mission” — REJECT

Late Kotono:

The evidence strongly contradicts detachment as the only route to individuation.

The pendant has robust textual meaning as a shared object and support. That does not by itself establish supernatural transmission. The relationship model does not need such a claim.

# 8. Relationship asymmetries that must remain visible

## 8.1 Knowledge asymmetry

Living Kotono does not know the full professional Mana. Living Mana knows more of Kotono's loneliness than her public brightness suggests, but still underestimates what the career's scarcity does to Kotono.

After death, the asymmetry reverses in part: Kotono obtains diary access Mana never received to Kotono's later life. Ghost Mana has some direct knowledge but remains inaccessible to Kotono for most of the period.

The mature solution is not omniscience. It is **plural testimony plus bounded authority**.

## 8.2 Temporal asymmetry

Mana's biography stops. Kotono's continues.

That means Mana cannot reciprocally revise herself through years of new experience in the same way Kotono can. Any “rivalry” or “goal” relationship after death is structurally asymmetric and should not be written as though two living professionals continue a normal contest.

## 8.3 Vocational asymmetry

Mana's vocation is self-authored in life. Kotono's first vocation is partly proxy-authored by Kotono in response to Mana's death. Kotono later makes idolhood her own.

The similarity of profession therefore has different causal origins.

## 8.4 Emotional-accounting asymmetry

Kotono initially narrates herself as the one who failed Mana. Late material reveals that Kotono also supported Mana. This strengthens reciprocity without making the relationship perfectly balanced or erasing guilt.

## 8.5 Presence asymmetry

Posthumous address can be meaningful within the diegesis. It is still not identical to the ordinary availability Kotono wanted in life.

The source's emotional force depends on preserving that distinction.
