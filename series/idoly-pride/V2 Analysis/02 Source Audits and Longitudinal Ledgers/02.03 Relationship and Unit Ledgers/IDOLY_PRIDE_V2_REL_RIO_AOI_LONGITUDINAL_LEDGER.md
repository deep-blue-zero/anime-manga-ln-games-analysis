# 1. Governing relationship model

The strongest longitudinal formulation is:

> **`CHOSEN_PARTNERSHIP_THROUGH_ANSWERABLE_DIFFERENCE_RIO_AOI`: Rio and Aoi do not become partners because they are naturally alike or because one completes a passive deficit in the other. They become partners because difference makes each woman's unrecognized self legible. Aoi's embodied freedom exposes the gap between Rio's technically correct performance and her lived enjoyment; Rio's structure, ambition, and professional seriousness give Aoi a reason to turn private pleasure into a durable shared vocation. Aoi then chooses Rio specifically. Across the relationship, singular particularity persists—Aoi refuses substitute partners, both repeatedly call the other their one partner, and private/domestic material explicitly names the other as special—but mature fidelity is not ownership. Aoi can let Rio leave while choosing to wait; Rio can later accept Aoi's self-authored withdrawal while refusing to treat it as a terminal goodbye. Their most serious failures occur when care or duty becomes unilateral: Aoi hides dissolution stakes and injury in order to protect Rio, while Rio later buries personal desire beneath leader responsibility and external proof. Their strongest repairs restore answerability. They disclose, argue, negotiate, accept correction, permit the other to make consequential choices, and return one another to suppressed or forgotten desire. The relationship therefore develops from productive complementarity into reciprocal self-recognition. Its mature specialness remains dyadically exceptional without making Ai and Kokoro secondary members: present LizNoir is exactly four, and the founding pair's most mature act is not to monopolize unit identity but to let a lesson first learned between two circulate through all four.**

Short form:

> **They remain singular partners because neither must become, obey, or own the other.**

The deepest relational paradox is:

> **Rio and Aoi become more durable as a pair when disagreement, temporary distance, outside relationships, and a four-person unit cease to be treated as threats to the pair's existence.**

A second capstone formulation is useful for downstream synthesis:

> **`RECIPROCAL_SELF_RETURN_RIO_AOI`: each woman becomes a privileged witness capable of recognizing when the other is performing a role that has become alienated from her own desire, and—without possessing final jurisdiction over the other's choice—can help return that desire to speech, movement, and action.**

# 4. Longitudinal relationship states

## 4.1 State I — productive incompleteness before partnership

The first Rio/Aoi relation is not “serious girl meets carefree girl” in a merely comedic sense. The source constructs an epistemic asymmetry. Rio has technique, discipline, and goal pressure but cannot see what her own dance has lost. Aoi sees it almost immediately because her bodily relation to dance is different.

Aoi's intervention is precise: Rio's movement can be correct while failing to reach people because enjoyment and body are disconnected. This is important for the whole relationship. Aoi's value to Rio is never reducible to cheering Rio up. She can perceive failures generated by Rio's own strengths.

Rio in turn does something equally important: she asks Aoi to dance *with* her. Aoi's spontaneous movement becomes relational rather than solitary. Rio's seriousness gives Aoi an external structure in which intrinsic enjoyment can become a shared vocation.

The pair is therefore founded by **difference that generates information**.

### State claim

`PRODUCTIVE_INCOMPLETENESS_PRECEDES_PARTNERSHIP_RIO_AOI`

Neither woman is the other's completed solution. Each makes a problem visible that the other cannot easily see alone.

## 4.2 State II — Aoi chooses Rio; professional pairing becomes particular attachment

Aoi's `莉央がいい` / `莉央と一緒に踊らせて` matters because it prevents the relationship from being described as pure agency design. Staff may create the opportunity, but Aoi supplies a direct choice.

That choice is repeated later under pressure. Aoi calls Rio her one partner, rejects replacements during Rio's absence, and continues orienting her professional future around the possibility of Rio's return. Particularity is therefore not a one-scene flourish.

But the correct category is **particularity**, not possession. Aoi's choice binds Aoi's own future orientation; it does not give her unilateral authority over Rio's.

## 4.3 State III — conflict becomes a communication technology

The C2 retrospective card establishes that early LizNoir was not harmonious offstage. Aoi skipped or resisted structures; Rio over-instructed; they fought despite perfectly coordinated dancing.

The turning point comes after conflict when Aoi goes directly to Rio and they spend the night discussing their different values, what they want from the first solo concert, and how LizNoir should operate. The resulting performance succeeds because both incorporate the other's intent.

Rio later calls that night the possible “real beginning” of their relationship. She also interprets Aoi's early complaints as Aoi's own imperfect communication method and says her condition for continuing with Aoi was to meet her seriously and directly.

This establishes a crucial mature rule early:

> **agreement is not the condition of partnership; answerable engagement is.**

## 4.4 State IV — Mana gives the pair a shared external horizon

Mana's importance to the dyad is not merely that both admire a powerful idol. She creates a worthy contest through which Aoi's otherwise non-institutional dancing acquires a desire to win *with Rio*.

Rio's competitive obsession and Aoi's more conditional relation to ranking remain different. Yet Mana gives their shared performance a question neither can answer alone: what can this pair become against a rival whose result would mean something?

This matters later because Mana's death removes a future-bearing rival at the same time it destabilizes Rio herself. The dyad loses both an opponent and Rio's capacity to continue.

## 4.5 State V — nonpossessive waiting

Rio's departure after Mana's death is the first severe test of whether singular partnership means ownership.

Aoi does **not** command Rio to stay. She says she will not stop her. She then authors her own stance: she will wait, and she will not say goodbye.

This is one of the corpus's strongest examples of **nonpossessive particularity**. Aoi refuses to pretend that Rio is interchangeable, but she also refuses to convert her attachment into jurisdiction over Rio's decision.

The replacement refusals intensify the particularity. Aoi is offered multiple plausible substitutes and rejects them. Yet the correct inference is not that Aoi could never form another meaningful bond in any possible future; it is that this professional/relational position is not fungible to her in the actual history.

When Rio returns, Aoi points back to the earlier declaration that Rio is her one partner. Rio's private, burdened future is then rewritten as `僕らの未来`—their future.

## 4.6 State VI — protective care becomes unilateral jurisdiction

Aoi's strongest relational failure grows directly out of attachment.

The pair has an explicit communication norm. Rio tells Aoi about consequential matters because they have promised to tell each other. Yet when Aoi learns that failure could dissolve LizNoir, she asks staff to conceal the fact from Rio. She later hides an injury for the same reason: Rio is already carrying too much; Aoi wants to protect her and deliver victory.

The motive is caring. The method is defective.

Aoi decides on Rio's behalf what Rio should be allowed to know about the future of their partnership. This removes Rio's opportunity to answer the risk as an equal participant.

Rio's confrontation is therefore relationship-constitutional. `私達はパートナーじゃなかったの！？` does not mean “because I am your partner I control your body.” It means the stakes belonged to both of them and should have been answerable by both.

Aoi's explicit declaration—Rio is an irreplaceable partner whom she trusts more than anyone—makes the failure more, not less, important. Trust as feeling is present; trust as shared jurisdiction is temporarily absent.

Rio's emotional appreciation of Aoi's motive does not ratify secrecy as a general norm. A relationship ledger must preserve both propositions simultaneously.

## 4.7 State VII — defeat without relationship invalidation

After the Tsuki defeat, Aoi converts injury into personal causal guilt. She wanted to make Rio win; she experiences the result as if her body had stolen Rio's future.

Rio refuses this moral accounting. She says LizNoir is Rio *and* Aoi, credits Aoi with making her own career possible, and says she was happy to be Aoi's partner.

The loss remains institutionally consequential. It does not become trivial. But it loses sovereign authority to decide whether the relationship was worthwhile.

This is a foundational pair-level instance of the series-wide principle that **results matter without possessing total jurisdiction over meaning**.

## 4.8 State VIII — historically real founder exclusivity

Immediately after the loss, both founders explicitly resist adding Ai and Kokoro. Aoi says LizNoir needs no members besides herself and Rio; Rio shares the underlying founder-exclusive model.

This state must not be erased by mature hindsight. The later four-person ontology is developmental precisely because duo exclusivity was once real.

At this point, “one partner” and “only real LizNoir members” are partially fused concepts. The subsequent arc separates them.

## 4.9 State IX — quartet expansion without dyad erasure

Ai and Kokoro do not simply occupy empty roster slots. Their presence reveals a problem the founders cannot solve by returning to old two-person mastery: Rio/Aoi's performance can become technically formidable but emotionally machine-like.

The younger pair helps restore heat, flexibility, and collective authorship. By the end of the reconstitution, Rio/Aoi can remain the founding dyad while recognizing four-person LizNoir as the current unit.

This is a categorical revision of founder exclusivity, not a demotion of founder specialness.

The later relationship therefore contains two simultaneously true propositions:

1. Rio and Aoi remain each other's singular founding partner in a way not replicated by Ai/Kokoro.
2. Ai and Kokoro are not secondary or derivative members of mature LizNoir.

Any synthesis that resolves this tension by deleting either side is weaker than the source.

## 4.10 State X — purpose conflict and self-authored withdrawal

Leadership recreates Rio's old dissociation in a new form. Earlier she separated body from enjoyment under family/professional pressure. Now she separates personal desire from role under leader-duty and summit pressure.

Aoi detects the mismatch. She asks the question that Rio's institutional ambition cannot answer: is this still enjoyable, and for whom are they pursuing the top?

Their disagreement becomes serious enough that Aoi suspends activity. The text later explicitly rejects a convenient external-causation model: Rara/Lanlan exploits the fracture, but Aoi owns the choice.

Rio's eventual response is one of the relationship's most important reversals. Earlier Aoi let Rio leave and refused goodbye. Now Rio accepts Aoi's self-authored distance while likewise refusing to interpret it as terminal severance.

The symmetry is not perfect—Rio places expectations around return—but the grammar has become reciprocal: **distance can be permitted without relation being declared dead.**

## 4.11 State XI — reciprocal self-return

The crisis ends only when both women recover a self the other can recognize.

Aoi discovers that she herself has changed. The old self-description “dance alone is enough” no longer fits a person who cannot stop thinking about Rio and LizNoir.

Rio, pushed by Ai/Kokoro, finally admits a self-interested desire beneath leadership: Kanzaki Rio wants the stage and summit for herself.

Aoi sees that performance and recognizes Rio again. Her formulation is unusually explicit: both she and Rio had forgotten the “real Rio.”

Aoi's return therefore is not submission to Rio's original goal. It is renewed choice after both partners become newly self-legible.

At reunion, Rio recalls Aoi's year of waiting and frames her own wait as balancing the relation rather than collecting repayment. Aoi accepts the “even” formulation. Their history becomes reciprocal without becoming a debt ledger.

## 4.12 State XII — dissent becomes a preserved relational office

The 2024 commercial event is decisive because it shows what mature disagreement looks like after reconciliation.

Rio sees a large commercial opportunity and believes growth may require adaptation. Aoi thinks the work is becoming so generic and off-brand that LizNoir's participation loses meaning. Aoi privately tells Rio, directly, `今の莉央は変だよ`.

They do not immediately converge. Rio argues for change, dialogue, and professional expression of another party's intent; Aoi insists there are compromises she will not accept for ranking or name recognition.

Rio's response is not “stop challenging me because I am the leader.” She says:

`貴方はそれでいいわ、葵`

Aoi should remain Aoi—the person who says what must be said.

The event does not make Aoi automatically right. Indeed, the eventual solution incorporates Rio's insistence on dialogue and adaptation as well as Aoi's insistence that LizNoir cannot become interchangeable content. What becomes canonical is the **function of dissent**.

Aoi is not merely tolerated as a difficult partner. Rio preserves Aoi's contradiction as a guardrail against Rio's own leader/metric capture.

The event deliberately ends with the deeper summit question unresolved. Mature partnership can hold an open problem without dissolving into either false consensus or relational panic.

## 4.13 State XIII — the founding lesson reverses direction

The 2025 creative-leadership event produces the clearest mature reversal.

Aoi voluntarily takes responsibility for creating a new LizNoir song around “freedom” and “passion.” Responsibility then induces a failure strikingly similar to early Rio's: Aoi over-intellectualizes the task until spontaneous movement is blocked.

Rio recognizes the deviation because she knows Aoi over years, not because she possesses infallible access to Aoi's interior. Her intervention is almost an inversion of origin 001:

- early Aoi to Rio: stop performing correct movement disconnected from enjoyment; dance freely;
- mature Rio to Aoi: you are thinking too much; dance freely; that is when you are most alive.

Aoi then asks Rio to dance **with** her. Ideas begin to arrive through shared movement.

# 7. Relationship ontology and category controls

## 7.1 What “partner” means here

`パートナー` is unusually load-bearing for Rio/Aoi. It carries at least four overlapping functions:

1. **performance partner** — the person whose movement is constitutively coupled with one's own;
2. **professional cofounder** — the other person with whom original LizNoir became a durable project;
3. **attachment category** — the non-interchangeable person one waits for, trusts, quarrels with, cooks/eats with, and describes as special;
4. **self-recognition witness** — the person whose difference makes one's own role distortions legible.

The ledger should neither collapse all four into “romance” nor strip the term down to a merely technical coworker label.

## 7.2 Particularity without possession

Aoi's replacement refusal and waiting year demonstrate particularity. The absence of coercion at Rio's departure demonstrates the boundary.

The mature model therefore rejects both extremes:

- **interchangeability model:** “any sufficiently good dancer could occupy Rio/Aoi's place”;
- **ownership model:** “because the partner is singular, one partner has jurisdiction over the other's exit, body, information, or future.”

The source supports **chosen particular fidelity** instead.

## 7.3 Special dyad, exact-four unit

The mature architecture is not “two plus two.”

Rio/Aoi carry historical intimacy, specific partner language, and a correction channel unmatched by the juniors. Ai/Kokoro carry constitutive unit membership, creative authority, burden-sharing rights, and the ability to correct/carry the founders.

This is best represented as nested but non-hierarchically exhaustive roles:

> **founding dyad ≠ whole unit**  
> **whole unit ≠ erasure of founding dyad**

## 7.4 Relationship and `ANSWERABLE_CONSTITUTIONALISM`

This relationship provides a local application of the inherited architecture without requiring the concept to be forced onto every scene.

The relevant constitutional question is: **who has standing to decide?**

- Rio may decide whether to leave after Mana's death; Aoi may decide whether to wait.
- Aoi may decide what to do with her body, but partnership-relevant dissolution/injury stakes cannot safely be converted into unilateral information control.
- Aoi may decide to suspend activity; Rio may oppose the choice and preserve a future-oriented claim without rewriting it as Aoi's non-choice.
- Rio may lead LizNoir; Aoi retains standing to dissent.
- Aoi may initiate creative responsibility; the completed work can still become shared authorship.

The mature relationship does not eliminate influence. It makes influence increasingly **answerable**.
