# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — AUTHENTICITY, PERSONA, PUBLICITY, MEDIA, AND SELECTIVE DISCLOSURE LONGITUDINAL LEDGER

## P2-E-04 — answerable construction with selective jurisdiction

---

# 0. Analytical responsibility

This is the fourth P2-E theme/institution longitudinal ledger.

P2-E-01 established that causal importance does not automatically create ownership. P2-E-02 established domain-bounded intervention and professional care without goal seizure. P2-E-03 established transformed continuity without posthumous jurisdiction. P2-E-04 asks what those limits look like when a person's profession requires **being seen**, when audiences and institutions operate through compressed public representations, and when performers deliberately construct personas, select what to disclose, stage intimacy, edit footage, manage scandals, cultivate reputations, or choose not to expose parts of themselves.

The governing problem is not “which self is real?” The source repeatedly makes that question too simple. An idol can be genuinely cheerful onstage and exhausted later. A harsh private voice can be a stable register without becoming the metaphysical truth beneath a fake smile. A hobby can be both privately loved and professionally broadcast. A deliberately manufactured persona can become an owned disposition through practice. A public counter-story can be both defensible and incomplete. A strategically edited video can be more representative than a supposedly raw clip. A factual status claim can remain false even when the speaker later proves worthy of the status.

This ledger therefore owns the cross-unit mechanism linking:

- constructed persona and practiced identity;
- public/private/mixed registers;
- selective disclosure and privacy jurisdiction;
- scandal, tabloid framing, and counter-public narration;
- status claims, hype, publicity, and factual answerability;
- editing, channel formats, and strategically selected “ordinary” content;
- performed intimacy versus private relational fact;
- fan curiosity and the boundary between reciprocal knowing and extraction;
- public memorial/media curation where omission changes historical representation;
- audience reinterpretation of an authored public object;
- the difference between representational authenticity and moral innocence.

It does **not** replace local character ledgers, the IIIX unit ledger, relationship ledgers, P2-E-03 public-memory ownership, P2-E-02 confidentiality/management ownership, P2-E-05 audience-reciprocity ownership, P2-E-06 labor ownership, P2-E-08 identity/vocation ownership, or later audiovisual/voice analysis.

# 1. Inherited boundaries from P2-E-01 through P2-E-03

P2-E-01's central rule remains binding:

> **causal importance creates duties of answerability; it does not automatically create ownership.**

For publicity, this means audience attention, fan loyalty, management investment, or institutional dependence do not automatically create ownership of a performer's whole private self.

P2-E-02 adds:

> **protect the capacity; do not seize the goal.**

Applied here, a manager or institution may legitimately manage professional consequences, but professional care does not automatically justify total private access or forced public confession.

P2-E-03 adds:

> **preserve provenance; transform continuation; do not make absence sovereign.**

Applied here, public memorialization is not automatically faithful. Media can preserve, transform, compress, or erase provenance. Public visibility is therefore a representational problem as well as an attention problem.

P2-E-04's specific question becomes:

> **When is a constructed public form an authored, truthful interface rather than a misleading claim, coerced exposure, or representational capture?**

---

# 2. Governing thematic model

The strongest formulation across the frozen snapshot is:

> **`AUTHENTICITY_AS_ANSWERABLE_CONSTRUCTION_WITH_SELECTIVE_JURISDICTION`: IDOLY PRIDE does not define authenticity as rawness, spontaneity, private disclosure, or freedom from strategy. Public selves are routinely made through rehearsal, styling, editing, role performance, publicity planning, selective revelation, and audience-facing craft. Such construction can remain authentic when it is answerable to what it claims to represent and when the person or relationship retains legitimate jurisdiction over what does not need to be public. Construction becomes ethically or epistemically defective when it materially invites a false factual belief, seizes another person's privacy, deletes provenance necessary to the represented story, turns audience interest into entitlement, or treats authorship itself as immunity from correction.**

The multi-register corollary is:

> **`MULTIREGISTER_SELFHOOD_WITHOUT_PRIVATE_SELF_MONOPOLY`: a person can inhabit professional, private, fan, family, competitive, theatrical, and mediated registers that are differently filtered without one register automatically becoming the only real self.**

The disclosure corollary is:

> **`SELECTIVE_DISCLOSURE_WITHOUT_SHAME_OR_ERASURE`: mature privacy is compatible with authenticity when the person no longer treats the private domain as disqualifying, yet still decides whether and how it becomes public.**

The public-narrative corollary is:

> **`PUBLIC_SELF_NARRATION_WITHOUT_DOCUMENTARY_COMPLETENESS`: subjects can possess stronger standing than exploitative outsiders to frame their own wounds and identities, but self-authorship does not make an idealized account exhaustive or render independently checkable facts optional.**

The accountability corollary is:

> **`REPRESENTATIONAL_ACCOUNTABILITY_WITHOUT_TOTAL_ACCESS`: audiences may reasonably ask whether a public representation is materially misleading without thereby gaining jurisdiction over every fact the performer has omitted.**

The shortest rule is:

> **construction is not falsity; privacy is not deception; authorship is not immunity.**

A second operational formulation is:

> **curate without lying; disclose without surrendering jurisdiction; perform without claiming private equivalence.**

# 3. Definitions and anti-definitions

## 3.1 Authenticity

Authenticity means a representation remains sufficiently answerable to the person, relationship, competence, aspiration, affect, or practice it purports to present.

It does **not** mean:

- unfiltered speech;
- lack of rehearsal;
- absence of styling or editing;
- full disclosure;
- moral innocence;
- factual truth of every associated publicity claim;
- a hidden private essence that invalidates professional conduct.

## 3.2 Persona

Persona means a deliberately legible public or professional mode. It can include voice, styling, affect, role expectations, media framing, and recurring performance choices.

Persona can be:

- authored or imposed;
- practiced or habitual;
- expressive or defensive;
- truthful, selectively truthful, fictionalized, or misleading depending on what it claims.

## 3.3 Publicity

Publicity is the active organization of visibility: interviews, programs, channels, hype, press timing, public narratives, branding, event formats, and other mechanisms that make a person or unit legible at scale.

Publicity is not synonymous with audience relation. P2-E-05 owns the fuller reciprocal and creative-audience problem.

## 3.4 Selective disclosure

Selective disclosure is intentional control over which true facts, practices, feelings, relationships, or experiences are made public.

Selective disclosure is not automatically:

- dishonesty;
- shame;
- fear;
- proof of insufficient trust;
- proof of healthy boundaries.

Its meaning depends on jurisdiction, materiality, and the public claim being made.

## 3.5 Misrepresentation

Misrepresentation occurs when a public claim or construction materially asks others to believe something the speaker/creator knows is false or materially distorts the object being represented.

A key distinction follows:

> **incompleteness is not automatically misrepresentation.**

## 3.6 Privacy jurisdiction

Privacy jurisdiction is the person's or relationship's legitimate authority over facts that do not become public property merely because the person is famous, professionally visible, loved by fans, or institutionally important.

# 4. Three representational layers

P2-E-04 repeatedly needs three layers kept separate.

## 4.1 Private jurisdiction

Some domains remain non-public unless a stronger, independently justified duty changes the boundary. Yu's doujin practice, Kana's family pain, IIIX domestic/humiliating footage, romantic private life, and Shizuku/Rio private preferences all demonstrate that visibility is not total access.

## 4.2 Authored public construction

Public selfhood is made. Kokoro builds `可愛いこころ`. Mana builds `アイドル長瀬麻奈`. Shizuku turns fishing and fan knowledge into work. IIIX edits a channel. Sunny members perform romantic scenarios. Kana times an interview. The fact of construction is ordinary professional evidence, not automatically a defect.

## 4.3 Representational accountability

Construction becomes answerable to the claim it makes. IIIX may edit real antagonistic behavior into a public channel without publishing everything. It cannot make a known false status claim true merely by later performing well. Kana may resist a tabloid frame but cannot make an idealized father relationship exhaustive truth merely because she authored the counter-story.

The thematic architecture therefore rejects both extremes:

> **total-transparency essentialism** and **authorship-as-immunity**.

# 5. Multi-register selfhood — the private self is not the hidden court of final appeal

Kana is the cleanest anti-essentialist case. Her sugary public speech, abrasive private commentary, professional adult literacy, vanity, attachment hunger, and material acts of care are all recurring. The source does not require one to expose the others as fraud.

Mana similarly defeats the “public fake/private real” binary. Her stage confidence is genuine professional competence and genuine audience orientation. Private guilt, fatigue, uncertainty, and dependence add information; they do not erase the professional self.

fran is more severe. Her private narration can be contemptuous, frugal, embarrassed, or irritated while her networking and fashion-facing professionalism remain competent and sincerely tied to goals she actually holds. Private harshness does not automatically falsify professional polish.

Chisa's safer/private contexts reveal more eccentric and high-affect behavior than her softer public register. Again, the difference is not evidence that one register is counterfeit.

This is the basis of `MULTIREGISTER_SELFHOOD_WITHOUT_PRIVATE_SELF_MONOPOLY`.

# 6. Construction is not the opposite of authenticity

Kokoro supplies the strongest explicit text. After repeated rejection, she does not discover a pre-social essence. She analyzes the industry, analyzes herself, changes presentation strategy, and tells Ai:

> `私は、自分を……赤崎こころを信じて突き進むよ`
>
> `『可愛いこころ』を本物にする`

The sequence makes persona developmental rather than merely cosmetic. Kokoro chooses a public form and then works to make it an owned capacity. Later private practice—poses, hair, posture, expression, comparison with Sumire's more effortless charm—shows that the craft persists when no immediate audience is present.

The important conclusion is not “anything chosen becomes authentic.” It is narrower:

> **deliberate construction and authenticity are compatible because a practiced public form can become part of authored personhood.**

That conclusion must still pass the answerability test established by Kana and IIIX.

# 7. Yu — selective disclosure without shame

`event_2023_008` gives the clearest direct rejection of compulsory transparency.

Yu's doujin activity is recurring collaborative creative practice with her father: drawing, deadlines, events, shared fandom, and serious labor. She calls both idol work and this practice `宝物`. Under scandal pressure, she converts anticipated reputational harm into a judgment that she lacks the `資格` to remain onstage. That is not merely PR calculation; it is `ANTICIPATORY_SELF_DISQUALIFICATION`.

The resolution does not demand confession as proof of courage. Yu no longer needs to hide manga/anime out of shame, but says:

> `隠す必要はなくなったけど、今まで通り楽しみたい`

This is the key distinction:

> **the end of shame does not create a duty of publicity.**

Authenticity therefore includes jurisdiction over an identity's visibility boundary.

# 8. Visibility is not access

Kana's arc radicalizes the same point. Her original fame motive partly functions as an attachment signal: be visible enough that her father can find and see her. Yet wanting measurable visibility never implies wanting total access from family, press, fans, or even trusted professional partners.

Yu wants to remain an idol without turning private creator life into branding. IIIX wants media relevance without publishing drunken footage, fran's apartment, every grief history, or a friendship label the members reject. Shizuku can put fishing on a program while still keeping the hobby independently hers.

The thematic distinction is:

> **visibility is a public relation to attention; access is a jurisdictional relation to information and intimacy.**

They can overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

# 9. Kana — public self-narration as defensive authorship

The C1-A father material gives the franchise's strongest scandal/publicity problem outside IIIX.

A tabloid compresses Kana into a legible injury narrative: dead mother, abandoning father, wounded idol. The problem is partly factual and partly jurisdictional. The tabloid is attempting to own what a private family history means in public.

Kana responds with media craft rather than withdrawal. She builds a competing interview, times publication so the tabloid becomes stale/reactive, and uses selective true material to seize narrative initiative.

The correct term remains:

> **`PUBLIC_SELF_NARRATION_AS_DEFENSIVE_AUTHORSHIP`**.

But Makino recognizes the counter-story as more harmonious than Kana's private experience warrants. Therefore:

> **greater standing to narrate one's wound does not create documentary omniscience.**

Kana's account can be ethically preferable to exploitative framing while remaining selectively idealized.

# 10. Kana — bounded disclosure as trust

Kana's relationship with Makino clarifies that trust should not be operationalized as maximum disclosure.

She entrusts private information and simultaneously controls circulation. For her, that boundary is not evidence of shallow trust; respecting the boundary is part of what makes the professional relationship trustworthy.

This yields:

> **`BOUNDED_DISCLOSURE_AS_TRUST_KANA`**.

The generalizable mechanism is not that all secrets prove trust. It is that a person's retained jurisdiction can itself be evidence that the relationship no longer demands self-erasure as the price of care.

# 11. IIIX in Las Vegas — the negative control

`event_2024_004_st-eve-2404-dice` prevents the ledger from turning “authored construction” into an empty defense.

fran proposes a status presentation equivalent to calling IIIX Japan's top idols because the local audience cannot easily verify it. Hype raises expectations. IIIX then performs genuinely well; a promoter is impressed, circulating reports reinforce prestige, and the group can later exploit the stronger reputation.

The correct sequence is:

1. **`STRATEGIC_STATUS_MISREPRESENTATION`**;
2. real high-level performance;
3. **`PERFORMANCE_BACKED_STATUS_BOOTSTRAPPING`**;
4. later reputation feedback.

The real performance does not travel backward in time and make the opening status claim true when spoken.

Thus:

> **talent can validate competence without retroactively validating a false factual representation.**

# 12. IIIX in 2026 — strategic authenticity

`event_2026_001_st-eve-2601-race` is the positive control.

The source explicitly does not contrast “fake public image” with “raw private truth.” IIIX has previously shown greater public harmony because friendly groups receive favorable audience response. When the members pursue `等身大の魅力`, they still do so strategically: they study Hoshimi, record selected ordinary routines, edit, discard unusable footage, and seek competitive media advantage.

Those facts matter because they remove several false equations:

- strategy ≠ falsity;
- editing ≠ falsity;
- commercial motive ≠ falsity;
- privacy ≠ falsity.

Their early “ordinary” clips are truthful but not especially distinctive. The stronger public object emerges when competitive board-game interaction exposes a recurring unit grammar: mockery, irritation, status play, competence recognition, explicit trust, and durable contact.

The canonical model remains:

> **`CURATED_ADVERSARIAL_AUTHENTICITY_IIIX`**.

IIIX becomes more representationally truthful not by abolishing curation but by curating material that is answerable to how the unit actually relates.

# 13. Privacy survives strategic authenticity

The 2026 IIIX source is equally important for what it withholds.

The group does not publish everything simply because “realness” has become a media strategy. Drunken footage is sealed. Domestic material can be excluded. fran's apartment does not become audience property. miho's grief history does not become mandatory content. The members do not owe an ordinary friendship label merely because viewers interpret their hostility as intimacy.

This produces a strict distinction:

> **authenticity is correspondence, not total exposure.**

The test is not “was anything omitted?” It is:

> **what claim does the released construction make, is the omission material to that claim, and who has jurisdiction over the omitted fact?**

---

# 14. Audience reframing — creators do not own interpretation either

Viewers of IIIX's adversarial content interpret mutual hostility as evidence of a friendship strong enough to survive it. IIIX rejects ordinary `友情` vocabulary but cannot dictate every audience category.

This does not mean the audience becomes sovereign. The performers' repeated distinction between `信頼` and sentimental friendship remains source evidence. But the released object enters a public interpretive field.

The resulting mechanism is:

> **`AUDIENCE_RELATIONAL_REFRAMING`**.

P2-E-04 owns only the representational limit: authored publicity does not guarantee authored reception. P2-E-05 will own the larger audience co-authorship and reciprocity problem.

# 15. Authentic grammar without moral sanitization

The support-deferred beach material supplies a necessary ethical guardrail.

IIIX demonstrates sophisticated media agency: rebuilding exposure, studying formats, creating unscripted pressure, using live attention, and converting publicity into leverage. A Kana sequence then shows how a polished public concern register can coexist with strong evidence of retaliatory/manipulative intent.

The exact intent should not be promoted beyond its supported class, but the structural conclusion is secure:

> **professional coherence and effectiveness do not prove ethical authenticity.**

Later adversarial publicity may accurately represent IIIX's real grammar. That still does not make every insult, grievance, pressure tactic, or retaliatory act affectionate or harmless.

Canonical guardrail:

> **`AUTHENTICITY_WITHOUT_ETHICAL_EXONERATION`**.

# 16. Shizuku — a real private hobby can become a public persona

Shizuku's fishing material offers a low-conflict positive case.

Fishing is genuinely enjoyed apart from work. It later becomes a public program and performance-adjacent identity resource. Rio watches Shizuku's fishing program and becomes interested enough to try the hobby, reversing an older one-way fan influence structure.

The important representational fact is:

> **public professional use does not retroactively make the private interest fake.**

Nor does public use exhaust the hobby's meaning. Shizuku still fishes alone and understands it as her own practice.

This supports a general rule for persona construction: professional selection can be truthful precisely because it selects something real without claiming to reveal everything.

---

# 17. Shizuku / Rio — reciprocal access changes fandom ethics

Shizuku remains a genuine Rio/LizNoir fan after becoming a professional peer. The fan register is not deleted by reciprocity.

What changes is the legitimate method of knowing. Once Rio can answer directly, collecting discarded traces to infer private preferences becomes less defensible than asking the person.

The mechanism is:

> **`DIRECT_RELATIONSHIP_SUPERSEDES_PARASOCIAL_EXTRACTION`**.

But reciprocal access is not total entitlement. Rio's willingness to answer some questions does not make every private fact available. This keeps P2-E-04's privacy rule compatible with P2-E-05's fandom rule.

# 18. Performed intimacy without private claim

The Sunny Valentine's material provides a clean genre/performance boundary.

Nagisa writes confession scenarios matched to the members' recognizable public personalities. The idols perform them. Makino explicitly says `恋愛をするなとは言いません` and notes that he avoids interfering in private life; his professional concern begins if private conduct becomes public and produces consequences.

Two terms remain useful:

> **`PERFORMED_INTIMACY_WITHOUT_PRIVATE_CLAIM`**
>
> **`PRIVATE_LIFE_JURISDICTION_RESTRAINT`**

A staged romantic script can be appropriate idol performance without being extracted as documentary evidence of a performer's private romance.

# 19. Role-mediated truth

The Shizuku/Suzu theatre material generalizes the point. A line inside a play can be emotionally resonant and structurally revealing while remaining scripted dialogue. Independent surrounding evidence can establish friendship, rivalry, admiration, or changing closeness—but the script itself cannot simply be quoted as an offstage confession.

Thus:

> **performance can mediate truth without collapsing fiction into biography.**

This matters far beyond romance. Idol work constantly turns affect, persona, narrative, and stylization into public form. P2-E-04 must preserve the category distinction between **truth expressed through a role** and **a literal factual claim made outside the role**.

---

# 20. The instability of `素`

Phase-1 E3 already revised a simplistic authenticity model: even the “ordinary” or `素` self can be deliberately professionalized into a unit concept.

IIIX 2026 makes the same point with different aesthetics. The members attempt ordinary-life content because “realness” is useful media strategy. Selection begins before filming; framing and editing continue after.

Therefore rawness is not a stable opposite of performance.

A source can still distinguish more-scripted and less-scripted situations. But the philosophical opposition “performance = constructed / rawness = authentic” is not supported by this corpus.

---

# 21. Mana — professional perfection as achieved presentation, not total personhood

Mana's corpus requires five layers to remain distinct: living person, `アイドル長瀬麻奈`, public myth, ghost Mana, and later inheritances.

The professional self is consciously constructed around singing for an audience. Mana's bright role is real: she genuinely loves singing, genuinely wants to reach people, genuinely takes responsibility for stage encounters, and genuinely develops extraordinary professional skill.

Private material adds guilt, insecurity, exhaustion, rivalry, stubbornness, and dependence. It does not reveal the public Mana to have been a fake shell.

The strongest formulation is:

> **professional perfection can be an achievement of presentation without being proof of weakness-free personhood.**

This distinction becomes especially important after death, when reception compresses Mana into “legend” and “perfect idol.”

---

# 22. Public myth is a representation layer, not the whole person

Mana's “legend” status is explicitly retrospective reception. That status can accurately register exceptional impact while remaining incomplete as self-definition.

The 2026 answer-song material demonstrates why plural testimony matters. Kotono primarily carries `お姉ちゃん`; fans and colleagues carry `アイドル長瀬麻奈`; rivals, Makino, successors, and institutions hold other situated knowledge.

The mature answer is not to crown the family view as the hidden truth behind a fake public Mana. It is to preserve **plural access to one person**.

P2-E-03 owns the memorial/historiographic consequence. P2-E-04 owns the representational rule:

> **a public myth can be socially real and causally powerful without becoming an exhaustive model of personhood.**

---

# 23. miho / Yō — polished public image can erase provenance

The U.S. IIIX archive crisis is the strongest negative case of curation by omission.

miho encounters a public history that edits Yō out so that miho can appear as a cleaner precursor to “perfect IIIX.” The resulting image is coherent. It is flattering. It is professionally legible. It is also historically defective because the omitted person is causally central to the story being told.

The key contradiction is:

> **private fidelity + professional success can coexist with public erasure.**

This proves that not every omission is a neutral privacy decision. When the representation claims to narrate a history, deleting causally necessary provenance can become representational capture.

P2-E-03 owns the grief/memory stakes. P2-E-04 owns the mechanism of image construction through deletion.

---

# 24. Chisa — borrowed adulthood and the danger of treating image as proof of growth

Chisa's adult-image anxiety is useful because the persona problem does not always involve deception toward an audience. Sometimes the person uses a desired image as evidence to herself that development has occurred.

Her difficulty is not that adult styling or experimentation is fake. The danger is converting a borrowed image into the required proof that she has become sufficiently mature. Later fan response allows a less absolute model: gradual visible effort can count.

Thus:

> **a chosen image can scaffold development without becoming the sovereign test of whether the underlying development is real.**

This distinguishes Kokoro's practice from Chisa's anxiety while keeping both inside the same construction framework.

---

# 25. Mixed motive is not automatic falsity

Many public acts in IDOLY PRIDE are mixed-motive:

- IIIX wants truthful media that also improves favorability;
- Kana wants defensive narrative control and enjoys winning the media contest;
- Kokoro's cute construction serves ambition and becomes genuinely enjoyed craft;
- Shizuku's hobbies/fandom can become professional resources without ceasing to matter privately;
- Mana's audience ethic contains love of performance, professional duty, and eventually dangerous self-demand.

The corpus therefore rejects the idea that commercial, competitive, defensive, or status motives contaminate a public act by definition.

But mixed motive is not exoneration either. The right question remains representational:

> **what does the public act claim, what does it omit, what audience inference does it invite, and does the subject possess jurisdiction over the omitted domain?**

# 26. A taxonomy of legitimate public construction

Across the snapshot, several forms of construction are repeatedly supportable.

## 26.1 Practiced persona

A chosen mode becomes part of personhood through voluntary repeated practice. Kokoro is the clearest case.

## 26.2 Professional register

A person speaks, behaves, or networks differently because the role genuinely calls for different competencies. fran, Kana, Mana, Chisa, and others use such switching.

## 26.3 Selected real material

A genuine hobby, relation, or recurring behavior is selected for public use without claiming to exhaust the private original. Shizuku fishing and IIIX 2026 are key cases.

## 26.4 Performed fiction

A script or stage form openly presents itself as performance; its affective truth does not depend on literal biographical equivalence. Sunny Valentine's and theatre cases belong here.

## 26.5 Defensive narration

A subject responds to exploitative framing by authoring a competing public account. Kana is the strongest case.

## 26.6 Strategic privacy

A person leaves material outside publicity without disavowing it as shameful or illegitimate. Yu and IIIX supply the clearest evidence.

---

# 27. A taxonomy of representational failure

## 27.1 False status

A factual claim is knowingly inflated because the audience cannot verify it. IIIX Las Vegas is the clearest negative control.

## 27.2 Materially misleading omission

The omission changes what a representation claims about its object. The Yō archive deletion is the strongest case.

## 27.3 Coerced exposure

A private fact is made public by scandal, tabloid, leverage, or relational pressure without sufficient jurisdiction. Yu's threat and Kana's tabloid frame expose this problem.

## 27.4 Privacy appropriation

Audience benefit or intimacy rhetoric is used to justify revealing another person's private details. Kokoro's early fan-intimacy tendency supplies a local example.

## 27.5 Persona overclaim

A chosen public form becomes a false proof of total identity or maturity. Chisa's borrowed-adult anxiety and public Mana mythology show different versions.

## 27.6 Ethical laundering

A genuine relational grammar is treated as morally harmless simply because it is authentic. The IIIX support-deferred material forbids this move.

# 29. Longitudinal grammar

The recurring cross-unit development can be modeled as an eight-stage grammar:

1. **multiple registers already exist** — private, professional, fan, family, stage, media, institutional;
2. **visibility pressure selects a public form** — styling, role, interview, performance, channel, brand, myth;
3. **construction proves useful** — it creates legibility, opportunity, protection, audience access, or professional coherence;
4. **construction is overread** — as exhaustive personhood, literal private fact, complete history, or proof of status;
5. **jurisdiction conflict appears** — scandal, extraction, false hype, archive deletion, privacy pressure, unwanted narrative ownership;
6. **subject or relation reauthors the interface** — selective disclosure, counter-publication, better curation, role clarification, provenance restoration;
7. **authenticity moves from rawness to answerability** — the question becomes whether the construction corresponds to the represented object and respects legitimate omissions;
8. **audience interpretation remains partly open** — publication creates reception that cannot be fully controlled by the creator.

This grammar is recurrent, not universal law. Individual arcs can enter at different stages or never traverse the whole sequence.

# 32. Evidence hierarchy for P2-E-04

For authenticity/publicity claims, the strongest evidence order is:

1. explicit statements about what is being performed, withheld, edited, or claimed;
2. public/private register contrast where both contexts are textually available;
3. source-confirmed production choices such as editing or publicity strategy;
4. independently corroborated behavior showing whether a public representation corresponds to recurring conduct;
5. audience reception as evidence of interpretation, not automatic truth about the performer;
6. historical/public myth as evidence of reception layer, not total personhood;
7. speculation about hidden intent only where strongly warranted and clearly labeled.

This is why Kana's retaliatory intent in the PK sequence stays below textual fact, while Yu's selective disclosure and Kokoro's `本物にする` commitment can support much stronger propositions.

# 37. What P2-E-04 establishes about authenticity

The frozen snapshot supports a model of authenticity with **three freedoms and three constraints**.

The freedoms are:

1. **freedom to construct** — a persona need not be spontaneous to be real;
2. **freedom to withhold** — not every true fact belongs to the public;
3. **freedom to transform** — private practices, ordinary routines, hobbies, roles, and inherited material can become professional forms without automatically becoming counterfeit.

The constraints are:

1. **factual answerability** — a public factual claim cannot be rescued by talent or authorship if it is knowingly false when made;
2. **jurisdictional answerability** — one person's publicity strategy does not automatically authorize exposing another person's private material;
3. **provenance answerability** — a representation that claims history can become false through omission when the omitted origin is causally necessary to the story being told.

These constraints explain why the same franchise can affirm both Kokoro's deliberately manufactured persona and condemn IIIX's Las Vegas status claim without contradiction.

# 38. What P2-E-04 establishes about privacy

Privacy is not treated as a binary wall between authentic and public life.

It is better understood as **selective jurisdiction over circulation**.

A private fact can be:

- irrelevant to the public claim and legitimately withheld;
- relevant but still protected absent a stronger duty;
- jointly consequential and therefore subject to bounded disclosure duties under P2-E-02;
- historically material to a public representation such that omission distorts provenance;
- transformed into public art/performance without becoming literal documentary disclosure.

The corpus therefore does not support “privacy always wins” any more than “the audience deserves the truth about everything.” It supports claim-specific adjudication.

# 42. Thematic capstone

IDOLY PRIDE's public world depends on construction. Idols rehearse. They select clothes and gestures. They write scripts. They edit video. They cultivate recognition. They manage interviews. They turn hobbies into content, rivalries into programs, ordinary routines into branding, and private experiences into songs. The series would be incoherent if construction itself invalidated authenticity.

But the corpus is equally unwilling to let “self-authored” become a magic word that absolves every representation.

IIIX can select real hostile banter and produce a public object more truthful to its unit grammar than generic friendliness. The same IIIX can exploit an audience's inability to verify prestige and make a status claim that remains misleading. Kana can seize narrative authority back from a tabloid and still produce an account more idealized than private experience. Yu can stop being ashamed and still refuse to publicize everything. Kokoro can manufacture `可愛いこころ` and make it genuinely hers. Shizuku can turn fishing into public work without surrendering the hobby's private meaning. Mana can be a genuinely extraordinary professional and still become flattened by the posthumous legend built around her. miho can love Yō privately while a polished public archive deletes Yō from the story.

These are not contradictions once authenticity is defined as **answerable construction** rather than unconstructed exposure.

The public form is legitimate not because it shows everything, but because what it chooses to show remains answerable to what it claims. The private domain is legitimate not because privacy is sacred in the abstract, but because attention and affection do not automatically create ownership. Self-authorship matters not because the subject can never misrepresent herself, but because coercive outsiders do not gain superior standing merely by possessing a platform.

The capstone formulation is therefore:

> **`AUTHENTICITY_AS_ANSWERABLE_CONSTRUCTION_WITH_SELECTIVE_JURISDICTION`: a person may build a public self, edit it, rehearse it, monetize it, protect parts of it, and let audiences interpret it without surrendering authenticity—provided construction remains accountable to its represented object, privacy is not used to materially falsify the claim being made, and publicity does not convert other people's lives or the subject's whole personhood into public property.**

Shortest form:

> **construction is not falsity; privacy is not deception; authorship is not immunity.**
