# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — AUTONOMY, MANAGEMENT, INTERVENTION, AND PROFESSIONAL CARE LONGITUDINAL LEDGER

## P2-E-02 — supported autonomy and bounded professional jurisdiction

---

# 0. Analytical responsibility

This is the second P2-E theme/institution ledger.

P2-E-01 established the broader constitutional rule that causal importance does not automatically create ownership. P2-E-02 narrows that problem to a more difficult professional question:

> **When is intervention a legitimate part of helping another person remain an author of her own vocation, and when does care become a substitute authorship that decides for her?**

The answer cannot be “never intervene.” The corpus repeatedly gives managers, veteran members, leaders, staff, and peers real obligations around bodily safety, scheduling, workload, professional feasibility, access to opportunities, privacy, and institutional conditions. The answer also cannot be “the caring expert decides.” The same corpus repeatedly treats protective secrecy, unconsulted vetoes, permanent caretaker roles, or institutional assimilation as failures when they seize jurisdiction outside a bounded role.

This ledger therefore owns the recurrent cross-unit mechanism connecting:

- autonomy and professional constraint;
- management and performer authorship;
- refusal and care;
- bodily safety and risk-taking;
- waiting/nonintervention and pedagogy;
- confidentiality and private truth;
- accompaniment and stage-threshold limits;
- professional boundary maintenance;
- institutional support and affiliation;
- reciprocal care toward the caregiver;
- the difference between preserving a person's capability and replacing her goal.

It does **not** replace Makino's character ledger, the Mana/Makino or Rui/Makino relationship ledgers, SUNNY PEACE's unit constitution, Rio/Aoi's partnership ledger, Haruko's character ledger, or IIIX's institutional-affiliation result. Those artifacts remain the canonical local owners of their facts and relationship/unit states.

---

# 1. Inherited constitutional boundary from P2-E-01

P2-E-01 stabilized:

> **`ANSWERABLE_INTERDEPENDENCE_WITHOUT_JURISDICTIONAL_CAPTURE`**

and:

> **`DIFFERENTIATED_AUTHORITY_WITH_RECIPROCAL_STANDING`**.

P2-E-02 preserves both, but they are too general to settle management-specific cases by themselves.

A manager may possess authority that a peer does not. A veteran may notice danger that a novice does not. A staff member may control access to a venue. A unit may impose rehearsal or rest expectations. A performer may have a strong reason to accept physical risk. A private disclosure may be professionally relevant without becoming generally shareable. An institution may provide resources without being entitled to reshape a unit's relational identity.

The governing question is therefore not whether power exists. It is:

> **What is the legitimate domain, duration, purpose, procedure, and exit condition of that power?**

---

# 2. Governing thematic model

The strongest P2-E-02 formulation is:

> **`SUPPORTED_AUTONOMY_THROUGH_DOMAIN_BOUNDED_INTERVENTION`: IDOLY PRIDE does not define autonomy as insulation from management, care, expertise, institutional dependence, or even temporary constraint. Autonomy is strongest when a person's authored vocation remains the governing end while professional intervention protects the bodily, informational, relational, and material conditions that make continued authorship possible. Legitimate intervention is therefore domain-bounded, proportionate to the relevant risk or professional responsibility, answerable to the affected person where circumstances permit, revisable as information changes, and temporary rather than self-expanding. The manager may refuse an opportunity, impose rest, negotiate conditions, withhold his own intervention, enforce a professional boundary, or translate desire into executable strategy. What he may not infer from those duties is general ownership of the idol's private truth, relationships, identity, or future. The strongest care enlarges or preserves the person's capacity to choose; the recurrent failure occurs when care replaces the choice it claims to protect.**

A complementary formulation is:

> **`PROFESSIONAL_CARE_AS_CAPABILITY_PRESERVATION_WITHOUT_GOAL_SEIZURE`: care is most legitimate when it protects the agent's durable capacity to pursue her own higher-order end rather than substituting the caregiver's preferred end.**

A timing corollary is:

> **`INTERVENE_TO_PRESERVE_CAPABILITY; WAIT_TO_PRESERVE_AUTHORSHIP`: action and non-action are both managerial tools. The correct choice depends on whether the immediate threat is to the person's capability to act or to her opportunity to author the answer herself.**

The shortest rule is:

> **protect the capacity; do not seize the goal.**

# 3. Definitions and anti-definitions

## 3.1 Autonomy

Autonomy here means **authorship of one's vocational ends, meaningful personal choices, and self-definition within real social and institutional constraints**.

It does not mean:

- never receiving advice;
- never being refused;
- always receiving the desired opportunity;
- freedom from schedules, contracts, team obligations, health facts, or resource limits;
- the right to make other people absorb unlimited costs;
- immunity from professional evaluation;
- permanent independence from care.

## 3.2 Intervention

Intervention means a professional or relational actor changes, constrains, delays, enables, redirects, or refuses an available course of action.

Intervention can include:

- declining work;
- conditioning participation on rest or monitoring;
- changing schedule or workload;
- refusing a requested career architecture;
- requiring shared infrastructure;
- delaying a solution so the group can diagnose itself;
- negotiating with organizers;
- preserving confidentiality;
- stopping rehearsal or work;
- creating institutional access;
- declining to cross a romantic/sexual boundary.

## 3.3 Professional care

Professional care is **role-mediated attention to another person's continued capability, safety, development, and conditions of work**.

It is not identical to affection, friendship, romance, parenting, or therapy.

## 3.4 Goal seizure

Goal seizure occurs when a caregiver or institution converts legitimate role authority into authority over the person's end itself.

Examples include:

- deciding what another person should want;
- treating “protection” as permission to hide jointly consequential information;
- treating indispensability as ownership of another member's career;
- treating institutional support as entitlement to personality assimilation;
- treating a manager's access as entitlement to romantic pursuit;
- turning temporary safety jurisdiction into general personal jurisdiction.

---

# 4. The five-part intervention test

Across the current corpus, a useful P2-E-02 diagnostic has five questions.

## 4.1 Domain

Is the matter actually inside the actor's legitimate role?

Health, rest, scheduling, professional feasibility, staff obligations, rehearsal conditions, and access to opportunities repeatedly fall inside Makino's managerial domain. Another person's private romantic truth or family meaning generally does not.

## 4.2 Purpose

Is the intervention preserving the person's own durable capability or substituting an externally preferred end?

The strongest Makino interventions oppose short-term actions because they threaten Mana's or Suzu's ability to continue the vocation they themselves chose. They are not anti-ambition.

## 4.3 Procedure

Was the affected person given explanation, information, contestability, or negotiation where circumstances allowed?

Mana's anger at `私に相談もなしに！` is decisive because the subject matter may be legitimate while the procedure is still defective.

## 4.4 Proportionality and duration

Does the intervention reach only as far and last only as long as the relevant risk or professional necessity?

A stop condition, mandatory rest, temporary monitoring, or trial affiliation is easier to justify than permanent control.

## 4.5 Return of jurisdiction

After the bounded problem is addressed, does decision authority return to the person or unit whose future is at stake?

The Hoshimi final supplies the clearest endpoint: the manager prepares conditions, but `全ては本人達次第だ`.

# 5. Mana/Makino — the foundational intervention dispute

The Mana origin material is the canonical stress test because it contains both a **real duty of care** and a **real procedural failure**.

Mana's career accelerates. Makino notices exhaustion and attempts to reduce workload. Mana continues because singing is not merely a task to her; it is the vocation through which she understands herself and her obligation to the people waiting to hear her.

Makino then rejects an opportunity without consulting her.

The corpus refuses to make either side trivially correct.

Makino is not wrong that:

- Mana's body is finite;
- collapse affects staff, organizers, family, and future opportunities;
- a manager has real responsibility for health and schedule;
- the performer is not a solitary production unit.

Mana is not wrong that:

- the opportunity matters to her self-authored vocation;
- he made a consequential choice without consulting the person whose career it was;
- a safety model can become too simple if it treats all risk as evidence that the goal is irrational;
- audience obligation is part of the professional meaning she herself assigns to singing.

The repair is therefore not “Makino learns never to veto” or “Mana learns to obey.”

They negotiate a constrained performance with an explicit stop condition and mandatory rest afterward.

That sequence stabilizes the ledger's basic distinction:

> **a legitimate domain does not make every procedure legitimate; a legitimate desire does not make every risk professionally acceptable.**

---

# 6. Refusal as care

Makino's role is not preference satisfaction.

`origin_mna_003_goodbye_to_goodbye` and the later refusal of Kotono's requested solo launch show that he can say no when he judges an immediate path incompatible with health or with a viable professional strategy.

This supports:

> **`CARE_CAN_INCLUDE_REFUSAL`: professional care may require refusing a requested action when accepting it would materially damage the person's own durable capacity or the conditions necessary for the stated vocation.**

But refusal requires a guardrail.

A manager can misunderstand the person's higher-order goal. He can exaggerate institutional risk. He can confuse his own comfort with the person's welfare. Refusal therefore remains answerable to explanation, evidence, changing conditions, and the possibility that the initial managerial hypothesis is wrong.

---

# 7. Goal-preserving constraint versus goal substitution

The strongest interventions in the locked snapshot preserve an authored end against a destructive short-term means.

For Mana:

- the end is continued idol singing and reaching listeners;
- the disputed means is accepting work under dangerous exhaustion.

For Suzu:

- the end is proving herself and sustaining idol vocation;
- the disputed means is proof-seeking overwork severe enough that Makino repeatedly offers rest or declining work.

For Sakura in Stellar:

- the end is performing with SUNNY PEACE at a consequential competitive moment;
- the disputed means cannot be treated as unlimited bodily self-sacrifice.

The theme is not “professionals know better than idols.”

It is:

> **professional authority is strongest when it can show how a proposed means threatens the agent's own end, and weakest when it silently substitutes a different end.**

---

# 8. Bodily safety is legitimate jurisdiction — but not total jurisdiction

Makino's current character ledger explicitly places health, rest, food, scheduling, and immediate bodily safety inside his professional responsibilities.

That proposition survives P2-E-02.

It is strengthened by the recurrence of:

- Mana's hospitalization/overwork dispute;
- Suzu's proof-seeking overwork;
- Rei's overwork crisis;
- Sakura's collapse;
- Sakura's later high-stakes health risk;
- Makino's own sleep/food neglect becoming visible to idols.

However:

> **bodily safety jurisdiction is not a blank check for personal sovereignty.**

The manager does not therefore own:

- the idol's romantic life;
- private family interpretation;
- grief meaning;
- friendship categories;
- disclosure of every private fact;
- ultimate artistic self-definition;
- lifelong career choice.

The corpus repeatedly distinguishes role-specific duty from general personhood superiority.

---

# 9. Supported risk — autonomy is not risk abolition

SUNNY PEACE supplies the strongest counterexample to both libertarian and paternalistic simplification.

After Sakura's overwork collapse, the unit develops a norm that rest is infrastructure and that compensatory overwork by the other four merely distributes self-sacrifice.

Yet later, `stellar_010` / `stellar_011` do not produce a rule that all serious risk must be prohibited.

Sakura wants to perform despite medically serious risk. The final arrangement preserves several facts simultaneously:

- the health risk remains real;
- Sakura's desire remains real;
- the members' support remains real;
- Makino's monitoring duty remains real;
- the performance proceeds under bounded conditions.

P2-E-02 therefore retains the SUNNY formulation:

> **supported risk rather than unrestricted autonomy or automatic prohibition.**

The ethical distinction is not risk versus no risk. It is whether risk becomes **informed, bounded, collectively answerable, and monitored** rather than privately converted into bodily debt.

---

# 10. Overwork reveals the limits of noble motive

The series repeatedly refuses to excuse self-destruction because the motive is admirable.

Rei overworks for SUNNY PEACE.

Sakura overworks because she believes the center should carry the group.

The other four overwork while trying to cover for Sakura.

Makino overworks while managing others.

The repeated correction is:

> **noble purpose does not immunize a work pattern from being harmful.**

This is analytically important because it prevents “care” from being evaluated only by intent. A member can sincerely want to protect or support someone and still reproduce a structure in which bodies are treated as expendable inputs.

P2-E-06 will own the fuller labor/sustainability model. P2-E-02 owns the narrower proposition that professional care must be able to interrupt even altruistically motivated self-escalation.

---

# 11. Waiting can be active management

`hoshimi_005_the_sun_moon_and_stars` provides the strongest evidence that nonintervention can be a professional act.

When the existing members recognize that the group lacks something, Makino does not immediately supply Mei as the answer. He chooses to watch a little longer and is willing to postpone debut while the members diagnose the problem.

This matters because management can seize authorship by solving too quickly.

The active ingredients of legitimate waiting are:

- the problem is not an immediate bodily emergency;
- the performers possess information the manager cannot substitute for;
- the developmental process of articulating the problem is itself valuable;
- delay is purposeful rather than negligent;
- the manager remains available to act when conditions change.

Thus:

> **waiting is legitimate when the principal risk is authorship foreclosure; intervention is more urgent when the principal risk is capability loss.**

---

# 12. Nonintervention has limits

The waiting model must not become romantic anti-management.

Urgent health risk, time-sensitive institutional obligations, contractual commitments, or safety conditions can make delay irresponsible.

The locked corpus does **not** supply a complete emergency-powers code. It does establish enough to reject two extremes:

- “consultation is always required before any protective action”; and
- “protective motive licenses unrestricted unilateral action.”

P2-E-02 therefore leaves the exact emergency threshold open rather than inventing a policy doctrine the source does not provide.

---

# 13. Management as desire-to-strategy translation

Makino's mature professional function is frequently neither command nor passive service.

He translates an idol's authored desire into executable strategy by handling:

- logistics;
- schedule;
- risk;
- venue/organizer negotiation;
- publicity conditions;
- group architecture;
- timing;
- resource access.

This is clearest when Mana wants her song to reach more people, when an apparently impossible performance requires safety constraints, and when the Hoshimi final requires professional groundwork for an idol-authored stage idea.

The resulting model is:

> **the idol need not author every means in order to remain author of the end.**

Professional specialization can enlarge autonomy rather than diminish it when the specialized actor remains answerable and does not redefine the goal by stealth.

---

# 14. The stage threshold — accompaniment ends before substitution

From Anime Episode 1 to `hoshimi_023_pray_for_you`, Makino's management is repeatedly spatialized or verbalized as accompaniment up to a threshold.

He can prepare.

He can evaluate.

He can negotiate.

He can create conditions.

He can remain present.

At the decisive performance, he cannot perform the idol's authorship for her.

The mature statement—`本当に大切な時、俺は見ていることしか出来ない`—does not mean management is powerless overall. It means the profession includes knowing where its power must stop.

This is the strongest P2-E-02 answer to the fear that care becomes irrelevant if it cannot guarantee outcomes:

> **care remains causally important without receiving authorship credit for the act only the performer can do.**

---

# 15. Group architecture as revisable professional hypothesis

Makino shapes Hoshimi's group structures. That is genuine managerial power.

The source nevertheless treats his initial structures as hypotheses rather than revelations of fixed essence.

SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest become communities that exceed their founding managerial metaphors. Center roles change. Support roles become contestable. Members generate their own governance and creative practices.

Therefore:

> **professional design is legitimate when it remains revisable by lived evidence.**

A manager can create a structure without becoming the permanent constitutional author of everyone inside it.

---

# 16. Haruko — indispensable does not mean owned

Haruko's career fork is especially useful because the pressure is not primarily health-related.

She receives a credible musical-theater opportunity and seriously considers leaving idolhood. SUNNY PEACE needs her. Makino can articulate why she is structurally important. Her absence would impose real costs.

Yet the unit does not acquire ownership of her career because those costs are real.

Rei's position is decisive: the choice belongs to Haruko.

Haruko then rechooses idolhood in the present tense while explicitly preserving the fact that idol careers are finite and alternatives are real.

Thus:

> **institutional dependence creates reasons to communicate stakes; it does not create a right to commandeer another person's career.**

This is autonomy under consequence, not autonomy without consequence.

---

# 17. Haruko — care as institutional method, with paternalistic risk

Haruko's care develops from personal kindness into a professional method:

- noticing fatigue and friction;
- pacing younger members;
- supplying food/drink;
- organizing schedules;
- creating conversational space;
- interrupting bodily self-escalation;
- transmitting institutional memory.

But P2-B1 explicitly refuses to idealize all of her methods.

Haruko sometimes uses strategic indirection to create the conditions for another person to confront a problem. That can be effective. It can also become paternalistic if the tactic starts controlling the other person's decision rather than opening space for it.

P2-E-02 therefore distinguishes:

> **scaffolding that makes speech/choice possible**

from:

> **manipulation that makes the desired answer more likely by concealing the caregiver's own intervention.**

The source does not support a universal ban on indirection. It supports an answerability requirement.

---

# 18. Care must include the caregiver

Haruko, Makino, Rei, Sakura, Shizuku, and Chisa collectively prevent care from becoming a permanent one-way office.

Haruko can care for younger members without being demoted to support staff.

Makino can hold safety responsibility without becoming bodily invulnerable.

Rui can prepare food and worry about Makino while remaining a managed talent.

Sakura can buy Makino dinner when he substitutes an energy drink for food.

Miho can question his late-work pattern.

The mature pattern is:

> **a caregiver's role creates responsibilities, not exemption from receiving care.**

Reciprocity remains differentiated. Idols do not become Makino's managers because they notice his exhaustion. They acquire relational standing to respond to the person whose work affects them and whose body is also finite.

---

# 19. Reciprocal care does not collapse professional asymmetry

Rui/Makino is an important control.

Rui trusts Makino, values his judgment, wants his praise, and later develops romantic attraction. She also cares for his embodiment and work habits.

None of those facts erase the management relation.

The current relationship ledger explicitly warns:

- do not equate care reciprocity with role equality;
- do not equate professional warmth with romantic reciprocity;
- do not treat professional gratitude as false because romantic feeling later emerges.

P2-E-02 generalizes the professional point:

> **reciprocal care can humanize an asymmetric professional relation without converting it into symmetrical authority.**

---

# 20. Professional anti-fraternization as self-limitation of power

Makino explicitly states that, as manager, he will not make a move on talent he manages.

This matters for P2-E-02 because the boundary is not merely a prohibition on desire. It is a self-limitation on what professional access may be used for.

The principle is:

> **access created by a role may not automatically be repurposed into personal jurisdiction or romantic pursuit.**

The Rui/Makino ledger keeps Makino-side reciprocity open and still preserves the conduct boundary. Mana's posthumous farewell is not a simple counterexample because death has already destroyed the ordinary living manager/client situation.

The locked snapshot does not establish what would happen if a management relation formally ended. P2-E-02 must not invent that future.

---

# 21. Confidentiality — knowing is not owning

Makino's later card/message material supplies a direct privacy boundary.

Kana can tell Makino a fuller private truth and explicitly limit its circulation. Miho's memory material similarly demonstrates that private provenance can remain hers even when parts of an image or story become publicly usable.

This supports:

> **`CONFIDENTIAL_ACCESS_WITHOUT_DISCLOSURE_OWNERSHIP`: professional access to private information does not itself transfer authority to circulate that information.**

The manager's role can make him a trusted witness without making him the owner of the truth.

The safeguard is equally important:

> the locked corpus does not establish an absolute secrecy rule against every possible imminent safety or legal duty.

P2-E-02 therefore preserves the exception as an open boundary rather than inventing a concrete legal standard.

---

# 22. Rio/Aoi — protective secrecy as failed care

Rio/Aoi supplies the clearest non-managerial control.

The pair already has a communication norm. Aoi then learns that failure could dissolve LizNoir and asks staff to conceal the fact from Rio. She later hides an injury because Rio is carrying too much and Aoi wants to protect her.

The motive is loving and professionally serious.

The method is defective.

Aoi decides what Rio should be allowed to know about stakes that belong to both of them.

Rio's protest—`私達はパートナーじゃなかったの！？`—is not a claim to own Aoi's body. It is a claim that jointly consequential information should not have been unilaterally removed from shared decision space.

This yields:

> **`PROTECTIVE_CARE_WITHOUT_BENEFICIARY_EXCLUSION`: care cannot normally justify editing another competent participant out of a decision whose stakes are materially hers.**

---

# 23. Appreciating motive does not ratify method

Rio can understand why Aoi concealed the information.

That understanding does not turn secrecy into the mature norm.

This is a crucial interpretive guardrail across the project:

> **forgiveness or appreciation of a caring motive is not evidence that the procedure was constitutionally sound.**

Without this distinction, benevolent failures become impossible to criticize precisely because they came from love or responsibility.

---

# 24. Institutional support — IIIX as the independence control

IIIX tests a different autonomy myth:

> self-authorship requires institutional independence.

The BIG4/post-BIG4 material rejects it.

IIIX accepts a reversible/trial professional affiliation with Hoshimi while remaining recognizably IIIX:

- the members continue living separately;
- abrasive relations persist;
- rivalry persists;
- Hoshimi warmth is not imposed as a personality norm;
- IIIX gains resources and professional ecology without surrendering unit identity.

The unit ledger names this:

> **`AFFILIATION_WITHOUT_DOMESTICATION_IIIX`**.

P2-E-02 generalizes:

> **institutional support can enlarge autonomy when membership supplies capabilities without demanding relational or identity assimilation as the price of help.**

---

# 25. Reversibility matters

IIIX's affiliation is especially useful because it is not framed as an irreversible surrender of jurisdiction.

Trial/reversible structure reduces the cost of testing cooperation and helps preserve credible exit.

This does not prove that every temporary arrangement is ethical or that every contract should be terminable at will.

It does establish a broader professional mechanism:

> **where possible, reversible arrangements can make dependence compatible with self-authorship because participation remains a live choice rather than a one-way transfer of identity.**

---

# 26. Hoshimi's institutional legitimacy is partly restraint

Hoshimi is not validated here as a complete institution-wide constitutional object. The P2-D warrant decision remains in force.

But P2-E-02 can own a narrower recurring institutional proposition:

> **Hoshimi is most legitimate when it provides professional infrastructure without claiming total jurisdiction over the identities it supports.**

Evidence includes:

- Makino's bounded management;
- member-authored unit development beyond founding design;
- Haruko's career choice remaining hers;
- IIIX affiliation without domestication;
- the final-stage limit where Hoshimi can prepare conditions but not author the performers' answer.

This does not prove Hoshimi is institutionally flawless. It defines the strongest recurring legitimate-use pattern in the current snapshot.

---

# 27. Professional care is not parental sovereignty

The corpus frequently uses age, experience, seniority, bodily risk, and concern.

Those factors can justify differentiated treatment.

They do not justify treating younger or less experienced performers as permanently lesser authors.

Haruko's veteran competence is functional, not an age essence. Makino's managerial competence is professional, not personhood superiority. Aoi's desire to protect Rio does not grant informational sovereignty. SUNNY PEACE's concern for Sakura does not turn Sakura into an object with no say in risk.

Therefore:

> **age-sensitive or expertise-sensitive care is compatible with equal standing only when the asymmetry remains tied to the relevant domain.**

---

# 28. Autonomy is compatible with dependence

The strongest through-line across P2-E-02 is anti-isolationist.

Mana needs Makino's professional labor.

Makino needs the idols' authored goals to make his profession meaningful.

SUNNY PEACE members need one another's burden-sharing.

Haruko can need group support while remaining a veteran.

Rui can need Makino's recognition without losing vocation.

IIIX can need institutional resources without ceasing to be self-authored.

Autonomy therefore does not mean the removal of causal dependence.

It means:

> **dependence must remain compatible with refusal, contestation, revised roles, private boundaries, credible exit, and the continuing possibility that the supported person authors a different answer.**

# 30. Countermodels and rejected simplifications

## 30.1 “Autonomy means nobody can stop you.” — REJECT

The corpus repeatedly grants legitimate bodily-safety and professional authority.

## 30.2 “Management is legitimate whenever the manager means well.” — REJECT

Mana and Rio/Aoi both show benevolent unilateralism can still violate answerability.

## 30.3 “If the performer wants the risk, prohibition is always paternalistic.” — REJECT

Health facts, staff obligations, and durable capability matter.

## 30.4 “If the risk is medically serious, participation is always exploitation.” — DOWNGRADE

SUNNY's supported-risk material preserves informed desire, bounded conditions, member support, and monitoring.

## 30.5 “The safest choice is always the ethical choice.” — REJECT

Safety is important but cannot become a universal sovereign that erases vocation and authored risk.

## 30.6 “Waiting is passive or negligent.” — REJECT

Hoshimi 005 shows deliberate waiting can protect diagnostic authorship.

## 30.7 “Waiting is always more respectful than intervention.” — REJECT

# 31. Longitudinal synthesis

The P2-E-02 evidence forms a recurrent sequence:

### Stage I — authored desire

The performer or unit has an end that is genuinely hers/theirs.

### Stage II — professional constraint becomes visible

Body, schedule, institution, staff, resource, relationship, or career feasibility creates a problem that desire alone cannot solve.

### Stage III — intervention risk

A manager/caregiver can either:

- under-intervene and abandon legitimate duty;
- over-intervene and seize jurisdiction;
- or define a bounded constraint that preserves capability while leaving the end authored.

### Stage IV — answerability

The affected person receives explanation, information, contestability, negotiation, or at minimum later accountability where prior consultation was impossible.

### Stage V — capability preservation

Rest, safety conditions, professional strategy, privacy, resource access, or institutional support protects the possibility of continued action.

### Stage VI — return of authorship

The performer/unit decides, performs, stays, leaves, risks, refuses, discloses, or continues under conditions that do not make the professional helper the author of the final answer.

This is why the mature model is neither laissez-faire nor paternalistic.

# 32. What counts as stronger evidence of legitimate intervention

Across the current snapshot, intervention is more strongly supported when several of these are present:

1. the domain is clearly role-relevant;
2. the risk threatens durable capability rather than merely disappointing the caregiver;
3. the agent's higher-order end is explicit rather than inferred solely by the caregiver;
4. reasons are disclosed;
5. the intervention is narrow and temporary;
6. alternatives are negotiated;
7. monitoring/feedback exists;
8. the intervention can be revised;
9. the supported person retains credible refusal/exit where compatible with the domain;
10. jurisdiction returns after the bounded condition passes.

None is a mechanical checklist supplied by the fiction. This is a P2-E analytical abstraction from recurrent evidence.

# 33. What counts as stronger evidence of jurisdictional capture

The risk of capture increases when:

- “I know you” substitutes for asking you;
- “I care about you” substitutes for sharing jointly relevant information;
- “you need me” becomes a reason you cannot leave;
- “the group needs you” becomes ownership of your career;
- “I manage you” becomes ownership of private truth;
- “I support you” becomes entitlement to intimacy;
- “we fund you” becomes identity assimilation;
- “you chose this career” becomes unlimited bodily debt;
- “I protected you once” becomes permanent authority;
- emergency or temporary power lacks an exit condition.

# 34. Relationship to P2-E-01

P2-E-02 **STRENGTHENS** P2-E-01 rather than replacing it.

P2-E-01 said:

> causal importance creates duties of answerability; it does not automatically create ownership.

P2-E-02 adds:

> **some forms of causal importance create real authority, including refusal and temporary constraint. The constitutional question is therefore not whether authority exists but whether it remains bounded to the domain that justifies it and returns authorship when that domain no longer requires intervention.**

This is a material refinement because it prevents “jurisdictional capture” from being misread as anti-authority rhetoric.

# 35. Phase-1 / Phase-2 claim transitions

The dominant transition pattern is **STRENGTHEN + REVISE**.

Phase 1 correctly identified answerable management, selective personal jurisdiction, bidirectional professional care, and autonomy/intervention tensions. Phase 2 adds sharper distinctions:

- refusal can be legitimate care;

- waiting can be active management;
- privacy is compatible with professional access;
- caregiver reciprocity does not erase role asymmetry;
