# 2. E1-B tranche thesis

> **INTERPRETATION — E1-B asks what must remain answerable when ambition becomes optimization.**

The four events repeatedly place successful idols under pressure to convert a legitimate goal — top idol, BIG4, ranking, survival, victory — into a totalizing metric. Each event then shows the metric becoming insufficient:

- Rei mistakes relentless acceleration for SUNNY PEACE's only route upward and begins treating rest/care as weakness.
- TRINITYAiLE turns post-scandal survival and a 99-win streak into a doctrine that equates losing with possible extinction.
- Rio treats visibility and BIG4 access as urgent enough to risk letting an external producer and sponsor overwrite LizNoir's authored identity.
- Kotono's BIG4-period addendum shows that even a legitimate desire to win becomes mature only when it is integrated with self-differentiation, reciprocal support, and a non-prescriptive account of what idol light is for.

The common corrective is **not anti-ambition**. Nobody is asked to stop trying to win.

Instead, E1-B repeatedly revises the question:

> not merely **"How do we win?"**
>
> but **"What form of ourselves must remain capable of answering the way we pursue victory?"**

This creates a lower-tier refinement of the Tier-A thesis of **answerable interdependence**:

> **Ambition remains legitimate when means, identity, relationships, bodily limits, and artistic purpose remain contestable from inside the relationship or unit.**

A second tranche-wide proposition is equally important:

> **Defeat is not one stable moral category.**

Across E1-B, defeat can become:

- information;
- humiliation;
- industry danger;
- identity threat;
- remembered artistic peak;
- liberation from a career;
- reason to restart a career;
- catalyst for more authentic rivalry.

The ethical and developmental meaning of defeat depends on what the person can do with it afterward.

# 3. Event 1 — `event_2023_009_st-eve-2309-backside`
## 遥か頂に向かう秀麗

## 3.1 Chronology route

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the event opens `サニーピースがBIG4になってしばらく経った頃` — some time after SUNNY PEACE has become BIG4.

The same source shows:

- IIIX already operating at Hoshimi, because miho is present as an internal peer/adversarial adviser;
- SUNNY PEACE carrying a significantly busier BIG4 workload;
- Rei asking about the remaining BIG4 and the gap to the top;
- no explicit statement that Tsuki no Tempest has already become BIG4.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the safest diegetic routing is **post-SUNNY BIG4 acquisition**, without forcing a more exact placement than the text supports. Later Tsuki/BIG4 conclusions must not be projected into this event merely because the event was released later.

Classification: `CHARACTER_MATURATION` + `UNIT_ETHICS` + `LABOR/BODY` + `COMPETITION`.

## 3.2 Rei's problem is not insufficient effort but metric capture

Rei begins from a real tension. BIG4 status creates more work, but she worries that daily professional busyness is becoming a substitute for forward development. SUNNY PEACE is happy, productive, and not obviously neglected; Shizuku explicitly says they receive proper rest, and Haruko notes that each job is approached with a goal.

Rei nevertheless cannot answer a deeper question:

> if SUNNY PEACE wants the top, is "doing today's work well" enough?

miho gives the crucial answer. She does **not** give Rei a universal training prescription. She says the top is so high that neither SUNNY PEACE nor IIIX can simply win by doing their present best, and that every person/unit requires a different growth method. Rei must think and test methods herself.

That advice is sound. Rei's error is what she does with it.

She turns an open-ended demand for experimentation into escalating pressure:

- more rehearsal;
- later rehearsal;
- practice on off-days;
- resistance to rest;
- comparison with Mei/Suzu's fast mutual feedback;
- increasing frustration with Haruko's pacing.

**INTERPRETATION:** Rei is temporarily captured by a performance metric she cannot fully specify. Because "top idol" remains abstract, she converts urgency itself into evidence of seriousness.

This is a useful distinction:

> **effort can become performative even when the person is genuinely hardworking.**

Rei is not faking ambition. She is using self-exhaustion to make an uncertain path feel morally legible.

## 3.3 Internalized ambition can create self-exploitation even inside a comparatively protective institution

This event matters for `OPEN-05` because it complicates any simple institution-versus-idol model of overwork.

The source supplies positive safeguards:

- Shizuku says they receive proper days off;
- Haruko repeatedly enforces rest;
- the unit recognizes fatigue;
- Haruko stops a live when Rei is injured;
- Haruko explicitly places Rei's body above a one-off victory.

Yet Rei still pushes herself to the point of dizziness and injury.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Hoshimi's labor ethics cannot be evaluated only by asking whether management overworks idols. High-achievement culture can be internalized by the idols themselves and become **self-exploitation under voluntary ambition**.

The institution therefore needs not only permission to rest, but relationships capable of making rest behaviorally real.

## 3.4 Haruko's kindness is not weak competitive will

The event deliberately lets Rei misread Haruko.

Rei sees Haruko:

- refuse to retaliate when other idols insult her past obscurity;
- stop Rei from fighting verbally on her behalf;
- pace practice conservatively;
- prioritize health over the tournament;
- withdraw after Rei lashes out.

Rei therefore concludes that Haruko is too gentle to become a top idol.

The text then reverses the meaning of gentleness.

When the rivals mock **Rei** after the injury, Haruko loudly orders them to apologize. She explains that idols exist to delight people; rivalry and mutual stimulation are legitimate, deliberate degradation is unnecessary. She then apologizes for raising her voice after securing the apology.

Haruko also explains that insults toward herself do not matter much to her, while insults toward people she cares about do.

**INTERPRETATION:** Haruko's gentleness is **selective restraint backed by boundary capacity**, not inability to oppose.

She is peaceful because she has criteria for when conflict is worth spending force on.

That is a substantially stronger ethical model than "Haruko is kind."

## 3.5 Competition without erasure appears outside BIG4's DoriKyun conflict

Haruko's explicit norm is:

> `競い合い、励まし合うことはあっても / わざわざバカにして、貶める必要なんてない`

The event therefore independently reproduces a principle already visible at Tier A:

> **competition can be intense without requiring the degradation or ontological erasure of the opponent.**

This matters because it prevents the BIG4 DoriKyun conclusion from remaining an arc-local ethic. E1-B shows the same distinction arising in ordinary professional competition.

## 3.6 Haruko's care is indirect and mildly paternalistic, but answerable

Haruko notices Rei is distressed but cannot get her to talk. She then:

- consults miho over drinks;
- uses a two-person unit battle as a pretext to spend more time with Rei;
- tells Rei the pairing is primarily so Haruko can learn from Rei's dance;
- does not reveal the full care motive herself;
- relies on miho later to make Rei realize what happened.

This is benevolent concealment.

The event does not condemn it because it works and does not remove Rei's decision-making authority. Rei accepts the unit, retains control over her training opinions, can be angry, and later initiates social time with Haruko herself.

Still, the source should not be flattened into "healthy communication." Haruko initially helps through **structured indirection** because direct access to Rei's concern is unavailable.

## 3.7 Rei's mature correction is not "stop being ambitious"

Rei does not abandon the top-idol objective.

Her final change is procedural:

- she recognizes she was isolating herself inside the unit;
- she begins sharing future-oriented proposals with all five members;
- she distinguishes "not panicking" from "continuing the status quo";
- she turns urgency into a discussable unit plan rather than unilateral self-punishment.

The closing movement is therefore:

> **private optimization pressure -> relationally answerable strategy.**

That is a high-value SUNNY PEACE unit development.

## 3.8 Haruko's personal idol goal remains deliberately unresolved

Rei asks Haruko what her idol goal is. The answer is interrupted by the final match and never supplied.

This should remain open.

Do **not** infer from Haruko's ethics that she lacks a personal competitive ambition, nor infer from her long career that her goal is merely support/legacy.

## 3.9 Event-local state deltas

### Rei

- ambition becomes explicit as a potential source of self-harm;
- learns to distinguish pressure from progress;
- learns that care/rest can be conditions of long-term ambition rather than its opposite;
- shifts from isolated problem-solving toward unit-level planning.

### Haruko

- care is shown as active, strategic, and boundary-capable;
- explicitly prefers personhood/body over a one-off competitive result;
- articulates a non-degrading competition ethic;
- remains a current idol whose own top-level telos is still unrevealed.

### miho

- functions as adversarial mentor rather than emotional caretaker;
- refuses to give SUNNY PEACE IIIX's answer;
- correctly identifies Rei's inability to read Haruko's supportive intent as immaturity;
- participates in cross-unit adult-professional advising without sentimentalizing it.

### SUNNY PEACE

- BIG4 success creates a second-order danger: busy professional success can obscure developmental direction;
- the unit begins converting that problem into explicit collective strategic planning.

## 3.10 Formal dependency

`adv-live-eve-2309-backside-04` is absent.

The textual stakes and result of the unit-battle sequence are readable. Exact choreography, camera, blocking, vocal/sonic delivery, and performance contrast remain formally deferred.

## 3.11 Primary locators

- `st-eve-2309-backside-001` — post-BIG4 work pressure; miho's individualized-growth doctrine; Rei's first failed attempt to accelerate the whole unit.
- `st-eve-2309-backside-002` — Haruko/miho conversation; two-person unit-battle proposal; Mei/Suzu rival pairing.

# 4. Event 2 — `event_2023_011_st-eve-2311-marathon-raid`
## 翔けぬけるVictoire　誓いのplume

## 4.1 Chronology route

The event opens by explicitly locating its formative wound at the **NEXT VENUS Grand Prix semifinal**, where TRINITYAiLE loses to SUNNY PEACE.

The current story occurs later:

- TRINITYAiLE has transferred to Hoshimi;
- its work breadth and fanbase have expanded;
- it has accumulated 98, then 99 consecutive live-battle wins;
- BIG4 is now a plausible next competitive frontier.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the manager proposes a ranking-5 opponent as the 100th battle, with BIG4 challenge to follow if they win.

Classification: `POST-TIER-A MATURATION` + `UNIT_CONSTITUTION` + `RANK/SCORE CRITIQUE` + `FAILURE`.

## 4.2 Winning has become both survival strategy and trauma response

Rui's doctrine is not simple arrogance.

After I-UNITY:

- a fabricated scandal nearly destroys her career;
- Yu and Sumire are endangered by association;
- TRINITYAiLE experiences the real possibility that the unit could end for reasons unrelated to performance quality.

Rui therefore concludes that they cannot afford to lose.

Yu adds a hard industry premise: one important loss can become career-ending; talented idols have disappeared after failing when it mattered.

Their 99-win streak is thus psychologically overdetermined:

- proof of recovery;
- public-legitimacy rebuilding;
- route to BIG4;
- reassurance that the unit still exists;
- defense against another collapse.

**INTERPRETATION:** TRINITYAiLE has converted post-scandal precarity into **victory dependence**.

The belief `we must keep winning` functions as a safety behavior.

## 4.3 Sumire's longer entertainment career supplies the dissenting sensor

Sumire is the only member initially unable to accept the safe route.

The manager helps her connect the present to her child-actor history:

- she was praised for stability;
- she followed direction well;
- she avoided mistakes;
- she gradually ceased receiving work anyway.

The text proposes that stable competence can become a local optimum. Sumire's move from acting to idolhood was itself a risky change that created the relationships and experiences she now values most.

Her eventual challenge to Rui and Yu is therefore not abstract motivational rhetoric. It comes from a career-specific memory:

> **safety can also erase you.**

## 4.4 The event sharply separates prudent risk from developmental challenge

Rui's objection is strong:

> choosing a likely loss is not necessarily "challenge"; it can be `無謀` — recklessness.

The event does not refute this by claiming that all risk is virtuous.

Instead, it establishes that TRINITYAiLE's present policy has moved too far toward certainty:

- choosing opponents they can reliably beat;
- repeating familiar songs;
- optimizing completion/score;
- preserving the streak.

The correction is not "ignore probability." It is:

> **a growth-oriented professional unit must sometimes accept bounded outcome risk when the safe path has stopped producing developmental information.**

This is a useful general distinction for later labor/management synthesis.

## 4.5 The fan survey makes defeat legible as artistic success without denying that it was a defeat

The key evidence is unusually strong because it comes from accumulated fan surveys preserved by Makino.

Asked what TRINITYAiLE activity was most memorable, fans repeatedly identify the **semifinal loss to SUNNY PEACE**, not the 99-win streak.

The members themselves agree that the lost semifinal contained exceptional intensity: rivalry pulled out more than their ordinary performance level.

Sumire's formulation is decisive:

> `ファンは勝敗じゃなくて私達を見てる`

This does **not** mean fans do not care whether idols win, nor that rankings are irrelevant.

It means:

> **result is not coextensive with performance value.**

TRINITYAiLE can lose a battle and still produce the performance fans remember as most alive.

This is one of E1-B's strongest pressures against score/rank reductionism.

## 4.6 "The best TRINITYAiLE is now" rejects both nostalgia and streak fetishism

Rui's revised statement is:

> `あの頃が一番良かったなんて、もう言わせない`
>
> `いつだって、トリエルの一番は今だってことを証明しましょう`

The point is not to restore the lost-semifinal version of the unit.

They instead create a new challenge:

- Yu repeatedly rewrites lyrics rather than accepting a safe first draft;
- Sumire contributes costume ideas;
- Rui proposes choreography that places Yu and Sumire more prominently rather than centering herself conventionally;
- all three accept a first-performance risk against a stronger opponent.

**INTERPRETATION:** TRINITYAiLE answers nostalgia by making **present-tense self-surpassing collaboration** the unit standard.

## 4.7 The center relation becomes more horizontal

Rui is still leader/center, but the new choreography matters.

Yu first says she will follow Rui anywhere, then corrects herself:

> `「後ろをついていく」のはちゃうな / 三人で肩を並べて、みんなで前に進むんや`

This is explicit relational grammar.

The mature unit is not:

> Rui flies; Yu and Sumire follow.

It is:

> the three fly shoulder to shoulder, with differentiated contributions.

This strengthens the Tier-A model of elite unithood becoming gratitude/reciprocity without abandoning high standards.

## 4.8 The manager's fan archive is guidance rather than verdict

Makino does not order the group to fight rank 5 after they initially refuse. He allows them to decide and tells them to discuss until satisfied.

He later supplies Sumire with preserved fan surveys.

The surveys are used as reflective evidence, not as a command:

- they reveal what fans actually remember;
- Sumire constructs the argument;
- the members decide to revise their strategy.

This is a comparatively strong example of producer guidance that **changes the information environment without seizing the decision**.

## 4.9 Victory does not close the problem

TRINITYAiLE defeats the top-5 opponent by a very narrow margin and receives the promised trip.

The event refuses a complete motivational closure. Rui privately recognizes:

- this was their current limit;
- BIG4 remains much harder;
- `絆の力だけではどうにもならない時` may come;
- she is unsure whether the unit can remain as it is under that pressure.

Yu answers relationally by recalling Rui's own dream: fly to a height no one else can reach, together.

This is reassurance, not proof.

**OPEN:** the event leaves a productive unresolved question about how an intimacy-centered unit behaves when solidarity alone does not solve an objective performance gap.

## 4.10 Event-local state deltas

### Sumire

- becomes the unit member who identifies safe stagnation;
- uses her pre-idol career history as analytical knowledge rather than mere wound;
- becomes willing to contradict Rui/Yu for the unit's future;
- demonstrates that the "young/junior" member can be the constitutional corrective.

### Rui

- victory obsession is recontextualized as survival/future-protection logic;
- accepts that a lost performance may retain greater artistic value than many wins;
- decentralizes stage prominence;
- preserves ambition while reopening uncertainty and challenge.

### Yu

- fear of loss is tied to protecting Rui/Sumire from pain and industry disappearance;
- translates the correction into creative labor through repeated lyric revision;
- explicitly revises her own follower language into shoulder-to-shoulder movement.

### TRINITYAiLE

- shifts from streak preservation toward bounded developmental risk;
- converts a three-person hierarchy into a more explicitly co-authored performance system;
- preserves BIG4 ambition while refusing to let the win streak become the unit's identity.

## 4.11 Formal dependency

`adv-live-eve-2311-marathon-raid-04` is absent.

The new-song strategy, member contributions, challenge decision, and outcome are textual facts. Exact formal proof about choreography, center distribution, sonic arrangement, and stage embodiment remains deferred.

## 4.12 Primary locators

- `st-eve-2311-marathon-raid-001` — semifinal-loss memory; 98-win present; rank/opponent selection unease.
- `st-eve-2311-marathon-raid-002` — 99th win; top-5 offer; scandal-based victory dependence; industry precarity logic.
- `st-eve-2311-marathon-raid-003` — Sumire child-actor stagnation; challenge argument; Rui/Yu's fear rationale.
- `st-eve-2311-marathon-raid-004` — fan-survey proof; loss as remembered peak; "best is now"; new-song co-authorship; center/horizontal reform.
- `st-eve-2311-marathon-raid-005` — narrow win; ordinary-life celebration; adult-future imagination; Rui's unresolved BIG4 anxiety.

# 5. Event 3 — `event_2024_005_st-eve-2405-race`
## すれ違いのディソナンス

## 5.1 Chronology route

The event depicts four-person LizNoir after substantial shared growth:

- Rio/Aoi/Ai/Kokoro are touring successfully;
- the unit is a regular top-10 VENUS-program presence;
- BIG4 is the explicit next target;
- the four-person formation is socially and professionally normalized.

No textual locator requires a more exact relationship to every main-story chapter.

Classification: `LIZNOIR_IDENTITY_CRISIS` + `PRODUCTION_ETHICS` + `SPONSOR_POWER` + `OPEN_ENDING`.

## 5.2 Rio's urgency makes visibility an instrumental good

The event begins from success. The tour has gone well, the four-person unit has matured, audiences are strong, and no one is injured.

Kokoro nevertheless asks for a visible result: ranking/BIG4.

Rio agrees that LizNoir needs more than technical improvement. It also needs increased public recognition.

This is the motive behind accepting the Tokyo Seaside tie-up despite Makino's warning that the external producer's communication feels wrong and despite incomplete written details.

Rio's logic is not irrational:

- the campaign is national;
- it includes a new song and costume;
- it can materially increase visibility;
- LizNoir is close enough to BIG4 that such acceleration could matter.

But E1-B makes urgency itself a risk variable.

## 5.3 Makino's warning is respected as evidence, not converted into paternal veto

Makino tells the members:

- he and Hashimoto cannot attend;
- he has a bad working impression of the external producer;
- the unit may refuse the job without penalty from him.

Rio chooses to accept.

Aoi initially prefers caution because he trusts Makino's professional intuition.

This is an important producer/idol boundary case:

> the manager supplies concern and an exit route; the adult/professional unit retains the choice.

Rio even tells Makino to learn to trust others and delegate.

The event then tests whether LizNoir can self-govern when its usual management buffer is absent.

## 5.4 The external campaign begins by treating LizNoir as interchangeable surface

The first creative direction emphasizes generalized cuteness:

- exaggerated smiles;
- cute poses;
- "rabbit" jumps;
- a vocabulary that does not arise from LizNoir's established performance identity.

Aoi identifies the core professional failure:

> the campaign, as designed, could be performed by another idol group without losing much.

That is not merely aesthetic conservatism.

It is a claim about **specificity**:

> if commissioned work cannot answer why these performers are the appropriate performers, it risks reducing them to generic celebrity inventory.

## 5.5 Rio and Aoi articulate two legitimate but conflicting theories of professional change

Aoi's position:

- change is acceptable;
- four-person LizNoir already proved that change can be generative;
- but growth does not justify work that empties LizNoir of its authored identity;
- ranking/visibility are not sufficient reasons for non-consensual identity dilution.

Rio's position:

- LizNoir cannot grow by expressing only what it already knows;
- interpreting another creator's intention can itself be artistic/professional development;
- past difficult relationships were resolved through continued dialogue;
- the group may need to learn forms not generated internally.

Neither position is treated as foolish.

**INTERPRETATION:** the event's real conflict is not authenticity versus selling out. It is **how far professional heteronomy can extend before external direction ceases to be collaboration and becomes substitution of authorship.**

## 5.6 Sponsor fandom becomes a form of capture

The external producer eventually confesses that the facility owner's son is a major LizNoir fan and that he used sponsor power to demand:

- his own lyrics;
- a specific cute image;
- a preferred center (Kokoro);
- other personal fantasies about what he wants LizNoir to perform.

This is a major audience/industry ethics case.

The sponsor's son is not hostile to LizNoir. He is a fan.

That is precisely the danger:

> **affection plus purchasing/institutional power can become a claim to author the performer.**

The source therefore distinguishes:

- fandom as reception;
- fandom as participation;
- fandom backed by client power that attempts to convert desire into jurisdiction.

## 5.7 The external producer fails because he abdicates mediation

Aoi defines the producer's job explicitly:

> stand between sponsor and performer, hear both sides, and coordinate.

The external producer instead:

- privately objects;
- submits to sponsor pressure;
- hides the conflict;
- compensates with performative cheerfulness;
- allows the performers to experience irrational direction without explanation.

The moral/professional failure is not merely "bad taste."

It is **failure of accountable mediation**.

Rio's solution is equally important. She does not demand total control and does not simply walk away. She proposes direct dialogue with the sponsor, using LizNoir's own standing as leverage.

The resulting campaign becomes co-produced rather than unilaterally imposed.

## 5.8 Successful compromise does not resolve the Rio/Aoi conflict

The revised campaign works:

- sponsor intention is understood rather than blindly obeyed;
- LizNoir's identity is retained;
- the campaign is popular;
- the costumes are embraced;
- candid ordinary-life footage is incorporated into the final advertisement.

The unit even recognizes that the result revealed a natural side of LizNoir not captured by the initial forced-cute direction.

However, the source refuses to treat commercial success as proof that Rio's earlier urgency was fully correct.

In the next live battle, Rio/Aoi coordination breaks down badly enough that a rookie opponent nearly defeats them.

Aoi then asks the decisive question:

> `そのためなら、何を捨ててもいい / 例えば、僕達らしさとか`

and then:

> `誰のために / なんのために、トップアイドルを目指すの？`

Rio cannot answer.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the event ends without resolution.

This must be preserved as a genuine open state.

## 5.9 The event's strongest conceptual distinction: change versus dispossession

The tie-up proves LizNoir can successfully broaden its expression.

The near-loss proves that "successful new image" does not automatically answer whether the unit's competitive telos is healthy.

Preferred formulation:

> **LizNoir needs change, but change must remain co-authored.**

A stronger normative version:

> **The right to surprise an audience is different from the audience's right to prescribe the self that must surprise it.**

## 5.10 Event-local state deltas

### Rio

- becomes more willing to accept external artistic direction as growth;
- urgency toward BIG4 is exposed as stronger than she admits;
- takes on direct leadership when Makino is absent;
- successfully negotiates a multi-party compromise;
- ends unable to articulate the ultimate purpose of top-idol ambition.

### Aoi

- functions as LizNoir's internal constitutional check;
- protects artistic specificity without rejecting change categorically;
- explicitly defines producer mediation as a professional obligation;
- refuses to let ranking become unquestioned telos;
- his unresolved doubt materially harms later unit synchronization.

### Ai / Kokoro

- help keep conflict from becoming totalized;
- provide adaptable performance capacity and ordinary social energy;
- demonstrate that "cute" material is not intrinsically degrading — the ethical issue is how it is authored and imposed.

# 6. Event 4 — `event_2024_006_st-eve-2406-contest`
## 君を照らすムーンライトロード

## 6.1 Chronology route — hard guardrail

This event must **not** be routed by release year alone.

The framing is explicit:

> `月のテンペストとどりきゅんの決戦前夜`

It then flashes back to:

- Kotono's departure from Tsuki;
- four-member Tsuki's decision to let her go while maintaining a return path;
- the reconstituted five-member unit preparing for the DoriKyun rematch;
- the two-week new-song development period before the decisive battle.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** this is a **BIG4-period expansion/addendum** embedded inside the previously audited main-story arc.

Classification: `RETROSPECTIVE_BIG4_ADDITIVE_EVIDENCE`, not post-BIG4 character maturation.

This chronology distinction is one of E1-B's most important methodological findings.

## 6.2 Tsuki's earlier acceptance of Kotono's departure was not passivity

Kotono initially thanks Sakura for not stopping her from leaving Tsuki.

Sakura corrects the record: she opposed it strongly.

The four remaining Tsuki members explain why they nevertheless accepted Kotono's decision:

- they had argued against it;
- they believed Kotono had seriously considered the choice;
- they were lonely and did not want separation;
- they treated respecting her resolved agency as part of friendship;
- they believed she would return;
- they committed to making Tsuki stronger enough to remain a place worth returning to.

Mei states the normative rule most clearly:

> `本当の友達なら、琴乃ちゃんを信じて、応援して / 送り出すべき`

The event therefore sharpens the BIG4 constitutional reading.

The four-member period is not merely "Kotono leaves and the others develop." It is:

> **the unit accepts painful separation while refusing to convert love into jurisdiction.**

They preserve relationship without claiming permanent custody over Kotono's choices.

## 6.3 "A place to return" is active infrastructure, not stasis

Suzu says they will protect `琴乃の帰る場所`.

Mei immediately revises "protect" into something stronger: they will make Tsuki so strong Kotono will want to come back.

This is important.

A return home is not created by freezing the old group exactly as it was. The home must remain alive enough to be re-entered by a changed person.

That closely matches the E1-A professional-continuity-platform model at unit scale.

## 6.4 Kotono learns Sakura's optimism as relational competence, not personality imitation

During SUNNY PEACE's earlier IIIX challenge, Kotono had regarded Sakura's certainty — `絶対にうまくいく` — as extraordinary strength.

Now, in the reconstituted Tsuki, she realizes why Sakura could say it.

Kotono can tell her own members:

> `私達が力を合わせれば必ず間に合う`
>
> `絶対にうまくいくわ`

The event does not make Kotono into Sakura.

It shows Kotono acquiring a **relational capacity** she first recognized in Sakura: confidence grounded in trust in other people rather than confidence in solitary self-sufficiency.

## 6.5 Public criticism after Kotono's return becomes a test of accountable reintegration

Kotono is aware of hostile commentary accusing her of:

- fake departure;
- shameless return;
- publicity manipulation;
- attention-seeking due insufficient ability.

The text does not pretend the press conference erased reputational consequences.

Kotono feels responsible for exposing the unit to hostile audience atmosphere and still worries about burdening the others.

The group answers collectively:

- she does not need to apologize to them for existing inside that criticism;
- all five chose to continue together;
- they will stand with her during the public test.

This is an important aftercare layer for the BIG4 refoundation.

## 6.6 The live dramatizes recognition change, but should not be universalized

The event's live-battle text shows:

- active heckling at the start;
- existing supporters responding;
- heckling gradually decreasing;
- penlight support increasing;
- the venue becoming blue;
- a direct `おかえり` call to Kotono.

Kotono experiences this as returning to her place and recognizing the stage as her road.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** this particular audience is narratively won over through performance.

**CAUTION:** do not universalize this into "good performance resolves public scandal" or "criticism was objectively invalid because the crowd changed." The event is about one enacted recognition transition.

## 6.7 Kotono's moonlight philosophy becomes reciprocal illumination

Kotono's prior aspiration is:

> become an idol who illuminates other people's paths like moonlight.

During the live she realizes:

> she herself has been walking a road illuminated by fans and other people.

This is a major maturation of the metaphor:

> **illumination is reciprocal.**

The center does not originate all light. The idol who guides others is also guided, sustained, and made visible by reception.

This parallels the BIG4 refoundation's broader movement from asymmetric center/support logic toward reciprocal reliance.

## 6.8 The two outsider stories prove that illumination is non-prescriptive

After the live, two different former/present idols respond to Tsuki's influence in opposite directions.

### Idol A

She had already lost faith after being crushed by DoriKyun, remained an idol partly because of fans and sunk costs, and uses a full-force defeat by Tsuki to finally retire with clarity.

Tsuki gives her courage to **leave**.

### Former top idol / venue staff member

She retired after deciding she had reached her limit, remained in the industry as support staff, and realizes after seeing Tsuki that she still wants the spotlight and has not exhausted her desire.

Tsuki gives her courage to **return**.

The same unit therefore "illuminates" two different paths:

- exit;
- re-entry.

This is one of the strongest event-level statements in the current corpus:

> **ethical idol influence does not prescribe the destination. It helps another person see enough to choose a path.**

Preferred term:

> **non-prescriptive illumination.**

This is analytically stronger than generic "giving people courage."

## 6.9 The new song is born from observed self-authorship, not abstract branding

The unit had chosen `新生月スト` as a concept but could not find the language.

After encountering the two outsider cases, Kotono can articulate the unit's function:

> people illuminated by Tsuki feel something, discover something, and move forward.

The lyrics are then described as combining:

- gentle, quiet accompaniment;
- strength/courage that blows away hesitation;
- moonlight rather than solar brightness.

**INTERPRETATION:** the new song is not simply a victory anthem. It is the unit turning its observed effect on other people's choices into a performance philosophy.

## 6.10 Hoshimi's cross-unit reciprocity materially helps Tsuki's DoriKyun preparation

SUNNY PEACE and other Hoshimi members assist the two-week crash program:

- other Hoshimi idols are available for additional help.

Kotono initially hesitates because this is Tsuki's battle.

Rei/Shizuku answer through reciprocity: Tsuki helped SUNNY PEACE against IIIX; now it is their turn.

Sakura gives the institutional/social formulation:

> `ユニットは違っても / 私達はデビューからずっと一緒の仲間`

This is strong support for the E1-A model of Hoshimi as **plural continuity platform** rather than merged-family sameness.

Units remain rivals and distinct authors. They can still loan developmental labor to one another.

## 6.11 Sakura/Kotono rivalry matures through differentiation rather than de-escalation

Kotono tells Sakura directly that she had long envied her:

- why can't I be strong like Sakura?
- shine like Sakura?

**INTERPRETATION:** identity differentiation makes rivalry healthier **by preserving comparison without making imitation the condition of worth.**

# 7. Cross-event synthesis before historical comparison

## 7.1 Rank and victory are necessary but incomplete languages

None of these events abolishes competition.

Instead:

- Rei keeps the top-idol goal;
- TRINITYAiLE pursues BIG4;
- LizNoir pursues BIG4;
- Tsuki prepares to defeat DoriKyun and then SUNNY PEACE.

What changes is whether ranking can stand in for purpose.

E1-B repeatedly says no.

A rank can establish:

- comparative legitimacy;
- opportunity;
- competitive position;
- public attention;
- strategic leverage.

It cannot fully establish:

- why a unit should exist;
- what it may ethically sacrifice;
- what performance means to fans;
- whether a person should stay in or leave idolhood;
- whether a relationship remains healthy;
- whether an artistic change is growth or dispossession.

## 7.2 "Answerability" now has an artistic-production form

E1-A applied answerability strongly to memory/inheritance.

E1-B adds a production rule:

> **professional direction is legitimate when performers retain meaningful capacity to answer, revise, refuse, reinterpret, or co-author what is being asked of them.**

This appears in:

- Rei bringing growth strategy back to SUNNY PEACE rather than dictating it;
- Sumire contesting Rui/Yu's win-streak doctrine;
- Aoi contesting Rio and an external producer;
- Rio negotiating directly with sponsor power;
- Tsuki accepting cross-unit help without surrendering unit authorship.

## 7.3 Care and ambition are not opposites

Haruko/Rei makes the point most explicitly.

But the same structure recurs elsewhere:

- Makino lets TRINITYAiLE decide whether to accept a dangerous challenge;
- Yu's loss aversion arises partly from not wanting to see Rui/Sumire hurt;
- Aoi's resistance arises from protecting LizNoir's authored identity;
- the four Tsuki members let Kotono leave because they take her resolved desire seriously.

Care becomes dangerous when it preempts the other's standing to choose.

Ambition becomes dangerous when it treats the other's body, identity, or relationship as expendable material.

The healthy form is therefore not a midpoint called "balance" in the abstract. It is **mutual standing to contest the path**.

## 7.4 E1-B distinguishes three forms of change

### Developmental change

Change that expands capacity while preserving authorship and reply.

Examples:

- TRINITYAiLE's new song and decentralized choreography;
- LizNoir's negotiated campaign revision;
- Kotono learning trust from Sakura without becoming Sakura.

### Adaptive change

Change required by context, not necessarily identity-defining, but compatible with selfhood.

Examples:

- LizNoir performing a broader image once it becomes intelligible and co-authored;
- SUNNY PEACE adjusting professional strategy to BIG4 workload.

### Dispossessive change

Change imposed such that the performer becomes replaceable surface for someone else's desire.

Example:

- sponsor-fan demands transmitted without mediation.

This vocabulary may be useful in later labor/industry synthesis.

## 7.5 Defeat becomes ethically plural

E1-B contains at least five distinct defeat structures:

1. **Rei/Haruko tournament withdrawal:** defeat/forfeit as bodily protection.
2. **TRINITYAiLE semifinal loss:** defeat as remembered artistic peak and developmental resource.
3. **TRINITYAiLE feared future loss:** defeat as plausible industry/reputation danger.
4. **Idol A vs Tsuki:** defeat as permission to exit a career she no longer chooses.
5. **Former staff idol's remembered defeats:** defeat as unresolved desire that later motivates return.

Therefore "failure is information" is true but incomplete.

A stronger model is:

> **failure becomes information only when a person or relationship retains enough continuity to interpret it.**

## 7.6 Unit identity is increasingly constitutional rather than aesthetic

Across E1-B, a mature unit is defined less by one style and more by rules for handling disagreement and change.

- SUNNY PEACE: individual urgency must become discussable unit strategy.
- TRINITYAiLE: junior dissent can revise leader doctrine; stage prominence can be redistributed.
- LizNoir: external change is acceptable if internal members can contest terms and co-author the result.
- Tsuki: members can leave, return, disagree, receive outside help, and compete without losing the unit's identity.

Preferred proposition:

> **A mature unit is not a fixed image. It is a repeatable procedure for changing without making members disposable.**
