# 2. Final E1-C governing thesis

E1-C materially advances the project’s treatment of persona and public identity.

The strongest final formulation is:

> **Public identity in IDOLY PRIDE is neither an inner truth merely revealed nor a false mask merely imposed. It is constructed in negotiated spaces among performer, partner, manager, audience, medium, institution, place, and memory. Construction is ethically mature only when its different dimensions remain answerable: authorship can be contested, relationships can answer back, provenance can survive without becoming ownership, and factual claims used to mobilize an audience remain answerable to truth.**

This requires four analytically separate questions.

### 2.1 Representational truth

Is a public assertion factually accurate?

TWINkle can be behaviorally close while still lying about its preexisting friendship history. The relation becoming real later does not retroactively make those claims factual.

### 2.2 Authorship

Did the performers meaningfully choose, shape, negotiate, revise, or refuse the form?

IIIX’s documentary is highly curated, yet the members actively author much of what the camera receives. Conversely, the new-song assignment begins under meaningful public-image pressure even though the members later take ownership of it.

### 2.3 Relational reality

Does the performed form correspond to, reveal, or help create an actual relation or capacity?

TWINkle is the clearest case where a performed relation becomes **constitutive**: joint work, shared time, practical assistance, rehearsal, disclosure, and mutual dependence generate a real relationship that did not fully precede the role.

### 2.4 Jurisdiction

Who has the power to impose, revise, terminate, or monetize the construction?

The difference between invited TWINkle fan polling and LizNoir’s sponsor capture in E1-B is not that one involves an audience and the other does not. It is a difference in concentration of power and performers’ retained authority.

The compact three-event synthesis is:

> **IIIX proves that curation can reveal. Hoshimi’s homecoming proves that recurrence can renew. TWINkle proves that performance can create. None of those propositions makes deception harmless, memory ownerless, or institutions neutral.**

# 3. `偽りのREALISM` — IIIX and curated authenticity

## 3.1 Chronology and disclosure state

**TEXTUAL FACT / STRONG INFERENCE**

The event occurs after IIIX’s move into Hoshimi and after a period of reputational damage. Miho says IIIX’s evaluation is finally recovering (`評価も戻りつつある`), while a live-battle framing still calls them `元BIG4`. The event ends with IIIX explicitly targeting the remaining BIG4 and the other elite Hoshimi units.

It therefore belongs to the **post-Hoshimi-transfer, pre-US-reauthorization** IIIX state. E1-D conclusions about the American dissolution/re-founding may not be projected backward into this event.

## 3.2 Documentary access does not equal transparent access

**TEXTUAL FACT** — `st-eve-2408-race-001`

Fran explicitly argues that allowing documentary cameras backstage does not matter if they simply refuse to show their `素`:

> `そこで私達の素を見せなければいいんだから`

The text therefore denies a naive documentary ontology from the outset. Camera proximity does not automatically reveal the private or “real” IIIX.

Yet the opposite inference would also be wrong. The polished version the cameras receive is not meaningless fakery. It demonstrates actual capacities:

- instantaneous professional code-switching;
- camera awareness;
- image management;
- staff-facing courtesy;
- posing and interview control;
- suppression of conflict when the job requires it.

**Final rule:**

> **Performed professionalism remains real professionalism.**

## 3.3 Curated authenticity, not an authentic/fake binary

The members continue their antagonism outside or beneath the camera’s preferred surface through:

- messages;
- foot-stepping;
- fake sweetness;
- strategic annoyance;
- private insults;
- revenge gestures.

The documentary captures neither raw private truth nor pure fabrication. The unit knows it is being observed and deliberately decides what kinds of truth will become broadcastable.

The final audit therefore admits:

> **CURATED_AUTHENTICITY** — a selectively produced public form that does not provide total transparency but can still reveal stable structural truths about the performers.

In IIIX’s case, the program can truthfully reveal that the unit is:

- adversarial;
- highly professional;
- internally competitive;
- capable of modular collaboration;
- camera-literate;
- willing to turn hostility into entertainment.

## 3.4 Soft public-image coercion

**TEXTUAL FACT / INTERPRETATION** — principally `st-eve-2408-race-002`–`003`

Makino proposes a collectively produced summer song partly because he hopes a common goal will make the unit cooperate during filming. Kana immediately identifies the constraint: with cameras present, saying “we cannot do that” becomes difficult. When the production side extends filming until the song is finished, the members recognize that what looked like a suggestion has become a public-image obligation.

Makino later apologizes for underestimating the task.

The event therefore does **not** qualify as a clean instance of unconstrained self-authorship merely because IIIX ultimately owns the result.

The final audit admits:

> **SOFT_PUBLIC_IMAGE_COERCION** — a formally rejectable option whose refusal is made materially harder by observation, reputation, pride, or public framing.

This is a narrower category than direct institutional force. It should not be generalized beyond the evidence, but it enters the institutional-pressure ledger because it demonstrates that media presence can constrain autonomy without anyone issuing an explicit command.

## 3.5 IIIX becomes a creative organism

**TEXTUAL FACT / STRONG INFERENCE** — `st-eve-2408-race-003`–`005`

The event gives one of the clearest distributions of production intelligence anywhere in the event layer:

- **kana** — lyrical hook, keywords, rap possibilities, communicative clarity, audience legibility;
- **miho** — choreography, reference analysis, spatial visibility, scoring/performance optimization;
- **fran** — costume design, stage-art concept, material/aesthetic realization.

Fran explicitly rejects the assumption that “working together” must mean constant co-presence. Each member can separately maximize her own strongest domain and still contribute to one collective artifact.

The final unit concept is:

> **MODULAR CREATIVE COLLABORATION** — IIIX coordinates through differentiated specialist authorship rather than through emotional fusion or continuous consensus.

This strengthens, rather than replaces, the established `腐れ縁` / hostile-recognition model.

## 3.6 Center identity is locally contestable

**TEXTUAL FACT / STRONG INFERENCE** — `st-eve-2408-race-004`

The strongest internal creative dispute concerns center placement. Fran invokes role identity:

> `スリクスのセンターは私よ`

Kana answers that making this automatic is:

> `ただの思考停止`

because the lyric and musical balance may communicate better if kana occupies the middle for that passage. Miho agrees with kana’s musical reasoning while still considering continuity of IIIX’s established visual image.

This strengthens `OPEN-15` but must remain carefully scoped:

> **Fran remains IIIX’s overall center. E1-C proves only that center can function simultaneously as an identity-bearing office and a locally contestable staging decision.**

The event does not establish a formal IIIX center succession or collective-center constitution.

## 3.7 The final argument is both real and produced

**CONFLICT / AMBIGUITY**

The source deliberately refuses to classify the climactic center fight as either completely spontaneous or completely fabricated.

Evidence that it is substantively real:

- kana gives specific musical reasons;
- miho agrees on artistic grounds;
- Makino notes the subject is more serious than their ordinary fights;
- miho rejects the implication that all of their private arguments are meaningless.

Evidence of conscious production:

- afterward, the members say a documentary where they simply work smoothly would be boring;
- they discuss having created a point of excitement for the program;
- fran recognizes the pleasure of becoming the instigator rather than the victim of a prank.

Final adjudication:

> **IIIX takes real internal friction and deliberately amplifies/packages it as television.**

That is a better description than either “the cameras caught the real IIIX” or “the whole conflict was fake.”

## 3.8 Competitive continuity

The event ends in victory, but the creative breakthrough does not transform IIIX into an anti-competitive art collective. They immediately reorient toward the remaining BIG4 and their strongest Hoshimi rivals.

`絶対勝利` remains live. Creative authorship expands what victory can contain; it does not replace victory as a goal.

# 4. `お待たせしました里帰り` — public memory as civic reciprocity

## 4.1 Chronology

**TEXTUAL FACT / STRONG INFERENCE**

The event explicitly describes Tsuki no Tempest as extremely busy after becoming BIG4. Sakura and Kotono are trying to realize a joint hometown-live promise despite the scheduling pressure created by success.

This is a **post-BIG4 Hoshimi public-memory event**.

## 4.2 Success can endanger ordinary relational continuity

Sakura and Kotono’s inability even to find sufficient time to discuss the project is a modest but useful counterweight to triumphal success narratives. BIG4 status creates opportunity and recognition, but also consumes the ordinary relational time in which shared goals are remembered and revised.

The solution is not retreat from status; it is active preservation of the promise through planning and institutional coordination.

## 4.3 Professional rivals are also audiences for one another

Sunny Peace members debate favorite Tsuki songs; Tsuki members answer with favorite Sunny songs. This is low-stakes material, but it reinforces an important point:

> **Competition without erasure includes the capacity to receive a rival aesthetically.**

The units do not need to pretend the other is artistically irrelevant in order to want to defeat it.

## 4.4 Sea Paradise as memory palimpsest

**TEXTUAL FACT / INTERPRETATION**

Sea Paradise accumulates at least three distinct temporal layers:

1. Mana’s childhood/family visits, including watching the stage with Kotono;
2. Mana’s own local performance and audience relation;
3. Sunny Peace and Tsuki no Tempest returning as established idols with a new song.

The place is not a neutral backdrop and not a shrine with one authorized meaning. It is a **memory palimpsest**: later lives write onto the same space without erasing the earlier layer.

## 4.5 Mana as local civic figure, not only national myth

The flashback gives Mana a place-specific identity. She describes the venue through family memory, the joy of seeing beloved people smile, and gratitude to Hoshimi. `星の海の記憶` is introduced as an expression of thanks to the city she loves.

The Mana archive therefore contains at least:

- private sister memory;
- intimate Makino memory;
- Hoshimi-agency history;
- national idol legend;
- **local civic memory**.

This strengthens `OPEN-18` without requiring any supernatural claim.

## 4.6 Haruko’s zero-line recognition wound

**TEXTUAL FACT** — Mana-era handshake sequence

One of the event’s most important historical corrections is that Hoshimi’s early generation was not an equal public dyad. Mana immediately receives a long handshake line while Haruko initially has no one.

Haruko internally confronts the possibility that nobody is interested in meeting her at all. Makino becomes the first person in her line; others then begin to follow. Haruko dryly calls the tactic `サクラ`.

The sequence matters for three reasons:

1. it preserves Haruko’s public-recognition asymmetry beside Mana rather than retrospectively smoothing it away;
2. it gives material historical depth to Haruko’s later patience with recognition, delayed blooming, and other idols’ insecurity;
3. it shows Hoshimi’s “founding continuity” was never identical celebrity status.

This does not revise E1-A’s current-status finding: Haruko remains a present, future-bearing idol.

## 4.7 Coordination seeding

Makino cannot make people become Haruko fans. He can make the first socially awkward action—being the first person in an empty line—less costly for others.

The audit retains the narrower descriptive term:

> **COORDINATION_SEEDING** — management reduces a coordination barrier so latent audience interest can become expressible, without claiming authority over the audience’s later preference.

This should not be inflated into proof that managers can manufacture authentic popularity at will.

## 4.8 Mana/Makino and the unfulfilled promise

The event adds direct relational material:

- Mana says leisure spaces let her see the smiles of people she loves;
- the dialogue places Makino inside the `大好きな人` implication;
- she takes his hand;
- they promise to return for another hometown live.

Mana dies before that dyadic promise can be fulfilled.

The later Sunny/Tsuki homecoming therefore should **not** be described as literally completing Mana and Makino’s promised future. It changes what can ethically be done with the memory of an interrupted future.

## 4.9 Functional inheritance without formal duplication

After hearing the old history, Kotono wants the current event to communicate gratitude and current growth. The decisive formal choice is that Sunny/Tsuki do not simply reproduce Mana’s local set or rely on `星の海の記憶` as sacred mandatory form.

They make a new song:

> `パラダイス！`

The audit therefore admits:

> **FUNCTIONAL_INHERITANCE_WITHOUT_FORMAL_DUPLICATION** — an earlier work’s relational function is inherited while its exact formal expression remains open to replacement, revision, or new authorship.

Mana turned local attachment and gratitude into performance. The later generation performs the same function with a song that belongs to its present relation with the city.

This is a particularly strong concrete realization of the broader V2 rule that inheritance must not become replacement or reenactment.

## 4.10 `お姉ちゃんの分まで` does not restore replacement logic

Kotono’s private statement at Mana’s autograph—carrying gratitude `お姉ちゃんの分まで`—is significant but should be interpreted within the event’s plural authorship.

It supports:

- stewardship;
- sisterly continuity;
- acknowledging gratitude Mana can no longer perform herself.

It does not by itself establish that Kotono now possesses Mana’s public role or that the present live is authorized only through blood relation.

## 4.11 Civic memory reciprocity

The event makes local public memory reciprocal:

- the city remembers and supports Hoshimi idols;
- idols return with labor, songs, collaboration, local references, gratitude, and another promise;
- the venue stores both sides of that relation over time.

The final audit admits:

> **CIVIC_MEMORY_RECIPROCITY** — place-based public memory remains alive through renewed exchange between performers and the community that remembers them.

## 4.12 Future-generative memory

The event closes by repeating a future-directed promise structure. Mana and Makino once promised to return. Sakura and Kotono now promise another future return by the living.

The repetition is not substitution. It demonstrates:

> **FUTURE_GENERATIVE_MEMORY** — remembrance remains living when it generates new work, relationships, obligations, or future meetings rather than only preserving a finished image of the dead.

This is one of the strongest E1-C contributions to the eventual Mana/Hoshimi synthesis.

# 5. `欺瞞の最強TWINkle` — representation, performance, and constitutive intimacy

## 5.1 Chronology guardrail

**TEXTUAL FACT / STRONG INFERENCE**

At this disclosure boundary:

- Kokoro is newly BIG4;
- kana is an established Hoshimi/IIIX performer;
- Kokoro still attends school;
- kana still hopes greater visibility, including the prize CM, may help her father notice her;
- the later American-arc confrontation with her father has not yet been admitted.

E1-D may later recontextualize this motive but cannot rewrite its present sincerity.

## 5.2 Audience-originated pairing with actual consent

A fan poll identifies kana/Kokoro as a desired temporary pair. Makino asks them to participate rather than directly assigning the relationship as non-negotiable. Both initially reject the pairing and later volunteer when the prizes align with their own interests.

This produces a useful mixed form:

> **externally proposed identity + internally accepted opportunity.**

It is neither pure self-origin nor strong coercion.

## 5.3 The initial friendship representation is factually false

The event does not hide what the characters are doing. Kana and Kokoro knowingly fabricate:

- an immediate friendship history;
- shared outings;
- older-sister/younger-sister intimacy;
- physical closeness;
- speech-register intimacy.

The source itself uses terms such as:

- `仲良しゴッコ`;
- `偽物`;
- `嘘`;
- `本物よりも本物の仲良し`.

This matters because V2 must not let a sophisticated theory of persona dissolve ordinary truth conditions.

> **At the level of representational truth, the early TWINkle narrative is deceptive.**

## 5.4 Kana’s media intelligence includes relational semiotics

Kana understands that audiences infer intimacy from socially coded signals:

- pronoun/address/register choice;
- touch;
- spatial proximity;
- shared anecdotes;
- platform polls;
- differentiated branding;
- visible familiarity.

Her expertise is not only “good at SNS.” She can treat **relationship grammar itself as media technology**.

This will be important for the later Japanese voice/register synthesis because it demonstrates that register can be intentionally performed as a public sign before the underlying relationship reaches the same state.

## 5.5 Kokoro’s register shift is strategic before it is intimate

Kokoro says casual speech with kana is difficult because polite speech is habitual; she normally uses casual speech only with Ai. Kana asks her to shift register because it will make TWINkle look special.

Therefore:

> **address/register change cannot be treated as automatic proof of intimacy without contextual analysis.**

In this event, linguistic intimacy is initially staged before it becomes relationally plausible.

## 5.6 Invited audience co-authorship

Kokoro proposes that the fans who asked for the pairing should also help define it. SNS voting helps determine angel/devil framing and public expectations; kana/Kokoro retain authority over lyrics, choreography, and implementation.

E1-C therefore admits:

> **INVITED_AUDIENCE_COAUTHORSHIP** — performers voluntarily open a bounded creative domain to distributed audience input while retaining meaningful implementation authority.

This is deliberately contrasted with E1-B `PERFORMER_CAPTURE`.

| Dimension | TWINkle fan participation | LizNoir sponsor pressure |
|---|---|---|
| Initiation | performers/institution invite | powerful sponsor imposes |
| Power | distributed | concentrated |
| Scope | bounded concept input | lyrics/image/center demanded |
| Performer authority | retained | initially constrained |
| Ethical risk | manipulation of voters | dispossession of performers |

The lesson is not “fans are good, sponsors are bad.” It is that **audience power must be analyzed through concentration, jurisdiction, information, and performer exit rights**.

## 5.7 Kokoro’s school wound survives BIG4

**TEXTUAL FACT** — `st-eve-2411-race-003`

Kana discovers that Kokoro remains socially isolated at school despite becoming BIG4. Kokoro links the history to:

- resentment around her wealth;
- teasing;
- her own escalating retaliatory pranks;
- hardened exclusion.

She admits that part of her desire to become a top idol was to become impossible for those classmates to ignore.

The event then supplies a clean limit on metric sovereignty:

> **Institutional visibility can certify professional status; it cannot compel reciprocal interpersonal recognition.**

Kokoro’s rank matters. It simply cannot do every kind of social work she wanted it to do.

## 5.8 The event refuses a pure victim/perpetrator binary

Kana is angry on Kokoro’s behalf, but the story also lets Kokoro recognize that her own retaliatory behavior intensified the school relation. Kana does not demand reconciliation. Her advice is strategic and bounded: there is no benefit in manufacturing unnecessary enemies when Kokoro has tools for making ordinary interaction less hostile.

This is best treated as a **recursive hostile system**, not a simplistic innocence narrative.

## 5.9 The idol smile as chosen social technology

Kokoro later uses an `アイドルスマイル` to ask a classmate for missed notes. The classmate responds positively and appears to support the performance. Kokoro explicitly refuses to overstate the result:

> `お友達じゃないです！　クラスメイトです！`

This is one of E1-C’s strongest anti-binary findings.

> **A deliberately performed public register can create a genuine social opening without making the underlying relationship deeper than it is.**

The smile is strategic and real in consequence. Kokoro does not have to identify spontaneous unfiltered behavior as the only authentic self.

## 5.10 False friendship becomes behaviorally real

Across the project, kana and Kokoro begin doing things that no longer reduce cleanly to the invented publicity narrative:

- kana brings Kokoro’s forgotten notebook to school;
- kana listens to and responds to the school problem;
- Kokoro hosts kana overnight;
- they write lyrics together;
- kana coaches Kokoro’s dance;
- kana helps with English work;
- they learn each other’s habits;
- Kokoro tries kana’s advice;
- they continue insulting one another rather than switching into sentimental harmony.

The relationship becomes **behaviorally real before it becomes terminologically settled**.

The audit admits:

> **CONSTITUTIVE_PERFORMANCE** — repeated performance of a role helps create practices, time, disclosure, and mutual labor through which a relationship or identity can become real rather than merely representing something that already existed.

This is causal, not mystical. The mechanism is sustained interaction.

## 5.11 The final fan-vote manipulation is not redeemed by genuine intimacy

Kana’s final MC implies that if they lose:

- TWINkle may dissolve;
- the song may never be performed again.

Makino immediately challenges the claim after the result. Kana defends herself by pointing out that she technically said `かも`.

The final audit therefore retains a hard distinction:

> **Relational authenticity does not confer representational truth.**

A relationship may genuinely have formed, the performers may own the stunt, and the audience may enjoy it—yet a misleading factual/emotional claim used to influence voting remains ethically contestable.

This is the principal reason the V1 shorthand “a persona is true when it is authored” is **QUALIFIED** rather than simply confirmed.

## 5.12 Participatory audience vs informed audience

TWINkle’s fans have real power:

- they create demand for the pair;
- help select the concept;
- vote the result.

They are also vulnerable to information asymmetry.

Future audience analysis therefore must separate:

1. **participatory power** — whether fans can shape outcomes;
2. **epistemic position** — whether the information on which they act is accurate enough for the participation to be meaningfully informed.

A participatory audience can still be manipulated.

# 16. Final E1-C conclusion

E1-C is the tranche where *IDOLY PRIDE* becomes much harder to describe with a simple vocabulary of “real self” and “idol persona.”

IIIX demonstrates that the camera does not uncover a pristine private truth. It changes behavior, narrows options, and creates incentives. Yet the public form the unit produces can still reveal something deeply accurate: IIIX really does collaborate through antagonism, specialist craft, and competitive self-assertion.

The Hoshimi homecoming demonstrates the same structure in memory rather than persona. Returning to Sea Paradise does not make the past happen again. Mana is not restored, her promise with Makino is not literally completed, and the current idols do not become substitute Mana. The living generation instead preserves the **function** of the old relation—local gratitude, memory, future promise—by producing a new form.

TWINkle makes the argument most dangerous and most precise. Kana and Kokoro begin by lying. Their public friendship story is factually false. But the production of that false story creates real time, real work, real disclosure, and real care. The performance becomes constitutive. At the same time, the final vote manipulation proves that this relational reality does not absolve representational dishonesty.

The mature E1-C rule is therefore:

> **Construction is unavoidable; answerability is the ethical variable.**

An idol identity can be authored, edited, repeated, inherited, branded, staged, or collaboratively produced and still be real. What matters is not the fantasy of an unmediated self, but whether the people involved retain meaningful capacity to answer what the construction says and does—and whether the audience is being asked to act on claims that remain answerable to truth.

This is now the governing prospective state for E1-D.
