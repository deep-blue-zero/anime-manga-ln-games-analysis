# Purpose

This file freezes the Phase-1B primary-source reading of the five SUNNY PEACE unit-origin bundles before any new retrieval of historical SUNNY PEACE analytical prose. It preserves the distinction between what the origin sources establish, what they retrospectively explain about the already-frozen anime/Hoshimi state, and what they materially revise.

The inherited anime endpoint remains immutable as a prospective anime record. The completed Hoshimi anime/game expansion audit is likewise not rewritten; origin-driven corrections are routed through an explicit addendum.

# Governing tranche thesis

**SUNNY PEACE is not constructed from people who are simply “sunny.” It is constructed from people whose capacity to shine has been interrupted, misrecognized, suppressed, instrumentalized, or made dependent on somebody else, and from one veteran whose own light has repeatedly failed to receive public recognition. Its founding logic is therefore reciprocal illumination: people who cannot sustainably shine alone become capable of doing so together without being required to become the same kind of person.**

This is stronger than the anime-only proposition that SUNNY PEACE occupies the brighter/more extroverted side of Hoshimi’s two-unit differentiation. The origins show that “sun” is not an innate temperament category. It is a relational achievement.

# 1. Chisa — desire had been moralized as burden

## Direct source facts

`st-group-sun-01-01-01` establishes that Chisa was an active and adventurous small child before a near-drowning accident. Saki blames herself and vows never to let Chisa out of her sight; Chisa responds by promising to become a `いい子` who will not cause her sister trouble. Afterwards Chisa plays outside less, while Saki becomes markedly overprotective.

Chisa’s later reserve is therefore not presented as an uncaused essence. The origin supplies a specific learned moral equation: independent desire can endanger people, frighten people, and burden the person she loves most.

The same mechanism suppresses her idol desire. She wants to sing and dance with Saki but pretends not to care because she fears holding Saki back. Meanwhile Saki has also suppressed her own idol dream in order to remain beside Chisa. The dependency is bilateral before either sister recognizes it.

At Hoshimi’s audition Chisa spends conspicuous effort praising Saki. Makino’s internal judgment is precise: Chisa is awkward but makes him want to root for her; the problem is whether such intense dependence will leave her hidden in her sister’s shadow and whether she can proceed without relying on Saki.

Saki passes and Chisa fails. Chisa initially treats that result as proof that her dream only had meaning if shared with Saki. Her involuntary grief at failure then reveals something she had not fully admitted to herself: `私……アイドルになりたいよ……！`

Makino later contacts her privately. He preserves the audition result—she did fail—but asks a different question: whether she still wants idolhood even if she cannot be in the same group as Saki. Only after she answers yes does he scout her, explicitly warning that her treatment and debut priority will not equal Saki’s and that they likely will not share a group.

In the retrospective frame Chisa states that separation from Saki was necessary for becoming a full-fledged idol. Shizuku’s act of taking her hand and the new SUNNY relationships provide an alternative support network. Chisa explicitly says that if she had remained in Saki’s group she might still be depending on her.

## Strong inference

Chisa’s basic developmental problem is not best modeled as generic low self-esteem or shyness. It is **self-suppression produced by a burden-avoidance ethic**. Her agency strengthens when desire can be expressed without being morally classified as harm to Saki.

The sisters form a **reciprocal sacrifice loop**: Chisa suppresses desire so as not to burden Saki; Saki suppresses desire so as not to abandon Chisa. Each sacrifice gives the other reason to sacrifice again.

## Revision pressure on Hoshimi audit

The completed Hoshimi audit correctly identifies bilateral individuation, but the origin adds a material recruitment fact: Makino identifies Chisa’s dependence during the audition and later tests her desire under a condition of separation before scouting her. Therefore the later unit split is not merely an artistic sun/moon experiment that incidentally individuates Chisa. **Chisa’s developmental need for non-identical placement is already present in Makino’s recruitment logic.**

Guardrail: this does not prove Makino predicted the full later sister arc or knew the final unit allocation from the first interview. He identifies a risk and deliberately creates a condition under which Chisa’s desire must become independently avowed.

# 2. Rei — measurable proof is a defense against paternal invalidation

## Direct source facts

`st-group-sun-01-02-01` places the origin of Rei’s dance desire in a theme-park parade. She experiences dancing as capable of enchanting others and explicitly wants to `ダンスでみんなに夢を届けたい`. Audience orientation therefore predates both family conflict and idolhood.

Her older brother becomes the first relational model of artistic self-authorship. Their father insists the brother become a doctor and dismisses music as useless; the brother refuses, saying `俺の人生は俺が決める`, and leaves home to pursue his band. Rei calls herself his first fan.

After the brother leaves, paternal expectation transfers to Rei. She is made to quit dance and is expected to inherit the medical path. She secretly resumes dance, re-discovers bodily joy, and is seen by a young girl whose admiration helps Rei recognize that dance is genuinely her own dream.

When Rei declares the dream, her father interprets it as flight from study and tells her he is disappointed. Rei responds by pursuing competition results as evidence capable of forcing recognition. Even winning proves insufficient. The origin makes the next escalation explicit: she needs an `無視出来ないくらいの成果`; dance must become paid professional work.

That requirement leads indirectly to Hoshimi. Rei initially treats idolhood instrumentally: it can make dance her job and thereby prove to her father that the choice is viable. She does not pretend she began as an idol fan.

After SUNNY’s debut, that instrumental origin creates a new shame. Rei sees other members’ love of idols and worries she is unworthy of the group. Chisa asks a crucial present-tense question: regardless of the initial reason, what does Rei feel now?

At SUNNY’s first handshake event, the same young girl who once admired Rei’s dancing recognizes her and identifies herself as a long-time fan. The childhood circuit closes: Rei once wanted dance to give people dreams; now someone tells her that her dancing did exactly that. Rei decides to pursue idolhood seriously because people receive and return her performance. Her father’s recognition ceases to be the reason she wants to continue.

## Strong inference

Rei’s severity and result-orientation are not generic perfectionism. **Measurement became a survival language in a family system that treated artistic desire as unserious until it could defeat an externally imposed standard of legitimacy.**

The mature movement is not rigor -> softness. It is **external-legitimacy -> audience reciprocity**. The original dream was relational; paternal conflict narrowed it into proof; idolhood allows that older relational motive to return at a higher level.

## Revision pressure on Hoshimi audit

The Hoshimi audit already correctly states “dance first, idolhood as professional container, then broader idol identity.” The origin strongly strengthens this and specifies the transformation mechanism: **fan recognition**, rather than abstract team belonging alone, converts idolhood from instrument into chosen vocation.

The brother/first-fan and young-girl/Rei relations should be routed to the later audience/recognition synthesis.

# 3. Shizuku — visibility must be reclaimed after punitive visibility

## Direct source facts

Shizuku becomes an intense idol fan through her older cousin Mone (`秋宮もね`), whom she calls an older sister. She follows Mone’s debut, becomes a live regular, and develops unusual fluency inside fan communities despite ordinary social difficulty. The fan identity `ぷにもちどろっぷ` gives her a participatory public in which shared interest makes speech easier.

Mone encourages young Shizuku to write honestly in an elementary-school graduation essay that she wants to become an idol. Classmates mock that aspiration because she is quiet, unsmiling, and socially awkward.

The ridicule develops into sustained bullying. Peers misrepresent her interactions with older idol fans, circulate abuse on a school underground site, hide belongings, and use her fandom and idol dream as material for public humiliation. A teacher then catastrophically mishandles the situation by publicly identifying her as the person claiming bullying and stating that bullying can have causes on the victim side.

Shizuku withdraws from school and deletes her fan social account. Idol performances become psychological support during this withdrawal. Mone gives her a model of fan/performer reciprocity: fans send support toward idols; idols send an `エール` back toward fans.

Even after moving, Shizuku thinks idolhood itself is closed to her. She therefore applies to Hoshimi intending to become a **manager**, a way of remaining near the thing she loves without exposing herself to the stage. Haruko’s mistaken routing places her in the idol audition.

After the failed accidental audition, Mone calls the classmates’ judgment a `呪いの言葉`. Makino later invites Shizuku into a proposed unit. When she refuses, he brings her to a studio and asks her to evaluate a trainee (Sakura). Shizuku accurately sees both Sakura’s technical weakness and her luminous pleasure.

Makino then articulates the group concept: people with potential who `一人では輝けない`, including a person who knows nothing and a person who knows so much that knowledge itself prevents forward movement, can join and advance step by step. He reframes Shizuku’s fan knowledge as professional competence: someone who understands fans this deeply can become an idol who values them deeply.

Shizuku chooses idolhood while still acknowledging poor speech, social difficulty and inability to smile easily. Later she stops trying to become Mone and seeks her own idol form. Mone’s retirement reverses their earlier fan/performer direction: Mone will now support Shizuku from the audience. Shizuku identifies SUNNY itself as `私の青春`.

## Strong inference

Shizuku’s story is not adequately described as “watcher becomes performer.” It is **visibility reclaimed after punitive visibility**. The school environment made being seen dangerous; fandom was initially a safer public in which she could participate without being judged by ordinary peer norms. Idolhood asks her to become visible again, but under a relational structure that treats her knowledge and temperament as resources rather than defects.

SUNNY is consequently both professional unit and reparative peer ecology. It supplies the ordinary adolescent social life that bullying had interrupted.

## Revision pressure on Hoshimi audit

This requires an addendum to the group-formation findings. The Hoshimi audit correctly says Makino’s formation was partly experimental, but the origin supplies a much more explicit conceptual design: **complementary incompleteness**. He is not merely sorting extroverts from introverts or bright from cool aesthetics. He is deliberately assembling people whose deficits and competencies can become mutually compensatory.

Guardrail: this does not imply all members share one deficit, nor that Makino fully understands every wound when he recruits them.

# 4. Haruko — the “support person” was recruited because kindness itself was treated as future infrastructure

## Direct source facts

Haruko’s idol desire begins at the first NEXT VENUS Grand Prix. As a lost child she is helped by former top idol and producer Hoshimura Megumi (`星村恵`). Megumi tells her that an idol needs `負けない心、挫けない心、諦めない心`. Haruko’s long-term goal becomes being able to meet Megumi again and introduce herself as `アイドルの佐伯遙子です`.

The industry repeatedly rejects Haruko as plain, insufficiently glamorous, and lacking `特別感`. Megumi does not deny that Haruko may be considered unsuited. Instead she rejects suitability as the governing moral gate: `向いてる人しかアイドルを目指しちゃいけないの？` What matters is whether Haruko can believe herself and persist in the desire.

Megumi connects her to the newly forming Hoshimi Production. Saegusa’s first judgment is also that Haruko may be unsuited—but for a different reason: she may be too kind for a ranking/competition environment. Haruko refuses the premise that success must mean harming rivals; she wants idols to cooperate and raise the industry together. Saegusa accepts the combination of other-directed kindness and self-directed refusal to give up as precisely the sort of person future Hoshimi idols may need. He names Haruko **Hoshimi Production’s first idol**.

Haruko meets Mana only afterwards. She immediately recognizes the girl Saegusa scouted because Mana visibly possesses the `華`, impression and specialness Haruko has repeatedly been told she lacks. Haruko debuts first, but Mana rapidly surpasses her. Haruko admits envy but not resentment; Mana is friend and colleague, and Mana’s belief helps Haruko continue believing in herself.

Saegusa retrospectively accepts management responsibility for failing to turn Haruko’s potential into work, rather than attributing the entire outcome to Haruko’s lack of talent.

After Mana’s death and Hoshimi’s institutional crisis, Haruko’s schedule becomes empty and she seriously considers ending idolhood. She also performs office/administrative labor because the agency is overwhelmed. New trainees create anxiety—new talent could reduce her already scarce work—but also curiosity and hope because Mana, once a junior, had repeatedly given her courage.

When Makino describes a new Sakura-centered “sun-like” group, Haruko quietly wants in and awkwardly attempts to signal that desire. Makino has already included her. His reason is explicit: the group contains members who cannot shine alone; it needs someone capable of enveloping, teaching and guiding them. Her experience, kindness, refusal to give up and intense love of idolhood are not ancillary traits; without them, he says, the group cannot exist.

Haruko initially thinks she will be the person who makes the scattered members shine. She later realizes that she too has joined their shared light rather than remaining outside it as caretaker. At the Grand Prix she finally fulfills the childhood self-introduction to Megumi as `星見プロ所属のアイドル サニーピースの佐伯遙子です`.

## Strong inference

Haruko’s role as “infrastructure” is not the consolation prize of a less talented adult. **It is the trait for which Hoshimi first selected her and the trait Makino later treats as structurally indispensable to SUNNY PEACE.** Her care work and endurance are themselves professional competencies.

At the same time, the origin refuses to let support become self-erasure. Haruko’s old problem is that she can imagine herself as the person who helps other stars more easily than as someone who deserves a star’s future herself. SUNNY succeeds when she becomes both support and supported member.

The institutional-history claim must be strengthened: Haruko is not simply a “pre-Mana bridge”; she is literally Hoshimi Production’s first idol, predating Mana’s recruitment.

## Revision pressure on Hoshimi audit

A Hoshimi addendum is required. The existing audit’s description of Haruko as continuity memory remains correct but understates her founding status and the original management rationale for selecting her. The addendum should also record Saegusa’s explicit self-critique about failing to create work that used Haruko’s potential.

# 5. Sakura — sunlight is re-authored after compulsory smiling, illness and death

## Direct source facts

Sakura has severe heart disease from early childhood. Her mother’s grief and guilt lead young Sakura to decide that she should smile so her mother will not worry or cry. As the illness worsens, her father works more to pay for treatment and her mother carries the household; Sakura consciously tries to be an undemanding `いい子` so as not to burden them.

She overhears nurses say she likely has only a few years to live. For the first time she explicitly confronts annihilation, pain and fear: she does not want to disappear and does not want to die.

A hospitalized girl later called Tomo (`友ちゃん`) sees through Sakura’s forced smile and tells her that when she is hurting she does not have to smile; she may cry. Sakura finally sobs and admits fear. Tomo becomes her closest friend and her `楽しいことの師匠`, introducing Sakura to the many enjoyable possibilities outside hospital life.

Tomo is also terminally ill. The two create `やってみたかったリスト` / bucket lists. This is the origin of Sakura’s later apparently inexhaustible appetite for experiences.

At a Hoshimi festival Sakura encounters Mana without knowing who she is. Later the story explicitly juxtaposes Tomo with the sick fan whose letter motivated Mana’s Reiz performance: Makino recognizes Tomo’s repeated phrase about going to her oshi’s live `這ってでも`. Tomo succeeds in attending that live before dying shortly before the next Hoshimi festival.

At death Tomo tells Sakura to fulfill the wishes Tomo can no longer fulfill. Sakura accordingly vows to survive, become healthy and do the things on the list for Tomo as well.

Sakura receives a heart transplant that Christmas. During painful rehabilitation she begins interpreting the transplanted heartbeat as encouragement to continue. Because this Phase-1B tranche inherits the anime endpoint, the project knows the donor is Mana; however, **this origin story itself does not newly disclose donor identity** and must not be cited as an independent explicit donor statement.

Two years later, while visiting Tomo’s grave, Sakura hears Haruko speaking at Mana’s grave. Sakura’s heart reacts to Mana’s name/Haruko’s speech. She tells the stranger not to quit because her chest seems to be telling her to keep going. Haruko, already considering retirement, decides to try a little longer.

Three years after the transplant, Sakura is wandering through Hoshimi pursuing her own experience list when her feet inexplicably take her to Hoshimi Production. Her heart strongly reacts. Haruko mistakes her for an audition candidate and escorts her into the agency. The story itself later identifies Haruko and Mana as indispensable causal predecessors of the future in which all of SUNNY PEACE gathers.

At home Sakura finally receives Tomo’s own completed notebook. Tomo anticipated Sakura’s likely response: Sakura might try to fulfill Tomo’s wishes in her place. Tomo explicitly rejects that substitution. These were **Tomo’s** wishes; Sakura should pursue **Sakura’s own** wishes. Tomo wants the notebook to help Sakura remember her and smile. She says Sakura’s smile made Tomo happy and asks her to keep making people happy with that smile.

Sakura then articulates the SUNNY-level form: she wants herself / SUNNY PEACE to illuminate the people she loves so that they can smile.

## Strong inference

Sakura’s brightness has at least three historical layers:

1. **protective performance** — smiling so her mother and family will not worry;
2. **permission for emotional truth** — Tomo teaches her that care does not require suppressing fear and grief;
3. **chosen generativity** — after receiving Tomo’s final message, Sakura can understand smiling not as compulsory masking but as something that genuinely reaches other people.

Therefore “Sakura is naturally sunny” is inadequate. Her brightness is both temperament and practiced ethical relation, forged through direct knowledge of mortality.

The bucket list is similarly misread if treated as comic hyperactivity. It is a mortality artifact: the practice of naming a future when future time was medically uncertain.

Most importantly, Tomo’s notebook gives Sakura an **earlier anti-substitution lesson** that formally anticipates the Mana inheritance crisis. Tomo tells the survivor not to become the executor of the dead person’s unlived life. Memory may inspire action, but the living person must pursue her own desires. Sakura later has to re-learn that principle under far more metaphysically confusing conditions when Mana’s actual heart is inside her body.

The heartbeat’s origin also complicates the anime endpoint. The endpoint is correct that “the heart has provenance without retaining jurisdiction,” but the origin shows why Sakura once trusted bodily intuition so deeply: the heartbeat had accompanied her through rehabilitation, survival, the encounter that kept Haruko in idolhood, and the path into Hoshimi. Treating it as meaningful was not irrational credulity produced only by Mana resemblance; it was a long-standing survival epistemology.

## Revision pressure on Hoshimi audit

An addendum is required for Sakura’s arrival at Hoshimi and for the causal role of Haruko/Mana. The Hoshimi audit already protects information order around the transplant and heart; the origin adds a pre-Hoshimi chain that explains why Sakura experiences the heart as guidance.

It also materially deepens SUNNY’s “sun” symbolism: sunlight is not innocence before darkness. It is a way of living after fear, illness and death without allowing them to monopolize the future.

# 6. Unit-level findings

## 6.1 SUNNY PEACE is a designed ecology of non-self-sufficient strengths

The clearest formation statement comes from Makino in Shizuku’s origin: he wants to gather people with potential who cannot shine alone, including the person who knows nothing and the person whose knowledge prevents movement, so that they can advance together. Haruko’s inclusion is justified as the experienced member who can envelop, teach and guide those people.

The five origins make the complementary pattern concrete:

- **Sakura:** enormous affective/generative force, but enters idolhood almost accidentally and initially knows little about it;
- **Chisa:** sincere desire but desire is inhibited by burden anxiety and dependence on Saki;
- **Shizuku:** immense idol literacy but visibility itself has become frightening;
- **Rei:** elite bodily/professional discipline, but idolhood begins as an instrument and must become relationally chosen;
- **Haruko:** industry experience, care and endurance, but years of non-recognition have eroded belief that she herself can still shine.

No member merely supplies what another lacks in a mechanical puzzle. The more important point is that each person has a strength that becomes distorted when exercised alone. The group creates conditions in which strengths can be redistributed without requiring personality homogenization.

## 6.2 “Sun” is not extroversion

Only Sakura straightforwardly resembles a conventional high-energy “sun” archetype, and even her smile has a history of protective compulsion. Chisa and Shizuku are quiet; Rei is severe; Haruko’s brightness coexists with long professional disappointment.

The unit’s solar identity is therefore better modeled as **circulated light**: helping another person recover or transmit possibility. This remains an interpretation pending later event/performance evidence, but the origin tranche supplies unusually strong structural support.

## 6.3 Each origin contains a problem of recognition

- Chisa: can her desire be recognized as hers rather than an attachment to Saki?
- Rei: can artistic life be recognized as legitimate without paternal ratification?
- Shizuku: can a person publicly misrecognized as laughable/unfit become visible on her own terms?
- Haruko: can durable professional worth exist without spectacular `華` or early market success?
- Sakura: can a life shaped by other people’s deaths remain recognizably her own life?

Recognition therefore looks likely to be one of SUNNY PEACE’s deepest common structures, stronger than a generic “hope” label.

## 6.4 The dead do not own the living

Sakura/Tomo gives the tranche’s clearest explicit statement. Tomo anticipates that Sakura will try to live her bucket list for her and tells her not to. This is a local friendship statement, but it is structurally resonant with the frozen anime’s Mana/Kotono and Mana/Sakura conclusions.

This should be routed to the later grief/inheritance synthesis as a major comparative case. It demonstrates that the franchise’s anti-substitution ethic is not exclusive to Mana’s supernatural legacy.

# 7. Hoshimi-audit addendum decision

**Decision: REQUIRED.**

At minimum the addendum must record:

1. **Chisa recruitment / split provenance:** Makino recognized sister-dependence during the audition and tested Chisa’s desire under separation before scouting her.
2. **Shizuku / group-concept provenance:** Makino explicitly describes a unit of people who cannot shine alone and whose complementary knowledge/ignorance can move them forward together.
3. **Haruko founding status:** Haruko is Hoshimi Production’s first idol; Saegusa recruited her partly because future idols would need her combination of kindness and persistence.
4. **Haruko’s SUNNY necessity:** Makino says her experience, kindness, persistence and idol passion are structurally necessary to the group, not veteran decoration.
5. **Sakura arrival / heart-guidance prehistory:** the origin supplies the pre-Hoshimi trajectory through Tomo, transplant rehabilitation, Mana’s grave/Haruko encounter, and the accidental audition.
6. **Sakura/Tomo anti-substitution:** this must be kept distinct from but routed toward the later Mana inheritance analysis.

The addendum must **not** rewrite the frozen anime endpoint or imply that an anime-only viewer possessed these explanations earlier.

# 9. Frozen conclusion before historical comparison

The dominant source-native revision is:

> **SUNNY PEACE is the franchise’s clearest early experiment in converting compromised individual forms of light into reciprocal social light. Its members are not gathered because they already embody uncomplicated positivity. They are gathered because each has a reason she cannot sustainably become what she wants alone. The unit’s brightness is therefore less a mood than a social technology: desire can be returned to the person who suppressed it, professionalism can be returned to its audience-facing purpose, visibility can be made safe enough to inhabit, care can include the caregiver, and inherited life can become one’s own future.**

This conclusion is now frozen for adversarial comparison. Historical SUNNY PEACE analyses retrieved after this point may confirm, refine, challenge, or reveal omissions, but they cannot be treated as having generated the primary-source findings above.
