# 2. E1-D prospective governing thesis

The two events form a deliberate **dissolution / re-authorization experiment**.

Part 1 does not merely introduce obstacles. It removes or discredits the external purposes that originally made IIIX useful to each member:

- **kana** finally reaches the father she hoped fame would bring back to her, but contact does not restore the family relation she imagined;
- **fran** receives credible investment for her fashion work, making a non-idol route toward her old dream newly viable;
- **miho** is forced to confront that her score-maximizing path toward “the top” can coexist with public erasure of Yō and may have drifted away from the audience-facing promise that originally mattered.

The resulting dissolution is therefore not random emotional collapse. It exposes the fragility of the original IIIX compact:

> **If the unit exists because each member needs it as an instrument for a separate external goal, then success, failure, or goal exhaustion can make the unit disposable.**

Part 2 does not restore the old compact. It produces a different structure:

- fran initially needs the unit again for brand promotion, but reveals costly aesthetic commitment to these specific partners;
- kana explicitly lacks a replacement life-goal and chooses a **provisional reason to continue** rather than pretending to possess a settled vocation;
- miho moves Yō's memory from sealed private possession toward public circulation and lets her promise become a motive that IIIX can participate in rather than a private burden that excludes them.

The resulting unit is still hostile, competitive, self-interested, and explicitly organized around `絶対勝利`.

The key change is subtler:

> **IIIX survives because its members choose to continue after the old instrumental bargain has already failed.**

This is not sentimental friendship replacing ambition.

It is **re-authorization after motive collapse**.

# 3. Part 1 — the old IIIX bargain reaches its endpoint

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Hoshimi cannot afford a month of hotel lodging, so IIIX shares an apartment. The arrangement produces forced ordinary interdependence:

miho explicitly inventories the first two goals while wondering whether they will actually be realized, then asks the same question of herself.

This makes the subsequent structure unusually controlled: the story immediately tests each external motive.

# 4. kana — recognition succeeds while the hoped-for family restoration fails

## 4.1 Fame actually accomplishes what kana thought it was for

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Kana's father sees the American broadcast, recognizes the possibility that the performer is his daughter, contacts the organizers, and meets her.

This matters because the story does **not** invalidate kana's strategy by saying celebrity visibility was useless.

It worked.

Her years of media work do, in fact, make her findable.

The failure comes afterward.

## 4.2 Kana did not merely want identification; she wanted restored kinship

**TEXTUAL FACT / STRONG INFERENCE**

Kana rapidly imagines ordinary future family life:

- they can meet whenever they want;
- perhaps he can return to Japan with her;
- they can spend time together from now on.

Her father's explanation also partially revises her childhood narrative. He says he had written letters and sent birthday presents after leaving her with her grandfather, but these stopped reaching her; he had gone abroad for medical treatment and later formed another family.

Kana therefore learns that the earlier disappearance was not exactly the malicious abandonment she had imagined.

But this factual correction cannot supply the relation she wants in the present.

## 4.3 `知り合いの子` is the decisive injury

**TEXTUAL FACT**

When kana unexpectedly arrives at her father's home, he describes her to his current wife/daughter as:

> `知り合いの子`

—an acquaintance / a child he knows.

Kana immediately understands that he is not prepared to publicly integrate her into his current family identity.

Her question:

> `お父さんにとって、叶ってなんなの？`

is therefore not a request for genealogical information. It asks what relational standing his recognition actually grants her.

The answer available in the scene is painfully inadequate.

## 4.4 Kana's idol project had outsourced moral repair to paternal recognition

**TEXTUAL FACT / INTERPRETATION**

In Part 2 kana says:

> `お父さんが見つけてくれたら / kana、きっといい子になれると思ってたのに`

She also imagines that with ordinary loving parents she might have become like Hoshimi's more openly prosocial girls—able to praise others, smile, and be liked without first becoming irritated.

This reveals that the father-search was not only about loneliness.

Kana had made paternal recognition responsible for a hoped-for transformation of self:

> **If the right parent finally chose her, perhaps she would become the person she believes she should have been.**

That makes the father's failure more existential than a disappointing reunion.

It collapses an external theory of how kana's own moral/personality life would be repaired.

## 4.5 Fran's intervention rejects outsourced self-authorship

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Fran's response is harsh:

> `あんたはもう、立派な大人でしょ`
>
> `なら自分のクソな性格くらい、自分で責任取りなさい`

This should not be sanitized into therapeutic wisdom. Fran is insulting, provocative, and deliberately refuses soft consolation.

But the argument is structurally important:

> **Past injury can explain kana without giving the absent father permanent jurisdiction over what kind of adult kana is allowed to become.**

This is an E1-D version of the project's broader authorship ethic.

## 4.6 Anti-pity is an explicit kana care requirement

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Fran refuses Makino's offer to accompany the conversation because his kindness risks producing `可哀想` language that kana would experience as humiliating.

Kana later confirms:

> `安っぽい同情なんかされるのが、一ッ番ムカつく`

This is important for future manager/peer-care analysis.

Care that is generically benevolent can still be badly matched to the recipient's self-conception.

## 4.7 Kana's final father scene is a boundary reclassification, not a proven permanent severance

**TEXTUAL FACT / INTERPRETIVE LIMIT**

On the morning of NexusFes, kana meets her father intending to say goodbye. He drives her to the venue and explicitly says he wants to do at least one father-like thing for her.

> `ファンのおじさん`

This is a deliberate reclassification from hoped-for paternal rescuer to spectator/supporter.

Do **not** overstate the scene as proof that kana will never speak to him again. The text freezes a new jurisdictional boundary, not a complete future-contact history.

## 4.8 Kana's E1-D endpoint is deliberately provisional

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Kana does not suddenly discover a grand replacement dream.

She says:

>
> `次にやりたいこと見つかるまでは`

Her choice to remain is **not** grounded in a false claim that she has solved her life.

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Fran's American exposure produces a credible investor/business partner, Ramsey, who likes her clothing work and offers favorable support for a revived fashion brand.

This matters because fran's reason for leaving IIIX is not failure.

It is **success outside the unit**.

The event therefore proves that instrumental units can dissolve when members become successful enough not to need the instrument.

## 5.2 Fran initially tries to save IIIX for explicitly self-interested reasons

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Ramsey's investment is tied to the idea that IIIX will wear fran's designs and win, producing publicity for the brand.

Fran has concealed the impending dissolution and panics when she realizes the brand plan depends on IIIX remaining active.

Her first re-entry motive is therefore not hidden friendship.

It is business survival.

This should remain visible.

## 5.3 Yet fran understands the members better than an outsider does

**TEXTUAL FACT / INTERPRETATION**

Fran repeatedly excludes Makino from the first personal conversations because she recognizes likely mismatches:

- Makino is a Yō-related `地雷` for miho in that moment;
- easy pity would infuriate kana.

The method is abrasive, but the epistemic point is strong:

With miho, fran attacks memorial passivity.

With kana, she attacks paternal determinism and self-pity.

Neither intervention is gentle. Both initially end with fran being thrown out.

This is not evidence that cruel pressure is universally necessary.

It is evidence that fran's specific relationship with these specific women permits a form of confrontational recognition that would likely fail if delivered by an outsider.

`OPEN-06` / DoriKyun-style pressure should not be generalized from this case.

## 5.5 The new costumes are revealed preference, not proof of sentimental friendship

**TEXTUAL FACT / STRONG INFERENCE**

Fran spends the money she had saved for the future brand on expedited high-end costumes for all three members. She says:

She also asks Makino not to tell the others she paid herself because she is embarrassed.

This materially strengthens the claim that her aesthetic project has become attached to **these specific bodies and partners**, not merely to an abstract brand.

However, the source itself immediately resists a sentimentalized interpretation: fran frames the expense as investment and reacts with disgust when Makino looks emotionally moved.

Therefore the admissible claim is:

> **Fran reveals costly specific commitment to IIIX as an aesthetic vehicle and to the members as the people her work must fit.**

Do not silently translate that into conventional friendship language.

## 5.6 Fran's post-collapse motive integrates beauty, business, and idol performance

**TEXTUAL FACT**

When miho articulates Yō's promise to become an idol who can make someone happy, fran says the idea also fits IIIX because beautiful things make people happy merely by existing, and IIIX is beautiful.

E1-D therefore does not force fran to adopt miho's memorial philosophy wholesale. She translates the shared unit purpose into her own aesthetic vocabulary.

This is the strongest Tier-A/E1 evidence so far for `OPEN-09` because the memory problem becomes not merely internal grief but **public archival distortion**.

## 6.2 IIIX's own branding contributes to Yō's exclusion

**TEXTUAL FACT**

> **miho did not merely fail to overcome public erasure; the polished IIIX identity she helped build supplied an incentive structure for that erasure.**

This does not make her responsible for the editor's choice in a simple moral sense.

It does mean memory and persona cannot be analyzed separately.

## 6.3 Private memory is insufficient for public survival

**TEXTUAL FACT / INTERPRETATION**

In Part 2 miho says:

> `葉ちゃんを忘れてほしくないのに、スリクスにいても`
>
> `彼女のことは思い出してももらえない`

Fran answers that if miho wants others to remember Yō, she has to do something that makes memory circulable: talk about her, perform associated material, put her back into public discourse.

Fran's accusation is severe:

> `話題にならなきゃすぐ忘れるのは、あんたの方じゃない`

and:

> `そもそも友達のためにあんた、何かしたの？`

The event thus distinguishes:

- **private fidelity** — miho never forgot Yō;
- **public memorial efficacy** — the surrounding culture can still forget or delete Yō.

Preferred term:

> **MEMORY_REQUIRES_CIRCULATION**

Not every memory must become public. But if the explicit objective is **public remembrance**, private recollection alone cannot achieve it.

## 6.4 miho initially responds with memorial proprietorship

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Fran proposes covering old Sundance material with IIIX. miho refuses because the song is an intimate memory and she does not want the others `べたべた触られる`—touching it all over.

This is not mere selfishness. The source presents the song as genuinely precious.

But it produces a structural contradiction:

> miho wants Yō publicly remembered while treating key memorial material as too private for circulation through the living unit available to her.

E1-D does not resolve this by forcing a cover.

Instead, the pressure moves miho toward a broader solution: speaking Yō's name/history, making her promise discussable, and using IIIX's public activity as a vehicle for the continuation of the promise.

## 6.5 Yō becomes a spoken member of IIIX's motivational ecology

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Near the finale kana directly asks why miho kept being an idol after Yō died.

miho answers in front of both members:

> `私は友達と……葉ちゃんと約束したから`
>
> `トップに立って……そして`
>
> `誰かを幸せに出来るアイドルになるって`

Fran explicitly says this is new information to her.

That fact is crucial.

The promise existed before IIIX, but until now it was not fully available to IIIX as shared motivational information.

After disclosure, kana can provisionally choose to accompany it and fran can translate it into her own aesthetics.

Preferred model:

> **PRIVATE VOW → DISCLOSED MOTIVE → PLURAL RE-AUTHORIZATION**

The vow remains miho/Yō in provenance. It does not become ownerless. But other living people can now participate in carrying its future consequences.

This is directly compatible with the E1-A rule:

> provenance matters without granting permanent unilateral jurisdiction.

## 6.6 miho does not claim complete closure

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Her internal monologue after the three members agree to continue is deliberately uncertain:

> `葉ちゃんとの約束を、あの子達と一緒に守るなんて`
>
> `そんな日が来るなんて、想像したこともなかったわ`

followed by:

> `これで約束を守ってることになるのか`
>
> `正直、よく分からないけど`

Therefore E1-D should **not** be summarized as “miho finally solves grief/Yō.”

It solves a narrower question:

> **Yō no longer needs to remain a sealed private memory in order for miho to protect the relationship.**

`OPEN-09` is materially advanced, not metaphysically or psychologically exhausted.

# 7. VENUS No. 1 — the score system can harm both loser and winner

## 7.1 The reigning No. 1 left the Japanese competitive system despite continuing to perform

**TEXTUAL FACT**

Yōko Boyle explains that the long-standing VENUS No. 1 idol eventually became distressed by the consequences of repeated victory. She had entered idol work wanting to make people happy but increasingly saw defeated idols crying backstage because she had beaten them.

She left Japan's front-line VENUS competition and now appears happier performing ordinary American stages.

This is a major expansion of `OPEN-07`.

The problem is not only that scores fail to capture every value.

The scoring institution itself can reshape the moral meaning of performance:

> **A performance intended as shared happiness can be institutionally converted into a repeated zero-sum act in which another person's visible pain becomes part of one's success.**

## 7.2 No. 1's distress does not mean competition itself is rejected

**TEXTUAL FACT / STRONG INFERENCE**

She ultimately accepts the exhibition battle.

The event places a decisive question immediately before that acceptance: what will IIIX do if it loses?

miho first says they will not lose, then answers the hypothetical:

> `別に、泣きはしませんよ`
>
> `次は貴方をどうぶっ倒すか、考えるだけです`

The text does not print an explicit causal sentence such as “therefore she accepted.”

However, the sequencing strongly supports the inference that IIIX's **non-terminal model of defeat** is relevant to the No. 1 idol's willingness to re-enter a battle.

Keep the distinction:

- FACT: she asks what happens if IIIX loses; miho answers that they will plan the next victory; the battle is accepted.
- INFERENCE: the answer helps make battle ethically tolerable to her.

## 7.3 Competition becomes safer when defeat does not erase the loser

This produces an important refinement of the series' rivalry model:

> **Competition is most compatible with answerable interdependence when the losing party retains a future from which to answer the result.**

This is closely related to E1-B's failure rule:

> failure becomes information only when enough continuity remains to interpret it.

The No. 1 idol's trauma is partly an ecosystem problem: too many defeated peers treated loss as psychic/career devastation.

IIIX offers a different grammar:

> lose → analyze → return → try to beat her next time.

# 15. Identity after fulfilled or failed goals

The two-part structure generates a broader series proposition.

External goals can support a vocation without being sufficient to sustain it forever.

Three failure modes appear:

- **goal fulfillment** can remove the need for the vocation (fran finds investment);
- **goal contact without relational fulfillment** can expose that the vocation was aimed at something it could never actually guarantee (kana finds father but not restored family);
- **goal displacement** can make the means replace the underlying reason for acting (miho's score/top focus crowds out audience happiness and public memory).

The mature question is therefore not:

> Did the original motivation succeed?

It is:

> **After the original motivation changes, fails, or succeeds, is there a reason the person chooses again?**

Preferred category:

> **VOCATIONAL RE-AUTHORIZATION**

This differs from simple perseverance.

Perseverance continues the old reason.

Re-authorization asks whether the activity is still worth choosing after the old reason has lost jurisdiction.

# 21. Frozen prospective synthesis

The cleanest source-native formulation after E1-D is:

> **IIIX begins as a coalition in which three damaged professionals use the unit to pursue separate external ends. The United States arc deliberately lets those ends collapse: kana's fame finds her father but cannot manufacture the family relation she imagined; fran's fashion route reopens strongly enough to make idolhood dispensable; miho discovers that score-maximizing ascent can leave Yō publicly erased and can displace the audience-facing half of their promise. The unit therefore reaches a genuine dissolution point. It continues only after the members choose new or revised reasons that can coexist: miho makes the Yō vow speakable and shareable, kana accepts a provisional future without outsourcing her whole self to paternal recognition, and fran integrates IIIX into an aesthetic/business project that has become specific to these performers. They retain hostility, separate motives, competitive aggression, and `絶対勝利`. What changes is the jurisdiction of failure. A defeat no longer proves the unit should disappear; it becomes something they can answer with another attempt. The mature IIIX revealed here is therefore not friendship replacing utility, but a plural, adversarial, self-renewing compact capable of surviving the collapse of the reasons that originally created it.**
