# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — STELLAR MAIN STORY PRIMARY FINDINGS FREEZE

## 0. Freeze declaration

This document freezes the V2 source-native reading of the Stellar main-story tranche before any historical V1 Stellar analysis is consulted.

The governing inherited state is:

`IDOLY_PRIDE_V2_PHASE1B_POST_BIG4_BASELINE.md`

The admitted primary-source sequence is exactly:

`stellar_001_to_soar_high` -> `stellar_011_all_my_youth`

covering 56 granular stories and 6,301 dialogue/narration utterances.

Later event stories, cards, bonds, messages, telephone material, historical synthesis prose, and retrospective character documents remain quarantined for purposes of this freeze. Knowledge already earned from the anime/Hoshimi/origin/Tokyo/BIG4 sequence remains admissible because it constitutes the inherited prospective state.

The purpose of this freeze is not to present the final interpretation of Stellar. It is to preserve what the primary main-story text itself establishes before historical comparison can exert anchoring pressure.

# 1. Executive source-native thesis

The Stellar arc is the main story's broadest test of the proposition that **an idol becomes stronger not by becoming independent of other people, but by learning how to convert dependence, rivalry, gratitude, memory, institutional support, and personal desire into self-authored reasons for continuing**.

BIG4 constitutionalized Tsuki no Tempest by replacing a one-directional orbit around Kotono with reciprocal, contestable reliance. Stellar generalizes that lesson across the entire competitive ecosystem.

Each major group enters Stellar with a form of success that has become unstable:

- **SUNNY PEACE** has reached BIG4, but Sakura converts the title into a moral debt she believes must be repaid by permanent victory and bodily availability.
- **TRINITYAiLE** has elite technique and extraordinary discipline, but its technical excellence has reached diminishing returns because the group has not yet articulated a sufficiently concrete answer to what its excellence is *for*.
- **IIIX** has optimized performance around `絶対勝利`, but optimization itself risks becoming a ceiling when calculation suppresses the members' most combustible individual qualities.
- **LizNoir** has become a stable four-person unit and BIG4 member, but still needs to define progress beyond the old Mana-centered horizon and beyond one tournament result.
- **Tsuki no Tempest** enters from BIG4 with its new reciprocal constitution intact and functions increasingly as evidence that rivalry, difference, and mutual correction can be developmental rather than unit-destroying.
- **DoriKyun** enters as external antagonistic pressure, but its own philosophy has begun to change after defeat: it still believes in ruthless competition, yet is increasingly willing to cultivate strong opponents rather than merely eliminate the weak.
- **Makino** enters with a principled commitment to idol autonomy but discovers that his fear of another Mana-like catastrophe can turn protection into overprotection.

The arc's strongest unifying movement is therefore:

> **from protection to supported risk; from technical perfection to authored purpose; from victory as proof to competition as reciprocal development; from inherited miracle to living continuation.**

This movement is not ethically frictionless. In particular, Sakura's health crisis exposes a genuine unresolved problem: respecting an idol's authored choice can conflict with preserving the future conditions under which she will still be able to choose. The source text partly resolves this through medical clearance, contingency planning, distributed support, and Sakura's informed insistence on performing, but it deliberately leaves enough risk that the decision should not be flattened into either "Makino was simply right to let her perform" or "Sakura was simply reckless."

# 3. Sequential findings

## 3.1 `stellar_001_to_soar_high` — success makes Hoshimi visible, and visibility creates obligation

The documentary opening establishes that Hoshimi Production has moved far beyond the marginal institution of the early narrative. The idols describe it not merely as an employer but as `帰って来る場所`—a place to return to—and speak of `家族のような仲間達`, companions like family.

This is **TEXTUAL FACT** at the level of the characters' own institutional self-description. It should not automatically be inflated into the claim that Hoshimi is literally or uniformly a family. IIIX in particular continues to preserve contractual and adversarial distance. The more defensible **INTERPRETATION** is that Hoshimi increasingly functions as a *chosen professional home* whose legitimacy comes from allowing different units to inhabit it without demanding emotional homogeneity.

Sakura's documentary language also reveals a pressure latent in the BIG4 endpoint. She frames her transplanted life and heart as something that should be used to make other people happy and energetic. That formulation is generous, but once BIG4 status is layered onto it, generosity begins to become obligation.

The new VENUS Grand Prix raises the stakes from unit ascent to total-industry supremacy: unlike NEXT VENUS Grand Prix, this tournament is explicitly framed as deciding the top among all idols.

TRINITYAiLE immediately wants to challenge SUNNY PEACE rather than wait passively for the tournament. The desire is not irrational impatience. The group has been waiting to prove that it has moved forward, and direct competition with the current BIG4 becomes an evidentiary test.

The most important Makino finding is the reappearance of trauma-shaped managerial fear. He worries that allowing two serious rivals to fight could psychologically break the loser. This is not ordinary strategic caution. It is a **STRONG INFERENCE** that the Mana catastrophe continues to deform his risk model: because he once failed to prevent irreversible loss, he is tempted to prevent exposure to potentially painful but developmentally necessary experiences.

Saki's realization that younger idols now look up to her widens the arc's burden theme. Success changes a person from aspirant into object of aspiration. The main story begins to ask whether recognition can be accepted without turning other people's admiration into a demand for invulnerability.

### Frozen delta

- Hoshimi becomes publicly legible as a major multi-unit institution.
- “Home/family-like” language becomes institutionally salient.
- Sakura begins converting BIG4 status into a duty to satisfy others' expectations.
- Makino's protective ethic is exposed to the danger of overprotection.
- TRINITYAiLE seeks a direct test against SUNNY PEACE.

## 3.2 `stellar_002_nemophila` — trust is not nonintervention, and technique without a core reaches a ceiling

DoriKyun's meeting with Saegusa is not a Hoshimi-recruitment scene. Saegusa has urged them not to retire because the idol industry still needs their pressure. Their decision to continue matters because BIG4's final refusal of erasure has become consequential: defeated rivals remain in the ecology and can continue changing it.

DoriKyun delivers one of the arc's cleanest criticisms of Makino:

> `心配も度が過ぎると害悪です`

and argues that a manager who keeps idols away from danger merely robs them of opportunities for growth. The corresponding imperative is:

> `信じて見守る`

This should not be reduced to “managers should stand back.” Haruko's parallel argument is that the idols are *not Mana* and that Makino should trust them. The arc's emerging management model is therefore **supported autonomy**: believe the idol can face difficulty, remain available, manage conditions and consequences, but do not author the answer simply to reduce the manager's anxiety.

Makino recognizes that the fearful party is partly himself. That is a genuine character-state correction.

The SUNNY PEACE–TRINITYAiLE match establishes a critical technical premise. TRINITYAiLE performs at or near its historical best, but loses by a very large margin. Miho identifies the group as lacking `核になるもの`, a core. The loss therefore does *not* support “they simply need to train harder.” They have already trained extraordinarily hard.

Sakura's victory response is more troubling than the victory itself. She says BIG4 must remain strong and proper, that she owes those who lost, and that complaining about the burden would insult those opponents. This converts gratitude into a potentially limitless debt:

> **because others lost to me, I lose the right to admit that winning is painful.**

That proposition is not explicitly endorsed by the text; Makino and later peers notice that Sakura's smile no longer looks fully joyful.

Shizuku's language toward Rui is also important. She can simultaneously call Rui a respected `推し` and imagine defeating her. Admiration is no longer hierarchical submission. The fan/performer and rival/performer axes become compatible.

### Frozen delta

- DoriKyun remains a future-facing external rival after defeat.
- Makino explicitly begins learning `信じて見守る`.
- TRINITYAiLE's technical excellence is established as insufficient by itself.
- `核` becomes a governing Stellar concept.
- Sakura's responsibility ethic starts becoming self-silencing.
- Admiration is shown as compatible with the desire to surpass.

## 3.3 `stellar_003_to_fly_higher` — TRINITYAiLE refuses to make one member carry the whole unit

Rui's response to defeat is to overtrain because “effort” is the only answer she trusts. She assumes the center/leader must pull the others upward. Yu and Sumire independently make the symmetrical mistake of treating themselves as insufficient support.

The group's first substantive breakthrough is therefore not performance technique but **responsibility distribution**. They recognize that win and loss belong to all three. This parallels, without duplicating, BIG4's correction of Tsuki no Tempest: a unit cannot remain healthy if one person becomes the sovereign bearer of success and failure.

Nagisa's conversation with Yu is especially important because Nagisa can now speak from earned experience. Her former form of care for Kotono became overidentification and dependency; the Tsuki split forced each member to become capable of pulling her own weight. The lesson transmitted to Yu is not “stop supporting Rui,” but “support that requires no selfhood is structurally weak.”

Sumire's conversation with Suzu provides a needed counterweight to the arc's increasingly severe rhetoric of strength. Asked what makes her an idol, Sumire eventually returns to love: singing with Rui, dancing with Yu, penlights, cheering, and making people smile. Suzu's response is that if Sumire still loves the work and wants to continue even when it is hard, she is a splendid idol.

This is **TEXTUAL FACT** and analytically important because it limits the possible meaning of `核`. A core does not have to be grand ideology. Persistent love and chosen continuation can themselves be legitimate vocational grounds.

TRINITYAiLE chooses an activity hiatus. This is not surrender. They deliberately accept the risks of lost jobs, ranking, momentum, fans, and publicity because continuing the same behavior has become inertial. Crucially, Makino does not simply grant the hiatus. The other Hoshimi idols help cover the professional obligations, and the decision becomes logistically possible through institutional solidarity. `仲間で友達` is therefore not sentimental decoration; it produces operational capacity.

Meanwhile, Sakura interprets TRINITYAiLE's hiatus as another debt she must repay by working and winning more. This confirms that her problem is not solved simply by other people telling her to rest.

The Hoshimi Idol Festival is proposed because Kotono and others recognize that Sakura's public smile has detached from spontaneous joy. The festival is therefore an attempt to reconnect professional performance with enjoyment, not merely to distract her.

### Frozen delta

- TRINITYAiLE rejects leader-centered responsibility.
- Nagisa becomes a transmitter of the lesson earned in BIG4.
- Sumire establishes persistent love as a legitimate idol core candidate.
- TRINITYAiLE voluntarily pauses activity to search for an authored answer.
- Hoshimi friendship becomes materially functional through job coverage.
- Sakura's self-imposed BIG4 debt intensifies.

## 3.4 `stellar_004_hoshimi_s_festival` — Hoshimi's legitimacy is plural participation, not emotional uniformity

The festival offers the main story's richest institutional snapshot so far.

LizNoir participates because Hoshimi has become a `居場所`, a place where the members can be themselves and remain in contact with serious rivals. Kotono similarly frames returning to Tsuki/Hoshimi as returning to a place she belongs. Rio cautiously extends family-like language to TRINITYAiLE.

IIIX participates for explicitly mixed reasons: publicity, monetization, competitive visibility, experimentation, and opportunity. That matters. If Hoshimi's institutional success required IIIX to imitate SUNNY PEACE's warmth, it would be less plural than the story claims. Instead, IIIX can remain transactional, abrasive, self-interested, and still contribute real creative value.

Fran and Chisa collaborate on design. Kana applies media/SNS instincts. Nagisa creates family-oriented programming. Rei's theme-park desire becomes usable material. Kokoro performs a villain role. Rio participates despite embarrassment. The event is created by many idols rather than bestowed from above.

This supports the **STRONG INFERENCE** that Hoshimi's strongest institutional form is a *platform for authored contribution*: individuals bring different skills and motives into a shared project without needing the same personality or philosophy.

The emotional attempt succeeds incompletely. Sakura is able to experience joy and collective gratitude, but immediately afterward collapses from overwork.

The collapse is decisive evidence that emotional reconnection cannot substitute for labor governance. “Remember why you love this” does not remove bodily limits.

### Frozen delta

- Hoshimi's “home” function gains cross-unit evidence.
- IIIX demonstrates that belonging need not require warm affect.
- Multi-unit collaboration becomes a concrete institutional strength.
- Sakura's burden becomes bodily rather than merely psychological.
- The text refuses the idea that joy alone cures structural overwork.

## 3.5 `stellar_005_we_are_the_sun` — Sakura's Mana inheritance becomes an ethical danger

Sakura is diagnosed with overwork rather than a transplant complication. The distinction matters medically, but it does not make the situation trivial.

When urged to rest, Sakura repeats an ethic strongly associated with Mana: some audience members may only be able to attend once, so each live is irreplaceable. Makino explicitly recalls Mana.

This is a major inheritance correction.

Earlier phases establish that Sakura does not need to become Mana's copy in voice, body, or idol identity. Stellar shows that **inheritance can recur at the level of professional ethics**. The dangerous repetition is not “Sakura sounds like Mana.” It is “Sakura begins treating the audience's singular opportunity as a claim on her bodily availability.”

SUNNY PEACE refuses to let Sakura perform immediately and covers for her with a deliberately redesigned four-person formation. The choice is significant: they do not pretend Sakura is present or preserve the old choreography as sacred. They adapt the work to current reality.

Yet carrying Sakura's missing share overloads the four members too. Makino therefore reduces the unit's broader workload. This establishes that distributed care cannot mean simply redistributing unsustainable labor across more bodies.

The ordinary-day sequence makes Sakura's absence legible in each member's leisure. Their spontaneous convergence at the hospital confirms that ordinary life is part of the unit's relational substance rather than dead time between performances.

The nemophila motif (`どこでも成功`) links present crisis to SUNNY PEACE's early history. Sakura checks off spending an entire day talking with friends from her “things I want to try” list. The scene matters because it represents a kind of life not reducible to output.

The key SUNNY PEACE correction is explicitly stated:

> `その重荷はサニーピースの全員で背負わせて`

The group does not reject competition. Rei specifically says mutual defeat and continued winning are part of BIG4. What changes is the *distribution* of the burden. Shizuku's formulation—divide pain among five and multiply joy—describes the mature unit model.

Makino's management model also becomes more precise. The four ask him to watch and support them so they can push themselves without going too far. This is neither paternalistic control nor laissez-faire autonomy. It is **negotiated supervision**.

### Frozen delta

- Sakura begins repeating Mana's self-consuming audience ethic.
- SUNNY PEACE demonstrates adaptive four-person professionalism.
- Workload risk cannot simply be redistributed from Sakura to the others.
- The unit explicitly distributes BIG4 burden across all five.
- Makino's `信じて見守る` becomes negotiated risk supervision.

## 3.6 `stellar_006_will_of_the_underdogs` — effort must be purposive, and `想い` is treated as performance capacity

TRINITYAiLE's hiatus initially produces more training but no meaningful growth. DoriKyun's intervention is therefore diagnostically sharp.

They state that, on technical performance alone, TRINITYAiLE may already exceed SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest and may rival LizNoir/IIIX. This makes the core problem impossible to explain as insufficient competence.

DoriKyun now takes seriously what it previously dismissed as `精神論`. Their loss to Tsuki has forced a revision: if an idol possesses the ability to turn `想い` into strength, that capacity may be effectively bottomless.

The relevant claim is not supernatural mind-over-matter. Within the text's performance logic, `想い` changes what technique communicates, how risk is taken, how an audience responds, and therefore how performance scores and affective impact change.

DoriKyun also makes an important distinction between disciplined repetition and meaningful `努力`. The group calls TRINITYAiLE's current training `惰性` because it is no longer directed toward a concrete destination. Their working definition of effort is action that may plausibly connect to an intended outcome, not suffering accumulated for its own sake.

Their homework—why are you idols, and what kind of idols do you want to be?—forces autobiographical reconstruction.

The three find a shared axis in `家族`, but “family” is not yet the final answer. It is a key that exposes how each idol's career was shaped by relationships of recognition, support, debt, abandonment, and promise:

- Yu's imaginative idol desire and later commitment to making Rui shine;
- Sumire's child-acting history, brother, financial/professional motives, and later own dream;
- Rui's father wound, BanPro choice, Mana rivalry, and desire to force Asakura to recognize her.

DoriKyun gives the partial answer “50 points.” That prevents the story from treating the first emotionally satisfying explanation as complete.

Rui then chooses to confront Asakura rather than leave the paternal wound abstract.

### Frozen delta

- TRINITYAiLE is established as near-ceiling technically.
- `想い` is reframed as a usable performance capacity rather than empty sentiment.
- `努力` is distinguished from inertial overwork.
- `家族` emerges as a clue, not yet complete doctrine.
- DoriKyun begins functioning as adversarial developmental pressure rather than pure elimination force.

## 3.7 `stellar_007_repaying_kindness` — TRINITYAiLE's core becomes reciprocal gratitude rather than paternal recognition

DoriKyun's “harsh treatment” gives each member a task designed to puncture a defensive habit:

- Sumire must ask Asakura for a childish souvenir, confronting her tendency to perform premature adulthood.
- Yu must risk being genuinely embarrassing in an attempt to make Asakura laugh, confronting fear of visible failure.
- Rui must call Asakura `パパ`, confronting the unresolved daughter/father relation she has converted into professional rivalry.

The assignments are deliberately comic, but the diagnostic logic is serious. DoriKyun explicitly says the purpose is not the task itself but what the idols discover while making themselves vulnerable.

Rui's conversation with Asakura provides a major clarification of her origin. She entered BanPro because he was there and wanted to become an idol so overwhelming that the father who abandoned Rui and her mother could not ignore her. Surpassing Mana was partly instrumental to that paternal project.

Asakura says he understood much of this, but chose not to act as her father because he believed his duty as agency president required fair treatment of idol Rui. Rui's response is crucial: professional correctness did not excuse communicative absence. Had he simply explained the boundary, she might have understood her own path earlier and experienced later crises differently.

Asakura admits the real failure as `甘え`: he assumed his daughter would understand without being told. He acknowledges that both as president and father, there had been insufficient dialogue.

He then explicitly recognizes Rui's talent and says he repeatedly had to suppress parental pride because she was so bright. Rui finally admits what she wanted: to be recognized and praised by him. Calling him `パパ` becomes possible only after the professional and personal roles are named rather than confused.

The conversation extends to Yu and Sumire. Asakura reveals that he had tracked their growth closely and viewed the trio as among the best idols he had sent into the world. His phrase `自慢の娘達` deliberately mixes professional mentorship and paternal metaphor.

The breakthrough that follows is not “Rui finally gets father's approval, therefore TRINITYAiLE is complete.” That would reproduce the dependency the arc is trying to overcome.

Instead, the trio articulates a broader destination:

> they cannot fly alone; many people have supported them; that support is not automatic; they will continually seek distinct forms of `恩返し`, embody those in performance, and carry those enabling relationships with them toward views none of them has seen before.

The final core is therefore **reciprocal gratitude made performable**.

The decisive wording is collective:

> `私達は、私達だけじゃ高く飛べない`

and

> `支えてくれた人達の数だけ形の違う恩返しを`

This transforms dependence from shame into vocation.

DoriKyun recognizes the performance change. Raime is irritated precisely because the new performance moves her emotionally. Lemon interprets the transformation as evidence that `想い` can become enormous performance force.

Makino's new costumes embody his own version of the same ethic. He prepared them *before* knowing the result because he trusted the trio to return changed. He explicitly repeats the lesson that a manager must believe and watch.

DoriKyun then enters VENUS Grand Prix itself, preserving the reciprocal competitive ecology.

### Frozen delta

- Rui/Asakura paternal-professional ambiguity is substantially clarified.
- The core failure is communication, not absence of professional respect.
- Rui receives wanted recognition without making it the endpoint of idolhood.
- TRINITYAiLE's mature core becomes gratitude/reciprocity with supporters, family, peers, and enablers.
- Dependence is re-authored from weakness into something to be acknowledged and returned.
- Makino demonstrates prospective trust through prepared costumes.

## 3.8 `stellar_008_opening_of_grand_prix` — gratitude becomes competitive language, and rivalry becomes a way to communicate care

TRINITYAiLE returns to competition undefeated and repeatedly sets self-bests. Importantly, the members remain humble enough to recognize that their scores have not yet simply invalidated SUNNY PEACE.

Sumire chooses Tsuki no Tempest for the pre-tournament exhibition because she wants to repay Suzu's friendship. Yu similarly identifies Nagisa as someone whose earlier care deserves return. Rui generalizes the idea to Tsuki as friends who understood darkness because they had lived through it.

This establishes a distinctive Stellar proposition:

> **for these idols, “repayment” does not mean yielding; it can mean giving a friend the respect of one's strongest possible opposition.**

The exhibition confirms the point. TRINITYAiLE beats Tsuki by a narrow margin. The manager suggests the difference may lie in audience response to the trio's newly communicable `想い`, but both sides explicitly refuse to interpret one result as a permanent hierarchy.

Suzu's response is especially healthy: she recognizes Sumire as a superb idol, accepts the defeat, and immediately demands another contest. This is post-BIG4 contestable center/rival grammar working outside Tsuki itself.

The Grand Prix draw gathers Hoshimi's rival units into one visible institution. Characters explicitly note that they spend more time with one another than with biological family and call themselves family-like, yet the tournament requires real elimination among them. The story therefore refuses the premise that intimacy and competition are opposites.

Makino's thought that the idols are enjoying `アイドルと、青春` for Mana's share situates professional idol work as a form of youth rather than a negation of youth.

Sakura's tournament speech is one of the first points where her burden language sounds partially re-authored. Rather than saying merely that BIG4 must win for those who lost, she says she loves idolhood and SUNNY PEACE and wants to stand at the `大好きの頂点`—the summit of what she loves. This does not erase the health/burden problem, but it restores first-person desire to her ambition.

### Frozen delta

- TRINITYAiLE defeats Tsuki narrowly and reaches a new competitive tier.
- “Repayment” becomes strongest-opposition rather than deference.
- Friendship and rivalry become mutually reinforcing.
- Hoshimi family-like intimacy coexists with real tournament elimination.
- Sakura's ambition begins shifting back from debt toward love/desire.

## 3.9 `stellar_009_challenge_for_friend` — IIIX discovers that imperfection can be more truthful than optimization

DoriKyun's pre-match analysis is notable because it respects IIIX's specialized live-battle intelligence. It argues that when raw ability is close, IIIX's capacity to optimize a stage for VENUS-program scoring gives it an additional edge.

Miho has in fact prepared a near-theoretical-max optimized performance.

Yet TRINITYAiLE's challenge and Makino's reminder of the I-UNITY upset force IIIX to revisit a lesson already present in its history: calculation can be overtaken by momentum when the opponent's stake becomes combustible.

Miho therefore proposes something unprecedented:

> `スリクス初の――完璧ではないステージ`

The unit abandons exact synchronization as the governing ideal and instead lets the three members compete with one another for attention onstage. Each tries to prove herself the superior member. The resulting slight “misalignment” is not diminished competence; it makes the group's three extreme personalities legible as separate diamonds colliding in one performance.

This is an important unit-level development because it arises *from* IIIX's hostile intimacy rather than from replacing it with generic friendship.

TRINITYAiLE still wins narrowly. Both groups set major self-bests.

Miho initially insists that results are everything, but her later statement creates a genuine qualification:

> if someone enjoyed the losing performance, she wants to believe it was not all meaningless.

That does not overturn `絶対勝利`. Fran and Kana immediately recommit to winning and experimentation. The stronger conclusion is that **IIIX's victory philosophy has acquired a second evaluative dimension: a failed experiment can still produce knowledge, stronger performance, and audience value.**

The internal competition continues after the loss. The members argue about which of them “won” the intragroup battle and immediately plan new experiments.

Miho's private thought then connects the discovery to Yo:

> they have opponents inside the unit with whom they can compete and become stronger together.

The relation to Yo is therefore not simple memorial replacement. The living unit has become a structure in which rivalry itself can sustain growth.

### Frozen delta

- IIIX deliberately breaks its perfect-unison formula.
- Internal rivalry becomes explicit performance material.
- The experimental stage produces the strongest/most distinctive IIIX live yet despite defeat.
- `絶対勝利` is qualified by learning and audience enjoyment, not abandoned.
- Miho recognizes living co-rivalry as a mode of growth and privately links it to Yo.

## 3.10 `stellar_010_miraculous_beat` — miracle is reclaimed from supernatural rescue, while Sakura's presentism reaches its ethical limit

LizNoir begins its quarterfinal by narrating itself as a four-person miracle. Rio and Aoi's original partnership remains foundational, but Ai and Kokoro reject being secondary additions. Their argument is persuasive: the current LizNoir exists because these exact four people met, fought, stayed, left, returned, and rebuilt together.

This is a major post-Mana correction. LizNoir no longer needs Mana as the external measure that gives its history meaning.

SUNNY PEACE enters with Sakura still physically compromised. The other four explicitly plan to support her. During the performance Sakura recognizes that the unit is compensating for her and begins interpreting herself as the reason they might lose.

The flashback establishes the medical state precisely:

- her transplanted heart is doing well;
- her overall stamina has declined;
- the doctor attributes this to accumulated load in a body that was historically fragile;
- ordinary life is not threatened;
- idol work is unusually demanding;
- rest and avoiding overexertion should prevent serious consequences;
- if she collapses again through overexertion, the result could be severe enough that she might have to stop idol activities.

Sakura explicitly says she cannot promise not to overdo it because the tournament matters too much.

During the live, a sudden heartbeat is followed by a dramatic recovery in her movement. This event is **TEXTUAL FACT**. Its mechanism is **CONFLICT / AMBIGUITY**.

Sakura wonders whether Mana caused a one-time miracle. Kotono rejects that causal claim: the voice and heart are already Sakura's, Mana would not intrude into a fair contest, and the “miracle” is more plausibly the necessary result of Sakura's accumulated effort and desire.

Sakura does not abandon spiritual-symbolic relation to Mana. She says she still wants to believe Mana watches them. The distinction is crucial:

> **Mana may remain witness without functioning as convenient external agency.**

This is one of the main story's strongest inheritance corrections.

SUNNY PEACE defeats LizNoir by an extremely narrow margin. LizNoir accepts the loss without dissolving its own value. Rio says this was the best LizNoir performance ever, that the four can still grow, that tournament victory is not the only definition of top, and that they will pursue their own summit globally and in the next Grand Prix.

Sakura's post-win conversation with Rui then exposes the arc's hardest ethical problem. Rui correctly identifies the stage as looking like a candle burning brightest before extinction. Sakura admits the medical warning but refuses to withdraw. Her reasons combine several things:

- the future is uncertain;
- there may not be another equivalent chance;
- she does not want retrospective regret;
- she wants to live fully now;
- SUNNY PEACE's mutual support makes idolhood possible;
- competition with strong rivals produces further light;
- showing smiles, dreams, hope, and maximum radiance is part of her idol pride.

These reasons are coherent and authored. They do not erase the risk.

Rui decides to tell Makino despite the possibility that Sakura, SUNNY PEACE, the public, and even TRINITYAiLE could resent her. Her decision is grounded in a different conception of care: if preserving Sakura's future requires accepting hatred now, Rui must be willing to pay the relational cost.

This establishes a genuine ethical collision rather than a simple right/wrong binary:

> **respecting present agency versus protecting future agency.**

### Frozen delta

- LizNoir explicitly becomes a four-person miracle and defines a horizon beyond one tournament.
- Sakura's “miracle” is textually ambiguous in mechanism but narratively re-centered on Sakura rather than supernatural Mana intervention.
- Mana becomes symbolic witness rather than deus ex machina.
- Sakura knowingly accepts meaningful health risk to continue.
- Rui treats willingness to be hated as part of serious care.
- The arc foregrounds present authorship versus preservation of future choice.

## 3.11 `stellar_011_all_my_youth` — Stellar resolves the tournament, but refuses to make the tournament the endpoint

Makino responds to Rui's disclosure by discussing Sakura's participation with SUNNY PEACE rather than making an immediate unilateral decision.

The resulting decision is framed by the text as a maturation of his management philosophy. He explicitly admits that he has previously confused “protecting idols” with overprotection. SUNNY PEACE asks him to trust them, support them, and create conditions in which they can push themselves without being abandoned to the consequences.

The important factual safeguards are:

- Makino consults Sakura's treating physician remotely;
- Sakura receives present-condition clearance to perform;
- the physician is put on standby for emergency response;
- staff are informed;
- SUNNY PEACE's four other members explicitly commit to covering and supporting Sakura.

These safeguards distinguish the decision from mere “she wants to do it, so let her.”

However, the earlier physician warning remains part of the same source tranche. Current clearance does not erase accumulated-risk concern. Therefore the normative claim “Makino was unambiguously right” is **not frozen as fact**.

The more defensible source-native conclusion is:

> the text defines legitimate management here as **risk-aware co-authorship**—take the idol's desire seriously, obtain relevant professional information, disclose and distribute the risk, create contingencies, and remain responsible for the conditions of the choice.

Rui initially believes that changing Sakura's participation would be the protective choice, but SUNNY PEACE and Makino choose participation under those conditions. Rui then accepts the contest and reasserts her own desire to face Sakura.

The semifinal produces SUNNY PEACE's tournament-best score, but TRINITYAiLE wins by a narrow margin.

Sakura's response is significant. She is sad, but also feels clear because the unit fully expressed itself. The loss therefore does something victory could not: it breaks the proposition that SUNNY PEACE's moral legitimacy requires permanent victory.

Rui's victory speech reconstructs the entire TRINITYAiLE arc as continuity through repeated defeat, scandal, expulsion, near-loss of stage, hiatus, and breakthrough. Winning is meaningful because it is evidence of continued becoming, not because it retroactively makes every painful event necessary.

DoriKyun loses to Tsuki in the other semifinal. The source text gives less granular performance detail here, but their presence at the final confirms they remain invested in the rivals who surpassed them.

A particularly sharp line comes from DoriKyun after Sakura has time to rest following semifinal elimination: Rui “saved” Sakura by beating her. This is **INTERPRETATION within the dialogue**, not a universal principle. It exposes the contingency of the tournament resolution: Sakura obtains rest partly because she loses. That fortunate consequence must not be used to prove that the earlier decision to risk participation was therefore correct.

The final is TRINITYAiLE versus Tsuki no Tempest. TRINITYAiLE wins the VENUS Grand Prix.

The awards sequence then deliberately refuses to make championship status ontological. Rui says winning does not mean they have reached the final summit. Sakura says ranking and title do not matter once they step onto a stage; they remain challengers. Kotono describes the idols as still incomplete and developing. Rio imagines the ideal idol horizon decades away. Fran jokes that ordinary idols would have retired by then, while the others imagine continuing as long as passion, desire, and audience remain.

Kotono's declaration:

> `青春全部かけて、アイドルの最高峰へ！`

must therefore be read alongside the insistence on long duration.

The arc does **not** simply say “burn all your youth in one glorious present.” Its ending says that youth can be invested wholly in idolhood while still opening into an extended future.

The final Makino/Mana thought reinforces living inheritance: Mana's `想い` continues through idols who are visibly different from her, and their careers continue after the tournament.

The epilogue then converts audience reception into new authorship. Three unidentified girls respond differently to SUNNY PEACE, Tsuki no Tempest, and the wider Hoshimi spectacle and begin imagining idol futures of their own. This is the main story's cleanest example yet of **inheritance without copying**:

> the existing idols do not reproduce Mana; they become the conditions under which entirely new people can want something for themselves.

Makino's next institutional action is therefore an audition for the next generation.

### Frozen delta

- Makino formalizes risk-aware supported autonomy.
- Rui/Sakura expose the difference between caring about a person's present desire and caring about her future capacity to choose.
- TRINITYAiLE defeats SUNNY PEACE, then Tsuki no Tempest, and wins VENUS Grand Prix.
- SUNNY PEACE's loss breaks the equation between “being worthy BIG4” and “never losing.”
- The champion itself refuses championship as final ontology.
- DoriKyun remains an external developmental antagonist.
- Main-story inheritance moves forward into new aspirants and a new Hoshimi audition.

# 4. Longitudinal character freezes

## 4.1 Makino Kouhei — from protective authorship toward risk-aware co-authorship

### TEXTUAL FACT

Makino explicitly recognizes that his fear can become overprotection. He changes course after peers and idols tell him that `信じて見守る` is part of management. In the Sakura semifinal decision he does not merely acquiesce: he consults the physician, obtains current clearance, arranges standby support, informs staff, and coordinates SUNNY PEACE's collective support.

### STRONG INFERENCE

His earlier overprotection is partly trauma-shaped by Mana's death. Stellar does not erase that trauma; it gives him a different procedural response to it.

### INTERPRETATION

The mature management model available at the Stellar endpoint is:

> **legitimate guidance protects the conditions for authored choice rather than protecting people from having consequential choices at all.**

This requires both restraint and positive action. A manager must sometimes say no; Stellar does not establish unlimited idol sovereignty. But fear alone is not sufficient warrant to author the idol's future.

### OPEN ETHICAL QUESTION

Sakura's case is intentionally difficult because the choice may jeopardize future agency. The text's safeguards make Makino's decision more defensible without making it morally costless.

---

## 4.2 Sakura — from inherited miracle to chosen sun, with presentism still unresolved

Sakura begins Stellar by turning BIG4 status into debt. She believes that because others lost to her, she must keep winning and loses standing to admit strain.

The festival and collapse expose the bodily cost of that proposition. SUNNY PEACE then redistributes the burden.

Her later Grand Prix speech reconstructs ambition around `大好き`: she loves idolhood, loves SUNNY PEACE, and wants to stand at the summit of that love. This is a healthier first-person desire than “I owe everyone permanent victory.”

Yet the health crisis demonstrates that a second problem remains. Sakura's conviction that every stage may be final and that future uncertainty justifies full present expenditure strongly echoes Mana's dangerous professional ethic.

The source therefore supports a **SPLIT finding** even before historical comparison:

- Sakura has substantially escaped *identity replacement* by Mana.
- Sakura has not fully escaped *ethical repetition* of Mana's self-consuming availability.

Kotono's miracle conversation further secures Sakura's personhood: the heart and voice are Sakura's. Mana may be loved as witness without being invoked as the causal author of Sakura's achievement.

By the endpoint Sakura can lose without treating loss as collapse of legitimacy. That is a meaningful correction to the BIG4 burden state.

---

## 4.3 Rui — recognition no longer needs to be extracted through domination

Rui's early idol trajectory was partly structured around forcing Asakura to recognize her by surpassing Mana and becoming impossible to ignore.

Stellar makes the actual wound verbal. Rui wanted paternal recognition and praise. Asakura had recognized her professionally all along but withheld fatherhood under an over-rigid boundary and failed to explain the separation.

Once this is spoken, Rui can call him `パパ` without surrendering professional selfhood.

Crucially, her group does not make paternal repair its final `核`. The repair becomes one part of a larger understanding that flight has always depended on many people. The mature Rui therefore moves from:

> **make the abandoning father look at me**

into:

> **carry acknowledged support forward and return it in authored form.**

Her decision to disclose Sakura's health risk despite expected resentment also demonstrates a stronger willingness to accept interpersonal cost for another person's future.

---

## 4.4 Yu — support becomes participation rather than self-effacement

Yu's Stellar development parallels Nagisa's BIG4 lesson without becoming a copy of it. She recognizes that she had treated “support Rui” as though it relieved her of equal responsibility for the unit's direction.

Her mature role is still relational—she does not stop caring about Rui—but support becomes one wing among three rather than invisible scaffolding beneath the center.

Her willingness to risk embarrassment during DoriKyun's exercise is part of the same movement: she becomes more willing to be visibly imperfect rather than preserving competence through self-protective cleverness.

---

## 4.5 Sumire — adulthood performance gives way to chosen youth

Sumire's defensive strategy includes behaving older and more composed than her age. Her family and child-acting history taught her to treat professionalism as early adulthood.

DoriKyun's absurd souvenir task makes the protected child register visible rather than shameful.

Suzu's earlier question—do you love idol work?—also matters. Sumire's answer makes her one of Stellar's clearest witnesses that a vocational `核` can be simple and still serious: she likes singing, dancing with her unit, penlights, cheering, and making people smile.

Her development therefore complicates `青春全部かけて`: investing youth in professional idolhood does not require performing adulthood so thoroughly that youth disappears.

---

## 4.6 IIIX — hostile intimacy becomes creative differentiation

IIIX's strongest Stellar development is formal and relational at once.

The trio discovers that the exact optimization that made it so effective can suppress the individual aggression that also makes it distinctive. By turning their mutual dislike/rivalry into stage competition, they create a less synchronized but more combustible performance.

This is not a friendship conversion.

The source instead strengthens the `腐れ縁` model: their relationship becomes developmentally valuable precisely because each member refuses to let the others win uncontested.

Miho's post-loss concession that audience enjoyment may prevent defeat from being meaningless is a meaningful qualification of result absolutism. Her private thought of Yo shows that living rivalry has become one way she can continue rather than merely memorialize.

---

## 4.7 LizNoir — the four-person present supersedes the two-person/Mana horizon without erasing it

Rio and Aoi's history remains emotionally foundational, but Ai and Kokoro explicitly claim equal constitutive status in the current unit.

The source supports the thesis that the present LizNoir is a four-person relation that could not simply substitute other people into those positions.

After losing to SUNNY PEACE, Rio refuses to define top as one tournament title. The group has a global horizon, a future Grand Prix, and continuing room to grow. This is a decisive break from the old structure in which Mana's absence could make idolhood feel endpoint-less or meaningless.

---

## 4.8 DoriKyun — external enemy becomes adversarial cultivator

DoriKyun does not become gentle, join Hoshimi, or abandon its conflict orientation.

What changes is the implied theory of how a strong idol ecosystem is produced.

Saegusa and their own defeat push them away from the idea that merely crushing weak idols will improve the industry. They experimentally help TRINITYAiLE find a breakthrough and are genuinely excited/angered when the result is strong enough to threaten them.

At the endpoint they explicitly reject Hoshimi absorption and promise to remain its strongest enemy.

This is not narrative failure to integrate them. It is a functional role:

> **the ecosystem benefits from an external rival whose relation is serious enough to develop others but independent enough to keep contesting Hoshimi's assumptions.**

# 5. Unit-state freezes

**Endpoint:** post-semifinal loss; still BIG4 at the narrative endpoint unless later material says otherwise; health/workload state under active management; relational burden redistributed.

Mature Stellar formula:

The unit remains competition-positive. It does not solve Sakura's problem by saying wins no longer matter. It solves part of the problem by refusing to let victory belong to Sakura alone.

## 5.6 DoriKyun

# 7. Thematic freezes

## 7.1 `想い` is not opposed to technique

Stellar does not say technique is unimportant. In fact, it repeatedly praises TRINITYAiLE's technique and IIIX's optimization.

The stronger thesis is:

> **technique determines what a performer can do; `想い` helps determine what the doing means, what risks become possible, what the audience receives, and whether mastery continues developing rather than congealing into repetition.**

DoriKyun's conversion from mocking `精神論` to treating `想い` as a performance capacity is central evidence.

## 7.2 Effort is purposive, not sacrificial

The arc contains huge quantities of training, but it explicitly rejects the equivalence:

> more suffering = more legitimate effort.

DoriKyun calls purposeless repetition `惰性`.

This is important counterevidence against interpreting Sakura's bodily expenditure as intrinsically noble because it hurts.

## 7.3 Gratitude can be agency-preserving

`恩返し` is TRINITYAiLE's core, but the text avoids making it servile because repayment is plural and authored.

One person may be repaid through a live battle. Another through a future stage. Family through invited witness. Fans through performance. A manager through proving trust justified.

The receiver does not define a single mandatory form.

## 7.4 Competition becomes a relational language

Across TRINITYAiLE/Tsuki, TRINITYAiLE/IIIX, SUNNY PEACE/LizNoir, and SUNNY PEACE/TRINITYAiLE, the strongest form of care is often refusing to reduce the other person to someone who must be protected from defeat.

This continues BIG4's “competition without ontological erasure” thesis and expands it:

> **rivalry can be gratitude, recognition, and care when the opponent's future remains legitimate after loss.**

## 7.5 “Family” is enabling relation, not biological purity

Family appears in several registers:

- Rui/Asakura biological rupture;
- Sumire's brother;
- Yu's family/creative origin;
- Hoshimi's family-like institutional language;
- LizNoir's four-person chosen history;
- SUNNY PEACE's burden-sharing;
- the broader supporter ecology TRINITYAiLE carries with it.

The arc's stronger category is therefore not blood family but **relations that make continued authorship possible**.

## 7.6 Miracle is naturalized without being disenchanted

Sakura's heartbeat event is not given a definitive physiological explanation. The text preserves felt miracle.

But Kotono explicitly refuses to assign Sakura's action back to Mana. The heart and voice belong to Sakura. The “miracle” is reinterpreted as something produced through accumulated living effort and desire.

Mana therefore remains sacred memory/witness without confiscating causal authorship from the living.

## 7.7 Youth is investment, but the ending resists suicidal presentism

`青春全部かけて` is a central endpoint phrase.

Yet the same ending imagines idols continuing twenty or thirty years later and explicitly says the idol journey continues after the Grand Prix.

The most defensible interpretation is:

> **to give one's youth wholly to idolhood is not necessarily to consume one's entire future in one present achievement.**

This distinction is essential when reading Sakura.

# 8. Institutional freeze: Hoshimi after Stellar

Hoshimi has become the dominant visible institution in the tournament ecology: five Hoshimi groups occupy the final eight and collectively receive a special award.

This success is not represented as proof that institutional questions are over.

The same arc shows new institutional risks:

- success generates excessive work;
- public expectations can be internalized as bodily debt;
- managers can confuse fear with protection;
- idols can confuse support with self-erasure;
- “family” can become rhetorically dangerous if it hides professional boundaries;
- competitive institutions can reward overexertion;
- fame creates another generation's expectations.

The strongest current Hoshimi legitimacy formulation is therefore:

> **Hoshimi is legitimate to the extent that it converts institutional capacity into options, safeguards, collaboration, and authored risk rather than using belonging as a claim over the idol's purpose.**

Its most important positive capacities in Stellar are:

- job coverage during TRINITYAiLE's hiatus;
- collaborative festival creation;
- cross-unit peer mentorship;
- medical/logistical support;
- permitting distinct unit philosophies;
- preserving external rivalry rather than absorbing every useful rival;
- generating future opportunity through audition rather than copying the current roster.

# 9. Mana/inheritance freeze

Stellar adds four major propositions to the series' inheritance model.

### 1. Dangerous inheritance can occur at the level of ethics

Sakura may reject being Mana's replacement while still reproducing Mana's willingness to spend her body for a singular audience encounter.

### 2. Mana does not own Sakura's achievements

Kotono explicitly says the voice and transplanted heart are Sakura's now.

### 3. Mana may remain witness without agency

Sakura wants to believe Mana watches them, but does not expect rescue.

### 4. Ethical inheritance ends in new desire

The final anonymous spectators do not want to *be Mana*. They see Sakura, Kotono, Hoshimi, and the Grand Prix and begin imagining their own futures.

The main-story inheritance sequence has therefore become:

> **Mana's miracle -> grief/replication pressure -> differentiation -> living performance -> new spectators author new desires.**

# 11. Counterevidence and interpretive limits

## 11.1 Do not flatten Sakura into “repeating Mana exactly”

Counterevidence:

- Sakura accepts collective support.
- She can rest when forced by circumstance.
- She explicitly loves SUNNY PEACE as a five-person unit.
- She can accept defeat without collapsing into identity failure.
- She says she does not expect Mana to rescue her.

The similarity is specifically strongest around bodily availability and “every stage may be the last” ethics.

## 11.2 Do not flatten Makino's growth into “always let idols choose”

Counterevidence:

- he obtains medical clearance;
- creates contingency support;
- previously orders broad workload reductions;
- continues active risk/logistics management.

The text supports more sophisticated co-authorship, not unlimited autonomy.

## 11.3 Do not claim TRINITYAiLE wins because “emotion beats technique”

Counterevidence:

TRINITYAiLE already has extraordinary technique. Its breakthrough adds a communicable core to existing mastery. The arc is additive, not anti-technical.

## 11.4 Do not claim IIIX becomes friendship-based

Counterevidence:

The members continue insulting, competing, and denying conventional affection. Their maturation consists of making that antagonistic relation productive.

## 11.5 Do not claim DoriKyun is domesticated

Counterevidence:

They explicitly reject Hoshimi and promise to remain its strongest enemy.

## 11.6 Do not treat tournament ranking as a stable total ordering of character worth

The text repeatedly notes narrow margins, context, audience response, temporary momentum, and multiple definitions of “top.” The champion itself rejects finality.

## 11.7 Do not overclaim the formal meaning of `stellar_011`

The text bundle supports dialogue and event-state conclusions. The exact artistic force of the culminating lives needs audiovisual/music/stage review.
