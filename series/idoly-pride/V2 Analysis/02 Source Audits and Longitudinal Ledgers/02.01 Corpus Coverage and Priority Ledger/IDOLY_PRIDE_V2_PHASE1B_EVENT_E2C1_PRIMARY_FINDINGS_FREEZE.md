# 1. Tranche-level finding — precursor evidence can retain authority without governing the mature thesis

E2-C1 is analytically useful precisely because it is **not** a second opportunity to restate conclusions already better established by E1.

The two stories preserve earlier states that later events transform:

- Kotono and Sakura can repair personal distance while Kotono's result-dependent comparative distress remains active;
- IIIX can already possess agency support, international ambition, unit loyalty, and media intelligence while still treating immediate victory as the justification for remaining together and while using publicity claims that are more deceptive than the later `CURATED_ADVERSARIAL_AUTHENTICITY` model.

Preferred archival principle:

> `PRECURSOR_AUTHORITY_WITHOUT_MATURE_SUPREMACY`

Definition:

> An earlier source retains independent authority for chronology, mechanism, and developmental state even when a later source provides the stronger mature formulation of the same thematic problem.

This tranche should therefore be cited when a later synthesis asks **when a problem was already active, what the earlier version looked like, or what changed**, rather than treated as the best final statement of rivalry, IIIX autonomy, or authenticity.

# 2. `音色の輝石が紡ぐ未来` — relational repair does not cure comparative outcome dependence

## 2.1 Safe chronology and institutional state

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Sunny Peace has become a new BIG4 and is publicly described as doing extremely well, while Tsuki no Tempest is experiencing a downturn. Both groups are invited as the joint NEXT VENUS Grand Prix winners to a festival of past champions.

The story therefore belongs to a state in which:

- Sunny and Tsuki remain close Hoshimi peers and former joint champions;
- their **public professional trajectories have visibly diverged**;
- that divergence has become legible to media/commentary outsiders;
- Kotono is already sensitive to the difference before the hostile video appears.

Preferred term:

> `COMPARATIVE_STATUS_DISTANCE`

This is not merely subjective jealousy. The story deliberately gives the jealousy a real institutional/public object: Sunny has reached BIG4 while Tsuki is publicly described as struggling.

## 2.2 Proximity can make disclosure harder, not easier

Kotono tells Sakura that she cannot discuss the problem **with Sakura specifically**, despite explicitly saying she trusts her:

> `さくらのことは信頼してる`
>
> `これは……私が自分で解決しないといけない問題だから`

Haruko then gives Sakura the relevant explanation:

> `ずっと近くにいるからこそ`
>
> `かえって言えないことってある`
>
> `それぞれプライドを持ってアイドルをやってるからね`

Preferred term:

> `COMPETITIVE_PROXIMITY_INHIBITED_DISCLOSURE`

Definition:

> A relationship may be emotionally trusted yet become a difficult site for disclosure when the other person's success is also the object against which the speaker is measuring herself.

This usefully qualifies any simple equation between intimacy and communicative ease. Sakura is precisely the person Kotono most wants to remain close to and the person who most directly embodies the professional comparison she cannot metabolize.

## 2.3 Haruko/Mana supplies a prior-generation version of the same failure mode

Haruko recounts an earlier period after Mana's debut in which:

- Mana keeps winning and receives increasing media work;
- Haruko has little work and remains in a school-to-lessons routine;
- the widening professional gap produces frustration and shame;
- Mana notices, but Haruko can only give a strained smile;
- the two gradually stop talking.

Sakura explicitly recognizes the structural resemblance:

> `今の私と琴乃ちゃんみたい`

This is not identity between the pairs. Mana/Haruko and Sakura/Kotono have different histories and roles. What is securely established is a **recurring Hoshimi relational mechanism**:

> professional divergence can convert a trusted peer into the most painful comparison object.

Preferred term:

> `INTERGENERATIONAL_COMPARATIVE_DISTANCE_PATTERN`

This is uniquely useful precursor evidence because it locates the Sakura/Kotono problem inside a prior Hoshimi history rather than treating it as an isolated mood episode.

## 2.4 Mana's repair technique is transmitted through Haruko to Sakura

Mana repairs the Haruko rupture through a deliberately low-pressure outing to eat curry. Haruko remembers that Mana hoped good food might simply make it possible to be together again. The immediate goal is not forced confession; the shared ordinary activity lowers the interpersonal pressure enough for disclosure to become possible later.

Sakura consciously reproduces this method with Kotono by taking her for tonkatsu.

Most importantly, Sakura tells Kotono that even if Kotono had never disclosed the jealousy, the outing would still have mattered:

> `別に聞けなくても良かったんだ`
>
> `琴乃ちゃんと久しぶりにゆっくり`
>
> `お話し出来れば……それだけで嬉しいから`

Preferred terms:

- `INTERGENERATIONAL_REPAIR_TECHNIQUE_TRANSMISSION`
- `NON_EXTRACTIVE_RECONNECTION`

Governing distinction:

> **Repair does not require making disclosure the price of receiving care.**

Sakura wants reconnection first. Confession becomes possible because the relationship has been made inhabitable again, not because Sakura successfully extracts an explanation.

## 2.5 Kotono's jealousy becomes answerable rivalry rather than denied attachment

Kotono eventually states the conflict without euphemism:

> `私、ずっとサニピに……さくらに嫉妬してた`
>
> `ううん、今もしてる`

She had even considered physical/relational distance as a way to avoid feeling the group gap.

Preferred term:

> `COMPARATIVE_AVOIDANCE`

Sakura's answer is not moral correction. She discloses that Sunny also feels pressure from Tsuki's recent growth and insists that she has never regarded Sunny's BIG4 position as proof of categorical superiority.

The two then explicitly state mutual refusal to lose to one another.

Preferred term:

> `RECIPROCAL_RIVAL_ANXIETY`

The rivalry is healthier after disclosure because each side becomes legible as both **beloved peer and genuine competitive threat**. The story does not require either dimension to disappear.

This finding is strongly overlapped by later E1 evidence about rivalry and growth; E2-C1's unique contribution is the **earlier mechanism and chronology of the rupture-repair cycle**.

## 2.6 Relational repair succeeds — and the result-dependence survives

This is the story's most important precursor function.

The festival performance is successful. Kotono reports real satisfaction with the performance, recognizes growth, and is genuinely glad she can speak freely with Sakura again.

Yet the final interior monologue explicitly refuses a complete cure:

> `それでも……月ストとサニピの`
>
> `今の状況に大きな差があるのは変わらない`

and then:

> `私は……心から笑えてない`
>
> `まだ、足りないんだ`
>
> `もっと……もっと結果を出さないと`

Preferred terms:

- `RELATIONAL_REPAIR_WITHOUT_COMPARATIVE_CURE`
- `OUTCOME_DEPENDENCE_RECURSION`

This is **unique precursor evidence** for a later, stronger E1 problem: restoring trust and communication does not by itself resolve the belief that professional legitimacy must be proven through enough visible results.

The correct developmental reading is therefore not:

> jealousy solved → healthy rivalry achieved.

It is:

> relational silence repaired → rivalry becomes speakable → comparative/outcome pressure remains active and can recur.

This is one of the most important reasons to preserve E2-C1 despite later thematic overlap.

## 2.7 Mana's song — deferred participation in inherited community

Mana had wanted to sing the closing song someday with future Hoshimi juniors. Haruko had repeatedly declined invitations during Mana's lifetime because she felt too inadequate to stand beside her.

In the present, Haruko says:

> `今なら、一緒に歌えるかな、私も`

Preferred term:

> `DEFERRED_PARTICIPATION_IN_INHERITANCE`

The festival performance lets Haruko participate in a communal possibility that she previously could not accept while Mana was alive.

This is not the strongest mature Mana/memory evidence in the current corpus; E1 already supplies more developed memorial-performance doctrine. E2-C1 nevertheless preserves a distinct chronology:

> inheritance can contain an invitation that becomes inhabitable only after the recipient's own later development.

The source also deliberately humanizes Mana's relationship to the song through Haruko's memory of the habitual curry joke. That supports the existing rule that memorial inheritance need not monumentalize Mana into a purely solemn symbol.

# 3. `不屈のChallenger～Roll the dice～` — IIIX before the U.S. re-authorization crisis

## 3.1 Safe chronology: IIIX is already under Hoshimi management

**TEXTUAL FACT:** kana describes IIIX as having fallen out of BIG4 because of Himeno and complains that they are now in a `零細事務所`. The player-manager handles negotiations with the American promoter, asks the promoter to take all three IIIX members if possible, travels with them, and later worries aloud that IIIX might drop its Hoshimi Production contract if overseas activity expands.

The event therefore supplies direct early evidence that:

- IIIX is already operating through Hoshimi Production;
- Hoshimi provides contractual and negotiating infrastructure for external work;
- the members retain enough autonomy to joke about salary, overseas activity, and contract continuation;
- the group has not been sentimentally absorbed into Hoshimi's internal culture.

Preferred term:

> `HOSHIMI_CONTRACTUAL_SCAFFOLD_WITH_UNIT_AUTONOMY`

This is unique chronology evidence and should remain retrievable even though later E1 material gives the stronger mature account of IIIX autonomy within Hoshimi.

## 3.2 The initial American offer is individual, but Makino expands it to the unit

kana receives the Las Vegas invitation because her own viral video reached an American promoter. The initial opportunity is therefore personally indexed to kana rather than organically generated by IIIX as a whole.

Makino negotiates for all three to appear:

> `出来ればⅢX三人一緒に`

This supports a bounded form of managerial unit stewardship: the manager does not erase kana's individual opportunity, but uses institutional work to preserve a possible three-person route.

Preferred term:

> `UNIT_PRESERVING_OPPORTUNITY_EXPANSION`

Do not overread this as proof that Makino must always convert individual opportunities into group opportunities. It is a source-specific action in a unit whose competitive identity depends heavily on the three members testing one another.

## 3.3 Domestic prestige is not portable

The promoter bluntly tells kana that IIIX is unknown in America. Ordinary Las Vegas visitors also fail to recognize the members, sometimes treating them simply as convenient bystanders.

Preferred term:

> `NONPORTABLE_PRESTIGE`

Definition:

> Professional status can be real within one evaluative/public sphere while carrying little automatic authority in another.

The event therefore provides a concrete international version of the corpus's broader `EVALUATIVE_JURISDICTION` principle. Japanese rank, name recognition, and prior prestige remain real facts but do not automatically establish equivalent standing in the Las Vegas entertainment environment.

## 3.4 IIIX's pre-crisis relational constitution is explicitly tied to immediate victory

fran initially responds to American anonymity with a relatively patient professional view: they lack overseas achievements now, but could eventually build them.

miho rejects this temporal concession:

> `いずれは……では意味がないの`
>
> `今勝たなくては、私達が一緒にいる意味はないんだから`

This is one of the tranche's highest-value precursor lines.

Preferred term:

> `IMMEDIATE_VICTORY_JUSTIFICATION`

At this point in the chronology, miho can still describe the meaning of IIIX's continued togetherness as conditional on **winning now**.

Her later explanation is equally revealing:

> `私は毎日、自分より上の才能を見せつけられているのに`

and she complains about tolerating the two `性悪ども` because of their talent.

Preferred term:

> `TALENT_JUSTIFIED_ADVERSARIAL_COALITION`

The relationship is not framed as ordinary friendship or comfort. Its value lies partly in remaining beside people whose superior abilities continually challenge one's own self-conception.

This evidence should **not** supersede the mature E1 IIIX thesis. It should be preserved specifically as the pre-crisis constitution against which later U.S. dissolution/re-authorization can be measured.

## 3.5 The story gives an early international-ambition baseline

The Las Vegas trip is not the birth of global ambition from nothing.

- kana explicitly wants worldwide fame and possesses strong enough English to negotiate fluently;
- fran already has Paris/fashion experience, speaks German and French, and imagines restarting apparel with genuine overseas expansion;
- miho treats American recognition as strategically valuable for Japanese recovery;
- by the end, the group can imagine future overseas work while still saying there is unfinished business in Japan.

Preferred term:

> `PREEXISTING_GLOBAL_AMBITION`

This is high-value chronology for later U.S. material: later international movement occurs inside an already established aspiration ecology rather than appearing as an entirely new desire.

## 3.6 IIIX uses strategic status falsehood before later authenticity maturation

fran's publicity strategy is explicit:

> `日本のトップアイドルと名乗ってもバレやしないわよ`

The group asks the promoter to spread the claim, arranges planted reactions in a casino, and relies on social proof to make bystanders infer importance.

Preferred term:

> `STRATEGIC_STATUS_MISREPRESENTATION`

This should be distinguished from E2-B1's later `CURATED_ADVERSARIAL_AUTHENTICITY`.

Here the represented status claim is knowingly inflated precisely because the local audience cannot verify it. The fact that IIIX then performs well and receives a better offer does not retroactively make the original claim fully truthful at the time it was deployed.

The story therefore adds an important boundary to `OPEN-16`:

> **construction, editing, strategy, and commerce can coexist with authenticity, but strategic construction is not automatically authentic merely because it succeeds.**

The event is best treated as an **earlier media-ethics state** from which later IIIX public self-representation becomes more answerable to the unit's actual relational grammar.

## 3.7 Performance can partially validate a status gamble without cleansing the original tactic

After IIIX's show, the promoter praises them and offers a more prestigious performance opportunity before they return home. The performance therefore generates real professional validation.

Preferred term:

> `PERFORMANCE_BACKED_STATUS_BOOTSTRAPPING`

Definition:

> A strategically inflated claim can create access to an evaluative encounter in which actual competence then produces genuine new opportunities.

The ethical and epistemic distinction must remain intact:

- the **access tactic** is strategically deceptive;
- the **subsequent performance success** is genuine within the source;
- the later opportunity is therefore not fictional merely because the original publicity was inflated.

This is a useful precursor to the series' recurring distinction between metric, reputation, performance, and social construction.

## 3.8 IIIX then exploits the feedback loop back in Japan

On return, kana and miho explicitly recognize that Japanese coverage is partly recycling social-media impressions from the Las Vegas audience. kana then argues that Japanese audiences like stories of domestic performers being praised overseas; miho agrees that, once the story exists, they can use it as publicity in Japan.

Preferred term:

> `TRANSNATIONAL_REPUTATION_FEEDBACK_LOOP`

The sequence is:

> domestic fame → weak portability abroad → manufactured local attention → real performance success → overseas social/media validation → renewed domestic publicity.

This is not evidence that status is unreal. It shows that status is partly **circulatory and jurisdiction-dependent**, with reputation moving between evaluative publics and being amplified through media framing.

# 4. Cross-event synthesis — two distinct precursor failures of relational security

The stories appear unrelated, but both preserve a state in which a relationship remains meaningful while its legitimacy is too dependent on external success.

Kotono:

> personal closeness with Sakura is restored, yet she still concludes that she needs more results before she can sincerely feel equal.

miho:

> IIIX contains real trust in talent and real reasons to remain together, yet she still says that if they cannot win **now**, being together has no meaning.

Preferred comparative term:

> `SUCCESS_CONDITIONED_RELATIONAL_SECURITY`

**INTERPRETATION:** external professional success is being asked to perform a relational function it cannot stably guarantee.

This is not yet a mature governing thesis because later E1 evidence already develops the relevant problems more strongly. E2-C1's value is historical-developmental: it shows the earlier form of the instability before later re-authorization, institutional maturation, or explicit philosophical correction.

> **Sakura and Kotono can repair the relationship before Kotono repairs her dependence on comparative results; IIIX can possess real unit identity, international ambition, and Hoshimi support before it learns whether victory is actually a sufficient reason to remain together or whether strategic public construction is answerable to what the unit really is.**
