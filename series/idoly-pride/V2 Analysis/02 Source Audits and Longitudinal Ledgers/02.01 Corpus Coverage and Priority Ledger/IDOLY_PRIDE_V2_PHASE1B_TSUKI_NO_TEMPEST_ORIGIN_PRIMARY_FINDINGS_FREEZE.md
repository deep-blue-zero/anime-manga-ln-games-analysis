# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — TSUKI NO TEMPEST ORIGIN PRIMARY FINDINGS FREEZE

## 0. Freeze statement

This document records the source-native findings from the five Tsuki no Tempest origin bundles **before any historical Tsuki no Tempest analytical prose is consulted**.

The inherited baseline is limited to the already-frozen anime endpoint, the completed Hoshimi anime/game expansion audit, and the completed SUNNY PEACE origin tranche. Later event, BIG4, Tokyo, Stellar, card, bond, message, or historical-analysis material is not permitted to determine this finding set.

The governing analytical distinction remains:

- **diegetic position** — where an event occurred in the characters' lives;
- **disclosure position** — when V2 is permitted to add that information to its model.

These origins contain childhood and pre-Hoshimi events, but those facts enter the V2 state **now**. They may retrospectively explain earlier anime/Hoshimi behavior; they may not be projected backward as knowledge that the earlier prospective reader possessed.

# 2. Executive source-native thesis

The five origins support a unit-level proposition distinct from, but complementary to, the SUNNY PEACE finding.

SUNNY PEACE's origin architecture asks how people whose light has been interrupted or misrecognized can **see one another correctly enough to shine without erasing difference**.

Tsuki no Tempest's origin architecture repeatedly asks a different question:

> **Who or what is allowed to determine the direction of a life?**

Each member enters the origin corpus with a powerful external or relational vector:

- **Saki** has taught herself that being a good daughter, excellent student, and protective older sister determines what she is permitted to want.
- **Suzu** initially organizes aspiration around the idealized figure of Mana and around a family whose love is real but whose educational structure has often substituted programming for listening.
- **Mei** is extraordinarily adaptable and useful to many groups but has difficulty locating a commitment that is hers rather than simply the next enjoyable request she can fulfill.
- **Nagisa** initially makes remaining beside Kotono the explicit reason she becomes an idol.
- **Kotono** turns Mana's death, diary, unfinished song, and her own guilt into an obligation to complete Mana's interrupted idol life.

The common movement is therefore not dependency -> independence. The texts are much less individualistic than that.

The common movement is:

> **borrowed, inherited, or externally structured direction -> chosen commitment.**

Relationships remain. Duty remains. Admiration remains. Memory remains. What changes is **jurisdiction**: the character increasingly becomes able to say why the relationship, duty, memory, or dream will continue to govern her.

The strongest unit formulation at the primary-source freeze is:

> **Tsuki no Tempest is an ecology for turning attachment into authorship.**

A more cautious formulation, suitable as the default canonical claim until later origins/events stress-test it, is:

> **The five Tsuki origins repeatedly distinguish being moved by another person from allowing that person to author one's future.**

This pattern is particularly strong in the Saki/Chisa, Nagisa/Kotono, Kotono/Mana, and Suzu/Mana material, and it is present in a different register in Mei's movement from generalized usefulness toward durable chosen belonging.

# 4. Shiraishi Saki — responsibility had become permission structure

## 4.1 Sisterhood is genuine love, but love acquires the force of command

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Saki and Chisa are one year apart, frequently cared for one another while their parents worked, and the childhood near-drowning intensified Saki's conviction that, as older sister, she had to protect Chisa.

Saki explicitly narrates the internal rule:

`私がお姉ちゃんなんだから、しっかりしなきゃ！`

Later parental language reinforces two identities:

`沙季は勉強を頑張ってるな。父さんも鼻が高いよ`

`沙季、お姉ちゃんとして千紗をお願いね`

Saki summarizes the result:

`優等生でないといけない`

`千紗を支え続けないといけない`

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the problem is not that Saki dislikes caring for Chisa. She repeatedly says the opposite. The problem is that a freely loved relationship becomes fused with a normative role: *because I love her, I must keep structuring my life so that I can protect her; because everyone trusts my competence, I must continue to be the competent one.*

This is a crucial distinction from a simple "parentification made Saki resent Chisa" reading. The source does not support resentment toward Chisa. It supports a self-binding interpretation of love.

## 4.2 Idolhood is suppressed before anyone explicitly forbids it

Saki's childhood idol desire is authentic and audience-oriented. She wants to sing and dance and make many people smile. Yet by adolescence she cannot speak the dream aloud and increasingly avoids idol talk.

Chisa notices the contradiction: Saki secretly practices choreography, buys magazines, goes to karaoke, and visibly enjoys live performance while insisting she does not want to become an idol.

The confrontation is significant because Chisa does not give Saki a new desire. She forces already-existing desire into language.

Saki finally says:

`私ね、本当はずっと、アイドルに憧れて……`

`いつからか、私もアイドルになりたいって思うようになったの`

and:

`私の歌やダンスで、沢山の人を笑顔に出来たら……`

`きっと、とても幸せなんだろうなって`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** no parent had yet explicitly prohibited idolhood. Saki had inferred incompatibility from accumulated expectations.

## 4.3 The parental "freedom" scene is psychologically sophisticated

At dinner, Saki's parents unexpectedly tell her she may pursue anything she wants. Her father even floats idolhood as an example, before assuming she has lost interest.

Saki explodes:

`散々期待したくせに、今更好きにしろだなんて……`

`無責任だよ……っ`

This should not be reduced to irrational anger or ungratefulness.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** externally, the parents are granting freedom. Internally, Saki experiences the statement as retroactively invalidating the years in which she believed compliance was the price of love and reliability. If the constraints were never absolute, what were all those sacrifices for?

This is one of the clearest origin examples of **internalized expectation outlasting the authority that produced it**.

The parents' later apology also prevents an easy villain model. They love their daughters and have misread the weight Saki assigned to their expectations.

## 4.4 Chisa and Saki discover the same trap from opposite sides

Chisa says:

`私が、ずっとお姉ちゃんに我慢させてたのかもしれない`

Saki rejects that causal simplification. She genuinely wants to support Chisa and says Chisa has also saved her many times.

The real discovery is Saki's formulation:

`私……千紗のそばにいることと`

`アイドルの夢を諦めることを一緒に考えてたみたい`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Saki distinguishes the relationship from the sacrifice she had attached to it.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** this is the bilateral correction to the SUNNY Chisa origin. Chisa had learned *my desire burdens Saki*. Saki had learned *my desire may take me away from Chisa*. Both treated love as requiring self-limitation, but neither actually wanted the other's self-erasure.

The relationship becomes healthier not because the sisters matter less, but because sacrifice loses its status as proof of love.

## 4.5 Saki's answer is integration, not rebellion

Saki immediately researches possible life architectures. She decides she still wants university, partly as risk management and partly because she genuinely wants further study. She looks for a school compatible with idol activity, geography study, commuting from home, and continued proximity to family.

She produces a detailed `アイドルと大学生を両立するには` presentation with schedules, graphs, admissions assumptions, and risk analysis.

In the Hoshimi interview she says:

`アイドルと学生の二足のわらじを履くことで`

`互いにいい影響を及ぼせるのでは、と考えています`

and:

`私にはそばにいてくれる大切な家族がいます`

`みんなに背中を押してもらえれば、どこまでも走っていける`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Saki does not reject family, education, responsibility, or planning. She re-authors their relationship to idolhood.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** this is an early model for Tsuki's mature ethic. Self-authorship is not "do whatever you want." It is the capacity to integrate commitments without treating one inherited role as possessing veto power over the rest of the self.

## 4.6 Guardrails

- Do not diagnose Saki with parentification from this corpus alone; the story supports role pressure and early caregiving, but the parents remain loving and responsive.
- Do not claim her parents forced her to give up idolhood; they did not.
- Do not erase Chisa's dependence problem. Saki's loving motives and Chisa's burden fears can both be true.
- Do not turn Saki's planning into pure anxiety. It is also a genuine competence and a form of agency.

# 5. Narumiya Suzu — admiration becomes a borrowed language for saying what she needs

## 5.1 Privilege and emotional deprivation coexist

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Suzu grows up materially privileged. Her father indulges her purchases; her mother imposes an enormous schedule of lessons—piano, ballet, calligraphy, painting, Japanese dance, riding, English, tea ceremony, and more.

Both parents describe educational deprivation in their own childhoods and appear to believe they are giving Suzu opportunities they lacked.

The problem is therefore not lack of love. It is a family structure in which love is often delivered as **provision and programming rather than presence**.

When her father works overseas and her mother is frequently absent for social obligations, Suzu says:

`私、お父様とお母様が見てくださっていると分かっていれば、もっと頑張れる気がするんですの`

and later:

`私……もっと、家族みんなで過ごしたい、ですわ……`

`私、ずっと寂しかったんですの……`

This is a major corrective to reading Suzu as merely pampered. She is pampered in goods and underheard in emotional needs.

## 5.2 Mana first appears as a complete performance event

At the Hoshimi festival, a storm causes Mana's outdoor live to be announced as canceled. Fans call for her; Mana appears anyway, asks whether she may sing, and the rain clears into a meteor-filled sky before `星の海の記憶`.

Suzu experiences this as revelation:

`あんなにキラキラした方、初めて見ましたわ！`

She then literally collides with Mana afterward and receives direct personal recognition. Mana tells her that energizing an honest, cute child like Suzu makes her happy as an idol.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Suzu's Mana attachment is not only parasocial. It includes a formative live performance and a brief private interaction.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the later `麻奈様` register is comic and excessive, but its emotional foundation is serious. Mana is the first public performer Suzu experiences as turning an otherwise disappointing day into wonder while also personally validating Suzu's responsiveness.

## 5.3 Mana becomes an affective survival technology

During the period of family absence and overscheduling, Suzu increasingly uses Mana's performances as emotional refuge:

`麻奈様がいれば、私はきっと大丈夫ですわ……！`

and:

`麻奈様のライブを観れば、笑顔になって現実も忘れられる`

The most important media moment is Mana's broadcast advice:

`自分の気持ちに、嘘はつかないで下さい`

`自分を信じて突き進めばきっと打ち破れる`

`ちゃーんと自分の足で進めば、気付いたら誰かがそばにいてくれますよ`

Suzu recognizes that she has been lying about loneliness. The next day she tells her mother and father directly that she wants more family time.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the family responds. Her father returns from the overseas posting; her mother reduces the lesson burden; the family spends more time together.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Mana's first major inheritance into Suzu's life is not "become an idol." It is **speak desire truthfully and move on your own feet**. Idol vocation comes later.

This matters enormously for later inheritance analysis because Suzu's imitation of Mana grows out of advice whose content is anti-imitation and self-directed.

## 5.4 Mana's death transforms reception into vocation

After Mana dies, Suzu is devastated. She describes the world without Mana as drained of color.

The pivot is seeing a little girl delighted by an idol on a shop television. Suzu recognizes her younger self and thinks about how many people Mana encouraged.

She concludes:

`私だって、麻奈様のように……誰かの背中を押せる存在に……`

`アイドルに……なってみせますわ……！！`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the vocation is recipient-oriented. She wants to reproduce for another person what Mana's performance did for her.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Suzu begins with succession language, but the ethical function beneath it is already generative rather than possessive. She does not merely want Mana's rank; she wants to become a cause of courage in someone else.

## 5.5 Parental opposition is not anti-idol prejudice

Suzu's mother explicitly says idolhood is a wonderful profession. Her objection is that Suzu has repeatedly avoided or abandoned demanding commitments and may understand the glamorous surface without understanding sustained labor.

She asks whether Suzu can really do the kind of work Mana must have done behind the scenes.

This is an important counterweight to Suzu's self-narration.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the parents identify a real character-development problem: persistence, worldly experience, and follow-through.

**INTERPRETATION:** the proposed U.S. study is comically extreme, but narratively it externalizes the family's concern that Suzu's declarations run ahead of demonstrated endurance.

Suzu responds by running away to the Hoshimi audition. This proves intensity of desire, not yet the full durability her parents asked for.

The retrospective tag supplies the later answer: her parents attend a live and praise her growth.

## 5.6 Inheritance guardrail

The source supports all of the following simultaneously:

- Suzu authentically idolizes Mana.
- Her idol desire is historically caused by Mana.
- Her language can be successor-like and imitative.
- Mana's most formative advice to her is about truthful self-direction.
- Suzu's chosen purpose is to encourage others, not merely reproduce Mana.

Therefore **Mana influence must not be collapsed into either "mere imitation" or "already fully self-authored."** The origin establishes a productive contradiction that later material must resolve longitudinally.

# 6. Hayasaka Mei — usefulness is not the same thing as belonging

## 6.1 Early social adaptation is deliberate

Mei says that as a child she had unusual friends whom classmates mocked. Her mother and grandmother could see these friends; her father could not.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the bundle does not explicitly name these companions as ghosts.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** within the franchise's established supernatural grammar, they are very likely nonordinary/supernatural acquaintances rather than ordinary school friends, but the origin itself should not be made to say more than it says.

Her family responds to her social isolation by helping her create disclosure rules:

- talk about the unusual friends only with family or highly trusted people;
- learn conventional manners so avoidable social friction does not compound the problem.

Mei later reports that this works: she has many friends and enjoys school.

This matters because her social ease is not simply innate innocence. Some of it is a learned accommodation after childhood stigmatization.

## 6.2 Mei becomes valuable everywhere without choosing anywhere

Mei's physical talent makes her a constant sports substitute. She accepts requests because helping is enjoyable. Yet she never settles into one club because nothing quite feels like *the* thing.

This is an important prehistory to her later interpersonal style:

> many groups need Mei; few relationships initially make Mei feel specifically chosen.

Her abundance of social contact therefore coexists with a lack of singular belonging.

## 6.3 Cheer introduces a serious collective project

The cheerleading captain notices not only Mei's athletic ability but her `華`—performative presence. Mei joins because the group looks joyful.

As the team approaches national competition, however, the atmosphere becomes increasingly severe. Mei keeps wanting performance to feel alive and enjoyable while the group defines seriousness through sacrifice, repetition, and tolerance of exhaustion.

The conflict becomes explicit when cheer practice and idol practice collide.

The captain first lashes out at idolhood, then immediately apologizes and states the real issue more fairly: two genuinely serious commitments may become materially incompatible.

She asks Mei to choose.

## 6.4 `本気` becomes Mei's conceptual problem

Mei says:

`今まで私は、全力で楽しむことが本気なんだと思ってた`

She realizes the captain and teammates mean something different by `本気`: persisting through difficulty toward a defined achievement.

Suzu provides the key formulation:

`どれだけ辛かろうと、その先にあるものを掴む喜びは何物にもかえがたいからですわ！`

Mei does not simply capitulate to this model. Her solution is dialectical.

She tells the captain she wants to honor the team's seriousness through nationals, **and** that the team needs to recover serious enjoyment:

`本気で頑張るのも大事だけど……それと同じくらい`

`本気で楽しむのも大事だと思う`

The captain agrees that performers must themselves retain vitality if they want to move spectators.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the cheer environment improves and the team wins the national competition before Mei leaves for idolhood.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Mei's function is not to oppose discipline. She prevents discipline from becoming affectively self-defeating.

This is an unusually strong origin-level basis for reading her later "lightness" as a professional/social competence rather than comic unseriousness.

## 6.5 Why Mei ultimately chooses idolhood

The decisive reason is not abstract celebrity ambition.

Mei tells Suzu that, despite many friends and many club connections, she had never become deeply close to one person. When Suzu said they would be together going forward, Mei felt uniquely needed:

`あんな風に芽衣を必要としてくれるのが……本当に、本当に`

and:

`だからね、芽衣が今アイドルをやってるのは、すずにゃんのおかげなんだよ`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Suzu's promise of durable togetherness is the emotional pivot in Mei's choice.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Mei moves from **generalized usefulness** to **particular belonging**. She does not stop being socially expansive; she acquires a relation worth organizing continuity around.

This is one of Tsuki's clearest versions of attachment becoming chosen direction.

## 6.6 Mei as cross-unit interpreter

The origin also retrospectively identifies Rei, the high-pressure cheer team, and early Kotono as similar in one respect: all look tightly wound around serious performance.

Mei's eye for this pattern helps explain why she can soften group climates without needing to dominate them.

**Guardrail:** do not make Mei a universal therapist. Her insight is experiential and embodied; she often arrives at it through observation rather than analytic language.

# 7. Ibuki Nagisa — accompaniment begins as devotion and becomes a principle

## 7.1 The first bond is highly intimate but not canonically labeled romance

Nagisa's first interest in Kotono is triggered by the notebook illustration. She describes the image with explicitly affective language:

`一目惚れってこういう感覚なのかな`

The retrospective conversation also jokes about `なれそめ` being a word used for couples, and Nagisa repeatedly calls Kotono cute and says she loves her.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the text intentionally uses yuri-adjacent or romantic-coded vocabulary around the relationship.

**INTERPRETATION:** the relationship can support a queer/romantic reading, but the origin does not explicitly establish a dating or confessed-romance relationship. Mature synthesis should preserve the coding without promoting it to a fact the text has not named.

## 7.2 Nagisa chooses Kotono under social cost

Kotono is already isolated. Peers resent Nagisa for spending time with her and plan to ostracize Nagisa as punishment.

Kotono intervenes:

`私のことを嫌うのは勝手だけど……`

`伊吹さんに何かするつもりなら、覚悟しなさい`

`私の友達を傷つけたら許さないから`

For both girls this is a recognition event.

Kotono discovers that she already thinks of Nagisa as a friend; Nagisa hears Kotono publicly name the bond despite social pressure.

This is important because the relationship is founded not on pity alone but on **mutual costly recognition**.

## 7.3 Mana explicitly entrusts Kotono to Nagisa—but Nagisa resists coercion

Mana privately meets Nagisa. Their conversation is playful enough to resemble a rivalry over who loves Kotono more, but it turns serious.

Mana asks Nagisa to remain Kotono's ally:

`これからもずっと、琴乃の味方でいてね`

Nagisa answers:

`はい。約束します`

However, when Mana asks Nagisa to bring Kotono to a live, Nagisa refuses to force Kotono. She says she will invite her if Kotono herself wants reconciliation.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Nagisa accepts the duty to stand beside Kotono while preserving Kotono's decision-making authority.

This is an early form of accompaniment rather than control.

## 7.4 After Mana's death, Nagisa practices persistence without demanded reciprocity

Kotono stops attending school and closes herself in her room. Nagisa visits essentially every day, often speaking through the closed door about ordinary life without receiving an answer.

This continues for about a month until Kotono finally opens the door and calls her name.

The formal relational pattern is significant:

> Nagisa does not wait for Kotono to become socially convenient before remaining her friend.

She supplies continuity before she can supply a solution.

When Kotono later explodes in grief, Nagisa recognizes that she cannot make the pain disappear. Her support is not successful because it immediately fixes Kotono; it is successful because it survives Kotono's inability to reward it.

## 7.5 The notebook creates continuity without simple replacement

Originally Kotono drew a lonely girl and Mana added a second girl holding her hand. After Mana's death, Nagisa encounters a later lonely-girl drawing and adds herself beside it with the message:

`いつまでも一緒だよ`

Kotono recognizes Nagisa's face in the drawing and cries because she understands:

`私は独りじゃないんだ`

This is a major image for the entire V2 corpus.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Nagisa takes up the *formal position* of the second figure in a relational image once co-authored by Mana and Kotono.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the story distinguishes **relational succession** from **person substitution**. Nagisa does not become a new Mana. She makes the same proposition—"you are not alone"—available in a new living relationship.

This should later be routed to the grief/memory/inheritance synthesis.

## 7.6 Nagisa helps Kotono see idol-Mana

Nagisa invites Kotono to watch Mana's live recordings. Kotono says she agrees only because Nagisa is with her.

Watching the performance lets Kotono perceive something she had resisted while Mana was alive: Mana genuinely loved idol work and used it to make others happy.

Nagisa therefore becomes a **mediator of interpretation**, but again not an authority who dictates the conclusion. She creates the conditions in which Kotono can see Mana differently.

## 7.7 The origin of Nagisa's idolhood is deliberately non-autonomous

When Kotono starts training to become an idol, Nagisa experiences a painful symmetry: Kotono's growing vocation reduces their shared time exactly as Mana's growing vocation once reduced Kotono's time with Mana.

Nagisa thinks:

`もしも琴乃ちゃんがアイドルになったら、ただの高校生の私はそばにいられなくなる`

and therefore auditions too.

In the interview she states with no ambiguity:

`私がアイドルになろうと思ったのは、琴乃ちゃんのためです`

and:

`大好きな琴乃ちゃんと一緒に夢を叶えること`

`それが、私がアイドルになる理由です！`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Nagisa's initial idol motive is relationally derivative.

That is not a flaw hidden by the story; it is the point from which her later development proceeds.

## 7.8 The origin itself supplies the later correction

Kotono's origin quotes the later Hoshimi conflict in which Nagisa refuses to abandon the other Tsuki members merely to follow Kotono:

`今はもう――琴乃ちゃんだけじゃない`

`私は、私達は、四人で頑張って……`

and:

`だから私――琴乃ちゃんとは一緒に行けない`

**STRONG INFERENCE:** this is one of the clearest longitudinal arcs currently available in Phase 1B. Nagisa does not "outgrow" loving Kotono. She outgrows the idea that love for Kotono exhausts the reasons she has for being an idol.

Her devotion becomes a broader principle of solidarity.

# 8. Nagase Kotono — grief first turns love into an impossible assignment

## 8.1 Mana is originally Kotono's whole social world

Kotono describes childhood Mana as the person who took her by the hand and showed her the world:

`明るくて優しいお姉ちゃんは私の憧れで`

`私の世界の全てだった`

When Mana becomes an idol, Kotono experiences increasing absence not as neutral scheduling but as a threat to the relationship's exclusivity.

Her recurring question is effectively scarcity-based:

`私とアイドルどっちが大切なの？`

Mana's answer—both—does not satisfy her.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** before bereavement, Kotono already has difficulty accepting that love can remain real while attention is distributed across a vocation and a broader public.

This is a precondition of the later grief problem.

## 8.2 Public Mana makes private distance harder

Mana's popularity creates a second injury. Kotono cannot simply distance herself from an absent sister because Mana is omnipresent in posters, music, school conversations, and people seeking access.

Peers initially approach Kotono because she is Mana's sister. When Kotono refuses favors, they reveal that the friendship was instrumental and later ostracize/bully her.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Kotono's distrust of others is partly trained by being treated as an access route to Mana.

This materially deepens the anime-native impression of her social guardedness.

## 8.3 The notebook is a co-authored technology of attachment

One night Kotono draws a lonely girl while waiting for Mana to come home. Mana returns late, sees the drawing, and adds a second girl holding the first girl's hand.

Thereafter Kotono leaves drawings and Mana adds the partner figure. Kotono describes the collection as almost an exchange diary through which the sisters communicated.

The notebook therefore carries multiple functions:

- a record of absence;
- evidence of continued mutual attention;
- a visual promise that physical absence does not equal abandonment;
- eventually, after Mana dies, an impossible expectation that the absent partner might somehow be drawn again.

This object should become a primary locator in the later motif index.

## 8.4 Nagisa is the first relationship not mediated by Mana's celebrity

Kotono initially assumes Nagisa wants access to Mana too. Nagisa's persistence makes that explanation increasingly impossible.

Kotono's decisive realization occurs when other students threaten Nagisa and she reflexively names Nagisa her friend.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** protecting Nagisa makes Kotono's own attachment legible to herself.

This is structurally similar to several other IDOLY PRIDE recognition scenes: action precedes explicit self-knowledge.

## 8.5 The last pre-death period is not continuous estrangement

The origin qualifies any simplified "Kotono and Mana were completely estranged before the accident" model.

Kotono once follows Nagisa's advice to play hard-to-get about an amusement-park invitation; Mana instead takes Makino. Kotono then decides she will not refuse the next invitation. She later sees an anime film with Mana whose theme song is `Gemstones` and says the outing briefly felt like a return to their earlier relationship.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** there was partial rapprochement before Mana's final period of intense work.

The tragedy is therefore sharpened: Kotono had evidence that repair was possible but not enough time to complete it.

## 8.6 The final conflict turns unfinished communication into guilt

Kotono recounts the final-night line explicitly:

`お姉ちゃんなんていなければ良かったのに`

She knows the accident was not causally her fault, but the inability to apologize turns emotional responsibility into something she experiences as unresolved moral debt.

Nagisa cannot remove this by simply saying "it wasn't your fault."

This supports the anime endpoint's later conclusion that Kotono must eventually stop outsourcing settlement of the final night to Mana.

## 8.7 Successful effort creates a powerful but potentially overgeneralized rule

After beginning recovery, Kotono's school attendance and grades are poor enough that admission to a desired high school looks unlikely. Because she wants to stay with Nagisa, she undertakes sustained study and succeeds.

Kotono says:

`努力は絶対報われる、頑張れば夢は叶うんだって思えるようになった`

and identifies this success as part of the confidence that lets her decide to pursue idolhood.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the high-school success is a causal antecedent of her idol decision.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** this experience may also help explain later rigidity. A belief that disciplined effort can overcome impossible odds is adaptive in exam preparation and idol training, but it can become dangerous when applied to grief, substitution, or uncontrollable loss.

This is a hypothesis for later longitudinal testing, not yet a settled diagnosis of Kotono's control style.

## 8.8 The diary gives love—and Kotono converts love into assignment

During division of Mana's belongings, Kotono finds Mana's diary. It tells her two crucial things:

1. Mana genuinely loved idol work despite mistakes, pressure, and guilt about neglecting Kotono.
2. Mana continued to love Kotono intensely; the final entries center Kotono's birthday and include `大好きだよ琴乃`.

This should have relieved one uncertainty: idolhood did not replace sisterly love.

But Kotono immediately produces a second interpretation:

`お姉ちゃんがやり残したことを私が代わりに果たさなきゃいけない`

`お姉ちゃんが立てなかった決勝のステージで、あの曲を歌うことが私のやるべきことなんだ`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the proxy/succession mission originates from Kotono's interpretation of the diary and unfinished performance.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** grief turns evidence of Mana's love into a **commission that Mana never explicitly issued**. Kotono moves from "Mana loved me" to "therefore I must finish Mana's life."

This is one of the strongest source-native foundations for the anime's later self-authorship arc.

## 8.9 Later Kotono explicitly revises the founding motive

The origin's retrospective endpoint states that she once did not understand why Mana loved idolhood, but now does because she herself loves it.

Her current dream is:

`月ストのみんなとアイドルの頂点に立つこと`

and:

`青春全部かけて、アイドルの最高峰へ`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the stated destination is now collective and living, not completion of Mana's unfinished song.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the object "idol summit" may superficially resemble inherited competitive ambition, but its authorship has changed. Kotono now names Tsuki as the subject of the dream.

That transformation should remain central to all later Kotono work.

# 9. Unit architecture emerging from the five origins

## 9.1 Not five versions of the same problem

The five origins are complementary rather than redundant.

- Saki brings **structure** but must stop treating structure as self-denial.
- Suzu brings **declared ambition and faith** but must prove that declaration can survive sustained labor.
- Mei brings **joy, permeability, and social flexibility** but must learn that seriousness sometimes requires exclusion and duration.
- Nagisa brings **persistent care** but must allow care to widen beyond a single person.
- Kotono brings **directional intensity** but must distinguish inherited obligation from chosen vocation.

The group therefore has an internal regulatory logic:

- Saki can give durable form to desire.
- Mei can prevent durable form from becoming suffocating.
- Suzu can name large desires without embarrassment.
- Nagisa can keep people relationally present through crisis.
- Kotono can supply intense forward orientation once the destination is genuinely hers.

**INTERPRETATION:** Tsuki's resilience may arise less from homogeneous temperament than from the fact that its members correct one another's failure modes.

This requires later event testing.

## 9.2 `本気` and sustainable commitment

Mei's origin gives the unit a useful conceptual vocabulary.

There are at least two forms of seriousness:

1. commitment as willingness to endure difficulty toward a goal;
2. commitment as preserving the lived joy that makes the performance worth sharing.

Tsuki needs both.

Kotono/Saki can drift toward overcontrol; Mei resists joyless discipline. Suzu can drift toward declarations without proof; the unit's work demands follow-through. Nagisa can over-center a relationship; unit commitment expands her field of care.

**OPEN HYPOTHESIS:** later Tsuki events may repeatedly dramatize not "work hard" but **how to sustain intensity without letting intensity destroy the reason for performing**.

## 9.3 The anime lyric now has deeper biographical provenance

The frozen anime endpoint identified Tsuki's semifinal/final lyric language around relational selfhood and non-imitation, including:

`君がいるから私でいいんだ`

and:

`誰かの真似じゃない自分の場所探せ`

The origins strengthen the biographical fit of that language.

- Saki needs others without becoming only the role they expect.
- Suzu begins through imitation of Mana but is called toward her own feet.
- Mei discovers a particular "you" who makes belonging possible without erasing her own definition of joy.
- Nagisa begins by following Kotono and later refuses to let following exhaust her identity.
- Kotono must stop being Mana's replacement while remaining Mana's sister and inheritor.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the song's relational selfhood is not ornamental branding. It accurately describes a recurring character architecture now visible across all five origins.

## 9.4 A cautious moon/orbit metaphor

The unit name invites a lunar/orbital reading, but V2 should avoid reverse-engineering all biography from branding.

A limited metaphor is nevertheless supported by the relational pattern:

> the members repeatedly define themselves in relation to another body—family, Mana, Kotono, teammates, expectations—and then have to discover a stable path that is relational without being merely derivative.

This is an **INTERPRETATION**, not a textual explanation of why Tsuki no Tempest is named Tsuki no Tempest.

# 10. Cross-tranche consequences already visible before historical comparison

## 10.1 SUNNY PEACE addendum is likely required

Saki's origin materially deepens the already-audited Chisa origin.

The existing SUNNY formulation that the sisters were trapped in a reciprocal sacrifice loop remains strong, but Saki adds an important asymmetry:

- Chisa believes spontaneous desire burdens Saki.
- Saki genuinely wants to support Chisa and does **not** experience Chisa herself as a burden.
- Saki nevertheless conflates love/support with surrendering an idol future.

The mature formulation should therefore be:

> **Neither sister wants the other's self-erasure; both have learned to interpret self-limitation as evidence of love.**

This warrants a controlled cross-tranche addendum rather than rewriting the frozen SUNNY document.

The parent Hoshimi audit should remain untouched. A Tsuki-origin addendum should route these facts as retrospective provenance.
