# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — TIER-A MAIN NARRATIVE CONSOLIDATION AUDIT

## 0. Executive judgment

The completed Tier-A narrative supports a broader governing model than the historical V1 thesis **“inherit the miracle without becoming its copy.”** That formulation remains foundational and is strongly confirmed, but it describes the opening form of a problem that the later game narrative repeatedly generalizes.

The Tier-A spine can now be stated as follows:

> **INTERPRETATION — *IDOLY PRIDE* is a professional ensemble drama about how people remain authors of their lives when those lives are profoundly shaped by inheritance, attachment, institutions, competition, audiences, gratitude, memory, and bodily limits.**

The narrative begins with the most literal possible authorship crisis: Sakura receives Mana's heart, Kotono receives Mana's unfinished absence, Makino receives a vocation formed around Mana, and Hoshimi receives a public miracle capable of becoming institutional destiny. The early answer is anti-substitution: the dead can matter without owning the living.

The later arcs then enlarge the same ethical problem:

1. **Hoshimi / Mana / origins — inheritance and relational authorship.** A person may be moved, saved, recruited, loved, taught, or given a dream by somebody else without that origin acquiring permanent jurisdiction over what the dream must mean.
2. **Tokyo — institutional authorship.** An agency is legitimate insofar as its structure enlarges meaningful authored choice rather than treating idols as instruments of an externally authored outcome.
3. **BIG4 — distributed personhood.** Dependence is not the opposite of autonomy. A unit becomes healthier when its members can remain mutually constitutive while retaining standing to refuse, criticize, compete, revise roles, leave, return, and continue.
4. **Stellar — supported autonomy across time.** Protection, gratitude, professional duty, audience obligation, and competitive ambition are all morally real, but none automatically receives sovereignty over a person's future. Bodies and future selves remain part of the constituency whose interests must be represented.

This yields the strongest cross-arc proposition presently available:

> **STRONG INTERPRETATION — Tier-A *IDOLY PRIDE* rejects both sovereign individualism and sovereign collectivity. Its preferred social form is answerable interdependence: people become themselves through others, but no relationship, institution, score, memory, or ideal is allowed unilateral jurisdiction over what their lives are for.**

That proposition is stronger than any one unit philosophy and is supported by independent structures across Sunny Peace, Tsuki no Tempest, TRINITYAiLE, LizNoir, IIIX, Makino/Hoshimi, and the Tokyo/BIG4/Stellar arc sequence.

The consolidation also produces an important negative result: **Tier-A does not contain one final definition of idolhood.** By the end of Stellar, several incompatible professional philosophies remain legitimate. The work prefers plural authored forms over a single post-Mana successor doctrine.

# 2. Tier-A chronology and disclosure reconciliation

The central chronology rule is now non-negotiable:

> **release chronology, diegetic chronology, disclosure chronology, and analytical-admission chronology are related but not identical.**

A later-disclosed origin can explain an earlier action without becoming something an earlier viewer/player could have known at the time.

## 2.1 Mana prehistory — the founding event predates Hoshimi's later mythology

The Mana origin bundles establish a pre-death sequence in which:

- Makino begins outside idol devotion and becomes involved because Mana specifically recruits his attention;
- management becomes an acquired vocation;
- Mana develops from an ordinary schoolgirl with exceptional professional force into public miracle;
- her work creates bodily, relational, and managerial costs;
- Mana/Kotono strain develops during the rise rather than appearing only after death;
- LizNoir/Mana competition already situates miracle inside a professional ecosystem.

Analytically, Mana's origin is **FOUNDATIONAL**, but it must not erase the anime's original disclosure order. The work repeatedly benefits from the difference between **what happened first** and **what the audience was allowed to understand first**.

## 2.2 Anime and `st-original-cmn` — parallel authoritative surfaces, not one master screenplay

The Hoshimi audit establishes three permanent cross-media rules.

First, the game Hoshimi layer is not redundant. It supplies dense Makino first-person interiority, professional reasoning, branch possibility, and secondary-character arcs that the anime compresses.

Second, the anime remains authoritative for audiovisual form: vocal delivery, timing, score, framing, blocking, gesture, color, editing, and performance staging.

Third, the media contain **material information-order and continuity variants**. The medical/transplant revelation occurs much earlier in game narration than in the anime; Rui/Asakura and several character developments are reordered; and the finale differs materially. The anime proves the two units separately before a tied result and joint stage, whereas the game moves toward a ten-person final and memorial return to `First Step`.

Therefore:

> **CONFLICT / VARIANT — there is no single scene-for-scene canonical chronology produced by mechanically merging anime and game Hoshimi.**

The correct V2 method preserves both surfaces and routes claims to the medium that actually establishes them.

## 2.3 Unit-origin disclosure — formative history enters retrospectively

The unit origins repeatedly disclose diegetically early material after the anime/Hoshimi endpoint has already been frozen.

This creates a legitimate two-layer reading:

- **prospective:** what the anime/Hoshimi narrative itself made knowable;
- **retrospective:** what the origins explain about how those characters arrived there.

The origins do not merely add trauma dossiers. They frequently correct causal structure:

- Chisa/Saki becomes a reciprocal sacrifice loop rather than one-way dependency;
- Rei's achievement drive is revealed as a distortion of an earlier audience-oriented dance desire;
- Shizuku's shyness becomes the consequence of visibility having become dangerous;
- Haruko becomes part of Hoshimi's pre-Mana institutional architecture;
- Sakura's smile is traced from protective emotional labor through permission to grieve into chosen generativity;
- Tsuki's attachments are revealed as vectors the girls repeatedly re-author rather than destinies that simply command them;
- LizNoir is shown through multiple constitutions rather than a timeless fixed unit;
- TRINITYAiLE repeatedly tests self-sacrifice, role inequality, and consultation;
- IIIX's later self-authored compact is shown to emerge from a unit originally assembled under corporate incentive.

## 2.4 IIIX — the chronology defect and its correction

IIIX is the strongest warning against treating one origin bundle as one chronological block.

The corrected partition is:

1. `origin_thrx_001`–`origin_thrx_002` / stories 01–10 — secure pre-Tokyo baseline;
2. `origin_thrx_003` / stories 11–15 — Grand Prix/I-UNITY concurrent material, not admissible at `tokyo_001`;
3. `origin_thrx_004` story 16 — still before the I-UNITY final boundary;
4. story 17 — the I-UNITY final boundary itself;
5. stories 18–20 — post-I-UNITY institutional collapse, dissolution possibility, reduced live, and voluntary continuation;
6. BIG4 opening — confirms freelance/self-produced IIIX as already established history.

Thus the IIIX encountered at Tokyo is **not yet** the mature freelance/self-managed IIIX. The post-I-UNITY voluntary continuation is a later fact, and “second founding” remains an interpretation rather than textual wording.

## 2.5 Tokyo → BIG4 → Stellar — the clean sequential spine

After the IIIX bridge, the post-Hoshimi main narrative proceeds cleanly:

- **Tokyo**: institutional governance and agency legitimacy;
- **BIG4**: Tsuki's constitutional crisis and refoundation, DoriKyun's elimination ethic, IIIX integration without domestication;
- **Stellar**: Hoshimi-wide tournament pressure, Sakura health/autonomy, TRINITYAiLE's `恩返し`, long-duration vocation, and a plural competitive endpoint.

Later event chronology must be mapped onto this spine rather than assumed to occur after it wholesale.

# 3. Main-narrative architecture — five transformations

## 3.1 Transformation I — miracle becomes a problem of jurisdiction

Mana's miracle is not rejected. Tier-A repeatedly treats it as real in at least the social and aesthetic sense: her performance can reorganize lives, create public memory, and make people experience something extraordinary.

The problem begins when miracle becomes **jurisdiction**.

Potential failures include:

- Sakura becoming a vessel for Mana because she carries Mana's heart/voice;
- Kotono allowing Mana's absent dream to author her whole vocation;
- Makino remaining permanent witness to the dead rather than manager of the living;
- Hoshimi becoming a shrine that reproduces one idol ideal;
- LizNoir treating Mana as terminal benchmark;
- miho treating Mana's public legend as something whose defeat is necessary for Yō to matter.

The early narrative's durable answer is not forgetting Mana. It is:

> **the dead may be causal, beloved, exemplary, and even supernaturally present without acquiring permanent authority to decide what living people must become.**

This is the first form of the series' authorship ethic.

## 3.2 Transformation II — received desire becomes chosen desire

The origins generalize the same structure outside bereavement.

Across Sunny Peace and Tsuki especially, people receive direction from others:

- Chisa from Saki;
- Rei from family conflict and audience encounter;
- Shizuku from fandom;
- Haruko from Saegusa/Hoshimi and later younger idols;
- Sakura from Tomo/Mana/Haruko;
- Saki from family responsibility;
- Suzu from Mana admiration;
- Mei from Suzu and the affective pleasure of performance;
- Nagisa from Kotono;
- Kotono from Mana and her interpretation of Mana's unfinished horizon.

Tier-A does not treat relational origin as contamination. It asks whether the character can **answer back to the origin**.

This produces a stronger rule than “follow your own dream”:

> **STRONG INFERENCE — a desire can be authentically one's own even when another person caused it, provided its meaning remains revisable by the person living it.**

That distinction becomes the bridge from character psychology to institutions.

## 3.3 Transformation III — Tokyo converts authorship into institutional legitimacy

Tokyo makes the moral grammar explicit at agency scale.

Makino's clearest formulation is that idols are not the office's tools; ideally, the idols should be able to **use the office in order to shine**.

The arc therefore distinguishes:

- authority that coordinates, discloses, protects, negotiates, and creates options;
- authority that treats people as means toward organizationally authored outcomes.

Hoshimi's main advantage is not sentimental goodness. It is **answerability**.

The institution may assign work, evaluate, negotiate, recruit, support, and structure careers, but its legitimacy depends on preserving the idol's standing to participate in defining what her career is for.

The best governing term remains:

> **fiduciary authority — power held for the development of the person rather than ownership of the person.**

Tokyo is also where the “family” metaphor becomes insufficient. Hoshimi can feel like home while still being a professional organization with hierarchy, labor, scheduling, contracts, reputational exposure, and managerial power.

## 3.4 Transformation IV — BIG4 rejects independence as the cure for dependency

Kotono correctly perceives that the old Tsuki arrangement contains asymmetry. The other four have too often been organized around her direction, and she has too often taken their support as a reason to carry more alone.

Her wrong solution is severance.

The four-member Tsuki then proves something important: the others **really can grow without Kotono**, and that growth is not merely waiting for her return. Nagisa must discover an idol self not reducible to supporting Kotono; Suzu learns the burden and legitimacy of center; Saki and Mei become more explicit contributors to procedure and continuity.

Kotono also grows independently. The arc refuses, however, to let those gains retroactively vindicate the unilateral departure.

The five-person reunion succeeds because the unit does not restore its old constitution.

The resulting principle is:

> **distributed personhood — people can be constitutive of one another without any one participant owning the role, identity, or future of the others.**

The center becomes a professional office rather than an ontological property. Suzu can hold it, lend one song, later entrust it to Kotono because the tested formation maximizes their chance to win, and still intend to take it back.

This is one of Tier-A's clearest demonstrations that **role legitimacy can be real without becoming permanent sovereignty**.

## 3.5 Transformation V — Stellar adds the future self to the constituency

Stellar adds time.

Sakura's audience ethic makes present commitment morally serious: any stage could be someone's only chance to see her. But the same ethic can produce a dangerous conclusion: therefore the present audience always outranks the performer's future body.

The semifinal dispute prevents the series from resolving this with either paternalism or romantic self-sacrifice.

Makino's best current model becomes **risk-aware co-authorship**:

- discover the idol's actual desire;
- disclose risk;
- make alternatives legible;
- build medical/logistical/staff safeguards;
- do not let managerial fear silently pre-author the life;
- remain responsible enough to intervene when necessary.

The exact threshold remains unresolved.

At the same time, the epilogue explicitly imagines idol lives extending twenty or thirty years. This retroactively qualifies every “give all your youth” slogan.

The future self is not an abstract stranger. She is another stakeholder in today's professional choices.

# 6. Relationship endpoint matrix

## 6.1 Mana / Makino

**Initial structure:** ordinary observer and exceptional performer become an asymmetric professional partnership that creates Makino's vocation.

**Tier-A movement:** intimacy, professional duty, overwork, death, ghostly continuation, second farewell, and management of living idols after Mana.

**Endpoint:** Mana remains personally foundational but no longer functions as the object around which Makino's professional life must orbit. His pride shifts from “the one who managed Mana” toward management of a plural institution.

**Open:** exact supernatural relation and later memorial practice.

## 6.2 Mana / Kotono

**Initial structure:** older-sister love is real but fails to become secure before career pressure and death.

**Tier-A movement:** grievance -> proxy-completion -> evidence that Mana loved Kotono -> farewell -> chosen idol vocation.

**Endpoint:** Kotono carries Mana without allowing sisterhood to exhaust the meaning of Tsuki or her own centerhood.

## 6.3 Mana / Sakura

**Initial structure:** biological and vocal inheritance creates the strongest substitution pressure in the work.

**Tier-A movement:** donor provenance becomes known; Sakura rejects vesselhood; Mana's ethic later returns as audience-oriented professional pressure.

**Endpoint:** provenance without jurisdiction. Mana can shape Sakura's history and ethical vocabulary without authoring Sakura's identity.

**Open:** heartbeat/miracle causality and sustainability of inherited audience ethic.

## 6.4 Sakura / Kotono

**Initial structure:** both are bound to Mana in incompatible ways, making each potentially legible to the other as theft, comparison, or judgment.

**Movement:** Mana-mediated tension becomes direct professional recognition and rivalry.

**Endpoint:** living rivalry in which neither functions primarily as proxy for Mana.

## 6.5 Saki / Chisa

**Initial structure:** reciprocal sacrifice loop; both translate love into self-limitation.

**Movement:** unit separation creates room for independent desire and peer recognition.

**Endpoint:** sisterhood survives because neither must remain the other's built-in life plan.

## 6.6 Kotono / Nagisa

**Initial structure:** devotion and accompaniment are causally central to Nagisa's idolhood and Kotono's recovery.

**Movement:** Nagisa discovers that remaining beside Kotono requires capacity to refuse Kotono.

**Endpoint:** unusually intimate chosen devotion with preserved individual agency. Romantic/yuri coding may be culturally legible, but explicit romance is **not** established by Tier-A.

## 6.7 Tsuki internal constitution

**Initial structure:** Kotono-centered asymmetry.

**Movement:** four-person autonomous growth + Kotono individual growth + accountable reunion.

**Endpoint:** disagreement is evidence of membership rather than threat to membership. Center is a role members can contest.

## 6.8 Rui / Yu / Sumire

**Initial structure:** unequal roles threatened by self-suppression, dispensability fear, and solitary leadership.

**Movement:** repeated crises expose unilateral sacrifice as unstable when beneficiaries are excluded from the decision.

**Endpoint:** three-person constitutive sisterhood/professional partnership whose inequalities remain answerable rather than erased.

## 6.9 Rui / Asakura

**Initial structure:** producer/father overlap converts professional recognition into a distorted relational test.

**Movement:** institutional competence does not substitute for paternal communication; Stellar makes family contradiction explicit.

**Endpoint:** movement toward explicit mutual recognition, not complete resolution.

## 6.10 Rio / Aoi and four-person LizNoir

**Initial structure:** Rio/Aoi dyad is genuinely constitutive, with Aoi restoring embodied expression to technically correct excellence.

**Movement:** the dyad survives but ceases to possess exclusive ownership of LizNoir identity.

**Endpoint:** Ai and Kokoro are constitutive members; the original duo remains privileged history without becoming ontological limit.

## 6.11 miho / Yō / Mana

**Initial structure:** private bereavement exists beside unequal public memorialization.

**Tier-A movement:** miho's anti-Mana project becomes visible; IIIX supplies present-tense professional life; evidence that some memory of Yō persists destabilizes simple erasure logic.

**Endpoint:** unresolved. Tier-A does **not** fully establish the later mature proposition that miho has converted grief into a publicly durable living memorial. That stronger V1 synthesis depends materially on quarantined later sources.

This is one of the most important deferrals produced by the consolidation.

## 6.12 IIIX triad

**Initial structure:** corporate coalition of incompatible motives.

**Movement:** institutional reward disappears; dissolution becomes possible; members voluntarily continue; Hoshimi affiliation does not sentimentalize them; Stellar makes collision part of stage design.

**Endpoint:** **adversarial interdependence / `腐れ縁`**. They need not call one another friends for the relationship to be chosen, durable, professionally meaningful, and creatively generative.

## 6.13 Tsuki / DoriKyun

**Initial structure:** DoriKyun offers strength as domination and elimination.

**Movement:** Tsuki absorbs useful pressure/seriousness without accepting the conclusion that losers should disappear.

**Endpoint:** competition becomes reciprocal development when victory does not claim jurisdiction over the loser's right to continue.

## 6.14 Makino / Hoshimi idols

**Initial structure:** manager authority easily risks paternalism because Makino genuinely knows more about the industry and carries trauma from Mana.

**Movement:** recruitment, unit design, Tokyo governance, BIG4 role flexibility, and Stellar health conflict successively constrain his authority.

**Endpoint:** Makino's role is not “let them do whatever they want” and not “protect them from themselves.” It is professional co-authorship whose legitimacy depends on maintaining the idol's standing while remaining accountable for material conditions.

# 8. The governing Tier-A master thesis

Historical V1 framed the franchise as **inheritance after miracle**. Tier-A confirms that framing but reveals that it is the opening case of a more general architecture.

A useful three-level model is now:

### Level 1 — inheritance

> **Can the living receive what the dead gave them without becoming copies or prisoners?**

### Level 2 — relationship and institution

> **Can people be changed, supported, recruited, managed, loved, and depended upon without another person or institution acquiring ownership of their purpose?**

### Level 3 — professional futurity

> **Can performers give themselves fully to audiences and competition while preserving the bodily, relational, and temporal conditions that allow a future self to keep choosing?**

These are not three unrelated themes. They are progressively larger versions of the same problem: **who has standing to author a life?**

The best current one-paragraph synthesis is therefore:

> **INTERPRETATION — *IDOLY PRIDE* begins with a dead miracle whose light threatens to become a command and grows into a plural professional ecosystem organized around answerable interdependence. Its characters inherit desires from the dead, from sisters, friends, rivals, audiences, managers, institutions, and one another. The work does not ask them to become self-created or self-sufficient. It asks whether those inheritances can remain revisable—whether people can receive help without becoming property, compete without erasing the defeated, remember without reproducing, and give themselves to performance without making the future self disappear from the moral field.**

This should govern the event rerank unless later evidence materially falsifies it.

# 12. Lower-tier routing rules after Tier-A freeze

The purpose of the next passes is **not** to decorate Tier-A with more examples. Lower-tier sources must be allowed to change the model when they earn the right to do so.

Every consequential event/card/bond/message/special finding should be classified as one or more of:

- **EXTENSION** — develops a dimension Tier-A establishes but does not complete;
- **STABILIZATION** — repeats a trait/state across time strongly enough to make it durable;
- **RELAPSE / RECURSION** — a supposedly resolved pressure returns in a new form;
- **REVISION** — later evidence materially changes the preferred Tier-A interpretation;
- **CONTRADICTION** — source tension cannot be reconciled without qualification;
- **TEMPORAL MATURATION** — shows a later endpoint that must not be projected backward;
- **TEXTURE** — social/voice/ordinary-life enrichment without state change;
- **FORMAL DEPENDENCY** — claim requires audiovisual/music/visual evidence;
- **CHRONOLOGY CORRECTION** — changes where material is admissible in the longitudinal model.

## 12.1 No silent backfill

If a 2025 event reveals a mature philosophy, the project must not rewrite an earlier Tokyo/BIG4 line as though that philosophy were already consciously available unless the source itself establishes continuity backward.

- best-supported diegetic position;

- retrospective targets;
- forward inheritance point;
- contradiction/revision potential;

- Haruko changing career assumptions;
- Rui/Asakura reconciling further;

but the correct operation is **temporal update**, not retroactive declaration that the earlier conflict was never real.

# 15. Final consolidation thesis

The full Tier-A spine begins with one dead girl whose extraordinary performance threatens to become the measure of everybody who survives her.

It does not end by finding a better singular measure.

It ends with:

- Sunny Peace carrying a center without making her carry everything;
- Tsuki no Tempest preserving attachment by allowing disagreement and contestable role;
- TRINITYAiLE winning because dependence becomes consciously acknowledged rather than hidden by excellence;
- LizNoir remaining proud while allowing the meaning and membership of the unit to change;
- IIIX remaining abrasive while proving that chosen interdependence need not resemble friendship;
- DoriKyun remaining outside as a rival whose truths need not authorize its cruelty;
- Makino becoming more responsible precisely by surrendering the fantasy that responsible management means authoring every safe answer himself;
- Hoshimi becoming stronger not by homogenizing its idols, but by creating infrastructure in which incompatible forms of idolhood can coexist;
- new aspirants entering because the existing idols have made desire transmissible rather than merely inherited.

The best current Tier-A definition is therefore:

> **INTERPRETATION — *IDOLY PRIDE* is the story of a miracle becoming an ecosystem. Its ethical achievement is not that everyone escapes dependence, grief, institutions, ranking, or obligation, but that these forces become increasingly answerable to the living people whose lives they shape. The dead can be remembered without owning successors; friends can be constitutive without becoming destinies; managers can hold authority without possessing purpose; rivals can make one another stronger without deciding who deserves to exist; and performers can give themselves fully to the stage only if the future person who must continue living remains part of the answer.**

That thesis is now strong enough to function as the governing Tier-A reference model.

It is **not yet the final full-series thesis**. The event, bond, card, message, special, telephone, music/3DMV, and visual layers must now be allowed to attack it.
