# Hoshimi Anime ↔ Game Expansion Audit — LizNoir Origin Addendum

## Purpose

This addendum records LizNoir-origin facts disclosed **after** the prospective anime endpoint and the Hoshimi anime/game expansion audit were frozen.

It does not change what the anime viewer knew at any episode boundary and does not silently replace the parent audit's first-pass claims.

The governing rule remains:

> **Origin disclosure may explain an earlier presentation retrospectively; it may not be projected backward as information the earlier adaptation or prospective analyst already possessed.**

The LizNoir tranche is particularly sensitive because its origin corpus continues well beyond prehistory into later two-person and four-person unit development. Four-member LizNoir is chronologically later than the anime and is therefore **not** part of the anime's frozen LizNoir state.

---

## A1. Saegusa did not leave BanPro because of Mana

**Primary sources:** `origin_liz_001_a_budding_lily`, `origin_liz_002_one_more_dream`, and especially `origin_liz_009_the_beginning_venus`.

### Added fact

Rio initially experiences Saegusa's departure through a story in which Mana has displaced LizNoir in his attention. Later institutional disclosure shows the sequence is different:

1. Saegusa had already become disillusioned with the industry/VENUS Program.
2. Rio's intensity delayed his departure long enough for him to create/debut LizNoir with Aoi.
3. He left BanPro.
4. He founded Hoshimi Production.
5. He encountered Mana only afterward.

### Revision to parent audit

Any Hoshimi/anime reading that treats Mana as the literal cause of Saegusa abandoning Rio must be corrected.

Canonical formulation:

> **Rio's abandonment grievance is psychologically operative but historically misattributed. Mana becomes the figure onto whom mentor-loss and rival-loss later converge; she did not cause the original mentor departure.**

### Guardrail

Do not use factual correction to trivialize Rio's hurt. The origin's interest is partly in how emotionally coherent narratives can become historically inaccurate under accumulated loss.

## A2. Rio's professional severity has economic/familial provenance

**Primary source:** `origin_liz_001_a_budding_lily`.

Rio enters entertainment to earn money and protect her mother/family café:

`一杯稼ぐわ`

`お母さんとあの店を守るために……`

### Cross-media significance

The anime presents Rio as an elite senior, demanding performer, and Mana rival. The origin establishes that her performance seriousness originally carried material stakes before it became intrinsic vocation.

Canonical refinement:

> **Anime-era Rio's pride is not simply elite temperament. It is the mature surface of a character who learned early to treat professional success as a way to force security into existence.**

This is retrospective explanation only. It is not anime-native disclosure.

## A3. Aoi is the original bodily counterforce to Rio's proof logic

**Primary source:** `origin_liz_001_a_budding_lily`.

Aoi's early diagnosis of Rio is explicit:

`莉央のダンスはすごくちゃんとしてる`

`でも、それだけじゃ響かないんだ`

`心と体がバラバラなんだ`

`莉央、踊ってても楽しくないんじゃない？`

### Revision to parent audit

The anime's Rio/Aoi pairing can now be read retrospectively as a very old professional/relational architecture. Aoi is not merely Rio's relaxed foil in the present. She is the person initially selected because she can return Rio's technically correct body to felt performance.

### Guardrail

Aoi is a woman; use she/her in English prose. Her use of Japanese `僕` is not evidence of male identity.

## A4. Mana changes the meaning of competition for both Rio and Aoi

**Primary source:** `origin_liz_002_one_more_dream`.

Mana is not only Rio's benchmark. She is the opponent who makes Aoi want to win and the performer through whom Rio's work becomes personally non-substitutable.

Rio preserves a fair-play floor when asked to authorize dirty tactics:

`長瀬麻奈には何もしないでください`

`LizNoirは正々堂々と、彼女と戦って勝ちます`

### Cross-media significance

The anime's senior-rival framing gains a deeper ethical history:

> **For Rio, defeating Mana had to be a performance answer because only a performance answer could resolve what Mana made Rio ask about herself.**

That is why Mana's death is uniquely destructive: the question survives while the respondent disappears.

## A5. Mana becomes a frozen rival benchmark because death removes futurity

**Primary source:** `origin_liz_002_one_more_dream`.

Rio calls Mana `運命の相手`. After Mana's death, Rio stops performing for roughly a year.

### Revision to parent audit

The anime's eventual revelation that Mana regarded Rio as a real rival should not be read simply as flattering closure. The origin shows why the information repairs something specific: Rio had been forced to live inside a rivalry whose other participant could no longer answer, lose, win, or acknowledge her.

Canonical formulation:

> **Mana's death turns rivalry from future encounter into permanent counterfactual. Later reciprocal recognition restores the fact that the rivalry had once been bilateral.**

This strengthens the Episode-11/12 distinction between the dead as judge and the dead as witness.

## A6. Rio's hostility toward Kotono partly reactivates the Mana wound

**Primary sources:** `origin_liz_003_impatience_of_hollyhock`, `origin_liz_004_love_heart`.

Aoi repeatedly reminds Rio that Kotono is not Mana but Mana's younger sister.

### Cross-media significance

The anime/Hoshimi rivalry between LizNoir and Tsuki gains a precise retrospective guardrail:

> **Kotono can trigger the unresolved Mana structure without actually being a Mana substitute.**

This matters because Rio's maturation requires recovering the ability to encounter living rivals as themselves rather than as instruments for resolving dead history.

## A7. The Tsuki defeat is a relationship hinge, not only a competitive result

**Primary source:** `origin_liz_004_love_heart`.

Aoi enters the decisive period carrying injury and concealed dissolution stakes. After defeat, Rio rejects the idea that the relationship's value has been invalidated:

`LizNoirは貴方と私`

`貴方がいたから、私はここまで来られた`

`貴方のパートナーでいられて私は幸せだった`

### Revision to parent audit

The anime's presentation of LizNoir as formidable predecessors remains valid, but the game-origin layer shows that defeat to Tsuki participates in a larger restructuring of Rio's relation to winning.

Canonical refinement:

> **The loss matters because LizNoir discovers that defeat can be survived without retroactively declaring the partnership, career, or desire meaningless.**

This gives later healthy rivalry with living units a stronger foundation.

## A8. Aoi's protection-through-secrecy adds an agency/care warning

Aoi hides both injury and decisive competitive stakes in order to protect Rio.

### Cross-media significance

This should enter the broader Hoshimi manager/care architecture as a peer-level analogue of a recurring IDOLY PRIDE problem:

> **protecting another person can become an unauthorized decision about what reality she is allowed to know.**

The motive remains loving; the method compromises shared agency.

Route to relationship/care and manager/production ethics later. Do not force an equivalence with Makino's decisions where the circumstances differ.

## A9. Four-person LizNoir is later canon and must not be backfilled into the anime

**Primary sources:** `origin_liz_005` through `origin_liz_010`.

The quartet eventually becomes a genuine mature LizNoir. That later truth must not erase the anime-era historical truth that Rio/Aoi present and understand LizNoir as a two-person unit.

The correct cross-media chronology is:

> **anime/frozen Hoshimi state: original Rio/Aoi LizNoir**  
> **later origin disclosure: dissolution crisis → Kokoro/Ai insertion → New York reconstruction → BIG4 quartet co-authorship**

The later quartet may recontextualize the durability of the LizNoir name. It may not be treated as already present in the anime's ontology.

## A10. “Smile” should not be projected backward as LizNoir's timeless missing essence

The four-person formation initially stages a conflict around `笑顔`, but New York reaches the deeper diagnosis:

`正確な動き、正しい発声。それはもちろん大切だけど`

`全てはこの胸の想いを表現するため`

`私達はロボットじゃない`

### Cross-media significance

If later quartet LizNoir is used to interpret the anime, the safe formulation is:

> **The later unit learns to subordinate technical control to communicative expression. This does not prove the anime-era duo “lacked smiles” in a simple sense.**

The anime's severe, precise performance image remains legitimate to its historical state.

## A11. Net effect on the Hoshimi audit

No frozen anime finding is overturned.

The LizNoir origins produce six principal upgrades:

1. **Saegusa chronology correction** — Mana did not cause his BanPro departure.
2. **Rio causal depth** — economic/familial duty precedes intrinsic idol vocation.
3. **Rio/Aoi dyadic provenance** — their present partnership is built on an original bodily/expression complementarity.
4. **rivalry precision** — Mana's death freezes a bilateral contest and later reciprocal recognition repairs that lost bilaterality.
5. **Tsuki defeat precision** — loss becomes survivable and therefore helps reopen future rivalry.
6. **chronology protection** — mature four-person LizNoir is later evidence and must not rewrite anime-era two-person identity.

The parent Hoshimi audit remains canonical for anime ↔ `st-original-cmn`. This file is the controlled origin-provenance layer.
