# 1. Tranche-level finding

The four stories initially appear heterogeneous: Kokoro's guilt, a LizNoir center emergency, an amateur theatre experiment, and a IIIX media-channel comedy. Read together, however, they extend the post-E2-A3 model in a coherent direction.

> **TEXTUAL FACT / INTERPRETATION — A role remains healthy when the person can answer it rather than being owned by it: guilt cannot assign permanent debt, centerhood cannot become ontological property, a dramatic role need not have one exclusive embodiment, and public persona need not require either false harmony or total exposure.**

The tranche therefore pressures four forms of excessive jurisdiction:

1. **past harm claiming the future**;
2. **prestigious office becoming permanent ownership**;
3. **pre-existing form claiming one uniquely correct performer**;
4. **public-image expectations claiming the private relationship.**

The strongest new formulation is:

> **INTERPRETATION — Answerable constitutionalism applies not only to institutions and unit membership but to roles themselves. A legitimate role can be inherited, contested, delegated, multiplied, revised, performed, or selectively disclosed without exhausting the person who occupies it.**

Preferred umbrella term:

> `ANSWERABLE_ROLE_CONSTITUTION`

This is a refinement of `ANSWERABLE_CONSTITUTIONALISM`, not a replacement.

# 2. `心愛溶けるビターチョコレート` — guilt, false debt, and future-facing repair

## 2.1 Kokoro's guilt begins from a causal overclaim

Kokoro overhears Rio and Aoi discussing an old incident from the period when LizNoir had only two members. She learns that, when Kokoro ran into Rio before the NEXT VENUS semifinal, Aoi protected Rio and sprained an ankle.

The crucial correction comes from Aoi herself:

> `言っておくけど、それは負けた理由じゃないよ`
>
> `あの日、僕はベストを尽くせた。そう思ってる`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Aoi explicitly denies that the injury caused LizNoir's defeat and states that she believes she performed her best.

Kokoro nevertheless constructs a stronger moral-causal story: she harmed Aoi, therefore she may have caused LizNoir's defeat, therefore she owes the seniors a compensatory success.

Preferred term:

> `GUILT_CAUSAL_OVERREACH`

Definition:

> A person converts a real contribution to harm into a broader causal or moral responsibility not assigned by the harmed party or established by the evidence.

This does **not** mean Kokoro bears no responsibility for the collision. The overreach lies in expanding a real mistake into ownership of LizNoir's loss and future restitution.

## 2.2 Benevolent concealment can produce distorted self-blame

Rio and Aoi had deliberately avoided telling Ai and Kokoro about the injury because they correctly anticipated that the juniors would feel guilty.

Aoi explains:

> `話したら、そんな風に負い目を感じるからさ`
>
> `僕も莉央も、そんなこと望んでない`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the seniors' concealment was protective rather than exploitative.

**INTERPRETATION:** E2-B1 nevertheless supplies another example of benevolent information control failing to guarantee the intended outcome. Kokoro encounters the fact accidentally, without the contextual explanation that the seniors would have supplied, and fills the missing causal space with self-accusation.

This does not justify universal disclosure. It supports a narrower rule:

> **Protective concealment carries epistemic risk: the person being protected may later encounter the fact without the interpretive context that originally motivated concealment.**

## 2.3 Guilt becomes solitary reparative overwork

Kokoro responds not by discussing the injury with the seniors but by attempting to create a new LizNoir Valentine song and event success by herself. She studies roughly one hundred borrowed performance discs plus online material, fills multiple notebooks, continues ordinary lessons, collapses asleep after finishing the first proposal, and repeatedly refuses help.

Her sleeping apology makes the hidden motive explicit:

> `莉央さん、葵さん……`
>
> `ごめんなさい……`

After the first proposal is rejected, she breaks down:

> `ダメだった……役に立てなかった……`
>
> `私、迷惑掛けてばかりで……`
>
> `ごめんなさい、ごめんなさい……！`

Later:

> `葵さんに怪我させたこと、償いたかったのに……`

Preferred term:

> `UNILATERAL_REPARATIVE_OVERWORK`

Definition:

> Self-imposed labor intended to repay a debt that the purported creditor has not demanded and may explicitly reject.

This is a strong `OPEN-05` data point: devotion, guilt, and freely chosen effort can become harmful even without an exploitative manager issuing the demand.

## 2.4 Kokoro also tries to monopolize guilt to protect Ai

Kokoro refuses to tell Ai because she regards herself, not Ai, as the person who physically caused the accident:

> `でもぶつかったのはこころで、愛は関係ないから……`

Aoi correctly anticipates that Kokoro is withholding the truth because she does not want Ai to inherit the same burden.

**INTERPRETATION:** Kokoro reproduces, at a smaller scale, the seniors' protective concealment: she tries to spare someone she loves by assigning herself exclusive responsibility.

Preferred term:

> `PROTECTIVE_GUILT_MONOPOLIZATION`

The motive is care; the cost is isolation and reduced relational answerability.

## 2.5 Ai refuses both abandonment and simple absolution

When the seniors consider simply telling Kokoro that they do not blame her, Ai asks for one more chance to reach her first:

> `二人がそう言ってくれたら`
>
> `こころはきっと安心します`
>
> `でも、それじゃこころも私も前に進めない`

Ai's solution is not to deny the past but to reorient its jurisdiction:

> `過ぎたことを、一人で悔やむんじゃなくて`
>
> `未来のことを、みんなで話し合おうよ`

Aoi supplies the parallel senior formulation:

> `過去の出来事のために、こころに頑張ってほしくない`
>
> `こころには、そんなことよりも`
>
> `未来のために、自分のために頑張ってほしいんだ`

Preferred terms:

- `FUTURE_FACING_REPAIR`
- `REPAIR_WITHOUT_DEBT_OWNERSHIP`

Governing rule:

> **Past harm may create reasons for apology, learning, care, and changed practice without giving the past permanent jurisdiction over the actor's future labor.**

## 2.6 The successful song emerges only after guilt loses sovereign status

Kokoro's first proposal is enormous but thematically empty. Aoi and Rio criticize it because the abundance of reference material cannot substitute for a clear governing image. After the relational confrontation, a mundane act of care—Rio sending Kokoro her favorite pudding—helps prompt the new concept: LizNoir's Valentine does not need to be simply sweet; it can be bitter as well.

Kokoro formulates:

> `甘いだけが正義じゃない`
>
> `ほろ苦いバレンタインでもいいんだってことです！`

The successful second proposal is only one page, but Rio says its intention is clear and the composer reports strong confidence in the song.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the story contrasts guilt-saturated accumulation with a simpler authored concept that becomes available after Kokoro stops treating the project as atonement. The text does not explicitly state that guilt was the technical cause of the first proposal's failure, so that causal reading should remain inference rather than fact.

## 2.7 Four-person LizNoir is reaffirmed as a future, not a debt settlement

At the end, Kokoro and Ai apologize. Aoi and Rio refuse the relationship grammar of continuing indebtedness:

> `謝るなんて水臭いのはナシだよ`

Rio then reframes the juniors as creators of something the founders could not do:

> `貴方達は、LizNoirの新境地を切り開いてくれた`
>
> `それは、私達には出来なかったこと`

Aoi:

> `君達との出会いには感謝してるんだ`
>
> `後悔したことなんて、ない`

This strongly corroborates the E2-A2 model of `CONSTITUTIVE_SUCCESSION` and `RECIPROCAL_SUCCESSION`.

# 4. `旗揚げ！劇団★見～二人のアリスとWONDERLAND～` — performed role, plural fit, and adaptive form

## 4.1 Makino's first response is neither prohibition nor immediate external exposure

Shizuku asks Makino for stage-acting work after seeing TRINITYAiLE's production. His first answer is that a novice with no acting experience cannot easily be placed directly into professional stage work.

Shizuku then articulates why live theatre matters to her:

> `映像とは違う、生の迫力……キャスト、スタッフ`
>
> `お客さんが一体となって作り上げる芸術……`
>
> `同じ公演でも、同じ演技は二度と出来ない……`
>
> `一期一会のエンタ―テインメント、私も挑戦してみたい`

Makino responds with an intermediate route: build a theatre segment inside a Sunny Peace live rather than assign her immediately to an external acting job.

Preferred term:

> `SCAFFOLDED_PROFESSIONAL_EXPERIMENTATION`

Definition:

> Create a bounded professional trial that respects a new aspiration without pretending the performer already possesses the experience required for the highest-risk or most externally accountable version of the work.

This is compatible with `risk-aware co-authorship`.

## 4.2 Shizuku and Suzu embody the same role differently

Both Shizuku and Suzu insist on playing Alice. During rehearsal, Shizuku recognizes Suzu's strengths: large voice, direct emotionality, confidence, and vivid physical presence. After both perform the audition scene, Shizuku tries to withdraw because she believes Suzu fits Alice better.

Suzu refuses that unilateral ranking. She identifies Shizuku's different strengths:

> `雫のよく通る綺麗な声`
>
> `繊細で心のこもった自然な雫の演技……私は好きですわ`

And:

> `雫の声と表情には、人を惹きつける魅力がありますわ`

Preferred terms:

- `PLURAL_ROLE_FIT`
- `RECIPROCAL_ABILITY_RECOGNITION`
- `COMPARATIVE_SELF_DISQUALIFICATION`

Governing rule:

> **Two performers can be genuinely different and both be valid embodiments of the same role. Difference does not require a single hierarchy unless the production form truly requires one.**

## 4.3 Production form adapts to performers

Yu, Sakura, and Kotono judge the two audition performances too different and too compelling for a clean ranking. Rather than force a winner, the group rewrites the production around two Alices.

Yu explains:

> `どっちのアリスも魅力的で甲乙付けがたかったんよ`
>
> `それでみんなで相談して「主役を二人にしてまえ」`

Preferred terms:

- `FORM_ADAPTATION_TO_PERFORMERS`
- `DUAL_PROTAGONISM`
- `NON_ZERO_SUM_CASTING`

This is an important qualification to merit-based contestability. Sometimes the correct response to plural excellence is not to sharpen the ranking procedure but to change the form so that the valuable difference can remain present.

This does **not** mean every office or role can be multiplied. It is a production-specific example of structural adaptation.

## 4.4 Performance can mediate real relational truth without becoming literal confession

The play makes the two Alices friends and partners. Suzu's scripted Alice/Cheshire-Cat dialogue includes:

> `だって雫は……私の大事な友達なのですわ！`

Because this is scripted performance, it is **not** admissible as a simple direct offstage confession.

However, the event's framing before and after the play establishes that Shizuku and Suzu are genuine friends and rivals, that the audition makes them recognize one another's strengths more explicitly, and that Kotono observes after the performance that the two appear to have become closer.

Preferred term:

> `ROLE_MEDIATED_RELATIONAL_TRUTH`

Definition:

> A performed role may provide form for a real relationship, allowing participants to rehearse, express, or deepen truths that are not reducible either to "mere acting" or to a literal one-to-one confession.

This directly extends the corpus's authenticity model: performance and construction are not opposites of truth.

## 4.5 Theatre is framed as co-produced liveness

Shizuku's own reason for wanting theatre identifies a formal principle:

> cast + staff + audience jointly produce an event whose exact performance cannot be repeated.

Preferred term:

> `CO_PRODUCED_LIVENESS`

This should be routed later to the performance/audiovisual synthesis but is admitted here as a textual performance philosophy.

# 5. `新春万福　素顔の晒し合いGAME` — IIIX, strategic authenticity, and trust without friendship label

## 5.1 IIIX explicitly admits its prior harmony image was constructed

>
> `私達も、対外的にはそのように見せてきたけど`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** IIIX has publicly presented itself as friendlier than its members understand their private relationship to be because that image was thought to improve favorability.

This is direct evidence for the persona/public-image problem under `OPEN-16`.

## 5.2 Their first attempt at "ordinary selves" is itself strategic and imitative

The group studies Hoshimi's success with `等身大の魅力` and initially considers using an official channel to show apparently ordinary off-duty life in order to win fans from rival Hoshimi units.

Preferred term:

> `STRATEGIC_AUTHENTICITY`

Definition:

> Truthful self-disclosure can be selected for competitive or commercial reasons without thereby becoming false; the ethical and analytical question shifts to what is selected, omitted, staged, and controlled.

# 6. Cross-event synthesis

## 6.1 Past harm and role are parallel jurisdiction problems

Kokoro's guilt and Rio's centerhood initially seem unrelated. Structurally, both concern whether an important fact can become permanent ownership.

- Kokoro really did contribute to Aoi's injury, but that does not grant the past ownership of all future labor.
- Rio really is LizNoir's established center, but that does not grant her ontological ownership of the office.

The tranche therefore strengthens a general rule:

> **FACTUAL OR HISTORICAL PROVENANCE DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY CREATE PERMANENT JURISDICTION.**

This aligns with prior Mana, inheritance, and family-lineage findings.

## 6.2 Competition can expose distinct value without requiring elimination

The tranche contains two different competitive resolutions:

- LizNoir preserves a singular center office but makes it contestable and delegable.
- Theatre discovers that the production does not actually require one Alice and changes the form to preserve two distinct strengths.

This distinction matters.

> **Some responsibilities require a singular office and should be contestable; other apparent zero-sum competitions are artifacts of a form that can itself be revised.**

Preferred distinction:

- `CONTESTABLE_SINGULAR_OFFICE`
- `ADAPTABLE_PLURAL_ROLE`

Do not collapse the two.

## 6.3 Performance is a technology of truth, not its opposite

E2-B1 supplies three different cases:

- Kokoro's forced "idol smile" fails to conceal distress from people who know her.
- Shizuku and Suzu use fictional roles to make real mutual recognition and closeness more legible.
- IIIX's ordinary public harmony image is less truthful than their deliberately recorded competitive banter, even though the latter is still edited and strategically deployed.

Preferred generalization:

> **The truth value of performance depends less on whether it is constructed than on whether construction remains answerable to the people, relationships, and realities it claims to represent.**

This substantially advances `OPEN-16`.

## 6.4 Strong bonds need not share one vocabulary

Ai and Kokoro directly use `親友`.

Shizuku and Suzu use friendship and rivalry vocabulary, with scripted performance helping deepen the relation.

IIIX explicitly names trust while denying friendship.

The corpus therefore should not normalize all durable idol bonds into one sentimental category. Relationship ontology remains plural.
