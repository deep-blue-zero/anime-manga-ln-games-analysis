# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — TOKYO MAIN STORY PRIMARY FINDINGS FREEZE

## 0. Freeze statement and integrity disclosure

This document records the source-native V2 findings from all fourteen Tokyo main-story bundles **before historical/V1 Tokyo analytical prose is consulted**.

It is not labeled `PRISTINE`.

During preparation for Tokyo, all four IIIX origin bundles had been read under the mistaken assumption that their entire endpoint preceded `tokyo_001`. While reading Tokyo, the chronology problem became visible: `origin_thrx_003` and `origin_thrx_004` contain I-UNITY-concurrent and post-I-UNITY material.

That error was corrected **before this Tokyo freeze** through:

- `IDOLY_PRIDE_V2_PHASE1B_IIIX_ORIGIN_CHRONOLOGY_CORRECTION_AND_TOKYO_GATE_REVISION.md`;
- `IDOLY_PRIDE_V2_PHASE1B_IIIX_PRE_TOKYO_BASELINE_v1.1.md`.

The Tokyo blocks were then reconstructed sequentially using only:

1. already-frozen non-Tokyo V2 state;
2. corrected IIIX baseline v1.1;
3. earlier Tokyo blocks.

The remaining contamination is epistemic rather than textual: the analyst has seen some later IIIX primary-source outcomes before processing their Tokyo placement. The mitigation is explicit temporal quarantine and claim-by-claim reconstruction.

Therefore:

> **Prospective integrity: `CONTROLLED_RECONSTRUCTION_WITH_DISCLOSED_PRIMARY_SOURCE_HINDSIGHT`.**

No historical/V1 Tokyo analysis has been opened for the findings below. No BIG4 or Stellar main-story hindsight is admitted.

# 2. Source-native executive thesis

Tokyo is the first post-anime arc in which the franchise turns the intimate problem of **self-authorship** into an institutional problem.

The anime and Hoshimi origins repeatedly ask whether a person can inherit love, talent, grief, family obligation, or professional expectation without allowing those things to author her life.

Tokyo asks the same question at a larger scale:

> **Who is allowed to author an idol's future when agencies, producers, rankings, media systems, family ties, rivalries, fandoms, and commercial institutions all possess real power over the conditions in which she works?**

The arc's strongest answer is not anti-institutional individualism. Hoshimi succeeds precisely because institutions, managers, rival units, staff, and fans can create options that isolated individuals do not possess.

The governing distinction is instead:

> **Legitimate structure enlarges authored choice; illegitimate structure converts people into instruments of somebody else's design.**

Makino's explicit management ethic crystallizes this:

`アイドルは事務所の道具じゃない`

`むしろアイドルが事務所を利用して輝いてくれればいい`

Tokyo therefore develops a political economy of idolhood without abandoning the emotional grammar established in the anime.

A second thesis concerns competition:

> **Tokyo does not reject winning. It rejects the sovereignty of winning.**

Competition can generate skill, self-knowledge, joy, rivalry, and performances that would not exist without opposition. But winning ceases to be legitimate when institutional manipulation substitutes for the encounter the score is supposed to measure.

A third thesis concerns idol ontology:

> **There is no single final answer to `アイドルとは何か`. The arc treats idolhood as a relational practice whose meanings are authored through performance, struggle, audience reciprocity, and chosen commitments rather than imposed by one metric or authority.**

SUNNY PEACE provides Tokyo's most developed positive formulation:

> **Idol performance is a recursive circulation of recognition: receive the audience's feelings, return them through song, receive them again, and convert the accumulating pieces of performer and spectator into expanding bonds.**

Finally, Tokyo advances the Mana problem from inheritance to historical supersession:

> **Mana can remain causally present without remaining the horizon of the living.**

The finale explicitly states that SUNNY PEACE has gone beyond Mana, while LizNoir gains a living rival and Miho's attempt to erase Mana through institutional control is displaced by a new legend generated through performance.

# 3. Sequential block ledger

## 3.1 `tokyo_001_new_wind` — success creates a new burden of authorship

The arc begins several months after the NEXT VENUS Grand Prix. Hoshimi has relocated to Tokyo because increased work has made the old operating environment insufficient.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Saegusa immediately leaves day-to-day control to Makino:

`悪い、後のことはしばらくお前に任せる`

and warns:

`今後、大きな事件が降りかかってくるかもしれない`

`でもお前なら、事務所を守れるはずだ`

Makino is therefore not merely inheriting Mana's personal dream. He is receiving **institutional responsibility** from a living senior professional.

Yet Makino privately remains insecure and asks the absent Mana what he should do.

Sakura and Kotono reverse the direction of care: the manager is allowed to rely on the idols. This continues the Hoshimi game's bidirectional management ethic.

Makino remembers Mana's wish that he become a top-idol manager. The correct source-native reading is **re-authorization**, not renewed posthumous jurisdiction. Mana supplies a remembered horizon; Makino chooses to carry it through responsibility to living idols.

Kotono similarly states:

`私が長瀬麻奈の妹なのは、事実`

`でも、だからこそ私は、私にしかなれないアイドルになるつもり`

The baton metaphor survives without substitution. The past can be carried because the runner is not required to become the previous runner.

**Prospective open questions after 001:**

- Why did Saegusa leave so abruptly?
- What institutional threat did he anticipate?
- Can Makino actually operate without using Mana/Saegusa as invisible authorities?
- How will increased solo work affect unit cohesion?

## 3.2 `tokyo_002_stray_wings` — the first institutional abandonment may be protective

TRINITYAiLE is abruptly terminated by BanPro/Asakura and approaches Hoshimi.

Rui experiences the act through the father/daughter wound: a second abandonment.

The origin baseline matters here because Rui's vocation predates Asakura. His action therefore threatens her **recognition structure**, not the existence of her idol desire.

Makino confronts Asakura. Asakura has preserved jobs and arranged noninterference, then asks Hoshimi to:

`TRINITYAiLEの……瑠依の進むべき道を示してやって欲しい`

At this block's endpoint, the strongest justified inference is **protective transfer under concealed crisis**. The exact crisis remains unresolved.

TRINITY chooses Hoshimi largely because SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest are nearby rivals. This prevents sentimentalizing the transfer.

Rui studies Hoshimi's repertoire intensely and collapses from overwork/stress. Yu emphasizes that Rui's apparent genius rests on extreme labor.

**Block delta:** Hoshimi begins to become not merely Mana's agency but a **refuge/infrastructure for displaced professional agency**.

## 3.3 `tokyo_003_shiny_melody` — continuity can itself be developmental

SUNNY PEACE receives an opportunity to create a new song. Increased solo work has reduced shared time, and Sakura interprets that dispersal as possible emotional drift.

Sakura begins performing the role of the strong center rather than admitting fear.

Makino realizes that he has partly idealized her as the group's strongest smiling person. He tells her that she may cry.

This is not a new lesson in V2 chronology. Sakura's origin already established Tomo giving her permission to cry. Tokyo therefore shows **recurrence under new professional pressure**: an old defensive habit can return even after earlier growth.

The group's return to ordinary shared life restores mutual disclosure. All five had been concealing similar insecurity.

The key philosophy is:

`変わらずにいることで……自分のスタイルを貫くことで生まれる成長もある`

and:

`今まで通りのスタイルで、だけど新しいものも取り入れて、今まで以上のものを作り出す`

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Tokyo rejects a simple modernist equation of development with visible rupture. **Continuity can be an authored mode of change.**

This becomes important later when SUNNY PEACE asks whether its apparently familiar affect actually counts as growth.

## 3.4 `tokyo_004_wear_feathers` — effective intervention is not automatically ethical intervention

Tsuki no Tempest gains a prestige opportunity and spirals into perfectionism while comparing itself to LizNoir.

Rui temporarily coaches them and diagnoses:

`完璧を目指し過ぎて`

`失敗を恐れ過ぎて`

`以前の自由さを忘れた`

She deliberately applies unusual pressure, including a false punitive frame, in order to break the pattern.

The intervention works.

But Rui also tells Makino this is a job he cannot perform because he is `優し過ぎる`.

The correct V2 conclusion is not that Rui proves harsher management ethically superior. The text establishes **efficacy**; it leaves a real informed-consent/manipulation problem.

TRINITY's members mediate the encounter, and Tsuki eventually recovers the fan-facing pleasure of the stage.

The block ends with Asakura's arrest for alleged embezzlement, transforming the private question of parental/professional abandonment into a public institutional crisis.

## 3.5 `tokyo_005_black_impact` — Hoshimi becomes an agency chosen for its governance ethic

Rui wants the truth both as an idol and as Asakura's daughter.

LizNoir returns from America after BanPro also terminates them, strongly reinforcing the inference that Asakura intentionally removed elite units before the crisis landed.

Rio and Rui re-evaluate the accusation: the Asakura they know invested heavily and preserved professional opportunities rather than behaving like a simple embezzler.

Asakura's letter asks Makino to continue the idols' careers and says:

`アイドル業界を守れるのはそこにいる四グループだけかもしれない`

`勝手な話だが、君に託す`

`どうか、娘を頼む`

The baton motif now expands beyond death. **Living professionals can transfer responsibility without transferring authorship.**

Most important is Makino's explicit agency philosophy:

`どんな仕事でも内容を正確に伝える`

`君達の意思を尊重する`

`アイドルは事務所の道具じゃない`

`むしろアイドルが事務所を利用して輝いてくれればいい`

This is one of the most load-bearing institutional statements in the V2 corpus so far.

LizNoir's later decision to remain with Hoshimi cannot therefore be reduced to convenience. Hoshimi increasingly represents a **governance model**.

## 3.6 `tokyo_006_farewell_and_oath` — care becomes unethical when it confiscates the beneficiary's decision

Himeno appears as Preta Porte's producer and treats agency business as an adult domain in which the idols themselves need not have standing.

Makino, Aoi, and Yu reject that separation because the idols are the people who must stand on the stage.

Himeno demands that TRINITY/LizNoir move. She claims Asakura's embezzlement is real and presents her own actions as protection, but the block leaves her narrative strategically incomplete.

Hoshimi is institutionally weak relative to Preta Porte's media power. Makino explicitly recognizes this asymmetry.

Rui then tries to transfer alone in order to prevent scandal around her father from damaging TRINITY and Hoshimi.

Yu and Sumire refuse unilateral protection. If Rui chooses a route framed publicly as betrayal, they will share it because:

`私達三人でTRINITYAiLE`

This extends the origin's anti-self-reduction ethic into **anti-unilateral-sacrifice**.

Himeno creates a fait accompli press conference before an ordinary consensual contract process has concluded.

TRINITY's public message nevertheless preserves self-authorship inside coercion:

`私達は私達の歌でファンにエールを送り続けたい`

`他のアイドルのみなさんと切磋琢磨して、より輝きたい`

`事務所は関係ありません`

The agency can control formal affiliation; it does not thereby own the meaning TRINITY gives its idol work.

## 3.7 `tokyo_007_struggle_for_idols` — political resistance and intrinsic vocation coexist

Hoshimi receives TRINITY's departure as a sacrifice but refuses to erase the meaning the trio chose for it.

Sakura compares externally imposed constraints to her own childhood illness: circumstance can constrain future action without acquiring the right to define the self.

Kotono offers an important refinement of sacrificial care. She says she could sacrifice herself for Sakura or the others in the same situation, but she would first search for a route that avoids separation.

This does not abolish sacrifice. It places an ethical burden on the actor to **expand the option set before surrendering the self**.

Rio then makes a strongly authored transfer to Hoshimi. Where she once treated agencies mainly as containers that provide a stage, she now chooses Hoshimi because it allows her to seek the top `正々堂々`.

The arc then moves to I.E.A./I-UNITY. Himeno's industry-recovery argument is not absurd: public trust has been damaged. But the procedural structure shows capture risk—advance coalition building, concentration of authority, and Hoshimi as principal dissent.

Makino says:

`君達アイドルが輝くための場所が誰かの考え一つで支配されていい訳がない`

`姫野さんのやり方じゃ、アイドルもファンも本当に笑えない`

Hoshimi chooses to participate both to resist capture and because the idols genuinely want to sing for fans.

**Block thesis:** political resistance does not replace intrinsic vocation; it becomes one condition for protecting the space in which vocation can remain authored.

## 3.8 `tokyo_008_enjoy_just_purely` — SUNNY PEACE discovers that familiar affect must become legible as professional strength

Hoshimi selects its I-UNITY seed through peer voting rather than unilateral management assignment.

IIIX's technical scale shocks the Hoshimi idols.

fran asks Sakura/Kotono what exists **beyond top idol**, exposing that the title itself may be an instrumental milestone rather than final telos.

Sakura worries that SUNNY PEACE has not grown because its most recognizable strength still resembles what it always was.

Kotono reminds her that Sakura did not begin idolhood in order to win. Rio says SUNNY PEACE's strength occupies a domain not reducible to victory/defeat.

Sakura narrates her path with the phrase:

`麻奈さんの心臓に導かれて`

This modestly reopens the heart-language ambiguity left by the anime. V2 should not treat the line as full regression. The causal vocabulary persists, but Sakura's current choices remain self-authored.

The group's key development is decentering light:

`私達みんなが、みんなを照らす……太陽になる時`

This directly strengthens the origin-derived thesis that SUNNY PEACE makes light **shareable rather than monopolized by one center**.

Their peers vote SUNNY the seed precisely because its heartfelt enjoyment and sun-like affect constitute an inimitable professional strength.

## 3.9 `tokyo_009_no_gray` — rivalry is co-production, and loss does not invalidate the self

The tournament draw forces TRINITYAiLE and LizNoir into an early match.

Both groups treat the encounter as something desired rather than narratively tragic. The rivalry produces a "big bang" effect anticipated by Asakura.

Himeno attempts to instrumentalize TRINITY, but Rui states:

`私達は、私達の意志と力だけでリズノワに勝います`

TRINITY wins narrowly after both units exceed prior levels.

LizNoir's response is critical. Ai and Kokoro experience the actual pain of losing; Rio refuses scapegoating:

`誰かのせいじゃない`

Aoi says the stage itself became so enjoyable that the political objective of blocking Himeno receded during the performance.

Rio states:

`貴方達とリズノワでいられて、心から嬉しい`

`アイドルでいられて……幸せ`

This strongly confirms the LizNoir origin thesis: competition is valuable because excellence becomes a medium for **human desire and relation**, not because the ranking outcome is metaphysically sovereign.

TRINITY's post-win loneliness creates a second problem. They chose their path, but the path isolates them from the people with whom they want to share the victory.

Makino respects the boundary rather than intruding. The open ethical tension is whether respecting chosen isolation can become passive enabling of a harmful sacrifice.

## 3.10 `tokyo_010_cold_diamond` — IIIX's conflict becomes concrete memory politics

Himeno orders TRINITY to throw its match against IIIX.

TRINITY refuses because a top achieved without real performance has no meaning and because match-fixing betrays fans.

They confront IIIX.

IIIX knows enough about Himeno's operations to become morally implicated. Miho's project also becomes concrete:

`長瀬麻奈を抹殺します`

The goal is not literal physical harm; it is symbolic/archival erasure through control over songs, rights, records, and cultural memory.

fran and kana do not share Miho's grief logic, but they can see career value in surpassing or eliminating the authority of an old legend.

This is the point at which the corrected pre-Tokyo baseline earns the escalation from "surpass records/traces" to a concrete **memory-governance project**.

IIIX is therefore not simply Himeno's puppet. Its private aims partially align with the structure she offers.

But TRINITY's refusal also establishes that elite professional ambition does not require accepting every available advantage.

The block ends with a fabricated Rui scandal and TRINITY's removal from competition.

## 3.11 `tokyo_011_moonlight_in_our_hands` — the right to decide whether one is a burden belongs to the supposedly burdened people too

Rui feels she has lost the top, the future, and the ability to share a stage with Hoshimi.

Makino responds with one of Tokyo's strongest anti-proxy lines:

`迷惑がかかるかどうかを決めるのは、君じゃない`

`星見プロのアイドル達だ`

This is not merely emotional reassurance. It is a theory of consent: **Rui cannot unilaterally decide what costs other people are willing to accept on her behalf.**

When she still cannot return as an idol, Makino offers a temporary coaching role rather than forcing a binary return/exit decision.

This is a major example of his managerial maturation:

> **good management enlarges choice-space rather than replacing the chooser.**

TRINITY coaches Tsuki, and the group receives them with `おかえりなさい`.

IIIX's moral logic becomes explicit when Miho says:

`私達三人は、恥じるようなことは何もしてない`

`ステージの上で、正々堂々、実力で勝負している`

`姫野さんが何をしようと、あの人の勝手`

This is not innocence. It is **compartmentalized responsibility**: IIIX treats the fairness of its direct performance as separable from corruption of the environment around the performance.

Tokyo's ethical challenge is whether that boundary is sustainable.

Kotono, meanwhile, recognizes:

`私にとってのアイドル活動には、もう、お姉ちゃんはあまり関係なくなっている`

and:

`お姉ちゃんじゃなく、ちゃんとファンと向き合おう`

This is one of the clearest post-anime confirmations that Mana has ceased to hold jurisdiction over Kotono's vocation.

## 3.12 `tokyo_012_what_is_idol` — defeat becomes knowledge rather than invalidation

IIIX narrowly defeats Tsuki after both groups perform at full strength.

Kana is dissatisfied because mere victory is not enough; she wants decisive proof. fran and Miho recognize that the opponent forced IIIX to reach higher.

The result therefore already contains a contradiction inside IIIX's victory sovereignty: the defeated opponent becomes a **co-producer of the victor's excellence**.

Tsuki experiences major defeat from the other side.

Kotono asks whether the opponents they defeated felt this same pain.

Rio rejects retrospective apology. Winners owe the defeated something other than pity: they must continue to carry the result forward. Losers must feel the pain honestly and then convert it into future action.

Aoi says:

`僕達アイドルのステージには『絶対』なんてない`

This is not anti-competition relativism. It is a denial that any result permanently settles personhood or future possibility.

Kotono therefore does not answer "what is an idol?" by winning. She learns that **loss itself can become capital**.

Saegusa then re-enters and reveals that he has been gathering evidence against the crisis around Asakura/Himeno. The earlier disappearance is recontextualized as covert institutional work rather than abandonment.

SUNNY PEACE enters the next phase still lacking its own sufficiently articulated answer.

## 3.13 `tokyo_013_may_the_sunshine_be_with_you` — SUNNY PEACE converts affect into a theory of reciprocal performance

SUNNY PEACE's answer is more specific than "we enjoy ourselves" or "we make fans happy."

They articulate a circulation:

`ファンの想いを受け止めて、歌に込めて返す`

and describe gathering the five members' pieces together with each audience member's pieces and converting them into bonds that spread outward.

The strongest formulation is:

> **SUNNY PEACE understands idol performance as recursive recognition: audience feeling enters the performance, the idols transform and return it, and the returned performance generates further audience feeling.**

This corrects any one-way "sun gives light to passive audience" model.

The group also refuses an anti-technical romanticism. Feeling and bonds require songs, costumes, training, and professional execution. Material form is the **vehicle of relation**.

SUNNY wins its semifinal.

Kotono sees Sakura becoming distant:

`あっという間に……遠くに`

This is left unresolved for later longitudinal work.

Before the final, IIIX gives Hoshimi enough information to limit sabotage opportunities. Makino/Rui/Kotono interpret it as `ⅢXなりの仁義`.

The correct finding is narrow:

> IIIX remains a willing beneficiary of Himeno's captured environment, but it also wants the **actual onstage comparison** to remain its own achievement.

The final sabotage cuts SUNNY's sound.

Sakura responds by beginning an a cappella `SUNNY PEACE HARMONY`, while the members move into the audience and ask fans to sing.

This is philosophically important because the doctrine becomes operational under deprivation. Their fan/idol reciprocity is not decorative rhetoric; it is a **resilience mechanism**.

Other units handle institutional sabotage so Makino can remain near the performers. Success is radically plural: no single savior owns the resolution.

## 3.14 `tokyo_014_with_beyond_the_miracle` — the living surpass the legend without erasing the dead

During the crisis, Makino experiences an unknown reassuring voice; Sakura and Kotono later report a similar Mana-like perception.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** multiple characters experience a voice-like reassurance associated with Mana.

**NOT TEXTUAL FACT:** Mana objectively returns as an independently verified ghost at this point.

Makino believes it was Mana. The ambiguity should remain bounded.

More important is what the voice does **not** do. It does not choreograph the solution, choose the song, defeat Himeno, or author SUNNY's philosophy. Living people perform those actions.

Makino then says to Mana:

`さくらは……サニーピースは`

`お前の、その先に行ったよ`

This is an unusually explicit **beyond-Mana** statement.

Rio and Aoi likewise recognize that SUNNY differs from the dead Mana as benchmark because SUNNY remains present:

`もう一生超えることの出来ない壁とは違う`

`今なお目の前に存在してくれている、ライバル`

Living rivalry restores temporality. A dead benchmark can freeze a career; a living rival can answer back, change, lose, win, and be met again.

IIIX also gives a precise judgment. Under ordinary repeatable conditions they believe they would beat SUNNY, but fran admits:

`でも、今、この瞬間だけは……サニーピースが、私達の上をいった`

The arc therefore refuses both metric nihilism and metric absolutism. SUNNY's victory is exceptional because a **singular relational event** becomes professionally real even if IIIX believes its average repeatable technical model remains stronger.

Miho says:

`私達が塗り替えようとしていた長瀬麻奈の伝説を……彼女達が塗り替えた`

This is one of Tokyo's decisive symbolic reversals.

Miho sought to correct public memory through suppression and overwrite. SUNNY changes the historical hierarchy by **creating a new performance worthy of memory**.

Yet IIIX does not become post-victory enlightened here. It explicitly restates:

`勝てなければ私達三人が一緒にいる意味なんて、ない`

`私達が共有してるものなんて、たった一つだけ 勝利しかない`

This confirms the chronology correction: the later voluntary "second founding" belongs after this Tokyo endpoint.

One month later, Saegusa's evidence demonstrates that Himeno forged the Asakura accounting case and manipulated the final environment. Himeno is arrested; Asakura is released; I-UNITY results are preserved as legitimate after investigation.

TRINITYAiLE and LizNoir choose to remain with Hoshimi after consultation with BanPro/Asakura/the members. Hoshimi is therefore no longer merely a temporary refuge; it becomes a **chosen institutional home**.

IIIX refuses the awards ceremony because second place has no value to them. Makino probes whether refusal is partly flight from SUNNY. The hunger remains unresolved.

The Mana-voice ambiguity is discussed without restoring her as governing authority.

The arc closes with Makino praying for the future of the living idols and with Saegusa speaking to an unknown ally who may later become an opponent.

# 4. Makino — from inheritor to institutional fiduciary

Tokyo is the strongest Makino material since the anime because his manager philosophy becomes explicit under institutional pressure.

The developmental sequence is:

1. **001:** insecurity after Saegusa disappears; Makino still seeks imagined guidance from Mana;
2. **002–005:** he becomes the professional node through which displaced units can retain options;
3. **005:** he explicitly states that idols are not agency tools;
4. **006–007:** he defends idols' standing in decisions that formally concern agencies/businesses;
5. **011:** he refuses Rui's right to decide unilaterally whether her existence burdens other people and creates a coaching path rather than a binary demand;
6. **013:** he allows other trusted people to solve sabotage while remaining near SUNNY, showing that responsible leadership is not omnipresent control;
7. **014:** he interprets Mana's dream as fulfilled through living successors going beyond her rather than by reproducing her.

Preferred V2 formulation:

> **Makino's authority matures into fiduciary authority: he has real professional power, but its legitimacy depends on using that power to widen the idols' capacity to choose, perform, and revise their own futures.**

This is not laissez-faire management. He intervenes, negotiates, shelters, delegates, and sometimes refuses an idol's self-description. The distinction is whose agency the intervention is organized to preserve.

# 5. SUNNY PEACE — from shared light to recursive public relation

The origin tranche established that SUNNY PEACE makes light shareable without making identity interchangeable.

Tokyo adds a second layer:

> **shared light becomes a public circuit.**

The group first confronts the fear that increased individual success is relational fragmentation. It then confronts the fear that maintaining its familiar affect means failing to develop. Finally it translates that affect into an explicit professional doctrine.

The decisive movement is:

- Sakura used to illuminate others;
- the members learn to illuminate Sakura;
- all five become suns to one another;
- fans enter the circuit as active contributors rather than passive recipients;
- the I-UNITY failure state proves the circuit can function when ordinary production infrastructure breaks.

The strongest Tokyo SUNNY thesis is therefore:

> **SUNNY PEACE's “sun” is not unilateral charisma. It is a distributed system that converts mutual recognition among members and fans into performative resilience.**

This also explains why the final can exceed IIIX in a singular moment without requiring the text to declare SUNNY technically superior under all repeatable conditions.

# 6. Tsuki no Tempest — attachment becomes responsibility to the future

Tsuki enters Tokyo with the origin-derived grammar:

> attachment → vow → testing → chosen commitment.

Tokyo subjects that model to professional loss.

The unit first becomes rigid through fear of failure, then recovers freedom through Rui's intervention. Later, after losing to IIIX, Kotono realizes that victory has a moral shadow: every previous opponent they defeated also had to carry pain.

This creates a more mature version of chosen commitment:

> **To continue choosing the stage means accepting both the responsibility of winning and the knowledge produced by losing.**

Kotono's Mana relation also moves decisively forward:

`私にとってのアイドル活動には、もう、お姉ちゃんはあまり関係なくなっている`

Her sister remains history and love. She is no longer the operating reason for idolhood.

The open seed at Tokyo's endpoint is Kotono's perception that Sakura is moving away from her. That comparison must remain unresolved until later material earns an interpretation.

---

# 7. LizNoir — living rivalry restores time

LizNoir's origins established that excellence matters when it transmits human desire and that pride is more stable than fixed unit form.

Tokyo strengthens both claims.

The TRINITY match produces the pain of loss but also one of Rio's clearest affirmations that being LizNoir and being an idol are sources of happiness in themselves.

The arc then resolves an older temporal pathology. Mana had become a permanently unavailable benchmark.

SUNNY's final allows Rio/Aoi to name a qualitatively different rival:

> a living rival remains in history with you.

You can meet again.

This produces a useful formulation:

> **Tokyo turns rivalry from frozen memorial jurisdiction back into reciprocal futurity.**

LizNoir does not need Mana to become less important historically. It needs the living to become more important prospectively.

# 8. TRINITYAiLE — anti-sacrifice becomes institutional practice

TRINITY's origins established asymmetry of role without asymmetry of personhood.

Tokyo puts that ethic under coercive institutional pressure.

Rui initially responds to scandal risk by making a familiar mistake: she decides on behalf of others that protecting them requires her disappearance.

Yu and Sumire refuse the unilateral structure. Later Makino states the principle even more directly: Rui cannot decide for Hoshimi whether helping her counts as an unacceptable burden.

The important Tokyo progression is therefore:

> **anti-self-reduction → anti-unilateral-sacrifice → shared standing in decisions about collective risk.**

TRINITY's temporary Preta Porte affiliation remains partly authored even under coercion because the trio chooses how to interpret and publicly frame what they still control.

Their refusal to throw the IIIX match then demonstrates that institutional vulnerability does not eliminate professional ethical floors.

At the endpoint, remaining at Hoshimi becomes a fully chosen institutional commitment rather than an emergency transfer.

# 9. IIIX — the arc's hardest ethical case

Tokyo confirms that IIIX is neither simple villain unit nor innocent professional victim.

The corrected entry state contains:

- corporate formation;
- distinct private motives;
- victory compact;
- Miho's anti-Mana memory grievance.

Tokyo then progressively establishes:

1. Himeno can map institutional power onto IIIX's existing instrumental motives;
2. IIIX notices enough warning signs to possess agency;
3. its members still value winning through actual performance;
4. they compartmentalize that performance from external corruption;
5. Miho escalates legacy rivalry into concrete symbolic-erasure politics;
6. fran/kana can support the strategic result without sharing Miho's grief;
7. IIIX occasionally limits Himeno because excessive sabotage would contaminate the meaning of its own victory;
8. SUNNY finally defeats IIIX in a singular moment without converting IIIX to anti-competition philosophy;
9. the victory compact remains active at the Tokyo endpoint.

The strongest ethical diagnosis is:

> **IIIX's central Tokyo failure is not fraudulent performance but responsibility compartmentalization. It wants the evidentiary dignity of a fair stage while treating corruption of the surrounding conditions as somebody else's moral account.**

This is why its claim `ステージの上で、正々堂々` is both true and insufficient.

Tokyo also shows that this failure is unstable. If external manipulation makes the comparison meaningless, it ultimately threatens the very thing IIIX wants from victory.

The later post-I-UNITY re-authorization remains future evidence and must not be imported backward into this freeze.

# 10. Himeno, Asakura, Saegusa, and institutional legitimacy

Tokyo refuses a simplistic "good producer / bad producer" contrast by differentiating forms of authority.

## 10.1 Himeno

Himeno's governing vice is not ambition alone. It is **jurisdictional capture**:

- idols are treated as children outside the business domain;
- agency affiliation can be manufactured as a fait accompli;
- press/media asymmetry becomes coercive leverage;
- institutional reform becomes a route to concentrated control;
- opponents are removed rather than merely defeated;
- audience trust and competitive structure become instruments.

She treats the environment as something that may be authored from above.

## 10.2 Asakura

Asakura appears cold and can be ethically inadequate in his paternal relationship, but Tokyo confirms that his dismissal of TRINITY/LizNoir was protective rather than predatory.

The origins remain important: professional correctness does not automatically vindicate his fatherhood. Tokyo resolves the institutional accusation, not every relational injury.

## 10.3 Saegusa

Saegusa's disappearance is retrospectively reclassified as covert protective/investigative labor.

His strategy is still paternalistic in the sense that he withholds information from Makino and the idols. Tokyo does not fully adjudicate whether this secrecy was the least harmful option.

## 10.4 Hoshimi

Hoshimi becomes the arc's positive institutional experiment because its governance ethic is explicitly anti-instrumental:

> idols should be able to use the agency as infrastructure for their own brilliance.

This does not make Hoshimi conflict-free or perfectly non-hierarchical. It identifies the direction in which authority is supposed to point.

# 11. Competition, metrics, and the meaning of a result

Tokyo produces the strongest evidence yet for a nuanced VENUS/competition thesis.

The arc does **not** say:

> scores are fake; feelings are real.

Instead:

- IIIX's technical repeatability is genuinely formidable;
- TRINITY/LizNoir competition raises both groups;
- IIIX/Tsuki competition raises both groups;
- losing causes real pain and real knowledge;
- SUNNY's singular final is still acknowledged by IIIX as legitimately superior in that moment;
- investigation later preserves I-UNITY results rather than declaring the entire competition void.

The target of critique is therefore better described as:

> **metric sovereignty and institutional capture, not measurement or competition themselves.**

A score can be useful evidence without becoming the total ontology of value.

A result can be legitimate without permanently settling future hierarchy.

A rival can help create the performance that defeats them.

Preferred V2 formulation:

> **Competition is a form of co-production whose ethical legitimacy depends on preserving the conditions under which the result can still mean what participants think it means.**

# 12. `アイドルとは何か` — plural answers and a meta-rule

Tokyo repeatedly asks what an idol is, but the source does not collapse the answer into a single slogan.

Different characters emphasize different dimensions:

- struggle;
- bond;
- aspiration/wing;
- particular relational devotion;
- fan-facing performance;
- joy;
- technical excellence;
- continuing after loss;
- reciprocal public recognition.

SUNNY PEACE provides the most elaborated unit answer, but it should not be imposed as the literal definition for all units.

The stronger meta-rule is:

> **Idolhood is not valid because one authority defines it correctly. It becomes meaningful through authored relation among performer, craft, audience, rivals, and the future the performer still chooses.**

This explains why Tokyo can validate very different units without making all values interchangeable.

# 13. Mana — from jurisdiction to history, then beyond history

Tokyo's Mana architecture has four stages.

## 13.1 Mana as remembered vocational horizon

Makino still recalls Mana's dream for him. Kotono still describes receiving a baton.

Neither is required to become Mana.

## 13.2 Mana as contested public memory

Miho turns Mana into a political object. The issue becomes who controls songs, rights, records, and historical attention.

This is a new scale of the same franchise problem: can the dead possess the future through the survivor's interpretation?

## 13.3 Mana as surpassed legend

SUNNY does not defeat Mana in a literal head-to-head contest. It creates an event whose meaning is described as going `その先`.

Miho recognizes that the legend she wanted to overwrite has been overwritten by **new creation**.

## 13.4 Supernatural residue without restored jurisdiction

The Mana-like voice keeps the metaphysical residue alive.

But the arc's causal organization remains living-person-centered.

Preferred V2 formulation:

> **Tokyo allows Mana to remain present as memory, possible supernatural residue, and historical cause while refusing to restore her as the author of what the living do next.**
