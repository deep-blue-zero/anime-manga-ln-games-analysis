# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — IIIX PRE-TOKYO BASELINE

## 0. Purpose

This document freezes the **maximum state that the Tokyo main-story pass is allowed to inherit about IIIX before reading `tokyo_001`**.

It is intentionally narrower than the full origin audit. Its purpose is prospective control.

Tokyo should not have to reconstruct IIIX from scratch, because the origin stories are an explicit hard prerequisite. But Tokyo also must not be contaminated by conclusions from historical analyses that already know what the Tokyo arc does.

Therefore:

> **Everything below is admissible inherited state. Everything about IIIX beyond this boundary must be earned prospectively by the Tokyo text itself.**

---

# 1. Unit identity at the gate

## 1.1 Two-founding model

IIIX has already undergone two constitutions.

### First founding — corporate IIIX

Preta Porte created IIIX as a prestige commercial project from three already marketable professionals.

- The company selected the members.
- The unit name invokes the diamond cut grade `3EX / Triple Excellent`.
- fran was assigned the center role as part of the concept.
- Songs, styling, promotion, and professional infrastructure were supplied externally.
- The members did not begin with a shared idol vocation.

Their initial cohesion was principally instrumental.

### Second founding — self-authorized IIIX

After I-UNITY:

- Himeno's scheme collapsed;
- Himeno was arrested/exposed;
- Preta Porte withdrew from idol operations;
- IIIX lost work and institutional backing;
- the members explicitly agreed to disband.

They then performed a smaller live, recovered neglected reasons for performing, and voluntarily chose to continue together.

They subsequently became freelance and began assuming greater responsibility for their own business infrastructure.

**Governing inference for Tokyo:**

> The IIIX entering Tokyo is no longer merely a corporate object. It is a unit its members have re-authorized under materially worse external conditions.

This does not make the first founding irrelevant. Corporate habits, instrumental motives, scoring philosophy, and professional hardness remain part of the unit.

---

# 2. Relationship state

The members repeatedly deny that they are ordinary friends.

This self-description must be preserved.

However, pure-utilitarian language is also insufficient. By the origin endpoint they have:

- shared private motives they normally conceal;
- relied on one another under professional pressure;
- identified external corruption as a threat to the meaning of their work;
- trusted one another's professional competence;
- chosen the same partners again after the agency stopped requiring the unit to exist.

**Preferred baseline term:**

> **adversarial interdependence**

Secondary acceptable term:

> **deliberately non-sentimental loyalty**

Do not pre-label the relationship as found family, conventional friendship, or affection-centered sisterhood.

Do not reduce it to mutual utility either.

---

# 3. Performance philosophy at the gate

IIIX remains a high-control competitive unit.

The following are established:

- strong VENUS-system literacy;
- deliberate technical-score optimization;
- high value placed on repeatability, precision, synchronization, and controllable execution;
- preference for defeating opponents at full force (`獅子搏兎`);
- belief that professional strength should be demonstrable rather than merely asserted.

The origins **do not** establish a conversion to anti-ranking or anti-competition philosophy.

What changed is narrower and more important:

> **IIIX now has evidence that measurable technical reliability is not exhaustive of stage value.**

SUNNY PEACE's I-UNITY final and the later intimate live make audience encounter harder to dismiss as mere score substrate.

Additionally:

> **IIIX has learned that external manipulation can contaminate victory by destroying the comparison the victory is supposed to prove.**

Thus legitimate rules-aware optimization and corrupt manipulation must remain analytically distinct in Tokyo.

# 4. miho at the gate

## 4.1 Stable facts

- miho's original idol vocation was formed with 藤代葉.
- The original shared ideal was to make spectators feel happy through performance.
- Yō died after illness.
- Yō explicitly told miho not to remain trapped by her and to continue with new companions.
- Mana's fatal accident then dominated public grief/coverage while Yō received little public attention.
- miho experienced the disparity as an injustice of memory.
- She developed a project of surpassing/overwriting Mana's public legacy.
- She possesses deep VENUS and competitive-analysis expertise.

## 4.2 Unresolved contradiction

The origin sequence destabilizes miho's revenge premise but does not abolish it.

Evidence of destabilization:

- defeating Tsuki does not give her the expected emotional resolution;
- she returns mentally to the original audience-happiness promise;
- a fan at the small live remembers Yō and Sundance directly.

Counterevidence to completed recovery:

`長瀬麻奈の記憶を塗り潰すまではね`

**Tokyo must therefore inherit both propositions simultaneously:**

1. miho has regained access to the audience-facing vocation that predates revenge;
2. miho still explicitly endorses the anti-Mana legacy project.

Do not resolve the contradiction in advance.

## 4.3 Correct causal model

Avoid:

> "miho hates Mana because Mana was better."

Prefer:

> **Mana becomes the symbolic carrier of a public-memory hierarchy in which miho believes Yō's life and death were made culturally negligible.**

This explains the intensity without endorsing the retaliation.

# 5. fran at the gate

## 5.1 Stable facts

- Fashion/modeling is fran's first and clearest vocation.
- She has Paris Collection experience.
- A failed fashion brand left her with roughly one hundred million yen of debt.
- Idol work initially functions as income, visibility, and leverage for fashion recovery.
- She is highly literate in business cost, contracts, law, office overhead, branding, and professional risk.

## 5.2 Ethical/professional boundary

fran rejects Himeno not because she abandons instrumentalism but because she refuses to surrender aesthetic/professional authorship:

`美的センスまで売り渡すつもりはないわ！`

This is the baseline for evaluating future compromises.

## 5.3 Plural vocation

After the smaller live:

`私も……別に、アイドルをやるのは嫌いじゃなかったし`

`ファッションの次くらいには好きでもいいでしょ`

Tokyo may therefore treat idolhood as a **genuine secondary vocation**, not merely a cynical job.

Fashion remains first.

# 6. kana at the gate

## 6.1 Stable facts

- kana's mother died at birth.
- Her father became absent and eventually disappeared from her daily life.
- Her grandfather exploited her celebrity and created financial/safety problems.
- kana eventually had to protect herself from that family exploitation.
- Modeling/public visibility became genuinely enjoyable as well as professionally useful.

## 6.2 Visibility has four simultaneous functions

Kana's attention-seeking should be modeled as:

1. genuine vanity/pleasure in being seen;
2. professional brand labor;
3. competitive status;
4. a public flare that might allow her missing father to find her.

Her line is explicit:

`kanaがテレビやネットに出まくれば`

`お父さんがそれを見て連絡くれるかもしれないじゃん`

## 6.3 Exploitative-adult literacy

Kana's rapid suspicion of Himeno is grounded in lived experience with an exploitative grandfather.

She is not merely a comic narcissist. She has unusually sharp radar for adults who package control as opportunity.

# 7. Himeno and Preta Porte at the gate

IIIX is not allowed to enter Tokyo analytically as "Himeno's unit" without qualification.

Established history:

- Himeno became producer after IIIX was already a successful elite unit.
- She exploited rather than created the members' instrumental motives.
- The members noticed warning signs but tolerated her because she appeared useful.
- miho had the deepest vulnerability to Himeno's anti-Mana / legacy leverage.
- fran and kana reached resistance earlier through aesthetic/business and exploitative-adult heuristics.
- IIIX ultimately rejected the corruption of victory conditions.
- Himeno was exposed/arrested.
- Preta Porte's idol activity collapsed.
- IIIX left and continued freelance.

**Prospective rule:**

> Future IIIX behavior must be attributed to the members unless the Tokyo source independently establishes renewed external control. Do not treat Himeno as the default hidden author of their choices after the origin endpoint.

# 8. Makino and Hoshimi at the gate

The origins provide independent continuity evidence for Makino.

- miho recognizes him as the same young man previously seen around Mana.
- kana forms a cautious but positive professional impression of him as a good-hearted entertainment adult who nevertheless appears capable of surviving the industry.

This supports the project's governing rule:

> **The game's manager/player is continuing Makino Kouhei, with player-facing name/choice parameterization layered onto that identity.**

No new Makino identity dispute is opened.

The origins end by routing IIIX toward Hoshimi, explicitly marking what follows as another story.

# 9. Institutional/financial state at the gate

IIIX is now in a much more precarious but more authored organizational condition.

Known responsibilities include:

- fran handling or thinking through economics, office location, legal structure and incorporation issues;
- miho contacting established industry networks for work;
- kana evaluating visibility and practical publicity opportunities;
- all three accepting that continuing may require worse commercial terms than those available under Preta Porte.

**Strong inference:**

> IIIX is becoming not just a performing unit but a small self-managed professional enterprise.

This should matter in Tokyo whenever they discuss money, agencies, contracts, leverage, management, independence, or what a producer is for.

# 10. Claims Tokyo is explicitly forbidden to inherit

Until the V2 Tokyo close read earns them, do **not** assume:

- IIIX is a pure philosophical negation of Hoshimi or SUNNY PEACE;
- miho's revenge project has ended;
- IIIX is willing to accept every form of sabotage or corruption;
- the members have become conventionally warm friends;
- the members are motivated only by money/status;
- the members have rejected technical scoring or competition;
- later Tokyo antagonism proves the second founding was false;
- later Tokyo cooperation proves the origin hostility was only a façade;
- Himeno or Preta Porte still controls IIIX after the freelance transition;
- V1 Tokyo interpretations are already V2 conclusions.

The entire purpose of the Tokyo pass is to **test** the pre-Tokyo model.

# 11. Questions Tokyo is now allowed to test

The next main-story arc should be read prospectively against these questions:

1. Does freelance/self-authored IIIX behave differently from corporate IIIX when confronted with Hoshimi?
2. Does miho's recovered audience vocation constrain her anti-Mana project, or do the two remain compartmentalized?
3. Can IIIX preserve a distinction between legitimate competitive ruthlessness and corrupt manipulation under new pressure?
4. Does the unit's adverse, non-sentimental loyalty hold when members' private goals diverge?
5. How does the group's new self-management alter its view of producers, agencies, and Makino?
6. Does IIIX continue to treat technical reliability as primary, or integrate audience encounter more deeply?
7. What exactly do the three mean by becoming/remain "top" after losing the institutional system that originally rewarded them?
8. Does contact with Hoshimi expose real philosophical opposition, or only different professional/relational grammars?

These are questions, not pre-decided answers.
