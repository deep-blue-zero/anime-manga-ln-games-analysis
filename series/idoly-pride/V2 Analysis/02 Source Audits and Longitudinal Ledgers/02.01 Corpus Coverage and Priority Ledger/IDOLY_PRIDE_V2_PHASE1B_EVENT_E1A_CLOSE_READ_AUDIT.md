## 0. Executive result

Phase 1B Event Close Read **E1-A** is complete.

The four events were read prospectively in the rerank-defined order, their source-native findings were frozen and SHA-256 locked, and only then was the historical analytical corpus consulted. The primary freeze therefore remains the auditable record of what V2 recovered without V1 interpretive prompting.

The governing E1-A conclusion is:

> **INTERPRETATION — E1-A transforms Mana from a potentially sovereign founding miracle into one node—still extraordinary, still beloved, but neither exclusive nor jurisdictionally supreme—inside a reciprocal network of inheritance.**

The four events collectively show that inheritance in *IDOLY PRIDE* is neither exact reproduction nor clean severance. Songs, institutions, rivalries, memories, and unfinished projects remain attached to their origins, but the living are entitled to answer, alter, re-perform, distribute, refuse, and extend them.

The tranche therefore materially strengthens the Tier-A formulation **provenance and inheritance without continuing jurisdiction**.

A second governing result concerns Hoshimi Production:

> **INTERPRETATION — Hoshimi is best modeled here as a professional continuity platform: a structure that preserves provenance and relationship while permitting changing membership, rivalry, authorship, historical distance, and future vocation.**

This is more precise than either “Mana's family” or “Mana's successors.” It is also more precise than the older poetic image of Hoshimi as a “sky.” The sky metaphor remains evocative; the continuity-platform model better explains the institution's actual operations in these events.

# 4. Event 1 — 煌めく奇跡をもう一度
## `event_2021_004_st-eve-2110-marathon`

### 4.1 Dramatic function

This event is the first E1-A test of what Hoshimi does after Mana's supernatural-adjacent presence has receded.

The opening explicitly says Hoshimi has been operating in Tokyo for some time. Sakura also states that the heart no longer tells her things after Mana disappeared. The event therefore occurs after a major ontological and emotional transition: Hoshimi can no longer rely upon Mana's continuing apparition to mediate remembrance.

The problem becomes institutional rather than ghostly:

> What do the living do on the first birthday after the dead person is no longer available even as a quasi-present witness?

### 4.2 Makino and Kotono: forgetting is not betrayal, remembering is not stasis

Makino's near-forgetting of Mana's birthday is a small but important ethical scene. His attention has migrated toward the active responsibility of making current idols successful. He experiences that forward movement as guilt.

The event refuses to resolve the tension by saying either:

- “you must always remember with equal intensity,” or
- “moving on means the birthday no longer matters.”

Instead, remembrance is moved out of private psychic vigilance and into repeatable social practice.

Kotono does something similar with Mana's diary. She does not merely treasure the diary as a relic. The diary becomes a prompt to create the kind of birthday celebration Mana had wanted among the living.

This is the first important E1-A distinction:

> **A relic preserves information; a ritual reactivates relationship.**

### 4.3 Sakura's proposal: grief becomes institution

Sakura's suggestion that Hoshimi celebrate Mana's birthday every year is a genuine institutional act. It creates a repeatable custom that no longer depends on Makino or Kotono successfully maintaining perfect private remembrance.

But the event immediately prevents ritual from becoming rigid reenactment. The dorm-party plan mutates into an improvised live at Mana's debut site. The remembered form is preserved—celebration through idol performance—while the concrete expression changes.

That is a small-scale model for the entire tranche:

> preserve provenance; permit transformation.

### 4.4 Kokoro as the indispensable non-witness

The event's most important V2 gain over the earlier compact reading is Kokoro.

Kokoro asks why so much effort is being spent on the birthday of someone who is dead. Ai partially understands the question. The text therefore inserts a character who has no autobiographical obligation to share Hoshimi's existing Mana culture.

Kokoro gathers several incompatible but complementary Mana memories:

- Makino's professional/intimate history;
- Haruko's ordinary friendship;
- Rio's knowledge of the labor beneath Mana's effortless-looking image;
- Kotono's family memory;
- Sakura's bodily inheritance.

No account becomes the master account.

By the ending, Kokoro still says that Mana is someone she never met and can never meet. She accepts the event, asks for recordings, and is intrigued by the prospect of becoming memorable herself, while still declaring `過去は振り返りません`.

This matters more than a simple “Mana becomes communal memory” formulation. The stronger conclusion is:

> **Hoshimi's memory culture is legitimate because it can include a participant who does not share witness grief and is not morally punished for that difference.**

The institution can remember without demanding emotional conscription.

### 4.5 Sakura's heartbeat and the supernatural limit

Sakura says she somehow felt guided on Mana's birthday despite the heart having ceased to communicate after Mana's departure. She explicitly answers `分かりません` when asked to explain it.

Kokoro later listens to the heartbeat and remarks that the legendary heart sounds ordinary.

The event deliberately holds two frames together:

- meaningful subjective experience;
- physically ordinary embodiment.

The sunlight over `First Step` adds another suggestive coincidence, but nothing crosses the threshold into objective supernatural proof.

**Result:** `OPEN-17` remains `OPEN`.

The correct formulation is not “Mana returned” and not “nothing supernatural happened.” It is:

> **The event allows the characters to live as though coincidence may carry relationship without requiring the ontology to be settled.**

### 4.6 Songs as reversible address

`song for you` no longer functions in only one direction. Sakura and Kotono articulate different meanings; Kotono can now hear the song as something that may be answered back toward Mana.

The encore `First Step` then becomes the first step of a new annual practice rather than simply a nostalgic performance of Mana's debut song.

This is the tranche's first clear example of a song retaining provenance while gaining new addressees and new institutional function.

### 4.7 Historical stress test

Historical event triage correctly described the story as a Mana ritual that makes her communal memory rather than only private wound. That core claim is **PRESERVE**.

However, the old priority map treated the event as merely `IMPORTANT` and considered it skippable once later Mana material was available. That priority is **REVISE**.

The fresh pass establishes four functions that justify E1/Core status:

1. it is a chronology anchor for the first post-departure birthday;
2. it creates recurring Hoshimi memorial practice;
3. Kokoro supplies the non-witness control needed for a non-coercive memory ethic;
4. it directly pressures `OPEN-17` without falsely resolving it.

**Transition:** `STRENGTHEN` content, `REVISE` priority.

# 5. Event 2 — Happy Smile Selfie
## `event_2022_007_st-eve-2207-contest`

### 5.1 Chronology correction is foundational

The event opens:

`ⅢXが星見プロに移籍して間もない、ある日`

This is an unusually valuable explicit chronology marker. The story must therefore be read as **entry-state IIIX**, not as evidence of the mature post-American unit.

That single restriction prevents several retrospective errors.

### 5.2 IIIX joins for leverage, not belonging

At entry, the members are direct about their motives.

Hoshimi is:

- a professional platform;
- a path back to the top;
- a means of restarting work;
- something to exploit strategically.

It is not yet:

- home;
- family;
- chosen companionship;
- proof that their internal bond has become self-acknowledged attachment.

The explicit self-description remains `ただのビジネスパートナー`.

The later discovery that the IIIX bond is more durable and existential than this statement admits must remain later.

### 5.3 Hoshimi's institutional test: affiliation without privilege

The event places former elite IIIX in deliberately ordinary conditions:

- shared backstage space;
- weak air conditioning/environmental discomfort;
- smaller or less prestigious positioning;
- no guaranteed crowd;
- normal rules and queues.

Makino explicitly says that even if Pret-a-Porter gave them special treatment, Hoshimi will not.

miho acknowledges that their prior treatment was exceptional and that the current conditions are ordinary.

This is meaningful positive evidence about Hoshimi. The institution gives IIIX a route back without pretending their former status remains current entitlement.

But the scope is narrow. It does **not** establish that Hoshimi has solved:

- workload;
- contract fairness;
- internal pressure;
- health management;
- compensation;
- all forms of exploitation.

`OPEN-05` therefore receives counterevidence, not closure.

### 5.4 Sakura distinguishes fair rivalry from institutional corruption

Sakura's insistence that IIIX fought SUNNY PEACE fairly at I-UNITY matters. She refuses to collapse the unit into Pret-a-Porter's scandal.

That does two things:

1. restores moral agency to IIIX as competitors rather than treating them as mere products of a corrupt agency;
2. makes Sakura's desire for continued rivalry intelligible as respect rather than naïve friendliness.

Her response to IIIX entering Hoshimi is not “now we can stop being rivals.” It is almost the opposite:

> now the rivalry can continue inside a shared institution.

This becomes one of E1-A's strongest proofs that incorporation need not require competitive erasure.

### 5.5 miho/Sakura: resemblance is trigger, not resolution

Fran names the resemblance directly: `彼女が葉ちゃんみたいだから？`

miho simultaneously retains an openly destructive Mana-directed goal: `私の目的は長瀬麻奈の存在を消し去ること`.

The event therefore does not authorize a later healing formulation.

At this point:

- Sakura activates Yō memory;
- Hoshimi activates Mana resentment;
- proximity forces miho to inhabit the contradiction;
- no reconciliation has occurred.

Fran's role is also easy to overread. She notices miho, drinks with her, and tells her to stop regretting choices she herself made. This is relationally significant, but it remains consistent with IIIX's armored early grammar. Fran does not reveal a secret wholesome unit underneath the business façade.

### 5.6 kana and the constructed selfie

kana's `可愛い後輩と一緒にランチ` framing is overtly strategic. She wants the visibility and goodwill attached to a cute senior/junior image.

Yet the public construction is not empty. The staged interaction creates an actual encounter with Sakura, produces mutual amusement, and becomes part of the relationship history.

This makes the event useful to `OPEN-16`:

> **persona can be intentionally authored and commercially instrumental without therefore being psychologically false or relationally sterile.**

The series is not offering a binary between “real self” and “media lie.” It is showing media form as one environment in which relation can occur.

### 5.7 Makino's fan judgment

Makino chooses to trust the heat of IIIX's fanbase despite uncertainty about turnout. The resulting crowd validates the judgment.

This should not be turned into an anti-data thesis. The more precise point is:

> **professional knowledge includes forms of relational judgment that are not reducible to an immediately available metric.**

That aligns with Tier-A's rejection of metric sovereignty without rejecting measurement itself.

### 5.8 Endpoint: integration without domestication

IIIX ends the event explicitly planning to become Hoshimi's top unit and take SUNNY PEACE's fans.

That is the correct endpoint.

Institutional incorporation has occurred.
Emotional domestication has not.
Competitive identity has not.
miho's Mana problem has not.
The unit's declared instrumental compact has not yet been rewritten.

### 5.9 Historical stress test

Historical analysis strongly converged with the event's importance and correctly identified it as the essential Hoshimi-entry story for IIIX/miho/Sakura. The broad historical formulation “Hoshimi as integration without domestication” is **PRESERVE** and **STRENGTHEN**.

The necessary correction is chronological. Some later historical formulations explain the event through the mature understanding that Hoshimi is “necessary” for IIIX or that their ugly bond is already the stabilized vehicle of miho's life beyond Sundance. Those ideas become defensible later, but this entry story itself has not earned them yet.

**Transition:** `PRESERVE` central thesis; `REVISE` any later-state back-projection.

# 6. Event 3 — 運命繋ぐ流星の軌跡
## `event_2022_010_st-eve-2210-race`

### 6.1 Chronology route

The event explicitly identifies itself as a story from shortly after LizNoir entered Hoshimi.

This makes it an early transfer-state text, not simply a mature Rio retrospective.

### 6.2 Aoi and Rio refuse to ventriloquize Mana

Aoi asks whether Kotono's Grand Prix victory “saved” Mana, since Mana herself never reached that stage.

Rio answers that Mana might be pleased or might be frustrated; she cannot know.

Later, Aoi wonders what Mana would think of current LizNoir. Rio says that no matter how much they wish, they can no longer talk to her.

This is a crucial epistemic control for the whole tranche.

Even characters who knew Mana personally refuse to treat imagined approval as certain fact.

The event later allows felt presence onstage, but that earlier humility remains part of the source and should constrain interpretation.

### 6.3 Rio's initial refusal: inheritance as protected property

Kotono asks LizNoir to sing Mana's songs for an album because LizNoir was special to her sister.

Rio refuses.

Her objection is not dislike of Mana. It is almost the reverse: she treats the songs as too closely attached to Mana and to the legitimate successor lines represented by Kotono/Tsuki and Sakura/SUNNY PEACE. LizNoir performing them feels to Rio like `奪う`—taking something that belongs elsewhere.

This is the tranche's clearest articulation of an **exclusive/proprietary inheritance model**.

Rio's position is ethically serious. It respects provenance. Its limitation is that it confuses respect for origin with a requirement that meaning remain inside one authorized lineage.

### 6.4 Aoi as relational mediator

Aoi recognizes that Rio's refusal is evidence that Mana remains important to her. He does not force Rio into confession; he creates circumstances where she and Kotono can encounter the song together.

Aoi's intervention matters because the eventual change does not arrive through abstract argument alone. Rio must hear herself inside the song again.

### 6.5 `星の海の記憶`: the song that had already crossed the boundary

Rio admits that the song mattered during the period when she tried to stop singing after Mana's death. Her key statement is:

`私が今も歌っていられるのは、間違いなく 彼女と、この歌に出会ったから`

This changes the inheritance problem.

Rio had treated Mana's songs as something she should not appropriate, yet Mana's song had already become part of the causal history of Rio's continuing vocation.

The song is therefore not merely Mana's property in a static sense. It has already acted upon another life.

### 6.6 The reciprocal-lineage discovery

The live introduction supplies the strongest new E1-A finding.

Kotono tells the audience that Mana decided to pursue idolhood after seeing Rio and Aoi's LizNoir perform at that same live house.

The causal chain is therefore:

> **Rio/Aoi LizNoir → Mana chooses idolhood → Mana creates `星の海の記憶` → the song helps Rio continue singing → Rio and Kotono re-perform it → Rio accepts future cover participation.**

This is not a one-directional “Mana gives light to later idols” lineage.

It is reciprocal.

Mana was herself an inheritor before she became an ancestor.

This strongly revises any mythology that makes her the self-originating source of Hoshimi's idol world. Her extraordinariness remains real, but it is socially caused and then socially causal.

### 6.7 Performance as conversation without ontological proof

After the live, Rio says that while singing she felt as though she spoke one line to Mana. At the grave, she internally addresses Mana and says she felt her beside her.

The emotional result is decisive: Rio accepts the cover project, stops treating forgetting as obligation, and returns Mana to the status of rival she will continue trying to surpass.

The ontological result is not decisive.

This is **dialogic memorial performance** whether or not a literal ghost was present.

### 6.8 LizNoir change is not betrayal

Kotono observes that current four-person LizNoir is far more lively than the older image she expected. Rio explicitly accepts that change can be good.

This connects unit inheritance to song inheritance:

- LizNoir can change without ceasing to be LizNoir;
- a Mana song can change context without ceasing to carry Mana.

Form identity is durable without requiring frozen repetition.

### 6.9 Formal dependency

`adv-live-eve-2210-race-005` is absent from the source bundle.

The following remain inadmissible until backfill:

- exact choreography;
- center/blocking details beyond text;
- camera grammar;
- lighting and color;
- arrangement/vocal execution;
- visual apparition or Mana imagery;
- audience-shot construction.

### 6.10 Historical stress test

Historical analysis correctly identified this event as essential to Rio/Mana/Kotono and summarized Rio's movement as gratitude and rivalry rather than static resentment or reverence. That is **PRESERVE**.

V2 materially strengthens it in three ways:

1. Rio begins from a coherent protected-lineage theory, not mere emotional reluctance;
2. the event is epistemically disciplined about not knowing Mana's wishes;
3. the LizNoir → Mana → Rio loop makes inheritance explicitly reciprocal.

**Transition:** `STRENGTHEN`.

# 7. Event 4 — 星々が奇跡と叶える約束の未来
## `event_2023_006_st-eve-2306-contest`

### 7.1 Dramatic function

This event is both a present-tense all-Hoshimi gratitude story and a retrospective institutional-origin disclosure.

It should not be flattened into “Mana and Haruko had an unfinished song.” Its function is larger:

> it shows how Hoshimi transmits an unfinished intention from a tiny early agency into a twenty-one-idol institution without treating the early form as the only authentic one.

### 7.2 Saegusa, LizNoir, Makino, Haruko: Mana's miracle is scaffolded

The flashback reconstructs Mana's emergence through a network:

- Saegusa hears Mana singing at the coast and scouts her;
- Saegusa sends her to see LizNoir;
- seeing Rio and Aoi helps Mana decide to become an idol;
- Mana enters Hoshimi alongside Makino;
- Makino supports her through lost voice and overwork;
- Haruko functions as senior, friend, training partner, and rival.

This is one of the strongest anti-self-origin myths in the corpus.

Mana remains exceptional, but the event refuses the fantasy that exceptionality is self-generated.

> **Miracle is socially scaffolded.**

That proposition matters for `OPEN-18` because Hoshimi's institutional history is now not simply “agency formed around Mana.” It is a reciprocal production process in which adults, peers, rivals, and management help make Mana possible.

### 7.3 Haruko's original self-erasure

When Mana asks Haruko to sing in the staff-gratitude live, Haruko first refuses because she judges herself too weak and unpopular to deserve standing beside Mana. She offers to work backstage instead.

This is not simple humility. Haruko has a positive desire to sing, but converts perceived inferiority into a theory that support from behind is more appropriate.

Mana directly rejects the substitution.

She reminds Haruko that:

- Haruko taught her as a senior;
- they trained together;
- they talked about their futures;
- Haruko's rivalry made Mana work harder;
- Haruko's presence prevented Mana from feeling alone.

Mana's key insistence is:

`遙子ちゃんと一緒じゃなきゃ嫌なの`

Haruko is not valuable because she can make Mana's stage function from backstage. Mana wants Haruko as a co-performer.

The old scene therefore establishes a principle that the present scene later returns to:

> **Care does not become more virtuous by erasing the caregiver's own claim to the stage.**

### 7.4 The unfinished song: provenance without exclusive possession

Mana and Haruko begin composing an original song to thank Saegusa and Makino. Mana dies before the planned live occurs.

In the present, Haruko initially believes that because she and Mana promised to write the lyrics together, she should finish them herself.

fran gives the deceptively simple correction: if one person is stuck, ask more people.

Haruko then states the decisive transformation:

`この曲はもう、私と麻奈ちゃんの二人だけの曲じゃない`

`この曲は星見プロのみんなの曲だものね`

This is not loss of authorship.

The song remains recognizably rooted in Mana and Haruko's gratitude. What expands is who has standing to complete and perform the work.

Preferred formulation:

> **provenance-preserving expanded authorship**.

### 7.5 The twenty-one-idol correction

Haruko says:

`星見プロ所属のアイドル二十人……いえ、二十一人で`

The arithmetic is straightforward:

- SUNNY PEACE: 5
- Tsuki no Tempest: 5
- LizNoir: 4
- TRINITYAiLE: 3
- IIIX: 3
- subtotal: 20
- Haruko: **21**

This is a high-confidence textual correction to any synthesis that casually treats Haruko as retired, quasi-staff, or merely a remnant of Hoshimi's old generation.

The event itself later prevents that reading a second time.

### 7.6 Past/current succession without vocational displacement

Haruko decides that the current generation should perform the inherited song. She contrasts the old Hoshimi, in which she and Mana would have sung it, with the current Hoshimi represented by the younger units.

Taken alone, this could be misread as graceful retirement.

Kotono immediately rejects that inference:

`これからの星見プロを引っ張って行くのは 遙子さんも一緒ですからね`

Haruko agrees:

`私もみんなと一緒に これからもっとアイドルとして輝いてみせるわ`

Therefore:

- Haruko can yield a particular inherited song;
- Haruko can describe herself as belonging to Hoshimi's historical past;
- Haruko still remains an active idol and participant in Hoshimi's future.

“Past” and “present” here describe **stewardship positions in institutional history**, not a binary between obsolete person and current person.

This is one of E1-A's most important distinctions.

### 7.7 Continuity of commitments

The present Hoshimi line makes the institution's temporal logic explicit:

- Mana and Haruko were once nearly the whole idol side of Hoshimi;
- new units formed;
- rivals joined;
- Tokyo expansion changed the scale;
- the agency continues.

Haruko's final thought is that places and members may change while what they value can remain.

That is neither essentialism nor stasis.

The “same thing” is not a fixed roster, fixed song ownership, fixed center, fixed building, or fixed degree of fame.

It is a transmitted pattern of professional relation:

- gratitude becomes performance;
- rivals sharpen one another;
- managers/staff materially support idols;
- idols answer support through serious work;
- newcomers inherit without needing to imitate founders.

### 7.8 Historical stress test

Historical event triage called this “core-adjacent” and correctly identified the old Mana/Haruko gratitude-live promise plus inherited staff gratitude. Those observations are **PRESERVE**.

The source-native pass establishes that the event deserves full Core status because it additionally contains:

1. Saegusa's direct role in Mana's recruitment and LizNoir exposure;
2. Makino's explicit formative support during voice loss/overwork;
3. Mana's rejection of Haruko's supportive self-erasure;
4. the twenty-one-idol enumeration;
5. provenance-preserving shared authorship;
6. explicit protection of Haruko's future idolhood;
7. a compact statement of Hoshimi continuity across changing place and membership.

**Transition:** `STRENGTHEN` content and **upgrade to Core**.

# 8. Cross-event synthesis

## 8.1 Mana becomes reciprocal rather than self-originating

The strongest E1-A correction to a simplified franchise mythology is causal.

Mana is not simply the origin from which later idol meaning flows.

Her own path depends upon:

- Saegusa's recognition;
- Rio/Aoi LizNoir's performance;
- Haruko's peer/senior relation;
- Makino's management;
- Hoshimi's material platform.

Then Mana becomes causal for others, including Rio's return to singing.

The mature structure is a loop, not a ray:

> **people make idols who then make other people capable of becoming idols.**

That is a more powerful formulation than “Mana's light is inherited,” because it preserves Mana's greatness without treating her as metaphysically or socially self-caused.

## 8.2 Memory can be communal without being compulsory

Kokoro is indispensable to this conclusion.

E1-A includes:

- sister memory;
- manager memory;
- rival memory;
- friend/senior memory;
- bodily inheritance;
- later-entry non-witness skepticism.

The institution remains legitimate only if these positions do not have to collapse into one approved relationship with Mana.

This gives the project a sharper distinction between:

- **shared ritual**;
- **shared emotion**.

Hoshimi can share the first without demanding the second.

## 8.3 Songs are inheritance technology

Three E1-A song cases establish the pattern:

### `song for you`

Specific origin and authorial history remain, but later listeners produce multiple directions of address.

### `星の海の記憶`

A Mana song becomes part of Rio's survival history and later an explicitly reciprocal performance between Mana's sister and the rival whose own performance helped create Mana's vocation.

### Haruko/Mana gratitude song

An unfinished two-person project expands into institution-wide authorship and performance while preserving its original gratitude and names.

The governing principle is:

> **transferability without amnesia**.

A song does not become ethically available because its provenance no longer matters. It becomes ethically available because provenance can be carried into a new act rather than used to prohibit all new acts.

## 8.4 Hoshimi is continuity without sameness

Across E1-A, Hoshimi successfully holds:

- a dead idol's birthday ritual;
- a skeptic who never met her;
- her sister;
- her heart recipient;
- former professional rivals;
- IIIX's active hostility and opportunism;
- an older idol from the founding period;
- new unit constitutions;
- ongoing internal competition.

This is why “family” is an insufficient primary model.

Family language may still appear and may be emotionally meaningful, but E1-A's stronger institutional feature is **standing without sameness**.

Members can remain:

- rivals;
- skeptics;
- difficult outsiders;
- historical seniors;
- younger successors;
- managers and staff with differentiated authority.

The institution is healthy to the extent that those distinctions remain answerable rather than being dissolved into belonging rhetoric.

## 8.5 Rivalry is a continuity mechanism

Rivalry repeatedly carries history forward:

- Sakura wants IIIX's continued challenge;
- IIIX wants to conquer Hoshimi from within;
- Rio preserves Mana as a rival rather than a saint;
- Haruko remembers Mana as someone who made her keep trying;
- current Hoshimi idols can compete even within a gratitude live.

This supports the Tier-A claim that competition is legitimate when it does not become ontological erasure.

## 8.6 Persona is production, not automatic falsity

kana's selfie work provides an early compact case.

The post is staged.
The wording is strategic.
The senior/junior image is commercially useful.
The interaction is nevertheless real enough to become part of both idols' relationship.

E1-A therefore strengthens the series-wide hypothesis that authenticity is not absence of mediation.

A more defensible working formulation is:

> **authenticity concerns whether a performed form can remain answerable to the person and relationships it organizes, not whether the form was spontaneous or commercially useful.**

This remains provisional pending later persona-heavy events.

# 13. E1-A contribution to the full-series model

The Tier-A baseline proposed **answerable interdependence**: people can be profoundly shaped by others without surrendering authorship, provided the relation preserves standing to answer, revise, refuse, compete, and continue.

E1-A supplies the memory/inheritance version of that thesis.

A person can inherit:

- a heart;
- a song;
- an agency;
- a rivalry;
- a promise;
- a public memory;
- a professional opportunity;
- another person's belief.

The ethical question is not whether the inheritance is “really theirs” in a purity sense.

It is:

> **Can the recipient answer it without being required to become its replica?**

Kokoro answers Mana by not becoming a mourner.
IIIX answers Hoshimi by joining without becoming sweet.
Rio answers Mana by preserving rivalry rather than worship.
Kotono answers Mana by inviting rivals into the songs.
Haruko answers Mana by giving an unfinished song forward while refusing to disappear from the stage herself.
Current Hoshimi answers its tiny founding form by becoming a large, internally competitive institution.

That is why E1-A belongs before the later event tranches. It establishes the rules under which later event development can count as continuity rather than contradiction.
