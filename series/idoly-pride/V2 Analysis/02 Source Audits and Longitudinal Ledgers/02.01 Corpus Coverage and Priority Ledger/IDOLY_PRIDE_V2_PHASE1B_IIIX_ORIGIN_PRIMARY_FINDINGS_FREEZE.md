# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — IIIX ORIGIN PRIMARY FINDINGS FREEZE

## 0. Freeze statement

This document records the source-native findings from all four IIIX origin bundles **before any historical IIIX analytical prose is consulted**.

The inherited state is limited to already-admitted V2 material: the frozen anime endpoint, Hoshimi anime/game audit, and completed SUNNY PEACE, Tsuki no Tempest, LizNoir, and TRINITYAiLE origin tranches. Tokyo main-story material and later event/card/bond/message material are excluded from determining this finding set.

This freeze is especially important because IIIX is a **hard prerequisite** for the Tokyo main-story pass. The endpoint here is not merely backstory. It is the state that Tokyo must inherit prospectively.

# 2. Source-native executive thesis

The four origin bundles establish two different IIIXs.

The **first IIIX** is an externally assembled corporate project. Preta Porte selects three already marketable women, assigns the unit concept, chooses fran as center by executive committee, supplies songs/costumes/advertising, and explicitly names the group after the diamond-cut grade `3EX / Triple Excellent`. Its members initially do not share a common idol vocation:

- **miho** carries an older idol vocation distorted by bereavement, failed recognition, and a promise to 藤代葉;
- **fran** treats idol work principally as a means to visibility, money, debt repayment, and eventual fashion entrepreneurship;
- **kana** treats public visibility as both self-branding and a search signal for an absent father.

Their first success therefore does not produce intimacy. It validates a corporate hypothesis: elite ingredients plus technical optimization can win even when the people involved do not like one another.

The **second IIIX** is born only after that structure collapses.

By the end of `Re-Polished`, Preta Porte is withdrawing from idol operations, Himeno has been exposed and arrested, work is disappearing, IIIX has lost I-UNITY, and the three explicitly agree to dissolve. They then perform at a reduced venue where the audience is small enough to see as individual people. The performance reactivates pleasures each had suppressed or instrumentalized: miho reconnects with the original promise to make spectators happy, fran recognizes that idol work is not her first love but is genuinely something she likes, and kana discovers that direct audience response is exhilarating rather than merely quantifiable visibility.

They then **choose to continue together despite still denying friendship and despite no corporation requiring the unit to exist**.

The strongest source-native unit thesis is therefore:

> **IIIX begins as a coalition of instrumental motives held together by externally rewarded victory, and becomes a self-authored professional compact only after the institution that assembled it can no longer guarantee status, money, or success.**

A second formulation captures the unit's unusual relational ethic:

> **IIIX does not require sentimental harmony to produce real loyalty. Its mature bond is adversarial interdependence: the members may dislike one another, but they trust one another's competence, refuse to be made accessories to external corruption, and repeatedly choose the same difficult partners because those partners can meet them at full strength.**

A third proposition is crucial for Tokyo:

> **The mature IIIX distinction is not “winning versus caring.” It is winning as proof of fully authored performance versus winning whose meaning has been contaminated by somebody else's manipulation.**

The unit openly optimizes for the VENUS Program, studies scoring mechanics, and seeks overwhelming results. The text does not present this as fraud. Their scores are possible because they can execute the technical strategy at extraordinary level. Their eventual conflict with Himeno arises because external sabotage and rigging destroy the evidentiary meaning of victory.

# 3. Four-stage chronology and disclosure map

## 3.1 `Diamond Mining` — corporate extraction and first founding

The opening frame occurs after the I-UNITY final, but the substantive story returns to miho's pre-IIIX history and the corporate assembly of the unit.

The metaphor of mining is exact. Preta Porte does not grow three idols together from a shared dream. It **extracts already valuable properties** from three careers:

- miho: established idol experience and VENUS literacy;
- fran: Paris Collection modeling prestige, fashion credibility, and high visual marketability;
- kana: long child-model career, enormous social-media reach, and public recognizability.

The agency supplies the cut and setting. `ⅢX` is explicitly derived from the top diamond cut grade `3EX`, and the group is a company-bet project.

## 3.2 `House of Cards` — defeat exposes the absence of shared meaning

The unit wins immediately and rapidly rises. That success allows the members to avoid confronting the fact that they have almost no shared account of what they are doing.

The first VENUS Grand Prix defeat to STROBOLIGHTS forces the question.

miho asks internally:

`勝ち続けることが出来ないなら――`

`私は、この子達と一緒にいる意味はあるのかしら？`

The answer that follows is not friendship. The three disclose their private motives and create an explicit compact:

> they do not have to like one another; they do not have to share the same final dream; they do have to cooperate completely in order not to lose again.

This is why `House of Cards` is an accurate title. Victory gives the arrangement a stable shape without yet giving it broad ethical depth. If victory disappears, the apparent reason for remaining together is threatened.

## 3.3 `Gears Go Awry` — the victory machine acquires a corrupt operator

Nine months after the first Grand Prix defeat, IIIX defeats STROBOLIGHTS by an enormous margin and enters BIG4. More than a year later, they remain elite and have hardened `獅子搏兎`—use full force even against weak prey—into professional doctrine.

Himeno Kiriko then enters as a producer who understands each member's instrumental desire and offers them highly targeted opportunities.

Her strategy is effective precisely because early IIIX has already accepted a highly transactional model of professional life.

The dangerous transition is:

> **rules-aware optimization → tolerating extra-performance manipulation because it appears to serve the same end.**

The members suspect Himeno's conduct well before the final crisis. Kana identifies her as resembling the exploitative grandfather who monetized and indebted her; fran and miho discuss the possibility that Himeno engineered Asakura's arrest and the industry council; miho understands that I-UNITY may be designed to place power in Himeno's hands.

Yet IIIX continues because Himeno's actions seem to align with the unit's winning interest.

## 3.4 `Re-Polished` — a second founding after loss

The fourth block reverses the original construction process.

At first, the three were selected by executives and held together by a promised upward trajectory.

At the end:

- Himeno's scheme fails;
- IIIX loses to SUNNY PEACE;
- Preta Porte's idol business collapses;
- sponsors and media work retreat;
- the members conclude that the victory-only compact has no remaining reason to exist;
- they say goodbye.

Only then do they discover that some reasons to perform survive the removal of the original reward structure.

The reduced-venue performance in `獅子搏兎` is therefore a genuine **second founding scene**.

The three do not become conventional friends. Instead they discover that:

- they enjoy the stage more than they admitted;
- audience response contains information their technical model underweighted;
- each sees the others as unusually capable peers;
- none wants to end on a performance model they now know was incomplete;
- two years of mutual antagonism has itself become a form of continuity (`腐れ縁`).

Their subsequent decision to operate freelance, secure office/legal structures, contact industry networks, and seek work without Preta Porte converts IIIX from **corporate product** into **co-produced professional entity**.

# 4. Takeda Mihoko / miho — grief becomes a theory of historical justice, then partially returns to vocation

## 4.1 Her original idol vocation is relational and audience-facing

miho did not begin with score maximization or revenge.

Her only close friend, 藤代葉, introduced her to idol lives and invited her to audition. Their founding fantasy is explicitly prosocial:

`魔法少女みたいに、私達も`

`ステージで誰かを幸せな気持ちに出来たらいいね`

Sundance therefore begins as:

> friendship + transformation + audience happiness.

This matters because later miho often sounds as though idolhood has always meant rank, proof, and record destruction. The origin shows this is a **distorted continuation**, not the original vocation.

## 4.2 The injury is not simply “Mana beat us”

葉's death occurs just as Sundance is approaching BIG4 level. Her final message is notably generous:

`あたしに囚われないで、新しい仲間とトップを取って`

and she says her remaining regret is only that she never once defeated Mana.

The psychologically decisive event follows immediately afterward. Mana's fatal accident dominates public attention while 葉's death receives almost no coverage.

miho experiences this not merely as rival jealousy but as **unequal public mourning**:

`葉ちゃんの死も悲しんでくれてもいいじゃない！！`

This converts competitive frustration into a theory of memory injustice.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** miho's later wish to “erase” Mana is a displaced attempt to reverse a perceived injustice in whose death counted publicly.

The logic is retaliatory symmetry:

> Mana's death erased 葉 from public attention; therefore miho will erase Mana from public memory.

That does not make the project ethically defensible. It does make it causally more precise than simple hatred of a superior idol.

## 4.3 She misreads 葉's promise by turning freedom into jurisdiction

葉 explicitly tells miho not to remain trapped by her.

miho converts the promise into:

- become top idol;
- surpass Mana;
- overwrite Mana's records;
- eventually remove Mana's songs and public traces.

This resembles a recurring franchise structure already visible in Kotono: loving evidence from the dead can be converted by the survivor into a mission the dead person did not actually issue.

But the cases are not identical. Kotono's proxy mission begins from sisterhood and unfinished work; miho's begins from grief plus a public-recognition grievance and becomes openly retaliatory.

## 4.4 Her greatest professional strength becomes part of the distortion

miho is the unit's experienced idol and scoring analyst. She understands VENUS mechanics, opponent histories, technical point reliability, audience-point volatility, and how to optimize timing and choreography for machine evaluation.

This competence is real.

The problem is not that she “cheats the algorithm.” The problem is that she increasingly treats the reliably measurable as the reliably meaningful.

The I-UNITY final exposes the gap. SUNNY PEACE's improvised/acappella response to sabotage creates a singular audience event that technical planning could not guarantee.

miho can immediately explain what happened in VENUS terms, but later recognizes a deeper difference:

> she was chasing Mana's legend; SUNNY PEACE was producing a new legend.

## 4.5 `何か、何かおかしい` is a self-recognition hinge, not full recovery

After defeating Tsuki, miho discovers the anticipated emotional payoff does not arrive.

She remembers the first promise:

`ステージで誰かを幸せな気持ちに出来たらいいね`

and asks:

`私、貴方の願いをちゃんと叶えられてる…？`

This is a genuine crack in the revenge structure.

However, the origin does **not** end with miho abandoning the wish to overwrite Mana. Even after going freelance she still says she will not retire until Mana's memory is painted over.

Therefore the correct pre-Tokyo state is:

> **miho has recovered access to her original audience-facing vocation without yet fully relinquishing the retaliatory historical project that grew around 葉's death.**

This unresolved contradiction must enter Tokyo intact.

## 4.6 A small audience changes the epistemology of performance

The late reduced-venue live is crucial because miho can see individual faces.

A Sundance-era fan remembers both her and 葉. That directly falsifies the totalizing grief belief that 葉 disappeared from everyone's memory.

Then, onstage:

`人は少ないけど、そのぶん顔は見えるものね`

`……気持ちいい……！！`

The scene reconnects audience response to **persons**, not only to aggregate VENUS scores.

That is a major pre-Tokyo development.

# 5. fran — instrumental idolhood with a non-negotiable aesthetic floor

## 5.1 Fashion transformed stigmatized difference into value

fran grows up disliking the conspicuous blond hair/blue eyes inherited from her German mother because she associates visible difference with social exclusion.

Fashion reverses the valence. A dress makes her feel transformable; modeling turns the features she disliked into professional assets; she reaches Paris Collection level.

Her first deep vocation is therefore not idolhood but **fashion as authored self-presentation**.

## 5.2 Idol work begins as capital acquisition

fran says directly:

`私にとって、アイドルはただの通過点に過ぎないの`

She wants higher-paying commercial work and broader fame so that she can finance a fashion future.

The later disclosure sharpens this dramatically: her first brand, `ERFOLG` (“success”), failed after she overextended into expensive production, leaving her with roughly **100 million yen in debt**.

She therefore needs:

- money;
- professional visibility;
- networks;
- business education;
- a second attempt at building a globally viable brand.

Her stock-trading, economics reading, cost sensitivity, and later concern with rent/incorporation/legal counsel are not random gags. They are continuity evidence for a character whose creative ambition has been financially disciplined by failure.

## 5.3 Cynicism is bounded by aesthetics

fran is openly transactional and can be caustic about the idol industry.

But her pragmatism has a limit:

`でもね、これでも私はデザイナーなのよ`

`美的センスまで売り渡すつもりはないわ！`

This is the clearest source-native explanation for why she ultimately turns against Himeno.

Himeno's sabotage is not merely risky business. It is **ugly**—small, cowardly, reputationally contaminating, and unnecessary to people fran believes can win through performance.

fran's mature professional ethic is therefore not anti-business. It is closer to:

> **if performance is business, the product still has to be one she can stand behind.**

## 5.4 “If I'm doing it anyway” becomes real commitment

The fourth block begins:

`アイドルが好きだと思ったことは一度もない`

`でも――どうせやるのなら`

`せっかくなら可愛く、格好良くやりたいじゃない`

By the reduced-venue live, she revises even the first sentence:

`私も……別に、アイドルをやるのは嫌いじゃなかったし`

and then:

`ファッションの次くらいには好きでもいいでしょ`

This is one of the cleanest examples in the corpus of a vocation becoming **secondary but genuine**.

Idolhood never replaces fashion. It becomes something fran wants to execute well because she has developed an aesthetic investment in what the work can be.

## 5.5 Center status is corporate assignment, later inhabited rather than mystified

fran is made center by executive decision because she best fits the unit concept `女性が憧れる女性`.

The source does not establish that this means she is universally the strongest performer. It establishes that Preta Porte sees her image as the most suitable focal point for the product.

Over time fran does inhabit center responsibility, but the origin keeps the institutional origin visible.

# 6. kana — visibility as vanity, labor, and attachment search

## 6.1 Her self-display has a real attachment history

kana's mother died giving birth. Her father was often away for work but, when present, spent intensive time with her. Then her grandfather tells her that her father is ill and will no longer return.

She waits.

Afterward the grandfather places her into child modeling and exploits her name commercially, including taking on debt through it. Other industry adults recognize the grandfather as harmful, and kana eventually leaves him and protects her address.

Her public visibility therefore has multiple motives at once:

- she genuinely enjoys attention;
- she has deep professional social-media fluency;
- popularity is a competitive scoreboard;
- **being visible everywhere is a strategy for making herself findable by her missing father.**

She says explicitly that if she is on television and the internet constantly, perhaps he will see her and contact her.

This does not make her vanity false. It makes vanity and attachment-seeking coexist.

## 6.2 Distrust is learned rather than merely bratty

kana rejects the standard idol-language of instant friendship:

`赤の他人にべったりで`

`何から何まで信じるなんてあり得ない`

The next internal line supplies the reason:

`実の家族にだって信じられない奴がいるのに`

She has learned that kinship itself does not guarantee safety.

This is why her unusually fast suspicion of Himeno deserves high evidentiary weight. She says Himeno feels like her grandfather **before** the others accept the risk, and subsequent events vindicate the warning.

## 6.3 kana has significant industry-adult literacy

The origin gives her more social intelligence than her abrasive persona initially suggests.

She knows when to switch into polished child-star behavior for important industry figures, understands public relations and SNS maintenance, notices exploitative-adult patterns, and later offers an unexpectedly calibrated evaluation of Makino:

> good-hearted adults like him sometimes survive and rise in entertainment, though most disappear; because he worked with Mana, he may “have something.”

This is not sentimental trust. It is professional pattern recognition.

## 6.4 Her idol pleasure becomes less purely abstract at the small venue

kana initially talks about audience value in terms of followers, trends, attention, and being the most visible person.

At the reduced venue she can see the people responding directly and says the experience was `サイコー`.

She immediately remains kana—she frames the spectators as people worshipping her—but the performance teaches her that audience response is not only a remote count.

She also recognizes that IIIX could have used this kind of direct appeal more effectively at I-UNITY.

Thus the late development is not “kana becomes humble.” It is:

> **kana expands the domain of what counts as useful/pleasurable visibility from metrics to embodied response.**

# 7. IIIX relational architecture — adversarial interdependence

## 7.1 The unit refuses compulsory friendship performance

The members repeatedly insist that they dislike one another.

They mock the industry's expectation that an idol group publicly perform intimate friendship, and kana calls that demand one of the most irritating parts of idol work.

The text therefore should not be overwritten with a conventional “they were friends all along” thesis.

## 7.2 Their behavior nevertheless exceeds pure transaction

The opposite flattening is also wrong.

Across the origins they:

- defend one another against external pressure;
- notice and respond to distress;
- disclose humiliating personal histories after defeat;
- trust each other's professional competence;
- tolerate personality traits they openly dislike;
- refuse to let Himeno's corruption attach itself to the whole unit;
- choose the same partners again when no agency compels them to do so.

fran tells kana not to bully miho too far. kana identifies the threat in Himeno before it directly harms her. fran notices when miho is struck and asks whether she needs medical attention. All three reorganize around the others' constraints in the freelance transition.

Therefore:

> **IIIX is neither false friendship nor absence of relationship. It is a deliberately non-sentimental form of loyalty.**

The members' own preferred vocabulary is closer to `腐れ縁`, competence, irritation, usefulness, and shared victory than to affection.

## 7.3 Victory is initially the only admitted shared object

The compact after the first defeat is explicit:

- miho wants to fulfill 葉's promise and overwrite Mana;
- fran wants money, debt repayment, and a future fashion brand;
- kana wants visibility sufficient to be found by her father;
- all three hate losing.

They therefore agree to cooperate fully around one shared object: **victory**.

This is why the I-UNITY defeat is existential for the first version of the unit. If victory is the only admitted common property, defeat appears to remove the relationship's justification.

## 7.4 The second founding discovers shared craft beneath shared victory

The apparent dissolution is reversed only when the three discover something they had not admitted:

> they have developed a **shared way of working** that has value even when the guaranteed upward reward disappears.

They know one another's pace, standards, habits, and capacity. They can challenge one another at a level new partners may not sustain. Each is annoyed by the others precisely because she has detailed expectations of them.

That is the mature pre-Tokyo bond.

# 8. Competitive philosophy — optimization, proof, and the line between gaming and corruption

## 8.1 “Point farmer” is partly true and analytically incomplete

miho openly explains the VENUS Program's measurement logic and the existence of reliable scoring strategies.

IIIX emphasizes:

- synchronized steps;
- stable pitch;
- technically measurable execution;
- repeatability;
- reduction of negative variance.

Their method is unusually compatible with machine evaluation.

But the source repeatedly emphasizes that knowing the scoring logic is not enough. The strategy only works because the three can execute at extreme level.

Thus:

> **IIIX does optimize the measurement system, but optimization is evidence of professional intelligence plus real skill, not a substitute for skill.**

## 8.2 Technical score is chosen partly because it is controllable

miho distinguishes technical points from audience-response points. Audience points can produce spectacular outcomes, but are volatile. Technical excellence offers a more reliable path.

This explains the unit's philosophy better than “they don't care about audiences.”

The early IIIX wants **controllable evidence of superiority**.

## 8.3 SUNNY PEACE exposes what control cannot guarantee

The I-UNITY final creates a perfect adversarial case.

Himeno's sabotage produces an audio failure. SUNNY PEACE converts that failure into an acappella performance that dominates the room.

IIIX recognizes two things simultaneously:

1. under normal conditions, they still believe they would beat SUNNY PEACE repeatedly;
2. in this singular performance, SUNNY PEACE went above them.

That willingness to acknowledge a specific superior event is important. Their pride is severe but not completely epistemically closed.

## 8.4 Rule-aware optimization and external manipulation are ethically different

The source draws a strong boundary between:

- learning how VENUS scoring works and performing to maximize score;
- bribery, fabricated scandal, coercive walkovers, sabotage, rights manipulation, and industry capture.

fran states the practical/ethical line most clearly: IIIX has always won by its own ability, and Himeno's “small tricks” are both ugly and liability-producing.

The group's resistance is therefore not a late discovery that competition is bad.

It is:

> **victory ceases to prove strength when somebody outside the performance has already degraded the opponent or contaminated the conditions of comparison.**

This distinction must govern the Tokyo read.

# 9. Himeno Kiriko and institutional capture

## 9.1 Himeno does not create IIIX's instrumentalism; she exploits it

The three already have transactional motives before Himeno arrives.

Her skill is identifying them:

- kana receives visibility-rich television work;
- fran receives fashion/network opportunities;
- miho is offered leverage over Mana's posthumous legacy.

This makes cooperation feel like rational exchange rather than corruption.

## 9.2 The unit possesses warning signals but rationalizes them

kana's grandfather analogy is the earliest moral alarm.

miho and fran later infer that Himeno may have engineered Asakura's downfall and the industry council. miho understands that I-UNITY can hand Himeno broad institutional power.

The failure is therefore not ignorance.

It is **motivated toleration**:

> if Himeno's manipulation points in the same direction as IIIX's desired outcome, the members initially believe they can use her without becoming responsible for her.

The I-UNITY sequence demonstrates why that firewall fails.

## 9.3 miho has the greatest individual complicity

fran and kana increasingly object to Himeno's methods because the methods are ugly, risky, and unnecessary.

miho continues longer because Himeno offers something uniquely aligned with her retaliatory Mana project.

When Himeno proposes obtaining Mana's music rights, miho initially distinguishes surpassing Mana from possessing the songs. Later, however, she openly adopts the plan to erase Mana's musical traces after victory.

Thus miho's grief makes her **more institutionally vulnerable** than the other two despite her greater idol-industry experience.

## 9.4 The unit eventually refuses Himeno on professional grounds

Their break with Himeno is not idealized moral purification.

They still want to win. They still dislike Hoshimi. They still speak harshly.

What changes is that they refuse to let her methods define the evidence of their strength.

fran's business language—cut a position once it exceeds the loss line—is revealing. IIIX uses the very instrumental rationality Himeno exploited to reject her when corruption becomes a threat to the product, the performers, and their autonomy.

# 10. Pre-Tokyo institutional state — corporate product becomes self-managed unit

This is the most important practical dependency result.

At the end of the origins:

- Himeno is arrested;
- Asakura's arrest is publicly revealed as false;
- Preta Porte is withdrawing from idol operations;
- IIIX leaves the agency;
- the three continue as freelance idols;
- fran handles the economics of office location, incorporation, and legal counsel;
- miho activates old industry relationships to seek work;
- kana thinks in terms of visibility, market access, and practical employment consequences;
- all three expect worse commercial terms than under Preta Porte but choose continuation anyway.

Therefore Tokyo must **not** begin from the assumption that IIIX is merely Himeno's weapon or Preta Porte's managed product.

The governing pre-Tokyo baseline is:

> **IIIX enters the next stage as a self-selected, increasingly self-managed professional unit whose members remain fiercely competitive and personally abrasive but have already rejected institutional sabotage as a substitute for proving strength.**

This does not imply ethical sainthood.

They remain:

- highly status-conscious;
- willing to humiliate competitors verbally;
- intensely victory-oriented;
- openly interested in money, attention, and legacy;
- comfortable analyzing scoring systems strategically;
- skeptical of conventional idol friendship norms.

What has changed is **ownership of the unit**.

# 11. Relationship to Hoshimi and Makino before Tokyo

## 11.1 Hoshimi is first encountered as both threat and epistemic problem

IIIX initially interprets Hoshimi's rise through the language of audience points, Mana-linked attention, and unpredictable score dynamics.

They then confront individual Hoshimi figures during I-UNITY and are forced to acknowledge that the group's improvement cannot be dismissed as mere publicity.

## 11.2 miho recognizes Makino's continuity with Mana

miho remembers seeing the young Makino around Mana before he became the Hoshimi manager.

This is useful continuity evidence for the project's governing identity rule: the manager she encounters in the game is naturally recognized as the same person associated with Mana's earlier professional life.

The origin does not treat him as a new anonymous player avatar.

## 11.3 kana's assessment is skeptical but positive

kana identifies Makino as a rare good-hearted entertainment adult who may have survived because he possesses unusual professional value, while fran notes that someone who worked with Mana may indeed “have something.”

This is not friendship or trust. It is the beginning of **professional recognition**.

## 11.4 The origin endpoint deliberately routes IIIX back to Hoshimi

The final story has all three summoned to Hoshimi Production after going freelance.

miho narrates:

`そこから先のことは、また別の話`

That endpoint functions as an explicit bridge into the next governing narrative layer.

For V2 sequencing purposes, this is exactly why the origin is a hard prerequisite before Tokyo.

# 12. Open tensions that Tokyo must inherit rather than solve retrospectively

## 12.1 miho's Mana project is not resolved

She has recovered the original audience-happiness promise but still states that she intends to overwrite Mana's memory.

**UNRESOLVED CONFLICT:** vocation recovery and retaliatory legacy politics coexist.

Tokyo must be allowed to determine whether this contradiction changes.

## 12.2 IIIX still defines itself heavily through victory

The small-venue live broadens the meaning of performance, but the unit's public and internal creed remains:

`いつだって全身全霊で――`

`最強を証明し続ける`

`全ては絶対勝利のために`

The origin does not replace competitive identity with audience service. It adds a second source of meaning.

## 12.3 The members still deny friendship

Their re-formation is voluntary but explicitly non-sentimental.

Whether later stories change the vocabulary or only deepen the same adversarial bond is an open longitudinal question.

## 12.4 Their management structure is newly fragile

Going freelance increases autonomy but removes Preta Porte's infrastructure, money, booking power, and producer layer.

The final Hoshimi summons occurs while IIIX is still solving:

- office/location costs;
- incorporation;
- legal representation;
- work acquisition;
- public reputation after Himeno;
- market access.

This creates a materially different bargaining position from the one they occupied as BIG4 under a large agency.

# 14. Primary-source close-reading conclusion

The origin corpus makes IIIX one of the franchise's strongest tests of whether moral and relational legitimacy requires conventional niceness.

They are abrasive. They are proud. They care about money, visibility, status, and winning. They optimize an AI scoring system. They sometimes speak with real cruelty. They cooperate with a corrupt producer longer than they should because her actions appear useful.

But the corpus refuses the simple inference that instrumental motives make them hollow.

Instead, it repeatedly tests whether something can become authentic **after beginning instrumentally**:

- fran can begin idol work for money and still develop real artistic investment;
- kana can begin modeling under exploitation and still make public performance genuinely hers;
- miho can begin the second career as grief-driven revenge and still recover the first promise to make spectators happy;
- three people can be assembled by a corporation and later become a unit they themselves choose.

The governing pre-Tokyo proposition is therefore:

> **IIIX's authenticity does not come from pure motives at origin. It comes from repeated acts of re-authorization: deciding, after the original incentives and institutions fail, that the work, the partners, and the competitive standard are still worth choosing.**

That state must be frozen before Tokyo begins.
