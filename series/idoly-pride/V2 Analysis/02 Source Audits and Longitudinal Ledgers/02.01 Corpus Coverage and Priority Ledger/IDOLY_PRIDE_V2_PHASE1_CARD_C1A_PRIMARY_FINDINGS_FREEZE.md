# 1. Tranche-level result — continuity without restoration

The seven cards converge on a pattern that is stronger than ordinary “memory” or “legacy” language:

> **`LEGACY_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_RESTORATION`**
>
> A lost, unavailable, or failed relational configuration can remain constitutive without being literally restored. Its meaning can be carried forward through re-authored work, embodied practice, later vocation, public transmission, or care for another person.

This is a **candidate C1-A refinement**, not yet a replacement for the series-level architecture. It extends the already-admitted `ROLE_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_ROLE_CAPTIVITY` into temporal and memorial domains.

The mechanism appears differently across the tranche:

- Kana does **not** recover the ordinary family she wanted, but memories of her father and the work originally undertaken to reach him become usable as care for fans and another displaced child.
- Haruko cannot literally perform beside Mana, but later inhabits the very lead role Mana once played and treats that succession as fulfillment of a promise in altered form.
- miho cannot recreate Sundance with Yō, so she refuses simple replication: `Friend Glass` is re-authored through IIIX while the original remains preserved and newly discoverable.
- miho's hair is not a static relic kept only because Yō once praised it; Yō's recognition becomes miho's own pleasure, pride, and bodily self-valuing practice.

This tranche therefore distinguishes **continuity** from **reconstruction**. The past remains active precisely because the present is allowed to differ from it.

# 2. kana — father, family, visibility, and the re-authoring of failed purpose

## 2.1 The “abandoned child” formulation is too strong

`st-card-kan-05-fest-02-01` explicitly presents a tabloid headline claiming that Kana lost her mother and was then abandoned by her beloved father. Kana rejects the strong abandonment claim:

- living apart was true;
- she says she was **not** abandoned;
- they remained in contact;
- he sent presents;
- later material confirms that he watched her television work and regularly sent reactions;
- she sent him emails and photographs.

Admit as source-native correction candidate:

> **`PATERNAL_CONTACT_WITHOUT_CORESIDENCE_KANA`**

However, this does **not** establish an ordinary intact father-daughter relationship.

The same card supplies the counterweight:

- her father tells Makino he had only recently seen Kana in person;
- father and daughter had never taken a photograph together;
- Kana hides her normal abrasive manner because she wants her father to think she is a “good child”;
- she says he was not a good father either;
- she says she does not really understand what a normal family is;
- he now has a wife and daughter separate from Kana;
- she ultimately says she had worked for years hoping to live happily with him but **“結局、家族にはなれなかった”**.

The correct combined formulation is therefore:

> **`CONTACT_WITHOUT_FAMILY_RECONSTITUTION`**
>
> Kana's father was not wholly absent, but contact, gifts, mediated spectatorship, and affection did not become shared family life. The wound is better modeled as failed family reconstitution than as total disappearance.

Do not flatten this into either “her father abandoned her completely” or “they were actually a normal loving family.” Both erase source-native contradiction.

## 2.2 Idol visibility initially had a private addressee

Kana says that television work allowed her father to see her, that his reactions encouraged her, and that since childhood she had wanted him to see her perform live at least once.

TEXTUAL FACT:

- being visible through entertainment work partly served as a bridge to a physically distant father;
- seeing him attend the American festival fulfills a long-standing childhood wish and is described publicly as a major turning point.

Strong inference:

> **`PATERNAL_VISIBILITY_AS_ORIGINAL_VOCATIONAL_MOTIVE`**

This should not be inflated into the claim that every part of Kana's career existed only for her father. The card shows a major motive, not exhaustive causation.

## 2.3 Kana rejects imposed victim narration by producing a counter-public account

Kana's response to the tabloid is not simple concealment. She constructs an alternative interview and deliberately times publication so that the tabloid becomes stale follow-up coverage.

Admit as candidate:

> **`PUBLIC_SELF_NARRATION_AS_DEFENSIVE_AUTHORSHIP`**

The card complicates “truth,” however. Kana's counter-interview contains genuine facts, but also stages a more harmonious father-daughter image than private experience supports. Makino explicitly recognizes that the public interview is partly Kana's idealized version.

Rule:

> **Self-authorship does not guarantee documentary transparency. Kana's counter-narrative is ethically and psychologically meaningful because it refuses a third party's ownership of her family wound, even while she selectively performs the family relationship she wished she had.**

This is especially relevant to `ANSWERABLE_CONSTRUCTION`: constructed public narrative is not automatically false in every dimension, but its authority must remain bounded.

## 2.4 The original private purpose fails; vocational meaning does not

Kana privately states that she had worked so hard because she wanted to live happily with her father and that, since the family did not materialize, all that effort felt wasted.

The card then refuses that endpoint. Reading a fan who describes a painful home life, draws strength from Kana, and writes that Kana's broadcasts help her endure produces a visible re-evaluation:

> `アイドルしてたこと……全部が全部、無駄って訳でもなかったなって`

Admit:

> **`VOCATIONAL_MEANING_CAN_OUTLIVE_FAILED_ORIGINAL_PURPOSE`**

Kana's work can cease to accomplish the childhood end that helped motivate it and still acquire reciprocal public meaning independent of that original goal.

This is not a claim that the family wound is healed. It is a claim that failed private purpose does not retroactively nullify every consequence of the work.

# 3. kana — memory transmission and the Kuromi corroboration

## 3.1 The later card independently recalls the “dream amusement park” encounter

`st-card-kan-05-snro-00-03` contains explicit retrospective dialogue:

- Kana says she recently went to a `夢の遊園地`;
- she says she met **Kuromi** there;
- that encounter caused her to remember a happy childhood amusement-park experience with her father.

This is direct card-level corroboration of retained memory.

Prospective status:

> **`OPEN_VERIFY_KANA_CARD_CORROBORATION` → RESOLVE_PENDING_AUDIT**

The later ordinary card independently establishes that Kana remembers the earlier Kuromi encounter and treats its emotional consequence as part of her own remembered history.

Critical safeguard:

> This does **not** by itself prove that every supernatural, crossover, ontological, or formal mechanism of the earlier Sanrio material should be treated as ordinary mainline world mechanics. The corroborated proposition is Kana's retained memory/reference, not unrestricted crossover ontology.

## 3.2 Kana recognizes structural unfairness without pretending she can fix it

The amusement-park card places Kana with a child from a residential care institution who attempts to run away. Kana recognizes the child's anger at rules created by adult institutional necessity and explicitly says she cannot change the child's environment; she can only understand why it feels unfair and listen.

Candidate support:

> **`CARE_WITHOUT_FALSE_RESCUE_PROMISE`**

This is unusually disciplined care for Kana: she does not sentimentalize herself as savior and does not deny the institution's coordination problem. She gives the child limited discretionary relief—time to enjoy the park—while still requiring a return.

## 3.3 Positive memory becomes a resource Kana intentionally transmits

Kana explains that the child's day should not end as a purely bad memory. Her own happy memory of being at an amusement park with her father helped sustain her, even though the hoped-for family endpoint later failed.

Admit as candidate:

> **`MEMORY_AS_TRANSFERABLE_RESILIENCE`**

The important mechanism is not “good memories fix trauma.” It is narrower:

> A positive memory can remain psychologically usable even when the relationship or hope attached to it later disappoints, and a person who knows this may intentionally create a similar reserve of experience for someone else.

This provides one of the clearest Kana examples of private wound becoming outward care without being erased.

# 4. 成宮すず — from defensive resistance to self-authored vocation

## 4.1 Parental positions are split, not uniformly oppositional

`st-card-suz-05-anml-00-01` distinguishes Suzu's parents:

- her father had opposed idol work earlier but, after the Grand Prix, proudly displays her CDs, merchandise, and photographs and tells others his daughter is the world's cutest idol;
- her mother remains stricter and has previously appeared at a work site trying to make Suzu quit and study abroad.

The family model is therefore not generic “parents oppose idolhood.”

Candidate:

> **`SPLIT_PARENTAL_AUTHORIZATION_SUZU`**

## 4.2 The motive for staying an idol has changed

Suzu explicitly distinguishes past and present:

- previously she resisted studying abroad partly out of stubbornness;
- after performing with the others, being supported by fans, and standing on large stages, she now wants to continue idol work **regardless of the study-abroad dispute**.

Admit:

> **`DEFENSIVE_ENTRY_TO_SELF_AUTHORED_VOCATION`**

A vocation that partly began or persisted as resistance to parental control has acquired positive internal reasons of its own.

This is important because it prevents later parental conflict from being treated as the entire meaning of Suzu's idol identity.

## 4.3 Conditional maternal approval produces proof-seeking overwork

When Suzu's mother interprets lower solo visibility as evidence that idolhood is failing and again proposes study abroad, Suzu demands every available job, including work she would normally reject. She explicitly accepts a schedule with almost no rest and adds voluntary practice between jobs.

Candidate:

> **`PROOF_SEEKING_OVERWORK_UNDER_CONDITIONAL_AUTHORIZATION`**

The source boundary matters:

- the intensified workload is substantially **Suzu-directed**;
- Makino repeatedly warns her about rest and offers to decline work;
- after the immediate threat recedes, he says the workload will return to normal.

Therefore do **not** use this card as simple proof of exploitative scheduling by Hoshimi Production.

## 4.4 Small work becomes part of professional legitimacy

Suzu initially wants high-impact public proof, but while doing mall/department-store jobs she explicitly reframes small work as real idol labor: slowly increasing recognition, serving fans, and taking every assignment seriously.

Candidate support:

> **`SMALL_WORK_AS_PROFESSIONAL_LEGITIMACY`**

The mother's later email remains conditional. She praises Suzu's growth and puts study abroad **on hold**, but keeps it as a threat if Suzu fails expectations.

Therefore:

> **`MATERNAL_ACCEPTANCE_REMAINS_PROVISIONAL`**

Do not upgrade this scene into settled family approval.

# 5. fran — plural vocation under market discipline

## 5.1 Idol and designer are concurrent serious vocations

After the American performance, fran has restarted her brand and explicitly describes herself as having to do both idol and designer work. The ending is even clearer:

> `どっちでも、しっかりトップ取ってやりますから`

Admit:

> **`PLURAL_VOCATION_WITHOUT_HIERARCHY_FRAN`**

Neither vocation is framed as a temporary hobby subordinate to the other. She intends excellence in both.

## 5.2 The parallel profession strips away idol-domain status

At the apparel trade show, fran confronts a legitimacy problem:

- she lacks formal fashion-school credentials;
- competitors treat her as an artist/model playing at fashion;
- she says this dismissal has happened before;
- a previous attempt to prove herself through spectacular excess contributed to a debt she describes as `一億`.

Candidate:

> **`CROSS_DOMAIN_STATUS_RESET`**

Success and fame in one professional field do not automatically produce recognized standing in another.

This is a useful institutional counterweight to celebrity-capital narratives.

## 5.3 Failure changes strategy without extinguishing authorship

fran says she learned that before making only what she personally wants, she first needs recognition and customer trust. She therefore enters a teen market outside her usual design comfort zone and seeks advice from Kana because of Kana's modeling/background knowledge.

Admit:

> **`MARKET_DISCIPLINE_WITHOUT_ARTISTIC_SURRENDER`**

and

> **`FAILURE_REVISES_STRATEGY_NOT_VOCATIONAL_COMMITMENT`**

The source does not present market adaptation as capitulation. It is a staged strategy for earning room to exercise more autonomy later.

## 5.4 Skills transfer across roles, but only after adaptation

When the booth initially fails, fran and Makino deliberately borrow techniques from idol promotion:

- visual attention through stage costume;
- performance video;
- direct personal presence;
- customer interaction skills acquired through prior idol sales/handshake work;
- Makino's own early Hoshimi sales experience.

The transfer succeeds, but not because idol skill automatically equals fashion skill. fran still needs product knowledge, market selection, buyers, design adaptation, and trade-show logistics.

Candidate strengthening of the earlier transfer rule:

> **`CROSS_ROLE_COMPETENCE_TRANSFER_WITH_CONTEXTUAL_ADAPTATION`**

This card supplies a particularly clean positive case for the B2-B warning that **competence transfers more easily than forms**.

# 6. 佐伯遙子 — deferred succession and promise fulfillment

## 6.1 Haruko's early musical goal is relational before it is vocational

The flashback occurs roughly six months after Mana's debut. Haruko auditions for the protagonist's friend role because Mana has been cast as lead. Haruko explicitly says she is not especially knowledgeable about musicals; the central attraction is the chance to stand beside Mana, work with her, and sing on the same stage.

TEXTUAL FACT:

> The first audition is motivated primarily by relational co-performance with Mana, not by a pre-existing independent ambition to abandon idolhood for musical theatre.

This limits any claim that musical theatre was always Haruko's hidden “true” vocation.

## 6.2 Failure becomes a durable motivational event

Haruko fails the audition despite believing the performance had gone well. She breaks down privately with Mana, interprets the failure through her broader history of disappointing expectations, and asks that only Mana retain the memory of her crying.

Mana's response matters because it does not promise immediate success; it tells Haruko that the work will not be wasted and that if she keeps going, a future shared stage remains possible.

Candidate:

> **`FAILURE_MEMORY_AS_LONG_HORIZON_MOTIVATION`**

Haruko later explicitly says she kept singing and dancing because she believed Mana's words.

## 6.3 The later fulfillment is transformed rather than literal

In the present, the same musical is revived. Haruko not only passes the audition but receives the **lead role once played by Mana**, rather than the friend role she originally wanted.

She cannot literally co-perform with Mana. The card stages that fantasy and then explicitly marks it as imagination: `現実にはあり得ない` / a future that might once have existed.

The actual fulfillment is different:

- same work;
- same role Mana once played;
- same stage tradition;
- Haruko carrying forward the part after years of development.

Admit:

> **`DEFERRED_PERFORMANCE_SUCCESSION`**

and

> **`PROMISE_FULFILLMENT_THROUGH_ROLE_INHERITANCE_NOT_COPRESENCE`**

This is a paradigmatic instance of `LEGACY_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_RESTORATION`.

## 6.4 Musical work expands Haruko's performance field; it does not require idol exit

The present Haruko takes the musical role while remaining embedded in Sunny Peace and continuing to speak of the long history of singing/dancing practice. Earlier, a separate possible future as a musical actor had existed, but the realized route is hybrid rather than replacement.

Candidate:

> **`PLURAL_PERFORMANCE_VOCATION_WITHOUT_IDOL_EXIT_HARUKO`**

This strengthens Haruko's broader non-totalizing vocation model while leaving her long-run idol-career endpoint open.

# 7. miho — hair as recognition internalized into self-valuing practice

## 7.1 The black hair is not merely a professional image choice

`st-card-mhk-05-pajm-00-03` states directly that early miho simply let her hair grow and did not care deeply about it. A long-time friend praised it as beautiful; miho says that praise made her happy, caused her to like her own hair, and motivated her to care for it.

Admit:

> **`RELATIONAL_RECOGNITION_BECOMES_SELF_AUTHORED_EMBODIED_VALUE`**

This is more precise than “miho keeps her hair because Yō liked it.” The recognition originates relationally, but miho explicitly says **she herself came to like it**, and caring for it is now enjoyable and a source of pride.

## 7.2 Public image labor can contain private memorial meaning

The same hair is also professional capital: miho treats hair maintenance as idol labor and receives a hair-care commercial precisely because of that maintained image.

Yet during the interview, the private origin remains active. She imagines that her old friend would see the commercial and praise her again.

Candidate:

> **`PRIVATE_MEMORY_WITHIN_PUBLIC_IMAGE_LABOR`**

The professional and memorial meanings are not mutually exclusive.

## 7.3 Memory does not freeze the body into a relic

miho experiments with products, salons, styling, and commercial presentation. The meaningful continuity is not literal stasis; it is the value she learned to recognize in herself.

Candidate:

> **`MEMORIAL_PRACTICE_WITHOUT_STATIC_RELIC`**

Do not infer that changing her hairstyle would necessarily constitute betrayal of Yō. The card establishes the origin and current meaning of care, not an immutable prohibition on change.

# 8. miho — `Friend Glass`, guilt, reinterpretation, and post-loss companionship

## 8.1 Professional absence during Yō's hospitalization becomes explicit guilt

miho recounts a prior Taiwan tour undertaken alone while Yō was hospitalized. At the time she expected a temporary recovery and believed they would perform together again. Yō sent reassuring photographs during the tour; when miho returned, Yō had deteriorated severely.

miho's retrospective question is explicit:

> Why did I leave my friend behind? If I had known, I would not have left her side.

Admit as source-native wound:

> **`GUILT_OVER_PROFESSIONAL_ABSENCE_DURING_IRREVERSIBLE_LOSS`**

Safeguard:

> The card does not establish that miho knowingly chose career over a dying friend, nor that management knew the outcome and coerced her away. Her guilt is real; retrospective culpability is not established.

## 8.2 Yō's death makes exact reproduction of Sundance impossible

miho initially wants IIIX to cover Sundance's signature friendship song `Friend Glass`. Practice fails because fran and Kana cannot inhabit the song as Yō did. miho reaches the decisive recognition:

> the song represented miho and Yō exactly, but Yō is gone, so it cannot simply be sung “as before.”

The answer is not abandonment of the song. It is re-authoring.

Admit:

> **`MEMORIAL_REPERFORMANCE_WITHOUT_REPLICATION`**

The IIIX version substantially rearranges the piece into **their** abrasive, competitive form of “friendship.”

## 8.3 Re-authoring preserves rather than erases the original

After the new performance:

- older fans recognize both continuity and difference;
- new listeners seek out the Sundance original;
- miho explicitly hopes Yō gains new fans even now;
- the new version does not claim to supersede the original.

Admit:

> **`LEGACY_TRANSMISSION_THROUGH_PRESENT_REAUTHORSHIP`**

The old work survives partly because the present refuses museum-piece imitation.

## 8.4 New companionship does not replace Yō

Earlier in the card miho privately admits that, after nearly losing IIIX, she does not want to be “left alone again.” At the end, asked what she would tell Yō, she says:

> `もう一人じゃないから、安心して`

Admit:

> **`NEW_COMPANIONSHIP_WITHOUT_REPLACEMENT`**

This is not “Yō has been replaced by Kana and fran.” The song itself preserves Yō's singular history while miho's present life gains other relationships capable of preventing permanent relational isolation.

This is the tranche's strongest memorial instance of `ROLE_CONTINUITY_WITHOUT_ROLE_CAPTIVITY`: loyalty to a dead friend does not require social arrest.

# 9. Cross-card synthesis before companion messages

The card-only freeze supports six major prospective transitions:

1. **Kana:** revise “father abandonment” into **contact without family reconstitution**, while preserving severe deprivation of ordinary family life.
2. **Kana:** strengthen the model of visibility as initially father-directed but later capable of becoming reciprocal public vocation.
3. **Suzu:** distinguish anti-parental stubbornness from later self-authored idol commitment; maternal approval remains conditional rather than settled.
4. **fran:** strengthen plural vocation from abstract identity into materially risky concurrent labor, with real market discipline and cross-role skill transfer.
5. **Haruko:** strengthen the alternative-performance horizon into a concrete late success defined by succession rather than literal restoration of the Mana relationship.
6. **miho:** separate two memorial mechanisms—**embodied self-valuing** through Yō's recognition and **public legacy re-authoring** through `Friend Glass`—while establishing that new companionship need not erase the dead.

No card here overturns the series-level thesis of answerable interdependence. C1-A instead deepens the temporal question: **how relationships remain causally and ethically present after their original form becomes unavailable.**
