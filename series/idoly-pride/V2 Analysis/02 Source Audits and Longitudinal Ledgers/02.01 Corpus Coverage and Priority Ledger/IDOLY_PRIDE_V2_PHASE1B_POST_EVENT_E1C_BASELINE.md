# 1. Persona/authenticity — governing four-axis model

Do not use a binary of:

> private = real / public = fake.

For any persona, media image, staged relation, or public narrative, separate:

| Axis | Governing question |
|---|---|
| **Representational truth** | Is the factual claim accurate? |
| **Authorship** | Can the performer meaningfully choose, shape, revise, or refuse the form? |
| **Relational reality** | Does the form reveal or help create a real relation/capacity? |
| **Jurisdiction** | Who can impose, terminate, monetize, or redefine the form? |

These axes may diverge.

A self-authored persona may still contain factual deception. A highly constructed public register may still express a genuine capacity. A fabricated role may help create a real relationship. None of those observations makes truthfulness or power distribution irrelevant.

# 2. Admitted persona/performance vocabulary

## 2.1 `CURATED_AUTHENTICITY`

A selectively produced public form can reveal stable structural truths without providing raw/private transparency.

Primary E1-C case: IIIX documentary.

Do not claim the documentary shows the unmediated “real IIIX.”

## 2.2 `REPRESENTATIONAL_PERFORMANCE`

A performed form that claims to depict an already-existing fact, relationship, or self.

It may be:

- truthful;
- selectively framed;
- misleading;
- false.

## 2.3 `CONSTITUTIVE_PERFORMANCE`

Repeated performance of a role may help **create** the relationship or practice it initially only staged, through ordinary mechanisms such as time, shared labor, disclosure, rehearsal, mutual assistance, and common stakes.

Primary E1-C case: kana/Kokoro TWINkle.

Do not generalize this into “faking a relationship always makes it real.”

## 2.4 Truthfulness guardrail

Relational authenticity does **not** retroactively make representational deception true.

The TWINkle final vote MC remains ethically contestable even after kana/Kokoro have developed real reciprocal behavior.
