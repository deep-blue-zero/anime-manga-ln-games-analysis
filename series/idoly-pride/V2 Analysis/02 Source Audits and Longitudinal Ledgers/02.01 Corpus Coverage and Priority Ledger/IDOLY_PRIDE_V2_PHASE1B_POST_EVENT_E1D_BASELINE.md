# 1. IIIX — post-US governing constitution

The preferred post-E1-D unit model is:

> **PLURAL MOTIVE COMPACT + ADVERSARIAL EPISTEMIC INTIMACY + NON-TERMINAL ABSOLUTE VICTORY**

## 1.1 Plural motive compact

IIIX no longer depends on a simple three-way mutual-utility bargain.

The members continue for different but compatible reasons:

- **miho** — Yō's promise, public memory, top-idol ambition, making someone happy, competitive proof;
- **kana** — provisional continuation after the collapse of the father-rescue fantasy while she searches for a next personally authored desire;
- **fran** — fashion/brand revival, beauty, business, public impact, and IIIX-specific aesthetic production.

Do not claim that they now share one identical dream.

The unit's coherence comes from compatible common action, not psychological fusion.

## 1.2 Adversarial epistemic intimacy

IIIX's hostility remains genuine.

E1-D nevertheless establishes that the members possess detailed knowledge of one another's:

- wounds;
- pride structures;
- triggers;
- refusal conditions;
- useful skills;
- humiliations;
- care aversions.

Preferred term:

> **ADVERSARIAL_EPISTEMIC_INTIMACY**

This does not mean cruelty is universally healthy. It describes this specific relationship ecology.

## 1.3 Non-terminal absolute victory

The unit retains:

> `全ては絶対勝利のために`

Do not soften IIIX into an anti-competitive group.

The decisive change is that defeat loses sovereign authority over whether the unit deserves to continue.

miho explicitly says that if IIIX loses to the reigning No. 1, the response will be to determine how to defeat her next time.

Preferred term:

> **NON_TERMINAL_ABSOLUTE_VICTORY**

Victory remains the absolute target; loss becomes answerable information rather than ontological erasure.

# 2. IIIX dissolution — corrected causal model

Do not summarize Part 1 as “all three get exactly what they wanted.”

Use three distinct pathways:

## 2.1 kana — contact without relational fulfillment

Her visibility strategy succeeds in making her father findable.

It fails to create the family relationship she imagined.

## 2.2 fran — external success threatens unit necessity

Her fashion/design work attracts a credible investor, reopening a route in which idol work can appear dispensable.

## 2.3 miho — strategic falsification

She is confronted with evidence that:

- Yō can be edited out of public history;
- IIIX's “perfect women” persona makes that omission narratively convenient;
- IIIX's score orientation has drifted toward self-referential optimization;
- the group still lacks the capacity required to reach the summit by its existing method.

Preferred description:

> **MOTIVE_COLLAPSE through contact, success, and strategic falsification.**

# 3. kana — post-E1-D state

## 3.1 Father motive

The old motive is no longer governing.

Fame did what kana hoped at the level of contact: her father saw her in American media and reached out.

But visibility could not force him to reconstruct the father/daughter relation kana wanted.

Governing distinction:

> **Visibility can create contact. It cannot compel kinship.**

## 3.2 Paternal recognition and self-authorship

Kana explicitly reveals that she had imagined being found by her father would allow her to become a “good” person—less irritated, more naturally smiling, easier to love.

E1-D therefore adds:

> **Past injury may explain personality without granting the absent parent permanent jurisdiction over what adult selfhood may become.**

Fran's intervention is harsh but directly challenges this outsourced moral authorship.

## 3.3 Anti-pity guardrail

Kana explicitly rejects `安っぽい同情`.

Do not treat generic tenderness as automatically effective care for her.

Care must remain answerable to the recipient's self-conception.

## 3.4 Father relationship endpoint

Kana accepts one late father-like act when he drives her to NexusFes, then says goodbye and calls him:

> `ファンのおじさん`

Preferred interpretation:

> **paternal rescuer → bounded spectator/supporter**

Do not infer permanent no-contact from E1-D alone.

## 3.5 Vocation endpoint

Kana does **not** leave the US arc with a fully settled new dream.

She says she has nothing in particular to do now and will accompany miho's “corny” motive until she finds the next thing she wants.

Preferred term:

> **PROVISIONAL_CONTINUATION**

Later positive vocation must be separately earned by later sources.

> `あいつらに似合う服じゃないと、意味がない`

This is strong evidence of costly commitment to IIIX as a specific aesthetic body.

But business motive remains real.

Do not force a false choice between:

For fran, those may be simultaneous.

## 4.3 Care method

Fran understands that:

- Makino is badly positioned for the first Yō confrontation with miho;

This strengthens her role as a difficult but precise peer reader.

It does not establish cruelty as a universal developmental necessity.

# 5. miho / Yō — post-E1-D public-memory state

`OPEN-09` receives its largest event-layer advance so far.

## 5.1 Public erasure is explicit

An American introduction video removes Yō from Sundance material, making miho appear to have been a solo idol.

Yōko Boyle explains that the dead partner is inconvenient to IIIX's `パーフェクトな女達` framing and becomes `ノイズ` within that story.

This establishes:

> **public persona can structure archival inclusion/exclusion.**

## 5.2 Private memory and public memory are distinct tasks

miho never forgot Yō.

That does not prevent the public from forgetting or misrepresenting Yō.

Preferred term:

> **MEMORY_REQUIRES_CIRCULATION**

Use only when the objective is public remembrance. The series is not claiming every private grief must be publicized.

## 5.3 Memorial proprietorship is challenged

When fran proposes covering old Sundance material with IIIX, miho initially refuses because the song is too precious to let the others “touch.”

This creates a genuine contradiction:

- miho wants public memory;
- miho also wants protected memorial enclosure.

E1-D does not solve this through an actual cover.

Instead it makes Yō's **name, history, and promise speakable inside IIIX**.

## 5.4 Promise disclosure

miho finally tells kana/fran that she and Yō promised:

- to stand at the top;
- to become idols who can make someone happy.

This produces the governing transition:

> **PRIVATE_VOW → DISCLOSED_MOTIVE → PLURAL_REAUTHORIZATION**

The promise retains miho/Yō provenance while other living people gain standing to participate in its future consequences.

## 5.5 No full closure

miho explicitly says she does not know whether continuing with these women really counts as keeping the promise.

Do not claim:

- grief complete;
- Yō replaced;
- memorial problem fully solved.

E1-D resolves the **circulation/jurisdiction problem**, not the entire bereavement arc.
