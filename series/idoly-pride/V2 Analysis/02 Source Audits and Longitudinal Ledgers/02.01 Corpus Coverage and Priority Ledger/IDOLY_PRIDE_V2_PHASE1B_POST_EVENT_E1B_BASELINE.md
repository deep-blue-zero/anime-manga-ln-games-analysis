# 1. Governing model after E1-B

Tier-A governing ethic remains:

> **answerable interdependence**

E1-A inheritance form remains:

> **provenance matters, but origin does not grant permanent unilateral jurisdiction over the living future.**

E1-B adds the ambition/professional form:

> **ambition remains legitimate when its means, identity, relationships, bodily costs, and artistic purpose remain contestable from inside the relationship or unit.**

The combined standard is now:

> **IDOLY PRIDE repeatedly values strong desire, competition, inheritance, professionalism, and mutual reliance, but treats them as ethically mature only when the people living inside them retain standing to answer, revise, refuse, compete, separate, return, or reinterpret.**

---

# 2. Cross-series performance/rank rule

E1-B establishes:

> **rank and victory are necessary but incomplete languages of idol value.**

Do not infer:

- ranking is fake;
- victory is unimportant;
- fans care only about emotion;
- loss is inherently superior to winning.

Instead preserve the distinctions:

- result affects access, public standing, career, and competition;
- performance may retain artistic/relational value beyond the result;
- defeat can be developmental, destructive, clarifying, or terminal depending on context;
- a score does not exhaust what a performance did.

---

# 3. Failure / defeat rule

Previous principle:

> failure can become information.

E1-B correction:

> **failure becomes information only when a person, unit, or relationship retains enough continuity to interpret it.**

A defeat can instead become:

- public/career extinction;
- shame without recovery infrastructure;
- bodily harm;
- an externally imposed narrative;
- clean closure;
- renewed desire;
- or useful comparative evidence.

Do not romanticize failure in later synthesis.

---

# 4. Mature-unit rule

E1-B admits the following working proposition:

> **A mature unit is not a fixed image. It is a repeatable procedure for changing without making its members disposable.**

This model permits:

- changed staging;
- changed center emphasis;
- new members;
- temporary separation;
- new professional genres;
- rivalry;
- disagreement;
- re-entry;

provided the unit's change does not simply convert members into replaceable material or strip them of meaningful answerability.
