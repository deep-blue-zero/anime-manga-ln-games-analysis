# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — BIG4 MAIN STORY PRIMARY FINDINGS FREEZE

## 0. Function of this document

This document freezes the source-native V2 reading of the complete BIG4 main-story tranche before any deliberate retrieval of historical V1 BIG4 analysis.

It is therefore not the final adversarial audit. Its purpose is to preserve what the BIG4 text itself earns when read sequentially from the frozen BIG4-entry baseline.

The inherited state is:

`POST_TOKYO_BASELINE`
→ `IIIX_POST_IUNITY_TO_BIG4_ENTRY_BRIDGE`
→ `BIG4_ENTRY_GOVERNING_BASELINE`
→ **BIG4 001–014**

The BIG4 pass was read in bundle order and story order. Later stories were allowed to revise earlier hypotheses only after those later stories became available in sequence.

Because limited BIG4 primary-source text and high-level historical BIG4 material had already been exposed during the chronology-repair process, this pass is not represented as historically pristine. Its integrity classification remains:

`CONTROLLED_RECONSTRUCTION_WITH_DISCLOSED_HISTORICAL_AND_PRIMARY_SOURCE_HINDSIGHT`

The controlling rule is nevertheless source-first: every substantive claim below must be supportable from the BIG4 tranche and its frozen inherited state.

# 2. Executive primary-source thesis

The BIG4 arc is the **refoundation of Tsuki no Tempest through distributed personhood**.

At the beginning of the tranche, Tsuki still operates inside an inherited hierarchy:

> Kotono is the moon; the other four help the moon shine.

That structure is not false. Kotono genuinely has been the unit’s central organizing force, and the other members themselves later acknowledge that she illuminated their way. But BIG4 demonstrates that the hierarchy has become developmentally unstable. Kotono experiences collective kindness as something she can lean on; the other four still assume that Kotono will somehow solve the group’s crisis; Nagisa’s self-definition remains dangerously close to “the person who supports Kotono”; and the unit’s public identity is legible from outside as close to a Kotono-centered one-person structure.

The rupture does not prove that the group was fake or that Kotono was a bad leader. It exposes a real asymmetry that had reached its limit.

The arc’s decisive movement is therefore not:

> Kotono leaves, discovers friendship matters, and returns to the old group.

It is:

> **Kotono and the other four separate, each side develops capacities the old structure suppressed, and the five reunite only after the old center/periphery model has become impossible to restore.**

Kotono acquires individual discipline, adversarial independence, greater bodily/performance precision, a harder tolerance for public judgment, and a clearer awareness of what kind of strength she does *not* want.

Nagisa acquires an idol desire that cannot be reduced to supporting Kotono.

Suzu acquires center responsibility and learns the difference between wanting the center and carrying it.

Saki, Mei, and the four-person unit learn that they cannot outsource the existence of Tsuki to Kotono.

The four develop a performance identity in which each member must become individually visible.

When Kotono returns, she does not simply resume ownership of the center. Suzu retains a legitimate center claim, explicitly frames center as contestable, and later voluntarily entrusts the decisive DoriKyun battle to Kotono because that is the strongest choice for *this battle*, not because Kotono possesses a permanent metaphysical right to the position.

The mature Tsuki answer becomes:

> **Each member can be a moon for someone.  
> Each member can be a storm that clears someone’s hesitation.  
> The unit is strongest not when four people orbit one light, but when five lights illuminate one another and can combine into one tempest.**

That is the structural meaning of BIG4.

# 3. The second governing thesis: DoriKyun are adversarial teachers whose diagnosis must be separated from their ethic

DoriKyun are neither disposable villains nor secretly benevolent mentors.

Their strongest claims are partly correct:

- the idol industry can exploit and discard people;
- institutional support can vanish when powerful adults become hostile;
- dreams without sufficient practical force can be destroyed;
- serious performance requires standards far above sentimental effort;
- repetition alone can plateau;
- performers may need to acquire tools from outside conventional idol training;
- battle, risk, hostility, and unfamiliar environments can produce capacities ordinary practice does not;
- entertainment exists for an audience and must be made compelling;
- a performer cannot merely admire a superior—she must learn to analyze, answer, and eventually challenge her.

Their historical extremity also has a material cause. They were pressured to throw a battle, refused through deliberate public victory, lost all work, and were abandoned by an agency unwilling to protect them from a powerful producer. Their later doctrine of overwhelming strength is therefore not arbitrary cruelty. It is a survival theory generated by institutional betrayal.

But the arc does **not** validate their normative conclusion.

DoriKyun universalize their wound:

> because weak idols can be exploited, weak idols should be crushed;  
> because the industry discarded us, only those who can resist destruction deserve to remain;  
> because victory protects autonomy, victory becomes the test of legitimacy.

Tsuki finally rejects precisely that conversion.

The arc’s answer is not “strength does not matter.” Strength matters intensely. Tsuki cannot beat DoriKyun by moral purity, kindness, or friendship alone.

The answer is:

> **strength is necessary, but strength is not morally self-legitimating.**

Tsuki appropriates DoriKyun’s seriousness, adversarial courage, willingness to challenge convention, and entertainment instinct while rejecting elimination as the purpose of competition.

This is why Kotono’s request after defeating them is so important: she does **not** ask DoriKyun to disband. She asks them to continue.

Victory becomes recognition rather than erasure.

# 4. Sequential freeze by bundle

## 4.1 `big4_001_dark_of_the_moon`

### Primary function

This bundle establishes the post-I-UNITY institutional world and opens two crises simultaneously:

1. IIIX has lost Preta Porte, lost its BIG4 seat, become freelance, suffered reputational damage, and is invited into Hoshimi on a trial basis.
2. Tsuki no Tempest is stagnating after its I-UNITY defeat while SUNNY PEACE surges into BIG4.

### IIIX / Hoshimi

**TEXTUAL FACT:** IIIX has left Preta Porte, become freelance, lost its BIG4 position, and is carrying the administrative burden and reputational consequences of independence.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Saegusa offers IIIX Hoshimi membership not because prior conflict has been forgotten, but because he frames the Himeno affair as a failure of the industry and wants to avoid leaving the idols themselves abandoned inside the damage.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Makino explicitly says he cannot forgive IIIX’s previous plan to erase Mana’s songs, yet the agency still permits a trial relationship.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hoshimi’s response separates **moral judgment from institutional abandonment**. Acceptance does not require pretending past conduct was harmless.

This becomes an important foil for DoriKyun’s later logic. Hoshimi believes people can remain answerable for wrongdoing while still being allowed a future.

### Tsuki

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Tsuki’s ranking/work position has worsened after I-UNITY, while SUNNY, TRINITYAiLE, and LizNoir are advancing.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Kotono explicitly asks why SUNNY could beat IIIX while Tsuki could not.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** The crisis is no longer simply “we lost.” Kotono has lost confidence in the *model of progress* the unit has been using.

## 4.2 `big4_002_cloudy_road_ahead`

### The problem of public narrative

Tsuki gives a live it experiences as successful; a credible media article nevertheless describes the group as stagnant and identifies Kotono as the chief weakness.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Makino tells Nagisa that actual improvement and public reevaluation do not occur on the same timetable.

**INTERPRETATION:** BIG4 distinguishes performance reality from reputational inertia. Improvement is not immediately self-validating in a professional entertainment economy.

### Kotono’s dependence problem

The other four defend Kotono. Kotono reads that kindness ambivalently.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Kotono is not rejecting care because she does not value it. She is afraid care has become a structure that permits her to remain unchanged.

This fear is not wholly delusional. Later outside evaluation confirms that the old five-person Tsuki could look like a Kotono-centered unit whose other members depended on her.

The mistake comes later, when Kotono turns a real dependence problem into the theory that connection itself is the obstacle.

---

## 4.3 `big4_003_adventure_of_life`

The training-camp/okapi sequence is not disposable comedy.

The girls discover that diversion, curiosity, play, and relaxation can improve the quality of serious training. Kotono stops treating every non-training moment as stolen time.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Nagisa articulates that someone capable of enjoying a challenge may be stronger.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is the first explicit crack in Kotono’s ascetic model. BIG4 repeatedly argues that performance strength is not produced by maximal suffering alone.

DoriKyun later appear to contradict this, but their own philosophy is also broader than simple overtraining: they have developed through many different challenges and fields. The story’s actual contrast is therefore not “fun versus discipline,” but **developmental plurality versus tunnel vision**.

---

## 4.4 `big4_004_decision_of_the_underdogs`

This is the DoriKyun doctrinal reveal.

### DoriKyun as entertainment radicals

They have accumulated extraordinary battle experience, direct audience engagement, cross-domain challenge work, and a public/private continuity unusual among the major rival units.

They repeatedly force opponents into situations where retreat carries reputational cost.

They have also contributed to idols quitting.

### Their advice to Kotono

After destroying Tsuki in battle, DoriKyun give serious developmental advice.

They distinguish:
- merely clinging to a superior;
- conventional straight-line practice;
- and acquiring new weapons through unfamiliar domains and extreme challenge.

Kotono reconstructs SUNNY’s I-UNITY victory as multiplicative rather than additive: many pressures, relationships, new performance elements, resistance to sabotage, focused effort, and enjoyment converged.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** DoriKyun correctly teach Kotono to stop asking only, “How can I perform the same developmental program harder?”

The destructive turn is Kotono’s next inference:

> if extraordinary individual power matters, then collective belonging may be the thing preventing it.

That inference is not earned by DoriKyun’s advice itself.

## 4.5 `big4_005_goodbye_to_the_moon`

This is the first major refoundation point.

### Kotono’s departure

Kotono says Tsuki is wonderful but that “wonderful” is insufficient to reach the top. She fears its kindness gives her somewhere to lean.

She asks to leave temporarily and accepts that the group may not take her back.

### Kotono asks Nagisa to come with her

This is crucial evidence against reading Kotono’s departure as pure solitary independence.

She wants separation from Tsuki, but she initially tries to preserve Nagisa as an exception.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Kotono has not yet understood the full dependence structure. She recognizes dependence on the group while under-recognizing her dependence on Nagisa.

### Nagisa refuses

Nagisa initially believes her own idol existence may collapse without Kotono. She then realizes:

- the other three matter to her;
- she wants to remain an idol;
- she wants to shine rather than only support;
- following Kotono would reproduce the very asymmetry that prevents her from one day standing beside her.

Her mature logic is not abandonment:

> **I am staying because I want a future in which I can stand beside you, not merely trail behind you.**

This is one of the arc’s most important character reversals.

---

## 4.6 `big4_006_choice_of_destiny`

### Four-person Tsuki is a genuine choice, not a waiting room

Makino gives the remaining members a meaningful option to disband.

He explicitly frames idol time as finite and refuses to demand halfhearted continuation for sentimental reasons.

All four choose to continue.

### Nagisa’s new state

Nagisa says she once understood herself mainly as someone supporting Kotono from behind, but now wants to shine and stand beside her.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Her idol desire has become self-authored enough to survive separation.

### Unit consequence

The four reject being a diminished copy of the five-person group. They immediately begin rewriting choreography, distribution, public communication, and performance architecture for four bodies.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is not “Tsuki without Kotono.” It is a temporary but real alternate constitution of Tsuki.

---

## 4.7 `big4_007_new_moon_new_bright`

### Public accountability and Suzu’s center

The four hold a press conference under intense media pressure.

Suzu becomes the decisive public defender of both the four-person Tsuki and Kotono’s seriousness. Her speech is emotional but does not simply attack the press; it asks the audience to understand that Kotono’s departure was not casual.

Afterward she volunteers for center.

Her motive is revealing: she wants to make four-person Tsuki so strong that attacks on Kotono and the unit become untenable.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Suzu’s old center fantasy matures into **burden-bearing representation**.

She still enjoys the grandeur of center language, but she now understands that center means accepting anxiety, scrutiny, coordination, and responsibility for the group’s public face.

### Kotono under DoriKyun

Kotono grows technically, but DoriKyun challenge even her idea of discipleship. They reject the role of admiring superiors and demand that she produce criticism and eventually become capable of “biting” them.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** DoriKyun explicitly ask whether she will wag her tail below them or glare at them as an equal.

This anticipates the mature arc: growth is not successful if it merely replaces dependence on Tsuki with dependence on DoriKyun.

### Kotono’s dangerous escalation

When DoriKyun detect that she still imagines eventual return, they call that insufficient commitment. Kotono then cuts remaining ties because she fears any contact will become “甘え / amae,” dependence.

**INTERPRETATION:** The arc now pushes Kotono’s individuation experiment into self-punitive absolutism.

---

## 4.8 `big4_008_keep_going`

### Failure becomes information

Four-person Tsuki deliberately enters repeated live battles. They lose, review, revise, test again, and begin discovering a genuinely distinct performance grammar.

A decisive outside observation is that the old five-person Tsuki looked like it depended heavily on Kotono, while the four-person group makes the individual members much more visible.

The members themselves accept that they had, at least partly, expected Kotono to “make it work.”

### Four storms

They articulate a new principle:

- not one central moon with supporting bodies;
- each member becomes individually forceful;
- the four retain coordination while increasing individual visibility;
- their separate storms combine into one larger tempest.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** The four-person period is not a detour that disappears when Kotono returns. It creates the unit’s mature distributed-agency architecture.

### DoriKyun rematch invitation

DoriKyun choose them again precisely because they lost catastrophically, lost a member, and still kept going.

Their selection criterion is cruel but revealing: refusal to break earns their attention.

Nagisa accepts in part because she wants Kotono to see that belonging can generate growth rather than prevent it.

---

## 4.9 `big4_009_battle_again`

### Nagisa confronts Kotono

Kotono says the four have improved but still cannot defeat DoriKyun.

Nagisa slaps her.

The key claim is not the slap itself. Nagisa identifies a contradiction:

> Kotono says her current road is right, but cannot look the others in the eyes.

Nagisa then explicitly promises to surpass Kotono.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** She says the four grew *because* they remained Tsuki, not merely despite remaining.

This is the direct answer to Kotono’s theory of separation.

### Four-person Tsuki’s best performance is still not enough

The four produce a score higher than any they had achieved as five.

DoriKyun respond by becoming even stronger; the challenge makes them perform above their ordinary level as well.

**INTERPRETATION:** Competition is relationally generative here. Tsuki’s strength does not merely approach a fixed benchmark; it provokes DoriKyun into a new ceiling.

This undermines a static power-ranking model.

### Kotono sees the contradiction in her strength theory

Watching the four, Kotono begins to doubt that destructive victory is the kind of strength she wanted.

---

## 4.10 `big4_010_goodbye_to_the_darkness`

### The normative climax

After DoriKyun wins and demands disbandment, Nagisa says:

> if victory requires abandoning everything, destroying opponents’ dreams, and crushing their futures, then she does not need that victory.

This is **not** a rejection of striving. The four had just risked everything to win. It is a rejection of treating victory as the highest moral value.

DoriKyun answer with their strongest argument:

> without strength, dreams are meaningless because agencies and the industry can discard you.

That argument is historically grounded in their own betrayal.

Kotono finally distinguishes the two concepts.

She calls the four genuinely strong because they:
- knew they might lose;
- risked disbandment;
- loved idols and one another;
- still stepped onto the stage;
- and did not flee.

She identifies herself as the weaker one because fear of darkness led her to turn away from the people closest to her.

### The legalistic but thematically exact “reconstitution”

Kotono accepts that the four-person Tsuki must dissolve under the wager and immediately proposes forming a new five-person Tsuki.

The maneuver is comic/cheeky at the literal level, but structurally accurate:

> **the old Tsuki cannot simply resume; a new five-person Tsuki must be constituted.**

### Reunion is conditional

The others do not accept Kotono merely because she cries and returns.

Each member gives a different condition:

- Saki: stop unilateral decision-making; share the process; professional responsibility matters.
- Suzu: recognize the hurt; prove the growth; center responsibility has changed her too.
- Mei: face the fans as seriously as she faces the members.
- Nagisa: never demean the shared time or the unit again; she is an idol in her own right and will not be subordinated.

**INTERPRETATION:** The reunion is an accountability process, not restoration through sentiment.

### Performance integration

When they train together again, both sides can detect the other’s growth.

Kotono says the path she chose was not correct, but it was not meaningless.

That is a mature distinction the document should preserve: the story does not retroactively erase the developmental gains produced by a bad decision.

---

## 4.11 `big4_011_back_of_entertainment`

### Center becomes contestable

In the comeback live, Suzu remains the formal center but gives Kotono one song in the center so Kotono can communicate through performance to an audience still angry about her behavior.

Afterward Suzu says center should belong to whichever member is best able to occupy it and invites all five to challenge her.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** This is the institutional proof that Tsuki has not returned to the old hierarchy.

Kotono is still a powerful center. She is no longer the unquestioned ontological center.

### Performance as accountable communication

Kotono cannot repair public trust by explanation alone. The comeback live turns her history—failure, solitude, shame, return—into performance material.

The story does not claim art cancels wrongdoing. It claims performance is the medium through which an idol can demonstrate what words alone cannot establish.

### What is still missing

Even after reunion and public recovery, the five cannot imagine a decisive victory over DoriKyun.

Kotono identifies DoriKyun’s lack of hesitation as a key difference and concludes that understanding their history may reveal the structure of their conviction.

## 4.12 `big4_011`, stories 54–55: DoriKyun’s historical wound and Tsuki’s refusal to inherit it

Miho provides a hearsay-based but narratively important account:

- DoriKyun began at Celestial Pro.
- A powerful producer tried to induce them to throw a live battle.
- They pretended to agree and then publicly destroyed the favored opponent.
- The producer retaliated.
- Their agency abandoned rather than protected them.
- They lost all work.
- They responded by challenging, defeating, and publicly cornering stronger and weaker rivals until they eventually became BIG4.

Miho explicitly labels parts of her account as rumor or inference, so the epistemic class is important.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** This is what Miho tells Kotono and what the main story asks the characters to reason from.

**CONFLICT/AMBIGUITY:** The exact historical details are secondhand within the diegesis unless another source later directly corroborates them.

Kotono’s response is central. She can understand:
- rage at corrupt adults;
- hatred of an agency that fails to protect idols;
- revenge;
- desire to reform the industry.

But she refuses the conclusion that Tsuki should become DoriKyun.

Her alternative is:
- face opponents directly;
- understand their feelings;
- exchange meaning through battle;
- deliver the best possible entertainment to spectators.

This is the first clean statement of **competition as reciprocal recognition**.

## 4.13 `big4_012_illuminate`

### Kotono’s answer to “what is an idol?”

Kotono returns to the school space associated with Mana and Makino and finally formulates her answer.

She reviews:
- beginning by chasing Mana;
- giving Mana’s song to Sakura;
- trying to become Nagase Kotono rather than “Mana’s sister”;
- losing to IIIX;
- seeing SUNNY find an answer and win;
- repeated stagnation;
- the split;
- darkness;
- the four members illuminating one another;
- their light reaching her.

Her answer:

> **For Kotono, an idol is someone who illuminates everyone’s path.**

The metaphor matters because it is reciprocal. Kotono’s earlier identity was the central moon. Her mature insight is that the other four became lights too.

### Mana inheritance is transformed, not abolished

Makino identifies both “sun” and “moon” qualities in Mana and sees them separately inherited by SUNNY and Tsuki.

Kotono has stopped trying to reproduce Mana, yet Mana survives through transformed dispositions.

**INTERPRETATION:** BIG4 gives one of the clearest statements of the V2 inheritance thesis:

> ethical inheritance is not copying the dead; it is letting what was received become new forms in living people.

### Learning DoriKyun’s entertainment grammar

DoriKyun initially refuses another rematch.

Kotono uses what she learned from them: she calls during a live broadcast and turns the challenge itself into entertainment, making refusal reputationally costly.

This is not simple imitation. It is appropriation without ideological surrender.

### IIIX as adversarial training partner

The five use IIIX as the nearest available analogue to DoriKyun’s oppressive performance pressure.

The resulting training is intense: 278 battles in seven days.

IIIX’s contributions reveal mature post-Tokyo roles:
- miho: efficiency, bodily economy, repeated-performance durability, standards;
- fran: visible form even in stillness, model-derived body authorship;
- kana: adult charisma, circulation/publicity, embodied presentation.

The training also establishes that current IIIX has continued to grow; Hoshimi does not domesticate them into weaker or softer rivals.

Kotono’s declared goal is no longer merely to beat an opponent. She wants to **receive/understand what the opponent puts into the battle**.

## 4.14 `big4_013_the_moonlight`

### Suzu relinquishes center without relinquishing self

Suzu concludes Kotono gives them the best probability of defeating DoriKyun in this specific battle and voluntarily entrusts the center to her.

She explicitly says victory is not everything, but refusing the strongest available plan would disrespect both their own work and DoriKyun.

Mei later tells Suzu that she held the four-person Tsuki together and thanks her.

Suzu cries.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** The arc closes Suzu’s center development by separating **center as recognition** from **center as permanent possession**. She becomes more legitimate as a center precisely when she can hand it over without disappearing.

### Resilience replaces victory absolutism

Mei says that even if they lose, this version of Tsuki will not break; with all five together they can begin again.

This is not complacency. She immediately says she has no intention of losing.

**INTERPRETATION:** The unit’s new strength is partly **recoverability**. Defeat is no longer ontological annihilation.

### Final DoriKyun battle

DoriKyun produces a personal best.

Tsuki answers with `月ノヒカリ`.

Textually, the performance integrates the complete arc: accumulated effort, separation, fear, regret, four-person development, reunion, and the new illumination philosophy.

Tsuki wins narrowly and becomes BIG4.

### Kotono’s victory demand

DoriKyun expects the winners to demand their disbandment.

Kotono refuses.

She explicitly disagrees with their practice of driving weaker idols out, but refuses to erase their aspiration to make idol entertainment greater.

She asks them to continue and fight again someday.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is the arc’s moral inversion of DoriKyun’s elimination logic:

> Tsuki proves superiority without demanding nonexistence.

DoriKyun’s private/prideful response does not become sentimental. Raimu tells Tsuki to be proud: they defeated DoriKyun and are strong.

That is enough.

### Formal limitation

The text bundle establishes:
- the song used;
- lyric text represented in the script;
- score outcome;
- explicit reactions;
- narration/dialogue about intensity.

It does **not** by itself establish the actual audiovisual means by which the final performance constructs “moonlight.” Any claim about musical arrangement, delivery, visual staging, choreography, editing, lighting, or audience camera position remains pending formal backfill.

---

## 4.15 `big4_014_epilogue`

The epilogue returns SUNNY and Tsuki to Hoshimi City after both have become BIG4.

The scene refuses to let rank dissolve ordinary relational continuity.

They are still:
- rivals;
- friends/peers;
- Hoshimi idols;
- people who joke, scare Suzu, plan a hometown live, and rely on Makino.

Kotono finally calls Sakura her rival explicitly.

The two unit centers are not allowed to monopolize the rivalry: Rei immediately reminds them that SUNNY and Tsuki are not just Sakura and Kotono.

LizNoir and TRINITYAiLE observe the rise of the two Hoshimi groups and resolve not to fall behind.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** BIG4 does not close competition. It pluralizes it. Becoming BIG4 is not a terminus but a redistribution of pressure across the whole professional field.

# 5. Kotono Nagase — frozen character delta

## Entry state

- post-I-UNITY stagnation;
- still carrying center/leader identity;
- increasingly unable to explain why effort is not producing advancement;
- sensitive to SUNNY’s victory over IIIX;
- fears Tsuki’s kindness is becoming developmental shelter.

## Crisis error

Kotono correctly recognizes that she needs:
- new inputs;
- individual development;
- stronger adversarial pressure;
- a self not reducible to group function.

She incorrectly concludes that:
- intimacy and growth are structurally opposed;
- responsibility requires solitary burden;
- contact with loved ones is equivalent to dependence;
- strength requires the ability to sever.

## Productive gains during the wrong path

The source explicitly shows gains:
- improved physical/performance precision;
- greater stamina/economy;
- more active observation;
- harder professional standards;
- greater awareness of entertainment as something consciously constructed;
- ability to challenge superiors rather than only follow;
- greater tolerance for public embarrassment and loss.

These gains must not be erased because the route that produced them was ethically/relationally bad.

## Mature state

Kotono can now:
- identify separation as a mistaken answer without calling every consequence meaningless;
- accept criticism from all four members;
- rejoin under conditions rather than demand restoration;
- share center legitimacy;
- learn from adversaries without becoming them;
- distinguish understanding DoriKyun from endorsing DoriKyun;
- define idolhood positively rather than only through Mana, victory, or rank;
- see herself as one light among several;
- use performance to illuminate rather than dominate;
- win without demanding the loser disappear.

### Frozen thesis

**Kotono’s BIG4 arc is the movement from central light under unbearable self-pressure to reciprocal light within a distributed unit.**

# 6. Nagisa Ibuki — frozen character delta

Nagisa is one of the tranche’s largest beneficiaries.

Her old relational grammar:
- observe Kotono;
- support Kotono;
- remain near Kotono;

# 11. DoriKyun — frozen unit/philosophy findings

DoriKyun’s doctrine is roughly:

1. Idolhood must be serious enough to survive extreme pressure.
2. Weakness invites exploitation.
3. The industry is corrupted by adults and half-serious performers.
4. Competition reveals who has real commitment.

6. A challenger who breaks under defeat did not possess sufficient resolve.
7. Radical confrontation can purify the industry.

- their audience consciousness;
- their courage in refusing a rigged system;
- their insistence that serious rivals deserve full force;
- their refusal to patronize challengers.

## 11.3 What the story rejects

The story rejects:
- victory as proof of moral worth;
- strength as license to erase weaker people;
- treating another idol’s future as legitimate collateral;
- turning one’s own institutional betrayal into a universal social-Darwinist rule.

## 11.4 Their mentorship mode

They do not nurture through reassurance.

They teach through:
- provocation;
- refusal to flatter;
- forcing a person to articulate why she is there;
- requiring analysis rather than passive admiration;
- providing a wall strong enough to expose false assumptions.

### Frozen thesis

**DoriKyun function as necessary but normatively incomplete teachers. Their pressure helps Tsuki become stronger; Tsuki’s eventual victory demonstrates why DoriKyun’s definition of strength is too narrow.**

# 15. Competition and ethical recognition

BIG4 develops three distinct competition models.

## Elimination competition

DoriKyun:
> defeat proves weakness; weakness may deserve removal.

## Protective/avoidant competition

Early Tsuki risk:
> avoid overwhelming threats, or interpret defeat as identity collapse.

## Reciprocal competition

Mature Tsuki:
> bring full force because the opponent deserves seriousness; learn the opponent; allow the battle to reveal both sides; do not require the loser’s erasure.

This third model is why Kotono can sincerely want to defeat DoriKyun and sincerely want them to continue afterward.

### Frozen thematic thesis

**The moral opposite of destructive competition is not noncompetition. It is competition without ontological erasure.**

The story acknowledges developmental gains, but also gives direct evidence of:
- unilateral decision-making;
- emotional injury;
- blocking communication;

“Necessary separation” is too strong. The source supports “separation produced development” more safely than “separation was necessary.”

Their advice can be good.

# 21. Frozen primary-source conclusion

The BIG4 arc is ultimately about learning that **a group does not become strong by making its members less dependent on one another; it becomes strong by making dependence reciprocal, contestable, and non-erasing**.

Kotono begins the arc fearing that kindness has made her weak.

The answer is not that she was simply wrong.

The old Tsuki really had concentrated too much initiative and symbolic weight in her. The other four really did grow when forced to act without her. Kotono really did gain capacities by leaving.

But the mature conclusion is different from her initial one:

> The problem was not that they needed one another.  
> The problem was that the ways they needed one another were too unequal.

The four learn to produce light.
Kotono learns to receive light.
Nagisa learns that love/support without selfhood cannot become equality.
Suzu learns that center is responsibility rather than possession.
Saki learns to convert care into process and critique.
Mei learns—and teaches—that resilience may matter more than invulnerability.

DoriKyun then gives the arc its hard external test. Their philosophy is powerful because it is born from a true institutional horror: talented idols can be coerced, blacklisted, abandoned, and erased. Tsuki does not defeat that truth with innocence. It becomes stronger, more technically disciplined, more media-aware, more adversarial, and more willing to take risks.

What Tsuki rejects is DoriKyun’s conclusion that the only secure world is one where the strong eliminate the weak.

That is why the decisive victory request is not “disband.”

It is:

> **continue.**

The winner does not need the loser to stop existing.

That is the mature competitive ethic the BIG4 primary text earns before historical comparison.
