# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — TRINITYAiLE ORIGIN PRIMARY FINDINGS FREEZE

## 0. Freeze statement

This document records the source-native findings from all six TRINITYAiLE origin bundles **before any historical TRINITYAiLE analytical prose is consulted**.

The inherited analytical state is limited to material already admitted by the Phase-1B sequence: the frozen anime endpoint, the completed Hoshimi anime/game comparison, and the completed SUNNY PEACE, Tsuki no Tempest, and LizNoir origin tranches. Later Tokyo/BIG4/Stellar main story, later event material, bonds, cards, messages, telephone audio, and historical TRINITYAiLE analyses are excluded from determining this finding set.

The chronology rule remains strict:

> **An origin disclosure may retrospectively explain anime/Hoshimi behavior without becoming evidence that the earlier prospective viewer or analytical pass already knew it.**

This matters especially for Rui's childhood idol dream and paternal history, Yu's learned self-suppression, Sumire's brother/family economy, and the unit's post-Grand-Prix family crisis.

# 2. Source-native executive thesis

The six origins make the anime shorthand "absolute center Rui plus two supporting members" insufficient as a mature unit model.

TRINITYAiLE is hierarchical in visible role. Rui is its center and most explicit directional leader. Yu frequently acts as strategist and relational translator. Sumire is the youngest member and eventually described as a beloved little sister and mood-maker. The text does **not** dissolve these differences.

What it repeatedly refuses is a second inference:

> unequal role does not entail unequal authorship, unequal entitlement to desire, or permission for one member to become less fully herself.

The strongest source-native unit thesis is therefore:

> **TRINITYAiLE is an argument that interdependence is not a dilution of excellence but one of its conditions. The unit is strongest when three people can occupy different roles without any member having to diminish her agency, ability, or desire in order for the hierarchy to function.**

A more compact formulation is:

> **TRINITYAiLE permits asymmetry of role without asymmetry of personhood.**

This proposition is generated across all three members:

- Rui initially believes top-idol legitimacy must be won by her **own power alone**. Asakura attacks this directly: she already relies on coaches, staff, institutional infrastructure, and later teammates. Her mature strength increasingly consists in asking others to stand at full strength beside her.
- Yu has learned that visibility invites resentment, so she strategically chooses the second position and converts self-suppression into service to Rui. Rui's strongest corrective is not "become center instead" but **stop deciding that your teammates need you to be smaller**. Yu may remain second by choice, but it must become an authored role rather than a defensive ceiling.
- Sumire enters with expressive resources Rui and Yu lack, yet repeatedly fears being dispensable because she is younger, less technically developed, or absent during a victory. The pair's answer is not that the unit literally cannot win without her; they already proved they can. The answer is that **raw competitive sufficiency is not the same thing as the identity they have chosen to inhabit**: `三人でトリエル`.

The unit's deepest developmental grammar is therefore:

> **solitary proof -> complementary formation -> role distortion -> full-strength reciprocity -> chosen three-person identity -> collective expansion of what each member is allowed to want.**

# 4. Tendo Rui — the "absolute idol" begins before Asakura and is later contaminated by him

## 4.1 Rui's vocation predates paternal revelation

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Rui is raised by an idol-loving mother and says that from early childhood she genuinely wanted to become an idol:

`わたし、アイドルになりたいの！`

Her retrospective narration describes idols as more dream-filled than picture books and brighter than stars, and says she wanted to shine as one of those stars.

Only **after** this desire already exists, while Rui and her mother are visiting training institutions, does her mother disclose that BanPro president Asakura Kyoichi is Rui's biological father who left mother and child.

**Canonical source-native conclusion:**

> **Asakura does not originate Rui's idol dream. He becomes attached to an already-existing vocation as an additional object of proof and recognition.**

This strongly protects the frozen anime finding that Rui's vocation should not be reduced to paternal recognition.

## 4.2 Paternal injury changes the grammar of the dream

After learning Asakura's identity, Rui chooses BanPro and reframes the dream:

`私達を捨てたこの人すら、絶対に振り向くほどの――`

`トップアイドルです`

`そんな、絶対的なアイドルに――私はなります`

The important shift is grammatical. Childhood Rui wants to **be an idol** because idolhood is beautiful. Trainee Rui wants to become an **absolute** idol whom even the father who abandoned her cannot ignore.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** paternal recognition does not create the vocation, but it hardens its criterion of success. Excellence becomes evidentiary: achievement must become so undeniable that Asakura's emotional non-recognition can no longer survive it.

This helps explain why Rui is attracted to absolute formulations, undefeated records, and benchmark figures without making those traits her entire personality.

## 4.3 Rui's first model of strength is defensive self-sufficiency

Rui experiences jealousy and sabotage at the training school yet refuses to report it, including to Asakura. She repeatedly insists that she will earn the summit through her own ability.

This is partly pride, but the paternal situation makes it more specific: help—especially from Asakura—would contaminate the evidentiary value of success. She wants recognition that cannot be dismissed as favoritism.

This later becomes socially costly. Rui initially rejects friendship with Yu because other trainees interpret the relationship as exploiting Yu's famous mother.

## 4.4 Asakura's grouping lesson is harsh but structurally decisive

After Rui and Yu both pass the debut audition, Rui objects to being placed in a group. Asakura answers that she is not self-made even now:

- coaches train her;
- staff support her;
- the institution makes performance possible.

He then says:

`一人では何も出来ないことを、まずは受け止めろ`

The phrasing is severe and should not be romanticized. Yet its structural proposition becomes one of the origins' central truths:

> **high performance is relationally and institutionally produced even when the performer experiences achievement as individual proof.**

Rui accepts this because she recognizes that the argument is substantially correct.

## 4.5 Rui refuses instrumental partnership

Yu offers Rui a striking bargain:

`うちのこと利用してくれてええよ？`

Rui refuses the logic:

`「利用する」って考え方は好きじゃないの`

and says that if they form a group, she wants them to be serious and **equal**:

`組むのなら真剣に、対等でいたい`

This is load-bearing. Rui's visible hierarchy is born alongside an explicit ethical commitment to equality between persons.

That commitment later explains why Yu's self-suppression enrages her: Rui does not experience a teammate making herself smaller as loving support. She experiences it as a violation of the very partnership she agreed to.

# 5. Suzumura Yu — second place changes from survival strategy to chosen relation

## 5.1 Yu's social ease contains chronic self-monitoring

Yu is the eldest daughter of famous actress Suzumura Kumiko and enters training already highly developed in singing, dance, and acting through elite education.

Yet attention to Yu is frequently attention to her mother. Rui's first important recognition is therefore simple:

`大女優の娘であろうがなかろうが`

`鈴村さんは鈴村さんでしょ？`

Yu says nobody had responded to her identity that way before.

This is the relational foundation of her attachment to Rui: Rui's bluntness performs recognition.

## 5.2 Yu learned to make herself socially survivable

Yu later makes the defensive strategy explicit:

`うちは元々、人の顔色うかがって`

`自分の意思なんて年中迷子`

and:

`目立たんように、常に二番手でいることを選んできた`

The point is not that Yu lacks ability. The origin establishes the opposite. She is good enough that visibility itself becomes dangerous: a famous mother plus high competence attracts envy.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Yu turns social intelligence into a technology of self-reduction. She becomes excellent at reading what other people need because direct self-assertion has historically carried interpersonal cost.

## 5.3 Rui converts defensive second-place into desired second-place—but not yet healthy second-place

Yu says Rui is the first person for whom she can sincerely choose the second position:

`自らの意思で……この人のためなら、二番手でいたい`

This is a real improvement over passive social survival. Yu has found somebody whose success she actively desires.

But the origin refuses to stop there.

Yu begins redirecting attention and performance opportunities toward Rui, including modifying a live battle so Rui receives more spotlight. She frames this as what is best for TRINITYAiLE and Rui.

Rui identifies the concealed cost: Yu is still using service as a reason not to inhabit her own full power.

## 5.4 Rui's challenge is radical trust, not center competition

Rui threatens the unit's dissolution unless Yu proves she can beat her at full strength. The threat is reckless and emotionally coercive in form; the text itself later jokes that Rui had not really thought through what she would do if Yu lost.

But the underlying demand is precise:

`優は、全力を出したっていいの`

`少なくとも、このトリエルの中にいる限りは`

`私もすみれも、優の全力を受け止めて……`

`さらに全力を尽くして、輝いてみせるから`

`だから……私達の実力を信頼して、委ねてほしい`

This is one of the most important relational propositions in the entire origin corpus:

> **Do not make yourself smaller because you assume the people you love cannot survive your full size. Trust them enough to meet you at full strength.**

## 5.5 Yu narrowly defeats Rui

**TEXTUAL FACT:** in the three-song performance contest, Sumire reports that Yu wins on total points, narrowly. Rui had won two of the three songs by small margins, but Yu's aggregate score is higher.

This materially qualifies any literal reading of Rui as "absolute" because she is always individually stronger than Yu.

The mature implication is richer:

> **Rui's center position is not validated by requiring every teammate to be individually inferior.**

Rui is delighted that Yu beat her because the result proves that the partnership can contain Yu's full ability.

## 5.6 Yu does not abolish care; she re-authors it

Yu calls her habitual self-suppression a `呪い` and admits she cannot simply stop overnight. Her first recovery step is deliberately transitional: she will still act "for TRINITYAiLE, Sumire, and Rui," but now the way she serves them will be **by giving her full effort**.

This is important because the text does not demand an instantaneous individualist cure. A relational motive can remain while the behavior it produces changes from self-erasure to full participation.

# 6. Okuyama Sumire — usefulness, family sacrifice, and the right to want more than one thing

## 6.1 Sumire is recruited for a real expressive surplus

Sumire arrives as a former popular child actor who is commuting from Yamagata and training alone because peers distrust the apparent ease of her career change.

Rui recognizes that her technique is still rough but says:

`私達にはないものを――`

`あなたにしかないものを、持っている`

The unit is therefore not Rui + Yu + marketable child celebrity. Sumire is recruited because she carries expressive resources the other two lack.

Asakura's marketing cynicism—famous actress's daughter plus former child prodigy—is explicitly present, but Sumire herself refuses to treat her past as shameful and says she would rather use it than run from it.

## 6.2 The two-person victory creates a genuine dispensability crisis

When weather prevents Sumire from reaching the Grand Prix final qualifier, Rui and Yu perform as a pair and produce the tournament's highest score.

Sumire's reaction is not irrational. She has direct empirical evidence that the pair can win without her:

`二人でも……勝てちゃうんだ`

and:

`私がいなくても本戦行けちゃうんだもん`

Rui and Yu do **not** answer by falsifying that evidence. Instead, the later three-person performance produces an even higher record, and—more importantly—they state that three-person TRINITYAiLE is the identity they want.

This creates a useful distinction:

> **competitive dispensability and relational/constitutive indispensability are different questions.**

A person need not be literally necessary for every successful output to be necessary to the form of life the group has chosen.

## 6.3 Sumire's entertainment career begins partly as family labor

The post-Grand-Prix family arc reveals that Sumire's beloved older brother is a highly capable pianist who studied abroad. Sumire became a child entertainer partly to help finance his overseas piano education. When child-actor work declined, she transitioned toward idol work partly because she wanted to keep supporting that dream.

Later, however, Sumire explicitly says she has acquired a second dream of her own:

`三人でトップアイドルになる`

This means her idol vocation has an important dual genealogy:

1. instrumental/familial labor for her brother;
2. later genuinely self-authored desire produced inside TRINITYAiLE.

## 6.4 Sumire and her brother enter a reciprocal-sacrifice structure

The brother gives up overseas study when family finances worsen and later says he will inherit the brewery so Sumire can pursue idolhood. Sumire responds by considering giving up idolhood to protect the brewery and restore his piano future.

Each loves the other. Neither wants the other's sacrifice. Yet each treats sacrificing her/his own dream as the responsible response to the other's sacrifice.

This resembles the Shiraishi-sister structure but should not be collapsed into the same causal history. The common pattern is narrower:

> **care can become recursively zero-sum when each person uses self-denial as the instrument for protecting the other's future.**

## 6.5 Sumire's decisive act is asking for help

Rui and Yu come to Yamagata after Sumire leaves the unit. Crucially, Rui does not simply impose rescue. After explaining their desire to help, she says:

`その手を取るか取らないかは……あなたが決めるのよ`

`選んで、自分の意志で`

Sumire finally states the apparently contradictory full truth:

`私！　二人ともっと歌いたい！！`

`でも……家族のことも助けたいよ！！`

and then:

`私を、助けて……！`

This is not dependency as regression. It is an act of authorship: Sumire stops pretending one desire must be murdered before she is morally entitled to voice the other.

## 6.6 "全部欲しい" is not magical thinking; it is institutional problem-solving

Rui says:

`私は、欲しいものは全部、手に入れます……！！`

Yu reframes desire similarly: if there are many things you want, try to claim all of them.

The text then grounds this rhetoric materially. The trio:

- researches the brewery's economic problem;
- distinguishes product quality from regional depopulation/market-access problems;
- plans a Yamagata performance and merchandising strategy;
- contacts the local chamber of commerce;
- obtains Himeno Kiriko's production support;
- uses TRINITYAiLE's celebrity as regional demand generation;
- attracts sponsors;
- creates enough economic capacity for the brewery to employ labor and survive without Sumire's permanent withdrawal.

Thus the claim is not "want hard enough and constraints disappear."

It is closer to:

> **before accepting a tragic zero-sum choice as inevitable, use relationships, professional knowledge, institutions, and collective labor to test whether the choice-space can be expanded.**

That is a much stronger and more defensible ethic.

# 7. Rui/Yu/Sumire — chosen sisterhood and non-substitutability

## 7.1 The sister language emerges after demonstrated mutual care

Post-Grand-Prix, Rui and Yu explicitly call Sumire their little sister and describe the trio as effectively three sisters. This is not merely cute marketing language inside the source. It determines action: when Sumire's father is hospitalized, Rui and Yu rearrange work and travel to Yamagata with her.

Later, Sumire calls them:

`私のお姉ちゃん達`

The unit therefore acquires a kin-like relational register in addition to professional partnership.

## 7.2 Sisterhood does not abolish professional standards

The same Rui who calls Sumire her sister also tells her that choosing family over a professional obligation created a real professional-priority problem. She does not say love makes scheduling irrelevant.

Her solution is initially too binary—family versus total professional separation—but she quickly adds the more important relational correction: Sumire should have **consulted the unit** rather than carrying the decision alone.

This distinguishes care from indulgence.

## 7.3 "Three-person TRINITYAiLE" is chosen, not empirically inevitable

The pair can perform without Sumire. Yu can outscore Rui. Rui can lead without being strongest on every metric. None of those facts dissolves the trio.

The unit's identity is therefore partly normative:

> **these three people repeatedly choose a form in which each other's presence matters beyond immediate performance utility.**

This is why replacement logic fails even when substitution is technically possible.

# 8. Rui and Asakura — recognition, pedagogy, and an unstable father/professional boundary

## 8.1 Asakura appears to recognize Rui's identity while refusing ordinary paternal recognition

At Rui's initial BanPro audition, Rui concludes that Asakura knows who she is but treats her only as one aspiring idol among many. The origin does not give us enough access to Asakura's interiority at that moment to state his exact motive as textual fact.

**OPEN HYPOTHESIS:** his professional coldness may combine deliberate anti-favoritism, emotional avoidance, and a genuinely harsh talent philosophy. Later material should separate these possibilities.

## 8.2 His best lesson and worst relational wound can be the same behavior

Asakura repeatedly denies Rui easy validation. Some of his professional interventions are substantively valuable:

- he attacks self-sufficiency;
- he forms Rui/Yu around complementarity;
- he accepts Sumire when she adds a needed expressive dimension;
- he insists on results rather than inherited celebrity;
- he later gives specific criticism even after acknowledging strong performance.

But Rui's private stakes are paternal. Therefore professional withholding is never experienced as merely professional assessment.

This produces a structural conflict:

> **Asakura can be correct as producer while remaining injurious as father.**

The text should not force these into one moral verdict.

## 8.3 Mana becomes paternal benchmark by mediation

Asakura explicitly tells TRINITYAiLE to confront Mana's recorded performance and says he has felt no comparable talent before or since. Rui then concludes that to be recognized as top idol she must surpass Mana.

Thus Mana enters Rui's identity partly through **Asakura's valuation**, not because Mana originally inspires Rui to become an idol.

This is important for the anime-era Rui/Mana relation: Mana is a secondary benchmark attached to a prior dream and paternal recognition problem.

## 8.4 Grand Prix defeat transforms paternal recognition

After SUNNY PEACE defeats TRINITYAiLE, Rui recognizes that SUNNY has moved beyond Mana comparison and that TRINITY can still grow into a future not governed by Mana.

Asakura then says:

`いいステージだった`

`いいアイドルになったな`

Rui's involuntary response after he leaves is:

`……パパ……`

The scene is not a simple "father finally validates daughter, therefore arc solved." It matters precisely because Rui has just discovered a horizon beyond the benchmark that paternal recognition helped install.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** recognition arrives only after the unit's future becomes less dependent on recognition as its organizing end.

## 8.5 Post-Grand-Prix relationship thaw remains incomplete

Asakura later invites Rui to dinner, remarks that she resembles her mother, and begins showing more ordinary concern and humor. Rui can privately say `パパ`, though direct use remains difficult.

The relationship has shifted from cold professional dyad toward father/daughter possibility, but the origin does not resolve abandonment, Rui's mother's experience, or Asakura's reasons. Those remain future-evidence questions.

# 9. Mana, SUNNY PEACE, and the release from benchmark inheritance

## 9.1 TRINITY initially receives Mana as a benchmark, not a loved predecessor

Rui, Yu, and Sumire study Mana because Asakura frames her as the singular talent to surpass. Rui's language becomes explicitly historical: become a unique idol who changes history, exceed Mana's legend, force Asakura to acknowledge them.

This differs from Suzu's gratitude-based Mana inheritance, Kotono's grief/proxy structure, Sakura's bodily inheritance, and Rio's interrupted rivalry.

## 9.2 The origin makes the anime semifinal a benchmark crisis

Before the semifinal, Kiriko says SUNNY PEACE has stopped looking at Mana while TRINITYAiLE and LizNoir remain bound by the compulsion to surpass her.

Rui overhears and initially defends the existing path. Yet she also recognizes Sakura as someone formerly subject to comparison with Mana who may have moved beyond that dimension.

At the semifinal, Rui concludes almost immediately that SUNNY is already "higher" in the relevant sense.

The crucial retrospective statement after defeat is:

`誰かを追いかけるのは止めて、自分達の道を行く`

and Rui realizes the trio can aim for an unseen summit:

`長瀬麻奈に縛られることのない、まだ見ぬ高みへ`

This **strengthens** the frozen anime endpoint but remains later origin disclosure.

## 9.3 Defeat is productive because it restores futurity

TRINITYAiLE is deeply hurt by losing and all three eventually cry. Yet Rui explicitly experiences defeat as proof that they can still grow.

The unit therefore moves from:

> victory as proof of completed absolute status

toward:

> defeat as evidence that a future remains available.

That is a major maturation in the franchise's competitive ethics.

# 10. Performance, hierarchy, and the meaning of "absolute"

## 10.1 "Absolute idol" is Rui's desired social effect, not proof of ontological solitude

Rui defines the absolute idol as one who gives dreams, shines dazzlingly, and cannot be ignored. Nothing in that definition requires literal social isolation.

Her early mistake is believing the route to that effect must be self-sufficient.

## 10.2 Center is a role that must survive teammate strength

Yu beating Rui is the strongest origin evidence against a simplistic center-as-best-at-everything model.

Rui does not protect center status by demanding Yu underperform. Later she tells Sumire that if Sumire wants center, she should come take it at full strength—and Rui will fight to keep it.

Thus center is treated as **contestable but presently owned**, not metaphysically guaranteed.

## 10.3 Hierarchy becomes ethical when it is compatible with challenge

A stable TRINITYAiLE can contain:

- Rui as center;
- Yu as strategist/supportive older-sister figure;
- Sumire as mood-maker/younger-sister figure;

only if none of those roles functions as a prohibition against exceeding expectations, disagreeing, competing, or naming personal desire.

This supports the broader unit thesis: asymmetrical roles, symmetrical personhood.

# 11. Care, labor, and production ethics

## 11.1 The trio repeatedly rejects unilateral sacrifice

The six origins contain several versions:

- Rui rejects Yu making herself smaller for Rui.
- Rui/Yu reject Sumire deciding alone that her absence is best for them.
- Sumire's brother rejects her sacrificing her career to finance his dream.
- Sumire rejects her brother sacrificing piano only for her.

The recurring proposition is not "never sacrifice." It is:

> **do not make a life-restructuring sacrifice on another person's behalf while excluding that person from the decision about what they actually want.**

## 11.2 Asking for help is repeatedly reclassified as agency

Sumire's `私を、助けて……！` is not presented as weakness. It is the first fully honest articulation of a multi-object desire she had been suppressing.

This connects TRINITYAiLE to the emerging IDOLY PRIDE ethics of accompaniment: help should increase another person's authorship rather than replace it.

## 11.3 Professional infrastructure is morally ambivalent but indispensable

The Yamagata solution depends on institutional capacities the girls cannot supply alone: staff, equipment, sponsorship, scheduling, local government/business coordination, and Kiriko's organizational access.

The sequence therefore falsifies the early Rui fantasy that authentic achievement must be isolated achievement.

At the same time, the final Kiriko scene reveals covert instrumental motives: she has intervened partly because she wants TRINITYAiLE for Pret-a-Porter and refers to them as useful pieces/resources.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** benevolent outcome and instrumental institutional motive coexist.

This should route forward to the labor/institution ledger rather than be moralized prematurely.

# 12. Sumire sibling arc and cross-unit comparative proposition

The Sumire/brother system is not identical to Saki/Chisa, but Phase 2 should compare them.

### Shiraishi sisters
Both sisters learn to equate self-limitation with love, recursively reinforcing dependence/protection.

### Okuyama siblings
Each tries to free the other by surrendering a personal dream, creating a reciprocal zero-sum structure around scarce money, family labor, and professional opportunity.

The shared proposition is:

> **love becomes dangerous when self-erasure is mistaken for the only credible proof that the other person's future matters.**

The correction in both cases is not reduced attachment. It is expanded agency, explicit communication, and a relational structure in which both people are allowed to continue wanting.

This remains a **comparative hypothesis** pending later source stress-testing.

# 13. Prospective anime/Hoshimi routing — what may and may not change

## 13.1 Rui's anime motivation

The origin **strengthens** the anime-endpoint claim that Rui's idol dream predates paternal revelation/recognition. It now has direct childhood evidence.

It **qualifies** any interpretation that the father issue is merely incidental. Once disclosed, Asakura substantially changes Rui's success criterion and helps install Mana as benchmark.

## 13.2 Yu's anime role

The anime can make Yu look like a naturally comfortable supportive member. The origin reveals that this role has developmental risk: support was once entangled with learned self-suppression.

This may retrospectively explain her relational intelligence, but it must not be projected backward as anime-explicit knowledge.

## 13.3 Sumire's anime role
