# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — PHASE 1B SUNNY PEACE ORIGIN AUDIT

## 0. Executive conclusion

The five SUNNY PEACE origin bundles substantially strengthen a proposition that the anime already made plausible but could not fully establish:

> **SUNNY PEACE is not a group of people who are naturally sunny. It is a deliberately assembled ecology of people whose ability to shine has been interrupted, misrecognized, suppressed, instrumentalized, or made dependent on somebody else. Their “sun” is produced by circulation: one person returns another person’s desire, courage, legitimacy, visibility, care, or future to her.**

This is not a metaphor imposed from later event material. It is built directly into the origin architecture. In Shizuku’s origin, Makino says he wants to gather people who possess potential yet cannot shine alone — `素質はあるのに一人では輝けない` — including a girl who knows nothing and a girl whose very knowledge prevents her from moving forward, so that they can proceed together. Haruko’s origin then supplies the complementary senior function: the unit requires someone able to surround, teach, and guide those girls, and Makino states that without Haruko the group cannot exist.

The five origins make the design concrete:

- **Chisa** has desire but has learned to treat desire as a burden to Saki.
- **Rei** has discipline and talent but has converted art into measurable proof for an invalidating father.
- **Shizuku** has extraordinary idol literacy but has learned that being publicly visible invites ridicule and social punishment.
- **Haruko** has experience, kindness, endurance, and institutional memory but years of failure have taught her to imagine herself as support more readily than as the person who deserves to shine.
- **Sakura** has enormous life-energy and generative warmth, but her smile begins partly as compulsory emotional labor, her future is entangled with illness and dead friends, and her transplanted heartbeat has become a trusted survival signal before it becomes a metaphysical identity problem.

The result is not five deficiencies mechanically fitting together. Each girl possesses a real strength that becomes distorted when isolated. SUNNY PEACE creates a social environment in which those strengths can recover their proper ends.

The strongest unit-level formulation after this tranche is therefore:

> **SUNNY PEACE converts compromised individual light into reciprocal social light.**

Or, in the language of recognition:

> **Each member begins with a problem of being seen correctly, and the unit becomes the place where recognition can be given without requiring self-erasure.**

This tranche also requires an explicit addendum to the completed Hoshimi anime/game expansion audit. The addendum does not overturn that audit. It supplies missing provenance for Chisa’s separation from Saki, Makino’s unit-design concept, Haruko’s founding institutional status, Haruko’s necessity to the SUNNY design, and Sakura’s pre-Hoshimi heart/Tomo/Haruko chain.

# 3. Shiraishi Chisa — desire had become morally equivalent to burden

## 3.1 Direct history: the accident creates an ethical rule, not merely a fear response

The first Chisa story supplies a major causal correction to any reading of her as simply innately shy. Young Chisa is described as active enough that adults must watch her carefully. During a family beach trip she disobeys an instruction not to go into the water alone, is swept away, and nearly drowns.

The psychologically decisive exchange is not merely the accident. It is the mutual guilt that follows it.

Saki promises:

`もう二度と目を離したりしないから……！`

`私が絶対に千紗を守るから……！`

Chisa responds:

`私、いい子になるから……`

`お姉ちゃんに迷惑を掛けない、いい子になるから……`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** after this event Chisa goes out less, becomes more withdrawn, and Saki becomes intensely protective.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Chisa does not merely learn “the sea is dangerous.” She learns a much broader moral rule: **my spontaneous desire can frighten or harm the person I love, therefore goodness means being easy to protect and not creating trouble.**

That rule explains why her later inhibition is relational rather than purely temperamental. She is not simply afraid of failure. She is afraid that wanting something will impose costs on Saki.

## 3.2 The idol dream is suppressed twice

The childhood sisters both enjoy idols. Chisa wants to sing and dance with Saki but does not say so because she fears dragging Saki down. Saki, for her part, also sacrifices or postpones idol desire in order to remain beside Chisa.

This produces a structure more precise than “dependent younger sister / protective older sister”:

> Chisa suppresses desire so as not to burden Saki.  
> Saki suppresses desire so as not to abandon Chisa.  
> Each sacrifice becomes evidence that the other person must continue sacrificing.

This is a **reciprocal sacrifice loop**.

The relevance to the frozen anime endpoint is substantial. The anime’s later individuation of the sisters is not the destruction of a healthy dependency. It is the unwinding of an arrangement in which both sisters have partly defined love as self-limitation.

## 3.3 The audition exposes the problem to Makino

Chisa’s Hoshimi interview is one of the most important manager scenes in the five origins. Her stated motivation is intertwined with changing herself and standing beside Saki, but at the end of her own interview the last thing she wants to say is:

`お姉ちゃんを、よろしくお願いします！`

Makino’s private assessment is explicit:

`不器用だけど一生懸命で`

`不思議と応援したくなる子だったな`

but:

`あそこまでお姉さんに依存していると`

`アイドルになっても姉の陰に隠れてしまいそうだ`

`姉に頼らずやっていく覚悟があるんだろうか`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Chisa fails the audition; Saki passes.

The result precipitates the first moment in which Chisa’s desire becomes undeniable to Chisa herself. She initially insists that becoming an idol only matters if she can do so with Saki. But her body contradicts the rationalization: she cannot stop crying. Saki asks whether the failure hurts because she truly wanted idolhood. Chisa realizes:

`私……アイドルになりたいよ……！`

This is one of the tranche’s cleanest self-recognition scenes. Desire becomes legible not because another person authorizes it, but because grief at its loss reveals its independent existence.

## 3.4 Makino does not erase the failure; he changes the question

Weeks later Makino calls. Importantly, he does **not** retroactively declare the audition a pass. He says clearly that she failed. What he wants to know is whether the desire survives separation:

`お姉さんと同じ事務所になれなくても`

`あなたはアイドルになりたいですか？`

Chisa answers yes. Only then does he scout her.

He also states the asymmetry frankly: as a rescued unsuccessful candidate, she will not receive treatment equal to Saki’s; Saki will have debut priority; they likely cannot join the same group.

This scene is important for Makino’s ethics and for Chisa’s agency. The intervention is paternalistic in the limited sense that Makino intentionally tests a developmental condition, but it does not fabricate desire for her. The decisive datum is whether Chisa can say “I want this” after the sister-dependent version of the dream has become unavailable.

## 3.5 Separation becomes a condition of sisterhood rather than its negation

The retrospective frame makes the origin’s interpretation unusually explicit. Chisa says that when debut approached she feared she could do nothing without Saki. Shizuku took her hand and said they would work together. Chisa then learned to orient herself toward SUNNY PEACE rather than exclusively toward Saki.

She concludes that if she and Saki had been placed in the same unit, she might still be depending on her, and that becoming a full-fledged idol required separation.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the story does not define individuation as becoming self-sufficient. Chisa moves from **exclusive dependence** to **distributed interdependence**. Shizuku and the rest of SUNNY are not substitutes for Saki; they make it possible for Saki to stop being Chisa’s only permitted support structure.

That distinction matters for the unit thesis. SUNNY PEACE does not teach “stand alone.” It teaches that one can possess a desire as one’s own while still accepting other people’s hands.

## 3.6 Hoshimi-audit consequence

The prior Hoshimi audit’s bilateral-individuation reading remains correct but is now incomplete.

**ADDENDUM REQUIRED:** Makino recognized Chisa’s sister dependence during recruitment and deliberately tested whether her idol desire remained valid under a condition of separation. Therefore the later sun/moon split has developmental provenance that precedes final unit allocation.

**Guardrail:** do not overstate this as proof that Makino foresaw the complete Saki/Chisa arc. The source supports risk recognition and a deliberately created test, not omniscience.

# 4. Ichinose Rei — proof is what desire becomes when desire is denied legitimacy

## 4.1 Her original dance dream is relational, not competitive

Rei’s earliest dance memory is a theme-park stage/parade. What moves her is not ranking but the ability of movement to enchant an audience. She thinks:

`ダンスはこんなにも人を魅了するんだ`

and wants:

`ダンスでみんなに夢を届けたい`

This matters because it gives us a baseline before family conflict distorts the dream. Rei’s original relation to performance is **recipient-oriented**. She wants to reproduce in other people the wonder she experienced as a spectator.

## 4.2 Her brother models artistic self-authorship

Rei’s older brother is a guitarist whose father expects him to become a doctor. When ordered to give up music he eventually declares:

`俺は父さんの人形じゃない！　俺の人生は俺が決める！`

Rei is his enthusiastic supporter and calls herself his first fan.

The brother therefore provides two models simultaneously:

1. **art as self-authorship** — a life can belong to the person living it rather than to family expectation;
2. **art as reciprocal support** — the performer is sustained by somebody who believes in the performance.

Both models later return in Rei’s own idolhood.

## 4.3 Paternal invalidation converts joy into measurement

After the brother leaves, paternal succession pressure moves to Rei. She is expected to follow the medical path and is forced away from dance. She later resumes in secret, rediscovers bodily enjoyment, and is seen dancing by a young girl who says she wants to move like Rei.

That encounter helps Rei identify the desire as her own. But when she declares it, her father interprets dance not as vocation but as escape from academic difficulty. He remains unmoved even when she wins a competition.

Rei eventually states that she needs results that cannot be ignored:

`無視出来ないくらいの成果が必要なんだって`

The critical point is the direction of causality.

A shallow model would be:

> Rei is a perfectionist, therefore she wants measurable achievement.

The origin supports a more precise model:

> Rei’s desire is repeatedly denied legitimacy, therefore she begins to treat measurable achievement as the language in which desire might finally become impossible to dismiss.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** result orientation is a defensive translation of artistic value into the evidentiary system of an authority that refuses subjective testimony.

## 4.4 Idolhood initially enters as an instrument

Hoshimi is attractive because it offers a way to turn dance into paid professional work. Rei does not rewrite her history into “I always wanted to be an idol.” She knows that idolhood initially functioned as a means of proving the viability of her chosen life.

The origin treats that instrumental motive as morally unstable from Rei’s own perspective. After debut she looks at members who visibly love idolhood and worries that she is using the position for impure reasons.

Chisa asks the right question:

not “Why did you begin?” but “What do you feel **now**?”

This is a small but important piece of SUNNY’s reciprocal architecture. Chisa, whose own arc required separating present desire from its sister-dependent origin, asks Rei to make the same temporal distinction.

## 4.5 The handshake event restores the original purpose of dance

At SUNNY PEACE’s first handshake event, a young fan recognizes Rei. It is the same girl who once saw Rei dancing and wanted to dance like her. She tells Rei that she has loved her dance since that encounter and now supports her as an idol.

The structure is exact:

- child Rei sees performance and wants to give people dreams;
- another child sees Rei and receives that dream;
- years later the recipient returns as a fan and tells Rei that the transmission succeeded.

Rei realizes:

`ファンの応援ってこんなに嬉しいものなんだ`

and that if people are moved and support her, she wants to pursue idolhood seriously.

By the present frame she can say:

`父を認めさせたくて始めた仕事だけど、今は`

`そんなことは関係なくアイドルを続けたいと思ってる`

`――だって私、アイドルが好きだから`

## 4.6 The real movement is external legitimacy -> audience reciprocity

The historical shorthand “controlled proof -> shared joy” is good, but V2 can state the mechanism more precisely:

> **Rei begins with performance as wonder, has it narrowed into proof under paternal invalidation, and recovers its original relational purpose when an audience member returns the feeling her performance created.**

She does not abandon discipline, ambition, evidence, or technique. She changes what those things serve.

This also explains why later Rei can retain a strong result orientation without being reducible to it. The discipline is real. The danger arises when measurable outcome again becomes the sole guarantor that a dream is legitimate.

## 4.7 Hoshimi-audit consequence

The Hoshimi audit’s “dance first; idolhood as professional container; later broader identity” finding is **CONFIRMED and STRENGTHENED**.

The origin supplies the missing conversion mechanism: **fan recognition restores audience reciprocity and allows Rei to stop treating paternal recognition as the central reason the career must succeed.**

No contradiction addendum is necessary for Rei, but this causal refinement should be inherited into later character and manager ledgers.

# 5. Hyodo Shizuku — the problem is not merely shyness; it is that visibility became dangerous

## 5.1 Fandom is her first successful public self

Shizuku’s older cousin, Akiya Mone (`秋宮もね`), becomes an idol while Shizuku is young. Shizuku becomes a devoted fan, attends lives, follows idol culture, and participates online under the name `ぷにもちどろっぷ`.

The fan community is important because Shizuku can communicate there more freely than in ordinary peer life. Fandom is not withdrawal from sociality. It is the first public context in which her attention and enthusiasm find a usable language.

Her elementary-school graduation essay names the desire directly: she wants to become an idol.

## 5.2 School turns visibility into punishment

Classmates mock the dream because they perceive Shizuku as gloomy, quiet, and unsmiling. The hostility escalates in middle school: her fan relations are maliciously misrepresented, possessions are hidden, her fandom becomes material for ridicule, and an online social world that had allowed expression becomes another vector of humiliation.

The teacher response makes the injury institutional rather than merely peer-level. Shizuku’s situation is publicly exposed and framed through the idea that the bullied party also has a cause in herself: `いじめはいじめられる側にも原因がある`.

This is a profound recognition failure. The lesson Shizuku receives is not simply “some children are cruel.” It is:

> when I become visible, other people may define what my feelings mean; authority may ratify their definition; my own account of myself may not control the public story.

She withdraws, deletes the fan account, and relies on idol DVDs as private emotional support.

## 5.3 Mone names the inherited judgment as a curse

Mone later calls the old judgment `呪いの言葉` — cursed words. The importance of this formulation is that Shizuku’s self-description (“I am not suited to be an idol”) is revealed as partly an internalized social verdict rather than neutral self-knowledge.

The origin does not solve this by telling her to become extroverted. Mone’s support and later Makino’s recruitment instead reopen the possibility that Shizuku’s existing traits might have idol value.

## 5.4 She first enters Hoshimi trying to remain on the audience/management side

Shizuku initially comes to Hoshimi seeking a part-time route toward becoming an **idol manager**. Haruko mistakenly sends her into the idol audition process.

This is psychologically coherent: Shizuku wants proximity to idolhood without exposing herself as the object being watched. Management would allow her fandom knowledge to matter while preserving the safer observer position.

After the mistaken audition fails, Makino nevertheless sees something in her and later invites her into the new group. She refuses because she continues to regard herself as unsuitable.

## 5.5 Makino’s studio demonstration defines SUNNY PEACE’s founding logic

Makino asks Shizuku to evaluate Sakura practicing dance. Shizuku immediately sees the technical weakness but cannot look away from Sakura’s joy and radiance.

Makino then gives the most explicit founding statement in the SUNNY origin corpus:

`素質はあるのに一人では輝けない……そういう子達を`

`集めて、新しいグループを作りたいと思っている`

He continues:

`何も知らない子と、知っているが故に前に進めない子が`

`手を組んで、一歩一歩進んでいくんだ`

This is crucial for both Shizuku and the unit.

Sakura is the “knows nothing” side of the local contrast: little idol knowledge, but enormous immediate light. Shizuku is the “knows so much that knowledge blocks movement” side: she understands fans and idols deeply but believes that very understanding proves she herself cannot occupy the stage.

Makino specifically reframes her fan perspective as vocational competence:

`誰よりもファンの気持ちが分かる君なら`

`誰よりもファンを大切にする素敵なアイドルになれる`

The story thereby makes a broader argument:

> **fan knowledge does not merely qualify Shizuku to serve idols from behind them; it can qualify her to become the kind of idol who knows what being reached feels like.**

## 5.6 Her declaration preserves difference rather than erasing it

Shizuku does not say “I have become outgoing, therefore I can be an idol.” She says:

`私……喋るのが苦手で……人と関わることも下手で……`

`上手に笑うことも出来ないけど……`

`それでも……そんな私だけど……アイドルになりたい`

This is one of the tranche’s strongest self-authorship statements because the connective `それでも` matters. The dream is not conditional on first becoming a different kind of person.

## 5.7 SUNNY PEACE becomes reparative visibility

Later in the origin Shizuku stops trying to become Mone. She enjoys fans’ voices and singing in her own way. When Mone retires and moves to the audience side, the direction of looking is reversed without the relationship disappearing.

Shizuku can finally tell Mone that SUNNY PEACE is her youth:

`今が……ここが……`

`サニーピースが、私の青春だよ`

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the movement is not adequately described as watcher -> performer. It is:

> **punitive visibility -> safe participatory fandom -> chosen visibility -> self-authored performance.**

SUNNY PEACE is therefore both a professional unit and a reparative peer ecology. It gives Shizuku a public that can see her without confiscating the meaning of what it sees.

## 5.8 Hoshimi-audit consequence

**ADDENDUM REQUIRED.**

The prior Hoshimi audit correctly described formation as partly experimental, but Shizuku’s origin provides Makino’s explicit design language: he is intentionally constructing a unit from **complementary incompleteness**.

Guardrail: this does not mean all members possess equivalent wounds or that Makino fully understands each one. The source supports a recruitment-level design principle, not therapeutic omniscience.

# 6. Saeki Haruko — support is not a consolation role; it is part of Hoshimi’s founding thesis

## 6.1 Haruko begins with persistence before she begins with success

As a child Haruko becomes lost at the first NEXT VENUS Grand Prix and meets former idol/producer Hoshimura Megumi (`星村恵`). Megumi gives her a doctrine built around the heart that refuses to lose, break, or give up.

Haruko’s later career repeatedly tests that doctrine. She is rejected from auditions for being plain, lacking `華`, and lacking `特別感`. She is not told she lacks effort. She is told that effort has not made her market-visible.

This matters because Haruko becomes SUNNY PEACE’s senior from a history of **unrewarded persistence**, not from effortless veteran authority.

## 6.2 Saegusa’s original decision makes Haruko institutional architecture

Megumi eventually connects Haruko to the newly forming Hoshimi Production. Saegusa’s first impression is that Haruko may be too kind for an industry structured around rankings and defeating rivals.

Haruko answers that she dislikes dragging others down and wants idols to cooperate in enlivening the industry. When challenged about being repeatedly told she is unsuited, she says:

`向いてない人がアイドルを目指しちゃいけないんですか`

and defines her ideal around believing in dreams, believing in people, and refusing to give up.

Saegusa’s response is foundational. He identifies both `他者を想う優しさ` and `自分を諦めない強さ`, and then projects her value forward:

`この先、星見プロから何人ものアイドルがデビューする`

`その時、彼女達の支えになるのは……`

`キミのような強くて優しいアイドルなのかもしれないな`

Then:

`キミは星見プロの未来に必要な人材だ`

`キミが、星見プロのアイドル第一号だ`

This is a major institutional fact.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Haruko is Hoshimi Production’s first idol.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Hoshimi’s eventual collective ethos is foreshadowed in its very first idol selection. Haruko is chosen partly because a future multi-idol institution may require somebody whose competitive strength does not depend on devaluing other people.

## 6.3 Mana does not invalidate Haruko’s worth, but she intensifies the wound

Haruko immediately recognizes in Mana the special radiance she herself has been told she lacks. She debuts first, then watches Mana overtake her at extraordinary speed.

The origin allows envy without converting it into hatred. Mana becomes friend, colleague, model, and source of belief. Haruko’s failure to become Mana does not prove that she lacks ethical seriousness; it demonstrates that the industry’s distribution of attention is genuinely unequal.

This is an important anti-sentimental point. The story does not pretend kindness automatically produces market success.

## 6.4 Saegusa accepts managerial responsibility

The origin includes a notable management correction: Saegusa acknowledges that Haruko’s stagnation is not simply evidence of personal inadequacy. He failed to create enough work that drew out her potential.

This matters to the later manager synthesis. The franchise is willing to attribute career outcomes partly to production decisions rather than treating every success or failure as a transparent measurement of the idol’s intrinsic worth.

## 6.5 Post-Mana Haruko nearly disappears into institutional maintenance

After Mana’s death and Hoshimi’s crisis, Haruko has little idol work and begins doing administrative/support tasks. She seriously considers giving up.

This is the extreme version of her long-running danger:

> the person selected because she can support future idols may become so useful as support that her own right to remain an idol disappears from view.

The new trainees produce both hope and insecurity. Haruko wants to join the new group, but she cannot bring herself to ask. She assumes that inserting an unsuccessful older idol into a rookie project may be selfish.

## 6.6 Makino’s answer: Haruko is not decoration; the unit structurally requires her

When the member list is finally shown to Haruko, her name is already on it. Makino says he regarded her inclusion as so obvious that he failed to tell her.

His explanation is direct:

`一人では輝けない彼女達を包み込んで、教え導いて`

`あげられる人が、このグループには必要なんです`

and:

`遙子さんの持つ経験と、優しさと、諦めない強さと`

`アイドルへの熱い想いが必要なんです`

`遙子さんなしに、このグループはありえません`

This upgrades Haruko’s role from “mature member who happens to help” to **design requirement**.

It also completes the logic first articulated by Saegusa years earlier. Hoshimi’s first idol was selected because future idols might need exactly this kind of strength; when the multi-idol future actually arrives, Makino independently makes the same judgment.

## 6.7 The group must return support to its supporter

Haruko initially imagines that she will make the inexperienced members shine. Looking back, she realizes that she also joined their shared effort and shone with them.

This is essential. If Haruko only remained the person who supports others, SUNNY PEACE would reproduce her old self-erasure in a benevolent form. The unit becomes successful as an ethical structure only when care circulates back toward the caregiver.

Her reunion with Megumi closes the childhood loop. Haruko can finally introduce herself not as a lost child or aspirant but as:

`星見プロ所属のアイドル`

`サニーピースの佐伯遙子です！`

## 6.8 Hoshimi-audit consequence

**ADDENDUM REQUIRED.** The Hoshimi audit must be expanded to include:

- Haruko as literal **first Hoshimi idol**;
- Saegusa’s original future-facing rationale for selecting her;
- Saegusa’s later management self-critique;
- Makino’s explicit statement that SUNNY PEACE cannot exist without Haruko.

The prior “pre-Mana bridge / living institutional memory” model remains correct but understates her architectural status.

# 7. Kawasaki Sakura — the sun begins as compulsory smiling and becomes chosen generativity

## 7.1 Childhood smiling is first an act of emotional protection

Sakura’s origin gives the strongest correction in this tranche to a surface archetype.

She is diagnosed with severe heart disease before elementary school. Her mother blames herself and cries. Young Sakura cannot understand the medical situation fully, but she understands that **she appears to be the reason her mother is unhappy**.

She decides:

`お母さんの前では笑っていよう`

because:

`私が笑顔でいれば、お母さんも安心して`

`笑顔でいてくれる`

As her condition worsens, her father works more for treatment and her mother carries household burdens. Sakura consciously tries to become a compliant `いい子` who does not add difficulty.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Sakura’s smiling predates her idol career and is partly an intentional strategy for regulating other people’s distress.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the “sunny” personality cannot be read only as transparent temperament. Smiling initially includes **protective emotional labor** — a sick child taking partial responsibility for the emotional stability of her caregivers.

## 7.2 The mortality revelation makes the smile untenable

Sakura overhears nurses say she likely has only a few years left. The sequence is unusually explicit. She asks what dying feels like, whether it hurts, whether her mind disappears, and finally says:

`私……消えたくない……やっぱり、死にたくないよ……`

Yet when a hospitalized girl approaches her, Sakura immediately produces the old rule:

`私は平気だよ`

`ほら、顔だって笑ってるでしょ？`

The girl — Tomo — sees that the “smile” looks like a face about to cry.

## 7.3 Tomo’s first gift is not happiness; it is permission to stop performing happiness

Tomo tells Sakura:

`辛い時は無理に笑わなくていいんだよ？`

and:

`笑いたい時は笑って、泣きたい時は泣いていいんだよ`

Sakura asks:

`いいの……？　私、泣いていいの……？`

Then she finally admits that she is afraid to die and cries in Tomo’s arms.

This is foundational for every later use of Sakura’s smile.

A mature reading cannot say:

> Sakura’s smile is meaningful because she always keeps smiling through pain.

The origin says something more ethically careful:

> **Sakura’s later smile can become meaningful because somebody first teaches her that love does not require smiling when she needs to cry.**

This separates chosen generosity from compulsory masking.

## 7.4 The bucket list is a technology of futurity

Tomo becomes Sakura’s `楽しいことの師匠`. The two begin writing `やってみたかったリスト` — lists of things they want to try.

The list is often comic in later Sakura material because it justifies her appetite for activities, food, travel, and experiences. The origin reveals the mortality structure under the gag.

For two children living with serious illness, naming things they want to do transforms an uncertain future into an enumerable field of possible life.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the list is a **technology of recovered futurity**. It turns “I may not have much future” into “there are futures I can name.”

Sakura’s later appetite for ordinary experience is therefore not merely genki excess. It is the continuation of a practice born under the threat of death.

## 7.5 Tomo, Mana, and the chain of transmitted encouragement

Sakura attends Hoshimi Festival while Tomo cannot. She encounters Mana without understanding her later significance. The origin later directly links Tomo to the sick fan whose message affected Mana around the Reiz performance: Makino recognizes Tomo’s characteristic determination to go to her oshi’s live `這ってでも`.

Tomo manages to attend before dying shortly before the next festival.

This creates a chain already characteristic of the franchise:

> idol reaches fan -> fan reaches friend -> friend later becomes idol -> received feeling is transmitted again.

The dead do not need literal agency for their feelings to continue moving through social relations.

## 7.6 Sakura initially converts Tomo’s death into obligation

At death Tomo asks Sakura to do things Tomo can no longer do. Sakura responds by promising to survive, become healthy, and perform experiences for both of them.

This is emotionally understandable, but the final story later corrects it.

The same structure will become important with Mana: the living person can easily turn gratitude into delegated life-purpose.

## 7.7 The transplanted heartbeat becomes a survival epistemology before it becomes an identity problem

After Sakura’s heart transplant, rehabilitation is exhausting. At a moment when she wants to stop, she feels the heart beat strongly and interprets it as encouragement:

`心臓が頑張れって言ってる……`

`負けるなって私を励ましてる……`

She decides to trust the feeling and continue.

Two years later she describes the heart as something that tells her important things. At Tomo’s grave she hears Haruko speaking at Mana’s grave, the heart reacts, and Sakura tells Haruko not to give up. Haruko admits that perhaps she came because she wanted somebody to tell her exactly that, and she decides to continue.

Three years after the transplant, Sakura’s feet carry her in the wrong direction while she is completing her experience list. She reaches Hoshimi Production, feels the same strong heartbeat, enters the building, and is mistakenly routed by Haruko into the audition process.

Because V2 already possesses the frozen anime endpoint, the project knows the donor is Mana. But the methodological distinction is essential:

**TEXTUAL FACT:** this origin repeatedly shows Sakura interpreting heartbeat as guidance.

**NOT A NEW TEXTUAL FACT FROM THIS BUNDLE:** explicit donor identity.

The origin explains why the later anime “heart oracle” is psychologically plausible. Sakura did not suddenly invent a supernatural theory after learning about Mana. She had spent years treating bodily sensation as a source of courage, and that habit had coincided with survival, Haruko’s continuation, and her own path into Hoshimi.

Therefore the frozen anime endpoint claim remains correct:

> **the heart has provenance without retaining jurisdiction.**

But the origin adds an important qualification:

> **relinquishing the heart’s authority is developmentally costly because trusting that heartbeat had once been adaptive and repeatedly meaningful.**

## 7.8 Tomo explicitly rejects substitution

The most important final move comes when Sakura receives Tomo’s completed notebook. Sakura immediately thinks:

`私がかわりに全部叶えるからね`

Tomo has anticipated exactly this response. Her written message says, in substance and explicitly, that the listed wishes were **Tomo’s** wishes. Sakura should not make them her own obligation; Sakura should fulfill **her own** desires.

Tomo asks only that the notebook help Sakura remember her and smile. She says Sakura’s smile made her happy and asks Sakura to keep making others happy.

This is the tranche’s clearest anti-substitution statement:

> **memory may shape a survivor’s future without becoming ownership of the survivor’s future.**

The formal resonance with Mana is extremely strong. Sakura receives this lesson before the Mana inheritance crisis becomes legible to her: a dead person’s unlived possibilities are not a command to become that person’s executor.

## 7.9 The sun is re-authored

The origin therefore gives Sakura’s smile three layers:

1. **protective performance** — smile so loved ones will not worry;
2. **permission for emotional truth** — Tomo teaches her that fear and tears do not destroy care;
3. **chosen generativity** — Sakura learns that her genuine smile can make other people happier and chooses to use it that way.

The final Sunny statement is not “I must smile no matter what.” It is:

`大好きな人達が笑顔でいられるように`

`私達サニーピースが、みんなを明るく照らすよ`

This is why calling Sakura “the sun” is legitimate only after the compulsory-smile history is preserved. The light is not innocence before darkness. It is a way of living after fear, illness, grief, and dependence without letting those experiences monopolize the future.

# 8. SUNNY PEACE as a system

## 8.1 Complementary incompleteness is explicit, not merely inferred

The most important new architectural fact is that Makino’s unit is designed around people with potential who cannot sustainably shine alone.

This should not be reduced to “the members cover one another’s weaknesses.” That would make the structure too mechanical. The origins instead show **strengths whose purposes have been bent**:

| Member | Genuine strength | Isolated distortion | What relational context restores |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chisa | care, sensitivity, sincere desire | care becomes burden-avoidance and desire suppression | desire can be owned without abandoning relation |
| Rei | discipline, bodily skill, ambition | achievement becomes proof demanded by invalidating authority | technique can serve people who receive it |
| Shizuku | attention, fandom knowledge, reception intelligence | self-knowledge becomes a case against visibility | audience knowledge becomes performer competence |
| Haruko | care, endurance, experience, professional memory | support threatens to replace her own right to shine | caregiver is also a member who receives support |
| Sakura | warmth, appetite for life, emotional immediacy | smile becomes compulsory; memory risks delegated living | joy can be chosen; the dead inspire without owning the future |

SUNNY PEACE works because these strengths are not amputated. They are returned to their relational ends.

## 8.2 “Sun” is circulated light

The origins decisively weaken any personality-sorting interpretation of sun versus moon.

Only Sakura cleanly fits the high-energy solar archetype, and even she complicates it. Chisa and Shizuku are quiet. Rei is controlled and exacting. Haruko’s warmth exists beside professional disappointment and adult self-consciousness.

A stronger interpretation is:

> **SUNNY PEACE is solar because light circulates through it.**

One member is seen by another; that recognition enables action; the action reaches somebody else; that recipient returns support. Rei’s handshake story is nearly a laboratory demonstration of this mechanism. Shizuku’s fan-to-idol movement is another. Sakura/Tomo is another. Haruko’s two generations of Hoshimi selection make it institutional.

This interpretation remains an **INTERPRETATION**, not a line of dialogue, and must be retested against later event/music/performance evidence. But the five origins provide unusually strong convergent support.

## 8.3 Recognition is the hidden common problem

All five origins can be reframed as recognition crises:

- **Chisa:** can a desire be recognized as hers rather than as an extension of Saki?
- **Rei:** can artistic life be recognized as legitimate without paternal ratification?
- **Shizuku:** can she become visible without other people dictating what her visibility means?
- **Haruko:** can durable professional worth be recognized without spectacular `華` or early market success?
- **Sakura:** can a life profoundly shaped by the dead still be recognized as the survivor’s own life?

The common solution is not generic self-esteem. It is **relational recognition that does not confiscate authorship**.

This may become one of the strongest comparative axes for the final SUNNY synthesis.

## 8.4 The anti-substitution ethic appears before Mana’s inheritance problem

Sakura/Tomo demonstrates that the franchise’s anti-substitution ethic is not a special rule invented only to solve the supernatural Mana/Sakura situation.

The same moral structure exists in an ordinary human friendship:

- a dying person has unrealized wishes;
- the survivor feels love and guilt;
- the survivor wants to fulfill those wishes in the dead person’s place;
- the dying person rejects that transformation of love into delegated identity;
- remembrance is preserved, but future authorship stays with the living person.

This should be routed as a load-bearing comparative case to the later grief/memory/inheritance synthesis.

# 9. Makino and Hoshimi — what this tranche adds to management analysis

## 9.1 Makino recruits developmental possibility, not only current polish

Three origin sequences are especially important:

- Chisa is not simply selected for current ability; Makino tests whether desire survives separation from Saki.
- Shizuku is not selected despite her fandom; her fandom is the reason Makino believes she can become unusually attentive to fans.
- Haruko is not retained as sentimental veteran continuity; Makino treats her experience/care/endurance as structurally necessary to the group.

This produces a clearer production philosophy:

> **Makino is willing to recruit for transformable relation between traits rather than only for isolated present-day star power.**

That remains different from omniscience. He sometimes discovers consequences after the fact. But the origins show more intentional developmental design than the Hoshimi retelling alone made visible.

## 9.2 Saegusa’s institutional philosophy predates Makino’s group design

Haruko’s origin matters because the “support can itself be idol strength” principle is not created only when Makino needs a senior for SUNNY. Saegusa selected Hoshimi’s first idol partly because he imagined that future idols might need a strong, kind person like Haruko.

Years later Makino reaches an operationally similar conclusion.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** there is a line of institutional continuity between Saegusa’s founding judgment and Makino’s SUNNY design. Hoshimi’s identity is not only inherited from Mana’s miracle; part of it predates Mana in the choice of Haruko.

This is a significant correction to any Hoshimi history that begins conceptually with Mana.

## 9.3 Management responsibility is explicitly acknowledged

Saegusa’s admission that he did not provide Haruko enough work capable of drawing out her potential is important for later professional-labor analysis. It denies a pure meritocratic reading of idol outcomes.

Failure to shine may reflect:

- talent;
- fit;
- timing;
- public reception;
- opportunity allocation;
- managerial imagination.

The series therefore gives producers ethical responsibility for creating conditions under which talent can become legible.

# 15. Final tranche judgment

The five SUNNY PEACE origins justify their `CORE` status not because they provide colorful backstory, but because they alter the causal model of the entire unit.

The anime can make SUNNY PEACE look like the brighter half of a sun/moon division. The origins reveal something much more interesting. Their brightness is built from:

- a girl who learned that wanting things could hurt her sister;
- a dancer who learned that desire would only be respected if it became incontrovertible proof;
- a fan who learned that public visibility could turn affection into humiliation;
- a veteran who learned that years of care could make her indispensable while still leaving her personally unseen;
- a survivor who learned to smile so other people would not cry, and then had to learn that she was allowed to cry herself.

Those histories do not disappear when the girls become idols. SUNNY PEACE works by changing the social conditions under which those histories operate.

The best Phase-1B formulation is therefore:

> **SUNNY PEACE is a system for making light shareable without making identity interchangeable.**

Chisa can receive support without becoming Saki’s dependent shadow. Rei can pursue excellence without making paternal recognition the sole judge of meaning. Shizuku can be seen without surrendering authorship of what being seen means. Haruko can support other people without being reduced to support. Sakura can carry the dead without living as their substitute.

That is the origin-level foundation. Later events may enrich, complicate, or challenge it, but they are no longer permitted to supply it retroactively.

**Phase-1B next dependency:** `origin_moon_001` -> `origin_moon_005` (Tsuki no Tempest origins).
