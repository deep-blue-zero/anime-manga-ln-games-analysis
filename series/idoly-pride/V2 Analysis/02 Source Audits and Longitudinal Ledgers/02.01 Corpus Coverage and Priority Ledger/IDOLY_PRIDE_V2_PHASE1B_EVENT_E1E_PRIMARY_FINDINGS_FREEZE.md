# 1. Executive prospective findings

E1-E strongly supports a late-series form of the governing thesis:

> **Maturity does not abolish conflict, hierarchy, memory, or rivalry. It makes them more answerable.**

Across the three events:

- Tsuki no Tempest makes center authority contestable among all five members;
- Kotono and Suzu lose a specific center selection without losing legitimacy, identity, or future standing;
- Nagisa wins the center as **Nagisa**, explicitly rejecting substitute status;
- Mei and Saki demonstrate that disappointment and supportive labor can coexist without self-erasure;
- Mana memory is reconstructed through multiple witnesses and relational positions rather than a single sacred image;
- Sakura explicitly distinguishes her present body from Mana while still experiencing emotionally meaningful presence;
- Kotono converts private sister-memory into a collaboratively authored answer song without surrendering sisterhood;
- DoriKyun explicitly reveals an anti-exploitation industry purpose, but preserves coercive purification language and humiliation tactics;
- SUNNY PEACE seeks DoriKyun pressure voluntarily, wins, and is recognized by DoriKyun without becoming DoriKyun-like;
- competition remains constitutive, but defeat or loss of office no longer carries ontological erasure.

The combined late-maturity model is:

> **role, memory, competition, and influence remain legitimate when they can be contested without annihilating the person or relationship that contests them.**

# 2. `event_2026_002` — ぶつかり愛のCenter Battle！

## 2.1 Chronology and state

The event occurs after Tsuki no Tempest has become/re-established itself as a BIG4-level unit and after its DoriKyun confrontation has already taught the unit that challenge remains meaningful even under risk of defeat.

Kotono explicitly states:

> `苦しくても、怖くても、挑戦することに意味がある`
>
> Even when it is painful or frightening, there is meaning in challenging.

More importantly, she adds:

> `もし負けても、その時はまた頑張ればいい`
>
> If we lose, we can simply work hard again then.

This is a late Tsuki analogue to E1-D's non-terminal victory model: defeat has ceased to possess existential jurisdiction over the unit.

## 2.2 Center is now a genuinely contestable office

The event directly resolves the narrowest form of `OPEN-15`.

At the point of the new song, all five members explicitly enter the center contest:

- Kotono;
- Suzu;
- Nagisa;
- Saki;
- Mei.

Suzu recalls her earlier principle:

> `センターとは……最も優れたアイドルが立つべきポジション。`
>
> `優れている方が立てばいい。簡単な話ですわ`

and explicitly extends the invitation to all five.

Kotono then says:

> `あの時から、みんなの気持ちが変わっていないなら――`
>
> `新曲は、私達五人の中からセンターを決めましょう`

This is not merely rhetoric. A measurable selection procedure is actually run, and **Nagisa wins**.

Therefore the strongest prospective formulation is:

> **CONTESTABLE_CENTER_OFFICE — Tsuki no Tempest treats center as an identity-bearing but non-proprietary professional office whose occupant can change through member-visible contest without dissolving prior center legitimacy.**

This generalizes the BIG4 Kotono/Suzu dispute beyond a dyadic exception.

## 2.3 Nagisa's victory is individuation, not substitution

Nagisa's internal movement is explicit:

> `今度は、琴乃ちゃんのかわりじゃなくて`
>
> `一人のアイドルとして`

She wants to stand not as Kotono's replacement but as an idol in her own right.

The event therefore strengthens the longitudinal Nagisa model:

- love/devotion to Kotono remains real;
- admiration remains causally important;
- but relational attachment no longer determines the entire meaning of her professional ambition.

Her center victory is strongest as:

> **RELATIONAL_INDIVIDUATION — the person becomes more individually authored without needing to weaken the attachment that helped form her.**

Nagisa can still adore Kotono, flirt with her, borrow/learn from her, and yet win against her as a distinct performer.

## 2.4 Kotono's response proves center loss is non-annihilating

Kotono's reaction is:

> `悔しいけど、文句ないわ`
>
> `今回は、本当に超えられちゃったわね`

This matters because earlier Kotono repeatedly linked centerhood, responsibility, Mana inheritance, and unit legitimacy.

Here she:

- wants the center;
- loses;
- feels disappointment;
- recognizes Nagisa's result;
- remains fully Tsuki;
- remains professionally ambitious;
- later supports Nagisa's center performance.

Thus:

> **A mature office can be lost without the person being displaced from the polity.**

This is one of the clearest institutional-personhood analogies in the game corpus.

## 2.5 Suzu's center legitimacy survives contestability

Suzu does not retreat from her claim. She says she will next defeat Kotono and Nagisa and regain center.

Her mature centerhood therefore is not self-sacrifice or symbolic stewardship alone. It includes:

- desire to hold office;
- willingness to contest;
- willingness to lose;
- willingness to recognize another winner;
- expectation of future re-entry.

This strengthens rather than weakens her previously established understanding of center as burden/office/service.

## 2.6 Mei's disappointment is a major anti-self-erasure result

Mei initially congratulates Nagisa, then privately cries with Suzu because she genuinely wanted center.

Her key line:

> `一員じゃないよ、芽衣は、芽衣は中心になりたかった`

She refuses the consolation that being merely “a member” should be enough.

That is analytically important. Mei's desire to help others does not require her to deny personal ambition.

Her motive combines:

- wanting to support the unit when Kotono/Suzu are overburdened;
- admiration for Suzu;
- admiration for Mana;
- desire to make people happy;
- desire to become the center herself.

Suzu validates the disappointment rather than moralizing it away.

Preferred category:

> **NON-ZERO-SUM CARE/AMBITION — care for others does not require disavowing the desire to occupy the valued position oneself.**

## 2.7 Saki's support role is chosen, not assigned essence

Saki loses the center contest but immediately contributes a detailed live-production proposal and states:

> `センター以外にも、力になれることはありますから`

Crucially she also says she intends to win the next center and has promises/ambitions of her own.

Therefore support competence cannot be used to re-essentialize Saki as “the responsible support person.”

She may provide supporting labor **and** seek visible center authority.

## 2.8 Center pluralization does not flatten difference

The five-member contest does not imply every member is equally suited to every song.

The selection depends on:

- song fit;
- expression;
- prior center experience;
- individual strengths;
- measurable performance.

Nagisa wins this song because the Valentine material especially fits her expression.

So the correct model is:

> **equal standing to contest ≠ interchangeable performance identity.**

## 2.9 Center leadership becomes distributed after selection

After Nagisa wins:

- Saki contributes staging/production planning;
- Mei contributes MC/costume concepts;
- Kotono/Suzu support and perform;
- Nagisa creates a fan-facing event segment.

Nagisa thinks:

> `本気でぶつかりあって、想いを交わせるのは、ライブバトルだけじゃないんだ`

This suggests a broader mature-unit model:

> **Competition selects a role, but co-authorship continues after selection.**

The winner does not monopolize the unit's work.

## 2.10 Relevant open-state transitions

- `OPEN-15` contestable center beyond Tsuki's Kotono/Suzu dyad: **RESOLVED in the affirmative for Tsuki.**
- Whether this generalizes to other units: still open.
- The center remains song-/context-sensitive; do not infer a permanent Nagisa succession.

# 3. `event_2026_003` — あなたに捧ぐWith your songs

## 3.1 Governing problem

Kotono wants to create an answer song to Mana's `song for you`.

Her initial concept is primarily:

- gratitude for irreplaceable days with her sister;
- determination to continue singing;
- a birthday gift to Mana.

But she cannot complete the composition while treating Mana primarily as the sister she personally knew.

The event's decisive movement is therefore:

> **from private kinship memory to plural-perspective authorship without surrendering kinship.**

## 3.2 Kotono explicitly separates “my sister” from “idol Nagase Mana”

Kotono says:

> `私は、私を愛してくれた家族としてのお姉ちゃんをイメージしてた`
>
> `けど、お姉ちゃんはただの家族じゃない`
>
> `みんなを笑顔にする、尊敬出来るアイドルだった`

This is not a repudiation of sisterhood. It is a recognition that no single relational perspective exhausts a person.

Preferred category:

> **PLURAL_PERSON_MEMORY — the dead person remains one person while different survivors possess legitimately partial, non-interchangeable perspectives on her.**

This directly extends E1-A's plural Mana-memory model.

## 3.3 Mana is de-idealized without being diminished

Haruko and Rio supply evidence that Mana:

- became upset about losing/tying a quiz competition;
- trained late because she compared herself to Haruko;
- was highly competitive;
- experienced anxiety/focus pressure;
- concealed those vulnerabilities from fans.

Suzu initially says she thought Mana was perfect.

Haruko answers, in effect, that **idol Nagase Mana was perfect precisely because she could carry ordinary insecurity without displaying it to fans**.

Sakura then asks whether that concealment was painful.

The event therefore complicates “perfect idol” rather than simply debunking it.

Mana's public image is:

- curated;
- effortful;
- partly protective;
- not identical to her whole private experience;
- still a real professional achievement.

This aligns strongly with E1-C's four-axis persona model.

## 3.4 Mana's empathy is linked to lived vulnerability

Kotono concludes that Mana's songs often feel as if they “stand beside” the listener because Mana herself understood anxiety and pressure.

This is interpretive but strongly text-supported.

Mana's brightness is therefore not simply ignorance of suffering.

Preferred formulation:

> **her consoling public brightness is partly an authored response to vulnerability rather than evidence that vulnerability was absent.**

## 3.5 Kotono's sister relationship becomes reciprocal in memory

The pendant sequence materially revises Kotono's internal family history.

Kotono finds Mana's matching pendant in the bag Mana carried on the day of the NEXT VENUS final and realizes:

> `私、お姉ちゃんの、支えになれてたんだね……`

This is a major relational correction.

Kotono's earlier grief often positioned Mana as:

- model;
- giver;
- protector;
- lost object;
- source of vocation.

E1-E adds stronger evidence that Kotono was also **a support for Mana**.

Preferred model:

> **RECIPROCAL_SISTER_SUPPORT — Kotono was not merely the younger sister who inherited from Mana; her existence and gifts also sustained Mana.**

This reduces the asymmetry without erasing age/role difference.

## 3.6 Object provenance again becomes transformed continuity

Mana split/reworked the original bead necklace after Kotono lost hers, adding star pendants and preserving “matching” status in altered form:

> `形は変わっても琴乃とお揃いなことは変わらないでしょ`

This is an unusually concise material analogue for the corpus's inheritance model:

> **form may change while relational provenance remains.**

The pendant is therefore a physical counterpart to E1-A's `provenance-preserving expanded authorship/use` and E1-C's `functional inheritance without formal duplication`.

## 3.7 `With your songs` is not Kotono sealing Mana into private ownership

Kotono deliberately asks:

- Haruko;
- Rio;
- Sakura;
- Suzu

to participate in the final performance because their perspectives helped the song become possible.

This is crucial.

A song originally framed as **Kotono's answer to her sister** becomes an answer performed by a small community of differently situated Mana witnesses/inheritors.

Preferred term:

> **POLYPHONIC_MEMORIAL_AUTHORSHIP — a personal memorial remains personally anchored while allowing other legitimate relationships to enter its final form.**

Kotono retains sister-specific authority; she does not claim exclusive interpretive jurisdiction.

## 3.8 Sakura explicitly distinguishes bodily present from Mana possession

The most important supernatural line is:

> `もう私の心臓に麻奈さんは宿っていないのに`
>
> `胸が凄く、熱い。まるで麻奈さんが側にいるみたい`

This must be preserved exactly in evidentiary status.

It supports:

- Sakura no longer experiences/frames her heart as literally housing Mana;
- she still experiences warmth/presence **as if** Mana were nearby;
- memorial/emotional presence remains meaningful.

It does **not** establish:

- objective ghost presence;
- literal postmortem causation;
- current supernatural residence in Sakura's body.

Therefore:

> `OPEN-17` remains open, but the **heart-as-current-container** reading is materially weakened by explicit text.

Preferred distinction:

> **FELT_PRESENCE_WITHOUT_BODILY_POSSESSION**

This strongly supports the E1-A memorial ontology:

> dialogic memorial experience can be real for characters without proving metaphysical causality.

## 3.9 Haruko's `今も寄り添って` is character belief, not narrator ontology

Haruko thinks:

> `きっと、麻奈ちゃんは今も私達に寄り添ってくれてるのね`

Suzu similarly addresses Mana as a continuing recipient.

These statements establish:

- living relational practice;
- character belief/hope;
- continuing address to the dead.

They do not cross the evidence threshold into objective supernatural fact.

## 3.10 Kotono's final Mana state

Kotono's endpoint here is unusually mature:

> `私にとって『長瀬麻奈』は大好きなお姉ちゃんで`
>
> `伝説のアイドルで……最高の目標だよ`

The categories coexist.

Mana is neither reduced to:

- private sister;
- untouchable public saint;
- supernatural mechanism;
- dead burden.

Kotono can love, learn, compete symbolically, and continue.

# 4. `event_2026_007` — PRIDE貫く頂点への道標

## 4.1 SUNNY PEACE deliberately seeks adversarial pressure after BIG4

Sakura explicitly explains that she has watched Tsuki and TRINITYAiLE grow through contact with DoriKyun and wants SUNNY PEACE to do the same.

She says:

> `サニピはBIG4になれました。でも――`
>
> `これでアイドルの頂点に立てたとは思っていません`

This is important for late-career ambition.

BIG4 is:

- meaningful;
- prestigious;
- not terminal;
- not sufficient to define “top idol.”

Sakura's desire remains future-generative rather than status-defensive.

## 4.2 Sakura's developmental model is reciprocal, not extractive

DoriKyun frames Sakura's request as using them as a stepping stone.

Sakura rejects that simplification:

> `どりきゅんと一緒に成長していければいいなって思ったんです`

Her stated goal is that stronger rivals can make better live battles for audiences.

Preferred term:

> **RECIPROCAL_ADVERSARIAL_DEVELOPMENT — rivals may use one another as developmental pressure while treating mutual improvement and better public performance as jointly valuable.**

This does not require friendship.

## 4.3 DoriKyun's humiliation method remains coercive

DoriKyun agrees to “joint practice” only after converting it into a public five-match humiliation spectacle under its own rules, with threats that refusal will be framed as cowardice.

This includes:

- asymmetric rule-setting;
- reputational pressure;
- deliberate insult;
- public spectacle;
- constrained refusal.

Therefore later ideological disclosure must **not retroactively sanitize the method**.

Their pedagogy remains ethically coercive even when it can generate useful pressure.

## 4.4 SUNNY PEACE wins on plurality, not one-dimensional superiority

The five-match structure tests:

- variety performance;
- luck;
- fandom/knowledge;
- teamwork;
- applied survival experience.

SUNNY PEACE initially loses badly, then returns through Shizuku's extreme idol knowledge and Sakura/Chisa's cooperative adaptation.

Their victory is therefore not proof that each Sunny member individually outclasses DoriKyun.

It demonstrates **plural unit competence**.

## 4.5 Chisa/Sakura cooperation is authored rather than self-erasing

Chisa initially notices she cannot jump as high as Sakura and briefly risks interpreting difference as deficiency.

She corrects herself:

> `この勝負はチームワークが鍵なんだから`
>
> `さくらちゃんが高く跳べるように私がサポートするんだ`

This is a useful late confirmation of Chisa's mature state.

Supporting Sakura is not evidence that Chisa should disappear. Her observation and fan/character knowledge become the causal contribution that allows Sakura's physical strength to matter.

This is **complementarity without hierarchy of personhood**.

## 4.6 DoriKyun recognizes SUNNY PEACE as “real idols”

After losing, DoriKyun keeps its promise and explicitly says Sunny are not weak/fake:

> `本物のアイドルだと認めてあげる`

The recognition is important because it comes **without ideological assimilation**.

SUNNY PEACE does not become cruel, eliminative, or DoriKyun-like in order to earn it.

Thus:

> **adversarial recognition can occur across persistent normative disagreement.**

## 4.7 DoriKyun's explicit industry purpose

The event finally gives a direct statement of purpose.

Kurumu says they intend to destroy:

> `アイドルを金稼ぎの道具としか思ってない大人や`
>
> `そこに群がる馬鹿なアイドル`

and:

> `アイドル業界をあたしらが浄化する`
>
> `アイドルという仕事を世界一のエンターテインメントにする`

Lemon adds that they wanted to prove/spread their correctness and overturn the industry from its roots.

This materially revises the DoriKyun model.

They are not merely:

- sadists;
- arbitrary gatekeepers;
- jealous antagonists;
- pressure-for-pressure's-sake.

They possess an identifiable **anti-commodification / anti-exploitation diagnosis** and a positive conception of idol work as serious entertainment.

However, their proposed solution is explicitly **purificatory and eliminative**.

Preferred model:

> **AUTHORITARIAN_ANTI_EXPLOITATION — DoriKyun correctly identifies some forms of commodification and instrumentalization, but converts that diagnosis into coercive authority to decide who counts as a legitimate idol and who should be crushed.**

This preserves both their insight and their danger.

## 4.8 DoriKyun's purpose is revised by counterexample, not defeated by argument

After encountering Tsuki, TRINITYAiLE, and SUNNY PEACE, DoriKyun begins to consider that increasing the number of such idols may change the industry without DoriKyun needing to purify it alone.

This is significant.

The rival units do not persuade DoriKyun through abstract debate. They become **counterexamples to DoriKyun's total theory of the field**.

Preferred term:

> **COUNTEREXAMPLE_REFORM — exposure to admirable rivals weakens a totalizing ideology by proving that legitimate professional forms already exist outside the ideologue's control.**

## 4.9 DoriKyun remains outside Hoshimi

Makino again invites them to join.

They refuse.

Therefore:

- mutual professional respect is real;
- developmental interdependence is real;
- ideological movement is possible;
- institutional absorption is not required.

This is a strong late example of plural professional ecology beyond Hoshimi ownership.

## 4.10 Manager implication

DoriKyun tells Makino that he has good judgment and asks him to continue raising “real idols.”

This is praise, not proof of managerial infallibility.

It does reinforce the Hoshimi legitimacy model: the agency's positive significance is partly that it can create conditions in which different idol forms mature without becoming interchangeable.

## 4.11 `OPEN-06` transition

`OPEN-06 — DoriKyun usefulness vs necessity` receives a major resolution:

- **usefulness:** strongly confirmed in specific cases;
- **necessity:** rejected as a universal rule.

The event itself shows Sunny **choosing** DoriKyun pressure because it sees prior developmental value. That is very different from concluding that cruel pressure is required for growth.

DoriKyun's own ideological shift further undermines universal necessity: the world may improve by increasing legitimate alternatives rather than by subjecting everyone to DoriKyun's eliminative method.

Preferred transition:

> `OPEN-06` → **PARTIALLY_RESOLVED: USEFUL_BUT_NOT_NECESSARY**

# 5. Cross-event synthesis before historical comparison

## 5.1 Answerability becomes late-maturity constitutionalism

E1-E's strongest cross-event pattern is that previously identity-heavy structures become **procedural and revisable** rather than disappearing.

- Center remains important, but anyone in Tsuki may contest it.
- Mana remains central, but no one perspective owns her memory.
- DoriKyun remains adversarial, but its ideology can be revised by rivals it does not absorb.

Preferred late-series term:

> **ANSWERABLE_CONSTITUTIONALISM — durable roles and institutions remain legitimate because they contain procedures through which members, rivals, or inheritors can contest their meaning without destroying the whole.**

This is an analytical term, not source vocabulary.

## 5.2 Mature competition is non-terminal but not soft

Across E1-D and E1-E:

- IIIX can lose and try again;
- Tsuki can lose a center contest and remain legitimate;
- DoriKyun can lose to Sunny and recognize them;
- Mei can lose and still desire the office next time.

The series therefore does not mature by abandoning competition.

It matures by reducing the extent to which **one loss gets to define the total person/unit**.

## 5.3 Plurality without relativism

E1-E does not say every choice is equally good.

- Nagisa wins because she performs the specific song best.
- DoriKyun's coercion remains ethically objectionable.
- Mana's different witnesses provide partial perspectives, not mutually incompatible arbitrary realities.

Plurality means **multiple legitimate standpoints remain answerable to evidence and one another**.

## 5.4 Memory becomes relationally plural and formally revisable

The Mana event reinforces a now-stable memory rule:

> **provenance remains important while form, performer, and address may change.**

This appears in:

- pendant reconstruction;
- answer song;
- multi-person performance;
- Kotono's sister/public-idol dual memory;
- Sakura's felt presence without bodily possession.

## 5.5 Influence without ownership

All three events contain influence that does not become ownership:

- Kotono/Suzu inspire Nagisa/Mei/Saki but do not own center;
- Mana shapes later idols but does not own their songs or bodies;
- DoriKyun pressures rivals but cannot claim their resulting growth as institutional property.

This is one of the strongest late confirmations of the series-wide self-authorship thesis.
