# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — PHASE 1B IIIX ORIGIN AUDIT

## 0. Executive conclusion

The four IIIX origin bundles establish that the unit cannot be understood through a single static description such as "villain unit," "score optimizers," "mercenaries," or even the historical V1 phrase **weaponized selfhood**. All of those capture real surfaces, but the origin sequence is longitudinally organized around a more difficult question:

> **Can a professional object created by an institution for instrumental reasons become something its members genuinely author after the institution, its incentives, and even its founding reason for cohesion have failed?**

The answer is yes.

The corpus therefore presents **two IIIXs**.

The first IIIX is a corporate construction. Preta Porte selects three women who already possess high-value careers or marketable assets, gives them a luxury-product concept derived from the diamond grade `3EX`, appoints fran as center, and supplies the production machinery around them. The members do not initially share an idol dream. Their motives are largely instrumental and mutually incompatible:

- **miho** carries an older idol vocation that grief has reorganized into victory, memory politics, and revenge against Mana's public legend;
- **fran** treats idol activity as a commercially useful bridge toward fashion, debt repayment, and a second chance at entrepreneurship;
- **kana** treats visibility as self-branding, labor, competitive status, and a signal that might reach the father who disappeared from her life.

Victory is the only object they can all admit wanting together.

The second IIIX appears only after that compact breaks. At the end of `Re-Polished`, IIIX has lost I-UNITY, Himeno's corruption is exposed, Preta Porte withdraws from idol operations, work dries up, and the women decide that the unit has no reason to continue. A reduced-scale live then reactivates something the first IIIX had systematically underweighted: **direct encounter with an audience**. miho rediscovers the old promise that idol performance can make spectators happy; fran admits that idol work is genuinely enjoyable even if fashion remains her first vocation; kana discovers that being seen by individual people is not reducible to follower count or television exposure.

The three then choose one another again **without the corporation, without guaranteed upward mobility, without friendship rhetoric, and without the premise that victory has already been secured**. They begin organizing office space, legal structure, work acquisition, and professional survival themselves.

The strongest V2 unit thesis is therefore:

> **IIIX begins as a coalition of instrumental motives held together by externally rewarded victory, and becomes a self-authored professional compact only after the institution that assembled it can no longer guarantee status, money, or success.**

This is best described as **authenticity through re-authorization**, not authenticity through pure origin. IIIX does not become "real" by discovering that its original selfish motives were false. The original motives remain partly intact. It becomes real because the members later receive an actual opportunity to stop and, with far fewer external rewards, **authorize the same collective again**.

The mature relational formulation is:

> **IIIX is adversarial interdependence: deliberately non-sentimental loyalty among people who may dislike one another, but who trust one another's competence, repeatedly protect the integrity of the shared work, and choose the same difficult partners because those partners can meet them at full strength.**

Finally, the origins establish a decisive ethical distinction for Tokyo:

> **IIIX does not mature from "winning" to "caring instead of winning." It learns to distinguish winning as evidence of fully authored performance from winning whose evidentiary meaning has been contaminated by somebody else's manipulation.**

That means Tokyo must inherit a unit that remains aggressive, status-conscious, technically strategic, and highly competitive. It must **not** inherit a simplistic redemption state. But it also must not inherit IIIX merely as Himeno's weapon or Preta Porte's product. By the endpoint of the origin sequence, ownership of the unit has changed.

# 2. The four-stage constitution of IIIX

## 2.1 `Diamond Mining` — an idol group designed like a luxury product

Preta Porte's formation logic is explicit. IIIX is a prestige project assembled from members who already possess high-value external credentials:

- miho brings professional idol experience and VENUS literacy;
- fran brings Paris runway prestige, visual marketability, and fashion-world credibility;
- kana brings long modeling experience, enormous social reach, and a highly developed personal brand.

The name itself derives from `3EX / Triple Excellent`, a top diamond-cut grade. This is not incidental decoration. The organization conceptualizes the group as **valuable raw properties intentionally cut into a premium commercial object**.

That means the first IIIX is not founded through shared aspiration. It is founded through **institutional selection**.

The members' mutual incompatibility is obvious from the beginning. fran says idolhood is merely a waypoint. kana is openly status- and attention-oriented. miho is the person most invested in idol competition as such, but even her motive is already entangled with grief and unfinished history.

Yet the unit succeeds immediately.

This gives IIIX its first paradox:

> **A group can be professionally real before it is relationally authored by its members.**

The performance results are not fake. The members are talented. The corporate model works. But success postpones the question of whether the three women themselves possess a reason to remain IIIX if the external reward structure stops working.

## 2.2 `House of Cards` — loss forces motive disclosure

The first serious loss to STROBOLIGHTS exposes the structure's fragility.

miho asks:

`勝ち続けることが出来ないなら――`

`私は、この子達と一緒にいる意味はあるのかしら？`

This is the most important founding question in the second block. The women have been able to coexist because the arrangement produces results. Once it stops producing results, they have no sentimental story to fall back on.

The response is unusually adult for an idol-unit origin. They do not invent friendship. They disclose the private reasons they need the unit to work.

- miho needs top-level victory to pursue the promise and grievance organized around Yō and Mana;
- fran needs money and professional leverage to recover from a failed fashion business and enormous debt;
- kana needs visibility both because she values attention and because continued public exposure may allow her missing father to find her.

Their compact is therefore not:

> "We finally realized we love one another."

It is closer to:

> **"Our private ends differ, but all three require this collective to become stronger. Therefore we will cooperate seriously enough that losing becomes unacceptable."**

`House of Cards` is exact because the unit's stability depends on a single shared load-bearing proposition: **victory makes staying together rational**.

## 2.3 `Gears Go Awry` — the difference between optimization and capture

After IIIX defeats STROBOLIGHTS by a massive margin and enters BIG4, the unit's professional doctrine hardens around `獅子搏兎`: even when the opponent looks weak, use full force.

This is not merely cruelty. It expresses their theory of proof. A result means more when the performer controls as many variables as possible and leaves as little room as possible for excuses.

Himeno Kiriko enters a system already predisposed toward measurable advantage.

Her skill is not creating IIIX's instrumentalism. It is **mapping her incentives onto it**.

She offers each member something unusually well targeted:

- kana: greater visibility;
- fran: fashion/business access;
- miho: power over the Mana-centered legacy structure she resents.

The third block therefore complicates moral responsibility. The members are not ignorant victims. Kana senses danger early. fran recognizes bad business and aesthetic vulgarity. miho and fran discuss suspicious institutional events. IIIX knows enough to understand that Himeno is manipulating the field.

Yet the group tolerates her because the manipulation appears useful.

The correct V2 diagnosis is **motivated toleration**.

Himeno exploits IIIX's existing weaknesses, but the women still exercise agency in continuing to work with her.

## 2.4 `Re-Polished` — defeat creates the conditions for authorship

The fourth block gives IIIX its ethical and institutional hinge.

fran's rejection of Himeno is especially revealing because it does not depend on sudden moral purification:

`でもね、これでも私はデザイナーなのよ`

`美的センスまで売り渡すつもりはないわ！`

The point is not that fran becomes altruistic. It is that her instrumentalism contains a **hard boundary of self-authorship**. She may sell labor and make strategic compromises, but she will not allow another actor to determine the form of the work so completely that the result ceases to be hers.

The I-UNITY final then distinguishes **repeatable excellence** from **singular event power**. IIIX performs extremely well and improves its own record. SUNNY PEACE nevertheless produces, in that moment, a performance whose audience relation exceeds the technical model IIIX had prepared for.

fran can say:

`でも、今、この瞬間だけは`

`……サニーピースが、私達の上をいった`

This is not surrender of competitive identity. It is unusually disciplined judgment: the better performance can be acknowledged without converting one defeat into an ontological statement that IIIX was always false.

Then the institution collapses.

The members decide to dissolve because the first compact has failed. They lost; Preta Porte no longer supplies the professional structure; the job pipeline disappears.

Only after the unit has become unnecessary do they discover reasons to keep it.

That is why the small live matters so much. It is not a sentimental coda. It is an **authorship test under reduced incentives**.

# 3. Takeda Mihoko / miho — grief, public memory, and the corruption of an audience-facing vocation

## 3.1 The original dream is not revenge

miho's earliest idol ideal is formed with her only close friend, 藤代葉.

Their language is explicitly about spectators:

`魔法少女みたいに、私達も`

`ステージで誰かを幸せな気持ちに出来たらいいね`

The line matters because it disproves a static reading of miho as naturally obsessed with ranking. The original vocation is relational and generative.

## 3.2 Yō's death becomes a crisis of public recognition

Yō dies after illness while Sundance is nearing the level from which the pair might have challenged the top of the industry. Her final instruction is notably anti-jurisdictional:

`あたしに囚われないで、新しい仲間とトップを取って`

She wants miho to continue rather than remain trapped by her absence.

The wound is then intensified by the timing of Mana's fatal accident. Mana's death becomes a national/industry-scale tragedy while Yō's death is barely registered.

miho's protest is precise:

`葉ちゃんの死も悲しんでくれてもいいじゃない！！`

**STRONG INFERENCE:** miho does not merely resent Mana for being more successful. She experiences the public asymmetry of mourning as a form of historical injustice.

Her later desire to erase Mana's legacy therefore has a retaliatory symmetry:

> **If Mana's death crowded Yō out of public memory, miho will crowd Mana out of public memory in return.**

This makes the project psychologically coherent while leaving it ethically indefensible.

## 3.3 The dead person's request becomes more compulsory in the survivor's hands

Yō tells miho not to be trapped by her.

miho nevertheless turns the relationship into an ongoing mission whose content grows beyond Yō's explicit request: not merely reach the top or beat Mana once, but overwrite records, cultural memory, songs, and symbolic traces.

This provides another franchise example of **posthumous evidence acquiring excess jurisdiction through survivor interpretation**.

The structural comparison to Kotono is useful but must remain qualified:

- Kotono constructs a proxy-completion mission around her sister's unfinished work;
- miho constructs a retaliatory memory project around unequal mourning and an unfinished rivalry.

Both cases demonstrate that love for the dead can produce a mission more compulsory than the dead themselves authorized.

## 3.4 Technical control is both real expertise and defensive epistemology

miho's VENUS knowledge is not cosmetic. She understands how to produce reliable technical points, how opponents behave, how audience points fluctuate, and how repeatability can reduce variance.

That is genuine professional expertise.

The danger is epistemic overreach:

> **what can be controlled and measured begins to look like the safest available definition of what is valuable.**

The I-UNITY final demonstrates the missing variable. SUNNY PEACE creates a singular relationship with the audience that cannot be guaranteed by IIIX's technical reliability model.

This does not invalidate IIIX's method. It reveals that the method is incomplete.

## 3.5 The revenge premise is destabilized, not resolved

After defeating Tsuki, miho discovers that victory does not deliver the expected catharsis. She returns mentally to the old promise and asks:

`私、貴方の願いをちゃんと叶えられてる…？`

Later, at the reduced venue, a fan remembers Yō and Sundance directly.

This is powerful counterevidence against miho's totalizing belief that Yō has disappeared from cultural memory.

But V2 must not overstate the change.

At the origin endpoint miho still says:

`長瀬麻奈の記憶を塗り潰すまではね`

Therefore:

> **The premise of revenge is destabilized, not extinguished.**

miho enters Tokyo in a genuinely contradictory state. She has recovered contact with the audience-facing vocation that preceded revenge **and** continues to endorse the retaliatory legacy project.

That contradiction must be allowed to develop prospectively rather than being retrospectively resolved for her.

# 4. fran — instrumental professionalism constrained by aesthetic authorship

## 4.1 Fashion is the first vocation

fran's childhood experience of visible difference makes fashion unusually important. Appearance can be transformed from something assigned by other people's gaze into something selected, designed, and authored.

Modeling and fashion therefore become more than status. They are a technology of self-definition.

This is why idolhood initially remains secondary:

`私にとって、アイドルはただの通過点に過ぎないの`

Her failed brand and approximately hundred-million-yen debt make the instrumental logic materially credible rather than cartoonish. She thinks about rent, legal exposure, cash flow, sponsorship, labor, and the opportunity cost of work because these things have already ruined one dream.

## 4.2 Instrumentalism is not absence of values

The historical V1 phrase "aesthetic spine" is useful if kept bounded.

fran will use idol work for money and leverage. But she refuses to treat all means as equivalent. Himeno's manipulation becomes unacceptable because it is ugly, strategically brittle, and incompatible with the kind of professional product fran is willing to sign her identity to.

Her key line:

`美的センスまで売り渡すつもりはないわ！`

This is better understood as **aesthetic self-jurisdiction** than moral purity.

## 4.3 The secondary vocation becomes real

At the reduced live, fran reaches a formulation that should govern later interpretation:

`私も……別に、アイドルをやるのは嫌いじゃなかったし`

`ファッションの次くらいには好きでもいいでしょ`

The story does not require her to demote fashion in order to legitimate idolhood.

This is important for the franchise more broadly: a profession can become genuine without becoming the person's singular highest calling.

fran's mature state is therefore **plural vocation**, not conversion from false dream to true dream.

## 4.4 Center does not mean universal superiority

fran's center position originates partly in corporate concept: a woman other women would admire, visual/fashion leadership, and the unit's luxury-brand identity.

Nothing in the origin corpus establishes a metaphysical hierarchy in which fran is simply "the best" member.

Her center role is a designed and then increasingly inhabited function.

# 5. kana — visibility as vanity, labor, survival technology, and search signal

## 5.1 Publicity has a trauma history

kana's public narcissism is real. She likes attention, thinks aggressively in terms of brand reach, and enjoys winning visibility contests.

But the origin adds a causal layer that prevents reduction to vanity.

Her mother died at birth. Her father became increasingly absent and then disappeared from her daily life. Her grandfather exploited kana's name and career while accruing debts and creating danger around her.

kana eventually has to distance herself and protect her own address.

Her line about trust is therefore not merely adolescent cynicism:

`実の家族にだって信じられない奴がいるのに`

## 5.2 Visibility doubles as a beacon

kana explicitly reasons that if she remains visible enough in television and online media, her missing father might see her and contact her:

`なら、kanaがテレビやネットに出まくれば`

`お父さんがそれを見て連絡くれるかもしれないじゃん`

This is one of the cleanest examples in the corpus of a public professional behavior having simultaneously commercial and intimate functions.

Her desire for attention should therefore be modeled as:

> **vanity + labor expertise + safety through self-branding + flare signal toward an absent parent.**

## 5.3 Kana's hostility toward Himeno is informed pattern recognition

Kana detects danger in Himeno earlier than the others and explicitly connects her to the exploitative grandfather.

That does not make kana morally infallible. It does make her suspicion evidentially stronger than casual dislike.

The origin repeatedly establishes that she knows how exploitative adults package control as opportunity.

## 5.4 Makino continuity receives independent corroboration

Before Tokyo, IIIX encounters Makino/Hoshimi personnel and miho recognizes Makino as the same young man she had previously seen around Mana.

Kana, meanwhile, gives him a skeptical but positive assessment as an unusually good-hearted entertainment adult and wonders whether surviving in the industry with that disposition means he possesses something exceptional.

This does not create a new Makino identity rule—the project already treats the game manager as continuing Makino Kouhei—but it supplies an **independent in-world continuity confirmation** from outside Hoshimi.

No Hoshimi audit addendum is required because the confirmation strengthens an existing governing rule without changing an earlier cross-media classification.

# 6. Relationship architecture — neither friendship denial nor pure utility is enough

The origin corpus creates a methodological trap because the women themselves repeatedly deny friendship.

Late formulations include:

kana:

`あんた達なんか大嫌いだけど`

`kanaのレベルについて来られるのは二人だけだし`

fran:

`私も、二人のこと好きでもなんでもないけど`

`センターのお供としては信頼しているわよ？`

miho:

`貴方達を友達と思ったことは一度もないけど……`

`でも二年以上やってたら、腐れ縁にもなるわね`

V2 should take this self-description seriously. It should not force IIIX into conventional friendship or found-family vocabulary against the text.

But it should also refuse the opposite simplification.

Their behavior demonstrates:

- mutual professional trust;
- disclosure of humiliating private motives;
- repeated attention to one another's distress;
- defense of shared performance integrity;
- resistance to being used against one another;
- voluntary re-selection after the institution no longer requires them to stay together.

The best formulation is therefore:

> **adversarial interdependence**

or:

> **deliberately non-sentimental loyalty.**

The historical phrase **earned indispensability** survives with one qualification. The source does not establish metaphysical irreplaceability. It establishes something more concrete:

> **given the standards, knowledge, history, and temperaments these women have developed, each sees the other two as unusually difficult to substitute without changing what the work is.**

Call this **earned non-substitutability** rather than literal indispensability.

# 7. IIIX, VENUS, and the ethics of measurement

## 7.1 “Point takers” is true and incomplete

IIIX understands scoring mechanics and deliberately seeks reliable technical points.

That does not mean their performances are fake. Strategy only works because the members possess the technical competence to execute it.

Their preference for controllable measures is also rational inside a competition system: audience points can be volatile; synchronized movement, pitch control, timing, and repeatable execution can be trained and predicted.

The right analytical question is therefore not:

> "Are they gaming the system?"

but:

> **"What dimensions of idol value become invisible when the controllable metric is allowed to stand in for the whole object?"**

## 7.2 SUNNY PEACE exposes incompleteness rather than falsity

At I-UNITY, SUNNY PEACE produces an unusually strong event-response under adverse conditions. IIIX recognizes the performance's force.

This does not prove that technical excellence is false and emotional spontaneity is true.

It proves that the relationship between performer and audience contains dimensions that cannot be fully reduced to repeatable control.

The reduced live later allows IIIX to experience that proposition directly at intimate scale.

## 7.3 The institutional critique is “metric colonization,” not necessarily anti-VENUS

Historical V1 called IIIX the franchise's strongest critique of the VENUS Program.

**Status: SPLIT / QUALIFIED.**

The origin corpus more clearly critiques:

- treating measurable reliability as exhaustive value;
- allowing ranking systems to colonize the reason performance exists;
- confusing system optimization with complete artistic judgment.

But VENUS itself can register audience response, and IIIX never rejects scoring or competition.

The stronger V2 institutional proposition is:

> **IIIX critiques metric colonization more strongly than measurement itself.**

This remains provisional until Tokyo, BIG4, Stellar, and later institutional materials are read.

# 8. Himeno, responsibility, and contaminated victory

Himeno is most analytically useful when neither absolved nor given total causal authorship.

She does not create IIIX's instrumental motives. She identifies and monetizes them.

The group already believes:

- victory matters;
- measurable advantage matters;
- professional life is transactional;
- individual members may use idolhood for ends beyond idolhood.

Himeno's corruption enters by expanding that logic outside the performance boundary.

The decisive difference is between:

1. **rules-aware competitive optimization** — choosing songs, choreography, timing, training, and strategy to maximize legitimate performance outcome;
2. **external manipulation of comparison conditions** — sabotaging opponents, fabricating scandals, engineering walkovers, coercing institutions, or controlling rights to destroy alternative actors.

IIIX's eventual rejection is therefore not anti-competition. It is a defense of the proposition that **victory must remain attributable to the performers**.

If Himeno has already destroyed the opponent outside the stage, the result no longer proves what IIIX wants it to prove.

That gives the unit an ethical floor even while leaving its broader legacy politics unresolved.
