# 1. Tranche-level prospective finding

The E2-A1 stories do not displace the post-E1 thesis of **answerable interdependence / ANSWERABLE_CONSTITUTIONALISM**. They extend it into a previously underdeveloped question:

> **What conditions are permitted to disqualify a person from standing as an idol, collaborator, member, or author of her own career?**

Across the tranche, several proposed disqualifiers fail to acquire unlimited authority:

- a vocation's lack of social indispensability;
- probable career instability;
- replaceability in a labor market;
- a more experienced performer's judgment about another person's potential;
- fear, trauma, or need for safeguards;
- age;
- lack of registration in the dominant evaluation system;
- technical inferiority under one measurement regime.

The strongest new candidate principle is:

> **NON_DISPOSABLE_STANDING — A person does not lose standing to participate, choose, continue, contribute, or be taken seriously merely because she is not maximally useful, frictionless, young, institutionally legible, technically dominant, or independently self-sufficient.**

This is not a claim that everyone must always be retained in every role or that comparative judgment is illegitimate. E1 already established that results and offices can be real. The narrower E2-A1 extension is that **a deficit in one evaluative dimension cannot silently become total jurisdiction over personhood or vocation**.

A second candidate principle is:

> **EVALUATIVE_JURISDICTION — Every evaluative system is authoritative only over the dimensions it can actually judge.**

The father can judge his worries as a parent; he cannot therefore own Rei's happiness. Rui can judge technique and professional preparation; she cannot therefore own Shizuku's goal or creative method. A safety risk can justify safeguards; it does not automatically justify Chisa's self-erasure from the group. VENUS can score a performance; that score does not exhaust what an audience can experience as idol value.

# 2. `event_2021_005` — Rei: chosen vocation against utility, replaceability, and finite horizon

## 2.1 Chronology and state anchor

The event explicitly occurs after SUNNY PEACE's NEXT VENUS Grand Prix victory: Mei asks why Rei's father still refuses recognition even after that win.

Safe chronology:

> `EARLY_POST_ANIME / POST_NEXT_VENUS_WIN`

Do not force a narrower placement without additional evidence.

## 2.2 The father's objection is not simple authoritarian dislike

Rei's father gives a coherent, if restrictive, account of legitimate work.

Primary locator: `st-eve-2111-backside-002`, beginning with `私は娘に地に足の着いた仕事についてほしかった`.

He wants Rei to pursue a stable occupation that helps people. He asks:

> `アイドルは、人々の生活に欠かせないものですか？`

He also argues that only a small minority succeed in entertainment and says:

> `娘の代わりなど、いくらでもいるでしょう`

Three different criticisms are therefore present:

1. **social-utility criticism** — idol labor is not indispensable;
2. **career-risk criticism** — success is structurally improbable;
3. **replaceability criticism** — the industry can substitute another performer.

The text does not require us to pretend these concerns are factually absurd. Rei herself later says she understands why he objects and does **not** simply declare his concern wrong.

## 2.3 Rei's answer: relational usefulness rather than indispensability

Primary locator: `st-eve-2111-backside-004`, from `アイドルだって……誰かを救うことが出来るの！`.

Rei does not prove that idols are essential infrastructure. Her answer is smaller and more relational:

- people spend money and time to meet them;
- fans say meeting them helps them face tomorrow;
- that response becomes hope for Rei as performer too.

The important structure is reciprocal:

> audience receives energy / performer receives reason and hope.

This supports the existing audience-reciprocity model while adding a limit:

> **social worth need not equal social indispensability.**

A form of work may matter profoundly without being necessary for bare social reproduction.

## 2.4 Success is not Rei's only criterion for legitimacy

Her father says continuing does not guarantee success.

Rei answers:

> `そうかもしれない`
>
> `でも、成功してもしなくても、私は続ける`

Primary locator: `st-eve-2111-backside-004`.

This is stronger than ordinary ambition. Rei separates:

- external success;
- the legitimacy of choosing the work.

She is not claiming that outcomes do not matter. She is claiming that **failure-risk does not retroactively make the vocation unauthorized**.

## 2.5 The future-career question is intentionally unresolved

The father asks what Rei will do after the day she must stop being an idol.

Rei answers:

> `今は分からない`

and says she is currently consumed by doing her best in the present.

This must **not** be rewritten into a solved career plan.

The event therefore does two things at once:

- validates idolhood as a chosen vocation even under finite-horizon uncertainty;
- leaves the material question of what comes after unresolved.

This is important for `OPEN-08` and `OPEN-14`: the story makes short-horizon uncertainty ethically insufficient to invalidate the present vocation, but it does **not** yet prove long-career institutional viability.

## 2.6 Rei's strongest self-authorship line

Primary locator: `st-eve-2111-backside-004`.

> `私の幸せは、私が決めたいの。`

followed by:

> `アイドルを、ダンスを、夢を諦めたら / 私は幸せになれないよ`

The father has standing as a concerned parent. He does not possess final jurisdiction over the definition of Rei's happiness.

This is a clean lower-tier example of **bounded parental authority**.

## 2.7 The father changes, but only partially

After seeing the live, he says he cannot judge entertainment quality but can see that a crowd is engaged and Rei is fulfilling her work.

Primary locator: `st-eve-2111-backside-005`.

His key line is:

> `アイドルなど、人生でほんの一時しか出来ない仕事です`
>
> `だが、人生を懸けるに値する仕事なのかもしれませんね`

He later sends only:

> `怪我にだけは気をつけろ`

The event does **not** turn him into a full enthusiast. The shift is narrower:

> **rejection → recognition of seriousness and value → guarded concern**.

That restraint makes the reconciliation more useful analytically than a total conversion would be.

## 2.8 Event-level operation

Provisional classification:

> `EXTENSION + STRENGTHENING`

Candidate additions:

- `VOCATIONAL_SELF_AUTHORSHIP`
- `RELATIONAL_SOCIAL_VALUE`
- `FINITE_HORIZON_DOES_NOT_NULLIFY_PRESENT_VOCATION`
- `BOUNDED_PARENTAL_AUTHORITY`

Interpretive limit:

> The event does not establish that all idol careers are sustainable, that Rei will never change vocation, or that parental concern about precarity is inherently illegitimate.

# 3. `event_2022_003` — Shizuku/Rui: expertise without unilateral jurisdiction

## 3.1 Chronology

Safe chronology:

> `POST_ANIME / HOSHIMI_MULTI_UNIT_CORESIDENCE`

The text supports a mature cross-unit Hoshimi environment but does not supply a hard main-story gate that warrants a narrower date.

## 3.2 Shizuku's underconfidence is real; Rui's recognition is also real

Rui notices Shizuku's unusual vocal potential and says her voice is a natural talent. The manager and professional creators independently identify the same possibility.

The story therefore does **not** frame Rui as wrong that Shizuku has unrealized capacity.

This matters because the ethical problem is not bad diagnosis. It is **what authority a correct diagnosis grants**.

## 3.3 Rui initially converts recognition into unilateral direction

Primary locator: `st-eve-2203-race-002`.

After Shizuku says solo work feels impossible, Rui arrives the next morning and declares:

> `今日から私が全面的にバックアップする`

Shizuku explicitly asks:

> `私の意思は……どこに……？`

and later:

> `拉致るのは、ダメっ……`

The tone is comedic, but the consent problem is textual and should not be erased by the joke.

Rui's logic is benevolent:

- Shizuku has talent;
- Shizuku lacks confidence;
- Rui wants to repay a fan's support;
- Rui knows professional training.

Yet benevolence plus expertise initially becomes **goal seizure**.

Candidate term:

> **BENEVOLENT_GOAL_SEIZURE**

## 3.4 Rui's professional theory of confidence is insufficiently person-specific

Primary locator: `st-eve-2203-race-003`.

Rui explains her own confidence through accumulated practice:

> `練習しかない。練習は裏切らないから`

She asks Shizuku to imagine beating the idol she regards as greatest and to aim for solo top status.

This is coherent for Rui and catastrophically mismatched to Shizuku's psychology. Shizuku becomes more intimidated rather than liberated.

The story therefore distinguishes:

> **a method that successfully produced one professional self**

from:

> **a universally correct method for producing confidence**.

## 3.5 Shizuku's actual idol philosophy is relational, not center-seeking

Primary locator: `st-eve-2203-race-003`, from `私は今、十分幸せだから`.

Shizuku explains:

- she had long abandoned the dream of becoming an idol after being told she could never do it;
- Hoshimi's warmth lets her possess some confidence;
- she wants to support the people around her;
- on stage she initially wants the others seen first;
- she regards the life she already has as sufficient happiness and wants to repay it.

This does not prove Shizuku lacks ambition forever. It proves that **Rui's assumed objective — solo prominence — is not Shizuku's current authored objective**.

## 3.6 Rui performs a real withdrawal of unilateral authority

After hearing Shizuku's account, Rui says:

> `無理強いしてごめんね`

and accepts that Shizuku may decline the solo route.

This is a meaningful ethical pivot because the story permits the mentorship project to end rather than insisting that Rui's original goal must be realized.

Only after that withdrawal does Shizuku make a new proposal herself:

> sing together with Rui.

Candidate term:

> **WITHDRAWAL_BEFORE_REAUTHORIZATION**

The eventual collaboration is not simply the original coercive project succeeding. Its goal has changed through Shizuku's answer.

## 3.7 Consent to the goal is not enough; method must remain answerable too

The pair then encounters a second problem.

Primary locators: `st-eve-2203-race-004` and `st-eve-2203-race-005`.

Rui defaults to TRINITYAiLE's workflow: begin synchronization quickly and treat professional lyrics as already sufficiently interpretable.

Shizuku comes from SUNNY PEACE practice where members discuss the lyrics and exchange interpretations before internalizing the song.

Because Rui is her idol, Shizuku suppresses disagreement. Her vocal performance deteriorates.

This creates an important two-level consent distinction:

1. **goal consent** — do I want this project at all?
2. **process authorship** — can I meaningfully answer how the project is made?

The story insists on both.

## 3.8 Rui's apology is unusually explicit about expertise and arrogance

Primary locator: `st-eve-2203-race-005`.

Rui says:

> `自分のやり方が正しいって、思い込んでた`
>
> `雫の言葉、気持ちを本気で理解しようとしてなかった`
>
> `あなたよりキャリアがあるからって / 心のどこかに、驕りがあったのかもしれない`

This is one of the cleanest lower-tier statements yet that:

> **greater experience does not produce unlimited epistemic jurisdiction over a less experienced collaborator.**

Shizuku also accepts responsibility for excessive deference. She says admiration prevented her from confronting Rui honestly.

The repair is reciprocal rather than assigning all fault to one side.

## 3.9 The repair mechanism is conversation and co-authorship

They simultaneously ask:

> `私と、もっと沢山、お話して下さい`

Then they discuss lyrics using Shizuku's extensive written analysis and Rui's perspective.

Candidate term:

> **DIALOGIC_MENTORSHIP**

or more precisely:

> **COAUTHORED_MENTORSHIP**

The mentor can teach, correct, and challenge, but the mentee remains a participant in defining the work.

## 3.10 Temporary cross-unit collaboration does not dissolve home-unit identity

Rui had first discussed the temporary unit with Yu and Sumire because she considers appearing on stage outside TRINITYAiLE a serious identity question. They eventually authorize the experiment as expansion rather than abandonment.

At the end:

- Rui still aims for the top with TRINITYAiLE;
- Shizuku still aims for the top with SUNNY PEACE;
- their temporary unit makes them better future rivals.

This is consistent with the post-E1 model that affiliation can expand without identity surrender.

## 3.11 Event-level operation

Provisional classification:

> `REVISION + EXTENSION`

Not a revision of the Tier-A thesis, but a revision of any simplistic model in which benevolent expert guidance is legitimate merely because the expert is correct about potential.

Candidate additions:

- `BENEVOLENT_GOAL_SEIZURE`
- `WITHDRAWAL_BEFORE_REAUTHORIZATION`
- `GOAL_CONSENT_VS_PROCESS_AUTHORSHIP`
- `COAUTHORED_MENTORSHIP`
- `EXPERTISE_WITH_BOUNDED_EPISTEMIC_JURISDICTION`

Interpretive limit:

> The event does not reject expertise, rigorous practice, or professional hierarchy. Rui's technical observations remain useful. The correction is about jurisdiction and fit, not anti-expertise egalitarianism.

# 4. `event_2024_007` — Chisa: trauma, safeguards, and refusal of self-disposability

## 4.1 Chronology

The event is a mature SUNNY PEACE story. DoriKyun is referred to as former BIG4, but the text does not supply a sufficiently hard main-story anchor to force exact placement.

Safe tag:

> `POST_ANIME_LATE / MATURE_SUNNY_PEACE`

## 4.2 Makino begins from refusal because of workload and fatigue

Primary locator: `st-eve-2407-dice-001`.

The manager says the members have recently been busy and tired and therefore intends to refuse the brutal survival-show offer.

This is significant after E1's labor analysis:

> management does not assume that member enthusiasm or prestige opportunity automatically overrides workload protection.

The members then actively ask to participate, including Chisa.

This is a cleaner example of **risk gate → member answer → conditional participation** than a model where the opportunity is imposed first.

## 4.3 Chisa discloses the relevant trauma before the work begins

Primary locator: `st-eve-2407-dice-002`.

Chisa explains that she nearly drowned as a child and has not swum in the sea since. She can tolerate pools and shallow surf but does not know whether she can enter deeper water.

She then says that if she cannot do it:

> `その時は、私は島に残るから、四人で脱出してね`
>
> `私は一人でも平気だから！`

The line is superficially generous. In context, it begins a repeated pattern of **preemptive self-removal**.

## 4.4 Chisa repeatedly converts vulnerability into a theory of burdensomeness

Across stories 002–004, Chisa repeatedly says or thinks:

- she must be useful;
- she must not hold the others back;
- she should remain behind if she cannot perform equally;
- she has made others worry;
- failure in raft construction is her fault;
- her desire may be wasteful.

Primary recurring language includes:

- `役に立たない物は必要ないよね`
- `みんなに迷惑をかけないように`
- `足を引っ張っちゃう`
- `私は一人で島に残る`

The strongest reading is not that Chisa enjoys sacrifice. It is that she has a **self-disposability heuristic**:

> when I create friction or require accommodation, I should remove myself before others have to choose me.

Candidate term:

> **PREEMPTIVE_SELF_DISPOSABILITY**

## 4.5 The group answers with role differentiation, not false sameness

When Chisa cannot enter the sea, the others do not insist that she is secretly identical in capability.

Shizuku says:

> `適材適所……自分に出来ることを頑張ればいい……`

This is analytically important.

The corrective is not:

> everyone can do everything.

It is:

> **different capacity does not equal lesser belonging.**

Chisa can contribute through raft work while others fish or swim.

## 4.6 The sea-glass sequence rejects purely instrumental value

Chisa initially wants to collect sea glass. Rei tells her to prioritize materials useful for the raft. Chisa internalizes:

> `役に立たない物は必要ないよね`

Later, after Rei encourages her to express wants, Chisa proposes decorating the raft with shells and sea glass even though this adds work and weight and is not mechanically necessary for escape.

The group accepts the proposal, and the decoration increases motivation and courage.

This is a strong structural echo between:

- Chisa's fear of being non-useful;
- objects she labels non-useful;
- the later discovery that non-instrumental beauty can matter to collective action.

Candidate term:

> **NON_INSTRUMENTAL_CONTRIBUTION**

Interpretive caution:

> The raft decoration is not a proof that utility never matters. The entire survival task depends on practical competence. Its role is to show that practical utility does not exhaust value.

## 4.7 Rei's intervention is permission to have wants

Primary locator: `st-eve-2407-dice-004`.

Rei tells Chisa that her consideration for others is admirable but that she is sometimes too restrained:

> `遠慮しないで、言いたいことがあれば言っていいし`
>
> `やりたいことがあればやっていいのよ`

and:

> `ここにいる誰も、千紗を迷惑だなんて思わないから`

The key correction is not generic self-esteem. It is **standing to impose some ordinary relational cost**.

Belonging that only survives when a person has no needs is not secure belonging.

## 4.8 Chisa's final participation is supported risk, not inspirational exposure therapy

Primary locator: `st-eve-2407-dice-005`.

Before agreeing to cross the water, Chisa:

- practices swimming with Shizuku watching;
- reinforces the raft using safer knots;
- asks staff for a life jacket;
- tries every idea she can think of to reduce fear and danger;
- explicitly says she is **still afraid**.

Only then does she say:

> `私も、みんなと行きたい！　みんなと一緒がいい！`

This is a crucial distinction.

The story does not say:

> courage means ignoring trauma and safety.

It says:

> **authored participation can be enlarged by safeguards without ceasing to be brave or voluntary.**

Candidate term:

> **SAFEGUARD_ENABLED_AGENCY**

## 4.9 The event corrects Chisa's idea that independence proves worth

Her opening fallback is:

> `私は一人でも平気だから`

Her endpoint is:

> `私も、みんなと行きたい！　みんなと一緒がいい！`

The progression is therefore not fear → fearlessness.

It is:

> **self-removal → acknowledged desire → supported participation**.

The fear remains.

## 4.10 Event-level operation

Provisional classification:

> `REVISION + TEMPORAL_MATURATION`

Candidate additions:

- `PREEMPTIVE_SELF_DISPOSABILITY`
- `NON_INSTRUMENTAL_CONTRIBUTION`
- `SAFEGUARD_ENABLED_AGENCY`
- `DIFFERENTIAL_CAPACITY_WITH_EQUAL_BELONGING`

This materially pressures `OPEN-03` and `OPEN-05`, but does not fully resolve Makino's intervention threshold because this case begins with disclosed risk, voluntary participation, and accessible safeguards rather than an acute medical emergency.

Interpretive limit:

> The exaggerated television-survival premise should not be treated as a realistic Japanese entertainment-industry safety manual. The useful evidence is the story's internal ethical architecture: disclosure, option to refuse, differentiated roles, concrete safeguards, and explicit continuing fear.

# 5. `event_2026_004` — IDOLY MATCH: plural idol institutions, aging, and metric jurisdiction

## 5.1 Chronology

Release position is April 2026. The story treats Rei, Mei, and Aoi as members of BIG4 units and therefore requires the mature post-BIG4 state.

Safe tag:

> `LATE_GAME_POST_BIG4`

Exact placement relative to every 2026 event/main-story beat is not hard-anchored by the bundle and should remain open.

## 5.2 The story explicitly defines idolhood outside VENUS registration

Primary locator: `st-eve-2604-dice-002`.

Aoi defines `野良アイドル` as idols who are not registered with VENUS Program. Reasons include:

- circumstances that lead them not to belong to an agency;
- dislike of AI evaluation;
- doing idol work as a hobby;
- doing it as a side job.

Crucially, Aoi adds that many still want to:

> `アイドルとして輝きたい`

This is the strongest source yet for a **plural institutional ontology of idolhood**.

Candidate term:

> **PLURAL_IDOL_INSTITUTIONALITY**

VENUS is dominant, consequential, and the chosen professional home of the protagonists. It is not the metaphysical licensing authority that makes idolhood real.

## 5.3 Audience existence gives the alternative institution social reality

Aoi points to a large audience who repeatedly comes to the underground arena for these performances.

The event therefore does not portray the space as merely private fantasy. It has:

- performers;
- audience;
- recurring norms;
- tournament procedures;
- local prestige;
- its own evaluation regime.

This matters because alternative legitimacy is socially instantiated rather than existing only as individual self-description.

## 5.4 Rei's dismissal is overturned by experience, not argument alone

Rei initially says an unofficial performance in a suspicious venue cannot have much value.

Aoi bets that if she feels nothing after seeing it, he will stop attending.

Within weeks, Rei and Mei are regular attendees.

This is a compact correction of institutional prejudice:

> **professional status did not give Rei sufficient evidence to judge the alternative scene before encountering its practice.**

## 5.5 The protagonists' secrecy is not automatically endorsed as good governance

Rei, Mei, and Aoi hide their attendance and competition entry from Makino and other members. They avoid using ordinary professional composers and studios so the activity will not be discovered.

The event treats this comedically and eventually punishes the deception socially when Saki catches them.

Therefore do **not** infer:

> legitimate alternative space means secret professional participation is always ethically clean.

The event's strong claim concerns the legitimacy of the alternative idol ecosystem, not the flawless judgment of the protagonists in bypassing management.

## 5.6 BEIJU is a direct challenge to youth-bounded idol ontology

Primary locator: `st-eve-2604-dice-004` and `-005`.

BEIJU is described as:

> `平均年齢88歳`

and as five friends from a nursing home.

They defeat the tournament favorite and then defeat the three Hoshimi/BIG4 performers.

DJばぁば explicitly accepts that she cannot imitate the younger group's powerful dancing and instead says:

> `私達らしいダンスを精一杯踊りましょうね`

The story does not require age-blind sameness. It allows age-related difference while retaining full idol standing.

## 5.7 Aoi explicitly names them “real idols”

Primary locator: `st-eve-2604-dice-005`.

Aoi says that although the protagonists are superior in singing/dancing skill, BEIJU makes the audience want to cheer more strongly. He then says:

> `何歳になってもやりたいことに挑戦して、努力して`
>
> `仲間と成長して、全力でお客さんを楽しませる……`
>
> `あのおばあちゃん達は本物のアイドルだよ`

This is a major pressure event for `OPEN-08`.

The safest split is now:

- **idolhood as an identity/practice across age:** strongly established as possible;
- **continuous mainstream professional career through old age:** not established;
- **Haruko's specific long-term mainstream career path:** still open.

Candidate refinement:

> `OPEN-08A — AGE_BOUNDED_IDOL_ONTOLOGY: STRONGLY RESOLVED AGAINST`
>
> `OPEN-08B — MAINSTREAM_LONG_CAREER_SUSTAINABILITY: OPEN`

## 5.8 BEIJU's future horizon is intentionally absurdly long and therefore analytically clear

DJばぁば says:

> `十年後も二十年後も歌い続けるから、また会いに来て`

The line is comic because of her age, but the comedy depends on treating the desire as admirable rather than delusional.

The event therefore refuses the idea that old age automatically transforms idol desire into category error.

## 5.9 The final contest directly limits VENUS metric sovereignty

Primary locator: `st-eve-2604-dice-005`.

Aoi says:

> `これがVENUSプログラムなら、多分僕達が勝っていた`

But this arena has no AI numerical evaluation. Judges and audience determine the result.

Rei concludes:

> `どちらがうまいかじゃない`
>
> `どちらがお客さんを楽しませられるか……`
>
> `どちらが素敵なアイドルかを決める勝負だった`

This is not evidence that VENUS scores are fake.

It is evidence that **different institutions operationalize different questions**.

Candidate term:

> **EVALUATIVE_REGIME_PLURALISM**

VENUS can answer something like comparative scored performance under its own rules. The underground tournament asks a differently weighted audience/judge question. Neither automatically possesses unlimited authority over every dimension of idol value.

## 5.10 The protagonists return to VENUS by choice, not because the underground was false

After losing, they say they will not continue performing underground.

Mei says their fans and beloved companions are waiting in VENUS Program; Rei says they will apply what they learned to their own stages.

Then Rei tells the underground arena:

> `これからも沢山の野良アイドルの居場所であり続けてね`

This is a particularly strong institutional conclusion:

> **plural legitimate homes do not require interchangeable belonging.**

The protagonists can affirm:

- the underground scene is a real home for others;

# 6. Cross-event synthesis

## 6.1 Four different authorities are bounded

The four events can be read as a sequence of bounded authorities:

| Event | Authority claiming relevance | Legitimate scope | Scope it cannot automatically seize |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rei/father | parent, social utility, career realism | concern, risk warning, evaluation of stability | Rei's final definition of happiness/vocation |
| Shizuku/Rui | senior expertise and recognized talent | technical advice, opportunity recognition, challenge | another person's goal and creative method |
| Chisa/Sunny/Makino | safety, team capacity, workload | refusal gate, safeguards, differentiated roles | automatic exclusion/self-erasure because support is needed |
| IDOLY MATCH/VENUS | institution and metric regime | scoring under chosen rules | total definition of idolhood or artistic worth |

This strongly advances the project-wide principle:

> **authority is legitimate when its jurisdiction is specific, answerable, and revisable.**

## 6.2 Non-disposability is not anti-judgment

The tranche repeatedly contains real judgment:

- Rei's father is right that entertainment work is precarious;
- Rui is right that Shizuku has unusual talent and can improve through practice;
- Chisa really does have a water-related limitation;
- BEIJU really is technically weaker in some performance dimensions;
- VENUS really could plausibly rank the BIG4 trio higher.

The correction occurs when one true judgment becomes a totalizing conclusion:

> precarious → therefore unauthorized vocation;
>
> talented → therefore must pursue my preferred route;
>
> afraid → therefore should remove herself;
>
> old → therefore not a real idol;
>
> lower technical score → therefore lower total idol value.

Candidate general formulation:

> **A true partial judgment becomes domination when it claims jurisdiction over dimensions it did not actually establish.**

## 6.3 “Standing” is the better term than “equality”

These events do not insist everyone is equal in every competence.

They repeatedly preserve difference:

- parents and children are not role-identical;
- Rui has more professional experience than Shizuku;
- Chisa has a specific vulnerability;
- BEIJU and the BIG4 trio have different physical/technical profiles;
- VENUS and the underground arena use different criteria.

The common protection is **standing**:

> the right to answer, choose, participate, refuse, ask for safeguards, define one's goal, and be evaluated under an appropriate rather than totalizing measure.

## 6.4 Career longevity requires a split question after IDOLY MATCH

Before E2-A1, `OPEN-08` was simply long-career viability.

The primary evidence now makes that too coarse.

### `OPEN-08B — Can the mainstream professional idol career itself remain materially/institutionally sustainable across a very long adult lifespan?`

Prospective answer:

BEIJU exists outside VENUS and does not establish mainstream employment continuity.

### `OPEN-14 — Haruko's specific long-career horizon`

Prospective status:

> **OPEN, but conceptual age ceiling weakened.**

Haruko no longer sits inside a world where old age itself can be assumed to terminate the category “idol.” Her specific professional path still lacks direct resolution.

## 6.5 Chisa provides the tranche's strongest practical autonomy model

The sequence around the sea shows the mature architecture most clearly:

1. disclose limitation;
2. manager initially protects against overwork/risk;
3. member chooses participation;
4. group differentiates roles;
5. member identifies desire rather than duty alone;
6. training occurs;
7. technical safeguards are added;
8. fear remains;
9. member chooses to proceed;
10. group proceeds together.

This is a strong lower-tier control case for `OPEN-03`:

> **good intervention can increase the range of authored action rather than choosing either abandonment or total prohibition.**

It does not settle the threshold for catastrophic or medically severe risk.
