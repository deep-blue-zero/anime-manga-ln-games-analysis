# IDOLY PRIDE V2 — PHASE 1B EVENT E4 PRIMARY FINDINGS FREEZE

## 0. Freeze rule and canon-weighting purpose

This document freezes the source-native findings from the final eleven event bundles in the 60-event Phase-1B routing map before any Tier-H/V1 analytical prose is consulted.

Inherited analytical authority is only:

- `IDOLY_PRIDE_V2_PHASE1B_POST_EVENT_E3_BASELINE.md`

The eleven sources were read in the frozen E4 routing order. E4 is **INDEXED / SELECTIVE / CAVEATED** rather than an equal-weight extension pass. Nine of the eleven sources are explicit franchise crossovers. Their dialogue and event-local outcomes are real corpus evidence, but their external characters, crossover meetings, alternate-world mechanics, supernatural premises, and resulting continuity are not automatically admitted as ordinary mainline IDOLY PRIDE facts.

The governing prospective rule is:

> **`CROSSOVER_CANON_WEIGHTING`**
>
> **A crossover event may preserve event-local facts, analogical contrasts, and character behavior that is independently compatible with mainline evidence, but it cannot by itself establish mainline chronology, ontology, relationships, institutions, or developmental transitions that depend on the crossover premise.**

Four routing labels are used below:

- **`MAINLINE_SUPPORT`** — uncaveated IDOLY PRIDE event evidence eligible for ordinary support-level admission.
- **`CROSSOVER_CORROBORATIVE_EVIDENCE`** — IDOLY PRIDE character behavior that coheres with a proposition already established by stronger mainline evidence. It can stabilize but not originate the mature claim.
- **`CROSSOVER_ANALOGICAL_EVIDENCE`** — an external contrast or guest interaction that illuminates a mainline concept without becoming a mainline fact.
- **`CROSSOVER_EVENT_LOCAL_FACT`** — true inside the crossover story, but not transferable into ordinary continuity without separate corroboration.

A fifth negative routing state applies when necessary:

- **`CROSSOVER_NONTRANSFERABLE_CONTINUITY`** — alternate-world, supernatural, guest-franchise, or crossover-specific continuity that must remain indexed but excluded from mainline reconstruction.

This vocabulary is archival, not a new series-level thematic ontology.

E4 does **not** overturn the mature E1/E2 architecture. Its main contribution is evidentiary discipline: the corpus contains attractive crossover material that often makes established traits unusually legible, but the same readability creates a risk of over-promoting guest-franchise scenarios into mainline canon.

# 3. Crossover 1 — 未来とつながるマジカルメロディ

## 3.1 Event-local reading

TRINITYAiLE is asked to participate in a Hatsune Miku anniversary context. Yu's Miku fandom is strong; Rui and Sumire begin from much less familiarity. The group struggles while trying to write a commemorative song because it over-thinks appropriate form and what a “Miku anniversary song” is supposed to be.

The manager does not immediately rescue them. He deliberately waits until they ask rather than forcing assistance on them. Miku then encourages the group to begin from what they actually feel rather than from a preconceived ceremonial form.

The most useful mechanism is that the song's object changes. TRINITYAiLE initially thinks in terms of **making a song for Miku**. Through collaboration, Miku becomes a participant in the work. The result is no longer simply praise directed at an object from outside; it becomes co-authored expression.

# 7. Mainline 1 — 月夜に輝く恋の魔法

## 7.1 Care and opportunity remain answerable

The manager and Satomi consider whether Tsuki should decline a major summer festival because the members have been busy and may need rest. The members overhear the concern and explicitly choose the opportunity while promising health management.

This is useful support for the existing care model:

> **Concern about workload is legitimate information; it does not automatically create authority to remove an opportunity without consulting capable performers.**

The event does not imply that every self-chosen workload is safe. It supplies an early case of consultation before intervention.

## 7.2 Creative work does not require autobiographical equivalence

Mei wants a summer song as a surprise for fans. The group goes to Hoshimi City's beach to acquire experiential material and initially spends significant time simply playing. Nagisa explicitly connects enjoying summer to the ability to put summer feeling into the song.

A local fireworks legend produces a romantic premise. All five members say they lack relevant private romantic experience at this point. They nevertheless build the song through imagination, desire, empathy, and analogy.

Admit as support:

- `IMAGINED_INTIMACY_AS_PERFORMANCE_MATERIAL`

Current rule:

> **A performer need not possess autobiographically equivalent experience in order to author or inhabit intimate material; imagination, analogy, aspiration, observation, and collaborative interpretation can supply truthful artistic access without converting the work into private confession.**

This strongly complements E3 `PERFORMED_INTIMACY_WITHOUT_PRIVATE_CLAIM`.

## 7.3 Tsuki's positive self-differentiation

The group explicitly compares itself with Sunny Peace while searching for a summer identity:

> `私達はサニーピースみたいに ザ・夏！ってグループじゃない`

and then reframes the difference positively:

> `夜空じゃなきゃ、花火も月も綺麗に見えない`
>
> `太陽みたいに眩しくはないけど――だからこそ綺麗な光がある`

This yields:

> **`AESTHETIC_SELF_DIFFERENTIATION`**
>
> **A unit can define its own artistic identity through contrast without treating another unit's strengths as a standard it must copy or defeat.**

Tsuki is not “failed Sunny.” Moonlight becomes a positive aesthetic and relational self-description.

## 7.4 Romantic vocabulary does not settle relationship ontology

After the performance, Mei describes recognizing that she is “in love” with Tsuki and wants to remain with the group and on stage. Nagisa and Saki immediately affirm the beauty of the feeling while also distinguishing it from ordinary romantic love.

Admit:

- `ROMANTIC_LANGUAGE_WITHOUT_ROMANTIC_IDENTITY`

Current rule:

> **Intense attachment may borrow the vocabulary or phenomenology of romance without that metaphor alone settling the relationship's literal category. Native distinctions made by the characters must be preserved.**

This is particularly important for later comparative relationship analysis: strong affection should not be normalized into romance merely because the text deliberately plays with romantic language.

# 14. Prospective cross-source synthesis

## 14.1 Construction, imagination, and truth

E4 repeatedly demonstrates that truth in performance does not require autobiographical identity.

The strongest mainline case is Tsuki's romantic summer song: they lack equivalent romantic experience yet can still create from imagination, desire, analogy, shared experience, and attention to what listeners might need.

Crossover sources reinforce the same architecture from outside:

- Miku collaboration turns feelings into jointly authored form;
- Aqours provides an external vocational mirror;
- HTT's ordinary life becomes knowledge required to inhabit its musical world;
- Rabbit House guests can contribute meaningfully despite different professional skill distributions.

This strengthens the existing authenticity model without changing its mature owner.

## 14.2 Difference without derivativeness

Tsuki's moon/sun contrast is the strongest new uncaveated contribution:

> another unit's strength can serve as a contrast that helps articulate identity without becoming a sovereign standard.

This is continuous with `INFLUENCE_WITHOUT_DERIVATIVENESS` but works at the level of unit aesthetic differentiation.

## 14.3 Attachment language requires category discipline

The uncaveated Tsuki event gives an explicit warning against collapsing all intense attachment into romance. Mei reaches for the language of being “in love” with Tsuki; her peers both affirm and reclassify it.

The mature synthesis should therefore preserve native relationship categories and contextual correction rather than treating emotionally intense language as dispositive.

## 14.4 Crossover evidence is most valuable as a controlled contrast set

E4's nine caveated stories are analytically useful precisely when their limits remain visible. They function like controlled perturbations: familiar IDOLY PRIDE characters are placed beside different performance institutions, guest values, or impossible premises. Stable behavior can corroborate a model; divergence can identify what depends on setting. But the experiment is not the continuity.

Prospective archival rule:

> **Crossover evidence is strongest for comparative behavior and weakest for continuity-dependent facts.**
