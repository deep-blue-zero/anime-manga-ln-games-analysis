# 2. E1-A tranche thesis

> **INTERPRETATION — E1-A converts Mana from a potentially sovereign founding miracle into a contested, transmissible, and reciprocal inheritance.**

Across these four events, the living repeatedly ask who is entitled to remember Mana, sing her songs, inherit Hoshimi, or claim continuity with her. The texts reject both extremes:

- **mausoleum logic** — the dead permanently own the institution, songs, relationships, and future;
- **amnesia logic** — progress requires severing the dead from the present.

Instead, E1-A develops a third structure:

> **memory remains legitimate when it can be answered, reinterpreted, shared with non-witnesses, and converted into new work without granting the dead unilateral jurisdiction over the living.**

This is not a claim that Mana becomes unimportant. It is a claim that her importance changes form.

A second E1-A thesis concerns Hoshimi itself:

> **Hoshimi Production becomes legible as an institution capable of continuity without sameness.**

It can:

- commemorate a dead founder without becoming only her shrine;
- accept IIIX without demanding sentimental assimilation;
- absorb former rivals such as LizNoir without erasing their prior identity;
- transmit unfinished artistic work across generations;
- preserve Haruko as a current idol rather than turning her into living heritage furniture;
- and make professional belonging compatible with rivalry, refusal, and different relationships to Mana.

# 3. Event 1 — `event_2021_004_st-eve-2110-marathon`
## 煌めく奇跡をもう一度

### 3.1 Chronology route

**TEXTUAL FACT:** the opening places the story **some time after Hoshimi Production's expansion into Tokyo** (`星見プロが東京に進出して しばらく経った`).

The cast configuration includes SUNNY PEACE, Tsuki no Tempest, TRINITYAiLE, and four-person LizNoir, but not IIIX. Sakura also says that since Mana disappeared, her heart has no longer communicated Mana's guidance.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** the safest route is **post-anime/game Hoshimi expansion, after Mana's ghostly departure, before IIIX's Hoshimi transfer**. Exact placement against every main-story chapter remains unnecessary for the event's semantic use.

Classification: `REVISION` + `TEMPORAL_MATURATION` + `OPEN-17/18 PRESSURE`.

### 3.2 The first post-departure birthday changes grief into practice

The event begins with Makino dreaming of Mana, then realizing that he had nearly forgotten her birthday while concentrating on making the current idols top-class performers.

This is important because the text does **not** frame continued life as betrayal, nor remembrance as automatic virtue. Makino's guilt comes from the fear that professional forward motion has made him neglect Mana. Sakura answers by refusing the zero-sum model: his drive to keep moving was itself partly produced by his relationship with Mana, and her birthday can become an annual shared practice rather than a private loyalty test.

Kotono undergoes a parallel movement. She reads Mana's diary entries about the missed 2016 birthday celebration and the joyous 2017 birthday live. Her response is not to preserve the diary privately. She chooses to reproduce the *social form* Mana wanted: celebration with other people.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Sakura proposes that from now on everyone at Hoshimi can celebrate Mana's birthday every year.

**INTERPRETATION:** this is the point where grief becomes **institutional ritual** rather than only personal mourning.

The ritual is not static. The planned dorm party is abandoned in favor of an improvised live at Mana's debut site. Memory therefore survives through adaptation, not exact reenactment.

### 3.3 Kokoro is the crucial non-witness control

Kokoro openly questions why everyone is making such a large event out of the birthday of someone who is already dead. Ai partly shares the question. This matters methodologically: the story does not assume that Mana's charisma should be self-evident to people who never met her.

Kokoro's distance prevents the audience from mistaking Hoshimi's internal memory culture for universal truth.

She spends the event collecting distinct accounts:

- Makino remembers two brilliant years and the devastation of Mana's death;
- Haruko remembers curry, encouragement, and ordinary friendship;
- Rio remembers the frightening amount of invisible labor beneath Mana's smile;
- Kotono remembers private domestic details;
- Sakura carries bodily and supernatural-adjacent inheritance.

Mana therefore emerges as a **plural remembered person**, not one canonical myth.

By the end, Kokoro remains explicit that Mana is still someone she never met and can never meet. She will participate in events, but she says `過去は振り返りません`; her priority is pursuing the top with Rio, Aoi, and Ai.

That refusal is not punished. Instead, she becomes interested enough to ask for Mana's Blu-rays and to imagine the appeal of becoming an idol remembered after death.

**INTERPRETATION:** healthy institutional memory does not require every member to feel the same grief. It allows **differential belonging to the past**.

### 3.4 Sakura's heartbeat demystifies the relic without resolving the supernatural

The event repeatedly flirts with literal supernatural continuation while protecting uncertainty.

Sakura tells Makino that Mana's heart no longer communicates with her after Mana's disappearance, but that on this birthday she somehow felt guided to Makino's location. Crucially, Sakura says `分かりません` and admits that this is the first such occurrence since Mana vanished.

Later, Kokoro asks to listen to Sakura's heartbeat and observes that the legendary idol's heart sounds ordinary, no different from her own.

This juxtaposition is analytically important:

- Sakura's subjective sense preserves the possibility of miracle;
- Kokoro's physical observation refuses fetishization of the transplanted heart as obviously magical object.

The sunlight that falls over the stage like a spotlight during `First Step` is similarly suggestive but not probative.

**OPEN:** `OPEN-17` remains unresolved. The event strengthens the series' willingness to stage meaningful coincidence and subjective supernatural experience while withholding objective confirmation.

### 3.5 `song for you` becomes a multi-directional relational object

Sakura explicitly says that `song for you` has different meanings for Mana, Kotono, herself, Makino, and the rest of Hoshimi. Kotono adds that the song can now function as a message back toward Mana as well as the message Mana originally intended to send.

Makino proposes that Mana ultimately wrote it toward the people she cherished.

The safest formulation is:

- **TEXTUAL FACT:** the characters now experience the song in multiple relational directions.
- **INTERPRETATION:** the song has escaped exclusive authorial ownership and become a reusable memory vessel.
- **CAUTION:** Makino's universalizing formulation is an in-character interpretation, not an omniscient statement that erases the song's original specific addressee.

### 3.6 `First Step` is not nostalgic repetition

The encore choice matters. `First Step` is Mana's debut song, but Sakura explicitly calls the day the **first step** in a new annual practice of remembrance.

The title is therefore repurposed from Mana's career beginning to the institution's beginning of a new memorial custom.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hoshimi does not preserve Mana by walking backward into her first step. It uses the inherited song to take another first step of its own.

### 3.7 Event-local state deltas

#### Makino

- grief remains active despite functional forward movement;
- fear of forgetting is exposed;
- accepts remembrance as compatible with continuing professional responsibility;
- is explicitly cared for by the idols rather than functioning only as caretaker.

#### Kotono

#### Sakura

- demonstrates that connection to Mana can be acquired without simulated personal grief.

# 4. Event 2 — `event_2022_007_st-eve-2207-contest`
## Happy Smile Selfie

### 4.1 Chronology route

The chronology is unusually explicit.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** `ⅢXが星見プロに移籍して間もない、ある日`.

The event also specifies:

- post-I-UNITY inactivity;
- reputational collapse after the Pret-a-Porter scandal;
- a period of freelance activity that did not advance IIIX toward its goals;
- IIIX's move to Hoshimi as an instrumental response to that problem.

Classification: `CHRONOLOGY_CORRECTION` + `EXTENSION` + `OPEN-05/10/16 PRESSURE`.

This source is therefore a strong early-Hoshimi IIIX anchor and must **not** be rewritten using the later US-arc reauthorization of the unit.

### 4.2 IIIX does not join Hoshimi because it wants a home

Fran states that continued freelance activity was taking them farther from their purpose, which is why they accepted Hoshimi's offer. When no work immediately appears, all three question the value of the transfer.

Their initial posture toward Hoshimi is explicitly instrumental:

- platform;
- work acquisition;
- route back to BIG4/top status;
- usable institution.

Fran later tells miho directly that Hoshimi is another thing to use as a stepping stone.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** at this stage, IIIX still describes itself as a coalition of separately motivated members whose interests align.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hoshimi affiliation precedes emotional affiliation. Institutional membership does not automatically produce belonging.

### 4.3 Hoshimi's first ethical test is status de-privileging

The event repeatedly strips IIIX of the privileges its former agency and BIG4 status had normalized:

- a smaller stage than Tsuki no Tempest;
- no managerial favoritism;
- shared backstage space rather than a private room;
- ordinary performance order;
- poor air conditioning;
- weather exposure;
- no ability to bypass other performers;
- no guarantee that reputation will fill the audience.

Makino explicitly tells them that Hoshimi will not give them special treatment and that their current industry evaluation is what the current booking reflects.

This is a significant institutional detail.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hoshimi's hospitality is not deference. It provides a route back into professional circulation while refusing to pretend IIIX's former status is still operative.

This is not enough to establish that Hoshimi is free of exploitation. The event does not audit contracts, compensation, workload, or every labor condition. But it provides counterevidence to the idea that Hoshimi's acceptance of IIIX depends on flattering them or buying their loyalty through privilege.

### 4.4 Sakura distinguishes rivalry from culpability

Miho asks why Sakura does not hate IIIX after the attempt to undermine Hoshimi. Sakura answers with two distinctions:

1. IIIX itself was not involved in the underlying fraud;
2. at I-UNITY, IIIX fought SUNNY PEACE honestly and at full power.

She then argues that IIIX's strength enabled SUNNY PEACE to exceed its own limits and that continued rivalry can make both units stronger.

This is not generic forgiveness. Sakura makes a culpability judgment and preserves competitive opposition.

**INTERPRETATION:** her relational ethic is **non-erasing rivalry**: an opponent can remain an opponent without being converted into an enemy whose future must disappear.

### 4.5 miho's Yō/Mana wound is institutionally activated, not healed

Sakura's brightness immediately reminds miho of Yō. Fran says this explicitly: `彼女が葉ちゃんみたいだから？`.

Miho's hostility toward Sakura is therefore overdetermined:

- Sakura resembles the lost partner whose absence structures miho;
- Sakura is also positioned as a successor to Mana;
- Hoshimi itself is the institution associated with Mana;
- IIIX has just lost to SUNNY PEACE and lost BIG4 status;
- miho still defines one of her goals as eliminating Mana's presence.

Miho explicitly wonders whether someone who hates Mana can coexist with the girls who inherit Mana's will.

Fran does **not** resolve that conflict. She tells miho that she chose both the SUNNY battle and the Hoshimi move herself; agonizing over the choice after the fact is useless. The result is functional recommitment, not mourning resolution.

**PRIMARY FREEZE:** do not describe this event as miho accepting Mana or Sakura. It establishes the *problem* of coexistence and the beginning of workable institutional proximity.

### 4.6 IIIX's hostile intimacy is already operational but not yet self-authored as attachment

Fran and miho's private drinking scene is one of the event's most valuable unit passages.

They deny friendship, insult each other, and describe the unit as business partnership. Yet Fran notices miho's abnormal expression, follows her to speak privately, diagnoses the Yō/Mana problem, and deliberately provokes her back into motion.

This is care in a grammar the speakers refuse to call care.

The event does not authorize the later mature claim that IIIX remains together because they positively choose the relationship itself. At this point, the text still gives each member an external goal:

- Fran — money;
- kana — find her father;
- miho — erase Mana's presence.

**INTERPRETATION:** the relationship already exceeds its own declared business-only description behaviorally, but its members have not yet admitted that excess as a reason for the unit's existence.

### 4.7 Persona is strategic without being simply false

Kana's lunch photo with Sakura is deliberately manufactured as `可愛い後輩と一緒にランチ` despite kana privately saying she is using the image to improve favorability and collect likes.

Yet several things are simultaneously true:

- the post is strategic;
- the affectionate caption is not an accurate statement of established intimacy;
- kana genuinely enjoys SNS work;
- Sakura genuinely interprets it as labor/skill;
- kana gradually finds Sakura interesting;
- the encounter becomes real social contact precisely through a staged public image.

Later, IIIX takes a smiling three-member selfie despite all three rejecting the label `仲良し`.

**INTERPRETATION:** the event resists a binary of authentic private self versus fake public persona. Performed friendliness can be commercial, tactical, aesthetically controlled, and still become a medium through which actual relationship develops.

### 4.8 Fans and Makino: relational knowledge versus pure calculation

Miho dismisses Makino's belief that IIIX fans will come as insufficiently analytical: `計算も分析も出来ないものは根拠になりません`.

The crowd appears, and IIIX's performance draws more people.

The correct reading is not “analysis is bad; feelings are right.” Miho's risk analysis about bad weather and damaged reputation is rational. The event adds another kind of professional knowledge: a manager who has witnessed a unit's fan relation can make judgments that are not reducible to current booking status or quantitative forecasts.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hoshimi's management model includes **relational professional knowledge** alongside metrics.

### 4.9 `ⅢXは止まらない` means continuity before belonging

Miho publicly declares that IIIX will not stop no matter what happens. The successful live restores bookings and gives the transfer practical value.

The event closes with IIIX still promising to defeat SUNNY PEACE and become Hoshimi's top unit.

This is crucial:

> **integration does not mean domestication.**

Hoshimi becomes viable because it can contain actors who want to beat one another and who still treat the institution instrumentally.

# 5. Event 3 — `event_2022_010_st-eve-2210-race`
## 運命繋ぐ流星の軌跡

### 5.1 Chronology route

**TEXTUAL FACT:** `これはLizNoirが星見プロに入ってすぐの話`.

The unit is already four members and is touring. The event therefore belongs to **early post-transfer four-person LizNoir**, before any later mature career conclusions should be imported.

Classification: `REVISION` + `TEMPORAL_MATURATION` + `FORMAL_DEPENDENCY` + `OPEN-17/18 PRESSURE`.

### 5.2 The event begins by refusing epistemic ownership of the dead

Aoi asks whether Mana would feel fulfilled because Kotono entered and won the NEXT VENUS Grand Prix. Rio answers that Mana might be happy or might be frustrated; Mana was too free-spirited to reduce to one answer.

They both wish they could ask what she thinks of them now.

This is a powerful corrective to legacy-as-certainty.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Rio and Aoi do not claim to know what Mana would authorize.

**INTERPRETATION:** the dead remain relationally present without becoming a ventriloquized source of convenient approval.

### 5.3 Rio's initial refusal is a proprietary theory of inheritance

Kotono asks LizNoir to cover Mana's songs because LizNoir was special to Mana. Rio refuses.

Her reason is not dislike. Aoi tells Kotono the opposite: Rio loves and values Mana so much that she thinks LizNoir must not cross that boundary.

Rio later states the logic herself: Mana's songs belong properly to Tsuki no Tempest and SUNNY PEACE; LizNoir singing them would feel like `奪う` — taking them away.

**INTERPRETATION:** Rio initially understands inheritance as a protected lineage. Respect becomes exclusion.

This is precisely the kind of unilateral memorial rule Tier-A warns against: Rio is protecting Mana by deciding, without Mana, who has standing to sing.

### 5.4 The crucial reversal: Mana was herself an inheritor of LizNoir

At the live, Kotono tells the audience that Mana decided to become an idol after hearing Rio and Aoi perform at that very live house.

This changes the structure of inheritance.

The line is not:

> Mana created everyone who came after her.

It is:

> LizNoir helped create Mana; Mana later became a rival and inspiration to LizNoir; Mana's song then helped Rio return after grief; now Rio sings Mana back into the present beside Kotono.

**INTERPRETATION — HIGH CONFIDENCE:** E1-A establishes **reciprocal inheritance**, not sovereign lineage.

Mana is a major node in the network, but not its unmoved origin.

### 5.5 `星の海の記憶` is a survival text for Rio

Rio admits that after Mana's death she spent roughly a year trying to leave singing. She repeatedly sang the line about intersecting fate and living tomorrow strongly, and says the song helped turn her heart forward.

She then states plainly:

> `私が今も歌っていられるのは、間違いなく 彼女と、この歌に出会ったから`.

This is one of the strongest textual statements in the tranche.

Mana's importance to Rio is therefore not only competitive standard or trauma. Mana's work becomes part of the causal history of Rio continuing as an idol.

### 5.6 Singing becomes dialogic memorialization

Rio says she wants to tell Mana `ありがとう` and wants Mana to see who she has become.

Kotono's response is characteristically stronger: if Rio wants to meet Mana, she should sing. Kotono says she still feels Mana onstage and cites the Grand Prix final as her own experience.

Again, this is **subjective testimony**, not objective ontology.

Rio accepts the experiment. During the performance she later reports that she felt she spoke to Mana for one moment.

The experience changes behavior: Rio reverses her refusal and agrees to participate in the album cover project.

**INTERPRETATION:** performance does not prove the dead are literally present; it creates a socially and emotionally legitimate medium in which the living can address them and revise their own relationship to memory.

### 5.7 Forgetting is rejected without making memory sovereign

At Mana's grave, Rio internally admits:

- she tried to forget Mana;
- she thought she had to forget;
- she could not;
- she now realizes she did not need to.

But the conclusion is not devotional stasis. She tells Mana that the question of whether LizNoir has become `本物のアイドル` is still unanswered and that she will continue trying to surpass Mana and reach the top.

**INTERPRETATION:** memory and rivalry become compatible. The relationship to the dead remains future-producing.

### 5.8 Aoi's role is relational mediation, not passive support

Aoi recognizes what Rio cannot state to Kotono, explains the refusal, engineers the second attempt, and knows Rio's private crying pattern. He gives the rest of LizNoir a way to support without intruding.

The event also explicitly contrasts old two-person LizNoir's severe atmosphere with the transformed four-person unit. Rio says the noisier form is a change and that it has been good.

This is an important early four-person LizNoir state: continuity with Rio/Aoi history does not require recreating the old dyad as the unit's only valid form.

### 5.9 Formal dependency

Therefore the current source establishes:

It does **not** establish, without additional source:

This event must carry `FORMAL_DEPENDENCY` into the audiovisual backlog.

### 5.10 Event-local state deltas

# 6. Event 4 — `event_2023_006_st-eve-2306-contest`
## 星々が奇跡と叶える約束の未来

### 6.1 Chronology route

The present-day cast includes all five major Hoshimi units, including IIIX. The event explicitly describes a Hoshimi that has expanded from the early local office into a multi-unit Tokyo-era institution.

Classification: `REVISION` + `TEMPORAL_MATURATION` + `OPEN-14/18 PRESSURE`.

The important disclosure chronology is that this event **retroactively reveals pre-death Mana/Haruko/Makino/Saegusa history** inside a later institutional frame. Those flashbacks may explain earlier relationships but must not be treated as information available to earlier prospective readers before this disclosure.

### 6.2 The present repeats a past desire without simply reenacting it

The current idols independently develop the idea of giving Saegusa, Satomi, and Makino a gratitude live. Haruko is startled because Mana and Haruko had once planned essentially the same thing for Makino and Saegusa.

This is not mystical repetition. The text provides an institutional explanation: similar forms of gratitude emerge from similar relationships between idols and the people who support their work.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hoshimi continuity can be reproduced through practice without requiring conscious imitation of Mana.

### 6.3 Mana's own origin is institutional and relational

The flashback supplies a compact prehistory:

- Saegusa hears Mana singing at the beach and scouts her;
- he directs her to a LizNoir live;
- that live helps Mana decide to become an idol;
- Mana joins Hoshimi together with Makino;
- Makino supports her through career problems including loss of voice and overwork;
- Haruko is already present as an idol senior, friend, training partner, and rival.

This matters because it decisively complicates any myth in which Mana alone creates Hoshimi's meaning.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Mana herself is produced through an existing network of recruiter, manager, senior/friend, rival performers, live culture, and local institution.

**INTERPRETATION:** the miracle is socially scaffolded.

### 6.4 Mana/Haruko: friendship and rivalry against self-erasure

Haruko initially refuses to sing with Mana because she believes she is too unpopular and unskilled to stand beside her. She offers to help as backstage support instead.

Mana does not answer by denying the skill/status gap. She answers relationally: Haruko is her fellow Hoshimi idol, friend, rival, and one of the people who made her work harder. She wants to sing with Haruko specifically; `遙子ちゃんと一緒じゃなきゃ嫌なの`.

Haruko then accepts while preserving rivalry: the next shared stage should happen when she has become popular enough not to lose to Mana.

**INTERPRETATION:** this is a foundational version of the series' later critique of unilateral self-reduction. Care and gratitude do not justify disappearing into support labor when the relationship itself recognizes you as a co-author.

### 6.5 The unfinished song is inherited through collaborative completion

Mana and Haruko intended to write an original gratitude song together. Mana dies before the live, and the lyrics remain unfinished.

In the present, Haruko first tries to complete the lyrics alone because the original promise was between the two of them.

Fran supplies the decisive practical insight: if one person cannot finish it, why not let more people contribute?

Haruko realizes that the song is no longer only hers and Mana's; it is now Hoshimi's song.

This is an unusually clean example of inheritance ethics:

> **preservation does not require freezing authorship at the moment of loss.**

The later group does not erase Mana/Haruko's origin. It completes the work precisely by acknowledging that the community capable of carrying its purpose has grown.

### 6.6 The 21-idol count prevents Haruko from being archived alive

Haruko says they should make the live with Hoshimi's `二十人……いえ、二十一人` idols.

The arithmetic is decisive:

- SUNNY PEACE 5
- Tsuki no Tempest 5
- LizNoir 4
- TRINITYAiLE 3
- IIIX 3
- Haruko 1

= **21**.

Haruko is therefore explicitly counted as a current Hoshimi idol.

This matters later when she describes Mana and herself as Hoshimi's past and the younger members as its present. Kotono immediately refuses a succession model that removes Haruko from the future: Haruko will help lead Hoshimi too.

Haruko accepts and states that she will continue shining as an idol with them.

**PRIMARY FREEZE:** `OPEN-14` is substantially strengthened toward long-career continuity. Haruko is not merely veteran witness, staff-adjacent elder, or living memorial object. The text explicitly preserves her current idol status inside the succession structure.

### 6.7 “Past” and “present” are temporal positions, not replacement rights

Haruko chooses not to sing the inherited song at the present live. She says that in the old Hoshimi, she and Mana were the appropriate singers; in current Hoshimi, the younger centers are appropriate.

That could superficially look like generational self-removal.

Kotono's reply changes its meaning: the younger generation will inherit the song, but Haruko remains part of the future Hoshimi.

The distinction is:

- **artifact stewardship can pass forward**;
- **personhood and vocation need not be surrendered with it**.

This is a major longitudinal principle for aging/adult idolhood.

### 6.8 Hoshimi's identity is continuity of commitments, not fixed membership or place

At the end, Haruko recalls the Hoshimi with only her and Mana as idols, then the formation of SUNNY PEACE and Tsuki no Tempest, then the addition of TRINITYAiLE, LizNoir, and IIIX.

She says that places and members change but what matters can remain unchanged: `大事にしているものは変わらない`.

The event does not give a complete corporate charter. But it does give Hoshimi an explicit **institutional self-understanding**:

> continuity can persist through changing membership, geography, scale, generations, and competitive relationships.

This is more precise than calling Hoshimi a family.

### 6.9 Gratitude is expressed as professional excellence

Before the live, Kotono says that because the audience consists of close supporters, they have *more* reason not to lower standards. Showing how much they have grown through their strongest current performance is itself the act of thanks.

Rio agrees that feeling cannot be transmitted through a lax live.

Fran tries to turn the situation into competition; Sakura cheerfully absorbs even that into collective excellence.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hoshimi gratitude is not sentimental exemption from professionalism. It is professional labor intentionally addressed to particular people.

This reinforces Tier-A's distinction between feeling and form: care requires adequate performance form to become communicable.

# 7. Cross-event synthesis before historical comparison

## 7.1 Mana moves through four memory regimes

### A. Personal loss

Makino dreams; Kotono reads diaries; Rio cries; Haruko remembers an unfulfilled promise.

### B. Communal ritual

Hoshimi celebrates the birthday and performs Mana's songs together.

### C. Reciprocal artistic lineage

LizNoir is not merely Mana's successor/rival. LizNoir helps cause Mana's idol vocation; Mana's song later helps Rio continue.

### D. Institutional inheritance

Mana/Haruko's unfinished gratitude song becomes material completed and performed by the expanded Hoshimi.

The progression is therefore not simply private → public. It is:

> **grief → plural memory → reciprocal lineage → institutional transmission**.

## 7.2 The dead are influential but not sovereign

E1-A repeatedly protects Mana from two distortions:

- forgetting her;
- using her imagined wishes as an unquestionable command.

Kokoro does not share eyewitness grief.
Rio and Aoi admit they do not know what Mana would think.
Kotono and Sakura sometimes speak confidently about what Mana would like, but the text keeps those statements within character perspective.
Supernatural-adjacent experiences remain subjective.

**INTERPRETATION:** Mana becomes ethically usable as inheritance only when the living retain the right to answer her rather than merely obey an image of her.

## 7.3 Hoshimi's mature institutional principle begins to emerge

Across the tranche, Hoshimi can now be provisionally described as:

> **a professional continuity platform that preserves provenance without demanding sameness.**

It supports:

- ritual memory;
- rivalry;
- re-entry after reputational collapse;
- unequal relationships to institutional history;
- common artistic projects;
- adult career continuity;
- non-exclusive inheritance.

This strengthens the frozen Tier-A phrase **plural professional home/platform** and makes “family” an even less adequate primary descriptor.

## 7.4 Songs become transferable without becoming ownerless

Three song structures matter:

- `song for you` — specific origin, later multiple directions of address;
- `星の海の記憶` — Mana song that becomes Rio survival text and shared memorial performance;
- Haruko/Mana gratitude song — unfinished two-person work completed by a larger institution.

The pattern is not that authorship disappears.

The pattern is:

> **provenance remains, while legitimate use expands.**

This is a strong model for the franchise's broader ethics of inheritance.

## 7.5 Rivalry repeatedly survives incorporation

- Sakura wants IIIX to remain a rival after entering Hoshimi.
- IIIX intends to overthrow SUNNY PEACE from inside the same agency.
- Rio continues wanting to surpass Mana while preserving her memory.
- Haruko's love for Mana includes wanting eventually to stand beside her without losing.
- Current Hoshimi idols turn even a gratitude live into competitive motivation.

The institution's positive function is therefore not harmony in the sense of reduced difference. It is **continued relation without forced erasure of competitive desire**.

# 10. Counterevidence and interpretive limits

1. **Do not overstate anti-myth demystification.** The events clearly continue to romanticize Mana and stage miracle-like coincidence.
2. **Do not treat Kokoro's skepticism as authorial rejection of memorial practice.** She joins willingly and becomes interested.
3. **Do not turn Hoshimi's equal-treatment of IIIX into proof of perfect labor ethics.** The event is narrow.
4. **Do not call early Hoshimi IIIX a chosen-family unit.** Their explicit self-description is still business/instrumental.
5. **Do not say miho has reconciled with Mana in `Happy Smile Selfie`.** She has not.
6. **Do not say Rio receives objective contact with Mana.** The text presents a felt stage conversation.
7. **Do not flatten Mana-song ownership into “anyone can use anything.”** Provenance remains central to why later use matters.
8. **Do not call Haruko retired, semi-retired, or staff-like based on age/function.** `event_2023_006` directly counts her as a current idol.
9. **Do not treat Haruko's choice not to sing the inherited song as self-erasure.** Kotono's immediate reply and Haruko's own future commitment make it selective stewardship, not vocational surrender.
10. **Do not infer choreography or staging from `event_2022_010` because its live asset is missing.**
