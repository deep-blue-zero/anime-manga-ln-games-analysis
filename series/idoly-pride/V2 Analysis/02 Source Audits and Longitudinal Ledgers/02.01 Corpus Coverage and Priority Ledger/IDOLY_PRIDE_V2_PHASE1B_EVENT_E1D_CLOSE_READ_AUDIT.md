# 1. Final E1-D thesis

The definitive E1-D formulation is:

> **The United States arc tests whether IIIX can survive the collapse of the reasons that originally made the unit useful. It can—but only by becoming a plural motive compact whose members choose continuation after the old instrumental bargain has already failed.**

Part 1 is not merely a breakup story.

It performs three different stress tests:

1. **kana — contact without relational fulfillment**  
   Fame succeeds at making her father findable, but cannot manufacture the family relation she believed recognition would restore.

2. **fran — success that threatens to make the unit unnecessary**  
   A credible fashion investor reopens her pre-idol route strongly enough that idol activity can appear expendable.

3. **miho — falsification of strategy rather than fulfillment of desire**  
   She does not simply receive a more direct Yō-memory opportunity. Instead she sees Yō literally edited out of public history, learns that IIIX's polished persona helps make that erasure narratively convenient, and is told that the score-oriented IIIX she built has drifted from Sundance's audience-facing purpose while still being too weak to reach the summit.

The dissolution therefore occurs because the original compact loses explanatory force from **three different directions**.

Part 2 then rebuilds IIIX without pretending those collapses never happened.

The members do not discover one common soul. They discover reasons that can coexist.

# 15. Final E1-D synthesis

The V1 corpus was already correct that this is the decisive IIIX re-founding arc. The V2 gain is in refusing to flatten why.

IIIX begins the United States trip still understandable as a highly successful instrument through which three adults pursue old wounds and ambitions. America gives that instrument an adversarial test by changing the relation between each woman and her original end.

Kana proves that visibility can work exactly as designed and still fail to produce the relation behind the design. Her father finds her. The child who wanted to be recovered by him is not recovered. The fantasy that paternal recognition would make her naturally kind, easy, and loved collapses. Her mature act is not to stop caring instantly, but to deny that unresolved fatherhood owns the future of her personality or career.

Fran proves the opposite danger. Her original world opens again. A credible investor sees her clothes and offers the possibility of a second fashion act. If IIIX was merely a vehicle back to fashion, success should end the group. Instead the brand itself becomes attached to IIIX's public body: these specific women, in clothes designed to fit them, winning on a stage that can make the work visible. Her business motive remains real, and precisely because it is real it can become the material channel through which specific commitment appears.

Miho receives the cruelest test. Her path has not preserved Yō. The American archival edit makes the problem literal: a dead partner incompatible with the “perfect IIIX women” story is simply removed. Meanwhile Yōko exposes the ethical gap between Sundance's audience orientation and IIIX's score optimization, then tells miho that IIIX is not even strong enough for the summit she has made into proof of fidelity. Miho's crisis is therefore not a temptation to pursue a purer Yō archive elsewhere. It is the discovery that her current method has allowed private fidelity, public erasure, and goal displacement to coexist.

Fran's confrontation forces the missing distinction. Remembering someone privately and preventing that person from disappearing publicly are different tasks. Miho initially wants both public survival and memorial enclosure. The US arc makes that contradiction unstable. By the final scene she can say the vow aloud: top, and become an idol capable of making someone happy. Once said, it becomes something the living can answer. Kana can borrow it provisionally. Fran can translate it into beauty. Neither becomes Yō. Neither owns the promise's origin. Both can participate in its future.

That is why `絶対勝利` still works at the end. IIIX has not discovered that winning is shallow. The reigning No. 1 herself provides the warning about a competitive institution in which every victory becomes another person's devastation. IIIX's answer is not to stop competing. It is to make defeat non-terminal. If they lose, they will think about how to beat her next time. Victory remains absolute as intention; it is no longer sovereign as a judgment of whether the group deserves another future.

The strongest mature E1-D formulation is therefore:

> **IIIX is a unit that survives the collapse of its founding utilities by learning to choose itself again without becoming one person. Its members remain vain, greedy, bereaved, abrasive, self-interested, ambitious, and often cruel. Their development is not purification. It is the acquisition of enough mutual knowledge and enough independent authorship that those differences can sustain rather than automatically dissolve common action. Their mature competitive ethic is not “winning does not matter.” It is: winning matters intensely, but losing does not get to decide whether we still exist.**
