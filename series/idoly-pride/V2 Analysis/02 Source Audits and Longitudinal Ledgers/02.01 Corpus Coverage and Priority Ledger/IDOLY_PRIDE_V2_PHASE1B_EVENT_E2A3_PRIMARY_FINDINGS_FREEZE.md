# 2. E2-A3 tranche thesis

The four events converge on a proposition that had been present implicitly in the corpus but is now textually difficult to avoid:

> **Idolhood can be a central chosen vocation without acquiring total jurisdiction over the person.**

A mature professional identity must coexist with other authored forms of life:

- private creative practice;
- family inheritance without career predestination;
- institutional participation beyond stage performance;
- ordinary peer belonging;
- school citizenship;
- temporary civic leadership;
- fandom;
- friendship;
- leisure and private interests.

The best compact term is:

> **`NON_TOTALIZING_VOCATION`**

Definition:

> A vocation is legitimate when it can organize serious commitment without claiming every other identity, relationship, interest, or period of life as subordinate material.

E2-A3 also adds a companion principle:

> **`PLURAL_ROLE_AUTHORSHIP`**

Definition:

> A person can genuinely author several roles at once, provided no role is treated as automatically entitled to erase the others and the practical burdens remain answerable.

This is **not** a doctrine of unlimited capacity. The tranche repeatedly shows that plural roles generate workload, scheduling, reputational, and bodily pressures. The ethical claim is therefore not “do everything.” It is:

> **No one role is automatically entitled to declare the others illegitimate.**

# 3. `おしごとシークレット` — privacy without self-erasure

## 3.1 Yu's hidden work is real creative labor

The apparent scandal is intentionally misframed. Yu is not in a secret romantic or exploitative relationship. She is spending nights in a hotel room with her actual father because they are trying to finish a doujinshi before a same-day doujin event while TRINITYAiLE prepares for a major summer festival.

The source establishes that:

- Yu and her father have participated in the doujin event every year;
- both draw the book together;
- her father comes from Kyoto to help because Yu's idol schedule has become too dense;
- Yu regards both idol work and doujin creation as `宝物`;
- the doujin space matters because it brings her into contact with people who love the same things;
- fellow attendees do not primarily treat TRINITYAiLE as celebrities there, but as participants sharing the same interest.

This is not merely an otaku gag. It establishes a second form of creative authorship that is neither derivative of Yu's idol profession nor reducible to publicity.

Admit:

> **`PRIVATE_CREATIVE_VOCATION`**

Yu's doujin activity is serious enough to demand labor, deadlines, collaboration, and identity investment, even though she does not ask for it to become her public professional brand.

## 3.2 Secrecy, privacy, and publicity are not the same thing

The most important line comes after the scandal threat has been neutralized. Sumire proposes talking openly about the doujin activity in the festival MC. Yu declines.

Yu does say she will stop hiding her love of manga and anime and will gradually speak about those interests. But she also says that doujin activity is not necessarily something one needs to announce publicly:

> `隠す必要はなくなったけど、今まで通り楽しみたい`

The crucial distinction is:

- **shame-driven concealment** is no longer necessary;
- **voluntary privacy** remains legitimate.

Admit:

> **`SELECTIVE_DISCLOSURE_WITHOUT_SHAME`**

Definition:

> Authenticity does not require total public disclosure. A person may stop treating an interest as shameful while still retaining authority over whether, when, and how that interest becomes public identity.

This is a substantial refinement of `OPEN-16`.

Earlier E1-C findings rejected authenticity as an unconstructed public self. E2-A2 rejected authenticity as purity from influence. E2-A3 now rejects a third insufficient definition:

> **authenticity as compulsory transparency.**

## 3.3 Yu's first response is self-disqualification

The malicious article threatens to frame Yu's doujin work as cynical self-promotion. Yu immediately concludes that she may bring harm to:

- Rui and Sumire;
- Hoshimi Production;
- the doujin community;
- the festival.

She therefore decides she lacks the right to appear onstage:

> `ステージに上がる資格、うちにはもうないから`

This is a familiar *IDOLY PRIDE* failure mode: concern for others becomes unilateral removal of the self.

Admit:

> **`ANTICIPATORY_SELF_DISQUALIFICATION`**

Definition:

> A person predicts that her presence will impose unacceptable externalities on others and treats that prediction as sufficient authority to remove herself without allowing the affected people to answer.

Rui, Sumire, and Makino reject the move. Their answer is not “there is no risk.” Their answer is that Yu does not hold unilateral jurisdiction over whether the group continues with her.

This is another application of `NON_DISPOSABLE_STANDING` and `ANSWERABLE_CONSTITUTIONALISM`.

## 3.4 Support does not require takeover

Rui, Sumire, Makino, and Yu's father help with the doujin event. They also preserve the summer-festival commitment.

The resolution is not that Yu chooses fandom over idol work or idol work over fandom. It is a coordinated attempt to preserve both.

However, the story also clearly marks the schedule as extreme. Makino explicitly calls it excessively crowded. Therefore the event supports **plural-role legitimacy**, not a generalized claim that any workload is acceptable if sufficiently meaningful.

# 4. `開演！ぷりてぃー★エンジェル` — lineage without predestination

## 4.1 Yu is the daughter of a famous actor, but not an inherited actor

The event repeatedly names Yu as the daughter of nationally famous actor **鈴村久美子**. Other people have long assumed that this family connection should naturally produce acting talent or an acting career.

The source explicitly rejects both assumptions.

Yu explains that her own childhood aspiration came from anime and stage imagery:

> `うちは、昔からアニメが大好きでな……`
>
> `大好きなアニメのキャラみたいに、可愛い衣装を着て`
>
> `ステージに立つことに、物心付いた時から憧れてたんよ`

She wants to be an idol and to test herself in Tokyo.

Her mother responds:

> `優の進む道は、優が決めてええんや`
>
> `ママが役者さんやからって、優もそうなること、ない`

Admit:

> **`INHERITED_CAPACITY_WITHOUT_INHERITED_VOCATION`**

Definition:

> Family lineage may transmit exposure, opportunity, skills, habits, contacts, or resources without acquiring authority to dictate the descendant's vocation.

This is one of the clearest parental-authorship statements in the current corpus.

## 4.2 Pedigree does not transfer competence

Yu enters the production assuming she has more acting competence than Rui and Sumire because:

- she is Kumiko's daughter;
- she acted as a very young child;
- adults praised her as a prodigy;
- she is deeply familiar with the source anime.

The professional director's judgment is blunt: she is very bad.

Yu later realizes that childhood praise such as `さすがあの鈴村久美子の娘` may have been inflated by pedigree.

Admit:

> **`PEDIGREE_NON_TRANSFERABILITY`**

Family prestige can create expectation and access. It cannot substitute for present demonstrated competence.

## 4.3 Fandom knowledge is not yet performance knowledge

Yu knows the source text exceptionally well. That initially makes her good at imitation and poor at inhabiting scenes not already given in the anime.

Kumiko identifies the problem precisely:

- Yu can reproduce what she has already seen;
- she lacks an imagined life for the character beyond existing footage;
- acting requires filling in ordinary speech, responses, laughter, and behavior not already supplied by the source.

Sumire independently describes acting as becoming friends with the character.

The result is a strong artistic principle:

> **Interpretive fidelity requires generative imagination, not merely accurate reproduction.**

Yu's doujin practice becomes an unexpected resource because she already knows how to imagine beyond the canonical frame.

Admit:

> **`TRANSFORMATIVE_FIDELITY`**

Definition:

> Faithfulness to a source can require imaginative extension rather than surface imitation when the performer must inhabit what the source leaves unstated.

## 4.4 Returning to family expertise is not surrendering authorship

When Yu cannot improve quickly enough, she returns home and asks her mother for instruction. Yu initially experiences this as humiliating: she went to Tokyo to test herself independently, so asking her mother for help feels like falling back on inherited privilege.

But the source frames the return differently.

Kumiko does not seize the role, choose Yu's career, or promise success. She says:

> `私に出来るのは、方向を示すことだけ。あとは優次第や`

Admit:

> **`VOLUNTARY_REENTRY_INTO_LINEAGE`**

Definition:

> Independence does not require refusing inherited support. A person can choose when to re-enter family expertise, use it as a resource, and remain author of the resulting path.

This is inheritance without ownership.

## 4.5 Makino is not required to be the center of every developmental intervention

Makino later notes that Yu solved the crisis without relying on him. He is mildly lonely about it but explicitly glad that she opened her own route with help from other people.

His conclusion is:

> `アイドルの仕事は――いや、俺達はみんな`
>
> `一人ではやっていけないのだから`

This strongly supports the existing move away from manager-centered rescue.

Healthy professional support is networked. The manager can remain important without being the exclusive route through which development must occur.

# 5. `Stars Journey` — institutional scale through distributed authorship

## 5.1 Hoshimi now functions beyond the metaphor of home

The event begins with Makino's long-standing wish to take **all twenty Hoshimi idols** on a nationwide tour.

The practical obstacles are professional rather than emotional:

- coordinating twenty successful idols' schedules;
- repeated national travel;
- song production;
- costume production;
- local media appearances;
- lodging;
- budget limits;
- maintaining ordinary work alongside the tour.

The tour nevertheless proceeds across multiple cities and repeatedly sells out.

This is major evidence for `OPEN-04`.

Hoshimi is not adequately described only as a family-like home. At this scale it is clearly:

> **a professional institution capable of coordinating differentiated units, personnel, creative production, budgets, travel, publicity, and national audiences.**

Admit:

> **`SCALABLE_PLURAL_INSTITUTION`**

Definition:

> An institution can enlarge its operational scale without requiring the people inside it to collapse into one homogeneous identity.

## 5.2 The manager's dream becomes a shared project rather than a managerial command

Makino initially describes the nationwide tour as **his** long-held dream, then dismisses it as nearly impossible because of logistics.

The idols answer that he should not tell them to pursue dreams while abandoning his own before trying. They also reject the idea that he should execute it alone:

> `全部一人でなんとかしようだなんて、水くさいですわよ`
>
> `無理しないで、もっと私達を頼って下さい`

This is significant because managerial care becomes reciprocal.

Admit:

> **`RECIPROCAL_MANAGERIAL_SUPPORT`**

The idols are not merely recipients of management. They can legitimately support the manager's aspirations and participate in making institutional work possible.

## 5.3 Twenty-person creation requires representative governance

The new song and costume cannot practically be designed by twenty people meeting constantly. Ai proposes selecting one representative from each unit for each creative team.

This produces a clear governance structure:

- representatives gather unit-specific ideas and requests;
- smaller teams deliberate;
- the resulting work remains answerable to all twenty members;
- leadership is distributed rather than concentrated entirely in Makino.

Admit:

> **`REPRESENTATIVE_CREATIVE_GOVERNANCE`**

This is a particularly useful institutional finding because it shows Hoshimi becoming scalable by creating **procedures**, not merely by relying on affection or managerial heroics.

## 5.4 Leadership does not require being the most expert person in the room

Ai becomes leader of the song-production team and immediately notices that miho is more decisive and technically useful in several aspects of the discussion.

Ai's distinctive contribution is not superior technical expertise. It is her ability to articulate a shared orientation: **gratitude**.

She names gratitude toward:

- Rio and Aoi;
- Kokoro;
- songs;
- Hoshimi;
- fans;
- the people walking with them.

That value becomes the song's guiding idea.

The event therefore supports a non-sovereign model of leadership:

> **A leader can provide direction, synthesis, and a shared reason for action while relying on other members' superior expertise.**

Admit:

> **`NON_SOVEREIGN_LEADERSHIP`**

This coheres strongly with the series' broader rejection of singular authority.

## 5.5 Hoshimi's unit plurality survives shared institutional production

The national tour creates deliberate cross-unit mixing:

- shared rooms;
- local outings;
- media appearances in unusual combinations;
- creative teams cutting across units.

But the story does not dissolve unit identities. Members repeatedly remain legible through their specific styles, tastes, histories, rivalries, and relationships.

Ai's late realization is explicit:

> `星見プロのアイドルは、全員が主役なんだって……`

She emphasizes that each has a different appeal and that mutual exposure produces new growth.

Admit:

> **`PLURAL_PROTAGONISM`**

Definition:

> Shared institutional membership does not require a single center or a single model of legitimate idolhood; the institution can organize a field of distinct protagonists.

This is one of the strongest Hoshimi-level confirmations of the series thesis so far.

## 5.6 Material constraint remains visible

The event does not portray scale as effortless.

Examples include:

- shared rooms because individual rooms exceed budget;
- the final party occurring in the dorm living room because the tour budget is exhausted;
- scheduling the tour around the narrow periods when the twenty members can align.

This is useful evidence against romanticizing Hoshimi as an infinitely resourced benevolent home.

At the same time, the text does not show members being forced to absorb clearly unacceptable costs in this event. `OPEN-05` therefore remains open.

# 6. `DOTABATA SCHOOL FESTIVAL` — idolhood beside ordinary life

## 6.1 Yu explicitly rejects the idea that professional idolhood should consume adolescence

Yu decides to run for student-council president despite already being a successful idol.

Her justification is direct:

> `アイドル活動もあるのに……`
>
> `それは分かってる。でも、学生として「今」しか`
>
> `出来ひんこともあるやん。`

This is the clearest statement in E2-A3 of `NON_TOTALIZING_VOCATION`.

Yu is not trying to escape idolhood. She remains serious about idol work. She is claiming that her student life contains opportunities whose value cannot be postponed indefinitely until after the professional career.

Admit:

> **`TIME_BOUNDED_LIFE_DOMAIN`**

Definition:

> Some forms of life are valuable partly because they are available only during a limited developmental period; professional success does not automatically justify sacrificing them.

## 6.2 Student-council leadership becomes civic rather than celebrity authority

Yu's campaign promise is not to make herself the center. She tells the students:

> `あなたの学校生活の主人公は、あなたなんです`
>
> `あなたが輝くためのお手伝いをさせて下さい`

This produces a school-scale analogue to Hoshimi's `PLURAL_PROTAGONISM`.

Admit:

> **`CIVIC_PROTAGONISM`**

Yu's leadership claim is that institutional authority should help other people inhabit their own school lives more fully.

This is not identical to idol management, but the same answerability ethic appears in another institution.

## 6.3 The event also exposes Yu's overextension pattern

The text does not romanticize the workload completely.

After becoming president, Yu:

- continues daily gate greetings;
- receives a growing stream of student requests;
- takes on administrative problems requiring staff approval;
- keeps performing idol work;
- prepares the school festival;
- writes and performs in a musical.

Rui explicitly says Yu is working too much and warns that collapse would defeat the purpose.

Yu's response is not to reduce commitments. She says she must work even harder, and Rui ultimately decides to support her more intensely.

This is analytically important.

Admit:

> **`ROLE_ACCUMULATION_RISK`**

Plural-role authorship is legitimate, but multiplying meaningful roles can still reproduce self-exploitation. The fact that every role is voluntarily chosen does not by itself solve the workload problem.

This materially advances `OPEN-05` without resolving it.

## 6.4 Kotono recovers ordinary peer belonging as something worth wanting

Kotono's story provides a second route into the school-life argument.

She observes that classmates who once called her `琴乃ちゃん` have reverted to the more distant `長瀬さん` after her professional schedule kept her away from school.

Kotono tells Nagisa that early in her career she treated work as much more important than school. After listening to Mana and Makino's perspectives, she came to believe student life also mattered.

She then voluntarily becomes festival committee representative despite limited attendance.

This gives us:

> **`ORDINARY_LIFE_RECOVERY`**

Definition:

> A highly visible professional can deliberately rebuild access to ordinary peer roles that fame, schedule, or self-prioritization had allowed to atrophy.

The event does not treat ordinary school belonging as inferior to professional achievement.

## 6.5 Fame can itself create social distance

Kotono's problem is not straightforward rejection. Classmates appear to become more formal and cautious around her after prolonged absence and rising professional visibility.

Admit:

> **`FAME_DISTANCE`**

Professional recognition can reduce ordinary relational ease even when peers are positively disposed toward the performer.

The answer is not renouncing fame. It is renewed participation and reciprocal approach.

## 6.6 Nagisa preserves school participation alongside idol work

Nagisa explains that she continues committee work precisely because her professional schedule reduces how often she can attend school. She wants the limited school time she has to remain substantive.

This supports `TIME_BOUNDED_LIFE_DOMAIN` and shows that the school-life claim is not unique to Yu.

## 6.7 Kotono's relationship to Mana's school legacy is neither avoidance nor repetition

Mana's school-festival live has become a school legend.

Kotono initially rejects the idea of adding a live because of practical preparation and permission constraints. Once classmates want it and the logistics become possible, she chooses to perform there with Nagisa.

Her motivation includes standing on the same school-festival stage Mana once occupied, but she explicitly imagines that she and Nagisa can create something that equals or surpasses it.

This is another instance of:

> **inheritance as invitation, not jurisdiction.**

Kotono does not erase Mana, imitate Mana, or refuse the space because Mana once occupied it. She re-enters it with a different relationship and a different present.

## 6.8 Yu's skills migrate across roles

The festival musical draws on multiple parts of Yu's life:

- her acting experience from `ぷりてぃー★エンジェル`;
- her doujin/creative imagination;
- her fandom literacy;
- her idol performance experience;
- her student-council role;
- her concern that every student should be able to feel like the protagonist of school life.

This produces an important clarification of plural-role authorship:

> Different roles need not remain sealed compartments. Skills and values can migrate among them without one role swallowing the others.

Admit:

> **`CROSS_ROLE_FERTILIZATION`**

# 7. Cross-event synthesis

## 7.1 The self is not a one-job constitution

E2-A3 adds a new scale to `ANSWERABLE_CONSTITUTIONALISM`.

Earlier tranches asked whether:

- units can revise themselves;
- centers can be contested;
- managers can intervene without ownership;
- public persona can be constructed without becoming dispossession.

E2-A3 asks whether a **person's own life constitution** can contain several legitimate domains.

The answer is yes, but not because all roles are equally important at every moment.

The answer is:

> **No role possesses automatic permanent supremacy.**

Yu may be:

- idol;
- doujin creator;
- fan;
- daughter;
- occasional actor;
- student;
- student-council president;
- scriptwriter.

Kotono may be:

- professional idol;
- sister carrying Mana's memory;
- student;
- classmate;
- festival committee member;
- Nagisa's intimate peer.

Hoshimi idols may be:

- performers;
- representatives;
- song-development participants;
- costume-development participants;
- leaders;
- rivals;
- institutional collaborators.

Plurality is not a dilution of seriousness. It is a protection against total capture by a single identity.

## 7.2 Authenticity becomes control over boundaries, not maximal exposure

Across E1-C, E2-A2, and E2-A3, the current authenticity model now has four negative boundaries.

Authenticity is **not**:

1. an unconstructed public self;
2. purity from influence;
3. preservation of a fixed inherited style;
4. total disclosure of every private identity or practice.

The emerging positive model is:

> **Authenticity is authored control over how a person relates to inheritance, influence, craft, collaborators, privacy, and public representation.**

## 7.3 Family inheritance is legitimate when it expands options rather than narrowing them

Yu's parents offer two very different forms of inherited support:

- her father co-creates doujin work with her;
- her mother provides elite acting knowledge.

Neither parent demands that Yu become a copy of them or convert the inherited resource into a particular career.

This is one of the cleanest family models in the corpus so far:

> **Good inheritance enlarges the child's authored possibility space.**

## 7.4 Hoshimi's mature institutional form is procedural as well as relational

`Stars Journey` significantly strengthens the existing Hoshimi model.

The agency's legitimacy does not rest only on warmth or trust. It also rests on practical mechanisms:

- delegation;
- representation;
- budget compromise;
- cross-unit coordination;
- distributed expertise;
- local publicity;
- creative participation;
- logistical planning.

The institution becomes more convincing precisely because it cannot do everything through Makino's personal care.

## 7.5 Plurality produces a workload problem

E2-A3 refuses an easy celebration of “having it all.”

Yu's two major stories both place meaningful additional work beside idol work:

- doujin production;
- student-council leadership and festival creation.

In both cases, the risk is overextension.

The current rule should therefore be:

> **Plural-role authorship is ethically valuable only when practical workload remains contestable.**

This is an E2-A3 refinement of the earlier body/workload ethics.

# 9. Open-register effects before historical comparison

## `OPEN-03` — Makino intervention threshold

**ADVANCED, remains open.**

Yu's acting crisis demonstrates a legitimate case where Makino is not the developmental center. He is explicitly pleased that Yu opened a route using her mother, director, Rui, and Sumire.

Implication:

> Good management includes knowing when not to monopolize the help network.
