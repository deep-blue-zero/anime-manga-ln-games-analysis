# 1. Tranche-level finding — reciprocity without unilateral accommodation

E2-B2 initially looks like a secondary-material tranche: two limited-unit competitions, a Sunny Peace national tour, and a TRINITYAiLE variety special. Read prospectively, however, the four stories form a strong test of the existing `ANSWERABLE_ROLE_CONSTITUTION` model.

The repeated failure mode is not dependence itself. It is **unilateral accommodation**: one person silently reorganizes herself around another person, assumes that care means yielding, or treats protection as something done *to* a less-authoritative recipient.

The repeated healthier alternative is:

> **INTERPRETATION — Mature interdependence requires reciprocal standing: people must be able to support one another without converting support into self-erasure, paternal ownership, or an obligation for the recipient to remain passive.**

Preferred umbrella term:

> `RECIPROCAL_STANDING`

Definition:

> A relationship or group structure in which asymmetries of age, skill, role, fame, or vulnerability can remain real while all affected parties retain standing to assert wants, contest methods, contribute care, and alter the shared form.

E2-B2 therefore adds an important qualification to the series thesis of **answerable interdependence**:

> **Interdependence is answerable only when help can move in more than one direction and when the recipient of care remains a co-author of what the care is for.**

This does not require perfect symmetry. The stories repeatedly preserve real asymmetries while denying those asymmetries total jurisdiction.

# 2. `心紡ぎ合う輝きの競演` — sisterhood without permanent supporting roles

## 2.1 The limited unit exposes latent roles hidden by the permanent units

Chisa and Saki enter the two-person tournament from stable five-person groups where neither normally acts as the principal directional force. Chisa identifies Rei as Sunny Peace's strong driver; Saki says Kotono usually fills that role in Tsuki no Tempest, while she often balances the group.

When the sisters form a two-person unit, both initially perform the same relational habit: each steps back to make the other more visible. The result is literal mutual disappearance. At one rehearsal beat they both retreat simultaneously, producing exactly the opposite of what either intended.

The competitors later diagnose the problem accurately enough to wound Saki:

> `譲り合ってどっちも目立ってない`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** their mutual deference lowers the unit's stage impact despite both members intending to help.

Preferred term:

> `RECIPROCAL_SELF_ERASURE`

Definition:

> A cooperative failure in which multiple participants each reduce their own claim to space for the other's sake, causing the shared form to lose rather than gain expressive force.

This differs from coercive suppression. No one orders either sister to disappear. The problem is a mutually reinforced model of benevolence.

## 2.2 Saki's elder-sister role has become too jurisdictional

Saki escalates the mistake after overhearing criticism. She decides that the unit should be built around Chisa and repeatedly distorts her own movement so Chisa can stand forward.

When Chisa finally confronts her, Saki states the core premise directly:

> `私は……お姉ちゃんだから`
>
> `あなたを、支えないと……`

The statement is loving, but its logic is too total. Saki treats “older sister” as an office whose proper fulfillment requires permanent unilateral support.

Chisa's answer is decisive:

> `私だって……いつまでもお姉ちゃんに支えられなくても`
>
> `一人でも大丈夫だから`
>
> `私にも……お姉ちゃんを支えさせて？`

Preferred terms:

- `SIBLING_ROLE_DETOTALIZATION`
- `RECIPROCAL_SISTER_SUPPORT`

Current rule:

> **Being an older sister can create reasons for care without assigning permanent supporting-character status.**

This strengthens the corpus-wide principle that relational roles are legitimate when they remain answerable rather than becoming ownership structures.

## 2.3 Chisa does not ask Saki to become less caring; she asks Saki to want something

Chisa's strongest intervention is not “stop helping me.” It is:

> `お姉ちゃんが本当にやりたいことはなんなの？`

She explicitly describes Saki as `自分を殺してる` and recalls the version of her sister who visibly loved performing before institutional balance roles became habitual.

This elicits one of Saki's clearest ambition statements in the corpus:

> `私だって、本当は……月ストのセンターをやりたい`
>
> `このユニットでも……もっともっと目立ちたい！`

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Saki explicitly wants Tsuki no Tempest's center and wants greater visibility in the limited unit.

This does not newly establish that Tsuki center is institutionally contestable; that is already governed by the later E1-E center evidence. What E2-B2 adds is an earlier and psychologically direct statement that Saki's balancing/support competence is **not identical to her preferred permanent identity**.

Preferred term:

> `SUPPORT_COMPETENCE_WITHOUT_SUPPORT_DESTINY`

## 2.4 Healthy sisterhood becomes assertive rather than less affectionate

After the confrontation, the sisters invert their practice. Instead of competing to yield, they compete to shine. They tell each other to step forward, move bigger, and claim more attention.

Chisa narrates the change explicitly: they now say everything they think, even though she once feared such exchanges would damage the relationship. Instead, she finds them enjoyable.

Nagisa initially mistakes the new directness for a fight. Saki and Chisa answer that it is serious discussion, not relational breakdown.

Preferred terms:

- `ASSERTIVE_MUTUALITY`
- `NON_ZERO_SUM_SIBLING_AMBITION`
- `CONFLICT_TOLERANT_AFFECTION`

The final performance confirms the form: both sisters become more visible and more forceful without ceasing to care for each other.

Governing rule:

> **Affection is not proven by yielding the spotlight; a durable relationship may be better expressed by giving the other person permission to compete with you.**

## 2.5 Mana/Kotono memory functions as an anti-regret catalyst, not a model to copy literally

Nagisa helps Chisa act by recounting Kotono's regret that she cannot now complain to, change alongside, or show growth to Mana. The relevant lesson Chisa takes is to speak while mutual revision is still possible.

This is not evidence that the Shiraishi sisters should reproduce Mana/Kotono's relationship. It is evidence for:

> `REVISION_WHILE_RELATIONSHIP_IS_LIVE`

A living relationship retains the possibility of reciprocal correction that memorial relation cannot fully reproduce.

# 3. `迷走ピリオド 涼やかな青春` — co-authorship versus optimizing the other person

## 3.1 Mei and Suzu begin with a misleading sense of perfect compatibility

Mei and Suzu describe themselves as an unusually close pair, are publicly told they resemble twins, and confidently enter the limited-unit tournament as a “strongest combination.” Their closeness is real; the problem is that both assume shared affection implies shared interpretation.

Their first concept word is `かっこいい`, but they attach different practical meanings to it.

Suzu imagines a hard, high-difficulty style designed to maximize Mei's known strengths. Mei imagines a “cool-cute” hybrid that combines what is recognizably good about both of them and remains enjoyable for fans to imitate.

Preferred term:

> `SHARED_LABEL_DIVERGENT_MEANING`

Closeness reduces neither the need for explicit interpretation nor the possibility of substantive disagreement.

## 3.2 Suzu turns care into unilateral optimization

Suzu's hidden premise is that Mei is technically stronger, so Suzu should design the unit around drawing out Mei's maximum potential and then work harder to keep up.

She later states it directly:

> `芽衣の方がダンスの技術が高いと思っていますの`
>
> `私が芽衣のレベルに合わせて頑張ればいいだけですわ！`

Mei immediately identifies the cost:

> `それだと芽衣ばっかりやりやすくなっちゃわない！？`
>
> `すずにゃんばっかり頑張ることになっちゃう！`

Preferred term:

> `UNILATERAL_OTHER_OPTIMIZATION`

Definition:

> A benevolent design strategy in which one participant treats the other's maximum expression as the shared goal and silently assigns herself the burden of adaptation.

The problem is not that Mei's strengths should be ignored. It is that Suzu makes Mei's excellence the organizing principle without first asking what **their** unit is trying to become.

## 3.3 Jealousy reveals an unacknowledged claim to authorship

Suzu's costume and choreography proposals are repeatedly passed over in favor of Mei's. She experiences this not only as artistic disagreement but as a threat to the self-image that she should be the competent person carrying the partnership.

She eventually accuses Mei of holding back and treating her as incapable:

> `私を……成宮すずを、舐めないでくださいまし！！`

The accusation is wrong as a description of Mei's intent. But it reveals a real need Suzu has failed to voice: she wants her labor, taste, and capacity to have constitutive standing in the unit.

Preferred term:

> `JEALOUSY_AS_AUTHORSHIP_SIGNAL`

This is not a claim that jealousy is intrinsically healthy. Here it becomes diagnostically useful because it exposes an unspoken demand for reciprocal recognition.

## 3.4 Mei's kindness has an accumulation cost

Mei initially tries to defuse Suzu rather than answer her directly. A remembered conversation with Mana becomes the turning point.

Mana recounts that after she once collapsed from overwork, Makino angrily confronted her when she still intended to perform two weeks later:

> `いいかげんにしろ！　長瀬麻奈は一人で歌ってるとでも思ってるのか？！`

Mana's retrospective lesson is that a person who is habitually kind and nonconfrontational can still accumulate unspoken frustration. She tells Mei that if Mei's chest ever becomes full, she hopes Mei finds someone to whom she can genuinely direct those feelings.

This yields two distinct prospective findings.

First:

> `KINDNESS_ACCUMULATION_RISK`

Habitual accommodation can conceal real emotional load from both the recipient and the accommodating person.

Second, the Mana anecdote materially advances `OPEN-03`:

> **STRONG INFERENCE — After an actual overwork collapse, forceful managerial confrontation can be compatible with care because the risk is no longer speculative and the performer's decision has consequences for a wider support network.**

This does not establish every legitimate intervention threshold or show exactly what Makino subsequently ordered Mana to do. It does provide one clear high-risk boundary case.

## 3.5 The relationship becomes more secure through conflict, not avoidance

Mei deliberately returns early to “fight” with Suzu. The conflict initially expands into ridiculous but genuine grievances, then reaches the relational core.

Mei says:

> `全部ひっくるめて……すずにゃんのこと、大好きなんだ`

Suzu admits how lonely the first separate walk home felt, and both confess that they had feared being burdensome or childish to the other.

Preferred terms:

- `TRUSTED_CONFLICT_CAPACITY`
- `RECIPROCAL_VULNERABILITY_DISCLOSURE`

Governing rule:

> **A relationship is not necessarily most secure when conflict is absent; security may be demonstrated by the ability to survive explicit disagreement without converting disagreement into abandonment.**

This is distinct from IIIX's `TRUST_WITHOUT_FRIENDSHIP_LABEL`: Mei and Suzu openly use affectionate language and do not reject friendship/intimacy categories.

## 3.6 The finished unit is synthesis, not compromise by subtraction

Once Suzu and Mei actually exchange their models, the unit becomes `かっこ可愛い`: not a midpoint produced by each surrendering half her idea, but a new form built from both members' strengths, tastes, humor, and shared Mana memory.

They co-write lyrics containing both personal motifs and `shiny`, which they explicitly connect to someone both love.

Preferred terms:

- `COAUTHORED_UNIT_SYNTHESIS`
- `MUTUALITY_OVER_OPTIMIZATION`

Governing rule:

> **A two-person unit becomes genuinely shared when neither member is merely the optimized object of the other's care.**

# 4. `笑顔のSUNNY 繋げるPEACE` — audience reciprocity at scale

## 4.1 BIG4 status expands responsibility rather than terminating development

Sunny Peace begins its first national tour after becoming BIG4. Fan letters from distant regions make visible a new problem of scale: many supporters want to experience the unit live, but geography has limited access.

The group chooses `繋ぐ` as the tour concept: connecting past and future, cities, idols and fans, dreams and reality.

This is not merely promotional wording. The DVD project operationalizes the concept by using fans' own letters to decide whom the group will visit and what dreams they will help realize.

Preferred term:

> `RECIPROCAL_AUDIENCE_COAUTHORSHIP`

The audience is not a sovereign author of Sunny Peace, but fan desires materially shape what the tour becomes.

## 4.2 Shizuku explicitly describes reciprocity rather than one-way service

Shizuku proposes the project because fans have already helped fulfill many of her own dreams since she became an idol. The group will now help fulfill some of theirs.

The relationship therefore cannot be modeled simply as performer → consumer benefit.

Preferred term:

> `CIRCULATING_DREAM_RECIPROCITY`

Definition:

> Performer and audience repeatedly become conditions of possibility for one another's future projects without collapsing into equal institutional authority.

This is compatible with, but more materially concrete than, earlier `INVITED_AUDIENCE_COAUTHORSHIP` language.

## 4.3 Fame has externalities the idols themselves do not fully perceive

Across the tour, Sunny Peace repeatedly discovers that its public scale exceeds its own intuition. Their appearance produces crowds, local media attention, tourism, “holy-site” behavior, business surges, and logistical disruption.

Rei eventually says:

> `サニピの人気を一番実感出来てなかったのは`
>
> `案外、私達自身だったのかもしれないわね`

Preferred term:

> `FAME_SCALE_EXTERNALITY`

This cuts in two directions:

1. their presence can generate real economic, cultural, and motivational value;
2. the same attention can inconvenience hospitals, public spaces, businesses, and ordinary operations.

The story acknowledges the second point when the group notes that crowds sometimes caused trouble. It does not deeply litigate responsibility for those externalities, so that remains an open synthesis question rather than a resolved ethics model.

## 4.4 Haruko becomes an explicit intergenerational aspirational figure

At the Nagoya talent school, a student explains that she became an idol aspirant because she admired Haruko and asks whether Haruko will perform with her if she succeeds.

Haruko promises she will.

**TEXTUAL FACT:** Haruko is not merely tolerated as an older/returning idol; she is explicitly the model through which a younger person imagines becoming an idol.

Preferred term:

> `INTERGENERATIONAL_IDOL_MODELING`

This materially advances `OPEN-14` by showing present aspirational legitimacy. It does **not** resolve Haruko's long-horizon career economics or how long she will remain active.

## 4.5 Sakura's promise is vocational commitment, not evidence of permanent destiny

A hospitalized fan tells Sakura that Sunny Peace's songs repeatedly gave her courage during illness and asks Sakura to keep singing.

Sakura replies:

> `約束するよ。これからも、みんなの力になれるように`
>
> `精一杯歌い続けるね`

This is strong evidence of present future-oriented commitment. It advances `OPEN-01`, but it must not be expanded into a claim that Sakura has surrendered all future authorship or promised lifelong idol activity in one fixed institutional form.

Preferred reading:

> `PRESENTLY_AUTHORED_CONTINUATION_COMMITMENT`

## 4.6 Audience impact becomes generative rather than merely consumptive

The tour's aftermath shows repeated secondary effects:

- local spaces become destinations for fans;
- a talent-school student hopes to enter Hoshimi;
- a hospitalized fan plans to become an idol herself after recovery;
- fans use Sunny Peace songs as courage for difficult life events;
- fan experiences become new stories circulated back to the unit through letters and social media.

Preferred term:

> `GENERATIVE_AUDIENCE_CIRCULATION`

The strongest end-state is Shizuku's:

> `私……アイドルになって、良かった……`
>
> `みんなと繋がれて、良かった……`

The tour thereby reinforces that professional value is partly relational and cannot be reduced to rank, revenue, or score, while still taking place inside a highly successful professional institution.

# 5. `迎春！翼に込める躍進の一念` — developmental asymmetry without subordinate standing

## 5.1 Sumire's problem is not that Rui and Yu deny equality

At the outset, Rui and Yu warn Sumire against excessive practice. Their reasons are professionally serious: recovery, injury prevention, long-term performance quality, and the fact that Sumire is still in a growth period.

Sumire internally acknowledges that they are worried because they see her as a real teammate. Her anxiety is nevertheless:

> `子供扱いされちゃってるのかな？`

This creates a distinction that the corpus needs:

> `CARE_RECEPTION_GAP`

Definition:

> Benevolent intent and egalitarian self-understanding do not guarantee that protective behavior will be experienced by the recipient as equal-standing care.

The relevant ethical question is therefore not only **what the caregiver means**, but **what status relation the recipient reasonably experiences**.

## 5.2 Equal standing does not require pretending developmental asymmetry is unreal

Rui explicitly notes that Sumire is a middle-school student in a period of physical growth and that an injury now could have lasting consequences. The story does not refute that claim.

What it rejects is the inference that age should make Sumire a permanently subordinate member.

Preferred terms:

- `DEVELOPMENTAL_ASYMMETRY_WITHOUT_STATUS_SUBORDINATION`
- `AGE_AWARE_EQUAL_STANDING`

Current rule:

> **Equal membership can coexist with age-specific safeguards. Equality does not require identical risk assumptions; safeguards become paternalistic when they erase the younger member's standing to understand, contest, and author the goal.**

## 5.3 Sumire chooses the hardest challenge partly to prove standing

Sumire volunteers for the long-distance marathon partly because she believes finishing it will make Rui and Yu stop treating her like a child.

This means the challenge has a mixed motive:

- genuine ambition and excitement;
- a desire to demonstrate competence;
- a status-repair motive produced by perceived paternalism.

Preferred term:

> `STATUS_PROVING_RISK_TAKING`

The story celebrates Sumire's determination, but the motive should not be flattened into pure self-development. Her need to prove equal standing is part of why she chooses the most physically demanding option.

## 5.4 Rui and Yu explicitly revise their model of care

After Sumire becomes lost, falls, and slows, Yu considers asking production to stop her. Rui refuses—not because she no longer cares about safety, but because she realizes why Sumire chose the challenge and believes unilateral rescue at that point would erase its meaning.

Rui also admits:

> `日頃から心配し過ぎることもあったと思うし`

Yu replies that although they naturally regard Sumire as equal, they do not know how Sumire has received their behavior.

This is a rare explicit self-audit of paternalistic risk inside a caring relationship.

Preferred term:

> `REFLEXIVE_CARE_REVISION`

## 5.5 Makino models accompaniment rather than goal seizure

Makino does not extract Sumire from the challenge. He appears on an absurdly decorated bicycle, checks whether she has been injured, runs alongside her, and reminds her that Rui and Yu are waiting.

Sumire later states that his accompaniment encouraged her.

Preferred term:

> `ACCOMPANIMENT_WITHOUT_GOAL_SEIZURE`

This is not nonintervention. It is active support that leaves the authored endpoint intact.

## 5.6 E2-B2 sharpens `OPEN-03` through proportional intervention

Taken together with the Mana recollection in `event_2023_010`, this tranche supplies two different risk states:

1. **Mana:** actual prior collapse from overwork, followed by intent to perform soon afterward; Makino responds with forceful confrontation.
2. **Sumire:** trained challenge with production/security infrastructure; she gets lost and suffers a minor fall but remains mobile and communicative; Rui/Yu wait, while Makino checks and accompanies rather than removing her.

The prospective framework is:

> `PROPORTIONAL_CARE_INTERVENTION`

Definition:

> The legitimate intensity of protective intervention should rise with credible risk, impairment, and loss of decision capacity rather than with caregiver anxiety alone.

This strongly advances `OPEN-03`, but does **not** fully resolve exact thresholds. The text does not provide a general decision rule for every medical or safety scenario.

## 5.7 Rui's failed glider challenge separates outcome ranking from meaningful success

Rui's fan-built glider loses a wing and fails to set the hoped-for distance record. Rui is disappointed, but she also says the effort left her with satisfaction because the team prepared together and she truly flew.

After Sumire wins the viewer vote, Rui adds that the concrete influence and bonds created by her own challenge are more valuable to her than first place:

> `そういう確かな影響力とか絆は`
>
> `一位って成績以上にとても嬉しいことだもの`

Preferred term:

> `MEANINGFUL_SUCCESS_BEYOND_RANK`

This strongly advances `OPEN-07`: rankings remain real and motivational, but they are not sovereign measures of value.

## 5.8 Sumire's successful return is an equality claim inside interdependence

At the finish, Sumire says:

> `私だって、トリエルの……一人だもん……`

and returns with `ただいま` to Rui and Yu's `おかえり`.

Her equality claim is therefore not “I need nobody.” It is “I belong fully enough that you can trust me to leave, struggle, and come back.”

Preferred term:

> `RETURN_CAPABLE_EQUALITY`

The airport epilogue then comically reverses the care direction: Sumire worries about Rui and Yu's ordinary-life incompetence and declares that she will stay with them.

Preferred term:

> `RECIPROCAL_CARE_REVERSIBILITY`

This is a key distinction from age-blind equality: the older members still possess legitimate age/experience-specific protective concerns, while the youngest member is also capable of meaningful care and practical authority in other domains.

# 6. Cross-event synthesis

## 6.1 Limited units are diagnostic laboratories for hidden permanent-unit roles

Both limited-unit stories are valuable because temporary two-person structures remove the normal distribution of labor.

- Saki and Chisa discover that both habitually occupy supportive/balancing positions.
- Suzu discovers that she has silently defined herself as the person who must optimize around Mei.
- Mei reveals organizational competence learned through earlier collaboration with Rei.

Preferred term:

> `LIMITED_UNIT_ROLE_DIAGNOSTIC`

A temporary formation can reveal a person's latent ambitions, habits, and asymmetries that a stable unit efficiently absorbs.

## 6.2 Care fails when it pre-decides the recipient's good

Across the tranche:

- Saki decides Chisa should be the one to shine.
- Suzu decides Mei's maximum technical expression should govern the unit.
- Rui and Yu risk deciding what Sumire should be protected from.
- Sunny Peace's fan project works precisely because it begins with fans' articulated dreams rather than inventing generic benevolence for them.

The shared principle is:

> **INTERPRETATION — Care becomes answerable when the person being helped retains standing to define what the help is for.**

Preferred term:

> `RECIPIENT_AUTHORED_CARE`

This does not eliminate expertise. Rui can know more about injury risk; Suzu can correctly identify Mei's dance strengths; Saki can genuinely support Chisa. Expertise becomes overreach when it converts a partial truth into unilateral authorship of the recipient's good.

## 6.3 Reciprocity is not symmetry

E2-B2 repeatedly refuses a simplistic equality model.

- Saki remains Chisa's older sister.
- Mei and Suzu have different technical strengths.
- Sunny Peace and its fans do not have equal institutional authority.
- Sumire really is younger and physiologically still developing.

Yet none of those asymmetries grants total jurisdiction.

Preferred formulation:

> **`RECIPROCAL_STANDING` is compatible with unequal expertise, age, visibility, responsibility, and institutional power so long as those asymmetries remain answerable and do not erase the less-powerful party's authorship.**

## 6.4 Conflict can be a form of care when the relationship can answer it

Chisa's confrontation with Saki and Mei's deliberate fight with Suzu both improve the relationship because conflict ends benevolent guesswork.

This strengthens, but does not universalize, the E2-B1 insight that secure relationships can survive explicit contestation.

Preferred term:

> `CORRECTIVE_CONFLICT`

Definition:

> Conflict that restores mutual intelligibility by forcing unspoken assumptions, wants, and burdens into answerable relation.

It should not be generalized into a claim that harsh confrontation is inherently developmental.
