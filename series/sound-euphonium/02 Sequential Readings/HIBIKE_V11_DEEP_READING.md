## 2. Executive thesis — the mediator enters the conflict

The final movement changes the geometry of the series. Earlier Kumiko could often mediate because the conflict belonged primarily to other people. By V11 the institution she must legitimate directly evaluates her own playing, threatens her desired soli beside Reina, and introduces a transfer whose excellence is exactly what Kitauji claims to reward. The resulting problem is not hypocrisy and not simply jealousy. It is **epistemic self-conflict under role entanglement**.

> **V11 asks whether a leader can preserve procedural, epistemic, social, and adaptive legitimacy when she is also a personally invested competitor whose own worth appears to be measured by the same opaque procedure.**

Kumiko can sincerely believe the better player should win, help Kanade become better, insist that Mayu must be allowed to participate, and still want the result to be herself. She can understand that social preference for the beloved president is illegitimate as a musical criterion while also fearing that Taki may have used exactly that preference when he selects her. The deepest threat is therefore not Mayu alone. It is Kumiko's declining confidence that she can tell where principle ends and self-interest begins.

The volume's movement is:

> **distributed leadership and inherited trust -> intensified success culture -> developmental/care externalities -> Mayu as merit-and-belonging stressor -> first-audition legitimacy -> private epistemic destabilization -> unresolved final-movement pressure**

V11 is Part One. Its proper endpoint is not resolution but a set of conflicts that can no longer be avoided.

## 3. Source-first mechanism map

### Institutional architecture

#### Team Oumae is genuinely distributed

The officer notebook and early chapter make role separation explicit. Kumiko wants president/vice-president to handle human-relational support so Reina can focus on musical instruction. Shuuichi handles logistics and often acts as soft social counterweight. Reina redesigns basic training and beginner practice. This is an institutional answer to the Asuka/Yuuko overconcentration problem, not merely three friends holding titles. `HIBIKE-V11 / S02 / P0002-P0051`

Yet role separation does not make externalities disappear. Reina's severe music-first instruction still creates relational consequences that Kumiko must absorb. By S03, Kumiko explicitly interprets solving those human consequences without limiting Reina as part of why the president role exists. `HIBIKE-V11 / S03 / P0685-P0702`

This exposes a structural risk: **an institution can formally separate musical and relational authority while still allowing one authority's method to generate labor for the other.**

#### Reproductive success produces pressure

Kitauji begins with 71 returning members and recruits 31 first-years, reaching 103. Prestige changes the incoming cohort and raises expectations. It also creates material constraints: instruments are finite; family ability to purchase instruments matters; ensemble size and instrument inventory shape who can play what. `S02 / P0681-P0707`

By V11 the institutional problem is no longer persuading people to take band seriously. It is preventing seriousness from hardening into a culture where optional extra effort becomes expected and where failure feels socially impermissible.

Kumiko explicitly warns members not to turn voluntary overtime into obligation and says that thinking 'I push myself, therefore others should too' is a dangerous sign. Anxiety may motivate work, but directing it at others is wrong. `S03 / P0703-P0727`

#### Four-layer legitimacy now collides with charisma history

Mirei articulates the generational legitimacy gap after the first audition. Third-years lived through Taki transforming a weak club into a national-level one; to them he has near-sacred authority. Younger students entered an already-strong Kitauji and cannot be expected to absolutize him in the same way. `S04 / P0819-P0821`

This is a major refinement of the V08-V09 legitimacy model. Procedural legitimacy is not enough, and even successful historical legitimacy decays across cohorts unless repeatedly demonstrated. The founder's achievement can become inherited mythology to people who did not witness it.

Kumiko is vulnerable to exactly that inherited trust. She tells Mirei that Taki's judgment is probably more correct than theirs and predicts the result will make sense later, then later privately asks herself whether she is simply doing whatever Taki says. `S04 / P0806-P0858`

### Kumiko @ V11

#### President as relational cartographer

Kumiko tracks the 103-member organization through section-leader reports: prior experience, social relationships, newcomer status, and emerging problems. `S02 / P0866-P0875` This is the mature form of the Oumae Consultation Office: relational information becomes leadership infrastructure.

But the same strength creates over-responsibility. Shuuichi notices her physical exhaustion; Kumiko knows intellectually that she cannot control every member's outcome but still struggles to leave known distress alone. `S03 / P0728-P0769`

#### First-year transmission

Kumiko tells Sally that she is certain high school life at Kitauji will be fun—explicitly the thing first-year Kumiko once wished someone had told her. `S02 / P0547-P0557` This is transmission through remembered lack: she tries to give newcomers the reassurance she did not receive.

#### Threat response to Mayu begins bodily before policy conflict

Mayu's clean Seira-derived sound and A-member history are enough to trigger Kumiko's bodily unease before any formal competition. When Reina calls Mayu immediate combat strength, Kumiko feels fear/unease. When Mayu later plays a simple scale beautifully, Kumiko recognizes `真由は上手い` and experiences `焦燥`. `S02 / P0794-P0803`; `P0960-P0963`

This matters because her later concerns cannot be modeled as purely abstract fairness philosophy. Personal threat is present early and somatically.

#### Kumiko wants procedural merit and also wants its result to be herself

Kumiko believes the better player should get the soli and tells Kanade that everyone should become as good as possible. She corrects Kanade's playing despite direct competition: `奏ちゃんは敵じゃないでしょ。みんな、少しでも上手いほうがいい`. `S04 / P0546-P0558`

At the same time, she privately wants to know whether she or Mayu is better. `S04 / P0279-P0292` She hears first-years hoping she wins because it would preserve social cohesion and is shaken by the existence of that preference. `S04 / P0577-P0593`

V11 therefore does not expose hypocrisy so much as a harder leadership condition: **a person can sincerely endorse a fair rule and still desperately prefer a personally favorable result.** Simulation must preserve both states simultaneously.

#### Kumiko's epistemic discipline degrades when personally implicated

Kumiko has learned to label uncertainty in other people's conflicts, but with Mayu/Taki she begins to seek certainty she cannot obtain. After Taki selects her for the first soli, she wonders whether he chose her for musical reasons or because choosing the president preserves club unity. She has an opportunity to ask and does not. `S04 / P0834-P0862`

This is a major self-application gap: she would tell another person to distinguish evidence from inference, but cannot fully do so when her own worth is being evaluated.

#### Leadership resemblance to Asuka is partial and dangerous

When questioning Kaho at Sally's temple, Hazuki comments that Kumiko sounded a little like Asuka. Kumiko consciously relaxes. `S03 / P0885-P0896` Later Kumiko explicitly recalls Asuka as a model for what to say to Sally. `S03 / P0956-P0976`

The source does not say Kumiko is 'becoming Asuka.' It shows that Asuka has become part of Kumiko's learned leadership repertoire—including both effective recognition and the risk of treating human interaction as a problem to solve.

#### Future remains underauthored

Kumiko has no clear post-graduation path and partly uses club busyness to avoid deciding. Taki tells her adulthood is not a magical transformation but an extension of childhood self under changed environment. `S03 / P0596-P0617`

Her father, changed after Mamiko's conflict, says he may disagree with an unstable path but will not coerce her; he also warns that dedication to band cannot become an excuse not to face the future. `S04 / P0640-P0668`

Michie gives the most structurally useful advice: real jobs reveal themselves after entry; if positive desire is unclear, reduce intolerable negatives; avoid turning indecision into a reason not to move. She observes that Kumiko appears interested in people and routes her toward humanities/social/education options. `S04 / P0886-P0928`

The final-movement future problem is therefore **authorship under incomplete desire**, not finding one hidden vocation already waiting inside Kumiko.

### Reina @ V11

#### Music-first severity remains principled but socially costly

Reina is consistent: A members should be the best current players; beginners do not receive exemption from musical requirements; `気持ちはいいから、結果で見せて`; no one gets a pass merely for inexperience. `S03 / P0459-P0484`

Sally's critique is equally sincere: Reina is `正しさの塊`, and forcing every one of 103 members toward a national-level standard may impose costs on students who are not yet contest-relevant. `S03 / P0941-P0968`

The novel does not settle this by declaring one side morally correct. Reina's method produces rapid improvement; it also produces visible crying and peer-care labor. The final movement must therefore distinguish **technical efficacy from total institutional optimality**.

#### Reina expects alignment from Kumiko

When explaining how competition raises standards, Reina asks/assumes Kumiko agrees. The narration notices the expectation. `S02 / P0370-P0380` This is a relationship-specific risk: Reina's trust in Kumiko can become an assumption of normative alignment.

#### Reina's Taki trust is near-absolute

She says Taki should be the person judging her because only an ear she absolutely trusts would make rejection acceptable. `S03 / P1060-P1077`; `S04 / P0030-P0038` She explicitly says Taki cannot make a judgment error in the audition discussion.

This is not simply admiration. It is an epistemic outsourcing mechanism: Taki's judgment is accepted as the standard capable of resolving painful musical uncertainty. V11 increasingly places Kumiko outside that certainty.

#### Ordinary Reina survives the high-stakes return

V10 calibration holds. Reina can joke with Kumiko and Shuuichi, laugh at old '麗奈先輩マジ天使' language, invite Kumiko to her house, reveal her mother knows extensive details about Kumiko, and express fear that after graduation Kumiko may not want continued contact. `S04 / P0363-P0482`

The key direct statement is relationally vulnerable: friendship is not under Reina's unilateral control. Even if she wants to meet, future Kumiko may not. Kumiko answers that the pleasure of hearing this fear outweighs her own anxiety. `S04 / P0459-P0475`

The dyad remains explicitly `特別` to both at the festival-night boundary. `S04 / P0477-P0482`

#### Musical-partner desire remains strong

Reina wants the third-movement soli and practices it with Kumiko. When she learns Kumiko practiced it with Mayu, she immediately insists Kumiko practice it with her too and becomes annoyed when Kumiko teases her about jealousy. `S04 / P0423-P0444`

This is evidence for selective possessiveness around a shared musical role, not proof of formal romance.

### Shuuichi @ V11

Shuuichi remains a genuine separate pillar of Team Oumae rather than merely Kumiko's ex-boyfriend. He handles attendance/logistics, offers lower-pressure leadership, and argues for enjoyment and social sustainability. His ordinary softness is institutionally useful but can become avoidance of necessary correction; Reina calls him out as the '仏の副部長'. `S04 / P0002-P0009`; `S06 / P0031-P0037`

The epilogue gives his own focalization. He intentionally keeps calling Kumiko `部長`; Reina reads this as stubborn distancing and tells him not to flee from responsibility merely because the romantic relationship is suspended. `S06 / P0031-P0037`

Shuuichi is not emotionally indifferent. Reina's `久美子のこと、泣かさんといてや` triggers the unspoken response that he is the one who wants to cry. `S06 / P0048-P0053`

V11 therefore freezes: **romantic suspension does not remove attachment; Shuuichi manages the attachment partly through formal-role distance.**

### Mayu @ V11

#### Adaptability is learned biography, not empty personality

Mayu has moved repeatedly across regions due to her parents' work and speaks standard Japanese because her parents do. She can perform dialect playfully on request. `S02 / P0183-P0197` Her social ease should therefore be modeled as adaptation competence shaped by repeated transfer, not as proof she has no stable self.

#### She wants environments where liking music remains possible

Mayu says she chose the strongest available school not primarily because of contest status but because ensemble is more enjoyable when everyone is skilled: `好きなものを好きでいられる環境を選ぼうって思って`. `S02 / P0809-P0833`

This is an important positive value. She is not simply trying to avoid conflict.

#### Self-removal is her conflict strategy

Mayu repeatedly asks whether she can decline audition/competition opportunities because a transfer taking a long-standing Kitauji student's place seems socially wrong to her. `S04 / P0083-P0106`; `P0560-P0568`

She says this was not how she behaved at Seira, where effort was socially rewarded. That means the behavior is context-sensitive, not a universal lack of ambition.

Her central defense appears to be: **if my competence creates conflict, reduce my claim rather than demand the institution absorb the conflict.**

This is not pure altruism. It also protects Mayu from becoming the hated outsider and from having to test whether others would still accept her after she defeats them.

#### Social openings are difficult to classify

Mayu is consistently warm, practically helpful (including sewing a junior's torn uniform), skilled, and willing to initiate invitations. Yet Kumiko and Kanade perceive something slippery about her. Midori's jellyfish metaphor—beautifully drifting but capable of a sting—is a character interpretation, not fact. `S03 / P0546-P0560`

V2 must not prematurely collapse Mayu into 'mask' or 'manipulator.' The key open question is whether her low-claim social style is deliberate self-protection, deeply habituated adaptation, or both.

#### She enjoys music sincerely

When Mayu and Kumiko trade the soli lines, Mayu's visible joy is direct focalized observation; she says simply `楽しかったね`. `S04 / P0277-P0290` Whatever defensive social strategy exists, the music itself is not portrayed as false.

### Kanade @ V11

V08-V10 calibration survives. Kanade's threat-detection activates strongly around Mayu. She refuses Mayu's invitation to first-name intimacy and maintains `黒江先輩`, despite normally liking skilled players. `S03 / P0240-P0250`

Her polite sharpness is not random hostility. Mayu's willingness to self-remove from auditions directly collides with Kanade's defended-merit history. Kanade interprets the stance as insulting to people who genuinely fight for selection and uses irony/keigo to expose the contradiction. `S04 / P0560-P0568`; `P0765-P0775`

Kanade also visibly reacts to Mayu's playing: at SunFes practice she says Mayu is good while her fingers tremble on her own euphonium. `S03 / P1110-P1113` This gives an embodied threat response rather than mere verbal antagonism.

### Sally / Sari @ V11

Sally is a major newcomer model, not simply a minor first-year.

She is earnest, experienced, socially responsible, and strongly identified with being serious: `私、真面目なところだけが取り柄`. `S03 / P0155-P0180`

Her vulnerability is high empathic identification plus inflated responsibility. She feels physical distress watching Kaho be scolded and experiences Reina as `正しさの塊`. She accepts Reina's technical logic yet asks whether all 103 people truly need the same strictness when only 55 enter A. `S03 / P0941-P0954`

She has recruited and retained peers and consequently interprets their suffering as her responsibility. Suzume later explains that Sally often believes unrelated trouble is her fault, has high pride and responds strongly to authority recognition. `S03 / P1024-P1038`

Kumiko's intervention succeeds because it names Sally's unrecognized labor: thanks for keeping all 103 present, and direct assertion `サリーちゃんがいれば、北宇治はもっとよくなる`. `S03 / P0960-P0976`

This extends recognition-before-correction: Sally does not first need proof Reina is wrong. She needs credible evidence from legitimate authority that her own care labor has value.

But Suzume also reveals Kumiko was deliberately maneuvered into giving that recognition. `S03 / P1024-P1038` This does not make the help false; it shows first-years can actively manipulate institutional channels to obtain needed care.

### Suzume, Kaho, Yayoi and Tsubame

Suzume is strategically social beneath comic presentation. She stages urgency around Sally to pull senior authority into the problem, openly admits she only wanted to help her friend, and articulates a preference for people whose strengths and flaws make them uneven rather than perfect. `S03 / P1014-P1041`

Her advocacy for Tsubame can become paternalistic. She assumes Tsubame's color-guard role may be exploitation and positions herself as sister's defender. Tsubame directly says it is her own choice because in her final year she wants to try things she previously avoided due to dislike of attention. `S03 / P0318-P0383`

The durable rule is: **support that bypasses the target's stated preference can become control even when motivated by love.**

Tsubame's V10 skill-vector result is strengthened. Her mallet talent is publicly recognized and later earns the marimba solo. The narration explicitly notes that talent may never bloom if nobody values/discovers it and credits Junna's leadership. `S02 / P0655-P0668`; `S04 / P0730-P0764`

Kaho begins as a fragile beginner visibly affected by Reina's strictness but continues improving. Mayu supplies practical/reassuring care after harsh rehearsal. Kaho is not yet A, but V11 repeatedly refuses to treat B status as stable identity.

Yayoi is physically capable, expressive, comic, and comfortable receiving practical help. Her torn uniform scene with Mayu supplies ordinary-care evidence rather than crisis characterization. `S03 / P1158-P1175`

### Mirei and the first-audition legitimacy fracture

The most important first-audition result is not the euphonium soli. It is Taki selecting beginner Suzume over more technically developed Satsuki for A.

Mirei privately challenges Kumiko because, by her observed technical criteria, Satsuki is better. She takes care not to publicize the objection because doing so would harm both Satsuki and Suzume and destabilize the institution. `S04 / P0781-P0821`

Taki later explains his criterion: Suzume's large, beautiful sound is more valuable to the current ensemble configuration than Satsuki's stronger general technique; high-range limitations can be worked around in this repertoire. `S04 / P0834-P0851`

This is a powerful anti-scalar musical model and a fairness lesson: **selection is not rank ordering of total musician worth; it is contextual optimization for a particular ensemble/repertoire.**

Mirei's deeper point survives even after Taki's reasoning proves coherent: younger cohorts do not owe the same prior trust as the third-years. The institution must make its reasoning legible enough to sustain credibility.

### Motomu @ V11

V09's epistemic discipline is rewarded. V11 finally supplies direct disclosure: Genichiro is Motomu's grandfather. `S03 / P1257-P1269`

Kumiko also sees Taki and Genichiro in friendly conversation and experiences a new suspicion that Motomu's selection may have been influenced by relationship. She suppresses the suspicion as shameful. `S03 / P1271-P1278`

The key modeling point is not whether the suspicion is true. It is that **hidden relational information can contaminate perceived procedural legitimacy even without evidence of actual favoritism.**

Midori's earlier privacy-respecting choice remains important: she had correctly understood that forcing disclosure would create exactly this kind of burden.

### Competition architecture and effort

#### Repeated auditions change incentive ecology

Team Oumae chooses to re-audition all members and solos before each competition. Reina argues that one early selection causes tension to fade; she wants current best membership at every stage. `S03 / P1043-P1078`; `S04 / P0011-P0038`

This implements adaptive legitimacy but also increases chronic threat. Mirei later describes the year as running while a lion chases from behind. `S04 / P0819-P0821`

The institution is therefore making a conscious trade: **greater responsiveness to improvement in exchange for higher continuous evaluative pressure.**

#### The better player is not context-free

Taki's Suzume/Satsuki explanation and the ensemble-contest precedent establish that 'best' means best fit to a role/configuration, not maximal individual technique. `S04 / P0843-P0851`

This should govern later Mayu/Kumiko claims. If either is selected for soli, the result is evidence about Taki's judgment for that musical role at that date—not a metaphysical ranking of them as complete musicians or people.

#### V11 first audition outcome

A euphoniums: Kumiko, Mayu, Kanade. Soli: Reina + Kumiko. `S04 / P0696-P0744`

Tsubame receives marimba solo. Hazuki finally earns A after two years in B. Suzume earns A while Satsuki falls to B. These results deliberately distribute emotional outcomes so 'merit' cannot be reduced to one moral narrative.

#### Kyoto result

V11 ends the main chapter at the opening of the performance, then the officer notebook confirms Kitauji receives gold and representative status, advancing with Ryuusei and Rikka. `S05 / P0004-P0011`

The volume does not resolve the Mayu/Kumiko problem. The repeated-audition system guarantees the question reopens before Kansai.

### Relationship state stress tests

#### Kumiko-Reina

Still mutually special and highly intimate. Reina's mother says Reina talks about Kumiko constantly; Kumiko is the first friend Reina has brought home. `S04 / P0383-P0408`

Reina admits future friendship frightens her because continued relation depends on Kumiko also wanting it. `S04 / P0459-P0475`

They agree their current relation is special. `S04 / P0477-P0482`

Musically, Reina clearly wants Kumiko as soli partner but maintains the doctrine that the better euphonist should win. That combination will become central when personal and musical specialness diverge.

#### Kumiko-Shuuichi

Publicly and functionally their best label is childhood friends / president-vice-president after romantic suspension. Kumiko notices Shuuichi has grown and wonders about his feelings but suppresses the question. `S02 / P0018`; `S02 / P0588-P0589`

Shuuichi uses formal-role naming (`部長`) as emotional distancing; Reina identifies it as avoidance. `S06 / P0031-P0037`

The relationship remains affectively live even without active dating.

#### Team Oumae

The trio's growing direct dyads matter. Kumiko notices mild exclusion when Reina and Shuuichi share leadership rapport without routing through her. `S03 / P0427`

A distributed leadership team should produce autonomous member-member ties. That is institutionally healthy even if it triggers small attachment responses in Kumiko.

### Negative constraints after V11 source-first pass

Do not model:

- Mayu as simply manipulative, passive, or ambitionless; her music enjoyment and prior Seira behavior contradict that.
- Kumiko as neutrally fair when her own musical role is threatened; she sincerely endorses fairness but is personally invested.
- Reina's technical correctness as proof her method is socially costless.
- Sally as merely fragile; she is already major peer infrastructure for first-years.
- Suzume as merely comic; she deliberately maneuvers senior authority to help Sally.
- Taki's selections as scalar musician rankings; repertoire/ensemble fit is explicit.
- third-year trust in Taki as automatically transferable to younger cohorts.
- Shuuichi's breakup as emotional indifference.
- Mizore/Nozomi or V10 models as directly central to V11; the final movement shifts pressure toward leadership, Mayu, and institutional legitimacy.

## 4. V10 backtest: which ordinary-state models survive high stakes?

V10 was valuable precisely because it prevented crisis behavior from becoming a total personality. V11 gives the first real held-out test.

### Kumiko

The warm, slightly rambling social Kumiko survives. She can still tease, soften embarrassment, enjoy ordinary class friendship, and speak without sounding like a formal president. But high stakes activate a different layer: continuous relational scanning, strategic concern about faction formation, bodily threat responses to Mayu, and a willingness to think explicitly about containing rebellion. The correct model is therefore not `ordinary Kumiko` versus `leader Kumiko` as two selves. Leadership adds a task-conditioned control layer over the same sensitivity to atmosphere that existed in V01.

### Reina

V10 proved that private Reina can be playful, vulnerable, and afraid of losing relationships. V11 does not erase that. The same person who tells beginners that feelings do not substitute for results also hesitates before the future because she cannot guarantee that Kumiko will keep choosing her. A reconstruction that uses only the rehearsal register will make her too severe; one that uses only the private register will make her institutionally implausible.

### Kanade

V10 showed calculated presentation can be authentic play rather than pure false-self behavior. V11 preserves that insight. Kanade's politeness around Mayu is both genuine social competence and an instrument of hostile testing. The fact that she can enjoy performance does not mean every performance is benign.

### Tsubame

V10's vector model of skill survives strongly. Once somebody has recognized her mallet capacity, V11 gives that capacity institutional consequence: a marimba solo. The prior B/full-band status was never a complete statement about her musicianship.

### Shuuichi

V10's ordinary gentleness survives, but the epilogue shows the cost of using gentleness as avoidance. Formal-role address lets him function beside Kumiko without reopening the suspended romance; Reina correctly sees that the same strategy can become emotional retreat.

### Modeling consequence

V11 therefore supports a general rule for the later character monographs:

> **state transitions are conditional activations, not replacements. High-stakes behavior should be predicted from ordinary baseline + activated threat/role mechanism, not from a crisis-only personality label.**

## 5. The prologue: success has become environment

The prologue begins from Taki rather than Kumiko. The important shift is ecological. Kitauji's concert band is no longer the embarrassing club whose seriousness requires explanation. Its success is institutionally visible enough that school staff assume another active year and offer ordinary support.

That normalization is itself a victory and a danger. Once exceptional success becomes environmental background, newcomers inherit an expectation whose founding struggle they did not witness. Third-years remember the weak club and Taki's transformation of it. First-years encounter a prestigious organization that already expects national gold. The same institutional fact therefore arrives with different moral histories.

The prologue's `音を楽しめ！` slogan matters for that reason. Result orientation is real, but the institution still claims musical enjoyment as a public value. The final movement will repeatedly test whether the methods used to preserve excellence remain compatible with that value for beginners, B members, leaders, and students whose ambitions differ.

The final prologue sentence broadens the subject beyond Kumiko. This is the story of the Kitauji concert band. V11 then makes that claim concrete by showing that Kumiko cannot control every person, every motive, or every consequence even though she is president.

## 6. Team Oumae: distributed authority and transferred externalities

The officer notebook opens with a deliberate institutional design. President and vice-president will absorb human-relational support; the drum major should be able to concentrate on musical instruction. This is the cleanest formal answer yet to the series' earlier overconcentration failures.

The design works. Reina can build demanding basic training. Shuuichi can handle logistics and continuity. Kumiko can gather section reports and mediate. Yet V11 refuses the fantasy that organizational charts eliminate interdependence. Reina's strictness generates crying, fear, and peer-care labor. Kumiko then interprets absorbing those consequences as part of the president's job.

That yields a new institutional distinction:

> **role separation can distribute authority without distributing externalities.**

One officer's locally optimal behavior can create work for another officer. The later full-series synthesis should therefore evaluate leadership not only by role clarity but by **externality accounting**: who bears the social, emotional, administrative, and musical costs produced by another role's policy?

Shuuichi also matters here because he is not merely the neutral middle. His softness can preserve livability, but Reina correctly identifies that excessive kindness may offload difficult confrontation onto Kumiko. Team Oumae is strongest when the three principles check one another rather than when one person silently absorbs the imbalance.

## 7. The 103-member band: success creates a new moral economy

Kitauji's scale is materially important. Instrument inventory, family purchasing power, room availability, A/B capacity, and administrative attention constrain what the institution can promise. Prestige has attracted enough members that not everyone can have the same performance opportunity even if everyone works seriously.

This changes the moral economy of effort. In V01 the urgent problem was insufficient seriousness. In V11 the dangerous slogan becomes the opposite: *because I can push harder, everyone should push harder*. Kumiko explicitly rejects that conversion of voluntary effort into social debt.

The point is not anti-excellence. Kitauji still votes for national gold. The point is that a high-performance culture can cease to distinguish:

- chosen extra work from expected extra work;
- challenge from humiliation;
- A-member needs from all-member developmental needs;
- and anxiety that motivates the self from anxiety projected onto others.

Sally's plot makes that distinction visible from below. She is not arguing that Reina's corrections are musically false. She is asking whether technical correctness alone determines how a 103-person institution should teach every member.

---

## 8. Sally and Reina: correctness has an implementation cost

Sally is a particularly important test because she does not oppose excellence out of indifference. She is an experienced clarinetist, socially responsible, and intensely serious. Her phrase that seriousness is her only strength is not self-description as weakness; it tells us how much of her identity is invested in being the person who does the right thing.

Reina frightens her partly because Reina is so often correct. `正しさの塊` captures the problem: a technically justified demand is difficult to resist without feeling dishonest or weak. Sally's distress arises when that correctness is applied across members whose contest relevance, experience, and tolerance differ.

Her argument that making a zero positive can be enjoyable while being angrily ordered to drag a negative to zero is not a rejection of improvement. It is a developmental-pedagogy distinction. Learning can be framed as possibility or as deficit correction. The same exercise can produce different motivation depending on which frame dominates.

Kumiko succeeds with Sally through the V08 recognition sequence. She does not first prove Reina wrong. She identifies what Sally thinks nobody sees: Sally has helped keep the newcomers present. `サリーちゃんがいれば、北宇治はもっとよくなる` gives her care labor institutional value.

But Suzume has engineered the encounter. This is analytically useful. The younger cohort is not a passive population waiting for benevolent senior care. They can learn the institutional routes, recognize which authority voice another person needs, and strategically summon it.

---

## 9. Suzume and Tsubame: loving protection can still overwrite agency

Suzume's comic register makes her easy to underestimate. V11 should end that mistake. She is socially tactical, knows Sally's pride pattern, and can manipulate urgency to pull Kumiko into a problem.

Her weakness appears in the opposite direction with Tsubame. Suzume loves her sister and sees herself as a protector. Because Tsubame historically disliked attention, Suzume initially treats the color-guard move as something that may have been done *to* her. Tsubame says otherwise. In her final year, she wants to try the thing she once avoided.

This establishes one of the final movement's cleanest care constraints:

> **a correct historical model of someone's vulnerability does not authorize overriding her present stated preference.**

Protective prediction must update when the target changes.

Tsubame's later marimba selection reinforces the same lesson from the musical side. Other people can recognize a capacity she did not know how to measure, but recognition should increase her range of choices rather than become another reason to decide for her.

---

## 10. Mayu Kuroe: self-minimization as conflict strategy

Mayu enters with nearly ideal credentials for the institution and nearly ideal characteristics for destabilizing its social story. She is a third-year transfer; she has Seira A-member experience; her sound is immediately strong; she carries a silver euphonium that triggers Asuka associations in Kumiko; and she has no accumulated claim on Kitauji history.

Her threat is intensified by the fact that she does not behave like an invader. She repeatedly suggests that established members should not be displaced for her sake. This is where the V2 model must avoid two symmetrical errors.

The first error is to take every self-effacing statement at face value and conclude she has no ambition. She chose a strong school because she prefers playing in a strong ensemble, competed successfully at Seira, enjoys trading soli lines, and auditions when required. Music matters.

The second error is to assume that because self-effacement has social effects, it must be calculated manipulation. Repeatedly offering to withdraw can pressure everyone else into reassuring her, but effect is not proof of intent. Her history of relocation makes low-claim entry a plausible learned safety strategy even when no conscious scheme exists.

The stable V11 mechanism is therefore:

> **when her competence threatens social cohesion, Mayu tends to reduce her own claim before asking the institution to absorb the conflict.**

This differs from Kanade's V08 self-sabotage while belonging to the same family of social-threat management. Kanade eventually learned that false evidence deprives others of agency. That history is why Mayu's rhetoric irritates her so intensely.

Mayu's photography extends the model but does not solve it. She is comfortable recording other people's belonging and viscerally dislikes seeing herself in photographs. The source does not tell us why. Relocation-based self-alienation, observer identity, aversion to fixed self-image, and expectation of impermanence remain possible. The correct state is **OPEN**, not diagnosis.

Her `Mama` role similarly needs epistemic restraint. Practical caretaking is real. It may give her a portable social function. It does not yet prove that she helps people *in order to* avoid personal desire.

---

## 11. Kanade and Mayu: the ethics of honest evidence

Kanade's V08 crisis is indispensable background. She once believed that giving the institution honest performance evidence could make her socially hated, so she corrupted the evidence herself. Natsuki and Kumiko forced her out of that strategy.

Mayu now proposes a cleaner-looking version of the same moral problem: perhaps she should simply not claim the opportunity. Kanade hears the cost immediately. A senior who preemptively withdraws can appear generous while denying everyone else the ability to make a real choice about whether she belongs.

Kanade's `黒江先輩` register should therefore be modeled as active distance. It is neither simple jealousy nor meaningless etiquette. Politeness becomes a safe surface from which she can inspect the contradiction.

Her own body still reveals threat. Mayu's playing tightens her expression and produces tremor. The post-V08 Kanade is not cured of competition anxiety. She has a different behavioral rule for what to do with it.

---

## 12. Kumiko and Mayu: fairness becomes self-evaluation

Mayu matters because she changes what the word `fair` costs Kumiko.

Kumiko's doctrine remains genuine. She tells Kanade that they are not enemies and corrects a rival because everyone being better helps the ensemble. She rejects the idea that Mayu should withdraw. These are not public lies contradicted by a secretly selfish interior.

The interior instead adds another truth: Kumiko wants to know who is better, wants to keep the soli, wants to remain Reina's musical partner, and feels Mayu's sound as bodily threat.

The V11 conflict is therefore structurally different from Reina/Kaori in V01. Kumiko is not only the younger player asking whether merit defeats seniority. She is also the beloved senior, president, incumbent partner, and person with institutional history. She now occupies the socially protected position whose dangers she once saw from below.

The overheard first-year conversation is the decisive social mirror. The students can imagine Mayu playing better and still hope Kumiko wins because Kumiko is president and one of theirs. That is exactly the social preference Mayu has been predicting.

When Taki then selects Kumiko, the result cannot restore innocence. She cannot hear her own playing from outside, cannot fully hear the judge's weighting, knows her presidency has social value, and fails to ask Taki why he chose her even when the question is available.

This is the final movement's most important epistemic change:

> **Kumiko's problem is no longer merely uncertainty about other minds. It is uncertainty about her own motivated interpretation of evidence concerning herself.**

---

## 13. Repeated auditions: adaptive legitimacy becomes chronic precarity

The repeated-audition system is a major V1 correction at the level of agency. Mayu tells Kumiko that Seira re-auditions. She does **not** straightforwardly propose Kitauji adopt the system. Team Oumae, especially Reina, uses the information as comparative evidence and chooses a policy.

The policy has a defensible musical logic. June should not permanently decide August or October. Improvement should matter. A member who declines should be replaceable. The strongest current ensemble should play.

But adaptive legitimacy has a human price. If every stage can reopen selection, every successful member remains evaluatively exposed. Mirei's lion metaphor captures the motivational ecology: improvement is possible, but so is continual pursuit.

This is not a reason to condemn the policy in advance. It is a reason to model its trade precisely:

> **re-audition increases responsiveness to current capacity while decreasing positional security.**

The institution must therefore be judged not only by whether the policy is musically rational but by whether it can sustain trust, developmental motivation, and truthful performance under the resulting pressure.

---

## 14. Suzume over Satsuki: the end of scalar merit

The first audition supplies a cleaner philosophical test than Kumiko versus Mayu because the affected person is not the president.

Mirei believes Satsuki is technically stronger. Taki does not deny the observation. His explanation changes the metric. Suzume produces a large, attractive sound useful to the current low foundation; her high-range weakness can be routed around in the present repertoire. The selection is therefore not `Suzume is the superior tuba player in every respect`.

It is:

> **for this ensemble, repertoire, balance, and role at this date, Taki predicts Suzume creates the better total result.**

That makes V11's merit model explicitly vector-valued and context-dependent. The same principle must later govern any Mayu/Kumiko comparison. A soli result is evidence about a specific task under a judge's criteria, not a metaphysical ranking of complete musicians or people.

Mirei's dissent still matters after the explanation. A legitimate procedure cannot live forever on explanations only the founder generation trusts. Younger members need enough reason to update their own models.

---

## 15. Taki: authority, explanation, and inherited mythology

V11 is unusually careful about Taki because three different Taki objects coexist:

1. **Taki the actual conductor**, who gives contextual musical reasons and refuses to claim he alone can take students to nationals;
2. **Taki as remembered founder**, whose arrival transformed Kitauji for the current third-years;
3. **Taki as inherited authority**, encountered by younger members who know the achievement but not the lived process that produced trust.

Reina's position is closest to epistemic outsourcing: she wants judgment from an ear she trusts absolutely and says Taki cannot make an audition mistake. Mirei's position is generational skepticism. Kumiko occupies the unstable middle. She tells Mirei that Taki is probably more correct than they are, then privately wonders whether she is simply obeying him.

This is not yet a collapse of Taki legitimacy. His Suzume explanation is musically coherent and Kumiko can understand it. The more important point is that **a correct answer once given does not automatically justify every future opaque answer**.

The mature institutional requirement is therefore not blind trust or total transparency. It is a continuing ability to generate enough legible reasons that inherited authority remains revisable rather than sacred.

---

## 16. Motomu: privacy can be legitimate and still contaminate perception

V11 finally confirms the hidden relation that earlier volumes correctly left unforced: Genichiro Tsukinaga is Motomu's grandfather.

Nothing in the source establishes audition favoritism. The important event occurs in Kumiko. Once she sees Taki and Genichiro speaking comfortably, an earlier selection becomes newly suspicious to her. She immediately experiences shame for thinking it.

The series thereby refuses a simple privacy/transparency solution. Motomu's family history is not public property merely because he competes. Yet hidden relationships can alter how observers interpret opaque institutional decisions.

This is a general epistemic lesson for the project:

> **new relational information can rationally change perceived conflict-of-interest risk without becoming evidence that corruption actually occurred.**

Simulation models should therefore distinguish suspicion thresholds from belief thresholds.

---

## 17. Kumiko and Reina: relational specialness versus musical specialness

V11 gives some of the strongest explicit relational-specialness evidence in the prose sequence. Kumiko is the first friend Reina has brought home. Reina's mother knows her because Reina speaks about her often. Reina admits that future continuation frightens her because friendship is not something she can guarantee alone. They explicitly call the present relationship special.

That should not be collapsed into a formal romance claim the text does not make. It should also not be minimized merely because Kumiko/Shuuichi has a canonical romantic history.

The important final-movement problem is orthogonal: **does being relationally special entitle Kumiko to remain musically special?**

Reina's doctrine says no. The better euphonist should play. Her desire says she wants Kumiko as partner. V11 lets both coexist because Taki selects Kumiko. V12 will be the real stress test if the two lines diverge.

Reina's reaction to Kumiko practicing with Mayu is therefore best stored behaviorally: she notices, reacts, insists on practicing the same passage with Kumiko, and denies jealousy. A jealousy reading is strong, but motive should remain labeled inference rather than narrator fact.

---

## 18. Kumiko and Shuuichi: suspension is not erasure

The clean source itself supplies the safest current public label: childhood friends, plus president and vice-president. Their former romance is suspended.

Yet suspension has not produced emotional neutrality. Kumiko notices his growth and is still capable of bodily fluster. Shuuichi deliberately calls her `部長`, using office as a buffer. The epilogue then gives his internal perspective and establishes that the breakup remains painful.

This requires a revision to stronger V1 wording. `Kumiko's romantic attachment is still present` compresses too much. The current model should be:

> **romantic/affective residue remains behaviorally available, while the active relationship is suspended and Kumiko's present romantic intention is not yet clearly re-declared.**

Shuuichi's distance is also not simple passivity. It is a coping strategy that allows him to remain operationally close without violating the boundary Kumiko asked for. Reina correctly identifies the cost: role-respect can become emotional avoidance.

---

## 19. Voice, body, and predictive behavior

V11 supplies enough state-conditioned evidence to tighten several voice models.

**Kumiko:** public leadership speech remains hesitant, warm, and slightly rambling. Internal language can be much harder: strategic, suspicious, worried about faction formation. Under self-relevant audition threat, bodily cues precede explicit conceptual admission.

**Reina:** public music language is declarative, low-hedge, and outcome-focused. Private Kumiko language admits fear, uncertainty, humor, and selective possessiveness. Do not import one register into the other context.

**Mayu:** controlled standard Japanese, soft questions, self-minimizing offers, and rapid local accommodation. She rarely supplies a hard demand that forces the group to reject her. Under musical enjoyment, however, affect becomes visibly brighter and less defensive.

**Kanade:** senior-directed politeness remains sharp enough to become adversarial without losing grammatical courtesy. Threat produces both more analytical speech and involuntary bodily tension.

**Sally:** careful politeness becomes repetitive causal explanation when she believes the moral legitimacy of her concern may be doubted.

**Suzume:** comic Kansai speech can carry tactical manipulation. Exaggeration does not imply low perceptiveness.

**Mirei:** precise, formal dissent distinguishes affection from procedural judgment. She can oppose a result without attacking the selected student.

**Shuuichi:** casual, deflective speech and role-formal address are both avoidance-capable. His interior voice in the epilogue is more direct about hurt than his outward dialogue.

**Taki:** procedural explanation, contextual criteria, and questions rather than charismatic self-assertion. The reverent institution is often rhetorically more absolutist about him than he is about himself.

---

## 20. Simulation rules promoted after V11

V11 justifies several new variables for later monographs and scenario testing:

- **self-implication load:** how much a character's own status is affected by the rule she is judging;
- **motivated-interpretation risk:** probability that personal stakes distort how ambiguous evidence is read;
- **policy externality profile:** who absorbs the social/emotional cost of a technically effective policy;
- **authority inheritance gap:** difference between witnessed legitimacy and reputation-mediated legitimacy;
- **withdrawal-as-care strategy:** tendency to reduce one's claim when success may hurt cohesion;
- **competition-security profile:** response to a system where successful status remains reopenable;
- **musical-versus-relational specialness separation:** whether task selection is interpreted as proof of relationship value;
- **suspicion threshold versus belief threshold:** how hidden relationships affect perceived legitimacy before evidence of wrongdoing exists;
- **protective-model update rate:** whether a caregiver revises her model when the protected person's preferences change;
- **role-distance coping:** use of formal office/relationship labels to regulate unresolved attachment.

The negative constraints are equally important. Do not make Kumiko a neutral judge, Mayu a manipulator by default, Reina socially costless because she is technically right, Sally weak because she is distressed, Suzume naive because she is funny, Taki infallible because he is respected, or Shuuichi indifferent because he honors the breakup.

---

## 21. What V11 has not resolved

Part One should remain a state boundary rather than a backporting opportunity. The following remain open into V12:

1. whether Mayu's self-removal strategy changes after an actual high-cost win or loss against Kumiko;
2. whether Kumiko can preserve trust when a defensible Taki decision harms her personally;
3. whether Reina's merit doctrine survives conflict with her desire for Kumiko as musical partner;
4. whether Team Oumae can keep externalities distributed rather than silently loading them onto Kumiko;
5. whether younger-member trust in Taki increases through explanation or erodes through further opacity;
6. whether Kumiko can separate musical specialness from relational specialness under direct displacement;
7. whether Mayu's portable low-claim identity is flexible strategy, deeper self-structure, or both;
8. whether Sally can care without converting peers' distress into personal guilt;
9. whether Shuuichi can return from role-distance to direct emotional speech;
10. what future Kumiko authors when president ceases to be available as an identity scaffold.

The correct V11 ending is therefore **not** `Kumiko proves she deserves the soli`. It is: **the institution has created a repeatable procedure whose next legitimate result may force every major value conflict in the volume to become concrete.**
