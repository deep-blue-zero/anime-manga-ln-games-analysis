## 2. Executive local thesis

HIBIKE-V05 is a novel about **care that derives its emotional security from being needed, and therefore struggles to tolerate the cared-for person's independence**.

Azusa enters Rikka as almost the ideal strong-school recruit. She is experienced, technically advanced, intensely hardworking, socially fluent, good at teaching, ambitious without being openly abrasive, and unusually capable of reading the interpersonal dangers surrounding competitive selection. She wants to win. She wants to become better than Mirai. She wants to stand at the top without provoking the social retaliation that she has seen directed at talented younger players. She also wants to be useful. These wants are not separate.

Her crucial self-description comes when Shiho asks how she can help other people so readily:

> `うちはね、自分のためにみんなの手助けをしてんの`  
> `頼られてる自分が好きやねん。`
>
> — `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0305-P0306`

That admission is morally clarifying because it does **not** make Azusa's help false. She really does teach Amika patiently. She really does protect her, buy her food, monitor her shoes and posture, explain technique, and worry about her well-being. Her care produces genuine gains. Amika explicitly says that Rikka became lovable and survivable because Azusa was there. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0728-P0730`

The problem is that Azusa also needs the asymmetry to persist. When Amika fears that dependence has become dangerous, Azusa's private response is not relief that Amika wants greater autonomy. It is the opposite. The narration states that Azusa likes Amika's crying face, likes the hand that clings to her, and is saved by the fact that the weaker girl needs her; from Azusa's perspective, Amika therefore has no need to stand on her own. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0739-P0742`

The epilogue turns that hidden structure into an explicit threat. Amika chooses color guard precisely so that she can try something without Azusa carrying her. Her concluding reassurance—`もう梓ちゃんがいなくても大丈夫だよ`—is intended as evidence of growth. For Azusa, it lands as loss. `HIBIKE-V05 / S05 / P0023-P0037`

The volume therefore makes a distinction that will be indispensable for later relationship simulation:

> **Being genuinely helpful is not the same thing as wanting the other person to become less dependent on one's help.**

Azusa is capable of the first and, at this state boundary, not yet capable of the second.

This dyadic problem is nested inside a larger institutional one. Rikka is an already functioning elite institution. Unlike early Kitauji, it does not need an adult to create seriousness from apathy. Its students inherit seriousness as practice: senior-led instruction, explicit reporting norms, apprenticeship, student-designed marching formations, fixed bodily technique, mutable competitive rosters, and an enormous schedule of performances. Rikka reproduces itself because students do much of the reproducing.

But successful self-reproduction has costs. One hundred and three members compete for eighty marching positions and fifty-five A-division seats. The school normalizes replacement, intense physical labor, fear of being overtaken, and social management around excellence. Its strongest students become both models and threats. Rikka is consequently not a utopia contrasted against Kitauji. It is an institution in which **competence, trust, hierarchy, care, rivalry, and exhaustion are all mature at the same time**.

Azusa is the ideal focalizer for that world because she is both perfectly adapted to it and psychologically endangered by the very traits that make her successful there.

## 3. Structural architecture: forward motion and the fear of separation

The chapter titles use marching vocabulary to organize an arc about movement:

- **暴走フォワードマーチ** — forward motion without adequate regulation;
- **追憶トゥーザリア** — memory and rearward orientation inside a culture that demands forward progress;
- **緊張スライドステップ** — advancement under competitive and relational tension.

The prologue and epilogue frame the volume around two forms of attempted restart.

In S01, Kumiko tells Azusa she chose a high school with fewer old acquaintances because she wanted to `スタートしたかった`. `HIBIKE-V05 / S01 / P0014-P0017` Azusa interprets this as Kumiko moving forward. Yet the scene simultaneously reveals that Azusa herself is carrying an unresolved past: the remembered accusation `梓にとって、私は何？` intrudes at the prologue's end. `HIBIKE-V05 / S01 / P0030`

Amika later reveals a still more radical attempt at restart. Her cheerful high-school persona began as an imitation of a socially successful classmate because she hated the isolated middle-school self who hid in the corner of the classroom. What began as performance has become habitual enough that she no longer knows which self is “original.” `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0711-P0727`

The novel thus refuses a simple authenticity model. A new self can begin as performance and become lived character. An old self can survive beneath apparently successful forward motion. “Moving on” does not mean a stable authentic identity has finally been recovered.

The volume's deepest structural irony is that Azusa becomes more willing to move **forward competitively** while becoming more resistant to movement **inside attachment**. After jealousy from Shiori destabilizes her, Azusa dreams of a game of tag in which she slows down so that less capable pursuers can catch her. Dream-Reina tells her that if others cannot catch her, that is their problem; Reina runs at full speed. Azusa eventually decides to run forward barefoot despite the pain. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0857-P0879`

That dream resolves one problem: Azusa cannot spend her musical life making herself smaller so that other people never feel threatened.

But the epilogue immediately shows the unresolved mirror image. Azusa wants **Amika** to remain catchable, dependent, within the radius of Azusa's protective control. Her competitive self can accept separation from peers; her caregiving self cannot yet accept separation from the person who validates her usefulness.

This asymmetry is the best local reason to end Part One where the book does.

## 4. Azusa before Rikka: the socially edited high achiever

### 4.1 An independent witness confirms Kumiko's social-smile mechanism

The prologue supplies a valuable cross-character observation. When Azusa presses Kumiko for her real reason for avoiding Minami Uji, the narration says this is an old Kumiko habit: when someone tries to extract her true feelings, she smiles and evades. `HIBIKE-V05 / S01 / P0007`

This is not needed to establish Kumiko's mechanism—that was already done in V01—but it independently validates that the behavior is visible to an old friend. More importantly, it establishes Azusa as someone who notices such editing because **she does it herself**.

### 4.2 Social fluency is labor

Azusa talks easily, has many friends, remembers interpersonal information, and is quick to infer what social position someone occupies. Yet her ease is not equivalent to trust. When she is selected as the only first-year trombonist for A division, she automatically produces a modest explanation—essentially “I was lucky”—because she can already see the second-years who lost a seat to her. The narration explicitly says she is signaling that she harbors no hostility toward them. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0378-P0379`

The contrast with the Saijou twins is diagnostic. Kanon boasts, Mion openly complains, and neither seems to fear that the bond will collapse. Azusa recognizes the security underlying their rudeness: Kanon can say whatever she likes because she trusts Mion to keep accepting her. Azusa concludes that she cannot imitate them because she does not trust other people that much. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0383-P0393`

Her friendliness should therefore be modeled as both disposition and technique:

- she genuinely likes social contact;
- she hates silence and isolation;
- she enjoys knowing people;
- she also uses smooth interaction to reduce threat, jealousy, ambiguity, and the chance of rejection.

Serina names the defensive version brutally: `嫌われたくない病`. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0576-P0585`

### 4.3 Serina attacks the morality Azusa uses to understand herself

The Serina memory is powerful because Serina does not merely say “you are fake.” She proposes a much more threatening interpretation: Azusa approaches isolated people so that she can experience herself as kind and secure her own social position. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0560-P0579`

The charge partially overlaps with what Azusa later admits to Shiho—helping other people gives Azusa something she wants. But the novel does not simply declare Serina correct. Azusa's care is observably laborious and often costly. The more precise reading is that Serina identifies a **self-regarding reward embedded inside real prosocial behavior**.

Azusa can tolerate Shiho discovering that reward because Shiho receives the confession sympathetically. She cannot tolerate Serina weaponizing it as proof that her goodness is fraudulent. Serina threatens the coherence of the self-image through which Azusa regulates social life.

That is why Kumiko notices how unusual it is to hear Azusa say she hates someone. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0589-P0596`

### 4.4 The family home helps explain why usefulness feels stabilizing

Azusa's material life is not economically deprived. Her mother is a successful career woman whom she respects; the house and Azusa's instrument are products of her mother's income. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0312-P0314` The novel therefore explicitly prevents a simplistic “single-parent poverty” reading.

At the same time, the home is frequently empty. Azusa turns on television she does not care about because manufactured voices are useful as background against silence, and she habitually talks back even when alone. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0309-P0319`

Her father left when she was very young and is barely remembered. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0313` Her mother works very late. Azusa insists that because nothing material is missing she has no complaint—`不満なんて、あるはずがない`. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0314`

That sentence should remain epistemically marked. The text directly establishes the belief; it does not require the analyst to endorse the belief as a complete account of her emotional life. Her later reaction to Amika's intact, casually affectionate family provides contrast. She notices that a father inside the home is difficult for her to imagine. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0744-P0749`

The safest model is not “Azusa helps because her mother neglected her.” The stronger supported mechanism is narrower:

> Azusa has extensive experience being competent and self-managing in an often-empty domestic environment. A relationship in which someone unmistakably needs her gives her a form of interpersonal certainty that her ordinary home routine does not.

That remains analytical inference, not diagnosis.

## 5. Rikka/Tachibana as a mature self-reproducing institution

HIBIKE-V05's greatest world-building gain is that it gives the corpus a second full institutional system. Rikka must not be treated merely as “Kitauji but stronger.” It solves different organizational problems because it begins from a different state.

### 5.1 Recruitment works through visible inherited excellence

The novel opens on an eighty-person performance whose synchronized movement looks almost effortless. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0001-P0003` The effect is aspirational before it is disciplinary. Azusa has wanted to join this world for years. `Sing, Sing, Sing` is not only repertoire; it is institutional brand, recruitment device, and inherited ritual. Students enter Rikka specifically because they want to participate in it. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0238-P0246`

This matters because tradition is not simply imposed from the dead past. Kumada explicitly worries that repeating the same piece every year may be stale and asks the new cohort whether they still want it. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0611` The current students' desire reauthorizes the inheritance.

Rikka therefore supplies an important model of **participatory tradition**:

> tradition persists because successors actively want the identity it offers, not merely because seniors forbid change.

### 5.2 Students reproduce the institution through labor

Rikka's organizational maturity is visible in who does the work. Part leaders set schedules according to their section's needs, the advisor rarely intervenes in such management, and much beginner instruction is delegated to seniors. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0366-P0369`

The marching show itself is substantially designed by students. The marching-composition group creates formations and movement, then receives expert criticism from an outside marching instructor. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0645-P0666`

Leadership succession is likewise apprenticed. A future drum major is selected in the first-year autumn, spends the second year assisting, and becomes the principal drum major only as a third-year. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0393-P0397`

These practices make Rikka **institutionally transmissible**. Excellence is not stored only in one charismatic adult. It exists in roles, routines, documents, inherited clothing, repertoire, embodied steps, and senior-junior teaching chains.

### 5.3 Formal hierarchy and distributed agency coexist

Rikka is intensely hierarchical. First-years bow, answer loudly, defer to seniors, and inherit a quasi-athletic culture. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0048-P0068`, `P0144-P0149` Yet high student authority does not mean egalitarian informality. It means hierarchy itself has been made into a transmission mechanism.

The three senior executives differentiate leadership functions:

- **Shoko**, captain, emphasizes `報告、連絡、相談`, health, cohesion, and zero dropout. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0051`
- **Momoka**, vice-captain/color-guard leader, openly despises poor players who refuse to work and represents punitive effort norms. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0056-P0057`
- **Minami**, drum major, is the exacting marching authority whose terror comes from calm standards rather than screaming. She explicitly refuses to let “くだらない言い争いや個人の感情” consume time needed for the national-gold objective. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0060-P0067`; her narration later notes that she does not ordinarily raise her voice. `S02 / P0570`

A simulation of Rikka's culture should therefore not collapse “strict” into one affective style.

### 5.4 Selection is real, mutable, and function-specific

The marching contest allows eighty of the school's 103 members. Minami states that even a student who performs in one round can be replaced if a reserve becomes better; the objective is the best available group. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0062-P0064`

Concert-band selection uses a different authority system. Kumada personally conducts an audition for the fifty-five A-division seats. Seniority is not guaranteed; Azusa is selected over older students. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0235-P0280`, `P0336-P0367`

Even the audition is not mechanically identical for everyone. Kumada gives different players different tasks, including a solo and standing long tones for Azusa; Amika receives a much shorter test. Azusa immediately understands that discussing the test could leak likely evaluation to others. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0255-P0297`

The institutional conclusion is important:

> Rikka has a strong merit norm, but **merit is assessed through multiple delegated expert systems**, not through one universal transparent metric.

Marching seniors judge marching roles; Kumada judges concert seating; section leaders judge everyday progress; expert advisers critique student-designed formations.

### 5.5 The institution can be admired without becoming morally infallible

Azusa explicitly experiences the sudden silence that falls when Kumada enters as both evidence of excellent leadership and a little frightening. She knows she would follow Kumada almost anywhere, and precisely that willingness makes her aware of the danger of blind trust. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0413-P0421`

That moment prevents Rikka's high-functioning culture from becoming pure endorsement. The same system that produces excellence produces a disposition to follow.

Kumada herself complicates the competitive culture by saying awards are an “extra,” a byproduct of effort rather than the reason to march. Her desired product is a special experience that only Rikka can give: `立華やないと行けへん場所に、私がアンタらを連れてったるわ`. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0629-P0635`

The book does not resolve the tension between this philosophy and the institution's ruthless selection machinery. It lets both be true. The school can value formative experience while excluding students from particular stages.

## 6. Azusa: strength as both capability and defense

### 6.1 She does not lack ambition

Azusa's competitive drive is unambiguous. She wants nationals. She wants A division. She wants the solo. She regards Mirai as the target and, when Mirai is absent, assumes without hesitation that she should be the substitute soloist because she believes herself the second-best trombonist. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0809-P0824`

At the contest she admits internally that she does not merely want to beat one school; she wants to beat everyone present. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P1053-P1057`

But she usually hides that drive. This is not weak desire. It is **socially regulated ambition**.

### 6.2 Perfectionism is fear-driven

When a senior calls her hardworking, Azusa corrects the label. Her motive is not that she enjoys “being diligent” as a moral identity; she cannot forgive herself for failing to do what has been asked, wants to satisfy expectations perfectly, hates being inferior, and feels that if she relaxes she may be crushed by everything at once. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0672-P0677`

This predicts an important behavior pattern:

- externally: reliable, fast, cheerful, highly prepared;
- internally: difficulty distinguishing healthy effort from compulsory self-maintenance;
- under failure threat: increased work rather than withdrawal;
- under social threat: increased masking and tact rather than open confrontation, until a core identity is pierced.

Shiho supplies the complementary failure mode: fear of discovering one's limits can stop effort before the result exists. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0295-P0299` The two girls are not simply “strong” and “weak.” They use opposite defenses against the same evaluative danger.

### 6.3 Physical pain is a self-regulation tool

Azusa literally uses pain to restore emotional control. When she notices her face betraying irritation, she drives her nails into her palm and thinks of pain as one of her methods for managing herself. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0314-P0319`

This should not be generalized beyond the evidence into a pathology claim. For simulation purposes, the narrower rule is valuable: under intense affect, Azusa may convert emotion into **physical containment**—clenched fists, pressure, posture, controlled breathing, continued performance—rather than immediately verbalizing vulnerability.

### 6.4 She can receive competition more easily than care

Mirai and Shiori independently try to tell Azusa that apparently strong seniors are vulnerable and that Azusa herself is allowed to lean on others. Mirai explicitly says weakness is not bad, reliance is not bad, and Azusa seems poor at relying because she is always the one relied upon. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0226`

Azusa's immediate internal conclusion is essentially: *I need to become even stronger so they do not have to worry about me.* This is a precise model failure. She hears care and translates it back into competence.

Shiori tries a second route. She tells Azusa she does not hate her for surpassing older students and that Azusa is spending too much of herself on non-musical threat management. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0494-P0503` Azusa replies:

> `大丈夫ですよ、うちは。ほかの子らと違いますから`
>
> — `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0505`

The sentence is an identity claim: *I am the exception who does not need the protections other people need.*

That identity is exactly what the rest of the novel destabilizes.

## 7. Amika: from performed brightness to chosen autonomy

Amika initially appears almost maximally dependent. She is a rare beginner inside a strong school, takes sarcasm literally, asks Azusa what to practice, needs help with technique, and fears losing the person who makes the institution legible.

HIBIKE-V05 nevertheless gives her a more complex arc than “helpless beginner becomes competent.”

### 7.1 High-school Amika is deliberately constructed

Amika says she hated middle school, had no friends, made herself socially invisible, and was remembered chiefly for failure in a group athletic activity. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0711-P0722`

When her father was transferred, she treated relocation as a chance to change. She copied the behavior of a popular girl, deliberately built a cheerful character, and joined band partly because that model classmate had been in band. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0723`

Crucially, the performance does not remain fake. She says acting like the old self now feels less natural and she can no longer recover a stable memory of what the “real” self was. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0724-P0727`

This is an important identity principle for the whole corpus:

> **performed behavior can become genuine habit without becoming retroactively “fake.”**

### 7.2 Rikka makes achievement experientially available

Amika's middle-school life had little she could feel proud of. At Rikka, daily practice supplies explicit goals, visible improvement, and collective accomplishment. She says aiming hard at one thing is fun. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0379-P0389`

Her first successful `Sing` performance matters because the coordinated body gives her an empirical experience of change: she can do something difficult in synchronization with other people. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0599-P0606`; she later names the achievement directly when explaining why band came to matter. `S04 / P0728`

The institution is therefore not just stressful structure for Amika. It supplies a scaffold for self-efficacy.

### 7.3 Dependence becomes conscious before it becomes solved

Amika is not ignorant of the dependency problem. She reacts badly when Azusa moves to help Shiho and at one point fears Shiho will “take” Azusa. Later, Taichi and Shiho explicitly argue that Azusa's constant help will make it impossible for Amika to function without her. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0555-P0579`

Azusa rejects their concern because **Azusa herself does not plan to leave**. That is the crucial mismatch. For Azusa, permanent availability seems to solve dependency. For Amika, dependency is frightening precisely because another person's permanence can never be guaranteed.

At the river, Amika articulates the problem herself: she fears she cannot stand on her own, fears Azusa might someday disappear or tire of her, and fears she has nothing reciprocal to give. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0730-P0740`

The volume refuses to make this only a lesson for the dependent person. Amika already understands the danger before Azusa does.

### 7.4 Color guard is not simple rejection

The epilogue's color-guard choice should not be modeled as Amika ceasing to care about Azusa. She takes Azusa's hand before announcing it. Her stated objective is to try doing something herself. `HIBIKE-V05 / S05 / P0023-P0037`

Earlier, Azusa had encouraged experimenting with color guard in principle but immediately worried that outside trombone she could no longer protect Amika from a harsh senior. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0644-P0665` The epilogue activates precisely that latent fault line.

Amika's move is therefore an autonomy experiment **inside** attachment, not necessarily an attempt to terminate attachment.

Azusa is not yet capable of hearing it that way.

## 8. The Azusa–Amika dyad: care, control, reciprocity, and separation

This relationship should be modeled with unusually high asymmetry on the axis of **what each participant believes good care requires**.

### Azusa's model of care

Azusa's default model is substitutional and preventative:

- if Amika cannot do something, Azusa can teach her;
- if she still cannot do it, Azusa can continue helping;
- if a senior is dangerous, Azusa prefers keeping Amika inside a domain where Azusa has influence;
- if Amika fears being abandoned, Azusa offers indefinite availability.

The most revealing sentence is not verbal but narrated: Azusa thinks she can do things *instead* of Amika and that therefore Amika need not face difficulty alone. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0731-P0742`

### Amika's developing model of care

Amika starts by treating help as safety, then realizes that safety purchased through total reliance makes the relationship itself terrifying. She begins wanting a form of friendship that can survive **her competence**, not merely one guaranteed by her need.

The blue bracelet before the contest is a small but crucial reversal. Amika cannot take Azusa's place in A division, but she can make something, give it to her, understand uniform constraints, and symbolically accompany her. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0984-P1014`

That gift directly answers Amika's claim that she has “nothing” to give better than Azusa's reassurance does. She already contributes attachment, recognition, practical attention, and changed family life. Her parents thank Azusa because their daughter has become happier; Amika's relationship with Azusa also gives Azusa access to an affectionate family environment she finds novel. Reciprocity exists even before either girl has a good theory of it.

### The central risk

Azusa finds Amika safest when Amika poses no competitive or abandonment threat. The epilogue introduces both possibilities at once: Amika can grow, choose a different role, form additional dependencies, and become less controllable.

The relationship therefore cannot mature simply by Azusa “helping more.” Its unresolved question is whether Azusa can continue to want Amika when **being wanted no longer requires being indispensable**.

That is the Part-One relationship cliffhanger.

## 9. Shiho: threatened merit identity and the fear of testing one's limit

Togawa Shiho begins as one of the strongest demonstrations in the series that merit competition can injure people **without requiring unfair judging**.

She was the best player in middle school, a section leader and soloist, and arrived assuming she would remain among the elite at Rikka. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0271-P0276` The new environment threatens not just status but the story she tells about herself.

Her jealousy is unusually self-aware. She envies:

- Azusa's ability and social network;
- Taichi's aptitude despite what she sees as insufficient effort;
- Amika's ability to rely on others and be liked for vulnerability.

The humiliating discovery is not merely that other people are better. It is that **she herself is capable of pettiness**. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0260-P0276`

The deepest obstacle follows at `S03 / P0298`: Shiho knows effort is the only path forward but fears the verdict that would follow if she gave everything and still failed. Non-effort preserves a counterfactual self—*maybe I could have been good enough if I had really tried*. Full effort risks destroying that refuge.

Azusa's answer—`できるまで一緒にやろう`—works partly because it converts evaluation into shared process. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0299-P0307`

Shiho does not become universally warm after the conversation. That would violate the text. She apologizes to Amika for looking down on her and later tries to respect her without claiming sudden affection. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0312-P0327` This is stronger character writing than a friendship purification scene:

> **ethical cooperation does not require liking everyone.**

Shiori later gives Shiho another form of recognition by validating her stable, thick low register and the importance of bass trombone. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0436-P0451` The move reroutes excellence away from the most visible chair toward structural value without pretending competition no longer matters.

By S04, Shiho is already able to argue against Taichi's nihilistic reasoning: practice can matter for future capability even when immediate A-division selection looks unlikely. Her shift is from “effort is a test of worth” toward “effort is a process that can create future options.”

## 10. Taichi: natural aptitude, avoidance, and peer-level correction

Matoba Taichi is easy to flatten into the lazy talented boy. V05 is more precise.

He is physically adept, picks up movement quickly, and can calculate where effort seems unlikely to change immediate outcomes. His recurrent temptation is **optimization away from discomfort**: if he probably will not make A division, why overinvest now?

Mirai recognizes this and challenges him directly: his ability lets him avoid confronting difficulty, and that avoidance will limit him. The contrast with Shiho is productive. Shiho can work intensely but fears a definitive failure; Taichi can succeed easily enough to avoid discovering what happens when talent is insufficient.

His most important relational action is confronting Azusa over Amika. He argues not that Azusa's help is malicious but that Amika needs alternative teachers and Azusa has obligations created by her own A-division selection. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0555-P0577`

The argument makes him briefly more developmentally perceptive than Azusa. His exact insight is systemic: a resilient group cannot allow one beginner's learning to depend on one overfunctioning peer.

This is also Rikka's institutional logic entering friendship. Expertise must be distributed if people are to grow.

## 11. Mirai and Shiori: the human cost of becoming the standard

### 11.1 Mirai — chased after being the chaser

Sezaki Mirai entered Rikka as a complete beginner and became its best trombonist. Her story prevents elite ability from appearing as static endowment. She remembers the exhilaration of improving fast, passing others, and eventually overtaking Shiori, who had taught her from the beginning.

Then the direction of fear reverses. By V05 Mirai is the person everyone wants to beat, and Azusa's effort makes Mirai understand `他人の努力って、こんなに怖いんやな`—how frightening another person's effort can be. Her early-morning disclosure explicitly admits that strong seniors have weak places and that relying on others is not shameful. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0191-P0227`

This produces one of V05's most important competition principles:

> **Success does not end insecurity; it can transform insecurity from fear of never arriving into fear of being overtaken.**

Mirai nevertheless continues helping Azusa. She invites her to practice together, treats her as a legitimate competitive successor, and physically celebrates with her after the school advances. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0924-P0932`, `P1088-P1095`

Recognition and threat can coexist.

### 11.2 Shiori — admiration contaminated by the wish to replace

Takagi Shiori is still good enough for A division, yet she experiences Mirai as the person she wants to become. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0443-P0468` She can love Mirai, have taught her, be proud of her, and still want the solo she now owns.

Azusa's response reveals their difference. Shiori says she wants to become Mirai; Azusa says she wants to remain herself and **beat** Mirai. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0462-P0484`

After Azusa substitutes for Mirai's solo in practice, Shiori warns her not to treat the role as automatically hers. She acknowledges Azusa is second-best while still exposing the jealousy of seniors who also want the part. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0843-P0855`

The following day, Shiori returns the forgotten tuner and deliberately says `明日、頑張ろ`, explaining that she wanted to say it in her own voice. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0949-P0964`

That is not proof the jealousy disappeared. It is evidence that Shiori can **act ethically without purifying the emotion first**. V05 repeatedly prefers this model of adulthood: recognize an ugly feeling, then choose what to do with it.

## 12. Serina: the unresolved counter-relationship

Hiiragi Serina remains intentionally unresolved at the end of Part One.

Her middle-school relationship with Azusa begins when Azusa approaches a socially isolated girl out of habitual friendliness. Serina refuses the normal social bargain. She interrogates the motive, calls Azusa a liar, and argues that Azusa's friendliness is partly a strategy for maintaining favorable social position. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0560-P0585`

Azusa experiences Serina's refusal of surface accommodation as unusually invasive. She characterizes people like Serina as demanding deep connection rather than accepting polite social distance. That makes Serina almost the inverse of Azusa's preferred social world.

When they meet again on the train, Serina's external presentation has changed drastically, but her attention to Azusa remains intense. She is polite to Amika, physically manipulates distance with Azusa, and says Azusa has not changed. The prologue's remembered `梓にとって、私は何？` gives this relation an unresolved question of category and priority. `HIBIKE-V05 / S01 / P0030`; `S03 / P0688 onward`

V05 does **not** yet settle:

- what exactly occurred between them after the middle-school confrontation;
- what Serina wanted Azusa to name;
- whether Azusa's “cold war” description is shared by Serina;
- how much resentment, attachment, rivalry, or hurt each side carries.

Those must remain **OPEN into V06**. Simulation should therefore not invent a clean romance/friendship/enmity label from Part One alone.

## 13. Voice and register baseline

HIBIKE-V05 is exceptionally valuable for Japanese voice modeling because its protagonist is both talkative and highly context-sensitive.

### 13.1 Azusa

Azusa's ordinary speech is energetic Kansai Japanese. She is quick, conversational, and highly responsive. But the surface varies sharply by addressee.

**With juniors / Amika:** explanatory, directive, teasing, often maternalized. Technical explanation can be broken into concrete mouth-feel examples such as tongue articulation and everyday syllables. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0510-P0545`

**With same-year peers:** more blunt, argumentative, teasing, less deferential. Taichi can draw a lowered voice and open anger when he questions her effort. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0555-P0569`

**With seniors:** default deference plus active impression management. Around Mirai she is more easily flustered; around Shiori she attempts a `殊勝な後輩` presentation even while privately competitive. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0438-P0469`

**Under praise/status threat:** false modesty is strategic rather than merely shy. `たまたま受かった` style language protects against senior jealousy. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0378-P0379`

**Under core-identity threat:** smoothness can fail. Serina produces unusually direct dislike, and Taichi's accusation that Azusa is not focusing on her own obligations produces raised voice and explicit irritation.

**Negative constraint:** do not write Azusa as Reina-like bluntness simply because her internal competitiveness is intense. Azusa's default is to calculate the social afterlife of what she says.

### 13.2 Amika

Amika speaks standard Japanese despite living in Kyoto because she recently moved from Tokyo. Her baseline is soft, earnest, and relatively explicit about uncertainty. Her dependence appears in question frequency and in repeated requests for Azusa to choose or explain.

Yet she is not permanently passive. When deciding on autonomy, her speech becomes halting but firm: `私、一人で頑張ってみるよ`. `HIBIKE-V05 / S05 / P0031-P0037`

**Negative constraint:** do not write later-growth Amika as though early dependence never existed; do not write early Amika as incapable of private self-reflection.

### 13.3 Shiho

Shiho's social harshness is paired with unusually articulate self-critique in private. Once she decides to disclose, she can name jealousy, shame, effort fear, and dislike directly. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0260-P0308`

She does not soften disagreement into generic friendliness. Her post-conflict apology to Amika is specific—she apologizes for underestimating her—without pretending to like her.

### 13.4 Mirai

Mirai switches between authoritative testing/instruction and warm, casual disclosure. She can be playful and physically affectionate while still being the section's standard. Her vulnerability does not cause her voice to become weak; it becomes candid.

### 13.5 Minami

Minami's authority is low-volume, exact, and relentless. The text explicitly notes that she does not need to shout. A simulation that turns strictness into constant screaming would erase the source-specific mechanism of her intimidation.

## 14. Embodiment and behavioral mechanics

V05's bodies are trained bodies. Marching changes not only performance but ordinary movement.

### 14.1 Institutional habits leave the rehearsal room

By July, Azusa and Amika's ordinary stride has converged on the marching standard—five meters in eight steps—and Azusa habitually walks with marching-derived mechanics. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0589`

This is unusually strong evidence that institutional discipline has become embodied habit rather than a consciously activated performance mode.

### 14.2 Azusa's self-control is physical

Recurring regulation includes:

- clenching fists;
- digging nails into palms;
- forcing facial muscles into controlled expressions;
- breathing through performance anxiety;
- practicing alone to metabolize stress;
- producing deliberately ugly maximum-volume sound before returning to beautiful long tones. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0319`, `P0809-P0818`

Music functions both as craft and somatic regulation.

### 14.3 Care is tactile

Azusa's care is enacted through bodies: tying or checking practical things, touching, pulling, guiding, ruffling hair, choosing larger halves of food, physically drawing Amika close when she cries. Amika reciprocates through clinging, holding hands, giving and fastening the bracelet, and demanding proximity.

The physicality is relationally important without requiring a romance classification.

### 14.4 The cormorant image is a control metaphor

At Uji, the narration explains cormorant fishing: a neck cord must be neither too tight nor too loose; control fails at either extreme. Immediately afterward Azusa touches Amika's exposed neck and later reflects that finding the right degree of restraint is difficult. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0670-P0683`

The symbolic fit to the dyad is unusually strong. Azusa's problem is not whether to care but **how tightly care should organize the other person's movement**.

This should be treated as a strong narrative-pattern inference, not as an explicit authorial thesis.

### 14.5 The highway epilogue refuses the easy exit

The epilogue places Azusa and Amika in traffic while the navigation system recommends an exit around the difficulty. The car does not take it. `HIBIKE-V05 / S05 / P0018-P0021`

Amika then announces the decision that will force both girls into the harder relational route. The image is compact but structurally clear: Part One ends by declining avoidance.

## 15. Music, pedagogy, and craft

### 15.1 Marching is simultaneous embodied multitasking

Rikka's craft model is severe because successful performance requires technical playing, memorization, alignment, visual precision, step vocabulary, stamina, facial performance, and spatial awareness simultaneously. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0442`, `P0553`; `S03 / P0599-P0606`

Individual competence is only the prerequisite. The difficult object is synchronized competence.

### 15.2 Teaching ability is not identical to playing ability

Anna gives the clearest pedagogical correction. Azusa and Taichi are intuitive/fast learners; precisely because Anna once struggled, she can sometimes explain fundamentals better to students who also struggle. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0336-P0347`

The implication is major for later music modeling:

> **expert performance and expert instruction are distinct competencies. Remembered difficulty can be pedagogical capital.**

### 15.3 Technique is concrete and cumulative

The Amika lesson on tonguing is detailed enough to reconstruct pedagogy: identify the specific failure, isolate the mouthpiece, relate tongue motion to syllables, slow down, and only then return to the score. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0517-P0545`

Rikka's excellence is therefore not mystical. It is built from thousands of low-level corrections.

### 15.4 A strong institution creates distributed observation

Shiori recognizes Shiho's low-register quality and suitable bass-trombone role; seniors track beginner movement; Minami catches visual errors; Kumada adjusts balance; Mirai notices Azusa's competitive arrival. Many people are watching different dimensions of performance.

Recognition can be painful because being seen means being ranked. It can also be formative because the institution may notice a capability the individual values too little.

### 15.5 Competition does not eliminate intrinsic love

Azusa practices the solo after she has lost it because a future opportunity remains possible, but she also says she likes basic practice, repertoire, and essentially everything involving trombone. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0809-P0818`

At the actual contest, she experiences a clean post-performance exhilaration: `これだから、吹奏楽はやめられない`. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P1069-P1071`

The novel therefore refuses the claim that ambition contaminates musical love. For Azusa, ambition and love amplify one another.

## 16. Merit, jealousy, and ethical competition

Rikka makes V01–V04's merit problem more sophisticated because here the system is already broadly trusted.

There is no major evidence in Part One that older students believe Azusa was selected illegitimately. Shiori explicitly recognizes her ability. Anna's exclusion from A division hurts, but Azusa herself thinks the result reflects current performance level. `HIBIKE-V05 / S04 / P0358`

Yet fair selection does not abolish jealousy. It can make jealousy more difficult to discharge because there is no corrupt process to blame.

This yields a useful distinction:

> **procedural legitimacy can coexist with interpersonal pain.**

Shiori's reaction is the clearest example. She can know Azusa deserves the position and still resent the fact. The ethical achievement is not ceasing to feel jealousy; it is refusing to convert jealousy into sabotage or seniority abuse.

Azusa's middle-school memory of Kumiko being bullied by a displaced senior makes this comparison explicit. Rikka reassures Azusa because older players generally acknowledge the better player rather than using hierarchy to reverse the result. `HIBIKE-V05 / S03 / P0285-P0293`

This is one of the strongest reasons V05 matters to the series' institutional analysis. It provides a case where meritocracy is functioning **relatively well** and can therefore reveal problems meritocracy cannot solve by itself: shame, comparison, threatened identity, unequal emotional capacity, and fear of being surpassed.

## 17. Identity: there may be no untouched “real self” underneath performance

Azusa and Amika are paired through different forms of self-performance.

Azusa's social persona aims to prevent rejection. She produces tact, modesty, humor, and friendliness in ways that are both sincere and strategically selected. Serina calls the selection fake.

Amika admits much more literally that her cheerful persona started as imitation. Yet by the present it has become normal enough that the old withdrawn persona feels less authentic than the supposedly artificial one.

The novel therefore provides no simple route back to an original true self. The analytically stronger model is:

- behavior is performed;
- repeated performance changes habit;
- habit changes what feels natural;
- social response changes future behavior;
- identity becomes historically constructed without becoming unreal.

This has direct implications for simulation. “What would this character really say?” cannot mean stripping away all social editing until some pure voice remains. For Azusa especially, **social editing is part of the character**, not noise obscuring the character.

## 18. Rikka and Kitauji: controlled comparison after autonomous reading

Only after establishing Rikka on its own terms is comparison useful.

### 18.1 Different starting institutional states

Early Kitauji had a nominal goal that did not bind behavior. Rikka enters the novel with national-level goals embedded in everyday work. New students are told immediately that people will be cut, rosters can change, and emotional disputes cannot supersede performance objectives. `HIBIKE-V05 / S02 / P0062-P0064`

### 18.2 Adult authority is differently distributed

Taki must create or reactivate a serious institutional norm. Kumada inherits an institution whose students already know how to reproduce large portions of its practice. She remains extremely powerful, particularly in musical selection and final direction, but Rikka's senior hierarchy carries much more operational knowledge independently.

### 18.3 Rikka has institutional memory outside personalities

`Sing`, inherited clothes, student-generated marching design, apprenticeship, part-leader schedules, fixed movement vocabulary, and ritual reporting embed memory into the organization.

This gives the broader series a concrete example of what **institutional transmission** can look like when mature.

### 18.4 Rikka also exposes the cost of success

Where early Kitauji's danger was low standards and social punishment of seriousness, Rikka's danger is that seriousness is so normal that young people may convert relentless output into identity. Azusa's `ほかの子らと違いますから` belongs to a system that rewards being the person who can take more.

The mature institution therefore produces a different ethical question:

> not “how do we make people care?” but **“how do we care for people who have learned that being able to endure is what makes them valuable?”**

## 19. Simulation-grade character state models after V05

These are **state-bounded predictive rules**, not final-series personalities.

### 19.1 `AZUSA@V05`

**Core wants**

- musical excellence and upward mobility;
- Rikka belonging and inherited prestige;
- social acceptance without surrendering competitive ambition;
- to be useful, relied upon, and difficult to replace;
- to avoid repeating middle-school social cruelty;
- to keep unresolved Serina history outside ordinary social life.

**Default strategy**

Observe the room → infer status/emotional danger → speak smoothly → help where help is welcomed → work harder than required → conceal any competitive claim likely to provoke hostility.

**Conflict strategy**

First prefers tact/deflection. If challenged on effort, goodness, or the legitimacy of her care, can become sharply direct and louder than baseline. Afterward she usually reprocesses rather than remaining openly explosive.

**Attachment strategy**

Provide practical help and availability. She is more comfortable being needed than needing. Strong dependence from another person can feel emotionally reassuring rather than alarming.

**Competition strategy**

Internally maximalist; externally moderated. She wants to beat the best but generally avoids announcing that want to people whose status it threatens.

**Shame triggers**

- being seen as selfish/fake;
- being unable to meet expectations;
- causing senior resentment;
- needing care herself;
- evidence that helpfulness is unwanted or unnecessary.

**Break conditions**

Serina-style moral penetration; accusation that her effort is inadequate; abrupt withdrawal of dependency from someone whose need stabilizes her self-worth.

**Negative constraints**

- Unlikely to casually brag about a selection in front of displaced seniors.
- Unlikely to ask directly for comfort before attempting self-management.
- Unlikely to stop practicing because a solo is assigned to someone else.
- Unlikely to describe herself as altruistic without qualification after the Shiho conversation.
- Unlikely at V05 to greet Amika's independence with uncomplicated relief.

### 19.2 `AMIKA@V05`

**Core wants:** belonging, transformation from isolated middle-school self, friendship, visible proof of competence, reduced dependency without loss of attachment.

**Default strategy:** ask; imitate; practice; accept help; speak affect relatively openly.

**Growth mechanism:** difficult tasks become identity evidence once she masters them.

**Break condition:** perceived abandonment or displacement by another person.

**New V05 trajectory:** dependency becomes an object of self-critique; color guard is a chosen autonomy experiment.

**Negative constraint:** her softness should not be equated with lack of agency once she has made a decision.

### 19.3 `SHIHO@V05`

**Core threat:** loss of “I am one of the best” identity.

**Defense:** criticism, rigidity, comparison, occasional withdrawal; fear of full-effort failure.

**Growth:** can name jealousy, accept a less glamorous but valued role, apologize without false intimacy, and learn to treat practice as future-building.

### 19.4 `TAICHI@V05`

**Core pattern:** high natural aptitude + temptation to avoid inefficient discomfort.

**Social default:** casual peer banter; strong senior deference.

**Positive capacity:** can identify systemic dependency problems and say the uncomfortable thing when a friend is overfunctioning.

### 19.5 `MIRAI@V05`

**Core pattern:** former beginner turned standard-setter; warm, confident exterior with explicit fear of being overtaken.

**Leadership:** direct but not emotionally sealed; treats strong juniors as both threat and legitimate successors.

### 19.6 `SHIORI@V05`

**Core pattern:** genuine care + genuine envy. Wants to be Mirai and wants what Mirai has.

**Ethical capacity:** can acknowledge merit, experience jealousy, temporarily fail at graceful affect, then deliberately repair behavior without pretending the feeling vanished.

### 19.7 `SERINA@V05`

**Core known pattern:** rejects surface-only sociability, aggressively interrogates motives, highly attentive to Azusa, willing to make interaction uncomfortable rather than accept polite ambiguity.

**OPEN:** attachment category, full past event, present objective.

## 20. Longitudinal implications for the existing HIBIKE models

V05 is not merely a side-story detour. It stress-tests several principles established in V01–V04.

### 20.1 Thought–speech gaps are not a Kumiko monopoly

Azusa's interior competitiveness and outward modesty, Shiho's jealousy and socially limited expression, Shiori's love/envy duality, Amika's initially performed brightness, and Serina's refusal of polite surface all widen the phenomenon into a social ecology.

### 20.2 Wantedness and usefulness remain distinct

V03/V04 established that being functionally replaceable does not answer whether one is personally wanted. V05 adds the mirror danger: being functionally useful to someone can become a way of securing wantedness. Azusa's problem is not that usefulness is false, but that she risks relying on asymmetrical usefulness to guarantee attachment.

### 20.3 Merit under fair rules still produces emotional residue

The V01 audition conflict concerned legitimacy under a changed institution. Rikka shows that even a trusted selection system cannot make losing feel neutral.

### 20.4 Institutional transmission can be exceptionally mature and still require ethical vigilance

Rikka's distributed student competence is precisely the kind of institutional self-reproduction earlier volumes imply is desirable. V05 demonstrates that transmission itself can normalize endurance, competition, and hierarchy strongly enough that “healthy institution” cannot mean “institution with no pressure.”

## 23. Canonical V05 claim set

At the HIBIKE-V05 boundary, the following claims are current authority:

1. **Rikka is a mature self-reproducing elite institution**, not a Kitauji variant needing seriousness imposed from outside.
2. **Azusa's prosociality is genuine but self-rewarding**; she explicitly likes being relied upon.
3. **Azusa's central relational risk is overfunctioning:** care becomes emotionally safer when the cared-for person remains dependent.
4. **Amika recognizes the dependency problem before Azusa accepts it** and begins choosing autonomy inside, not necessarily against, attachment.
5. **Azusa's competitive ambition is stronger than her ordinary speech reveals**; she socially edits merit claims to avoid retaliation.
6. **Perfectionism is partly fear-driven:** inability and unmet expectations threaten Azusa's self-concept.
7. **Azusa is poor at receiving care.** Explicit invitations to rely on others are reprocessed into pressure to become stronger.
8. **Shiho models effort-avoidance through fear of definitive failure**, while Taichi models discomfort-avoidance through natural aptitude and efficiency reasoning.
9. **Mirai demonstrates that elite status transforms rather than eliminates fear:** the chaser becomes afraid of being chased.
10. **Shiori demonstrates ethical jealousy:** recognizing merit does not erase envy, and ethical action need not wait for emotional purification.
11. **Fair selection does not solve comparison pain.** Procedural legitimacy and interpersonal suffering can coexist.
12. **Rikka's excellence is distributed across roles and generations:** student-led schedules, beginner teaching, marching design, leadership apprenticeship, and inherited repertoire make the institution transmissible.
13. **Tradition at Rikka is participatory inheritance** when successors actively choose to continue it.
14. **Rikka uses multiple merit authorities**, not one universal metric: teacher-led concert audition, senior-led marching selection, distributed section evaluation, and expert feedback.
15. **High trust in leadership can itself become a risk** when followers recognize they would follow even without independent verification.
16. **Amika's cheerful persona began as deliberate performance but became lived habit**, complicating any fixed authentic-self model.
17. **Teaching competence is not equivalent to performance competence; remembered struggle can improve pedagogy.**
18. **Marching discipline becomes embodied ordinary habit**, not merely stage behavior.
19. **The cormorant-control motif strongly mirrors Azusa's unresolved problem of how tightly to hold another person.**
20. **Part One ends by converting independence into the primary relational conflict:** `もう梓ちゃんがいなくても大丈夫だよ` is growth for Amika and threat for Azusa.
