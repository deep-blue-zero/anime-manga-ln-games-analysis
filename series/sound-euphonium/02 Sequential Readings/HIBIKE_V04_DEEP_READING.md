### Why V04 is analytically non-optional

The anthology is not a decorative appendix to the opening trilogy. For V2 it serves three indispensable functions.

First, it is an **epistemic correction mechanism**. The first three books are overwhelmingly organized through Kumiko's proximity and interpretation. V04 repeatedly relocates focalization into Aoi, Hazuki, Riko, Shuuichi, Midori, Mizore, Takuya, Reina, and others. Claims that were previously inferred from behavior can therefore become direct evidence of what another character notices, wants, fears, misunderstands, or suppresses.

Second, it is a **character-model calibration volume**. Crisis scenes reveal what a person does when the stakes are exceptional. They are poor by themselves at predicting what that person does on an ordinary afternoon. V04 supplies food arguments, ghost hunting, school shopping, coffee, manga roleplay, joking, commuting, cultural-festival play, casual jealousy, awkward rejection, and winter conversation. These low-stakes situations are not analytically lesser; they are what prevents a later simulation model from overproducing crisis behavior.

Third, the anthology tests one of the trilogy's largest ideas: **functional value and personal value are not the same thing**. A tuba can be indispensable while rarely heard as an isolated foreground voice. Kyoko can be essential to a performance without standing onstage. Aoi can be functionally replaceable and still ache to be personally wanted. Asuka can become technically replaceable by Natsuki while remaining relationally irreplaceable to Kumiko. The anthology turns that distinction into a recurring social ontology.

## 2. Executive thesis: the hidden life is part of the canon

HIBIKE-V04's deepest contribution is that it makes **hiddenness itself** an object of analysis.

The title promises “secret stories,” but secrecy here rarely means sensational revelation. More often it means the ordinary material that a protagonist-centered mainline cannot see:

- the thought Aoi swallows after Asuka tells her the band will manage without her;
- Hazuki's pain after she chooses to support Kumiko anyway;
- Riko's envy of the very arguments she watches Natsuki and Yuuko have;
- Mizore's suspicion that Yuuko may only pity her;
- Reina's childhood inability to understand a classmate whose material conditions differ from her own;
- Shuuichi's performed adulthood and his view of Kumiko from outside Kumiko's narration;
- the exact institutional logic by which Yuuko and Natsuki are selected as the next leadership pair;
- the winter scene in which Kumiko's reciprocity toward Shuuichi becomes explicit from Shuuichi's focalization.

The result is not an omniscient correction in which the anthology finally tells us the one objective truth about everyone. V2 must retain the epistemic discipline established in V01–V03: **a new focalizer provides direct evidence of that focalizer's interior, not universal access to reality**. Aoi's account tells us with high confidence what Aoi wants and how she experiences Asuka. It does not automatically tell us every motive behind Asuka's answer. Riko's conviction that Natsuki and Yuuko are close is evidence from a perceptive outsider, not mathematical proof that their inner accounts would be identical.

Within that limit, however, V04 materially upgrades the corpus. It shows that the mainline's visible conflicts rest on an enormous substrate of invisible labor, unspoken comparison, private embarrassment, ordinary humor, missed timing, and relationship-specific performance.

For the character-modeling project, V04 establishes a governing rule:

> **Default behavior must be modeled from ordinary scenes, alternate focalization, and relationship-conditioned language at least as seriously as from dramatic climaxes.**

The anthology's formal polyphony therefore does not interrupt the series' development. It changes what counts as evidence.

---

## 3. Formal architecture: polyphony without omniscience

The stories range from early first-year practice to after the third-years' retirement. They do not form a continuous plot, but they do form an analytical progression.

The opening stories recover **how pleasure, learning, and hidden labor begin**. The middle stories move into **romantic scripts, friendship conflict, bodily insecurity, abandonment, and ordinary youth**. The final stories expose **institutional succession, material opportunity, inherited musical identity, and explicit romantic reciprocity**.

The volume repeatedly performs a reversal:

1. a character or activity looks secondary from the outside;
2. focalization enters that apparently secondary position;
3. the reader discovers that a complete private world was always operating there.

This is particularly strong in Aoi, Hazuki, Riko, Mizore, and Shuuichi. The mainline can easily make them look like satellites around Kumiko, Reina, or Asuka. The anthology refuses that hierarchy.

That formal move also supports an ethical one. Kitauji's ensemble succeeds only because many forms of contribution remain partially invisible. The anthology makes the literary form imitate the musical form: **what is not carrying the melody may still be carrying the piece**.

# 4. Story-by-story close reading

## S01 — 「北宇治高校吹奏楽部の日常」: ensemble pleasure before ambition hardens

The first story returns to the earliest days of the low-brass first-years. Its comic opening—Kumiko discovers the dictionary's unexpected `ユーフォニューム` alongside `ユーフォニアム` and `ユーフォニウム`—immediately situates character inside ordinary material practice rather than dramatic symbolism. Hazuki still jokes about “UFO,” Midori jumps into conversation, and Asuka arrives with the threat of a two-hour organological lecture. `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0004 / ユーフォニアムだったりユーフォニウムだったり` `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0043 / 「もしも聞きたいことがないなら、これからユーフォニアムの楽器の成り立ちを講演してもいいねんけどね、二時間くらい！」`

For voice modeling this is important Asuka evidence. Her teacher persona is not simply severe expertise. She uses exaggerated self-introduction, mock-grandiosity, aggressive enthusiasm, physical proximity, and comic timing. Yet when instruction begins she becomes concrete and competent. She comes specifically because beginner Hazuki needs help and explains that experienced Kumiko and Midori may find the lesson boring but should do it properly anyway. `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0072 / 「初心者の一年生がいるパートは三年が教えることになってな」`

The key musical moment is intentionally trivial: the three first-years play a simple basic-training piece together. Hazuki lights up and says that playing together is fun. Kumiko remembers that she once felt exactly the same—that as a child, simply making one piece with other people had been sufficient. Competition later complicated that pleasure. `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0094 / 「おおー、みんなで吹くのってめっちゃ楽しいね」` `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0098 / 小学生のときは吹いているだけで楽しくて、みんなでひとつの音楽を作る、それだけで満足できていた`

This is a useful counterweight to any synthesis that lets Reina's competitive desire monopolize the meaning of seriousness. The series does not replace pleasure with ambition. It preserves both.

Kumiko's language also gives a precise pre-V01-end state. When Midori says that if they are going to do it, she wants to do it seriously, Kumiko answers not `頑張る` but `私も、頑張りたいとは思ってるよ`. The `とは思ってる` construction still gives the desire an exit route. `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0114 / 「私も、頑張りたいとは思ってるよ」` This is exactly the deniable-desire mechanism V01 later resolves.

But the body already moves more directly than the sentence. When Hazuki asks to play again, Kumiko raises her hand too: `私も吹きたいです`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0121 / 「わ、私も吹きたいです」` The anthology therefore strengthens the V2 distinction between **spoken self-protection and action-level appetite**.

Asuka answers the three girls with `その意気やよし。それじゃ、もう一回やってみよな`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0124 / 「その意気やよし。それじゃ、もう一回やってみよな」` The ending is not about winning. It is about wanting one more repetition because ensemble sound itself is pleasurable.

### Modeling delta

- **Kumiko@early-V01:** verbally hedged about long-range commitment, behaviorally quicker to claim immediate musical pleasure.
- **Asuka:** high-energy comic pedagogue; explanation is itself a pleasure; can make elementary practice socially engaging rather than merely correct.
- **Hazuki:** beginner frustration converts rapidly into visible delight once participation becomes collective rather than isolated note production.
- **Midori:** treats seriousness and pleasure as naturally compatible.

## S02 — 「科学準備室の京子さん」: performance depends on people outside the performance

The ostensible plot is a school ghost story. Kumiko, Hazuki, and Midori encounter Kyoko and Kaori while older students are preparing costumes. The first-years' behavior is unusually valuable precisely because nothing morally catastrophic is happening: Midori treats the mystery as an adventure, Hazuki becomes half-terrified, Kumiko alternates skepticism and susceptibility, and the three move as an ordinary friend group.

Kyoko's importance is more structural. She is involved in the production work that makes the marching presentation possible. The story makes room for costume meetings, fabric, planning, and backstage competence—the things a competition-centered narrative tends to leave outside the frame. The “ghost” story is therefore also a labor story.

The end exposes Kaori's quiet envy. Watching the first-years' chaotic energy, she says the low-brass section looks fun. Kumiko answers brightly that it is. Kaori then lets the more private sentence slip: `いいなあ、低音は`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S02 / P0190 / 「ふふ、低音パートってほんとに楽しそうやね」` `HIBIKE-V04 / S02 / P0194 / 「いいなあ、低音は」`

Kumiko hears the implication and deliberately pretends not to. `HIBIKE-V04 / S02 / P0195 / 聞こえていないふりをするのがいちばんだと思ったから` This is a small but excellent sample of her social regulation. She is perceptive enough to identify the vulnerable underside of Kaori's comment and chooses non-engagement because acknowledgment would force a conversation neither girl has invited.

This is not necessarily cowardice. In V2 terms it is an example of **selective non-intervention as tact**, and it should be kept distinct from the more self-protective evasions of V01.

### Modeling delta

- **Kumiko:** silence can be an intentional kindness or boundary-preserving tactic, not only avoidance.
- **Kaori:** capable of expressing envy in a soft, low-pressure way rather than converting dissatisfaction into accusation.
- **Midori/Hazuki/Kumiko group:** comic default behavior should be part of their later simulation profiles.

## S03 — 「あの子には才能がある」: Aoi's private wound and the cruelty of accurate functional language

This is one of the anthology's most important epistemic corrections because it shifts directly into Aoi.

The opening demolishes any simple model of Aoi as merely “the serious senior who prioritizes exams.” She begins by admitting that she once believed herself a genius. She had top marks, expected admission to an elite high school, failed, and then constructed a story in which Kitauji was the rational alternative. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0001 / 自分は天才なのだと思っていた` Her later conflict with band is inseparable from comparative self-worth.

At Kitauji she encounters someone she experiences as the genuine article: Asuka. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0002 / そして高校に進学し、斎藤葵は本物の天才に出会ってしまった` Aoi watches the newly serious band and resents the timing: why did the club have to transform *this* year, when she needs to study? She is not simply relieved to have quit. She continues listening, wondering who will enter A ensemble, and policing herself for caring. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0007 / 部活を辞めてしまった自分には、なんの関係もない話じゃないか`

The contrast with Asuka becomes humiliating because Asuka treats scarce study time as a challenge she simply expects to overcome. When Aoi asks whether she is anxious about competing against students who can study while Asuka practices, Asuka says: `その程度の時間の差で、自分がほかのやつらに負けるとは思わんから`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0074 / 「その程度の時間の差で、自分がほかのやつらに負けるとは思わんから」`

Aoi's response—`ずるいよね`—links this story to the later Reina childhood story. “Unfairness” here is partly the encounter with another person's unequal starting point or capacity. But the anthology refuses to make talent morally simple. Asuka does not apologize for her competence, and Aoi does not actually want her to fail.

The most revealing exchange comes when Aoi asks whether Asuka has anything she lacks. Asuka answers: `いっぱいある。けど、隠すのが得意やねん`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0083 / 「いっぱいある。けど、隠すのが得意やねん」` Read after V03, this is extraordinarily high-value evidence. The polished Asuka exterior is not evidence of an absence of deprivation; it is an explicit self-described concealment strategy.

Then comes the wound. Aoi confesses guilt for leaving before the competition. Asuka reassures her in purely functional terms: tenor saxophones are numerous, so one person leaving causes no serious operational problem. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0092 / 「人数少ないオーボエとかファゴットの子が辞めたんやったらめっちゃ困るけど、テナーサックスっていっぱいいるから。一人ぐらいいなくなっても全然問題ないよ」`

The statement is factually useful and emotionally devastating. Asuka intends to remove Aoi's guilt by proving that the band does not require her. Aoi discovers that what she wanted was almost the opposite. Alone afterward she asks herself whether she had hoped Asuka would say: `葵がいないとやっぱり無理だよ、戻ってきて`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0096 / 葵がいないとやっぱり無理だよ、戻ってきて`

This is direct internal evidence. V1 was right to identify a hidden request, but V2 can now state it more strongly: **Aoi wants evidence of personal irreplaceability even after she has chosen functional withdrawal**.

Her final recognition is painful because it is self-implicating: she quit, yet feels abandoned. Functional reassurance fails because her problem was never only whether the saxophone part would survive.

### Connection to V03

V03 established that Asuka can be technically replaced by Natsuki while remaining personally wanted by Kumiko. S03 gives the inverse injury: Aoi is told she is technically replaceable when what she wants is relational evidence that her absence matters.

This makes **functional necessity** and **personal irreplaceability** separate axes across the series rather than one story's metaphor.

## S04 — 「少女漫画ごっこ」: scripts are useful precisely because reality refuses them

The shōjo-manga roleplay is easy to dismiss as comedy, but for V2 it is unusually valuable as a controlled experiment in social scripts.

Midori enthusiastically casts friends into romance archetypes and treats high school as a space where the forms she knows from fiction might become real. Hazuki is more resistant and embarrassed. The fun comes from watching characters try on positions that their actual bodies, speech habits, and relationships do not comfortably inhabit.

The story therefore does not simply mock romance fiction. Scripts provide characters with language for desires they do not yet know how to articulate. They can rehearse roles before reality demands a decision. But the script never controls reality completely.

The final joke is exemplary. Midori remains thrilled by the possibility of romantic high-school scenes while Hazuki responds to a bicycle two-riding fantasy by pointing out the traffic-law violation. `HIBIKE-V04 / S04 / P0123-P0125` The humor is character-specific: Midori expands life through narrative possibility; Hazuki punctures the narrative with practical reality.

### Modeling delta

- **Midori:** narrativizes social life, but this should not be confused with gullibility. Later stories show that she can distinguish a useful story from an excuse.
- **Hazuki:** romance embarrassment often appears as practical or comic resistance rather than lack of desire.
- **Group humor:** the girls' speech works through rapid mutual correction, teasing, and exaggerated role assignment.

## S05–S06 — 「好きな人の好きな人」: Hazuki acts before certainty and remains hurt after doing the decent thing

Hazuki's two-part story is the anthology's largest correction to a Kumiko-centered romantic account. It places the crush, discovery, confession, rejection, and aftermath inside Hazuki's own attention.

Her attraction begins through ordinary embodied kindness rather than abstract romantic destiny. She is drawn to Shuuichi through concrete encounters, proximity, and the fact that he is useful and decent to her. Musical admiration becomes part of that attraction, but it is not the only cause.

The crucial ethical distinction is that Hazuki does not wait until action can be emotionally safe. She recognizes that Shuuichi may like Kumiko and is tempted to withdraw before she has exposed herself. Midori refuses to let “I might lose” become a retrospective alibi for never choosing. That support is consistent with Midori's broader V2 model: she uses romantic narratives to expand agency, not to eliminate consequences.

Hazuki confesses and is rejected. What happens afterward should not be romanticized into effortless selflessness. She eventually tells Shuuichi that Kumiko is her important friend and that she will help the two of them get together: `久美子はうちの大事な友達ですから。二人がくっつくよう協力したるわ`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S06 / P0184 / 「ま、久美子はうちの大事な友達ですから。二人がくっつくよう協力したるわ」`

That is real generosity. But the text immediately blocks a clean sainthood reading. Hazuki laughs; Shuuichi follows; their laughter sounds `ひどく作り物めいていた`, and neither names that fact. `HIBIKE-V04 / S06 / P0187 / それはひどく作り物めいていたけれど、二人とも互いに指摘しようとはしなかった`

The correct model is therefore:

> **Hazuki chooses prosocial conduct while the pain remains active.**

This distinction matters enormously for character simulation. A model that makes Hazuki cheerful because she has “accepted” the rejection would erase the very moral work the scene portrays. Her goodness consists partly in doing something she finds painful without pretending it does not hurt.

S06 also embeds one of the anthology's key musical metaphors in direct instruction: the tuba is not flashy but is indispensable. The lesson does not make the tuba “secretly a soloist.” It validates contribution without converting support into foreground. That is an important ethical distinction for a series about ensemble life.

## S07 — 「犬と猿とおかん」: argument as privileged access

Riko's focalization turns Natsuki and Yuuko's bickering into a relationship study. Their argument begins over something as trivial as what condiment belongs on a fried egg, but the content is less important than the permission structure.

Yuuko is socially forceful; Natsuki resists rather than yielding. Riko is cast as the “mother” who can stabilize the situation, but her final response is not relief at being more peaceful than they are. She envies them.

She realizes that she cannot enter the peculiar atmosphere between the two girls. `HIBIKE-V04 / S07 / P0130 / 二人のあいだにある独特な空気のなかに、梨子は決して立ち入ることができない` Her conclusion is blunt: `あの二人、仲いいよね`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S07 / P0131 / 「あの二人、仲いいよね」`

The story therefore strengthens—but also qualifies—the V1 idea of conflict as intimacy. Conflict is not inherently evidence of closeness. What matters is **mutual permission to oppose without the relationship collapsing**. Natsuki and Yuuko can irritate each other because each expects continued engagement.

That distinction is essential for simulation. Their characteristic friction should not be generated as generic hostility. It is a high-contact relationship mode with a stable expectation of return.

## S08 — 「背伸び」: Shuuichi and the comedy of performed masculinity

This story is a rare extended Shuuichi focalization and consequently valuable for reconstructing his ordinary male social environment rather than only his function in Kumiko's arc.

The core joke is black coffee. Shuuichi participates in a small masculine performance of adulthood, attempting to consume something because it signifies maturity rather than because he enjoys it. His friends are more direct about the posturing than the girls around Kumiko usually are; the male microculture permits a different form of teasing and status negotiation.

Taki appears briefly outside his conductor function. His ordinary interaction with Shuuichi has none of the ritual distance of rehearsal authority. When Taki interprets the coffee as evidence of adult taste, Shuuichi is left feeling even more childlike because the compliment has accidentally exposed the performance.

The ending is excellent negative-constraint evidence. Shuuichi decides to give the coffee away, drinks Pocari, and admits internally that this is more appropriate: `やっぱり、自分にはこれぐらいがちょうどいい。大人にはまだなれそうにない`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S08 / P0136 / やっぱり、自分にはこれぐらいがちょうどいい。大人にはまだなれそうにない`

He is therefore not best modeled as a naturally self-assured “normal boy.” He repeatedly experiments with adult masculinity, recognizes when the performance fails, and can retreat into self-deprecating realism.

## S09 — 「こんぷれっくす」: the body is interpreted through borrowed eyes

Midori's name and body insecurities reveal a character who can extend generous interpretation outward more easily than she can fully apply it inward.

Her parents gave her the reading “Sapphire” because they wanted a special child. Midori dislikes the conspicuousness and asks others to call her 緑. The name therefore makes “specialness” into a family-imposed identity before it becomes a Reina-style self-chosen aspiration.

The shopping conversation with Hazuki works through reciprocal body comparison. Each girl notices qualities the other cannot comfortably receive as praise. The story's quiet insight is that self-image is not self-generated in isolation: people borrow one another's gaze.

At the end Midori notices Hazuki's embarrassment and thinks she is genuinely cute. The problem is not that positive evidence is absent; it is that Hazuki cannot automatically inhabit the external view. That pattern connects to Aoi and Asuka as well: **being told how one appears does not make the external description experientially usable**.

For a future Midori model, this story is also a warning against flattening her into pure confidence. Her exuberance coexists with embarrassment about being made conspicuous.

## S10 — 「きみのいなくなった日」: Mizore's abandoned-day interior

This short story is one of the highest-value retrospective sources for V02 because it gives direct access to Mizore on the day Nozomi disappears from band.

Mizore's persistence is stark: she remains partly because Nozomi remains. Her status in A ensemble is not evidence of secure belonging; as the only oboist, she is functionally difficult to replace. The story therefore exposes how institutional necessity can coexist with profound relational precarity.

When Nozomi disappears without telling her, Mizore freezes. Yuuko is the person who immediately reaches toward her and imagines a future in which they can go home together.

But V04 prevents a retrospective sentimental rewrite in which Mizore instantly recognizes Yuuko as a second secure attachment. Mizore's internal response is cruel and suspicious: `もしも私がかわいそうじゃなかったら、優子は私と一緒に帰ろうなんて思ってくれた？` `HIBIKE-V04 / S10 / P0067 / もしも私がかわいそうじゃなかったら、優子は私と一緒に帰ろうなんて思ってくれた？`

She does not say it. She swallows the thought and gives an ambiguous smile. `HIBIKE-V04 / S10 / P0068` This is important for two models at once.

For Mizore, withdrawal and low verbal output should never be confused with the absence of sharp or even hostile private judgments. She can self-edit too.

For Yuuko, later importance is strengthened rather than weakened by the mistrust. Their bond is not retroactively pure from inception. Yuuko becomes a second anchor through repeated presence, not because Mizore immediately accepts the offer as equivalent to Nozomi.

This strongly supports V02's plural-support model while preserving relational history.

## S11 — 「北宇治高校文化祭」: ordinary youth as anti-overfitting evidence

The cultural-festival story is the anthology's largest reservoir of low-stakes behavioral evidence. Costumes, crepes, fortune telling, attractions, teasing, jealousy, and the haunted house allow characters to act outside competition's evaluative pressure.

This matters structurally. A character model trained only on auditions, family crises, romantic confessions, and national competition will mistake exceptional regulation for personality itself.

Kumiko and Shuuichi repeatedly miss small cues. Kumiko shows flashes of jealousy and attention that she would struggle to formulate directly. Reina participates socially rather than remaining the aloof trumpet exceptionalist of simplified summaries. Kaori's costume and Asuka's fortune-teller role both show how the festival permits temporary identities that are playful without being meaningless.

The haunted-house ending is particularly useful Reina evidence. Reina enjoys frightening Kumiko enough that she visibly shakes with laughter. `HIBIKE-V04 / S11 / P0444-P0449` This is not “soft Reina replacing severe Reina.” It is a different relationship-conditioned mode. With Kumiko, in a private low-stakes setting, Reina can be mischievous, physically expressive, and openly amused.

The episode therefore adds a negative constraint:

> A plausible Reina simulation must not turn every interaction into a declaration of excellence, moral severity, or intense romance. She is capable of ordinary adolescent play, especially with a trusted person.

Likewise Kumiko's response belongs in her embodiment model: startled reactions, complaints, embarrassment, and playful retaliation are part of her default social life.

## S12 — 「新三年生会議」: succession institutionalizes complementary conflict

The leadership-succession story is small but architecturally important. It shows that Kitauji does not reproduce itself by appointing the single “best” individual. It composes leadership relationally.

Yuuko becomes president and Natsuki vice-president. Riko explains why the pairing can work: Yuuko can run hot and overextend; Natsuki is one of the few people capable of stopping her. `HIBIKE-V04 / S12 / P0057 / 「優子って暴走することも多いから、そういうときのあの子を抑えられるのはこの部で夏紀だけやと思うねん」`

Riko makes the relational principle explicit: people who truly dislike one another usually do not keep engaging in the same way; their arguments indicate access and persistence. `HIBIKE-V04 / S12 / P0059 / 「ほんまに仲が悪かったら、あんなふうにお互いに絡んだりしいひんし」`

This is more than shipping subtext. Institutionally, Asuka's generation converts an informal dyadic property into governance architecture. The future president is not selected in isolation; the vice-president is selected partly as a regulator whose relationship with the president allows correction.

The story also reminds us that succession is felt bodily and temporally. Takuya and Riko walk home holding hands and realize that becoming third-years means approaching separation. `HIBIKE-V04 / S12 / P0069 / 「……俺らも、三年か」` The institution's continuity is built out of individuals who know they are temporary.

## S13 — 「お兄さんとお父さん」: merit, opportunity, and Reina's early social blind spot

This story substantially strengthens the material-opportunity layer that V1 recognized but had not systematized.

Reina's musical infrastructure is explicit. She attends piano lessons nearly every day, also studies violin, receives trumpet instruction from her father, has multiple instruments available at home, and loves practice itself. `HIBIKE-V04 / S13 / P0001-P0002` Her excellence is neither pure innate genius nor pure resource advantage. It is an interaction between abundant opportunity, family expertise, intense intrinsic motivation, and repeated labor.

The childhood piano conflict gives the series one of its clearest statements about unequal starting conditions. A friend, Yuka, loses the school accompaniment role to Reina and tells her: `麗奈ちゃんには、うちの気持ちなんて絶対わからへん`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S13 / P0006` Reina initially cannot understand why. From her perspective the teacher compared performances and chose the better one.

Then Yuka explains the material difference. Reina has instruments at home; Yuka does not. Yuka practices at the lesson studio or school and is grateful her family can pay even for that access. `HIBIKE-V04 / S13 / P0011-P0018` Her accusation is not that Reina did nothing. It is that Reina interprets the world as though effort is equally available because she has never experienced the constraints that make effort harder to convert into performance.

The best V2 formulation is therefore not “Reina's meritocracy is false.” The text plainly shows that she practices obsessively and performs at a high level. Rather:

> **Performance judgment can be accurate while the conditions producing performance remain unequal.**

This distinction preserves both sides of the series' fairness problem.

The episode also gives strong evidence for Reina's social attention bias. She treats losing as a problem to solve through more effort and expects others to process it similarly. Yuka's pain is difficult for her to model because the causal structure of Yuka's life is outside Reina's experience.

The second half introduces young Taki through Reina's father. Her father praises him; Reina memorizes the name. `HIBIKE-V04 / S13 / P0061 / 滝昇。紹介された名前を忘れないように、麗奈はその名を脳裏に焼きつける` This is valuable origin evidence, but V2 should not collapse later attachment into destiny. At this point the event establishes a respected adult embedded in her father's musical network.

The final paragraph is equally revealing: playing with her father makes the school conflict disappear into the past. `HIBIKE-V04 / S13 / P0065` Music is not only ambition for Reina; it is an affective recovery environment.

### Modeling delta

- **Reina:** high resource access + high labor + high appetite for mastery; none can be removed without distorting her.
- **Reina's fairness intuition:** strongly outcome/performance oriented, with an early blind spot for unequal opportunity.
- **Reina under interpersonal hurt:** can discard an unresolved social conflict quickly when absorbed into music/family attachment; this may look cold from outside.

## S14 — 「とある冬の日」: Kumiko becomes visible from outside herself

The final story is a major relationship artifact because it is focalized through Shuuichi. It therefore lets us observe Kumiko without relying on her own ability to name her motives.

The opening image is already corrective. Shuuichi watches Kumiko and Reina leaving together and recognizes their easy closeness. Reina gives Kumiko a slightly embarrassed smile. `HIBIKE-V04 / S14 / P0005 / 今日も二人は仲がいい` From Kumiko's mainline focalization, her bond with Reina often feels uncertain, extraordinary, or difficult to categorize. From outside it is also simply visible as habitual closeness.

Shuuichi's own behavior is marked by delay, self-consciousness, and fear of the changing calendar. Riko's warning that people can only remain together in the current arrangement for a limited time creates pressure to act. This echoes the anthology's larger timing theme: opportunities disappear even when nobody actively rejects them.

The confession scene is bodily before it is eloquent. Shuuichi sweats, grasps Kumiko's wrist, repeats `俺`, then finally produces the blunt `好きやねんけど` and, when she asks who, `お前を！`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S14 / P0252-P0260`

Kumiko's response matters just as much. She does not answer with a polished romantic script. She covers her face, crouches, says her heart is pounding, then returns to familiar teasing. `HIBIKE-V04 / S14 / P0261-P0270` This is excellent evidence for her state-addressable simulation model: high affect first disrupts her body and speech, after which she uses ordinary interaction to regulate the intensity.

Then she lends Shuuichi her scarf, draws him close, rises on tiptoe, and tells him quietly: `私も、秀一のこと好きだよ`. `HIBIKE-V04 / S14 / P0275-P0282`

This is explicit reciprocal romantic evidence. It does not erase Kumiko/Reina intimacy; the story begins by emphasizing that intimacy. Nor does it permit later material to be backported. At this state, however, any character model that says Kumiko has no romantic attraction toward Shuuichi is incompatible with the locked prose.

The scene also supports a general relationship-model rule:

> **Different bonds can occupy different forms of intimacy without requiring the text to convert them into a single ranking.**

The anthology repeatedly resists the demand that one relationship make another unreal.

# 5. Character-modeling synthesis after V04

## 5.1 Kumiko — alternate viewpoints sharpen the thought/speech/action model

V04 provides two different corrections to a Kumiko-only account.

Early in the volume, Kumiko can say `私も、頑張りたいとは思ってるよ` while raising her hand more directly to say `私も吹きたいです`. Her action-level desire remains easier to access than her abstract self-declaration. By the winter story, Shuuichi's viewpoint shows how visible her attachments can be to other people even when Kumiko herself treats them as unresolved.

Her silence around Kaori also complicates the “Kumiko avoids difficult statements” model. Sometimes she withholds because she does not want responsibility; sometimes she withholds because a vulnerable remark does not require forced acknowledgment. A simulation model needs a **motive-sensitive silence rule**, not a generic evasiveness switch.

## 5.2 Reina — severity is only one register

Reina's childhood story and cultural-festival behavior widen the model in opposite directions.

The childhood story strengthens her competitive fairness intuition and her blind spot toward structural inequality. She believes losing should convert into self-improvement because that is how she herself reacts. But her life has offered unusually rich conditions for converting effort into skill.

The festival shows the other side: with trusted Kumiko, Reina can be playful, mischievous, physically expressive, and delighted by a stupid joke. Her simulation profile must therefore condition “Reina directness” on domain and addressee.

## 5.3 Asuka — social brilliance can fail by using the wrong ontology

V03 made Asuka's self-concealment and competence central. V04 gives direct evidence from Aoi that Asuka can injure someone while trying to reassure her because she answers a **relational question in functional language**.

Aoi wants to know whether she is wanted. Asuka answers whether her saxophone chair is operationally required.

That mismatch should become a durable character-model rule. Asuka is not socially unintelligent. She is extremely socially capable. The problem is that she can choose to translate emotion into systems, tasks, and replaceability when doing so protects efficiency or reduces burden. That translation is sometimes helpful and sometimes morally underdescriptive.

## 5.4 Hazuki — prosocial choice without emotional purification

Hazuki's model must preserve simultaneous truths:

- she is hurt;
- she still likes Shuuichi;
- she values Kumiko;
- she chooses not to punish either of them;
- she can joke while hurting;
- the joke may sound fake because the hurt has not vanished.

This is precisely the kind of contradiction a simulation system tends to flatten if trained from summary adjectives such as “cheerful” and “selfless.”

## 5.5 Midori — narrative imagination as agency support

Midori's romantic imagination is not merely comic genre-awareness. She repeatedly uses stories to make action thinkable. At the same time, she notices bodies, embarrassment, and social reality sharply enough not to confuse fantasy with evidence.

She is therefore best modeled as an **expansive interpreter**: someone who offers frames that make other people's possibilities larger, while often remaining surprisingly practical about their actual choices.

## 5.6 Mizore — silence contains judgment

S10 is critical negative evidence against “Mizore has little to say internally.” She can generate a cutting suspicion about Yuuko and then suppress it. Future simulation should preserve the gap between minimal speech and potentially sharp internal appraisal.

## 5.7 Natsuki and Yuuko — antagonistic surface, secure return

Riko's viewpoint and the succession story jointly establish that their arguing is not a sign of fragile relation. It is one of the relation's operating languages. The key predictive feature is not the frequency of conflict but the expectation that conflict does not terminate contact.

Yuuko's leadership also benefits from precisely the person most willing to resist her. Natsuki is not merely comic opposition; she becomes a governance control surface.

## 5.8 Shuuichi — ordinary adolescence, direct feeling under pressure

V04 substantially improves Shuuichi reconstruction. He participates in male posturing, is capable of self-deprecation, worries about maturity, notices Kumiko/Reina closeness, delays romantic action, and under enough pressure finally speaks very directly.

His directness is not Reina-style baseline directness. It is **threshold directness**: hesitation accumulates until the only remaining path is a blunt statement.

# 6. Voice and register findings

V04 is unusually valuable because focalizer change gives us speech in different social ecosystems.

### Aoi

Aoi's internal language is comparison-heavy and status-aware. She repeatedly converts emotion into school ranking, exam probability, and relative talent. With Asuka, she suppresses explicit rivalry because being seen as rivalrous would itself feel humiliating. The result is a voice with frequent swallowed sentences and self-correction.

### Hazuki

Hazuki uses casual Kansai speech, practical reactions, and humor to manage embarrassment. After rejection she can still make an outwardly generous statement while the narration marks the laughter as artificial. Her voice therefore should not be generated as transparent access to affect.

### Midori

Midori's self-reference as 緑 remains distinctive. Her high-energy speech is highly affiliative: she recruits others into games, imagined scenes, and emotional possibilities. Yet she can become serious without losing her characteristic warmth.

### Riko

Riko's speech is soft and regulating. She often interprets other people relationally and can articulate why conflict might be evidence of closeness. Her calmness is not absence of private envy.

### Mizore

Minimal outward speech coexists with internally elaborate suspicion. This gap is essential. Do not generate long explanatory monologues for her merely because the model knows her internal state.

### Reina

Child Reina already uses `アタシ` and a performance-centered fairness logic. Adult Reina's low-stakes Kumiko register can include visible embarrassment and laughter. The voice ledger should therefore separate public evaluative Reina from private/playful Reina rather than seek one average register.

### Shuuichi

Shuuichi's male-peer speech permits more direct teasing and rougher self-presentation than his Kumiko-facing speech. In romantic escalation he hesitates, repeats starts, then becomes blunt. `好きやねんけど` / `お前を！` is not his ordinary turn style; it is a pressure release.

### Kumiko from outside

Shuuichi's focalization is especially useful because Kumiko's own narration cannot tell us how her closeness, embarrassment, or body language looks to others. V04 confirms that her behavior often communicates more than her self-description does.

# 7. Ordinary life as evidence, not filler

V04 establishes a methodological rule that should remain active through the rest of V2.

A high-stakes scene systematically distorts behavior. Under audition pressure, bereavement, family coercion, or confession, characters speak and act at the edges of their regulation capacity. If those scenes dominate a model, the generated character becomes permanently intense.

This anthology supplies calibration data:

- what Hazuki complains about when learning an instrument;
- how Midori turns a mystery into a group activity;
- how Kumiko handles a vulnerable aside she thinks she should not touch;
- what Natsuki and Yuuko fight about when nothing important is at stake;
- how Shuuichi performs adulthood through coffee;
- how Reina behaves in a haunted house;
- how couples walk home after institutional meetings;
- how Kumiko returns to teasing immediately after romantic shock.

These details are not trivial to the simulation objective. They are the evidence that determines whether a novel scenario feels inhabited rather than merely thematically correct.

A mature character monograph should therefore preserve separate fields for:

- **crisis behavior**;
- **task behavior**;
- **ordinary social behavior**;
- **play/humor behavior**;
- **embarrassment regulation**;
- **relationship-specific defaults**.

V04 is the first volume to make that requirement impossible to ignore.

---

# 8. Hidden labor, visibility, necessity, and irreplaceability

V1 was already strong here, and V2 strengthens the architecture.

The anthology repeatedly separates four questions that are often conflated:

1. **Is the work visible?**
2. **Is the function necessary?**
3. **Is the performer replaceable?**
4. **Is the person wanted?**

The answers can differ.

- The tuba is not usually foregrounded, but the ensemble depends on the low foundation.
- Costume and production work can be invisible to an audience while remaining necessary to the performance event.
- Aoi's tenor-sax role is replaceable, yet she wants her *person* to be missed.
- Mizore is functionally difficult to replace as the only oboe, but that does not create secure belonging.
- Asuka can be replaced in performance by Natsuki in V03, yet Kumiko still wants Asuka specifically.

This separation is one of the strongest series-level concepts emerging from V2 because it connects musical form, institutional design, and interpersonal ethics without reducing one to another.

A character's need to be wanted should therefore never be inferred merely from being necessary, and a person's replaceability in a task should never be taken as evidence that the relationship is replaceable.

---

# 9. Material opportunity and the limits of outcome-only fairness

S13 moves the material-opportunity theme from background inference to explicit text.

Reina's friend identifies the exact problem with an outcome-only fairness model: the teacher may genuinely choose the better performance, but the performers did not have equal conditions for producing that performance. Home instruments, money, lesson access, family expertise, and practice time are part of the causal chain.

The series still does not become a total socioeconomic critique. Reina's own appetite for practice is unusually strong, and her later trumpet excellence cannot be explained by resources alone. But V2 can now state with high confidence:

> **Hibike! treats merit judgments as potentially accurate without treating the distribution of merit-producing opportunity as equal.**

This should become a longitudinal question rather than a one-story theme. Later claims about auditions, university, private instruction, instrument ownership, and professional aspiration should be tagged for material-precondition evidence.

---

# 10. Relationship plurality after the anthology

V04 makes it increasingly untenable to model relationships as a single ranked ladder.

Kumiko can be visibly and habitually close to Reina while also reciprocating Shuuichi romantically. Hazuki can continue caring about both Kumiko and Shuuichi after rejection. Mizore can eventually depend on Yuuko without making Nozomi irrelevant. Natsuki and Yuuko can use conflict as intimacy while Riko has a different, calmer relation with both. Takuya and Riko's quiet couplehood is not made less real by its lack of dramatic centrality.

The series' relational ontology is therefore plural rather than zero-sum:

> **Different people can occupy different kinds of irreplaceability.**

This does not mean jealousy or ranking never occurs. It means the analyst should not impose exclusivity where the text does not.

# 12. Cumulative delta after V04

The opening trilogy checkpoint froze the movement:

> **deniable desire → plural/contested desire → accountable first-person agency under uncertainty → transmission**

V04 does not extend that linear arc in the same way. It changes the dimensionality of the evidence.

After V04 the project can add:

> **the visible arc is only one voice inside a larger ensemble of private states.**

Several durable V2 principles are now established:

1. **Focalization is evidence-class control.** What Kumiko sees, what another character says, and what another focalizer privately thinks must remain distinct.
2. **Ordinary behavior is essential reconstructive evidence.** Crisis cannot serve as a character's default personality.
3. **Functional and relational value are orthogonal.** Necessary, replaceable, visible, and wanted are separate properties.
4. **Pro-social behavior need not imply emotional purification.** Hazuki is the clearest case.
5. **Conflict can be intimacy when the relationship guarantees return.** Natsuki/Yuuko establish the pattern.
6. **Material opportunity affects merit production without erasing performance reality.** Reina's childhood makes the distinction explicit.
7. **Different relationships can encode different forms of irreplaceability.** Kumiko/Reina and Kumiko/Shuuichi are not mutually annihilating facts.
8. **Self-editing is widely distributed.** Kumiko, Aoi, Mizore, Hazuki, and others all contain gaps between thought, speech, and outward performance.
