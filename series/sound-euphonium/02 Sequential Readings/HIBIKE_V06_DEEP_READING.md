## 2. Executive local thesis

HIBIKE-V06 completes the Rikka duology by transforming Azusa's core problem from **“I feel secure when I am needed”** into the harder question:

> **Can I accept being loved, wanted, and valued when the other person no longer depends on me?**

Part One established the contradiction. Azusa's care for Amika was real, patient, materially effective, and self-rewarding. She also preferred an arrangement in which Amika's need for her remained obvious. Part Two refuses the easy cure of simply ordering Azusa to “back off.” When Shiho tells her to stop interfering, Azusa initially overcorrects from overcare into emotional withdrawal. She stops doing things for Amika, but she also starts refusing Amika's invitations and suppressing resentment. Removing the behavior does not remove the attachment logic underneath it.

The decisive correction comes from Mirai. She does not tell Azusa that relationships must become perfectly disinterested. She rejects that premise. `利用して何が悪いん？` she asks; if both people receive something from a friendship, reciprocity does not make affection fraudulent. More importantly, Mirai gives Azusa the sentence she has lacked since Serina asked `梓にとって、私は何？`: **someone can need Azusa without needing her services.** `私は梓のことを必要としてる` is explicitly separated from “because you help me.” `HIBIKE-V06 / S04 / P0407-P0429`

That relational lesson is immediately tested in four different directions:

1. **Amika** becomes more independent but remains attached, and later helps Azusa solve a problem Azusa could not solve herself. `HIBIKE-V06 / S04 / P0437-P0458`; `S05 / P0564-P0568`
2. **Serina** and Azusa finally admit that both had wanted exclusive importance from the other, allowing their old friendship to be reclassified from one-sided charity to mutually special attachment distorted by pride and possessiveness. `S05 / P0506-P0543`
3. **Azusa's mother** demonstrates that care does not have to be earned by being easy to raise or undemanding: `仕事も大事やけど、梓のほうがもっと大事やんか`. Azusa then asks for a hug and receives it. `S05 / P0444-P0463`
4. **Mirai's injury** forces Azusa to inherit a visible role without becoming Mirai. The mentor's central instruction is `梓はさ、梓やねんで`; succession requires individuation, not imitation. `S05 / P0399-P0421`

The institutional arc mirrors the relational one. Rikka proves resilient precisely because **no single performer is functionally indispensable**: when Mirai breaks her ankle, Taichi can enter the formation, Azusa can inherit the solo, Shiori can document routes, and injured Mirai can change from performer to observer/coach. Yet the book does not infer that people are therefore interchangeable. Mirai's absence hurts. Her leadership still matters. Her teammates still cry. Functional redundancy and relational specificity coexist.

The duology therefore arrives at a mature model of both care and institutions:

> **Healthy continuity does not require keeping another person dependent. It requires allowing roles, skills, help, and expectations to move between people without treating the people themselves as disposable.**

That is why the final image is not the national gold medal. It is Azusa, now Rikka's retiring president, reserving Mirai's inherited jacket for a first-year she expects great things from and repeating the sentence once given to her: `恵里佳にはいちばん期待してるから`. `HIBIKE-V06 / S06 / P0040-P0051`

## 3. Structural architecture: from arrested motion to transmitted motion

The four chapter titles continue the marching-vocabulary architecture, but Part Two repeatedly turns technical movement terms into problems of psychological regulation. These readings are **[D/E] narrative-pattern inferences**, not claims that the narrator supplies a fixed allegorical glossary.

### 3.1 `妄想マークタイム`: movement without relational progress

A mark time is movement in place. That is an apt structure for Azusa's early response to the Amika crisis. She keeps training, teaching, performing, and physically moving through an elite institution, yet relationally she is stuck. Shiho's confrontation makes the stasis explicit: `梓はいつまであみかに頼るの`. `HIBIKE-V06 / S02 / P0469-P0471`

The chapter's harshest irony is that Azusa sees Amika being treated as a full performer and wants to rescue her back into beginner status. Shiho argues that Momoka's strictness signifies equal expectation: `あの子はいま、初心者やなくて一人前の奏者として扱われてる`. `S02 / P0502-P0506` Azusa's body is moving forward; her mental model of Amika has not caught up.

### 3.2 `瞬間パレードレスト`: stopping long enough to perceive the self

Part Two's seat-concert sequence is an important interruption. Azusa performs accurately at the Kansai competition but describes her mental state as empty and unjoined to the music. Her fingers and lips reproduce trained motion; her attention is elsewhere. `HIBIKE-V06 / S03 / P0187-P0191`

Mirai's response is not “practice more.” She asks what Azusa likes besides band. Azusa can initially produce almost nothing beyond potatoes. Mirai orders a full day away from music because `梓は自分に厳しすぎんの`. `S03 / P0243-P0258`

The parade-rest logic is therefore not laziness. It is a temporary stop necessary to prevent forward motion from becoming self-erasure.

### 3.3 `無意識テンハット`: unconscious relational habit becomes conscious

The central third chapter repeatedly exposes behavior Azusa is performing without owning. She says she is not angry at Amika while internally thinking `先に突き放したんは、向こうやし`; Shiho later tells her she was asked to create distance, not `心を閉ざせ`. The shift from overcare to rejection is therefore not deliberate cruelty. It is an unconscious defense against feeling discarded. [A/B]

Mirai's intervention converts that implicit mechanism into explicit self-knowledge. Azusa finally says that being the only person Serina needed felt good, that she liked Amika's dependent hand, and that Amika choosing color guard felt like being abandoned. `HIBIKE-V06 / S04 / P0394-P0404`

### 3.4 `青春ベルアップ`: visible adulthood without pretending invulnerability

By the final chapter, the central question is no longer whether Azusa can identify the problem. It is whether she can perform under pressure without rebuilding the old defense.

Mirai's injury creates the greatest temptation: Azusa decides she has to become the absent senior, works through vomiting and fever, and nearly creates another accident. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0308-P0389` The eventual “bell up” is not the discovery that she can carry everything. It is the reverse. Azusa learns to accept support, perform the inherited solo in **her own sound**, and stand in the national spotlight without treating the role as proof that she must be Mirai.

The epilogue then converts personal success into transmission. Azusa has become captain and future professional-track musician; the institutional question is whether she can hand forward what she received. She does. `HIBIKE-V06 / S06 / P0012-P0051`

## 4. The childhood prologue: usefulness begins before Rikka

HIBIKE-V05 suggested that an economically secure but often-empty single-parent home helped make self-management and usefulness emotionally stabilizing for Azusa. V06 strengthens the mechanism with direct childhood evidence and makes it less speculative.

As an elementary-school child, Azusa overhears her mother worrying about leaving her alone during summer vacation despite calling her reliable: `あの子もまだ子供なんやから`. `HIBIKE-V06 / S01 / P0005-P0009` Azusa does not respond by asking her mother to work less or by expressing loneliness. She converts the adult's concern into a problem she can solve. When she sees the school brass-band notice and learns that it operates through summer, her internal conclusion is immediate: **this can remove her mother's worry.** `S01 / P0012-P0029`

This is not proof that Azusa's entire musical identity is reducible to maternal burden management. The source later repeatedly establishes that she genuinely loves music, practice, improvement, performance, and ambition. But it establishes an important developmental ordering:

> **Music first enters Azusa's life as both pleasure/opportunity and a way for a child to make herself easier for a working parent to manage.**

That matters because V06 later shows the same transformation under stress. When others are worried, Azusa's reflex is to become more useful. When Mirai is injured and the band feels unsafe, she decides she must become good enough to remove everyone else's anxiety. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0303-P0310`

The mature problem is therefore not simply “perfectionism.” It is a learned causal rule:

`someone I care about is worried` → `I should increase my competence/usefulness` → `the relationship/environment will become safe again`

The rule works often enough to become powerful. V06 is the first book that forces Azusa to learn where it fails.

## 5. Azusa and Amika: independence is not abandonment

### 5.1 Shiho identifies the reversal before Azusa can

When Azusa sees Momoka reducing Amika to tears during color-guard training, her immediate impulse is intervention. The coaching is severe: `泣いたら上手くなる？` and `立華には代わりなんていくらでもいる`. `HIBIKE-V06 / S02 / P0452-P0462`

Azusa reads the scene through the history of the helpless beginner she protected. Shiho reads a different state. Her central accusation is structurally exact:

- `梓はいつまであみかに頼るの`
- `相手がおらんくなって困るのは、あみかやなくて梓自身`
- Azusa answers, `あみかには、うちがおらんとあかんねんて！`

`HIBIKE-V06 / S02 / P0469-P0496`

The argument is not that Momoka is automatically morally right because she is strict. Shiho's narrower claim is that **Amika is now subject to the same performance standard as other members**, and rescuing her from any harsh evaluation would restore the very exceptional beginner status Amika is trying to leave. `S02 / P0502-P0513`

For simulation, this produces a crucial distinction in Azusa's help behavior:

- if another person lacks capacity and asks/needs concrete assistance, Azusa is unusually effective;
- if another person is deliberately building capacity through difficult independent practice, Azusa may misread non-intervention as abandonment or cruelty and overhelp.

### 5.2 Azusa overcorrects from enmeshment to withdrawal

After being challenged, Azusa does not immediately discover reciprocal friendship. She attempts a mechanical fix: stop helping.

The shoelace sequence is an excellent behavioral micro-marker. Instead of kneeling to tie Amika's loose lace, Azusa merely points it out and watches Amika tie it herself. The thought that she may never tie it again hurts. The action is developmentally correct; the emotion remains organized around loss. [B]

The next stage is worse. Azusa begins refusing ordinary bids for closeness. When Amika asks to go home or practice together, Azusa distances herself while insisting she is not angry. Her interior line—`先に突き放したんは、向こうやし`—shows that anger is present but disowned. Shiho explicitly says she asked Azusa to create distance, **not close her heart**. [A/B]

This is a useful general modeling rule:

> When Azusa learns that a caretaking strategy is unhealthy, her first correction may be **binary suppression** rather than calibrated reciprocity.

She is a high-control character. Once a behavior is classified as wrong, her instinct is to remove it decisively. Emotional systems are less binary than marching errors.

### 5.3 Amika's ambition makes equality concrete

Amika's independence is not merely an abstract developmental goal. She acquires an achievement ethic of her own. After being praised by Momoka, she tells Azusa that she refuses to use effort as an excuse for losing: `努力って言葉を負けた言い訳にしたくない。勝った理由にしたいの`. `HIBIKE-V06 / S03 / P0461-P0469`

She then agrees that the target is not simply nationals but national gold. `S03 / P0470-P0476`

This matters because Amika is no longer asking to be recognized as “independent” in a generic adolescent sense. She wants to become a contributor whose performance alters collective outcomes. The cared-for person has developed standards that Azusa herself respects.

### 5.4 Repair does not erase attachment

Mirai gives Azusa a new model: friendship can evolve from one-way help into mutual help. `HIBIKE-V06 / S04 / P0418-P0427`

The following morning, Azusa initiates reconciliation. Amika does not demand restitution; she says there is nothing Azusa needs to apologize for. Azusa finally recognizes the fact her caretaking obscured: `最初から、彼女は強い子だった`. `S04 / P0437-P0445`

The bracelet sequence then restores physical closeness without restoring dependency. Amika's wish is that **everyone** reach nationals, and the pair directly exchange affection: Azusa says she likes Amika's positive quality; Amika hugs her and says `私も、梓ちゃんのこと大好き！`. `S04 / P0446-P0458`

The key transformation is therefore not “Azusa lets Amika go.” It is:

> **Azusa begins learning that a person can move from dependent junior to capable peer without leaving the relationship.**

### 5.5 Amika eventually becomes the helper

The strongest proof comes later. After Azusa collapses, Amika encounters Serina and pressures her to visit. That intervention creates the reconciliation Azusa had been unable to initiate for years. When Amika apologizes for interfering, Azusa instead thanks her: `あみかのおかげであの子と仲直りできたよ`. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0557-P0568`

This reverses the original dyad. The girl once carried by Azusa now performs care **for Azusa**, and Azusa accepts the help as help rather than as evidence of her own failure.

That is the duology's clearest evidence that reciprocity has become behavioral rather than theoretical.

## 6. Azusa and Serina: the old wound is mutual specialness, not one-sided charity

V05 deliberately left `梓にとって、私は何？` open because Part One supplied only fragments of the old relationship. V06 closes the main causal uncertainty.

### 6.1 Serina's ethics are anti-disposability

Serina first enters Azusa's history as the person who refuses social smoothing when it requires accepting exploitation. When classmates falsely blame Azusa for revealing a secret, Serina provides the factual correction and later attacks the value of `上っ面な友情`. She would rather be alone than participate in a friendship system where a person can be used and discarded. [A]

Azusa, by contrast, forgives partly to preserve the group atmosphere. The difference is not simply “truthful Serina versus fake Azusa.” Azusa values continuity and social peace; Serina values relationships whose commitment can withstand explicit truth.

This predicts their eventual catastrophic mismatch.

### 6.2 Azusa turns an outsider into a special friend—and then fears losing specialness

On a school trip, Azusa chooses Serina when others would rather exclude her, criticizes Serina's aggressive outsider posture, and persists in contact. Serina gradually permits access. She even refuses to use the same form of address as everyone else because she wants a distinctive relation: `ほかの子と一緒なんは、嫌やから`. [A]

Azusa enjoys the exclusivity. The problem appears when Serina succeeds socially. As other friendships develop, Azusa feels jealousy and begins withdrawing while rationalizing that Serina no longer needs her. `HIBIKE-V06 / S04 / P0259-P0263`

Serina correctly perceives the withdrawal and interprets it through her deepest fear: disposability. Her confrontation is explicit:

- `佐々木が私を捨てようとしてる`
- `ここまでアンタのこと好きにさせて`
- `私は、アンタと対等になりたかった`
- `梓にとって、私は何？`

`HIBIKE-V06 / S04 / P0271-P0300`

Azusa has an answer—she wanted to be Serina's friend—but cannot say it because the answer is contaminated by motives she is ashamed to own. Pride then prevents pursuit. The relation freezes into polite surface contact.

### 6.3 Mirai supplies the missing conceptual language

Azusa's breakthrough with Mirai is not that she learns Serina was entirely right. Mirai actively rejects that moral absolutism.

Azusa says that helping because she liked being needed means she was merely using people. Mirai responds: `利用して何が悪いん？` If both people receive something, use and affection are not mutually exclusive; moreover, `好きじゃないと` one would not do that much for another person. `HIBIKE-V06 / S04 / P0401-P0418`

Mirai then gives the direct counterexample: she likes and needs Azusa, **not because Azusa performs services for her**. `何かをしてくれるから梓のことが好きなんと違う`. `S04 / P0423-P0429`

This is the duology's most important moral correction because it prevents a false binary:

- selfish reward → fake affection;
- pure selflessness → real affection.

Takeda instead presents relationships as mixtures of need, reward, affection, competition, support, possessiveness, and ordinary enjoyment. The ethical question is whether the mixture permits the other person to remain a subject rather than a dependency object.

### 6.4 Reconciliation requires both girls to own the selfish part

The sick-day conversation finally makes the relationship bilateral.

Azusa says, `うちさ、芹菜のことちゃんと好きやった` and then admits the compromising part too: she wanted Serina to need **only her**. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0506-P0516`

Serina answers with her own compromised motive. After transferring to Kitauji and developing other friendships, she wanted Azusa to regret discarding her and to say that Serina was better than Amika. `S05 / P0522-P0529`

Then both state the attachment directly:

- Serina: `あのときの私には、梓が必要やった。好きだったの`
- Azusa: `うちだって、芹菜が必要やったよ`

`HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0530-P0533`

### 6.5 Relationship classification at V06

The evidence now supports a narrower and stronger classification than V05:

**Canonical relational state:** mutually special friendship/attachment with explicit affection, past mutual exclusivity, jealousy, abandonment sensitivity, possessive impulses, and a repaired channel for future contact.

**Not established:** an explicit romantic relationship or mutually declared romantic desire.

The prose carries enough intensity that a reader may reasonably discuss romantic or yuri-coded implications, but a simulation-grade model should not silently convert `好き` in this friendship context into a settled dating label. The text gives us something more useful than a label anyway: a detailed mechanism of how each behaves when specialness feels threatened.

Serina's side: confront, accuse, demand explicit relational definition, resist smoothing.

Azusa's side: deny jealousy, distance first, preserve pride, then—after V06 growth—become more capable of explicit ownership and repair.

## 7. Mirai: mentorship as non-instrumental recognition and individuation

Mirai is the decisive corrective figure in Part Two, but she is not an infallible therapist. Her authority works because she combines elite competence, humor, frankness about her own weaknesses, and a relationship with Azusa that Azusa already experiences as secure.

### 7.1 She identifies dependency without shaming the desire to matter

Mirai says Azusa is the type who feels safe when someone needs her. `HIBIKE-V06 / S04 / P0377-P0392` She connects Amika's move to color guard with the desire to stand at the same eye level and compete as an equal.

What distinguishes Mirai from Shiho is not factual contradiction. Shiho saw the same structure earlier. The difference is relationship conditioning. Azusa experiences Shiho's accusation as threat; she can accept the same diagnosis from an admired senior whose affection feels less contingent.

This is a simulation-grade reminder that **the same proposition delivered by different people does not have the same psychological effect**.

### 7.2 She refuses the purity model of friendship

Mirai's `利用して何が悪いん？` argument is easily misread as cynicism. It is almost the opposite. She insists that reciprocal benefit does not invalidate affection and that needing someone need not be justified by services. `HIBIKE-V06 / S04 / P0407-P0427`

That is why `私は梓のこと好きやで` matters more than generic reassurance. Mirai is giving Azusa evidence that **wantedness can be non-instrumental**.

### 7.3 Injury reveals the mentor's own self-editing

When Mirai collides during rehearsal, she immediately says she is fine despite a visibly swelling ankle. Minami condemns the concealment as ego because continued participation could endanger other marchers. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0131-P0165`

Mirai's fracture requires two months of recovery, eliminating her from nationals. In front of Azusa she remains composed and reassuring, but after Azusa leaves the infirmary she hears Mirai crying behind the closed door. `S05 / P0185-P0216`

This keeps Mirai's model reciprocal with Azusa's. The mentor who tells Azusa to rely on others also has a senior-role reflex to hide pain from juniors. Her virtue is not perfect psychological consistency; it is greater capacity to correct the pattern when confronted.

### 7.4 `梓はさ、梓やねんで`: inheritance without cloning

Mirai's injury hands Azusa the solo she wanted, but in the worst possible way. The institutional script is clear: Azusa is next in line. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0196-P0203`, `P0241-P0267`

Azusa responds by trying to reproduce Mirai. Her thought loop becomes `未来先輩みたいに`. She practices through breaks, ignores vomiting, denies obvious illness, and nearly collides during rehearsal. `S05 / P0308-P0389`

Mirai's correction is explicit:

- `なんでも頑張りゃいいってもんやない`
- `頼ることは悪くない`
- `梓はさ、梓やねんで`
- `私の真似をしてほしくてソロのポジションを託したんと違う`

`HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0396-P0421`

This is Rikka's strongest statement about succession. A mature institution must transmit standards **without requiring personality replication**. Mirai chooses Azusa because she trusts Azusa's capacity to become a good soloist as Azusa.

### 7.5 Injured Mirai changes role rather than becoming absent

After the fracture, Mirai returns as an elevated observer and special coach. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0576-P0600` She sees from above, calls problems, makes jokes, restores morale, and still contributes to the national performance without standing inside the formation.

This is a direct institutional analogue to the wantedness/function distinction established in V03 and V04:

> losing one function does not erase a person's relational or institutional value.

Mirai is replaceable as a marcher. She is not thereby irrelevant.

## 8. Receiving care: the skill Azusa lacks

V06 makes “receiving care” an explicit character-model dimension because Azusa repeatedly fails at it in different registers.

### 8.1 Concern becomes another obligation

After vomiting before nationals, Azusa tells Amika she is fine. When Amika says other seniors are worried, Azusa's interpretation is not “I am supported.” It is “I need to stop worrying them by becoming more reliable.” `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0311-P0325`

This converts care into performance pressure. The mechanism is self-amplifying:

`others worry` → `Azusa feels she has failed` → `Azusa works harder` → `health/performance deteriorates` → `others worry more`.

### 8.2 Overwork becomes an institutional safety problem

The book refuses to romanticize that loop. Azusa nearly collides during marching while febrile, collapses, and registers a temperature of thirty-nine degrees. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0354-P0389`

Mirai's objection is not merely personal-health language. If Azusa had influenza, coming to rehearsal could have harmed the entire organization: `よかれと思ってやってることが部内に悪影響を及ぼすこともある`. `S05 / P0396-P0404`

The ethical correction is important:

> Self-sacrifice ceases to count as responsible contribution when it transfers unmanaged risk to everyone else.

That principle also explains Minami's anger at injured Mirai earlier. Rikka's culture celebrates effort, but V06 builds an internal critique of effort when it suppresses safety information.

### 8.3 Her mother breaks the usefulness contract

Azusa's discomfort intensifies when the school calls her mother away from work. She immediately insists she can go home alone because she does not want to burden her. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0423-P0434`

Her mother's answer is direct: `仕事も大事やけど、梓のほうがもっと大事やんか`. `S05 / P0444-P0449`

This targets the childhood prologue with almost diagrammatic precision. The child joined band partly to eliminate the mother's childcare problem. The teenager is now told that being inconvenient does not demote her below work.

Azusa then performs a behavior almost unimaginable from the opening state: she asks, awkwardly, whether her mother would be put off if she requested a hug. Her mother embraces her and says `いつでも大歓迎`. `S05 / P0455-P0463`

This is not merely a tender family scene. It is a **behavioral acquisition**:

> Azusa can formulate a need, expose it to another person, and allow the other person to meet it without immediately repaying the service.

That capability matters directly for later simulation.

## 9. Rikka after the accident: resilience, redundancy, and the moral limits of elite culture

V05 established Rikka as a mature self-reproducing institution. V06 stress-tests that thesis under real disruption.

### 9.1 The accident exposes the physical stakes of embodied coordination

Marching is a tightly coupled system: eighty people move in different directions within a constrained floor while carrying metal instruments. When a sousaphone player slips one beat out of phase, Mirai cannot see her in time and the collision breaks Mirai's ankle. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0125-P0167`

Minami's safety speech is therefore not abstract discipline. One mistake can become a multi-person accident. She tells everyone to leave the frame immediately if something is wrong. `S05 / P0155-P0165`

This complicates the aesthetic language of machine-like precision used elsewhere. Rikka's formations look like engineered systems because they **are** systems with collision dependencies. The beauty is produced by risk-managed coordination.

### 9.2 Redundancy is institutional care

Rikka can continue because it has built substitutes before crisis:

- Taichi has practiced as a reserve and receives Mirai's marching position;
- Azusa already sits immediately behind Mirai in performance hierarchy and receives the solo;
- Shiori has enough operational knowledge to produce a detailed movement map overnight;
- Mirai can relocate into observation/coaching;
- the rest of the section absorbs the emotional and technical shock.

`HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0239-P0275`

This is the strongest demonstration so far of a principle first articulated around Asuka:

> **Making a role replaceable is not an insult to the person who occupies it. It is a condition of institutional resilience.**

The tragedy would be greater, not smaller, if Mirai's injury made the entire ensemble collapse.

### 9.3 Yet senior invulnerability is itself a hazard

Rikka also produces a cultural pressure to appear strong. Shiori observes that seniors cannot cry in front of juniors; Azusa hears Mirai's sob only after the infirmary door closes. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0233-P0238`

Mirai's initial concealment of pain and Azusa's later fever denial are structurally similar. Both are attempts to protect the group from one's weakness; both can increase risk.

The mature institutional lesson is therefore not simply “rely on the team.” It is:

> **A high-performance institution needs channels where weakness can be disclosed without converting disclosure into status collapse.**

V06 shows Rikka partly possessing those channels—Minami enforces them, substitutes exist, peers care—but not fully escaping the culture that makes disclosure difficult.

### 9.4 Elite intensity has a post-graduation cost

Mirai tells Azusa that an unusually high share of Rikka graduates stop playing after high school because they burn out. The school provides a concentrated level of shared purpose and practice that ordinary adult life rarely reproduces. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0630-P0640`

This is one of the duology's most important corrections to any romantic strong-school reading. Rikka is highly successful at **reproducing Rikka**, but the individual relationship to music after Rikka is less guaranteed.

Mirai's answer is pluralization: contest performance is not the only form of musical life. One can join ensembles, play individually, or find other stages. `S05 / P0635-P0640`

Azusa ultimately chooses the unusually demanding route of music college and a professional aspiration. `HIBIKE-V06 / S06 / P0025-P0029` That choice is hers; it is not evidence that every Rikka student should continue the same way.

## 10. Effort, talent, and what “fair” competition actually does

The duology's merit system survives V06, but becomes more psychologically and institutionally complex.

### 10.1 Fair selection still hurts

Rikka's dynamic rosters remain real. Reserves practice specific positions because they can replace current performers if they improve. `HIBIKE-V06 / S03 / P0455-P0456` Before nationals, the possibility of another member change continues to generate anxiety. `S05 / P0050-P0053`

No evidence suggests the selections are merely corrupt seniority politics. Yet fairness does not neutralize emotion. Students can legitimately fear being replaced, resent a more talented junior, or feel relief when retained.

The V2 rule therefore remains:

> **Procedural legitimacy and emotional pain are different variables.**

### 10.2 Shiho and Taichi illustrate opposite defenses against failure

Shiho's earlier strategy is to withhold full effort so defeat never establishes her true limit. Taichi's is to exploit aptitude and efficiency so that he can avoid the discomfort of sustained work.

V06 forces Taichi into the scenario his old philosophy postponed. Mirai's injury creates a real opening. He finally commits to residual practice, improves rapidly, and enters the national formation. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0247-P0268`

That does not retroactively prove Shiho was wrong to resent how easily he learns. The novel preserves the talent asymmetry. What changes is that talent is finally combined with sustained effort.

### 10.3 Amika rejects effort as consolation prize

Amika's formulation—`努力って言葉を負けた言い訳にしたくない。勝った理由にしたい`—is almost a manifesto for Rikka's outcome ethic. `HIBIKE-V06 / S03 / P0466-P0475`

But V06 surrounds it with counterweights:

- effort can be misdirected;
- effort can injure the performer or the group;
- effort does not guarantee every desired result;
- the seat-concert ensemble receives silver despite intense work;
- a person can contribute outside the final performing roster.

Rikka's mature position is therefore not “effort always wins.” It is closer to:

> effort must become effective skill, and effective skill must remain compatible with the collective system in which it operates.

## 11. Music and marching pedagogy: automaticity creates expressive bandwidth

HIBIKE-V06 contains some of the corpus's densest craft description because marching requires music, movement, spatial memory, stamina, visual uniformity, and safety to operate simultaneously.

### 11.1 The seat-concert loss demonstrates limited attention

Azusa's Kansai performance is technically accurate but experientially empty. Her fingers and embouchure reproduce learned motion while her mind is consumed by relational conflict. `HIBIKE-V06 / S03 / P0187-P0213`

The scene proves that trained automaticity can preserve correctness under distraction—but also that correctness alone does not satisfy Azusa's musical standard.

### 11.2 Skilled ears create asymmetric responsibility

Mirai explains that stronger players perceive ensemble defects that less developed players literally cannot yet hear. Senior correction is therefore sometimes necessary not because juniors are lazy, but because perception itself is skill-dependent. `HIBIKE-V06 / S03 / P0217-P0231`

That is a sophisticated pedagogical point:

> expert responsibility includes lending perception to people who have not yet acquired it.

It also prevents “just let everyone self-correct” from becoming a false egalitarian ideal.

### 11.3 National preparation shifts from movement to sound

After Rikka qualifies for nationals, Minami says the movement is now sufficiently developed that the next deficiency is musical. `動きだけで必死になってるあいだは、金賞なんか取れません`; merely producing the score's notes is no longer sufficient. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0004-P0011`

The sequencing matters. Repetition has automated enough bodily coordination to release attention for sound quality, dynamics, direction of the bell, and the relationship between formation and acoustic projection. `S05 / P0054-P0065`

This can be formalized as:

`motor instability` → `motor automaticity` → `available attentional bandwidth` → `musical/expressive refinement`

The physical and musical layers are not separate crafts stacked together. Each sets constraints on the other.

### 11.4 Enjoyment is deliberately trained, not opposed to discipline

After injury and illness damage morale, Mirai and Minami deliberately run an unstructured `Sing, Sing, Sing` session where members move and improvise freely. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0576-P0600`

This is analytically important because Rikka's motto—`We enjoy music / We love music / We are RIKKA`—could otherwise sound like branding pasted onto punitive training. V06 shows enjoyment used as a **recovery technology** and as part of performance quality.

At nationals, Minami tells the ensemble that the six minutes should become a memory of joy, explicitly permitting mistakes rather than allowing terror of error to dominate the final performance. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0750-P0758`

The strictest marcher in the band can therefore say both “mistakes matter” during training and “today I will forgive them” before performance. The registers are situation-dependent, not contradictory.

### 11.5 Azusa's solo becomes authorship rather than imitation

The national solo is described as strong but softer/different from Mirai's. The narration emphasizes that Azusa has pursued **her** best performance. In the isolated acoustic space of the solo, she experiences pleasure in being heard and wants more people to hear her own playing. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0781-P0786`

This is the payoff to `梓はさ、梓やねんで`.

Inheritance succeeds when the successor produces a recognizable self, not an authorized copy.

## 12. National gold: meaningful result, insufficient final meaning

Rikka wins national gold. The result is not ironic or narratively hollow. Azusa experiences overwhelming accomplishment and says directly, `うち、立華に来てほんまよかったです`. `HIBIKE-V06 / S05 / P0797-P0809`

The V2 analysis should neither trivialize nor absolutize that outcome.

It matters because:

- Rikka explicitly set national gold as its shared target;
- selection, practice design, injury response, and pedagogy were organized around it;
- Amika wanted effort to become a reason for winning rather than an excuse for losing;
- Azusa wanted the national stage since childhood;
- the ensemble adapted successfully after losing a central performer.

It does **not** prove:

- that every harsh coaching method was therefore ethically justified;
- that every selection pain was beneficial;
- that burnout is irrelevant;
- that the same practices would suit every institution;
- that result exhausts the value of the year.

The book itself refuses such simplification by placing the epilogue years later. The terminal image is transmission, not the trophy.

## 13. The epilogue: Rikka successfully reproduces itself

S06 leaps forward to Azusa's own retirement. This is the strongest longitudinal confirmation of V05's institutional thesis.

Azusa has become **club president**. `HIBIKE-V06 / S06 / P0012-P0015` Amika remains beside her, now a stable companion rather than a dependent beginner. Azusa has been accepted to music college and intends to pursue professional music despite recognizing the uncertainty of that path. `S06 / P0025-P0030`

A first-year trombonist, Erika, tells Azusa that seeing her perform years earlier inspired her to attend Rikka. `S06 / P0022-P0029` Recruitment through visible inherited excellence has reproduced itself: Mirai once functioned as Azusa's model; Azusa is now someone else's.

The jacket makes the transmission explicit. Azusa still treasures Mirai's old jacket, but has reserved **her own** for Erika. The sentence is deliberately repeated:

- Mirai to Azusa: `梓にはいちばん期待してるから`
- Azusa to Erika: `恵里佳にはいちばん期待してるから`

`HIBIKE-V06 / S06 / P0040-P0047`

Then Azusa repeats another inherited formulation: `お礼は本番の演技でええよ`. `S06 / P0048-P0051`

This is institutional reproduction at three levels:

1. **material:** jacket and visible artifacts;
2. **linguistic:** phrases and mentoring scripts;
3. **normative:** expectation that the successor become excellent enough to carry the institution forward.

Crucially, Azusa does not demand that Erika remain dependent on her. The old need-to-be-needed structure has been converted into a forward-facing expectation: *I believe you can become strong enough to carry this after I am gone.*

That is the cleanest completed answer to the Amika problem.

## 14. Voice/register update

### 14.1 Azusa

Azusa's baseline is sociable Kansai speech (`うち`, casual particles, fast repartee) with strong register control upward. She can sound easygoing while internally operating at much higher competitive intensity.

V06 adds a vulnerable-state sequence useful for simulation:

- **defended distress:** `大丈夫`, humor, minimization, task language;
- **pressured confrontation:** sharper, categorical assertions (`あみかには、うちがおらんとあかんねんて！`);
- **insight threshold:** halting first-person confession, shame markers, explicit ownership of ugly motives;
- **secure vulnerability:** direct `好き`, `必要`, apology, or request for physical comfort.

The progression is relationship-conditioned. Mirai can elicit confession earlier than Shiho because Azusa trusts her judgment and affection more securely.

### 14.2 Amika

Amika's standard-Japanese baseline remains open, emotionally legible, and affirmation-heavy. Her development is visible not through becoming harsher but through more substantive goal language: contribution, winning, independence, and deliberate intervention on Azusa's behalf.

Do not model maturation by draining the cheerfulness from her voice. The book explicitly treats cheerful performance as a self that became real through use.

### 14.3 Serina

Serina's Kansai register remains barbed, compressed, and confrontational. She uses accusation where Azusa uses smoothing. Even after socializing more successfully at Kitauji, the old directness remains visible when intimacy is activated.

Her speech is particularly sensitive to disposability. When she fears replacement or abandonment, she demands relational definition rather than settling for vague reassurance.

### 14.4 Mirai

Mirai mixes senior authority with jokes, teasing touch, exaggerated casualness, and unusually direct relational affirmation. She often uses lightness to make difficult material receivable. Yet injury shows the senior-role cost: she also hides distress from juniors until the audience is safe.

### 14.5 Minami

Minami's register is operational, calm, exact, and norm-enforcing. Anger tends to sharpen precision rather than become indiscriminate volume. Her reaction to Mirai's injury is a safety argument, not simply panic. Before nationals, however, she deliberately shifts into an affectively warm motivational register.

## 15. Embodiment and behavioral micro-patterns

V06 materially strengthens the embodiment ledger.

### Azusa

- uses bodily labor to regulate emotion: practice, steps, long tones, exercise;
- social threat manifests in stomach distress, clenched hands, held breath, nail pressure, and compulsive forward motion;
- under overwork, she vomits, denies illness, continues moving, and almost collides;
- hugs are normally received from secure others rather than requested—until she asks her mother directly;
- instrument protection persists even during physical crisis; after nearly falling she clutches the trombone;
- embodied marching becomes automatic enough that her body can continue when conscious perception is delayed.

### Amika

- visible crying during harsh training coexists with continued task engagement;
- touch remains affiliative: hand-holding, hugs, tying the bracelet;
- independent competence appears first in mundane physical acts such as tying her own shoes and later in color-guard performance.

### Serina

- conflict is strongly embodied: grabbing clothing, pushing, mounting during the old confrontation; later anger again uses proximity and grasping;
- grooming change—the haircut—functions as deliberate social self-reconstruction rather than passive makeover;
- she is capable of blunt exit behavior after emotional saturation rather than endless negotiation.

### Mirai

- touch is a mentoring language: shoulder pull, one-armed hug, head pat, hair ruffle, forehead touch;
- injury denial shows role-conditioned bodily suppression;
- after injury, she adapts physically to a new observational role rather than disappearing from practice.

These behaviors should be preserved in later simulation rather than translating all affect into dialogue.

## 16. Rikka and Kitauji: controlled comparison after the completed duology

Only after V05–V06 is it safe to compare institutions without reducing Rikka to a foil.

### 16.1 Formation problem

**Early Kitauji:** must create seriousness, legitimacy, and skill in an institution where norms are contested.

**Rikka:** inherits seriousness and must manage the costs of an already legitimate high-performance culture: succession, burnout, injury, replacement anxiety, and the ethics of extreme effort.

### 16.2 Adult authority

Kitauji V01–V03 concentrates large implementation authority in Taki while preserving bounded student authorship of the objective.

Rikka distributes more everyday reproduction downward: student leaders design formations, teach beginners, monitor roles, and regulate peers, while adults still provide expert and formal authority.

### 16.3 Redundancy

Kitauji learns to stop depending on singular charisma or irreplaceable players.

Rikka begins with reserve systems and succession chains already built. Mirai's injury demonstrates why that redundancy matters.

### 16.4 Cultural risk

Kitauji's early danger is insufficient seriousness and coercive atmosphere politics.

Rikka's danger is **overinternalized seriousness**: students can turn fatigue, pain, or help-seeking into evidence of personal failure.

### 16.5 Shared ethical endpoint

Both institutions become strongest when they distinguish:

- role from person;
- performance judgment from total human worth;
- responsibility from martyrdom;
- tradition from mere repetition;
- support from permanent dependency.

This comparison should remain provisional until later Kitauji volumes show whether those distinctions survive leadership succession.

## 17. Simulation-grade character states after V06

### 17.1 `AZUSA@V06`

**Stable strengths**

- socially fluent, observant, highly industrious;
- genuinely enjoys helping and teaching;
- ambitious and outcome-oriented;
- responsive to clear expert standards;
- capable of self-criticism and repair once defenses break;
- increasingly capable of receiving help and expressing personal need.

**Core mechanism**

Azusa seeks relational certainty through usefulness. V06 does not erase this tendency; it gives her an alternative source of certainty: **non-instrumental wantedness and reciprocity**.

**Likely behavior under ordinary strain**

- take on tasks before asking others;
- minimize personal discomfort;
- offer concrete assistance quickly;
- smooth status threat verbally;
- work harder when anxious.

**Likely behavior under attachment threat**

- initially deny jealousy or resentment;
- risk binary withdrawal if told her help is unwanted;
- after V06, more capable of returning with explicit first-person ownership rather than sustaining silent distance.

**Break condition**

Institutional disruption + fear of disappointing admired people can reactivate overwork and imitation. Mirai's injury is the clearest case.

**Negative constraints**

Do not model mature Azusa as cured of perfectionism, indifferent to being relied upon, or casually able to rest. Do not model her help as fake manipulation. Do not make her emotionally confessional by default; direct vulnerability still requires sufficient security or accumulated pressure.

### 17.2 `AMIKA@V06`

**State:** capable junior/peer in formation, emotionally open, more ambitious, and demonstrably able to care for Azusa rather than merely receive care.

**Likely behavior:** ask directly, maintain closeness while pursuing independence, interpret support as compatible with personal agency, and intervene relationally when she believes the other person is stuck.

**Negative constraint:** maturation does not convert her into an icy self-sufficient archetype. Her explicit goal is not distance from Azusa but standing on her own feet **inside** a continuing bond.

### 17.3 `SERINA@V06`

**State:** socially more integrated than in middle school, still prickly and anti-smoothing, reconciled enough with Azusa to permit future contact.

**Attachment rule:** if someone is special, vague or disposable treatment is intolerable. She prefers painful definition to ambiguous surface harmony.

**Negative constraint:** do not make her generically antisocial. She demonstrably learned to build multiple friendships. The harshest register is attachment-activated, not her only social mode.

### 17.4 `MIRAI@V06`

**State:** elite mentor who has moved from model performer to injured observer and then future predecessor.

**Mentoring rule:** identify the junior's actual mechanism, use humor/touch to reduce defensive threat, state expectations directly, and insist that succession produce the junior's own version rather than imitation.

**Negative constraint:** do not model her as invulnerable. She hides pain from juniors and needs private space to cry.

### 17.5 `TAICHI@V06`

**State:** aptitude-plus-effort finally activated by concrete opportunity. National substitution gives him evidence that sustained work can matter.

**Negative constraint:** this does not erase his efficiency-seeking temperament. Future effort is most plausible when stakes, role, and social accountability are concrete.

### 17.6 `SHIHO@V06`

**State:** still blunt and sometimes physically impulsive, but morally serious about equality and about not infantilizing Amika. Her friction with Azusa is often diagnostically useful even when delivery is poor.

### 17.7 `MINAMI@V06`

**State:** high-control operational leader whose strictness includes safety responsibility and morale management. She can pivot from exacting correction to explicit permission for joy at the performance threshold.

## 18. Longitudinal implications for the HIBIKE corpus

The Rikka duology adds several mechanisms that should now be treated as corpus-wide analytical questions rather than Rikka-only curiosities.

### 18.1 Dependency/autonomy is its own relational axis

Closeness cannot be modeled only as “high” or “low.” A dyad can be intensely close while becoming more autonomous, or apparently close because one party is structurally dependent.

### 18.2 Receiving care is distinct from giving care

Characters who are excellent caregivers may be poor recipients. Azusa proves that generosity cannot be used as a proxy for comfort with vulnerability.

### 18.3 Functional redundancy can be an ethical good

V03 Asuka/Natsuki and V06 Mirai/Taichi/Azusa converge: institutions should survive the temporary loss of a key member. That protects both institution and person from making continued participation a moral hostage situation.

### 18.4 Transmission requires differentiation

The successor must inherit standards without becoming a clone. Shindō→Asuka→Kumiko and Mirai→Azusa→Erika now form parallel transmission architectures.

### 18.5 Performance culture can create both excellence and self-erasure

A mature strong school is not simply “better” than a rebuilding school. Rikka creates exceptional repeatability and also conditions in which students may hide pain, equate rest with failure, or burn out after graduation.

### 18.6 Social performance can become authentic identity

Amika's cheerful persona and Azusa's socially edited competence both reinforce V04's anti-essentialist insight: repeated social behavior is not necessarily a mask concealing a purer untouched core. Practice changes persons as well as performances.
