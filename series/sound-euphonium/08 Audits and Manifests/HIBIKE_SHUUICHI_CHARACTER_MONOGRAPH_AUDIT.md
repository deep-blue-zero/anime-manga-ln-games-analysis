The audit is downstream of the completed Japanese-primary Phase-1 corpus. It does **not** treat the monograph's own internal validation as proof. It independently tests whether the artifact is sufficiently source-traceable, longitudinally disciplined, relationship-conditioned, responsibility-sensitive, linguistically constrained, uncertainty-aware, and falsifiable to advance toward simulation-grade authority.

Shuuichi presents a different reconstruction problem from Kumiko or Reina. The corpus gives him less sustained interior focalization and less explicit self-theory. His strongest evidence is therefore distributed across:

1. **romantic autonomy-respect generalized into a universal moral doctrine**;

### Audit result

> **PASS WITH MINOR REVISIONS — PROMOTION DEFERRED**

Shuuichi v0.1 passes the independent audit at the level of its core architecture. The central model survives direct checks against the locked Japanese prose:

- known-person baseline comparison rather than generalized social clairvoyance;
- practical care before abstract explanation;
- independent musical desire without entitlement to selection;
- thresholded romantic directness rather than baseline confidence;
- high attachment with relatively low day-to-day monopolization;
- real hurt under relationship suspension without coercive override;
- pragmatic procedural directness with authority;
- role-formal distance as emotional coping;
- under-correction as a genuine vice-presidential weakness;
- concrete support as a mature role identity;
- post-graduation continuity without total identity/schedule merger;
- ordinary Kansai voice whose emotional intensity is often carried by timing, body, and interaction structure rather than grand declarations.

Two narrow calibration drifts require correction before promotion from `active_provisional` to `audited_provisional`:

1. **R-01 — autonomy-scope drift.** The source strongly supports Shuuichi refusing to override a decision he recognizes as principally Kumiko's, and V14 extends this to successor-generation non-jurisdiction. It does **not** establish a universal moral doctrine that “people retain jurisdiction over their own decisions” in every domain. The rule must be bounded to decisions he recognizes as genuinely belonging to another person, especially intimate peer choices and post-role successor choices, while preserving his capacity for disagreement, questioning, warning, and role-based intervention.
2. **R-02 — burden-attention drift.** The monograph correctly models Shuuichi as quick to notice concrete, visible, immediately actionable burdens. Its phrase “when work distribution is uneven,” however, is broader than the evidence warrants. V11 explicitly demonstrates that he can fail to perceive or correct a **diffuse relational/corrective labor asymmetry** that his own softness helps shift onto Kumiko. The attention rule must distinguish visible task load from systemic social labor.

Neither finding requires rejection of the core thesis, state architecture, Japanese voice model, musical model, Kumiko relationship model, Team Oumae role, or simulation formula after calibration.

## 6. Semantic-fidelity audit — high-leverage claims

### 6.1 Familiar-person baseline recognition

The V01 claim is strong and correctly scoped.

Shuuichi does not infer Kumiko's entire psychology. He recognizes a repeated pattern in someone he has known for years. The prose explicitly frames his quick recognition as childhood-friend familiarity and gives him the diagnostic question:

> `もしかしてまたアレか？　周りに流された？`

followed by a warning that inability to state her own opinion will cause problems: `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0211-P0218`.

The monograph's phrase **known-person deviation / baseline comparison** is better supported than a global “socially perceptive” label.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 6.2 Hazuki: practical help without romantic appropriation

V04 establishes an important non-Kumiko calibration.

Shuuichi helps Hazuki with her tuba case and interacts normally. When he realizes her festival invitation is moving toward romantic pressure, the prose emphasizes discomfort, wandering gaze, and word choice intended not to wound her. His initial refusal is not cruel, and later romantic rejection remains concise rather than humiliating: `HIBIKE-V04 / S05 / P0040-P0073`; `S06 / P0075-P0099`.

This supports:

- practical helpfulness outside romance;
- embarrassment-sensitive refusal;
- reluctance to expose another person's vulnerable intent before they can say it;
- no “pity romance.”

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 6.3 Romantic threshold directness

The V04 confession is correctly modeled as **pressure-accumulated directness**, not baseline romantic fluency.

The sequence contains repeated false starts (`俺、`), breath regulation, partial statement (`好きやねんけど`), Kumiko's demand for a referent, strengthened grip, and then `お前を！`, with narration describing the final delivery as nearly desperate/throwing caution away: `HIBIKE-V04 / S14 / P0247-P0261`.

A model that generates immediate polished confession language would be less source-faithful than v0.1.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 6.4 Independent musical desire

The monograph's strongest musical rule is directly warranted.

In V07, Shuuichi explicitly says he does not think he will actually get the solo, yet practices because:

> `吹きたいから`

He then turns the same logic toward Kumiko's desire: `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0297-P0316`.

The correct causal abstraction is:

> **wanting legitimizes effort; wanting does not establish entitlement to selection.**

This is a strong model-defining rule and prevents both “musically incidental boyfriend” and “merit-entitled competitor” drift.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 6.5 Support without false technical hierarchy

When Kumiko asks whether he thinks she can do it, Shuuichi answers immediately that he does and says `お前ならできる`: `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0311-P0325`.

The monograph correctly does **not** inflate this into a claim that Shuuichi is the decisive technical adjudicator of euphonium soli. His support is about confidence in Kumiko and legitimacy of effort.

## 7. Minor semantic-calibration findings

### 7.1 R-01 — autonomy heuristic must be domain-bounded

#### Current drift

The artifact contains several formulations that are stronger than the total evidence:

- core thesis: `autonomy as a limit on what care is allowed to do`;
- compact formula: `If another person's choice is theirs to make...`;
- Section 10.1 heading: **“People retain jurisdiction over their own decisions”**;
- Section 10.1 confidence label: **“Highest-confidence heuristic.”**

The underlying evidence is strong. The problem is the **quantifier and scope**, not the mechanism.

#### What the source establishes

High-confidence evidence supports a strong non-override prior when Shuuichi recognizes the matter as principally another person's decision:

- Kumiko's festival time with Reina — V08;
- Kumiko's request to suspend the relationship — V09;
- retrospective acceptance of that painful decision — V14;
- successor generation having its own method — V14.

The uncited V12 Mayu/Taki probe strengthens the mechanism in a useful way: Shuuichi independently thinks Kumiko is better, is visibly bothered by Taki's decision, asks Kumiko whether she really accepts it, and only then relaxes when she says she genuinely does. That is **active concern + independent judgment + respect for her stated position**, not passivity.

But the corpus does not establish a universal anti-intervention theory across strangers, emergencies, safety threats, all moral disputes, or every institutional decision.

#### Required corrected formulation

Use a bounded rule such as:

> **When Shuuichi recognizes a decision as principally belonging to another person—especially an intimate peer's personal choice or a successor generation's post-role domain—he has a strong prior against overriding it, even when he dislikes the outcome. This is a domain-bounded autonomy policy, not evidence of a universal anti-intervention moral theory.**

Preserve:

- active disagreement;
- warning and questioning;
- role-based intervention;
- hurt and jealousy;
- V08/V09/V12/V14 continuity.

Do **not** weaken the non-coercive relationship finding itself.

Revision type: **REVISE**  
Severity: **minor**.

### 7.2 R-02 — practical-burden attention must distinguish visible load from diffuse labor

#### Current drift

Section 6.1 B states:

> “He notices when a person is carrying something, **when work distribution is uneven**, and when a task can be made easier through concrete help.”

The middle clause is too general.

#### What the source establishes

Shuuichi is reliably quick with **concrete, visible, immediately actionable burdens**:

- Hazuki carrying the tuba case;
- Kumiko carrying multiple garbage bags;
- waiting for Kumiko;
- taking task load;
- practical vice-presidential support.

But V11 supplies a direct counterexample to reliable detection of *all* unequal work distribution: Reina must tell him that his softness is shifting difficult correction and relational labor onto Kumiko.

The uncited officer notebook goes further: Shuuichi admits that he dislikes correcting juniors and would prefer everyone simply get along, while also knowing that this is insufficient for a Nationals-bound band.

#### Required corrected formulation

Use a distinction such as:

> **Shuuichi reliably notices concrete, visible, immediately actionable burdens—carrying, waiting, task load, practical help—and is quick to reduce them. He is less reliable at detecting diffuse institutional or relational labor asymmetries, especially when his own low-pressure style helps create them.**

This does not weaken the care model. It makes the care/institution contrast more predictive.

Revision type: **REVISE**  
Severity: **minor**.
