# Sound! Euphonium V2 — Kumiko–Reina Reciprocal Model Audit
## Directional compatibility, asymmetry preservation, conflict/repair handshakes, and cross-model falsification

## 1. Audit purpose and decision

This artifact audits the first completed pair of independently audited-provisional Tier-A character models in the *Sound! Euphonium* V2 corpus:

- `HIBIKE_KUMIKO_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md` v0.3;
- `HIBIKE_REINA_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md` v0.3.

The question is not merely whether the two documents contain compatible descriptions of the Kumiko–Reina relationship. Two summaries can agree because they repeat the same interpretive conclusion. A reciprocal model must do more.

For a shared situation, it should be possible to begin from **Kumiko's state, attention, appraisal, voice, and decision rules** and separately from **Reina's state, domain, attention, judgment, voice, and decision rules**, then obtain two outputs that:

1. can coexist in the same scene;
2. preserve genuine asymmetry rather than forcing mirrored feeling or identical motives;
3. generate canonical conflict when the characters' mechanisms diverge;
4. generate repair without erasing the disagreement that produced the rupture;
5. change when state, setting, domain, or third-party involvement changes;
6. remain source-traceable without using one model as primary evidence for the other; and
7. fail in identifiable ways rather than explaining every possible interaction after the fact.

### Audit result

> **PASS WITH BINDING BRIDGE CONSTRAINTS — NO MONOGRAPH PATCH REQUIRED**

The two v0.3 models are reciprocally compatible at the `audited_provisional` level. Their strongest shared conclusions are generated through materially different directional mechanisms:

- Kumiko tends to move from rapid social/perceptual detection through interpretation, editing, delayed ownership, and eventual first-person claim.
- Reina tends to move from domain classification and settled judgment toward compressed assertion, while attachment domains produce delay, embarrassment, repetition, reassurance-seeking, or bodily leakage.

Those mechanisms reproduce both the intimacy and the most severe conflicts in the source. The relationship is not being simulated by one shared “very close friends / romantic coding” variable.

No contradiction was found that requires changing either monograph. Several distinctions must nevertheless govern all future pair simulations. These are recorded in Section 14 as **binding reciprocal bridge constraints**. The constraints clarify how the two models connect; they do not revise either character's psychology.

## 3. What this reciprocal audit can and cannot prove

### 3.1 What it can test

This audit can determine whether:

- the two models produce compatible directional predictions;
- each preserves the other's autonomy and independent motives;
- state tags align without backporting later capacities;
- one model's action supplies a plausible trigger for the other's response;
- musical selection, emotional priority, bodily comfort, formal status, and future expectation remain separate variables;
- conflict and repair can be generated from mechanisms rather than plot summary;
- the models survive uncited-source probes and controlled counterfactual changes;
- cross-model contradictions are visible and falsifiable.

### 3.2 What it cannot prove

Both monographs were built from the same locked Phase-1 Japanese corpus, deep readings, ledgers, and analytical method. They are not statistically independent models trained on disjoint evidence. Agreement therefore cannot by itself prove external validity.

This audit also does not complete:

- synthetic Japanese dialogue realization;
- voice-naturalness judgment by a native speaker or dedicated linguistic evaluator;
- laboratory-blind held-out testing;
- triangle-level reciprocal testing with independent Shuuichi, Taki, Mayu, or Asuka models;
- later-supplement contradiction review;
- adult long-distance modeling beyond the prose boundary.

The correct claim is **reciprocal architectural validity within the locked V01–V14 prose boundary**, not unrestricted character simulation.

## 4. Independence and circularity audit

### 4.1 Direction of construction

The Kumiko monograph predates the Reina monograph and contains no reference to `HIBIKE_REINA_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md`. Reina's monograph identifies Kumiko v0.3 as a completed counterpart and uses it in its internal reciprocal-validation section, but its substantive claims about Reina remain grounded in Japanese prose, locators, deep readings, and ledgers.

The counterpart model is therefore used as a **consistency target**, not as primary evidence.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 4.2 Citation-set comparison

Current-model locator metrics are:

| Metric | Kumiko v0.3 | Reina v0.3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fully-qualified locator occurrences | 101 | 211 |
| Unique locator strings | 75 | 81 |
| Expanded paragraph positions across occurrences | 2,392 | 4,067 |

Across the complete monographs:

- exact shared locator strings: **5**;
- shared expanded paragraph positions: **355**;
- union of expanded paragraph positions: **2,511**.

The low exact-string overlap matters because the documents do not simply cite the same scene list and paraphrase one relationship thesis twice. The shared paragraph overlap is expected: both characters inhabit many of the same scenes, and broad ranges can overlap even when the selected locator expressions differ.

This metric is supporting evidence, not proof of conceptual independence.

### 4.3 Lexical mirroring check

After removing names and source locators from the principal Kumiko–Reina relationship sections, the two documents share no exact English sequence of six or more words. Their thematic similarity is substantial, but their formulations are not copied mirror text.

Again, lexical difference alone does not prove analytical independence. In combination with distinct attention, decision, conflict, and voice mechanisms, however, it supports the conclusion that the two models are not one relationship summary split into two files.

### 4.4 Shared-source limitation

The models still inherit common Phase-1 interpretations, especially:

- relational plurality rather than a single zero-sum ladder;
- distinction between role selection and personal worth;
- V12 as principled rupture rather than arbitrary misunderstanding;
- formal relationship taxonomy remaining open;
- music as both intimacy channel and possible source of dependency.

The reciprocal audit therefore treats these shared theses as hypotheses to stress-test against directional evidence, not as premises that automatically pass.

Disposition: **PASS WITH EXPLICIT SHARED-SOURCE LIMIT**.

## 6. Directional mechanism audit

### 6.1 Kumiko → Reina direction

When the dyad is active, Kumiko's model predicts that she disproportionately attends to:

- Reina's sound and conviction;
- whether Reina chooses her musically or experientially;
- changes in physical distance;
- future-separation cues;
- who receives priority;
- whether musical selection is being experienced as relational judgment.

Kumiko's characteristic risks are:

- protecting Reina through concealment;
- reading role choice as total relationship value;
- treating shared symbolism as more explicit than Reina has made it;
- delaying direct admission of jealousy or replacement fear;
- expecting the relationship to survive through presumed understanding.

Her mature repair capacity is first-person and relational: she can state love/specialness, preserve disagreement, and expose herself to refusal rather than manufacture a neutral argument.

### 6.2 Reina → Kumiko direction

With Kumiko, Reina's model predicts:

- more humor and longer affective turns than in public musical speech;
- bodily proximity and touch permission;
- higher expectation of being understood;
- stronger injury from concealment;
- experience-specific priority and musical-partner desire;
- rejection and future-continuity fear;
- willingness to apologize after a serious rupture.

Her characteristic risks are:

- assuming Kumiko shares her standards/Taki framework;
- treating Kumiko's broader institutional concerns as diluted commitment;
- using current sound and trusted evaluation to totalize causal judgment;
- denying jealousy while behavior communicates it;
- expecting musical continuity to carry relationship continuity.

Her mature repair capacity withdraws the relationship-damaging claim while preserving standards.

### 6.3 Reciprocal result

The mechanisms are compatible because each person's vulnerability falls partly outside the domain where she is most competent:

- Kumiko is strong at reading relational complexity but less reliable when her own specialness is threatened.
- Reina is strong at settled musical evaluation but less reliable when outcomes depend on another person's autonomous choice or when institutional legitimacy exceeds sound alone.

The dyad therefore produces neither effortless mutual understanding nor arbitrary drama. It produces **high access plus relationship-specific blind spots**.

Disposition: **PASS**.

## 8. Asymmetry-preservation audit

### 8.1 Specialness is mutual salience, not identical theory

Both models may use “specialness,” but the term must not imply that the two characters conceptualize the bond identically.

- Kumiko often links specialness to selection, comparison, naming, musical equality, and fear of replacement.
- Reina links it more strongly to privileged access, truth expectation, shared experience, partner choice, bodily ease, and future mutual choice.

The relationship is mutually salient. Its internal meanings remain asymmetric.

Disposition: **PASS WITH BRIDGE CONSTRAINT**.

### 8.2 Musical equality is not automatically reciprocal self-description

Kumiko explicitly wants to stand beside Reina and become equal. Reina repeatedly chooses Kumiko as a desired partner and evaluates her seriously. The source does not require Reina to formulate that relationship through Kumiko's exact “equality” aspiration at every state.

Downstream simulation must not turn Kumiko's aspiration plus Reina's partner choice into an unsupported claim that both consciously hold the same equality theory.

Disposition: **PASS WITH BRIDGE CONSTRAINT**.

### 8.3 First choice is domain-specific

`いちばん` and first-choice evidence is strong in the V10 ensemble-selection context. It should not become a universal ranking in which each character must choose the other over every friend, lover, teacher, career, or musical obligation.

Disposition: **PASS WITH BRIDGE CONSTRAINT**.

### 8.4 Possessiveness has different triggers

Kumiko's possessive reactions are especially sensitive to:

- musical comparison;
- naming and privileged address;
- being a fallback;
- replacement by a better player;
- future separation.

Reina's are especially sensitive to:

- reserved first experiences;
- desired musical partnership;
- Kumiko practicing a shared role with someone else;
- rejection of an invitation;
- future mutual choice.

The models preserve this difference.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 8.5 Taki and Shuuichi remain real coordinates

The pair models do not require either:

- erasing Reina's explicit romantic intent toward Taki; or
- erasing Kumiko's romantic continuity with Shuuichi.

Nor do those other relationships make Kumiko–Reina intensity analytically meaningless. The model architecture correctly rejects a single zero-sum ladder.

Disposition: **PASS**.

## 9. Musical role versus relational role audit

The models must distinguish at least five variables:

1. who is currently the better performer;
2. who is selected for a role;
3. whom a character privately wanted as partner;
4. whom a character experiences as emotionally special;
5. whether the relationship continues after the role changes.

V10 and V12 provide the strongest stress tests.

### 9.1 Reina's side

Reina can:

- want Kumiko as partner;
- choose Kumiko when she judges Kumiko best for the requested role;
- accept Mayu as selected partner;
- perform professionally with Mayu;
- remain attached to Kumiko;
- repair with Kumiko without falsifying the performance judgment.

### 9.2 Kumiko's side

Kumiko can:

- want to be chosen first;
- feel musical replacement as relationship threat;
- suffer a legitimate loss;
- distinguish functional replacement from personal replacement;
- remain angry or hurt without sabotaging the role;
- continue the relationship after selection changes.

### 9.3 Reciprocal result

The role/relationship interface is robust. A simulator that produces sabotage, permanent rejection, or automatic emotional indifference after a role change is violating both models.

Disposition: **PASS**.

## 10. Conflict and repair handshake

### 10.1 Apparent tension

The Kumiko model says that resumed musical coordination becomes full repair only when paired with explicit speech. The Reina model says shared practice can reopen connection before complete verbal explanation.

These are not contradictory if repair is modeled in stages.

### 10.2 Required repair-stage model

#### Stage 0 — unresolved rupture

- task coordination may continue;
- neither ordinary politeness nor technically competent performance proves relational repair.

#### Stage 1 — channel reopening

- practice, walking together, waiting, or physical proximity can reduce threat;
- Reina is especially likely to use shared activity here;
- Kumiko may accept the activity without yet believing the issue is resolved.

#### Stage 2 — explicit acknowledgment

- the injury, disagreement, concealment, insult, or fear becomes speakable;
- Kumiko often uses first-person disclosure;
- Reina can retract a relationship-damaging claim and apologize.

#### Stage 3 — renewed chosen intimacy

- touch, teasing, practice, future planning, or ordinary time again carries low-threat meaning;
- underlying philosophical differences may remain.

### 10.3 V12 application

Kumiko initiates repair through explicit affection and bodily invitation. She does not retract the claim that Taki is imperfect or that questioning can be legitimate. Reina reciprocates affection, acknowledges Kumiko's correctness, and apologizes for `部長失格`.

This is not compromise by averaging. It is repair through **plural correctness plus withdrawal of the relational injury**.

Disposition: **PASS WITH BINDING REPAIR-STAGE CONSTRAINT**.

## 11. Voice and register handshake

### 11.1 Baseline asymmetry

Kumiko's private speech with Reina remains principally standard Japanese, with more direct questions, teasing, and reduced social editing than in public. Reina's private Kumiko register is Kansai-rich, affectively expanded, and more repetitive or embarrassed than her public musical speech.

Generated dialogue should therefore remain audibly asymmetric.

### 11.2 Conflict voice

In serious conflict:

- Kumiko tends to begin with qualification, explanation, or a question, then becomes sharply first-person when the issue reaches agency or legitimacy.
- Reina tends to state the settled premise more quickly and may compress disagreement into a declarative judgment.

V12 demonstrates that both can become direct without sounding interchangeable.

### 11.3 Repair voice

Kumiko can use a somewhat theatrical or awkward explicit declaration when presumed understanding has failed. Reina may answer with embarrassment, short affective language, and eventually a precise apology.

### 11.4 Current limit

This audit evaluates voice **specification**, not generated Japanese naturalness. No fixed synthetic dialogue suite was rendered and judged against source distributions.

Disposition: **PASS AT SPECIFICATION LEVEL; REALIZATION DEFERRED**.

## 14. Binding reciprocal bridge constraints

These constraints govern downstream Kumiko–Reina simulation. They are pair-interface rules, not new claims about either character in isolation.

### B-01 — Specify both character states

Never simulate “Kumiko and Reina” without explicit state boundaries. At minimum identify the source-era equivalent for each.

### B-02 — Specify the primary domain

Distinguish:

- musical evaluation;
- performance execution;
- institutional legitimacy;
- private attachment;
- future continuity;
- ordinary play.

The same disagreement changes when its domain changes.

### B-03 — Do not mirror specialness

Mutual salience does not imply identical motives, labels, or conscious theories of the bond.

### B-04 — Separate musical selection from relational selection

A selected performer is not automatically the emotionally preferred person. An emotionally preferred person is not automatically exempt from audition or comparison.

### B-05 — Bound first-choice claims

`いちばん` evidence is strong but context-specific. Do not convert it into total priority over every relationship and obligation.

### B-06 — Preserve naming focalization

Kumiko's possessive interpretation of being the only Kitauji person using `麗奈` is direct evidence. Reina's amused response is direct evidence. A Reina-owned exclusive naming demand is not established.

### B-07 — Model repair in stages

Shared practice or proximity may reopen the channel. Serious rupture is not complete until the relevant injury is acknowledged. Task competence is not proof of repair.

### B-08 — Preserve disagreement after repair

V12 repair does not make Reina reject standards or Taki, and it does not make Kumiko retract contestability or broader legitimacy. The relationship survives principled difference.

### B-09 — Do not erase Taki or Shuuichi

Other relationships remain real. Their existence neither cancels Kumiko–Reina intensity nor forces it into one formal taxonomy.

### B-10 — Keep future promises uncertain

The Niagara promise and other future plans are meaningful acts of coordination. They are not evidence that every imagined future occurs.

### B-11 — Touch is state- and setting-conditioned

High bodily comfort is well supported, but touch frequency, initiation, and meaning vary by public/private setting, stress, and state.

### B-12 — Do not use one model to fill the other's unknowns

If Reina's motive is open, Kumiko's interpretation does not settle it. If Kumiko's private meaning is open, Reina's expectation does not settle it. Preserve `G / underdetermined` where the prose does.
