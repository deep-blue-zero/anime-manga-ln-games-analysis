# Sound! Euphonium V2 — Asuka Character Monograph Audit
## Pro-level adversarial promotion audit of `HIBIKE_ASUKA_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md` v0.1

## 1. Audit purpose and decision

This document independently audits the fourth Phase-2 Tier-A reconstruction artifact:

> `04 Character Modeling/HIBIKE_ASUKA_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md`

Target version: **v0.1**  
Target authority state: **active_provisional**  
Target declared readiness: **provisional_pass**  
Target Drive ID: `146tjbGt20wdQPa-p9XikNr_WClObPM2a`  
Target SHA-256: `015c6aae81508d761ae9191c0fe8435b25e7459e9d689a87e8d677013c695584`  
Target byte size: **109,912 bytes**

This audit was deliberately run as a **Pro-level adversarial semantic audit** rather than as a second drafting pass. Its governing instruction was to assume that the v0.1 architecture might be coherent, elegant, and wrong. It therefore did not ask merely whether the monograph “sounds like Asuka.” It attempted to falsify or materially narrow six high-leverage claims:

1. **jurisdiction-first decision policy**;
2. **function-first exposure policy**;
3. **social-prediction strength without relational omniscience**;
4. **theatricality as a multivalent native social language rather than a simple mask**;
5. **Kaori focalization discipline and relationship-category restraint**;
6. **adult continuity without pretending that post-graduation Asuka is unchanged**.

The audit also searched for:

- temporal-state compression hidden by publication-order retrospectives;
- direct counterexamples to “managerialization under threat”;
- relationship interpretations silently promoted from focalizer theory to Asuka-owned psychology;
- Japanese register distinctions omitted by the first draft;
- apparently supportive scenes that actually constrain the governing theory;
- uncited passages capable of falsifying the model;
- cross-model circularity with Kumiko, Reina, and Shuuichi;
- and claims whose causal confidence exceeds what the prose reveals.

### Audit result

> **PASS WITH TARGETED REVISIONS — PROMOTION DEFERRED**

The Asuka v0.1 monograph passes at the level of its central architecture. The Japanese-primary evidence strongly supports the following core reconstruction:

- Asuka is an unusually fast social, rhetorical, institutional, and musical modeler, but not an omniscient reader of interiority.
- She does not accept universal responsibility merely because she sees a problem.
- Once she recognizes a domain as hers, she can intervene with extraordinary force, frame control, technical precision, and tactical theatricality.
- Her performance register is not equivalent to falseness; it can persuade, defend, entertain, teach, play, or regulate distance.
- Competence is genuine pleasure and identity as well as autonomy-preserving leverage.
- Under high exposure she often translates desire into function, reason, procedure, or management.
- Her relationships with Kaori and Kumiko cannot be reduced to utility, yet the source does not authorize an invented formal category for Asuka–Kaori.
- Post-graduation material preserves the same recognizable machinery while showing greater choice, lower compulsory office burden, more voluntary mentorship, and more ordinary play.

The audit nevertheless found **four required revisions**. One is structurally meaningful rather than cosmetic:

1. **A-01 — temporal state compression and evidence-routing error.** `ASUKA@PRE_KUMIKO` combines first-year and second-year Asuka despite a real change from near-total non-jurisdiction to a protected low-brass domain. It also incorrectly routes V13 S03 successor-design evidence into pre-Kumiko chronology even though that scene explicitly belongs to the Taki/Kumiko-era third year.
2. **A-02 — threat-response sequence is incomplete.** The phrase “under personal threat she becomes more managerial, not less” misses a direct first-person refusal immediately before maternal violence. The source supports **brief authorship assertion followed by managerial de-escalation after escalation**, not uninterrupted management.
3. **A-03 — family-conditioned self-reference is missing.** Peer/intimate `うち` remains the baseline, but the mother-facing crisis uses `私` at precisely the moment Asuka asserts and then stabilizes the threatened self. This is a meaningful voice constraint.
4. **A-04 — relational-model inadequacy is conflated with perceptual failure.** The Yuuko/Mizore `保険` formulation is contested and reductive, not objectively disproven; the Aoi exchange proves a damaging function-first response, not whether Asuka failed to perceive wantedness, perceived it and refused jurisdiction, or perceived it and defended against answering.

These findings do **not** require rejection of the compact identity thesis, the jurisdiction architecture, the multivalent-theatricality account, the competence model, the music model, the Kaori uncertainty boundary, the adult continuity model, or the majority of the state sequence. They do require a bounded v0.2 patch before promotion.

## 2. Audit protocol

The audit uses nine complementary tests.

### 2.1 Canonical-target lock

Before semantic review, the target was re-fetched from its canonical Drive ID and fixed by:

- Drive ID;
- byte size;
- SHA-256;
- front-matter version and authority state;
- numbered modeling-section count;
- locator multiset;
- placeholder scan.

This ensures that the findings apply to the corpus-authoritative v0.1 artifact, not to one of the intermediate drafts used during construction.

Canonical target characteristics:

- **109,912 bytes**;
- SHA-256 `015c6aae81508d761ae9191c0fe8435b25e7459e9d689a87e8d677013c695584`;
- version `0.1`;
- status `active_provisional`;
- readiness `provisional_pass`;
- Sections **1–24 exactly once**;
- no actionable `TODO`, `TBD`, `FIXME`, or placeholder residue.

The literal strings `TODO`, `TBD`, and `FIXME` occur only in the monograph’s own validation report stating that no such placeholders remain. They are not unresolved work markers.

### 2.2 Mechanical locator audit

Every fully qualified source locator in the canonical monograph was parsed against the deterministic V2 locator indexes. For every cited range the audit checked:

- volume ID;
- section ID;
- first paragraph;
- last paragraph;
- range direction;
- and every interior paragraph position.

A range therefore fails if its endpoints resolve but an interior paragraph does not.

### 2.3 Primary-source identity audit

The audit independently hashed the locally materialized Japanese EPUBs against `HIBIKE_SOURCE_INVENTORY.md` / the Phase-1 source lock. The directly evidence-bearing Asuka volumes are:

`V01, V02, V03, V04, V07, V09, V10, V12, V13`.

`V08, V11, and V14` were also materialized and hash-checked for negative-control and omission testing. The Rikka novels V05–V06 remain within the corpus boundary but were not padded into Asuka’s evidence footprint merely for symmetry.

### 2.4 Hypothesis-falsification pass

The Pro pass treated each governing claim as a hypothesis with an explicit failure condition.

Examples:

- **Jurisdiction-first fails** if Asuka repeatedly intervenes across clearly unowned domains merely because she can, or if she remains passive inside a domain she directly claims.
- **Function-first exposure fails** if high-risk desire is normally stated directly before functional reasoning, or if functional language disappears after V03.
- **Prediction-without-omniscience fails** if the source consistently confirms Asuka’s motive claims as objective truth rather than character interpretation.
- **Multivalent theatricality fails** if theatrical behavior appears only when she is manipulating or concealing.
- **Kaori discipline fails** if the prose directly supplies a settled mutual relationship label or Asuka-owned abandonment-test policy.
- **Adult continuity fails** if later Asuka either loses her characteristic regulation machinery or remains behaviorally identical despite retirement and changed context.

### 2.5 Temporal-routing audit

Because V10 and V13 contain retrospective material from different school years, the audit reconstructed scene chronology independently of publication order. A state was considered justified when the source showed a meaningful change in at least one of:

- perceived jurisdiction;
- decision policy;
- role responsibility;
- relationship access;
- disclosure threshold;
- institutional reasoning;
- or available knowledge.

### 2.6 Relationship and focalization audit

Every high-leverage relationship claim was classified by evidence ownership:

- Asuka’s direct speech/action;
- viewpoint observation;
- another character’s theory;
- repeated narrative pattern;
- analytical inference;
- or open question.

This is especially important for Kaori, Natsuki, Aoi, Kumiko, and the Yuuko/Mizore interpretation.

### 2.7 Japanese voice-specification audit

The audit checked:

- self-reference;
- addressee-conditioned address;
- Kansai morphology;
- theatrical/expository register;
- low-voice persuasion;
- cold compression;
- mock-polite aggression;
- family-crisis speech;
- post-recognition simplicity;
- and mentor/diagnostic speech.

This stage audits the **specification**, not a full synthetic-dialogue suite. Independent Japanese realization remains a later gate.

### 2.8 Uncited-source backtesting

Six passages not included in the monograph’s v0.1 locator set were selected before adjudication and tested against the model. A probe passes only if the governing architecture predicts its direction without importing the passage after the fact.

### 2.9 Preliminary reciprocal review

The audit compared Asuka v0.1 directionally with the audited-provisional Kumiko, Reina, and Shuuichi models. These checks test obvious incompatibility and circular reconstruction; they do not replace a dedicated reciprocal audit.

## 6. Pro-level adversarial audit of the six governing claims

### 6.1 Claim 1 — jurisdiction-first policy

### v0.1 claim

Asuka first determines whether a problem belongs to a domain she owns or has chosen to own. Outside that domain she can refuse the hero/reformer role despite understanding the problem; inside it she may seize control aggressively.

### Adversarial evidence

First-year V10 supplies the strongest apparent challenge. Asuka is the only remaining first-year low-brass member, yet she says she did not stop the others from quitting, only wants to play euphonium, and dismisses Kaori’s appeal that she could reform the entire club: `なんでうちがそんなことしなあかんの`. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0020-P0074`

Second-year V13 looks almost opposite. Asuka’s noninterference has helped create a low-brass “kingdom,” and she enters the senior–Natsuki confrontation with overwhelming rhetorical force. Afterward she explicitly bounds the intervention: `今回だって、夏紀が低音じゃなかったら助けてなかった`. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0475-P0481`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0775-P0820`

An uncited V01 probe further constrains the policy. When Hazuki’s private problem does not affect A-member competition function, Asuka says it is honestly irrelevant to her, asks what benefit intervention would have, and is irritated that the discussion consumes practice time. `HIBIKE-V01 / S04 / P0670-P0704`

### Disposition

> **PASS, but the state model must be revised.**

The apparent contradiction is developmental. First-year Asuka has not yet claimed the low-brass social domain merely because she occupies it. By second year, low brass has become a deliberately protected local jurisdiction. The governing policy survives; `ASUKA@PRE_KUMIKO` does not.

The audit rejects two simplifications:

- “Asuka is secretly responsible for everyone but pretends not to be.”
- “Asuka never cares unless a competition result is affected.”

The source instead supports **selective, historically acquired jurisdiction**.

### 6.2 Claim 2 — function-first exposure policy

### v0.1 claim

Asuka tends to translate vulnerable desire into function, utility, reason, or management before stating what she personally wants. Under personal threat she becomes more managerial rather than more transparent.

### Supporting evidence

The V03 Kumiko confrontation strongly supports function-first defense. Asuka dismantles “everyone wants you back,” questions other people’s sincerity, proposes her own replaceability, and frames disappearance as the ensemble’s best outcome. Kumiko makes progress only when she concedes the functional argument and states her own unlaundered desire: she wants to perform with Asuka. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0540-P0590`

V04 confirms that functional compression persists after the apparent V03 breakthrough. Aoi expresses guilt and relational vulnerability; Asuka answers that the ensemble functioned without her. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0060-P0100`

### Disconfirming/qualifying evidence

The mother confrontation contains a direct exception. Ordered to say she wants to quit, Asuka begins:

> `私、部活を辞めたくな──`

She is struck before completing the refusal. Only afterward does she regulate the room, reassure her mother, reduce public collateral damage, and convert the crisis into a manageable departure. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0416-P0448`

### Disposition

> **PASS WITH REQUIRED SEQUENCING REVISION.**

The source does not support uninterrupted managerialization. It supports:

1. coercive binary demand threatens authorship;
2. Asuka produces a brief direct first-person refusal;
3. refusal triggers physical escalation;
4. she pivots into de-escalatory management, self-minimization, and protection of surrounding people.

This is more predictive than the v0.1 shorthand. It explains why direct truth can emerge under extreme threat even though function-first language remains the baseline in less binary confrontations.

### 6.3 Claim 3 — social prediction without relational omniscience

### v0.1 claim

Asuka often predicts behavior and institutional movement accurately while underdescribing the lived meaning of attachment.

### Supporting evidence

The claim is strongly supported in broad form:

- she rapidly reconstructs the Mayu/Kumiko/Reina/Taki conflict from limited cues in college; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0595-P0636`
- she identifies Yuuko’s hidden self-damage risk and Natsuki’s ability to disrupt presidential perfection; `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0189`
- she predicts Nozomi’s regret and later triages Nozomi/Mizore around actual destabilization risk; `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0171-P0185`; `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0538-P0560`
- she can still state compressed relational interpretations as if they were complete.

### Overreach found

The monograph calls the `保険` description and the Aoi exchange “two canonical failures.” That formulation overstates what the source establishes.

`保険` is Asuka’s functional interpretation of Yuuko’s place in Mizore’s emotional system. Kumiko rejects the language as morally inadequate. The scene establishes **contestation and reductive compression**, not an objective narrator-certified proof that Asuka’s diagnosis is false. `HIBIKE-V02 / S04 / P0448-P0468`

Aoi’s scene establishes that Asuka’s answer is function-first and injurious. It does not reveal whether Asuka:

- failed to perceive Aoi’s need to feel personally wanted;
- perceived it but judged it outside her jurisdiction;
- perceived it and protected herself from answering;
- or knowingly chose a harsh but bounded response.

The monograph later acknowledges this uncertainty in §21.8, but the core thesis uses stronger causal language than the evidence permits.

### Disposition

> **PASS WITH REQUIRED EPISTEMIC SEPARATION.**

The v0.2 model must separately track:

1. **behavioral prediction accuracy** — what Asuka expects someone/system to do;
2. **relational-model adequacy** — how completely her explanation represents the person’s lived attachment;
3. **response/care policy** — what she chooses to say or do after perceiving the situation.

A damaging response does not by itself prove a perceptual miss. A useful behavioral prediction does not prove a complete relational model.

### 6.4 Claim 4 — theatricality is multivalent

### Adversarial test

If theatricality were only a mask or manipulation device, it should diminish sharply in low-stakes scenes without an institutional audience.

It does not.

Post-graduation scenes include:

- a secret joint-concert surprise because secrecy is more entertaining;
- ghost-prank staging;
- mock-grandiose coaching;
- massage-chair play;
- wooden-sword fantasies;
- souvenir theatrics;
- cookie theft and exaggerated domestic whining;
- physical clowning around trusted people.

`HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0401-P0458`; `HIBIKE-V10 / S07 / P0096-P0175`; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0595-P0672`

### Disposition

> **PASS — NO REVISION REQUIRED.**

The best model remains:

> **Theatricality is one of Asuka’s native social languages. Its function must be inferred from context, not from its mere presence.**

It can conceal, but it can also create pleasure, recruit attention, teach, regulate embarrassment, soften intimacy, or simply amuse her.

### 6.5 Claim 5 — Kaori focalization discipline

### Adversarial test

The audit searched for direct Asuka-owned confirmation of:

- a general abandonment-testing policy;
- a settled mutual romantic label;
- a direct answer to Kaori’s graduation declaration;
- or a simple equivalence between cohabitation and canonical dating status.

None is supplied within the locked prose boundary.

Kaori’s view that Asuka’s harsh utility language tests whether others will leave is privileged and psychologically plausible, but remains Kaori’s interpretation. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0066-P0104`

The source strongly establishes relational density:

- Kaori’s love declaration;
- desire for primacy;
- bodily closeness;
- cohabitation wish;
- later actual cohabitation;
- rings and domestic coordination;
- Asuka’s unusually safe childish/playful behavior around Kaori.

`HIBIKE-V09 / S04 / P1369-P1394`; `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0076-P0116`; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0595-P0672`

It does not settle a formal taxonomy.

### Disposition

> **PASS — NO RELATIONSHIP-CATEGORY PATCH REQUIRED.**

The monograph correctly preserves both high intimacy and categorical uncertainty. A-04 should improve causal caution in related scenes, but the Kaori section itself is epistemically disciplined.

### 6.6 Claim 6 — adult continuity

### Adversarial test

The audit tested two opposite errors:

- adult Asuka is “healed” into a fully literal, transparent caretaker;
- adult Asuka is unchanged except for age.

V07 and V12 support neither.

Continuities include:

- teasing and theatrical regulation;
- strategic surprise;
- technical confidence;
- rapid systems analysis;
- physical affection;
- provocative reframing;
- pleasure in controlling the conversational frame.

Changes include:

- no current Kitauji executive office;
- voluntary rather than compulsory mentorship;
- greater exit freedom;
- more ordinary domestic play;
- more ability to return responsibility to current actors;
- less need to preserve the image of universally sufficient exceptional competence.

### Disposition

> **PASS — NO REVISION REQUIRED.**

“Context liberation” remains the best high-level explanation. Adult Asuka is not a different person, but changed context materially alters which parts of the same repertoire are necessary and which can become playful or chosen.

## 7. Required revision A-01 — temporal state split and V13 evidence rerouting

### 7.1 Current drift

The v0.1 state table defines:

> `ASUKA@PRE_KUMIKO` — first-/second-year retrospective material in V10 and V13

and describes Asuka as already having built a protected low-brass domain. It also routes V13 S03 complementary-leadership reasoning into the pre-Kumiko state and claims that Asuka already thinks this way before Kumiko’s class arrives.

This compresses distinct years and misdates one scene.

### 7.2 First-year evidence: non-jurisdiction even inside low brass

V10 S03 explicitly presents first-year Asuka as the only remaining first-year low-brass member. She refuses to stop peers from quitting, says she only needs to play euphonium, and dismisses Kaori’s invitation to reform the club. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0020-P0074`

Her policy at this point is not “protect the low-brass domain.” It is closer to:

- preserve access to playing;
- do not accept responsibility merely because others leave;
- do not let Kaori’s belief in her capability draft her into whole-club reform;
- retain chosen individuals only when Asuka sees a reason, without claiming universal care.

### 7.3 Second-year evidence: low brass becomes a claimed local jurisdiction

V13 S02 explicitly identifies Asuka as a second-year. She does not intervene in the broad conflict merely because she can. She remains uninvolved while the wider intergenerational abuse continues. Yet when Natsuki’s conflict becomes a low-brass problem, Asuka enters with total frame control, removes Natsuki, and then states that she would not have helped if Natsuki were outside low brass. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0475-P0481`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0553-P0571`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0585-P0612`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0775-P0820`

This is not merely “more evidence of the same state.” It changes the decision boundary:

- first year: shared part membership does not itself create responsibility;
- second year: low brass has become a protected domain whose disruption can activate forceful intervention.

That meets the modeling method’s state-boundary threshold.

### 7.4 V13 S03 is third-year, not pre-Kumiko

V13 S03’s successor-design conversation references:

- Taki’s arrival;
- the destruction of previous Kitauji norms;
- Kumiko defeating Natsuki for A membership;

## 8. Required revision A-02 — threat-response sequencing and simplicity conditions

### 8.1 Current drift

The compact thesis says:

> “Under personal threat she becomes more managerial, not less; under genuine low-risk recognition she can briefly become startlingly simple.”

Both halves are directionally useful but too absolute.

### 8.2 Direct refusal precedes management

When Asuka’s mother demands an immediate false declaration of quitting, Asuka does not begin by converting the issue into utility. She starts a first-person refusal:

> `私、部活を辞めたくな──`

The sentence is interrupted by violence. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0416-P0422`

Only after being struck does Asuka:

- stop others from intervening;
- reassure the room;
- frame the problem as public disruption;
- calm her mother;
- absolve her mother;
- apologize to staff;
- and organize departure.

`HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0422-P0448`

### 8.3 Corrected causal sequence

The model should predict:

> **When autonomy seizure becomes binary and immediate, Asuka can assert first-person authorship directly. If direct refusal produces escalation or threatens collateral damage, she rapidly pivots into managerial de-escalation, room control, and self-minimization.**

This is compatible with function-first defense in ordinary high-exposure conflict. It simply identifies a stronger threshold at which direct authorship can break through before the familiar management system reasserts itself.

### 8.4 “Low-risk simplicity” is the wrong label

Shindō’s recognition is not psychologically low-stakes. It is highly important. What changes is not importance but **defensive load**:

- the performance has already happened;
- the evaluation is resolved;
- the recognition arrives without a demand that Asuka surrender control;
- Kumiko is a trusted recipient;
- no immediate bargaining is required.

The ensuing `うちも` is better classified as:

> **post-recognition / reduced-defensive-load simplicity**

rather than “low-risk simplicity.” `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1445-P1485`

The model should therefore allow simple first-person speech in two different conditions:

1. **reduced defensive load after recognized evaluation**;
2. **extreme coercive compression where authorship must be asserted immediately**.

The emotional valence differs, but both reduce Asuka’s ability or need to sustain elaborate functional framing.

### 8.5 Required patch

Revise:

- the compact identity thesis;
- crisis conflict policy;
- “managerial family-crisis register” description;
- “private low-risk simplicity” heading and rule;
- state-specific V03 behavior;
- generation algorithm;
- negative constraints;
- evidence matrix row that currently says the mother crisis produces “more management, not less.”

The revised row should encode **direct refusal → violence/escalation → managerial de-escalation**.
