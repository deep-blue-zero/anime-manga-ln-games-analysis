# Sound! Euphonium V2 — Kumiko–Asuka Reciprocal Model Audit
## Relational standing, jurisdiction, mentorship asymmetry, transmission, and cross-model falsification

## 1. Audit purpose and decision

This artifact closes the formal Kumiko–Asuka reciprocal gate explicitly deferred by the independent Asuka audit. It audits the interaction between:

- `HIBIKE_KUMIKO_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md` v0.3; and
- `HIBIKE_ASUKA_CHARACTER_MONOGRAPH.md` v0.2.

Both are already independently `audited_provisional`. The present task is therefore not to summarize their relationship, nor to decide whether Kumiko “understands” Asuka. It asks a stricter modeling question:

> **Can Kumiko's and Asuka's independently specified attention, appraisal, decision, exposure, care, voice, authority, and state mechanisms generate the same canonical relationship without collapsing mentorship into obedience, relational access into omniscience, or affection into jurisdiction?**

The audit is deliberately centered on three architecture-defined pressure regimes:

1. **V03 rupture** — Kumiko's objective/institutional arguments fail against Asuka's functional frame; the interaction changes only when Kumiko owns a nonfunctional first-person desire.
2. **V07 chosen mentorship** — school office authority has ended, affection is easier to state, but technical asymmetry and Asuka's deflective intimacy style remain.
3. **V12 institutional provocation** — adult Asuka can reconstruct the current conflict quickly and stress-test Kumiko's reasoning, but she no longer owns Kitauji's current decisions; responsibility must return to President Kumiko.

The pair must also explain the transition between those regimes. A model that merely reproduces one iconic V03 confrontation but cannot explain V03 graduation, V07 mentorship, or V12 post-role consultation fails.

### Audit result

> **PASS WITH BINDING MENTORSHIP / JURISDICTION BRIDGE CONSTRAINTS — NO MONOGRAPH PATCH REQUIRED**

Kumiko v0.3 and Asuka v0.2 are reciprocally compatible at the `audited_provisional` level. Their shared scenes are generated by different mechanisms rather than by a mirrored relationship summary.

Kumiko's side is characterized by strong observational appetite, sensitivity to register shifts and microaffect, initial deference to senior authority, a tendency to seek explanations before claiming personal standing, and a developmental move toward bounded first-person authorship. Her most successful interventions do not depend on proving that she knows another person's whole interiority. In V03 she succeeds precisely when she gives up that impossible claim and says what **she** wants.

Asuka's side is characterized by rapid social and institutional modeling, jurisdiction-first intervention, functional compression under exposure, rhetorical frame control, real but selectively expressed attachment, and care routed through competence, teasing, touch, transmission, or catalytic perspective. Her predictive skill is substantial but not omniscient; her postgraduation availability increases without restoring school authority or erasing defensive style.

The relationship therefore works because the two mechanisms constrain one another:

- Asuka can destroy Kumiko's weak universalized claims without being able to invalidate Kumiko's owned desire.
- Kumiko can become a rare relational challenger without thereby gaining veto power over Asuka's self-owned choices.
- Asuka can become a chosen mentor without becoming Kumiko's permanent decision-maker.
- Kumiko can inherit Asuka's music, techniques, and some institutional lessons without becoming an Asuka clone.
- Asuka's confidence can matter enormously to Kumiko while remaining insufficient to substitute for Kumiko's own authority.

The central reciprocal correction is therefore:

> **relational standing, perceptual access, technical authority, institutional jurisdiction, and decision ownership are separate variables.**

No canonical scene or held-out probe requires a patch to either monograph. The reciprocal layer instead adds binding interface constraints in Section 16.

## 6. Directional mechanism audit

### 6.1 Kumiko → Asuka mechanism

A reliable Kumiko-side model of Asuka interaction has five stages.

1. **Observe intensely.** Kumiko notices voice changes, eye behavior, technical competence, physical gestures, and inconsistencies between Asuka's public register and situational strain.
2. **Form a theory but retain uncertainty.** Early Kumiko is fascinated precisely because Asuka appears to be looking at a different world. Her observations are not automatic mind-reading.
3. **Try explanation or general argument first.** Under senior authority Kumiko initially seeks reasons, asks questions, and reaches for socially defensible claims.
4. **Under sufficient pressure, own a partial claim.** V03's mature breakthrough is not “I know what you really want.” It is “I want this.”
5. **Do not convert attachment into ownership.** Once Asuka's decision is clearly Asuka-owned, Kumiko can remain sad, ask for continued music, resist saying goodbye, and still refrain from claiming a right to decide Asuka's future.

This mechanism is consistent from V01 through V12. The observable behavior becomes more direct, but epistemic uncertainty and jurisdiction boundaries remain.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 6.2 Asuka → Kumiko mechanism

A reliable Asuka-side model has six stages.

1. **Rapidly classify the situation.** Asuka models people, incentives, role structure, and likely failure modes quickly.
2. **Identify jurisdiction.** If the problem belongs to her domain, she can intervene decisively. If not, she prefers bounded entry, provocation, or refusal of ownership.
3. **Use a high-leverage register.** Humor, theatricality, counterquestions, technical explanation, or cold compression can control the frame while limiting her own exposure.
4. **Treat Kumiko as unusually responsive but not omniscient.** Kumiko earns access because she keeps returning, asks difficult questions, can tolerate being unsettled, and eventually makes a nonfunctional claim.
5. **Route care through action more readily than mirrored confession.** Head-touch, teasing, coaching, transfer of repertoire, and confidence-giving often carry attachment.
6. **Hand current ownership back.** Postgraduation Asuka may diagnose Kitauji sharply, but the current leader must decide.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 6.3 Why V03 works from both directions

The V03 rupture is only reproducible if both models remain asymmetric.

Asuka challenges Kumiko's universalized `みんな` claim and points directly at Kumiko's tendency to stop before the final interpersonal boundary. Kumiko cannot beat the argument on Asuka's terms. The source explicitly narrates her sense that Asuka's logic is correct. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0540-P0557`.

Kumiko then changes the **type of claim**. She concedes the objective frame and states her own desire to perform with Asuka and hear her euphonium. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0561-P0571`.

That claim is powerful precisely because Asuka cannot refute it by proving herself replaceable. The claim does not say “the band objectively needs you”; it says “I want you.” Asuka can contest the first statement's evidence. She cannot disprove Kumiko's ownership of the second.

Asuka's response also remains character-specific. She does not answer with a symmetrical therapeutic disclosure. After Kumiko's fear returns, Asuka teases her for saying it if she is so frightened, then admits she was glad and reveals embarrassment behaviorally. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0572-P0590`.

The pair therefore passes only if **Kumiko's breakthrough is first-person authorship and Asuka's breach is reduced frame control, not reciprocal omniscient confession**.

### 6.4 Why V12 is not a repeat of V03

V12 recreates the sensation of being verbally cornered, but the jurisdiction is reversed.

Asuka can rapidly infer the Mayu/Reina/Taki conflict and provocatively suggest that Mayu's withdrawal would solve the problem. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0604-P0621`. Kumiko still finds the pressure difficult to answer.

But college Asuka no longer owns Kitauji's present executive domain. Kaori explicitly cautions Kumiko not to treat everything Asuka says as authoritative. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0622-P0628`. Asuka then asks what Kumiko wants rather than issuing the correct current policy. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0629-P0637`.

Her later analysis of Taki can improve Kumiko's model of the institution while preserving Taki's fallibility and effort. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0640-P0651`. The interaction ends in reassurance, touch, and humor, not transfer of current office. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0652-P0670`.

The mature reciprocal relation is therefore not “Kumiko finally becomes able to beat Asuka in arguments.” It is **Kumiko can deliberately seek Asuka's perspective without surrendering the ownership of what follows**.

## 8. Asymmetry-preservation audit

### 8.1 Relational access is asymmetric

Kumiko gains unusual access to Asuka, but not by becoming equivalently unreadable, rhetorically dominant, or technically authoritative. Her distinctive contribution is often the opposite: she can eventually risk a plain personal claim after elaborate arguments fail.

Asuka in turn becomes more available to Kumiko without adopting Kumiko's soft probing style. Even in affectionate postgrad scenes she remains recognizably teasing, performative, and competence-routed.

Result: **PASS**.

### 8.2 Perception is asymmetric

Kumiko is unusually attentive to Asuka's microbehavior, register changes, and emotional leakage. This is not evidence that she knows Asuka's whole causal history.

Asuka is unusually fast at modeling behavior, incentives, and institutions. This is not evidence that her compressed relational explanation captures the whole meaning of another person's attachment.

The pair is therefore most stable when each can be perceptive **and still wrong about a different kind of thing**.

Result: **PASS**.

### 8.3 Authority is asymmetric and stateful

Early Asuka has seniority, technical superiority, low-brass leadership, and informal club authority. V03 does not erase this.

Postgrad Asuka loses current office jurisdiction but keeps expertise and historical perspective. V12 Kumiko has current presidential authority despite still being vulnerable to Asuka's rhetorical and symbolic weight.

Result: **PASS**.

### 8.4 Affection is not mirrored speech

Kumiko becomes capable of unusually literal affective statements toward Asuka. Asuka frequently receives them through embarrassment, teasing, touch, or task transition rather than by returning the same verbal form.

A reciprocal simulator must not equate mutual importance with symmetrical wording.

Result: **PASS**.

## 9. Jurisdiction and mentorship audit

### 9.1 V03 relational claim does not create veto rights

The graduation scene is decisive. Kumiko thinks about asking whether Asuka will quit euphonium but suppresses the question because, if quitting is Asuka's decision, Kumiko does not possess standing to stop it. She can still say she is lonely and wants to hear more. `HIBIKE-V03 / S05 / P0033-P0043`.

This shows that V03's successful `私は` claim is not a general license to override Asuka. The relationship becomes more real precisely because Kumiko can own desire **without converting desire into entitlement**.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 9.2 V07 mentorship is chosen, not restored office

Asuka voluntarily offers one-on-one coaching and gives highly specific musical correction. `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0451-P0489`.

Yet she is no longer vice-president or current low-brass leader. She can teach, advise, tease, and evaluate because both parties accept the interaction, not because school hierarchy still compels obedience.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 9.3 Technical asymmetry remains legitimate

Relational growth does not require technical leveling. Kumiko can admire Asuka's sound, receive exact correction, and want to develop in that direction while retaining her own musical trajectory.

The pair therefore rejects a common simulation shortcut: “greater intimacy means less authority asymmetry.” Some authority dimensions can disappear while expertise asymmetry remains.

Disposition: **PASS**.

### 9.4 V12 consultation cannot become delegated presidency

The V12 visit begins because Kumiko chooses to invoke a one-time alumni-help channel. It does not authorize Asuka to reassume Kitauji office. Asuka diagnoses and provokes; Kaori constrains her rhetorical authority; the decisive question returns to Kumiko's own desired club; and Kumiko leaves knowing she is the current president.

Disposition: **PASS**.

## 10. Conflict and repair handshake

### 10.1 Ordinary friction

Low-level Kumiko–Asuka friction often takes the form:

1. Asuka makes an exaggerated, provocative, or instrumentally compressed claim;
2. Kumiko objects, questions, or reacts visibly;
3. Asuka laughs, teases, or continues the frame;
4. no major relational repair is required because the relationship can tolerate low-stakes asymmetry.

V02's `保険` dispute is a useful example. Kumiko rejects the relational reduction without requiring either party to confess hidden hurt.

### 10.2 High-stakes rupture

The V03 crisis follows a different sequence:

1. Kumiko approaches using socially defensible rescue claims;
2. Asuka dismantles their epistemic and institutional basis;
3. Kumiko's normal interpretive strategy fails;
4. Kumiko switches from third-person/general truth to first-person desire;
5. Asuka's frame-control advantage collapses because the claim is ownership-bounded;
6. Asuka acknowledges affect indirectly and behaviorally;
7. later music and transmission stabilize the new relationship state.

A simulator that jumps directly from argument to therapeutic mutual disclosure is wrong for both characters.

### 10.3 Post-rupture repair is not permanent transparency

V07 proves that V03 does not permanently remove Asuka's defenses. Literal affection still produces embarrassment and teasing. The growth is better access and chosen continuity, not full transparency.

### 10.4 V12 uses consultation, not repair

## 16. Binding reciprocal bridge constraints

These constraints govern downstream Kumiko–Asuka simulation. They are pair-interface rules, not monograph revisions.

### KA-01 — Specify both states

Never simulate generic “Kumiko and Asuka.” Identify the relevant Kumiko and Asuka state boundary first.

### KA-02 — Separate relational standing from jurisdiction

Kumiko's right to say what she wants does not create a right to decide Asuka's self-owned future. Asuka's importance to Kumiko does not create current authority over Kumiko's presidency.

### KA-03 — Preserve V03 claim-type change

The canonical breakthrough is not superior objective reasoning. It is the move from universalized necessity to owned first-person desire.

### KA-04 — Do not make Kumiko omniscient about Asuka

Kumiko is highly attentive to Asuka's leakage and register changes. Preserve uncertainty about hidden motive and full causal history.

### KA-05 — Do not make Asuka omniscient about relationships

Asuka can be behaviorally and institutionally predictive while still compressing relational meaning too aggressively. Operational success does not settle ontology.

### KA-06 — Preserve the authority gap historically

V03 intimacy does not instantly make them peer strategists. Early seniority and technical authority remain real.

### KA-07 — Postgrad access does not restore school office

`ASUKA@POSTGRAD_V07` and `ASUKA@COLLEGE_V12` may teach, advise, provoke, and reassure. They do not automatically own current Kitauji decisions.

### KA-08 — Technical mentorship may remain asymmetric after emotional access increases

Greater affection does not require equal musical skill or equal diagnostic authority.

### KA-09 — Do not mirror affective wording

Kumiko may state affection literally. Asuka often answers through teasing, embarrassment, touch, humor, or concrete care rather than repeating the same sentence structure.

### KA-10 — Preserve the one-time-help boundary

Asuka's alumni help channel is meaningful because it is bounded. Do not turn it into permanent remote presidency or unlimited rescue availability.

### KA-11 — Treat Asuka's V12 rhetoric as catalytic, not final authority

Asuka may force Kumiko to articulate a value or notice a structural fact. Kaori's explicit warning and Kumiko's later re-ownership prevent the model from turning Asuka into an oracle.

### KA-12 — Mentor reassurance is not self-sustaining confidence

Kumiko can be strongly buoyed by Asuka's approval and still lose that confidence when she returns to the task. Downstream action must be generated by Kumiko's own state and role.

### KA-13 — Transmission is not cloning

Notebook, repertoire, coaching, and institutional lessons may pass forward. Kumiko's mature voice and leadership must remain recognizably Kumiko's.

### KA-14 — Preserve third-party independence

Kaori, Reina, Shuuichi, Natsuki, Mayu, Kanade, and current leaders are not satellites of the Kumiko–Asuka relation. Their independent evidence may constrain either character's interpretation.

### KA-15 — Model conflict and repair in stages

For high-stakes rupture, permit frame collision → claim-type shift → indirect affective acknowledgement → changed action/transmission. Do not force immediate therapeutic debrief.

### KA-16 — Keep uncertainty explicit

When the prose supports a behavioral prediction more strongly than a motive claim, preserve that asymmetry in confidence. Pair consistency must never be used to manufacture hidden interior facts.
