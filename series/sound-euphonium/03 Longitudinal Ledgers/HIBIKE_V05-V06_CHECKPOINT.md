## 1. Purpose and authority

This checkpoint freezes the first complete V2 reading of the Rikka/Tachibana duology before the sequential project returns to Kitauji. HIBIKE-V05 and HIBIKE-V06 are unusual inside the current corpus because they have no V1 sequential-reading counterparts. They are therefore not revision exercises. ## 2. Frozen duology thesis

The Rikka duology can be compressed as:

> **usefulness-secured attachment → autonomy crisis → non-instrumental wantedness → reciprocal care → individuated succession and transmission**

This sequence operates simultaneously at the character, relationship, and institutional levels.

Azusa begins as someone whose competence is real but whose psychological security is partly organized around being useful. Helping others feels morally good, socially rewarding, and relationally stabilizing. The problem is not that her generosity is fraudulent. The problem is that usefulness can become proof that she is wanted.

Amika's growth destabilizes that contract. As Amika becomes more capable, Azusa can no longer rely on dependence as evidence of closeness. V05 therefore ends with an autonomy crisis: Amika moves toward color guard and toward a version of herself that does not require Azusa to carry her.

V06 demonstrates that simply stopping help is not the solution. Azusa initially converts non-interference into withdrawal, resentment, and a defensive claim that Amika pushed her away first. The mature alternative is **reciprocity**: Amika can remain close while becoming competent; Azusa can matter without being indispensable; both can help the other; care can circulate rather than flow one way.

Mirai supplies the conceptual pivot. She distinguishes being useful from being liked and wanted. She also refuses the fantasy of pure relationships uncontaminated by need, benefit, jealousy, ambition, or dependence. A relationship can contain instrumental benefits without being reducible to instrumentality, provided the participants continue to recognize one another as persons rather than functions.

The same structure governs Rikka itself. A mature institution cannot be ethically healthy if a single person's continued performance is required for survival. Mirai's injury therefore becomes a test of the school's design. Rikka has enough distributed expertise, documentation, role knowledge, and trained substitutes to reconfigure. Mirai remains deeply valued even when her marching function is replaced. Functional redundancy protects relational value rather than negating it.

Finally, succession does not mean cloning. Mirai's most important correction to Azusa is that Azusa must perform as Azusa, not reproduce Mirai. The epilogue then demonstrates successful transmission: Mirai's jacket, language of expectation, mentoring posture, and institutional standards pass through Azusa to Erika, but Azusa does not disappear into Mirai and Erika is not required to become Azusa.

## 3. Azusa state transition: competence without compulsory usefulness

### 3.1 The pattern predates Rikka

HIBIKE-V06's childhood prologue prevents a purely institutional explanation of Azusa's overfunctioning. Her mother worries about leaving her alone despite describing her as reliable, and young Azusa searches for an activity partly because joining something will solve that worry. Usefulness is therefore connected early to love, reassurance, and the reduction of burden.

That history matters because it keeps the model from making Rikka the sole cause. Rikka strongly rewards Azusa's existing dispositions, but it does not create them from nothing. The school offers a setting in which competence, anticipation, service, reliability, and self-discipline become highly legible virtues.

### 3.2 V05 baseline

At `AZUSA@V05`, the following are simultaneously true:

- she is genuinely prosocial;
- she likes being relied upon;
- she is highly ambitious despite modest social presentation;
- she fears disappointing expectations;
- she manages status threat through smoothing and self-control;
- she is better at giving care than receiving it;
- she can treat physical discomfort as proof of seriousness;
- she is socially perceptive but not automatically self-perceptive.

Her central defense is therefore not “kindness as manipulation.” A better model is **competence as both capability and attachment insurance**.

### 3.3 V06 crisis

Shiho names the reversal directly: the person endangered by Amika's growing independence is not necessarily Amika but Azusa. Azusa's own outburst confirms the mechanism: she insists that Amika needs her.

Azusa then attempts the wrong correction. She stops performing small acts of care and treats emotional distance as the price of respecting autonomy. This is analytically important because it distinguishes:

- **reducing control**, which is healthy;
- from **withdrawing affection**, which is not required.

A simulation-grade Azusa model should therefore not assume that once she recognizes overcare she immediately becomes relaxed and securely attached. Her first likely correction is overcorrection.

### 3.4 Receiving care is acquired behavior

Azusa's V06 collapse is not merely a work-ethic subplot. It exposes a missing skill. She can detect other people's needs, but she converts concern directed at herself into further obligations. When ill, she worries about the band. When people try to help, she tries to become worthy of the help by getting stronger.

The sequence with her mother is therefore structurally decisive. Her mother states that Azusa matters more than work, and Azusa finally asks whether she may be hugged. The hug is significant because it is care that Azusa neither performs nor earns. She asks; another person gives.

That behavior should be treated as a real state transition. `AZUSA@V06` remains perfectionistic and ambitious, but she has evidence that direct need does not automatically degrade dignity.

### 3.5 Mature V06 model

`AZUSA@V06` is best represented as:

- ambitious, socially fluent, highly observant and intervention-prone;
- still vulnerable to overfunctioning when a valued group is threatened;
- still inclined to interpret dependence as evidence of relational security;
- newly capable of distinguishing usefulness from wantedness;
- newly capable of asking for care explicitly;
- more able to tolerate reciprocal rather than one-directional support;
- more able to inherit responsibility without imitating a predecessor exactly.

Negative constraint: V06 does **not** make Azusa carefree, low-achieving, indifferent to being useful, or immune to jealousy. The change is an expanded repertoire, not personality replacement.

## 4. Azusa and Amika: attachment survives differentiation

The duology's most important relationship experiment is whether closeness can survive a reduction in dependence.

At the beginning, Azusa's care is asymmetrically competent. She notices, teaches, fixes, ties, guides, and anticipates. Amika benefits from that care, but the structure also risks keeping Amika in the role of recipient. Amika identifies the problem before Azusa fully does. Her turn toward color guard is an attempt to discover what she can do without being carried.

The crucial V06 correction is that **autonomy does not require emotional severance**. Azusa initially behaves as though she must choose between helping and leaving Amika alone. The relationship matures when both girls discover a third possibility: continuing affection with less structural dependence.

Amika's performance ethic makes that independence substantive. She explicitly refuses to use “effort” as consolation for losing; she wants effort to become the reason she wins. She wants a national result and a contribution that can be evaluated. She is not merely pursuing symbolic self-esteem.

The repair then demonstrates continuing affection. Azusa realizes that Amika was strong from the beginning, while Amika continues to express direct love and physical warmth. The relationship is not preserved by pretending nothing changed. It is preserved by changing its structure.

The strongest proof of reciprocity comes later when Amika helps Azusa solve the Serina problem. Azusa thanks her. This is exactly the kind of reversal that the dyad previously lacked: Amika becomes competent at caring for Azusa in a domain where Azusa cannot simply solve the problem alone.

### Frozen dyadic rule

At the end of V06:

> **Azusa–Amika closeness is compatible with rising autonomy because their relationship has begun to move from asymmetric caregiving toward reciprocal care.**

Simulation implications:

- Amika is unlikely to accept permanent infantilization once she recognizes it.
- Azusa is likely to feel threatened before she can consciously identify dependency loss as the trigger.
- Azusa may initially reduce warmth when trying to reduce control.
- Direct reassurance that Azusa is wanted apart from services is disproportionately stabilizing.
- Amika can now plausibly intervene on Azusa's behalf rather than merely receive Azusa's intervention.

## 5. Azusa and Serina: mutual specialness without forced romantic canonization

V05 deliberately left Serina unresolved. V06 supplies enough evidence to close several questions while leaving one major label open.

The old relationship was not simply Azusa helping a difficult outsider. Serina rejected superficial friendship norms and wanted a relationship with Azusa that was explicitly distinct from Azusa's ordinary friendships. Azusa, in turn, later experiences Serina's growing social independence as loss. Both girls wanted specialness.

Their confrontation makes the bilateral structure explicit. Serina accuses Azusa of making her care deeply and then discarding her. She asks what she is to Azusa and states that she wanted to become Azusa's equal. Azusa, meanwhile, is too proud to chase after her even though she could.

The V06 reconciliation is unusually important because both girls admit the selfish component. Azusa says she genuinely liked Serina and wanted Serina to need only her. Serina admits wanting Azusa to regret letting her go and wanting to outrank Amika. Both describe the other as necessary at the time.

This resolves the dyad as **mutually special friendship/attachment with historical exclusivity, jealousy, abandonment sensitivity, and explicit affection**.

It does **not** establish an explicit romantic relationship. The source does not identify them as dating, does not give an unambiguous romantic confession, and does not authorize a mandatory romantic label. A yuri-coded or romantic reading can be argued later, but it must remain an interpretation rather than being silently promoted to textual fact.

Simulation implications:

- Serina is highly sensitive to being made socially interchangeable after being offered specialness.
- Azusa may react to a valued person's growing network by preemptively withdrawing before admitting jealousy.
- Both can use confrontational language when the underlying issue is fear of disposability.
- Neither should be modeled as merely possessive; both also want equality and honest recognition.
- Post-reconciliation contact is possible, but the source does not establish the exact adult equilibrium.

## 6. Mirai: the standard who refuses to become a mold

Mirai's V05 status as the person being chased becomes substantially more complex in V06.

She understands Azusa's dependency pattern without moralizing it into ugliness. Her response to instrumental benefit is deliberately anti-purist: people use relationships to receive comfort, help, recognition, motivation, and security; the presence of benefit does not by itself invalidate affection.

More importantly, Mirai gives Azusa the missing form of reassurance: she likes Azusa and needs her without making that need conditional on service. This is non-instrumental wantedness stated by a person Azusa admires intensely.

The injury then humanizes the standard. Mirai initially attempts to continue despite a serious ankle injury. Minami has to expose the danger of hiding it. After the diagnosis, Azusa hears Mirai crying. The institution's exemplar is therefore neither invulnerable nor morally exempt from the same self-erasing habits that threaten Azusa.

Mirai's later instruction—`梓はさ、梓やねんで`—becomes the duology's succession principle. Azusa is not honored by becoming a copy. Mirai chose her because Azusa possesses a distinct musical and personal identity.

By nationals, Azusa's solo is explicitly her own sound. By the epilogue, she passes Mirai's expectation onward to Erika. The institution reproduces itself precisely because the successor is allowed to differentiate.

### Frozen mentorship rule

> **Healthy transmission preserves standards, artifacts, memory, and responsibility while refusing identity cloning.**

This now becomes a corpus-wide comparative principle alongside Shindō→Asuka→Kumiko.

## 7. Rikka as a mature self-reproducing elite institution

The completed duology establishes Rikka as more than “the strong marching school.” Its defining feature is that excellence has been converted into distributed practice.

### 7.1 What Rikka reproduces well

Rikka distributes institutional knowledge across students:

- beginners are taught by peers;
- section routines are locally governed;
- leadership is apprenticed;
- formations and routes are documented;
- multiple students understand the logic of marching and sound;
- selection is role-sensitive rather than merely seniority-sensitive;
- repertoire carries institutional memory;
- visible performance recruits future students.

This is why Mirai's injury does not destroy the organization. Taichi can take the marching position; Azusa can inherit the solo; Shiori has route information prepared; Mirai can continue contributing from a changed role.

This is not accidental resilience. It is an institutional achievement.

### 7.2 Redundancy as care

The duology sharply distinguishes **personhood from function**. Replacing someone's task does not mean replacing the person. Indeed, functional redundancy can protect people by allowing illness, injury, graduation, or role change without turning participation into an obligation owed to everyone else.

This converges with V03's Asuka/Natsuki logic and should now be treated as a general HIBIKE principle:

> **An institution becomes more humane when it can survive the temporary absence of a valued person without thereby implying that the person is not valued.**

### 7.3 The cost structure of elite culture

Rikka is not idealized simply because it is mature. V06 exposes at least three internal risks:

1. **pain concealment** — Mirai initially tries to continue despite injury;
2. **overwork as virtue** — Azusa tries to become Mirai, becomes ill, vomits, develops a high fever, and nearly creates another collision risk;
3. **post-graduation burnout** — Mirai notes that many Rikka graduates stop playing because the peak intensity of high school is difficult to reproduce or sustain.

The mature institutional judgment is therefore double:

> Rikka is exceptionally good at reproducing elite performance, and some of the same norms that support excellence can encourage self-erasure if not bounded by explicit safety and care norms.

### 7.4 Safety is an ensemble ethic

The injury and illness scenes convert self-care from private preference into collective responsibility. Hiding a condition can endanger other performers in a tightly coordinated marching environment. Rest is therefore not merely kindness toward the individual; it is part of competent ensemble governance.

Minami's intervention and Mirai's later correction both matter because they redefine strength. A performer who refuses necessary help is not automatically more committed.

## 8. Marching pedagogy: automaticity, perception, and expressive bandwidth

The duology provides a distinctive craft model compared with Kitauji's concert-band focus.

Marching is simultaneous embodied multitasking. Breath, tone, memorized music, spacing, direction, stride, visual uniformity, stamina, bell orientation, acoustic projection, and awareness of other bodies must be coordinated at once. Early training is therefore necessarily procedural and bodily.

V06 demonstrates why automaticity matters. When performers are still consumed by movement, they lack attention for sound. Once movement becomes sufficiently embodied, cognitive bandwidth becomes available for balance, dynamics, phrasing, expression, and spatial acoustics.

The seat-concert episode reinforces the same structure from another angle. Technically correct playing can remain emotionally or attentively empty. Mirai's explanation that stronger players perceive deficiencies weaker players cannot yet hear establishes **perceptual hierarchy** as part of pedagogy. Expertise changes what one can notice, not merely what one can physically execute.

`Sing, Sing, Sing` then functions as a deliberate morale technology. Enjoyment is not opposed to discipline; it is periodically cultivated to restore the desire that relentless evaluation can exhaust.

At nationals, Azusa's inherited solo becomes evidence of authorship. The achievement is not reproducing Mirai's sound but producing a strong, recognizable performance of her own.

## 9. Competition, effort, and fair pain

Rikka confirms an argument already emerging at Kitauji: fair procedures do not eliminate emotional injury.

Selection can be legitimate and still produce jealousy. A person can admire a rival and wish to replace her. A leader can fear the junior chasing her. A talented student can use aptitude as an excuse to avoid sustained discomfort, while another can withhold effort to preserve the fantasy that failure never tested her true limit.

Amika's formulation is especially important because she rejects effort as a consolation prize. She wants effort to explain victory rather than excuse defeat. This preserves competitive seriousness without allowing results to become the only measure of human worth.

The duology therefore supports a three-part distinction:

- **procedural fairness** — was the role assigned by defensible criteria?
- **performance truth** — how well did the person actually perform?
- **emotional residue** — what jealousy, fear, humiliation, relief, or pride remains even when the procedure was fair?

Mature analysis must keep all three.

## 10. Relationship and character-model dimensions frozen by the duology

V05–V06 permanently add two dimensions to the simulation architecture.

### 10.1 Dependency/autonomy response

For any important dyad, ask:

- Does the character experience being needed as proof of being loved?
- What happens when the other person becomes more autonomous?
- Does the character support independence, sabotage it, withdraw, overhelp, or renegotiate the relationship?
- Can the character tolerate closeness without indispensability?

Azusa demonstrates why ordinary “relationship closeness” scores are insufficient.

### 10.2 Receiving-care strategy

For each character, distinguish:

- competence at giving help;
- comfort asking for help;
- comfort accepting unsolicited care;
- tendency to repay care immediately;
- tendency to transform concern into obligation;
- vulnerability to shame when becoming a burden.

Azusa is high in caregiving competence and initially low in receiving-care comfort. V06 changes the latter without erasing the former.

### 10.3 Negative constraints

After the duology, downstream character simulations should reject the following flattenings:

- Azusa is **not** secretly selfish beneath fake kindness. Her kindness is real even when it also regulates her security.
- Amika is **not** simply weak or dependent; she actively seeks equality and competitive contribution.
- Serina is **not** merely hostile or antisocial; her aggression is tied to anti-superficiality, specialness, and disposability fears.
- Mirai is **not** an invulnerable ideal; she cries, hides injury, fears succession, and needs others.
- Rikka is **not** simply an authoritarian machine; distributed student agency is one of its strengths.
- Rikka is **not** simply a healthy meritocracy; its performance culture can reward dangerous self-erasure.
- National gold does **not** retroactively prove that every cost was justified.

## 11. Controlled comparison with Kitauji

Only after the autonomous Rikka model is complete is comparison useful.

### Kitauji V01–V03

Kitauji's opening problem is institutional reconstruction. Seriousness must be made credible; procedures must be established; student desire must become governable; authority remains unusually concentrated in Taki and several highly salient students.

### Rikka V05–V06

Rikka begins from institutional maturity. Seriousness is assumed and reproduced through routines, peer teaching, role documentation, succession, and embodied habit. Its question is not whether excellence can be created but **how a culture of excellence can remain humane and self-renewing**.

### Shared endpoint

Both institutions ultimately encounter the same ethical distinction:

> people should be deeply valued without being made functionally irreplaceable.

Kitauji discovers this through Asuka's threatened absence and Natsuki's substitution. Rikka demonstrates it more structurally through Mirai's injury, Taichi's replacement, Azusa's solo succession, and the epilogue's generational handoff.

The comparison also creates a useful diagnostic for later Kitauji volumes: as Kitauji matures, does it begin to reproduce standards through distributed practice, or does it continue depending on exceptional individuals?

## 13. Frozen institutional model

At the V06 boundary, Rikka should be modeled as:

**Strengths**

- high procedural maturity;
- distributed student expertise;
- embodied discipline;
- routinized succession;
- redundancy;
- high expectations;
- strong peer teaching;
- role-specific evaluation;
- expressive performance after automaticity;
- identity/recruitment through visible tradition.

**Risks**

- pain concealment;
- overwork normalization;
- prestige pressure;
- comparison anxiety;
- burnout after graduation;
- temptation to confuse devotion with self-neglect;
- trust in hierarchy becoming automatic compliance.

**Corrective resources already inside the institution**

- peers willing to expose unsafe behavior;
- role redundancy;
- mentorship that emphasizes individuality;
- deliberate play/enjoyment;
- explicit permission to rely on others;
- routes for changing roles without losing belonging.

This internal corrective capacity is important. The text does not position the critique as an external rejection of elite performance. The critique arises from people committed to the institution who want excellence to remain survivable.

## 14. Transmission architecture

The duology establishes one of the clearest succession chains in the corpus:

> **Mirai → Azusa → Erika**

What transmits:

- a jacket/artifact;
- language of expectation;
- performance responsibility;
- a standard of seriousness;
- the experience of being selected and trusted;
- the obligation to make the role one's own rather than reproduce the predecessor mechanically.

This parallels but does not duplicate:

> **Shindō → Asuka → Kumiko → future juniors**

The two chains together support a broader HIBIKE thesis:

> **institutional continuity is strongest when inheritance carries memory and standards while allowing successors to become different people.**

This principle should be tested explicitly in later Kitauji leadership transitions.
