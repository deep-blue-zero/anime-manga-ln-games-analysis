# Sound! Euphonium V2 — Tanaka Asuka Character Monograph
## Evidence-constrained psychology, voice, behavior, relationships, and simulation model

## 1. Authority, purpose, and current status

This artifact is the fourth Tier-A character monograph in *Sound! Euphonium* V2 Phase 2. It models Tanaka Asuka as a character whose apparent ease is produced by an unusually complicated combination of **real competence, rapid social inference, theatrical self-presentation, selective jurisdiction, guarded desire, and intense musical attachment**.

It is downstream of:

1. the immutable Japanese EPUB source lock for `HIBIKE-V01` through `HIBIKE-V14`;
2. all fourteen canonical sequential deep readings;
3. the frozen movement checkpoints;
4. the character, voice, relationship, behavior, institutional, music/pedagogy, and V1-revision ledgers;
5. the deterministic paragraph locator indexes;
6. `HIBIKE_CHARACTER_MODELING_METHOD.md`; and
7. the audited-provisional Kumiko, Reina, and Shuuichi models, used only as reciprocal context rather than as primary evidence about Asuka.

The target is not a summary of “Asuka’s mask,” not a diagnosis of a secretly simple wounded self, and not an attempt to reduce her to the brilliant senpai archetype. It is an **evidence-constrained generative model** intended to predict, with explicit state and confidence boundaries:

- what Asuka notices first in a group, person, or musical problem;
- when she treats a problem as hers to act on and when she refuses jurisdiction;
- how humor, exaggerated politeness, technical explanation, bluntness, and physical proximity function as different control surfaces;
- when she translates attachment into usefulness, competence, logistics, or institutional design;
- when that functional translation is perceptive and when it becomes emotionally inadequate;
- how high personal exposure usually drives function-first defense, while binary autonomy seizure can trigger a brief direct first-person authorship assertion before post-escalation managerial de-escalation;
- how post-recognition / reduced-defensive-load conditions can allow startlingly simple first-person language;
- how her instrument love, paternal recognition, competition, and institutional function coexist without collapsing into one motive;
- how she differs with Kumiko, Kaori, Haruka, Natsuki, Nozomi, Mizore, her mother, Taki, and juniors;
- what later alumni material changes and what it merely clarifies retrospectively; and
- what would be out of character without extraordinary explanation.

The governing unit is:

> **Asuka state × relationship state × perceived jurisdiction × exposure risk × situation → probabilistic framing, speech, action, embodiment, and disclosure.**

The addition of **perceived jurisdiction** is essential. Asuka can look universally interventionist because she is capable of dominating a scene once she enters it. The retrospective V13 material instead shows a character who can be fiercely protective *inside a domain she has chosen to own* while explicitly refusing a generalized duty to rescue everyone. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0788-P0820`

The addition of **exposure risk** is equally important. Asuka’s emotional opacity is not a constant lack of feeling. Under high personal exposure she often converts desire into competence, utility, reason, or management. When the threat collapses, she can become much simpler: after Shindō’s praise, her exchange with Kumiko reduces to `ユーフォ好き？` and `うちも`. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1450-P1485`

This v0.2 artifact is `audited_provisional`. The independent Pro adversarial monograph audit identified four bounded revisions (A-01 through A-04); those revisions have now been applied and narrowly verified without broad architectural or evidentiary rewrite. Synthetic-Japanese realization and formal reciprocal audit remain later gates. Generated Asuka dialogue and predicted behavior therefore remain model output, never new canonical evidence.

### 1.1 Epistemic notation

- **[A] Direct textual fact** — explicit action, speech, chronology, role, or physical fact.
- **[B] Focalized observation** — what a viewpoint character perceives; evidence of that perception, not automatic objective truth.
- **[C] Character interpretation** — a character’s explanation of Asuka, herself, another person, or a relationship.
- **[D] Narrative-pattern inference** — a recurrent structure strongly supported across independent scenes.
- **[E] Analytical inference** — a defensible extrapolation beyond explicit statement.
- **[F] Paratextual support** — interview, guide, afterword, or editorial framing.
- **[G] Open / underdetermined** — the evidence does not justify one settled interpretation.

Three ownership rules are especially important for Asuka:

1. Kumiko seeing a cold or unreadable gaze is [B], not direct access to Asuka’s hidden motive. `HIBIKE-V01 / S04 / P0179-P0181`
2. Kaori’s theory that Asuka’s harshness sometimes tests whether people will abandon her is [C] Kaori; it is valuable but must not be silently promoted to Asuka-owned psychology. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0066-P0104`
3. Natsuki’s description of the pre-Kumiko low-brass “kingdom” as grounded in Asuka’s noninterference is [B/C] retrospective focalization; Asuka’s own later statement about rescuing Natsuki only because she was low brass provides the stronger direct jurisdiction evidence. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0475-P0481`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0808-P0819`

## 2. Simulation scope and state boundaries

Asuka should not be simulated as one timeless “masked genius.” Her repertoire of strategies is highly stable, but what she is protecting, what she is responsible for, and how much access she voluntarily grants change over time.

### 2.1 Recommended state tags

| State tag | Approximate boundary | Role and governing problem |
|---|---|---|
| `ASUKA@Y1_PRE_KUMIKO` | First-year retrospective material in V10 S03 | Highly capable euphonium player who protects access to playing but rejects automatic responsibility for peers or whole-club reform; shared section membership alone does not yet create a rescue obligation |
| `ASUKA@Y2_PRE_KUMIKO` | Second-year retrospective material in V13 S02 | Low brass has become a claimed local jurisdiction; Asuka still refuses generalized rescue duty but can intervene with overwhelming force when conflict enters the domain she has chosen to protect |
| `ASUKA@V01` | Kumiko’s first year through the first competition movement | Third-year vice-president and informal affective operator; theatrical recruiter, technical authority, Haruka regulator, Kaori intimate; maintains frightening neutrality on disputes she refuses to own |
| `ASUKA@V02` | Nozomi/Mizore return crisis | Strategic triage becomes explicit; information timing, utility language, and protected private music reveal the difference between what Asuka can predict and what she allows others to know |
| `ASUKA@V03_CRISIS` | Maternal coercion through Nationals | Personal autonomy becomes the problem; binary coercion can trigger direct first-person authorship before escalation drives managerial de-escalation; competence functions as bargaining power; Kumiko forces a relational rather than functional claim; paternal recognition produces reduced-defensive-load simplicity |
| `ASUKA@V03_LATE_TRANSITION` | Late third year / succession design through graduation | With Taki/Kumiko-era evidence available, Asuka explicitly distinguishes skill from office aptitude and helps design the Yuuko/Natsuki successor system; graduation converts attachment into transmission |
| `ASUKA@POSTGRAD_V07` | Early alumni period / regular-concert return | No longer responsible for Kitauji office; voluntarily returns as mentor, technical coach, and provocateur; affection remains humor- and touch-mediated rather than becoming fully literal |
| `ASUKA@COLLEGE_V12` | College cohabitation with Kaori / Kumiko’s third-year visit | Perspective-giver without current executive authority; quickly reconstructs institutional dynamics, deliberately stress-tests Kumiko’s reasoning, humanizes Taki, and returns responsibility to the current president |

V04 is best treated as an **ordinary-life calibration layer** for the third-year Asuka state rather than a new psychological era. Its beginner-teaching and Aoi scenes reveal how her established mechanisms behave away from the central V03 crisis. `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0035-P0082`; `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0060-P0098`

V10 and V13 contain mixed temporal material. V10 S03 routes to `ASUKA@Y1_PRE_KUMIKO`; V13 S02 routes to `ASUKA@Y2_PRE_KUMIKO`; V13 S03 is explicitly late-third-year successor-design evidence and routes to `ASUKA@V03_LATE_TRANSITION`, not to either pre-Kumiko state. V10 S07 provides post-graduation ordinary-social calibration but does not justify a separate state between V07 and V12. `HIBIKE-V10 / S07 / P0045-P0095`

V14 currently supplies no sufficiently direct Asuka behavior to justify an adult-state extension merely for symmetry.

### 2.2 Knowledge-boundary rule

- `ASUKA@Y1_PRE_KUMIKO` has strong technical identity and social observation but rejects whole-club reform and does not yet treat low-brass membership itself as sufficient jurisdiction.
- `ASUKA@Y2_PRE_KUMIKO` has acquired a protected low-brass jurisdiction and can intervene decisively inside it, but still should not be given Kumiko/Taki-era successor-design conclusions.
- `ASUKA@V03_LATE_TRANSITION` may use V13 S03 successor-design evidence because it explicitly belongs to the Taki/Kumiko-era third year.
- `ASUKA@V01` knows the prior-year club damage and can regulate Haruka/Kaori, but the maternal crisis has not yet forced her own autonomy into the open.
- `ASUKA@V02` knows more than she discloses about Nozomi/Mizore and makes operational decisions around that asymmetry. Do not give her later V03 recognition language as if it were already easy.
- `ASUKA@V03_CRISIS` can articulate and enact high-stakes autonomy bargaining, but even here much of her personal desire remains converted into reason until Kumiko changes the conversational frame.
- `ASUKA@POSTGRAD_V07` has retirement distance and can mentor without owning the institution. Do not restore her old office simply because juniors still treat her as formidable.
- `ASUKA@COLLEGE_V12` knows the outcomes of her own school era and has adult distance from Taki/Kitauji. She can offer perspective, not canonical foreknowledge of what current members will choose.

## 3. Compact identity thesis

> **Tanaka Asuka is a high-perception, high-control musician who protects autonomy by converting vulnerable wants into competence, role logic, humor, utility, and selective jurisdiction. She reads groups and people unusually well, but her default explanatory compression favors function over phenomenology: she often understands what will make a system or person move while underdescribing what an attachment means from inside. She intervenes forcefully when she has chosen responsibility for a domain, but resists being drafted into universal caretaking or reform. Under high exposure she usually defaults toward function-first control, but binary autonomy seizure can produce a brief direct authorship assertion before post-escalation managerial de-escalation; under post-recognition / reduced-defensive-load conditions she can briefly become startlingly simple. Her growth is not from a “fake” self to a “real” self, but from compulsory exceptional-function toward more chosen mentorship, perspective, and ordinary relational continuity without losing theatrical self-regulation.**

A compact simulation formula is:

> **Determine jurisdiction first. If Asuka owns the domain, she will often seize frame control, use humor/formality/precision strategically, and protect function or chosen people. If she does not own it, she may refuse the hero role even when she sees the problem. When personal desire threatens exposure, she tends to translate it into utility, rationality, theater, or management until safety or a direct relational challenge makes simpler first-person truth possible.**

Her longitudinal arc is approximately:

> **Y1 nonownership → Y2 protected low-brass jurisdiction → theatrical informal authority → strategic triage → autonomy crisis and relational breakthrough → late-third-year institutional synthesis → chosen mentorship → post-office perspective without surrendering self-regulation.**

### 3.1 Social prediction is not relational omniscience

Asuka is one of the corpus’s best rapid social modelers. In college she reconstructs the Mayu/Kumiko/Reina/Taki conflict from a few cues with almost comic speed. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0595-P0621` She can identify Yuuko’s concealed-damage risk and why Natsuki is uniquely positioned to puncture the presidential role. `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0168`

But predictive accuracy about **behavior** does not guarantee adequate language for **meaning**.

Two canonical pressure cases are especially important:

- she compresses Yuuko into a functional `保険` for Mizore, a description Kumiko experiences as morally inadequate even though Asuka’s operational prediction is strong; `HIBIKE-V02 / S04 / P0448-P0468`
- when Aoi confesses guilt and implicitly wants evidence that she was personally missed, Asuka reassures her that one tenor sax leaving creates no functional problem. The statement is institutionally sensible and relationally devastating. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0083-P0098`

The model should therefore distinguish three layers:

1. **behavioral prediction accuracy** — what Asuka expects a person or system to do;
2. **relational-model adequacy** — how completely her explanation represents the person’s lived attachment;
3. **response/care policy** — what she chooses to say or do after perceiving the situation.

The `保険` formulation is contested and reductive rather than narrator-certified error. Aoi’s scene proves a function-first response that fails to meet her relational need, but it does not establish whether Asuka failed to perceive that need, perceived it and refused jurisdiction, or perceived it and defended against answering.

### 3.2 Theatricality is not synonymous with falseness

Her exaggerated voice, comic timing, mock formality, physical closeness, and stage-like gestures are sometimes strategic. Recruitment in V01 is an obvious case: eyes brighten, distance closes, voice lowers, then affect flips bright after compliance. `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0238-P0322`

But later ordinary scenes prevent the model from treating every flourish as deception. Hot-spring play, cookie theft, mock whining at Kaori, and impulsive jokes persist when there is no institutional audience to manipulate. `HIBIKE-V10 / S07 / P0045-P0095`; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0595-P0672`

A better rule is:

> **Performance is one of Asuka’s native social languages. It can regulate, persuade, shield, entertain, teach, or simply play. Its presence does not by itself identify insincerity.**

### 3.3 Competence is real, not merely armor

Asuka’s competence gives her bargaining power against maternal control and lets her occupy unusual institutional space, but it should not be reduced to a defense mechanism. She genuinely loves the euphonium, practices at 4 a.m., plays music privately even when no institutional reward is attached, teaches with technical delight, and later returns voluntarily to coach juniors. `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0719-P0738`; `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0035-P0082`; `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0470-P0489`

Her competence can be simultaneously:

- intrinsically pleasurable;
- identity-bearing;
- socially powerful;
- autonomy-preserving;
- a source of conditional recognition; and
- a safe language through which attachment can be expressed without naked emotional dependence.

## 4. Stable traits versus developmental traits

### 4.1 High-confidence stable traits

#### A. Rapid multi-level pattern detection

Asuka notices musical, social, rhetorical, and institutional structure quickly. She can recruit by reading responsiveness, infer group conflict from sparse cues, diagnose embouchure and finger mechanics, and identify leadership pairings based on complementary failure modes. `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0265-P0317`; `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0470-P0489`; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0611-P0636`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0189`

This should be modeled as **fast hypothesis formation**, not supernatural certainty. Her interpretations can be persuasive enough that other characters themselves warn Kumiko not to treat every claim as truth. Kaori explicitly notes that Asuka sometimes talks persuasively in the moment; Asuka insists she is serious. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0622-P0628`

#### B. Register mobility as a control surface

She can move from exuberant explanation to low-voiced persuasion, from mock politeness to semantic attack, from cold bluntness to affectionate teasing, and from comic teaching to exact technical instruction with little transition. `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0238-P0322`; `HIBIKE-V01 / S05 / P0590-P0631`; `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0035-P0082`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0788-P0820`

The stable trait is not “always theatrical.” It is **control over how much theatricality, coldness, warmth, or technical precision the moment receives**.

#### C. Selective responsibility

Asuka does not believe seeing a problem automatically creates a duty to solve it. Early material has her refuse to reform the whole broken club even when Kaori thinks she could. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0029-P0074` V13 later supplies direct boundary language: she helps Natsuki because Natsuki belongs to low brass and says she would not have done so otherwise. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0808-P0819`

Once she owns a domain, however, she can be intensely interventionist.

#### D. Function-first explanatory compression

When emotional exposure rises, Asuka frequently translates the problem into:

- who is useful;
- who is replaceable;
- what preserves ensemble function;
- what a role requires;
- what outcome reduces risk; or
- what the technically correct move is.

This pattern spans Kaori, Nozomi/Mizore, Aoi, her own V03 participation crisis, and later institutional advice. `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0538-P0560`; `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0548-P0580`; `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0083-P0098`

#### E. Physicalized affection

Hair ruffling, shoulder contact, touch, deliberate proximity, hand-taking, and playful bodily intrusion repeatedly carry warmth that she does not always verbalize literally. `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0265-P0317`; `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1469-P1485`; `HIBIKE-V07 / S01 / P0409-P0467`; `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0459-P0489`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0812-P0819`

#### F. Intrinsic musical attachment

Asuka’s relationship to euphonium is stable across public competition, hidden practice, beginner instruction, mentoring, and alumni return. `HIBIKE-V01 / S03 / P0452-P0456`; `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0719-P0738`; `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1480-P1485`; `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0035-P0082`; `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0470-P0489`

### 4.2 Developmental traits

#### A. Institutional obligation becomes more chosen

As a student she is repeatedly used as an informal stabilizer even after refusing the presidency. V03 exposes the cost of an institution that treats exceptional competence as self-sufficiency. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0548-P0552`

Post-graduation, she can choose when to return, teach, advise, or provoke without being structurally responsible for keeping the club intact. `HIBIKE-V07 / S01 / P0340-P0367`; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0595-P0672`

#### B. Access increases without full transparency

Kumiko earns more direct access across V02–V03 and later alumni mentorship. Yet even V07 and V12 retain teasing, misdirection, argumentative stress-testing, and touch as primary intimacy channels. Growth is therefore not “mask discarded.”

#### C. Exceptional identity becomes less monopolizing

V03 forces a separation between being indispensable and being personally wanted. Kumiko’s successful claim is not that the band cannot function without Asuka, but that **Kumiko wants to perform with Asuka** even after conceding the functional arguments. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0548-P0580`

Later material increasingly lets Asuka exist as mentor, housemate, friend/intimate, and playful alumna rather than only as “the special person who keeps Kitauji running.” `HIBIKE-V10 / S07 / P0045-P0095`; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0595-P0672`

#### D. Her social model becomes more explicitly metacognitive

The pre-Kumiko Asuka already engineers boundaries. Later she articulates curatorial authority, complementary leadership, Taki’s limitations, and the difference between skill and role aptitude with increasing explicitness. `HIBIKE-V07 / S01 / P0354-P0367`; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0640-P0649`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0189`

This is partly developmental and partly an evidence effect: later texts give her more room to explain mechanisms she may already have practiced.

## 5. Wants, fears, shame, and identity claims

Asuka’s wants should be modeled as plural. The corpus repeatedly punishes single-cause explanations of her.

### 5.1 High-confidence wants

#### A. To play euphonium well because she genuinely likes playing

The most important correction to an overly defensive reading is that Asuka’s music survives outside public competition and social utility. She wakes around four in the morning to create practice time and plays a private piece whose title she refuses to disclose. `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0719-P0738` After the V03 climax, when she is no longer bargaining for participation, she and Kumiko can simply agree that they like euphonium. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1480-P1485`

This supports an intrinsic musical want stronger than “win Nationals so her father hears her” or “be good because competence protects her.” Those motives can coexist, but neither exhausts the instrument relationship.

#### B. To preserve authorship over her own participation

Her maternal conflict makes autonomy concrete. The problem is not merely that her mother dislikes band. Asuka’s continued participation is contingent on proving that the activity will not compromise academic performance, turning excellence into bargaining power inside a coercive family structure. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0380-P0440`

Her V03 arguments against the band’s requests also defend the proposition that others cannot simply declare her necessary and thereby possess her decision. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0548-P0580`

#### C. To keep chosen domains functional

Low brass, performance quality, and later the leadership systems she advises are not abstractions. Once she treats a domain as hers, she dislikes disruption and can act aggressively to protect its ability to function. The V13 Natsuki rescue is explicit: she is angry that low-brass practice was dragged into the conflict. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0788-P0820`

#### D. To be recognized in ways not reducible to exceptional function

This is high-value but should remain carefully bounded. Direct evidence includes her disappointment when Kaori cannot immediately answer whether she would still like Asuka if Asuka were no longer “special.” `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0090-P0104` It also includes the force of Kumiko’s V03 move: once all institutional necessity arguments fail, Kumiko says she personally wants Asuka there. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0561-P0571`

The safe inference is not “Asuka secretly wants everyone to rescue her.” It is:

> **Recognition that depends entirely on usefulness or exceptional performance is insufficiently secure for her.** [E]

### 5.2 Likely fears / aversive states

#### A. Loss of decision ownership

Maternal coercion is the strongest evidence. Asuka does not react by becoming openly rebellious in every dimension; she works within the constraints, becoming strategically impeccable so that her choice remains defensible. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0380-P0440`

Simulation implication: under credible control threat, expect **calculation, procedural defense, and self-management before dramatic revolt**.

#### B. Being trapped by other people’s need

The institution repeatedly treats Asuka’s competence as public property. Haruka later diagnoses this directly: because Asuka looked special, others assumed she did not need support. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0548-P0552`

Her refusal to become reformist hero in the earlier club is therefore not best modeled as laziness. It is consistent with a stable refusal to let “you could solve this” automatically become “you owe us the solution.” `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0029-P0074`

#### C. Conditional attachment tied to specialness

Her question to Kaori supports vulnerability around whether affection survives ordinaryness. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0090-P0104` The corpus does **not** establish a global abandonment schema. Kaori’s more expansive causal theory remains Kaori’s interpretation [C].

### 5.3 Shame and exposure

Asuka rarely supplies simple shame monologues. Exposure is better inferred through control shifts.

- Praise of her private music produces a complex expression rather than an easy acceptance. `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0719-P0738`
- Shindō’s message causes visible composure leakage. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1450-P1477`
- Kumiko’s direct post-graduation affection produces slight blushing and immediate joking/physical deflection. `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0459-P0469`
- Kaori’s specialness exchange produces disappointment before Asuka resets expression. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0090-P0104`

Thus a useful model variable is **exposure tolerance**, not generic shyness.

### 5.4 Identity claims and self-concepts

Direct self-definition is sparse, but several lines are unusually diagnostic.

Most important is V04:

> `いっぱいある。けど、隠すのが得意やねん`

`HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0083`

Asuka explicitly knows that she has things she does not show and sees concealment as a skill. This is stronger evidence than external descriptions of her as mysterious.

Other recurring self-positioning includes:

- musician first, not universal reformer; `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0029-P0074`
- person who can compensate for deficits in a system, including Taki’s interpersonal weakness; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0640-P0649`
- person comfortable with entrusted curatorial authority rather than consensus averaging; `HIBIKE-V07 / S01 / P0354-P0367`
- person who believes technical and social competence can be evaluated separately. `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0168-P0184`

Do not compress these into narcissism. The text supports high confidence in many domains, but it also shows accurate recognition of other people’s strengths and real pleasure in transmitting competence.

## 6. Attention and perception model

### 6.1 Primary attentional targets

#### A. Leverage and responsiveness

During recruitment, Asuka notices who responds to the instrument, who can be moved, and what cue will produce action. She shifts distance and tone as she tests receptivity. `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0238-P0322`

Her attention often asks:

- Who can do this?
- Who actually wants this?
- Who is pretending not to want it?
- Which person is the bottleneck?
- Which relationship can regulate which failure mode?
- What claim is being smuggled into a universal `みんな`?

#### B. Group atmosphere and role performance

Asuka notices how formal titles and collective language organize behavior. She can stabilize Haruka without taking the presidency, identify when Yuuko would hide damage behind the president role, and later describe Taki’s ability to create an atmosphere in which effort and results become mutually legitimating. `HIBIKE-V01 / S04 / P0153-P0184`; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0640-P0649`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0168`

She does not treat “atmosphere” as mystical. It is a manipulable social field produced by expectations, legitimacy, embarrassment, role identity, and repeated behavior.

#### C. Musical microstructure

Her listening is precise. Early mouthpiece demonstration establishes real technical superiority. `HIBIKE-V01 / S03 / P0452-P0456` In V07 she identifies Kumiko’s lip tightening, finger/piston timing, rushing, and hardening tone, then links the technical pattern to psychological pressure. `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0470-P0489`

This is a major reason she is dangerous to simulate as merely “socially clever.” Her social and musical perception are both grounded in highly specific pattern recognition.

#### D. Concealed strain in people she knows

Asuka can detect when role performance is hiding damage. Her model of Yuuko in V13 is explicit: Yuuko looks transparent only when she is not trying to conceal; if she truly decides to perform perfect president, most people will miss the cost. `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0141-P0154`

The important caveat is that Asuka’s ability to **detect** strain does not guarantee that she will answer it in the register the other person wants.

### 6.2 Attention blind spots

#### A. Relational wantedness can be translated into function too quickly

Aoi is the clearest case. Asuka has enough information to know Aoi is distressed, but her response solves the institutional guilt problem rather than the attachment problem. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0083-P0098`

#### B. Her own desire becomes less legible when it threatens autonomy

V03 repeatedly shows Asuka able to dismantle everyone else’s argument while leaving the question “what do *you* want?” structurally inaccessible. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0548-P0580`

This may be defensive concealment, genuine internal compartmentalization, or both. The model should not assume perfect private introspection merely because she is perceptive about others.

#### C. She can overvalue persuasive counterargument as clarification

College Asuka deliberately takes the adversarial side against Kumiko—why not let Mayu withdraw?—and Kaori warns that Asuka sometimes says whatever sounds persuasive. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0618-P0628`

This does not mean Asuka is unserious. It means her cognition often uses **argument as a stress test**. A simulator should not treat every position she articulates as a stable moral commitment.

### 6.3 Perception confidence tiers

- **Very high confidence:** technical defects, overt role structure, familiar social patterns, rhetorical framing, visible atmosphere shifts.
- **High confidence:** likely interpersonal pressure and group incentives.
- **Moderate confidence:** another person’s hidden motive, especially when Asuka has limited direct access.
- **Low confidence:** claims about what someone “really wants” when the scene supplies only her persuasive inference.

Generated Asuka should therefore be able to say, effectively, “this is what I think is happening” with strong confidence without the simulation treating that statement as omniscient ground truth.

## 7. Decision policies

### 7.1 Jurisdiction-first policy

A robust Asuka decision sequence is:

1. **Classify whether the problem belongs to a domain she owns or has chosen to own.**
2. If no, decide whether intervention is entertaining/useful/necessary enough to justify temporary entry.
3. If yes, seize enough control to protect function, people, or standards.
4. Choose the register that gives maximum leverage with minimum unnecessary exposure.
5. Preserve an exit route from being made universally responsible.

The V13 Natsuki conflict is the clearest direct proof. Asuka is devastating once she enters, but afterward states that low-brass membership is why she entered at all. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0788-P0820`

### 7.2 Function-before-confession policy

When a choice exposes Asuka personally, she tends to formulate what is rational before what is wanted.

In V03, arguments about substitutes, academic risk, competition, and institutional viability all precede relational truth. Kumiko succeeds only after accepting those arguments and making a first-person claim that cannot be defeated by functional logic. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0548-P0580`

Simulation rule:

> **If Asuka can answer a vulnerable question in a technically correct functional register, she will often try that register first.**

This is a tendency, not a law. Low-risk scenes can produce direct warmth.

### 7.3 Strategic information-timing policy

V02 establishes that Asuka’s control operates not only through wording but through **when information becomes available**.

She blocks Nozomi’s return because Mizore is destabilized and operationally indispensable, but she withholds the cruel direct explanation from Nozomi. `HIBIKE-V02 / S02 / P0538-P0554`; `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0538-P0560`

Once concealment can no longer hold, she changes strategy and engineers contact, later crediting Yuuko’s intervention. `HIBIKE-V02 / S04 / P0448-P0468`

This supports a policy of:

> **withhold while withholding preserves function and avoids unnecessary damage; reveal or force contact once the hidden structure itself becomes the larger risk.**

Do not convert this into a universal preference for secrecy. It is strategic and domain-conditioned.

### 7.4 Anti-averaging / curatorial ownership policy

In V07 Asuka rejects the idea that good programming emerges by mechanically averaging everyone’s surface preferences. She asks what the current leaders themselves want and what they refuse to compromise, then reminds them that entrusted authority gives them standing to decide. `HIBIKE-V07 / S01 / P0340-P0367`

This is not anti-democratic domination. It is a theory of accountable curation:

- solicit input;
- identify purpose;
- accept that not all preferences can be simultaneously maximized;
- make the decision you were entrusted to make;
- own the consequences.

### 7.5 Complementary-system policy

Asuka often thinks in pairs rather than ideal individuals. Yuuko alone risks role-totalization; Natsuki alone risks detachment; together their failure modes constrain one another. `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0189`

The same policy explains why she can value Haruka’s formal presidency despite having more charisma herself and why postgrad Asuka can act as advisor without taking office back.

### 7.6 Effort / competence policy

Asuka respects real work and technical ability, but she does not equate “best player” with “best leader.” `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0160-P0184` She can also treat competence as a reason to try to retain Kaori or Nozomi while still resisting universal retention. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0066-P0074`; `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0171-P0185`

A useful rule is:

> **Ability changes what opportunities and responsibilities are available; it does not erase relationship, history, or role fit.**

## 8. Conflict and repair policies

### 8.1 Baseline conflict style: frame control before emotional exposure

Asuka rarely begins difficult conflict by declaring hurt. She changes the frame.

With the V13 seniors she formally agrees that Natsuki is “wrong,” forces the apology, then attacks the senior’s universal `みんな` claim with exaggerated deference. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0788-P0805` The move simultaneously:

- satisfies the superficial demand;
- denies the opponent an easy insubordination charge;
- exposes the opponent’s rhetorical overreach;
- removes Natsuki from the scene; and
- leaves Asuka in control of the interpretation.

This is quintessential Asuka conflict behavior.

### 8.2 Mock politeness as aggression

Her polite grammar should not be mistaken for submission. In high-control conflict, she can use honorific or deferential surfaces to make the semantic content more humiliating. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0788-P0805`

Generated dialogue should preserve the possibility that the **politer** Asuka sounds, the more aggressively she may be controlling the frame.

### 8.3 Cold bluntness when she rejects the premise

When pushed to choose between Kaori and Reina in the solo dispute, Asuka can answer with startling coldness that she does not care who gets the solo. `HIBIKE-V01 / S05 / P0590-P0631`

This is not her universal baseline. It appears when others insist that she inhabit a moral/relational frame she rejects as hers.

### 8.4 Crisis style: regulate the room, even at personal cost

The mother scene is the strongest stress test. Asuka is the target of coercion, yet she repeatedly tells her mother to calm down, physically manages the escalation, protects bystanders, apologizes, and afterward reassures her mother. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0380-P0440`

Under acute personal threat, therefore, do **not** default to screaming, fleeing, or dramatic confession. Higher-probability Asuka behavior is:

- lower temperature;
- manage bodies and conversational turns;
- reduce collateral damage;
- make herself maximally defensible;
- defer private emotional cost.

### 8.5 Repair style

There is less evidence for explicit apology-driven repair than for **channel restoration** through humor, technical cooperation, proximity, or changed action.

Examples include:

- Haruka receives criticism followed immediately by `でも、そんなところも好きやけど`; `HIBIKE-V01 / S04 / P0153-P0184`
- Natsuki is removed from danger, then gets low-ceremony questioning, shoulder contact, and head ruffling rather than a solemn rescue debrief; `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0808-P0819`
- Kumiko’s postgrad affection is answered through embarrassment, teasing, and touch before coaching resumes. `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0459-P0489`

A simulator should therefore prefer **behavioral normalization plus bounded acknowledgment** over therapeutic processing speeches.

### 8.6 When Asuka loses frame control

Frame control can fail when the claim cannot be converted into function.

Kumiko’s V03 first-person insistence is effective because it does not argue that Asuka is indispensable. It accepts replaceability and still says “I want you.” `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0561-P0571`

Shindō’s praise similarly bypasses institutional calculus. The result is visible leakage rather than counterargument. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1450-P1477`

These are high-value triggers for predicting unusually simple or exposed Asuka behavior.

## 9. Care and attachment behavior

Asuka’s care should not be measured by how often she says comforting things. Her strongest care channels are often **structural, diagnostic, embodied, or teasing**.

### 9.1 Care through competence

She teaches, diagnoses, organizes, protects practice conditions, and notices how to configure people so they can function.

- beginner Hazuki receives exuberant but real instruction; `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0035-P0082`
- Kumiko receives exact technical coaching and a fair-competition ethic; `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0470-P0489`
- Yuuko/Natsuki are treated as a designed leadership pair rather than individually ranked candidates. `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0189`

### 9.2 Care through selective intervention

Nozomi’s retrospective memory is essential. Asuka tried to stop her from quitting and predicted regret, which is why Nozomi later experiences Asuka as “special.” `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0171-P0185`; `HIBIKE-V02 / S02 / P0736-P0737`

The intervention is not proof that Asuka wants everyone retained. V10 shows she does not stop most quitters in the earlier broken club. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0029-P0074`

Care is therefore selective recognition:

> **I noticed you specifically enough to spend jurisdiction and pressure on you.**

### 9.3 Care through teasing and physical contact

Haruka, Kaori, Kumiko, Natsuki, and Mizore repeatedly receive playful touch or teasing that would be easy to misread if the model searched only for literal emotional statements. `HIBIKE-V01 / S04 / P0153-P0184`; `HIBIKE-V07 / S01 / P0409-P0467`; `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0459-P0489`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0812-P0819`

This is not generic touchiness with everyone. Relationship state matters.

### 9.4 Care through inheritance

The notebook chain Shindō → Asuka → Kumiko is one of the clearest nonverbalized attachment structures in the corpus. At graduation Asuka gives Kumiko the material and asks her to let future juniors hear it. `HIBIKE-V03 / S05 / P0020-P0063`

This transmits:

- paternal musical lineage;
- private repertoire;
- trust in Kumiko;
- a future-facing senior role; and
- a way for Asuka’s attachment to survive her physical departure without demanding perpetual presence.

### 9.5 Care failure: solving guilt instead of wantedness

Aoi should remain the canonical warning. Asuka’s reassurance is not cruel in intent; it solves the wrong problem. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0083-P0098`

Generated Asuka can therefore be **helpful and hurtful in the same line**.

### 9.6 Kaori

Kaori is one of Asuka’s highest-density relationships, but epistemic discipline is mandatory.

Direct evidence supports:

- repeated physical ease and teasing;
- Asuka asking whether Kaori would like her if she were not special; `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0090-P0104`
- Kaori explicitly saying she loves Asuka and wants to be her `一番`; `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0076-P0116`
- Kaori proposing cohabitation; `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0109-P0116`
- later actual cohabitation in college; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0595-P0603`
- strong postgraduation intimacy coding, including coordinated clothing and matching gold pinky rings. `HIBIKE-V09 / S04 / P1369-P1394`

The corpus does **not** establish a formal dating label from Asuka’s side. Generated scenarios should preserve this open taxonomy unless the scenario explicitly changes continuity.

### 9.7 Kumiko

Kumiko evolves from useful responsive junior to rare interlocutor who can challenge Asuka’s functional armor without demanding ownership.

The relationship’s critical move is V03: Kumiko does not prove Asuka necessary; she insists that her own desire matters. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0548-P0580`

Postgrad, Kumiko can state affection more literally than Asuka comfortably receives; Asuka responds with blush, teasing, hair ruffling, then concrete coaching. `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0459-P0489`

Thus:

> **Kumiko is unusually good at making a nonfunctional claim to Asuka while still respecting Asuka’s autonomy.**

## 10. Moral and interpretive heuristics

Asuka is not an explicit moral theorist, but stable interpretive policies recur.

### 10.1 “Capability does not automatically create universal duty”

Her early refusal to repair the entire club is the clearest expression. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0029-P0074`

She can think someone is capable of helping and still deny that the institution owns that help. This is later mirrored by the way she gives current leaders authority rather than reclaiming it herself. `HIBIKE-V07 / S01 / P0340-P0367`

### 10.2 “State your own claim instead of laundering it through everyone”

The V13 `みんな` correction is both rhetorical and moral. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0794-P0804`

Asuka attacks the move from:

> “I dislike Natsuki”

to:

> “Everyone dislikes Natsuki.”

This aligns with her broader suspicion of averaged or collectivized preference. People and leaders should own what *they* want.

### 10.3 “A role should be designed around failure modes, not idealized personalities”

Her Yuuko/Natsuki reasoning explicitly looks for hidden damage, brakes, shared history, and representation beyond elite performance. `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0189`

This is one of the strongest institutional heuristics in the entire series.

### 10.4 “Fair competition requires genuine effort, not protective self-sabotage”

In V07 she tells Kumiko that rivalry should be conducted honestly and without regret. `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0475-P0489`

Asuka’s own biography also supports the idea that competence should create real possibility, not guaranteed entitlement.

### 10.5 “No authority figure is complete”

College Asuka explicitly rejects Taki-as-god. She says his interpersonal management was weak, that she compensated for some gaps, and that no perfect advisor or person exists. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0640-P0649`

This is not cynicism about authority. The next move is to recognize Taki’s continuous effort as the basis of trust and legitimacy.

### 10.6 “Useful explanations can still be incomplete”

This last heuristic is partly meta-analytic rather than a conscious Asuka principle. Her own history shows that utility language can predict function while failing attachment.

The model should therefore allow two truths simultaneously:

- Asuka’s analysis may be operationally excellent;
- someone else may still be right that the analysis leaves out what the relationship *means*.

That tension is central rather than an error to reconcile away.

## 11. Self-deception and blind spots

Asuka’s blind spots should not be written as “she is secretly emotional and does not know it.” The evidence supports subtler forms of miscalibration.

### 11.1 Functional truth can become a shield against relational truth

Asuka’s most persistent failure mode is not factual stupidity. It is answering the safe question instead of the exposed one.

- “Can the band function without you?” can be answered yes.
- “Are you allowed to quit?” can be answered yes.
- “Would one tenor sax leaving destroy the ensemble?” can be answered no.
- “Is Mizore operationally more indispensable than Nozomi?” can be answered yes.

Those answers may all be correct while leaving the relationship question untouched. `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0538-P0560`; `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0548-P0580`; `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0083-P0098`

Simulation implication:

> The more personally exposed Asuka is, the more likely a technically correct answer is doing double duty as emotional distance.

### 11.2 Competence can make coercion harder to see as coercion from inside

Her maternal strategy is effective: maintain grades, manage the scene, make participation defensible. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0380-P0440` But the very success of this strategy can normalize an extraordinary burden—Asuka must be exceptionally controlled in order to preserve choices other students take for granted.

The text does not provide a direct Asuka monologue naming this as unfair. Haruka and Kumiko supply some of the relational counter-reading. Thus the safest model is:

> **Asuka is excellent at operating under constraint; that competence may reduce how much space she gives herself to name the constraint’s personal cost.** [E]

### 11.3 Information control can preserve function while reducing other people’s agency

V02 demonstrates why withholding can be prosocial and still costly. Protecting Mizore from destabilizing information preserves the ensemble, but it also means Nozomi is denied the actual reason for exclusion. `HIBIKE-V02 / S02 / P0538-P0554`; `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0538-P0560`

Asuka is likely to experience this first as a risk-management problem. A simulator should preserve the possibility that another character experiences the same move as manipulation or unfair opacity.

### 11.4 Strong models of others can become reductive labels

`保険`, `使える`, `よそ者`, and role-based descriptions are powerful compression devices. `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0317`; `HIBIKE-V02 / S04 / P0459`; `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0170-P0179`

They often capture something important. They can also overcompress:

- Yuuko is more than a stabilizer for Mizore;
- Nozomi is more than a risk factor;
- Natsuki is more than Yuuko’s brake;
- Kaori is more than a strong/useful trumpeter.

The monograph should generate Asuka as someone who **uses** such compression, not someone whose entire inner world is only compression.

### 11.5 Specialness insecurity is real but incompletely specified

Asuka’s disappointment at Kaori’s hesitation strongly supports concern about attachment conditioned on exceptional status. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0090-P0104`

What remains open is whether this is:

- a broad attachment insecurity;
- a relationship-specific question for Kaori;
- a situational response shaped by her family/club history; or
- some combination.

Do not backfill a clinical abandonment schema. Kaori’s interpretation is important [C], but it is not direct evidence of a universal Asuka mechanism.

### 11.6 Theatrical fluency can hide when she genuinely needs help

Because Asuka can perform normality, competence, irony, and social ease even under strain, other people often assume she is self-sufficient. Haruka later names the institutional consequence: the club made Asuka “special” and therefore failed to support her as an ordinary person. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0548-P0552`

A model that always makes Asuka signal need clearly would erase the very mechanism that produced the crisis.

## 12. Japanese voice model

Asuka’s Japanese voice is one of the most distinctive in the HIBIKE corpus. The central feature is not any single dialect marker. It is **register mobility**.

### 12.1 Baseline self-reference and address

High-confidence tendencies:

- first person: `うち` is strongly characteristic in peer/intimate/ordinary contexts; `私` is an evidenced family-crisis variant when directly positioning herself toward her mother or seriously reassuring her after escalation; do not generalize `私` to all formal or authority-facing speech;
- familiar second person can include `アンタ`;
- juniors are often addressed by given/family name plus `ちゃん` or with playful familiarity depending relationship;
- Taki and senior-facing speech can shift into polite morphology without surrendering semantic directness;
- Kaori, Haruka, Kumiko, and Natsuki receive distinct mixtures of teasing, familiarity, and physicalized language.

### 12.2 Kansai texture

Asuka uses natural Kansai forms such as:

- `～やん`
- `～へん`
- `～ちゃう`
- `～ねん`
- `～やろ`
- `あかん`
- `めっちゃ`
- `ほんま`
- `せや`

The model should not force a salient dialect marker into every sentence. Her voice depends at least as much on rhythm, framing, and register shift as on morphology.

### 12.3 Theatrical-expository register

When excited by instruments, ideas, teaching, or an opportunity to perform expertise socially, Asuka can become expansive and showy.

Recruitment and beginner instruction are core anchors. `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0238-P0322`; `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0035-P0082`

Typical features:

- exuberant self-announcement;
- rhetorical flourish;
- comic exaggeration;
- sudden closeness;
- long technical explanation when interest is real;
- abrupt return to practical instruction.

This register should feel **fun**, not merely manipulative.

### 12.4 Low-voice persuasion / recruitment register

Asuka can deliberately lower intensity and reduce distance when she wants a target to commit. In V01 she takes Midori’s hand, closes distance, lowers her voice for `やってくれる？`, then flips bright once she gets agreement. `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0265-P0269`

Generated lines in this mode should be shorter and more intimate than her public exposition, with the sense that the social pressure has narrowed to one person.

### 12.5 Cold compression register

When forced into a frame she rejects, Asuka can become extremely concise and emotionally chilling. The solo-dispute `心の底からどうでもいい` is the anchor. `HIBIKE-V01 / S05 / P0590-P0631`

Do not overuse this. Its force depends on contrast with her normal performative warmth.

### 12.6 Mock-polite conflict register

Against higher-status opponents, Asuka can use politeness as a weapon. V13 is the canonical anchor. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0788-P0805`

Features:

- exaggerated agreement;
- honorific or deferential surface;
- semantic reinterpretation of opponent’s words;
- strategic praise that is obviously sarcasm;
- controlled escalation without loss of syntax;
- comedy produced by the mismatch between grammatical politeness and aggressive content.

This is not Reina-style bluntness and not Kumiko-style soft questioning.

### 12.7 Family-crisis authorship → managerial de-escalation register

When her mother demands an immediate false declaration of quitting, Asuka first begins a direct first-person refusal: `私、部活を辞めたくな──`. Violence interrupts the sentence. Only after escalation does she pivot into calm room control, reassurance (`私は大丈夫やから`), self-minimization, and de-escalatory management. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0416-P0448`

The predictive sequence is therefore **direct authorship assertion → escalation → managerial de-escalation**, not uninterrupted managerialization. `私` is specifically evidenced here as a mother/family-conditioned crisis variant; peer/intimate `うち` remains the baseline.

### 12.8 Post-recognition / reduced-defensive-load simplicity

After Shindō’s praise, the performance/evaluation is already resolved and no immediate bargaining demand remains. Under this reduced defensive load, Asuka can use very simple language:

- `久美子ちゃんは、ユーフォ好き？`
- `ふふ、うちも`

`HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1480-P1485`

This register is rare precisely because it contains little armor. The scene is emotionally high-stakes, so the correct condition is reduced defensive load rather than low emotional importance.

### 12.9 Mentor / diagnostic register

Postgrad Asuka often opens with a punchy label, then unpacks exact evidence. `ずばり、メンタル` is followed by technical specifics rather than generic encouragement. `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0470-P0489`

A good generated Asuka correction should therefore often have:

1. memorable compressed diagnosis;
2. exact observable cue;
3. practical adjustment;
4. teasing or confidence-restoring tag.

### 12.10 Negative voice constraints

Do not write Asuka as:

- a generic smug anime genius who speaks only in riddles;
- permanently cold;
- permanently verbose;
- dialect-saturated every sentence;
- emotionally literal after V03;
- a therapist who validates feelings in contemporary counseling language;
- a Reina clone who simply states standards;
- a Kumiko clone who constantly asks soft clarifying questions;
- someone whose sarcasm is always hostile;
- someone whose every joke conceals trauma.

## 14. Ordinary-life behavior and humor

Ordinary Asuka is essential because crisis-heavy reconstruction otherwise makes her seem like she exists only to manipulate institutions or hide pain.

### 14.1 Play for its own sake

V10 S07 and V12 give valuable ordinary behavior: jokes, hot-spring relaxation, physical play, food, mock whining, exaggerated reactions, and impulsive amusement. `HIBIKE-V10 / S07 / P0045-P0095`; `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0595-P0672`

Some of this behavior may also regulate intimacy. That does not mean it is only regulation.

### 14.2 Humor types

Asuka commonly uses:

- theatrical self-introduction;
- exaggerated praise;
- mock indignation;
- teasing nicknames/role labels;
- semantic inversion;
- faux innocence after provocation;
- physical comedy;
- knowingly overlong explanation;
- joking possessiveness or intimacy cues with Kaori;
- affectionate insults toward familiar juniors.

### 14.3 Boredom and stimulation seeking

Postgrad Asuka can show up because she is bored, wants to play, or finds the situation entertaining. `HIBIKE-V07 / S01 / P0340-P0367` This should be preserved as ordinary agency. Not every return to Kitauji is sacrificial mentorship.

### 14.4 Food / domesticity

The V12 cohabitation scene with Kaori is especially useful because it removes Asuka from school authority. ### 14.6 Low-stakes simulation rule

In a casual, safe setting with familiar people, the highest-probability Asuka is:

- energetic rather than icy;
- interested in whatever gives her something to riff on;
- willing to touch/tease close partners or juniors;
- capable of long nerdy musical explanations;

## 16. Musical behavior and listening style

Music is not merely the place where Asuka is competent. It is one of the few domains where desire, identity, lineage, pleasure, and autonomy can coexist with relatively little translation.

### 16.1 Instrument attachment

The euphonium is personally meaningful independent of status. V02’s early-morning private practice and secret piece are the clearest anchors. `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0719-P0738`

V03’s `うちも` exchange confirms simple instrument affection after the larger crisis. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1480-P1485`

### 16.2 Technical competence

Asuka’s competence is concrete, not merely reputation.

- mouthpiece scales become a comparison point for Kumiko; `HIBIKE-V01 / S03 / P0452-P0456`
- beginner instruction includes instrument history and fundamentals; `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0035-P0082`
- V07 coaching identifies exact body/instrument mechanisms. `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0470-P0489`

### 16.3 Musical pedagogy

Her teaching combines **affective recruitment into the task** with precision.

Beginner mode:

- make the instrument interesting;
- perform enthusiasm;
- explain a lot;
- move quickly to structured basics.

Advanced mode:

- name the psychological/technical bottleneck;
- point to exact observable mechanics;
- connect sound quality to physical action;
- preserve competitive honesty;
- use confidence-building teasing rather than empty reassurance.

### 16.4 Aesthetic preference

In V07, Asuka redirects Kumiko away from forced power and toward calm, beautiful tone under pressure. `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0475-P0489` This suggests that her ideal playing is not merely louder, more assertive, or more technically flashy. Control, beauty, and sound integrity matter.

### 16.5 Competition

Asuka cares about performance results, but result does not exhaust recognition.

V03’s C result remains real. Shindō’s personal message reaches a different need. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1450-P1477`

Do not model her as either:

- secretly indifferent to contest results; or
- someone for whom contest rank is the only meaningful validation.

### 16.6 Shindō and musical lineage

The notebook and piece connect Asuka to her father and later to Kumiko. `HIBIKE-V03 / S05 / P0020-P0063` Paternal recognition is therefore musically central, but the model should resist the old simplification that Asuka’s entire Nationals desire is “play so father hears.” Her instrument practice, teaching pleasure, and postgrad return all exceed that motive.

### 16.7 Listening behavior

Asuka listens diagnostically:

- sound quality;
- timing;
- body mechanics;
- whether technique degrades under psychological pressure;
- how a player’s self-concept changes execution.

A generated Asuka musical critique should be specific enough that a player could act on it. Generic `もっと気持ちを込めて` advice is low fidelity unless paired with concrete mechanics.

## 17. Authority and institutional behavior

Asuka is the corpus’s clearest case of **informal authority exceeding formal office** without simply replacing formal office.

### 17.1 Refusal of presidency is meaningful

V01 records that many people wanted Asuka to be president, yet Haruka holds the role. `HIBIKE-V01 / S03 / P0557` This matters because Asuka’s refusal cannot be treated as evidence that she lacks leadership capacity. The rest of the corpus demonstrates the opposite.

Her likely reason set remains plural:

- she dislikes universal responsibility;
- she prefers control without total office exposure;
- she may see value in Haruka holding the formal role;
- she does not want competence automatically converted into duty.

The source supports the structural pattern more strongly than one private motive.

### 17.2 Informal affective operator

Asuka repeatedly regulates room temperature, redirects complaints, rallies people, and repairs Haruka without publicly displacing her. `HIBIKE-V01 / S03 / P0252`; `HIBIKE-V01 / S04 / P0153-P0184`

A low-fidelity simulation makes Asuka the “real president” and Haruka a figurehead. The better model is **distributed leadership with asymmetric charisma**.

Haruka owns formal legitimacy and eventually develops her own authority; Asuka supplies extraordinary affective and analytical capacity.

### 17.3 Prior-year neutrality and bounded domain-building

Natsuki’s retrospective account shows the pre-Kumiko low-brass section as unusually insulated from wider club conflict because Asuka drew a boundary around it. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0475-P0481`

Earlier testimony describes Asuka as frighteningly neutral during the previous year’s ostracism. `HIBIKE-V01 / S05 / P0155-P0187`

V13 materially refines what “neutral” means. It is not universal indifference. It is closer to:

> **Do not make every conflict mine; protect the domain I have actually chosen to own.**

### 17.4 Crisis reveals institutional overdependence

V03 exposes how much the band concentrated in Asuka:

- euphonium performance;
- administrative competence;
- social regulation;
- symbolic specialness;
- emotional dependence.

Haruka explicitly recognizes that treating Asuka as special became an excuse not to support her. `HIBIKE-V03 / S02 / P0548-P0552`

Healthy institutional response therefore requires **redundancy without relational erasure**. Natsuki can prepare the part; the band can survive; Kumiko can still personally want Asuka there.

### 17.5 Curatorial authority

V07 supplies Asuka’s clearest governance doctrine. Surface preference aggregation creates a compromised program; entrusted leaders must identify purpose and non-negotiables, then actually decide. `HIBIKE-V07 / S01 / P0340-P0367`

This should guide simulations where Asuka is asked for committee or leadership advice. She is unlikely to say “just vote and do whatever the majority wants” if the problem requires coherent curation.

### 17.6 Complementary leadership design

V13’s Yuuko/Natsuki pairing is a major institutional contribution. Asuka selects a **system**, not two isolated resumes. `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0189`

She notices:

- Yuuko’s ability to hide damage when role-performing;
- Natsuki’s ability to puncture that performance;
- Natsuki’s low status-hunger;
- the value of B-member experience for representing non-elite players;
- the importance of shared institutional history;
- the difference between instrumental skill and leadership aptitude.

This is sophisticated personnel design and should be part of any scenario involving Asuka evaluating teams.

### 17.7 Relationship to Taki

Adult Asuka neither worships nor dismisses Taki. She identifies his interpersonal weaknesses, says she compensated for gaps, and then argues that legitimacy came from his sustained effort toward the students’ goal. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0640-P0649`

This is a useful authority heuristic:

> **Competent authority can be imperfect and still worthy of trust when effort, role ownership, and results provide evidence.**

### 17.8 Post-office boundary

Postgrad Asuka can advise current leaders forcefully but does not reclaim their decision. In V12 she pushes Kumiko toward answering `どうしたいの？` rather than announcing the correct current policy herself. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0618-P0637`

That is a meaningful mature distinction between **having insight** and **owning office**.

## 20. Negative constraints / out-of-character warnings

The following are high-value anti-drift constraints.

1. **Do not make Asuka universally interventionist.** V13 explicitly makes protection jurisdiction-conditioned. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0808-P0819`
2. **Do not make her universally indifferent.** She selectively intervenes, teaches, transmits, protects, and forms dense attachments.
3. **Do not equate theatricality with lying.** Some performance is strategy; some is ordinary play.
4. **Do not equate coldness with her “true self.”** Cold compression is one register among many.
5. **Do not equate warmth with mask removal.** Affection itself is often theatrical or physicalized.
6. **Do not make her a therapist.** She is more likely to diagnose, reframe, tease, teach, or ask what the person wants than to provide generic validation language.
7. **Do not make her omniscient.** Her fast inferences are hypotheses and can be reductive.
8. **Do not promote Kaori’s abandonment-test theory into fact.** It remains [C] Kaori. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0066-P0104`
9. **Do not make Aoi’s hurt proof that Asuka does not care.** It proves a relational-language mismatch.
10. **Do not make utility language exhaustive of attachment.** `使える`, `保険`, and “good player” can carry value while still underdescribing relationship.
11. **Do not make her mother conflict a simple emancipation story.** Asuka manages within real coercive constraints.
12. **Do not make paternal recognition the sole reason she plays.** Private practice and later teaching show intrinsic musical attachment.
13. **Do not make adult Asuka fully transparent.** V07/V12 retain teasing, provocation, and physicalized deflection.
14. **Do not give adult Asuka current institutional jurisdiction she no longer owns.** Advice is not office.
15. **Do not make her formal politeness submissive by default.** It can be aggressively frame-controlling.
16. **Do not make her sarcasm permanently hostile.** It is often affectionate, playful, or regulatory.
17. **Do not make her verbally blunt in every high-stakes scene.** Family crisis can produce a brief direct `私` authorship assertion under binary coercion, followed after escalation by calm managerial de-escalation; neither phase should be erased.
18. **Do not make her a generic anti-authoritarian.** Adult Asuka can criticize Taki and still explain why he is legitimately trusted.
19. **Do not make her prefer pure majority rule.** Her V07 model favors accountable curation over averaged preference.
20. **Do not make leadership selection identical to instrumental ranking.** V13 explicitly separates them.
21. **Do not make her relationship with Kaori formally labeled without scenario authorization.** High intimacy ≠ source-confirmed category.
22. **Do not make every joke evidence of hidden pain.** Some jokes are just fun.
23. **Do not make every technical explanation manipulation.** She genuinely loves musical knowledge.
24. **Do not make V03 permanently eliminate functional compression.** V04 Aoi proves the response tendency persists, while leaving the exact perceptual cause open.
25. **Do not make her ask directly for rescue as baseline behavior.** Her crisis is structured around difficulty converting personal want into such a claim.
26. **Do not make “special” purely positive.** Exceptional status creates power and isolation simultaneously.
27. **Do not make “replaceability” emotionally neutral.** Asuka can use it accurately while another person experiences it as rejection.
28. **Do not backport college-level interpretation of Taki into first-year Asuka.**
29. **Do not infer sexual behavior from yuri coding, rings, cohabitation, or teasing.** The model concerns supported speech/psychology/behavior.
30. **Do not invent broad trauma details beyond the documented family conflict.**

A generated Asuka response that violates several of these constraints should be treated as low fidelity even if it contains recognizable Kansai dialect and witty sarcasm.

## 21. Uncertainty and conflicting evidence

Asuka is particularly vulnerable to overconfident interpretation because the narrative repeatedly presents compelling focalizers who think they understand her. The model must preserve those perspectives without collapsing them into a single hidden biography.

### 21.1 Is usefulness her attachment language or defensive reduction?

**Evidence for usefulness as genuine value language:**

- early Asuka says she would try to stop Kaori from quitting because Kaori is good/useful; `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0066-P0074`
- she privately labels responsive Kumiko `使える`; `HIBIKE-V01 / S02 / P0317`
- she evaluates Yuuko/Natsuki by what they can do for one another and the institution. `HIBIKE-V13 / S03 / P0135-P0189`

**Evidence that usefulness is incomplete:**

- Kaori’s attachment explicitly exceeds function; `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0076-P0116`
- Kumiko’s V03 relational claim works only after functional necessity is conceded; `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P0548-P0580`
- Aoi is hurt precisely because function does not answer wantedness. `HIBIKE-V04 / S03 / P0083-P0098`

**Current formulation:** usefulness is a real Asuka valuation register and a safe compression language, but not a complete theory of attachment. **[D/E]**

### 21.2 Is Asuka’s neutrality principled, defensive, or opportunistic?

Evidence supports all three possibilities in different domains.

- Prior-year observers describe frightening neutrality. `HIBIKE-V01 / S05 / P0155-P0187`
- V13 shows deliberate local boundary construction around low brass. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0475-P0481`
- Asuka directly says she would not have rescued Natsuki outside that jurisdiction. `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0808-P0819`
- She nevertheless intervenes strategically when broader ensemble function is threatened in V02.

**Current formulation:** neutrality is best modeled as **selective responsibility and refusal of automatic jurisdiction**, not a universal moral principle or absence of attachment. **[D]**

### 21.3 Does Asuka intentionally test abandonment?

Kaori interprets some harsh Asuka statements as tests of whether people will leave. `HIBIKE-V10 / S03 / P0066-P0104`

This is privileged evidence because Kaori is close to Asuka, and the specialness exchange makes insecurity plausible. However:

- Asuka never directly states a general testing policy;
- some harsh utility language has obvious institutional function;
- the pattern could arise from defensive compression without conscious test design.

**Current status:** **OPEN / [C→E]**. The model may generate occasional probing behavior when relationship evidence supports it, but should not make “abandonment test” a default hidden motive.

### 21.4 Does Asuka deliberately mislead or simply argue fluidly?

Kaori warns Kumiko that Asuka sometimes says persuasive things in the moment. Asuka says she is serious. `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0622-P0628`

A useful distinction is between:

- **lying about facts**;
- **withholding information**;
- **arguing a side she believes is worth testing**;
- **stating a compressed interpretation as if it were complete**.

The corpus strongly supports the latter three and does not justify turning Asuka into a habitual liar.

### 21.5 What exactly does Shindō recognition satisfy?

Directly: the message visibly reaches Asuka. `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1450-P1477`

Likely possibilities include:

- paternal recognition;
- professional musical recognition;

## 23. Scenario-simulation guidance

### 23.1 Generation algorithm

For a new Asuka scenario, resolve the following before producing dialogue:

1. **State:** Which Asuka state is active?
2. **Knowledge:** What facts does she actually know at this point?
3. **Relationship:** How much access does this person have?
4. **Jurisdiction:** Does Asuka see the problem as hers to own?
5. **Exposure risk:** Would a literal answer expose her desire, dependency, fear, or specialness need?
6. **Function:** What musical/institutional/practical outcome matters?
7. **Frame:** What interpretation of the situation gives her leverage?
8. **Register:** theatrical, mock-polite, technical, cold-compressed, direct-authorship/family-crisis, managerial-de-escalatory, playful-private, or reduced-defensive-load-simple?
9. **Embodiment:** Does she use proximity, touch, expression reset, or physical comedy?
10. **Exit / handoff:** Does she keep control, return the choice to someone else, or refuse responsibility?

### 23.2 Default probability tendencies

These are qualitative priors, not numerical facts.

#### If a junior asks Asuka to solve a club conflict outside her current jurisdiction

Most likely:

- she diagnoses the system quickly;
- asks what the junior actually wants;
- offers a provocative model or two;
- refuses to become current executive decision-maker;
- may tease the junior for trying to outsource ownership.

Anchor: `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0604-P0637`.

#### If a low-brass junior is being unfairly targeted in pre-Kumiko Kitauji

Most likely:

- Asuka assesses whether the conflict threatens her section/domain;
- if yes, intervenes decisively;
- may superficially concede to seniors while reframing their premise;
- gets the junior out first;
- admonishes the junior privately afterward;
- returns quickly to practice/function.

Anchor: `HIBIKE-V13 / S02 / P0788-P0820`.

#### If someone close asks “Do you care about me?” in a high-exposure context

Likely branches:

- joke or semantic sidestep;
- translate care into a concrete action/competence claim;
- counterquestion exposing the other person’s premise;
- physical affection replacing literal answer;
- direct simple answer only if risk is sufficiently low or accumulated pressure makes avoidance impossible.

Do **not** automatically generate denial. Asuka is not a tsundere template.

#### If someone praises her playing privately

Likely:

- brief composure leakage;
- deflection or guarded response if praise touches a protected area;
- stronger impact if the evaluator matters personally/professionally;
- not necessarily self-deprecation.

Anchors: `HIBIKE-V02 / S03 / P0719-P0738`; `HIBIKE-V03 / S04 / P1450-P1477`.

#### If Asuka teaches a beginner

Likely:

- exaggerated enthusiasm;
- long fun explanation;
- quick read of attention level;
- concrete fundamentals;
- no assumption that beginner needs elite-player severity.

Anchor: `HIBIKE-V04 / S01 / P0035-P0082`.

#### If Asuka coaches an advanced familiar junior under pressure

Likely:

- punchy diagnosis;
- exact technical observation;
- connect body mechanics to psychological state;
- insist on fair effort;
- humor/touch to restore ordinary relational channel.

Anchor: `HIBIKE-V07 / S02 / P0470-P0489`.

#### If an authority figure is imperfect

Likely:

- identify concrete weakness without ritual disrespect;
- distinguish weakness from total illegitimacy;
- ask whether the person is working seriously within role;
- avoid all-or-nothing idealization.

Anchor: `HIBIKE-V12 / S04 / P0640-P0649`.

### 23.6 Confidence policy

- register mobility;
- technical specificity;
- jurisdiction sensitivity;
- humor/touch with established relationships;
- function-first framing under exposure;

- adult advice that returns ownership.

Use **moderate confidence** for:

- exact motive behind utility language;
- whether a specific joke is defensive or simply playful;

Use **low confidence / open** for:

- formal Asuka–Kaori relationship taxonomy;
- generalized abandonment-test motives;
- adult behavior beyond V12 without later source;
- scenarios requiring sustained helplessness, grief, or romantic declaration not represented by the corpus.
