# AOT Japanese Voice and Vocabulary Ledger

## Purpose

This ledger serves two jobs:

1. preserve translation-sensitive wording with interpretive significance; and
2. accumulate a written-Japanese idiolect/discourse model for later character reconstruction.

It does **not** model seiyuu timbre, pitch, pause, breath, or delivery.

## Current character fingerprints

### Eren Jaeger / エレン・イェーガー

**Current pattern:** categorical, first-person, future/action-oriented language; recurrent freedom/existence vocabulary; under severe guilt the same categorical structure can invert from entitlement-to-exist into unnecessary-self judgment. By V18 some decisive grammar becomes explicitly plural (`オレ` -> `オレ達`) without erasing intensity.

**Diagnostic anchors:**

- V01: `駆逐` and categorical enemy removal after Carla's death;
- V04 `AOT_V04_E001`: birth as ground of world-claim;
- V07 `AOT_V07_E005`: categorical self-blame after outcome;
- V16 `AOT_V16_E004`: `いらなかった` self-negation;
- V18: `オレ達ならできる`, pluralized action/confidence.
- V19: `オレの…オレ達の…故郷`, `取り返してやる`, `ぶっ殺して` bind pluralized home/reclamation to continuing violent categorical grammar (`AOT_V19_E004`).

**Reconstruction caution:** do not turn every line into a freedom slogan or shouted declaration. Ordinary peer speech requires more direct backfill.

### Mikasa Ackerman / ミカサ・アッカーマン

**Current pattern:** relatively restrained verbal profile compared with the amount of action evidence; operational directness becomes especially important under immediate threat. Relationship evidence often carries more diagnostic weight than lexical frequency.

**Diagnostic anchors:** V07 objective/vengeance correction with Levi; V10 immediate preemption after the Warrior reveal; V18 non-intervention in the Eren/Jean fight.

**Reconstruction caution:** low dialogue density makes overconfident idiolect generation risky. Do not substitute anime performance or fandom shorthand for manga evidence.

### Armin Arlert / アルミン・アルレルト

**Current pattern:** descriptive, explanatory, counterfactual, and model-revising speech; frequently reframes the category of the problem. Can articulate severe strategic propositions under pressure rather than remaining uniformly conciliatory.

**Diagnostic anchors:**

- V04 `AOT_V04_E004`: expands "fighting" beyond bodily sacrifice;
- V07 `AOT_V07_E001/E003`: separates hindsight from choice-time knowledge and contemplates discarding humanity;
- V18: `常識` / `勘` discourse around violating a failed search model.
- V19: causal/logistical explanation around horses and enemy waiting strategy, followed by conditional `交渉` / `話をしよう`; later uses Annie as deliberate emotional leverage (`AOT_V19_E001-E003`, `AOT_V19_E015`, `AOT_V19_E017-E018`).

**Reconstruction caution:** intelligence should not become frictionless certainty or uniformly polite diplomacy.

### Jean Kirstein / ジャン・キルシュタイン

**Current pattern:** less categorical than Eren; concrete, situational, socially aware language; fear and complaint can coexist with responsible action.

**Diagnostic anchor:** V04 `AOT_V04_E006`, leadership grounded in seeing what ordinary weak people need and what should be done now.

**Reconstruction caution:** do not erase irritation, fear, or peer friction in order to make later leadership sound permanently polished.

### Historia Reiss / ヒストリア・レイス

**Current pattern:** major developmental register change. Early saintly/mission-oriented self-presentation is progressively rejected; later speech can become bluntly self-authoring and anti-sacrificial.

**Diagnostic anchors:** V10 true-name declaration; V16 `使命`, rejection of `神`, `私は人類の敵…エレンの味方`.

**Reconstruction caution:** model the temporal boundary. "Christa" social performance and later Historia cannot be flattened into a single stable speaking style.

### Ymir / ユミル

**Current pattern:** blunt, anti-euphemistic, often confrontational language used to expose self-deception, especially around martyrdom and imposed worth. Care can be carried by verbal abrasion.

**Diagnostic anchors:** V10 `AOT_V10_E002-E003`, including rejection of using others' deaths and demand to live under one's own name.

**Reconstruction caution:** bluntness is relationship- and purpose-conditioned; it should not become random cruelty.

### Reiner Braun / ライナー・ブラウン

**Current pattern:** role-fractured language. Practical soldier behavior and deeper `戦士` duty grammar coexist. V10 makes the semantic opposition between `兵士` and `戦士` central.

**Diagnostic anchors:** `AOT_V10_E001`, `AOT_V10_E006-E007`; V19 `イヤ迷うな`, coercive `証明`, and partner-facing `頼んだぞ相棒` show self-command, identity policing, and familiar trust inside Warrior register (`AOT_V19_E005`, `AOT_V19_E010`, `AOT_V19_E014`).

**Reconstruction caution:** later knowledge must not retroactively make every soldier utterance consciously fake.

### Levi / リヴァイ

**Current pattern:** concise, objective-oriented imperatives under command pressure; willing to state uncertainty rather than manufacture certainty when the decision genuinely belongs to another person.

**Diagnostic anchors:** V07 `作戦の本質を見失うな`; V16 `好きな方を選べ`; V19 crisis frustration such as `クソ…うんざりだ` remains terse and operationally situated (`AOT_V19_E006` context).

**Reconstruction caution:** current seed over-samples crisis/command speech. Do not infer constant terseness, emotional emptiness, or anime-specific vocal delivery without low-stakes manga backfill.

### Erwin Smith / エルヴィン・スミス

**Current pattern:** public strategic/command rhetoric can diverge from private motive. His speech must distinguish institutional justification from what he admits under intimate challenge.

**Diagnostic anchors:** V07 sacrifice/choice language; V18 `建て前` confrontation and admission concerning truth versus victory; V19 sharply separates public `心臓を捧げよ` from private `私だけが…自分の夢を見ている` / corpse-mountain self-indictment (`AOT_V19_E006-E009`).

**Reconstruction caution:** eloquent command role is not evidence that ordinary private speech is equally formal in all contexts.

### Hange Zoë / ハンジ・ゾエ

**Current pattern:** explanatory and experimental speech that moves quickly between demonstration, practical uncertainty, tactical constraint, and lightly playful naming. Technical confidence is compatible with explicit admission that a mechanism remains unproven.

**Diagnostic anchors:** V19 Thunder Spear briefing: `雷が落ちたようだろ?`, `だから「雷槍」って呼んでる`, and `工夫しないとね`; Hange explains by showing, states the weapon's limits, and assigns its environmental requirements (`AOT_V19_E006-E007`).

**Reconstruction caution:** do not turn technical enthusiasm into constant comedy or certainty; V19 specifically preserves experimental uncertainty.

### Bertolt Hoover / ベルトルト・フーバー

**Current pattern:** V19 establishes a major state-dependent voice transition from deferential/low-initiative presentation to calm first-person ownership and declarative fatalism. Uses `僕`, recognizes manipulation explicitly, and can articulate attachment without softening the lethal conclusion.

**Diagnostic anchors:** `僕が決めた!!`, `君達は大切な仲間だ ちゃんと殺そうと思ってる`, `君達は誰も悪くないし 悪魔なんかじゃないよ でも全員死ななきゃいけない`, and internal `全部仕方なかった` / `世界はこんなにも残酷じゃないか` (`AOT_V19_E018-E024`).

**Reconstruction caution:** this is a **V19 developmental state**, not permission to rewrite earlier Bertolt as secretly always this verbally autonomous. Calmness is not lack of attachment.

### Zeke / ジーク

**Current pattern:** conversational surface can remain casual while rank, threat, and mission authority are severe. This produces a mismatch between social tone and coercive content.

**Diagnostic anchors:** V19 Warrior-Chief interaction with Reiner, including threat of losing the Armored power and the rank term `ジーク戦士長`; public battlefield address can become elevated/performative (`AOT_V19_E013`).

**Reconstruction caution:** evidence is still concentrated. Casual phrasing must not be mistaken for egalitarian relationship structure.

### Marco Bott / マルコ・ボット

**Current pattern:** direct, socially interpretive, and procedural under betrayal. His last appeal is framed not as innocence alone but as a failure of process: `まだ…ちゃんと…話し合ってないじゃないか`.

**Diagnostic anchor:** V19 flashback (`AOT_V19_E008-E012`).

**Reconstruction caution:** the sample is small and dominated by catastrophe; do not infer a full generative idiolect from the famous final plea.


## Volume 20 voice deltas and strengthened anchors

### Erwin — private desire versus public command

V20 provides the strongest same-volume contrast yet. In private with Levi, Erwin uses first-person desire and uncertainty around the basement (`俺は…このまま地下室に行きたい…`). In public he moves into collective historical rhetoric and repeated imperatives: the dead receive meaning from the living, and `兵士よ怒れ / 兵士よ叫べ / 兵士よ戦え`. This **strengthens**, rather than merely repeats, the public/private register model.

### Levi — direct choice under acute clarity

`俺は選ぶぞ` is a major qualification to the earlier `自分で選べ` pattern. Levi's Japanese should not be modeled through one slogan. When epistemic uncertainty belongs to the chooser, he can return agency; when time is acute and the objective is clearer while the trusted person is trapped by private desire, he can take ownership of the decision. `夢を諦めて死んでくれ` is brutally direct but relationally specific.

### Armin — analytical explanation, tactical lie, and positive-horizon language

V20 combines three registers: causal explanation of Colossal resource mechanics; intimate sea-language with Eren; and deliberate deception using his reputation for honesty. `僕がエレンにウソついたことあった?` is especially important generatively because the utterance is itself instrumental. Armin's sincerity cannot be modeled as an inability to lie.

### Jean — practical command under admitted uncertainty

`何でも試すしかねぇ!!` and `必ず手掛かりがあると信じろ!!` preserve Jean's concrete, rough, peer-facing command register. He gives Armin space to think without becoming a polished strategist-orator himself.

### Bertolt — calm declaratives can contain premature closure

V20 preserves the V19 high-agency voice. He can recognize Armin (`やっぱり君は頭がいいよ`) and offer lethal mercy (`今 楽にしてやる`) without abandoning the attack. His calm internal reasoning nevertheless reaches a false conclusion when he believes no nearby threat remains. Calmness therefore should not be translated into epistemic omniscience.

### Zeke — baseball/play register as coercive affect regulation

`初球`, `完全試合`, and `何事も楽しまなくちゃ` extend the earlier casual-command fingerprint. The register is not low-stakes. V20 explicitly shows him noticing emotional escalation and using the play frame to regulate himself.

### Eren and Mikasa — compressed execution language

Eren's `殺った` after extracting Bertolt and Mikasa's direct `ライナー 出て` during the Armor attack are compact action confirmations. Neither should be generalized into total emotional flatness; both occur at terminal tactical moments.

## Volume 21 voice/state deltas

### Eren — categorical advocacy plus blunt autobiographical self-diagnosis

The serum argument preserves categorical high-stakes syntax (`人類を救うのは…アルミンだ!!`) while the sea sequence adds unusually direct retrospective self-critique: the childhood dream was forgotten as revenge, Titan-killing, and hatred occupied his mind. This strengthens a voice model in which categorical phrasing can target the self as well as an enemy.

### Mikasa — extreme attachment remains action-heavy

In the serum confrontation Mikasa's bodily escalation, tears, and refusal carry more information than long explanatory turns. Her direct verbal profile remains comparatively compressed even under severe grief. Do not generate lengthy abstract monologues as default evidence of intensity.

### Levi — terse decision surface over dense relational memory

V21 is among the strongest evidence that Levi's spoken compression is not emotional emptiness. His decision is assembled through remembered sea-talk, Erwin's dream, Kenny's `酔っ払って` principle, and Erwin's childhood gesture, while his overt lines remain short and decisive.

### Hange — grief register

V21 adds a marked emotional-state voice: simple repetition and direct affect (`辛い…辛いよ`), explicit inability to accept loss, and acknowledgment that maintaining sanity is difficult, followed by `それでも 前に進まなきゃいけない`. This differs sharply from Hange's technical/explanatory register without replacing it.

### Floch — survivor meaning / devil rhetoric

Floch's V21 crisis voice escalates from concrete casualty comparison to categorical political naming (`悪魔`) and finally first-person survivor meaning (`俺の意味`). The rhetoric is forceful, accusatory, and autobiographically anchored rather than impersonal doctrine.

### Zeke — familial interpretive claim

`理解者`, `被害者`, and `洗脳` mark a new relational register toward Eren. Zeke does not merely announce kinship; he uses kinship to claim interpretive authority over Eren's relationship with Grisha. Current evidence does not show how this register behaves in ordinary sibling interaction.

### Grisha — memoir voice versus live ideological speech

V21 gives two surfaces that must remain distinct:

- retrospective memoir narration capable of self-indictment (`あの日の愚かな子供のままだったのだろうか`-type reflection);
- live Restorationist speech whose certainty outruns his source competence, most clearly when he admits the ancient language is barely deciphered yet says the truth is obvious because he believes in Founder Ymir.

Generative modeling must not collapse retrospective narrator Grisha and younger ideological Grisha into one timeless register.

### Bertolt — helpless-death delta

The restrained Bertolt's cries for help are a large state-dependent departure from V19/V20 calm fatalistic declaratives. This is evidence for emotional delta, not a revision that makes the earlier voice false.

## Volume 22 voice/state deltas

### Eren — categorical vocabulary with interrogative uncertainty

V22 strengthens the distinction between **directness** and **certainty**. Eren's internal reasoning about Dina/Historia is compressed and consequence-oriented; at the sea he uses concrete enemy/elimination vocabulary (`向こうにいる敵`, `全部殺せば`) and collective self-reference (`オレ達`) but ends with `自由になれるのか?`. Generative voice should preserve the possibility that an Eren utterance can sound categorical lexically while remaining genuinely unresolved pragmatically.

### Armin — epistemic openness under survivor doubt

Armin can say `フロックが正しい` and accept the proposition that Erwin should have survived, then immediately resist treating the future as closed: `オレ達はまだ何も知らないんだよ!` and `可能性はいくらでも広がっている!`. His hopeful register is empirical/possibility-based rather than generic reassurance.

### Levi — responsibility without hindsight certainty

`オレにはわからないな 正しい選択なんて 未来は誰にもわからないハズだ` extends Levi's uncertainty grammar into retrospective decision ownership. He does not need to deny responsibility in order to deny counterfactual certainty.

### Floch — self-lowering survivor rhetoric as political standing

Floch combines abasement (`腰抜け`, `雑魚`) with aggressive entitlement to evaluate power (`値踏みする権利くらいはあるだろ!?`). His rhetoric gains force by speaking from expendability rather than elite competence. This should not be flattened into simple boastfulness or simple low self-esteem.

### Ymir — existential bluntness and intimate irreverence

The letter preserves Ymir's anti-euphemistic philosophical register (`何の意味も無い`) while pairing it with world affirmation and a jokingly intimate regret about not marrying Historia. Blunt existential language and affectionate humor belong to the same relational voice.

### Eren Kruger — imperative mission doctrine versus source-aware qualification

Kruger can become highly imperative when directing Grisha (`戦え`, `壁の中で人を愛せ`, `お前が始めた物語だろ`) while becoming more qualified when comparing historical claims. `この世に真実など無い` must not be generated as indiscriminate nihilism: he immediately reasons from demographic plausibility and evidentiary limits.

### Grisha — self-indictment remains a distinct state

`ダメな父親`, `ダメな夫`, `ダメな男` and the later recognition of having imposed ideology on Zeke deepen the retrospective/self-accusatory register. This still must remain distinct from younger Grisha's belief-driven Restorationist certainty.

### Hange — post-basement analytical integration

Hange's V22 public/technical language turns the newly discovered outside world into an operational threat model while preserving distinctions between what is established and what is inferred. The characteristic pattern remains explanation + provisional synthesis rather than omniscient exposition.


## Volume 23 voice/state deltas

### Gabi — categorical merit language plus performative peer confidence

Gabi's high-stakes self-reference is strongly declarative: `私は負けない`, `私が収容区からみんなを解放する`, and the repeated `覚悟` frame convert inherited ethnic burden into first-person competence/duty. Outside the immediate attack she can become boastful, teasing, and theatrically social when retelling success. Do not generate her as a solemn propaganda reciter in every context.

### Falco — direct care and lower theatricality

Falco's V23 register is comparatively plain and relational. `彼はもう戦闘能力の無い捕虜だ` frames humanitarian restraint procedurally; concern for Gabi is often enacted before it is explained. Under Reiner's loyalty threat he can switch into institutionally safe/formal correction, showing that politeness/orthodoxy may be defensive rather than spontaneous belief.

### Reiner — institutional menace, private entrustment, domestic anecdote

V23 adds three sharply different registers to one character. With Falco, Reiner can invoke punitive institutional consequences in clipped disciplinary form before pivoting to intimate instruction: `お前がガビを救い出すんだ` / `この真っ暗な俺達の未来から`. At family dinner, the expected `悪魔` narrative leaks into mundane irritated anecdotes about Sasha and classmates. Generative Reiner must therefore switch by role/interlocutor rather than remain uniformly grave.

### Zeke — formal strategy and crude secrecy deflection

Before Marley command, Zeke packages strategic modernization needs and filial loyalty into institutionally legible speech. With Colt, he becomes familiar (`悪かったな コルト`) and then uses a deliberately crude joke about his `秘密` to close a dangerous line of questioning. Casual humor can be a boundary-setting move rather than evidence of full candor.

### Porco — comparative bluntness and grievance

Porco's Reiner-directed speech is overtly comparative and low on politeness padding. Gratitude is rejected in favor of status/history claims: he saved Marley's Armor, not Reiner personally. This register should remain relationship-conditioned rather than generalized to every interlocutor.

### Pieck — low-energy familiar compression

Pieck's remarks about relearning two-legged walking add a dry, matter-of-fact ordinary register. Current evidence is too narrow for elaborate comedic generation, but it is useful negative evidence against treating every Warrior as permanently martial in casual speech.


## Volume 24 voice/state deltas

### Reiner — identity declaration, self-enforcement, and transmitted persistence

V24 gives unusually broad Reiner voice states: child aspiration, panic, coercive leadership, ordinary trainee mediation, mission rhetoric, partner familiarity, and near-silent adult collapse. Load-bearing anchors include `帰れない`, `ライナーは死んだ`, `俺がマルセルに…なるから…`, `奴らは友達じゃない`, `進み続けるしかねぇよな`, and `頼んだぞ相棒`. A generative model must condition strongly on role/state rather than average these into one blunt register.

### Annie — vulgar anti-euphemism plus self-implication

`マーレもエルディアも全部クソったれ!! ... 私もそうだ!!` is a key pragmatic pattern: Annie attacks grand moral labels and then includes herself among self-interested actors. Her directness is emotionally forceful, not detached philosophical neutrality.

### Eren — clandestine calm and extended causal reflection

Adult Eren's Falco register materially expands the written-voice model. He can use long explanatory turns under controlled concealment: `大抵その「何か」は自分の意志じゃない`, `自分で自分の背中を押した奴`, `希望かもしれないし さらなる地獄かもしれない`. This is state-conditioned reflective speech and does not erase the acute-action categorical voice frozen at V19. He also uses gentle relational phrasing (`君はいい奴だから 長生きしてくれるなら嬉しいよ`) while manipulating information.

### Falco — earnest confession under frustration

Falco's ordinary directness culminates in `お前のためだよ!!`. He can remain hesitant or embarrassed until competitive misunderstanding forces a blunt motive disclosure.

### Gabi — peer incredulity and framework-preserving reframing

Gabi's `はぁぁぁ??` response to Falco and her quick reframing of Udo's foreign-hostility evidence demonstrate an expressive peer register distinct from her public ideological declarations.

### Willy Tybur — dramaturgical politics and burden metaphors

Recurring political vocabulary includes `物語`, `語り手`, `演出家`, `舞台`, `初演出`, `操舵輪`. Willy speaks as someone who converts historical/political structure into stagecraft while simultaneously describing authority as personally heavy.

### Magath — dry material correction

`銅像の中は空洞ですし` is representative: Magath often punctures symbolic language with concrete institutional/material facts. His voice should not be generated as purely ceremonial military speech.

### Jaeger grandfather — professional caution -> repetitive self-blame

The grandfather begins in measured doctorly/advisory speech and breaks into repeated first-person causal blame (`私だ…すべては私がぁぁ…`). The transition is state evidence; it should not become his default conversational register.

## Voice confidence rule

A recurring lexical theme can be HIGH as interpretation and still only MODERATE as generative voice evidence. Generative voice requires recurrence across ordinary as well as high-stakes contexts where possible.


## Volume 25 voice/state deltas

### Eren — experiential humanization -> categorical continuation

V25 gives Eren one of his broadest single-scene registers. He can narrate lived evidence (`敵と同じ屋根の下で / 敵と同じ飯を食った`), state a de-essentialized conclusion (`海の外も壁の中も同じなんだ`), offer interpersonal empathy, and then close with highly categorical self-description: `多分…生まれた時からこうなんだ / オレは進み続ける / 敵を駆逐するまで`. Generative Eren should therefore preserve **reflective causal speech plus categorical action closure**, not choose one as the "real" voice.

### Reiner — repeated rejection of exculpation

`違う` becomes a pragmatic hinge. Reiner interrupts an account that could excuse him and shifts into first-person causal blame: `時代や環境のせいじゃなくて…俺が悪いんだよ`. In current suicidal state, direct responsibility language can become excessive self-condemnation; do not treat it as neutral forensic self-assessment.

### Willy Tybur — confession, universal appeal, enemy designation

Willy's stage voice moves from interested history to personal mortality and then to coalition imperative. `私は死にたくありません` is followed by a cross-national `一緒に未来を生きてほしい`, then by the request to fight the Paradis enemy. The rhetorical pattern is **universal life-desire -> shared future -> named threat**, not simple nationalist bombast.

### Mikasa — concrete moral rebuke

`民間人`, `子供`, and `取り返しがつかない` anchor a concise moral register. Even when confronting Eren on irreversible harm, she remains concrete and relational rather than abstractly philosophical.

### Jean — command and epistemic restraint

`民間への被害は最小限に抑えろ!!` gives a direct protected-category command inside combat. Later `この戦いの先に何があるのか / それを見極めるためには…生き残らねぇと` combines practical survival language with acknowledged uncertainty.

### Floch — retaliatory collective rhetoric

Floch converts remembered Wall-side suffering into present permission for escalation. His rhetoric is forceful, collective, and grievance-based rather than detached strategic calculation.

### Gabi — grief-driven directness

After the festival attack, Gabi's language loses much of the V24 comic/competitive surface and becomes immediate: she wants to return because Udo and Zofia were killed and she does not understand why. This is a state shift, not a permanent replacement of her peer register.

### Porco — violated Titan hierarchy

`俺は巨人だぞ!? / こいつら人間の姿のまま俺を殺す気か!?` encodes an expectation about bodies as much as fear. The shocked rhetorical questions are diagnostic of a model failure: Titanhood was supposed to make human attackers hesitate.

### Zeke / Paradis closing imperatives

`逃がすな / 殲滅しろ` versus `死ぬな / 生き延びろ` creates a formal lexical opposition at the V25 boundary. These lines should be preserved as combat-state imperatives, not generalized as complete philosophies of either speaker/faction.


## Volume 26 voice/state deltas

### Eren - self-command becomes grammatically explicit

The prison-mirror ending compresses Eren's current high-stakes register into a causal proposition followed by repeated imperative: `戦わなければ / 勝てない / 戦え / 戦え`. The crucial new feature is directionality: the imperative is aimed inward. Generative Eren therefore needs both **self-authored declaration** and **self-directed coercive command**. The earlier reflective V24-V25 register remains real; V26 does not reduce him to slogans.

### Armin - counterfactual moral accounting after mass violence

Current Armin explicitly enumerates alternatives around Eren's plan and still reaches `やるしかなかった` while naming soldiers and civilians killed and the destruction of the path to reconciliation. His written voice remains analytical, but the analysis now includes his own implication rather than describing violence from outside it. `もう…わからない` after saying he believed he understood Eren better than anyone is also a diagnostic confidence downgrade, not generalized indecisiveness.

### Jean - proportionality through concrete rhetorical question

After Sasha's death and the capture of Gabi/Falco, Jean does not shift into abstract pacifist doctrine. He asks concretely whether throwing children from the airship would make `この…殺し合い` end. This preserves his established practical register: moral resistance is expressed through immediate causal consequences rather than polished universal theory.

### Sasha - one word carrying longitudinal identity

`肉` is lexically minimal but generatively important because its force comes from recurrence. It is evidence that terminal speech need not become solemn, ideological, or military; an ordinary appetite-signature can remain the last accessible word.

### Gabi and Falco - grief/action versus causal-context asymmetry

Gabi's V26 register remains action-coupled and grief-saturated; Falco's speech is comparatively explanatory and protective because he has heard more of the reciprocal history. Do not average them into one Warrior-candidate voice simply because they move together.

### Volunteers - geopolitical/technical lexicon enters ordinary dialogue

V26 expands the written vocabulary of Paradis into ships, modern weapons, technology, deterrence, royal blood, time, and the `地鳴らし`. Yelena and Onyankopon currently have useful domain voice evidence, but not enough ordinary/private breadth for unrestricted idiolect generation.


## Volume 27 voice/state deltas

### Eren - affection, accusation, and operational compression share one direct register

V27 expands Eren without making him less recognizable. In the railroad flashback, `お前らが大事だからだ / 他の誰よりも… / だから…長生きしてほしい` is emotionally explicit but syntactically direct. In prison, `他のやり方があったら!! 教えて下さいよ!!` converts the same directness into accusatory demand. After escape, `ジークの居場所を特定する / それだけだ` compresses the current objective into minimal command. Generative Eren should therefore vary **speech act and emotional object**, not replace his directness with a one-note slogan register.

### Historia - wrongness named before duty accepted

`いいわけが無い / こんなことが許されるはずは無い` precedes acceptance of Beast inheritance. This strongly supports the post-Christa anti-euphemism rule: even when Historia chooses burden, she need not sanctify it linguistically.

### Hange - humor can coexist with internal command collapse

The outward rebellious-phase joke sits beside `何で私なんか団長にしたんだよ…`. Hange's playful surface can function as confrontation management while internal confidence is severely degraded. Do not generate humor as evidence that Hange experiences the scene as low stakes.

### Armin - conditional strategy versus relational hope

Armin's Founder reasoning uses conditional, model-building language and can include the severe succession branch. The closing `話し合えばきっと…わかってくれる` is much simpler and relational. Written voice should preserve this register split: technical uncertainty produces branching explanation; personal hope can become concise and repetitive.

### Mikasa - interruption and action remain semantically important

V27 again gives Mikasa less extended exposition than surrounding speakers. `何があっても私はエレンを―` is interrupted and must remain so; completing it invents evidence. Scarf touch, refusal of Louise's intimacy, and immediate protection of Armin carry relational meaning that her sparse speech does not spell out.

### Jean - concrete accountability plus ordinary irritation

Jean's present Eren critique is anchored in who was exposed or lost rather than abstract political theory. The railroad flashback preserves peer irritation as a low-stakes control: adult Jean is not a permanently formal squad leader.

### Connie - kinship metaphor under grief

`俺とサシャは双子みてぇなもんだった / 自分が半分なくなっちまったみてぇだ` is unusually direct emotional self-description. It should remain grief-state evidence, not a generalized tendency toward elaborate introspection.

### Gabi - `善良なエルディア人` as self-category under threat

`私は善良なエルディア人なの!!` shows that political vocabulary can become first-person identity maintenance. Under Kaya's challenge, Gabi initially reaches for broad historical indictment rather than hedged interpersonal reasoning. The voice model should permit categorical escalation when identity boundaries are threatened.

### Falco - person-level causal correction

Falco's strongest V27 moral speech is concrete rather than grand: he explains the military reason for the attack and states that Kaya's mother was not guilty. His register supports **causal correction + apology** without requiring a full ideological manifesto.

### Kaya - repeated victim-specific questioning

Kaya's argument works through repetition and scale control: what did her mother do, why was she eaten, what was the reason? Her speech should not be stylized as abstract political philosophy; its force comes from forcing general claims back onto a specific person.

### Levi - naming as moral compression

`その村じゃない / 「ラガコ村」だ` is a high-value Levi anchor. Naming the place/victims performs moral correction without extended confession. Agreement with Zeke that time is short remains proposition-specific.

### Yelena - ideological vocabulary of autonomous agency

`主体的な思想` is now a core Yelena lexical target. Her strategic speech can combine reverence for historical transformation, self-aware admission of deception, and insistence that the high-leverage actor possess his own conviction.

### Floch and public factional voice - strategic saviorhood and inherited slogans

Floch's speech frames Eren as the person capable of saving the Eldian state and describes hidden support inside the military. The crowd's `心臓を捧げよ` proves that inherited institutional slogans can migrate into anti-institutional mobilization. Any generative use of that phrase now requires speaker/faction/time-state tagging.


## Volume 28 voice/state deltas

### Eren — freedom as categorical self-definition and accusatory ontology

V28 gives the strongest current cluster of Eren's explicit freedom vocabulary: `オレは自由だ`, `自由意志`, `無知`, `不自由`, and `奴隷`. The voice remains direct and categorical but is not merely slogan repetition. Eren answers causal questions, cites Zeke as an information source, constructs biological/memory explanations of Armin and Mikasa, and converts abstract freedom language into direct second-person judgments.

**Reconstruction constraint:** use this register for high-stakes autonomy/identity conflict. Do not project `自由`/`奴隷` language into ordinary conversation without a matching trigger.

### Mikasa — fragmented denial plus Eren-specific self-interpretation

Mikasa's V28 response remains sparse. `違う` and the Eren-specific claim that she became strong because it was *him* carry more weight than extended theoretical speech. Her immediate restraint of Armin is then followed by visual shock rather than a long verbal explanation. Written-voice modeling should preserve the possibility that action/silence carries state information that her dialogue does not elaborate.

### Armin — causal interrogation and value-internal challenge

Armin first tests Eren's autonomy claim by asking whether the secret Yelena meeting was also his free choice. After Eren attacks Mikasa, Armin shifts to a value-internal accusation: is hurting Mikasa the freedom Eren wanted? `誰が…奴隷だ` is a compressed refusal of Eren's imposed identity rather than a discursive essay. V28 therefore adds anger-state brevity without erasing Armin's diagnostic reasoning.

### Hange — `ジークがそう言っただけだ` as source discipline

The force of `だけ` is methodologically important. Hange downgrades an accepted mechanism because its evidentiary basis is merely Zeke's testimony, then moves to provisional precaution. Technical voice remains explicit about source status even under emergency.

### Levi — terse command remains emotionally saturated

V28 strengthens the anti-caricature rule. `紅茶があるだろ` returns as an ordinary preference line now tied to retrospective operational guilt about allowing wine. Levi's rebuke that Zeke does not know how many comrades the Corps has had to kill compresses grief/history into tactical speech. Concision remains a register feature, not evidence of low attachment.

### Zeke — regretful surface, epistemic monopoly, and salvation vocabulary

Adult Zeke's voice repeatedly pairs conversational/regretful markers with extreme coercion: `やりたくなかった`, `悲しいよ`, `決別`, `わかりっこない`, and `俺達にしか…わからない`. He also frames Paradis's belief that it has `選択肢` as delusion. The later `唯一の救い` cluster converts the same epistemic exclusivity into moral finality.

**Reconstruction constraint:** do not infer egalitarianism from Zeke's casual or sympathetic surface. His written register can soften the delivery of decisions that leave others no agency.

### Artur Blouse — dialectal ethics without register elevation

The forest speech remains in rural/dialectal forms (`〜や`, `〜んや`) while delivering the volume's most sophisticated intergenerational responsibility argument. Seriousness should not cause a generated Artur voice to become polished standard-Japanese political prose.

### Gabi — categorical enemy speech opens into interpersonal uncertainty

`私が憎くないの?` is a high-value V28 delta. Gabi's current voice can move from inherited categorical identity to a direct personal question when concrete people violate the reciprocal-hatred model she expects. This is destabilization, not yet a stable replacement register.

### Floch — temporal legitimacy and coercive future rhetoric

Floch increasingly speaks through a contrast between future/new order and obsolete military/custom. Eren is framed as the leader who can save Eldia, and political disagreement becomes evidence of belonging to a dead era. This register should be tagged as current factional rhetoric, not generalized backward into pre-survivor Floch.

### Grisha — parental second person merged with collective salvation

`お前が みんなを救うんだ` is a key V28 anchor. The grammatical movement from child `お前` to collective `みんな` compresses Zeke's personhood into a public mission. Praise for Zeke's historical “understanding” is likewise relationship-conditioned ideological approval.

### Ksaver — ordinary reciprocal speech, scientific explanation, then surrogate-parent confession

Ksaver's register spans light catch/baseball companionship, relatively calm technical explanation of Titan research, blunt survival advice concerning Grisha/Dina, and emotionally exposed confession about his wife/son. The continuity is not a single lexical tic but a low-grandiosity interpersonal style that can carry both genuine care and catastrophic political conclusions.

### Lexical network after V28

The following terms now require speaker/state tagging rather than theme-only retrieval:

- `自由 / 自由意志` — Eren self-authorship; not equivalent to reciprocal freedom.
- `奴隷 / 家畜` — deprived-agency vocabulary; application can itself become coercive.
- `救う / 救い` — Sasha->Nicolo rescue, Grisha's mission for Zeke, Ksaver/Zeke euthanasia; morally non-uniform.
- `子供` — object of inherited responsibility, political mission, and reproductive abolition.
- `選択肢` — Eren claims choice as selfhood; Zeke treats others' options as illusion.
- `決別` — Zeke's soft-surface label for coercive rupture.
- `森` — Artur's social ontology of inherited killing, later literalized by the Levi/Zeke battle.

## Volume 29 voice/state deltas

### Eren — apparent ideological assent and combat compression share the same direct shell

`オレはやる`, `その日を迎えるまで 進み続ける`, `そうだろ? 兄さん` and later `来いよ……ライナー` preserve an unusually direct first-person/second-person register across alliance and combat. The surface continuity is important, but it does **not** prove terminal motive continuity. A generative model should preserve directness while allowing audience/incentive-dependent strategic opacity.

### Zeke — tentative ontology, confident brother role

Zeke's report of reconstruction is phenomenologically hedged: years may have passed, or an instant; `あれが…「道」…なのか`. By contrast, his battlefield relation to Eren is confident and warm: `よく一人で耐えたな`, `後はお兄ちゃんに任せろ`. Do not model Zeke as uniformly categorical: unknown mechanisms can receive hedging even when political conclusions are absolute.

### Armin — private hedge versus public performance

`嘘だと思う` is a high-value epistemic anchor because `と思う` marks his Eren reconstruction as inference. In Yelena's presence, he can perform emotionally emphatic agreement and world-savior language that his private plan contradicts. **Audience-conditioned sincerity** is now a mandatory voice variable.

### Mikasa — unresolved why-question plus object-action brevity

Mikasa does not replace the scarf crisis with a long doctrine. She asks why Eren would hurt them if he were pretending and later says `そう…置いて行く`. Sparse speech plus object handling remains more canonical than explanatory monologue.

### Pieck — dry tactical economy and explicit trust hierarchy

`私はマーレを信じてない` paired with trust in fought-alongside comrades is her strongest current ideological/relationship voice anchor. `そこ` is a compact tactical reply whose literal meaning, hostility, and ambush function coincide. Pieck's wit should remain dry and context-sensitive rather than flamboyant.

### Gabi — ideological complexity collapses into `人`

`悪魔なんていなかった… / この島には…人がいるだけ` replaces an elaborate inherited category with a simpler person noun. Her register remains emotionally intense and direct even as its ontology changes.

### Falco — concrete confession and ordinary future nouns

Falco's love confession is structurally practical: he names Warrior candidacy, Armor inheritance, living long, and marriage rather than elaborate romantic metaphor. His emotional voice remains causal and concrete.

### Onyankopon — futurity in one declarative line

`子供は未来だ!!` condenses his anti-euthanasia position into a future-centered claim consistent with earlier pluralist speech. Do not merge it with Yelena's salvation vocabulary; both discuss the world, but their moral subject differs.

### Reiner — self-projected mercy language under hostile action

`もういい`, `これ以上誰も苦しめなくていい`, `もう眠れ` is empathetic/exhaustion-state language directed at Eren while combat continues. It should be tagged specifically to high-stakes mirrored-suffering contexts.

### Yelena / Magath — competing world-savior registers

Yelena's Zeke/Eren theology and Magath's `真の英雄ヘーロス` demonstrate that hero/salvation vocabulary is factionally portable. Retrieval or generation must tag speaker, institution, audience, and desired political order.

### Lexical network after V29

- `信頼 / 信じる` — collaborator selection, comrade loyalty, model confidence, exploitable faith.
- `救う / 救済` — euthanasia, world-saving heroes, child/future protection; morally non-uniform.
- `進む` — shared forward-motion shell with potentially divergent objectives.
- `子供 / 未来` — Onyankopon futurity, Nile protection, Falco ordinary future, euthanasia's negation.
- `悪魔 / 人` — explicit Gabi category replacement.
- `嘘` — Armin's Eren hypothesis and strategic explanation variable.
- `騙し討ち` — tactical surprise plus broader audience/information asymmetry.

## Volume 30 voice/state deltas

### Eren Jaeger / エレン・イェーガー

V30 gives one of the cleanest demonstrations that Eren's categorical register is compatible with strategic deception rather than transparent motive disclosure. He explicitly retracts the V29 euthanasia presentation with `こんなふざけた計画 オレは到底受け入れられない` and `あんたに話を合わせていただけだ`, then returns to the older existence grammar `オレがこの世に生まれたからだ`. In Grisha's memory he shifts into imperative, debt-activating speech (`何をしてる 立てよ 父さん`, `進み続けるんだ`) and in Paths toward Ymir into direct personhood/choice language (`お前は奴隷じゃない`, `ただの人だ`, `お前が決めていい`).

**Model constraint:** terse categorical diction is high-confidence; proposition-level sincerity is context-dependent. Do not generate every categorical Eren utterance as transparent confession.

### Zeke Jaeger / ジーク・イェーガー

The Colt exchange confirms the split between conversational empathy and absolute command. `弟を想う気持ちは…よくわかる` is immediately followed by `だから…残念だ` and the scream. In Paths his register toward Eren is older-brother rescue/paternalism, while toward Ymir it becomes explicit sovereign grammar (`俺は王家の血を引く者だ`, `俺の命令に従え`).

**Model constraint:** warmth, regret, and familial language do not predict shared decision authority.

### Grisha Jaeger / グリシャ・イェーガー

V30 expands Grisha's written voice across incompatible states: pleading physician (`子供だけは…`-type refusal), revolutionary mission under pressure, post-violence epistemic protest, and explicit paternal repair (`すまない…私はひどい父親だった`, `お前を愛している`, `もっと…一緒に遊んでやればよかった`). This sharply raises confidence that his voice model must be state-indexed rather than uniformly doctrinal.

### King Fritz / 初代フリッツ王

His register is pure assignment and possession: `お前は自由だ` is materially contradicted by a hunt, while `起きて働け`, `お前はそのために生まれてきた`, and reproductive/cannibalistic commands define persons through function. The lexical opposition with Eren's `オレがこの世に生まれたからだ` is analytically load-bearing.

### Ymir Fritz / 始祖ユミル

**Abstention remains mandatory.** V30 supplies almost no ordinary spoken-Japanese sample from Ymir herself. Her tears, attention, obedience, and eventual break from Zeke's command are behavior/visual evidence, not a license to invent an idiolect. Any free-generation voice model for historical Ymir is currently **NOT READY**.



## Volume 31 voice/state deltas

### Eren Jaeger / エレン・イェーガー

The Founder broadcast is a high-authority population-address sample that remains recognizably Eren rather than becoming bureaucratic narration: `オレの名は`, `オレの目的は`, `オレはその望みを拒む`, and the return of `駆逐` organize identity, threat model, purpose, rejection, and stopping rule in direct sequence. The register is categorical and low-hedge but can still carry multi-step causal explanation.

**Model constraint:** V31 resolves the operational Rumbling target in direct speech, but the categorical form must not be generalized into complete transparency about every hidden motive or future-memory state.

### Mikasa Ackerman / ミカサ・アッカーマン

The Marley `家族` answer and later concise scarf reclamation reinforce the frozen sparse/action-carried voice model. Large relational state changes occur through a few words plus object handling and retrospective framing.

**Model constraint:** do not compensate for sparse canonical speech by generating long abstract relationship monologues unless a later source state specifically warrants them.

### Armin Arlert / アルミン・アルレルト

V31 adds two useful extremes. At the genocide scale he can label the event bluntly as `前代未聞の大虐殺`. Under executive overload with Mikasa he becomes fragmented, irritated and self-comparing rather than polished. His later Connie intervention is again compressed by urgency and embodiment.

**Model constraint:** analytical sophistication does not imply uniformly calm or elaborately explanatory delivery. State-dependent overload is now canonical.

### Jean Kirstein / ジャン・キルシュタイン

Jean continues to approach moral questions through material accounting: enemies, survival, beneficiaries, costs, and what the group is actually going to do. Even when discussing genocide he sounds like a person trying to determine who pays and who benefits before he converts judgment into action.

**Model constraint:** idealized abstract moral rhetoric should remain less likely than concrete consequence language.

### Annie Leonhart / アニ・レオンハート

Annie's V31 self-accounting is unusually explicit without changing her low-temperature register. She can say that lives once lacked value, that her father changed what mattered, that others also have important people, and that her crimes are irreversible—then add the severe qualification that she would repeat them to return to him.

**Model constraint:** flattened affect must not be generated as shallow cognition or lack of moral vocabulary.

### Connie Springer / コニー・スプリンガー

The Ragako sequence anchors ethical conflict in ordinary family terms such as `母ちゃん`, not political abstraction. His eventual reframe—wanting to be the kind of soldier his mother would be proud of—changes the means while preserving the family register.

### Onyankopon / オニャンコポン

His execution speech is high-affect and concrete: homeland, family, what he helped Eldia for, what the Rumbling will destroy, and the contradiction between Paradis knowing indiscriminate killing and accepting it for others. Anger organizes a reciprocity argument rather than dissolving into generic shouting.

### Floch Forster / フロック・フォルスター

Floch's public register now fuses collective victory, imperial rebirth, `自由`, loyalty testing, and death threat. He speaks as if the victorious collective has one authorized will while personally enforcing who may remain inside it.

### Lexical network after V31

- `家族 / 父 / 母ちゃん / 仲間` — nonfungible attachment targets whose protection can support care or instrumental violence.
- `島の悪魔 / 帝国 / 敵` — political categories that compress plural persons into governable target classes.
- `駆逐 / 大虐殺 / 世界を救う` — competing totalizing action vocabularies whose moral valence cannot be inferred from heroic form alone.
- `帰る / 守る / 救う` — benevolent-seeming terminal verbs whose reconstruction requires separate modeling of permissible means.

## Volume 32 voice/state deltas

### Eren Jaeger / エレン・イェーガー

V32 makes Eren's lexical continuity ethically diagnostic. In private he tells Floch `世界を滅ぼす` and returns to the old extermination grammar `一匹残らず駆逐する`; with Historia he rejects a hereditary sacrifice system in concrete embodied terms (`島の生贄`, bearing children, parent and child consuming one another); in remembered friendship he says he wants the others' lives to `続いてほしい` and for them to `ずっと…幸せに生きていけるように`. These are not contradictory voice models. They show one stable direct register carrying both particular care and population-scale exclusion.

**Model constraint:** warmth toward named intimates cannot be used as a proxy for universal moral restraint. `駆逐` remains a high-confidence Eren lexical shell whose target class changes across the series.

### Historia Reiss / ヒストリア・レイス

Historia's V32 objection is one of her clearest post-V16 direct self-authoring samples: `そんなの間違ってる!!` and the claim that silence would leave her unable to `胸を張って生きていく`. She does not retreat into Christa-style deferential self-erasure even when the speaker opposing her is the person who once defended her from sacrifice.

**Model constraint:** Historia can accept heavy public burden while still naming a proposed solution wrong in direct, morally owned language.

### Armin Arlert / アルミン・アルレルト

At the harbor Armin deliberately performs categorical in-group loyalty—`そんなことやるわけねぇだろ!!`—as cover for a bloodless deception plan. The utterance is intentionally false at the proposition level but exactly fitted to Samuel and Daz's expected political frame.

**Model constraint:** audience-conditioned deception is now repeatedly demonstrated. Dialogue-form sincerity must be inferred from objective, audience, and action, not surface earnestness.

### Connie Springer / コニー・スプリンガー

Connie's harbor language exposes the cost of moral action through peer vocabulary rather than doctrine. `俺達は仲間じゃないのかよ!?` and the remembered ordinary promise to increase land and eat meat turn `裏切り者` into a relational wound. His eventual gunfire is followed by screaming rather than triumphant justification.

### Annie Leonhart / アニ・レオンハート

After judging Liberio/family rescue effectively lost, Annie's register contracts to exhaustion: `私が戦う理由はなくなった`, `私は降りる`, `もう…戦いたくない`. The low-temperature delivery remains compatible with severe affect and terminal-motive collapse.

### Theo Magath / テオ・マガト

Magath's V32 revision uses accountability vocabulary rather than heroic absolution: `軽々しくも正義を語った`, `正当化`, and finally the counterfactual wish that the Warrior children could have `ただ普通に生きることができたら`. His last exchange with Shadis is stripped down to names rather than titles.

### Lexical network after V32

- `裏切り者 / 仲間` — belonging vocabulary becomes morally reciprocal; opposition to one's own side can be experienced as real betrayal without making genocide permissible.
- `話し合う` — Marco's unfinished demand returns as a standard the coalition recognizes precisely after dialogue has already failed repeatedly.
- `正当化 / 正義 / 責任` — adult actors distinguish explaining causal history from cleansing their own participation.
- `世界を滅ぼす / 駆逐` — Eren's elimination vocabulary now has a directly specified human-civilizational target.
- `生贄 / 子を産ませる / 親子` — hereditary survival systems remain framed through bodies and intergenerational jurisdiction.
- `胸を張って生きる / 普通に生きる / 幸せに生きる` — ordinary-life language functions as an ethical counterweight to systems that instrumentalize descendants or outsiders.



## Volume 33 voice/state deltas

### Eren
V33 adds one of the strongest private motive anchors in the corpus: `ガッカリした`. It must sit beside, not replace, the categorical public/Paths register: `地鳴らしは止まらない`, `お前達は自由だ`, `進み続ける`, and `戦え`. His high-stakes voice can therefore move from shame-filled confession to extremely compressed categorical declarations without requiring either register to be fake.

### Armin
Armin's intimate register becomes unusually direct in `会いたかったからだ…アニに`, while his moral self-accounting remains unsentimental: `僕もとっくにバケモノだよ`. After formal succession, the final challenge `君のどこが自由なのか` crystallizes his late command voice: inquiry persists, but it can become adversarial rather than conciliatory.

### Mikasa
Mikasa remains lexically sparse but high-information. `殺さない` sets the present action constraint; `帰ってきて…` compresses attachment, opposition, and a desired relational restoration into almost no explanatory abstraction.

### Hange
Hange's succession criterion is epistemic: `理解することをあきらめない姿勢`. Terminal combat then preserves the old fascination register in `…やっぱり巨人って 素晴らしいな`, demonstrating identity continuity under death rather than a new endorsement of the Rumbling.

### Levi
Levi's Hange farewell is extremely compressed: `心臓を捧げよ`, then `じゃあな ハンジ`. After the Paths failure he returns immediately to terse command framing: `どうする？ 団長`. The V33 sample reinforces that brevity and grief are compatible.

### Annie
Annie's withdrawal is stated without ideological grandeur: `人類を救うとか 私には…よくわからない` and `私はもう戦えない`. Her language centers motivational exhaustion and particular attachments rather than universal doctrine.

### Reiner / Fort Salta institutional register
Reiner's self-accounting remains atonement-resistant: saving the remaining world would not make him able to forgive himself. Fort Salta's adult speech shifts into formal collective responsibility: the catastrophe is attributed to adults who cultivated hatred and exported unresolved problems to the island. This is institutional confession, not evidence that individual perpetrators on either side become innocent.

### Lexical network after V33

- **Eren:** `ガッカリした / 自由 / 止まらない / 進み続ける / 戦え`
- **Armin:** `会いたい / バケモノ / 理解 / 団長 / どこが自由`
- **Mikasa:** `殺さない / 帰ってきて`
- **Hange:** `理解をあきらめない / 巨人 / 素晴らしい`
- **Levi:** `心臓を捧げよ / じゃあな / 団長`
- **Annie:** `よくわからない / もう戦えない`

V33 therefore strengthens state-conditioned voice simulation precisely by showing how the same character moves between confession, intimacy, institutional transfer, grief, and categorical action language.


## Volume 34 voice/state deltas

### Eren

The final volume supplies the strongest exception set to an over-categorical Eren simulator. In private terminal disclosure he repeatedly uses rough uncertainty and ordinary vulnerability: `何でかわかんねぇけど`, `やりたかったんだ`, and `死にたくねぇ`. These coexist with the established categorical `進み続ける / 駆逐 / 戦え / 自由` register. The table-scene admission also matters: Eren says he was desperate to push his friends away but partly moved with `勢いと流れ`, so later voice reconstruction must distinguish strategic intent from perfectly scripted speech.

**Model constraint:** private uncertainty, jealousy and fear are not out-of-character exceptions to be discarded. They are source-canonical states that sit beneath the public categorical shell.

### Mikasa

The cabin sequence and final action keep Mikasa's voice characteristically sparse. `帰りたい` compresses an entire desired relational world; Eren's request that she discard the scarf is answered primarily through action when she rewraps it and later thanks him for having wrapped it around her. Her decisive lethal action therefore should not be paired with invented ideological monologue.

### Armin

Armin's V34 register ranges from ordinary-life phenomenology to formal evidentiary diplomacy. His most important existential language is not abstract doctrine but `このなんでもない一瞬` and the thought that he may have been born for a childhood race with Eren and Mikasa. Before Muller he shifts to formal proof: their inability to become Titans while rifles are trained on them is `我々が人である何よりの証明`. Three years later his peace strategy becomes `僕達の物語`.

**Model constraint:** Armin does not end as a naive believer that conflict disappears. His voice combines evidence, acknowledged uncertainty, moral injury and narrative invitation.

### Zeke

Zeke's terminal revision becomes almost anti-doctrinal. `ただ投げて / 取って / また投げる` names the purposeless repetition of catch with Ksaver. The simplicity of the syntax is the point: a theory built on suffering/reproduction is punctured by an ordinary relation whose value does not need further justification.

### Historia / postwar public language

Historia's narration preserves uncertainty rather than claiming final peace. The public island rhetoric instead reuses `勝てば生きる / 負ければ死ぬ / 戦わなければ勝てない / 戦え`. Eren's lexical grammar survives him institutionally, which means recurrence of wording is not proof of personal continuity or endorsement by the narrator.

### Lexical network after V34

- **Eren:** `自由 / 進み続ける / 戦え / やりたかった / わかんねぇ / 死にたくねぇ`
- **Armin:** `なんでもない一瞬 / 生まれてきた / 証明 / 物語`
- **Mikasa:** `帰りたい / マフラー / ありがとう`
- **Zeke/Ksaver:** `投げる / 取る / 繰り返す`
- **Postwar polity:** `戦え / 和平 / 物語`

V34 therefore closes the written-voice corpus with a crucial simulation warning: the series' most famous categorical slogans are only one register among confession, ordinary memory, grief, proof, humor and uncertainty.
