# 『進撃の巨人』V01–V27 Approximately-75% Checkpoint Synthesis
## Freedom becomes constitutional: recognition, inherited worlds, strategic bodies, and the sovereignty of protection

## 0. Checkpoint function, authority, and source boundary

This document is the canonical **provisional cross-volume synthesis for Japanese manga Volumes 1–27**. It performs four related but non-identical tasks:

1. it synthesizes the cumulative literary, political, ethical, character, linguistic, and formal architecture of V01–V27;
2. it re-adjudicates the thirty-two major longitudinal claims preserved in `AOT_CHECKPOINT_50P_V01-V19_SYNTHESIS.md` without modifying that earlier checkpoint or making its boundary-limited conclusions appear retrospectively defective;
3. it audits the closed V20–V27 prospective character-model experiment against the immutable V01–V19 prediction register;
4. it defines the exact evidentiary and administrative conditions under which Volume 28 may be opened.

It does **not** replace the twenty-seven canonical volume readings. The evidence route remains:

> checkpoint synthesis → volume deep reading → evidence ID and image locator → original Japanese manga page when exact wording, panel construction, visual sequence, or disputed interpretation is load-bearing.

The checkpoint was constructed from the canonical V01–V19 synthesis; the native v2.1 readings for V19–V27; the frozen V01–V19 prediction register; the complete V20–V27 adjudication ledger; the V27-current behavior, relationship, ordinary-life, Japanese-voice, and readiness ledgers; and selective renewed inspection of the Japanese V25–V27 CBZs. The primary-source reinspection concentrated on the Eren/Reiner basement and declaration sequence, Liberio civilian distinctions and terror reversals, Armin's Colossal silhouette and the V26 mirror sequence, Historia's royal-blood burden, the railroad memory, the Gabi/Kaya farm confrontation, Yelena's testimony, the Zackly assassination and public crowd, Eren's escape, and Armin's final dialogic wager.

No Volume 28 page, dialogue, plot development, adaptation material, guidebook, interview, fandom summary, or later-series hindsight has been used to close a V27-bounded uncertainty. Where V01–V27 leaves causal architecture open—especially Eren's complete motive structure, Historia's pregnancy decision, Zeke and Yelena's complete program, the origin and full operation of Paths, and the political destination of the pro-Eren movement—this checkpoint preserves the question.

### 0.1 Authority relation to the V19 checkpoint

`AOT_CHECKPOINT_50P_V01-V19_SYNTHESIS.md` remains immutable and authoritative for what could responsibly be synthesized at the V19 boundary. This checkpoint does not overwrite it. It records how later evidence changes the status of its claims through the controlled vocabulary:

- `PRESERVE`
- `STRENGTHEN`
- `REVISE`
- `DOWNGRADE`
- `REJECT`
- `OPEN`

A `REVISE` or `REJECT` transition here is not an accusation that the V19 checkpoint was careless. It means V20–V27 created evidence unavailable at V19 that requires a narrower, more differentiated, or differently organized formulation.

### 0.2 Governing V27 thesis

The V19 checkpoint concluded that the first half of the manga progressively destroys inherited categories—human/Titan, true/false self, state/humanity, safety/freedom, monster/person—while requiring characters to act after those categories become unreliable.

By V27, category collapse has not produced peace. It has produced a new problem:

> **Freedom becomes constitutional.**

Here “constitutional” does not mean that the manga has supplied a stable written constitution. It means that freedom can no longer be modeled only as an individual's escape from confinement or even as an individual's authorship of action under inherited constraints. Multiple persons, institutions, peoples, and factions now claim authorship simultaneously. Their projects collide. The central question becomes:

> **What reciprocal limits, jurisdictions, procedures, and recognitions can prevent one actor's self-authorship from turning everyone else into a strategic resource?**

This is why V27's local formulation—**protection becomes a sovereignty problem**—is not merely an isolated political complication. It is the mature V27 form of a series-long trajectory linking walls, bodies, archives, military chains of command, royal inheritance, Titan succession, secrecy, sacrifice, friendship, home, and freedom.

The updated checkpoint standards are therefore:

- **Ethical:** authored responsibility must become reciprocal restraint; a person's or institution's commitment does not by itself confer the right to spend other persons.
- **Epistemic:** plural evidence and revisability must now include source position, strategic interest, information asymmetry, and the possibility that intimate knowledge has become temporally stale.
- **Political:** legitimacy depends not only on survival and comparative accountability, but on who may authorize sacrifice, who bears it, who can contest it, and whether protection preserves the protected as political subjects.
- **Character-modeling:** state, knowledge, pressure, relationship, role, constraint, and voice remain necessary, but the V20–V27 holdout shows that strong crisis-domain prediction is not equivalent to ordinary-life fluency or unrestricted novel-situation reconstruction.

# I. V01–V27 macroarchitecture

The first twenty-seven volumes can be organized into seven cumulative movements. These are not sealed arcs. Each movement changes the meaning of material inherited from the previous one.

## Movement A — V01–V04: catastrophe, birthright, plurality, and the first collapse of monster certainty

The opening movement creates a spatial and moral diagram that the later manga repeatedly reverses: humanity inside, Titans outside; home inside, annihilation outside; small bodies below, overwhelming bodies above; military action as the route from passive terror to agency. Yet even here, the structure is less simple than its imagery initially permits.

Eren's freedom desire precedes Carla's death. Her death transforms that desire into punitive and exterminatory form, but it does not create the underlying refusal of confinement. Armin's outside-world book supplies positive horizons rather than merely an enemy to destroy. Mikasa's survival and attachment show that personhood is relationally sustained before it becomes philosophically argued. Trost then destroys the model of the singular hero: survival requires heterogeneous capacity, tactical reasoning, labor, logistics, fear management, and people whose courage does not guarantee reward.

Eren's Titan transformation begins the first ontological collapse. The body classified as the enemy can contain first-person continuity, memory, attachment, and protective intention. The question “human or Titan?” becomes inadequate before the institutional world has language for the inadequacy.

## Movement B — V05–V08: trust under uncertainty, infiltrated humanity, and institutions that cannot wait for certainty

The Female Titan movement converts the earlier human/Titan boundary into an epistemic and institutional crisis. The enemy can inhabit the form, speech, training, and social relationships of a comrade. Trust cannot be reduced to successful prediction, because characters must choose before outcomes are known. Levi's uncertainty ethic refuses a procedural guarantee against regret. The Survey Corps becomes most legitimate not because it knows everything, but because it creates practices for acting while acknowledging what it does not know.

At the same time, Erwin's plans expose the danger inside competence. Secrecy, decoys, selective disclosure, and sacrifice may be strategically effective while concentrating decision authority in a few hands. The manga does not resolve this by choosing naïve transparency. It establishes a recurring problem: institutions sometimes require information control, but information control also creates unaccountable power and makes other people bear risks they did not authorize.

Annie's exposure further dismantles the empty-monster model. Her guarded affect, selective mercy, lethal mission commitment, relational vulnerability, and desire to return to her father coexist. Recognition neither acquits her nor makes opposition unnecessary.

## Movement C — V09–V12: home, language, role fracture, and recognition without acquittal

The Clash movement relocates the enemy inside ordinary speech and shared history. Reiner and Bertolt do not simply remove masks to reveal their “real” selves. Repeated social conduct has produced psychologically and morally real roles. Reiner's soldier identity is not erased by the Warrior mission; the conflict between them is precisely what makes him legible.

Ymir and Historia make naming, imposed identity, and chosen obligation central. “True self” cannot mean merely recovering an original name. Historia's later authorship emerges through conduct, relation, refusal, and responsibility. Ymir distinguishes a role imposed on her from obligations she later chooses to accept. Freedom is already becoming compatible with selected attachment rather than synonymous with isolation.

The movement also demonstrates that shared humanity does not prevent lethal conflict. Reiner, Bertolt, Ymir, Eren, Mikasa, and the 104th can recognize one another as persons while remaining organized by incompatible missions and homes. Home is therefore no longer only place. It is a relational and political claim made by multiple sides.

## Movement D — V13–V17: state violence, inherited memory, bodily office, and authored identity

The Uprising movement turns the internal human state into an explicit object of judgment. Government and humanity materially diverge. The royal order preserves itself through secrecy, memory manipulation, inherited Titan office, bodily succession, and the conversion of persons into instruments of a political theology.

The coup does not establish that successful revolution purifies authority. The replacement order is comparatively more legitimate because it is more accountable to public survival and less dependent on systematic deception, but it remains elite, coercive, and strategically secretive. Comparative legitimacy is real without becoming moral innocence.

Historia's refusal of Rod's script and Eren's crisis of worth transform freedom into authorship under inheritance. Yet V27 will later prevent the Uprising from being simplified into “Historia always refuses sacrifice” or “self-authorship always produces liberation.” V13–V17 establishes the importance of refusing imposed identity and inherited command. It does not prove that authored responsibility can never include accepting a burden under coercive conditions.

The Founder also expands Titan power beyond weaponry. It is archive, office, architecture, memory regime, infrastructure, and transferable political inheritance. More complete memory does not necessarily create freedom; the Reiss inheritance shows that an archive can transmit domination so completely that remembered authority displaces present judgment.

## Movement E — V18–V20: unconditional worth, recognized enemies, sacrifice without reimbursement, and victory's residue

Carla's birth-based affirmation in V18 universalizes worth beyond exceptional capacity. V19 then brings the Shiganshina battle to a point where Reiner and Bertolt can no longer be treated as unknowable monsters. Bertolt's calm is not proof that he has become inhuman. It is a marked transition from delegated agency toward authored lethal commitment. Recognition, comradeship, guilt, and opposition coexist.

V20 closes the battle by testing what remains after heroic rhetoric is stripped away. Erwin's charge, Armin's self-sacrifice, Levi's serum decision, Bertolt's plea, and the surviving recruits do not yield a doctrine in which sacrifice contains its own meaning. Meaning survives through promises, names, remembered speech, private dreams, grief, and the responsibilities of those who continue. Tactical success does not reimburse the dead.

Levi's choice for Erwin is neither a simple application nor a betrayal of his earlier uncertainty ethic. It is a relationship-conditioned decision under changed conditions: Erwin can no longer meaningfully choose, the mission that organized his life has consumed him, Armin remains tied to a future horizon, and Levi accepts authorship rather than pretending an objective algorithm decided. This is a crucial revision: returning choice to another person is an important ethical tendency, not a universal procedure applicable when agency has collapsed.

## Movement F — V21–V24: the world as interested testimony, inherited scripts, obsolete solutions, and self-authored hell

The basement does not replace mystery with transparent history. It changes the epistemic scale. The photograph establishes technologically advanced human civilization outside the Walls; Grisha's archive and inherited memories establish a world of Marleyan rule, Eldian subordination, restorationist counter-history, child instrumentalization, and contested accounts of ancient power. But the archive is positioned testimony. Marleyan official history and Restorationist counter-history are both interested. Oppression does not automatically make every counter-narrative accurate. Grisha's own interpretive habits show how a person can reject one inherited ideology while accepting another too quickly.

The serum inheritance and the historical archive therefore form parallel structures. Both transfer power, memory, obligation, and unresolved commands into a new bearer. The ethical question is not only what is inherited, but whether the recipient can interpret, revise, refuse, or redirect it.

V22 moves from revelation to action under incomplete knowledge. Paradis discloses parts of the truth, constructs provisional models of Titans and Paths, recognizes the mindless Titans as transformed compatriots, and reaches the sea. The sea is both fulfilled horizon and relocated boundary. Armin sees expanded possibility; Eren sees enemies across the water and asks whether eliminating them would produce freedom. The question remains interrogative at V22. It is not retroactively converted into a settled later policy.

V23 turns “the outside world” from archive into society. Marley must be decomposed into state command, imperial strategy, racial hierarchy, Warrior institutions, internment-zone families, candidate children, conquered subjects, foreign enemies, and ordinary social life. Gabi, Falco, Reiner, Porco, Pieck, Zeke, Colt, and the Braun family inhabit different positions inside that system. The enemy category becomes internally populated.

V24 then shows people inheriting compromised scripts and becoming responsible for what they continue. Marcel's manipulation of Reiner's selection destroys the idea that Warrior status cleanly expresses merit. Reiner responds not by returning to an untouched authentic self, but by constructing a role: `俺がマルセルになる`. Annie can reject euphemistic Warrior language while remaining an interested participant. Young Reiner helps transmit persistence and exterminatory language to Eren. Adult Eren distinguishes a hell imposed from outside from a hell entered by pushing one's own back—and states that what lies beyond may be hope or another hell. Self-authorship increases ownership; it does not guarantee moral goodness.

## Movement G — V25–V27: recognition without reconciliation, victory as captivity, and protection as sovereignty

V25 is the decisive test of whether recognition automatically reduces violence. Eren tells Reiner that the people across the sea and inside the Walls are the same. Reiner rejects total structural exculpation and names his own desire to be a hero. Neither recognition ends the attack. Willy's declaration and Eren's transformation make private understanding and public enemy construction simultaneous. The manga has not returned to monster ontology. It has made war recognizably human.

V26 refuses to let Liberio remain a clean victory. Eren's indispensability becomes leverage over his own chain of command. Armin becomes the Colossal terror once associated with the destruction of his home. Sasha dies after extraction. Gabi's inherited ideology becomes reinforced by first-person bereavement. Falco's better causal understanding does not override attachment. Zeke appears as cooperating strategic actor without becoming trusted. The Volunteers reveal that Paradisian modernization is relationally imported rather than self-generated. The Rumbling proposal turns royal-blood bodies into deterrent infrastructure.

The final mirror sequence—`戦わなければ / 勝てない / 戦え / 戦え`—does not simply prove determination. Eren addresses himself as commander and commanded object. Freedom-seeking becomes self-discipline, perhaps self-coercion. Victory produces captivity because successful action binds Eren, Armin, Mikasa, Jean, the military, the Volunteers, Gabi, Falco, Reiner, and the civilian populations more tightly into one causal structure.

V27 brings that structure inside Paradis. Every major actor claims protective necessity. Historia accepts a royal-blood reproductive and inheritance burden she explicitly recognizes as wrong. Eren rejects the future in which Historia and her descendants are treated like livestock, but his resistance does not by itself confer the right to override the military. The military's containment of Eren, the detention of the Volunteers, Yelena's secret recruitment, Floch's internal network, Zackly's assassination, and public mobilization around Eren fracture the relation among formal office, strategic capacity, popular legitimacy, and actual coercive power.

Protection has become a sovereignty problem because the dispute is no longer only over which plan is strategically correct. It is over **who may decide**, whose body or future may be spent, whose information may be withheld, whose attachment counts as evidence, and whether those claimed as beneficiaries retain standing to reject the means used in their name.

# II. Governing checkpoint thesis: freedom becomes a problem of coexistence and jurisdiction

## 2.1 Freedom as spatial horizon

In the early manga, freedom is easy to picture and difficult to achieve. The Walls are visible. The outside is imaginable through Armin's book. The Titans are spatial obstacles. Eren's anger can attach itself to a map: inside versus outside, cage versus horizon.

Yet the early formulation already contains two distinct goods. One is negative freedom—the removal of confinement. The other is positive access to seas, deserts, ice fields, knowledge, movement, and a life not organized entirely by inherited fear. Carla's death adds vengeance, but Armin's book preserves the positive horizon that prevents freedom from being reducible to enemy extermination.

## 2.2 Freedom as authorship under inherited constraints

The middle volumes make spatial escape insufficient. A person can leave one enclosure while remaining governed by inherited scripts, names, memories, institutions, or relational compulsions. Historia's imposed Christa role, Reiner's Warrior mission, Bertolt's delegation, Ymir's cult identity, Eren's Founder inheritance, and the Reiss ideology all show that confinement can inhabit the self.

Freedom therefore becomes the capacity to judge what one has inherited and to author conduct without pretending that origin, role, memory, or relation disappear. The manga does not idealize an unencumbered self. Ymir's chosen obligation to Historia, Historia's public responsibility, Mikasa's attachment, Erwin's duty, and Levi's promises show that obligations can be authored rather than merely imposed.

## 2.3 Why self-authorship is not salvation

V20–V27 force a major revision of any celebratory self-authorship model.

Bertolt's authored decision in V19 produces lethal clarity. Grisha can reject Marleyan ideology and impose a restorationist mission on Zeke. Kruger can understand historical uncertainty and still coerce Grisha into a mission. Reiner can recognize that his status was manipulated and still continue the inherited operation. Willy can accept Tybur complicity and choose political authorship through staged sacrifice. Eren can distinguish imposed from self-entered hell and keep moving without knowing whether the result is hope or worse hell.

Agency is ethically important because it locates responsibility. It is not a certificate of justice.

The strongest V27 formulation is:

> **Self-authorship tells us who is acting; it does not settle whether that actor has the right to impose the action's costs on others.**

## 2.4 Freedom becomes constitutional

Once several actors possess agency, their freedoms can collide. Eren seeks freedom from military containment. Historia's freedom is threatened by a state strategy that turns reproduction and shortened succession into public infrastructure. The military claims authority to contain Eren in order to prevent unilateral use of the Founder. The Volunteers claim that formal institutions are too slow or compromised. Floch and the crowd claim that Eren's strategic indispensability authorizes political elevation. Armin seeks dialogue while contemplating lethal succession. Mikasa seeks information while protecting Armin from immediate danger. Gabi clings to an identity category that Falco increasingly contests at the level of particular persons.

No single act of “choosing for oneself” resolves this. Freedom has become constitutional because coexistence requires rules about:

- who controls force;
- who controls information;
- who can authorize bodily sacrifice;
- how leadership can be contested;
- when emergency action becomes usurpation;
- whether a protector can be refused by the protected;
- how past intimacy should be updated when present conduct diverges;
- whether a people can defend itself without recreating the logic that made people into strategic assets.

The old Survey Corps freedom horizon survives as refusal of inherited enclosure and commitment to a world not yet known. It is endangered when “keep moving forward” becomes a self-validating political mandate detached from reciprocal accountability.

## 2.5 Self-coercion and the internal Wall

The V26 mirror sequence changes the meaning of determination. Eren's imperatives are not addressed to an external subordinate. The commander and commanded occupy one body. This permits at least four interacting readings at V27:

1. Eren is affirming an enduring dispositional core: action is necessary because failure to fight means defeat.
2. Eren is choosing commitment under uncertainty, making forward motion an authored discipline.
3. Eren is coercing himself, narrowing the range of permissible feeling and reconsideration.
4. Eren is acting under influences—strategic, relational, inherited, or external—not yet fully legible at V27.

The evidence does not authorize reducing these to one. The important transition is that confinement no longer needs a wall or king. A freedom-seeking subject can construct an internal command structure severe enough to function as a new enclosure.

## 2.6 A revised ethical standard of freedom

At V19, freedom could be formulated as authorship under inherited constraints plus access to positive horizons. V27 requires an addition:

> **Freedom is authored, revisable agency exercised within a world of other agents whose bodies, relationships, futures, and right to refuse remain morally real.**

This is not pacifism. The series remains structured by catastrophic threats, coercive institutions, and decisions that cannot wait for consensus. It is a standard for judging emergency action: necessity may explain unilateral force, but it cannot automatically legitimate it or erase the standing of those made to bear its costs.

# III. Personhood, recognition, enemy categories, and dehumanization

## 3.1 Titan form never settles personhood

The V01–V19 corpus progressively established that Titan morphology cannot decide moral status. Eren, Ymir, Annie, Reiner, Bertolt, the Reiss line, Grisha, and the mindless Titans all connect human continuity to transformed bodies in different ways. By V22, the Scouts' recognition of the remaining Titans as compatriots makes the ontological correction explicit.

V21–V27 strengthens rather than replaces this conclusion. Eldian bodies become strategic because they can inherit Titans, be transformed by spinal fluid, transmit memories, activate powers through bloodline contact, and serve as deterrent infrastructure. None of these capacities makes the body less personal. The moral danger lies precisely in treating a person-bearing body as a mechanism.

## 3.2 Enemy categories are produced, not merely discovered

The manga's enemy categories are sustained by institutions, stories, rituals, spatial segregation, bodily markers, and repeated speech.

- Marley teaches the island-devil category through schooling, family aspiration, military service, and the promise of “good Eldian” recognition.
- Paradis initially constructs Titans as an exterior species enemy because its memory regime hides human causation.
- Warrior training converts children into inheritors of political history and military office.
- Willy constructs a public threat narrative through staged disclosure, testimony, sacrifice, and anticipated attack.
- Floch and the V27 crowd elevate Eren through savior language and the appropriation of `心臓を捧げよ`.
- Gabi's armband functions as a portable boundary she restores even after Falco throws it away.

Categories are not therefore unreal. They organize access, danger, military roles, and political consequences. But they are made and maintained through practices. This means they can be challenged—though not necessarily dissolved—by particular encounters.

## 3.3 Recognition without reconciliation

The V25 basement is the cleanest direct test of a central V19 claim. Eren recognizes that the people beyond the sea and those inside the Walls are the same. He understands Reiner's coercive environment. Reiner recognizes his own authorship beyond structural explanation. Their mutual intelligibility does not produce reconciliation.

Recognition matters because it prevents the enemy from being morally converted into a nonperson. It does not determine the strategic conclusion. Eren still attacks; Reiner remains implicated; civilians and children die; the conflict continues.

This is not evidence that recognition is worthless. It changes the meaning of violence. Violence after recognition can no longer be justified by ignorance of the victim's humanity. The actor must either claim necessity despite personhood or retreat into a category that the encounter has already exposed as false.

## 3.4 Concrete persons against hereditary guilt

V27's Gabi/Kaya confrontation moves the recognition problem from political abstraction to a murdered mother. Gabi invokes ancestral Eldian guilt; Kaya asks what her particular mother did to deserve being eaten. The argument is powerful because it forces a category to answer to a person.

Kaya does not provide a complete theory of history or collective responsibility. She exposes a category error: historical causation and inherited membership do not establish the personal culpability of an individual victim. Falco's apology advances the correction by acknowledging present Marleyan reconnaissance and violence rather than relying on ancient guilt.

Gabi is destabilized, not converted. She continues to restore the armband, defend her category, and act from grief. The checkpoint must therefore distinguish:

- contradiction entering a worldview;
- interpersonal pressure on a category;
- behavioral accommodation to particular people;
- full ideological revision.

V27 establishes the first three in partial form, not the fourth.

## 3.5 Recognition can travel through behavior rather than doctrine

Sasha's rescue of Kaya produces posthumous behavioral inheritance. Kaya wants to become like the person who saved her. Nicolo's attachment crosses the Marley/Paradis category through food and ordinary shared life. The Blouse family's hospitality and later response refuse to make inherited enemy membership identical to individual guilt.

None of these people delivers a comprehensive ideology. Their conduct changes what later conduct becomes imaginable. This is a crucial counterweight to the series' overt inheritance systems. Missions, memories, sins, and Titan powers can be inherited; so can rescue, hospitality, appetite, care, and the desire to resemble someone who treated a stranger as worth saving.

## 3.6 The danger of moral lucidity without de-escalation

By V27, the manga has repeatedly shown that people can:

- understand an enemy's history;
- identify with an enemy's fear;
- preserve affection for a former comrade;
- accept personal responsibility;
- distinguish civilians from combatants;
- and still continue a violent project.

This produces a harsher ethical world than either naïve reconciliation or monster dehumanization. The task is not only to make enemies recognizable. It is to build institutions, relationships, and decision rules capable of converting recognition into restraint when restraint is possible—and of preserving personhood when violence remains unavoidable.

# IV. Titan and world ontology at the V27 boundary

The checkpoint must separate what V01–V27 establishes from what remains testimonial, inferred, or open.

## 4.1 Materially established by V27

The following propositions are strongly supported within the current source boundary:

- human civilization exists beyond the Walls at a technological level materially different from Paradis;
- Eldians can become mindless Titans through administered Titan/spinal-fluid material;
- some Titans are inherited intelligent powers carried by human persons;
- inherited powers can transmit memories, though access is uneven, selective, and not fully understood;
- the Founder interacts differently with royal blood;
- nonroyal Eren has accessed Founder effects through contact with a royal-blood Titan, creating a contact hypothesis rather than a complete mechanistic theory;
- the Attack Titan is associated in testimony with resistance to control and memory phenomena that may not follow ordinary temporal direction;
- the Nine Titans are finite strategic assets transmitted by consumption and shortened lifespan;
- anti-Titan military technology is reducing the strategic dominance of Titan warfare;
- Marley has used Eldian bodies, Warrior succession, and mindless Titans as imperial military infrastructure;
- the Walls contain an immense Titan-based deterrent architecture whose activation is politically tied to the Founder and royal-blood problem;
- Zeke provides a mechanism for Ragako involving his spinal fluid and gas dissemination, supported by observable effects but still delivered as interested testimony;
- Paths or an equivalent cross-person connective structure is the current best model for memory and Titan inheritance phenomena, not a fully mapped ontology.

## 4.2 History remains source-positioned

Marleyan ancient history, Eldian Restorationist counter-history, Tybur testimony, Kruger's claims, Grisha's interpretation, and later institutional accounts cannot be merged into one omniscient chronicle. Each source has access, interest, ideological pressure, and rhetorical purpose.

Willy is especially important because his speech demonstrates that grounded disclosure, selective framing, strategic inference, personal complicity, and political staging can coexist. “Propaganda” is too blunt if it means fabricated falsehood; “truth” is too blunt if it erases interested arrangement.

The correct V27 epistemic posture is layered:

1. distinguish directly observed events from remembered or reported history;
2. distinguish testimony from mechanism independently corroborated by effects;
3. preserve incompatible accounts where the manga has not adjudicated them;
4. track who benefits from making one account authoritative;
5. revise models without pretending revision produces final neutrality.

## 4.3 Titan power as office, archive, body, and infrastructure

At V27, “Titan power” is not one object. It is at least:

- a combat capacity;
- a transformed bodily state;
- a memory archive;
- a political office transmitted through succession;
- an imperial technology;
- a hereditary threat structure;
- an architectural component of the Walls;
- a deterrent promise;
- a reason institutions seek custody over persons;
- a shortened-life burden imposed on children and future descendants.

This multiplicity matters because moral analysis changes with function. Destroying an active combatant, coercing an heir, controlling an archive, and maintaining a deterrent are not the same act even when all involve Titan power.

## 4.4 Obsolescence does not dissolve inherited institutions

Marley recognizes that conventional and anti-Titan weaponry is eroding Titan supremacy. Yet the Warrior system continues to ask children to compete for shortened lives and inherited missions. An institution can therefore outlive the strategic solution that originally legitimated it.

The royal-blood plan proposed to Paradis risks reproducing the same structure: future children become an inheritance chain maintaining a deterrent whose political necessity is assumed in advance. V27 makes the analogy morally visible without proving the situations identical.

## 4.5 Open ontology questions at V27

The following remain open and must not be closed by later knowledge:

- the full origin and metaphysical status of Titan power;
- the complete topology, agency, and temporal operation of Paths;
- the exact interaction among royal blood, Founder custody, and contact;
- whether inherited memories transmit only information or also dispositions, compulsions, or selection effects;
- the full basis and limits of Zeke's unusual abilities;
- the complete historical truth of the ancient Eldian/Marley conflict;
- the exact practical range and cost of the Wall Titan deterrent;
- the complete strategic and ideological program shared—or not shared—by Zeke, Yelena, Eren, and the Volunteers.

The current ontology is sufficient for political actors to act and insufficient for them to claim epistemic innocence.

# V. Political legitimacy, sovereignty, military institutions, and insurgent authority

## 5.1 The state is necessary and never identical to the people

The V13–V17 coup established a durable distinction between population, government, military institution, and the abstract word “humanity.” The old royal state protected a memory regime and inherited theology at the expense of public agency and survival. The replacement order is comparatively more legitimate because it exposes more truth, directs resources toward survival, and allows a wider range of revision. It remains coercive, strategically secretive, and concentrated in military hands.

V20–V27 strengthens this comparative rather than absolute account of legitimacy. Paradis cannot survive through interpersonal sincerity alone. It needs chains of command, research, diplomacy, custody rules, food, railways, ports, intelligence, succession plans, and control of catastrophic force. Yet each capacity can become a means of domination.

The series' political question is therefore not “institution or freedom?” It is:

> **What institution can coordinate survival without naturalizing its own right to define, confine, or spend the people it claims to protect?**

## 5.2 Eren's indispensability and the distinction between capacity and authority

By V26–V27, Eren's possession of the Founder, Attack Titan, and War Hammer makes him strategically indispensable. That fact gives him leverage. It does not automatically give him legitimate jurisdiction over Paradis.

The military's mistake would be to treat custody and succession as self-justifying because Eren is dangerous. Eren's mistake would be to treat indispensability as democratic or moral authorization. V27 places both possibilities under pressure:

- Eren can escape confinement and frustrate the institution's succession options;
- the military has genuine reason to fear unilateral action and external influence;
- public supporters read his capacity as proof that he alone can save Eldia;
- Floch possesses an internal network that converts sentiment into organizational force;
- Zackly's assassination destroys procedural trust without proving Eren ordered it;
- the immediate objective Eren states after escape is narrow—locate Zeke—not a complete political manifesto (`AOT_V27_E042-E043`).

The checkpoint must preserve the difference among **power**, **leverage**, **popularity**, **office**, and **legitimacy**. V27 shows them separating; it does not yet show which new arrangement will stabilize.

## 5.3 Protection as jurisdiction over bodies

Historia's V27 position makes sovereignty bodily. The royal-blood plan asks her to bear children, inherit the Beast Titan, accept a shortened life, and transmit the burden to descendants. It is described as public necessity, but its operation depends on a particular person's reproductive future.

Historia explicitly recognizes the plan as wrong—`いいわけが無い` and `こんなことが許されるはずは無い`—and nevertheless accepts the burden under existential pressure (`AOT_V27_E001-E002`). This is neither unconstrained consent nor simple erasure of agency. She assesses a political threat and chooses within a drastically narrowed field.

Eren's rejection—especially the comparison to livestock—correctly identifies the instrumental structure (`AOT_V27_E003-E004`). But his moral objection does not answer the institutional question: what alternative can protect the population, who may implement it, and how are risks distributed while alternatives are sought?

The volume therefore refuses two shortcuts:

- “Historia agreed, so the plan is legitimate.”
- “Eren objected, so Eren's alternative is legitimate.”

Consent under threat and protection without accountable jurisdiction are both morally unstable.

## 5.4 Information sovereignty

Information control has been central since Erwin's clandestine operations and the Reiss memory regime. V22–V27 makes it relationally intimate.

Eren withholds the royal-blood contact hypothesis partly to protect Historia. This is care through unilateral information control. Yelena secretly seeks Eren's `主体的な思想`, presenting recruitment as cultivation of autonomous conviction. The military constructs a Zeke-control hypothesis from suspicious contacts but lacks complete proof. Eren's friends use remembered affection as evidence about his current intentions, then confront behavior that no longer fits the model. The public knows enough to see Eren as indispensable and not enough to know the full strategic dispute.

Information sovereignty asks:

- who may decide that another person should remain uninformed for their own protection;
- when secrecy protects a person and when it removes their agency;
- when a state may withhold information to preserve security;
- when citizens' exclusion makes the state unable to claim their consent;
- whether ideological influence can be distinguished from self-authored conviction when the influencer seeks precisely to produce a willing subject.

V27 supplies no universal rule. It establishes that information asymmetry is one of the principal technologies through which protection becomes control.

## 5.5 The Volunteers and imported agency

Paradisian modernization is not a solitary national achievement. The Volunteers bring technical knowledge, maritime access, rail development, food practices, language mediation, and strategic connection. This matters both materially and ideologically.

The Volunteers demonstrate that agency can be relationally imported without becoming foreign ownership. Paradis gains capacities through cooperation with people whose motives differ. At the same time, Yelena's secret activity and the uncertainty around Zeke show why cooperation cannot be equated with trust.

The correct institutional model is neither purity nor paranoia. Levi's conduct toward Zeke provides a useful micro-rule: proposition-level cooperation and competence recognition can coexist with hostile containment. Trust is decomposable. One may accept information, coordinate on a narrow objective, and still deny broader motive trust (`AOT_V27_E029-E031`).

## 5.6 Popular legitimacy and the appropriation of inherited symbols

The crowd's response to Zackly's assassination and Eren's escape converts the Survey Corps slogan `心臓を捧げよ` into insurgent political language (`AOT_V27_E038-E041`). The phrase once organized sacrifice within a military institution. In V27 it legitimates opposition to that institution in the name of a more authentic struggle.

This is not merely hypocrisy. Symbols can migrate because the institution and its followers disagree over who still embodies the founding mission. Yet migration also simplifies. Eren's explicit speech does not fully authorize the savior role projected onto him. His political image now exceeds his stated position.

The scene creates a model-reconstruction warning: **public character** is not identical to private intention. A political movement can act through a character model it has constructed, even when the person has not articulated the complete doctrine attributed to them.

## 5.7 “Correct arguments” and strategic incompatibility

Chapter 108's title, `正論`, names a recurring structure. Multiple positions can be individually defensible:

- the military has reason to contain an unpredictable Founder;
- Eren has reason to reject Historia's instrumentalization and distrust delay;
- Armin has reason to seek dialogue;
- Jean and Connie have reason to update their model of Eren from recent conduct;
- Mikasa has reason to resist reducing attachment to evidence of approval;
- Historia has reason to accept responsibility under existential threat;
- the Volunteers have reason to distrust established imperial and island institutions.

The catastrophe arises because defensible positions are strategically incompatible, held under asymmetric information, and backed by unequal coercive capacities. Moral seriousness therefore requires more than identifying the best isolated argument. It requires analyzing interaction effects.

# VI. Sacrifice, bodily instrumentalization, inheritance, and intergenerational burden

## 6.1 Sacrifice has no intrinsic moral credit

The V19 checkpoint rejected the view that successful sacrifice reimburses the dead. V20 decisively strengthens that claim. Erwin's recruits, Armin's body, Bertolt's death, and the serum decision do not become morally clean because the operation succeeds. The surviving characters inherit obligations, grief, and political consequences.

The distinction should be preserved:

- **self-sacrifice** can be authored and causally intelligible;
- **commanded sacrifice** can be necessary and still coercive;
- **retrospective success** can show that a sacrifice had effects without proving it was just;
- **memorial meaning** is constructed by survivors and cannot return agency to the dead.

This is why Floch's testimony matters. The apparently anonymous mass contains persons whose deaths gave others tactical opportunity. Their expendability gives survivors standing to question elite narratives of necessity.

## 6.2 The series' sacrificial systems

By V27, sacrifice appears through several institutional grammars:

- soldiers ordered into suicidal operations;
- children trained to inherit Titans and shortened lives;
- royal-blood descendants converted into a deterrent succession chain;
- parents imposing political missions on children;
- comrades volunteering to become successors;
- a political leader staging his own death as narrative evidence;
- people using their bodies as weapons because no less destructive tool seems available;
- civilians placed in danger by leaders who anticipate or exploit enemy attack.

These are not morally interchangeable. The checkpoint's purpose is to prevent the vocabulary of “sacrifice” from flattening consent, age, information, coercion, beneficiary, and causal alternatives.

## 6.3 Inheritance as power and command

Inheritance in *Attack on Titan* transfers more than capacity. It can transfer:

- memories;
- political office;
- shortened lifespan;
- family expectation;
- national grievance;
- mission language;
- guilt;
- role identity;
- strategic dependency;
- desired future;
- the obligation to interpret what a predecessor could not finish.

The ethical quality of inheritance depends on whether the recipient can revise it. The Reiss archive nearly abolishes revision. Grisha imposes restorationist purpose on Zeke before Zeke can author it. Warrior selection turns children into national instruments. Reiner inherits Marcel's vacant role through trauma and self-construction. Armin inherits the Colossal Titan and the moral burden of using it. Historia is asked to inherit an entire deterrent regime.

The manga's strongest anti-instrumental principle at V27 is not “inherit nothing.” It is:

> **An inheritance that cannot be questioned, refused, reinterpreted, or ended approaches domination.**

## 6.4 The child as strategic future

The Warrior and royal-blood systems both locate national survival in children's bodies. Adults describe this as future protection. The child experiences it as a life already allocated.

Gabi embraces candidate status because the institution links personal excellence, family elevation, community redemption, and ethnic worth. Falco enters the competition partly to protect her. Reiner's candidate-child responsibility keeps him alive while also binding him to the same system that destroyed him. Historia's hypothetical descendants are discussed as future bearers before they exist as persons.

This is the intergenerational danger of deterrence: a present generation can call a future chain “necessary” and thereby pre-author the lives of people who cannot consent.

## 6.5 Historia: burden acceptance after self-authorship

Historia's V27 decision is among the most important complications to the V19 model. Her Uprising development did not produce a universal refusal of burdens. It produced resistance to imposed identity and a stronger capacity to judge and choose.

At V27 she:

- recognizes the royal plan as morally unacceptable;
- understands the threat facing the population;
- accepts a burden directed at her own body and future;
- does so within a field structured by state power and existential danger.

The current model must distinguish:

1. refusal of imposed self-erasure;
2. resistance to another valued person's self-erasure;
3. acceptance of responsibility for public survival;
4. consent under coercively narrowed alternatives;
5. willingness to shoulder a burden;
6. willingness to be treated as an instrument.

These variables can diverge. V27 strengthens Historia as a responsibility-bearing political actor while revising the simple prediction that authored identity leads to straightforward refusal of sacrificial duty.

## 6.6 Eren's anti-instrumentalization and its limit

Eren's defense of Historia is genuine evidence of person-centered attachment and moral resistance. It cannot be dismissed merely because his later institutional conduct is coercive. The contradiction is diagnostic:

- he rejects a system that turns Historia and her children into livestock;
- he withholds information to protect her;
- he resists military plans that would replace him or control the Founder;
- he increasingly treats his own strategic judgment as grounds for unilateral action.

A person can oppose instrumentalization in one relation while reproducing coercive structure elsewhere. The checkpoint should not flatten Eren into hypocrite or hero. It should ask where his jurisdictional boundary lies—and note that V27 does not yet reveal a complete answer.

# VII. Violence, proportionality, civilian distinction, responsibility, and complicity

## 7.1 From monster-killing to human war

The early visual and ethical economy allows Titans to appear as targets whose destruction protects human life. Once Titan personhood and human operation become visible, violence requires new distinctions. By V25, the attackers and defenders are human-organized systems using transformed bodies, artillery, civilians, children, propaganda, and urban space.

The collapse of monster ontology does not make force unnecessary. It removes a convenient exculpation.

## 7.2 Civilian distinction at Liberio

V25 deliberately preserves civilian and child deaths as morally real. Willy anticipates an attack and uses the festival as a stage; this does not erase Eren's agency. Marley embeds military and political targets in civilian space; this does not make all people in the space combatants.

Mikasa's rebuke separates protection from approval. She assists Eren and condemns the irreversible killing of civilians and children. Jean attempts to preserve a distinction between military targets and collective retaliation. Floch reads enemy civilian life through a framework of reciprocal devastation. Sasha does not shoot an unarmed Gabi in the relevant moment, providing a narrow negative targeting constraint rather than proof of universal mercy.

These differences matter for character modeling. “Fights for Paradis” cannot predict targeting behavior without a proportionality rule, threat classification, and relationship to collective guilt.

## 7.3 Armin and dialogue after complicity

Armin's V19 formulation as a revising model-builder already exceeded the pacifist archetype. V26 makes the simple archetype untenable. He directly participates in catastrophic Colossal violence. His later reflection is not the innocence of someone who remained outside the act. It is moral accounting by a perpetrator who still seeks alternatives.

V27 then places dialogue beside lethal contingency. Armin proposes talking to Eren and considers transferring the Founder if Eren cannot be trusted (`AOT_V27_E016`, `E037`, `E044`). This is not incoherence. It is the mature structure of his decision grammar:

- preserve model revision and communication while evidence permits;
- do not assume a cherished relationship guarantees current alignment;
- retain severe options when the failure cost is existential;
- continue to experience the moral cost rather than converting violence into technocratic neutrality.

“Dialogue after complicity” is therefore more accurate than pacifism.

## 7.4 Responsibility without total structural exculpation

Reiner's V25 confession is a key ethical control. Structural explanation matters: he was a child, indoctrinated, selected by a manipulated process, motivated by family and status, embedded in military command, and transformed by Marcel's death. Yet he refuses the conclusion that these causes erase his authorship. He continued partly because he wanted to be a hero.

This does not mean he alone caused the war. It means causal explanation and personal responsibility can coexist. The series resists two symmetrical errors:

- individual blame that ignores institutions and coercion;
- structural explanation that dissolves every choice into a system.

## 7.5 Gabi's grievance becomes partly self-authored

Gabi begins with an inherited category structure. Liberio gives it first-person confirmation: people she loves are killed, her home is attacked, and Sasha becomes a concrete target. Her later actions cannot be reduced to propaganda, because bereavement creates a personally owned retaliatory motive.

At the same time, personal pain does not validate collective guilt or eliminate causal complexity. Falco understands that the attack followed earlier Marleyan violence and still follows Gabi into danger. The pair demonstrates that better causal knowledge is not equivalent to emotional freedom from attachment.

## 7.6 Victory as causal captivity

Liberio is strategically productive and morally entrapping. It secures Zeke, seizes the War Hammer, damages Marley, and demonstrates Paradisian reach. It also:

- intensifies global threat perception;
- kills civilians and children;
- implicates Armin in mass destruction;
- creates Gabi's personal revenge motive;
- kills Sasha;
- deepens Eren's conflict with his chain of command;
- depends on a Zeke/Volunteer relationship not fully understood;
- turns the 104th into participants in a strategy they did not collectively authorize.

Victory is captivity because success narrows the next field of action. The participants inherit consequences that make withdrawal, reconciliation, and institutional trust more difficult.

## 7.7 Proportionality as concrete cost accounting

Jean's development is strongest when described not as softness but as concrete proportionality. He repeatedly asks what a choice costs particular people, whether a target remains a threat, and what retaliation will produce. His V26 grief does not become indiscriminate violence. His V27 challenge to the old model of Eren is likewise concrete: remembered affection cannot erase present exposure and estrangement.

This gives Jean a distinctive political-moral role. He is neither pure idealist nor pure realist. He insists that strategy remain answerable to bodies, civilians, comrades, and downstream costs.

# VIII. Home, family, attachment, grief, and relational motivation

## 8.1 Home after the sea

Home begins as the place lost to Titans and desired through return. By V27 it is distributed across incompatible claims:

- Shiganshina as Eren, Mikasa, and Armin's lost home;
- the 104th as a mobile relational home;
- Warrior families and Liberio as Reiner, Gabi, Falco, and the candidates' home;
- the Blouse farm as rebuilt ordinary life and care infrastructure;
- the forest, rail line, port, prison, and military headquarters as spaces where protection and confinement overlap;
- the sea as once-promised boundary that now reveals other people's homes beyond it.

A politics of home must therefore answer whether one group's return or security requires another group's dispossession.

## 8.2 Attachment is neither moral permission nor illusion

The V25–V27 corpus repeatedly separates attachment from approval.

- Mikasa protects Eren and condemns civilian killing.
- The 104th remember Eren's declaration that they are precious to him and confront conduct that endangers them.
- Armin seeks to reconstruct Eren's motives and considers lethal succession.
- Reiner's attachment to the Warrior children reactivates action without curing him.
- Falco's attachment to Gabi overrides safer judgment despite better causal understanding.
- Nicolo's attachment to Sasha crosses enemy categories through ordinary life.

Attachment is causally powerful and morally underdetermined. It can sustain life, distort judgment, motivate protection, invite control, preserve dialogue, or pull a person back into danger.

## 8.3 The railroad scene and temporally stale intimacy

The V27 railroad memory is indispensable because it combines explicit affection with ordinary embarrassment. Eren refuses his friends as successors because they matter to him and he wants them to live long lives. The group blushes; Jean redirects discomfort through irritation. The scene provides strong ordinary-register and relationship evidence (`AOT_V27_E013-E014`).

Its later placement makes that evidence diagnostic rather than merely sentimental. Jean and Connie ask whether the remembered Eren still explains the present one. The correct conclusion is neither that past affection was false nor that it determines current action. Intimate knowledge can be true of an earlier state and insufficient for a later one.

This revises an important modeling assumption:

> **A close relationship supplies privileged evidence, not timeless omniscience.**

## 8.4 Grief as relationship, politics, and model update

Sasha's death produces several different grief grammars:

- Connie's direct relational loss;
- Jean's cost accounting;
- Mikasa's quiet presence at the grave;
- Nicolo's attachment through food and shared life;
- the Blouse family's mourning and hospitality;
- Gabi's retaliatory purpose;
- Eren's difficult reaction, whose complete meaning is not settled by one facial expression or laugh.

Grief does not have one political direction. It can intensify retaliation, preserve ordinary memory, build cross-category relation, or force revision of a leader's strategy.

## 8.5 Reiner and relational anti-suicide tethers

Reiner's recovery must not be generalized. Candidate-child responsibility interrupts his suicide attempt and later reactivates him when the children call for protection. This is not evidence that his despair has resolved. It is a relationship-specific tether.

The distinction matters for reconstruction. In a novel situation, “Reiner protects children to whom he feels responsible despite self-destructive collapse” has stronger warrant than “Reiner regains generalized hope when others need him.”

## 8.6 Behavioral inheritance through Sasha, Kaya, and the Blouse family

Kaya's desire to become like Sasha is not doctrinal conversion. It is behavioral inheritance. Sasha once treated a stranger's life as worth immediate risk; Kaya reorganizes her future aspiration around that act (`AOT_V27_E027-E028`).

The Blouse farm gives the inheritance institutional form. It houses children displaced by war and creates an ordinary environment where Gabi and Falco can encounter Paradisian people outside battle. Care is not only private virtue; it becomes infrastructure.

## 8.7 Family as protection and instrument

Family in V01–V27 is never one moral category.

- Carla's love grounds unconditional worth.
- Grisha's mission transforms Zeke into an instrument.
- Reiner seeks maternal recognition through Warrior status.
- Gabi's family ties candidate achievement to community redemption.
- the Jaeger grandfather recognizes parental severity retrospectively after irreversible loss.
- Historia's descendants are imagined as a strategic family line.
- the Blouse family refuses to let bereavement erase hospitality and particular responsibility.

Family can preserve personhood or become the most intimate mechanism of political coercion. The difference lies in whether love recognizes a child as an author or scripts the child's life as a vehicle for an adult project.

# IX. Knowledge, memory, testimony, and revision after the basement

## 9.1 The basement changes the scale, not the need for method

The photograph is decisive material evidence that the Walls are not humanity's world. The surrounding archive is interpretive. The first lesson after the basement is therefore methodological: a major revelation can expand the evidence base while increasing the number of competing accounts.

Grisha's archive, inherited memories, Marleyan education, Restorationist materials, Kruger's testimony, Tybur history, Zeke's mechanism claims, and Yelena's self-account all require source criticism. The fact that a source suffers oppression, possesses secret access, or confesses complicity affects credibility but does not make every proposition true.

## 9.2 Model revision as character competence

Armin, Hange, Jean, Pixis, Nile, and Falco repeatedly demonstrate different forms of revision.

- Armin generates alternatives and keeps uncertainty explicit, though attachment can delay updating.
- Hange integrates technical evidence, institutional threat, grief, and command responsibility without gaining an escape from politics.
- Jean tests remembered relationship claims against present costs.
- Pixis reconstructs secret contact through behavioral anomaly rather than certainty.
- Nile explicitly marks the absence of evidence in speculation about Historia's pregnancy (`根拠は無いんだな？`).
- Falco uses causal history to challenge Gabi's inherited moral category.

Revision is not emotional neutrality. The strongest reasoners remain attached, afraid, guilty, or institutionally pressured.

## 9.3 Intimacy as evidence and bias

Eren's friends know him better than the military in many domains. Their knowledge is not therefore complete. They may overfit an earlier state, interpret opaque action through attachment, or resist a model whose moral implications are painful.

Conversely, institutions may detect behavioral anomalies while lacking relational knowledge that explains motive. V27 shows neither source is sufficient alone. A reliable character model must combine:

- longitudinal intimate evidence;
- recent behavior;
- changed knowledge state;
- altered role and power;
- strategic context;
- negative evidence and unexplained deviation.

## 9.4 Testimony under strategic interest

Zeke and Yelena are high-impact but partially opaque. Their claims must be decomposed:

- what they state;
- what observable effects support;
- what their opponents infer;
- what institutional behavior establishes;
- what motive remains unknown.

Zeke's Ragako explanation currently fits observed transformation and his known command relation, but it is still interested testimony. Yelena confirms secret contact and describes a desire to draw out Eren's autonomous thought; this establishes influence effort, not total causal authorship over Eren.

## 9.5 Knowledge can itself instrumentalize

Information about royal-blood contact threatens Historia. Warrior knowledge allocates children to succession. The Reiss archive disciplines the heir. Public knowledge of Eren's capacity creates a savior movement. Knowledge is therefore not automatically liberating.

The mature epistemic standard is not maximum disclosure in every moment. It is **revisable, accountable control over knowledge whose distribution changes other people's agency**.

# X. Ordinary life and low-stakes evidence as moral and reconstructive infrastructure

## 10.1 Ordinary life is the end, not narrative filler

The series' crisis density can make meals, teasing, chores, dialect, food preferences, embarrassment, family conversation, and work appear secondary. The cumulative corpus rejects that hierarchy. Ordinary life supplies the content of what survival is for.

Without ordinary evidence, “home” becomes an abstraction and sacrifice becomes military arithmetic. Sasha's appetite, the 104th's peer friction, Warrior family dinners, the Liberio festival, Blouse farm hospitality, Nicolo's cooking, Armin's embarrassment with Hitch, and the railroad scene show lives that are not exhausted by strategic function.

## 10.2 V20–V27 ordinary-life expansion

The holdout window is uneven:

- V20 is overwhelmingly crisis-bound and provides little clean ordinary-life transfer.
- V21–V22 add childhood curiosity, family memory, Ymir's letter voice, the sea, and small moments of wonder.
- V23–V24 provide the strongest Marley ordinary-social tranche: train interaction, family dinner, candidate teasing, festival food, caretaker behavior, and Pieck's bodily habits.
- V25 offers almost no clean low-stakes baseline.
- V26 returns food, Volunteers' technical-cultural exchange, Sasha's terminal `肉`, and post-action grief.
- V27 provides the adult 104th railroad scene, cemetery/meal interaction, Blouse farm chores and hospitality, Nicolo/Blouse social friction, and the Armin/Hitch/Annie-crystal interaction.

These scenes materially improve reconstruction but do not erase the crisis bias.

## 10.3 Ordinary evidence corrects heroic caricature

Ordinary scenes reveal mechanisms that combat scenes cannot:

- Eren's direct affection produces group embarrassment rather than only ideological intensity.
- Jean manages tenderness through irritation.
- Reiner can function as caretaker and socially embedded adult despite severe collapse.
- Gabi's rigidity becomes visible against Falco's faster practical adaptation.
- Nicolo's food creates relation across enemy categories.
- Sasha's appetite becomes a compact posthumous sign through which comrades recognize her.
- Armin's analytical interest in Annie coexists with embarrassment and Hitch's teasing.

A model built only from battles would produce voices that are too solemn, values that are too abstract, and relationships that are too consistently explicit.

## 10.4 Remaining ordinary-life deficits

At V27, the corpus still underdetermines many domains:

- sustained civilian work outside military or institutional roles;
- hobbies and preferences unrelated to survival;
- boredom and unstructured leisure;
- romance under stable, reciprocal conditions;
- long-term domestic routines;
- ordinary conflict after danger has passed;
- adaptation to unfamiliar modern institutions;
- speech in contexts without military, grief, political, or intimate stakes.

These deficits are not minor. They are the main reason no V27 character model should be advertised as an unrestricted simulator.

# XI. Character longitudinal synthesis at the V27 boundary

Character claims in this section are time-indexed. “Strong” means strong within observed states and domains, not timeless possession of a single essence. V27 does not authorize canonical full-series models.

## 11.1 Eren Jaeger — freedom drive, attachment, strategic opacity, and contested jurisdiction

Eren remains the series' clearest example of a stable disposition producing radically different behavior as knowledge, power, role, and relationship change.

### Stable elements

Across V01–V27, Eren repeatedly:

- reacts strongly to confinement and externally imposed limits;
- converts perceived threat into action pressure;
- prefers direct causal closure to passive endurance;
- recognizes birth as sufficient ground for worth and freedom;
- experiences the suffering or instrumentalization of valued people as a call to intervention;
- uses categorical high-stakes language when commitment closes alternatives;
- keeps moving when uncertainty would cause many other characters to suspend action.

These elements do not determine one ideology. They form a decision grammar.

### Developmental revisions

Early Eren treats the Walls and Titans as the principal confinement. Uprising-era Eren confronts inherited guilt, bodily office, and the possibility that his own existence is illegitimate. V18 restores a universal worth claim through Carla. V19–V20 show him embedded in distributed action and loss. V21–V22 expand the enemy boundary into history and geography. V24 establishes his distinction between imposed and self-entered hell. V25 confirms that human recognition does not disable his lethal commitment. V26 makes forward motion a self-addressed imperative. V27 converts his relation to Paradis into a sovereignty crisis.

### Attachment and exposure

The railroad flashback is a hard constraint against models that treat Eren as affectively detached from the 104th. He explicitly values them and wants them to live long lives. His defense of Historia likewise rejects bodily instrumentalization.

Yet attachment does not consistently reduce exposure. Liberio forces his friends into a rescue operation and exposes them to political and moral consequences. This does not prove the earlier care was false. It shows that Eren can rank an urgent strategic path above the immediate safety, approval, or agency of people he values.

The model must therefore represent:

> **care without deference; protection without consultation; attachment that can motivate both resistance to sacrifice and unilateral risk imposition.**

### Autonomy, influence, and self-coercion

At V27, three reductive models are unsupported:

- Eren as a perfectly autonomous sovereign whose every action transparently expresses a settled plan;
- Eren as a passive object wholly manufactured by Zeke or Yelena;
- Eren as an unchanging child whose later conduct is fully explained by early exterminatory anger.

The evidence supports an interacting architecture: durable freedom drive, self-authored commitment, strategic constraint, selective secrecy, relation-specific protection, possible inherited or external influence, and self-coercive narrowing. Yelena's stated desire to cultivate `主体的な思想` is compatible with influence through autonomy rather than command.

### V27 current formulation

Eren is a strategically indispensable, deeply attached, increasingly unilateral actor whose anti-instrumentalization ethic is genuine but unevenly generalized. His current intention cannot be reconstructed from one slogan, one memory, one political projection, or one external theory. The immediate post-escape objective is locating Zeke. His complete decision architecture remains `OPEN`.

## 11.2 Mikasa Ackerman — attachment without moral surrender

Mikasa's early attachment to Eren is survival history, family relation, gratitude, trauma organization, and practical orientation. It cannot be reduced to an abstract loyalty switch.

Across the corpus, attachment can generate:

- rapid protection under existential threat;
- attempts to control risk;
- vengeance after perceived loss;
- resistance to Eren's self-sacrifice;
- frustration with his autonomy;
- growing recognition that his choices cannot be owned;
- moral disagreement while remaining physically allied.

V25 supplies decisive counterevidence to a pure-loyalty model. Mikasa protects Eren and condemns civilian and child killing. V27 adds a second distinction through Louise: admiration, imitation, and shared pro-Eren positioning do not create reciprocal political intimacy. Mikasa treats the scarf and rescue memory as private relational foundations, not a banner automatically transferable to a faction.

Her written voice remains relatively sparse, concrete, and action-linked under danger. Sparse speech should not be interpreted as sparse moral judgment. At V27, Mikasa is strong for threat/relationship states and still under-covered in broad ordinary life.

## 11.3 Armin Arlert — model revision, moral injury, and dialogue after complicity

Armin's core strength is not “intelligence” in isolation. It is the ability to generate models, hold alternatives, use weak signals, and revise when reality resists the current explanation. His imagination of positive horizons also prevents strategy from becoming pure threat management.

The corpus repeatedly complicates this:

- he can manipulate opponents through their attachments;
- he can kill;
- he can accept severe sacrifices;
- he can inherit and deploy the Colossal Titan;
- he can overestimate his knowledge of Eren because intimacy creates confidence;
- he can continue seeking dialogue while constructing lethal fallback branches.

V26 is the crucial moral transition. Armin becomes an agent of mass-destruction warfare and then accounts for the cost. V27 shows him explicitly downgrading the sufficiency of his old Eren model while refusing to abandon communication.

His mature V27 grammar is:

> **revise, explain, search for a branch that preserves persons, retain catastrophic options when the failure cost is existential, and do not erase the moral injury of using them.**

This is strong prospective evidence for crisis reasoning and voice. It is not proof that Armin will always find a peaceful synthesis or that dialogue is his only real commitment.

## 11.4 Jean Kirstein — practical leadership as proportionality and accountability

Jean's development is not a linear ascent from selfishness to idealism. His practical orientation persists and changes target. Early concern with survival and status becomes concrete leadership because he notices what plans cost ordinary bodies.

His strongest traits include:

- rapid recognition of tactical and social cost;
- willingness to act despite fear;
- irritation as a defense against embarrassment or tenderness;
- skepticism toward heroic abstraction;
- preservation of civilian and threat distinctions;
- accountability demands directed at people he loves;
- grief that does not automatically become indiscriminate revenge.

V25–V27 give Jean unusual importance. He opposes Floch's collective-responsibility logic, experiences Sasha's death through everyday friendship, and challenges the use of Eren's past affection as a complete current model. His pragmatism is therefore not “doing whatever works.” It is proportionality tied to specific people and downstream consequences.

## 11.5 Historia Reiss — authored identity, public responsibility, and constrained burden

Historia's early Christa role is imposed erasure sustained by the expectation that self-negation will be praised as goodness. Her development through Ymir, the Uprising, and queenship establishes that authenticity is behavioral and political: she chooses a name, relation, refusal, public role, and care infrastructure.

V27 revises the simple rule that this development produces categorical refusal of sacrifice. Historia calls the royal-blood plan wrong and accepts it. The decision can express responsibility and constrained consent at once.

Her model must preserve distinctions among:

- saintly self-erasure performed for approval;
- chosen public duty;
- another person's attempt to sacrifice themselves;
- her own willingness to bear cost;
- state capture of reproductive and bodily future;
- acceptance of a burden because all visible alternatives appear worse.

The pregnancy is established; the complete motive, timing, and relational architecture behind it are not. Any model that fills those gaps at V27 is overreaching.

## 11.6 Reiner Braun — cumulative selves, responsibility, shame, and relational reactivation

Reiner has the strongest time-indexed state architecture in the V27 corpus.

The evidence supports historically real, interacting states:

- child seeking maternal and social recognition;
- sincere believer in Warrior status;
- candidate selected through Marcel's manipulation;
- survivor after Marcel's death;
- self-author of the `俺がマルセルになる` substitution;
- 104th soldier who repeatedly enacts care and comradeship;
- Warrior maintaining mission boundaries;
- returned son and failed promise;
- mentor/caretaker to candidates;
- suicidal adult;
- strategist capable of accurate adversary modeling;
- confessed perpetrator refusing total structural exculpation.

No one state is the hidden true Reiner. Later understanding does not make earlier sincerity fake. Role conflict and repetition produce real identities whose incompatibility becomes traumatic.

V25 strengthens responsibility without simplistic blame. V26–V27 show that candidate responsibility can reactivate him. This is relational, not generalized recovery. He can infer Eren's likely urgency and advocate early action while remaining psychologically devastated.

## 11.7 Bertolt Hoover — delegated agency and the V19 authored mission state

Bertolt's earlier passivity is real. He often delegates initiative, follows Reiner, and manages fear through group role. V19 produces a marked state transition in which he accepts incompatibility and chooses lethal mission action while recognizing former comrades.

V20 validates that state under comparable mission conditions: he can show concern, address former comrades, and continue. His later helpless death in V21 is not a failed prediction of mission composure because agency, bodily control, available alternatives, and mission viability have collapsed. It is a useful confound control.

The current model is strong for the V19 mission/combat state and weak for broad ordinary or far-transfer reconstruction.

## 11.8 Annie Leonhart — anti-euphemism, survival logic, and selective relation

Annie's model should resist both emotional emptiness and hidden-softness simplifications. She can reject Warrior euphemism, recognize self-interest, preserve selective mercy, desire return to her father, participate in lethal infiltration, and maintain guarded relation.

V24's historical backfill strengthens her anti-euphemistic voice and survival-centered reasoning. It does not provide enough current ordinary or post-crystal breadth for unrestricted modeling. At V27, Armin's communication toward her is evidence about Armin and the one-sided relational channel, not proof of reciprocal present intimacy.

## 11.9 Ymir — imposed roles, chosen obligations, and freedom without isolation

Ymir's life is structured by names and roles assigned by others. Her development matters because she does not answer imposed identity by rejecting all relation. She chooses obligations, especially toward Historia, and accepts severe cost.

Her letter reinforces a voice capable of humor, bluntness, care, and self-conscious narration. Her model remains temporally bounded because later direct evidence is absent. The strongest transferable rule is that freedom for Ymir means ownership of obligation, not freedom from obligation.

## 11.10 Erwin Smith — public duty, private dream, and the limit of instrumental leadership

Erwin's command model remains strong at the V19 boundary. He can convert uncertainty into coordinated action, use secrecy and sacrifice, and embody public purpose while privately organized by the basement question. Public duty and private obsession are causally fused rather than mutually exclusive.

V20 prevents the “discard humanity” maxim from becoming a universal law. Erwin's leadership works partly because he is willing to instrumentalize himself and others; it also produces a structure in which the dead accumulate behind his project. Levi's final decision refuses to restore him to that machinery.

Erwin is strong for command, strategy, and public/private register. Ordinary-life transfer remains sparse.

## 11.11 Levi Ackerman — objective discipline, relationship-specific choice, and hostile cooperation

Levi's core is not emotionless efficiency. He repeatedly combines:

- high objective discipline;
- rejection of false certainty;
- proposition-specific trust;
- relationship-conditioned responsibility;
- terse moral compression;
- willingness to carry promises beyond the person who made them;
- refusal to treat sentiment as automatic command.

The serum decision revises his choice ethic by showing that returning authorship to another person is conditional on that person's ability and state. V26–V27 strengthen his capacity to prioritize objective over revenge while maintaining hostility toward Zeke. Naming Ragako resists Zeke's ideological abstraction. Agreement that time is short does not become motive trust.

His model is strong in command/crisis and thin in ordinary life.

## 11.12 Hange Zoë — curiosity, technical method, grief, and governance failure pressure

Hange combines exuberant curiosity, experimental practice, explanatory speech, technical imagination, and the capacity to revise models. Later command adds institutional responsibility without erasing the earlier voice.

V26–V27 places Hange under a problem scientific competence cannot solve alone: strategic opacity, political fracture, and responsibility for containment. Self-doubt and invocation of Erwin do not erase procedural persistence. Humor and caveat remain compatible with crisis.

Hange is strong for technical/command/governance states. Delegation, ordinary playfulness under low stakes, and private life remain less thoroughly tested than the crisis corpus might suggest.

## 11.13 Sasha Blouse — ordinary identity, target distinction, and posthumous causality

Sasha's appetite is not a joke detached from character. It encodes scarcity history, embodied pleasure, peer recognition, food-mediated relation, and the ordinary life the military project claims to protect.

Her non-shot of unarmed Gabi gives a narrow target-discrimination control. Her death makes ordinary evidence causally decisive: comrades recognize her through `肉`, Nicolo and the Blouse family connect through cooking, and Kaya's rescue memory generates behavioral inheritance.

No new living-state behavior exists after V26. Posthumous effects should not be misused to invent late-state choices.

## 11.14 Connie Springer — peer truth-testing and grief

Connie is strongest in peer, family, grief, and immediate moral reaction. His response to Ragako, Sasha, and Eren's opacity supplies direct emotional evidence often absent from more strategically articulate characters.

V27's demand for an explanation of Eren's laugh is not mere incomprehension. It tests whether a cherished friend still inhabits the relationship model through which Connie understood him. The model remains partial outside peer/crisis contexts.

## 11.15 Floch Forster — survivor standing, retaliatory politics, and organizational escalation

Floch begins from survivor grievance: those treated as expendable have standing to judge commanders and elite choices. This contains a legitimate political insight. His development then pushes that insight toward savior politics, collective retaliation, and clandestine organization.

V25 distinguishes his civilian-responsibility model from Jean's. V27 shows him converting rhetoric into network capacity. His support for Eren is real, but the movement's slogans and actions cannot automatically be attributed to Eren's complete authorship.

Floch is strong within survivor/political and factional states, with little ordinary/private evidence.

## 11.16 Zeke Jaeger — command competence, family history, interested testimony, and unresolved terminal motive

Zeke's current model includes:

- high strategic and command competence;
- capacity for casual or joking register amid violence;
- family history shaped by parental mission imposition;
- selective information withholding;
- cooperation with Paradis under profound mistrust;
- ability to explain mechanisms in detail while remaining an interested source;
- a relationship with Levi defined by objective interdependence and moral hostility.

V27 does not settle his complete program or the degree to which Eren shares it. Treating Zeke as either sincere savior or transparent deceiver would exceed the boundary.

## 11.17 Gabi Braun — merit, inherited guilt, bereavement, and defensive category restoration

Gabi is not a passive vessel for propaganda. She reasons within Marley's bargain: become exceptional, prove good Eldian status, inherit power, redeem family/community, and destroy the island enemy. Tactical brilliance and sincere moral belief coexist.

Liberio converts doctrine into personal grievance. V27 then confronts her with concrete Paradisian civilians and an unanswerable victim-specific question. Her first response is not immediate empathy; it is escalation and category defense. She restores the armband Falco discards.

The current model must allow contradictory evidence to increase defensive rigidity before producing revision. Gabi is domain-strong within candidate, ideology, grief, and enemy-contact states; her longer revision trajectory remains open.

## 11.18 Falco Grice — causal empathy, apology, protective opposition, and attachment-dominated risk

Falco often possesses better causal information than Gabi. He shows humanitarian restraint, recognizes reciprocal violence, apologizes to Kaya, adapts more quickly to the farm environment, and directly restrains or contradicts Gabi.

Yet he follows her into danger. Attachment overrides the safer conclusion. This is a strong negative constraint against rationalist simulation: correct information does not automatically dominate relational commitment.

His care increasingly permits opposition rather than simple compliance. That distinction gives his relationship model substantial prospective value inside the observed domain.

## 11.19 Kaya, Nicolo, and the Blouse family — particular responsibility against inherited categories

Kaya's trauma is morally specific. Her questions insist that a theory of ancestral guilt answer for one mother. Her desire to imitate Sasha converts rescue into prosocial aspiration.

Nicolo demonstrates that ordinary shared food can create attachment before ideological reconciliation. The Blouse family transforms mourning into hospitality and preserves the distinction between enemy membership and individual culpability.

These characters are narrower in volume coverage but unusually strong for recognition, grief, food, hospitality, and victim-specific responsibility.

## 11.20 Yelena and the Volunteers — ideological mediation, technical transfer, and motive asymmetry

Yelena is not merely a messenger. She selects, withholds, recruits, frames, and seeks high-leverage actors. Her claim that she wanted Eren to form autonomous thought suggests an influence model in which self-authorship is cultivated because autonomous conviction is more powerful than obedience.

The Volunteers collectively provide real material capacities and reasons for gratitude. Their motives are not uniform, and cooperation does not settle trust. Yelena is strong within ideological-strategic roles and weak in ordinary/private transfer.

# XII. Relationship architecture through V27

## 12.1 Eren / Armin — shared horizon, epistemic confidence, and model failure

The relationship begins with Armin's book and a shared positive horizon. Armin gives Eren a world to seek; Eren gives Armin action confidence. Their complementarity can become asymmetrical: Armin believes he understands Eren through shared history, while Eren increasingly acts through information Armin does not possess.

V26 makes the model failure explicit. V27 shows the relationship's mature tension: Armin preserves dialogue because the history remains evidentially meaningful, and he constructs a lethal contingency because it is no longer sufficient. The relationship is neither broken nor epistemically sovereign.

## 12.2 Eren / Mikasa — survival bond, control, autonomy, and moral disagreement

Mikasa's bond with Eren is foundational but not static. She protects, resists risk, grieves, controls, follows, rebukes, and gradually recognizes his autonomous path. Eren alternately depends on, rejects, protects, and exposes her.

V25 proves that protection does not equal approval. V27 proves that public imitation of Mikasa's attachment does not capture its private meaning. Their relationship remains high-authority evidence for behavior and a source of model error when treated as unconditional alignment.

## 12.3 Eren / Historia — mutual worth recognition and anti-instrumentalization

Their Uprising relation is grounded in seeing one another outside inherited worthlessness scripts. Eren later withholds information and rejects the royal-blood plan to protect Historia.

The relation is ethically important because it shows Eren opposing another person's instrumentalization even when the person accepts burden. It also raises the tension between protection and deference. V27 does not establish every motive behind either person's later choices.

## 12.4 Reiner / the 104th — enacted selfhood and incompatible homes

The 104th relationship makes Reiner's soldier role real through repeated care, discipline, humor, and shared danger. Recognition after exposure cannot restore the prior social world, but neither can it erase it.

V25's basement works because both Eren and Reiner carry that history. Understanding is intimate enough to destroy the monster story and insufficient to reconcile strategic homes.

## 12.5 Reiner / Gabi / Falco — burden repetition and relational rescue

Reiner sees candidate children entering the system that formed him. They function as dependents, mirrors, successors, and tethers. His responsibility to them can interrupt suicide and reactivate action.

The relation may reproduce the Warrior system if protection means helping them inherit it. It may also create the possibility of interruption because Reiner knows the cost from inside. V27 leaves that direction open.

## 12.6 Levi / Erwin — trust without purification

Levi understands both Erwin's public command and private dream. He follows without imagining Erwin morally pure. The final serum decision is relationship-conditioned mercy, burden, and authorship.

Their relation supplies a model of trust based on competence, motive knowledge, and accepted imperfection rather than innocence. It also exposes the danger of making one person's dream the infrastructure through which many lives are spent.

## 12.7 Levi / Zeke — cooperation without trust

Levi and Zeke can agree on time pressure, exchange mechanism claims, and preserve an operational relation while maintaining moral hostility. This is among the corpus's clearest examples of decomposed trust.

A model that treats cooperation as reconciliation will fail. A model that treats hatred as making cooperation impossible will also fail.

## 12.8 Sasha / Nicolo / Kaya / Blouse family — ordinary relation as anti-category infrastructure

This network is one of V27's most important political relationships. Food, rescue, hospitality, grief, and imitation create cross-enemy bonds without requiring prior doctrinal agreement.

The network demonstrates how ordinary acts can become institutions of recognition. It also prevents the main political narrative from monopolizing the meaning of history.

## 12.9 Gabi / Falco — attachment, disagreement, and unequal revision speed

Falco's care is not obedience. He discards the armband, restrains Gabi, corrects causality, apologizes, and stays. Gabi's attachment to identity and grievance makes her revision slower.

Their relation is an important prediction constraint: interpersonal commitment can preserve proximity during ideological divergence and can lead the more reflective person into danger.

## 12.10 Eren / Floch / supporters — leader, projection, and movement autonomy

Floch and the supporters organize around Eren's strategic and symbolic value. Eren accepts assistance and gives an immediate objective. V27 does not establish that he authored every slogan, bombing decision, or political meaning assigned to him.

A movement can become partly autonomous from the person it elevates. Future analysis must distinguish Eren's direct command, tacit acceptance, strategic use, and supporter projection.

# XIII. Written Japanese voice and register development through V27

This section concerns the Japanese language of the manga page: pronouns, lexical clusters, sentence structure, address, register shifts, typography, and context. It makes no claim about anime pitch, timbre, pacing, breath, or seiyuu performance.

## 13.1 Eren — categorical grammar with expanding internal complexity

Eren's high-stakes voice remains unusually direct, first-person, future-oriented, and action-closing. Early `駆逐` language, birthright claims, self-negation under guilt, pluralized `オレ達`, reclamation language, V24's `進み続ける`, V26's `戦え`, and V27's compressed post-escape objective belong to one family without being interchangeable.

The model must preserve several registers:

- categorical enemy-removal and action closure;
- reflective causal language in the basement;
- direct relational affection in the railroad memory;
- accusation and frustration toward institutions;
- self-command in the mirror;
- operational compression after escape.

“Eren speaks only in freedom slogans” is a bad model. The same direct grammar can carry care, self-condemnation, analysis, threat, and narrowed objective.

## 13.2 Mikasa — sparse language, operational directness, and concrete moral judgment

Mikasa's written voice is less verbally dense than her action evidence. Under immediate threat she tends toward direct, operational speech. Her V25 condemnation of civilian and child killing is concrete rather than abstractly philosophical. V27's response to Louise is terse and discriminating; the private rescue memory carries more of the relational meaning than an explicit speech would.

Generation must therefore resist compensating for sparse dialogue by importing fandom shorthand, anime performance, or florid internal monologue.

## 13.3 Armin — branching syntax, explanation, manipulation, and simple relational hope

Armin's characteristic speech includes explanation, counterfactuals, conditional branches, reframing, and admission of uncertainty. Under pressure he can formulate severe strategic propositions. He can also lie tactically and weaponize another person's attachment.

V26–V27 broadens rather than reverses the pattern. Technical and strategic thought becomes morally implicated after his use of the Colossal Titan. His final V27 hope—`話し合えばきっと…わかってくれる`—is comparatively simple and relational. The ellipsis and unfinished certainty matter: it is a wager after constructing a lethal branch, not proof that he sees no danger.

## 13.4 Jean — concrete register with persistent peer friction

Jean speaks situationally. Complaint, fear, irritation, and socially aware judgment coexist with responsible action. His later leadership does not require a permanently polished command register.

The railroad scene is a valuable control: embarrassment is managed through irritation. His V27 accountability toward Eren remains concrete—exposure, death, behavior—not grand ideological denunciation.

## 13.5 Historia — anti-euphemistic judgment inside burden acceptance

Historia's register changes from Christa's saintly self-presentation toward blunt self-authorship. V27 adds a crucial complication: she names the royal plan as wrong before accepting it. The anti-euphemistic judgment survives burden acceptance.

This prevents two voice errors: writing later Historia as permanently defiant in every situation, or restoring a passive saintly register whenever she accepts sacrifice.

## 13.6 Reiner — role-indexed speech and responsibility language

Reiner's voice must be indexed to state and role. Soldier practicality, Warrior identity policing, partner familiarity, self-command, caretaker speech, suicidal collapse, and confessional responsibility are not one flat idiolect.

In V25, `違う` rejects Eren's broad structural exculpation. Reiner's self-condemnation is emotionally loaded, not detached forensic analysis. V27's strategic advocacy shows that adversary reasoning can return without proving psychological recovery.

## 13.7 Levi — terse syntax with relational density

Levi's speech is concise and objective-oriented, but terseness does not equal emptiness. `俺は選ぶぞ` marks ownership; naming `ラガコ村` restores victims to a conversation Zeke frames mechanistically; agreement on time pressure remains proposition-specific.

His model requires attention to what a short line is doing in the relationship, not merely how few words it contains.

## 13.8 Hange — explanation, playfulness, caveat, and command strain

Hange's technical voice can move rapidly among demonstration, naming, humor, mechanism, limitation, and uncertainty. V27 command strain does not erase that structure. Nor does technical enthusiasm guarantee political confidence.

A good reconstruction can preserve memorable playfulness and explicit caveats in the same passage. A bad reconstruction makes Hange either constant comic exuberance or a generic grave commander.

## 13.9 Gabi and Falco — categorical identity versus causal correction

Gabi's speech is saturated with merit, identity, candidate status, inherited guilt, and enemy category. Grief makes the language more personally urgent. In the Kaya confrontation, abstract historical justification is pressed against a specific victim and becomes defensive.

Falco is less theatrical and more causal. He corrects, restrains, apologizes, and acknowledges present wrongdoing. His voice should not become adult neutrality; attachment and adolescent urgency remain visible.

## 13.10 Kaya, Yelena, Floch, and collective political voice

Kaya's repeated questions are victim-specific rather than abstract theory. Their force comes from refusing to let historical categories escape the particular mother.

Yelena's `主体的な思想` is a compact ideological key: she values Eren as an autonomous high-leverage subject, not merely an obedient receiver.

Floch and the crowd use savior rhetoric and inherited Survey Corps language. The collective voice simplifies a complex actor into a political sign. This language must be kept separate from Eren's direct utterances.

## 13.11 Voice-readiness conclusion

At V27, written-Japanese voice evidence is strongest for high-stakes and relationship-conditioned speech among Eren, Armin, Jean, Reiner, Levi, Erwin, Hange, Gabi, and Falco. Mikasa's low dialogue density, Historia's limited ordinary sample, and the crisis-heavy corpus constrain free generation. Speech fidelity remains a separate gate from behavioral plausibility.

# XIV. Manga form and visual architecture

## 14.1 Enclosure evolves from wall to institution to self-command

The Walls initially organize the page through overwhelming vertical mass and confined human scale. Later, enclosure appears in forests, cages, cells, underground rooms, hardened crystal, inherited bodies, rail lines, offices, and panel structures that restrict sight.

V27's bars, rails, tables, windows, and controlled rooms continue the enclosure motif after geographic expansion. The world is larger; the decision space can be narrower.

## 14.2 Reversal of foundational images

The manga repeatedly returns to early visual structures with changed agents:

- Titan above / human below becomes Marleyans terrified by Paradisian Titans;
- the Colossal silhouette that destroyed Armin's home becomes Armin's own body over Liberio;
- the Scouts' sacrificial slogan becomes a crowd's insurgent claim against military command;
- the sea shifts from positive horizon to a line beyond which other human homes and enemies exist.

These are not simple ironies. They force the reader to transfer moral attention across reversed positions.

## 14.3 V25: basement and stage

V25 constructs a vertical political form. Below, Eren and Reiner speak privately, recognize similarity, and confront responsibility. Above, Willy arranges history, audience, declaration, and anticipated sacrifice. Eren's offered hand and the timing of transformation convert private recognition into public violence.

The formal simultaneity is the point: recognition and enemy construction are not successive moral stages. They can occur at once.

## 14.4 V26: the Colossal reversal and the doubled mirror

Armin's Colossal body occupies the visual position once held by the incomprehensible enemy. The page asks the reader to recognize the terror geometry from the other side. This does not make Armin identical to Bertolt or erase strategic context. It makes moral perspective impossible to stabilize through silhouette alone.

The final mirror splits Eren into speaker and addressed image. The visual form supports both self-authorship and self-coercion. A person can become the institution issuing orders to himself.

## 14.5 V27: strategic bodies and bounded sight

Historia remains visibly present while succession policy is discussed. The page refuses to let the body disappear into a deterrence diagram.

The railroad sunset provides open warmth, peer embarrassment, and relational ease before present-day fracture. The armband makes political category portable on a child's body. At the farm, adults can misread lethal conflict as ordinary play because the political category is visually hidden inside domestic space.

Zackly's chair becomes a bomb container, transforming an object of coercive spectacle into the mechanism of institutional rupture. The crowd gathers below the shattered office, separating popular energy from formal authority. Eren's open-ground coat silhouette makes a person into an icon at the moment his direct speech remains narrow.

The final carriage returns to faces rather than slogans. Armin's unfinished dialogic confidence is protected as ambiguity rather than converted into authorial endorsement or ridicule.

## 14.6 Faces, hands, and bodies as moral evidence

Across V01–V27, faces and hands repeatedly interrupt category:

- recognition occurs through a known face inside an enemy role;
- offered or withheld hands stage trust, rescue, coercion, and betrayal;
- injured, burned, transformed, pregnant, imprisoned, or weaponized bodies keep policy attached to persons;
- small facial reactions preserve shame, grief, embarrassment, or uncertainty that declarative speech would flatten.

Formal analysis is therefore not decorative corroboration. It is often where the manga preserves contradictions that plot summary erases.

## 15.1 Readiness is multidimensional

The corpus distinguishes at least:

- developmental-state coverage;
- decision-grammar coverage;
- relationship-conditioned coverage;
- epistemic and attention style;
- Japanese written-voice coverage;
- ordinary-life and low-stakes coverage;
- negative constraints;
- scenario distance.

A character can be strong in crisis decisions and weak in domestic life, strong in one historical state and weak in a later one, or behaviorally legible while linguistically under-sampled.

Strong provisional at V27 across high-stakes state, decision, relationship, epistemic, and voice domains; ordinary coverage is moderate. Suitable for D0 and carefully bounded D1; D2 requires explicit branching; D3 is exploratory only.

Strong provisional and the best temporal/state reconstruction subject. Childhood, candidate, Warrior, soldier, family, mentor, suicidal, confessional, and reactivated-strategist states are differentiated. Transfer fails if these states are collapsed.

Provisional-high for threat and relationship states. Moral disagreement, selective attachment, and nonreciprocity toward admirers are important controls. Broad ordinary and high-dialogue generation remain limited.

### Levi and Hange

## 15.5 No canonical full-series models at V27

# XVI. Separate prospective-model audit: frozen V01–V19 register against V20–V27

## 16.1 Experimental design and what was genuinely prospective

The character-model experiment has a stronger provenance claim than ordinary retrospective interpretation because its construction and validation sets were separated in time.

Before Volume 20 was analytically opened:

- the V01–V19 checkpoint was completed;
- the character-model architecture, reconstruction method, and schema were established;
- fifty-one predictions were written in `AOT_CHARACTER_MODEL_PROSPECTIVE_PREDICTION_REGISTER_V01-V19.md`;
- each prediction specified a character/domain, relevant conditions, scenario distance, expected behavior, exception or failure mode, counterevidence criterion, supporting construction evidence, and confidence;
- the file was frozen as immutable.

Volumes 20–27 were then read sequentially. Later evidence could be scored, but the prediction wording could not be softened, broadened, or deleted. This protects the experiment from the most common retrospective error: rewriting a model after seeing the outcome and then describing the match as prediction.

The experiment is nevertheless not a randomized or comprehensive benchmark. The manga supplies the test conditions it happens to contain. The register emphasizes D0–D1 canon-near and analogous situations. Some predictions receive repeated tests; others receive none. Character appearance, state, role, and narrative focus are not balanced.

## 16.4 What `PARTIAL` means in this experiment

The twenty-two `PARTIAL` events are not one category of error. They include several analytically different cases.

### Condition mismatch

The predicted mechanism appears, but the later event differs in stake, relationship, role, or available alternatives. Examples include Mikasa's V27 nonintervention under a much higher-stakes sovereignty crisis than the frozen low-to-moderate-stakes condition, or Jean making concrete-cost accusations about Eren to peers rather than confronting Eren directly.

### Mechanism present, sequence incomplete

Armin recognizes that his Eren model has failed and lowers confidence, but V26–V27 does not yet show a fully tested replacement model. The direction is correct; the complete predicted revision cycle has not occurred.

### Domain support without the exact trigger

Hange may preserve evidence discipline under command distress without receiving the exact grief/anger-investigation test specified in the frozen rule. Reiner's attachment to the 104th remains evident without a new direct lethal encounter matching the predicted condition.

### Voice expansion rather than contradiction

Eren's V24–V25 reflective speech broadens the repertoire. It does not contradict categorical high-stakes closure because the controlled pre-action state differs from the acute action condition. `PARTIAL` records useful expansion without pretending it was the exact test.

### Anti-caricature support with incomplete scenario match

Levi's naming of Ragako supports emotional and moral density inside terse speech, but it is not a clean immediate command-amid-loss test.

A rigorous audit should therefore avoid treating all `PARTIAL` events as half-credit. Some primarily reveal model underspecification; others reflect conservative test eligibility; others are supportive evidence outside the exact frozen condition.

## 16.5 The zero-FAIL result: evidence and cautions

The absence of `FAIL` events is meaningful but not dispositive.

### Evidence for genuine model quality

Several predictions survived strong, close-condition tests:

- Eren's recognition of a former comrade does not automatically inhibit lethal action in the V25 basement.
- Armin's moral injury after causing mass death does not erase severe strategic reasoning in V26–V27.
- Jean's human-killing threshold and proportionality survive Sasha's death.
- Levi prioritizes objective and information over revenge while maintaining hostility toward Zeke.
- Reiner's state architecture is strengthened by publication-later flashback evidence unavailable to the construction set.
- Bertolt's V19 authored mission state persists into V20.
- Eren's written voice remains direct and categorical while supporting more than slogan speech.
- Mikasa's sparse operational voice and selective moral judgment persist.
- Erwin's public/private register split receives direct same-volume confirmation.

These are not trivial matches. Some occur at D0 or close D1 conditions and could reasonably have contradicted the construction model.

### Conservative eligibility rules

The ledger correctly declines to manufacture failures. Historia's V27 acceptance of her own burden is not scored against `HISTORIA-01`, which predicts resistance when **another valued person** frames self-erasure as noble duty. Bertolt's V21 helpless death is not treated as contradiction of a V19 mission/combat prediction after physical control and mission viability disappear.

Conservative eligibility increases validity but lowers stress coverage. A model can avoid failure because the narrative has not supplied the specified condition.

### Crisis-domain redundancy

The holdout contains many battle, command, grief, institutional, and high-stakes voice events. It repeatedly tests a subset of mechanisms that the first-half manga had already sampled richly. Success in these domains is real and partly redundant.

### Register coverage gaps

Fourteen predictions remain untested, including the explicit ordinary-life cross-character control. Many later-important characters—Gabi, Falco, Kaya, Yelena, current Floch—were not mature enough at V19 to receive comparable frozen models. The experiment can validate the V19 model suite only where the construction set existed.

### Correct conclusion

The zero-FAIL result is best explained by a mixture:

> **strong state-conditioned modeling + disciplined eligibility + repeated crisis-domain testing + incomplete coverage.**

It should increase confidence in bounded reconstruction. It should not be translated into a global simulation score.

## 16.6 Strongest robust mechanisms across multiple holdout tranches

The most durable results are not individual scene matches but mechanisms supported across different volumes.

### Recognition is not a universal violence inhibitor

Bertolt, Reiner, Eren, Mikasa, and the cross-model rules repeatedly show that recognizing personhood, former comradeship, or causal similarity does not automatically remove incompatible objectives. This mechanism survives multiple characters and conditions.

### Development is state accumulation, not exposure of earlier falsity

Reiner's childhood ideology, constructed Marcel role, soldier life, Warrior role, family position, suicidality, confession, and reactivation support a layered state model. V24 is especially valuable because the flashback evidence was publication-later and unavailable to the frozen construction set.

### Moral injury does not imply paralysis or indifference

Armin remains capable of severe reasoning after becoming the Colossal weapon. Jean preserves proportionality after bereavement. Levi can retain moral hostility and objective discipline. These results constrain both sentimental-collapse and cold-utilitarian caricatures.

### Trust is decomposable

Levi/Zeke, Paradis/Volunteers, and Eren's institutional relations show that actors can cooperate on propositions or objectives without broad motive trust. Trust should be modeled by domain and relation, not as a binary alliance flag.

### High-stakes voice has stable grammar and state variation

Eren, Mikasa, Armin, Jean, Levi, Erwin, Reiner, and Hange show recognizable written-language tendencies that survive development. The holdout also demonstrates that stable grammar can carry different speech acts and that controlled states can broaden repertoire.

### Intimacy can become stale evidence

Armin, Jean, Connie, Mikasa, and the 104th collectively show that a historically accurate model of a close person can lose predictive power after major knowledge, role, and power transitions. Model confidence must decay or be revalidated across discontinuities.

## 16.7 New constraints the frozen register did not predict

A holdout audit should record not only pass/fail results but important phenomena outside the construction model.

V20–V27 adds or sharpens:

- Eren's self-command as possible self-coercion;
- Historia's acceptance of her own public burden while condemning the structure;
- Gabi's defensive category restoration under concrete counterevidence;
- Falco's attachment-driven risk despite better causal understanding;
- Reiner's candidate-child anti-suicide tether and later strategic reactivation;
- Yelena's influence-through-desired-self-authorship model;
- Floch's transition from survivor grievance to clandestine organizational capacity;
- posthumous behavioral inheritance from Sasha to Kaya;
- the distinction between a leader's direct speech and a movement's projection;
- popular legitimacy separating from formal office;
- ordinary food and hospitality as cross-enemy relation infrastructure.

These are model gains, not retrospective “wins” for frozen predictions. They belong in the mutable ledgers and this checkpoint.

## 16.8 Character-specific audit conclusions

### Eren

The holdout strongly validates high-stakes voice, recognition-without-deescalation, and attachment under objective pressure. It also reveals the need to model self-coercion, strategic opacity, and relation-specific anti-instrumentalization. Complete motive remains open.

### Mikasa

Threat preemption and sparse operational voice receive support. Selective nonintervention and objective correction remain only partially stress-tested. Broad ordinary transfer is weak.

### Armin

Anomaly-driven reasoning, manipulation, moral injury, severe contingency, and model-confidence downgrade are supported. The complete replacement of a failed intimate model remains untested.

### Jean

Practical leadership and proportionality receive strong tests. Direct accountability confrontation is partially tested; ordinary peer irritation receives a valuable low-stakes pass.

### Historia

Anti-euphemistic high-stakes voice passes. The most consequential V27 development—accepting her own burden—falls outside the exact frozen conditions and requires a revised mutable model rather than a manufactured failure.

### Reiner

The temporal/state architecture performs exceptionally well. Several relationship-specific and partner-entrustment predictions remain partial because the exact action conditions do not recur.

### Levi, Erwin, and Hange

Levi and Erwin receive strong command-domain validation. Hange's model remains plausible but less fully tested across the frozen conditions. All three retain ordinary-life deficits.

### Bertolt and Annie

Bertolt's V19 mission state is validated and later confounded under noncomparable helplessness. Annie's key frozen prediction remains untested; current readiness depends partly on V24 backfill and earlier evidence rather than direct holdout success.

## 16.9 Readiness implications

The experiment warrants promotion in confidence, not promotion to unrestricted authority.

It supports:

- D0 reconstruction in well-sampled states for the strongest characters;
- carefully bounded D1 transfer when relationship, role, stakes, and knowledge are close;
- explicit branch-and-confidence output for D2;
- abstention or exploratory labeling for D3;
- stronger use of negative constraints against common caricatures;
- continued prospective adjudication after the checkpoint without altering historical predictions.

It does not support:

- a single percentage for “character accuracy”;
- cross-era state blending;
- confident romance or domestic simulation where the manga lacks evidence;
- anime-performance imitation from manga punctuation;
- treating thematic understanding as equivalent to speech or behavior generation;
- canonical full-series models before V34 and final validation.

## 16.10 Prospective audit verdict

**Verdict: PASS WITH SUBSTANTIAL COVERAGE LIMITS.**

The V01–V19 character architecture demonstrates genuine predictive warrant in crisis, relationship, command, moral-injury, state-transition, and written-voice domains. The experiment is methodologically credible because prediction text was frozen and eligibility remained conservative. Its strongest result is not “zero failures.” It is that state- and relationship-conditioned models repeatedly outperformed global trait caricatures.

The principal unresolved risk is domain imbalance. The next volumes should continue adjudication where frozen or checkpoint-updated rules receive fair tests, while ordinary-life, later-state, and underrepresented-character evidence remains a first-class readiness constraint.

# XVIII. Strongest surviving hypotheses, counterevidence, and unresolved interpretation

## 18.1 Hypothesis: freedom's mature problem is jurisdiction, not merely escape

**Support:** Walls, Titan custody, Reiss inheritance, Warrior succession, Eren's unilateral action, Historia's reproductive burden, military confinement, and insurgent authority all concern who may decide for whom. V26 self-command shows jurisdiction can move inside the person; V27 shows it breaking between person and institution.

**Counterevidence / limit:** The manga still gives substantial moral force to decisive unilateral action under catastrophic time pressure. Levi's direct choice, Armin's sacrifice plan, Eren's interventions, and the Scouts' operations show that procedural contestability is not always available.

**V27 judgment:** `STRENGTHEN`, with the qualification that legitimate emergency action may be unilateral locally without creating permanent sovereign entitlement.

## 18.2 Hypothesis: recognition is necessary for moral truth and insufficient for peace

**Support:** Titan personhood, Reiner/Bertolt/Annie, Eren/Reiner, Mikasa/Eren, Gabi/Kaya, and the terror reversals repeatedly destroy nonperson categories without resolving objective conflict.

**Counterevidence / limit:** Sasha/Kaya, Nicolo/Blouse, Falco/Kaya, and some public truth practices show recognition can change behavior and create new relations. It is not merely contemplative moral pain.

**V27 judgment:** `STRENGTHEN`. Recognition is best modeled as a condition that can enable restraint, accountability, or relation—not as a sufficient cause of reconciliation.

## 18.3 Hypothesis: ordinary life is a political technology of de-escalation

**Support:** food, rescue, hospitality, family dinner, peer teasing, farm work, and remembered appetite create relations that enemy doctrine did not authorize. The Blouse network is the strongest example.

**Counterevidence / limit:** Ordinary familiarity does not automatically transform state policy, military objectives, or inherited grievance. Reiner lives among the 104th and continues the mission; Gabi can inhabit the farm and preserve the armband.

**V27 judgment:** `STRENGTHEN` as a local and relational mechanism; `OPEN` as a sufficient route to durable institutional peace.

## 18.4 Hypothesis: Eren's present architecture combines autonomy and influence

**Support:** durable freedom drive, self-authored language, direct choices, Yelena's recruitment, Zeke's relationship claim, inherited memories, selective secrecy, and self-directed imperatives all plausibly interact. Eren's immediate post-escape objective is his own concise statement, while external actors build larger theories around him.

**Counterevidence / limit:** V27 does not reveal enough of Eren's internal process to weight the components. His political supporters may project a doctrine he has not articulated, and Yelena's claim about cultivating autonomy is self-interested testimony.

**V27 judgment:** `OPEN`. Any single-cause account—pure autonomy, total manipulation, inherited compulsion, or unchanging childhood essence—is under-supported.

## 18.5 Hypothesis: Historia's burden acceptance is authored but coercively constrained

**Support:** she explicitly condemns the plan and accepts it under existential threat. Her later voice does not revert to Christa euphemism. Her queenship and orphan policy establish public responsibility.

**Counterevidence / limit:** Pregnancy motive, timing, alternatives perceived, private deliberation, and possible relationships affecting the decision remain absent. “Authored” must not be used to launder structural coercion.

**V27 judgment:** `REVISE` the anti-sacrifice shortcut; preserve both agency and constraint.

## 18.6 Hypothesis: Eren's defense of Historia generalizes into an anti-instrumental ethic

**Support:** livestock language, search for alternatives, universal birthright history, and explicit attachment support a person-centered objection.

**Counterevidence / limit:** Liberio and the command crisis show Eren exposing or overriding other valued people. His anti-instrumentalization may be relation-specific or subordinated to a larger strategic architecture.

**V27 judgment:** `OPEN`. Genuine local principle is established; scope of generalization is not.

## 18.7 Hypothesis: the post-coup state is losing legitimacy because it abandoned the Survey Corps freedom ethic

**Support:** confinement of Eren, Volunteer detention, succession planning, secrecy, public outrage, and internal defection create a visible legitimacy crisis.

# XXI. Checkpoint conclusions

## 21.1 Narrative conclusion

The manga's first twenty-seven volumes do not proceed from false mystery to solved world. They proceed from visible enclosure to increasingly distributed forms of constraint.

- The Walls confine bodies and knowledge.
- Titan categories confine personhood.
- roles confine identity.
- archives confine interpretation.
- military institutions confine action.
- inherited bodies confine future generations.
- enemy narratives confine moral attention.
- attachment can confine judgment.
- self-command can confine the freedom-seeking subject from within.

Each expansion of knowledge destroys one enclosure and reveals another. The sea does not cancel freedom. It changes its scale from escape to coexistence among peoples with incompatible histories and homes.

## 21.2 Ethical conclusion

The strongest V27 ethical proposition is:

> **No person's recognition, suffering, love, competence, strategic indispensability, or self-authored commitment automatically gives them the right to spend another person.**

This standard does not eliminate emergency action or tragic choice. It prevents necessity from becoming self-validating. It requires decision-makers to preserve personhood, acknowledge coercion, distinguish civilians, expose uncertainty, and remain answerable for who bears the cost.

## 21.3 Political conclusion

Protection becomes sovereignty when plans for survival allocate bodies, information, authority, and future generations. Paradis's crisis is not only whether it can survive Marley. It is whether it can defend itself without reproducing the political logic by which people become weapons, archives, bloodlines, deterrents, or crowds organized around an irreplaceable savior.

Formal office, actual capacity, public support, and legitimate authority are no longer aligned at V27. The next phase must be read as a contest over their recombination, not as a simple conflict between competent rebels and treacherous bureaucrats or between lawful order and irrational extremists.

## 21.4 Character conclusion

The principal characters are now better modeled through **decision architectures** than through themes or traits.

- Eren: freedom drive + attachment + direct closure + unilateral protection + strategic opacity + possible self-coercion.
- Mikasa: selective attachment + threat preemption + moral discrimination + action-heavy voice.
- Armin: model revision + positive horizon + manipulation + moral injury + dialogue with severe fallback.
- Jean: practical proportionality + concrete accountability + peer friction.
- Historia: authored identity + anti-euphemistic judgment + public responsibility + constrained burden.
- Reiner: cumulative states + shame + responsibility + relational reactivation.
- Levi: objective discipline + uncertainty honesty + relationship-specific choice + decomposed trust.
- Hange: curiosity + caveat + technical method + governance strain.
- Gabi: merit identity + inherited category + bereavement + defensive restoration.
- Falco: causal empathy + apology + protective opposition + attachment-dominated risk.

These formulations are stronger than common caricatures and remain bounded by source, state, relationship, and ordinary-life coverage.

## 21.5 Prospective-model conclusion

The frozen-register experiment passes as a methodological demonstration of bounded predictive warrant. The event record is strong, but the honest summary is:

- 64 scored events;
- 41 PASS, 22 PARTIAL, 0 FAIL, 1 CONFOUNDED;
- 37 of 51 predictions tested at least once;
- 28 predictions with at least one PASS;
- 9 partial-only;
- 14 untested;
- repeated weighting toward crisis, command, relationship, and high-stakes voice.

The result justifies continuing state-conditioned prospective work. It does not justify a percentage claim or unrestricted character simulation.

## 21.6 Checkpoint identity

The V19 checkpoint's governing phrase was **freedom after the collapse of categories**.

The V27 checkpoint's governing phrase is:

> **freedom becomes constitutional.**

The central three-volume hinge is:

> **recognition without reconciliation → victory producing internal captivity → protection becoming a struggle over sovereignty.**

The strongest compact formulation of the complete V01–V27 movement is:

> **The series begins by asking how people escape a world that denies their freedom. By V27, it asks how several freedom-seeking people and institutions can share a world without making one another into the walls, weapons, archives, and sacrifices of their own survival.**
