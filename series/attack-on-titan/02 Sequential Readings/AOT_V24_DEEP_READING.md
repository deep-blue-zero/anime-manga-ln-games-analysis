# 『進撃の巨人』Volume 24 Deep Reading
## Inherited scripts and chosen continuations: lies, hands, hells, political theater, and Reiner's return to the person he helped create

## 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 23 made the world beyond the sea socially inhabitable. Volume 24 asks a harder question: **once a person discovers that the role handed to them was built from propaganda, family fantasy, coercion, accident, or another person's manipulation, what does it mean to continue?**

The answer is not that discovering the lie automatically produces freedom. Nearly every major figure in Volume 24 learns, remembers, or admits that the script governing them is compromised:

- Reiner learns that the father he hoped to reunite with wants nothing to do with him or his mother;
- Marcel admits that Reiner's Warrior selection was manipulated to protect Porco;
- Annie rejects the moral vocabulary surrounding both Marley and Eldia as self-serving;
- Reiner recognizes that the original Paradis mission has become a survival trap and nevertheless chooses to continue it;
- adult Reiner understands that his childhood dream and island-devil ideology were false or radically incomplete, yet remains inside the Warrior system;
- Eren explicitly distinguishes being pushed into hell by others from pushing one's own back, but does not claim that self-authorship guarantees a good destination;
- Willy Tybur openly describes politics as story, narration, stagecraft, and direction while simultaneously assuming personal responsibility for steering a state his predecessors refused to steer;
- Falco accepts Eren's encouragement as a reason to move forward, while unknowingly carrying Eren's letters and ultimately carrying Reiner himself into Eren's clandestine room.

The volume therefore makes a crucial distinction between **origin** and **continuation**.

A motive can originate in another person's wish. A role can originate in manipulation. A political story can be staged. A child can be selected through a lie. None of those facts alone determines what later agency means. The moral and psychological problem moves to the moment when the person knows enough to see the compromise and still chooses, refuses, redirects, or reproduces the role.

This is why Chapter 97's title, 「手から手へ」, is more than a reference to Eren's letters. Across Volume 24, things move from hand to hand:

- Titan powers;
- family expectations;
- Warrior candidacy;
- responsibility for younger children;
- political authority;
- letters;
- stories;
- motivational language;
- guilt;
- and finally people themselves.

Reiner receives his role partly because Marcel protects Porco. He then announces that if Marcel is needed, he will become Marcel. He tells young Eren to do what must be done and keep moving. Years later adult Eren gives Falco a theory of people who push themselves into hell and encourages him to continue forward. Falco, believing he is helping an injured veteran contact family, becomes the courier who brings Reiner down into the room where Eren waits.

The transmission is not a mystical causal loop at this boundary. It is a **social chain of language, role, responsibility, and action**.

A second governing structure is the volume's treatment of lying. 「嘘つき」 does not isolate one liar. Volume 24 is full of lies with different functions and moral weights:

- Karina sustains a fantasy about Reiner's absent father;
- Marley turns Warrior inheritance into ethnic redemption;
- Reiner tells himself and his comrades that their mission gives them a world-saving purpose;
- he labels the 104th “island devils” after living among them as friends;
- Eren fakes memory loss and illness severity, uses the name Kruger, and misrepresents his letters as family correspondence;
- Willy plans public political theater in which historical truth and strategic persuasion will be deliberately staged together.

The volume does **not** flatten all of these into equivalent deception. Some lies protect, some recruit, some preserve a role, some manipulate trust, and some help a person survive a contradiction they cannot yet integrate. The analytical task is to ask what each lie does and who bears its cost.

The chapter titles form an argument:

1. **「嘘つき」 — Liar:** inherited identities and political strategies are built on interested stories, omissions, and self-deception.
2. **「希望の扉」 — The Door of Hope:** the literal family door closes on Reiner, but he keeps searching for another route through mission, role, and survival; the “hope” available to him is already morally compromised.
3. **「手から手へ」 — From One Hand to Another:** roles, letters, responsibility, and motivational language are transmitted between people.
4. **「よかったな」 — Good for You / I'm Glad:** the phrase is first attached to Falco's possibility of *not* inheriting a Titan and finally to Reiner's return home, where the person waiting for him is Eren. Relief and menace occupy the same words.

The volume's central thesis is:

> **Volume 24 separates being given a script from choosing to continue it. Self-authorship matters, but it does not purify the destination: a person may push their own back toward hope, toward another hell, or toward a role whose false origins they now understand.**

A second thesis follows:

> **The series' recurring command to keep moving is not the private essence of one character. It is socially transmitted, reinterpreted, and weaponized across relationships—most strikingly from Reiner to Eren and then from Eren to Falco.**

# 3. Narrative architecture and causal close read

## 3.1 Chapter 95 — 「嘘つき」: a state needs a story, and a child needs a father

Chapter 95 begins in the current Marley strategic crisis rather than immediately returning to Reiner's childhood. This framing matters because the flashback is nested inside an institution already worried that the logic which created Reiner is becoming obsolete.

### Titan obsolescence becomes an ethnic survival crisis

Marleyan leadership acknowledges that conventional weapons will eventually overtake Titan power. That military development is immediately translated into a racial-political problem. If Eldians cease to be useful as Titan weapons, the state and world may feel even less need to preserve their rights or lives. The line that the world no longer needs to fit Eldians inside the definition of human rights turns technological obsolescence into a threat of political disposability.

Reiner names the situation 「民族存亡」の危機: a crisis of ethnic survival.

The proposed solution is revealingly circular. Marley has learned that dependence on Titan supremacy is strategically dangerous, yet its answer is to reclaim the Founding Titan and Paradis resources quickly enough to restore national power. The state responds to the declining viability of the old system by trying to extract one final, decisive advantage from that system.

Pieck raises the deeper legitimacy problem: even a successful operation will not automatically erase the world's hatred of Marley and Eldians. A military victory cannot by itself rewrite political meaning.

The meeting therefore concludes that **a story needs a narrator**: `物語には語り手が必要だ`. The Tybur family can supply that narrator because its reputation is unusually favorable abroad and because its history is associated with opposition to King Fritz and possession of the War Hammer Titan.

This is one of V24's most important institutional claims. Narrative is not treated as decoration added after strategy. It is part of strategy's operational infrastructure. Marley needs external audiences to understand Paradis in a particular way before a military program can acquire legitimacy.

### Reiner's childhood: the family fantasy behind the Warrior fantasy

The chapter then reconstructs the child Reiner who wanted to become a Warrior. His motive architecture is considerably more personal than “he believed propaganda.” Becoming an honorary Marleyan would allow his mother and him to leave the internment zone. He believes this can reunite them with his Marleyan father and create an ordinary family.

When Reiner approaches that father, the fantasy collapses violently. The man is not waiting for reunion. He fears exposure will destroy his current family and tells Reiner that he will escape Reiner and Karina, calling them an Eldian devil mother and child.

The door closes.

This literal closed door is one of the volume's strongest visual condensations. Reiner's original “door of hope”—Warrior success leading to family integration—has already failed before the Paradis mission begins.

Karina does not respond by dismantling the dream. She tells Reiner that he can still complete the mission and implies that his father must be praying for his success. The public crowd likewise hails the selected Warriors as the chosen people who will save everyone from the island devils.

Reiner therefore does not simply continue believing the same thing after the paternal rejection. The motive is **re-authored**. If he cannot become the son who restores his family, he can become `世界の英雄`, a hero of the world. The political role substitutes for the impossible family role.

That substitution matters for later reconstruction. Hero aspiration is compensatory without being fake. The child has lost one concrete future and uses a public mission to build another.

### Selection is revealed as another interested story

The candidate material also dismantles the idea that Reiner's selection transparently proved superior aptitude. Porco attacks the decision and insists Reiner was the weaker candidate. Marcel later apologizes and admits the key hidden fact: he influenced the military's impression so Porco would not be chosen, because Marcel wanted to protect his younger brother from inheriting a Titan and its shortened life.

The revelation lands immediately before Marcel is killed protecting Reiner from the Pure Titan later revealed to be Ymir.

This creates an exceptionally cruel causal sequence:

1. Marcel manipulates selection to protect Porco;
2. Reiner receives the Armor partly because of that manipulation;
3. Marcel confesses;
4. Marcel then physically protects Reiner and dies;
5. Reiner survives carrying both the borrowed position and the knowledge that its original selection was compromised.

The Warrior system thus begins Reiner's Paradis mission with a profound authorship deficit. He has been told he is chosen, then told he was not really the rightful choice, then saved by the person whose manipulation put him there.

The later identity fracture is not surprising in retrospect, but the volume must still preserve contingency: this newly supplied cause **recontextualizes** the fracture; it does not mean every prior soldier action was consciously fraudulent.

## 3.2 Chapter 96 — 「希望の扉」: after the real door closes, mission becomes the only route home

Chapter 96 opens with Reiner running after Marcel's death. The core problem is now immediate: the team has lost a member and a Titan power; the original plan has catastrophically failed before reaching the Walls.

### `帰れない`: mission continuation begins as survival

Reiner's first decisive argument is not heroic. It is `帰れない`: they cannot go back.

He recognizes that returning in failure may mean losing the Armor and being eaten. He insists that the operation continue because success is the only path that still leads home.

This is a crucial correction to any model in which Reiner's later mission persistence is powered by one motive. At this boundary it combines:

- fear of death;
- fear of punishment;
- guilt over Marcel;
- need to justify survival;
- longing for home;
- public Warrior identity;
- and the desire to turn a compromised selection into something that can still mean success.

### Annie strips away the heroic vocabulary

Annie supplies the chapter's strongest adversarial voice. She tells Reiner to admit everything to Magath and say the failure is his responsibility. More importantly, she attacks the ideological language surrounding their role:

`何が名誉マーレ人だ!! 選ばれし戦士だ!! マーレもエルディアも全部クソったれ!! 自分のことしか考えてないくせに!! 私もそうだ!! 生きて帰んなきゃいけないんだよ!!`

The force of the line comes from its refusal to purify Annie herself. She does not claim to stand outside selfishness. She includes herself: `私もそうだ`. Her position is not moral sainthood; it is anti-euphemistic survival honesty.

She also tells Reiner that if he was supposed to die, he should accept the guilt and die. Her violence toward him is severe. This should prevent sentimental readings in which her rejection of ideology automatically makes her the ethically clean member of the group.

### `ライナーは死んだ`: identity substitution as an emergency construction

Reiner eventually responds with one of the most important identity statements in the first twenty-four volumes:

`ライナーは死んだ…`

If Marcel is what the group needs, he says, then `俺がマルセルに…なるから…`.

This is not the revelation of a hidden authentic self. It is the explicit **construction of a replacement role** under trauma and survival pressure. Reiner attempts to solve both a tactical vacancy and a personal legitimacy crisis by becoming the person whose death made his continued existence possible.

The scene strongly validates the V01-V19 model that Reiner is vulnerable to discontinuous role organization rather than processing contradictory obligations through one smoothly integrated perspective.

At the same time, the text does not permit a simplistic diagnosis that everything after this moment is “fake Marcel.” Roles can acquire reality through repeated action and relationship. That is exactly what the later 104th soldier identity will demonstrate.

### Breaching the Wall as logistics, not destiny

The Warrior operation then proceeds through concrete bodily labor and contingent decisions. Annie uses the Female Titan to draw Pure Titans. Reiner and Bertolt must manage endurance and transport. The Wall breach is not presented as a clean execution of an omnipotent plan; it is an improvised recovery from a mission that nearly ended before it began.

The flashback later has Reiner recognize the deeper personal collapse:

- he wanted to become a Warrior to fulfill his mother's wish and live happily with both parents;
- the father he imagined did not exist as a willing family partner;
- his mother continued dreaming a dream she knew could not be fulfilled;
- Reiner was not supposed to be selected;
- and he was supposed to die on the mission's first day.

His response is not philosophical clarity but refusal of closure: `まだ終わりたくない…まだ何もわかってないんだ`.

That sentence is an important Reiner rule. When the inherited script fails, the desire to understand can become another reason not to stop.

### The hanged man's story and the problem of unexplained confession

The Warriors later hear the story of a man who abandoned his children and fled, only to hang himself. Bertolt retains the story strongly enough that it returns in dreams. He asks why the man told them.

At the V24 boundary, the manga does not supply a final answer. The story becomes another instance of speech handed to listeners who may not understand why they were chosen to receive it. Confession does not automatically absolve, nor does it guarantee the recipient can use what was passed on.

### Intelligence work and self-authored mission meaning

The Warriors infer that the visible king may be a puppet and construct an infiltration strategy: enter military life, gain access toward the center, gather information, and use the chaos of another breach if necessary.

These are **character inferences**, not omniscient historical narration. The method must preserve that source status.

Annie continually presses the practical cost: time is disappearing from their limited terms, the operation is inefficient, and friendship with island people creates danger. Reiner responds by building a stronger moral narrative around the mission. He declares that humanity's fate is in their hands and that he has finally understood the meaning of why they are there.

This is meaning-construction under pressure. It may sincerely motivate him without becoming objective proof that the mission is morally right.

## 3.3 Chapter 97 — 「手から手へ」: Reiner gives Eren a language, Eren gives Falco a direction

Chapter 97 is the volume's central transmission chapter. Its structure moves repeatedly from one relationship to another, showing how words and obligations migrate.

### Soldier Reiner exists in ordinary social space

The trainee scenes matter because they occur after Reiner has explicitly constructed the Marcel replacement role and after he has already committed to the Warrior mission. Yet he can still joke, mediate, eat, observe peers, and function inside the 104th's ordinary social world.

This is powerful anti-caricature evidence. A covert role does not prevent genuine low-stakes behavior from developing inside the role environment. The scene cannot prove that Reiner has forgotten the Warrior mission; the night meetings show the opposite. But it demonstrates that ordinary soldier Reiner is behaviorally real enough to have a social register distinct from mission speech.

### Dehumanizing language becomes active self-enforcement

At night Annie reports that her infiltration attempt nearly failed. The Warriors debate whether to leave. Reiner instead decides to proceed with another Wall breach.

When Annie notes that people they know may die, Reiner says they are not friends: they are different, island devils.

This statement should **not** be taken as transparent ontology. By this point the chapter has just shown him interacting with those people socially. The dehumanizing category is therefore functioning partly as **role maintenance**. He must say the relationship does not count in order to keep the mission executable.

That makes the language morally important rather than unreal. Self-enforced dehumanization can enable action even when the speaker possesses contradictory humanizing evidence.

### Bertolt's dream: another story remains unprocessed

Bertolt continues dreaming about the man who hanged himself and wonders why the man told them what he had done. This modest scene supports the broader model that Bertolt's internal life contains unresolved human material even while he remains mission-capable. It does not by itself explain his later decisions.

### Reiner -> Eren: persistence is transmitted

The chapter then produces one of the most significant lexical relationships in the reread.

Young Eren, exhausted and discouraged, asks Reiner how to become like him. Reiner's answer is practical rather than metaphysical: do what has to be done.

The sequence then folds Warrior planning and Eren's own determination together. Reiner and the Warriors resolve to act; the language of continuing forward becomes visually and verbally linked across scenes.

Most importantly, Reiner directly tells Eren that he is going to wipe out every Titan and that `お前ならやれる`.

This **does not originate Eren's extermination vow**. Eren's Titan hatred and drive precede the conversation. But Reiner reinforces it. The man later devastated by Eren's persistence once helped validate persistence as the answer to apparent inadequacy.

This requires a revision to any overly individualistic reading of “keep moving.” V24 makes the grammar relational and transmissible.

### Reiner's suicide attempt and the remaining children

In the adult present, Reiner places a rifle in his mouth. The scene is not metaphorically ambiguous: the panel sequence depicts preparation for suicide.

He stops when he hears Falco outside and thinks of the candidate children: `俺にはまだ…あいつらが…`.

The modeling implication must be narrow. The children supply a relationship-based interruption to self-destruction. The scene does **not** establish that Reiner is cured, that he now wants to live generally, or that trauma has been resolved. It establishes that responsibility for younger people can still create a reason not to die in that moment.

This is a major expansion of Reiner's relational repertoire: obligation can operate as an anti-suicide tether even when self-directed meaning has collapsed.

### Eren's cover story and Falco's trust

Adult Eren appears in the veterans' hospital under a false presentation. He tells Falco that his memory-loss story is a lie and that he simply does not want to go home. Even this confession is nested inside a larger deception: Falco knows him as `クルーガーさん`, and Eren later misrepresents the purpose of his letters.

The volume therefore requires careful source discipline. Eren's speech cannot be globally classified as either “honest confession” or “lie.” Different layers carry different truth status.

When Falco says he is unlikely to inherit a Titan, Eren replies that this is good because Falco seems like a good person and he would be glad if Falco lived a long life. Falco explains that he wants to prevent a girl—clearly Gabi in context—from inheriting the Armor.

Eren immediately infers the relational structure before Falco states it directly. This is useful evidence against treating adult Eren as socially oblivious or emotionally unreadable merely because his current presentation is quiet.

### The theory of hell: self-authorship without moral purification

Falco says he lacks power and fears he will end without accomplishing anything. Eren responds with the volume's central agency formulation.

Looking at traumatized veterans, he says that if people knew what war would do to them—destroying body, mind, freedom, and even selfhood—no one would willingly enter the battlefield. Most people are pushed into hell by `他人や環境`, other people or environment.

Then he distinguishes another case:

`ただし 自分で自分の背中を押した奴の見る地獄は別だ`

Those who push their own backs see something beyond that hell. It may be hope. It may be another hell. Only the people who continue moving can know.

This passage **strengthens** the checkpoint's model of agency as authorship under constraint, but it also prevents a romantic version of that model. Self-authorship does not make the action good. It does not guarantee hope. It changes the relation between agent and uncertain future.

The strongest formulation at V24 is:

> **Authorship changes responsibility and epistemic ownership; it does not sanctify the destination.**

This distinction is vital for later Eren modeling. A simulator that equates “Eren chose it himself” with “Eren therefore believes it is morally right or hopeful” would already be unsupported at the V24 boundary.

### Falco becomes a courier

Eren asks Falco to send letters outside the internment-zone inspection system, claiming he merely wants to tell family that he is safe. Falco agrees.

The deception is calm, relational, and instrumental. Eren uses accurate knowledge of Falco's decency and trust while giving him a false account of the task's purpose.

This is not equivalent to sadism or indifference. Eren has just expressed concern that Falco avoid a shortened Titan life. The volume forces the same coexistence seen elsewhere in the series: **care can coexist with manipulation**.

## 3.4 Chapter 98 — 「よかったな」: relief from inheritance, the burden of steering, and a homecoming turned into confrontation

Chapter 98 moves among several layers—military planning, candidate rivalry, political theater, family guilt, international reception, festival comedy, and finally the basement encounter. Its formal achievement is to let ordinary life become maximally vivid immediately before political and personal collision.

### Reiner's current military competence and protective leak

Reiner contributes practical intelligence to renewed Paradis planning: Titan movement patterns, probable northern approaches, southern naval losses, and enemy attention. He is still an operational Warrior, not merely a traumatized observer.

Yet his resistance to Falco inheriting a Titan leaks into ordinary conversation. Watching Falco improve, Reiner remarks that Falco does not also need to become a Titan. The institution around him immediately reminds him that such a preference is not how Marley will decide succession.

His protective wish is therefore real but politically weak. V24 continues the V23 pattern in which Reiner sees the next generation's danger more clearly than he can openly dismantle the system producing it.

### Falco finally states the relational motive

Falco beats Gabi in a race. Gabi cannot understand why he is suddenly so determined, especially because his family already stands to benefit from Colt's planned Beast succession.

Falco finally blurts out: `お前のためだよ!!`

Gabi interprets this at the immediate competitive level—how can obstructing her be “for” her?—and does not understand the protective substitution. The scene is useful because affection is not automatically legible to the recipient. Falco's motive can be sincere and still produce conflict.

### Gabi integrates counterevidence instead of abandoning ideology

Udo explains that foreign hostility toward Eldians may be even worse than Marley's and that being sent back to an internment zone can itself be treated as national humiliation elsewhere.

Gabi does not immediately abandon the good-Eldian model. She instead incorporates the information: that makes the forthcoming gathering even more important, because they can show the world that they are not devils.

This is a strong model-revision constraint. Gabi is capable of receiving counterevidence, but early counterevidence is likely to be assimilated into the existing framework when that framework still offers a plausible rescue path.

### Willy and Magath: taking the helm inside a staged story

Willy's conversations with Magath openly use theater and house-repair metaphors. Willy jokes about discovering talent as an `演出家`; Magath describes the military as a decaying house that needs major demolition. The metaphors encode a real institutional purge/restructuring plan.

Willy then gives Magath command responsibility. Magath insists that the military belongs to the state, not personally to him. Willy replies that the country belongs to its people—Marleyans and Eldians—and describes himself as the person who has taken the steering wheel.

The steering wheel is heavy. He understands why predecessors avoided taking it and says he would like to let go, but believes the era no longer permits that.

This creates a deliberate parallel with Eren's self-pushed hell. Both passages distinguish inherited structure from chosen continuation. But neither figure is morally absolved merely because he chooses to take responsibility.

Willy also says he is not a uniquely destined hero; his turn happened to come around. That undermines a simple great-man reading even while the political scene visibly concentrates power in his hands.

Magath's remark that “rats” have already entered the house introduces an infiltration concern. At the V24 boundary, Eren is visibly one clandestine outsider, but the line must not be expanded with later information about every actor or operation.

### Eren and the Jaeger grandfather: guilt flows backward through generations

Eren meets the doctor who is, from the reader's established V21-V22 knowledge, his paternal grandfather, while continuing to use the name Kruger.

The grandfather recognizes that Falco is carrying messages for the apparent patient and warns that suspicious errands could endanger the Grice family. He explains that Falco and Colt's family carries political suspicion because of the earlier Restorationist connection and that Warrior service offers protection.

He then advises Eren to return to his family if his mind is healthy enough: regret becomes too late once reunion is impossible.

Eren asks whether he has regrets concerning family. The grandfather breaks down. He remembers the day Grisha left the Walls with Faye, blames his own severity and expectations, and collapses into `私だ…すべては私がぁぁ…`.

This scene extends the intergenerational guilt architecture. V21-V22 showed Grisha recognizing how he imposed political purpose on Zeke. V24 shows Grisha's father retrospectively blaming himself for pressuring Grisha. Each generation can look backward and find a way it treated the child as the carrier of an inherited expectation.

The pattern should not be reduced to “parents cause everything.” Children act, institutions act, and historical conditions matter. The significant point is that parental self-critique repeatedly arrives **after irreversible loss**.

### Kiyomi and Udo: one protective act does not define a state

At the reception Udo spills wine on Kiyomi Azumabito's kimono. She publicly claims responsibility and privately warns him that he does not know what might happen to him if the incident were blamed on an Eldian child.

This is strong evidence about **Kiyomi's action in this scene**. It is not yet sufficient evidence about Hizuru's complete policy toward Eldians. The method must preserve the scale of the claim.

The wider reception also complicates the idea of permanent international enemy categories. A foreign ambassador jokes that yesterday's enemies can be today's friends and toasts peace after states have recently fought over resources. International hostility is real but politically contingent.

### Willy's stage: extermination desire is acknowledged, not yet answered

Willy tells the assembled international audience that Paradis contains people of the same blood as him and recounts the cycle in which Marley, once oppressed by Titans, used Eldian Titans to oppress other countries.

He explicitly says he understands why people wish for the eradication of Eldians. He then announces that he has derived an answer to the apparently endless problem and will present it the next day on a stage where he himself will serve as first director.

At V24 the content of that answer is **not yet available**. It would violate the spoiler boundary to import it.

What V24 does establish is the form: history, political legitimacy, fear, and future strategy are being converted into public theater in front of invited witnesses. The audience is not incidental. Willy is constructing an event intended to make people participate in a shared interpretation.

### Festival: ordinary life immediately before the stage

The next morning Gabi wakes to music and discovers the internment zone transformed by a festival. The candidate children eat repeatedly, argue, tease, and enjoy foreign food. Reiner trails behind them paying vendors, visibly drained by the expense while the children remain delighted.

The sequence is important precisely because it is not a battlefield. It gives Gabi, Falco, Udo, Zofia, and Reiner strong ordinary-social controls:

- excitement;
- food enthusiasm;
- teasing;
- impulsive competition;
- shared laughter;
- adult caretaker fatigue;
- and the ability to experience a good day inside a coercive political world.

Gabi says she wishes there could be a festival every day and has the feeling something is changing. The observation is tragically open-ended at the V24 boundary. The volume ends before defining what the change will mean.

### `よかったな`: Falco delivers Reiner to Eren

At the theater, Falco tells Reiner he has found the acquaintance he mentioned and leads him away. Reiner is initially irritated at the interruption, then follows.

The route descends a narrow stairwell into a basement. The spatial rhyme is deliberate: a major truth encounter is again placed underground, but unlike Grisha's archive, this basement contains a living person from Reiner's buried past.

Eren is waiting.

Falco is cheerful because he believes he has helped two acquaintances reunite. Eren tells Reiner that it has been four years. Reiner can only say `…エレン`.

The final line is Eren's:

`よかったな`

`故郷に帰れて`

The phrase is devastating because Reiner's entire volume has been about failed and substituted homecoming. As a child, he wanted Warrior success to create a family home. After Marcel's death, mission success became the only path by which the Warriors could return home. In V23 he physically returned to Liberio but remained psychologically inhabited by Paradis. At the end of V24, the person who greets him as having returned to his hometown is Eren—the boy Reiner once encouraged to keep moving and destroy every Titan.

The volume ends before Reiner responds. That response belongs to Volume 25 and must remain outside this artifact.

# 4. The architecture of the lie

Volume 24's title architecture requires distinguishing kinds of untruth rather than collapsing them into one moral category.

## 4.1 Family fantasy

Karina's claim that Reiner's father is surely wishing for his success protects a child from immediate collapse while preserving the very Warrior fantasy that exposes him to danger. Protective affect and coercive reproduction coexist.

## 4.2 Selection fiction

The institution treats Reiner's selection as meritocratic proof. Marcel reveals that interpersonal manipulation helped produce the result. Reiner's later effort to justify being selected is therefore partly an attempt to convert a contingent outcome into deserved identity.

## 4.3 Dehumanizing self-enforcement

Reiner's statement that the 104th are not friends but island devils conflicts with the ordinary interactions the chapter itself depicts. The lie is psychologically functional: it helps keep mission and attachment from becoming impossible to hold simultaneously.

## 4.4 Operational cover

Eren's false identity, false memory-loss presentation, and false description of his letters are deliberate tools for clandestine action. They demonstrate social calibration rather than confusion.

## 4.5 Political theater

Willy does not conceal that he is staging a narrative. `物語`, `語り手`, `舞台`, and `演出家` become explicit political vocabulary. The ethical question therefore becomes not whether politics has narrative form, but how evidence, fear, audience, and coercion are arranged within that form.

The series' epistemic method remains consistent: **a story can be strategically necessary without becoming true merely because it is persuasive.**

# 5. Reiner: the strongest temporal reconstruction yet

Volume 24 is the largest Reiner reconstruction artifact in the corpus so far because it supplies not one new “truth” about him but a causal sequence connecting multiple states.

## 5.1 Child motive: belonging before ideology

The initial Warrior aspiration is inseparable from family. Honorary Marleyan status is imagined as a technology of reunion. Ethnic redemption and world-hero rhetoric later attach to that desire.

## 5.2 Paternal rejection: the first promised future collapses

The father does not merely fail to recognize Reiner; he treats recognition itself as danger. The child loses the imagined family future before the mission starts.

## 5.3 Marcel's confession: chosen status becomes borrowed status

Reiner learns that selection was manipulated and then survives because Marcel protects him. This creates debt, survivor guilt, legitimacy anxiety, and a literal vacancy in the team.

## 5.4 `俺がマルセルになる`: role substitution

Reiner responds by trying to become what the team needs. The statement is evidence for role fracture, but not proof that later roles are unreal. It establishes the mechanism by which he handles unbearable incompatibility: construct a role capable of carrying the next task.

## 5.5 Mission as return path

`帰れない` is foundational. Continuing the mission is initially the only route that protects him from immediate punishment/death and still offers return home.

## 5.6 Mission as moral meaning

Later he upgrades survival necessity into world-saving purpose. This is neither purely cynical nor objectively validated. Reiner is capable of sincerely believing a meaning he has helped construct because he needs a reason for the role to remain livable.

## 5.7 Soldier Reiner as emergent reality

Ordinary 104th life develops inside the infiltration role. He mediates, jokes, teaches, encourages, and forms recognizable relational habits. These behaviors cannot be erased by saying the initial role was instrumental.

## 5.8 Adult collapse and candidate tether

By adulthood Reiner understands much of the ideological failure and is suicidal. The candidate children interrupt that collapse. Responsibility for the future of people like Falco and Gabi gives him a reason to remain alive that is distinct from the old “save the world” script.

## 5.9 The final basement

Eren represents several of Reiner's histories at once:

- island enemy;
- former comrade;
- student of his encouragement;
- victim of the original Wall breach;
- and now clandestine adult actor inside Reiner's hometown.

The final encounter therefore places Reiner before the accumulated consequences of roles he can no longer keep spatially separated.

# 6. Eren: self-authorship, relational perception, and instrumental trust

V24 is the first large post-timeskip behavioral tranche for Eren after the V22 ocean hinge. It should be used carefully because Eren is actively clandestine and deceptive.

## 6.1 Adult quiet does not mean low agency

His low-volume hospital register contrasts sharply with earlier shouted categorical speech, but the difference is state-conditioned. He is hiding in hostile territory. The calmness is operationally appropriate, not evidence that the earlier Eren was false.

## 6.2 He reads Falco relationally

Eren infers that the superior Warrior candidate Falco wants to save is a girl. He responds to Falco's embarrassment without ridicule and tells him that living longer would be a good outcome.

## 6.3 He can care and manipulate simultaneously

The same Eren who expresses concern for Falco's life recruits him as an unwitting courier. This should enter the model as a coexistence rule, not be forced into “care was fake” or “manipulation was harmless.”

## 6.4 `自分で自分の背中を押す`

The hell speech is Eren's most explicit V24 theory of agency. It distinguishes:

- externally imposed action;
- self-authored action;
- and the unknowability of what lies beyond continued action.

Crucially, hope and deeper hell are both possible outputs.

This **STRENGTHENS** the checkpoint's self-authorship framework while **REVISING** any tendency to treat agency as synonymous with emancipation.

## 6.5 Reiner's language survives inside Eren's later grammar

The trainee flashback establishes that Reiner once told Eren to do what must be done, keep going, and trust that he could exterminate the Titans. Adult Eren's `進み続ける` language therefore participates in a social history.

That does not reduce Eren to Reiner's influence. The drive existed before the encouragement. The stronger claim is relational: **Eren's native persistence was recognized, reinforced, and given language by a person who later became one of its targets.**

# 7. Annie: anti-euphemistic survival without innocence

Volume 24 substantially improves Annie's historical model.

She repeatedly distinguishes the group's institutional language from its practical interests. Her rejection of `名誉マーレ人` and `選ばれし戦士` exposes the self-serving structure beneath the rhetoric. Yet she explicitly includes herself among people acting for themselves.

This gives Annie a characteristic epistemic posture:

- suspicious of heroic language;
- attentive to survival incentives;
- impatient with inefficient mission drift;
- willing to name self-interest;
- and still capable of severe violence and coercion.

She should therefore not be modeled as either detached nihilist or secretly pure realist. Her relative honesty about motive coexists with brutality.

The later infiltration discussion also demonstrates practical flexibility. Annie proposes routes through domestic/service relationships and repeatedly questions whether Reiner's plan justifies the time cost. She is not simply the team's fighter; she is an active critic of operational assumptions.

# 8. Falco and Gabi: two ways of inheriting a future

V24 deepens the divergence introduced in V23.

Gabi still treats Titan inheritance as achievement, community redemption, and proof. Falco increasingly treats the same inheritance as a death sentence he wants to absorb instead of her.

The difference is not yet a complete ideological opposition. Falco remains inside the Warrior institution. He does not present a developed theory of abolishing the system. His action is relationship-first: if someone must inherit, he wants it to be him rather than Gabi.

Gabi, meanwhile, receives difficult information about foreign hostility and absorbs it into the good-Eldian framework instead of abandoning that framework. This is realistic model behavior: people do not necessarily discard a worldview after one counterexample, especially when the worldview contains an explicit explanation for why more proof is needed.

Their low-stakes scenes are equally important. Gabi boasts, misreads Falco's motives, jokes, gets excited about food, and enjoys the festival. Falco can be earnest, embarrassed, competitive, and proud of progress. Neither should be simulated as an ideology-delivery device outside crisis.

# 9. Willy Tybur and Magath: narrative power and reluctant political authorship

Willy enters V24 as a politically powerful figure who refuses the fiction that his family is merely symbolic. He acknowledges that the Tyburs allowed Marley to imprison Eldians and pursue imperial expansion while they watched.

This is an important responsibility distinction:

> **non-intervention by a powerful actor can itself become authorship of the resulting structure.**

Willy then chooses to take the helm. His language is not triumphalist. The steering wheel is heavy; he would rather let go; he considers himself someone whose turn happened to arrive.

That does not reduce his power. It shows how the manga separates subjective burden from objective authority.

Magath supplies a complementary institutional realism. He describes a country whose citizens experience expansion as text in newspapers while Eldians absorb battlefield risk. His proposal to restore Marleyan conscription is designed partly to make Marleyans encounter the cost of their own wars rather than outsourcing it to racialized bodies.

The Helos statue crystallizes their political critique. Willy praises the heroic image; Magath points out that the bronze figure is hollow. The visual does not merely say “heroes are fake.” It says that political communities can organize themselves around an empty representation whose efficacy does not depend on literal substance.

The later stage project therefore has a dangerous duality. Willy may be trying to assume responsibility his family evaded, but he does so by becoming a narrator/director before a mass audience. Responsibility and manipulation are not mutually exclusive.

# 10. Intergenerational guilt and the child as carrier

Across V21-V24 the Jaeger family increasingly demonstrates how adults turn children into carriers of unresolved projects.

- Grisha's father pressures Grisha toward respectable medical succession and obedience.
- Grisha later pressures Zeke toward Eldian restoration.
- each father later looks backward and blames himself after losing the child relationally.

Reiner's family adds a parallel but distinct mechanism:

- Karina invests Reiner with the dream of family reunification and ethnic redemption;
- Reiner's father refuses the relationship;
- Reiner converts the failed family task into a world-hero task.

The Warrior candidates reproduce the pattern institutionally. Children are asked to carry military strategy, family privilege, ethnic respectability, and national survival inside bodies with fixed expiration dates.

V24's recurring question is therefore not merely “what do parents teach children?” It is:

> **At what point does an inherited project become the child's own, and what responsibility follows when the child continues it after learning what produced it?**

The volume does not give one answer. Reiner continues. Annie resists the rhetoric but continues for survival. Falco redirects the inheritance toward protective substitution. Eren distinguishes imposed and self-imposed movement. Willy assumes an inherited political burden while staging a new story around it.

# 11. Ordinary-life and social-control expansion

V24 is unusually valuable for reconstruction because it repeatedly moves away from combat.

## 11.1 Trainee meal life

Young Reiner is capable of ordinary mediation and humor around Eren and Jean. This strengthens the reality of soldier Reiner as a social state rather than a pure mission mask.

## 11.2 Candidate rivalry

Falco and Gabi race, argue, misunderstand each other's motives, and interact under peer observation. The relationship has ordinary embarrassment and status friction independent of battlefield danger.

## 11.3 Festival food

Gabi, Udo, Zofia, and Falco become visibly childlike in the festival sequence: ice cream, street food, repeated purchases, jokes, excitement. Reiner functions as the increasingly exhausted adult paying behind them.

## 11.4 Willy's family

Willy's introduction of his family gives him a domestic/social surface distinct from political theater, although the evidence remains narrow.

## 11.5 Jaeger grandfather's tea habit

The grandfather says he sometimes comes to the hospital for tea. This is small but legitimate ordinary-life evidence. His later breakdown should not erase the mundane baseline that precedes it.

and modestly adds social texture for Willy and the Jaeger grandfather.

It still does **not** establish broad hobby, romance, household-routine, or civilian-life models for most major Paradis characters.

# 13. Relationship-conditioned analysis

## 13.1 Marcel -> Reiner: protection creates the role it tries to prevent for Porco

Marcel's manipulation saves Porco from inheritance but places Reiner in the Armor position; his later physical protection saves Reiner again at the cost of Marcel's own life. Reiner inherits both position and debt.

## 13.2 Reiner -> Annie: mission need turns fear into coercive leadership

Reiner's post-Marcel leadership is not stable confidence. He uses physical force, guilt, collective consequence, and the absence of a return path to keep Annie inside the mission.

## 13.3 Reiner -> Bertolt: entrustment survives the constructed role

`頼んだぞ相棒 / 任せろ` gives the pair an ordinary familiar trust register inside mission planning. The relation is not reducible to Reiner issuing orders.

## 13.4 Reiner -> Eren: mentor speech becomes historical irony

Reiner tells Eren he can accomplish the extermination goal and supplies a model of doing what must be done. Adult Eren returns as the person who has continued moving.

## 13.5 Reiner -> Falco: responsibility interrupts self-destruction

Falco is part of the candidate-child group that gives Reiner a reason not to pull the trigger. Reiner later tries to steer him toward inheriting the Armor only as a means of saving Gabi from it—protection through another burden.

## 13.6 Falco -> Gabi: substitution finally becomes explicit

Falco says the competition is for her sake. Gabi cannot yet decode protection as opposition to her own stated ambition.

## 13.7 Eren -> Falco: trust is both valued and used

Eren encourages Falco, recognizes his care for Gabi, wishes him a long life, and recruits him into clandestine correspondence under false pretenses. The relationship therefore must be modeled as mixed rather than assigned a single moral label.

## 13.8 Willy -> Magath: power is delegated through shared critique

Willy chooses Magath because Magath sees the country's structural problem clearly enough to criticize it. Their alliance is not egalitarian—the Tybur family can confer enormous authority—but Magath resists personal ownership language and insists on institutional framing.

## 13.9 Jaeger grandfather -> Eren: unknowingly advising his grandson

The grandfather advises “Kruger” not to leave family regret unresolved, unaware that he is speaking to the grandson produced by the family rupture he mourns. The dramatic irony does not make the advice false; it intensifies its intergenerational reach.

# 15. Longitudinal claim transitions from V23

## `STRENGTHEN` — institutions reproduce obsolete solutions through children

V23 established that Marley knows Titan supremacy is declining while training new inheritors. V24 shows the political response: seek Founder/resources and produce a legitimating story rather than dismantle the underlying Warrior inheritance system.

## `STRENGTHEN` — Reiner's states accumulate rather than replace one another

V24 provides the causal backfill: childhood family hope, manipulated selection, Marcel substitution, survival continuation, heroic mission meaning, ordinary soldier life, dehumanizing self-enforcement, adult collapse, and candidate protection all coexist in one history.

Marcel's confession shows that selection was influenced by sibling protection and impression management. Reiner's later identity is partly a response to discovering he did not earn the role in the way he believed.

Reiner can live socially among the 104th and still call them devils to preserve mission actionability.

Eren's persistence predates Reiner's encouragement, but V24 establishes a relational transmission channel: Reiner validates the language and later adult Eren theorizes continued movement for Falco.

Eren's distinction between externally pushed and self-pushed hell directly strengthens the checkpoint's architecture.

Only the staging and premise are available. The actual answer remains outside V24.

# 16. Visual and formal analysis

## 16.1 The closed paternal door

The literal door shutting on young Reiner externalizes the collapse of his first hoped-for future. The title 「希望の扉」 later becomes deeply ironic: the family door is closed, so the mission becomes the substitute door.

## 16.2 Marcel's confession immediately before death

The sequencing denies Reiner time to metabolize the revelation. Knowledge and loss arrive as one causal unit. The borrowed role becomes inseparable from the person who dies protecting him.

## 16.3 Campfire circles and role manufacture

Warrior planning scenes repeatedly use enclosed group composition. Children sit inside a small social world constructing decisions about an enormous population they know only through doctrine and partial intelligence.

## 16.4 The rifle barrel

Reiner's suicide scene reduces the frame to body, weapon, finger, and sound outside. The candidate children enter not through an abstract speech but through an interruption in the acoustic environment.

## 16.5 Hospital bench / baseball object

Eren's long conversations occur seated, visually low-energy, while objects are passed through the correspondence system. The baseball equipment functions at minimum as a received object linked to an unseen contact; its sender and full meaning remain open at V24.

## 16.6 Helos as a hollow statue

The heroic figure is materially empty. The visual makes Magath's critique literal without proving that symbolic heroes have no political power; in fact, the entire volume shows that hollow stories can be operationally powerful.

## 16.7 Stage, audience, witnesses

Willy's political project is visually built as theater before its content is delivered. Seats, curtains, foreign dignitaries, banners, and rehearsal convert policy into a spectacle of witnessing.

## 16.8 Festival abundance before descent

The bright crowded festival gives the candidate children one of the corpus's richest sequences of ordinary pleasure. Immediately afterward Falco leads Reiner away from the crowd and down into a dark basement.

## 16.9 The stairway

The descent stretches over multiple panels with Reiner following a child who believes he is doing a favor. Spatially, knowledge and danger lie below the public stage. The same volume thus contains two theaters: the official stage above and Eren's private encounter below.

## 16.10 Final split faces

The last page divides Eren and Reiner into adjacent vertical panels. Eren's `よかったな / 故郷に帰れて` crosses the history between them while Reiner can only answer with Eren's name. The composition suspends response until the next volume.

# 19. Open questions at the Volume 24 boundary

1. **What exactly is Willy Tybur's promised “answer” to the Eldian/Titan problem?** V24 supplies the staging, not the content.
2. **What is Eren's full objective in Liberio?** His infiltration and correspondence are established; his complete plan is not.
3. **Who is corresponding with Eren and what is the full meaning of the baseball equipment?** The object is present; the sender and operational significance remain unresolved.
4. **How will Reiner respond to Eren?** The volume ends before the answer.
5. **Does Eren's self-pushed-hell philosophy describe his current choice, a general theory, a message to Falco, or all three?** The speech supports several levels but does not disclose the whole present plan.
6. **Can Willy's assumption of responsibility alter the state without reproducing narrative coercion?** His intentions and method coexist; outcome remains open.
7. **Will Falco's protective substitution actually prevent Gabi from inheriting the Armor?** His motive is explicit; institutional control remains elsewhere.
8. **Will Gabi's good-Eldian model survive further contradictory evidence?** V24 shows assimilation of counterevidence, not final stability.
9. **What is Zeke's relationship to the current Paradis plan beyond the public military rationale already established?** Earlier private-motive uncertainty remains open.
10. **How much of Reiner's present survival is sustained by candidate responsibility?** The suicide attempt is interrupted once; broader stability is not established.
11. **Why did the hanged village survivor tell the Warriors his story?** Bertolt's recurring question remains unanswered.
12. **What is the practical extent of foreign sympathy toward individual Eldians versus state-level hostility?** Kiyomi's act and international friendships cannot be generalized beyond their evidence.
13. **Who are the “rats” Magath believes have entered Marley?** Eren is visibly clandestine, but the category may exceed what V24 establishes.
14. **Can a role built from a lie become ethically redeemable through later self-authorship, or does continuation deepen responsibility?** V24 sharpens but does not settle this philosophical problem.

# 20. Volume 24 synthesis

Volume 24 is the series' most sustained analysis so far of what happens **after a person discovers the origin story of their role is compromised**.

Reiner is the central case. He did not become a Warrior because merit transparently selected him. Marcel manipulated the situation. He did not become a Warrior to pursue abstract empire. He wanted a family. The father he imagined refused him. Marcel then died protecting him. Reiner could not go home safely, could not justify survival cleanly, and could not tolerate the empty place Marcel left. His response was to build another self: `俺がマルセルになる`.

That construction did not prevent the later soldier role from becoming real. Volume 24 deliberately shows Reiner eating, mediating, advising, and living among the 104th while also meeting Annie and Bertolt at night to continue the mission. When he calls those peers devils, the category functions less as ignorance than as **active maintenance of a mission boundary threatened by experience**.

The result is not absolution. Reiner continues to help produce catastrophic harm. But the volume makes simple hypocrisy inadequate as a model. He survives by repeatedly constructing roles capable of carrying obligations that his existing self cannot integrate.

Eren emerges as the volume's second major case because he theorizes that distinction explicitly. Most people enter hell because others or the environment push them. Some push their own backs. Only the latter can discover what lies beyond—and the outcome may be hope or another hell.

The speech brings the first-half self-authorship architecture into unusually explicit language while adding a severe correction: **agency is not salvation**.

That insight also reframes the recurring `進み続ける` grammar. Reiner once told Eren to do what had to be done and affirmed that Eren could fulfill his extermination goal. Adult Eren now tells Falco that only those who keep moving can know what lies beyond their chosen hell. Falco receives the encouragement, carries Eren's letters, and eventually brings Reiner to him.

The “hand-to-hand” architecture is therefore causal and ethical. People transmit more than information. They transmit ways of making action feel possible.

Willy Tybur provides the political-scale version of the same structure. His family inherited power and avoided steering. He decides to grasp the wheel, accepts responsibility for earlier non-intervention, and prepares to give the world a staged political story. Like Eren's self-authored hell, the act of choosing responsibility cannot itself determine whether the next destination is hope or deeper catastrophe.

The final `よかったな / 故郷に帰れて` is consequently not merely a cliffhanger taunt. It closes the volume's argument about home, role, and transmission. Reiner has spent his life trying to get home through borrowed scripts. Eren—the person Reiner helped shape and harm—now greets him there.

At the V24 boundary, the next question is not yet what Eren will do. It is more basic:

> **What does responsibility mean when the person standing before you is living evidence that your inherited role became someone else's chosen continuation?**

Volume 25 may answer the immediate encounter. Volume 24 ends precisely before that answer can be known.
