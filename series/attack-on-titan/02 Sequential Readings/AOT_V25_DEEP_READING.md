# 『進撃の巨人』Volume 25 Deep Reading
## Recognition destroys innocence, not conflict: confession below the stage, declaration above it, and the conversion of understanding into war

## 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 24 ended by placing Eren and Reiner face-to-face beneath a public festival at the exact moment Willy Tybur prepared to give the world a politically decisive story. Volume 25 makes those two spaces move in parallel.

Above ground, Willy reconstructs the public history of Marley, Eldia, Helos, Karl Fritz, the Founding Titan, and Paradis. He admits that the inherited heroic story is false or radically incomplete, confesses Tybur complicity, identifies Eren Jaeger as the present danger, and asks an international audience to join him in war.

Below ground, Eren reconstructs the private history of his own hatred. He forces Reiner to revisit the breach of Wall Maria, Carla's death, the Warrior children's ignorance, and the institutional environment that produced them. He then tells Reiner that crossing the sea destroyed the simple category in which everything outside the Walls looked like an enemy. He lived under the same roof as those people and ate the same food. Some were hateful; some were good. `海の外も壁の中も同じなんだ`.

Neither reconstruction produces peace.

That is the volume's decisive proposition:

> **Recognition can destroy the innocence of an enemy category without destroying the conflict that category organized.**

Eren now understands Reiner more accurately than he did at the reveal in Volume 11. Reiner likewise refuses an absolving account in which propaganda and childhood coercion erase his authorship. The two arrive at a more complete picture of one another precisely before Eren transforms and kills.

The volume therefore refuses two symmetrical simplifications.

The first is:

> if people truly understand each other, they will stop fighting.

Volume 25 answers: not necessarily. Understanding can make violence more morally conscious without making objectives compatible.

The second is:

> if structures causally explain a perpetrator, personal responsibility disappears.

Reiner answers this himself. Eren offers him the strongest contextual mitigation the manga has yet given a former enemy: child indoctrination, ignorance, historical environment, and unbearable mission pressure. Reiner still insists that these facts do not exhaust the moral account. He chose to force the Paradis mission onward after Marcel's death because he wanted to become a hero. His confession is an insistence that causal explanation and responsibility can coexist.

Willy's public speech creates the corresponding political problem. He dismantles one false story in order to build a new coalition-producing story. Some of what he says is supported by Tybur family memory and by evidence already available in the corpus; some remains interested historical testimony; some is a strategic threat model. But the rhetorical function is clear. Political narrative is not merely description. Once an audience accepts a narrated danger as a shared danger, it can authorize coordinated violence.

This is why Chapter 100's title, 「宣戦布告」, is so exact. A declaration of war is a speech act: words change the political state of the world when spoken under the right conditions. Isayama then literalizes that principle. As Willy speaks the declaration, Eren's transformation converts political language into physical destruction. The stage and basement, public story and private recognition, speech and bodily force become one event.

The volume's second thesis follows:

> **Narrative recognition can itself become operational infrastructure for war. A truer story does not automatically produce a more peaceful politics; it may instead produce a more persuasive enemy coalition.**

And a third thesis emerges from the ending:

> **The old human/Titan moral geometry has collapsed. By Chapter 102, Titan bodies are military platforms inside a war between organized human systems, and even the Warrior Titans can experience humans as the terrifying attackers.**

Porco's shock—`俺は巨人だぞ!? / こいつら人間の姿のまま俺を殺す気か!?`—reverses the affective structure of the earliest volumes. Titanhood no longer guarantees that the person inside the Titan occupies the position of monster and humans the position of helpless prey.

The volume ends on opposed imperatives. Zeke orders `逃がすな / 殲滅しろ`. Eren's side is framed by `死ぬな / 生き延びろ`. Extermination and survival are no longer cleanly allocated to “monster” and “human.” They are rival military grammars spoken by people who now understand much more about one another than the children of Volume 1 did.

# 3. Narrative architecture and causal close read

## 3.1 Chapter 99 — 「疾しき影」: the visible festival is already a trap

Chapter 99 inherits V24's theatrical structure but changes its tone. The festival is no longer merely an interval of ordinary abundance before political speech. Suspicion, surveillance, concealment, and prearranged sacrifice run underneath it.

### Falco completes the handoff chain

Falco has already carried Eren's letters and brought Reiner into the basement. What looked from Falco's perspective like aid to an injured veteran is now revealed as participation in an enemy operation. His trust does not become retrospectively foolish; it becomes evidence of the asymmetry Eren exploited.

This matters because V24's `手から手へ` structure now acquires a cost. Passing something from hand to hand is not neutral. The recipient may not know what the object, message, or person will do once delivered.

### Pieck converts suspicion into redundancy

Pieck's response to the suspicious soldier is diagnostically different from panic. She notices the anomaly, does not immediately resolve it into certainty, and creates a backup path by communicating with the Panzer unit. When she and Porco are dropped into a narrow shaft designed to prevent safe transformation, her earlier precaution becomes strategically decisive.

Her behavior adds a useful character-model rule: **Pieck's relaxed social presentation is compatible with high contingency awareness.** Familiarity and humor should not be mistaken for inattentiveness.

### Willy and Magath make sacrifice part of the political plan

The most disturbing material in the chapter is the Willy/Magath conversation. Willy does not merely suspect the festival may be attacked. The Tybur-Magath operation needs the enemy to reveal itself before international witnesses, and the military leadership concentrated at the event is politically expendable enough to be included in the bait.

Magath forces the human cost into the conversation. Many of the people who will die are Eldians. Willy's answer does not deny it. The exchange reaches the reciprocal accusation that exposes Marley's moral position: Magath can call Eldians descendants of devils, but he has also ordered Eldians across minefields as disposable weapons. He concludes that `私達は悪魔に違いない`.

This line should not be overread as a complete conversion or repudiation of Marley. Its function is narrower and more important: **the devil category folds back onto the system that deploys it**. The institution cannot preserve moral innocence simply by assigning inherited evil to its subordinated ethnic population.

Willy also knows that his own death may be required. He uses himself, the gathered leadership, and the Liberio population as material inside a political proof.

The ethical structure differs from Erwin's charge in V20. Erwin speaks to soldiers whom he explicitly asks to participate in a lethal military operation. Willy's plan includes civilians and political guests who are not fully informed participants in the sacrificial design. Both cases involve political/military authors converting foreseeable death into future utility; their consent structures are not the same.

## 3.2 Chapter 100 — 「宣戦布告」: two historical reconstructions converge on one act

Chapter 100 is one of the densest formal constructions in the analyzed corpus because the stage and basement do not simply alternate. They comment on one another.

### Willy revises the Helos story but remains an interested narrator

Willy tells the audience that the inherited legend in which Helos and the Tybur family defeated the Eldian Empire is not the complete truth. In his account, Karl Fritz deliberately ended the Great Titan War, colluded with the Tyburs, created the Helos fiction, withdrew to Paradis, built the Walls, and imposed a vow renouncing war on royal inheritors of the Founding Titan.

This cannot simply be promoted to omniscient historical truth because Willy is:

- a Tybur heir;
- speaking from inherited family memory;
- delivering a political performance;
- constructing a coalition for a current strategic purpose.

At the same time, it is not analytically honest to dismiss the speech merely because it is strategic. The project should preserve multiple layers:

1. **materially established prior facts** — humanity outside the Walls, Marley, Eldian Subjects of Ymir, Titan inheritance, royal-blood mechanisms as partially understood;
2. **Tybur-family historical testimony** — Karl Fritz / Tybur / Helos reconstruction;
3. **current threat inference** — Eren's possession of the Founder and the possibility of Wall-Titan catastrophe;
4. **rhetorical framing** — converting that threat into a common international enemy.

The speech is most important not because it settles every ancient-history question, but because it shows how political action is built from a mixture of evidence, memory, inference, and staged narration.

### Eren reveals the letters and the limit of Falco's consent

Below ground, Falco realizes the hospital patient has deceived him. Eren apologizes and acknowledges that Falco helped him. He also tells him the letters were not actually going to family; they reached comrades.

This is another clear example of Eren's V24 rule: **concern and exploitation can coexist**. He does not need to despise Falco in order to use him. Moral recognition does not imply informational equality.

### Eren and Reiner reconstruct Wall Maria

Eren returns Reiner to the origin of their conflict: the breach, the deaths, and Carla.

At first Eren offers a severe but recognizable contextual account. Reiner and the other Warriors were children. They were told the island people were devils. They were ignorant. Adults and history pushed them into the mission. Eren can now say that he understands Reiner's suffering.

That movement is a major revision from early enemy ontology. The boy who once wanted to destroy every Titan can now causally reconstruct how a person became the enemy who destroyed his home.

But Reiner refuses to let that causal explanation become an acquittal.

### Reiner: causal explanation does not erase authorship

Reiner insists that after Marcel's death Annie and Bertolt wanted to abandon the mission. Reiner forced them onward. He wanted to be a hero. He wanted recognition.

The confession culminates in:

`時代や環境のせいじゃなくて…俺が悪いんだよ`

and then:

`お前の母親が巨人に食われたのは俺のせいだ!!`

followed by the desire to be killed and disappear.

The scene is important precisely because neither Eren nor Reiner is wholly sufficient as the moral interpreter.

Eren is right that Reiner was a coerced child inside an ideological institution.

Reiner is right that this did not remove every decision point.

The manga preserves both levels. Structural causation and personal authorship are not competitors where one must eliminate the other.

### `海の外も壁の中も同じなんだ`

Eren's own counter-history is experiential rather than archival. He says that before crossing the sea everything outside the Walls looked like an enemy. Then he slept under the same roof and ate the same food as them. There were infuriating people and good people.

This is among the clearest collapses of the enemy-category ontology in the series.

But Eren does not conclude:

> therefore there is no enemy.

He concludes that the people on both sides are the same kind of human material.

That distinction is load-bearing. **De-essentializing the enemy does not eliminate objective conflict.**

### Willy's desire to live

Willy's rhetoric is also more contradictory than a simple extermination speech. He admits that he once hated his own Eldian blood and wished for Eldian disappearance. Yet on stage he also says he does not want to die. The desire to live becomes the common denominator through which he appeals across national and racial differences.

He asks those who do not want to die to join hands and live the future together.

Then he defines the Paradis enemy against which that future should be defended.

The structure is politically powerful because universal life-desire and enemy construction occur in the same speech. The universal does not eliminate exclusion; it becomes the emotional basis for a new coalition.

### `やっぱりオレは…お前と同じだ`

As Willy asks different peoples to unite, Eren offers Reiner his hand.

`やっぱりオレは…お前と同じだ`

This should not be flattened into “Eren forgives Reiner,” “Eren says both sides are morally identical,” or “Eren admits he is evil in exactly the same way.”

The strongest V25 reading is procedural and existential:

- both crossed an enemy boundary;
- both acted within inherited conflict;
- both learned the people on the other side were ordinary human beings;
- both nevertheless continued into violence;
- both can claim structural pressure without becoming non-agents.

The symmetry is real. It is not total equivalence.

### `生まれた時から` and `進み続ける`

Eren follows with:

`多分…生まれた時からこうなんだ`

`オレは進み続ける`

`敵を駆逐するまで`

This is a major self-interpretive statement but should remain exactly that: **Eren's current self-understanding**.

It strongly revises any model in which current Eren believes he became this person only because of Marley, Reiner, Grisha, or recent inherited memories. He identifies continuity reaching back to birth.

It does not by itself establish:

- a biological essence;
- metaphysical predestination;
- Titan programming;
- perfect continuity of motive;
- or that every later action was psychologically predetermined in Volume 1.

The V24 finding still stands: Reiner helped reinforce a persistence grammar that already existed. V25 strengthens the preexisting-drive side of that formulation.

### Declaration and transformation

Willy begins the formal declaration. Eren begins transforming.

The page architecture synchronizes them so that neither can be read as merely commenting on the other. The speech act creates a state of war politically; the transformation creates it bodily and visually.

Eren's attack kills Willy and inflicts mass civilian casualties at the exact moment the stage has succeeded in positioning Paradis as the common threat.

That does not prove Eren caused the entire diplomatic coalition. Willy had already constructed the argument and planned around an expected attack.

Nor does Willy's plan make the civilians' deaths morally unreal.

The scene is built so that strategic predictability, political exploitation, enemy agency, and civilian victimization all remain simultaneously true.

## 3.3 Chapter 101 — 「戦鎚の巨人」: the staged victim becomes a combat system

The declaration immediately gives way to the War Hammer Titan.

### The Tybur family converts political privilege into military power

The War Hammer bearer transforms only after Willy's death. The family that narrated the political order also possesses a Titan with extraordinary material capabilities.

The War Hammer can externalize hardened structures into weapons. The power is therefore formally appropriate to the Tybur role: it gives shape to instruments from an apparently fluid substrate, just as Willy gave political form to a coalition from dispersed fear.

This symbolic correspondence should not replace mechanism. The ability is literal military technology inside the story before it is a metaphor.

### Eren fights as a model-builder, not only as force

Eren initially confronts a Titan whose powers he does not understand. The battle gradually becomes an information problem.

He observes the created weapons, the regeneration behavior, and eventually an anomaly in how the War Hammer's body formed.

This is important for character modeling because current Eren is not merely “more ruthless.” He is also more disciplined as a battlefield observer. The volume later makes the reasoning explicit in Chapter 102.

### The Scouts return in the enemy's city

The arrival of Mikasa and the Scouts reverses the visual language of the earlier series. ODM-equipped humans now descend into a modern city whose defenders experience them as the attacking irregular force.

Mikasa's first major relational action is not triumphant approval. She asks Eren to come back.

The plea preserves attachment while already suggesting that Eren's action cannot be treated as automatically identical with the group's moral judgment.

## 3.4 Chapter 102 — 「後の祭り」: after the festival, everyone inherits the cost

The title 「後の祭り」 carries the idiomatic sense of being too late—after the festival, after the point when something can be undone. The literal festival has ended. The political and moral consequences are now irreversible.

### Magath reads the attack as political proof

Magath seals the internment zone and orders that the Founding Titan and the Paradis force not escape.

His reasoning is not simply “they attacked us, therefore retaliate.” He understands that the attack has made Willy's stage argument materially vivid before the world's representatives. Participating governments now have direct casualties and witnesses. The political cost of tolerating Paradis has changed.

This is another reason the volume cannot be reduced to “Willy lied and Eren proved him right.” Willy expected violence and used that expectation strategically. Eren still chose to supply the violence. Planned exploitation does not erase the agency of the actor being exploited.

### Jean and Floch split over retaliatory arithmetic

Jean sees fires spreading through the internment zone and orders civilian harm minimized:

`民間への被害は最小限に抑えろ!!`

Floch answers from the memory of Wall-side victimization. People inside the Walls were eaten and killed because of people outside. From his perspective, the current destruction is not yet enough.

This is the clearest emerging internal political split in the Paradis force at the V25 boundary.

Jean's position is not nonviolence. He is participating in the attack. His distinction is between military operation and unrestricted retaliatory transfer onto civilians.

Floch's position is not random sadism in the textual evidence available here. It is retaliatory collective accounting: previous civilian suffering on one side licenses or at least reduces concern for civilian suffering on the other.

The disagreement therefore concerns **where responsibility may be transferred**.

That issue is likely checkpoint-relevant at V27 and should be tracked explicitly.

### Mikasa becomes the internal moral countervoice

Mikasa confronts Eren in unusually direct moral language:

`あなたは…民間人を殺した`

`子供も…殺した`

`もう…取り返しがつかない…`

This is one of the most important constraints against a caricature of Mikasa as an unconditional ratifier of Eren.

She still fights beside him. She still protects the operational position. She still asks him to return.

But attachment does not prevent moral judgment.

This yields a strong relationship rule:

> **Mikasa can remain behaviorally aligned with Eren in immediate survival/mission terms while explicitly condemning what he has done.**

Loyalty, protection, and moral approval are not the same variable.

### Gabi's grief becomes self-authored return to battle

Gabi experiences the festival's destruction through the deaths of people she knows. Colt orders her to stay with family while he searches for Falco. Gabi refuses the protected-child role and returns toward the fighting because she does not understand why Udo and Zofia were killed.

Her action is important because it complicates any model in which ideology alone causes her militancy. V25 adds direct grief and witnessed violence. The institutional script remains, but experience now supplies new personal fuel.

This is the same structural problem the series has repeatedly applied elsewhere: inherited conflict becomes self-sustaining when later violence creates fresh first-person reasons to continue it.

### Sasha sees Gabi and does not fire

A Scout kills Marleyan gate guards from above. The next visual sequence makes Sasha and Gabi see one another.

Sasha does not shoot the unarmed child. Connie then calls her away after the route is closed, and Gabi picks up a fallen rifle.

The scene should not be inflated into a universal Sasha pacifism rule. It is stronger as a negative constraint: **enemy location and ethnic/political membership do not automatically make an unarmed child a shoot target for Sasha**.

That matters precisely because the operation is otherwise lethal.

### Jean: survival before interpretation

Jean later says:

`この戦いの先に何があるのか`

`それを見極めるためには…生き残らねぇと`

This preserves a longstanding Jean structure. He can act decisively without pretending to possess a finished account of what the action means strategically or morally.

Survival is epistemic: they have to live long enough to determine what follows.

### Eren identifies the War Hammer anomaly

Eren notices the detail that bothered him. When the War Hammer appeared, the Titan body built upward from the feet rather than presenting the ordinary emergence pattern centered on a human in the nape.

He follows the cable into the ground and finds the actual bearer enclosed in crystal beneath the battlefield.

This is high-value evidence against an Eren model built only from categorical will. His combat cognition now includes:

- anomaly detection;
- comparison with prior Titan expectations;
- mechanistic hypothesis;
- immediate exploitation of the inferred vulnerability.

The crystal then resists his bite. Successful model revision does not guarantee immediate operational closure.

### Porco experiences humans as monsters

Porco attacks Eren from surprise, attempting to recover the Founder and War Hammer.

Levi and the Scouts interrupt him.

Porco's reaction is one of the volume's strongest formal reversals:

`俺は巨人だぞ!?`

`こいつら人間の姿のまま俺を殺す気か!?`

The words reproduce the affective grammar of early *Attack on Titan* from the opposite side. The extraordinary body is afraid of ordinary human bodies because those humans possess technology, experience, coordination, and the willingness to attack.

The category “Titan” has ceased to determine who occupies the monster position.

### The volume's opposed imperatives

Pieck re-enters with the Panzer unit. Zeke appears as the Beast Titan.

Zeke orders:

`逃がすな`

`殲滅しろ`

The final page answers with the Paradis-side imperative:

`死ぬな`

`生き延びろ`

This is not a symmetrical proof that the sides are ethically identical. It is a formal demonstration that the conflict has moved beyond the old ontology. Organized human systems now contain Titan and non-Titan bodies on both sides, and each can imagine its own violence as necessary to prevent annihilation.

# 4. Recognition without reconciliation

Volume 25 is the strongest evidence so far for one of the V19 checkpoint's central claims.

## 4.1 Eren recognizes Reiner causally

Eren can now articulate:

- Reiner was a child;
- adults taught him islanders were devils;
- he was historically situated;
- he suffered;
- life across the sea contains ordinary good and bad people.

That is not superficial “enemy humanization.” It is a causal reconstruction.

## 4.2 Reiner recognizes his own decision points

Reiner refuses to let the structural account swallow authorship. He identifies the post-Marcel continuation decision and his desire to be a hero.

His self-condemnation may itself be psychologically extreme; the text does not require accepting “everything was solely Reiner's fault.”

But it does require rejecting the opposite simplification that he had no morally relevant agency.

## 4.3 Recognition does not make objectives compatible

Eren still has an objective that makes him willing to transform beneath civilians and political guests.

The volume therefore upgrades the checkpoint formulation:

> Recognition is not merely insufficient to guarantee reconciliation. **Recognition may precede consciously authored violence.**

That is a harder ethical world than one in which violence happens because people fail to see one another.

## 4.4 Understanding can increase responsibility

Once the dehumanizing story collapses, later violence can no longer be narrated as violence against unknowable monsters.

Eren knows the people are people.

Reiner knows the islanders were people.

Magath knows Marley has behaved demonically.

Willy knows Tybur privilege rested on abandonment.

The moral problem shifts from ignorance toward what agents do with knowledge that no longer permits innocence.

# 5. Eren and Reiner: sameness without equivalence

## 5.1 The hand

Eren tells Reiner to stand and offers a hand.

Formally, the gesture resembles reconciliation.

Narratively, it occurs immediately before transformation.

That juxtaposition is the point. The series uses a familiar visual grammar of mutual recognition while refusing its expected pacifying outcome.

## 5.2 `同じ`

`同じ` does not erase differences in age, agency, scale, target, or context.

Reiner entered Paradis as an indoctrinated child soldier and helped initiate a catastrophic breach.

Eren enters Liberio as an older and far more informed actor.

The manga is not asking the reader to pretend those variables are equal.

It is drawing a process analogy:

- inherit a conflict;
- enter the enemy world;
- discover people there are human;
- continue anyway.

## 5.3 Reiner's confession is not simple absolution-by-self-hatred

Reiner's desire to disappear and be killed is a genuine psychological fact in the scene.

It should not be treated as the final ethical verdict.

His self-hatred may make him more willing than the text is to assign total causal responsibility to himself. Eren's structural account supplies relevant counterevidence.

The best model preserves both:

- Reiner has morally relevant agency;
- Reiner is not the sole cause of the historical system that put him there.

## 5.4 Eren's self-description

`生まれた時からこうなんだ` is important because current Eren insists on continuity rather than narrating himself as a passive product of Reiner's attack.

This **STRENGTHENS** the first-half finding that Carla's death did not create the freedom/anti-confinement drive.

But current Eren's retrospective statement does not make every childhood action transparent evidence of his present strategy.

The correct transition is continuity with development, not predestination.

# 6. Willy Tybur and Magath: political theater becomes self-fulfilling evidence

## 6.1 Willy is not merely lying

Willy exposes a false inherited heroic narrative.

He acknowledges Tybur wrongdoing.

He risks and loses his own life.

Those facts matter.

## 6.2 Willy is not merely telling the truth

He is also staging an event designed to produce political alignment.

He chooses emphasis, sequence, threat framing, audience, sacrificial exposure, and a named enemy.

Truth content and strategic narration coexist.

## 6.3 The attack supplies visible proof

Eren's transformation physically demonstrates the kind of threat Willy describes.

But because Willy expected and planned around that possibility, “proof” here is politically engineered as well as materially real.

A useful institutional formulation is:

> **Propaganda and evidence are not mutually exclusive categories. Political actors can organize real evidence into a strategically interested narrative.**

That is more analytically precise than either “Willy tells the truth” or “Willy is just propagandizing.”

## 6.4 Magath's devil reflexivity

Magath's admission that Marleyans may themselves be devils does not dissolve his military role.

Instead, it shows a form of institutional moral awareness that can coexist with continued command.

This parallels the volume's broader theme: recognition of wrongdoing is not equivalent to exiting the system that produces it.

# 7. Eren: current-state reconstruction after V25

Volume 25 produces one of the largest Eren model deltas since the sea.

## 7.1 Calm recognition is compatible with extreme action

The basement conversation is controlled, reflective, and relationally perceptive.

Minutes later Eren transforms under a populated stage.

A simulator should therefore not map calm affect to low action propensity.

## 7.2 He can distinguish causal explanation from operational decision

Eren grants Reiner structural mitigation.

He does not infer from that mitigation that he must stop.

This is a sophisticated but severe decision pattern:

1. model the person accurately;
2. recognize structural causation;
3. preserve the objective;
4. act despite the recognition.

## 7.3 `駆逐` survives dehumanization collapse

The vocabulary of elimination remains, but its object has changed.

Early `駆逐` operated inside a human-versus-Titan ontology.

Current `駆逐` is spoken after Eren explicitly says the people outside and inside are the same.

That makes the word ethically more charged, not less.

## 7.4 Eren is an increasingly competent tactical model-builder

The War Hammer sequence adds nontrivial cognitive evidence:

- he recognizes an anomalous transformation pattern;
- infers a remote body location;
- finds the physical connection;
- extracts the bearer;
- tests the crystal;
- updates again when the test fails.

This should raise confidence in Eren's current combat-reasoning model without converting him into Armin. Eren's style remains action-coupled and rapidly exploitative rather than discursively exploratory.

# 8. Reiner: confession after fracture

## 8.1 V24 showed substitution; V25 shows later integration

V24 gave direct evidence of `ライナーは死んだ / 俺がマルセルになる`.

V25 does not erase that fracture. It shows that years later Reiner can narrate the decision more coherently.

This is a longitudinal state transition:

- acute role fracture;
- long-term behavioral inhabitation of conflicting roles;
- later retrospective integration through confession.

A correct character model must allow all three states.

## 8.2 His moral reflex is anti-exculpatory

When Eren offers contextual mitigation, Reiner moves toward personal blame.

This can become excessive self-condemnation, but it is a stable enough current feature to constrain reconstruction: Reiner is unlikely to accept easy moral reassurance when he believes his own decision contributed directly to another person's suffering.

## 8.3 Suicidal desire remains active

`俺を…殺してくれ` and `もう…消えたい` preserve the suicidal state introduced in V24.

The scene does not establish recovery.

It also does not show complete passivity: Reiner still engages Eren, remembers, explains, and has already acted to protect Falco during the transformation.

The model must permit severe self-erasure desire alongside situational protective function.

# 9. Mikasa: attachment, mission, and moral independence

V25 is an important anti-caricature volume for Mikasa.

## 9.1 She returns to Eren

She fights as part of the operation and directly supports his survival.

## 9.2 She asks him to return

`お願い…帰ってきて` is relational rather than tactical.

It does not sound like a commander issuing an instruction.

## 9.3 She condemns the civilian killings

Her language is unambiguous.

The key modeling rule is:

> **Mikasa's attachment to Eren does not collapse her moral judgment into his.**

A hypothetical model that predicts automatic endorsement because “Mikasa always sides with Eren” would fail this volume.

## 9.4 Her voice remains concise

Even when morally condemning Eren, she does not deliver an extended philosophical lecture. The speech is concrete:

- civilians;
- children;
- irreversibility.

This is consistent with the frozen written-voice prediction while expanding its emotional range.

# 10. Jean and Floch: the first clear retaliatory fault line

## 10.1 Jean

Jean participates in a lethal operation but insists on minimizing civilian harm.

His later `生き残らねぇと` formulation preserves epistemic modesty: the group does not yet know what lies beyond this battle.

This is consistent with his long-running practical realism.

## 10.2 Floch

Floch's counterargument is collective-retaliatory.

Wall-side civilians suffered because of outside forces; therefore outside civilian suffering is not automatically disqualifying and may be understood as repayment.

The important point is not to reduce him to “cruel.” V25 supplies a political logic that should be tracked longitudinally:

- survivor grievance;
- collective responsibility;
- retributive proportionality;
- action as proof that Paradis will not wait passively to die.

## 10.3 The split

Jean and Floch are both fighting the same enemy in the same operation.

Their divergence is therefore not hawk versus pacifist.

It is over **how far the enemy category transfers responsibility from combatants and institutions to civilians**.

This should become a high-priority V27 checkpoint thread.

# 11. Gabi, Falco, Sasha, Pieck, Porco

## 11.1 Gabi: violence supplies new experiential reasons

V23–V24 established Gabi as an active reasoner inside Marleyan ideology.

V25 adds personal bereavement and witnessed attack.

Her decision to go back toward the battle is therefore no longer explained adequately by propaganda alone.

The system has begun manufacturing its own future evidence through the enemy's violence.

## 11.2 Falco: exploited trust does not become stupidity

Falco correctly realizes he was deceived once evidence becomes available.

His earlier trust was reasonable inside the relationship state Eren constructed.

This distinction matters for social cognition modeling: being deceived by a skilled and information-advantaged actor is not evidence of general gullibility.

## 11.3 Sasha: non-targeting as negative evidence

Sasha sees Gabi and does not fire.

The scene supports a bounded negative rule rather than a broad pacifist trait.

## 11.4 Pieck: precaution before certainty

Pieck's suspicion and Panzer-team backup show contingency planning.

She can maintain a casual social register while independently preserving an escape/recovery option.

## 11.5 Porco: Titan superiority is psychologically destabilized

Porco is not merely physically attacked. His expectation about the hierarchy of bodies is violated.

He is a Titan.

The humans attack anyway.

That shock is an epistemic event: his model of Paradis humans is changing under direct combat evidence.

# 12. Japanese language and voice notes

## 12.1 Eren — reflective recognition to categorical closure

The basement voice moves through several registers:

- contextual: explaining what he learned across the sea;
- relational: `ライナー…お前と同じだよ`;
- empathic: acknowledging Reiner's suffering;
- self-interpretive: `生まれた時から`;
- categorical: `進み続ける / 敵を駆逐するまで`.

This is not one-note slogan speech.

It is also not highly hedged academic exposition. Eren's reflective mode remains direct and concrete.

## 12.2 Reiner — anti-exculpatory confession

Reiner's repetitions of `違う` reject Eren's mitigation.

His speech becomes increasingly first-person and causal:

- I continued;
- I wanted to be a hero;
- it was my fault.

The register is psychologically important because it contracts a systemic history into personal responsibility.

## 12.3 Willy — universal life desire plus enemy designation

Willy's public register combines:

- historical narration;
- confession;
- first-person fear of death;
- universal audience address;
- political imperative.

The rhetorical success comes partly from moving from `私` to a shared future before reintroducing the named enemy.

## 12.4 Mikasa — concrete moral language

Mikasa's moral condemnation is built from ordinary nouns and irreversible consequence, not abstract ethical terminology:

- `民間人`;
- `子供`;
- `取り返しがつかない`.

That lexical concreteness fits her broader speech profile.

## 12.5 Jean — operational restraint

Jean uses imperative command under combat but grounds it in a specific protected category: civilian harm.

Later, his survival language is practical rather than heroic.

## 12.6 Floch — grievance made collective

Floch moves from historical injury to a present collective permission structure.

His speech is forceful and socially distributive: `俺達` and the people inside the Walls matter as a group whose suffering has not been answered.

## 12.7 Porco — shocked body hierarchy

`俺は巨人だぞ!?` is revealing because Titan identity is treated as a reason humans should fear attacking him.

The following question—whether humans intend to kill him while remaining human—registers a collapsed expectation.

## 12.8 Closing command contrast

`逃がすな / 殲滅しろ`

versus

`死ぬな / 生き延びろ`

is a compact lexical summary of the volume's final conflict state.

## 13.1 Eren -> Reiner: understanding without rescue

Eren offers Reiner understanding and a hand.

Future reconstruction must preserve both.

## 13.7 Porco -> Scouts: enemy recognition begins through violated expectation

Porco learns something about the Scouts not through dialogue but through their willingness to kill him.

Recognition in this volume is not always humane in form. Combat can also update interpersonal and group models.

# 16. Visual and formal analysis

## 16.1 Above stage / below basement

The most important spatial device is vertical.

Public history is narrated above.

Private responsibility is confessed below.

The two spaces are causally connected but socially separated: the audience hears one story while Eren and Reiner conduct another.

## 16.2 The offered hand

The hand produces the visual expectation of reconciliation.

Its immediate proximity to transformation weaponizes that expectation.

## 16.3 Speech bubble to explosion

The declaration pages turn rhetoric into action almost without transition.

This is manga-form argument, not merely plot simultaneity.

## 16.4 Crowd scale

The stage audience is first drawn as a political collective and then as bodies inside blast geometry.

The change forces the reader to move from “world representatives” and “festival crowd” back to vulnerable persons.

## 16.5 War Hammer materiality

The War Hammer repeatedly changes the battle silhouette by producing new weapons.

Visual surprise is part of the information problem Eren must solve.

## 16.6 Mikasa's return

Mikasa's familiar body language and equipment re-enter a city that has been framed for two volumes through Marleyan ordinary life.

The same Scout visual grammar that once signified home-side resistance now appears as foreign attack.

## 16.7 Sasha/Gabi sightline

The panel sequence matters because the non-shot is visible.

Gabi is seen.

Sasha leaves.

Absence of fire becomes evidence.

## 16.8 The buried body

The War Hammer bearer beneath the ground literalizes the volume's suspicion that the visible body may not contain the operative center.

This also echoes the stage/basement structure: what appears above is not necessarily where agency physically resides.

## 16.9 Porco encircled

A Titan crouches amid humans moving toward him without terror.

The visual reversal supports Porco's verbal shock.

## 16.10 Opposed commands

Zeke's Titan face and Eren's human face frame incompatible imperatives at the volume boundary.

The closing image refuses the old allocation in which “human” meant survival and “Titan” meant extermination.

# 20. Volume 25 synthesis

Volume 25 is the point at which *Attack on Titan* makes human recognition morally insufficient in the strongest possible way.

Earlier volumes undermined the category of the inhuman enemy. Annie could care about Armin. Reiner's soldier identity could become real. Bertolt could call the 104th important comrades while deciding to kill them. The basement proved the enemy lived in a larger human world.

Volume 25 goes further because Eren can now say all of this himself.

He has crossed the sea.

He has eaten their food.

He has lived under their roof.

He understands Reiner was an ignorant child under adult ideology.

He understands Reiner suffered.

He says the people outside and inside are the same.

Then he continues.

That is not a failure of the manga's humanization project. It is the project's harshest consequence. If violence persists after dehumanization collapses, the moral burden can no longer be assigned primarily to ignorance. It moves toward objective conflict, institutional design, inherited fear, strategic calculation, retaliation, and chosen continuation.

Reiner refuses the opposite escape. He will not let structural explanation erase his decision after Marcel's death. His confession gives the series one of its clearest formulations of authored responsibility under constraint.

Willy provides the political analogue. He dismantles an inherited heroic myth and uses the revised story to construct a new war coalition. The resulting narrative is neither pure truth nor pure propaganda. It is strategically organized historical testimony and threat interpretation. Its power becomes visible when Eren attacks at the moment of declaration.

The formal simultaneity is the volume's core achievement.

Above: `宣戦布告`.

Below: `同じ`.

Then: transformation.

Recognition and declaration arrive together.

The second half makes the political consequence bodily. Mikasa returns not as unquestioning support but as an attached moral critic. Jean and Floch expose competing rules for civilian responsibility. Gabi's inherited ideology receives new fuel from witnessed loss. Sasha's non-shot preserves a civilian/child target distinction. Eren solves the War Hammer mechanism through anomaly detection. Porco discovers that being a Titan no longer guarantees the position of terror.

The volume closes with two command grammars:

`逃がすな / 殲滅しろ`

and

`死ぬな / 生き延びろ`.

At the beginning of the series, extermination belonged rhetorically to humanity's response to Titans and survival belonged to humanity's threatened condition. By Volume 25, both grammars circulate through opposing human military systems.

The V25 longitudinal conclusion is therefore:

> **The collapse of monster ontology does not end war; it makes war recognizably human.**

And its character-model conclusion is:

> **A person may understand the enemy, understand the system that produced them, recognize similarity, retain attachment, feel guilt, and still continue into severe violence. Reliable reconstruction must model those variables independently rather than treating empathy as a master switch.**
