# 『進撃の巨人』Volume 29 Deep Reading
## Whom can freedom trust? Strategic deception, relational evidence, and the politics of believing another person

## 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 28 asked who has the right to define salvation for another person and answered with a warning: the forest reproduces itself whenever adults convert children, bodies, relationships, or future generations into instruments of an escape they have chosen. Volume 29 does not leave that problem behind. It changes its operating medium.

By the beginning of V29, almost every important public information channel has become compromised. Governments have lied. Restorationists have over-read fragments. Zeke has manipulated medical information. Yelena has organized secret political networks. The military has hidden succession plans. Eren has attacked without authorization, withheld motives, and supplied biologically deterministic interpretations of his closest friends. Pieck enters the volume apparently betraying Marley, while Marley itself organizes a surprise invasion based on incomplete intelligence. Armin eventually responds to Eren's explicit statements by proposing that those statements themselves are the deception.

Under those conditions, the political problem of freedom becomes an epistemic problem:

> **When information is a weapon, freedom depends on whom one can trust—but trust is not ethically self-validating, because the same relational faith that preserves personhood can also hand one person jurisdiction over everyone else's future.**

This is why `信頼` becomes a structural keyword even when it is not repeated as a slogan. Ksaver tells Zeke that the royal-blooded Titan can draw out the Founder's power but the decisive will belongs to the Founder holder; Zeke therefore requires `信頼のできる誰か`. He chooses Eren. Yelena's politics transforms that trust into religious confidence in two saviors. Pieck rejects loyalty to Marley while explicitly affirming trust in the comrades with whom she has fought. Armin rejects Eren's latest words because his accumulated model of Eren makes those words less credible than a strategic-deception hypothesis. He then asks others to trust Onyankopon's behavioral history rather than the fact that Onyankopon belongs to the same Volunteer category as Yelena.

The volume therefore distinguishes at least four kinds of trust:

1. **instrumental trust** — another person possesses a power or decision point one cannot control directly;
2. **relational trust** — repeated conduct gives a person evidentiary weight beyond institutional affiliation;
3. **ideological faith** — the trusted actor is treated as a savior whose project becomes self-justifying;
4. **adversarial trust-modeling** — one predicts what an enemy, friend, or ally will or will not do and acts on that model.

None is automatically virtuous. Zeke's trust in Eren is necessary to a plan that would remove reproductive agency from an entire people. Yelena's faith licenses coercion. Pieck's trust in comrades sustains mutual rescue but also enables a lethal ambush. Armin's trust in his long model of Eren may be insightful, or it may be a desperate refusal to update enough; V29 cannot yet decide. The volume's method is not “trust your friends.” It is to ask **what evidence a trust relation contains, what power the relation transfers, and what happens if the model is wrong**.

That concern changes the meaning of recognition as well. V25 established recognition without reconciliation. V28 showed enemy categories failing under person-level contact. V29 makes recognition explicitly epistemic. Gabi can finally say `悪魔なんていなかった` because repeated observations of concrete people falsify the ontology she inherited. Yet the discovery that “there are only people” does not end the battle, erase Sasha's death, or compel Kaya to forgive her. Recognition improves the model; it does not settle the politics.

The four chapter titles form an unusually coherent progression:

- `支え` asks what supports a person when body, institution, and inherited worldview fail;
- `天地` expands the conflict vertically and rhetorically, placing heavenly savior claims above bodies fighting on roofs, streets, prisons, and airships;
- `断罪` turns the invasion into punishment and proliferates competing judgments about who deserves to be condemned or saved;
- `騙し討ち` reveals deception not as a side tactic but as the volume's governing political form.

The title `騙し討ち` applies most obviously to Pieck's tactical feints, but its analytical reach is wider. Armin performs agreement to Yelena while privately rejecting euthanasia. Eren may be deceiving Zeke and Yelena—or Armin may be deceiving himself about Eren. Marley arrives through surprise. Zeke's earlier wine infrastructure remains a delayed ambush inside bodies. Even trust becomes a tactical resource.

The resulting V29 thesis is therefore not that deception destroys freedom. Sometimes deception protects freedom against coercive actors: Hange's lie about Levi, Pieck's feint against Eren, or Armin's performance toward Yelena can all be understood as attempts to create room for action. The sharper rule is:

> **Deception becomes ethically diagnostic according to whose agency it protects, whose agency it captures, and whether the deceiver converts another person's trust into unilateral jurisdiction over their body or future.**

V29 thus carries the V27 checkpoint's “freedom becomes constitutional” thesis into the epistemic constitution of relationships. If people are to remain subjects rather than instruments, they need not only formal standing and bodily agency but some defensible way to decide whose testimony deserves provisional belief.

# 3. Narrative architecture and causal close reading

## 3.1 Chapter 115 — 「支え」: support can restore a body, transmit a doctrine, or conceal a motive

The chapter begins with Zeke in a state where ordinary political agency has collapsed almost completely. After the Thunder-Spear detonation, he experiences silence, sensory loss, and bodily failure. This matters because V28 ended by showing him choose self-destructive escalation as an expression of certainty. V29 begins by making him radically dependent.

The title `支え` therefore operates on several levels. A body can be physically supported or reconstructed. A child can be emotionally supported by a surrogate parent. A political project can be supported by another person's decision authority. A lie can support a vulnerable person long enough to escape. And an ideology can function as psychological support precisely because it converts intolerable ambiguity into a total solution.

### Ksaver turns the Founder problem into a trust problem

The remembered conversation with Ksaver clarifies a crucial mechanical limit. A royal-blooded Titan may be able to draw out the Founder's ability through contact, but the decisive use of that power belongs to the Founder holder. Zeke cannot simply substitute his royal blood for Eren's will. The plan therefore requires a `信頼のできる誰か`.

This changes the political structure of the Founder. Earlier royal institutions tried to fuse bloodline, office, memory, and authority inside one inheritance chain. Zeke's solution separates access from decision. Royal blood supplies a condition; another actor supplies the operative will. The mechanism is thus intrinsically relational.

That relationality does not make it democratic. It may make the system even more dependent on private trust. A population-level decision can hinge on an interpersonal bond whose evidentiary basis is unavailable to everyone who will bear the outcome.

The scene also deepens Ksaver's role. His reassurance that he is always watching over Zeke is followed by Zeke's promise to find the trusted person and his address `父さん`. The baseball/catch relation from V28 has now become surrogate parenthood in explicit language. The ordinary relation is real; so is the political inheritance that travels through it.

### Eren's apparent assent is strong evidence but not yet transparent motive

The private Eren/Zeke meeting is deliberately dangerous for hindsight-driven interpretation because it contains explicit statements that look like closure. Eren calls Grisha wrong, says that he too was wrong as the son Grisha raised, agrees that non-birth would prevent future suffering, and states `オレはやる`. He then connects the project to the familiar persistence vocabulary: `その日を迎えるまで 進み続ける`, closing with `そうだろ? 兄さん`.

At the V29 boundary, the responsible reading must hold two facts simultaneously.

First, **these are genuine utterances and therefore genuine evidence**. A model that simply declares them “fake” because later knowledge or intuition says so would violate the prospective method. Eren is capable of strategic concealment, but strategic concealment is not a license to disregard speech whenever it conflicts with an earlier model.

Second, **the scene is itself a trust negotiation between actors with asymmetric information**. Zeke needs a Founder holder who will implement the plan. Eren needs access to Zeke's royal-blood mechanism. Both know that direct contact is dangerous. Neither institutional public nor friends are present. The conversation's function is therefore not only ideological confession; it is also alliance formation.

The baseball makes that structure visual. Zeke cannot safely shake Eren's hand, so he throws him the object inherited from Ksaver. The object is a token of trust passed without the physical contact that would activate the power relation. It links Ksaver → Zeke → Eren while keeping the decisive bodily circuit open but incomplete.

V29 consequently does **not** resolve Eren's full motive. It establishes that Eren can convincingly inhabit Zeke's salvation vocabulary and that Zeke has reason to regard him as the trusted other required by the plan.

### The unknown girl turns bodily survival into an ontological question

Zeke's apparent death is interrupted by one of the volume's most formally discontinuous sequences. A Titan encloses him, and he later recalls an unknown girl shaping his body from earth or sand in a place with strange temporal properties. He says it felt as though years passed and as though only an instant passed, and asks whether that place was `道`.

The evidence hierarchy here matters. The manga supplies Zeke's firsthand experience. Zeke supplies the interpretation `あれが…「道」…なのか…`. The volume does **not** supply a full objective cosmology.

Still, the experience materially strengthens several earlier hypotheses. Titan bodies and Subject-of-Ymir bodies may be related to a nonordinary network or place; bodily reconstruction can occur through a process not reducible to visible biological regeneration; subjective time there is not ordinary clock time; and Zeke's prior conviction that Subjects of Ymir are connected through Paths now receives experiential rather than merely theoretical reinforcement.

Politically, the experience is likely to strengthen Zeke's epistemic exceptionalism. He already believed himself to possess knowledge unavailable to ordinary Paradisians. Now he has undergone an experience almost impossible to verify socially. The danger is not that the experience is imaginary; the body itself shows that something extraordinary occurred. The danger is that private access to extraordinary evidence can make a person still more willing to treat others' ignorance as proof they should not decide.

### Yelena transforms strategy into theology

Back in Paradis, Yelena's occupation discourse makes explicit what V28 only implied. She presents Zeke as a figure who would already have saved the world if Paradis had trusted him, and calls him a god of world salvation. The rhetoric is not ornamental. It solves a legitimacy problem for a faction that lacks stable constitutional authority.

If Zeke is not merely a strategist but a savior, disagreement can be reframed as ignorance or sin. Political uncertainty becomes moral hierarchy. This is the same formal move by which inherited state doctrine often protects itself: elevate one account beyond ordinary contestation.

The armband system adds a visual and institutional irony. Paradis has learned a classification practice resembling the enemy's segregating apparatus. The point is not that every armband is morally equivalent to Marley's internment system. It is that institutions under existential pressure import techniques whose genealogy they condemn. Enemy recognition becomes institutional imitation.

### Hange's lie is protective deception

Hange's response to Floch and the Jaegerists provides a useful counterexample to the volume's coercive deceptions. Hange claims Levi is dead and supplies a plausible blast-injury explanation while physically supporting the badly wounded Levi. The lie is meant to prevent captors from verifying or finishing him and to create an escape opportunity.

This does not make deception intrinsically good. It clarifies the criterion: Hange's deception protects the endangered subject's remaining agency and life from a coercive faction. It does not secretly redesign Levi's future or enroll his body into a project without his knowledge. The manga is increasingly interested not in truth-versus-lie as a simple moral binary, but in **who controls whom through information**.

### Gabi reaches epistemic disorientation before moral reconstruction

Near the chapter's end, Gabi's inherited vocabulary remains available—she can still say `悪魔`—but it no longer predicts the people around her. Her question is effectively: if these are devils, why do their actions not fit the model? `わからない` becomes an important developmental state.

The gap between model failure and replacement belief should not be skipped. V29 does not begin with a fully reformed Gabi. It begins with contradiction accumulating faster than the inherited ontology can absorb.

## 3.2 Chapter 116 — 「天地」: heaven belongs to savior narratives; earth belongs to people testing one another

The chapter title's verticality is literalized through roofs, towers, airships, underground cells, and the skyward arrival of Marley. But `天地` also describes a rhetorical split. Yelena speaks in world-saving terms that place Zeke/Eren above ordinary political judgment. Pieck, by contrast, repeatedly reasons from ground-level relationships, family obligations, military constraints, and observable behavior.

### Pieck turns apparent surrender into a test of Eren's model

Pieck's rifle confrontation first looks like a simple hostage reversal. Eren instead reasons that she cannot shoot him because Marley's strategic priority is the Founder. He does not need to know Pieck's feelings to infer the constraint. This is a clean example of his mature combat-political reasoning: identify the opponent's objective, distinguish threat appearance from action space, and act as if the constraint is real.

Pieck then appears to offer cooperation. The reader is placed in exactly the epistemic position the volume analyzes: a plausible statement cannot be judged by surface sincerity alone. Pieck has reasons to hate Marley; she has reasons to protect Gabi; she has reasons to want Eldian freedom; she also has comrades in the Warrior unit.

### Yelena discloses the euthanasia plan and tries to monopolize its moral frame

In the prison sequence, Yelena reveals the plan in unusually direct terms: Subjects of Ymir would lose the ability to have children, extinction would occur gradually, and the Rumbling would protect the population during the transition. The plan is presented as a way to end the world's cycle of hatred without immediate massacre of existing Subjects of Ymir.

This disclosure matters because it separates **mechanism** from **evaluation**. The characters can now understand what the plan proposes even if they reject Yelena's claim that it is salvation.

Armin's tearful `感動…致しました` is especially important precisely because the volume later gives us his private reasoning. If read in isolation, the performance could be mistaken for ideological conversion. Once Chapter 118 is reached, it is clear that Armin does not endorse ending Eldian reproduction. The earlier display is therefore at minimum strategically useful performance and most plausibly deliberate manipulation of Yelena's expectations.

That is a major Armin update. The dialogue-oriented character is capable of lying or performing emotional agreement when the interlocutor holds coercive power and direct argument would reduce action space. Dialogue has never meant naïve transparency, but V29 makes the point unusually explicit.

### Pieck distinguishes state loyalty from comrade trust

Pieck's conversation with Gabi supplies one of the most important relationship statements in the post-basement corpus. Her family motive explains why becoming a Warrior was personally meaningful; her father mattered. Her political reasoning then separates Eldian rights from the bargain of proving “good Eldian” status to Marley. Human rights must be won by the people themselves rather than granted as reward for obedience.

The later formulation is even cleaner: `私はマーレを信じてない`, followed by trust in the comrades with whom she has fought.

This is not liberal individualism in the abstract. It is a hierarchy of evidentiary reliability. The state is not trusted because its institutions have repeatedly instrumentalized Eldians. Concrete comrades are trusted because shared danger supplies repeated behavioral evidence. Pieck can therefore deceive Eren without considering herself disloyal to the people she actually recognizes as “hers.”

The distinction is also a warning against factional flattening. “Marley” cannot predict Pieck's beliefs any more than “Volunteer” can predict Onyankopon's beliefs. Relationship models and institutions cross-cut one another.

### `そこ`: pointing turns language into an ambush surface

Eren demands that Pieck show him where the enemy is. Her answer `そこ` points directly to him. The utterance works simultaneously as literal truth, hostile classification, and trigger for Porco's concealed attack.

Manga form makes the moment stronger than a paraphrase. The pointing gesture creates a visible vector; the floor below Eren becomes the hidden attack plane; the apparently cooperative speech is retroactively reclassified as tactical setup. The chapter's vertical geometry—roof, building, underground Jaw Titan, incoming airships—turns information asymmetry into space.

Pieck also protects Gabi during the attack. Deception and care are therefore not opposing personality states. The feint exists inside a relationship-conditioned rescue policy.

### Marley arrives through surprise because it refuses Eren's timetable

The invasion is itself an epistemic strike. Paradis's factions are preoccupied with internal sovereignty and Zeke/Eren contact. Marley acts sooner than the defenders' preferred model would suggest. V29 repeatedly punishes actors who treat their own plan as the only active plan.

## 3.3 Chapter 117 — 「断罪」: every side claims judgment, and recognition does not stop the sentence

`断罪` can be read as condemnation, judgment, or punishment. The Marleyan counterattack is framed as retaliation for Liberio; Porco carries personal anger; Reiner reads Eren through his own suicidal history; Magath invokes the need for a new Helos. But no single actor possesses uncontested jurisdiction to “judge” the war.

### Eren recognizes the surprise attack as evidence of an adversary model

Eren quickly notices that Marley's attack is too desperate and too well-timed to be explained by stale assumptions. Rather than treating the invasion as random, he infers that the enemy has updated and chosen a high-risk move. This continues the volume's trust/modeling theme in adversarial form: good strategic reasoning requires treating opponents as agents with models of their own.

Onyankopon advises retreat or use of the War Hammer's defensive possibilities. Eren chooses direct engagement. This is consistent with his established preference for forward action under obstruction, but V29 should not reduce that choice to an abstract “always attacks” rule. He is also trying to protect the decisive strategic position and is operating before Zeke's arrival.

### `来いよ……ライナー`: recognition and lethal opposition coexist cleanly

Eren's challenge to Reiner is one of the volume's most economical voice anchors. By now the two men possess unusually deep knowledge of one another's suffering and causal histories. That recognition produces no requirement to stand down.

The scene therefore gives a clean prospectively fair test of the frozen Eren model: former comrade, recognized personhood, existential opposition, continued lethal action. V25's “recognition without reconciliation” survives intact.

### Reiner's mercy language is self-projection inside continued combat

Reiner's side of the exchange is equally important. He asks why Eren continues to struggle, tells him it is enough, says he no longer has to make others suffer, and eventually urges him to sleep. These lines are not evidence that Reiner ceases to oppose Eren. He continues fighting with the objective of taking the Founder.

The speech instead reveals self-projective empathy. Reiner knows what it is to desire an end to unbearable forward motion. He can recognize Eren's exhaustion through his own history and still conclude that the mission requires stopping him by force.

This is exactly the kind of relationship state the project's reconstruction architecture is designed to preserve: **attachment/recognition and hostile action are independent dimensions**.

### Porco turns instrumental humiliation into personal revenge

Porco's attack contains a narrower but useful motive. Eren previously used him as a tool to break the War Hammer crystal. Porco now wants repayment. This is not simply Marley state strategy; it is personal retaliation inside the state mission.

Eren's assessment that Porco alone is not a sufficient threat likewise separates individual capability from team context. The Jaw becomes dangerous in combination with Reiner, Pieck/Magath, soldiers, and air power. The manga continues refusing singular-hero causality even when individual Titans are spectacular.

### Marley manufactures its own salvation myth

Magath's invocation of a `真の英雄ヘーロス` is structurally paired with Yelena's savior theology. Both sides desire an actor whose victory can be narrated as world salvation. The symmetry should not be flattened into moral equivalence; their institutions and objectives differ. The important formal point is that **saviorhood is a political technology available to multiple camps**.

A “hero who saves the world” simplifies plural harms into one legitimating figure. That is precisely the danger V27-V29 has been tracing: if survival is personified in one indispensable actor, constitutional restraint becomes easier to suspend.

### Zeke arrives as brother and strategic pivot at once

Zeke's entrance closes the chapter by fusing intimacy and battlefield function. He tells Eren he has endured alone and says the rest can be left to his older brother. `お兄ちゃんに任せろ` is affectively different from Yelena's theological speech. It is familial, even protective.

Yet that care sits inside the euthanasia project. Relationship sincerity does not guarantee that the project preserves the loved person's independent objective. V29 again warns against using warmth as a proxy for egalitarian agency.

## 3.4 Chapter 118 — 「騙し討ち」: strategic deception is everywhere, but its ethical direction differs

The final chapter turns the title into a cross-cutting principle. Pieck/Magath stage an apparent tactical failure. Armin performs ideological alignment. Eren's entire alliance with Zeke may be a strategic deception—or may not be. Trust and deceit become inseparable from action under coercive uncertainty.

### Onyankopon breaks the Volunteer category from inside

When Onyankopon reaches the prisoners, suspicion is rational. The Volunteers participated in a network that poisoned military leadership and helped produce the current crisis. Yet he denies knowledge of the euthanasia plan and grounds his opposition in a simple line: `子供は未来だ!!`.

Armin does not ask the group to trust him because he belongs to a morally good category. He asks them to compare this claim with the Onyankopon they have actually known: the engineer, pilot, modernization partner, and person who spoke of different peoples existing because the world is interesting in its plurality. That history makes forced childlessness inconsistent with his prior pattern.

This is one of the volume's cleanest epistemic statements: **person-level longitudinal evidence can defeat category inference**.

It also does not make Onyankopon infallible. The conclusion is provisional credibility, not saintly exemption from scrutiny.

### Armin builds an alternative Eren model instead of accepting the latest utterance

The cell discussion is the volume's central epistemic scene. Mikasa repeats Eren's claim that her attachment may not be her own will. Armin responds not with certainty but with `嘘だと思う`. The hedge is crucial.

He then constructs a causal alternative. If the Founder holder ultimately controls how the power is used, Eren could appear to cooperate with Yelena and Zeke in order to gain access to the mechanism, then choose a different use. Rejecting Yelena at first contact could have triggered an unpredictable response; apparent acceptance creates room to maneuver. Armin proposes a limited Rumbling demonstration against gathered world military forces as the strategically plausible alternative.

Several disciplines are required here.

First, Armin's argument is **not objective authorial revelation**. It is a character model built by Eren's closest surviving friend under severe emotional pressure.

Second, it is not empty denial. Armin cites a real contradiction: the Eren he knows has spent much of his life opposing the denial of birth, freedom, and future, making voluntary commitment to sterilizing his people seem behaviorally anomalous. He also has newly disclosed mechanical evidence that Eren retains the decisive Founder choice.

Third, the argument may contain motivated reasoning. Armin wants a version of Eren he can still help. The correct V29 state is therefore not “Eren is secretly doing X.” It is: **Armin has produced a coherent, evidence-based counter-model that makes strategic deception a live explanation.**

The project must carry both models forward.

### Mikasa's scarf removal is an action under unresolved interpretation

Mikasa asks the question Armin's model does not solve: if Eren is pretending, why did he choose specifically to hurt them, and why tell her he hated her? The volume refuses to answer.

She later leaves the scarf behind: `そう…置いて行く`. This is a high-value behavioral transition. The scarf has been one of the manga's longest-running relational objects, tied to rescue, home, and Eren's promise. Leaving it is therefore not neutral.

But the evidence does not authorize a permanent doctrine such as “Mikasa has rejected Eren” or “Mikasa is finally free.” It authorizes a narrower state claim: **under a crisis in which the meaning of her attachment has been externally pathologized, Mikasa creates physical distance from the relationship's most visible token before returning to battle.**

That is significant precisely because it leaves her future interpretation open.

### Pixis converts compromised bodies into a triage problem

Pixis's command response is characteristic of his late-series pragmatism. The military body has been compromised by spinal-fluid wine; he cannot make that fact disappear. He therefore differentiates equipment and roles. Clean soldiers receive priority access to ODM gear while contaminated personnel, including commanders, still contribute.

This is institutional action after sovereignty failure. It is neither heroic restoration nor collapse into panic. Pixis preserves function by acknowledging unequal risk states rather than pretending the organization remains homogeneous.

### Armin deceives Yelena to preserve an alternative plan

When the group re-enters public interaction, Armin loudly frames their action as support for Zeke/Eren saving the world and joining the Jaegerist objective. Yelena responds by saying she trusts him.

Because the preceding pages establish Armin's rejection of euthanasia and alternative Rumbling hypothesis, the performance is analytically unambiguous in one respect: **his public endorsement is not reliable evidence of ideological conversion**.

This is a powerful reconstruction constraint. Armin's high-dialogue orientation does not imply truthfulness toward a coercive interlocutor. He will manipulate an opponent's expectations when direct disclosure would sabotage the objective.

The scene also contains a moral irony: Yelena's trust in Armin is secured precisely by Armin exploiting the savior frame she wants to hear. Ideological faith reduces her ability to detect dissent.

### Nile sends a child away from the battlefield

Nile's interaction with Falco is brief but belongs to the V28 forest argument. He identifies Falco as a child and tells him to go home. The military officer refuses to treat the child solely as enemy manpower at a moment when the entire surrounding system is doing exactly that.

The action does not solve the war. It changes the direction of responsibility in the Artur Blouse sense: an adult uses authority to try to move inherited danger away from the child rather than demand that the child complete the adult conflict.

### Gabi reaches personhood without receiving forgiveness

Gabi's Chapter 118 transition is one of the strongest category revisions in the manga so far because it is both cognitive and behavioral. She helps direct the Blouse family and civilians toward safety. She hears Kaya say that she still cannot forgive her and wants to kill her. Then she states the replacement model:

`悪魔なんていなかった…`

`この島には…人がいるだけ`.

The word choice matters. The answer to the failed devil category is not “good Eldians,” “innocent islanders,” or a new collective purity. It is `人`—people. That is a smaller and more defensible ontology.

Gabi also links the insight to Reiner, saying she finally understands him, and recognizes the recursive structure by which people who have not seen the other side decide in advance that everyone there is a devil and then repeat the same violence.

This does not produce reconciliation. Kaya's anger remains. That is essential. If recognition required immediate forgiveness by the victim, the moral cost of recognition would be displaced back onto the harmed person. V29 instead allows Gabi to revise and Kaya to remain wounded simultaneously.

### Falco turns romantic attachment into a concrete future model

Falco's confession adds unusual ordinary-life evidence inside an extreme crisis. He admits his accidental role in carrying Eren's letters and therefore his sense of causal responsibility for deaths in Liberio. He then tells Gabi that he loves her and reveals why he pursued Warrior candidacy: he wanted to prevent her from inheriting the Armor, wanted her to live long, and imagined marriage/a shared future.

This retroactively sharpens his earlier protective acts without reducing them to romance alone. His moral restraint and contextual reasoning existed independently, but the attachment explains why he repeatedly accepts personal risk even when he understands the causal structure better than Gabi does.

Most importantly, Falco's desired future is not military status. Status was instrumental. The terminal preference is **Gabi alive, not shortened by Titan inheritance, with an ordinary shared life**.

That makes his attachment a direct counter-image to the adult systems around him. The Warrior institution converts children into inheritable weapons; Falco enters that institution partly to stop its inheritance from consuming the person he loves.

### Pieck's final feint makes `騙し討ち` literal

The apparent neutralization of the Cart creates a false tactical conclusion. The anti-Titan cannon remains operational through Pieck and Magath's decoupled setup, and Zeke is struck. The deception succeeds because observers infer that disabling one visible body disables the whole weapon system.

This is the volume's strategic epistemology in miniature: **a model fails when it mistakes the visible carrier for the complete causal system**.

The final page then freezes the story at another uncertainty. Reiner/Marley understand that if Zeke remains alive he may scream. Falco, Gabi, and Colt react to that possibility. The main story ends before V29 establishes whether the scream occurs.

The correct boundary statement is therefore threat, not outcome.

# 4. Character-state updates

## 4.1 Eren Jaeger — explicit assent and inferred deception must coexist as competing models

### Starting state

At V28, Eren's anti-instrumentalization of Historia coexisted with coercive treatment of Mikasa and Armin. His self-conception remained strongly organized by freedom, self-authorship, and forward motion. His complete relation to Zeke's plan was unresolved.

### V29 evidence

V29 adds two apparently contradictory evidence classes.

**Direct speech to Zeke:** Eren calls himself and Grisha wrong, treats non-birth as salvation, says `オレはやる`, links the project to `進み続ける`, and addresses Zeke as `兄さん`.

**Armin's counter-model:** Eren's lifetime pattern makes euthanasia seem radically anomalous; mechanical authority ultimately belongs to Eren; apparent cooperation could be the lowest-risk route to Founder access; limited Rumbling deterrence would fit a different protective objective.

Eren himself also continues to display the tactical traits already well supported: fast constraint inference against Pieck, anomaly detection regarding Marley's attack, willingness to engage Reiner directly, and compressed challenge speech under combat pressure.

### Current model

The responsible model must retain **dual hypotheses**:

- H1: Eren sincerely accepts substantial or complete elements of Zeke's euthanasia doctrine.
- H2: Eren is strategically performing agreement to gain royal-blood access and preserve freedom of final Founder choice.

V29 does not permit collapsing either hypothesis.

A third possibility—mixed sincerity, where Eren shares Zeke's diagnosis of inherited suffering but rejects or plans to alter the remedy—also remains open.

### Negative constraints

Do not treat Armin's faith as privileged narrator truth. Do not treat Eren's explicit assent as automatically transparent. Do not project V30+ motive knowledge backward. Do not convert `進み続ける` into proof of one particular policy; both Eren and Zeke can use forward-motion vocabulary for different ends.

## 4.2 Zeke Jaeger — trust is the missing mechanism between private salvation and population-level power

V28 made Zeke developmentally intelligible. V29 makes his plan structurally intelligible.

He needs Eren because royal blood alone does not settle Founder's use. Ksaver's mechanism therefore turns the plan into a trust dependency. Zeke's baseball, brother language, and battlefield reassurance show that this is not merely a cold contractual alliance. He has incorporated Eren into the relational position once occupied by the hoped-for trusted collaborator.

His unknown-girl experience also gives him extraordinary new personal evidence about Paths-like bodily reconstruction. That should raise confidence in his belief that Subject-of-Ymir bodies are connected through nonordinary mechanisms while simultaneously raising the risk of epistemic monopoly: he knows something others cannot independently access.

His current speech toward Eren—`よく一人で耐えたな`, `お兄ちゃんに任せろ`—should be modeled as real fraternal affect even while his political project remains coercive at population scale.

## 4.3 Armin Arlert — character modeling itself becomes an in-story survival tool

V29 is one of Armin's most valuable reconstruction volumes.

He does not simply “believe in Eren.” He compares present testimony with a longitudinal behavioral model, identifies a severe contradiction, checks the newly disclosed Founder mechanics, considers Yelena's incentives, and produces a strategic alternative. Crucially, he says `嘘だと思う`, preserving uncertainty.

He also demonstrates that persuasion can include deliberate performance. His tearful admiration before Yelena and later pro-savior declaration function to lower suspicion and secure freedom of action.

The current Armin model therefore requires:

- high tolerance for model revision;
- strong use of historical-person evidence;
- explicit causal branching;
- willingness to manipulate a coercive interlocutor;
- emotional investment that can bias interpretation;
- preference for a deterrent/time-buying strategy over both immediate annihilation and euthanasia when he perceives it as available.

## 4.4 Mikasa Ackerman — relationship agency is asserted through distance before it is verbally solved

Mikasa does not receive a clean answer to the V28 attack on her self-understanding. Instead, she asks the hardest unresolved question: why would a deceptive Eren choose to injure them emotionally, and why target her bond specifically?

Leaving the scarf is therefore important because it is action under unresolved meaning. She does not need a completed theory before changing what she does with the relationship's most visible symbol.

This strengthens a longstanding modeling rule: Mikasa's significant state transitions are often carried by action, object handling, silence, and brief utterance rather than extended self-analysis.

## 4.5 Jean Kirstein — pragmatic doubt resists both total condemnation and easy rescue narratives

Jean reacts differently from Armin. He agrees that Eren's behavior feels unlike him but stops short of treating Armin's reconstruction as certain. He remains angry about Eren's unilateral actions and still does not want him dead.

This is mature Jean: concrete accountability plus reluctance to absolutize one explanatory model. His skepticism is not lack of attachment; his attachment is not exculpation.

## 4.6 Connie Springer — betrayal anger remains compatible with renewed coordination

Connie's anger toward the Volunteers and Eren is understandable after Sasha's death and the wine conspiracy. Yet he can listen to Onyankopon and Armin and re-enter coordinated defense when the alternative is catastrophic defeat.

This continues the distinction between emotional judgment and operational choice. Connie need not forgive to cooperate.

## 4.7 Pieck Finger — loyalty is relational before it is national

V29 substantially improves Pieck's model.

Her family motive makes Warrior status personally grounded. Her political critique rejects the “good Eldian” bargain. Her apparent defection uses truths—Marley is not trustworthy, Eldians need rights—as cover for a tactical deception. Her explicit statement that she trusts comrades rather than Marley resolves the apparent contradiction.

She also shows high-level pattern inference about Eren/Zeke and high-risk tactical planning with Porco/Magath. Protecting Gabi during the ambush confirms that operational deception coexists with immediate caretaking.

Current Pieck should therefore be modeled as:

- skeptical of state ideology;
- strongly relationship-loyal;
- comfortable with tactical deception;
- analytically observant;
- willing to work inside an institution she does not trust for the sake of people/objectives she does;
- not reducible to cynical detachment.

## 4.8 Gabi Braun — enemy ontology collapses into person ontology

V27-V28 destabilized Gabi's category. V29 gives the explicit replacement.

The most important point is that she does not merely learn “island Eldians can be good.” She states that there were no devils here, only people. That is a deeper revision because it rejects the category's ontological premise rather than granting exceptions.

Her behavior changes with the model. She helps civilians and the Blouse family. She recognizes Reiner's earlier conflict. She apologizes to Falco for pulling him into her trajectory.

But the model must preserve guilt, fear, grief, and the fact that Kaya does not forgive her. The new state is not innocence. It is **recognition after complicity**.

## 4.9 Falco Grice — protective reason receives an ordinary future endpoint

Falco already had strong person-level causal reasoning and protective attachment. V29 reveals the ordinary future his behavior was serving. He wanted Gabi to avoid the Armor, live long, and share a married future.

This is valuable because it prevents his protective behavior from being modeled as abstract altruism. His ethics are relationally motivated without being wholly relationship-limited: he also shows concern for enemy civilians and causal fairness.

His admission about carrying Eren's letters adds guilt complexity. Better causal understanding does not mean he has avoided complicity.

## 4.10 Reiner Braun — compassion can take the form of trying to stop another person by force

Reiner's V29 speech toward Eren is deeply colored by his own suicide history. He sees endless struggle and wants it to end. `もういい` and `もう眠れ` are not mockery. They are compassion filtered through his own model of relief.

Yet he continues the mission. The correct model is not “Reiner still hates Eren” or “Reiner cannot fight someone he understands.” It is: **recognition changes the emotional meaning of hostile action without necessarily changing the hostile action itself**.

## 4.11 Onyankopon — pluralism and futurity separate him from Yelena's faction

`子供は未来だ!!` aligns with his earlier pluralist worldview. A project that ends reproduction is not a hidden extension of what he previously valued; it contradicts it.

This is why Armin's trust has evidentiary basis. Onyankopon becomes an important warning against organizational category errors. Shared affiliation with the Volunteers does not imply shared terminal objectives.

## 4.12 Yelena — savior faith creates both commitment and epistemic vulnerability

Yelena is increasingly best modeled as a person whose political faith organizes perception. Her killing of Greiz, theological language around Zeke, and interpretation of Armin's performance all show the same structure: those aligned with the salvation project are morally legible; those outside it are degraded or obstacles.

Her vulnerability is that she wants confirmation of the world-saving narrative. Armin can therefore manipulate her precisely by performing the expected reverence.

## 4.13 Pixis — command after contamination

Pixis continues to demonstrate proportional institutional thinking. He cannot purify the military of compromised bodies, so he allocates scarce equipment by transformation risk and continues to use contaminated personnel where possible.

This is one of the strongest late-series examples of command under irreversibly degraded conditions rather than ideal planning.

## 4.14 Nile Dok — adult responsibility briefly defeats enemy categorization

Nile's decision to send Falco home is small relative to the battle but analytically important. He sees “child” before “enemy candidate.” That is the Artur Blouse principle expressed institutionally: adults should prevent children from being consumed by the conflict when they still can.

## 4.15 Hange and Levi — support becomes survival infrastructure

Hange's V29 role is primarily protective/operational. The false-death claim and river escape show that the commander can use deception to preserve Levi's life under factional pursuit. Levi himself is physically unable to exercise ordinary command. Their relation therefore temporarily reverses the frequent pattern in which Levi operationally protects Hange or the Corps.

The evidence is sparse for current Levi cognition because of his injuries; no broader personality conclusion should be forced from silence.

# 5. Relationship-conditioned trust, recognition, and betrayal

## 5.1 Zeke / Ksaver — trust is inherited as both care and political architecture

Ksaver does not merely teach Zeke a doctrine. He teaches him that the doctrine requires another person. Their catch relationship creates the emotional grammar through which Zeke later approaches Eren: shared secret, baseball token, paternal/brotherly language, world-changing plan.

This is a strong example of why relationship modeling cannot be separated from ideology. Zeke's politics is carried through attachments.

## 5.2 Zeke / Eren — brotherhood is both sincere relation and unverified strategic alignment

Zeke's side of the relationship is comparatively clear. He wants Eren as the trusted other, reads him as a fellow victim of Grisha, and speaks protectively on the battlefield.

Eren's side remains harder. He uses brother language and apparent ideological agreement. But the mechanical asymmetry gives him a reason to perform. V29 therefore requires asymmetric confidence: **high confidence in Zeke's belief in the alliance; lower confidence in Eren's terminal alignment**.

## 5.3 Armin / Eren — intimacy becomes a source model that can override direct testimony

Armin's most radical act is epistemic: he decides that knowing Eren over time may be better evidence than Eren's recent explicit claims.

This can be insightful or dangerous. Intimacy gives access to behavioral regularities, but it can also produce motivated disbelief when a person changes. The volume intentionally leaves both possibilities alive.

## 5.4 Mikasa / Eren — the bond survives as an unanswered question rather than a settled identity

Mikasa's scarf decision and her question about Eren's cruelty show that the relation is no longer a taken-for-granted source of meaning. She is actively testing whether the old interpretation can survive current conduct.

That is a more complex state than either “loyal slave” or “independent rejection.”

## 5.5 Pieck / Gabi — care is the constant beneath tactical deception

Pieck's entire apparent-defection performance is compatible with immediate protective conduct toward Gabi. The relationship therefore gives continuity across deceptive surface states. A model that reads the apparent betrayal literally would fail because it ignores the stable attachment.

## 5.6 Pieck / Marley / comrades — institutional distrust does not equal social isolation

Pieck can reject the state while trusting the people embedded in its military. This is analogous to several Paradis relationships: institutional legitimacy and interpersonal loyalty are different axes.

## 5.7 Reiner / Eren — empathy changes the requested end, not the combat condition

Reiner does not simply want to defeat Eren. He wants Eren's struggle to stop. The distinction is psychologically meaningful even though the battlefield result sought is still Eren's defeat/capture.

## 5.8 Armin / Yelena — one-sided trust becomes exploitable

Yelena trusts the version of Armin who appears moved by salvation. Armin uses that expectation. This is not symmetrical betrayal because their power positions differ; Yelena's faction is imprisoning and controlling the others. But it still demonstrates that trust can be strategically manufactured.

## 5.9 Armin / Onyankopon — longitudinal consistency creates provisional credibility

Armin does not erase the possibility of deception. He compares Onyankopon's present denial with earlier speech and behavior. The result is a probabilistic judgment: the euthanasia project is inconsistent with the person he has observed.

This is perhaps the volume's healthiest trust model because it remains evidence-based and revisable rather than theological.

## 5.10 Gabi / Kaya — moral recognition does not create a duty to forgive

Gabi's revision and Kaya's continuing hatred coexist. The relationship therefore blocks a sentimental model in which recognizing the other's humanity automatically heals the victim.

This is a crucial constraint for later synthesis: reconciliation, if it occurs at all, cannot be treated as the logical consequence of epistemic correction.

## 5.11 Gabi / Falco — confession changes attachment from behavioral fact into explicit future claim

Falco's attachment was already obvious through action; V29 makes its content explicit. Gabi also begins reciprocating responsibility rather than taking his protection for granted. Their relationship moves from asymmetric protection toward mutual disclosure at the exact moment bodily danger is greatest.

# 6. Titan/Paths ontology and power mechanics

## 6.1 Royal blood supplies access; Founder possession supplies decisive will

Ksaver's explanation is the most important mechanical clarification in the volume. The royal-blooded Titan is not simply the sovereign operator. Contact may unlock the Founder's power, but the Founder's holder decides how it is used.

Politically, this produces a two-key system with no constitutional procedure. A private pair can potentially decide a population's bodily future.

## 6.2 Zeke's reconstruction provides first-person evidence for a nonordinary body-production process

The unknown girl's shaping of Zeke's body and his temporal disorientation materially strengthen Paths-related ontology. The safest formulation remains phenomenological: Zeke experienced a place/process where an unknown girl rebuilt his body, subjective duration was abnormal, and he interpreted it as `道`.

The girl's identity and the system's complete causal rules remain open.

## 6.3 Spinal-fluid contamination remains latent command infrastructure

V29 does not repeat V28's forest scream but keeps its consequences active. Falco's exposure becomes an urgent battlefield variable. Military contamination is now both organizational and personal: commanders must allocate roles around the possibility that one distant actor can transform soldiers.

## 6.4 The Founder is now both deterrence weapon and reproductive instrument

Yelena's disclosure and Armin's counter-plan compete over the same mechanism. One model uses Founder authority to eliminate reproduction; the other imagines a limited Rumbling demonstration to buy decades.

This is politically important because “control of the Founder” is no longer one policy. The same power supports radically different constitutional futures. The decisive question is therefore not possession alone but **authorized use**.

## 6.5 Titan technology is increasingly embedded in combined-arms war

Marley's invasion demonstrates the transition already anticipated in V23: Titans operate alongside airships, anti-Titan artillery, infantry, rifles, and coordinated signaling. Pieck's Cart/cannon system is especially important because the Titan body is a mobile platform within a larger weapon assemblage.

The old distinction “Titan power versus modern technology” is obsolete. Mature warfare combines them.

# 7. Freedom, salvation, future, and reproductive sovereignty

## 7.1 V29 turns freedom into a problem of trusted delegation

If one actor cannot use the decisive power alone, freedom depends on delegation or partnership. But delegation at this scale is dangerous because the affected population has not delegated anything. Zeke and Eren's private trust relation potentially substitutes for public consent.

The checkpoint's constitutional question therefore sharpens: **who authorizes the authorizer?**

## 7.2 `子供は未来だ` directly opposes euthanasia's definition of mercy

Onyankopon's line compresses a competing moral ontology. Yelena/Zeke define future nonexistence as rescue from inherited suffering. Onyankopon defines children themselves as future.

These are not merely different policies. They disagree about whether the existence of future subjects is a harm to be prevented or a value whose possibilities must remain open.

## 7.3 Falco's ordinary future makes the abstract dispute concrete

Falco wants Gabi to live long and imagines marriage. The euthanasia plan would abolish the category of future he is articulating, while the Warrior system threatens it through shortened Titan inheritance.

V29 therefore places reproductive sovereignty between two coercive systems: one reproduces children as weapons; the other would end reproduction to stop weapon inheritance. Falco's ordinary desire is a third answer: **a future neither instrumentalized by war nor prevented in advance**.

## 7.4 Savior narratives are anti-constitutional shortcuts

Yelena's Zeke, Magath's Helos, and the wider fixation on Eren all demonstrate the temptation to personify collective survival. The savior solves coordination by collapsing debate into allegiance.

The manga does not deny that singular actors may possess singular capabilities. It shows the political danger of converting capability into unquestionable jurisdiction.

# 8. State, military institutions, insurgency, and international war

## 8.1 Paradis has moved from legitimacy crisis to fractured coercive sovereignty

V27 showed military authority and Eren-centered legitimacy diverging. V29 shows the practical result: prisoners, armbands, factional control, contaminated officers, and an external invasion striking during internal disorganization.

The state is not absent. Pixis still commands; soldiers still coordinate. But sovereignty is plural and unstable.

## 8.2 Yelena's order reproduces techniques of the enemy it opposes

Classification by armband is the clearest sign. Again, the historical scale and purpose differ from Marley's long-standing ethnic segregation, but the borrowed technique matters. Under pressure, revolutionary orders often inherit the administrative grammar of prior domination.

## 8.3 Marley's counterattack converts Liberio grief into strategic urgency

Reiner and Porco carry personal retaliation; Magath carries military judgment; the broader operation seeks the Founder. These layers should not be collapsed. State strategy contains people with relationship-specific motives.

## 8.4 Pixis preserves command by differentiating risk rather than pretending unity

His contaminated/clean soldier triage is institutional realism. It also contrasts with savior politics. Instead of relying on one pure actor, Pixis works with a damaged organization and distributes roles according to actual constraints.

## 8.5 The battle demonstrates the cost of internal capture

Paradis faces Marley while many of its capable soldiers have been imprisoned, coerced, poisoned, or divided. The strategic cost of domestic sovereignty breakdown becomes physical. V27's internal political question was not separate from international survival; V29 demonstrates their causal coupling.

# 9. Violence, responsibility, complicity, and forgiveness

## 9.1 Falco's confession expands responsibility beyond intention

Falco did not know the wounded soldier was Eren and did not intend the Liberio attack, but his delivery of letters contributed causally. His guilt is therefore a useful test case for responsibility without intent.

The manga does not provide a simple verdict. It makes causal implication psychologically real while preserving knowledge constraints.

## 9.2 Gabi accepts responsibility without demanding immediate absolution

Gabi apologizes and revises her worldview. Kaya still wants to kill her. The scene refuses to force the victim to ratify the offender's development.

This is ethically stronger than instant forgiveness because it separates moral improvement from entitlement to reconciliation.

## 9.3 Reiner's compassion does not make his violence nonviolent

His `もう眠れ` register changes the meaning of his action but not its material force. A relationship model must therefore distinguish motive, affect, and act.

## 9.4 Deception can protect agency or capture it

Hange lies to keep Levi alive. Armin lies to Yelena to preserve strategic options. Pieck deceives Eren as an enemy combatant. Zeke's wine system deceives subjects into bodily vulnerability. These belong to the same formal category—information asymmetry—but have different agency effects.

The useful ethical distinction is not “lying bad / truth good.” It is whether the deception **defends a subject's capacity to choose under coercion or secretly converts that subject into a tool**.

# 10. Knowledge, testimony, deception, and epistemic method

## 10.1 V29 makes source position inseparable from content

Eren's euthanasia assent, Armin's counter-model, Yelena's salvation explanation, Pieck's apparent betrayal, Onyankopon's denial, and Zeke's Paths account all carry different evidentiary weights because the speaker's position and incentives differ.

The method therefore cannot ask only “what was said?” It must ask:

- what does the speaker know?
- what do they want the listener to believe?
- what power changes if the listener accepts it?
- what prior behavior supports or contradicts the claim?
- what independent material evidence exists?

## 10.2 Armin's Eren model is a case study in Bayesian intimacy

Armin effectively starts with a strong prior built from years of Eren behavior. New explicit testimony sharply conflicts with it. Instead of instant replacement, he seeks a latent variable—strategic deception—that could make both old behavior and new circumstances consistent.

That is rational model repair, but it carries overfitting risk. A close friend can always invent an unseen motive to preserve the old person. V29 responsibly leaves that risk open.

## 10.3 Pieck demonstrates adversarial signaling literacy

Pieck can speak true propositions in service of a false apparent alignment. She knows that deception is strongest when built from real grievances and real motives. Her statement that Marley is untrustworthy is not negated by the fact that she is deceiving Eren.

This is a critical source-reading lesson: **a deceptive discourse can contain true claims**.

## 10.4 Gabi's revision is driven by prediction failure rather than abstract refutation

No one defeats the devil ideology with a perfect historical lecture. The model fails because people repeatedly act in ways the category says they should not. Gabi's epistemic change is therefore cumulative and behavioral.

That makes it especially relevant to character simulation: the strongest evidence for another person's nature is often observed conduct across changing contexts rather than self-description or inherited label.

# 11. Japanese voice and vocabulary

## 11.1 Eren — direct assent, brother address, and combat compression

`オレはやる`, `進み続ける`, and `そうだろ? 兄さん` preserve Eren's direct first-person commitment register while adding unusually intimate kinship address toward Zeke. In battle, `来いよ……ライナー` compresses the relationship and challenge into almost no exposition.

**Reconstruction constraint:** do not infer one terminal ideology from the persistence lexicon alone. The same direct form can serve strategic alliance performance, genuine conviction, or combat challenge.

## 11.2 Zeke — fraternal tenderness inside population-scale certainty

Zeke's `よく一人で耐えたな` and `後はお兄ちゃんに任せろ` are warm, protective, and role-marked. They should not be flattened into sarcastic villain speech. His coercive politics can coexist with authentic brotherly tenderness.

His Paths description is comparatively tentative: `何年も…ような気がする`, `一瞬だったような気もする`, `あれが…「道」…なのか`. This is useful negative evidence against modeling Zeke as categorical about every unknown mechanism.

## 11.3 Armin — hedge in private, performance in public

`嘘だと思う` is methodologically crucial. Armin offers a strong interpretation but grammatically marks it as interpretation. In front of Yelena, however, he can speak in declarative pro-savior terms he does not privately endorse.

A generative model therefore requires **audience-conditioned sincerity**. Armin's words are not equally transparent across interlocutors.

## 11.4 Mikasa — unresolved questions and action-bearing brevity

Her speech focuses on the unresolved why: if Eren is lying, why hurt them; why say he hates her. `そう…置いて行く` makes the scarf decision with minimal elaboration.

Do not generate a long independence manifesto at this boundary. The manga gives action plus unresolved question.

## 11.5 Pieck — dry directness and explicit trust hierarchy

`私はマーレを信じてない` followed by trust in fought-alongside comrades is one of her clearest ideological/relational voice anchors. The statement is neither grandiose nor hesitant.

Her `そこ` reply to Eren is a high-value example of compact tactical wit: literal, adversarial, and action-triggering at once.

## 11.6 Gabi — category collapse expressed through simpler nouns

`悪魔なんていなかった` / `人がいるだけ` is powerful partly because the replacement vocabulary is simpler than the ideology it destroys. Her speech moves from categorical accusation to person-level recognition and apology.

This does not mean all future Gabi speech should become soft or self-effacing. The new state remains intense and direct.

## 11.7 Falco — confession is concrete, future-oriented, and relational

Falco does not romanticize through elaborate metaphor. He says he likes Gabi, explains the Warrior-candidate motive, and names practical future goods: long life, marriage, shared future. His voice remains concrete even at maximum emotional exposure.

## 11.8 Onyankopon — moral argument through future language

`子供は未来だ!!` is a concise ideological anchor consistent with his earlier pluralist register. Unlike Yelena's savior theology, Onyankopon's argument centers the future subjects themselves rather than the greatness of a chosen actor.

## 11.9 Reiner — exhaustion language becomes compassion language

`もういい`, `これ以上誰も苦しめなくていい`, `もう眠れ` carries his own history into the way he addresses Eren. The register should be tagged as high-stakes empathic/hostile state, not ordinary Reiner speech.

## 11.10 Yelena and Magath — rival savior vocabularies

Yelena speaks of world salvation through Zeke/Eren. Magath invokes `真の英雄ヘーロス`. The shared lexical field is politically important: “saving the world” is not owned by one ideology and should always be source-tagged by speaker, audience, and institutional project.

### Lexical network after V29

- `信頼 / 信じる` — necessary collaboration, comrade loyalty, interpersonal model confidence, and exploitable faith.
- `救う / 救済` — euthanasia, world salvation, military heroism, and protecting future children; never morally uniform.
- `進む` — Eren/Zeke forward-motion language; insufficient alone to establish shared terminal aim.
- `子供 / 未来` — adult responsibility, Falco's ordinary future, Onyankopon futurity, euthanasia's negation.
- `悪魔 / 人` — Gabi's explicit ontological revision.
- `嘘` — Armin's hypothesis about Eren; deception as an active explanatory variable.
- `騙し討ち` — tactical surprise and broader epistemic structure.

# 12. Manga form and visual architecture

## 12.1 The baseball is a trust object that cannot complete contact

The object travels from Ksaver's relational world into Zeke's alliance with Eren. But because Zeke and Eren cannot safely touch, the baseball substitutes for handshake/contact. Visually, trust is passed while activation is deferred.

The object therefore joins scarf, key, photograph, and book as a material carrier of relation and worldview.

## 12.2 Paths-like space breaks ordinary panel-time continuity

The unknown-girl sequence uses a radically different spatial field from the crowded political/military pages. Sparse horizon and bodily reconstruction externalize Zeke's report that subjective years and an instant may coexist.

The form does not prove a complete metaphysics, but it marks the experience as ontologically discontinuous from ordinary battlefield time.

## 12.3 `天地` is staged through vertical warfare

Roofs, floors, subterranean Porco, descending Marleyan soldiers, airships, and upward/downward sightlines make heaven/earth a literal battle architecture. The supposedly transcendent “world-saving” projects are continuously returned to bodies on structures and streets.

## 12.4 Pieck's pointing finger converts conversational space into target geometry

The roof scene uses gesture as causal bridge. Eren asks for the enemy's location; the panel makes Pieck's pointing body the answer before Porco erupts from below. Dialogue, vector, and attack plane become one composition.

## 12.5 Reiner/Eren combat is framed as intimate opposition rather than anonymous spectacle

Repeated close views and direct address keep recognition present inside Titan-scale violence. The formal effect is to prevent giant-body abstraction from erasing the persons who know one another.

## 12.6 Mikasa's scarf removal works through object absence

The meaning is not only in a spoken declaration but in the break of a long visual continuity. An object habitually attached to her body is separated from it. The panel can therefore carry state transition without explanatory monologue.

## 12.7 The final Cart feint punishes visible-body inference

The composition encourages a false conclusion from the apparent condition of the Cart before revealing continued cannon capacity. This formally enacts the volume's epistemic thesis: the visible surface may not disclose the operative system.

# 13. Bodies, technology, logistics, and material politics

## 13.1 Bodies remain infrastructure

Royal blood, Founder possession, spinal fluid, Titan inheritance, and reproductive capacity all make bodies political infrastructure. V29's central trust question exists because access to world-changing power is distributed across particular bodies.

## 13.2 Combined-arms Marley reveals the diminishing isolation of Titan power

Air transport, rifles, artillery, mobile Cart platform, Jaw attack, and Armored combat are integrated. The island's older Titan-centric threat model is insufficient.

## 13.3 Prison release is a logistics decision as much as a moral one

The Jaegerists cannot defend Paradis alone. The prisoners are needed. This restores action space to people previously treated as political threats because battlefield necessity changes the coalition structure.

## 13.4 Pixis's ODM allocation is a scarce-resource ethics

Giving clean soldiers priority recognizes transformation risk while refusing to discard contaminated personnel. Material allocation reveals how command values lives under uncertainty.

## 13.5 Falco's body becomes a contested future before transformation occurs

His spinal-fluid exposure means Zeke's possible scream can override every personal plan he has just articulated. The contrast between confession of ordinary future and remote bodily vulnerability is deliberate and cruel.

# 14. Motifs, callbacks, and longitudinal echoes

## 14.1 The outside-world book has become a dispute over whether future people should exist

Early freedom imagined seas and horizons. By V29, the question has moved from reaching the world to deciding whether one's people should have descendants inside it. The scale of “outside” has become temporal as well as spatial.

## 14.2 The scarf and baseball are rival relationship objects

The scarf carries rescue/home/attachment from Eren to Mikasa. The baseball carries surrogate-parent/project trust from Ksaver to Zeke and then toward Eren. In V29 one object is left behind while the other is passed forward.

The contrast is not a simple separation-versus-union symbol. It shows relationships being actively renegotiated through material objects.

## 14.3 `進み続ける` is now a contested inheritance

Eren uses forward-motion language in apparent agreement with Zeke; Zeke later speaks of moving forward with Eren. Earlier volumes tied the phrase to Eren's self-authorship and even to Reiner's transmitted persistence. V29 proves that shared vocabulary can conceal divergent ends.

## 14.4 “Hero who saves the world” returns on opposing sides

Yelena's savior theology and Magath's Helos invocation reveal that war narratives converge on similar legitimating forms. The identity of the hero changes; the political need for singular salvation persists.

## 14.5 The forest principle survives without the literal forest

Nile sends a child home. Onyankopon calls children the future. Falco imagines marriage rather than inheritance. Gabi recognizes people rather than devils. These are all V29 continuations of the V28 question: can adults and institutions stop passing unfinished violence downward?

# 15. Counterreadings, adversarial tests, and limitations

## 15.1 Counterreading: Eren's euthanasia assent should simply be treated as sincere because the text states it directly

**Strength:** direct speech is high-value evidence, and Eren uses emotionally congruent persistence/brother vocabulary.

**Limit:** the interaction is also structurally a trust negotiation in which Eren needs Zeke's access. V29 itself later supplies Armin's mechanically grounded deception hypothesis. Therefore sincerity is plausible but not closed.

**Adjudication:** **OPEN.** Preserve direct-speech evidence and strategic-deception hypothesis simultaneously.

## 15.2 Counterreading: Armin “knows Eren,” so his interpretation is effectively authoritative

**Strength:** Armin has unusually long behavioral evidence and identifies a real contradiction between euthanasia and prior Eren values.

**Limit:** intimacy can produce motivated reasoning. Armin wants Eren to remain recoverable and has incomplete information.

**Adjudication:** Armin's model is **strongly plausible character inference, not objective fact**.

## 15.3 Counterreading: Pieck's distrust of Marley means she is politically anti-Marley in every operational sense

**Strength:** her statement is explicit and her rights argument rejects the state's ideological bargain.

**Limit:** she continues fighting with Marleyan comrades and coordinates an attack under Magath because her trust and objectives are relationally/instrumentally cross-cutting.

**Adjudication:** reject binary loyalty modeling. Pieck can distrust the state and remain committed to comrades and shared military objectives.

## 15.4 Counterreading: Gabi's `悪魔なんていなかった` completes her redemption/reconciliation arc

**Strength:** it is a major cognitive and moral transition with behavioral evidence.

**Limit:** Kaya remains furious; Gabi remains implicated in Sasha's death; wider war structures are unchanged.

**Adjudication:** **REVISE worldview, do not declare reconciliation complete.**

## 15.5 Counterreading: Mikasa leaving the scarf means she has definitively rejected Eren

**Strength:** the scarf is a load-bearing relational object and leaving it is deliberate.

**Limit:** she immediately continues acting inside a crisis shaped by concern for Eren and has not supplied a final interpretation of the relationship.

**Adjudication:** meaningful temporary distancing / agency assertion; terminal meaning **OPEN**.

## 15.6 Counterreading: Zeke's Paths experience confirms all of his prior metaphysical claims

**Strength:** he has direct extraordinary experience and bodily reconstruction is materially real.

**Limit:** he himself uses tentative language and the identity/rules of the unknown girl/place remain unexplained.

**Adjudication:** strengthen Paths/body-network hypothesis; reject total cosmological closure.

## 15.7 Counterreading: deception is the volume's moral evil

**Strength:** wine, ambushes, and political secrecy have catastrophic consequences.

**Limit:** Hange's lie protects Levi; Armin's performance protects strategic autonomy; Pieck's feint is an enemy combat tactic rather than secret population redesign.

**Adjudication:** deception is morally non-uniform. Agency effect and power asymmetry are the more useful criteria.

# 17. What V29 changes in the series-so-far model

## STRENGTHEN

### 17.1 Trust must be modeled as evidence plus transferred power

V29 strongly strengthens the checkpoint's constitutional account of freedom. Trust is not merely an emotion. Ksaver/Zeke/Eren show that trust can transfer world-changing decision power. Pieck and Armin show that trust also structures which testimony survives institutional collapse.

Current formulation: **a relationship becomes politically consequential when one person's belief in another changes who gets to decide for third parties.**

### 17.2 Recognition is increasingly epistemic

Gabi's change is driven by prediction failure: the “devil” category cannot explain actual people. Pieck evaluates Marley against comrades. Armin evaluates current Eren speech against longitudinal Eren behavior.

Recognition therefore means more than moral humanization. It changes source weighting.

### 17.3 Ordinary future desire remains a critical anti-instrumentalization baseline

Falco's marriage/long-life wish, Nile sending him home, and Onyankopon's `子供は未来だ` all strengthen the V28 forest thesis. Political systems are best evaluated partly by whether ordinary futures remain possible.

### 17.4 Saviorhood is a recurring legitimacy shortcut across factions

Yelena's divine Zeke/Eren and Magath's Helos show that mutually opposed systems can use the same hero grammar. This strengthens the need to separate strategic indispensability from legitimate authority.

## REVISE

### 17.5 Eren's current motive model must become explicitly multi-hypothesis

**REVISE.** Before V29, Eren's relation to Zeke was unresolved. V29 supplies direct euthanasia assent but also a mechanically plausible deception reconstruction from Armin.

Current formulation: **Eren's explicit speech supports alignment; his incentives and prior model support strategic deception; neither can yet be privileged into settled fact.**

### 17.6 Pieck should no longer be modeled primarily through “Marley loyalty”

**REVISE strongly.** V29 gives an explicit trust hierarchy. She distrusts Marley but trusts comrades and pursues Eldian rights through self-directed reasoning.

Current formulation: **institutional participation is subordinate to relationship loyalty, family obligation, and strategic judgment.**

### 17.7 Gabi's enemy model has crossed from destabilization to explicit replacement

**REVISE.** V28 showed `わからない`; V29 gives `悪魔なんていなかった…人がいるだけ` and corresponding protective behavior.

Current formulation: **the inherited devil ontology is rejected at the person-category level, while grief, guilt, and non-forgiveness remain active.**

### 17.8 Armin's dialogue orientation must include strategic deception

**REVISE.** Earlier models that treat dialogue as transparent sincerity are insufficient. V29 demonstrates deliberate audience management under coercive conditions.

Current formulation: **Armin prefers understanding/communication but can lie, perform, and withhold when doing so preserves the possibility of a less catastrophic plan.**

## DOWNGRADE

### 17.9 “Latest explicit speech should dominate intimate historical evidence when modeling motive”

**DOWNGRADE as a general inference rule.** V29 makes this epistemic shortcut unsafe. Eren's explicit speech is important, but the story itself gives Armin a reason to treat it as potentially strategic. Current speech should be weighted heavily, not treated as motive transparency by default.

## REJECT

### 17.10 “Volunteer affiliation is a reliable proxy for euthanasia alignment”

**REJECT.** Onyankopon's prior values and direct denial contradict it, and Armin finds that contradiction credible. The Volunteer category must be decomposed by person/network involvement.

# 21. Volume thesis and questions carried forward

Volume 29 begins with Zeke requiring a trusted person and ends with almost every major actor depending on a model of another person's intent.

Zeke trusts Eren to use the Founder. Yelena trusts saviors. Pieck distrusts Marley but trusts comrades. Eren trusts his reading of Pieck's constraints. Reiner reads Eren through himself. Armin distrusts Eren's words because he trusts a longer behavioral model. Mikasa cannot yet decide what Eren's cruelty means. Armin asks the group to trust Onyankopon's history over his category. Gabi finally trusts the evidence of people over inherited devil ontology. Falco exposes his future because he may be running out of time.

The volume's deepest move is therefore to make **trust a political technology**.

Trust can preserve personhood because it allows accumulated behavior to resist dehumanizing categories. It can preserve agency because Hange, Armin, or Pieck can use relational knowledge to outmaneuver coercion. But it can also scale private certainty into public domination. If Zeke's trust in Eren authorizes two brothers to decide whether an entire people can have children, interpersonal confidence has become sovereignty.

The V27 checkpoint's constitutional question thus acquires an epistemic clause:

> **Freedom among plural actors requires not only limits on who may spend another person's body or future, but revisable procedures for deciding whose account of necessity, loyalty, identity, and salvation deserves belief.**

V29 does not supply those procedures institutionally. It supplies character-level approximations: compare words against behavior; distinguish state from comrades; preserve uncertainty; test models against constraints; revise categories when people falsify them; do not confuse understanding with forgiveness; and do not confuse trust with legitimate authority.

The strongest carried-forward questions are:

1. Is Eren's euthanasia assent genuine, strategic, or mixed?
2. Is Armin reconstructing Eren accurately or protecting an obsolete intimate model?
3. Can Mikasa preserve attachment while reclaiming authority over what that attachment means?
4. Will Zeke's extraordinary private experience deepen insight or harden epistemic monopoly?
5. Can Paradis recover institutional decision-making while survival depends on Eren/Zeke contact?
6. Can Gabi's person-level recognition survive renewed large-scale battle and unresolved victim anger?
7. What happens when Falco's ordinary future desire collides with the remote bodily power created by spinal-fluid exposure?
