# 『進撃の巨人』Volume 33 Deep Reading
## Freedom as collision: desire beyond necessity, inherited guilt, and the limits of adversarial permission

## 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 32 made responsibility expensive. The anti-Rumbling coalition could no longer define itself through innocence: its members lied to former comrades, killed people they knew, accepted that cooperation did not imply forgiveness, and acted without possessing a guaranteed political solution for Paradis. Volume 33 asks what freedom means after that cost has already been accepted.

The governing thesis is:

> **Freedom becomes adversarial permission rather than reciprocal politics: Eren refuses to remove his friends' power to resist him, but treats their freedom to oppose him as sufficient justification for forcing a lethal collision while the people outside that contest are denied any comparable agency.**

A sharper operational formulation is:

> **V33 separates respecting another person's capacity to choose from respecting the jurisdiction of everyone affected by one's choice. Eren can genuinely love his friends, preserve their powers, and tell them that they are free while still unilaterally deciding the fate of the outside world. The coalition answers not from moral purity but from guilt, attachment, and an increasingly explicit belief that responsibility requires limiting what one's own fear and love may authorize.**

This extends the post-checkpoint sequence:

- V27: freedom becomes constitutional;
- V28: inherited salvation becomes a question of who gets children out of the forest;
- V29: trust becomes operational infrastructure under deception;
- V30: freedom becomes a struggle over causal authorship;
- V31: freedom becomes responsibility after complicity;
- V32: responsibility becomes betrayal when one's own side demands unlimited permission to survive;
- V33: **freedom becomes collision when actors preserve one another's capacity to resist but no longer share a constitutional limit on what may be done to everyone else.**

The volume also makes Eren's motive architecture harder in a productive way. V31 established the operational objective of exterminating life outside Paradis. V32 established that particular love for Historia and his friends can coexist with that objective. V33 adds a private confession that cannot be reduced to defensive necessity: when Eren learned that humanity existed beyond the Walls, he was `ガッカリした`—disappointed. He knows the outside population is vastly larger than Paradis, knows the moral arithmetic does not vindicate him, saves Ramzi in the immediate moment, apologizes to him in tears, and still proceeds along a path that later kills him. Remorse and desire therefore coexist. The deep-reading model must resist two symmetric simplifications: Eren is neither a purely reluctant machine forced by circumstances nor a purely affectless exterminator for whom those circumstances and attachments are irrelevant.

Armin receives the volume's answering freedom image. His long-standing attraction to what lies beyond the Walls survives the destruction of the simple childhood map. The outside world did not match the dream he and Eren once shared, yet Armin still wants to believe that something unknown remains beyond what they have already seen. V33 therefore contrasts two responses to disappointment. Eren experiences the populated world as the collapse of an imagined empty horizon and moves toward clearing it. Armin experiences the failure of an imagined world and keeps the category of the unknown open.

The final page turns that philosophical difference into an explicit challenge. After the coalition descends onto the Founder body, the text promises to ask Eren again, after dragging him out, `「君のどこが自由なのか」って`. Freedom is no longer allowed to remain Eren's self-certifying vocabulary. It becomes a claim subject to interrogation by other people.

## 3. Chapter-by-chapter close reading

### 3.1 Chapter 131 — 「地鳴らし」: the freedom dream confesses its hidden demand

Chapter 131 begins not from the coalition but from people already condemned by Eren's decision. Ramzi and Halil appear in the Marley city among refugees and petty economic survival. The choice is important. The Rumbling is not introduced here as a strategic map effect or a distant military abstraction. The chapter gives it named civilian faces before returning to Eren's memory of Ramzi.

Eren sees Ramzi being beaten for theft and initially tries to walk away. The hesitation is not indifference. It is produced by knowledge. Eren knows that saving one boy in the street while moving toward a future in which that boy will be killed by the Rumbling creates an unbearable contradiction. The immediate rescue would let Eren experience himself as compassionate even though he already understands the later catastrophe in which he will participate.

He turns back anyway.

This is a critical negative control for any model that treats Eren's future violence as proof that local empathy has vanished. He cannot ignore the suffering in front of him. He intervenes, carries Ramzi away, and later breaks down in front of a child who cannot understand his language. The problem is precisely that **local empathy survives**.

Eren's internal reasoning then refuses a clean defensive justification. He considers the demographic scale. If Paradis disappears, the number of deaths would be smaller than if the outside world is destroyed. He understands that the arithmetic does not make his chosen outcome morally preferable by aggregate life count. He also says that he cannot accept the destruction of Paradis. Defensive commitment is therefore real, but it does not exhaust the scene.

The decisive confession is the line that he was disappointed after discovering humanity outside the Walls: `壁の外に人類が生きてると知って…オレは…ガッカリした`.

This line revises the motive model more deeply than a simple confession of hatred would. The source does not say that disappointment is the only cause of the Rumbling. Eren's political fear, protection of the island, future-memory structure, hatred of inherited captivity, and attachment to friends remain materially evidenced. But V33 now makes it unsafe to model extermination as only a reluctantly chosen instrument. An old desire is implicated: the imagined outside had functioned as a horizon of unclaimed freedom, and the existence of other people meant that horizon was already occupied by histories, societies, borders, enemies, and obligations.

Eren's apology to Ramzi is therefore not exculpatory remorse. It is high-quality evidence that the person who wants the path and the person horrified by its victims are not separable into neat moral selves. He tells Ramzi that he is sorry while crying, knowing the boy cannot understand the confession. The apology does not transfer agency to Ramzi and does not change the outcome. It is structurally private: Eren receives the emotional release of confession from someone who cannot consent to the project or even understand the words.

The chapter then makes the future present. Ramzi and Halil run before the Wall Titans. The money the boys have been accumulating scatters. Bodies are not transformed into military statistics; they are crushed within the same visual field that had made Ramzi a specific object of Eren's pity.

Against this, child Eren appears above the clouds and declares `自由だ`.

The image is one of the strongest formal contradictions in the series. The child's viewpoint is elevated above steam and carnage. The world appears empty, open, and cloudlike precisely because the bodies underneath have disappeared from that visual horizon. Freedom is represented as a perceptual achievement produced by occlusion. This does not authorize a literal claim that adult Eren has simply become his childhood self or is unaware of the killing; the preceding pages prove the opposite. The safer reading is representational: the manga places the childhood dream of an unobstructed world inside the realized mechanism that clears obstructing people from view.

The child calls to Armin. That matters because the original outside-world dream was relational rather than purely solitary. Yet the person who shared it is no longer experiencing freedom the same way.

The shipboard conversation between Armin and Annie makes the contrast explicit through ordinary intimacy. Annie describes the years in crystal as a peculiar deprivation: she lacked normal bodily participation but remained conscious enough for loneliness to become severe. Hitch's and Armin's voices were among the few recurring contacts she had, and she says listening became something she anticipated.

She asks why Armin continued speaking to someone who could not answer. Armin's response is direct and unusually vulnerable: `会いたかったからだ…アニに`—he wanted to see Annie.

The blushes, pauses, and Annie's reaction provide strong mutual-attraction evidence, but the V33 boundary does not require inflating this into a completed romantic partnership. What is already clear is that Annie is no longer merely an enemy object in Armin's model. Years of one-sided conversation had relational content for both parties despite radically asymmetric embodiment.

Annie describes Armin as a good person who would talk even to an enemy and links that disposition to his continuing wish to speak to Eren. Armin rejects the flattering category. He has killed soldiers and children, betrayed islanders who raised him, and killed comrades. `僕もとっくにバケモノだよ` is not theatrical self-hatred detached from function; it is a refusal to place himself outside the moral ledger merely because his current goal is to stop genocide.

That line should be held beside V32's coalition structure. Armin's commitment to dialogue does not arise because he thinks himself purer than violent people. It persists after he has accepted his own capacity for violence.

Armin then returns to the outside-world dream. He admits that some part of him still hoped that he and Eren might one day travel an unknown world together. Annie points out that the outside did not turn out to be the unknown world they imagined. Armin nevertheless says he wants to believe there is still something beyond the Walls that they do not know.

This becomes the chapter's alternative freedom model:

- Eren's disappointed horizon responds to an occupied world by wanting the obstruction removed;
- Armin's disappointed horizon responds to failed expectation by keeping uncertainty open.

Neither has the childhood map anymore. The difference is what each does when reality refuses to match it.

### 3.2 Chapter 132 — 「自由の翼」: command is transferred as an obligation to keep understanding

Chapter 132 begins with time, machinery, and an approaching impossible body. The flying boat has to be prepared while the Rumbling closes on Odiha. Strategic reasoning is explicitly provisional: the group infers likely paths and military objectives, and the surviving plan depends on getting the aircraft airborne before the Wall Titans arrive.

Annie then changes her state. She tells Mikasa that she does not intend to board the flying boat. Her explanation is not a new pacifist doctrine. `人類を救うとか 私には…よくわからない` is consistent with her longstanding refusal of grand ideological language. The category “humanity” has never functioned for Annie as a natural object of devotion; she was born into a system in which that humanity persecuted Eldians, turned children into weapons, and made her father-return objective the organizing reason for participation.

If Liberio and her father are already lost, the motive that repeatedly pulled her through violence no longer provides the same direction. She says `私はもう戦えない` and wants whatever time remains to be peaceful. This is a state transition, not evidence that earlier willingness to fight was false.

Her conversation with Mikasa further clarifies Mikasa's V31-V32 trajectory. Annie asks whether Mikasa intends to kill Eren to save humanity. Mikasa answers `殺さない`. Her stated intention is to bring back Eren, who has gone far away. The scarf remains in her possession but is not being worn.

These details should remain separate:

1. Mikasa has reclaimed custody of the scarf after Louise and has not discarded the relationship object.
2. She is choosing not to wear it at this moment.
3. She opposes Eren's massacre.
4. She does not currently define the mission as killing Eren.
5. She wants him to return.

None of those facts, at V33, settles whether such a return is possible or what Mikasa would do if the alternatives narrow further.

Levi's reentry is physically constrained but cognitively meaningful. His injuries remain severe, but he participates in target inference and later in command. The model should not transform consciousness and motive into restored combat capacity; the volume itself keeps those variables distinct.

Floch's final intervention extends the island-survival ideology beyond defeat. He has clung to the ship, fires on the aircraft, and damages the fuel system. His dying speech does not reveal that his prior politics were merely opportunistic. He continues to believe that the people of Paradis will be slaughtered if Eren is stopped and frames Eren as the island's devil and hope.

That consistency matters. Floch can be wrong about what unlimited violence is permitted and still possess a coherent terminal fear. The ethical argument is stronger if his fear is treated as real rather than dismissed as counterfeit.

The damage creates the chapter's decisive time constraint. The flying boat cannot immediately leave, the Rumbling is arriving, and no one can buy the necessary time without facing the Wall Titans directly.

Armin volunteers.

Reiner rejects that solution because Armin's Colossal power is the coalition's major remaining weapon against Eren. Hange then claims the cost as the current commander. The wording is explicit: Hange brought everyone this far and continued after many comrades were killed; Hange will settle that responsibility.

The succession scene is one of the most important command moments in the corpus. Hange appoints Armin the fifteenth commander of the Survey Corps and names the qualification that matters:

`調査兵団団長に求められる資質は 理解することをあきらめない姿勢にある`.

The office is defined not by charisma, martial dominance, noble birth, purity, or possession of correct answers. It is defined by a **posture toward uncertainty**: refusing to give up trying to understand.

This is a mature institutional answer to the series' epistemic history. Erwin pursued truth through terrible command tradeoffs. Hange turned unknown bodies and technologies into questions. Armin repeatedly reconstructs failed models. At the moment the Survey Corps as an intact institution is nearly gone, its transferable constitutional principle becomes continued understanding.

Hange's farewell with Levi compresses their relationship into a tiny ritual. Hange says the moment has finally come. Levi, whose relationship to Survey Corps slogans has never been a matter of uncomplicated rhetoric, presses his fist to Hange's chest and says `心臓を捧げよ`. Hange notes that it is the first time Levi has said it to them.

The scene is powerful precisely because Levi remains terse. The register does not become a speech about decades of friendship or grief. The unusual use of the institutional phrase carries the relational evidence.

Hange then confronts the Wall Titans alone. The action is tactically instrumental—they have to slow the advance long enough for the aircraft to launch—but it is also character-complete at the V33 boundary. While fighting the largest possible manifestation of the object that has occupied Hange's curiosity for years, Hange still says, in effect, that Titans are wonderful: `…やっぱり巨人って 素晴らしいな`.

Curiosity survives terror, disillusionment, command failure, political collapse, and imminent death. This should not be mistaken for approval of the Rumbling. It is identity persistence: the capacity for astonishment that coexisted with scientific discipline from the early corpus remains available even at the end of Hange's observable life.

Hange succeeds in buying the time. The flying boat launches. Levi's `じゃあな ハンジ` is again minimal and sufficient.

The chapter closes with Hange in a liminal image among Erwin and other dead Survey Corps members. Erwin tells Hange that the role was fulfilled; Hange immediately turns the reunion back toward the difficulty of command and the desire to tell everyone what happened.

At V33, the correct ontological claim is modest. The manga **depicts** an after-death or liminal reunion. That is strong evidence about the scene's emotional grammar and Hange's relationship to the Survey Corps dead. It is not by itself a complete metaphysical model of an afterlife, Paths, souls, or postmortem consciousness.

The chapter title `自由の翼` therefore lands differently from its early-series heroic usage. The Wings of Freedom are no longer primarily an emblem of charging beyond the Walls. They become a chain of responsibility transferred from dead to living: Hange gives Armin the office, the epistemic obligation, and enough time to continue.

### 3.3 Chapter 133 — 「罪人達」: guilt becomes a basis for coalition, while Eren turns freedom into the right to collide

The new commander enters his office without ceremony. The aircraft is already in crisis and the plan is incomplete. The coalition has no confirmed location for Eren's human body within the enormous Founder structure. Armin's Colossal transformation could destroy a huge area, but he treats it as a final option after exhausting the possibility of talking to Eren.

That ordering matters after the harbor. Armin is not a doctrinal pacifist and no longer believes dialogue guarantees success. He is willing to manipulate, deceive, kill, and consider catastrophic force. Yet he continues to rank communicative access above irreversible destruction when a window remains.

Levi supplies a narrower objective: kill Zeke if possible. The causal model is explicitly uncertain and descended from Hange's working hypothesis; Zeke's position is unknown. Levi nevertheless asks the others for help and says he will finish Zeke. The scene preserves an important separation between bodily impairment and objective continuity. His body may not permit the old level of action, but the Zeke mission remains cognitively organized.

The next conversation binds the coalition through crime rather than absolution. Connie cannot get Samuel and Daz out of his mind. He killed faces he knew. Jean now understands Reiner, Bertolt, and Annie from a position he once could not occupy: being called a traitor while believing one's violence is required to save people.

Reiner does not accept this convergence as forgiveness. He says his sins are beyond atonement and that even if the remaining human population is saved, he cannot forgive himself. The operative conclusion is modest: then at least save the people who remain.

Connie agrees that the impossibility of atonement does not remove the obligation to act.

Jean tells Reiner, `俺達は同じだ…ライナー`. The phrase must be modeled structurally rather than flattened into moral equivalence. Jean is not saying that every causal history and every killing has become identical. He is saying that he has crossed the threshold into killing people in the name of saving other people and can no longer maintain the earlier clean outside position from which Reiner's contradiction seemed uniquely monstrous.

This is V33's mature dirty-hands formulation:

> **Guilt may destroy the right to self-righteousness without destroying the obligation to choose.**

The group then examines a troubling fact. Eren has command over Subjects of Ymir and Titan power, yet their shifter abilities remain available. Armin infers that Eren is intentionally allowing them to act freely. Reiner develops a separate hypothesis: perhaps Eren wants the Founder transferred, or perhaps he wants someone to stop him.

Both claims are actor hypotheses at this point. The source does not yet convert Reiner's speculation into narrator fact.

The coalition is abruptly brought into Paths, where a child-form Eren and Ymir appear. This gives them the communication window Armin wanted.

The appeal is strikingly non-righteous. Armin and the others say that enough destruction has occurred to deter the world for a long time. They argue that Paradis can now be protected without exterminating every remaining person. More importantly, they accept shared blame: they were wrong and helped push Eren to this point. Reiner asks Eren not to massacre more people for their sake. Annie admits that Sasha's death made her hate Eren, yet she also recognizes how unbearable Eren's position must have been. Mikasa says she wants to share the weight of his sins because she carries the same sin and asks him not to push them away. Her request condenses to `帰ってきて…`.

Levi's participation retains his idiosyncratic register rather than becoming sentimental. Even in this metaphysical communication space he attempts to reduce the conflict to a concrete, almost abrasive interpersonal possibility rather than deliver a grand moral speech.

Eren's answer closes the negotiation.

`地鳴らしは止まらない`.

He will not leave Paradis's future to chance. He will continue moving forward.

At first the child form seems to disappear, and Armin desperately asks why Eren left their Titan powers intact if he did not intend to trust them or talk. Eren then states the rule that defines V33:

`オレは自由を手に入れるため 世界から自由を奪う`

and

`だが お前らからは何も奪わない お前達は自由だ`.

The distinction is deliberate. Eren is willing to take freedom from “the world” in order to obtain his own freedom, but he will not take freedom from these particular people. They are free to defend the world's freedom; he is free to continue advancing.

This looks superficially like respect for autonomy. It is not nothing. Eren does not simply use Founder authority to neutralize his friends, rewrite their capacities, or force obedience. Their resistance remains materially possible because he permits their shifter power and does not preempt their choice.

But the structure is not reciprocal politics. The people being trampled are not offered the same freedom. The rule applies inside a privileged relationship circle: Eren and his friends may exercise opposed convictions, while the external population becomes the object over which those convictions collide.

Eren names the endpoint: so long as mutually unyielding beliefs remain, they will collide. `オレ達がやることは ただ一つ 戦え`.

This is why “Eren preserves his friends' freedom” cannot be elevated into a universal ethical defense of his project. V33 distinguishes **non-domination of chosen intimates** from **recognition of universal standing**.

Armin tries to keep communication open, but Eren says discussion is unnecessary. If they want to stop him, they must do it physically. The coalition returns to the aircraft and Armin recognizes that the hope of negotiation has been extinguished. Levi's response is immediate: `どうする？ 団長`.

The address matters. Armin's new status is not ceremonial. The failure of his preferred communicative route becomes his first diagnostic command problem.

The chapter then cuts to the ship carrying Kiyomi, Annie, Gabi, Falco, and others. The quieter setting supplies a different form of responsibility. Kiyomi admits that she helped connect Zeke and Eren and that there is no technique capable of paying back the mass killing that followed. She also acknowledges the Azumabito pursuit of family profit and status: they lost sight of what mattered before losing it.

Her question—why people only notice after loss—echoes the entire volume's adult-responsibility structure. Kiyomi suggests that what matters may be a willingness to respect others without reducing them to profit and loss.

Annie silently reviews memories of her father and the people with whom she lived and fought. The sequence is visually important because her “I cannot fight” state is not represented as absence of attachment. She has more than one relational world now.

Falco introduces a new embodied possibility. Because he became a Titan through Zeke's spinal fluid, he believes Beast characteristics have appeared in his Jaw. He reports an inherited memory of flying above the clouds and has the strong feeling that he may be able to do the same.

The epistemic quality must remain cautious. This is not yet established mastery. Falco has a memory, a mechanistic hypothesis, and a bodily intuition. Gabi immediately wants to act on it. Annie objects that a failed transformation could sink the ship and kill everyone, reminding the reader of Falco's loss of control in V32. The risk model is explicit.

Kiyomi chooses the attempt anyway. If continuing passively only increases regret, she is willing to risk the ship. This is not mathematical proof that the attempt will work. It is a decision under catastrophic option scarcity.

The chapter closes on a train carrying Marleyan and Eldian families toward Fort Salta while the last aerial forces mobilize. The old segregated political categories are physically packed into one survival space. Parents speak about children still fighting elsewhere. Karina Braun, who once needed Reiner's Warrior success to legitimate family identity, now regrets making him carry that burden and says the armband obtained through sacrificing everything for her child should be thrown away. Her retrospective realization is late, but analytically important: status that once appeared to protect the child is reclassified as the instrument through which the child was consumed.

### 3.4 Chapter 134 — 「絶望の淵にて」: at the abyss, adults finally name the machinery of hatred

Chapter 134 widens the frame before returning to the protagonists. The Rumbling advances through multiple civilian settings with no clean distinction between military and nonmilitary life. Industrial city, refugee crowd, prayer, home, and landscape all become parts of the same approaching front.

The most important visual sequence occurs at a cliff. A mass of civilians is driven toward the edge by the advancing Wall Titans. People fall. A mother holding an infant is pushed into the crush. The baby does not fall with her. Instead, hands lift it upward and pass it backward through the crowd.

The image is a direct counterpoint to the hereditary systems that have dominated the manga:

- children have been handed down Titan burdens;
- royal bodies have been treated as reproductive infrastructure;
- Warrior families have turned children into instruments of ethnic redemption;
- parents have pushed missions into children;
- states have assigned inherited guilt.

Here adults and strangers pass forward only the child's life. They cannot save themselves, but their last coordinated act attempts to move a future person away from the edge.

The scene should not be sentimentalized into proof that humanity has transcended violence. It occurs because civilization has already failed catastrophically. Its analytical force is narrower: under total collapse, human beings still display a capacity for future-directed care that Eren's world-erasure policy cannot represent if the outside population is treated as a single enemy mass.

At Fort Salta, Marleyan military personnel and Eldian families see the Rumbling arrive. The remaining aerial fleet becomes the last large-scale military countermeasure. A Marleyan officer addresses soldiers and refugees over the loudspeaker.

His speech is one of the strongest institutional confessions in the late corpus. He says the burden on the soldiers' shoulders cannot be calculated and that, whatever happens, responsibility does not belong to them alone. `この責任は 我々すべての大人達にある`.

The category “adults” is doing substantial work. It interrupts the recurring practice of placing the historical problem onto children and then demanding that those children redeem it.

The officer then identifies the political mechanism. They used hatred, kept cultivating hatred, believed hatred could save them, and dumped the problems they could not solve onto the `悪魔の島`. The result is the monster now returning the hatred they continued to give.

This is not narrator endorsement of a simplistic formula in which Marley alone “caused” Eren and therefore Eren bears no agency. V30-V33 have made Eren's self-authorship, desire, choices, and causal participation too explicit for that. The speech instead adds an institutional causal layer: individual responsibility and structural responsibility coexist.

The officer's vow is prospective and therefore fragile. If they somehow see a future again, he swears they must not repeat the same mistakes. He asks the people present to swear goodbye to the age of mutual hatred and to begin a world in which they care for one another.

The source does not yet show whether such a world can be built. At the `絶望の淵`, the moral insight has arrived before the political capacity to implement it. That delay is itself part of the tragedy.

The aerial assault begins. Bombs fall toward the Founder structure and the Wall Titans. For a moment the remaining refugees witness the possibility of military intervention.

A Beast-Titan-shaped body appears on the Founder structure and destroys the airships with thrown projectiles. At the V33 boundary, it is important not to over-specify the ontology. The body resembles the Beast Titan and functions as the immediate anti-air threat. Zeke's precise bodily location and the exact mechanism by which this form is appearing are not established by the pages alone.

The bombing force is destroyed. The last conventional military answer fails.

The emotional montage at Fort Salta returns to families. Children and parents imagine or recognize the people who are still fighting. The speech about adult responsibility therefore remains tied to particular relationships rather than floating as abstract reconciliation rhetoric.

Then an aircraft appears.

The coalition's flying boat arrives after the destruction of the Marleyan airships. Onyankopon is barely keeping it moving. There is no time to search the enormous Founder body. The new operational target becomes the Beast-shaped Titan: use everything available to destroy it and attempt to stop the Rumbling.

The coalition jumps.

The sequence is a formal inversion of earlier Survey Corps launches. Characters who began on opposite sides of the world's military partitions now descend together onto the largest Titan structure in the series. The action does not erase their prior differences. It makes those differences subordinate to the immediate prohibition against continuing extermination.

Reiner transforms and engages the Beast-shaped Titan while the others deploy ODM gear. The people at Fort Salta—Marleyan soldiers, Eldian families, civilians—watch figures they cannot yet fully understand arrive as the last active resistance to the catastrophe.

The volume's closing verbal challenge reframes its title-level question of freedom. Once Eren is dragged out, he will be asked again:

`「君のどこが自由なのか」って`.

The question is not merely “why are you doing this?” It attacks the self-description itself. A person who says he is moving for freedom is surrounded by future memories, inherited violence, childhood disappointment, unavoidable attachments, chosen obligations, and an action he describes as something he must continue. V33 does not yet answer whether Eren is free. It establishes that the answer cannot be obtained simply by repeating Eren's own vocabulary.

## 4. Governing synthesis: freedom is not self-certified

Across the volume, `自由` appears in incompatible forms.

### 4.1 Eren's empty-horizon freedom

The child-cloud image presents freedom as unobstructed movement over a world whose human inhabitants have disappeared from view. Its emotional authenticity is not in doubt. That is precisely why it is dangerous. A sincere experience of freedom does not tell us whether the conditions producing it are legitimate.

### 4.2 Armin's open-world freedom

Armin's horizon is no longer empty. It is uncertain. His childhood map failed, yet he wants there still to be something not known. This form of freedom tolerates the existence of other subjects because discovery does not require prior clearance of the world.

### 4.3 Hange's epistemic freedom

Hange defines leadership through not giving up on understanding. This is freedom from prematurely closing the model, not freedom from all constraints. It is institutionalized revisability.

### 4.4 Eren's adversarial freedom

In Paths, Eren gives his friends real tactical freedom. He does not remove their powers. He tells them they are free to oppose him. Yet the outside population is not party to this duel. This is why V33 distinguishes **freedom among intimates** from **a constitution of reciprocal standing**.

### 4.5 The coalition's bounded freedom

The coalition acts under guilt, grief, injury, and uncertainty. Its members are not “free” in the sense of being uncaused or unburdened. Their freedom appears instead as the ability to choose a limit on what their own side, history, and attachment may demand of them.

The volume therefore makes a strong longitudinal claim:

> **AOT increasingly treats freedom not as the absence of causes but as a contested practice of authorship under causes. The moral question is not whether a person was influenced, wounded, inherited a script, or loved someone. It is what jurisdiction that person claims after those facts are acknowledged.**

## 5. Eren: remorse, desire, and selective non-domination

V33 materially revises Eren's terminal-motive model without making it simple.

The evidence now requires at least four simultaneous layers:

1. **Paradis survival:** Eren refuses to leave the island's future to chance.
2. **Particular attachment:** he does not want to sacrifice Historia and wants his friends to live long lives.
3. **Childhood freedom demand:** the outside world occupied a horizon he imagined as open; discovering humanity outside produced disappointment.
4. **Causal/future structure:** his behavior remains entangled with future memories and recursive inherited causality established in V30.

The Ramzi sequence prevents any one layer from becoming a total explanation. Eren knows Ramzi as a person, saves him, apologizes, and later kills him through the Rumbling. Recognition is not absent. It is overruled.

The Paths confrontation then shows that Eren's non-domination is selective. He refuses to remove his friends' Titan abilities because they are free. That is genuine evidence against a model in which he simply wants obedient intimates. But it does not imply universal respect for autonomy. The same speech explicitly says he is taking freedom from the world.

A useful modeling rule after V33 is:

> **Eren may preserve autonomy inside a valued relationship even while claiming unilateral authority over outsiders; relationship-respect is therefore not a reliable proxy for universal political restraint.**

Another rule concerns sincerity. Eren's categorical speech should not automatically be read as fully transparent motive disclosure—V29-V30 already demonstrated strategic deception—but V33 contains private/rememorative material not reducible to public performance. The `ガッカリした` confession deserves high weight because it appears within a scene of shame, self-accusation, and apology rather than a negotiation designed to manipulate another actor.

## 6. Armin: dirty hands become command rather than disqualification

V33 is a major Armin volume even though he receives less combat spectacle than several others.

The Annie conversation establishes explicit personal desire without converting him into an uncomplicated romantic hero. He wants to see Annie and has maintained a relationship through years in which she could not answer. Annie's loneliness makes the contact matter to her as well.

At the same time, Armin rejects the “good person” category. His self-description as already a monster is consistent with V31-V32 moral injury and with his refusal to use current anti-genocide action to launder Liberio.

The key readiness gain comes from command succession. Hange's criterion names the longitudinal trait the corpus has been recording since early volumes: Armin does not give up on understanding when models fail. The manga itself now elevates that disposition into the formal criterion for Survey Corps leadership.

Volume 133 then immediately stress-tests it. Armin prefers dialogue with Eren. Dialogue occurs. It fails diagnostically. Eren explicitly says further discussion is unnecessary. Armin does not insist that the old relationship model must still work; he returns to the plane and admits the negotiation route has collapsed.

Levi's `どうする？ 団長` marks the behavioral transition from being the person who offers models to being the person who must decide after the preferred model fails.

The final challenge to Eren—asking where his freedom is once he is dragged out—also shows Armin's characteristic move. He does not merely deny Eren's self-concept. He turns it into a question whose answer has to survive contact with observable constraint.

## 7. Mikasa: attachment remains explicit without becoming automatic agreement

Mikasa's V33 evidence is more precise than a binary “let go / cannot let go” model.

She says she does not intend to kill Eren. She keeps the scarf but does not wear it. In Paths she offers shared guilt and asks him to return. These are strong attachment signals.

But she is also physically present in the coalition that intends to stop the Rumbling, and she does not accept Eren's exterminatory policy as legitimate protection. Attachment therefore remains strong without restoring obedience.

The correct state is:

> **Mikasa seeks relational recovery while participating in coercive opposition to Eren's project.**

That formulation leaves the terminal threshold open. V33 does not yet show what she will do if “bring him back” and “stop him” become irreconcilable.

## 8. Annie: from singular return motive toward plural relational belonging

Annie's refusal to board the plane should be interpreted through her terminal motive history. For years, returning to her father organized her willingness to endure and commit violence. With Liberio apparently lost, that objective collapses.

Her withdrawal is therefore behaviorally coherent rather than evidence of a suddenly pacifist personality. She says she cannot fight anymore and wants peace.

Yet the ship material complicates the old singular model. Annie remembers her father, but the visual memory sequence also contains the people she lived among. Armin is now explicitly a desired person. Gabi and Falco matter. Kiyomi's speech about regret and respecting others confronts Annie with a wider relationship field than the one that existed at the beginning of her Warrior mission.

The volume does not yet show whether that widening produces renewed combat participation. At V33 the key update is motivational: **father-return remains central but no longer exhausts Annie's attachment landscape**.

## 9. Hange and Levi: death does not flatten voice

Hange's final chapter is unusually diagnostic because it preserves several traits simultaneously:

- command responsibility;
- willingness to self-sacrifice for an operational need;
- distributed trust in Armin;
- explicit epistemic principle;
- Titan fascination;
- relationship intimacy with Levi;
- frustration with the burden of command.

The result is not “Hange dies heroically” as a generic role conclusion. It is a state-consistent convergence of the character's scientific and command identities.

Levi's contribution is almost the inverse: very little language carries very high relational weight. `心臓を捧げよ` is important because it is atypical for him in precisely this interpersonal position. `じゃあな ハンジ` completes the farewell without expanding into confession.

This provides another strong anti-caricature control: **terse voice is compatible with deep attachment**.

## 10. Reiner, Jean, and Connie: sameness without equivalence

The `俺達は同じだ…ライナー` sequence should become a longitudinal relationship hinge.

Jean has now occupied a structural position that once belonged only to the Warriors in his experience: killing people he knew while telling himself the larger purpose is saving lives. Connie has direct moral injury from Samuel and Daz. Reiner continues to regard his own acts as beyond atonement.

They can therefore recognize a shared structure without erasing differences in history, coercion, age, objective, or scale.

This is an important general rule for the corpus:

> **Recognition of structural similarity does not require moral equivalence, and non-equivalence does not cancel the epistemic value of the comparison.**

The same principle applies to Eren and Reiner. Reiner's ability to generate the “perhaps Eren wants someone to stop him” hypothesis arises from self-recognition. But because it is self-projective, it should be treated as a hypothesis with a known source of possible bias rather than a privileged window into Eren.

## 11. Falco and Gabi: embodied hypothesis under catastrophic option scarcity

Falco's flight idea is a clean example of how the project should distinguish evidence layers.

He possesses:

- Beast spinal-fluid exposure;
- a known Jaw Titan body;
- an inherited memory associated with flying above clouds;
- a subjective sense that he may be able to reproduce that capacity.

He does **not** yet possess:

- demonstrated flight control;
- a tested transformation under current conditions;
- proof that the remembered Beast trait will manifest in the required form.

Gabi's response is action-oriented optimism. Annie supplies the failure case: Falco previously could not control his first transformation, and a failed experiment here could sink the ship.

Kiyomi accepts the risk because remaining passive also has terminal cost. The scene therefore belongs in the model as **high-risk experimentation under option collapse**, not as pre-confirmed ability.

## 12. Karina Braun and the parental-revision architecture

Karina's material is brief but important. Earlier Reiner development was shaped by her investment in Warrior status, honorary Marleyan recognition, and family restoration. At Fort Salta, with Reiner absent and catastrophe arriving, she regrets having made him carry that burden.

The armband becomes the object of revision. What once signified achieved status is now described as something acquired by sacrificing everything for her child. The direction of value flips: institutional recognition is no longer proof that parental strategy succeeded; it is evidence of what the child was made to pay.

This belongs beside:

- Grisha's apology to Zeke;
- Magath's wish that the Warrior children could simply have lived ordinary lives;
- Artur Blouse's insistence that adults keep children out of the forest;
- Shadis and Magath taking adult responsibility at the harbor;
- the Fort Salta officer assigning the current burden to adults rather than children.

Late AOT is increasingly explicit that adults cannot redeem their failures by assigning redemption projects to the next generation.

## 13. Fort Salta: institutional self-indictment without automatic absolution

The Marleyan speech is ethically significant because it breaks several familiar rhetorical habits.

First, it does not place the catastrophe solely on the shoulders of the soldiers now asked to fight it.

Second, it identifies hatred as something used and cultivated, not merely felt. Hatred becomes political technology.

Third, it identifies the island-devil story as a dumping mechanism for unresolved problems.

Fourth, it calls for a future that does not repeat the structure.

None of this absolves Marley. The confession arrives when Marley lacks the power to continue its prior system. Nor does it absolve Eren by converting him into the deterministic product of Marleyan hatred. The value of the speech is causal pluralism: it makes institutional authorship visible at the same time the manga has made individual authorship visible.

The phrase `我々すべての大人達` also makes the speech continuous with the child-protection thesis developed since the Blouse farm. The enemy of inherited violence is not historical forgetting. It is adults taking responsibility for history without turning children into the currency of repayment.

## 14. The baby at the cliff: future without assigned mission

The baby-passing image deserves separate treatment because it is almost wordless and therefore easy to reduce to generic pathos.

The crowd has no political mechanism left. People are falling. The mother cannot save herself. Yet a chain of strangers collectively moves the infant away from immediate death.

The action has no guarantee of success. It is not strategic victory. It is future-directed care stripped of ideology.

In the longitudinal corpus, children have repeatedly been imagined as:

- successors;
- carriers of blood;
- vessels of Titan power;
- proof of national redemption;
- hostages to deterrence;
- inheritors of guilt;
- reasons to continue war.

Here the infant is carried without a stated mission. The people pass forward a life, not a burden.

That makes the sequence the strongest visual condensation so far of the anti-instrumentalization baseline: **a future person should first be allowed to exist before being told what historical problem their existence must solve.**

## 15. Written Japanese voice and lexical state

### Eren

V33 strongly confirms the categorical high-stakes register while broadening its emotional surroundings. Key anchors include:

- `ガッカリした` — devastatingly ordinary lexical choice for the collapse of the childhood outside-world image;
- `自由だ` — child-horizon declaration;
- `地鳴らしは止まらない` — categorical refusal;
- `オレは進み続ける` — continuity with the recurrent advance grammar;
- `お前達は自由だ` — selective grant/recognition of friends' agency;
- `戦え` — collision reduced to action.

The important voice conclusion is that categoricality coexists with tears, shame, apology, and intimate address. It is not evidence of emotional flatness.

### Armin

Armin moves between embarrassed intimacy, self-indictment, analytical planning, failed negotiation, and command. His most important lexical anchors are:

- `会いたかったからだ…アニに`;
- `僕もとっくにバケモノだよ`;
- continued desire to believe in an unknown world;
- explicit recognition that negotiation has failed after Paths;
- the final question `君のどこが自由なのか`.

His voice remains more interrogative and model-oriented than Eren's. Even the final challenge is framed as a question.

### Mikasa

Mikasa's V33 register remains sparse. `殺さない`, the statement that she will bring Eren back, and `帰ってきて…` carry large relational weight without abstract exposition.

### Hange

Hange's final voice preserves both institutional and idiosyncratic registers. The commander criterion is abstract and formal; `やっぱり巨人って 素晴らしいな` is recognizably Hange even at death. Technical/intellectual fascination and command seriousness remain integrated.

### Levi

`心臓を捧げよ`, `じゃあな ハンジ`, and the later concise command questions preserve the pattern in which emotional density is carried by compression rather than elaboration.

### Annie

Annie retains anti-grandiose vocabulary. `人類を救うとか 私には…よくわからない` rejects the universal heroic slogan while her personal attachment speech is much more concrete.

## 16. Manga-form and visual grammar

### 16.1 Height as moral occlusion

Child Eren above the clouds is visually free because the victims are below the steam. The page geometry turns perspective into ethics: what looks unobstructed from above is annihilation from below.

### 16.2 The return of crowds as persons

Chapter 134 repeatedly fills panels with civilians, families, refugees, worshippers, and workers. The outside world is not represented only through national leaders or military formations. This visually attacks the category compression required by extermination.

### 16.3 Hands and transferred responsibility

The volume repeatedly uses acts of passing or transfer:

- command passes from Hange to Armin;
- Levi's fist touches Hange's chest;
- the baby is passed backward through the crowd;
- the coalition passes from aircraft into freefall;
- inherited memories and Titan traits pass into Falco.

Transfer is not morally neutral. What matters is what is handed forward: burden, office, life, memory, or weapon.

### 16.4 Child forms in Paths

Eren and Ymir appear as children while declaring a rule that forces adults into lethal conflict. The image keeps the developmental origin of the conflict visible without converting childhood injury into innocence.

### 16.5 Final descent

The coalition's drop onto the Founder body combines the old Survey Corps visual grammar of aerial approach with a radically changed political subject. The unit is no longer a national force confronting nonhuman Titans. It is a mixed coalition confronting a former friend whose body has become the vehicle of human extermination.

## 18. Series-so-far claim transitions after V33

### STRENGTHEN

**S1 — Recognition does not automatically inhibit violence.**

Eren's relationship to Ramzi is one of the strongest demonstrations yet. He recognizes, saves, pities, apologizes to, and later kills the same person through a mass action.

**S2 — Ordinary future remains an anti-instrumentalization baseline.**

Karina's regret, the Fort Salta children, the infant at the cliff, and Hange's transfer of responsibility all reinforce the rule that children should not be assigned the work of redeeming adult history.

**S3 — Armin's core epistemic identity is model revision, not optimism.**

Hange institutionally names refusal to give up understanding as his command qualification, and V33 immediately gives him a failed negotiation that he must recognize as failed.

**S4 — Guilt can coexist with action.**

Armin, Reiner, Jean, Connie, Kiyomi, Karina, and the Fort Salta officer all act or revise without first becoming innocent.

### REVISE

**R1 — Eren's Rumbling motive can no longer be modeled primarily as defensive necessity plus friend protection.**

Those motives remain real, but V33 adds direct private evidence of disappointed desire toward the populated outside world. The model must represent multiple motive channels simultaneously.

**R2 — “Eren respects freedom” must be split by target.**

He preserves the coalition's capacities while explicitly taking freedom from the outside world. Freedom-recognition is relationship-bounded, not universal.

**R3 — Annie's father-return terminal variable remains central but is no longer sufficient to explain the full current relationship state.**

Her memory field now includes Armin and former comrades/peers as meaningful attachments.

**R4 — Survey Corps leadership after Hange is explicitly epistemic.**

Armin's command authority should now include an institutional mandate to continue understanding under failed models, not merely battlefield cleverness.

### DOWNGRADE

**D1 — “If Eren leaves his friends free to oppose him, he must secretly intend their victory” is downgraded.**

Reiner raises a version of this hypothesis, but Eren's own Paths statement is compatible with adversarial permission: he may value their freedom while still sincerely advancing and requiring them to fight if they want him stopped.

**D2 — “Annie's current withdrawal indicates generalized moral pacifism” is downgraded.**

Her explanation is motive collapse, exhaustion, and desire for peace after presumed loss of her father/home, not a universal anti-violence doctrine.

### REJECT

**J1 — “Eren's empathy toward an outsider proves the Rumbling is only an unwilling necessity.”**

Ramzi evidence directly rejects this shortcut. Empathy and shame coexist with both action and an admitted personal disappointment about the populated outside world.

**J2 — “The coalition's status as world-savers makes its members morally clean.”**

Armin explicitly rejects the good-person category and calls himself a monster; the coalition repeatedly describes itself through crime and betrayal.

### OPEN

- Whether the child-form Eren/Ymir Paths presentation corresponds to a distinct current consciousness state or primarily to representational/Paths form.
- The full distribution and constraint structure of Eren's future memories.
- Whether Reiner's “perhaps Eren wants someone to stop him” hypothesis is correct, partly projective, or wrong.
- Whether Mikasa can maintain `殺さない / 帰ってきて` if stopping Eren requires a terminal choice.
- Whether Falco can actually produce and control the predicted flying Jaw/Beast hybrid body.
- Whether Annie reenters active combat after her motivational field broadens.
- Zeke's precise location/status and the ontology of the Beast-shaped body on the Founder.
- Whether the Fort Salta vow against mutual hatred survives beyond immediate catastrophe.
- Whether the coalition can stop Eren and what stopping means physically.
- The outcome of the final battle generally. **V34 remains locked.**

## 23. Volume-level conclusion

Volume 33 refuses the simplest moral categories precisely when the story is closest to apocalypse.

Eren is not cleansed by remorse, but neither is remorse fake. Armin is not purified by trying to save the world, but guilt does not remove his responsibility to command. Annie is not converted into a universal altruist, but her relationship world has become larger than the singular father-return objective that once organized her. Jean and Connie do not become Reiner's moral equivalents, but they can no longer pretend that “killing people to save people” belongs only to an alien enemy psychology. Marleyan adults finally recognize that they cultivated hatred and assigned its cost to children, but recognition arrives when the machinery may already be impossible to stop.

The volume's deepest argument concerns the phrase `お前達は自由だ`.

Eren does something morally nontrivial by refusing to erase his friends' capacity to oppose him. But V33 also shows why this is insufficient. He decides who belongs inside the circle whose freedom matters. Outside that circle, entire populations are objects of his project. His friends receive the dignity of resistance; Ramzi receives an apology he cannot understand and then death.

The distinction is constitutional rather than sentimental.

> **Freedom is not established because the powerful actor allows a favored opponent to fight back. It requires limits on the jurisdiction any actor may claim over people who are not merely pieces in that fight.**

Armin's final promised question therefore lands as the volume's proper endpoint. Once Eren is dragged from the machinery that manifests his will, the coalition intends to ask not whether he wanted freedom, but where, exactly, his freedom is.

That question remains open at `page0188`.
