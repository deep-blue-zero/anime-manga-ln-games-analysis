# 『進撃の巨人』Volume 31 Deep Reading
## Who may be saved after everyone is implicated? Genocide, thawing categories, and coalition after complicity

## 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 30 ended by converting freedom into a problem of causal authorship. Eren rejected Zeke's reproductive sovereignty, exposed the extent to which Grisha's decisive act was itself entangled with Eren's future pressure, addressed Ymir as a person rather than property, and activated the Rumbling. Yet Volume 30 deliberately stopped before specifying the Rumbling's complete operational target. Volume 31 supplies that missing declaration and then refuses to let the declaration remain a distant strategic abstraction.

The volume's governing thesis is:

> **Freedom becomes responsibility after complicity: once Eren converts Paradis's survival into a claim over every life beyond the island, the remaining characters must decide whether belonging authorizes participation, whether guilt disqualifies rescue, and whether former enemies can build a reciprocal coalition without first becoming innocent.**

A sharper operational formulation is:

> **V31 turns freedom from causal authorship into reciprocal refusal. To act freely is no longer merely to escape another person's command; it is to refuse the comfort of letting nation, family, grief, inherited hatred, or past guilt decide in advance whose life may be used up.**

This is not a volume in which moral categories become simple. Eren's announced objective is unambiguous at the operational level: he says he will use the Wall Titans to trample all land outside Paradis until the life there has been exterminated. But the volume simultaneously shows why several tempting simplifications fail:

- the people outside the island are neither an abstract hostile mass nor secretly harmless; refugees offer hospitality while political advocacy can still scapegoat Paradis;
- Paradis is neither a homogeneous beneficiary nor merely a victim; Eren's protection kills island civilians and Floch converts survival into a coercive new state order;
- characters who decide to oppose the Rumbling are not purified heroes; many have killed, enabled killing, inherited enemy institutions, or benefited from Eren's violence;
- recognition does not erase responsibility, but responsibility does not make coalition impossible;
- family attachment can motivate protection, atrocity, self-sacrifice, and ethical reversal depending on whether another person's body is treated as a legitimate means.

The chapter titles form a deliberate progression:

1. **「島の悪魔」 / Island Devils** — the category used to justify extermination is shown as a political construction that can be reproduced even inside rights advocacy, while the supposed “devils” eat, drink, grieve, and are themselves divided.
2. **「氷解」 / Thaw** — Eren literally dissolves hardening, but the more important thaw is categorical: Annie's crystal opens; Gabi and Kaya cross the enemy/victim boundary through rescue; the old military order collapses; identities once treated as fixed become behaviorally revisable.
3. **「夕焼け」 / Sunset** — an old political day ends. Annie gives a retrospective account of how a narrow relationship survived moral numbness; Floch inaugurates a violent “new Eldian Empire”; Armin reaches executive overload; Hange and Levi reappear outside the new order.
4. **「矜持」 / Pride / Dignity** — dignity is separated from factional purity. Connie refuses to save his mother through a child's body, Onyankopon refuses survival through submission, Jean refuses beneficiary comfort, Mikasa reclaims a relationship symbol on her own terms, and former enemies assemble around the deliberately compromised phrase `世界を救いに`.

The volume therefore does not answer V30's personhood question by saying “recognize everyone and violence stops.” It gives a harder answer: **personhood becomes ethically meaningful only when it changes what one permits oneself to do with another person's future.**

# 3. Narrative architecture and close reading

## 3.1 Chapter 123 — 「島の悪魔」: the category becomes impossible to keep abstract

### The outside world first arrives as ordinary excess, not geopolitics

The Marley visit retrospectively fills the experiential gap between the Scouts reaching the sea and the later Liberio operation. Cars, clothing, markets, food, strangers, and urban density make “the outside world” sensorially concrete. The sequence is politically important because it refuses to introduce the mainland only through military institutions. The group encounters ordinary commercial life before encountering the political mechanisms that define Paradis as expendable.

The novelty is not purely joyful. Every ordinary detail is double-coded by the later knowledge that this world is also the object of Eren's announced extermination. The manga therefore gives the reader people and places before it gives the totalizing target category.

### Refugee hospitality destroys the fantasy of a morally homogeneous outside

When the Scouts come into contact with displaced refugees, the scene moves from observation to shared bodily life: food, alcohol, music, touch, sleep, and conviviality. These people do not need to become politically representative in order to matter. Their analytical function is more basic: they are persons whose lives cannot be accurately represented by the category “the world that wants Paradis dead.”

This is not a counterclaim that the outside world is secretly benign. The same chapter later demonstrates organized political hostility. The point is epistemic and ethical: **collective threat does not eliminate person-level heterogeneity.** V29 established this principle with Onyankopon against a monolithic Volunteer category; V31 applies it to the population Eren is about to define as a single exterminable outside.

### Eren asks Mikasa for a relationship interpretation that V28 had denied her

At the refugee camp Eren asks why Mikasa cares about him: because he saved her as a child, or because he is family. He then asks directly: `オレは…お前の何だ?`

Mikasa answers: `あなたは…家族…`.

This scene must be held against V28, where Eren told Mikasa that her attachment could be explained through inherited Ackerman compulsion. Here, in private, he asks her to interpret the relationship herself. That does not prove the V28 theory was knowingly false in every component, nor does it tell us why Eren asks. Several hypotheses remain live at the V31 boundary:

- he genuinely wants a self-authored answer from Mikasa;
- he is comparing the present to future-memory fragments;
- he is testing whether a foreseen answer changes;
- he wants reassurance but cannot ask for it directly;
- he is gathering relational information before an already-forming decision.

The scene's high-confidence result is narrower: **Eren treats Mikasa's own account of the relationship as information worth soliciting.**

### Mikasa's later counterfactual is evidence about Mikasa, not proof of causal branching

Mikasa retrospectively wonders whether a different answer could have changed what followed. This is important character evidence because it shows self-blame and counterfactual grief. It is not proof that “family” caused the Rumbling or that another romantic answer would have prevented it. The manga gives Mikasa the painful human tendency to identify one intimate fork in an event whose causal architecture is vastly larger than one reply.

That distinction matters for simulation: Mikasa can retrospectively over-assign causal weight to her own relational action when Eren becomes incomprehensible. A model that treats every self-blaming counterfactual as narrator-confirmed causation would erase the difference between character cognition and story fact.

### Rights advocacy can reproduce exclusion rather than dissolve it

The Subject-of-Ymir political meeting is one of the volume's most important institutional scenes. It demonstrates that discourse of rights and humanitarian concern exists outside Paradis. Yet the proposed political solution distinguishes mainland Subjects of Ymir from Paradis by preserving the latter as the dangerous “island devil” object.

The ethical structure is therefore triangular:

1. protect one stigmatized group;
2. gain legitimacy by displacing danger onto another related group;
3. make the excluded group bear the political cost of the inclusion project.

Eren leaves. The scene supplies real evidence for his pessimism about current diplomatic categories, but it does **not** logically establish that extermination is necessary. It shows that one available rights framework is built through scapegoating. Necessity remains a separate argument.

### Eren's broadcast resolves the operational target that V30 left open

Through the Founder, Eren addresses all Subjects of Ymir. He identifies himself, states that all hardening of the Paradis walls has been undone, and says his purpose is to protect the people of the island where he was born and raised.

Then the stopping condition becomes explicit. The outside world's long hatred, he says, will not stop until not only Paradis but all Subjects of Ymir are killed. He rejects that outcome. His response is to have the Wall Titans trample all land outside the island until the life there is driven from the world.

At V31 this means:

- Eren's **operational Rumbling objective is no longer OPEN**;
- the target is not merely a military coalition or deterrent demonstration;
- the stated beneficiary is Paradis;
- the stated means is continental/global surface destruction outside the island;
- the stated stopping condition is extermination of life in that target space.

The lexical continuity with early Eren is deliberately severe. `駆逐`—once directed at Titans imagined as the inhuman obstacle to freedom—has now expanded to life beyond the island. The word's persistence makes the transformation legible precisely because the object has changed.

This does not mean every hidden motive has been solved. Operational objective and complete inner motive remain distinct variables. But after V31, an Eren model may no longer preserve “Rumbling target unknown” as if the broadcast did not occur.

## 3.2 Chapter 124 — 「氷解」: hardening dissolves, and categories begin to thaw under pressure

### Eren's protection produces dead Paradisians

The walls' collapse damages nearby buildings and kills/injures island civilians. Public reactions split. Some residents treat the casualties as necessary sacrifices for island survival. Others—especially people directly harmed—reject the abstraction.

This is the first immediate constitutional test of Eren's declared beneficiary category. “Protect Paradis” does not map cleanly onto “protect every Paradis person.” The project can produce persons inside its protected population who experience themselves as spent by the protection.

That matters because the story has repeatedly criticized political language that treats bodies as resources. V31 does not exempt Eren because his beneficiaries are familiar characters. A protection project still raises the question: **who has jurisdiction to impose irreversible costs on the people being protected?**

### Jean articulates the beneficiary logic without hiding from what it is

Jean is one of the first to say aloud why Eren's action can appear strategically attractive. If the outside population disappears, the enemies threatening the island and the accumulated grievances disappear with it. He also notes the causal history: the outside world decided the islanders were devils and tried to eliminate them, and the feared disaster has now occurred.

The importance is that Jean does not need a caricatured nationalist psychology to understand the argument. He can state it in its strongest form because he is a beneficiary.

Armin answers with the moral scale: what is happening has gone far beyond what was required; it is an unprecedented mass slaughter. Jean then states the uncomfortable distributive fact: **they are among those who benefit most from it.**

This is the volume's complicity hinge. Moral opposition does not come from standing outside the benefit structure. Jean and Armin are being saved by the very act they must decide whether to oppose.

### The surviving Zeke Titans create a Founder-control anomaly

Zeke-created Pure Titans continue attacking Paradis personnel even after Eren has obtained Founder access. Armin/Jean recognize the anomaly: if Eren possesses the Founding Titan's power, why are these Titans still uncontrolled?

At V31 the evidence supports only an unresolved mechanism question. Possibilities include:

- Eren/Ymir cannot control them in the assumed way;
- control exists but has not been exercised;
- the Rumbling operation is prioritized over local cleanup;
- Zeke-originated control has a distinct residual structure.

The manga does not yet choose among them. This is exactly the kind of point the v2.1 method requires us to leave open rather than turn an inference into cosmology.

### Connie tries to solve inherited grief with another child's body

Connie decides to take Falco to Ragako and feed him to his Titanized mother, thereby transferring the Jaw Titan and restoring her human form. His line is brutally simple: `ラガコ村の母ちゃんに食わせる`.

The action is understandable at the level of attachment. Connie has lived for years with the unresolved horror of his mother's transformation. The means, however, reproduce the body's instrumentalization problem in miniature: Falco becomes a succession resource whose child status and future can be overridden by someone else's family grief.

Armin immediately sees a second-order cost: Falco is not an isolated body. He is connected to Reiner, Pieck, Gabi, and the remaining Warrior field. Using him to save Connie's mother can create a new war precisely when catastrophic conflict already surrounds them.

The scene therefore aligns personal ethics and systems reasoning. Falco matters both because he is a child/person and because relationships make bodies politically nonfungible.

### Gabi saves Kaya, and person-level revision survives renewed war

Gabi's V29 breakthrough—there were no devils on the island, only people—would be analytically weak if it disappeared the instant she was endangered again. V31 provides a much stronger test. Kaya is threatened by a Pure Titan; Gabi kills it and saves her.

The manga visually associates Gabi's rescuing posture with Sasha. Kaya's recognition is not a claim that Gabi has become Sasha or that killing Sasha is forgiven. Rather, an enemy-category relation that once seemed morally fixed is interrupted by a concrete action in the same relational slot: someone puts her body/skill between Kaya and death.

The Blouse family and Nicolo likewise protect Gabi. This creates a reciprocal network in which:

- Gabi remains responsible for Sasha's death;
- Kaya's prior rage was real;
- the family does not need to erase memory;
- present rescue still changes what each person can truthfully say about the other.

This is stronger evidence than verbal reconciliation because it survives crisis.

### Reiner loses his armor at the same moment Annie loses her prison

Eren's undoing of all hardening has cross-faction consequences. Reiner's armor/hardening is stripped, and Annie's crystal also dissolves. The Founder's intervention therefore does not respect the simple friendly/enemy sorting that ordinary combat does.

Gabi is the one who notices the implication. If Eren removed Reiner's hardening, then Annie's hardening should also be gone. Armin follows the causal implication. This is compact evidence for the series' mature epistemic style: mechanical consequences can be inferred across apparently separate scenes.

### Floch converts survival into a sovereignty claim

As the old military order collapses, Floch and the Yeagerists move quickly. Volunteers are disarmed; opponents are coerced; a new Eldian imperial order is announced. Floch says Eren told him the plan in advance and that he organized around it.

That testimony is evidence about Floch's claimed knowledge and the existence of coordinated preparation. It is not license to infer every component of Eren's ideology from Floch's later state project.

The distinction is crucial. Eren's broadcast states an exterminatory protective objective. Floch translates victory into a domestic sovereign program: submission to a “new Eldian Empire,” public execution, and the right of the victorious in-group to rule. The two are causally connected without needing to be psychologically identical.

## 3.3 Chapter 125 — 「夕焼け」: the old order ends without making anyone innocent

### Annie's return begins with bodily weakness, not mythic reentry

Annie emerges from the crystal physically diminished. The scene rejects the possibility that four years of immobilization simply freeze a character in heroic readiness. Her body has to reenter time.

Hitch provides the social bridge. Their interaction retains the dry friction characteristic of their earlier relationship rather than converting Annie's return into solemn exposition alone.

### Annie's childhood makes instrumentalization familial before it is military

Annie says she was abandoned as a baby after her Eldian ancestry was discovered. A foreign Eldian man adopted her because he wanted to train her into a Marleyan Warrior. Her body was selected as a mechanism for his improved social status.

The sequence is an important echo of V30. King Fritz turns Ymir's body and descendants into state infrastructure; Annie's adoptive father turns a child into a Warrior project. The scales differ radically, but the grammar is homologous: the child's capacity becomes the adult's route to a desired future.

Annie eventually breaks his leg. His reaction is not simple rejection; he is pleased that the injury proves the training has made her strong enough. Even resistance is absorbed into the instrumental project.

### Annie's father becomes the first person whose life acquires nonfungible value

Annie says that at the time she did not care what happened or how many people in other countries died. She did not think even her own life had value. The restoration mission to the island begins from that numb state.

Then her father apologizes. He tells her what he taught her was wrong, begs her to return, and identifies her not as a social-mobility instrument but as his daughter. Annie says this changed the structure of value for her: she now has a father waiting for her, and she can understand that other people likewise have someone important.

Her next admission prevents sentimental absolution. She believes she has committed irreversible crimes, yet says that if returning to her father required it, she would do the same things again.

This produces a precise Annie model:

- relational attachment is real;
- moral knowledge is real;
- guilt/recognition is real;
- none of them automatically overrides the return objective;
- severe participation is therefore not evidence of emotional indifference.

The statement is valuable prospective evidence for the frozen Annie model, but because the V31 scene is retrospective self-accounting rather than a fresh superior/peer loyalty-test under the exact frozen condition, it should improve mutable readiness without being forced into a headline holdout score.

### Floch's new order demonstrates that liberation can become a new coercive center immediately

Floch's regime does not wait for the Rumbling to finish before demanding submission. The Volunteers are forced to accept a new political hierarchy or die. The rhetoric is victory, freedom, and survival; the mechanism is public coercion.

This matters to the V27 checkpoint thesis. “Freedom becomes constitutional” did not mean the series was moving toward a particular modern institutional form. It meant that freedom claims become incomplete unless they specify reciprocal limits and jurisdiction. Floch supplies the negative case: the victorious group calls itself free while making continued life conditional on political obedience.

### Armin reaches executive overload

Mikasa asks Armin what to do amid simultaneous crises: Eren's global extermination, Connie taking Falco, Annie's release, military collapse, and the disappearance of any stable command structure.

Armin snaps. He says he does not know; he invokes Erwin as the person who would have known; he cannot supply the omnipotent strategic answer others implicitly want from him.

This is essential negative evidence for character reconstruction. Armin's high analytical ability must not be modeled as infinite executive bandwidth. Under sufficient simultaneous responsibility he can become angry, overwhelmed, and temporarily unable to integrate every problem.

Yet the scene does not end in permanent collapse. He identifies an actionable local priority—Falco/Connie—and moves. The state transition is therefore:

`overload -> explicit incapacity -> scope reduction -> action`

not `overload -> genius plan` and not `overload -> total dysfunction`.

### Mikasa reclaims the scarf rather than accepting Eren's interpretation of it

Louise, gravely wounded, tells Mikasa that Eren told her the scarf should be thrown away. Mikasa asks for it back.

This revises the V29 scarf state. There, after Eren's attack on her identity, Mikasa left the scarf behind. V31 shows that physical distancing was not surrender of interpretive ownership. When she learns that Eren wanted the object discarded, she reclaims it.

The best bounded formulation is:

> **Mikasa reclaims custody of the relationship symbol from both Eren's attempted disposal and Louise's derivative attachment.**

This is not yet proof that Mikasa has chosen Eren's political project, rejected it, romantically resolved the relationship, or returned to a pre-V28 self. It is evidence that Eren does not possess unilateral authority to dictate what the relationship means to her.

### Jean is offered the most seductive version of complicity: simply enjoy the benefit

Floch's offer to Jean matters because it is personalized. Jean can stop bearing the burden of impossible moral choices. The island is being saved. A victorious regime exists. He can return to the self who once imagined a safe interior life.

The manga gives that temptation visual form through a quiet domestic future. That imagined ordinary life is not evidence that Jean secretly wants genocide as a terminal value. It is evidence that the benefit Eren offers is genuinely attractive to someone tired of war.

Jean's later refusal therefore has ethical weight precisely because he can imagine the alternative.

### Hange and Levi reenter from outside the victorious narrative

Hange approaches Pieck and Magath while supporting a severely injured Levi. The V29 protective lie has succeeded: Levi survived. Hange presents themselves without weapons and asks not to be eaten; the exchange is pragmatic rather than trust-saturated.

This is the embryo of the anti-Rumbling coalition. Former enemies do not first discover that they were morally the same all along. They discover an objective that can no longer be pursued within the old ally/enemy partition.

Levi's injury remains an important modeling constraint. His presence should not be converted into broad current-state cognitive conclusions when he is physically devastated and has little speech.

## 3.4 Chapter 126 — 「矜持」: dignity is what remains when innocence is unavailable

### Connie's deception makes Falco's trust part of the ethical problem

Connie takes Falco toward Ragako while withholding the actual plan. Falco's willingness to follow is itself relational evidence: he does not yet understand that the person guiding him intends to use his body.

The sequence deliberately resembles larger-scale political deception. A protected or trusted person can be carried toward an outcome whose decisive cost is hidden from them. V31 compresses the same sovereignty problem from Founder-scale politics into a road trip with a child.

### Armin makes the cost reciprocal by offering his own body

Abstract argument has not stopped Connie. Armin therefore changes the decision geometry. He moves toward Connie's mother's Titan mouth and offers himself as the body that can be consumed instead.

The action is manipulative in the minimal sense that Armin deliberately creates a choice Connie cannot evade. But unlike coercion that hides another person's cost, Armin makes the cost visible by placing his own body inside it.

Connie immediately saves Armin.

This is one of the clearest ethical mechanisms in the post-checkpoint corpus:

1. Connie authorizes sacrificing Falco because Falco has been abstracted into a means to restore his mother.
2. Armin substitutes a person Connie cannot abstract in the same way.
3. The equivalence forces Connie to confront the rule he was applying.
4. Connie rejects the rule rather than merely choosing a different victim.

The result is not that Connie stops loving his mother. He says he wants to become a soldier his mother could be proud of. Family attachment is retained while its permissible means change.

That is **reciprocal refusal of instrumentalization** in miniature.

### The pie scene restores ordinary social friction without pretending the crisis has ended

Annie's ravenous eating and Connie's laughter are brief but valuable low-stakes controls. Annie can be embarrassed, hungry, irritated, and socially recognizable immediately beside apocalyptic stakes. The scene is analytically useful because character simulation needs precisely these non-doctrinal surfaces.

It should not be inflated into a stable “favorite food” preference. The diagnostic observation is state-linked appetite/comic social friction after prolonged crystal confinement.

Hitch's note to Annie similarly preserves indirect affection: gratitude for four years of one-sided conversation is delivered through dry roommate language rather than sentimental declaration. Annie's social field contains attachment expressed through friction.

### Onyankopon refuses to buy life with endorsement of the regime destroying his home

Floch stages the execution of Yelena and Onyankopon. Onyankopon's refusal is among the volume's clearest reciprocity speeches. He helped Eldia in order to free his homeland from Marley; Eren's Rumbling now threatens to crush that homeland and kill his family. He refuses to apologize to the new regime merely to be permitted to live.

Most importantly, he invokes the islanders' own experience of arbitrary mass killing: they know what it is to have people suddenly and indiscriminately killed, so how can they fail to understand what is now happening to others?

This is not a claim that victimhood automatically produces ethical wisdom. V31 shows the opposite: remembered victimhood can become a justification for retaliatory totalization or a basis for reciprocal limits. Onyankopon demands the latter.

### Jean's four shots convert moral refusal into operational coordination

Floch orders Jean to shoot Onyankopon. Jean fires four times but does not kill him. Pieck then extracts Jean, Yelena, and Onyankopon, and later Armin identifies the four shots as the prearranged signal that the operation has begun.

Jean's decision therefore cannot be read as simple hesitation or poor marksmanship. The refusal has been converted into plan architecture.

This completes the temptation sequence from Chapter 124–125:

- Jean can articulate why genocide benefits them;
- he can imagine a safe life under the victorious order;
- he does not deny that Eren has removed enemies threatening the island;
- he nevertheless helps build the operation that opposes the beneficiary logic.

His pragmatism is not replaced by idealism. It becomes the means by which moral refusal is made executable.

### Coalition forms without reconciliation

The final movement brings together people whose prior relationships include invasion, killing, betrayal, imprisonment, ethnic hatred, military coercion, and personal bereavement. The group includes former Scouts, Warriors, Marleyan command, Volunteers, and children shaped by both systems.

The key analytical mistake would be to call this “everyone puts aside their differences” and stop there. The differences are not gone. The coalition is possible because a narrower shared constraint has become dominant: they oppose the extermination of the outside world.

This is **objective alignment without moral purification**.

Pieck and Magath do not become innocent because they oppose Eren. Reiner and Annie do not cease to be responsible for earlier violence. Armin and the Scouts do not cease to be implicated in Liberio. Hange and Levi do not suddenly trust every Marleyan institution. Gabi's revision does not erase Sasha. Jean does not stop caring about Paradis. Connie does not stop loving his mother. Mikasa does not stop loving or being bound to Eren.

The coalition therefore advances the corpus's relationship architecture: cooperation can be modeled as a state produced by **shared prohibition** rather than shared biography, affection, ideology, or innocence.

### `世界を救いに` is deliberately uncomfortable hero language

The volume ends with the group moving “to save the world.” After the preceding chapters, that phrase cannot function as uncomplicated heroic coronation. The people saying/embodying it have spent the volume confronting what they have done, what they benefit from, and whom they once treated as expendable.

Its force comes from that contamination. “Saving the world” is not a declaration that they have become morally pure enough to deserve hero status. It is a commitment to act despite not being pure.

That is the chapter title's `矜持`: dignity is not spotless identity; it is the refusal to let one's compromised history become an excuse for the next irreversible wrong.

# 4. Character-state updates

## 4.1 Eren Jaeger — the operational target becomes explicit, while complete interior motive remains layered

### Starting state

At V30, several points were established:

- Eren had deceived Zeke about euthanasia;
- he grounded rejection in his own fact of birth;
- he could influence Grisha through future-memory recursion;
- he addressed Ymir as a person with a decision rather than as royal property;
- the Rumbling began;
- but the precise Rumbling target/stopping rule remained analytically locked.

### V31 evidence

V31 resolves the operational question. Eren states that his purpose is to protect the people of Paradis and that the Wall Titans will trample the land outside the island until life there is exterminated.

This is not an inference from frightening imagery. It is first-person current speech delivered through the Founder to Subjects of Ymir.

### Current model

Eren now requires a model capable of holding four propositions simultaneously:

1. he rejects Zeke's authority to erase future Eldian births;
2. he recognizes Ymir as a person rather than an instrument;
3. he claims Paradis protection as the purpose of his action;
4. he authorizes the destruction of persons outside Paradis at population scale.

The contradiction is not evidence that one of these must secretly be fake. It is the central ethical problem. Eren's anti-instrumentalization is **relationship- and boundary-conditioned**, not yet universalized into reciprocal personhood.

### Negative constraints

Do not model:

- “Eren's target remains unknown” after V31;
- “Eren only intends a military demonstration” at this boundary;
- “because Eren recognizes Ymir, he therefore universally respects self-authorship”;
- “Mikasa's answer caused the Rumbling”;
- “Eren saw every future detail and merely reenacted it.”

The operational objective is clear. The full interior architecture—future-memory expectations, grief, protective attachment, fatalism versus choice, and whether he anticipates opposition—remains partly open.

## 4.2 Mikasa Ackerman — from scarf distancing to self-authored custody

V29 showed Mikasa leaving the scarf after Eren attacked the meaning of her attachment. V31 adds two crucial states.

First, the Marley memory shows her responding to Eren's private question with `家族`, then later wondering whether another answer could have changed the path. This establishes counterfactual self-blame but not causal proof.

Second, Louise tells her Eren wanted the scarf discarded. Mikasa takes it back.

The state transition is therefore not:

`attachment -> rejection -> attachment restored`.

A better model is:

`attachment under hostile reinterpretation -> physical distancing -> unresolved counterfactual grief -> refusal to let Eren/derivative followers own the symbol's meaning`.

Her relationship to Eren remains active, but **obedience to Eren's interpretation of that relationship does not**.

## 4.3 Armin Arlert — accurate moral categorization coexists with overload and self-endangering intervention

Armin's V29 strategic-deception model was vindicated in V30. V31 gives him a more severe diagnostic failure: whatever residual model of Eren remained, Eren's Founder broadcast explicitly announces global extermination.

Armin does not continue insisting that Eren must secretly be executing the limited-Rumbling plan. He calls the event an unprecedented massacre. This is another clean instance of revising a close-person model when decisive counterevidence arrives.

At the same time, Armin's executive function is not infinite. He becomes overwhelmed when Mikasa asks him to solve all simultaneous crises. Invoking Erwin exposes a persistent inadequacy comparison.

His recovery is not a sudden master plan. He narrows scope, follows Connie/Falco, and later uses a severe embodied gambit: offering his own body to Connie's mother. This strengthens a state-dependent model in which guilt and overload can coexist with harsh reasoning rather than producing either emotionless competence or permanent paralysis.

## 4.4 Jean Kirstein — beneficiary realism becomes chosen opposition

Jean receives one of V31's strongest longitudinal tests.

He can see Eren's strategic logic. He can say plainly that the outside world's disappearance would remove the threat. He can admit that he and the others benefit. Floch can therefore offer him something psychologically real: stop carrying impossible responsibility and live inside the victory.

Jean imagines that life—and declines it.

His four-shot signal is the decisive behavioral evidence. Jean's moral judgment becomes planning rather than rhetorical purification. He is still the character who notices concrete costs, fears, incentives, and feasible moves. The difference is that realism is no longer allowed to define “whatever benefits us” as sufficient.

## 4.5 Connie Springer — attachment crosses into instrumentalization, then reverses under reciprocity

Connie's V31 arc is deliberately ugly before it becomes reparative. He intends to feed Falco to his mother. This is not a thought experiment. He deceives the child and moves him toward the Titan.

The motive is one of Connie's deepest attachments, which is precisely why the evidence matters. Love does not guarantee ethical means.

Armin's substitution forces Connie to see the same rule applied to someone whose personhood he cannot suppress. Connie saves Armin, abandons the plan, and reframes what honoring his mother would mean.

This materially improves Connie reconstruction:

- grief can push him into severe instrumental reasoning;
- he can deceive under attachment pressure;
- his moral boundary is recoverable through reciprocal concretization;
- repair need not depend on ceasing to want his mother back.

## 4.6 Gabi Braun — revised personhood survives threat and becomes reciprocal rescue

V29 changed Gabi's explicit ontology from “devils” to people. V31 supplies the stronger behavioral test: she saves Kaya under renewed mortal danger.

This pushes her developmental state from verbal recognition to **recognition after complicity with repeated prosocial action**. It also matters that Kaya's response does not require forgiveness. Gabi can become a person who protects Kaya while remaining the person who killed Sasha.

The mature model therefore needs continuity through guilt, not reset through absolution.

## 4.7 Falco Grice — ordinary future desire remains vulnerable to other people's family projects

V29 gave Falco unusually concrete future preferences: Gabi's long life, love, marriage. V30 made his body subject to Zeke's scream and Porco's succession choice. V31 puts the same body under Connie's family claim.

Falco's recurring structural role is not passive victimhood; it is to expose how political/familial systems convert a child's body into an available future resource. His trust in Connie makes the violation sharper.

At V31 he remains alive because Connie rejects the instrumental rule before it becomes irreversible.

## 4.8 Annie Leonhart — moral awareness and father-return attachment coexist without absolution

Annie's V31 retrospective is among the clearest self-model statements in the series. She describes a childhood in which her value was assigned instrumentally, a period in which lives—including her own—felt valueless, the father's late reclassification of her as daughter, and the resulting desire to return.

She now understands that other people have important persons too. She also says she would repeat what she did if that were the condition for returning to her father.

This blocks two bad models:

- “Annie is emotionally indifferent, so severe violence is easy for her.”
- “Annie feels guilt, therefore she would automatically refuse violence.”

Her behavior is relationship-terminal and coercion-sensitive. Guilt changes the meaning of action without reliably eliminating the action.

## 4.9 Reiner Braun — resignation is challenged by the people still choosing futures around him

Reiner is physically exhausted after Eren removes hardening. He understands Eren's action through attachment: Eren is protecting the island and the people important to him.

Gabi does not accept resignation because Falco and her family remain concrete objects of care. Later Reiner is awakened into a coalition containing former enemies. At V31 the important state change is not a new Reiner ideology; it is that the suicidal/exhausted Warrior is again given a relationally concrete task by others who still need action.

The coalition's stability and Reiner's subsequent behavior remain V32 questions.

## 4.10 Hange Zoe — protective deception becomes cross-faction coalition-building

V29 Hange lied to save Levi. V31 confirms the protective deception succeeded and extends it: Hange approaches Pieck and Magath without weapons and proposes cooperation across former enemy lines.

This does not mean Hange now trusts Marley. It shows a mature ability to separate objective alignment from identity category. The governing objective—stop global extermination—is strong enough to justify negotiation with people Hange recently fought.

## 4.11 Levi Ackerman — survival is canonical; current active-capacity inference must remain bounded

Levi is alive. That fact can now replace V29 uncertainty.

But V31 does not justify pretending his ordinary command model is fully active. He is severely injured, bandaged, and speech-limited. His presence in Hange's coalition matters; the exact breadth of current physical/cognitive capacity remains constrained by source state.

## 4.12 Floch Forster — survival rhetoric becomes victorious coercion

Floch now has actual coercive control rather than merely insurgent momentum. His politics are explicit: the outside world will be destroyed; the new Eldian order has won; Volunteers may submit or die.

This is a crucial distinction in the Eren/Floch relationship model. Floch can sincerely believe he is carrying forward Eren's will while adding a domestic institutional program whose details should not simply be projected back into Eren's mind.

Floch's governing pattern is increasingly:

`existential threat -> charismatic/secret legitimacy -> coercive consolidation -> public loyalty test`.

## 4.13 Onyankopon — universalization through remembered victimhood

Onyankopon has one of V31's clearest moral consistency rules. He helped Paradis because he wanted liberation from Marley, not because he wanted a new empire to destroy everyone outside the island. When threatened with death, he refuses to validate the regime that is about to erase his homeland and family.

His argument universalizes Paradis's own grievance: people who know indiscriminate killing is unjust should be capable of recognizing that injustice when they become beneficiaries rather than targets.

This is not abstract cosmopolitanism detached from attachment. His homeland and family are exactly what make the universal claim concrete.

# 5. Relationship-conditioned behavior

## 5.1 Eren → Mikasa — asks for self-description, later tries to dispose of the symbol

The Marley memory and Louise report must coexist. Eren once asks Mikasa to tell him what he is to her; later he tells Louise the scarf should be discarded. The direction of relationship pressure changes dramatically.

Do not compress these into a single “true feeling.” The observed pattern is that Eren both solicits Mikasa's interpretation and later attempts to restructure the material symbol of the bond without her participation.

## 5.2 Mikasa → Eren — attachment persists without surrendering interpretive authority

Mikasa's `家族` answer, V29 scarf abandonment, and V31 scarf reclamation are not contradictions if modeled as state transitions. Her bond remains salient while her relationship to Eren's account of the bond becomes adversarial.

## 5.3 Gabi ↔ Kaya / Blouse family — reciprocity without mandatory forgiveness

Gabi saves Kaya. The family protects Gabi. Kaya's prior desire for revenge does not have to be rewritten as false. V31 instead demonstrates that a relationship can contain unresolved harm and still acquire new reciprocal evidence.

## 5.4 Connie → Falco — trust is violated before the moral rule is revised

Falco follows Connie under incomplete information. Connie's deception is relationship-specific instrumentalization. The later reversal does not erase it; it becomes part of the evidence required to model Connie's shame and repair.

## 5.5 Armin → Connie — intervention works by relational substitution

Armin does not win through abstract argument. He makes himself the alternative body. The tactic relies on knowing Connie's attachment to him and the kind of person Connie wants to be.

This is a relationally informed severe intervention, not a generic “Armin talks people down” pattern.

## 5.6 Annie → father — terminal attachment survives moral awakening

Annie explicitly recognizes other people's important relationships because she now has a father waiting for her. The same relation can motivate both moral expansion and willingness to repeat violence. Terminal attachment is therefore not equivalent to pro-social action.

## 5.7 Annie ↔ Hitch — friction is an attachment register

Hitch's goodbye/thanks note uses dry roommate language. The relationship is not verbally sentimental, but four years of one-sided conversation and the note itself make attachment legible through habitual irritation.

## 5.8 Jean → comrades / Paradis — loyalty does not equal beneficiary compliance

Jean's desire for safety and loyalty to Paradis survive his opposition to Floch/Eren's exterminatory outcome. This is an important relationship-conditioned distinction: one can reject what is done “for us” without rejecting the people named as “us.”

## 5.9 Hange/Levi → Pieck/Magath — cooperation starts at objective alignment, not trust completion

The initial approach is cautious and materially constrained. Hange emphasizes lack of weapons; Levi is injured. The coalition does not need affection to begin. It needs an overlap strong enough to create the next interaction.

## 5.10 Former enemies → coalition — shared prohibition precedes shared identity

The final group does not share a nation, historical narrative, or moral self-conception. It shares a negative commitment: the outside world's extermination must not simply proceed uncontested.

That is a high-value simulation rule. Coalition behavior should not be generated as if every dyad has suddenly become friendly.

# 6. Politics, sovereignty, and genocide

## 6.1 Eren's project is protective in stated purpose and genocidal in stated means

Both halves must be preserved. Calling it “mere genocide without a protective motive” loses source testimony. Calling it “self-defense” without the exterminatory stopping condition loses scale and jurisdiction.

The ethical structure is:

`real threat to Paradis -> claimed protective purpose -> unilateral authority -> extermination of outside life`.

The analytical question is not whether the threat is imaginary. It is whether a real threat licenses unlimited jurisdiction over noncombatants, refugees, children, allies, dissidents, and people who never individually authored that threat.

## 6.2 The beneficiary population is internally plural

Wall-collapse casualties and civilian disagreement matter because they prevent “Paradis” from functioning as a single chooser. Some islanders endorse the cost; others lose family/property and reject it.

A collective can be protected at a strategic level while containing members who never consented to the imposed means.

## 6.3 Rights discourse can fail constitutionally by solving inclusion through scapegoating

The Subject-of-Ymir advocacy meeting offers a nonviolent political language yet preserves Paradis as the excluded devil category. This is a sophisticated failure, not proof that rights discourse is fake. The institution attempts inclusion but lacks reciprocity across the boundary it needs for political acceptance.

## 6.4 Floch demonstrates the domestic afterlife of existential emergency

Once survival is framed as requiring exceptional coercion, the exceptional mechanism can become the foundation of ordinary rule. Floch does not merely celebrate battlefield success; he demands submission, performs executions, and claims the authority to define who belongs in the new order.

The question “what happens after we survive?” therefore arrives before the external threat is over.

## 6.5 Coalition is a constitutional experiment before it is a military one

The final group must act with people whose prior claims of authority they rejected. No shared constitution yet exists. What exists is a minimal reciprocal rule: **the extermination of everyone outside the island cannot be accepted merely because some members benefit from it.**

This is the smallest possible political common ground, but it is enough to make coordinated action conceivable.

# 7. Genocide, guilt, complicity, and moral equivalence

## 7.1 Complicity is not sameness

V31 places many compromised actors in the same coalition, but it does not flatten their responsibilities.

- Reiner and Annie participated in the Paradis operation.
- Pieck and Magath served Marleyan imperial warfare.
- Armin destroyed the Liberio harbor and killed civilians/soldiers.
- Jean and Connie participated in the raid.
- Hange/Levi led military violence.
- Gabi killed Sasha.
- Eren is currently directing global extermination.

These are not morally identical acts. “Everyone has blood on their hands” would be analytically lazy if used to erase scale, agency, age, coercion, or alternatives.

The more useful conclusion is that **prior guilt does not remove standing to oppose a new wrong**.

## 7.2 Benefiting from wrongdoing creates a harder test than condemning an enemy's wrongdoing

Jean's arc is central because the Rumbling benefits him. Opposition therefore requires refusing a good delivered through an impermissible mechanism, not merely condemning something that harms his own side.

Onyankopon supplies the opposite perspective: he is directly harmed by the same project. Their coalition becomes ethically meaningful when beneficiary and victim can coordinate without pretending their stakes are identical.

## 7.3 Connie shows how quickly victimhood can reproduce instrumentalization

Connie is himself a victim of Zeke's Ragako atrocity. That history does not prevent him from considering the use of Falco's body to repair his family. The volume rejects any model in which having suffered instrumentalization automatically immunizes a person against reproducing it.

Ethical learning requires active reciprocity, not merely memory of pain.

## 7.4 Annie refuses easy confession-as-absolution

Her statement is morally difficult because she does not ask the reader to convert guilt into innocence. She admits the crime as irreparable and preserves the father-return motive strongly enough to say she would repeat it.

That makes her more reconstructable, not more exonerated.

# 8. Freedom, pride, and reciprocal refusal

## 8.1 V30's self-authorship problem meets V31's problem of limits

V30 asked who authors a choice. V31 asks what follows when a self-authored choice authors the future of everyone else.

Eren's rejection of Zeke's reproductive plan was grounded in refusal to let someone else decide that Eldians should not be born. In V31 Eren himself decides that outside life may be erased to preserve Paradis. The structural tension is now explicit.

Self-authorship is therefore insufficient as a complete ethics. A person can choose freely and still choose to annihilate other choosers.

## 8.2 `矜持` is not factional pride

The chapter title's pride/dignity is distributed among characters who refuse different forms of cheap survival:

- Connie refuses restoration of his mother through Falco;
- Onyankopon refuses life purchased by submission to Floch;
- Jean refuses a comfortable beneficiary future;
- Armin risks himself rather than let moral reasoning remain abstract;
- Mikasa refuses to surrender the scarf's meaning to Eren's directive;
- the coalition refuses to let prior guilt function as permission for passivity.

The common structure is a limit voluntarily imposed on one's own permissible action.

## 8.3 Reciprocal freedom requires accepting constraints on oneself

This is the strongest continuation of the V27 checkpoint. Freedom becomes constitutional when it includes not only “nobody may control me” but also “my survival/desire does not authorize me to control every cost imposed on others.”

V31 repeatedly stages that shift at different scales:

- Eren fails it globally;
- Floch fails it domestically;
- Connie nearly fails it familially, then reverses;
- Jean accepts it as a beneficiary;
- Onyankopon demands it from victors;
- the coalition begins from it.

# 9. Epistemic modeling and causal restraint

## 9.1 Eren's broadcast is high-authority evidence for objective, not omniscient evidence for causation

The first-person Founder broadcast should carry major weight for operational intent. But it does not answer every causal question around Eren's earlier behavior, especially the Marley visit.

Do not infer from the later declaration that every earlier silence was calculated with complete knowledge.

## 9.2 Mikasa's counterfactual remains actor cognition

Her thought that another answer might have changed events is evidence of grief and responsibility attribution. The causal claim remains unverified.

## 9.3 Floch's “Eren told me” claim establishes testimony, not total ideological identity

Floch is a direct participant with access claims; his statement matters. Yet later state-building choices are still Floch's observed choices. The method requires separating shared plan knowledge from assumed complete worldview identity.

## 9.4 Founder control remains mechanically incomplete

Zeke's Pure Titans continuing to attack is direct anomalous evidence. The correct move is to preserve competing mechanism hypotheses until later source material resolves them.

## 9.5 Armin's model revision remains strong precisely because he stops defending a preferred answer

Armin wanted Eren's hidden plan to be a limited Rumbling. V31 gives decisive contrary evidence. He names the massacre rather than bending the evidence to preserve the preferred model.

This is a clean prospective validation of the frozen `AOT-PP-ARMIN-03` rule under a new, independent diagnostic event.

# 10. Japanese voice and vocabulary

## 10.1 Eren — categorical first-person register scales to global jurisdiction

The Founder broadcast is a high-value Eren voice sample because it is formal enough to address a population but still unmistakably his lexical field:

- `オレの名は`;
- `オレの目的は`;
- `オレはその望みを拒む`;
- `駆逐`.

The register is declarative and low-hedge. The sentence architecture moves from identity -> threat model -> purpose -> rejection -> action/stopping condition.

A simulation constraint follows: Eren's categorical language can carry detailed causal structure. “Short slogans only” remains an inadequate voice model.

## 10.2 Mikasa — relational evidence remains sparse and object-carried

Her important V31 relationship utterances are short: `家族` in the memory, and the concise request to reclaim the scarf. The larger state transition is carried by silence, framing, memory, and object handling.

Do not compensate for sparse source voice by generating long abstract explanations in her mouth.

## 10.3 Armin — moral categorization can become blunt under scale shock

Armin's `前代未聞の大虐殺` language is unusually direct. Under overwhelming contradiction he can abandon diplomatic softening and name the scale categorically.

His later overloaded speech toward Mikasa is more irritable and fragmented than his normal explanatory mode. Voice therefore needs an executive-overload state rather than treating every Armin line as polished analysis.

## 10.4 Jean — practical accounting precedes moral action

Jean's V31 speech is built around consequences: enemies, survival, benefits, who receives those benefits, and what action follows. Even when discussing genocide, he reaches ethics through concrete distributive facts rather than detached theory.

## 10.5 Annie — flattened affect is not absence of explicit moral vocabulary

Annie can state that she once did not care who died, that she now recognizes other people have important persons, and that she has committed irreversible crimes. Her low-temperature delivery should not be mistaken for low self-awareness.

The phrase-level pattern is particularly useful: blunt self-description, relational exception, then morally difficult qualification.

## 10.6 Connie — ordinary family language carries severe ethical content

`母ちゃん` remains the center of the Ragako decision. Connie's moral crisis is not narrated in political vocabulary; it is built through child/friend/mother terms. His later desire to be a soldier his mother could be proud of redefines the relationship norm without reducing attachment.

## 10.7 Onyankopon — anger turns reciprocity into accusation

Onyankopon's execution speech is high-affect but structured. He names why he helped, what the Rumbling will destroy, and the contradiction between Paradis's experience of arbitrary killing and its willingness to benefit from the same structure.

This is not generic idealism. The voice is grounded in homeland, family, and concrete betrayal.

## 10.8 Floch — collective freedom rhetoric becomes command speech

Floch's public register combines victory, empire, enemy elimination, belonging, and threat. He speaks for a collective while deciding who may remain alive inside it. This is useful evidence for how ideological certainty compresses plural subjects into one authorized “we.”

### Lexical network after V31

A useful V31 semantic network is:

`家族 / 父 / 母ちゃん / 仲間` -> attachment and nonfungible persons

`島の悪魔 / 帝国 / 敵` -> category formation and political boundary

`自由 / 駆逐 / 大虐殺 / 世界を救う` -> competing totalizing moral-political registers

`帰る / 守る / 救う` -> apparently benevolent verbs whose ethical meaning depends on what bodies are spent to achieve them

# 11. Manga form and visual architecture

## 11.1 Refugee conviviality makes later extermination spatially personal

The refugee sequence is not simply expositional backstory. Bodies share space: sitting, drinking, touching, sleeping. When Eren later names the outside as a total target, the reader has already occupied that space with him.

The formal effect is to prevent genocide from remaining a map-level abstraction.

## 11.2 Eren and Mikasa are visually isolated during the relationship question

The `オレは…お前の何だ?` scene narrows the frame from collective travel to two people at the edge of a displaced community. The placement matters: an intimate question about belonging occurs amid people who have already lost secure belonging.

Relationship and geopolitics are not separate layers here; the page makes them spatially adjacent.

## 11.3 Wall Titans transform the architecture of protection into moving bodies

V30 turned walls from enclosure into stored force. V31 shows the cost of that transformation at ground level. Buildings and civilians near the walls are damaged as the protective infrastructure moves.

The visual argument is constitutional: infrastructure that “protects everyone” can still crush particular people when activated.

## 11.4 Gabi/Sasha visual rhyme carries recognition without identity substitution

Kaya's perception of Gabi through a Sasha-associated rescue image works because the manga can align positions/actions without claiming equivalence of persons. It is a visual relationship update: Kaya recognizes a familiar protective behavior inside the person linked to her greatest recent loss.

## 11.5 Annie's crystal dissolution is a literal version of the chapter title

`氷解` operates mechanically and relationally. Hardening melts; Annie returns to human space; older fixed categories begin to soften. The title does not promise reconciliation—thaw exposes unresolved material as much as it frees it.

## 11.6 Connie's mother's mouth turns instrumentalization into physical geometry

The Ragako sequence stages Falco above/near the mouth that would convert him into a resource. Armin then places his own body into the same geometry. The ethical comparison is spatial before it is verbal.

## 11.7 Mikasa's scarf functions as portable interpretive territory

The scarf's movement matters across volumes:

`worn -> attacked as evidence of slavery -> left behind -> held by Louise -> ordered discarded by Eren -> reclaimed by Mikasa`.

The object becomes a record of contested authorship over relationship meaning.

## 11.8 Jean's imagined home makes complicity desirable before he rejects it

The domestic image is important because the manga does not make the wrong path aesthetically empty. Safety, quiet, and ordinary life are precisely what Jean has wanted since training. The later decision gains force because the alternative looks like something worth having.

## 11.9 Four gunshots are an off-panel social system

Jean's shots initially look like execution action. Only later does Armin identify the repeated shots as a signal. The reader's interpretation is revised through distributed coordination.

This formally mirrors the coalition itself: people acting in separated spaces can already belong to one plan before the group is visually assembled.

## 11.10 The final convergence refuses a clean heroic tableau

The anti-Rumbling group contains injured bodies, former enemies, adults, children, perpetrators, victims, and people with incompatible national loyalties. The final `世界を救いに` gains its meaning from the visual heterogeneity of the group.

The “world-saving party” is deliberately not composed of morally uncontaminated representatives.

# 12. Bodies, family, and the politics of succession

## 12.1 Falco's body remains a contested inheritance surface

Across V29–V31 Falco's body is repeatedly targeted by systems he did not author:

- candidate inheritance logic;
- Zeke's spinal-fluid coercion;
- Jaw succession through Porco;
- Connie's attempt to transfer that power to his mother.

His ordinary future desire remains the strongest counterfactual baseline against these systems.

## 12.2 Connie's mother is both person and political body

Connie's desire is not abstract Titan research. His mother is a person he wants back. Yet the mechanism available to restore her would make another child the raw material of family repair.

The volume insists that the emotional legitimacy of the end does not settle the legitimacy of the means.

## 12.3 Annie's body was made into family capital before it became state capital

Her adoptive father trained her for Warrior selection because her success would improve his position. The Marleyan institution exploits a prior family instrumentalization rather than creating the entire pattern from nothing.

This complicates any clean state/family binary. Families can resist state logic, but they can also reproduce it.

## 12.4 The Rumbling is population policy executed through inherited bodies

The Wall Titans are literally inherited human/Titan bodies stored as architecture. Eren's stated project uses them to decide which population has a future. This continues the V30 link among bodies, reproduction, sovereignty, and historical command.

# 13. Motifs, callbacks, and longitudinal echoes

## 13.1 “Devils” returns as a category people use to make killing administratively imaginable

Early Marley material taught children to see islanders as devils. Gabi later rejects that ontology. The rights meeting in V31 shows that even humanitarian politics can preserve the label strategically. Floch then embraces the island's demonized identity as victorious force.

The motif's recurrence shows that category rejection is never one-time; institutions can keep reproducing the category after individuals revise it.

## 13.2 The forest ethic returns through Falco

Artur Blouse's V28 principle was that adults must carry history so children can leave the forest. Connie's plan threatens to place Falco directly back into inherited adult grievance. His reversal is therefore a concrete recovery of the forest ethic without anyone needing to quote it.

## 13.3 `駆逐` changes object while preserving Eren's action grammar

The same lexical architecture that once made Titans the total obstacle now operates at global human scale. This is one of the strongest reasons to model voice continuity and ethical transformation separately.

## 13.4 “Going home” remains a dangerous terminal preference

For Annie, `帰る` gives life nonfungible value—but can also justify repeating atrocity. For the 104th, home/Paradis survival motivates Eren's genocide but also Jean's temptation. Home is not morally self-validating; it is a terminal preference whose permissible means must be modeled separately.

## 13.5 Eren's `家族` question echoes later disputes over who may define a bond

Mikasa calls Eren family. Eren later tries to have the scarf discarded. Mikasa reclaims it. The relationship becomes a small-scale version of the volume's political question: **does being deeply connected to someone give them jurisdiction over the meaning of your own attachment?** V31 says no.

## 13.6 “Save the world” returns after repeated criticism of savior language

V29 paired Yelena's Zeke/Eren savior theology with Magath's Helos rhetoric. V31 ends with a group moving to “save the world.” The callback should make the reader cautious rather than automatically triumphant.

The difference is procedural: the coalition is not yet asking to be worshiped as singular savior authority. It is an unstable multi-party effort organized around stopping a totalizing act. Whether that difference survives later action remains open.

# 14. Counterreadings and adversarial tests

## 14.1 Counterreading: the refugee night proves the outside world was peaceful and Eren simply ignored an easy solution

Too strong. The chapter itself shows political advocacy that scapegoats Paradis and a broader environment in which exterminatory threat is real. The refugee night proves person-level heterogeneity, not easy diplomatic solvability.

## 14.2 Counterreading: the rights meeting proves genocide was inevitable

Also too strong. It proves one influential framework sought inclusion by excluding Paradis. A failed or hostile political avenue is not logically identical to proof that every non-genocidal avenue is impossible.

## 14.3 Counterreading: Mikasa's “family” answer caused the Rumbling

Unsupported at V31. Mikasa imagines the counterfactual, but causal confirmation is absent. Her thought is high-confidence evidence of self-blame and uncertainty.

## 14.4 Counterreading: Eren's goal is still ambiguous

The complete psychological motive remains layered; the **operational objective is not ambiguous** after the Founder broadcast. Any reading that treats extermination of outside life as mere fandom inference underweights direct source speech.

## 14.5 Counterreading: because Wall collapse kills islanders, Eren is not really protecting Paradis

This overcorrects in the opposite direction. Eren explicitly states protection as purpose. The more precise conclusion is that a project can be sincerely protective at aggregate level while imposing lethal unchosen costs inside its beneficiary population.

## 14.6 Counterreading: Gabi saving Kaya means Kaya has forgiven her

Unsupported. V29 explicitly preserved Kaya's anger. V31 adds reciprocal rescue evidence. Forgiveness, memory, and present conduct must remain separate variables.

## 14.7 Counterreading: Connie was only bluffing about Falco

# 19. Volume-level synthesis

Volume 31 is the point at which Attack on Titan stops allowing “freedom” to function as a morally sufficient word.

Eren is acting against a real exterminatory threat. His refusal of Zeke's euthanasia preserved the premise that Eldians have no obligation to disappear for the comfort of others. His recognition of Ymir in V30 broke a command relation that had persisted for two thousand years. None of that disappears.

But V31 reveals the limit of a freedom ethic centered only on the self or in-group. Eren's solution to being denied a future is to decide the future of everyone outside Paradis. The same series that condemned turning Historia into reproductive infrastructure, Falco into a succession body, or Ymir into hereditary state machinery now asks what it means when the protagonist uses an inherited body-system to render the outside world's personhood politically irrelevant.

The answer is distributed rather than spoken by a single moral authority.

Mikasa takes back a scarf that Eren tried to define for her. Connie refuses to buy his mother's life with Falco's after Armin makes the rule reciprocal. Gabi protects the child who once wanted revenge for Sasha. Annie admits that understanding other people's attachments does not make her past violence disappear or guarantee future restraint. Jean refuses the seductive argument that being a beneficiary is enough. Onyankopon demands that Paradis generalize the injustice it already understands from its own suffering. Hange brings Levi to former enemies because stopping extermination has become more important than preserving old categories.

None of these people becomes pure.

That is why the last phrase works. `世界を救いに` is not a return to a simpler story where heroes save innocents from monsters. The people who go to save the world include people who have been monsters in someone else's story, people who benefited from atrocity, people who killed civilians, people who killed friends, people who wanted revenge, people who nearly sacrificed a child, and people who still love the person they may now have to stop.

V31's mature ethical claim is therefore not “good people unite against evil.” It is:

> **A compromised person can still draw a line. A victim can still reproduce coercion. A perpetrator can still recognize another person's life. A beneficiary can still reject the benefit. And coalition becomes possible when people stop asking whether they are innocent enough to act and start asking what they are no longer willing to permit themselves to become.**

That is the volume's `矜持`.
