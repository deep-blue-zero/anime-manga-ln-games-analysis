# 『進撃の巨人』 / *Attack on Titan*
## Armin Arlert: Knowledge, Imagination, Dialogue, and Responsibility — V01–V34
### From the book beyond the Walls to revisable understanding, dirty hands, ordinary value, command, and narrative diplomacy

## 0. Function, authority, and anti-caricature boundary

This document is the canonical specialist home, once promoted from provisional publication state, for Armin Arlert's full-series literary analysis across the complete Japanese manga boundary. Its governing question is fixed by the synthesis architecture:

> **How does Armin's imaginative and epistemic orientation mature from curiosity about an unknown world into a method of model revision, dialogue, manipulation, command, moral responsibility, and post-catastrophe political testimony?**

The answer cannot be reduced to “Armin is the smart one,” “Armin believes in talking,” or “Armin represents hope.” Those descriptions identify visible surfaces while missing the architecture beneath them. Armin's defining continuity is not possession of correct answers. It is a recurring willingness to **keep the model revisable**: to imagine that the current map may be incomplete, attend to anomalies, use partial evidence, ask what another person wants, distinguish what is known from what is inferred, exploit interpersonal expectations when necessary, and reopen a future after a preferred explanation fails. The same continuity can support discovery, deception, self-sacrifice, atrocity, negotiation, and diplomacy. That is why his mature method cannot be called innocent.

The controlling claim authority is `AOT_FULL_SERIES_CLAIM_REVISION_LEDGER.md`. Three claims govern this specialist.

- `AOT-FSCR-C020` is **STRENGTHEN**: Armin's mature method is **revision under complicity, not pacifist innocence**. He can deceive, kill, command, weaponize empathy, revise intimate beliefs, and accept morally compromised testimony while retaining guilt and a commitment to understanding.
- `AOT-FSCR-H009` is **STRENGTHEN**: dialogue is **institutionally serious rather than innocent idealism**. Direct failure with Eren does not abolish the method. Armin lowers confidence, assumes command, uses force, revises through Zeke, reasons materially with Muller, and enters diplomacy.
- `AOT-FSCR-M003` is **STRENGTHEN**: moral injury does not erase severe reasoning. Guilt, intimacy, responsibility, tactical cognition, command, and diplomacy coexist rather than replacing one another.

The specialist therefore rejects two opposite caricatures.

The first is **Armin the pure pacifist**: a morally clean character whose value lies in preferring speech to violence. The source does not support it. Armin invents a psychologically cruel lie about Annie to destabilize Bertolt; shoots a human attacker to save Jean; deliberately deceives Eren by exploiting the sea promise and his reputation for honesty; becomes the Colossal Titan and destroys Liberio's harbor with mass casualties; performs ideological alignment to manipulate Yelena; considers a lethal Founder-succession branch; lies to Samuel and Daz in an attempt to secure the flying boat; accepts that anti-genocide action will dirty his own hands; and ultimately fights and helps kill Eren.

The second is **Armin the cold mastermind**: a frictionless genius whose emotion merely decorates correct strategic calculation. That reading is equally false. Armin freezes before solving the Colossal problem in V20. He carries survivor guilt after being chosen over Erwin. He admits that he no longer understands Eren. He reaches explicit executive overload in V31. He experiences disgust, grief, self-reproach, and replacement anxiety. His final private conversation with Eren is not a controlled interrogation conducted from emotional distance. He is angry, frightened, attached, manipulative in places, morally compromised, and still trying to produce an outcome he can live forward from.

The mature Armin is neither innocence nor omniscience. He is a person whose strongest intellectual habit survives contact with his own implication in violence.

### 0.2 Evidence rule: five capacities must remain separate

This synthesis distinguishes five recurrent Armin capacities that are often collapsed into a single trait called “intelligence.”

1. **Imagination** — capacity to represent possibilities not present in the current environment: the sea, unknown phenomena, alternative strategies, another person's reasons, a future not yet observed.
2. **Model revision** — capacity to treat anomalies and prediction failure as evidence requiring reconstruction rather than as threats to identity.
3. **Dialogue and audience modeling** — capacity to use speech to discover, delay, persuade, pressure, deceive, expose, or create a new decision frame.
4. **Coercive and violent competence** — willingness to kill, sacrifice, threaten, transform, or manipulate when the situation closes less destructive branches.
5. **Responsibility-bearing narration** — willingness to tell a story of what happened without pretending that narration cleans the teller, beginning with survivor burden and culminating in `僕達の物語`.

These capacities interact but are not synonyms. Imagination can be morally beautiful and strategically dangerous. Dialogue can be sincere or deceptive. Model revision can produce a better inference without producing a good outcome. Violence can be tactically necessary within Armin's model while remaining morally injurious. Narrative can manipulate, as Willy Tybur demonstrates elsewhere, or can expose the speaker to judgment, as Armin does after Titan power ends.

# I. Mature formulation

## 1. Armin's deepest continuity is not cleverness but an open model of reality

Armin enters the series holding a book. That is not incidental iconography. Before the military recognizes him as useful, before he develops operational confidence, before he inherits the Colossal Titan, and before he becomes commander, his most consequential act is to make **absence imaginable**. The sea, fields of ice, burning water, deserts, and other outside phenomena matter because they turn the Wall from a natural horizon into a contingent boundary. Eren already experiences confinement as humiliation; Armin gives that negative feeling a positive object. The book does not tell the boys exactly what freedom is. It proves that the world exceeds the map they have been given (`AOT_V01_E002`).

That structure persists through the ending. Armin's mature strength is not that he always knows what lies outside a boundary. It is that he can continue treating the boundary as revisable. V02's rogue Titan is useful before anyone can explain it because Armin changes the question from “what category is this being?” to “what behavior is it producing and can that behavior alter our options?” (`AOT_V02_E005`). V03's defense of Eren changes the military question from essence—human or Titan—to demonstrated allegiance, controllability, and utility (`AOT_V03_E004`). V18's search for Reiner turns architecture itself into evidence when the enemy does not fit the expected location model (`AOT_V18_E009`). V20's Colossal-Titan solution emerges because visible thinning, Hange's earlier experiments, heat production, and finite body mass can be assembled into a revised resource model (`AOT_V20_E012`). V29's `嘘だと思う` reconstructs Eren from mechanics, incentives, and longitudinal conduct rather than treating the latest speech as transparent truth (`AOT_V29_E037`). V31 then abandons that preferred reconstruction when Eren's own broadcast supplies decisive contrary evidence (`AOT_V31_E013`).

The continuity is therefore **revision**, not correctness.

This distinction is essential because Armin is wrong repeatedly. He cannot instantly solve every battlefield. He misreads or fails to change people. His Annie/Bertolt interpersonal model loses predictive power. He substantially vindicates his V29 Eren-deception hypothesis in V30, then discovers in V31 that the remaining limited-Rumbling model is catastrophically insufficient. He hopes direct appeal can still reach Eren in V33; Eren explicitly rejects discussion. A trait model that predicts “Armin reaches the right answer” will fail. A process model that predicts “Armin will use the failure itself to rebuild the next decision frame” performs much better.

This is why Hange's final criterion is so exact. Armin becomes fifteenth commander not because he is a smaller Erwin, not because he is morally pure, and not because the story declares him the smartest living person. Hange names `理解することをあきらめない姿勢`: the posture of not giving up on understanding (`AOT_V33_E021-E022`). The institution recognizes the process the manga has already been testing for thirty-three volumes.

## 2. Imagination is epistemic before it is optimistic

“Hope” is too vague for Armin because his positive orientation is usually grounded in **unobserved possibility** rather than emotional reassurance. After the serum decision, he can agree that Erwin should have survived and doubt his own worth, yet still resist closing the future. Levi refuses to claim certainty about the unchosen counterfactual, and Armin turns that uncertainty outward: they still do not know everything; possibilities remain (`AOT_V22_E026-E027`).

At the sea, this becomes the sharpest contrast with Eren. The childhood destination is physically realized, but realization does not end interpretation. Armin encounters the ocean through curiosity and wonder. Eren, burdened by inherited history, looks across the same water and relocates the enemy boundary. A shared image therefore produces divergent freedom grammars. For Armin, discovering that the childhood map was incomplete invites further discovery. For Eren, discovering that the hoped-for space is populated contributes to a crisis in which freedom increasingly appears to require removal of other people (`AOT_V22_E031-E032`).

V33 returns to this difference explicitly. Armin tells Annie that even though the imagined outside world was not what he and Eren expected, he still wants to believe there is something unknown beyond what they have already seen (`AOT_V33_E012`). This is not an assertion that the unknown will be good. The series has made that impossible. It is an assertion that disappointment does not entitle the disappointed observer to collapse the future into one exhaustive model.

Armin's imagination is therefore not the belief that the world is benevolent. It is resistance to **premature closure**.

That resistance can itself be dangerous. A person who can imagine more branches can also imagine manipulative branches, sacrificial branches, and weapons. Armin's imagination produces the rogue-Titan plan, the Colossal defeat plan, the Annie/Bertolt emotional attack, and the harbor deception. The moral value of imagination depends on what it serves and what costs it is willing to impose.

## 3. Understanding is a practice, not a verdict of innocence

Armin repeatedly tries to understand enemies because person-level models create operational options. V06's false report about the “suicidal bastard” tests Annie's reactions. V12's fabricated description of Annie's torture targets Bertolt's attachment. V19's conversation with Bertolt begins as an attempt to reopen dialogue and becomes a deliberate attempt to exploit his earlier emotional vulnerability (`AOT_V19_E017-E018`). V28 interrogates Eren's causal story by asking whether even his secret Yelena meeting counts as free choice; after Eren attacks Mikasa's autonomy, Armin turns Eren's own freedom/slavery vocabulary back against him (`AOT_V28_E017-E024`).

None of this makes Armin neutral. Understanding is frequently instrumental. He wants to know because knowing may expose leverage. The moral significance lies elsewhere: even after people become targets, Armin resists flattening them into objects whose interiority no longer matters.

That resistance does not prohibit killing. This is one of the series' harshest Armin lessons. Bertolt can understand Armin and keep burning him. Armin can understand Bertolt's attachment and weaponize it. Armin can understand Eren more deeply than almost anyone and still help kill him. At the ending he can hear Eren's full confession, remain his friend, condemn the massacre as `最悪の過ち`, and accept responsibility for what follows (`AOT_V34_E041`).

Understanding is therefore neither acquittal nor agreement. It changes what can truthfully be said about a person and what options can be constructed around them. Sometimes those options are conciliatory. Sometimes they are coercive. Sometimes the accurate conclusion is that discussion has failed.

## 4. The mature Armin thesis

The full-series formulation is:

> **Armin's defining continuity is a refusal of epistemic and moral closure. He imagines possibilities beyond inherited maps, reconstructs models when evidence breaks them, treats other persons as sources of reasons and not only as targets, and seeks communicative openings where they can alter outcomes. But the same character can lie, manipulate, kill, command, and accept dirty hands. His mature ethics therefore lies not in remaining innocent but in continuing to revise and understand from inside complicity, while refusing to convert either guilt or empathy into absolution.**

The terminal extension is equally important:

> **What begins as the book beyond the Walls becomes a politics of testimony. After Titan power disappears, Armin can no longer rely on a supernatural asset or a guaranteed common truth. He exposes his own vulnerability as evidence, identifies himself to an armed adversary, and later offers `僕達の物語`—the account of enemies who killed one another and nevertheless arrived to ask for peace. The final instrument is not certainty. It is a revisable story offered to another person's judgment.**

# II. Longitudinal development

## 5. V01–V03: the book, vulnerability, and the first reclassification of the impossible

Armin begins as physically vulnerable and socially low-status. The narrative does not compensate by making him secretly omniscient. His first importance is relational and imaginative. He possesses an object that lets Eren conceptualize a world beyond confinement. The childhood trio consequently begins through unequal reciprocal capacities: Armin supplies horizon, Eren supplies intervention and forward momentum, and Mikasa supplies practical survival.

The V01 rescue in Trost makes that asymmetry concrete. Armin freezes inside the Titan's mouth; Eren pulls him free and is swallowed (`AOT_V01_E007-E008`). The event matters to Armin's later identity because survival repeatedly arrives through costs paid by others. His eventual survivor burden does not begin only with Erwin. The series trains him early to experience continued life as something acquired through another person's expenditure.

V02 establishes the cognitive pattern that will become his signature. The “rogue Titan” does not fit the known Titan model. Armin does not need a full ontological explanation before using the anomaly. He infers that a Titan attacking Titans can change the tactical geometry and proposes exploiting it (`AOT_V02_E005`). This is a basic but durable rule: **when ontology is uncertain, observed behavior may still support action**.

V03 extends that rule from monsters to persons. Eren's partial Titan body places him inside an impossible category. The military response tends toward essence: if he is Titan-like, he is threat. Armin's defense instead treats demonstrated conduct as evidence. Eren protected Mikasa and Armin; that behavior has to count in the decision. Armin is not proving a universal philosophy of personhood in abstract terms. He is changing the operational question from “what is he?” to “what has he done, whose side does that behavior indicate, and what possibilities does his existence create?” (`AOT_V03_E003-E004`).

The later nape intervention adds a second dimension. Armin reaches Eren through childhood memory and the outside-world horizon (`AOT_V03_E008`). Knowledge is already relational: the right evidence is sometimes not technical information but a value-laden shared image capable of reconnecting a person to a prior objective.

## 6. V04–V07: fighting, hindsight, and the dangerous theory of discarding humanity

Armin's early strategic thinking should not be mistaken for a taste for self-sacrifice. V04 supplies the line `命を投げうつことだけが戦うことじゃない`: throwing away one's life is not the only form of fighting. This is important because later Armin repeatedly risks his life. The later behavior is therefore not evidence that he always glorified sacrifice. It emerges inside a broader model where the proper question is what action changes the outcome.

V07 gives two of the most important early epistemic claims in the series. First, Armin distinguishes judgment made after results are known from decision quality under the information available at the time. `結果を知った後で…` begins a sustained attack on hindsight moralism. The series repeatedly asks characters to choose without certainty; Armin recognizes early that a later bad outcome does not by itself prove the chooser could have known better (`AOT_V07_E001`).

Second, he proposes that people capable of changing something may need to discard something important, even `人間性` (`AOT_V07_E003`). This idea is powerful and dangerous. Later volumes neither simply vindicate nor reject it. Erwin's charge, Armin's own self-sacrifice, Hange's command, the harbor killings, and the final coalition all show that terrible costs can be necessary within a plan. But the full series also demonstrates that **moral numbness is not the same thing as effectiveness**. Armin remains effective while retaining guilt. Reiner's capacity to feel does not eliminate action. Jean's hesitation can improve moral perception. Hange's grief does not eliminate command. The early “discard humanity” formulation must therefore be treated as an immature compression of a harder truth: action may require crossing ordinary moral thresholds, but dehumanization is neither a necessary prerequisite nor a reliable strategic advantage.

This correction matters for Armin himself. A weaker development model would say that he learns to become harder. The actual development is closer to learning how to act **without claiming that hardness cleans the act**.

## 7. V08–V13: empathy becomes leverage

The middle pre-Uprising volumes sharpen Armin's ability to model how another person's relationships constrain them. This is where “talking” becomes visibly ambiguous.

Against Annie and Bertolt, Armin uses knowledge of attachment as a weapon. He can reopen speech because speech creates time and information, but he is not committed to transparency. In V12 he invents Annie's torture to destabilize Bertolt (`AOT_V12_E006`). The content is cruel precisely because he predicts it will matter to the listener.

This is a foundational constraint for later reconstruction: **Armin's empathy is not equivalent to gentleness**. If he knows what another person cares about, he may use that knowledge to protect them, persuade them, or injure them strategically. The continuity is audience modeling. The morality depends on stakes and target.

That same structure will later appear with Eren in V20, Yelena in V29, Connie in V31, Samuel/Daz in V32, Zeke in V34, and Muller after Titan power disappears. The methods differ, but each involves identifying what evidence or cost the interlocutor cannot easily ignore.

## 8. V14–V16: the human-killing threshold and moral injury

Armin's shooting of the human attacker aiming at Jean is one of the most important anti-caricature events in his arc (`AOT_V14_E008`). The act is tactically defensible: hesitation would likely have allowed Jean to die. Yet the manga refuses to transform defensibility into psychic cleanliness.

Levi later tells Armin that his hands are already dirty and thanks him for the life he saved (`AOT_V15_E001`). The pairing is exact. “You saved Jean” and “you killed a person” remain simultaneously true. The moral lesson is not that killing was secretly harmless because the cause was good. It is that responsible action can create an irreversible burden even when the alternative would have been worse.

This becomes the seed of Armin's mature dirty-hands ethics. He does not respond by renouncing all severe action. Nor does he protect himself by pretending the victim ceased to be a person. The memory remains behaviorally active.

V16 and the Uprising aftermath also matter because Armin's competence is increasingly socialized into group action rather than lone cleverness. The series is already preparing the difference between **having an idea** and **bearing command responsibility for the consequences of ideas**.

## 9. V17–V20: model failure, command trust, deception of Eren, and self-sacrificial cognition

Return to Shiganshina is the major conversion point between early strategist Armin and the later responsibility-bearing figure.

V18/V19 first elevate his anomaly detection. He searches the Wall itself for Reiner because the ordinary location model has failed (`AOT_V18_E009`). In V19 he reconstructs enemy logistics from the quadrupedal Titan and horse-targeting behavior (`AOT_V19_E001`). Hange gives him local command responsibility because demonstrated capability now warrants role-specific trust (`AOT-RB-020`).

The Bertolt confrontation then exposes the limit of an old interpersonal model. Armin tries the familiar Annie lever. Bertolt recognizes the manipulation and does not collapse into the old deferential state. Mikasa and Armin independently perceive that he seems like a different person (`AOT_V19_E023`). This is a crucial precursor to Armin's later handling of Eren: **long acquaintance is evidence, not immunity from state change**.

V20 tests whether Armin can update fast enough under catastrophic pressure. He cannot immediately solve the Colossal Titan. Jean must carry practical command while Armin freezes. This negative evidence is as important as the eventual solution. His stable capacity is not instant brilliance; it is eventual reconstruction once relevant evidence becomes available.

The solution itself is exemplary. Visible thinning, prior Hange experiments, heat production, and finite muscle mass become a resource model (`AOT_V20_E012`). Armin then designs a plan in which his own body will absorb lethal heat while Eren uses the opening.

The primary pages make the relational manipulation explicit. Armin tells Eren that they promised to go to the sea together and asks, `僕がエレンにウソついたことあった?` (`AOT_V20_E015`). The utterance is itself part of the lie. He uses a history of honesty as strategic capital because he knows Eren will otherwise break the plan to rescue him. This is not betrayal for advantage. It is deception in service of a shared objective whose cost Eren would refuse if fully disclosed.

Three mature Armin traits therefore converge in one scene:

- **model revision** produces the Colossal solution;
- **relationship knowledge** predicts Eren's rescue behavior;
- **deception** preserves Eren's compliance with a plan Armin believes necessary.

Armin's self-sacrifice should not be romanticized as proof that he values himself least. He explicitly says he cannot die before seeing the sea and then suppresses that desire because the plan requires it. The act is severe precisely because the positive horizon remains real.

The prospective register later scored this sequence as a clean PASS for anomaly-driven model reconstruction and a clean PASS for negotiation/manipulation coexistence. It also added a vital negative constraint: model revision may be preceded by freeze. The character is not a calculation machine.

## 10. V21–V22: survival chosen over Erwin, replacement anxiety, and the sea split

Armin does not experience survival as uncomplicated validation. The serum decision makes him the beneficiary of an explicit comparative choice against Erwin. Eren argues that Armin will save humanity and invokes the sea as evidence that Armin retains a future-directed imagination Eren himself has increasingly lost (`AOT_V21_E003`, `AOT_V21_E006`). But Armin wakes into an institutional burden he did not choose.

He learns that only nine Scouts survived, that he received the serum rather than Erwin, and that future expectations now attach to him. Hange assigns future contribution; Levi refuses the fiction that Armin can simply replace Erwin. The distinction is protective and demanding at once: Armin is not Erwin, but being different does not release him from having survived (`AOT_V21_E009`).

V22 turns this into explicit survivor doubt. Armin can say Floch is right about the comparative utility argument and that Erwin should have survived. Yet he does not convert that judgment into a closed future. Levi's refusal to claim knowledge of the correct unrealized timeline matters here. Armin's answer is not “I am secretly better than Erwin.” It is that the unobserved future is still unobserved. They know too little to treat one counterfactual as settled fact (`AOT_V22_E026-E027`).

This is one of the most mature forms of his imagination. The early book says “the world is larger than the Walls.” The post-serum version says **the future is larger than the counterfactual story guilt is currently telling me**.

The sea then changes from promised object into diagnostic divergence. Armin's wonder is genuine. He handles the new environment through curiosity and discovery. Eren instead looks past it toward enemies and asks whether killing them all would make the group free (`AOT_V22_E031-E032`). The same childhood symbol now reveals two different ways of processing a disappointed horizon.

Armin's open-world orientation is not ignorance of danger. He has just inherited memories and military responsibility. It is a refusal to treat the existence of other people beyond the sea as evidence that the horizon itself was fraudulent.

## 11. V23–V27: the outside world becomes political, the Colossal becomes complicity, and Eren stops being legible

The Marley arc forces Armin's epistemic method into a domain where knowledge itself carries mass-casualty power. He is no longer only the observer who finds a weak point. He is the Colossal Titan.

Liberio is the decisive moral conversion. Armin transforms at the harbor and destroys military infrastructure on a scale that also kills soldiers and civilians (`AOT_V26_E007`). The visual and political effect deliberately places him inside the silhouette once associated with terror from outside the Walls. This is not an accidental symmetry. The boy whose book made the outside world desirable now inhabits the body that can erase portions of that world.

His post-Liberio reasoning is accordingly not pacifist outrage from a clean position. He enumerates alternatives, recognizes the military logic that made the attack appear necessary, names the soldiers and civilians killed, and worries that the operation destroyed a path toward reconciliation. He can reach something like `やるしかなかった` while refusing to let “had to” mean “was good.” When he admits `もう…わからない` after saying he believed he understood Eren better than anyone, the line is a confidence downgrade, not generalized intellectual collapse (`AOT-BD-091`).

V27 formalizes the mature branch structure. Armin still prefers direct three-person dialogue with Eren and Mikasa because the relationship may contain information unavailable to institutions. At the same time he reasons that if Eren and Zeke become irreconcilably opposed to the island's survival, a trusted successor may need to inherit the Founder (`AOT_V27_E016`). Negotiation and lethal contingency are not mutually exclusive identities.

The volume also adds small but important ordinary controls. The railroad flashback shows Armin participating in peer embarrassment and humor; when Eren's statement of attachment leaves the group blushing, Armin deflects by blaming the sunset (`AOT-EL-040-E041`). Hitch's teasing when she catches him touching Annie's crystal makes him flustered and defensive (`AOT-EL-044`). These scenes matter because later command evidence must not erase the person who can be awkward, playful, and embarrassed in low-threat peer settings.

By the end of V27 the central problem is not whether Armin still cares about Eren. It is that the **intimate model is stale**. Eren's choices increasingly occur outside the trio's shared procedure. Armin still believes conversation may update the model, but he no longer has grounds for treating historical intimacy as current transparency.

## 12. V28: Eren attacks Armin's authority over his own mind

The table confrontation is a direct assault on Armin's epistemic selfhood. Eren asserts `オレは自由だ` while explaining Armin's attachment to Annie as an effect of Bertolt's inherited memories. The structure matters more than whether every factual claim is true: Eren claims privileged access to the causal origin of Armin's judgments and uses that claim to deny Armin's self-authorship.

Armin does what his mature model predicts. He first tests Eren's freedom assertion causally: was the secret meeting with Yelena also simply Eren's free choice? (`AOT_V28_E017`). When Eren degrades Mikasa, Armin's register compresses. He attacks physically and then turns Eren's value language back on him: is hurting Mikasa the freedom Eren wanted; who is the slave here? (`AOT_V28_E024`).

The scene is important because Armin's epistemic style is not emotionless. A clear threat to a close person's dignity can move him from diagnostic questioning into anger-state brevity and violence. “Rational Armin” and “emotional Armin” are not separate characters. Emotion changes register and tactic while the causal challenge persists.

Later evidence also weakens Eren's biologized account. But Armin's specialist does not need to prove that Bertolt's memories have zero effect on him. Titan inheritance may shape salience. The critical limit is that inheritance cannot be used as an all-purpose explanation that voids current agency, especially when Armin repeatedly demonstrates independent audience modeling, guilt, embarrassment, desire, and strategic judgment.

## 13. V29–V30: inference under opaque speech and the vindication of hedged reconstruction

V29 is a textbook demonstration of Armin's source criticism. Eren appears to endorse Zeke's euthanasia plan. Armin does not simply trust his friend, nor does he simply discard prior knowledge. He builds a competing hypothesis from mechanics and incentives: Eren may be deceiving Zeke in order to obtain Founder access for a limited Rumbling.

The crucial Japanese anchor is `嘘だと思う` (`AOT_V29_E037`). `と思う` marks inference. Armin does not say he has directly recovered Eren's true mind. This is exactly the epistemic behavior a rigorous reconstruction should preserve: confidence can be high enough to act without becoming certainty.

The Yelena interaction adds audience-conditioned sincerity. In private, Armin rejects euthanasia. In Yelena's presence, he performs emotional agreement and savior language strongly enough to gain trust (`AOT_V29_E043`). This is a clean recurrence of the earlier rule: speech content cannot be mapped directly to belief without audience, objective, and action state.

V30 substantially vindicates the euthanasia-deception component when Eren directly rejects Zeke's plan and confirms that he had been playing along. That success should not be used to turn Armin into a mind reader. V31 immediately demonstrates why. A model can correctly explain one deception and still be disastrously incomplete about the actor's larger objective.

The lesson is methodological: **successful inference raises confidence in a process, not immunity from later falsification**.

## 14. V31: genocide names the failure, overload limits the strategist, and Connie forces embodiment

Eren's Founder broadcast destroys the residual bounded-Rumbling model. Armin does not reinterpret the announcement to preserve what he wanted to believe. He names the event `前代未聞の大虐殺`—an unprecedented massacre (`AOT_V31_E013`). The primary page confirms the categorical force of the phrase. The analytical speaker can abandon hedging when the evidence is no longer ambiguous.

This is one of the cleanest full-series validations of `ARMIN-03`: stale interpersonal models should be revised after diagnostic failure. The important continuity is not trust in Eren. It is willingness to let evidence lower or destroy that trust model.

But V31 immediately prevents this competence from becoming superhuman. Mikasa asks Armin what to do while multiple crises collide: global extermination, Connie taking Falco, Annie's release, military collapse, Yeagerist seizure of power, and the disappearance of stable command structure. Armin snaps and says he does not know. He invokes Erwin as the person who would have known (`AOT_V31_E027`).

The scene supplies a mandatory **executive-overload state**. High reasoning ability does not imply infinite simultaneous-problem integration. Armin can become irritable, self-comparing, and temporarily fragmented. The correct reconstruction response is not to lower his intelligence; it is to represent bandwidth and inherited-role pressure.

The Connie/Falco crisis shows how he recovers. Abstract argument fails. Connie's plan instrumentalizes a child to restore his mother. Armin recognizes both the wrong and the relational logic driving it. Rather than repeat a moral lecture, he makes the rule reciprocal by offering his own body to Connie's mother's Titan (`AOT_V31_E034-E035`). Connie saves him.

The intervention is severe and relationship-specific. Armin deliberately creates a choice Connie cannot easily abstract. He uses his own nonfungibility to expose the hidden moral structure of Connie's plan. This is neither ordinary persuasion nor simple self-sacrifice. It is **embodied argument**.

The event also sharpens the distinction between dialogue and communication. Armin's method is not “use words.” It is “find a form of evidence the other person must process.” Sometimes that evidence is a sentence. Sometimes it is a body placed into the rule.

## 15. V32: anti-genocide without innocence

V32 gives Armin's dirty-hands ethics its clearest direct statement. Magath offers to bear the morally filthy work so the former Scouts can remain righteous. Armin answers `断ります` (`AOT_V32_E020`). He rejects the possibility of being correct without dirtying his own hands.

This refusal must be read carefully. It is not a declaration that all means are now acceptable. The very next operational choice is an attempt to avoid bloodshed. Armin and Connie race into the occupied harbor with a fabricated story designed around Yeagerist assumptions: the Cart Titan and Marleyan remnants have escaped, the flying boat is needed for pursuit, and of course Armin and Connie would not be trying to stop Eren because that would doom the island. The primary pages show how categorical the performance is (`AOT_V32_E021`).

Armin's internal objective is bloodless extraction if the mechanics and aircraft can be secured. Deception comes first because transparent moral persuasion would announce the coalition's actual objective and likely trigger immediate violence.

The ruse fails. Floch notices inconsistencies; Samuel shoots Armin; Daz moves to restore the explosive threat; Connie eventually kills former comrades. Samuel's accusation `裏切ったんだろ?` and memory of a shared future of expanded land and meat refuse the coalition a clean heroic identity (`AOT_V32_E024`). From Samuel's perspective, Armin and Connie really are traitors to a comradeship and island-survival project.

This sequence is the mature correction to the early “discard humanity” theory. Armin can do what the situation requires without pretending that the other side has ceased to be human, that the betrayal is unreal, or that anti-genocide purpose cleans the blood.

That is what “revision under complicity” means behaviorally. The model keeps the victim and the cost inside the representation.

## 16. V33: command succession makes understanding institutional

V33 begins by returning Armin to the outside-world horizon in a quieter register. His conversation with Annie makes explicit what the corpus had only gradually established: his interest in Annie was not solely Titan-memory contamination or strategic curiosity. When Annie asks why he kept visiting someone who could not answer, Armin says `会いたかったからだ…アニに`—because he wanted to see her (`AOT_V33_E012`).

The scene is important for Armin's self-concept. He rejects the “good person” category and owns his killings. Yet he also preserves a simple personal desire and the belief that unknown parts of the world may remain. Intimacy, guilt, and curiosity coexist without being reduced to one another.

Hange's succession then transforms a personal method into an institutional principle. Facing a delaying action likely to kill them, Hange claims command responsibility and names Armin fifteenth Survey Corps commander. The stated qualification is `理解することをあきらめない姿勢`—the posture of not giving up on understanding (`AOT_V33_E021-E022`).

This is not ordinary promotion. The Survey Corps is nearly destroyed as an organization. What transfers is not an intact bureaucracy but a constitutional habit: continue asking what the unknown is doing to the model.

The story immediately stress-tests the principle. Armin plans around the unknown location of Eren's human body and holds his Colossal transformation as a final option after attempting dialogue (`AOT_V33_E026`). The ordering matters because he is no pacifist. He knows his own catastrophic-force capacity and is willing to use it. But where a communicative branch remains, he ranks it above irreversible destruction.

Paths provides the window. The coalition's appeal is not framed as moral purity. They acknowledge guilt and responsibility. Armin asks why Eren left their Titan powers intact and insists they can still talk (`AOT_V33_E034`). Eren rejects the premise: discussion is unnecessary; the only offered route is physical stopping.

Armin returns to the aircraft recognizing that the hope of negotiation is gone (`AOT_V33_E037`). Levi's `どうする？ 団長` turns failed dialogue into immediate command responsibility.

This is the final proof that Armin's dialogue orientation is not dogma. **When dialogue fails diagnostically, he updates.** He does not infer that communication was stupid to attempt. He infers that this interlocutor, in this state, has closed that branch.

## 17. V34A: captivity, self-reproach, and the separation of bodily incapacity from cognition

V34 begins by humiliating the new commander operationally. Armin intends to retain the Colossal as a decisive reserve but is captured by an okapi-like Titan before he can transform (`AOT_V34_E003`). Promotion does not create control. Exceptional power can be neutralized by circumstance.

Inside captivity, Armin's first response is severe self-reproach. He compares his current inability to the lives and expectations placed on him, a continuation of the Erwin replacement burden (`AOT_V34_E013`). But the sequence does not end in self-condemnation. He separates the body's trapped condition from the fact that he is still thinking (`AOT_V34_E014`).

That distinction is deeply consistent with the early series. When categories fail, find the remaining behavior. When the body cannot execute command, cognition can still search for mechanism. Armin begins modeling Ymir, the Founder, and the inherited shifters rather than treating capture as pure defeat (`AOT_V34_E006`).

The liminal encounter with Zeke therefore emerges from epistemic persistence inside physical helplessness. The battlefield problem becomes a problem about the value of life (`AOT_V34_E015`).

## 18. V34B: ordinary life is not a consolation prize but Armin's final ontology of value

Zeke offers a totalizing biological model: life reproduces, survives, fears death, and suffers in order to continue. Armin does not answer with a rival metaphysical system. He answers with memory.

He remembers racing Eren and Mikasa toward the hill tree, warm wind and falling leaves. He says they may have been born for something as apparently trivial as that moment: `生まれてきたんじゃないか` (`AOT_V34_E017`). The sequence expands: reading a book on a rainy day, watching a squirrel eat a nut, walking through a market with friends. Armin names `このなんでもない一瞬`—this nothing-special instant—as something of extraordinary value (`AOT_V34_E018`).

The primary page sequence matters because the argument is phenomenological rather than doctrinal. Armin does not prove that life has one universal purpose. He demonstrates that **instrumental accounts fail to exhaust experienced value**.

This is the deepest continuity back to V01. The outside-world book mattered not because it promised conquest or reproduction but because seeing something, learning something, and sharing that discovery with another person could be sufficient reason to move beyond the Wall. V34 generalizes the structure while shrinking its scale. Meaning does not require the sea. It can exist in a leaf.

Zeke initially perceives the held object differently: the leaf appears to him as a baseball. His memory of Ksaver is throwing and receiving, again and again, without a larger purpose (`AOT_V34_E019-E020`). Armin changes Zeke's model not by defeating it with a more comprehensive abstraction, but by supplying an example that makes the abstraction insufficient.

This is one of the clearest demonstrations of Armin's dialogue method at its best. He does not tell Zeke what he “really” values. He offers concrete experience and lets Zeke retrieve his own counterexample.

The result has operational consequence: Zeke changes action and exposes himself. Yet the scene should not be reduced to “Armin wins an argument.” The argument works because Zeke already contains the relevant relationship memory. Armin creates the interpretive opening.

## 19. V34C: understanding Eren without making the massacre good

Armin's final private conversation with Eren is the most ethically difficult dialogue in his arc because intimacy, condemnation, manipulation, grief, and inherited burden all occupy the same exchange.

Armin presses Eren on Mikasa, future knowledge, the scale of death, the heroization plan, Ymir, and Eren's own desire. He provokes the humiliating admission that Eren does not want Mikasa to forget him and does not want to die. He remains physically and emotionally close. He takes Eren's hand.

This closeness must not be translated into exoneration. Armin names what Eren did `君の最悪の過ち`—his worst mistake (`AOT_V34_E041`). The Japanese primary pages preserve the contradiction in the same exchange that contains Armin's ethically compromised gratitude for Eren becoming a mass murderer “for us.”

The best reading is not that Armin secretly approves genocide, nor that the gratitude is morally clean because he later condemns it. It is **shared-burden speech from an implicated beneficiary addressing a dying friend**. Armin knows that Eren's program was partly organized around protecting him and the others. He cannot stand outside the consequences as a pure judge. He also cannot make the act good.

His promise is therefore prospective: he will not let the catastrophe become meaningless. That is not permission to retroactively justify it. It is acceptance that survivors inherit consequences they did not wholly author and must decide what to do with them.

Eren tells him that the person who will save humanity is Armin. The phrase should not be treated as prophecy. Its narrative function is transfer of expectation to the character whose most stable strength is revisability. The next scene immediately tests whether that strength can operate without Titan spectacle.

## 20. V34D: Muller's rifles and the conversion of vulnerability into evidence

After Titan power disappears, Muller and armed soldiers confront the surviving Subjects and demand proof that they are no longer Titans. Armin does not ask for blind trust. He makes present vulnerability into an argument.

If they still possessed Titan power, he reasons, they would use it to resist. Yet rifles are pointed at them and they remain powerless. This is `我々が人である何よりの証明です`: the strongest proof that they are human (`AOT_V34_E046`). He then identifies himself fully as `パラディ島のエルディア人 / アルミン・アルレルト` and as the person who killed Eren Yeager.

The move condenses Armin's mature method:

1. identify the interlocutor's fear;
2. identify material evidence available in the present;
3. expose oneself rather than demand immunity from judgment;
4. frame the evidence in terms the interlocutor can evaluate;
5. attach a consequential identity claim that changes the story of who is standing there.

The argument is not guaranteed to work by metaphysical truth. It is politics. Armin's body, history, and credibility become the evidence surface.

The contrast with Titan power is deliberate. As the Colossal, Armin could transform and annihilate. After Titan power ends, his political leverage is the opposite: **he cannot transform, and that inability is evidence**.

## 21. V34E: `僕達の物語` and narrative diplomacy

Three years later, the former enemies travel as peace delegates toward a heavily militarized Paradis. The scene does not pretend conflict is over. Armin explicitly says fighting will not disappear. Annie asks whether the island will believe them.

Armin's answer is `僕達の物語` (`AOT_V34_E049`). They can tell the story of people who tried to kill one another and ended up standing together, asking for peace.

This should not be sentimentalized into “storytelling solves war.” The earlier corpus has already shown narrative's darker political forms. Willy Tybur constructs a staged story with a narrator because political mobilization needs legibility. Propaganda, inherited history, and national myths can organize killing. `物語` is not inherently emancipatory.

Armin's use is different because the story implicates the tellers. It is not “we were always righteous.” The delegation contains Warriors, Scouts, perpetrators, victims, traitors, and beneficiaries of the Rumbling. The narrative's political value lies in its capacity to make a previously impossible relation **describable** without erasing the violence that preceded it.

That is the endpoint of the book. Armin began by bringing a representation of an unseen world into a closed society of children. He ends by bringing a representation of an improbable relation into a closed political environment of adults.

The object has changed from geographic book to shared testimony. The epistemic wager is the same: the current map may not be exhaustive.

# III. Epistemic architecture: imagination, anomaly, uncertainty, and revision

## 22. Armin's imagination is an epistemic faculty before it is an optimistic temperament

The easiest flattering caricature of Armin is that he is “the hopeful one.” That description is too vague to explain either his early importance or his later severity. His first distinctive contribution is not optimism as mood. It is the ability to treat a representation of the unseen as actionable evidence that the present world may be incomplete.

The V01 book matters because it supplies a geography that neither Armin nor Eren has personally observed (`AOT_V01_E002`). Armin's response is not merely escapist fantasy. He behaves as though the discrepancy between represented possibility and current enclosure is intellectually consequential. The book changes what can be asked. The sea, deserts, ice fields, and other unknown places become evidence that the Wall does not exhaust reality.

This produces a recurrent structure across the manga:

1. the current model appears closed;
2. an anomaly, testimony, representation, or unexplained residue appears;
3. Armin refuses to identify the limits of the current model with the limits of reality;
4. he generates an alternative explanation or action branch;
5. the branch is tested against consequences;
6. success or failure changes confidence rather than retrospectively making uncertainty unreal.

That structure explains why Armin can be hopeful without being generically cheerful. His optimism is often **model-relative**: there may be an unobserved route because the available model has not demonstrated that all routes are closed. V22 makes this explicit when he can accept Floch's accusation that Erwin should have survived and still refuse the stronger conclusion that the future is therefore exhausted (`AOT_V22_E026`). The response is not “everything will work out.” It is closer to “we still do not know enough to warrant closure.”

The final volume repeats the structure at its smallest scale. Zeke offers an apparently comprehensive biological model of life. Armin does not need a larger theory. A remembered leaf, a race, a rainy book, a squirrel, or a market walk is enough to show that the theory is not exhaustive (`AOT_V34_E017-E019`). The intellectual form is continuous with V01 even though the scale reverses: the child uses an unseen world to break a closed local model; the adult uses an ordinary moment to break a closed universal model.

## 23. Anomalies matter because Armin can suspend ontology long enough to use behavior

A second recurrent feature is Armin's willingness to reason from **what a thing does before he knows what a thing is**.

In V02, the rogue Titan that attacks other Titans violates the existing enemy category. Armin does not need a finished theory of Titan shifters before the anomaly becomes tactically useful (`AOT_V02_E005`). In V03, he similarly reframes Eren before the military tribunal-like confrontation: rather than asking the frightened soldiers to accept a complete metaphysical account of Eren's nature, he emphasizes demonstrated allegiance and utility (`AOT_V03_E003-E004`).

The same habit becomes more sophisticated later. In V18, a failed search model is itself evidence; Armin asks whether “common sense” about where a human body could hide is the problem and finds Reiner inside the Wall structure (`AOT_V18_E009`). In V19 he links logistics, prior Titan behavior, horse vulnerability, and enemy incentives into a waiting strategy (`AOT_V19_E001`). V20's Colossal problem initially freezes him, but the recovery comes by observing resource expenditure rather than clinging to the assumption that overwhelming size implies inexhaustible heat (`AOT_V20_E012`).

This matters for character reconstruction because “smart Armin” cannot mean instantaneous answer generation. His canonical intelligence often begins by **allowing the anomaly to remain anomalous**. He can be shocked, frightened, or temporarily stalled. The high-confidence behavior is not frictionless certainty but renewed search once a mismatch becomes legible.

## 24. Armin's characteristic uncertainty is graded, not indecisive

Armin's Japanese and decision history repeatedly distinguish directness from certainty. He can state a severe plan while preserving uncertainty about its premises; he can believe a model and later lower confidence without treating the earlier inference as dishonest.

V07 provides an early explicit epistemic ethic: outcome knowledge after the fact cannot simply be projected backward into a decision made under uncertainty (`AOT_V07_E001`). This is important because the series repeatedly invites retrospective omniscience. Armin resists that temptation before he has any special institutional authority.

The later Eren crisis tests the same rule against intimacy. Armin once believed he understood Eren better than almost anyone. After Liberio and the widening gap between Eren's behavior and Armin's model, `もう…わからない` is not global helplessness. It is a **confidence downgrade about a particular person** (`AOT_V26_E026-E027`). By V29, his strategic reconstruction of Eren is explicitly hedged as `嘘だと思う`—“I think it's a lie”—rather than presented as direct access to motive (`AOT_V29_E037`).

That grammar should control literary interpretation as well as simulation. Armin can:

- hold a working hypothesis;
- act on it;
- publicly present stronger confidence than he privately possesses if deception requires it;
- revise after disconfirmation;
- preserve attachment after confidence in his own understanding collapses.

None of those operations is equivalent to chronic indecision.

## 25. Hindsight is morally dangerous because it can erase the chooser's information state

Armin's V07 distinction between decision-time knowledge and result-time knowledge becomes more ethically significant as his own decisions accumulate bodies.

After the Liberio attack, he cannot simply say that the operation “worked.” He knows the Colossal transformation destroyed military infrastructure and killed people. He can still reconstruct why the operation appeared necessary and why Eren's unilateral action constrained the available response (`AOT_V26_E007`). This is not moral exoneration by necessity language. It is an attempt to keep three propositions simultaneously visible:

- the actors had a specific information and constraint set;
- the action may have had strategic reasons;
- the resulting deaths remain morally consequential.

That is the mature extension of the V07 rule. Refusing hindsight simplification does not mean refusing judgment. It means judging an action without pretending the actor had information or options not actually available while also refusing to let constrained choice erase harm.

## 26. Armin's intelligence is socially distributed more often than solitary

Armin is frequently treated by other characters and by readers as “the strategist,” but some of his best reasoning is enabled by other people creating the conditions in which he can think.

V20 is exemplary. Armin stalls. Jean does not replace him; Jean creates time and observational space, orders experimentation, and insists a clue must exist (`AOT_V20_E012`). Armin then reconstructs the Colossal's resource mechanic. The successful plan is therefore not evidence that Armin is an isolated genius whose companions merely execute instructions. It is evidence of **distributed cognition under trust**.

The same pattern appears differently with Eren and Mikasa. Early Armin can articulate possibilities that Eren converts into movement and that Mikasa protects materially. Later, those roles no longer align automatically, but the triad still demonstrates that cognition, action, and relation are often distributed rather than located in one “smart” person.

This is also why Hange's succession decision should not be read as appointing an omniscient replacement. Hange selects Armin because he does not give up trying to understand (`AOT_V33_E026`). The criterion is process, not possession of superior answers.

## 27. Epistemic openness has a danger side

Armin's openness to non-obvious possibilities is not automatically morally benign. V07's willingness to contemplate discarding ordinary humanity in order to change anything is the clearest early warning (`AOT_V07_E003`). His imagination can model severe means as well as peaceful alternatives.

That danger matures rather than disappears. Armin can propose Founder succession if Eren and Zeke oppose Paradis (`AOT_V27_E016`). He can deceive Yelena and Samuel/Daz. He can use Annie as emotional leverage against Bertolt (`AOT_V19_E017`). He can deliberately become the Colossal weapon in Liberio.

The ethical value of Armin's imagination therefore does not lie in generating only humane options. It lies in keeping models revisable and persons cognitively present even when severe options enter the tree. The distinction becomes especially important in V32: the alliance may have to kill former comrades, but Armin and the others do not gain moral cleanliness by declaring those victims nonpersons.

## 28. Terminal epistemic limit: Armin does not become the series' omniscient interpreter

The ending grants Armin unusually broad access to Eren's confession and unusually large political responsibility. It does not make him an infallible narrator of the entire metaphysics.

He can press Eren, expose contradictions, hear motive fragments, and later tell a political story. But several questions remain outside his demonstrated knowledge:

- the exact mechanics and counterfactual freedom of Founder temporal experience;
- the complete causal architecture of Ymir's relation to Mikasa;
- the full information Eren possessed at every earlier point;
- every strategic alternative available to Paradis;
- the durable outcome of postwar diplomacy.

This ceiling matters because Armin's thematic importance can tempt an interpreter to turn his final language into authorial omniscience. The source supports something subtler: Armin becomes a privileged **responsibility-bearing witness whose method remains revision**, not a final doctrinal mouthpiece who dissolves all ambiguity.

# IV. Dialogue: method, manipulation, failure, and institution

## 29. Dialogue in Armin's repertoire means preserving an interlocutor as a source of reasons

Armin's dialogue orientation is often misdescribed as pacifism. The stronger formulation is procedural: when circumstances permit, he tries to preserve a channel through which another person can reveal motives, update information, or change action.

That channel can be morally attractive, but it is also strategically valuable. A living, speaking opponent can provide information a dead opponent cannot. A person whose motive is understood can be influenced differently from a faceless enemy. Dialogue therefore belongs to Armin's epistemic architecture as much as to his ethics.

V19 makes the point in a hard case. After recognizing that restraint of Reiner has become implausible, Armin still reopens a negotiation window with Bertolt when circumstances change (`AOT_V19_E017`). He then weaponizes Annie's supposed suffering because he believes that relationship matters to Bertolt. The same interaction contains sincere interest in stopping the battle through speech and deliberate manipulation.

The coexistence is canonical. Armin does not require transparent sincerity from himself before speech counts as a tactic.

## 30. Audience modeling is the hidden machinery beneath Armin's speech

A mature Armin model must ask not only “what does he believe?” but also “what does he think this audience can process?”

This is visible across very different scenes:

- to frightened soldiers in V03, Eren's demonstrated conduct and military usefulness are more persuasive than an impossible complete ontology (`AOT_V03_E004`);
- to Bertolt, Annie is chosen as leverage because Armin models an intimate vulnerability (`AOT_V19_E017`);
- to Eren in V20, Armin uses their history of trust and the sea promise to make a lethal deception credible (`AOT_V20_E015`);
- to Yelena, he can perform emphatic agreement that matches the worldview she expects while privately pursuing another plan (`AOT_V29_E037`);
- to Samuel and Daz, categorical in-group loyalty is the correct surface for a bloodless harbor deception (`AOT_V32_E020-E021`);
- to Zeke, an abstract rebuttal gives way to a concrete memory that lets Zeke retrieve his own counterexample (`AOT_V34_E017-E019`);
- to Muller, present material vulnerability is more useful than a moral plea (`AOT_V34_E046`);
- to postwar Paradis, a story of former enemies now traveling together is proposed because institutional mistrust requires legible testimony (`AOT_V34_E049`).

This is why surface earnestness is not enough to classify an Armin utterance. His speech has to be read against **audience, objective, confidence, relationship, and available evidence**.

## 31. Manipulation does not negate the dialogue ethic; it exposes its dirty-hands form

Armin repeatedly uses people instrumentally. The Annie gambit against Bertolt is the obvious case. So is the V20 lie to Eren. So is the performance for Yelena. So is the harbor deception.

A weak defense would say these acts are exceptions to his “real” conciliatory personality. The stronger account is that they belong to the same practical intelligence. He wants communicative channels because people have beliefs, relationships, and reasons. The very fact that he takes those interior states seriously makes them available as leverage.

This creates an ethical tension rather than a contradiction. **Recognition can become manipulation when another person's subjectivity is modeled primarily as a control surface.** Armin never fully escapes that danger.

The mature correction is not that he stops influencing people. It is that later dialogue increasingly includes his own implication and vulnerability. Before Muller, he does not stand outside the problem and demand trust. He places his own body and identity into the proof. In postwar diplomacy, the delegation's story is useful precisely because its tellers are implicated in the violence they describe.

## 32. Bertolt is the first major proof that understanding another person does not guarantee control

The V19 Bertolt negotiation is important because it denies Armin a comforting theory of empathy. Armin correctly identifies Annie as a meaningful relation. Bertolt sees the tactic, understands what Armin is doing, and nevertheless continues the mission.

The failure teaches a rule the series will later make explicit with Eren: **accurate access to one value in another person does not imply access to a value strong enough to determine the decision**.

This should prevent an overpowered Armin simulator. He can infer motives without automatically predicting behavior. People contain competing commitments, thresholds, role states, and newly authored priorities. Bertolt's changed state in V19 is precisely what Armin and Mikasa have to recognize after older assumptions cease to work.

## 33. Eren is the hardest diagnostic failure because intimacy raises the prior

Armin's model of Eren carries unusually strong prior confidence. They share childhood memory, the outside-world dream, battlefield trust, and years of mutual rescue. That makes the later failure psychologically and epistemically more damaging than misreading an ordinary enemy.

V26 supplies the confidence collapse. V27 still leaves relational hope open: perhaps a direct conversation among Eren, Mikasa, and Armin can restore intelligibility (`AOT_V27_E016`). V28's table encounter instead produces coercive testimony, humiliation of Mikasa, an attack on Armin's autonomy, and physical violence (`AOT_V28_E017`). V29 reconstructs Eren strategically as possibly lying, but Armin does not pretend this restores intimate understanding.

V33 is the decisive test. Armin appeals to the fact that Eren has left their Titan powers intact and asks for talk; Eren explicitly closes discussion and offers only a fight (`AOT_V33_E034-E037`). Armin returns from Paths with a lowered model: this branch is no longer available.

The result is not “dialogue was foolish.” It is **diagnostic failure producing tactical revision**. Armin assumes command and participates in lethal stopping.

## 34. Zeke demonstrates a different kind of successful dialogue: model interruption, not conversion by rhetoric

The V34 Zeke encounter succeeds partly because Armin does not demand that Zeke accept an opposing grand doctrine.

Zeke's biological account contains truth. Reproduction and survival matter. Death is frightening. Life can be brutal. Armin does not have to falsify those observations. He offers experiences the model does not explain away: a race, leaves, books, animals, markets (`AOT_V34_E017-E019`).

Zeke then supplies his own memory of playing catch. The change is internally mediated. Armin provides the anomaly; Zeke revises.

This is the strongest version of Armin's dialogue ethic because it preserves the interlocutor's authorship. The outcome still serves a military objective, but the speech does not simply overwrite Zeke with Armin's worldview.

## 35. Muller demonstrates dialogue after the special power disappears

The Muller scene is politically important because it tests Armin's method without Titan leverage. The Subjects cannot transform. Guns are pointed at them. Armin cannot win by superior physical threat.

He therefore reasons from what the soldiers themselves can observe: if the group were still Titans, being held at gunpoint would create every incentive to transform. Their inability to do so is evidence (`AOT_V34_E046`). Armin then names himself and accepts the morally loaded identity of the person who killed Eren Yeager.

This is not a plea for Muller's kindness. It is an argument that respects the other side's security concern enough to answer it materially.

Dialogue here is **institutionally serious** because it operates in the structure of fear, proof, identity, and authority rather than imagining that goodwill alone dissolves conflict.

## 36. `僕達の物語` is dialogue scaled from interpersonal exchange to public institution

Postwar diplomacy extends the same logic to a collective audience. The island is militarized; the delegation includes people whom Paradis can plausibly view as traitors or enemies; the world has just experienced catastrophic extermination. Armin does not claim that a conversation will erase those facts.

He proposes `僕達の物語` (`AOT_V34_E049`). The political wager is that testimony about former enemies who came to act together can expand the island's model of what relations are possible.

This differs from naïve faith in speech in three ways:

1. the tellers have coercive and violent histories;
2. the story does not promise that conflict ends;
3. the story is only an opening condition for politics, not politics' guaranteed conclusion.

Armin ends the manga not as someone who has proved dialogue always works, but as someone who has accumulated enough evidence of failure to know exactly why keeping the possibility of revision open still matters.

## 37. Dialogue's ethical floor is personhood, not nonviolence

The strongest unifying formulation is that Armin resists making communication failure equivalent to ontological expulsion.

He can decide that an opponent must be fought. He can kill. He can help destroy a harbor. He can betray comrades. He can accept that Eren has to be stopped. But the opponent's status as a person with motives, attachments, history, and capacity for suffering remains cognitively active.

That is why his violence so often produces moral injury instead of triumph. The cost of preserving personhood is that killing a person cannot be made clean simply by placing them in an enemy category.

This is the inverse of dehumanization as a psychological efficiency technique. Armin's method is operationally harder because the human meaning of the target remains present.

# V. Violence, dirty hands, and moral injury

## 38. Armin is never canonically defined by an inability to use violence

The pacifist caricature fails very early. Armin's V04 claim that throwing away one's life is not the only form of fighting expands the concept of struggle; it does not abolish force. By V07 he can articulate severe strategic propositions. By V12 he uses intimate cruelty as a weapon. By V14 he shoots a human attacker to save Jean (`AOT_V14_E008`).

The progression matters because later Colossal responsibility is not a complete reversal. The magnitude changes enormously; the basic capacity to choose harmful means under pressure does not suddenly appear after the serum.

A reconstruction that makes Armin physically incapable of choosing lethal force until “corrupted” by later events would therefore be historically false.

## 39. The first human kill establishes the moral-injury pattern

After Armin kills the attacker threatening Jean, the source does not reward him with clean heroic certainty. V15's aftermath preserves the burden: Levi frames the act in terms of the life Armin saved, but the dead person's humanity is not erased (`AOT_V15_E001`).

This supplies an early pattern that the later manga repeatedly strengthens:

- action can be necessary within the local constraint set;
- the actor can still experience the action as morally injurious;
- recognizing necessity does not require celebrating the self who acted;
- guilt does not automatically imply the action was strategically irrational.

That four-part structure becomes central after Liberio and the harbor.

## 40. The Colossal Titan makes Armin inherit the logic of disproportionate force

Armin's survival in V21 is inseparable from inheritance of one of the series' largest weapons. This makes his survivor burden more than a comparison with Erwin's leadership. He inherits a body whose normal military use can produce destruction on a scale radically larger than ordinary combat.

Liberio forces him to use it. The image of Armin looking over the devastation is not merely an inversion of Bertolt's earlier role. It is the point at which Armin's epistemic and ethical method has to operate **from inside mass-casualty agency** (`AOT_V26_E007`).

His later search for dialogue is therefore not innocence preserved outside war. It is dialogue after becoming part of the machinery he condemns.

## 41. Moral injury is information, but it is not automatically a command veto

Armin's guilt changes behavior and self-conception. It can produce hesitation, self-accusation, and the sense that the path to reconciliation has been damaged. Yet the corpus repeatedly rejects the equation:

> feeling morally injured = losing the capacity for severe reasoning.

V26 has Armin enumerate consequences while still reconstructing necessity. V31 combines genocide naming with planning and an extreme bodily substitution proposal. V32 has him participate in an operation against former comrades. V33 transfers command to him. V34 has him reason through captivity, persuade Zeke, confront Eren, and speak to Muller.

This is the stabilized `AOT-FSCR-M003` claim: moral injury is real and behaviorally consequential, but it does not erase tactical cognition, command, or diplomacy.

## 42. V31's Connie intervention turns moral principle into reciprocal bodily risk

The Connie/Falco crisis is one of Armin's clearest ethical-action scenes because he does not merely argue that feeding a child to Connie's mother is wrong. He places himself into the sacrificial structure (`AOT_V31_E027`).

The move is strategically risky and emotionally extreme. But its ethical grammar is important: if Armin is going to reject the instrumentalization of Falco, he refuses to make the rule safe only for himself. He offers his own Titan body as the cost-bearing substitute.

This should not be romanticized into ideal decision-making under all circumstances. It does establish a recurrent Armin tendency: when persuasion fails and another person is about to be made into a means, he may convert an abstract principle into **self-exposure**.

That same grammar reappears more politically before Muller: credibility is increased by placing his own vulnerable body and identity inside the argument.

## 43. The harbor is the strongest correction to any fantasy of clean anti-genocide resistance

V32 is crucial because the alliance's morally defensible objective does not produce morally clean means. Armin's plan is initially deception intended to avoid bloodshed (`AOT_V32_E020`). The situation collapses. Samuel and Daz are former comrades. Connie eventually kills them. Armin is shot.

The sequence makes “stopping genocide” insufficient as a purification mechanism. The alliance betrays people with whom it shared training, food, fear, and ordinary plans. Those relational facts do not disappear because the alliance's larger cause is better.

Armin's mature ethics is therefore not “choose the good side and become clean.” It is closer to: **choose under responsibility, preserve the humanity of people harmed by that choice, and do not use the righteousness of the objective to erase the local betrayal.**

## 44. `断ります` rejects moral cleanliness as a strategic luxury

The Magath confrontation is another important anti-caricature. When a framing is offered that would let the islanders escape the role of morally contaminated actors, Armin refuses it (`AOT_V32_E021`).

His refusal matters because Armin is highly sensitive to guilt. Precisely for that reason, a model of him as someone who seeks absolution at every opportunity would be plausible but wrong. He can decline an exculpatory framing when it falsifies shared responsibility.

This becomes essential to V34. His relationship with Eren is not resolved by standing outside the catastrophe as a pure judge. He accepts implication while still calling Eren's act the worst mistake.

## 45. Dirty hands do not mean all choices become equivalent

One danger of emphasizing complicity is moral flattening: if everyone has killed, betrayed, or benefited, perhaps all acts become morally interchangeable.

Armin does not support that conclusion. He can acknowledge his own Colossal killings and still identify the Rumbling as unprecedented mass slaughter (`AOT_V31_E013`). He can say he is already a monster without inferring that Annie, Eren, the Warriors, the Scouts, and every civilian therefore occupy the same responsibility position.

His moral language becomes anti-purity without becoming anti-distinction.

This is important for the full series because *Attack on Titan* frequently places perpetrators and victims in overlapping roles. Armin's mature posture provides one way to preserve differentiated judgment without relying on fantasies of uncontaminated standing.

## 46. Understanding Eren is not a retroactive pardon

The final conversation is where the dirty-hands thesis is most vulnerable to misreading. Armin's intimacy, grief, gratitude, and burden-sharing are morally compromised because Eren's mass murder was partly organized around saving Armin and their friends. Armin cannot receive that fact as though it has nothing to do with him.

But the conversation still includes condemnation (`AOT_V34_E041`). Understanding explains why a person acted and preserves relation at the threshold of death. It does not convert victims into acceptable costs.

The distinction should be stated sharply:

- **understanding** asks how motives, constraints, relationships, and choices fit together;
- **forgiveness** concerns a relationship and need not be complete;
- **justification** asks whether the act was warranted;
- **absolution** removes or cancels blame;
- **responsibility-bearing** asks what the survivor must do with consequences already produced.

Armin moves most clearly toward the first and fifth. The manga does not authorize collapsing them into the third or fourth.

# VI. Command, succession, and distributed responsibility

## 47. The serum decision makes Armin a successor before he is psychologically ready to be one

Armin does not choose to survive over Erwin. Others choose for him. He wakes into a comparison whose terms are almost impossible: the dead commander associated with world-changing victories versus a young strategist who now possesses the Colossal Titan (`AOT_V21_E009`).

This creates a persistent replacement burden. Armin does not simply gain status. Survival becomes an externally imposed demand to justify why he was allowed to live.

The V22 confrontation with Floch is therefore formative. Armin can accept the proposition that Erwin should have survived and still continue forward (`AOT_V22_E026`). His development does not require convincing himself that Levi's choice proved he was “better than Erwin.”

That refusal is healthier epistemically than a compensatory superiority narrative, but it leaves him vulnerable to recurrent self-comparison.

## 48. Armin's command style is not Erwin imitation

The architecture should resist a common succession simplification: Erwin dies, Armin inherits strategic genius, Hange later transfers command, therefore Armin becomes the same leadership function in a younger body.

The source differentiates them.

Erwin's command can mobilize people through public rhetoric, concentrated authority, extreme sacrifice, and a private epistemic obsession. Hange's command is improvisational, investigative, institutionally self-critical, and increasingly burdened by political collapse. Armin inherits from both but does not become either.

Hange's stated reason for selecting him is decisive: `理解することをあきらめない姿勢`—his stance of not giving up on understanding (`AOT_V33_E026`). Succession is explicitly tied to epistemic process, not rank seniority, charisma, or willingness to spend lives.

## 49. Formal command arrives at the moment his preferred relational method fails

The timing of Hange's appointment is structurally severe. Armin becomes commander immediately before Hange's death and just before the final confrontation with Eren confirms that direct dialogue will not stop the Rumbling.

He is therefore not rewarded with authority because his method has proven universally successful. Authority arrives precisely when he has to decide what remains of the method **after one of its most important applications fails**.

Levi's `どうする？ 団長` after the Paths encounter compresses the change. Armin cannot remain the person who only proposes possibilities for someone else to authorize. He has to convert diagnostic failure into action.

## 50. Command for Armin is often branch management rather than singular certainty

Armin's strongest strategic speech often has conditional form: if this model is true, this branch follows; if it fails, another branch must remain available.

V27's combination of hoped-for dialogue and Founder succession contingency is a clear case (`AOT_V27_E016`). The two branches are morally and relationally very different, but Armin can hold them in one decision tree.

This pattern distinguishes him from an idealized “conviction leader” whose strength is always categorical certainty. Armin's authority often consists in **maintaining a branching model long enough to avoid premature closure**, then collapsing branches when evidence demands it.

## 51. Distributed agency remains a feature even after he becomes commander

Armin's appointment does not make the coalition a one-person system. Levi continues to supply tactical judgment. Mikasa owns the Eren relationship threshold in ways Armin cannot. Jean and Connie make decisions in their own domains. Reiner and the Warriors contribute unique capabilities and knowledge. Zeke's terminal choice cannot be commanded by Armin; it is elicited through dialogue.

This matters for both ethics and reconstruction. Armin should not be generated as a commander who automatically dominates every meeting because he has the title. His strongest leadership often creates or preserves **conditions for other people to act**.

The postwar delegation embodies that principle. `僕達の物語` is plural. Armin can narrate, but the story's credibility depends on the co-presence of people whose histories cannot be replaced by his explanation.

## 52. Command overload is a real state, not evidence that the commander role is fraudulent

V31 provides a sharp overload sample. Eren has initiated mass extermination, island politics are collapsing, Connie has taken Falco, Annie is newly returned, and Armin is being pulled across incompatible crises. His behavior is strained and sometimes desperate (`AOT_V31_E027`).

A robust character model needs to preserve the possibility that Armin's normally articulate reasoning can compress, become self-sacrificial, or lose interpersonal smoothness when concurrency exceeds capacity.

That does not mean the strategic identity was fake. It means cognition has bandwidth limits.

This point is especially important because fictional “genius” reconstructions often erase exhaustion. The manga does the opposite: it repeatedly makes the cost of thinking under catastrophe visible.

## 53. Postwar diplomacy is command transformed, not command abandoned

The ending does not remove Armin from responsibility after the Titan war. It changes the medium. The alliance no longer has Titan power; the island is heavily militarized; conflict persists. Armin's role shifts toward representation and testimony (`AOT_V34_E049`).

That is not a retreat into soft politics after “real” military action. In the completed arc, diplomacy is one more form of responsibility under uncertainty. The same person who once inferred Titan behavior and later commanded a final battle now has to model audiences, credibility, historical narratives, and future relation.

The continuity is not profession. It is method.

# VII. Relationship architecture

## 54. Eren: shared horizon, epistemic intimacy, model failure, and burdened witness

Eren is Armin's most important longitudinal relationship because the bond begins in shared imagination before either boy has meaningful institutional power.

Armin brings the outside-world book; Eren supplies the explosive response to enclosure. The relation becomes reciprocal through rescue, military trust, battlefield deception, and the sea promise. Armin is not simply Eren's “brain.” He is a custodian of a positive horizon Eren repeatedly loses and partially recovers.

V20 demonstrates the bond's operational depth: Armin can lie precisely because Eren trusts him (`AOT_V20_E015`). V21 demonstrates Eren's corresponding valuation when he argues for Armin's life using both public utility and their shared dream (`AOT_V21_E003-E006`).

The sea then exposes divergence. Armin can still encounter the outside as discovery; Eren experiences the new geography through enemies and inherited memory (`AOT_V22_E031`). The same dream no longer produces the same world.

The late relationship is therefore not simple betrayal of an unchanged childhood bond. It is **epistemic divergence inside enduring attachment**. Armin keeps trying to model Eren because the prior relationship makes intelligibility matter. When the model fails, the attachment remains. When Eren must be stopped, the attachment remains. When Eren confesses, Armin becomes a witness who can hold love, anger, understanding, condemnation, and future responsibility together (`AOT_V34_E041`).

For reconstruction, the key variables must be separated:

- affection: extremely high;
- trust: historically high, later severely damaged;
- confidence in motive model: collapses after Liberio;
- willingness to deceive Eren: demonstrated;
- willingness to oppose physically: terminally demonstrated;
- desire to understand: persists after trust collapse;
- willingness to absolve: not established.

## 55. Mikasa: triadic history without interpretive ownership

Armin and Mikasa share a relationship that can disappear in Eren-centered readings even though the corpus repeatedly makes it behaviorally important.

Mikasa protects Armin; Armin helps articulate problems Mikasa often expresses through compressed judgment and action. The serum conflict shows that both are willing to challenge Levi over Armin's life, but their emotional and discursive modes differ. Later, Armin and Mikasa jointly confront Eren but do not have identical objectives.

The final arc makes that distinction crucial. Armin's political objective is to stop the Rumbling and preserve a possibility of dialogue; Mikasa retains a more relation-centered desire to bring Eren back until the lethal threshold closes. Neither owns the other's interior authority.

Armin's terminal conversation with Eren includes Eren's possessive confession about Mikasa, making Armin a witness and intermediary. That does not grant Armin authority to define what Mikasa's love means. The Mikasa specialist correctly retains that authority in her own source-grounded trajectory.

For simulation, Armin/Mikasa interaction should support:

- very high mutual trust in crisis;
- long-history shorthand;
- Armin often verbalizing a model Mikasa evaluates concretely;
- no assumption that Mikasa obeys his relational interpretation;
- mutual willingness to act independently when their Eren thresholds diverge.

## 56. Annie: enemy-model complexity becomes ordinary relational preference

Armin's relation with Annie is valuable because it links three modes often falsely separated: strategic attention, moral ambiguity, and simple desire for company.

Earlier, Annie is an enemy whose behavior Armin can analyze and whose relationship to Bertolt can be weaponized. During crystal isolation, Armin repeatedly visits and speaks in a context where Annie cannot respond normally. V27 provides bounded embarrassment when Hitch catches him at the crystal (`AOT_V27_E034`). V33 later strips away an over-intellectual explanation: `会いたかったからだ…アニに`—he went because he wanted to see her (`AOT_V33_E013-E014`).

The scene is diagnostically important because Armin often explains himself in causal or strategic language. Here the simplest motive is relational preference.

At the same time, his attachment does not require declaring Annie a “good person.” He tells her he too has long since become a monster. The relation develops inside mutual moral contamination rather than through purification of one partner for the other's comfort.

The manga supports reciprocal attachment strongly enough to constrain reconstruction. It does not establish every detail of a completed postwar romantic partnership, so later domestic specifics remain open.

## 57. Hange, Erwin, and Levi: succession as differentiated trust

Armin's relation to Erwin is largely mediated by comparison and inheritance. Erwin becomes the dead standard against which Armin fears he was wrongly chosen. The healthiest development is not overcoming Erwin by declaring himself superior; it is becoming capable of action without resolving the counterfactual.

Hange's role is different. Hange works with the living Armin and eventually names the quality worth institutionalizing: refusal to give up understanding (`AOT_V33_E026`). This is an act of epistemic trust.

Levi's relation to Armin includes the serum choice, later command recognition, and the terse transfer of responsibility after dialogue with Eren fails. Levi does not repeatedly reassure Armin that choosing him was correct. The relationship therefore should not be modeled as paternal confidence repair. It is closer to practical recognition: Armin is alive, has capacities, and must act from the present rather than solve the dead counterfactual.

## 58. Jean, Connie, and the 104th: intelligence does not erase peerhood

Armin remains a peer as well as strategist. This matters for low-stakes voice and for command.

V27 gives a high-value ordinary control: after Eren's emotionally direct statement about wanting his friends to live long lives, the group becomes embarrassed; Jean reacts to the atmosphere; Armin jokingly blames the sunset for everyone's red faces. This is not command-room Armin. It is a young adult among old friends.

Jean is also important cognitively. V20 shows Jean giving Armin the space to recover and think. The relationship is not “smart leader and ordinary subordinate.” Jean can stabilize the operational environment when Armin stalls.

Connie supplies an even harder relational test. The Falco/mother crisis and later harbor violence force Armin to act around a friend whose grief and desire have become dangerous. His willingness to put his own body into the equation demonstrates that peer attachment can generate self-risk rather than simply verbal management.

## 59. Zeke: dialogue between rival totalizing tendencies

Armin and Zeke are not close in the ordinary relational sense, but their V34 exchange is one of Armin's most important intellectual relationships because it pairs two highly analytical characters whose models differ in scope and emotional history.

Zeke tends toward explanatory closure: biology, reproduction, suffering, salvation through ending the reproductive future. Armin's mature response is not anti-intellectualism. He does not say theory is bad. He supplies an experience that a total model has failed to value.

The scene therefore models a form of high-level dialogue useful for reconstruction: Armin can engage an ideological opponent by **finding the underexplained remainder**, not only by disputing premises head-on.

## 60. Relationship summary: understanding is directed, not universalized

Armin's relationship behavior should not be compressed into a trait such as “empathetic toward everyone.” His understanding is directed and state-conditioned.

He may:

- intensely model a close friend and still be wrong;
- manipulate an enemy through a valued relationship;
- desire ordinary contact with a former enemy;
- accept institutional trust without feeling worthy of it;
- act violently against former comrades while retaining grief;
- help an ideological opponent recover a private source of value;
- present different degrees of sincerity to different audiences.

The common feature is not universal warmth. It is that other minds remain **causally and morally real** in his model.

# VIII. Japanese written voice, register, and audience-conditioned sincerity

## 61. `僕` anchors continuity, but register changes with task

Armin's most stable first-person form is `僕`, which fits the explanatory and reflective register that dominates many of his scenes. That continuity should not be mistaken for one fixed degree of softness or politeness. His syntax and lexical temperature change substantially with objective.

In analytical contexts he often builds causal or counterfactual structures: what would follow if a premise were true, what anomaly remains unexplained, what alternative might still exist. In intimate contexts his language can become strikingly simple. In anger, it can compress sharply. Under deception, it can become categorical. In formal diplomacy, it can become evidentiary and institutional.

A generative model therefore needs at least five register modes rather than a single “intelligent polite Armin” voice:

1. **analytical-explanatory** — causal chains, alternatives, uncertainty markers;
2. **intimate-positive-horizon** — simple language around shared exploration, companionship, or wanting to see someone;
3. **adversarial-compressed** — short value-internal challenges under anger;
4. **performative-deceptive** — surface certainty calibrated to an audience;
5. **formal-representative** — public proof, identity, diplomacy, collective narration.

## 62. `と思う` and `わからない` are epistemic controls, not weakness markers

Armin's language often preserves the distinction between a hypothesis and a fact. `嘘だと思う` in the Eren reconstruction is especially important because the hedge is diagnostic, not ornamental (`AOT_V29_E037`).

Likewise, `わからない` can mark a bounded failure of a model. After Liberio, Armin's admission that he no longer understands Eren is meaningful precisely because he once believed the opposite. It is not an invitation to generate generic uncertainty about topics where he has strong evidence.

Simulation should therefore represent uncertainty **locally**. An Armin model that hedges every statement would sound timid and erase his capacity for categorical action. An Armin model that never hedges would erase one of his clearest epistemic virtues.

## 63. Tactical lying is fully canonical

V20 is the strongest intimate example. Armin's reputation for honesty with Eren becomes the instrument that makes the lie effective: `僕がエレンにウソついたことあった?` (`AOT_V20_E015`).

The point is not merely that Armin “can lie.” The more important voice rule is that **sincerity is audience-conditioned and objective-conditioned**. A truthful-sounding autobiographical appeal may itself be part of a deception plan.

V29 and V32 strengthen the rule in public/political settings. Armin can perform a stronger proposition than he privately holds when doing so creates an opening. `そんなことやるわけねぇだろ!!` at the harbor is intentionally false at the proposition level but tailored to Samuel and Daz's expected in-group frame (`AOT_V32_E020`).

This makes a naive “truthful Armin” language model unsafe. The model must track what he knows, what he believes, what he wants the audience to believe, and what action the utterance is serving.

## 64. Anger reduces exposition without erasing diagnosis

V28 adds a crucial high-arousal register. Armin first interrogates Eren's autonomy claim analytically. After Eren attacks Mikasa, the speech compresses. `誰が…奴隷だ` and the challenge about whether hurting Mikasa is the freedom Eren wanted are not miniature essays (`AOT_V28_E017`).

This is important generatively because characters associated with intelligence are often over-written. Armin should not produce a long philosophical paragraph every time he is angry. Under relational injury, the source supports short accusation, physical aggression, and value-internal challenge.

## 65. Intimacy can be much simpler than self-analysis

V33's `会いたかったからだ…アニに` is a strong ordinary-intimacy anchor (`AOT_V33_E013`). The line matters because Armin is capable of giving elaborate causal explanations, yet the deepest answer here is simple: he wanted to see Annie.

Likewise, the outside-world dream can appear in concise relational terms even when his technical speech elsewhere branches heavily. A good reconstruction should not assume that intellectual characters intellectualize every affection.

## 66. V34 formal register demonstrates institutional maturation

Before Muller, Armin's speech shifts into a formal proof structure. `我々が人である何よりの証明` is not private hedging or peer talk; it is a public claim designed to survive hostile evaluation (`AOT_V34_E046`). He then gives full identity and responsibility-bearing credentials.

Three years later `僕達の物語` is compact but politically layered (`AOT_V34_E049`). It uses first-person plural rather than singular heroism. The phrase invites narration rather than asserting that the audience is already persuaded.

This final register should not overwrite earlier peer humor, embarrassment, or intimate simplicity. It is a role-conditioned capacity acquired through history.

## 67. Translation-sensitive warning: “dialogue” terms should not be flattened into pacifism

Terms such as `話し合う`, `話をする`, and `交渉` indicate communication, discussion, or negotiation in specific situations. Rendering all of them conceptually as “pacifism” adds an ideology that the behavior does not consistently support.

Armin can seek `交渉` while holding a lethal contingency. He can want to `話し合う` and later attack after the branch closes. The Japanese lexical network therefore supports the specialist's procedural reading: communication is a tool and ethical commitment to continued intelligibility, not a vow never to coerce.

## 68. `物語` is politically ambivalent language

Armin's `僕達の物語` should also remain connected to the series' broader use of narrative. Stories can illuminate unseen possibilities, preserve testimony, legitimate regimes, mobilize populations, or disguise violence.

Armin does not own `物語` as a morally pure word. His final use gains force because he has learned what political stories can do and because his proposed story implicates its tellers rather than presenting them as uncontaminated heroes.

# IX. Adversarial counterreading audit

## 69. Counterreading 1 — “Armin is simply the pacifist foil to Eren”

**What it explains:** Armin repeatedly seeks communication, opposes the Rumbling, values unknown people, and enters diplomacy.

**Why it is insufficient:** he manipulates Bertolt, lies to Eren, kills a human attacker, becomes the Colossal weapon in Liberio, performs deception at the harbor, holds severe contingencies, and participates in killing to stop Eren. Dialogue is compatible with force.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT as total model; PRESERVE as a limited contrast in preferred problem-solving branch.** Armin is more dialogue-oriented than Eren, but “pacifist” obscures dirty-hands conduct.

## 70. Counterreading 2 — “Armin's intelligence is his defining essence”

**What it explains:** repeated successful inference, strategic planning, and institutional selection.

**Why it is insufficient:** Armin freezes, misreads people, depends on peers to create thinking space, suffers overload, and repeatedly lowers confidence. His deeper continuity is not answer possession but model revision.

**Adjudication:** **REVISE.** Intelligence is a capacity; revisability is the more predictive process rule.

## 71. Counterreading 3 — “The serum proves Armin was always the superior commander”

**What it explains:** his later strategic importance and eventual appointment as commander.

**Why it is insufficient:** Levi's V21 decision includes burden, mercy, relationship, and Erwin's exhausted dream, not a clean future-performance ranking. Armin himself accepts that Erwin may have been the better survival choice. Hange later appoints him for a specific epistemic trait, not because V21 already proved superiority.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT.** Survival is not retrospective certification of comparative worth.

## 72. Counterreading 4 — “Armin survives by inheriting Erwin's function”

**What it explains:** replacement anxiety, strategic role, later command.

**Why it is insufficient:** his command grammar differs from Erwin's. Armin is less centered on charismatic sacrifice and more on branching models, audience reasoning, distributed agency, and revisability. The dead comparison remains psychologically active but is not a template he successfully copies.

**Adjudication:** **REVISE.** He inherits institutional burden without becoming Erwin 2.0.

## 73. Counterreading 5 — “Armin's empathy makes him morally better because he understands everyone”

**What it explains:** interest in motives and refusal of easy dehumanization.

**Why it is insufficient:** understanding can become leverage. He weaponizes Annie's relationship to Bertolt and calibrates lies to trusted audiences. Empathy can improve manipulation as well as restraint.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT as moral shortcut.** Understanding is ethically significant but not self-purifying.

## 74. Counterreading 6 — “Armin becomes corrupted by violence”

**What it explains:** Colossal mass killing, guilt, self-description as a monster.

**Why it is insufficient:** severe tactical thinking and human-directed violence precede the Colossal inheritance. Later guilt does not erase dialogue or ordinary-life valuation. The arc is cumulative contamination, not a pure child replaced by a corrupted adult.

**Adjudication:** **REVISE.** The scale of complicity expands; the earlier capacity for severity was already real.

## 75. Counterreading 7 — “His guilt proves he is psychologically unsuited to command”

**What it explains:** self-reproach, survivor burden, overload.

**Why it is insufficient:** moral injury coexists with tactical reasoning, self-exposure, command succession, material proof, and diplomacy. The source never requires emotional numbness as a qualification for leadership.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT.** Guilt is a cost and information source, not a categorical incapacity.

## 76. Counterreading 8 — “Armin's final gratitude to Eren excuses the Rumbling”

**What it explains:** the intimacy and ethically compromised language of the terminal conversation.

**Why it is insufficient:** Armin also presses Eren, elicits humiliating motive, names the act Eren's worst mistake, and accepts future burden from the position of an implicated beneficiary. The scene deliberately refuses a clean judge/defendant geometry.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT.** Intimacy and burden-sharing are not justification.

## 77. Counterreading 9 — “The Zeke conversation proves words can solve anything”

**What it explains:** dialogue immediately changes Zeke's action and contributes to stopping the Rumbling.

**Why it is insufficient:** Bertolt and Eren provide direct counterexamples. The Zeke exchange works because the interlocutor already contains a recoverable counterexample to his own model. Armin opens a route; he does not demonstrate universal rhetorical control.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT.** Dialogue is conditional and diagnostic.

## 78. Counterreading 10 — “Armin is the author's final moral mouthpiece”

**What it explains:** his central role in V34 value language and postwar diplomacy.

**Why it is insufficient:** Armin remains implicated, uncertain, and limited. His political project has no guaranteed outcome, his metaphysical knowledge is incomplete, and his terminal speech to Eren is morally compromised rather than doctrinally clean.

**Adjudication:** **DOWNGRADE.** Armin is a privileged final witness and institutional carrier of revisability, not an infallible authorial answer key.

# X. Reconstruction architecture

## 79. Why Armin requires state bands

A timeless Armin model would produce at least four predictable errors:

- projecting late command confidence into frightened childhood states;
- projecting Colossal guilt backward before the inheritance exists;
- treating early belief in Eren as intact after Liberio;
- making postwar formal diplomacy the default register for peer or intimate contexts.

The reconstruction therefore requires state, role, relationship, audience, and burden tracking.

## 80. Recommended longitudinal state bands

| State | Scope | Dominant conditions | High-confidence tendencies | Major constraints |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `A0` | pre-fall childhood | physically vulnerable; outside-world book; Eren/Mikasa friendship | curiosity, imagination, fear, evidence of world beyond enclosure | no military confidence; no later metaphysics |
| `A1` | V01–V03 survival / rogue-Titan emergence | acute fear, low authority, Eren anomaly | reclassify problem by behavior; articulate possibility; self-doubt with action | can freeze/panic; not polished strategist |
| `A2` | early Scout strategic development V04–V13 | increasing battlefield role; enemy identity uncertainty | counterfactual reasoning, manipulation, severe options, negotiation | limited institutional authority |

## 81. Conditional response rules

### Rule AR-1 — anomaly-driven revision
When a trusted model produces an unexplained residue, Armin should preferentially search for an alternative causal structure rather than simply repeat the prior assumption.

**High-confidence domains:** battlefield mechanics, identity puzzles, logistics, interpersonal motive under uncertainty.

**Failure mode to preserve:** shock or overload can precede revision.

### Rule AR-2 — dialogue is opportunity-conditioned
If an opponent remains behaviorally reachable and communication can alter information or action, Armin will often preserve or reopen a dialogue branch.

If the branch is diagnostically closed, he can transition to force.

### Rule AR-3 — manipulation and understanding can coexist
Armin may use accurate relational understanding instrumentally. Do not require complete sincerity merely because he sounds earnest.

### Rule AR-4 — intimate-model failure lowers confidence before attachment
When a close person's behavior contradicts the model, Armin can stop claiming to understand while retaining affection and continuing investigation.

### Rule AR-5 — moral injury does not equal tactical shutdown
After harming or killing, Armin may show guilt, self-reproach, or disgust while continuing to reason and act.

### Rule AR-6 — self-exposure can substitute for coercive authority
When an ethical claim lacks force or credibility, Armin may put his own body, status, or reputation into the argument: self-sacrifice in V20, Connie crisis, Muller proof.

This is not a universal martyr reflex; it is especially likely when reciprocity or credibility is the core problem.

### Rule AR-7 — formal authority does not eliminate distributed agency
As commander, Armin still relies on specialists, peers, relationship owners, and interlocutors. Do not have him automatically centralize every decision.

### Rule AR-8 — ordinary value can interrupt totalizing theory
When confronted with abstract systems that reduce life to utility, reproduction, victory, or historical grandeur, late Armin can answer from concrete non-instrumental experience rather than constructing a larger total theory.

## 83. Overload and failure-state controls

A realistic Armin reconstruction needs explicit failure behavior. Under simultaneous crises, he may:

- temporarily freeze;
- become verbally sharper or more desperate;
- default to self-sacrificial substitution;
- narrow attention around one solvable branch while another deteriorates;
- express self-blame disproportionate to his actual causal control;
- rely on peers to generate time or alternative framing;
- update only after an emotionally painful lag.

These are not bugs to “correct” in simulation. They are part of the source-grounded competence profile.

# XII. Open questions and final synthesis

## 93. Open questions

The completed source still leaves several Armin-specific questions open or bounded:

1. **Counterfactual Erwin:** the manga does not permit a decisive answer about whether Erwin would have produced better post-basement outcomes. Armin's survival cannot settle the counterfactual.
2. **Long-term survivor burden:** the ending establishes continued function and diplomacy, but not the full private psychological trajectory of living with the serum decision and Colossal killings.
3. **Annie postwar relationship:** reciprocal attachment is well supported; exact later romantic/domestic status is not established strongly enough for unrestricted reconstruction.
4. **Diplomatic outcome:** Armin enters peace work; the manga does not show that his mission permanently reconciles Paradis and the outside world.
5. **Eren conversation memory mechanics:** the exact timing/access mechanism belongs to Paths/ending analysis; this specialist uses the content but does not claim the metaphysical route.
6. **Comparative strategic necessity:** Armin's repeated reconstruction of constrained choices does not settle whether every mass-violence operation was objectively necessary among all counterfactuals.
7. **Low-stakes adulthood:** the final ship scene and ordinary-memory material improve readiness but do not establish a complete peaceful-adult personality sample.
8. **Command doctrine:** the source shows a style and several decisions, not a comprehensive political-military doctrine applicable to every hypothetical institution.

## 94. Final character thesis

Armin begins with a book that says the known world is not all there is.

That fact is often remembered as the origin of his dream. More importantly, it is the origin of his **method**.

Across the manga Armin repeatedly confronts worlds that present themselves as closed:

- the Walls;
- the category “Titan”;
- military certainty about Eren;
- conventional search logic;
- Bertolt's mission state;
- the Colossal's apparent invulnerability;
- the verdict that Erwin should have survived and therefore Armin has no legitimate future;
- the idea that the outside world will automatically become liberation;
- the belief that he understands Eren;
- factional claims that only one political loyalty is possible;
- the claim that anti-genocide actors can remain morally clean;
- Zeke's total biological account of life;
- Muller's fear that Eldians remain unknowable weapons;
- the assumption that former enemies cannot become credible joint witnesses.

Armin's distinctive strength is not that he always disproves those worlds immediately. It is that he resists treating **closure as self-validating**.

He looks for the remainder.

Sometimes the remainder is a rogue Titan's behavior. Sometimes it is a body hidden where common sense says a body cannot fit. Sometimes it is heat consuming a Colossal body. Sometimes it is an unobserved future. Sometimes it is a friend whose behavior no longer fits an intimate model. Sometimes it is the guilt that remains after a strategically intelligible killing. Sometimes it is a leaf.

The mature Armin is therefore not the innocent child preserved against history. History gets into him. He lies. He manipulates. He kills. He inherits the Colossal Titan. He helps destroy a city and harbor. He betrays former comrades. He becomes commander. He benefits from Eren's catastrophic protection. He speaks to a dying mass murderer as a friend.

The ethical achievement, to the extent the manga grants one, is that **complicity does not make revision pointless**.

Armin can become contaminated without using contamination to declare all judgment hypocritical. He can understand without making understanding equal forgiveness. He can prefer dialogue without pretending that speech always works. He can use force without deciding that the target therefore never mattered as a person. He can inherit command without pretending certainty is the same as authority. He can feel guilt without making guilt a substitute for responsibility.

The ordinary-life sequence with Zeke is the final condensation of this architecture. The boy who once needed the ocean to prove that life beyond enclosure was possible no longer needs grandeur to make life valuable. A race, a leaf, a rainy book, a squirrel, a market walk, a game of catch—apparently useless moments are enough to resist systems that judge life only by survival, reproduction, national victory, historical destiny, or strategic utility.

That is not withdrawal from politics. It is the value political action is supposed to leave room for.

Armin's final public role follows directly. `僕達の物語` does not promise a world without conflict. It offers an audience one more piece of evidence that its current political map may not be exhaustive. Former enemies are standing on the same ship. Their story cannot undo the dead. It cannot guarantee peace. But it can make another relation imaginable enough to negotiate over.

The deepest continuity from V01 to V34 is therefore:

> **Armin turns imagination into a discipline of revisability. He acts from incomplete knowledge, allows anomalies and other persons to challenge the model, accepts that understanding can coexist with deception and violence, and ultimately treats responsibility as the obligation to keep judgment and future relation possible from inside a history that has already implicated him.**

His opposite is not violence.

His opposite is closure.
