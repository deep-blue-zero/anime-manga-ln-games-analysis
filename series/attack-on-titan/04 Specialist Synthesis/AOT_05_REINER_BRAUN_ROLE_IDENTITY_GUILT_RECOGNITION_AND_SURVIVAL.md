# 『進撃の巨人』 / *Attack on Titan*
## Reiner Braun: Role, Identity, Guilt, Recognition, and Survival — V01–V34
### From the child who needed to become a hero to the survivor who outlives the Armor

## 0. Function, authority, and anti-caricature boundary

This document is the canonical specialist home, once promoted from provisional publication state, for Reiner Braun's full-series literary analysis across the complete Japanese manga boundary. The governing question fixed by the synthesis architecture is:

> **How does Reiner's life expose the difference between imposed roles, sincerely inhabited roles, retrospective guilt, and continuing responsibility?**

Reiner is unusually vulnerable to interpretive shortcuts because the manga itself dramatizes discontinuity in his self-understanding. He can speak as a Wall soldier while abducting Eren for the Warriors. He can coerce Annie into abandoning Marco and later react as though Marco's death were an inexplicable atrocity committed by somebody else. He can return to Marley as a celebrated Armored Titan while remembering the supposedly demonic 104th through affectionate ordinary detail. He can place a rifle in his mouth and, moments later, stop because younger children still exist around him. He can insist that his sins are beyond atonement while continuing to fight to save people. He can lose the Armored Titan—the institutional role that organized his childhood worth—and remain a person with family, peers, embarrassment, humor, and political responsibility.

The specialist therefore refuses three tempting reductions at the outset.

The first is **clinical shortcut**. Reiner's role fracture, memory discontinuity, dissociative-looking transitions, guilt, suicidality, and contradictory self-reports are psychologically serious. But the manga's literary evidence does not authorize a retrospective clinical diagnosis as the master key. The most source-faithful model is role-indexed and developmental: different social roles are genuinely inhabited, behaviorally predictive, mutually incompatible in some states, and only partially integrated later.

The second is **authenticity purification**. There is no single hidden "real Reiner" waiting behind disposable masks. Warrior Reiner is not the real self underneath fake soldier kindness; soldier Reiner is not the real self buried under fake Warrior obedience. Child believer, substitute-Marcel, Warrior, 104th soldier, perpetrator, suicidal survivor, candidate caretaker, coalition fighter, and post-Titan family member are all historically real states. Their reality does not make them equally admirable or equally voluntary. It means later knowledge cannot erase the fact that he actually acted, cared, lied, protected, betrayed, commanded, and chose from inside each of them.

The third is **redemption accounting**. Reiner's later opposition to the Rumbling, burden shielding at the harbor, protection of children, cooperation with former victims, and postwar diplomacy do not reimburse Marco, Wall Maria, Trost, the people killed by the Warrior operation, or the damage of his continued mission. The manga does not give him a moral ledger in which enough heroic acts make the earlier balance disappear. His terminal development is better described as **survival under continuing responsibility without a clean theory of atonement**.

Two claim-revision ledger entries control the specialist.

- `AOT-FSCR-C015` is **STRENGTHEN**: Reiner's identities remain cumulative, real, and responsibility-bearing. The complete manga preserves the child believer, Warrior, 104th soldier, substitute-Marcel, perpetrator, suicidal survivor, candidate caretaker, coalition fighter, and post-Titan family member as historically real states. Responsibility concerns what he continued, concealed, revised, and chose after each state became available, not discovery of a purified true self.
- `AOT-FSCR-M004` is **STRENGTHEN**: Reiner must be reconstructed through cumulative real states. Later integration does not render an earlier state fake, and state realism does not absolve betrayal or erase discontinuity.

The specialist also closes three inherited open-question routes at its own level.

- `O19-13` resolves toward **context-dependent cumulative-state architecture**. Reiner's role fracture is not simply steadily worsening or steadily healing. Different combinations of mission pressure, guilt, relationship cues, exhaustion, threat, and available responsibility can reactivate distinct response organizations.
- `O27-21` is **REVISE**. Reiner helps interrupt the reproduction of the Warrior system relationally—especially through the candidate children and later anti-Rumbling action—but he does not personally reform the institution that made him. Titan power ends through the terminal series mechanism; his contribution is to refuse some of the system's reproduced harms from inside its aftermath.
- `O27-22` is **STRENGTHEN** toward **both**. Reiner's later reactivation is genuine survival action and continuing role captivity/guilt at once. Relationship tethers can restore movement without producing self-forgiveness or psychological completion.

### 0.2 Evidence rule: five distinctions must remain separate

Reiner analysis becomes unreliable whenever the following variables are collapsed.

1. **Role origin** — how a social identity was assigned or made available: family expectation, Marleyan institution, Marcel's manipulation, military cover, peer recognition, survivor need.
2. **Role enactment** — what Reiner repeatedly did while inhabiting it: protect, command, joke, conceal, kill, care, report, mentor.
3. **Subjective identification** — whether Reiner understands himself through the role in a given state.
4. **Moral authorship** — which decisions he initiated, continued, coerced, concealed, revised, or owned under available information and constraint.
5. **Retrospective interpretation** — how later Reiner, other characters, or later readers describe what earlier states meant.

A role can be externally imposed in origin, sincerely inhabited in enactment, unstable in conscious identification, and still responsibility-bearing. Conversely, later guilt can be accurate about authorship while inaccurate if converted into total sole causation for an entire historical catastrophe.

# I. Mature formulation

## 1. Reiner's deepest continuity is not one identity but a method of becoming what the situation requires

Reiner's life is structured by **substitution** before the reader has language for it. As a child, he wants to become a Warrior not primarily because combat itself is the desired end but because Warrior status promises to solve a family problem. If he becomes an honorary Marleyan, perhaps the socially impossible family can become legitimate. When his Marleyan father rejects that fantasy, public heroic identity supplies a replacement future: if he cannot become the son whose success reunites a family, he can become the `世界の英雄` whose success proves collective worth.

Marcel's confession then destroys the merit story beneath even that identity. Reiner was not transparently selected because he was the best candidate; Marcel manipulated the evaluation to protect Porco. Marcel dies protecting Reiner immediately after revealing the distortion. Tactical loss, survivor debt, humiliation, and status collapse arrive together.

Reiner's response is not to discover a deeper authentic self. It is to construct another role. `ライナーは死んだ` is followed by `俺がマルセルに…なるから…`. The wording is unusually explicit: identity substitution is a deliberate emergency technology. If the person the group needed has died, Reiner will become that person. The replacement serves several simultaneous functions: it gives the mission a leader, converts survivor guilt into duty, prevents immediate return to a home where failure may mean death and disgrace, and offers Reiner a reason to treat his continued life as more than an accident.

That pattern does not mean every later Reiner state is consciously chosen in the same way. The crucial continuity is more abstract: **when one identity becomes impossible to inhabit, Reiner tends to reconstruct action through another available role that tells him what he owes and who still needs him**.

The 104th soldier role is the most important example because it begins as cover and becomes socially real through repetition. He protects Connie. He teaches and encourages Eren. He participates in ordinary meals, teasing, fatigue, ambition, and mutual dependence. Others recognize him as reliable. Conduct produces relationship, relationship produces obligation, and obligation becomes psychologically active enough to interfere with the mission that generated the cover in the first place.

Later states repeat the structure without repeating the content. The adult Warrior role remains active after its child-hero story has collapsed because Marley, family, military comrades, and candidate children still organize responsibilities around him. When self-directed meaning collapses far enough for suicide, Falco and the candidates reactivate a caretaker role. When the Rumbling removes his armor and leaves him exhausted, Gabi and the coalition supply another task. When he judges his sins beyond atonement, saving those who remain becomes a narrower reason to act. When Titan power disappears entirely, he survives the institutional identity rather than disappearing with it.

## 2. Cumulative selfhood is more demanding than mask theory

Mask theory is attractive because it offers moral tidiness. If soldier Reiner was only a performance, betrayal can be understood as the unveiling of the real enemy. If Warrior Reiner was only coercive indoctrination, later remorse can reveal the real good person. Both versions simplify by declaring one time-indexed self counterfeit.

The manga consistently resists that move.

In V10 Reiner's body is the evidence. He risks his arm to protect Connie before the reveal. Later knowledge changes what else the reader knows about that body—it can regenerate, it belongs to the Armored Titan—but does not make the protective act unreal. V11 makes the structure explicit when Reiner speaks from the soldier state as though promotion, rest, and Christa still belong to his future, and Bertolt has to remind him that he is not a soldier but a Warrior. The horror lies precisely in the fact that the role has become a moral world.

V19 deepens the point with Marco. Reiner is not passively watching another perpetrator. He is the one who recognizes Marco's dangerous inference and turns Annie's participation into a proof of Warrior belonging. Then, after Marco is eaten, the soldier-comrade organization of Reiner's mind appears to return strongly enough that he asks why Marco is being eaten. The same person has ordered the act and experiences its result through an incompatible role-state.

V23 refuses to let adulthood simplify the architecture. Reiner can perform loyal Warrior homecoming while his supposedly demonizing story about the 104th leaks ordinary affection. The content of his memories—potatoes, difficult personalities, reckless classmates, different kinds of people—undermines the category being performed. `色んな奴らがいて そこに俺達もいた` is not a declaration that the mission ended. It is evidence that he cannot describe the island without locating himself inside its social world.

V32 then brings the incompatible histories into one room. Reiner can narrate Marco's death as perpetrator, remember his own discontinuity, accept Jean's violence, continue the operation, and accept that Jean cannot forgive him. Integration does not mean the disappearance of contradiction. It means enough access to multiple histories that the contradiction can be borne without one state erasing the others.

## 3. The reality of the soldier self intensifies betrayal

A common exculpatory mistake follows immediately from the cumulative-self thesis: if soldier Reiner really cared, perhaps the betrayal should count for less because he was psychologically divided.

The opposite is often true.

If his comradeship were fake, then Reiner would have deceived people he never regarded as socially real. Because the relation became real, he continues a mission against people whom he knows through shared meals, training, protection, embarrassment, competence, fear, and ordinary irritating intimacy. That is morally harder, not easier.

This is one reason Eren's V11 disgust is intelligible even when Eren's dehumanizing conclusion is not. The category contradiction feels obscene: how can the same dependable person have breached the Walls? Eren's response is to deny the coexistence and reduce Reiner to pest, murderer, and nonhuman enemy. The manga gradually shows why that move fails as description. But rejecting dehumanization does not require weakening accountability. It makes the betrayed relation part of the harm.

The same logic governs Reiner's own later confession. He does not say, in effect, "none of that was me." He increasingly speaks in first-person causal terms. The moral task becomes not identifying the real state but asking what this cumulative person did with the knowledge, loyalties, coercions, and alternatives available at each stage.

## 4. Role is neither destiny nor mere costume

The mature formulation can be stated compactly:

> **Reiner is a cumulative role-formed self whose identities can originate in coercion, family fantasy, institutional assignment, emergency substitution, or strategic cover and nevertheless become psychologically real through repeated conduct and recognition. Their reality neither fixes future action nor erases responsibility. It determines the contradictory obligations from which later action is chosen.**

This formulation preserves development without pretending that later reflection makes earlier conduct transparent. It also explains why Reiner's survival is so often relational. A person whose self has repeatedly been organized around what a role requires can lose the will to continue when every available role becomes morally impossible—then move again when another person's concrete future creates a responsibility narrower than heroic self-justification.

# II. Longitudinal development

## 5. V01–V06: dependable peerhood appears before suspicion can settle

Reiner is not initially introduced as a philosophical problem. His early value lies in ordinary military sociality: strength, steadiness, recognizable competence, directness, and willingness to expose himself to danger. That matters methodologically. Later revelation should not be allowed to colonize every earlier panel and convert all help into knowingly manipulative theater.

By V06 the Female Titan sequence gives reason for suspicion around Reiner's survival and the Titan's altered direction, but the volume correctly preserves uncertainty. The source boundary does not yet authorize a verdict. This is important because the reader's later knowledge can otherwise produce retrospective omniscience. Reiner's identity is first experienced by peers and reader through conduct, not through the hidden mission brief.

## 6. V09–V10: protection and physiology become inseparable

V09 begins placing Reiner around anomalies he understands more deeply than he admits: Ragako, Ymir's literacy, and outside-cultural knowledge. Yet his behavior remains interpersonally embedded enough that denial or concealment cannot be read as evidence of emotional emptiness.

V10 supplies the foundational control case. At Utgard he puts his arm into a Titan's mouth to protect Connie. Afterward the arm's regeneration reveals hidden Titan physiology. The same wound is therefore both **genuine protective behavior** and **evidence of concealed enemy identity**.

The volume's reveal is deliberately anti-spectacular in conversational staging. Reiner says he is the Armored Titan with a strange casualness that reflects the instability of the two lived worlds rather than a theatrical villain unmasking. Mikasa's attack forces the categories into physical conflict, but the psychological problem remains: the friend and the enemy occupy one history.

## 7. V11–V12: soldier and Warrior become incompatible moral worlds

V11 gives the most explicit early role fracture. In the giant forest Reiner discusses the future like a Wall soldier; Bertolt corrects him with `兵士じゃないだろ…僕らは戦士なんだから`. The line does not merely remind Reiner of a job title. It reactivates a mission identity whose claims have become difficult to hold continuously beside 104th belonging.

Ymir's diagnosis of guilt and divided role is useful but should remain literary rather than medical. The behavior demonstrates state discontinuity, conflicting moral world-models, and an inability to keep both role systems fully active at once. It does not give the analyst license to invent a clinical taxonomy the manga never establishes.

V12 then restores mission legibility around the Coordinate. Reiner immediately recognizes Eren's power and understands it as strategically decisive. This matters because role fracture does not mean permanent cognitive collapse. When the mission object becomes concrete enough, Warrior reasoning can become clear and effective again.

The contrast is already predictive: **identity instability is context-conditioned rather than synonymous with incompetence**.

## 8. V18–V20: operational Reiner remains capable under a psychologically compromised history

Return to Shiganshina shows how dangerous it would be to model Reiner only through guilt. He hides inside Wall architecture, survives an apparently fatal attack through a mechanism the Scouts do not yet understand, reads enemy objectives, prioritizes logistics, and fights within a coordinated Warrior plan.

V19 contains a compact behavioral rule when Erwin presents himself as a salient command target. Reiner notices the opportunity, then corrects himself: `イヤ迷うな 先に殺すのは馬だ`. Emotionally or strategically attractive salience is real; mission priority can override it. This is not the behavior of a person whose identity conflict has erased tactical control.

The same volume returns to Marco and shows the opposite domain. Reiner turns Annie's violence into an identity test. Membership in the Warrior side has to be proven by irreversible harm against a person they know. The act demonstrates how institutions and small-group role enforcement can convert uncertainty into atrocity without requiring hatred of the victim.

Then the soldier self returns as shock. Reiner's failure to integrate what happened does not make the event less his. The contradiction becomes one of the project's strongest examples of why **subjective discontinuity and moral authorship have to be modeled separately**.

## 9. V23: adult homecoming reveals that homeland no longer contains one home

The Marley focalization transforms Reiner from hidden enemy into a person living inside the social machinery that produced him. He returns as the Armored Titan, veteran, family success, mentor figure, and living advertisement for the honorary-Marleyan bargain. None of those roles has the simplicity they had in childhood.

His interaction with Falco and Gabi is especially important because Reiner now stands on the adult side of the child-soldier reproduction system. He recognizes the candidates not as abstract successors but as specific children with rivalries, affections, anxieties, and shortened futures. Falco's desire to inherit the Armor partly to save Gabi gives Reiner a relational situation structurally unlike his own childhood prestige drive, but still inside the same machinery.

The family dinner demonstrates the impossibility of full ideological return. Reiner knows how to perform the expected account of island devils. Yet what comes out is ordinary peer memory. Humanization here is not liberation. It is traumatic contradiction. The social reality of the 104th survives inside a setting where Reiner remains a Warrior.

This is why V23 should not be described as the point where he "learns the islanders were human." He already knew them as human through lived relation. The adult tragedy is that recognition has become impossible to deny while the institutional role still persists.

## 10. V24A: the family motive precedes the heroic ideology

The childhood flashback clarifies that Reiner's original Warrior desire is relational and status-mediated. He imagines military success making an impossible family socially possible. Honorary-Marleyan recognition promises not just personal advancement but legitimate belonging.

His father's rejection destroys that hope. The father does not welcome the child who has worked toward reunion; he fears exposure and treats Reiner and Karina as a threat to the family he actually acknowledges. The closed door therefore precedes the Paradis mission as an identity wound.

Karina's response matters because she does not dismantle the Warrior fantasy after its family premise fails. She redirects it. Public praise supplies a new role: world hero. This is not evidence that Karina maliciously designs Reiner's suffering. It is evidence that parental love, racialized aspiration, state prestige, and self-deception can collaborate in turning a child into political infrastructure.

## 11. V24B: Marcel's manipulation destroys merit and creates survivor debt

Marcel's confession attacks another layer of Reiner's identity. Reiner's selection does not transparently prove that he was the superior candidate. Marcel shaped military impressions to protect Porco.

The temporal placement is devastating: Marcel reveals the manipulation and is almost immediately killed while saving Reiner. Reiner survives because the person who helped place him in the role dies for him after admitting the placement was compromised.

This produces multiple debts that cannot be satisfied simultaneously.

- Returning home means admitting mission failure and confronting a status system that may remove his Armor and his life.
- Continuing means forcing Annie and Bertolt deeper into a mission whose legitimacy has already fractured.
- Living means carrying the fact that Marcel died in his place.
- Leadership becomes a way to make survival causally necessary rather than accidental.

`帰れない` is therefore more than physical inability to go home. The home available to the child has become structurally incompatible with the person who survived.

## 12. V24C: `ライナーは死んだ` is construction, not revelation

Reiner's response—`ライナーは死んだ` and `俺がマルセルに…なるから…`—should govern every later identity analysis.

It is an act of construction. Reiner names one identity dead and announces a replacement role. The declaration solves an immediate practical problem while also giving survivor guilt a behavioral program. He will supply leadership, competence, and meaning associated with Marcel because the group needs Marcel and because remaining "Reiner" has become intolerably associated with inadequacy, manipulated selection, family failure, and the person Marcel died to save.

The manga later shows that the substitution is never total. Reiner's own temperament, attachments, insecurities, and needs remain. That failure is analytically useful. Roles can reorganize behavior without erasing the person who performs them.

## 13. V24D: ordinary trainee life converts cover into belonging

The trainee material is not filler between mission scenes. It demonstrates how the substitute role encounters a social environment capable of creating new obligations.

Reiner eats with the 104th, trains, protects, advises, competes, jokes, and is recognized by others as a reliable peer. The repeated conduct creates a soldier identity that is not reducible to the mission that made infiltration necessary.

This is the point where "acting" becomes philosophically inadequate. A person can begin by performing a role for instrumental reasons and become changed by what the role repeatedly requires. The consequences are not limited to internal feeling. Other people organize trust around the performance. The socially created self therefore carries reciprocal moral stakes.

## 14. V24E: the rifle scene establishes conditional survival, not cure

Adult Reiner's suicide attempt is explicit. The rifle is placed in his mouth; the scene narrows to body, weapon, finger, and acoustic interruption. The correct model cannot euphemize this into general sadness.

It also cannot overgeneralize from the interruption. Reiner stops when he hears Falco and thinks of the candidate children: `俺にはまだ…あいつらが…`.

The children do not cure him. They provide a reason not to die **in that state**. More precisely, responsibility for specific younger people restores a role through which continued existence becomes immediately actionable. The self can remain intolerable while another person's future remains important enough to inhibit self-destruction.

This distinction is essential to the later survival architecture. Reiner does not need a stable positive desire for his own life in order to keep choosing actions that preserve it. Relational obligation can create local anti-suicide force.

## 15. V25: Eren gives Reiner explanation; Reiner refuses acquittal

The basement conversation is one of the manga's strongest responsibility scenes because Eren offers Reiner a contextual account that early Eren could not have imagined giving. Reiner was a child. He was taught the islanders were devils. Adults, history, and institutional environment pushed the Warrior children into the mission. Eren can understand that causal environment because living outside the Walls has destroyed his own simple enemy category.

Reiner responds by refusing to let explanation exhaust authorship.

He returns to the decision after Marcel died. Annie and Bertolt wanted to withdraw. Reiner forced continuation. He wanted to become a hero. The confession contracts from system to first person: `時代や環境のせいじゃなくて…俺が悪いんだよ`.

This line should be protected from two opposite abuses.

The first is exculpatory: because structural causes are real, perhaps Reiner's authorship disappears. His own account blocks that.

The second is punitive: because Reiner says it was his fault, perhaps the narrator has certified him as the sole cause of everything that followed. The broader manga blocks that as well. Child indoctrination, Marleyan command, family motive, manipulated selection, Marcel's death, existential mission structure, Titan inheritance, Bertolt and Annie's agency, and larger history remain causally real.

Reiner's confession is best understood as **moral overcorrection against the temptation to disappear into context**. Its truth is that he had decision points and motives that matter. Its danger is that guilt can compress an entire causal network into the self because total self-condemnation feels more coherent than living inside distributed responsibility.

## 16. V25: `同じ` creates recognition without equivalence

Eren offers Reiner a hand and says they are the same. The phrase becomes a longitudinal hinge because later Jean will use a structurally related formulation.

Sameness here does not mean identical coercion, identical victims, identical objectives, identical scale, or identical moral standing. It identifies a shared architecture: people act inside history and environment, recognize ordinary humanity across the line, still continue violent projects, and then confront the difference between causal explanation and authored action.

Reiner becomes unusually important to Eren because Eren can use Reiner as a model of how a person crosses a line while understanding the people on the other side. That recognition can deepen self-understanding without preventing Eren's attack.

For Reiner, the encounter is equally severe. The boy he once encouraged to keep moving has become the adult capable of reproducing the structure Reiner himself cannot forgive. Recognition therefore does not resolve guilt. It externalizes it.

## 17. V26–V30: persistence after confession is not a restored hero narrative

The post-Liberio sequence keeps Reiner inside combat and protection without restoring childhood heroic innocence. He still fights because Marley, the Warrior unit, Gabi, Falco, family, and the wider conflict create concrete obligations. His ability to perform severe military action should not be read as evidence the suicidal state was false. Nor should the earlier suicide attempt be treated as proof that later combat is merely automatic.

Reiner has always been capable of acting from roles whose demands outrun his self-directed desire. The mature difference is that the role stories have lost much of their innocence. He no longer needs to believe himself a world-saving hero to act protectively.

## 18. V31: the Rumbling removes Armor and exposes exhausted relational survival

Eren's global undoing of hardening strips Reiner's armor. The image is symbolically powerful but must first be read materially: his body loses the defensive structure central to the Armored Titan role at the same moment the political world collapses into extermination.

Reiner is exhausted and understands Eren partly through attachment logic—protecting the island and people important to him. That recognition does not produce endorsement. It initially contributes to resignation.

Gabi refuses the resignation because Falco and their families are still concrete futures. Later Reiner is awakened into a coalition containing former enemies. The state change is not conversion to a new grand ideology. Other people still need an action he can perform.

This repeats the rifle architecture at larger scale: **relationally concrete responsibility can reactivate movement after self-directed meaning collapses**.

## 19. V32: Marco returns as an event that cannot be integrated by forgetting

The campfire forces the coalition to share a space before it has reconciled histories. Yelena's attack on heroic self-narration names the crimes each side would prefer to subordinate to the current mission. Reiner responds by giving Marco's death back to the room.

He admits taking the ODM gear and forcing Annie's participation after Marco discovered the Warrior identities. He remembers Marco's protest that they had not talked. He also remembers the discontinuity after Marco was eaten: Reiner reacted as though the Titan's act had arrived from outside himself.

This is crucial because later self-awareness does not rewrite the original fracture. Integrated confession and remembered discontinuity coexist. The mature Reiner can narrate the causal act precisely **because** he no longer has to pretend the earlier state was unified.

Jean's beating is not staged as a cleansing punishment. Gabi takes a kick meant for Reiner. Jean later returns to the operation and tells Reiner he still cannot forgive him. Reiner accepts that condition.

The relation thereby reaches a more mature form than reconciliation language would allow: **functional coalition without compulsory forgiveness**.

## 20. V32: burden shielding is Reiner's strongest later-care behavior

At the harbor, Reiner and Annie recognize that the former 104th members may have to kill island comrades. Reiner tells them not to fight if the Warriors can carry that burden and asks them to protect Gabi and Falco.

This is not self-sacrifice as theatrical redemption. It is care expressed through threshold knowledge. Reiner knows what it is to cross from comradeship into mission killing and tries to keep others from reproducing his own threshold if a different task allocation remains possible.

The behavior is particularly important because it turns guilt into a prospective constraint rather than only retrospective suffering. Reiner cannot undo Marco. He can sometimes use the knowledge of what that kind of act costs to shape who bears the next act.

## 21. V33: guilt destroys righteousness without destroying obligation

After the harbor, Connie and Jean can understand Reiner from a position they previously lacked. They have killed people they knew while believing a larger rescue objective required it. Jean says `俺達は同じだ…ライナー`.

Again, the comparison is structural rather than scalar. The histories, coercions, objectives, ages, and scales differ. What has changed is epistemic position: Jean can no longer imagine that only the Warriors could become the kind of person who kills known comrades while telling himself another group must be saved.

Reiner does not receive the recognition as forgiveness. He says his sins are beyond atonement and that even saving those who remain would not make him forgive himself. Then the conclusion narrows: if atonement is impossible, save the people who remain anyway.

This is the specialist's mature survival ethic:

> **The impossibility of moral reimbursement does not eliminate future obligation.**

Reiner can believe that the past cannot be paid back and still reject the inference that therefore no future action matters.

## 22. V33: self-recognition generates insight and bias at once

Reiner hypothesizes that Eren may want someone to stop him. The inference is psychologically plausible precisely because Reiner recognizes his own history in Eren: unbearable continuation, role pressure, violence after recognition, and self-destructive desire mixed with mission.

That same source of insight creates a bias. Reiner's self-model can be projected onto Eren. The architecture therefore treats the hypothesis as actor reasoning, not privileged narration. Later Eren evidence cannot be forced to match it simply because Reiner has suffered a structurally similar problem.

This is an important reconstruction rule: Reiner is often perceptive about people whose conflicts resemble his own, but similarity increases both empathy and projective error.

## 23. V33: Karina's parental revision reverses the value of the armband

At Fort Salta, Karina finally reinterprets the system she once treated as protection. The armband and Warrior achievement no longer signify successful parental strategy. They become evidence of what the child was made to sacrifice for family status and security.

This does not retroactively turn Karina into an uncaring mother or erase the racialized constraints within which she acted. It does make one of Reiner's deepest childhood premises collapse externally as well as internally. The mother who once needed the child to become a Warrior no longer wants the role to define his worth.

The significance for Reiner is not merely emotional validation. The family system that helped create role dependency begins to withdraw its demand.

## 24. V34: final combat gives Reiner function without singular herohood

The last battle repeatedly places Reiner inside distributed action. He rescues and is rescued. He fights inherited Titan bodies. He restrains the source organism while Jean's explosives, Levi's Zeke kill, Armin's transformation, Mikasa's decision, Falco's mobility, Annie/Pieck combat, and many other actions form the causal network.

This is an appropriate terminal combat role for a character whose childhood was organized around becoming the singular `世界の英雄`. The manga does not finally vindicate that fantasy by making Reiner the one hero who saves the world. He becomes one necessary person among many compromised people.

## 25. V34: the Armor ends and Reiner does not

When Titan power disappears, Reiner recognizes that he is no longer the Armored Titan. Karina responds with relief rather than grief over lost status.

The moment closes the family-role architecture with unusual precision. Armor was once supposed to turn a stigmatized child into a worthy son, honorary Marleyan, family savior, and public hero. At the end, mother and son do not need that inherited weapon to authorize belonging.

The best formulation is therefore not "Reiner is redeemed when the Armor disappears." It is:

> **The role that once promised to make Reiner worthy disappears, and the person survives it.**

That distinction is the terminal answer to `ライナーは死んだ`. The adult no longer has to die so another necessary role can replace him.

## 26. Postwar Reiner: ordinary embarrassment matters

The diplomatic ship deliberately returns Reiner to an awkward ordinary register. Jean's vanity and Reiner's romantic embarrassment/humor coexist with the knowledge that these people have killed, betrayed, transformed, mourned, and survived.

This tonal choice is not trivial comic relief. It prevents trauma from becoming another totalizing identity. A person does not have to remain permanently monumental in order for suffering to have mattered.

At the same time, ordinary reentry should not be overread as cure. The manga does not provide a clinical recovery report or exhaustive peaceful-adult profile. It provides evidence that social spontaneity, embarrassment, peer friction, and future-oriented public duty remain behaviorally available.

## 27. Peace delegation: survival becomes exposure to judgment

Three years later Reiner travels toward a militarized Paradis as part of a peace delegation. He understands the absurdity of the position: people who broke the Walls, betrayed the island, and helped kill the man many islanders worship must now ask that island to hear them.

This is the opposite of childhood hero status. The delegation cannot rely on inherited prestige or moral innocence. It arrives through testimony, risk, and the possibility of rejection.

Reiner's survival therefore ends not in private absolution but in public exposure. He remains a person whose past is politically relevant and whose future conduct still matters.

# III. Role ontology: imposed identities, enacted selves, and responsibility

## 28. Reiner is the series' clearest test of whether performed roles can become real

The Reiner problem is often phrased as a question of authenticity: which Reiner is real? The manga instead supplies a more difficult ontology of identity.

A social role can be assigned for strategic reasons and nevertheless become part of the person through enactment. Reiner enters the Walls as a Warrior performing soldier belonging. But "performing" is not synonymous with emotional nonparticipation. Training requires bodily cooperation. Survival requires trust. Peer life creates memories. Protection creates obligations. Advice changes other people. Other people respond to the role and give it reciprocal weight.

This is why the soldier state remains causally important after the mission is exposed. If it were only a private fantasy, Bertolt would not need to restore the Warrior identity verbally and Reiner would not fracture around Marco. The role is sustained by a network of recognition, not solely by Reiner's inner belief.

The converse also holds. The Warrior identity is not fake merely because it begins in propaganda and family aspiration. Reiner genuinely wants to succeed, save the family he imagines, protect his group, fulfill the mission, prove himself, and later protect Marley-side people. Manipulated selection changes the genealogy of the role but does not retroactively delete the years of conduct organized through it.

The result is not an essence hidden beneath roles. The result is **historical personhood made from roles whose claims can conflict**.

## 29. Imposition is a causal fact, not a permanent excuse

Reiner's childhood is saturated with external structure.

- He is born into a racialized society that legally and socially separates Eldians from Marleyans.
- His mother invests Warrior status with family-restoration meaning.
- The state teaches the child that military inheritance can prove worth and rescue the ethnic group.
- Marcel distorts selection to protect Porco.
- Titan succession gives the selected child a shortened life and an institutional identity.
- Failure can mean disgrace, loss of power, and death.

Those conditions radically reduce the usefulness of a fantasy in which young Reiner freely invents the entire project. But they do not freeze him permanently in the category "coerced child." V24 and V25 deliberately locate later decision points after parts of the structure become visible to him.

Moral analysis therefore has to be time-indexed. The question at age ten is not identical to the question after Marcel's death; the question after years among the 104th is not identical to either; the question for adult Reiner after returning to Marley is different again.

A causal explanation becomes ethically informative only if it also tracks when new information and alternatives enter the state.

## 30. Self-authorship begins before freedom is complete

Reiner never reaches a condition of total unconstrained choice. That is not unique to him; nobody in the series does. What changes is the degree to which he can identify the forces acting through him and still decide how to continue.

After Marcel's death, his choice to continue is already constrained by return risk, indoctrination, guilt, survival, and the team's precarious position. Yet Reiner does not describe himself as a passive object. He constructs a role and pressures the group toward continuation.

After living with the 104th, he knows more about the people targeted by the mission than the childhood doctrine allowed. That knowledge does not automatically grant an easy exit, but it changes the moral information available.

By the V25 basement confession, Reiner can explicitly distinguish environment from his own hero desire and post-Marcel insistence. His account is severe because self-authorship has become retrospective: he can see a decision point that the child and substitute-Marcel states did not describe in the same moral vocabulary.

The complete manga therefore supports a layered principle:

> **Responsibility increases with neither pure freedom nor pure hindsight. It follows the actual intersections of constraint, information, motive, available alternative, and chosen continuation.**

## 31. Role capture is strongest when the role solves an identity wound

Reiner's roles are powerful because they do more than tell him what to do. They answer wounds that ordinary self-description cannot easily answer.

Warrior status promises a family.

World-hero status promises value after paternal rejection.

Marcel substitution promises legitimacy after manipulated selection and survivor guilt.

Soldier reliability creates peer belonging inside an infiltrated society.

Adult Warrior competence preserves family/community function after the original ideology has collapsed.

Candidate caretaker gives a reason to remain alive after heroic meaning implodes.

Coalition fighter gives guilt a future-directed task after self-forgiveness becomes unavailable.

The psychological power of these roles therefore comes from their ability to transform **"why am I still here?"** into **"what does this position require next?"**

That mechanism can be adaptive and dangerous. It permits endurance, protection, coordination, and survival. It can also make continuation easier than reevaluating whether the role itself remains morally permissible.

## 32. Reiner is not uniquely weak for needing roles

The manga surrounds Reiner with characters whose identities are similarly relational and institutional: Survey Corps commander, queen, Warrior, Ackerman protector, soldier, brother, father, patriot, revolutionary, doctor, heir. Reiner is distinctive not because everyone else possesses pure interior authenticity while he performs masks. He is distinctive because the text makes the seams visible.

His case therefore reveals a general series principle: identity is partly **something one does with and for other people**, not simply a private property waiting to be discovered.

The moral danger appears when the role's demands become self-validating. If being a Warrior proves worth, then violence required by the Warrior role can become proof of belonging. Marco's death is the most explicit case: Annie is asked to demonstrate who she is by committing the kind of act that the role demands.

## 33. `兵士` and `戦士` organize different moral horizons

The soldier/Warrior vocabulary is not interchangeable.

The `兵士` state is socially embedded inside the Walls. Its obligations are concrete: protect comrades, survive training, perform military duty, manage immediate threats, joke, eat, complain, compete, recover ordinary life.

The `戦士` state is tied to a distant homeland and mission. It requires concealment, capture of the Founder/Coordinate, loyalty to absent institutions and families, and willingness to sacrifice the local relationships created by infiltration.

Bertolt's reminder in V11 functions because it switches the horizon of obligation. Reiner's conflict is not only about memory. It is about which community currently defines what action counts as duty.

The later coalition complicates the binary. Reiner can no longer simply return from soldier to Warrior. He acts with people from both sides against a genocidal objective that neither role was originally built to handle. The terminal state is therefore not integration by choosing one old label. It is action after the old role system has become insufficient.

## 34. Post-Armor identity is not rolelessness

The end of Titan power does not produce a metaphysical naked self. Reiner still has relations and roles: son, former Warrior, former comrade, survivor, delegate, peer.

What changes is that one coercive inherited role no longer monopolizes his social value. The end of Armor status removes a bodily institution that had made his lifespan, family status, military function, and ethnic respectability converge in one identity.

That release does not erase history. It pluralizes the available future.

# IV. Family, childhood ideology, and the machinery of worth

## 35. The Warrior dream is initially a family dream

Reiner's childhood motive should not be summarized as generic nationalism. His ideology is relationally anchored. The child believes military distinction can solve exclusion at the scale he understands most intimately: mother, father, household.

This gives the state's bargain extraordinary emotional force. If becoming a Warrior can make the family whole, then the institution appears to transform bodily sacrifice into love. The child's own shortened future can be experienced as a path to family permanence.

The tragedy is that the family being pursued is partly imaginary. The father does not share the plan. Karina's hope and Reiner's aspiration cannot create consent from the absent parent.

This becomes one of the series' recurring anti-instrumentalization patterns: a desired future can be emotionally sincere and still be built by assigning another person's will in advance.

## 36. The father's rejection converts private shame into public hero aspiration

Reiner's father's rejection is not merely a sad biographical detail. It changes the structure of the child's desired recognition.

The original goal is intimate: be acknowledged as family.

After the door closes, the replacement goal becomes public: be recognized as hero.

That movement matters because public ideology can absorb private injury. The state does not need to invent every emotional need it exploits. It can offer a grand role that converts an already existing wound into political usefulness.

Reiner's later hunger to be seen as necessary, reliable, heroic, or responsibility-bearing cannot be reduced to this one moment, but the scene gives the pattern an early root.

## 37. Karina loves Reiner and helps instrumentalize him

A simplistic family reading must choose between "Karina loves him" and "Karina uses him." The manga supports both dynamics at once.

Karina's own position is constrained by racial hierarchy and abandonment. Warrior status appears to offer security, recognition, and reunification. Her investment in Reiner's success is therefore understandable as survival and love inside an oppressive system.

Yet the cost is borne by the child. His body becomes the route through which family status is supposed to improve. The thirteen-year curse and military role are normalized as opportunity.

V33's Karina revision matters because she eventually recognizes the inversion: the status object meant to protect the child was acquired by sacrificing him to the system.

This does not reimburse Reiner for childhood. It removes one lingering family demand from the terminal identity field.

## 38. Marcel's protection reproduces another asymmetry

Marcel manipulates Reiner's selection to save Porco. The motive is deeply relational: protect a younger brother from inheriting a shortened military life.

The method transfers the cost to another child.

This is the same structural problem the series will explore repeatedly: particular love can be ethically real and still become dangerous when it authorizes assigning strangers or less-valued people to absorb the burden.

Marcel's later sacrifice for Reiner complicates the relation further. The person who unfairly shifted institutional danger onto Reiner then gives his life to save Reiner. There is no clean moral label that cancels either act.

Reiner inherits both the damage and the debt.

## 39. Porco's rivalry keeps the compromised selection alive

Porco's resentment matters because it prevents the manipulated selection from remaining a private historical fact. Reiner continues to live beside a person whose family history exposes the contingency of his Armor status.

This does not mean Porco is simply correct and Reiner simply fraudulent. Military selection, inherited power, later performance, and current competence are not the same question. But Porco keeps visible the fact that Reiner's status cannot function as uncomplicated proof of merit.

That pressure reinforces Reiner's tendency to seek worth through conduct rather than secure self-certification.

## 40. Gabi and Falco make Reiner face the system from the adult side

The candidate children change Reiner's ethical position because he can now see the machinery reproducing itself around people he knows.

Gabi resembles the high-identification child state more closely: she treats Warrior succession as honor, proof, collective rescue, and personal worth.

Falco's motive is more substitutive and protective: inherit so Gabi does not have to.

Reiner's adult relation to them cannot be reduced to ideological mentoring. The children become specific futures. They are also reminders of the system's capacity to turn care into inherited mortality.

The rifle scene makes that relation literal. Their existence is enough to interrupt Reiner's attempt to remove himself from the future.

## 41. Candidate care is not yet institutional reform

It would overstate Reiner's development to claim that he becomes a systematic abolitionist of the Warrior system before the institution collapses. He remains a participant in Marleyan military structure and continues fighting.

His interruption is local and relational. He cares what happens to these children. He tries to protect them. He is increasingly legible as an adult who knows what the role cost.

This matters precisely because the moral development is incomplete. The series often locates change first in local refusal before institutions change. Reiner's case should be preserved at that scale.

## 42. The terminal family scene reverses childhood causality

At the beginning, the logic is:

**become Armor → become worthy → save family → belong.**

At the end, Titan power disappears and Karina is relieved.

The terminal logic becomes:

**the son remains → the role can disappear → belonging does not require inherited weaponhood.**

That reversal is one of the cleanest role-identity conclusions in the manga.

# V. Guilt, dissociation, self-condemnation, and responsibility

## 43. Guilt is not one Reiner state

Reiner's guilt takes several forms that should not be collapsed.

- **Unintegrated fracture:** the Marco scene, where Warrior action and soldier perception cannot coexist coherently.
- **Role-compensatory guilt:** becoming Marcel and making continued survival useful.
- **Traumatic recall:** adult return and memory leakage around the 104th.
- **Suicidal self-erasure:** the rifle scene.
- **Causal confession:** telling Eren he chose continuation and wanted to be a hero.
- **Relational confession:** telling Jean/coalition what happened to Marco.
- **Non-atonement cognition:** judging his sins beyond repayment while continuing action.
- **Prospective burden shielding:** trying to prevent others from crossing the same killing threshold.

A model that uses one global "guilt level" loses the developmental logic.

## 44. Marco is the decisive guilt event because Reiner is both initiator and shocked witness

Marco's death has unusual force because it contains no clean external villain for Reiner to blame.

He identifies the threat Marco poses to the mission.

He restrains him.

He pressures Annie into removing the ODM gear.

He participates in abandoning Marco to a Titan.

Then the resulting death becomes psychologically discontinuous enough that Reiner reacts as though the Titan alone explains what happened.

This structure makes guilt more difficult than simple remorse. The person must later integrate an act that one role-state could not fully recognize as authored by the same self.

## 45. Dissociative discontinuity is evidence, not acquittal

The text makes the discontinuity behaviorally real. It should neither be mocked as cowardice nor converted into a legal-metaphysical transfer of agency.

Reiner's body and decisions remain causally involved. Annie and Bertolt remember what happened. Marco's death does not cease to be an act of the Warrior group because Reiner's soldier-state perception reemerges afterward.

The right analytical distinction is:

> **subjective integration can fail while causal and moral authorship remain.**

Later confession becomes meaningful because Reiner can eventually hold more of those layers together.

## 46. Self-condemnation can be morally clarifying and epistemically excessive

V25's `俺が悪いんだよ` is ethically important because Reiner refuses to make structural explanation do the work of acquittal.

But guilt has its own simplifying temptation. If everything can be made "my fault," then the causal field becomes psychologically coherent again. State institutions, parental pressure, child socialization, Marcel's manipulation, Titan succession, war, and the choices of other actors can collapse into one self-blaming story.

The specialist therefore distinguishes **accepting one's authorship** from **claiming sole authorship of history**.

The former is supported and necessary.

The latter is not.

## 47. Suicide is connected to moral exhaustion but not reducible to guilt arithmetic

The rifle scene follows years of role failure, trauma, killing, survivor debt, family performance, and a future in which Reiner cannot imagine a satisfying self-directed reason to continue. It would be too narrow to say he attempts suicide simply because he feels guilty for one crime.

The scene instead represents **collapse of viable self-continuation**. Every major identity is compromised:

- family savior was based on a rejected father;
- chosen Warrior status was manipulated;
- soldier belonging was betrayed;
- world-hero story is morally broken;
- military survival has reproduced violence;
- the future is another succession of children inheriting the role.

Falco's voice does not solve those contradictions. It introduces a narrower reason to remain present.

## 48. Anti-suicide tethers are relational, conditional, and task-shaped

Reiner's later survival should not be romanticized as "he learns to love himself." The manga does not supply that arc explicitly.

What it repeatedly supplies is **external future**.

The candidates still exist.

Falco is missing or threatened.

Gabi still needs help.

Families may die.

The Rumbling has to be stopped.

Former comrades can be shielded from a killing burden.

The coalition needs the Armor.

The organism has to be restrained.

Peace delegation still requires people willing to appear before former enemies.

Each task can make continued existence locally intelligible even when self-forgiveness remains unavailable.

## 49. Guilt can become future-oriented without becoming redemptive currency

Burden shielding at the harbor is the clearest example. Reiner uses what he knows about betrayal to change the next distribution of harm.

This is ethically significant because it turns remorse into constraint: **do not make these people become what I became if another division of labor is possible**.

But the act does not pay Marco back. It is not a moral transaction. The past becomes knowledge that can alter the future without being reimbursed by it.

## 50. `罪は償えない` and the survival of obligation

Reiner's V33 non-atonement stance is easy to misread as nihilism. If the crime cannot be repaid and self-forgiveness is impossible, why continue?

The text's answer is not a theory of cosmic forgiveness. It is narrower: save the people who remain.

This produces a form of responsibility that survives the failure of redemption. A morally damaged person does not acquire permission to stop caring about future victims simply because no future act can erase the past.

## 51. Reiner's guilt is a check on heroic self-myth, not a guarantee of correct action

Yelena's critique of `世界を救う` applies strongly to Reiner. He has already lived through one identity in which being a world-saving hero justified catastrophe. The coalition's new "save the world" language is therefore dangerous if it becomes another cleansing myth.

Reiner's persistent guilt can help prevent that myth from becoming psychologically total. He does not enter the final battle as a newly pure hero.

Yet guilt itself is not infallible. It can produce self-destruction, over-attribution, hesitation, projective inference about Eren, and willingness to absorb unreasonable burdens.

The mature model must therefore treat guilt as **morally informative, behaviorally powerful, and epistemically fallible**.

# VI. Recognition: seeing the person without dissolving the conflict

## 52. Recognition is Reiner's gift and wound

Reiner's life demonstrates that recognizing another person's humanity can increase suffering without automatically reducing violence.

The 104th become real people.

Marco remains a person while being sacrificed.

Eren becomes a victim Reiner later understands from the other side.

Gabi and Falco are children inside a system Reiner can recognize from experience.

Jean becomes a former victim who can later recognize structural similarity without forgiveness.

Recognition does not settle the conflict. It removes the emotional shelter of a simple enemy ontology.

## 53. Reiner and Eren recognize one another across asymmetrical histories

The V25 basement is often treated as the definitive Reiner/Eren mirror. It is better understood as one of several mirror states.

Eren can now understand why a child crossed the sea and attacked people he did not know. Reiner can see the adult consequence of the persistence grammar he once helped reinforce. Both have lived among people assigned to enemy categories and found ordinary humanity there. Both continue destructive projects after that recognition.

But the comparison has limits. Their information, ages, coercive structures, strategic capacities, objectives, scales of violence, and terminal choices differ.

Recognition is analytically useful precisely because it can identify shared mechanism **without converting mechanism into moral equivalence**.

## 54. Eren's forgiveness is not the central question

Eren's contextual understanding can sound like an offer of absolution, but Reiner rejects the idea that environmental explanation exhausts his responsibility. The scene thereby prevents the victim's recognition from becoming a magical cleansing authority.

Even if Eren were to forgive Reiner personally, Eren could not retroactively speak for all the people harmed by the Warrior operation. Reiner's own later relation to guilt reflects that wider problem.

The moral importance of the scene lies not in a transferable pardon but in the coexistence of understanding, first-person authorship, and ongoing violence.

## 55. Jean's `同じ` arrives only after Jean crosses his own threshold

Jean cannot understand Reiner structurally in the same way while he remains outside the experience of killing known comrades for a larger rescue objective. The harbor changes that epistemic position.

When Jean later says `俺達は同じだ…ライナー`, the statement is earned through dirty hands. It does not mean Jean has become Reiner or that the histories are equal. It means one moral phenomenon once treated as uniquely monstrous has become experientially legible.

This makes the scene a recognition event rather than reconciliation.

## 56. Recognition without forgiveness is one of Reiner's terminal relational forms

Jean's explicit refusal to forgive is not a failure of the coalition. It is part of what makes the coalition morally credible.

Reiner does not demand emotional closure as payment for his participation. Jean does not have to pretend the past has been repaired to coordinate against the Rumbling.

The mature relation can therefore contain:

- anger;
- remembered betrayal;
- structural recognition;
- tactical trust;
- shared current objective;
- absence of forgiveness.

This is richer than a simple enemies-to-friends arc.

## 57. Recognition can make burden shielding possible

Reiner's attempt to spare the former 104th from harbor killing depends on his ability to recognize what the threshold will mean to them. He is not merely assigning roles by comparative combat capability. He is modeling the moral injury of the act.

This is one of the clearest ways later Reiner uses recognition prospectively rather than only suffering from it retrospectively.

## 58. Recognition can also create projective error

The hypothesis that Eren may want to be stopped emerges from Reiner's capacity to see himself in Eren. That makes it psychologically grounded, not privileged truth.

The analyst should therefore apply a general rule:

> **Reiner's strongest analogical insights often arise where another person's structure resembles his own, and those are also the places where self-projection must be audited most carefully.**

# VII. Survival without clean redemption

## 59. Survival is not the reward for becoming morally good

The manga does not build a transactional rule in which Reiner is allowed to live because later conduct earns survival.

Many better and worse people die. Survival is contingent, embodied, relational, strategic, and historical.

Reiner's continued life should therefore not be interpreted as authorial acquittal. It is a condition under which responsibility continues.

## 60. Reiner repeatedly survives after identities that should have justified survival collapse

The pattern is striking.

He survives paternal rejection after family reunion was the goal.

He survives Marcel after merit and team structure collapse.

He survives the 104th reveal after soldier belonging becomes incompatible with mission.

He survives Return to Shiganshina after severe bodily destruction.

He survives the ideological collapse of the island-devil category.

He survives a suicide attempt.

He survives Liberio and renewed war.

He survives the loss of Armor.

Each survival requires a new answer to what continuation is for. None is guaranteed to be morally adequate.

## 61. Relationship tethers are not sentimental because they can be coercive as well as sustaining

The same relational architecture that saves Reiner can trap him.

Karina's hopes sustain purpose and help bind him to the Warrior system.

Marcel's sacrifice sustains mission continuation through survivor debt.

The 104th sustain soldier belonging that later makes betrayal unbearable.

Candidate children interrupt suicide and create adult obligation.

The coalition creates a reason to fight while also asking him to confront past victims directly.

Relationships are therefore not a universal cure. They are causal structures that can produce life, duty, guilt, coercion, and revision.

## 62. The candidate children change survival from self-preservation to stewardship

The rifle scene is the clearest pivot. Reiner is not persuaded that his own life has suddenly become intrinsically delightful. He remembers that younger people still exist inside a machine he understands from experience.

That creates a stewardship logic: **I may not know how to justify myself, but my absence would leave these children inside the future without what I can still provide.**

Later conduct repeatedly develops this structure.

## 63. The coalition gives Reiner a future-oriented task without giving him innocence

The anti-Rumbling group is deliberately composed of compromised actors. Reiner is neither uniquely dirty nor cleansed by their company.

What matters is that the current prohibition—do not permit the outside world's extermination—creates objective alignment strong enough for action. Reiner can contribute military knowledge, Titan force, relationship understanding, and burden shielding while still believing earlier sins remain.

This is **responsibility after disqualification from self-righteousness**.

## 64. The end of Armor is release from inherited role, not release from history

When Titan power disappears, Reiner's future is no longer physically tied to a state weapon or thirteen-year succession system. That is genuine liberation from one infrastructure.

But peace negotiations still contain his history. Paradis remembers the Wall breach. The delegation's credibility cannot rely on pretending the past is gone.

The end therefore separates two things that childhood fused:

- **being allowed to exist as Reiner**, and
- **having to answer for what Reiner did**.

Both remain.

## 65. Ordinary humor is evidence of surviving plurality

Reiner's later awkwardness, embarrassment, and peer banter matter because guilt is no longer the only active register. A person can remember grave harm and still participate in mundane social life.

This should not be simulated as emotional whiplash or proof of recovered innocence. Human behavior is plural. The capacity for ordinary humor is evidence that Reiner's identity field has expanded beyond role and self-condemnation.

## 66. Peace work is a fitting terminal burden because it cannot be completed by force alone

The Armored Titan is a role built around making Reiner useful through destructive bodily power. Postwar delegation requires something else: appearing before people who have reason to hate him and participating in a process whose outcome cannot be guaranteed by superior force.

The task is therefore structurally anti-heroic in the childhood sense. It offers no singular victory, no proof of worth, and no ability to erase the past.

It requires continued presence.

# VIII. Heroism, masculinity, usefulness, and the danger of being needed

## 67. Reiner's hero desire is a theory of worth

"Wanted to be a hero" should not be reduced to vanity. It is a value system in which worth is proven by usefulness under danger and recognized by others.

That system can produce courage, protection, competence, and leadership. It can also make failure intolerable and assign moral meaning to continued mission regardless of what the mission becomes.

The child who needs hero status is therefore vulnerable to institutions that can define what heroic usefulness requires.

## 68. Reliability becomes a socially reinforced identity

The 104th respond to Reiner as the dependable older-brother-like peer. Their recognition is not irrelevant to his later fracture. It rewards a version of Reiner capable of carrying other people's burdens.

That role can be genuinely prosocial and psychologically dangerous. If worth is experienced primarily through being the person others can rely on, then admitting weakness, dependency, moral uncertainty, or need may threaten identity coherence.

Adult Reiner's suicidal collapse occurs after the heroic-usefulness system can no longer make the self tolerable.

## 69. Reiner often expresses care by taking weight rather than by offering reassurance

This pattern appears across states.

He physically protects comrades.

He assumes leadership after Marcel.

He presses Bertolt toward decisiveness and entrusts him as `相棒`.

He mentors candidates.

He tries to carry harbor killing so former 104th members do not have to.

He uses the Armor/body as shield in combat.

The mature reconstruction should therefore model care through **burden acceptance and task assumption** more readily than through long verbal self-disclosure.

## 70. Burden acceptance can become self-destructive

The same pattern has a danger side. Reiner can assign himself too much causal responsibility, accept death or suffering as deserved, and treat personal endurance as the default solution to group problems.

Later care does not simply vindicate this tendency. The manga repeatedly distributes final victory across many people, undermining the fantasy that one person must become the necessary hero who carries everything.

## 71. The final distributed battle revises the childhood hero script

Reiner is vital in V34. He is not sufficient.

That is precisely the right correction. The child wanted a role that would prove worth through world-saving singularity. The adult participates in a network where everyone covers another person's limitation.

Heroism becomes less about being the one indispensable body and more about continuing to perform a bounded responsibility inside collective action.

# IX. Relationship architecture

## 72. Karina Braun: love, aspiration, instrumentalization, and late revision

Karina is one of Reiner's most important causal relationships because her love is inseparable from a social system that makes the child's body a route toward family legitimacy.

The relation should not be flattened into abuse or exonerated as pure care. Karina wants security and recognition for a family injured by racial hierarchy and paternal abandonment. She genuinely believes Warrior success can help. Yet the child is made to carry the project.

Reiner's later model of family cannot therefore treat maternal approval as a neutral positive reward. It is historically entangled with the identity system he spends the series surviving.

V33 and V34 revise the relation without pretending childhood did not happen. Karina recognizes the cost of making him a Warrior and welcomes the end of Armor status. The mother finally separates the son's value from the role through which she once tried to secure it.

## 73. The Marleyan father: unavailable recognition as a formative absence

The father is important less as an ongoing interpersonal relationship than as a negative structure. Reiner organizes childhood effort around imagined paternal recognition, then discovers the imagined future was never mutually authored.

The rejection teaches a brutal lesson: achievement cannot force another person to recognize the relationship one wants.

Reiner responds by seeking a role whose recognition is public rather than private. That substitution helps explain why hero status can become psychologically adhesive.

A reconstruction should not overpopulate later adult thought with the father absent explicit evidence. His strongest role is formative: the closed door changes what kind of recognition the child seeks next.

## 74. Marcel Galliard: protector, manipulator, dead leader, and substitute identity

Marcel occupies several incompatible positions in Reiner's history.

He unfairly manipulates selection to protect Porco.

He reveals the manipulation.

He dies saving Reiner.

His absence creates a tactical leadership vacuum.

His qualities become the material for Reiner's announced replacement self.

This makes Marcel neither benevolent mentor nor simple betrayer. Reiner's survivor debt is powerful because he inherits both injury and protection from the same person.

The specialist should therefore resist language such as "Reiner becomes Marcel" if it implies successful psychological copying. More accurately, **Marcel becomes a role-template Reiner uses to organize his own continuation**.

## 75. Porco Galliard: rivalry as an external memory of compromised worth

Porco carries the sibling cost of Marcel's choice. His hostility repeatedly reminds Reiner that the Armor role was not awarded through a morally neutral merit process.

Reiner does not respond by abandoning all claims to competence. Nor does Porco's criticism prove every later Reiner achievement fraudulent. The relationship keeps status contingent and uncomfortable.

Porco's own protective actions later complicate rivalry further. Their relation belongs more fully to the Warrior-ensemble specialist, but for Reiner it functions as a check against using inherited title as uncomplicated proof of deserved worth.

## 76. Bertolt Hoover: partner, identity restorer, and person Reiner pressures toward agency

Bertolt is the person most consistently positioned beside Reiner across the early Warrior mission. Their relation is asymmetrical but not one-directional.

Reiner often organizes action, supplies confidence, and later tells Bertolt to stop leaving decisive choices to him. `相棒` marks familiar entrustment rather than abstract command.

Bertolt, in turn, restores the Warrior horizon when Reiner slips into the soldier state. `兵士じゃないだろ…僕らは戦士なんだから` is not merely correction of vocabulary. It is relationship-mediated identity regulation.

The pair therefore demonstrates that roles are socially maintained. Reiner's Warrior self is not held entirely inside his own cognition; another person can remind him what world they are supposed to inhabit.

## 77. Annie Leonhart: coercion, shared mission, and the danger of proof

Reiner's relation to Annie is one of the strongest controls against sentimentalizing his own victimization by Marley.

He is himself a child inside coercive structure, yet he can reproduce coercion downward and laterally. Marco's death shows this clearly. Reiner challenges Annie's Warrior belonging and invokes her father, turning her deepest relationship into leverage for an atrocity.

The scene establishes a general rule:

> **Being coerced by an institution does not prevent a person from becoming an enforcer of the same institution's demands on others.**

Later coalition cooperation cannot erase that history. It can only create new conduct under different conditions.

## 78. Eren Jaeger: admired junior, betrayed victim, mirror, and projective danger

Reiner's relation to Eren is temporally layered.

As trainee, Reiner encourages him. Eren regards Reiner as a model soldier. Their early relation is asymmetrical mentorship among peers: the stronger, steadier cadet helping the furious younger boy direct effort.

At reveal, that admired figure becomes the person who helped destroy Eren's home. Because admiration was real, betrayal is especially violent.

In Liberio, the relation reverses. Adult Eren possesses the calm strategic initiative; Reiner is the person forced into confession. Eren offers contextual understanding that Reiner refuses to let become total acquittal.

Later Reiner recognizes structural features of himself in Eren strongly enough to generate the "perhaps he wants to be stopped" hypothesis. The relation thus becomes a mirror capable of insight and distortion.

For simulation, Eren should not be assigned one static Reiner affect. The relationship can activate admiration-memory, shame, fear, recognition, guilt, tactical urgency, and analogical reasoning depending on state.

## 79. Jean Kirstein: victim anger to functional non-forgiveness

Jean is the best late-series test of whether Reiner can cooperate without demanding absolution.

Jean has direct reason for rage through Marco. He beats Reiner after the confession. He later participates in the same anti-Rumbling operation and states explicitly that he cannot forgive him.

Reiner accepts the boundary.

After Jean kills known comrades during the harbor operation, `俺達は同じだ…ライナー` adds structural recognition without reversing the non-forgiveness. Their mature relation is therefore layered rather than binary:

**anger + non-forgiveness + recognition + trust sufficient for action + peer-level later ordinary interaction.**

This is high-value reconstruction evidence because it predicts cooperation without emotional closure.

## 80. Marco Bott: absent person with continuing causal presence

Marco dies early relative to the complete series but remains one of Reiner's most persistent moral relationships.

The relation is not built from a large quantity of dialogue. It is built from the mismatch between Marco's ordinary peerhood and the way the Warrior mission converts him into a security problem.

His last procedural protest—there has not yet been proper discussion—later becomes a central coalition memory. Marco therefore survives in Reiner's model as evidence of an alternative foreclosed before it was tested.

The specialist should not romanticize this into proof that conversation would definitely have solved the conflict. Its function is narrower: Reiner participated in making information exchange impossible before deciding the person had to die.

## 81. Gabi Braun: kinship, ideological repetition, and the child whose future remains open

Gabi gives Reiner a living image of the child ideology he once inhabited. She is family, candidate, intensely identified Warrior aspirant, and someone capable of later revision.

Reiner's care for her therefore operates on two levels. She is a specific loved child, and she is evidence that the inherited script is still recruiting the next generation.

Her later refusal of Reiner's resignation in V31 is important. The younger person becomes the one insisting the exhausted adult continue because other concrete lives still matter. The direction of protection reverses without erasing Reiner's caretaker role.

## 82. Falco Grice: anti-suicide tether and alternative candidate motive

Falco is especially important because his relationship to Warrior inheritance differs from Reiner's childhood model. Falco is willing to inherit partly to prevent Gabi from doing so.

That protective substitution reveals the system's danger through another form of love: one child can volunteer to absorb the shortened future so another does not have to.

For Reiner, Falco's voice interrupts suicide before Falco even knows that is what he is doing. Later Falco's vulnerability repeatedly supplies a concrete reason to act.

A simulation should preserve the asymmetry. Reiner is protective and burden-bearing toward Falco; Falco is not merely a therapeutic object whose purpose is to save Reiner. He has his own motives and agency.

## 83. Pieck and Magath: professional coalition after ideological collapse

Reiner's adult Warrior relationships include people with whom competence, state service, and personal familiarity coexist. Pieck and Magath help situate Reiner inside a military world that is neither reducible to family nor 104th attachment.

The later coalition demonstrates that professional coordination can survive major political revision. Magath's own regret about child soldiers further externalizes a critique Reiner has lived bodily.

These relations should be modeled as role-competence networks more than confessional intimacy unless a specific scene supports otherwise.

## 84. The anti-Rumbling coalition: shared prohibition without shared innocence

Reiner's coalition membership is not a return to the 104th and not a new unified national identity. It is a temporary and then historically consequential alignment around a shared prohibition: the outside world's extermination cannot be accepted.

The coalition works because members do not need identical motives. Reiner can act from guilt, candidate/family protection, recognition, and remaining duty; Jean can act without forgiveness; Annie can act from father attachment; Armin from anti-genocide reasoning and dialogue; Mikasa while loving Eren.

This makes coalition identity less totalizing than Reiner's earlier roles. It does not require one story to consume every other relationship.

## 85. Postwar delegation: former roles become testimony rather than command authority

The peace delegation places Reiner in a social form where history cannot be hidden and the old military role cannot guarantee success. He travels as someone whose presence itself tells a story about former enemies standing together.

This is one reason the endpoint belongs under survival rather than redemption. The future requires him to continue appearing in relation to people who may reject the account.

# X. Japanese written voice, address, and state-conditioned register

## 86. `俺` supplies continuity while role and affect alter syntax

Reiner's first-person `俺` remains broadly stable enough that voice reconstruction cannot rely on pronoun switching as a substitute for state modeling. What changes more strongly is sentence function, certainty, command pressure, confession density, and the degree to which he explains versus simply acts.

The model should therefore track **register state**, not invent separate speaking personalities.

## 87. Soldier Reiner tends toward practical, direct, peer-accessible speech

In the 104th domain, Reiner's voice can be encouraging, blunt, joking, corrective, and task-oriented. His reliability is social as well as physical. He does not need ceremonial language to act as an informal stabilizer.

This repertoire is essential for low-stakes reconstruction. Reiner should not speak like a permanently traumatized confessor in every ordinary room.

## 88. Warrior-command Reiner compresses toward task and role

Under mission urgency, Reiner can narrow rapidly to objective language. V19's `イヤ迷うな 先に殺すのは馬だ` shows self-correction in compact operational form. The command register is not evidence that emotion disappears; it shows attention being forcibly returned to priority.

With Bertolt, `頼んだぞ相棒` carries trust through brevity. The relationship permits concise entrustment rather than elaborate motivational speech.

## 89. Role fracture can produce category language that sounds temporally misaligned

V11's soldier-state speech is diagnostically strange because the conversational future being described belongs to a role the current mission has already made impossible. The effect should not be simulated by random confusion. It is triggered by specific guilt/identity conditions.

A reconstruction that turns Reiner into chronically disoriented speech would overfit the most spectacular state.

## 90. Confession contracts toward first-person causality

The V25 basement sequence is marked by repeated rejection of mitigation and increasing first-person ownership. Short formulations such as `違う` and `俺が悪いんだよ` matter because Reiner is resisting an account that could dissolve him into environment.

The register becomes anti-euphemistic. He names hero desire and continuation rather than hiding behind Warrior doctrine.

This does not mean every guilty state produces fluent confession. Earlier Marco discontinuity demonstrates the opposite.

## 91. Self-condemnation should not be translated into universal melodrama

Japanese shortness matters. Reiner's most severe self-accounting is often effective because it is compressed rather than ornate. A model that expands every guilt state into long poetic monologues risks changing his voice.

The mature reconstruction should prefer:

- concise causal admission;
- blunt self-devaluation where source-supported;
- task return after admission;
- fewer abstract moral lectures than an analyst might be tempted to give him.

## 92. `相棒` belongs to familiar entrustment, not generic friendliness

Reiner's use of `相棒` toward Bertolt carries mission history and partnership. It should not be generalized as a catchphrase for every close associate.

Address terms need relationship conditioning.

## 93. Ordinary postwar humor demonstrates register recovery

V34's diplomatic-ship material is valuable because Reiner can participate in awkward, comic, mildly vain or romantic social texture after the crisis. The existence of this register should block a reconstruction that makes adult Reiner incapable of ordinary embarrassment or humor.

It should not be inflated into dense knowledge of his peaceful hobbies, domestic preferences, or long-term romantic behavior beyond source.

## 94. Translation-sensitive warning: `兵士`, `戦士`, and "hero" are role architectures

English equivalents can make these words look like interchangeable labels for military function. In Reiner's arc they carry different social worlds.

`兵士` is tied to the Wall military and lived comrade identity.

`戦士` is tied to Marley-side mission, inherited Titan status, homeland obligation, and the identity system Bertolt reactivates.

`世界の英雄` carries the child's compensatory public-worth fantasy.

A specialist or simulator that flattens all three into "soldier" loses the identity conflict.

# XI. Adversarial counterreading audit

## 95. Counterreading 1 — "Reiner has dissociative identity disorder, and that explains the arc"

**What it gets right:** Reiner exhibits severe discontinuity, incompatible role organization, memory/perception disturbance around Marco, guilt, and behavior that resembles dissociative phenomena.

**Why it is insufficient:** the manga does not provide the clinical evidence needed to make a retrospective diagnosis the controlling explanatory category. The literary structure is more precise about what the text actually shows: role formation, guilt, incompatible social worlds, contextual switching, trauma, and partial later integration.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT as canonical master formulation; preserve only as a possible external clinical analogy if a later explicitly separated project authorizes it.**

## 96. Counterreading 2 — "Soldier Reiner was just acting"

**What it gets right:** the soldier identity begins as infiltration cover.

**Why it fails:** spontaneous protection of Connie, peer interaction, Eren mentorship, V11 soldier-state activation, Marco fracture, and V23 ordinary memory all show the role became psychologically and relationally real.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT.** Origin as cover does not imply later emotional unreality.

## 97. Counterreading 3 — "Warrior Reiner was the real self beneath the soldier mask"

**What it gets right:** the Warrior mission predates the 104th and remains strategically powerful.

**Why it fails:** Warrior identity itself is historically constructed through family aspiration, state ideology, manipulated selection, Marcel substitution, and role maintenance. Calling it "real" only relocates the authenticity simplification.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT.** Both major role systems are historically real and constructed.

## 98. Counterreading 4 — "Reiner is only a victim of propaganda and therefore not morally responsible"

**What it gets right:** he is absolutely a child victim of racialized military socialization, family pressure, state propaganda, and an inherited weapon system.

**Why it fails:** the manga repeatedly identifies authored continuation points, most explicitly after Marcel. Reiner himself rejects total environmental exculpation.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT total exculpation; STRENGTHEN structural mitigation without transfer of authorship.**

## 99. Counterreading 5 — "Reiner alone caused everything because he says it was his fault"

**What it gets right:** his hero desire and insistence on mission continuation matter causally and morally.

**Why it fails:** Reiner's self-report is a guilt-saturated first-person account inside a distributed causal history. Marleyan institutions, adult command, child indoctrination, Titan politics, family, Marcel, Annie, Bertolt, and many later actors retain agency.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT sole-causation reading.** Reiner's confession establishes authorship, not historical monopoly.

## 100. Counterreading 6 — "The suicide attempt proves Reiner truly wants death; later survival is inconsistent"

**What it gets right:** the suicide attempt is direct and must not be softened.

**Why it fails:** desire to die in one severe state can coexist with conditional reasons to remain alive in another. The candidate children interrupt the attempt; later relational tasks repeatedly reactivate action.

**Adjudication:** **REVISE to state-dependent suicidality plus relational anti-suicide tethers.**

## 101. Counterreading 7 — "Gabi and Falco save or cure Reiner"

**What it gets right:** the candidates are central to survival reactivation.

**Why it fails:** the source does not show generalized cure, self-forgiveness, or elimination of trauma. The children give Reiner future-oriented responsibility.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT cure language; preserve relational tether.**

## 102. Counterreading 8 — "Eren and Reiner are the same, so their acts are morally equivalent"

**What it gets right:** the manga deliberately constructs structural mirrors around recognition, continuation after humanization, causal environment, and responsibility.

**Why it fails:** same mechanism does not mean same scale, information, objective, capacity, coercion, target, or moral evaluation.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT equivalence; preserve analogical recognition.**

## 103. Counterreading 9 — "Jean saying they are the same means Reiner is forgiven"

**What it gets right:** Jean reaches a new experiential understanding after killing former comrades.

**Why it fails:** Jean explicitly states that he cannot forgive Reiner, and Reiner accepts that fact. Coalition proceeds anyway.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT.** The scene is recognition without forgiveness.

## 104. Counterreading 10 — "The anti-Rumbling alliance redeems Reiner through heroism"

**What it gets right:** later actions matter morally. Reiner protects, fights genocide, shields others from burden, and risks himself for a future beyond the Warrior mission.

**Why it fails:** the narrative never converts those actions into reimbursement. Reiner himself continues to deny atonement, and later characters do not need to erase the past to cooperate.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT clean redemption; preserve continuing responsibility and morally meaningful revision.**

## 105. Counterreading 11 — "The end of Armor completes Reiner's redemption"

**What it gets right:** the end of Armor is a major identity release and reverses the family-status architecture.

**Why it fails:** losing the role changes the future, not the past. It does not erase the people harmed while he carried it.

**Adjudication:** **REVISE:** end of inherited role, not cancellation of responsibility.

## 106. Counterreading 12 — "The peace delegation proves reconciliation succeeded"

**What it gets right:** former enemies reach a level of cooperation sufficient for diplomacy.

**Why it fails:** Paradis is militarized, war remains possible, and the delegation is approaching a society with strong reasons to reject it. The manga does not show permanent peace.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT outcome certainty; preserve diplomacy as ongoing exposure to judgment.**

## 107. Counterreading 13 — "Reiner is the most morally honest character because he takes all the blame"

**What it gets right:** he displays extraordinary willingness to own guilt and resist contextual self-exoneration.

**Why it fails:** self-blame can be epistemically distorted. Moral honesty is not maximized by claiming causal responsibility that belongs to others as well. Reiner's guilt can clarify and overcompress simultaneously.

**Adjudication:** **DOWNGRADE.** His honesty is important; his self-condemnation is not omniscient.

## 108. Counterreading 14 — "Role fracture makes Reiner unreliable in combat"

**What it gets right:** severe psychological conflict can alter behavior.

**Why it fails:** Return to Shiganshina and later campaigns show substantial tactical competence, mission prioritization, and protective action. The fracture is context-dependent, not a global competence deficit.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT global impairment.** Model specific triggers and state transitions.

## 109. Counterreading 15 — "Postwar humor trivializes his trauma"

**What it gets right:** tonal shift can feel abrupt after catastrophe.

**Why it fails:** ordinary social range is itself part of the series' positive value system. Permanent monumental solemnity is not required to respect suffering.

**Adjudication:** **REJECT.** Humor demonstrates surviving plurality, not erased history.

# XII. Responsibility without absolution

## 110. Structural causation must not become moral disappearing ink

Reiner is one of the best cases for the series' distinction between explanation and absolution. The more the reader learns, the harder simplistic blame becomes: he is a racialized child soldier, family instrument, manipulated candidate, traumatized survivor, coerced infiltrator, and participant in an institution larger than himself.

Yet the same source supplies decision points and first-person motive. Explanation expands the causal graph. It does not erase the node called Reiner.

## 111. Personal authorship must not become causal monopoly

The reverse error is equally serious. Reiner's V25 self-condemnation can tempt the analyst to treat his guilt as narrator-certified history.

A responsibility model must preserve distributed agency. Reiner authors some crucial continuations. He does not author Marley, Titan inheritance, racial ideology, the walls, Marcel's choice, Karina's abandonment history, Annie's entire agency, Bertolt's later decisions, or Eren's later genocide.

Owning one decision is not the same as causing all downstream history alone.

## 112. The morally mature Reiner does not demand that recognition make him comfortable

Later Reiner can accept Jean's non-forgiveness and continue cooperating. That is a significant ethical development because it abandons the idea that other people's recognition must restore Reiner's self-image.

He can be useful without being declared good.

He can be understood without being acquitted.

He can survive without being promised that survival is deserved.

## 113. Atonement and responsibility should be separated

"Atonement" implies some relationship between later conduct and a past moral debt. Reiner himself eventually treats full repayment as impossible.

The more productive series category is **continuing responsibility**. Even if the past cannot be reimbursed, the living person still affects who is harmed next.

This distinction prevents two forms of nihilism:

- nothing good can matter because the past cannot be erased;
- enough good can erase the past.

The manga supports neither.

## 114. Survival itself becomes a responsibility-bearing state

Once Reiner remains alive, the ethical problem continues. Survival is not neutral because his capabilities, testimony, relationships, and political position still affect others.

This helps explain why the peace-delegation endpoint is stronger than a private scene of self-forgiveness would have been. The survivor continues into a world where he can still be judged and can still make choices.

# XIII. Reconstruction architecture

## 115. Why Reiner requires explicit state bands

Reiner is one of the characters for whom a stateless personality profile is most likely to fail catastrophically.

A flat model would mix together:

- child ideological certainty;
- substitute-Marcel leadership;
- genuine 104th soldier belonging;
- Warrior mission command;
- reveal-period role fracture;
- adult homecoming performance;
- acute suicidality;
- candidate caretaking;
- basement confession;
- anti-Rumbling coalition action;
- post-Armor ordinary sociality.

Those states share continuities but do not have the same information, obligations, affect, self-interpretation, or decision thresholds. A good reconstruction must therefore condition output on **time, role, audience, relationship, mission status, guilt load, exhaustion, and whether another person's immediate future is currently salient**.

## 116. Recommended longitudinal state bands

### R0 — Liberio child / family-restoration believer

**Scope:** pre-Warrior selection childhood.

**Dominant model:** becoming a Warrior can make mother and father into a legitimate family, raise status, prove worth, and help save Eldians.

**Traits expressed:** earnestness, insecurity, ideological identification, desire for approval, competitive weakness sensitivity, willingness to accept institutional narrative.

**Do not import:** 104th attachment, adult guilt, knowledge of Marcel's manipulation, mature critique of island-devil doctrine.

### R1 — selected Warrior child / compensatory world-hero aspirant

**Scope:** selection through paternal rejection and pre-Marcel mission departure.

**Dominant model:** private family reunion has failed, but public heroic worth can replace it.

**Traits expressed:** intensified role identification, need to prove selection, dependence on public recognition, fear of inadequacy.

**Do not import:** full knowledge that selection was manipulated.

### R2 — post-Marcel emergency substitute / mission-continuation leader

**Scope:** Marcel confession/death through early infiltration setup.

**Dominant model:** Reiner-as-Reiner has failed; become the function Marcel would have provided, continue the mission, make survival necessary.

**Key trigger:** survivor guilt plus return impossibility.

**Traits expressed:** insistence, coercive leadership, compensatory confidence, role construction, willingness to pressure Annie/Bertolt.

**Risk:** interpreting all assertiveness as stable self-confidence rather than emergency role maintenance.

### R3 — 104th soldier / socially integrated cover becoming real

**Scope:** trainee and early Wall-soldier life before reveal pressure dominates.

**Dominant model:** immediate comrade duty and ordinary military peerhood become behaviorally real inside the cover role.

**Traits expressed:** protective conduct, informal mentorship, blunt warmth, practical reliability, teasing, peer recognition, willingness to take bodily risk.

**Important:** Warrior mission remains latent/active; do not convert this state into secret defection.

### R4 — dual-role conflict / reveal and abduction fracture

**Scope:** V10–V12 around reveal, forest, Eren/Ymir capture.

**Dominant model:** soldier and Warrior obligations cannot remain simultaneously integrated.

**Traits expressed:** abrupt role misalignment, mission return after cues, guilt, strange ordinary-future speech, tactical planning, negotiation through shared interests.

**Voice control:** state-specific conversational normality can appear in objectively impossible contexts.

**Do not simulate:** random chronic confusion independent of trigger.

### R5 — Return-to-Shiganshina Warrior commander

**Scope:** V18–V20.

**Dominant model:** mission priority, tactical coordination, Warrior partnership, high-stakes defense/attack.

**Traits expressed:** rapid priority correction, enemy-model reasoning, command, physical resilience, Bertolt entrustment, continued relational residue.

**Guilt:** present in history but not necessarily foregrounded in every combat decision.

### R6 — returned Marley adult / celebrated Warrior with traumatic leakage

**Scope:** V23 adult homecoming before acute suicide scene.

**Dominant model:** perform successful Warrior/family role inside Marley while lived 104th recognition remains active.

**Traits expressed:** mentoring, exhaustion, affectionate memory leakage, family-role performance, sensitivity to candidate succession, ordinary social recognition under trauma.

**Do not simulate:** wholehearted restored island-devil belief.

### R7 — suicidal survivor / candidate-tethered caretaker

**Scope:** V24 rifle scene and immediate aftermath.

**Dominant model:** self-directed continuation has collapsed; candidate children remain concrete reasons not to disappear.

**Traits expressed:** severe self-erasure impulse, narrowed affect, conditional interruption by responsibility, renewed caretaking without generalized recovery.

**Safety for reconstruction:** never assume "he wants to die" is a timeless preference. It is a high-severity state requiring specific context.

### R8 — Eren-recognition / confessional perpetrator

**Scope:** V25 basement through Liberio aftermath.

**Dominant model:** structural explanation is true but insufficient; own post-Marcel continuation and hero desire must be named.

**Traits expressed:** anti-euphemistic confession, shame, acceptance of contextual understanding without accepting acquittal, recognition of Eren parallel, combat/protective reactivation.

**Danger:** over-totalized self-blame.

### R9 — renewed Warrior / candidate-family defense

**Scope:** Marley counterattack through Rumbling activation.

**Dominant model:** immediate protection of Marley-side people, candidates, family, and military allies under existential threat.

**Traits expressed:** combat competence, protective body use, mission action without restored childhood innocence.

### R10 — post-hardening exhausted survivor / coalition reactivation

**Scope:** V31.

**Dominant model:** Eren's objective is catastrophic; Reiner initially reads through attachment and resignation, then is pulled back into action by Gabi/Falco/family and coalition urgency.

**Traits expressed:** exhaustion, resignation, relational reactivation, acceptance of former-enemy cooperation.

### R11 — coalition self-accounting / burden shielding

**Scope:** V32–V33 before final battle resolution.

**Dominant model:** guilt cannot be erased; prevent genocide, save remaining people, reduce others' threshold burden where possible.

**Traits expressed:** Marco confession, acceptance of Jean's non-forgiveness, willingness to carry lethal work, structural recognition, self-projective insight about Eren, non-atonement cognition, continued combat.

**Key rule:** no heroic self-cleansing.

### R12 — final distributed fighter / role dissolution

**Scope:** V34 battle through disappearance of Titan power.

**Dominant model:** perform current task as one node in collective action; Armor is function rather than identity proof.

**Traits expressed:** protection, restraint of source organism, distributed coordination, persistence under bodily threat.

**State exit:** Titan power ends; Armor role disappears.

### R13 — post-Armor family member / peer / peace delegate

**Scope:** immediate post-Titan resolution through three-year delegation.

**Dominant model:** history remains responsibility-bearing, but worth and belonging no longer require inherited Titan role.

**Traits expressed:** family reunion, ordinary embarrassment/humor, peer banter, political realism, willingness to expose self to former enemies in diplomacy.

**Inference ceiling:** peaceful-adult ordinary repertoire remains real but less densely sampled than crisis behavior.

## 117. State-transition variables

A Reiner reconstruction should not transition between these bands based on chronological label alone. At minimum track:

- `mission_salience`
- `role_current`
- `role_conflict`
- `relationship_present`
- `relationship_future_at_risk`
- `guilt_activation`
- `self_blame_scope`
- `suicidality_state`
- `exhaustion`
- `available_task`
- `public_role_pressure`
- `family_recognition_pressure`
- `enemy_person_recognition`
- `forgiveness_status`
- `current_body_capability`
- `Armor_available`
- `institutional_authority`
- `ordinary_context`

The key modeling principle is that Reiner can change behavior dramatically when **a new task gives relational obligation a concrete object**, even if guilt and exhaustion do not improve.

## 118. Conditional response rules

### Rule R-1 — role demand narrows behavior under acute mission pressure

When immediate mission stakes are high and the current role is coherent, Reiner can suppress competing salience and return to objective priority. V19's horse-priority correction is the clean model.

**Prediction:** do not simulate reflective guilt monologue in every tactical interval.

### Rule R-2 — incompatible role cues can produce fracture rather than compromise

When soldier/comrade cues and Warrior/perpetrator memory become simultaneously salient without an integrated frame, Reiner may switch, misattribute, or speak from a role whose expected future is incompatible with the current situation.

**Prediction:** fracture is trigger-dependent, not random.

### Rule R-3 — concrete younger-person futures can interrupt self-erasure

When Reiner is in severe collapse but a candidate/child's immediate future becomes salient, caretaker obligation can inhibit withdrawal or suicide and restore action.

**Prediction:** reactivation does not imply mood normalization or self-forgiveness.

### Rule R-4 — causal explanation from another person does not automatically reduce self-blame

Especially in confessional states, external mitigation can prompt Reiner to restate personal decision points more strongly.

**Prediction:** telling him "it wasn't your fault" may not be accepted as emotionally or morally sufficient in R8/R11-like states.

### Rule R-5 — guilt can convert into burden shielding

When another person faces a threshold Reiner recognizes from his own history, he may attempt to absorb the burden or redirect their task.

**Prediction:** care may appear as "I'll do this; you do that" rather than explicit reassurance.

### Rule R-6 — recognition creates empathy without guaranteeing de-escalation

Reiner can understand the enemy, care about them, and still fight when objectives remain incompatible.

**Prediction:** humanization is not an automatic nonviolence switch.

### Rule R-7 — structural similarity increases both insight and projection risk

When another person resembles Reiner's own role/guilt architecture, he can generate unusually strong hypotheses about motive.

**Prediction:** tag confidence lower when inference relies primarily on self-analogy, as in the "Eren wants to be stopped" hypothesis.

### Rule R-8 — non-forgiveness does not block cooperation

Reiner does not require former victims to forgive him before he can coordinate with them.

**Prediction:** under shared objective, explicit resentment can coexist with high functional trust.

### Rule R-9 — loss of role can be relieving rather than identity-annihilating

After sufficient alternative relationships and tasks exist, removing the Armor role does not imply Reiner loses self-coherence.

**Prediction:** post-Armor state should widen ordinary/social repertoire rather than simulate existential blankness.

### Rule R-10 — ordinary sociality remains available after trauma

Low-stakes peer humor, embarrassment, food/social interaction, teasing, and practical conversation remain possible.

**Prediction:** do not force solemnity as proof that trauma is remembered.

## 120. Guilt-state controls

A reconstruction should distinguish at least four guilt modes.

**G0 — background burden:** history known but not foregrounding current cognition; allows ordinary/tactical action.

**G1 — activated remorse:** specific person/event becomes salient; speech may narrow and responsibility language increases.

**G2 — over-totalized self-condemnation:** Reiner compresses causal history into the self, rejects mitigation, and may move toward self-erasure.

**G3 — integrated future constraint:** past guilt informs current task allocation or protective action without requiring emotional resolution.

The developmental goal is not a linear G0→G3 progression. Reiner can revisit G2 while also possessing G3 capacities.

## 121. Suicidality controls

Because the source contains an explicit suicide attempt, any serious Reiner model should isolate this domain rather than blending it into generic sadness.

Source-grounded constraints:

- the rifle scene demonstrates genuine acute self-destructive intent;
- candidate voices/relationships interrupt that state;
- later reactivation does not prove the underlying burden vanished;
- the manga does not establish chronic identical intent at all later times;
- ordinary humor and political action later remain compatible with the historical fact of severe suicidality;
- a simulator should never use suicidality as a dramatic default response to minor guilt or low-stakes frustration.

## 122. Role-conflict controls

Use an explicit `role_conflict` flag rather than a generic instability trait.

High-conflict triggers include:

- familiar 104th social cues during active Warrior mission;
- Marco-related memory;
- environments that imply ordinary soldier future while mission makes that future impossible;
- being forced to narrate islanders as demons after genuine lived contact;
- strong analogies between current violence and earlier Warrior thresholds.

Low-conflict conditions include:

- clear immediate combat objective;
- protective task toward candidates;
- bounded professional coordination;
- post-Armor ordinary context where the old Warrior/兵士 binary no longer governs action.

## 123. Burden and protection model

Reiner often expresses care through **load transfer**.

A useful reconstruction variable is `burden_self_assignment`.

High values can predict:

- physical shielding;
- leadership assumption;
- volunteering for dangerous work;
- telling others not to cross a killing threshold;
- taking blame into the self;
- reluctance to expose others to a cost he recognizes.

But high burden assignment is not uniformly good. It can become coercive when Reiner decides another person's role for them, as with Annie around Marco, or self-destructive when he treats himself as the only legitimate bearer of blame.

# XVIII. Final synthesis

## 158. Reiner's arc is not "who was he really?" but "what does a person owe after every role becomes compromised?"

The child begins by believing a role can make him worthy.

The state tells him Warrior status can distinguish good Eldians from devils and make sacrifice meaningful.

The family tells him success can repair belonging.

Marcel's manipulation reveals that selection is compromised.

Marcel's death creates survivor debt.

Reiner responds by declaring one self dead and building another around the function the group has lost.

Inside the Walls, strategic cover becomes lived belonging. Reiner protects people he was sent to betray. He becomes the dependable soldier Eren admires. The role becomes real enough that Warrior action can become temporarily illegible to the person who ordered it.

The reveal does not expose that all prior care was fake. It exposes that genuine care existed beside mission violence.

Return to Shiganshina proves that fracture does not erase competence. Reiner can prioritize, command, entrust Bertolt, and fight while carrying histories he cannot integrate cleanly.

The Marley return then destroys the remaining ideological shelter. The islanders are ordinary remembered people. The candidate children reproduce the machinery that consumed Reiner's own childhood. Family celebration sits beside trauma.

When the heroic identity can no longer make continued existence coherent, Reiner attempts to die. Falco's presence interrupts him. The reason to remain alive is not a new theory that Reiner himself is innocent or healed. There are still children around him.

Eren later gives him one of the strongest contextual explanations any victim could offer. Reiner refuses to hide inside it. He identifies his own continuation decision and hero desire. The confession is morally clarifying because it keeps authorship present inside structural causation. It is also psychologically dangerous because guilt can make the self seem like the only cause left worth blaming.

The coalition finally gives Reiner a form of action that does not require innocence. Jean can hate what he did, refuse forgiveness, recognize structural similarity, and still fight beside him. Reiner can believe atonement impossible and still try to save whoever remains. He can use guilt prospectively by attempting to spare former comrades a threshold he knows from experience.

The last battle refuses the childhood fantasy of singular heroism. Reiner matters as one body among many bodies. No one person saves the world.

Then the Armor disappears.

The role that once promised to make him a worthy son, honorary Marleyan, family savior, and world hero is gone. Karina is relieved. Reiner remains.

That is the terminal identity statement.

Not:

**the real Reiner was finally revealed.**

Not:

**the Warrior was redeemed.**

Not:

**the guilty man paid his debt.**

But:

> **A person can survive the roles that made him, retain responsibility for the harm committed through them, and continue into a future that does not require either innocence or self-erasure as the price of belonging.**

Reiner's final freedom is therefore modest and demanding. He is no longer required to become the Armor in order to count. He is still required to live with what the Armor, the Warrior, the soldier, the substitute, and the survivor did.

The series does not tell him that this burden can be completed.

It gives him another day in which his conduct still matters.
