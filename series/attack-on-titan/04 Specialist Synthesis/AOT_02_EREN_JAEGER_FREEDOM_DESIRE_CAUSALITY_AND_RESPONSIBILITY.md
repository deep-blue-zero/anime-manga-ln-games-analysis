# 『進撃の巨人』 / *Attack on Titan*
## Eren Jaeger: Freedom, Desire, Causality, and Responsibility — V01–V34
### From anti-confinement and positive horizon to self-coercion, exterminatory jurisdiction, and non-transferable responsibility

## 0. Function, authority, and anti-duplication boundary

This document is the canonical specialist home for Eren Jaeger's full-series literary analysis once promoted from provisional publication state. Its governing question is the one assigned by the post-V34 architecture:

> **What remains continuous in Eren across radically changing knowledge states, and how should desire, future memory, coercion, care, violence, self-authorship, and responsibility be related without reducing his terminal behavior to one motive?**

The answer requires a longitudinal model rather than a verdict attached only to the Rumbling. Eren is first presented as a child who experiences secure enclosure as intolerable deprivation, then as a bereaved exterminatory subject, a human who inhabits a Titan body, a military asset under custody, an inheritor of actions he did not understand, a friend whose positive horizon is mediated by Armin, an infiltrator who understands the humanity of his enemies, a strategic deceiver, a coercive participant in recursive memory causality, a protector of selected persons, the author of population-scale extermination, and finally a frightened young man who cannot fully explain one of his own deepest desires. These states are not interchangeable. They are also not separate characters.

The controlling retrospective authority is `AOT_FULL_SERIES_CLAIM_REVISION_LEDGER.md`. The central Eren claim, `AOT-FSCR-C001`, is **STRENGTHEN**: his resistance to confinement and positive outside-world horizon predate Carla's death, but the complete motive architecture cannot be reduced to that disposition, to bereavement, or to any later single objective. `AOT-FSCR-H004` is **REVISE**: neither sovereign autonomy nor total future-memory compulsion describes his terminal agency. `AOT-FSCR-H006` is **REJECT**: his sincere protection of Historia and his friends does not generalize into a universal anti-instrumental ethic. `AOT-FSCR-M001` is **REVISE**: any reconstruction must remain stateful and multi-causal.

This document does **not** take over every adjacent question. The exact mechanics of the Attack Titan, Founder time, Dina's route past Bertolt, Ymir Fritz's causal position, and recurrence after the final tree remain primarily assigned to `AOT_19_ENDING_CAUSALITY_MEMORY_HISTORICAL_RECURRENCE_AND_POSTWAR_TIME.md`. The general philosophical account of freedom and responsibility belongs to Document 14. Comparative relationship theory belongs to Document 16. Mikasa, Armin, Reiner, Zeke, Historia/Ymir, and the political institutions receive independent specialist homes. They appear here only insofar as their relationships are constitutive of Eren's state and conduct.

### 0.1 Evidence and epistemic rule

The analysis distinguishes:

- **observed conduct** — what Eren says or does at a defined source boundary;
- **stated objective** — the outcome he publicly or privately names;
- **strategic function** — what an act appears designed to accomplish;
- **desire** — what he wants, including wants that conflict with one another;
- **causal influence or constraint** — memory, inheritance, institutions, relationships, bodily limits, and anticipated outcomes that shape the decision environment;
- **motive weight** — the relative contribution of a factor to terminal action;
- **first-cause psychology** — why the relevant desire exists at all.

The completed manga supports strong conclusions at the first five levels. It supports only moderate conclusions about exact motive weights and leaves the deepest origin of Eren's flattening desire **OPEN**. The final `何でかわかんねぇけど / やりたかったんだ` is therefore evidence of an irreducible desire layer and of Eren's incomplete self-knowledge. It is not a narrator-supplied explanation of where that desire came from.

# I. Mature formulation

## 1. Eren is continuous without being reducible to an essence

The strongest full-series formulation is:

> **Eren is continuous through a recurrent response architecture: he experiences imposed enclosure as theft of rightful participation in the world; converts deprivation into an actionable obstruction; seeks restored agency through movement, combat, knowledge, or unilateral decision; and treats the consequences of choosing as burdens he must personally carry. That architecture is repeatedly altered by attachment, bereavement, inherited memory, bodily power, institutional custody, future knowledge, and political fear. As his power and knowledge expand, the same anti-confinement grammar becomes capable of assigning whole populations the status of obstruction. His terminal action is therefore neither the execution of one timeless essence nor the mechanical performance of a fixed future. It is a multi-causal act authored under severe causal entanglement, driven in part by genuine particular care and in part by an intrinsic wish for an unobstructed world, and judged by the exterminatory jurisdiction he claims over other persons.**

Continuity here means more than a repeated slogan. Several dispositions and operating rules remain recognizable from childhood to the ending:

1. **Confinement is experienced personally before it is theorized politically.** Eren does not begin by calculating collective security. He experiences the Walls as a condition resembling livestock life (`家畜`) because safety has been purchased through a horizon he did not choose.
2. **Birth grounds standing.** His V04 claim—he seeks the world because he was born into it—returns in V30 as his answer to Zeke's euthanasia plan. Birth is not merely biological fact; it is the minimum claim against preassigned nonexistence, enclosure, and hereditary instrumentalization.
3. **Agency is action-coded.** `戦え`, `駆逐`, and `進み続ける` repeatedly turn fear, grief, or uncertainty into motion. The object and audience change, and those changes are morally decisive.
4. **Attachment is behaviorally real.** Eren repeatedly risks or subordinates himself for Armin, Mikasa, Historia, and the 104th. He wants selected people to live long lives. Later exposure of those people to danger is a contradiction inside his conduct, not proof that the earlier care was counterfeit.
5. **Failure produces intensified ownership.** After the original Levi squad dies, Eren turns uncertainty into total self-blame and imagines a better unilateral choice could have controlled the outcome. The same control-seeking response later operates on vastly larger scales.
6. **He is capable of evidence-sensitive tactical revision.** The War Hammer battle, Founder mechanics, and his deception of Zeke show a strategic reasoner, not a person moved only by undifferentiated rage.
7. **Recognition does not automatically inhibit violence.** Annie, Reiner, Falco, Ramzi, Grisha, Ymir, and eventually the entire outside world are recognized as persons in different senses. Recognition increases the moral truth of what Eren does; it does not reliably make him stop.

None of these continuities is a sufficient explanation of the Rumbling. Together they define the character architecture through which later causes operate.

## 2. The decisive terminal contradiction is not care versus no care

The ending does not reveal that Eren secretly never cared, nor that care fully explains him. The central contradiction is **genuine particular care joined to universalized jurisdiction**.

Eren refuses to let Historia and her descendants become hereditary reproductive infrastructure. He tells the 104th that they matter more than anyone and that he wants them to live long lives. He wants Mikasa and his friends to be happy after his death. He accepts becoming an enemy whom the alliance can defeat, in part so they can appear as the people who saved the remaining world. These are substantive motives and desired outcomes.

Yet he also decides that the outside population may be reduced to the obstacle whose removal secures Paradis and realizes the landscape of his childhood expectation. He knows many people beyond the island are not personally his enemies. He rescues Ramzi, apologizes to him, and continues toward the act that kills him. He leaves his favored friends free to fight him, but does not extend equivalent political standing to the people being trampled. His anti-instrumental principle is therefore **relationship- and in-group-bounded**. Its sincerity makes the contradiction sharper, not weaker.

## 3. The decisive causal contradiction is not freedom versus determination

Eren's late agency cannot be modeled as either unconstrained sovereign choice or total puppetry.

He has future-successor memory available through Grisha, affects Grisha's conduct inside a recursive memory relation, enters Founder time where past and future are experienced without ordinary separation, and moves toward an anticipated result associated with Mikasa's choice. He tells Armin that his head became disordered under the Founder's temporal condition and frames some consequences as unavoidable. The exact linear mechanism—especially the Dina/Bertolt/Carla sequence—remains elliptical.

At the same time, he lies strategically to Zeke, withholds information, selects allies, pressures Grisha with precise knowledge, asks Ymir for power through person-recognizing language, states a public stopping rule for the Rumbling, rejects proposed alternatives, admits private disappointment at the populated world, and says he probably would have flattened it even without knowing his friends would stop him. Causal entanglement constrains and helps produce the act; it does not erase the conduct through which Eren continues it.

The appropriate ethical conclusion is not metaphysical voluntarism. It is **non-transferable responsibility under causes**: future memory, inheritance, love, fear, victimization, and political danger explain and limit Eren without automatically transferring authorship of the exterminatory decision to fate, Ymir, Grisha, the outside world, or his friends.

# II. Analytic vocabulary: separating the factors commonly collapsed into “Eren's motive”

A large share of disagreement about Eren comes from treating unlike explanatory categories as rival answers to one question. The following distinctions are mandatory.

| Category | Eren-specific example | What it can establish | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Disposition** | intense resistance to confinement | a recurring tendency across states | a complete explanation of genocide |
| **Positive horizon** | Armin's book; sea; unknown world | what freedom was originally for | that the horizon remained unchanged after V21–V23 |
| **Trigger** | Carla's death | why extermination becomes an immediate object | why the terminal target becomes humanity outside the island |
| **Target object** | Titans, traitors, enemies across the sea, eventually life outside Paradis | whom/what the elimination grammar currently addresses | whether the target class is morally coherent |
| **Stated objective** | protect Paradis; destroy life outside; enable alliance heroization | an outcome Eren names | exhaustive motive transparency |
| **Relational preference** | friends live long and happily; protect Historia | desired outcomes for selected people | a universal ethic or a guarantee his means serve those outcomes |
| **Strategic method** | Liberio attack, Zeke deception, alliance opposition | how he seeks leverage or a result | why he wants the ultimate result |
| **Constraint** | Founder mechanics, future memory, limited lifespan, military custody | reduced option-space and altered cognition | automatic absolution |
| **Anticipated result** | Mikasa's choice and the end-state reached through it | a future toward which he moves | full knowledge of every intervening detail or motive |
| **Desire** | unobstructed/flattened world; staying with Mikasa and friends | what he wants even when ashamed or conflicted | its first cause or moral legitimacy |
| **Self-rationalization** | necessity, protection, “no other way” language | Eren's own organization of action | a narrator-certified exhaustive explanation |
| **Responsibility judgment** | continuing extermination after recognizing persons and alternatives | the jurisdiction he claims and harm he authors | a claim that he was causally unconditioned |

The mature model is vectorial rather than scalar. Different factors dominate at different moments, and some are mutually incompatible. Eren can want his friends to live and expose them to lethal danger; want Mikasa to be free and want her attachment to remain exclusively his; recognize Ymir as a person and ask her to enable catastrophe; understand Reiner and proceed to attack; want Paradis protected and also want to realize the empty world he imagined; move toward an outcome he has seen and still make strategic choices inside that movement.

# III. Longitudinal development I — from the cage to inherited guilt (V01–V18)

## 4. V01–V04: positive horizon, bereavement, and birth-grounded entitlement

Eren's freedom desire is already active before Carla's death. Armin's book gives the unknown world concrete positive content, and the Walls are intolerable because they make participation in that world conditional on a security order he did not authorize. The livestock comparison is therefore not yet a theory of political rights. It is an experiential judgment: a life may be safe and still be unfree if its horizon is permanently assigned by others (`AOT-BD-001`; `AOT_V01_E002-E003`).

Carla's death does not create the desire to leave. It gives that desire a target and emotional grammar. `駆逐してやる` converts loss into total elimination. This distinction matters across the entire series. Revenge is a major early cause and a durable affective channel, but it is never the whole architecture. The positive world-horizon is older; the later disappointment at humanity outside the Walls would otherwise be unintelligible.

Trost then shows that intensity does not guarantee control. Eren rescues Armin at apparent cost to himself (`AOT-BD-002`), confirming that attachment can override self-preservation before any grand political plan exists. His return from the Titan nape makes his body a problem of authorship: he is a person, a monster-shaped capacity, and a military object at once. Armin's defense reframes the question from essence to demonstrated allegiance, but Eren's strategic value immediately increases institutional custody.

The V04 declaration—`オレが!! この世に生まれたからだ!!`—is the first clear statement of his normative core. Birth is enough to claim access to the world. The sentence is not yet an argument for domination. It is a refusal of the idea that only the exceptional, authorized, or safe deserve an open horizon. The same grammar later supports his rejection of Zeke's euthanasia project and resonates with his opposition to hereditary sacrifice. It can also become dangerous when the birth-grounded claim of one subject is treated as though it cancels the birth-grounded standing of others.

## 5. V05–V08: strategic wager, trust, choice, and the human enemy

Eren's position as humanity's asset intensifies the gap between power and autonomy. At the tribunal and in the Survey Corps, he offers himself as a strategic wager—`全部オレに投資しろ`—rather than asking to be accepted through ordinary mutual understanding. This is an early form of self-instrumentalization: he secures room to act by translating his personhood into utility.

The Female Titan expedition gives Eren one of his most durable failure responses. He follows the Levi squad's collective decision; the squad dies; he retrospectively converts uncertainty into total personal fault: `オレが選択を間違えた`. The psychological structure is important. If the outcome is wholly his fault, then a better choice by him might have controlled everything. Grief thus produces not only guilt but a fantasy of unilateral counterfactual mastery (`AOT-BD-005`). Later Eren will increasingly distrust institutions and shared decision procedures, and this earlier pattern helps explain why uncertainty can push him toward absorbing the decision rather than distributing it.

Annie complicates the action grammar. Eren can fight an abstract Female Titan more readily than a recognized person. His failed transformation when recognition interrupts clear purpose shows that violence requires a target model he can act through. Yet once the model stabilizes, recognition does not create a permanent veto. This is the first of several cases in which person-recognition changes the meaning and cost of violence without guaranteeing restraint.

## 6. V09–V12: changing enemy objects, dehumanization, and the Coordinate contradiction

Reiner and Bertolt force Eren to confront human enemies whose relationship to him is not fictive. His language becomes conduct-focused and then category-denying: betrayal is `気持ち悪い`; the perpetrators become `ただの人殺し`; he declares them no longer human. The move reveals both moral perception and defensive simplification. Eren correctly identifies atrocity and deception, then tries to make lethal action psychologically clearer by withdrawing moral membership from the perpetrators.

The series refuses to let that withdrawal settle the matter. Reiner's soldier conduct, Bertolt's recognition of Eren's suffering, Ymir's divided obligations, and the existence of an external homeland keep the enemy human. The target of `駆逐` is no longer a natural species category. It is increasingly a political and relational category imposed on persons.

The Coordinate compounds the contradiction. The character who experiences freedom as resistance to imposed bodily control acquires a capacity to direct other bodies. At V12 this remains poorly understood and appears under emergency pressure. Retrospectively, however, it establishes the structural danger at the center of Eren's arc: exceptional power gives him the means to convert his own model of obstruction into commands imposed on others.

The Mikasa promise in the same volume—`何度でも巻いてやる`—should not be treated as a decorative counterpoint. It shows Eren's capacity for concrete, repeatable care at the moment of despair. His later relation to Mikasa cannot be understood if this promise is dismissed as false. Nor can the promise pre-authorize Mikasa's future or erase her right to oppose him.

## 7. V13–V18: exchangeable vessel, inherited guilt, self-negation, and restored plural agency

The Uprising turns Eren's body from heroic exception into hereditary office. The state and Reiss system treat him as a replaceable vessel. He learns that Grisha arranged the transfer by making the child Eren consume him, an act Eren neither understood nor consented to. The result is a genuine inheritance wound: his current power and existence are causally connected to violence performed through his body before he could author it.

Under the Reiss chapel revelation, Eren's birth-based claim collapses into its negative. If his existence caused deaths and deprived humanity of the “right” inheritor, perhaps he should not exist; he asks to be consumed. This is not a discontinuity proving the earlier freedom ethic false. It shows that his self-worth is highly conditional on the causal model he currently accepts. The same subject who grounds standing in birth can invert that principle into ontological guilt when he believes his existence has illegitimately occupied the world (`AOT-BD-011`).

Historia's refusal matters because she rejects the abstract demand that Eren erase himself. Her particular loyalty and her own rejection of inherited role return minimal agency to him. Eren then uses the “Armor” material to protect others, repurposing inherited Titan substance from cage and office into chosen shelter. The scene does not purify the power; it shows that inherited capacity can be reauthored.

By V18, Armin again supplies the positive horizon that Eren's punitive drive had narrowed. Eren recalls recognizing his own `不自由` through the existence of the outside world, then shifts from singular heroic grammar to `オレ達ならできる`. This is a high-value counterexample to accounts that make Eren's deepest motivation only hatred. Shared freedom and plural agency can reactivate him. The later tragedy lies partly in the loss of that plural horizon.

# IV. Longitudinal development II — recognition, outside society, and political rupture (V19–V27)

## 8. V19–V22: lethal opposition, Armin's horizon, the archive, and the sea question

The Shiganshina battle tests whether recognition has become restraint. It has not. Eren fights and kills former comrades because the missions remain incompatible. His action is not the blind rage of Trost: he participates in a coordinated plan, accepts temporary concealment while Armin burns, and executes the decoy/capture sequence rather than impulsively abandoning the larger objective (`AOT-BD-033`). Attachment is therefore neither absent nor sovereign. Trust in Armin's agency can inhibit direct rescue when rescue would destroy Armin's chosen contribution.

The serum conflict then exposes how Eren's attachment recruits argument. He insists on Armin not only because Armin is beloved but because Armin's imagination and strategic record are public goods. He invokes the sea and admits that his own life had narrowed into revenge and hatred while Armin retained the dream (`AOT-BD-035`; `AOT_V21_E003`, `AOT_V21_E006`). This admission matters for terminal interpretation. Eren knows from within that a punitive objective can displace a positive horizon. Later claims of necessity therefore do not emerge from a character who has never distinguished those motivational forms.

The basement archive transforms the outside from unknown horizon into populated history. Eren receives Grisha's memories, the existence of Marley, Dina's royal blood, the Attack Titan inheritance, and a world in which Eldians are both persecuted and historically implicated in empire. The information increases his causal and political burden. It does not tell him one unambiguous thing to do.

Eren's response to the royal-blood hypothesis is selective nondisclosure. He connects contact with Dina's Titan to the Coordinate but withholds the hypothesis because disclosure could expose Historia to forced Titanization (`AOT-BD-045`). Protection here is real and paternalistic at once. Eren makes a high-impact informational decision for Historia without asking her, because he forecasts irreversible institutional exploitation. The act anticipates the later pattern in which care becomes unilateral jurisdiction.

At the sea, Armin's positive horizon and Eren's obstruction model separate visibly. Eren recognizes the landscape from inherited memory, points beyond it to enemies, and asks whether killing them all would make `オレ達` free. The Japanese sentence ends as an interrogative (`自由になれるのか?`). At the V22 boundary it is not yet a settled policy declaration. Retrospectively it identifies the mechanism by which his freedom cognition relocates confinement: the Wall ceases to be the obstruction; the inhabited world beyond the sea becomes the new boundary.

## 9. V23–V24: the outside becomes ordinary, and self-authorship loses moral innocence

Marley is not merely the revelation that Eren's enemies have reasons. It gives the outside world ordinary density: children compete, families eat together, candidates tease one another, soldiers return home, and imperial power operates through differentiated institutions. Eren's later destruction is therefore not directed at an abstract map. The sequential structure makes him—and the reader—encounter persons before the terminal exterminatory claim is complete.

The Liberio infiltration displays adult Eren's expanded social and strategic range. With Falco, he can express concern that a younger person live long, infer his attachment to Gabi, and use that understanding to recruit him as an unwitting courier (`AOT-BD-072`). Benevolence, accurate empathy, and instrumental deception coexist in one conversation. This pattern should govern interpretation of later relationships: care does not guarantee transparency, and deception does not prove care unreal.

Eren's description of hell supplies a mature self-authorship vocabulary. Some are pushed by others or by their environment; others push their own backs. Beyond either path may lie hope or a deeper hell. The point is not that chosen hell is good. It is that choosing changes ownership while leaving moral valence uncertain (`AOT-BD-073`). This is one of the clearest textual warnings against treating Eren's authenticity as justification.

V24 also retrospectively complicates `進み続ける`. Reiner's training-era encouragement contributes socially to Eren's continuation language without originating the child's earlier drive. The lexicon is inherited and relationally reinforced, but not reducible to one transmitter. Eren's later self-command is therefore both recognizably his and historically entangled.

## 10. V25–V26: Reiner recognition, Liberio, tactical intelligence, and self-command

The basement conversation with Reiner is the strongest pre-Rumbling evidence against a model in which Eren must dehumanize every victim before acting. He says the people across the sea and inside the Walls are the same. He understands Reiner as an indoctrinated child and recognizes that Reiner's motives included both imposed mission and personal desire. Reiner, in turn, refuses total structural exculpation.

The transformation still follows. Recognition is not exposed as false; it is shown to be insufficient. Eren's objective remains incompatible with Reiner's world, and he proceeds while knowing the people around the stage are persons. This is a more severe ethical state than early Titan hatred because the protective fiction of nonpersonhood has weakened (`AOT-BD-078`; `AOT_V25_E007-E015`).

Liberio also demonstrates that Eren's categorical affect does not erase instrumental intelligence. He infers the War Hammer's remote-body mechanism from anomaly, follows the cable, extracts the bearer, tests the crystal, and later repurposes Porco's Jaw as an affordance when direct force fails (`AOT-BD-080`, `AOT-BD-090`). He is capable of rapid model revision under pressure. Any account that calls the terminal action mere unreasoning rage must explain this sustained strategic competence.

The mirror sequence in V26 transforms `戦え` into self-command: `戦わなければ / 勝てない / 戦え / 戦え`. The phrase once addressed Mikasa under immediate threat; now Eren directs it inward. This is not simply resolve. It is a technique of self-coercion. Doubt, grief, and conflict are converted into an imperative shell that keeps the actor moving. The shell may be sincere and psychologically necessary while also suppressing revisability.

Victory returns Eren to custody. He has acted outside command, forced his allies into extraction, and converted his strategic indispensability into leverage. The moral problem is no longer only whether his attack had strategic effects. It is whether his exceptional power grants him authority to make the institution and his friends bear consequences they did not authorize.

## 11. V27: Historia, the 104th, and the sovereignty break

V27 concentrates the strongest evidence for Eren's particular care. He rejects a plan that would make Historia bear children `家畜みてぇに`, shorten her life, and turn her descendants into hereditary military infrastructure (`AOT-BD-102`). The earlier livestock term returns in a bodily and generational form. Eren recognizes in Historia's proposed future the same structure he hated in the Walls: safety purchased through lives preassigned by others.

His objection remains active even when Historia is willing to accept burden. This is both morally serious and paternalistic. Eren refuses to treat consent given under existential pressure as sufficient, but he also reserves for himself the authority to decide which sacrifice she may choose. The later V32 memory confirms that the protection is not a retrospective fabrication.

The railroad conversation is equally direct: the 104th matter more to him than anyone, and he wants them to live long lives (`AOT-BD-105`; `AOT_V27_E013-E014`). Later accounts that explain the entire Rumbling as protecting his friends capture one real motive. They fail when they convert it into the only motive or assume his chosen means reliably serve it. Eren will involve those same friends in an unknown-survival battle, later admit that he did not know whether they would live, and still want their happiness.

The confrontation with Hange and the prison escape show how care, urgency, and sovereignty become fused. Eren knows the military cannot physically contain him and demands another path. When the institution cannot supply one he accepts, he converts latent power into open defection (`AOT-BD-103`, `AOT-BD-114`). The immediate order—locate Zeke—remains narrower than the terminal ideology. Yet the constitutional break is already decisive: Eren no longer recognizes the military as having final authority over the power only he can activate.

At the V27 boundary, the legitimate open question was the relative weight of disposition, political necessity, memory, Zeke/Yelena influence, chosen commitment, and self-coercion. Later volumes answer some components and make the full architecture more complex rather than simpler.

# V. Longitudinal development III — self-certification, recursive causality, and the Rumbling (V28–V34)

## 12. V28: “I am free” as self-certification and denial of reciprocal agency

The table confrontation is not reliable motive testimony, but it is reliable evidence of Eren's current interaction design. He wounds his hand to control the room through latent transformation threat, asserts `オレは自由だ` and `自由意志`, and redescribes Armin and Mikasa as unfree through inherited memory or Ackerman biology (`AOT-BD-121`). The later text supplies counterevidence to those diagnoses: Zeke describes Mikasa's behavior as love rather than host compulsion, and Armin's identity cannot be reduced to Bertolt's memories.

Eren's error is not merely factual. The scene reveals a structural asymmetry in his freedom claim. His own actions are certified as autonomous from inside his conviction; the objections of the two people who know him best are pathologized as products of forces they cannot recognize. He claims privileged access to his own authorship while denying theirs.

The physical domination of Armin and emotional attack on Mikasa also cannot be treated as perfectly optimized theater. V34 confirms a distancing intention, but Eren says he was desperate, partly moved by momentum, and did not execute the performance cleanly. The cruelty has strategic function and excess. This matters because a pure secret-plan reading turns every harmful act into frictionless benevolent technique and erases the character's own acknowledgment that he acted badly.

## 13. V29–V30: Zeke deception, birth against euthanasia, Grisha coercion, and Ymir

V29 presents Eren as Zeke's apparent ideological and fraternal partner. He says `オレはやる`, uses `兄さん`, and links the euthanasia project to continued movement. At that source boundary, sincerity remains open. The relationship and behavior ledgers correctly preserve the utterance without converting it into settled alignment.

V30 adjudicates the presentation as strategic deception. At the Founder activation point Eren says he never accepted the euthanasia plan, that he matched Zeke's story to reach this place, and that the project is unacceptable because he was born into the world (`AOT-BD-152`; `AOT_V30_E017-E018`). The V04 birth claim returns after twenty-six volumes as an anti-erasure constraint. Eren's autonomy is real enough to deceive the person who believed he uniquely understood him.

The memory tour then makes that autonomy causally entangled. Eren pressures Grisha to kill the Reiss family by invoking Faye, Dina, Kruger, the Restorationists, and the mission Grisha began. He uses an accurate model of his father's wounds as leverage (`AOT-BD-153`; `AOT_V30_E029-E031`). Grisha's decision remains Grisha's in one sense—he performs the act—but Eren is materially involved in producing it through the future-memory chain. The inherited father-son relation becomes recursively authored from both directions.

Eren later says that four years earlier he saw a future-self memory through Grisha—`あの景色`. The statement confirms anticipatory knowledge without specifying that he saw every step, every casualty, or one exhaustive motive. Memory is selective evidence, not omniscience.

The Ymir encounter brings Eren's personhood language and catastrophic project together. Against Zeke's royal command, he tells Ymir she is neither slave nor god but `ただの人`, that she need obey no one, and that she may decide (`お前が決めていい`). This is relationally different from royal compulsion. It also cannot be generalized into innocence. Eren asks the recognized chooser to lend him power to end the world (`AOT-BD-160`; `AOT-RB-137`). Treating a person as an agent does not validate the outcome requested, and the text leaves Ymir's independent motive incompletely narrated.

## 14. V31–V32: explicit exterminatory stopping rule and bounded anti-instrumental care

Once the Rumbling begins, Eren's public broadcast removes ambiguity about the operational objective. He identifies himself, states the purpose of protecting Paradis and its people, rejects the outside world's wish for the island's destruction, and declares that the Wall Titans will trample the lands beyond the island until the life there is eliminated (`AOT-BD-161`; `AOT_V31_E008-E010`). This is not merely deterrent demonstration language. The stopping rule is exterminatory.

The Marley memory embedded in V31 preserves a prior moment of openness. Eren asks Mikasa what he is to her. The question is not evidence that one different answer would certainly have changed history; the manga does not establish that counterfactual. It does establish that Eren solicits a relational definition during a period when he already possesses future knowledge and is approaching his break with the diplomatic horizon. His later instruction that the scarf be discarded and the V34 cabin request to forget him are distancing acts that coexist with his desire to remain uniquely loved.

V32 places the Rumbling objective beside Historia's resistance. Eren tells Floch `世界を滅ぼす`. Historia objects that most people outside are not personally enemies and invokes the moral analogy to Carla. Eren retains the exterminatory solution. In the same memory sequence, he refuses Historia's hereditary sacrifice even when she says she can accept the reliable survival method (`お前がよくても…オレは違う`).

This is the decisive evidence for rejecting `AOT-FSCR-H006`. Eren's anti-instrumentalization is sincere and bounded. He will not permit a person he loves to become reproductive and military infrastructure; he will permit innumerable other persons, including innocents he recognizes as such, to be killed for the island and his desired result. The contradiction is not “hypocrisy” if that word implies no real care. It is **partial care combined with universal jurisdiction over those outside the protected circle**.

Zeke's explanation that Mikasa's attachment is love rather than biological compulsion further weakens Eren's table diagnosis. Eren responds by saying his friends' lives will continue after his death and that he wants them to live long and happily (`AOT-BD-188`). Desired outcome and chosen means remain separate variables.

## 15. V33: Ramzi, disappointment, empty-horizon freedom, and adversarial permission

The Ramzi sequence is the strongest demonstration that empathy and remorse survive inside Eren's exterminatory program. He first walks away, returns to rescue Ramzi from immediate violence, and later apologizes to him while knowing the boy lies within the future catastrophe. Eren's tears are not exculpatory and should not be dismissed as fake. They show that local person-recognition and affective remorse are behaviorally real but insufficient to stop the larger project (`AOT-BD-190`; `AOT-RB-165`).

The private confession adds the motive layer that every purely strategic interpretation must confront: when Eren learned humanity survived beyond the Walls, he was disappointed—`ガッカリした`. The populated world violated the horizon he had imagined with Armin. He wanted it all to disappear (`AOT-BD-191`; `AOT_V33_E007`). This does not replace Paradis protection, friend protection, anti-sacrifice, future knowledge, or alliance heroization. It prevents those factors from exhausting the motive.

The visual sequence makes the ethical distortion concrete. The actual Rumbling crushes Ramzi while child Eren experiences the cloudscape as `自由だ`. The empty horizon is produced by suppressing the victims whose bodies make it empty. The image is not proof that adult Eren lacks awareness—his apology proves the opposite. It externalizes how one desire can organize perception: freedom is experienced as unobstructed openness by bracketing the persons treated as obstruction.

In Paths, Eren tells the coalition that he will not stop, will not entrust the island's future to chance, and will not take away their freedom to oppose him. Their freedoms will collide (`AOT-BD-200`; `AOT_V33_E035-E040`). This is genuine permission in a narrow sense: he leaves their powers intact and accepts that they may kill him. It is not reciprocal political freedom. The coalition receives adversarial agency because they are his selected intimates and because their resistance participates in the result toward which he moves. The populations underfoot receive no comparable standing.

## 16. V34: cabin release, lethal opposition, final self-accounting, and incomplete self-knowledge

The cabin sequence constructs a private-life counterfactual or Paths experience whose exact ontological status remains interpretation-sensitive. Its relational content is clearer. Eren and Mikasa live in seclusion after she gives a different answer; Eren says they abandoned everything and asks her to promise that after he dies she will discard the scarf and forget him. The request recognizes a future beyond him while also attempting to author how she will remember.

Mikasa does not obey that memory directive. She retains the scarf, kills Eren when stopping him requires it, kisses him, grieves, buries him, and continues remembering. Her action is the terminal proof that love and lethal refusal are independent variables. For Eren analysis, the importance is that his attempt at release does not settle her freedom; she authors the form of attachment that survives him.

The final Armin conversation strips away several public shells without producing one transparent essence.

First, Eren explains the table cruelty as a desperate attempt to push his friends away so that they would oppose him. He also says he acted partly through momentum and admits it was wrong (`AOT-BD-213`; primary V34 p0190). The political design is real but imperfect.

Second, he states that the Rumbling will kill roughly eighty percent of humanity and that the survivors will be unable to retaliate immediately. He affirms the design in which his friends defeat him and become the people who saved the remaining world (V34 p0191–p0192). This is an anticipated strategic result, not proof that the Rumbling was only staged defeat.

Third, he explains that Ymir continued serving King Fritz through an attachment he calls love, that he cannot fully understand the depth of her heart, and that Mikasa's choice is tied to Ymir's release. Eren says he does not know exactly what Mikasa will do but knows the result of her choice and has moved toward it. The distinction between knowing a result and knowing every act or motive must remain intact (V34 p0193–p0197).

Fourth, he admits that he killed eighty percent, involved his friends in battle without knowing whether they would survive, and moved toward the result. He describes Founder time as a condition in which past and future do not exist separately for him and says his head became disordered. The Dina/Bertolt/Carla implication follows in fragmented speech. The page makes Eren causally implicated; it does not provide a complete linear mechanism or demonstrate that every decision was externally compelled (V34 p0198–p0199).

Fifth, the categorical shell collapses around Mikasa and death. Eren initially tells Armin he does not know whether Mikasa can forget him, then reacts violently when Armin suggests she may find another man. He wants her to think of him for years, is ashamed of the wish, says he wants her happiness, and admits he does not want to die and wants to remain with Mikasa and everyone (`AOT-BD-215`; V34 p0201–p0204). Possessiveness and altruistic release coexist. Neither cancels the other.

Finally, Eren says that even if he had not known his friends would stop him, he thinks he still would have flattened the world. Asked why, he answers that he does not know, but wanted to do it—`何でかわかんねぇけど / やりたかったんだ` (`AOT-BD-214`; V34 p0205–p0207). The statement rejects a purely self-sacrificial hero plan. It also refuses complete first-cause closure. Eren knows the desire as his; he cannot fully explain its origin.

Armin condemns the act as the worst error, shares future burden, and does not convert understanding into absolution. The friendship carries truth, anger, grief, and responsibility simultaneously. Eren entrusts the future to Armin, but he cannot transfer the authorship of what he has done.

# VI. The terminal motive architecture

## 17. No single motive is fake; no single motive is sufficient

The completed manga supports at least eight terminal motive/constraint vectors. They should be modeled separately before any account attempts to rank them.

### 17.1 Intrinsic anti-obstruction and flattening desire

The oldest stable disposition is resistance to confinement. Its positive early object is the unknown world; its dangerous terminal mutation is the desire for a world matching the unobstructed horizon imagined from Armin's book. V33's `ガッカリした` and V34's `やりたかったんだ` establish that the Rumbling is not only a means to security or defeat. Eren wants the flattened result in a way he cannot fully derive for Armin.

This vector is **high-confidence as a terminal desire** and **open as first-cause psychology**. The text does not tell us whether the desire should be explained as temperament, the Attack Titan inheritance, trauma, future-memory feedback, the visual ideal of the book, a destructive response to disappointed expectation, or some irreducible combination. “He was born this way” is too complete. So is “the future forced the desire into him.”

### 17.2 Paradis and population protection

Eren explicitly states that his purpose is protecting the island and its people from the outside world's exterminatory wish. The strategic situation is real: Paradis faces hostile states, technological change, and limited time. This factor cannot be dismissed as propaganda merely because other motives exist.

It also cannot settle the legitimacy or scope of the response. Protecting Paradis explains why an outside threat matters. It does not by itself explain the choice to continue until life beyond the island is eliminated, especially after Eren recognizes innocent persons and alternative partial strategies. Protection becomes exterminatory jurisdiction when the survival of one population is treated as sufficient warrant to erase all others.

### 17.3 Protection of selected friends and alliance heroization

Eren wants the 104th to live long lives. He wants Mikasa and Armin to survive. He anticipates a result in which the alliance defeats him and is treated as humanity's saviors. V34 confirms this political design.

The vector is genuine and internally unstable. Eren does not know whether those friends will survive the battle he imposes on them. He kills Hange indirectly through the conditions of the Rumbling, exposes the others to lethal combat, and cannot guarantee the postwar political result. “For his friends” therefore names a motive and anticipated benefit, not a morally or causally complete plan.

### 17.4 Historia and rejection of hereditary-body sacrifice

Eren's refusal of the fifty-year plan is especially strong because it binds childhood anti-livestock language to a specific bodily future: Historia and descendants would be made reproductive instruments and consumed through Titan succession. His protection begins as nondisclosure at V22 and becomes explicit refusal at V27/V32.

This vector helps explain why Eren rejects strategies others call necessary. It also reveals the boundedness of his ethic. He will not accept coercive sacrifice imposed on Historia, but accepts exterminatory sacrifice imposed on people outside the intimate/in-group boundary. The local care remains real; universalization is rejected.

### 17.5 Ending Titan power through the result associated with Mikasa

Eren moves toward a future result connected to Mikasa's choice, and Titan power ends after that choice. The final conversation indicates that this endpoint matters to the path he follows. Yet Eren says he does not know exactly what Mikasa will do, and the causal relation among Ymir, Mikasa, Eren, and the end of Titan power remains partly assigned to the ending/causality specialist.

This vector should therefore be phrased carefully: **Eren moves toward an anticipated result linked to Mikasa's choice and the terminal transformation of the Titan system**. It should not be inflated into omniscient certainty that he scripted every action from the beginning.

### 17.6 Future-memory and Founder constraint

Future knowledge changes the available decision environment. Eren sees selected future-self memory through Grisha, knows at least some endpoint information, and later experiences Founder time as nonsequential. These conditions can create self-fulfilling pressure: actions are taken because a result has been seen, and the seen result exists because those actions occur.

Constraint is not one motive among others in the same sense. It shapes how all motives are processed. It can reduce perceived alternatives, disrupt temporal cognition, and make “moving toward” an anticipated result feel unavoidable. It does not erase the evidence of strategic selection, deception, preference, and refusal inside the path.

### 17.7 Self-coercion and the advance imperative

`戦え` and `進み続ける` are not simply values. They are methods for converting uncertainty into action. Eren repeatedly commands himself, treats hesitation as a threat to agency, and uses forward motion to prevent grief or doubt from stopping him. The method becomes increasingly recursive: the person who values freedom disciplines himself into continuing the path he recognizes as hell.

This vector helps explain how contradictory motives can coexist without producing revision. Eren does not need to resolve every contradiction before acting; the imperative shell keeps action ahead of reflective integration.

### 17.8 Ordinary attachment, jealousy, shame, and fear of death

The terminal disclosure restores motives that public readings often discard as too ordinary: Eren wants to live, remain with Mikasa and his friends, and preserve a unique place in Mikasa's memory. He is jealous, possessive, ashamed, frightened, and capable of wishing for her happiness at the same time.

These desires do not excuse the Rumbling and are not comic debris beneath the “real” philosophical Eren. They demonstrate that the exterminatory author never becomes a purified ideological machine. His categorical public voice and his vulnerable private voice are state-dependent registers of one person.

## 18. Relative weighting: what can and cannot be concluded

The evidence supports the following qualitative weighting without pretending to mathematical precision:

| Vector | Warranted status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-confinement / unobstructed horizon | **constitutive continuity; terminally decisive** | predates Carla; returns in V33–V34 private motive evidence |
| Paradis protection | **explicit major objective** | named in Rumbling broadcast and private planning |
| Selected-person protection | **explicit major relational preference and strategic design** | V27, V32, V34 |
| Historia / anti-hereditary sacrifice | **explicit major constraint on accepted alternatives** | V22, V27, V32 |
| Alliance heroization / anticipated defeat | **explicit strategic result** | V34 |
| Mikasa-linked/Titan-end endpoint | **material anticipated result; mechanism bounded** | V34; exact Ymir mechanics open |
| Future-memory / Founder temporality | **major constraint and causal environment** | V30, V34; not exhaustive compulsion |
| Self-coercive advance grammar | **major process mechanism** | V24, V26, V29–V30 |
| Revenge for Carla | **major early transformation; insufficient terminal explanation** | V01 onward; Eren later recognizes its narrowing |
| Personal death fear / jealousy / desire for continued life | **explicit terminal private motive-state** | V34 |

The deepest open issue is not whether all these factors exist. It is how they are weighted at each decision point and why the flattening desire exists at all. `AOT-FSCR-O27-01` therefore remains revised rather than closed: the final model is explicitly multi-causal, while exact weights and first cause remain open.

# VII. Causality, memory, and agency without metaphysical shortcuts

## 19. Three temporal mechanisms must not be conflated

Eren's late causal condition involves at least three distinguishable layers:

1. **Titan-memory inheritance:** memories from predecessors can shape present identity and knowledge.
2. **Attack Titan future-successor memory:** Grisha can receive selected memory from a future inheritor; Eren sees future-self memory through Grisha.
3. **Founder/Paths temporal condition:** after full Founder access, Eren describes past and future as not separately present to him.

These layers operate differently. Ordinary inherited memory can influence without determining. Future-successor memory creates recursive information flow. Founder time alters cognition and causal framing at the terminal stage. A statement about one layer must not be generalized automatically to all earlier action.

## 20. Eren and Grisha: recursive influence is real, total authorship is not established

The Reiss chapel memory demonstrates that Eren can affect Grisha through the future-memory relation. He does not merely witness Grisha's decision. He pressures him with intimate knowledge and mission debt, telling him to stand and continue. Grisha then acts and later expresses horror, embraces Zeke, and asks him to stop Eren.

The scene resists two symmetrical simplifications. “Grisha chose freely, so Eren is irrelevant” ignores the intervention. “Eren controlled Grisha like a puppet” ignores Grisha's own history, judgment, bodily action, and later response. The better description is **recursive co-authorship under asymmetrical informational leverage**. Eren materially helps produce the inherited violence that helped form him.

## 21. Seen future is not complete omniscience

Eren's `あの景色` statement confirms selected future knowledge. It does not specify a complete film of events. Later uncertainty—about whether friends survive, about exactly what Mikasa will do, about his own motive's origin—demonstrates limits.

Selective future memory can still exert enormous pressure. If an endpoint is known without all intermediate causes, present action may be interpreted through the endpoint and alternatives may become difficult to imagine. But the text repeatedly shows Eren testing, deceiving, asking, choosing allies, and articulating objectives. The future affects deliberation; it does not make deliberation absent.

## 22. Founder temporal collapse and the Dina implication

In V34 Eren says the Founder's power disordered his head because past and future do not exist separately but simultaneously. The fragmented Bertolt/Dina statement makes him causally implicated in the route that preserved Bertolt and sent Dina toward Carla. The page is intentionally compressed and emotionally destabilizing.

The responsible inference is limited:

- Eren is not merely a passive recipient of the Carla event in the final causal picture.
- The Founder condition entangles him with an event that originally supplied his revenge object.
- The manga does not supply a full mechanism specifying the scope, timing, alternatives, or exact intentional structure of this intervention.

Document 19 must own the final mechanism adjudication. For this character monograph, the literary consequence is enough: Eren's motive architecture loops through one of its own formative traumas. Cause, desire, and responsibility can no longer be arranged as a simple line from victimization to response.

## 23. Why “slave to freedom” is illuminating but incomplete

The phrase captures a real paradox. Eren turns freedom into an imperative that disciplines him. He keeps advancing through a path he calls hell, follows an anticipated result, and experiences the Founder condition as constraining. His self-certification can become dependence on the identity of the person who always moves forward.

Yet “slave to freedom” becomes misleading when used as total explanation. Eren is not shown as lacking preferences, strategic competence, deception, or the capacity to reject another future. He refuses Zeke's program precisely because it violates his birth-grounded value. He selects the Rumbling's stopping rule and asks Ymir for power. He admits wanting the result.

The phrase is strongest as a description of **self-coercive identity structure**, not as a metaphysical acquittal.

# VIII. Care, recognition, and the limits of Eren's relational ethics

## 24. Armin: positive horizon, trusted agency, and terminal condemnation

Armin is constitutive of Eren's freedom before he is an opponent of Eren's solution. The book gives Eren a positive world beyond the Walls. Armin's continued capacity to dream later exposes how revenge narrowed Eren. During Shiganshina, Eren trusts Armin's chosen strategy enough not to rescue him prematurely, then fights for his life at the serum decision.

The sea marks their divergence: Armin receives an expanded world; Eren receives relocated obstruction. This is not a clean split between idealism and realism. Armin later commits severe violence and command decisions. The difference is that Armin's imagination remains capable of treating unobserved persons and alternatives as live, while Eren increasingly converts uncertainty into unilateral closure.

V34 does not reconcile their ethics by revealing that Armin secretly agrees. Armin presses Eren, condemns the massacre, hits him over Mikasa, shares grief, and accepts responsibility for the future. Their intimacy makes truthful condemnation possible. Eren entrusts the future to the friend whose horizon he both inherited and destroyed.

## 25. Mikasa: home, contested autonomy, release, and possessiveness

Eren's childhood rescue gives Mikasa a survival imperative and a new home, but their relationship is never exhausted by either event. He protects her, resents forms of protection he experiences as control, promises to wrap the scarf again, asks what he is to her, later pathologizes her attachment, and finally attempts to release her from memory while privately wanting her devotion to continue.

The contradiction is not evidence that one side is false. Eren wants Mikasa free from a life organized entirely around him and wants to remain irreplaceable to her. His cabin request—discard the scarf, forget him—is an attempted authorship of her post-Eren life. Her refusal to follow that instruction, combined with her lethal opposition, makes her agency independent of both obedience and renunciation.

For Eren, Mikasa is therefore not merely a protected person or romantic endpoint. She is the relationship in which his difficulty accepting another person's autonomous attachment becomes most visible. He seeks causal explanation for her love, misdescribes it as compulsion, receives counterevidence, asks for release, and still cannot relinquish possessive desire.

## 26. Historia: bodily autonomy and paternalistic protection

Historia activates Eren's anti-instrumental response more clearly than any political abstraction. He withholds the royal-blood hypothesis, rejects hereditary reproduction and consumption, and refuses to treat her willingness to bear burden as sufficient. He sees the plan as a return to livestock existence.

Historia also disproves any idea that protection purchases agreement. She tells Eren the world-destruction plan is wrong and that silence would violate the proud life she chose. Their relationship demonstrates two truths at once: Eren's care is genuine, and the protected person retains authority to oppose the protector's politics.

The monograph therefore rejects both “Historia is only an excuse” and “Historia proves Eren's universal bodily-autonomy ethic.” She is a central relational constraint on what he will accept for his circle. The boundary of that circle is the ethical problem.

## 27. Reiner: recognition of shared formation without reconciliation

Reiner is Eren's clearest mirror because both inherit enemy structures, continue under self-authored motives, and understand themselves through advance language. Eren's basement recognition is unusually complete: he understands child indoctrination, environmental pressure, ordinary life among supposed devils, and Reiner's personal motive.

The attack proceeds because recognition does not settle jurisdiction, objective, or responsibility. Reiner's confession also matters: structural explanation does not remove his own continuation. Eren accepts that principle for Reiner before the ending applies it to him.

The mirror is not identity. Reiner seeks death and is tethered back by responsibility; Eren moves toward a desired catastrophic horizon and fears death when it arrives. Their relation exposes shared causal entanglement and divergent terminal authorship.

## 28. Zeke: brotherhood as strategic misrecognition

Zeke interprets Eren through his own childhood: another son damaged by Grisha who must be rescued into the euthanasia solution. His affection and trust are real, but they preserve Eren's autonomy only inside an assumed destination. Eren exploits that model through apparent ideological assent and brother address.

The deception shows Eren's agency but not emotional emptiness. Tactical warmth and relationship affect need not be identical to ideological alignment. What is clear is that Zeke's claim to be Eren's privileged understander fails. The younger brother's birth-grounded value directly rejects the older brother's salvation through nonbirth.

## 29. Grisha: inherited mission, filial knowledge, and coercive reversal

Eren is initially the child whose life and body are organized by Grisha's decisions. The memory loop reverses the asymmetry: the son becomes the future observer who pressures the father to complete the massacre. Filial knowledge becomes coercive leverage.

This reversal is central to Eren's responsibility because it prevents him from locating all inherited violence safely in the past. He does not merely carry Grisha's story; he helps produce one of its decisive acts. At the same time, the act remains embedded in Grisha's own losses and mission. Their relation is a causal knot, not a one-way chain of blame.

## 30. Ymir Fritz: person-recognition without ethical guarantee

Eren's address to Ymir is one of his most compelling person-recognizing acts. He rejects the slave/god binary, calls her a person, and tells her she may decide. It breaks with Zeke's reliance on royal command and speaks directly to her imposed role.

But recognition becomes recruitment. Eren wants the power she mediates. The language of choice cannot be used to absolve him or to infer that Ymir independently authorizes every consequence with full political understanding. The interaction demonstrates a recurrent Eren pattern at maximal scale: he can recognize another's agency while orienting that agency toward his own catastrophic objective.

## 31. Falco and Ramzi: care without honesty, empathy without restraint

Falco and Ramzi are the terminal minor-character tests of Eren's relational range. With Falco, Eren is observant, encouraging, and deceptive. With Ramzi, he is rescuing, remorseful, and lethal through the larger program. Neither child is a mere symbol. Each shows that local care can survive while being subordinated to strategic or horizon-level desire.

These cases prohibit the claim that Eren kills only because he cannot see people. He sees them. The ethical failure is that seeing does not become a limit on the jurisdiction he claims.

# IX. Japanese voice, vocabulary, and the direction of the imperative

## 32. The direct shell and its state-dependent fractures

Eren's default Japanese register is direct, low-hedge, first-person, and action-oriented. `オレ` dominates; `オレ達` emerges when agency becomes shared. `お前` can be intimate, confrontational, or coercive depending on relationship and scene. The direct shell makes his declarations memorable, but it should not be mistaken for stable self-transparency.

His speech becomes interrogative or explicitly uncertain at high-value boundaries:

- the sea question ends in `自由になれるのか?`;
- private Ramzi confession includes tears and disappointed self-disclosure;
- V34 uses `わかんねぇ`, `と思う`, shame, hesitation, and `死にたくねぇ`.

The terminal register does not reveal a different “real Eren” beneath a wholly fake public one. It reveals dimensions that the categorical shell suppresses.

## 33. `自由`, `自由意志`, and `不自由`

These terms must remain distinct.

- `不自由` names experienced deprivation: Eren recognizes himself as unfree because a desired world is closed.
- `自由` often names nonconfinement, authorship, or an unobstructed condition.
- `自由意志` in V28 raises the more specific question of whether a person acts through free will rather than inherited or biological compulsion.

Eren's tragedy includes moving from a legitimate perception of `不自由` to a self-certifying use of `自由` that denies others' `自由意志`. The vocabulary does not contain one stable moral proposition. Its direction and scope change.

## 34. `家畜` and `奴隷`

`家畜` first frames life inside the Walls as safe domestication. It returns in the rejection of Historia's reproductive future. `奴隷` becomes a more explicit status claim in arguments about Ymir, Mikasa, Armin, Zeke, and Eren himself.

The terms are related but not interchangeable. Livestock imagery emphasizes lives bred, contained, and used as infrastructure. Slave language emphasizes obedience, ownership, and denied authorship. Eren can correctly diagnose both structures in others and fail to recognize the analogous jurisdiction he exercises over victims outside his circle.

## 35. The changing audience of `戦え`

The same imperative traces Eren's development:

1. **Eren → Mikasa:** fight as immediate survival and refusal of victimhood.
2. **Eren → self:** fight as inward discipline and suppression of hesitation.
3. **Eren → Grisha:** fight/advance as coercive pressure using inherited dead.
4. **Eren/child image → coalition:** fight as adversarial permission after dialogue is refused.
5. **institutional afterlife:** the lexeme survives beyond Eren and can be appropriated politically.

Shared vocabulary does not mean shared ethical content. The progression shows an emancipatory imperative becoming self-coercive and then other-coercive.

## 36. `駆逐` and changing target classes

`駆逐` supplies lexical continuity while its object changes dramatically:

- Titans as apparent nonhuman predators;
- identified shifters and traitors;
- enemies beyond the sea;
- finally life outside Paradis.

The continuity matters because Eren repeatedly organizes freedom through elimination. The changing object matters more because the moral status of the target cannot be held constant. A translation or synthesis that preserves only “drive out” may understate the exterminatory shell; one that treats every use as morally identical erases the growth of Eren's knowledge.

## 37. `進み続ける` and the difference between movement and justification

Advance language belongs to several characters and institutions, not only Eren. In Eren it functions as identity, process rule, and coercive technique. V24 explicitly separates those pushed by others from those who push their own backs while refusing to promise hope. V30 turns the phrase against Grisha. The final series therefore blocks the equation:

> continued movement = freedom = moral right.

Movement proves persistence and ownership of a path. It does not prove the path legitimate.

## 38. Birth language

`生まれた` links several layers of the corpus. For Eren it grounds access to the world and rejection of euthanasia. For Carla it grounds unconditional worth apart from exceptional ability. In the Historia conflict it opposes descendants being born into preassigned bodily office. In the Rumbling, however, Eren's birth-grounded standing collides with the equally real standing of those born outside the island.

The mature contradiction is not that the birth ethic disappears. It is that Eren applies it asymmetrically.

# X. Responsibility without simplistic voluntarism

## 39. Five questions that must remain separate

A responsible judgment of Eren distinguishes:

1. **Causal explanation:** what histories, memories, relationships, institutions, and future information produced the decision environment?
2. **Psychological understanding:** what did Eren fear, desire, recognize, and fail to integrate?
3. **Authorship:** what conduct did he initiate, continue, select, conceal, or strategically shape?
4. **Justification:** did the reasons provide legitimate authority for the action and its scope?
5. **Absolution:** do causal limits or desired benefits erase responsibility for the harm?

The manga supports rich answers to the first three. It refuses the move from understanding to justification, and from causation to absolution.

## 40. Evidence of constrained agency

Eren's agency is materially constrained by:

- being born into the Walls and later into an inherited Titan office;
- Carla's death and prolonged warfare;
- Grisha's memories and the Attack Titan chain;
- a shortened lifespan;
- military custody and political time pressure;
- outside hostility and technological change;
- future memories whose selection and completeness he does not control;
- Founder time that disrupts ordinary temporal experience;
- an anticipated endpoint connected to Mikasa and Ymir.

A judgment that ignores these conditions produces a fantasy of self-created evil detached from the series' causal architecture.

## 41. Evidence of continuing authorship

Eren nevertheless:

- distinguishes and rejects Zeke's plan;
- lies to reach Founder access;
- chooses not to disclose the Historia hypothesis;
- leaves the military and coordinates with supporters;
- attacks Liberio before receiving full Founder power;
- pressures Grisha using chosen arguments;
- asks Ymir for catastrophic power;
- states the Rumbling objective and stopping rule;
- refuses coalition requests to stop;
- admits knowing outside innocents are not personal enemies;
- admits personal disappointment and desire;
- says he probably would have flattened the world even without the alliance-stop knowledge.

A judgment that ignores these acts produces a fantasy of puppetry unsupported by the source.

## 42. The jurisdiction test

Eren's responsibility becomes clearest through jurisdiction rather than metaphysical freedom. Even under causal constraint, what authority does he claim over other persons?

He claims the authority to:

- withhold information from Historia for her protection;
- reject her chosen sacrifice;
- force his friends into an operation they did not approve;
- define Armin and Mikasa's minds for them;
- pressure Grisha to act;
- solicit Ymir's power;
- decide that people outside Paradis may die for island survival and his desired horizon;
- permit selected friends to resist while denying victims any comparable participation.

Some of these claims are defensible or caring in local form; others are catastrophic. The common structure is unilateral decision under the belief that Eren sees a necessity others cannot or will not act upon. His terminal failure is not simply “wanting freedom too much.” It is converting his own authorship into authority over the existence of other agents.

## 43. Responsibility is non-transferable, not solitary

Calling responsibility non-transferable does not mean Eren alone causes the catastrophe or that every other institution is innocent. Marleyan imperialism, global anti-Eldian politics, the Tybur declaration, Paradis military failures, Zeke's plan, Yelena's networks, Floch's movement, Grisha's decisions, Ymir's action, and the alliance's choices all matter. Responsibility is distributed.

“Non-transferable” means Eren cannot make his portion disappear by locating causes elsewhere. He may be caused and still be an author. Others may share responsibility without absorbing his.

# XI. Strong counterreadings and their limits

A specialist synthesis should not become a machine for making every rival interpretation disappear. Several common readings of Eren isolate real structures. The problem arises when one structure is treated as exhaustive and the inconvenient evidence is redescribed as disguise, coercion, or inconsistency. The following counterreadings are therefore presented in their strongest defensible forms before adjudication.

## 44. Counterreading A — Eren is fundamentally a self-sacrificing hero who makes himself the enemy

### Strongest case

The final conversation with Armin confirms a political function in which the alliance becomes the group that defeats the person threatening the world. Eren permits his friends to retain their Titan powers and the freedom to oppose him. The resulting survivors become publicly legible as world-saving figures, Titan power ends, and the surviving outside population is temporarily unable to launch immediate retaliation against Paradis. Eren also expects his own death and asks Mikasa, in the cabin sequence, to forget him and live after him. From this angle, the Rumbling can look like a horrific self-sacrifice designed to purchase a future for selected others.

### Why the reading is insufficient

The final confession does not say that alliance heroization exhausted the motive. Eren states that he would probably have flattened the outside even without knowing that his friends would stop him. He also exposes those friends to lethal uncertainty rather than executing a controlled theater in which their survival is secured. Sasha and Hange die during the larger strategy; Armin, Mikasa, Jean, Connie, Reiner, Annie, Pieck, Levi, and others face outcomes Eren does not fully know. Approximately eighty percent of humanity is not a theatrical casualty figure but the material result of the act.

The heroic reading also confuses **one strategic function** with **the whole desire architecture**. Eren does make the alliance into possible heroes. He simultaneously wants selected friends to live, wants the Titan system to end, wants Paradis to survive, fears death, resents losing Mikasa, and possesses a desire to flatten the populated outside. These motives can coexist without becoming morally equivalent.

### Adjudication

**STRENGTHEN as a real strategic and relational component; REJECT as an exhaustive explanation.** Eren is partly self-sacrificial, but he is not merely a martyr whose victims are incidental to a benevolent plan.

## 45. Counterreading B — Eren is a deterministic puppet of future memory

### Strongest case

V30 establishes that the Attack Titan can receive future-successor memories and that Eren, through Grisha's memories, had already seen a future-self memory years earlier. The Grisha sequence is recursively structured: Eren pressures Grisha from within a memory relation that itself depends on Grisha later transmitting Titan power to Eren. V34 adds the Founder's temporal collapse, in which past and future no longer present themselves to Eren as ordinarily separable. He knows a destination associated with Mikasa's choice while not knowing every intermediate action. His language of inevitability, advance, and `仕方が無かった` can therefore support a reading in which the future has already captured him.

### Why the reading is insufficient

The source never states that every later act is mechanically compelled by a complete immutable script. Eren receives selected memories, not omniscience. Grisha explicitly asks why he was not shown everything. Eren distinguishes Zeke's plan from his own and successfully deceives Zeke. He chooses what arguments to direct at Grisha. He requests Ymir's power. He declares the Rumbling's scope. He refuses the alliance's request to stop. Most damaging to the total-puppet reading, he says he likely would have flattened the world even without the knowledge that his friends would halt him.

Temporal constraint and authored desire therefore overlap. The future can shape the field in which Eren acts without becoming a separate agent that absorbs his conduct. Indeed, the recursive structure may intensify responsibility: the future he sees is partly a future produced by the person who sees it.

### Adjudication

**REVISE.** Eren acts under severe temporal and cognitive constraint, but “puppet” is too strong. The warranted formulation is **authorship under recursive causal entanglement**, not causal independence and not authorless compulsion.

## 46. Counterreading C — Eren is principally a nationalist defender of Paradis

### Strongest case

Eren's Paths broadcast names protection of the island as his purpose and identifies the outside world's exterminatory wish. Marley has already attacked Paradis repeatedly, weaponized Eldians, sought the Founder, and helped mobilize a wider coalition. The fifty-year plan would continue royal-blooded reproductive and inheritance sacrifice; Zeke's euthanasia plan eliminates future Subjects of Ymir; anti-Titan technology reduces the island's strategic window. The Rumbling therefore has a recognizable national-security logic. Eren also coordinates with Floch and allows a movement bearing his name to seize political power.

### Why the reading is insufficient

The nationalist model cannot explain the whole character before nationalism becomes available. Eren's original horizon is participation in the world, not sovereignty doctrine. His most stable positive bonds are personal rather than abstractly national. He opposes the state's intended use of Historia and does not simply defer to Paradis institutions. He assaults Paradis's military order, deceives its leadership, and exposes island civilians to collapsing Walls and internal political violence. His final account includes disappointment with the populated outside and a wish to flatten it even apart from the alliance-stop plan.

Nor does Eren become the complete ideological author of Yeagerism. Floch and the movement convert his action into a more coherent nationalist doctrine than Eren himself articulates. The posthumous regime can inherit his violence without exhausting his motive.

### Adjudication

**STRENGTHEN as a major objective and strategic context; REJECT as the deepest or sole cause.** Eren acts for Paradis, but he is not reducible to Paradis nationalism.

## 47. Counterreading D — Eren was “born this way”: an essential psychopath or monster

### Strongest case

Eren's anti-confinement intensity predates Carla's death. As a child he kills Mikasa's kidnappers with striking decisiveness and frames the encounter through a struggle-to-live logic. At Liberio he tells Reiner that he may have been like this from birth. V33–V34 expose a desire for the outside to disappear and a flattening wish that Eren himself cannot derive from a clean strategic calculation. The return to the birth image after `やりたかったんだ` can make the desire appear ontologically basic.

### Why the reading is insufficient

“Born this way” in Eren's mouth is self-description, not clinical or metaphysical narration. The series shows intense empathy, attachment, tactical cooperation, shame, grief, guilt, fear, and self-sacrifice. He is not affectively indifferent to persons or suffering. His violence is also historically transformed by bereavement, military socialization, inherited memory, outside-world knowledge, time pressure, and Founder experience. The same child who kills kidnappers does so inside a concrete rescue relation; that scene cannot simply be projected onto population-scale extermination without accounting for changes of scale, target class, power, and knowledge.

The essential-monster reading also removes the moral contradiction that the manga labors to construct. The Rumbling is horrifying not because an inhuman being finally reveals itself, but because a recognizably loving, frightened, intelligent, socially formed person knowingly claims exterminatory authority.

### Adjudication

**DOWNGRADE as total character explanation.** Preserve a pre-traumatic aggressive anti-obstruction disposition and an unusually low threshold for lethal action under certain frames. Reject psychopathology or metaphysical monstrosity as source-established essence.

## 48. Counterreading E — Carla's death and revenge explain everything

### Strongest case

Carla's death supplies the first concrete exterminatory object. `駆逐` becomes attached to Titans, and Eren's military identity develops around destroying the beings he understands as responsible. Later revelation that human agents and states produced the Titan threat can redirect rather than erase the revenge structure. V34's Dina implication makes the original trauma recursively central to Eren's own causal chain.

### Why the reading is insufficient

The outside-world desire and hatred of enclosed livestock life precede Carla's death. Eren's enemy category changes repeatedly, and he eventually recognizes that many outside victims did not kill Carla or choose the structures that threatened Paradis. His dialogue with Reiner explicitly reaches beyond personal revenge into recognition of shared formation and chosen continuation. The terminal confession includes objectives and desires that cannot be reduced to avenging Carla.

The Dina implication also does not make revenge a simple first cause. It destabilizes that causal story by entangling Eren with the event that formed his later hatred. The source does not provide enough mechanism to say that Eren deliberately authored Carla's death in the ordinary planning sense; it does establish that his terminal causal position cannot be narrated as innocent origin followed by external injury.

### Adjudication

**PRESERVE as a major trigger and early enemy-object generator; REJECT as sufficient terminal motive.**

## 49. Counterreading F — Eren does everything for his friends

### Strongest case

Eren repeatedly rescues Armin and Mikasa, defends Historia, values the 104th, refuses hereditary sacrifice, and says he wants his friends to live long lives. Alliance heroization and the end of Titan power materially benefit surviving friends. His fear of death is expressed through wanting to remain with Mikasa and everyone. Particular attachment is plainly not propaganda added at the end.

### Why the reading is insufficient

Eren's strategy coerces the friends it claims to protect. It places them in combat, forces them to kill former comrades, exposes them to the risk of death, and makes them inherit political and emotional burdens they did not choose. He lies to Armin and Mikasa about their own minds. He permits no comparable standing to the innumerable friends, parents, children, and ordinary communities outside Paradis. The final confession separates his wish for his friends from his wish to flatten the world.

A friends-only reading therefore mistakes sincerity for sufficiency. Eren's care is real precisely enough to reveal its boundedness. He protects selected relationships by claiming jurisdiction over everyone else's relationships.

### Adjudication

**STRENGTHEN as a central motive vector; REJECT as exhaustive and REJECT its universal moralization.**

## 50. Counterreading G — Diplomacy failed, so the Rumbling was the only realistic option

### Strongest case

The outside political environment is deeply hostile. Marley has organized imperial violence and seeks renewed Founder capture. Willy Tybur mobilizes the world through a declaration that combines real threat information with political staging. Hizuru's cooperation is self-interested and limited. The fifty-year plan perpetuates royal-blood sacrifice and depends on uncertain deterrence. Zeke's alternative is reproductive extinction. Time is short because shifter lifespans and military technology are moving against Paradis. The manga does not present an easy liberal solution waiting to be chosen.

### Why the reading is insufficient

“Difficult alternatives” is not equivalent to “only one possible scope of violence.” The source contains multiple Rumbling scales, deterrence proposals, alliance-building efforts, technological strategies, and political uncertainties. None is guaranteed. Neither is global extermination shown to produce durable peace. Eren leaves the Survey Corps' collective process and helps create the crisis through the Liberio attack. He also says that an intrinsic desire would likely have driven flattening even without the exact alliance-stop outcome.

The monograph therefore cannot prove that a non-genocidal strategy would have succeeded. It also cannot accept necessity as a fact merely because every alternative is dangerous. Necessity claims are themselves political judgments about acceptable risk, time, sacrifice, and whose lives count.

### Adjudication

**OPEN on comparative strategic feasibility; REJECT “only option” as source-established fact.** The Rumbling has strategic causes and consequences, but terminal scope cannot be derived from necessity alone.

## 51. Counterreading H — Eren is “a slave to freedom”

### Strongest case

The phrase captures a central irony. Eren repeatedly defines himself through movement against obstruction, then becomes governed by the imperative to continue. `戦え` turns inward; `進み続ける` becomes self-command. Future memory, shortened life, causal recursion, and the image of an empty outside horizon make his freedom desire look compulsory. He leaves friends the freedom to fight him while refusing reciprocal negotiation, producing a person who can permit opposition yet cannot release the path he has made constitutive of himself.

### Why the reading is insufficient

“Slave” can obscure distinctions the manga preserves. Eren lies, revises tactics, recognizes alternatives, expresses conflict, and makes relationship-specific exceptions. His motives are plural, not one drive operating without mediation. Causation by a strong desire does not eliminate agency any more than love, duty, or fear automatically does. The metaphor can also create false absolution if it implies that “freedom” acted through him as an external master.

### Adjudication

**STRENGTHEN as an interpretive paradox; REVISE as a causal account.** Eren becomes increasingly self-coercive, but the imperative remains part of his authored response architecture rather than a separate agent.

## 52. Counterreading I — Eren is an omniscient mastermind controlling every outcome

### Strongest case

Eren successfully deceives Zeke, coordinates with Floch, influences Grisha, reaches Ymir, initiates the Rumbling, arranges memory conversations, anticipates a result connected to Mikasa, and creates the conditions for the alliance to become humanity's saviors. His silence and composure after the timeskip can make him appear to possess a complete script.

### Why the reading is insufficient

The source repeatedly marks missing information. Grisha is not shown everything. Eren does not know the exact content of Mikasa's choice. He says his friends were drawn into lethal uncertainty without knowing whether they would survive. His table-scene distancing is admitted to have been badly executed. His own mind is disordered by Founder time. He cannot explain the deepest origin of one major desire. The final political future is not under his control.

Strategic competence and partial future knowledge do not equal omniscience. “Mastermind” is especially misleading when applied to outcomes that depend on other agents refusing, improvising, surviving, and acting beyond Eren's control.

### Adjudication

**REJECT as total model; PRESERVE high strategic intelligence, selective foresight, and successful manipulation within bounded domains.**

## 53. Counterreading J — `やりたかったんだ` reveals the one authentic motive beneath all rationalizations

### Strongest case

The final confession strips away cleaner strategic justifications. Eren admits that he likely would have flattened the world even without knowing his friends would stop him and says he wanted to do it without being able to explain why. This desire is anticipated by V33's disappointment that the outside was populated and by child-Eren's experience of empty-horizon freedom above the Rumbling. The reading can therefore treat Paradis, friends, Historia, Titan-ending, and causal constraint as secondary rationalizations around a more basic wish.

### Why the reading is insufficient

A motive can be irreducible without being exclusive. The confession occurs in the same conversation that affirms alliance heroization, Titan-ending causality, care for friends, Founder temporal disorder, jealousy, shame, and fear of death. Earlier conduct independently supports those motives; they are not invented only when Eren needs an excuse. The phrase `わかんねぇけど` also marks incomplete self-knowledge. It cannot authorize the analyst to supply a single hidden essence more confidently than the character or text does.

### Adjudication

**STRENGTHEN the intrinsic desire layer; REJECT mono-causal authenticity.** The desire is not “the truth” that makes every other motive false. It is the factor that prevents every benevolent or strategic explanation from becoming sufficient.

# XII. Character reconstruction and simulation constraints

This document is a literary specialist synthesis, not a generated simulation model. It nevertheless establishes constraints that any later `AOT_CHAR_EREN_JAEGER_RECONSTRUCTION_MODEL.md` must obey. A plausible imitation of surface directness is not enough. The model must preserve state, information boundary, relationship conditioning, contradiction, and confidence limits.

## 55. Eren must be represented as state-conditioned, not scalar

A single “how freedom-driven is Eren?” variable would erase the structure that the corpus actually supports. At minimum, reconstruction should distinguish the following state bands.

### E0 — Pre-fall childhood: enclosed positive-horizon state

**Boundary:** before Carla's death.  
**Dominant evidence:** Walls as livestock enclosure, Armin's book, birth-grounded entitlement, willingness to use lethal force during Mikasa's kidnapping.  
**Operating rule:** an imposed boundary is experienced as personal deprivation even when it provides safety.  
**Do not import:** Titan revenge, future memory, nationalism, Founder knowledge, or terminal flattening self-accounting.

### E1 — Bereaved exterminatory state

**Boundary:** fall of Shiganshina through early cadet formation.  
**Dominant evidence:** Carla's death, `駆逐`, humiliation, bodily insufficiency, military entry.  
**Operating rule:** grief is converted into a target class and a training program.  
**Risk:** overpredicting indiscriminate rage; Eren still cooperates, forms peer bonds, and responds to competence.

### E2 — Cadet/early soldier relational state

**Boundary:** training and Trost.  
**Dominant evidence:** rivalry, shame, rescue, Armin trust, Mikasa conflict, first Titan-body experience.  
**Operating rule:** worth is action-coded; being protected can feel like a threat to agency, but relationship loyalty remains strong.  
**Voice:** blunt, reactive, more socially legible than the later infiltrator shell.

### E3 — Custody, wager, and self-blame state

**Boundary:** Survey Corps custody through Female Titan aftermath.  
**Dominant evidence:** dependence on Levi/Erwin, choice under uncertainty, original Levi squad deaths, radicalized self-blame.  
**Operating rule:** uncertainty after loss increases the appeal of unilateral ownership.  
**Simulation caution:** Eren does not always reject command; he can consent to trust and then retrospectively punish himself for doing so.

### E4 — Human-enemy and Coordinate state

**Boundary:** Clash of the Titans.  
**Dominant evidence:** Reiner/Bertolt recognition, humanizing knowledge, dehumanizing withdrawal of membership, Hannes's ordinary-life memory, first Coordinate activation.  
**Operating rule:** recognition raises betrayal intensity and does not guarantee restraint.  
**Voice:** emotionally volatile; moral category language becomes explicit.

### E5 — Inherited-guilt and recoverable-plurality state

**Boundary:** Uprising through return to Shiganshina.  
**Dominant evidence:** vessel status, Grisha inheritance, self-negation, Historia intervention, restored willingness to participate, Armin/Erwin decision environment.  
**Operating rule:** inherited guilt can collapse self-worth, but relationship evidence can restore plural agency.  
**Prohibition:** do not portray Eren as continuously confident or as already committed to the Rumbling.

### E6 — Post-basement/sea fracture state

**Boundary:** outside-world archive through pre-Marley departure.  
**Dominant evidence:** historical uncertainty, royal-blood hypothesis, Historia nondisclosure, sea question, future-memory burden after the hand kiss.  
**Operating rule:** positive horizon becomes occupied by enemy structure; care increasingly takes the form of unilateral information control.  
**Epistemic limit:** exact content and completeness of future knowledge remain bounded.

### E7 — Infiltrator and rupture state

**Boundary:** Marley infiltration through restaurant-table rupture.  
**Dominant evidence:** Reiner recognition, Falco instrumentalization, Liberio attack, strategic deception, military break, Historia/Floch arrangements, Armin/Mikasa cruelty.  
**Operating rule:** social understanding can coexist with operational exploitation; intimacy is converted into a vulnerability to manage.  
**Voice:** compressed, controlled, declarative, capable of precise interpersonal cruelty, with later evidence that the control is partly performed and imperfect.

### E8 — Paths author/recursive-causality state

**Boundary:** contact with Zeke through Ymir's release.  
**Dominant evidence:** euthanasia deception, birth refusal, Grisha pressure, future-memory revelation, request to Ymir.  
**Operating rule:** Eren rejects inherited teleology while using inherited persons and memories to produce his chosen path.  
**Simulation caution:** do not treat memory access as omniscience or exact remote-control power.

### E9 — Rumbling/withdrawn author state

**Boundary:** Paths broadcast through alliance approach.  
**Dominant evidence:** explicit exterminatory stopping rule, refusal to negotiate, freedom granted to friends as adversarial permission, Ramzi confession, child-horizon experience.  
**Operating rule:** reciprocal politics is replaced by collision: others may fight, but Eren will not revise.  
**Affect:** remorse and empathy remain present without becoming brakes.

### E10 — Terminal confessional state

**Boundary:** cabin and Armin conversation.  
**Dominant evidence:** request that Mikasa forget him, jealousy, shame, fear of death, desire to remain with friends, strategic self-accounting, temporal disorder, `やりたかったんだ`.  
**Operating rule:** the compressed shell fractures; motives are admitted without being fully ordered.  
**Prohibition:** do not retroactively make this degree of emotional disclosure available in ordinary earlier conversation.

## 56. Governing response rules

A reconstruction model should encode conditional rules rather than slogans.

### Rule 1 — Obstruction must first be classified

Eren does not react identically to every limit. A physical Wall, institutional custody, a friend's protection, incomplete information, an enemy military, a hereditary reproductive plan, and another person's refusal can all feel obstructive, but they trigger different responses. The model must identify what kind of obstruction Eren believes he faces and whether he recognizes the obstructing party as agent, structure, betrayer, protector, or victim.

### Rule 2 — Action restores self-coherence

When uncertain, ashamed, frightened, or grieving, Eren often regains coherence through action: training, fighting, deciding, advancing, infiltrating, or forcing a confrontation. This does not mean he is incapable of reflection. It means reflection that does not terminate in an actionable orientation is often experienced as intolerable suspension.

### Rule 3 — Particular attachment can override abstract doctrine locally

Historia's body, Armin's life, Mikasa's safety, and the 104th's future matter in relationship-specific ways. The model should not infer universal humanitarianism from these exceptions. Nor should it infer that later coercion proves the attachments false.

### Rule 4 — Recognition changes shame and responsibility more reliably than it changes targeting

Eren can understand Reiner, empathize with Ramzi, read Falco, and know the outside is populated. This increases hesitation, sorrow, or self-condemnation. It does not reliably prohibit violence once he has assigned a target a role inside the chosen path.

### Rule 5 — Self-blame tends to increase control-seeking

After disastrous collective or delegated choices, Eren does not simply become more deferential. He often concludes that he should have acted more decisively. This rule helps explain movement from the original Levi squad loss to later unilateralism without pretending that one event mechanically causes genocide.

### Rule 6 — Future knowledge narrows experienced possibility but does not eliminate strategic reasoning

The model should represent known endpoints, unknown details, selective memory, and recursive influence separately. Eren can feel a path is fixed while still choosing tactics, lies, timing, and relationship interventions within it.

### Rule 7 — Once self-certification hardens, opposition becomes confirmation

In late states, refusal by others does not necessarily reopen deliberation. It can confirm that freedoms are colliding and that he must continue. This is a dangerous late-state rule and should not be projected onto early Eren, who repeatedly changes course in response to evidence and command.

## 58. Strategic intelligence and information asymmetry

Eren should be modeled as strategically intelligent but not uniformly superior. Evidence supports:

- anomaly-based combat inference against the War Hammer;
- successful deception of Zeke;
- exploitation of Falco's trust;
- compartmentalization with Floch and Historia;
- anticipation of military and political reactions;
- use of silence and partial truth as control instruments;
- selective timing of disclosure;
- capacity to operate inside hostile territory while reading social cues.

Evidence does not support:

- perfect prediction of every battle outcome;
- complete knowledge of friends' survival;
- complete access to all future events;
- flawless interpersonal manipulation;
- complete control over Ymir, Mikasa, Armin, Grisha, or the alliance;
- durable control of postwar politics.

A high-quality reconstruction must therefore allow Eren to be wrong, surprised, emotionally clumsy, and dependent on other people's agency.

## 59. Affect and fracture rules

Eren's apparent emotional flatness after the timeskip is state and function, not a new absence of affect. The model should distinguish at least four modes.

### Public/operational compression

Short declaratives, low disclosure, controlled gaze, and refusal to justify more than necessary. This mode protects strategy and prevents relational negotiation.

### Confrontational activation

Sharper imperatives, categorical language, threat framing, and bodily forwardness. Earlier versions are more explosive; later versions are colder but not affectless.

### Private remorse without de-escalation

Ramzi is the clearest case. Eren can collapse, apologize, recognize innocence, and still continue toward the known harm. The model must not convert tears into a reversal that the source does not support.

### Terminal intimate fracture

With Armin, the shell breaks into jealousy, embarrassment, fear, desire for life, and incomplete self-understanding. This is not Eren's default conversational mode. It requires exceptional intimacy, terminal knowledge, and a context in which operational concealment is ending.

## 60. Japanese voice constraints

Any later dialogue generation should preserve the following tendencies without turning them into parody.

- First-person `オレ`, direct clause structure, and action-centered verbs remain baseline.
- `戦え` is not a universal catchphrase to be inserted whenever conflict appears. Its audience changes: self, Mikasa, companions, enemies, and eventually the condition of agency itself.
- `駆逐` should track target construction. Using it casually for trivial obstacles would flatten its moral history.
- `自由` can be horizon, self-certification, accusation, or adversarial permission. Context must determine which.
- `生まれた` language marks standing and existential entitlement; it is especially salient in disputes over nonexistence, enclosure, or inherited bodily function.
- Late Eren uses controlled compression and withheld premises. He should not deliver polished philosophical essays in ordinary dialogue.
- Emotional fracture may produce colloquial roughness, repetition, embarrassed denial, or incomplete explanation rather than elegant self-analysis.

The manga provides no performed-audio authority. Typography, punctuation, and balloon form may inform written-register analysis but must not be converted into claims about seiyuu delivery.

## 61. Low-stakes and ordinary-life limits

Eren is unusually well evidenced under danger, mission pressure, betrayal, confinement, and relationship crisis. His unrestricted mundane-life reconstruction is weaker. The corpus supports:

- childhood curiosity about the outside;
- peer competitiveness and irritation;
- intense attention to competence and agency;
- enjoyment of belonging within the 104th even when not verbally demonstrative;
- ordinary embarrassment and possessiveness revealed late;
- attachment to home and remembered companionship, often recognized retrospectively;
- capacity for social observation during infiltration;
- food, rest, humor, and domestic evidence only in scattered states.

It does **not** robustly support broad claims about entertainment tastes, consumer preferences, adult domestic routine, career interests outside conflict, or behavior in long periods of stable peace. A simulator should answer such questions cautiously, preserve uncertainty, and avoid filling gaps with generic shōnen-protagonist traits.

## 62. Prohibited extrapolations

A reconstruction derived from this corpus must not:

1. make Eren omniscient after the hand kiss;
2. make every act consciously designed from childhood;
3. treat all harshness as hidden benevolence;
4. treat all benevolence as manipulation;
5. infer universal anti-instrumentalism from Historia or friend protection;
6. equate recognition with forgiveness or de-escalation;
7. turn `やりたかったんだ` into a complete first-cause theory;
8. treat the Dina implication as a fully specified ordinary command mechanism;
9. make late compressed speech the voice of every earlier state;
10. infer anime vocal performance from manga marks;
11. simulate genocide advocacy as Eren's response to every threat or confinement;
12. erase shame, fear, jealousy, dependence, or desire for ordinary life in order to make him “cooler”;
13. erase strategic intelligence in order to make him merely impulsive;
14. erase causal constraint in order to create a metaphysically self-originating villain;
15. erase authorship in order to create a morally empty puppet.

## 63. Reconstruction readiness and confidence boundary

The completed sequential corpus makes Eren a **model-generation-eligible** character with unusually strong high-stakes, relational, ideological, tactical, and state-transition evidence. It does not justify one unrestricted personality emulator.

High-confidence domains include:

- responses to confinement and denied agency;
- battle and operational reasoning;
- attachment under threat;
- strategic concealment;
- relationship-conditioned protection and coercion;
- betrayal recognition;
- future-memory burden as experienced constraint;
- late-state self-command and refusal;
- terminal shame, death fear, and partial self-accounting.

Moderate-confidence domains include:

- ordinary peer interaction outside acute crisis;
- reflective conversation with trusted intimates;
- leadership under nonterminal political pressure;
- reactions to persons who resemble known relationship roles.

Low-confidence domains include:

- prolonged peaceful adulthood;
- unfamiliar romance outside the Mikasa relation;
- contemporary consumer or occupational settings;
- comedic cross-series interaction without conflict anchors;
- unrestricted ideology transfer to worlds with different metaphysics and institutions.

Any later validation audit should test whether the model preserves these confidence boundaries rather than rewarding fluent invention.

# XIII. Final synthesis

## 64. What remains continuous

What remains continuous in Eren is not a single proposition such as “freedom above all,” and not a hidden biological essence. It is a **response architecture**.

He experiences unchosen confinement as a theft of rightful participation. He seeks an actionable obstruction that can be fought, crossed, exposed, or destroyed. He restores self-coherence through movement and decision. He treats birth as minimum standing in the world. He forms intense particular attachments. When collective or delegated choice ends in catastrophe, he tends to increase self-blame and unilateral control. He is capable of tactical evidence revision, but once a path becomes fused with his identity and anticipated future, opposition can become confirmation that he must continue.

This architecture is present before Carla's death, but it is not complete before Carla's death. Bereavement supplies a target and exterminatory grammar. Titan embodiment transforms the body from limit into weapon. Military custody teaches Eren both dependence and resentment. The deaths of the original Levi squad intensify the belief that choosing personally might control loss. Reiner and Bertolt reveal human enemies inside trusted roles. Grisha's memories burden Eren with inherited action and political history. Armin's horizon is preserved and then inverted when the outside proves populated. Historia makes bodily sovereignty concrete. Future memory narrows perceived possibility. Marley makes enemies ordinary. Reiner supplies recognition without absolution. Zeke supplies an antinatalist opposite and a route to Founder access. Ymir supplies person-recognition, power, and a causal destination Eren does not fully understand. Ramzi destroys the innocence of abstraction. Mikasa and Armin expose the difference between being loved and possessing another person's decision.

Continuity is therefore the channel through which history operates, not an excuse to pretend history changes nothing.

## 65. How desire, care, coercion, and violence relate

Eren's terminal action contains at least eight real factors: intrinsic anti-obstruction/flattening desire; protection of Paradis; protection and heroization of selected friends; refusal of Historia's hereditary sacrifice; the result that ends Titan power; future-memory and Founder constraint; self-coercive advance; and ordinary attachment, jealousy, shame, and death fear.

These factors are not equally exculpatory, equally conscious, or equally deep. Some are objectives. Some are constraints. Some are strategic functions. Some are desires. Some are relationship commitments. The manga never supplies a numerical weighting, and the final confession actively resists a clean hierarchy.

The most important moral relationship among them is this:

> **Particular care does not cancel exterminatory jurisdiction. It makes the asymmetry visible.**

Eren recognizes Historia's body as not belonging to a state plan. He wants friends to possess futures not consumed by Titan inheritance. He understands Falco and apologizes to Ramzi. Yet he does not generalize that standing to the outside world's persons. He protects chosen people by making himself the authority over everyone else's future. His ethical collapse is therefore not absence of love. It is love joined to a jurisdiction it cannot justify.

## 66. How causality and responsibility relate

The full series rules out two easy pictures.

The first is sovereign voluntarism: Eren does not create himself from nothing. He is produced through birth, enclosure, trauma, relationships, institutions, inherited bodies, memories, war, shortened life, political time pressure, and a future that feeds into its own causes.

The second is total compulsion: Eren distinguishes plans, lies, selects tactics, pressures Grisha, requests Ymir's power, declares a target and stopping rule, refuses dialogue, and admits desire beyond the cleanest strategic account.

Responsibility therefore survives without causal independence. It is neither solitary nor transferable. Marley, global anti-Eldian politics, Paradis institutions, Grisha, Zeke, Floch, Ymir, and other actors retain their own portions of causal and moral responsibility. Their responsibility does not absorb Eren's. His causes explain the field in which he acts; they do not automatically grant authority over victims or make the resulting harm authorless.

## 67. The mature Eren thesis

The most compressed warranted formulation is:

> **Eren Jaeger is a causally entangled author whose lifelong resistance to unchosen confinement develops from a positive demand to participate in the world into a self-coercive demand that the world conform to his path. His terminal violence is irreducibly multi-causal: it protects selected people and a threatened island, rejects hereditary sacrifice and reproductive extinction, moves toward an endpoint associated with Mikasa and the end of Titan power, operates under selective future memory and Founder temporal disorder, and also fulfills an intrinsic desire for an unobstructed world. He understands more of his victims' humanity than an enemy-abstraction model allows, but recognition does not reliably inhibit action once he has claimed jurisdiction. He is neither a sovereign first cause nor a deterministic puppet. His tragedy is that genuine care, strategic intelligence, victimization, inherited causality, and fear coexist with knowingly continued exterminatory authorship; none transfers his responsibility away.**

This formulation preserves the character's humanity without converting humanity into defense. It preserves constraint without converting constraint into innocence. It preserves desire without pretending desire is self-explanation. It preserves love without making victims disappear behind the people Eren loves.

## 68. What remains open

The following questions remain deliberately unresolved here:

- the deepest developmental or metaphysical origin of Eren's flattening desire;
- the exact relative weight of Paradis protection, friend protection, Titan-ending, intrinsic desire, and future constraint;
- how much of the future Eren saw at each pre-Founder boundary;
- whether the future-memory structure permits meaningful counterfactual divergence and in what sense;
- the exact mechanism of Eren's influence on Dina's route;
- the exact causal relation among Eren, Ymir, Mikasa's choice, and the end of Titan power;
- the extent to which Founder temporal collapse should be described as cognitive injury, metaphysical simultaneity, or both;
- what narrower non-genocidal strategies could actually have survived the political and military environment;
- whether Eren's terminal self-accounting is complete enough for him to understand his own conduct.

These are not omissions to be patched by confident paraphrase. They are source-defined limits routed to later specialist and reference work.
