# Attack on Titan — Character Modeling and Simulation Architecture v1.0

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the downstream architecture for reconstructing *Attack on Titan* characters as temporally bounded, evidence-grounded behavioral and speech models.

It does **not** replace the literary deep-reading method. `AOT_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2.md` remains the governing protocol for reading the manga. Character models consume the evidence generated by that protocol and reorganize it for a narrower question:

> Given a specific version of a character, what ranges of action, reasoning, social response, and Japanese speech are strongly supported, weakly supported, or outside the evidence boundary in a novel situation?

The goal is **bounded reconstruction**, not unrestricted roleplay and not hidden-canon invention.

## 2. Architectural principle

The project should preserve a strict authority stack:

1. original Japanese manga page;
2. canonical V2 volume deep reading and evidence IDs;
3. longitudinal thematic/character/relationship/voice ledgers;
4. checkpoint and full-series syntheses;
5. character reconstruction model;
6. model validation audit;
7. optional hypothetical scenario output.

Each lower layer is more derived than the layer above it. A plausible generated response cannot retroactively become evidence.

## 3. Why Attack on Titan needs time-indexed models

A single timeless profile is especially dangerous for this series. Characters change because their knowledge, political role, bodily state, loyalties, self-conception, and available moral categories change.

A model therefore requires an explicit **model boundary**. Examples include:

- Eren @ V04;
- Eren @ V18;
- Reiner @ a pre-reveal boundary;
- Historia @ pre-name-restoration versus post-V16;
- Levi under ordinary squad command versus acute mission triage.

Later states do not erase earlier repertoires. They may revise them, expand them, suppress them, or expose earlier misreadings.

## 4. The state vector

Before predicting behavior, instantiate at least the following dimensions:

- **publication/time boundary**;
- **knowledge state** — what the character actually knows and believes;
- **role state** — cadet, soldier, commander, prisoner, royal claimant, infiltrator, civilian, etc.;
- **relationship state** — interlocutor, trust, attachment, rivalry, betrayal, rank, dependency;
- **public/private setting**;
- **stake level** — ordinary, socially consequential, dangerous, existential;
- **time pressure**;
- **physical state** — injury, exhaustion, hunger, transformation limits, bodily capability;
- **emotional state** — fear, grief, shame, anger, relief, resolve, numbness, exhilaration;
- **information uncertainty**;
- **institutional constraints and orders**;
- **available alternatives**;
- **current self-conception**.

Behavioral prediction without this state vector is normally stereotype generation.

## 5. Model components

Every substantial character model should distinguish the following.

### 5.1 Stable dispositional core

Recurring tendencies that survive multiple contexts, such as attention habits, thresholds for intervention, response to uncertainty, relation to autonomy, willingness to accept help, or characteristic forms of responsibility.

### 5.2 Developmental states

Time-bounded configurations of the character. These should be described as states, not rewritten into a single retrospective essence.

### 5.3 Decision grammar

Conditional patterns connecting problem recognition to action. A useful rule has the form:

> When condition X is present, under relationship/role Y and state Z, response family A becomes more likely unless inhibitor B or competing value C overrides it.

This is preferable to adjective claims such as "reckless," "loyal," "cold," or "smart."

### 5.4 Attention and epistemic style

What the character notices, ignores, verifies, assumes, asks about, distrusts, or updates quickly. AoT makes this especially important because information asymmetry repeatedly governs action.

### 5.5 Relationship-conditioned behavior

How behavior changes with specific people and relationship classes. Directionality is mandatory: A -> B and B -> A are separate observations.

### 5.6 Ordinary-life and low-stakes repertoire

Conversation, humor, irritation, training, food, work, rest, embarrassment, teasing, boredom, and minor disagreement are required wherever the source supplies them. This is the control sample against crisis-state overfitting.

### 5.7 Written-Japanese voice fingerprint

Model:

- self-reference;
- address terms;
- politeness/register;
- clause and turn length;
- bluntness/hedging;
- commands, questions, refusals, apologies, reassurances, complaints;
- lexical clusters;
- rhetorical abstraction versus concrete practical phrasing;
- state and relationship deltas.

The manga does not authorize claims about seiyuu timbre, pitch, breathing, pause length, or vocal delivery.

### 5.8 Emotional and bodily deltas

A character's crisis speech or injured behavior should be modeled as a delta from baseline rather than treated as the baseline itself.

### 5.9 Values in behavioral form

Values matter only insofar as the model can connect them to choices, thresholds, tradeoffs, and exceptions. "Freedom matters to Eren" is analytically true but insufficient for simulation until the model specifies what kinds of perceived constraint trigger which behaviors at which states.

### 5.10 Negative constraints

Record behaviors or voices that would likely be wrong. Examples of error classes include:

- sloganizing a character who usually speaks practically;
- making Mikasa's every response about Eren;
- making Levi permanently affectless because command speech is terse;
- making Armin conciliatory in situations where the canon supports severe strategic reasoning;
- making Reiner's soldier care "fake" because later mission identity is known;
- making Eren uniformly confident when canon repeatedly shows fear, guilt, hesitation, or altered self-conception.

Negative constraints are not prohibitions against development. They are anti-caricature controls.

## 6. Reconstruction-support ledgers

The minimum modeling layer is:

- `AOT_CHARACTER_BEHAVIOR_AND_DECISION_LEDGER.md`;
- `AOT_RELATIONSHIP_CONDITIONED_BEHAVIOR_LEDGER.md`;
- `AOT_EVERYDAY_LIFE_PREFERENCES_AND_LOW_STAKES_BEHAVIOR_LEDGER.md`;
- `AOT_JAPANESE_VOICE_AND_VOCABULARY_LEDGER.md`;
- `AOT_CHARACTER_MODEL_READINESS_AND_COVERAGE_LEDGER.md`.

These are not replacements for the existing Eren, trio, 104th, Warrior, institutional, sacrifice, personhood, or knowledge-state ledgers. They are cross-cutting retrieval surfaces for model construction.

## 7. Character-model lifecycle

### Stage A — evidence accumulation

Sequential readings and ledgers collect evidence. No simulation authority is claimed.

### Stage B — boundary-specific provisional reconstruction

Once a character has enough recurrence across multiple situations, a provisional model may be emitted. Its front matter must state the exact source and timeline boundary.

### Stage C — prospective prediction freeze

At the V18-V19 50% checkpoint, before Volume 20 is analyzed, create:

`AOT_CHARACTER_MODEL_PROSPECTIVE_PREDICTION_REGISTER_V01-V19.md`

The register should freeze testable predictions for characters with sufficient V01-V19 evidence. It should include both expected behaviors and predicted exceptions/limits. Later evidence may score those predictions but must never rewrite the frozen wording.

### Stage D — rolling adjudication

As V20-V34 are analyzed, mark prediction tests as `PASS`, `PARTIAL`, `FAIL`, `NOT_TESTED`, or `CONFOUNDED`. Failed predictions become model-revision evidence, not embarrassment to be edited away.

### Stage E — full-series reconstruction

After V34 and full-series synthesis, build canonical character models for characters whose coverage warrants them.

### Stage F — validation and cross-model consistency

Run per-character validation and a suite-level audit for contradictory relationship states, timeline leakage, voice convergence, and scenario overreach.

Do not create models merely to complete a roster. Coverage must justify the artifact.

## 9. Priority tiers

Priority is based on likely modeling value, not moral importance.

**Tier A initial candidates:** Eren, Mikasa, Armin, Reiner, Jean, Historia, Levi.

**Tier B candidates after sufficient full-series coverage:** Erwin, Hange, Ymir, Annie, Bertolt, Connie, Sasha, and later-emerging major characters with adequate dialogue/action evidence.

A character may move tiers. The readiness ledger, not fame, decides promotion.

## 10. Novel-situation inference protocol

Before generating a hypothetical response:

1. choose the character time boundary;
2. reconstruct the knowledge state;
3. identify social role and interlocutor relation;
4. classify stakes and time pressure;
5. identify closest canonical analogues;
6. identify unmatched scenario features;
7. retrieve behavioral, relationship, ordinary-life, and voice evidence;
8. generate **response families**, not a single inevitable action;
9. select the best-supported response and note plausible alternatives when uncertainty is material;
10. assign a confidence band and scenario-distance class.

The output should distinguish:

- **fact** — what canon directly establishes;
- **inference** — what repeated patterns support;
- **simulation** — the hypothetical continuation;
- **uncertainty** — where no close analogue exists.

## 11. Scenario-distance classes

`D0 — CANON_NEAR`: same or nearly identical situation class and relationship state.

`D1 — ANALOGOUS_TRANSFER`: novel event, but canonical pressures and social roles have close analogues.

`D2 — MODERATE_TRANSFER`: important unfamiliar features; model still has relevant value/behavior evidence.

`D3 — FAR_TRANSFER`: modern technology, institutions, cultural conventions, intimacy states, or life experiences absent from the manga. Output can be exploratory but not high-confidence reconstruction.

Confidence should fall as scenario distance rises unless unusually strong cross-context behavior supports the inference.

## 12. Speech generation boundary

A speech model may reconstruct likely **written Japanese discourse behavior**. It should not imitate long canonical passages, and it should not overfit signature slogans.

When no evidence exists for a speech act—flirting, customer service, office-email language, modern internet slang, etc.—the model should explicitly lower confidence rather than derive an answer from general stereotypes.

English renderings are downstream translations of a Japanese model. They should not become the evidence used to define the Japanese voice.

## 13. Everyday-life bias correction

AoT is structurally crisis-heavy. This creates a known sampling bias: many characters are richly observed in combat and poorly observed during leisure or ordinary domestic life.

The architecture therefore requires:

- systematic harvesting of low-stakes scenes;
- explicit `NO_EVIDENCE` fields for unsupported preferences;
- no inference that a crisis-optimized behavior is the person's normal social baseline;
- use of ordinary scenes as control samples during validation.

A character can be high-confidence for battlefield command and low-confidence for a dinner conversation. Readiness is domain-specific.

## 14. Prospective validation advantage of the current project state

Because the V2 analytical corpus currently stops at V18 while V19-V34 sources remain unanalyzed, the project has a rare opportunity for genuine prospective testing.

The analyst should exploit that opportunity rather than retrospectively claiming predictive success after reading the whole series. The V01-V19 prediction register must therefore be frozen before V20 analysis.

This does not mean later volumes are "test data" forever. After scoring, their evidence joins the corpus and may revise the model. The historical prediction result remains preserved as evidence about the earlier model's predictive warrant.

## 15. Optional anime-performance layer

If the anime is later added for performance modeling, create a separate layer covering:

- timing and pause;
- pitch and timbre;
- volume/intensity;
- breath and hesitation;
- muttering versus projection;
- performance-specific emotional leakage;
- adaptation-specific line changes.

The manga model remains the authority for manga characterization and Japanese text. Anime performance may enrich delivery but must not silently overwrite manga ambiguity or wording.

## 16. Cross-project methodological precedents

This architecture adopts proven ideas from other project corpora—especially stable-state separation, ordinary-life control samples, relationship-conditioned register, synthetic-output firewalls, and holdout validation—but treats those as **methods**, not evidence about AoT characters.

## 17. Success condition

The architecture succeeds when a future model can answer not merely "what is this character like?" but:

> Given this exact version of the character, with this knowledge, relationship, role, physical/emotional state, and pressure, which response families are most plausible, how would their written Japanese likely differ from another state, and how far does the evidence actually warrant that prediction?

That answer must remain traceable to canonical Japanese evidence.
