Only the first eighteen Korean headings survive as explicit metadata. After `전직 퀘스트`, this map uses **EPUB split-region + scene anchor**. The manhwa `cXXX` labels are likewise preserved as continuous adaptation segments rather than being mistaken for Korean chapters.

## 2. Adaptation categories

- **Preserved** — The source event, relationship, proposition, or causal function remains substantially intact.
- **Compressed** — Source material is shortened, summarized, or deprived of some interior, institutional, economic, or explanatory texture.
- **Omitted** — Source material with a meaningful local function is absent from the corresponding manhwa sequence.
- **Expanded** — The adaptation gives substantially more visual, relational, action, reaction, or temporal emphasis without changing the basic source function.
- **Transformed** — The source event remains recognizable but its emphasis, psychology, ethical framing, or thematic function changes materially.
- **Relocated** — Source material is moved to a different local position, volume, or dramatic context.
- **Recomposed** — Several source passages are rearranged, integrated, or cross-cut so that the resulting local sequence performs a materially different dramatic or thematic function.
- **Manhwa-original** — Material has no counterpart in the supplied Chugong prose corpus at the relevant level; includes the post-prose Suho continuation.
- **Source-uncertain** — The source relationship cannot presently be resolved beyond the stated zone without inventing precision; used especially where the raw Korean EPUB is not remounted.

### `Recomposed` threshold

`Recomposed` is not a synonym for “changed.” It is used only where **several source passages, revelations, or vignettes are rearranged or integrated so that the local sequence performs a materially different dramatic or thematic job**. The Architect confrontation, the Japan decision, Volume 13’s closure, and the side-story Part architecture are the clearest cases.

## 5. Side-story and ending architecture

### Volume 13: the numbered main manhwa crosses the prose main-story boundary

The Korean main story formally ends inside `index_split_030`. The manhwa does not stop its numbered main-series closure at the same local point. Chapter 40 instead integrates reset/aftermath material and selected later epilogue concerns into a life-beyond-usefulness ending centered more directly on Jinwoo’s peaceful-world existence and Cha. This is **Relocated + Recomposed**, not merely “the same ending drawn differently.”

The Korean formal ending involving Go Gunhee is therefore not treated as the local endpoint of Manhwa Volume 13. Comparable Go/Elixir/end-of-life material appears later, especially in Volume 15 Part 4, but the exact relationship of every Go passage remains **Source-uncertain** until the raw Korean EPUB is remounted and paragraph-level crosswalk can be performed.

### Volume 14: numbered `외전` become long Parts

The Korean source preserves twenty-one numbered/titled `외전`. The manhwa instead presents large “Side Stories” Parts 1–3. Those Parts braid post-reset recognition, police/guardianship, Cha material, Igris/shadow memory, and ordinary-life vignettes. The correct macro classification is therefore often **Recomposed**. This document deliberately does not assign every Part to a fabricated exact sequence of Korean side-story numbers.

### Volume 15: the hard prose/manhwa terminal boundary

Volume 15 provides the cleanest source boundary in the whole adaptation. In Part 4, V15 p053–p054 shows Jinwoo deciding to seal Suho’s power and shadow-related memories so he can live normally; p055–p059 stages the shadows’ farewell/dream transition; p060 closes the Part. This corresponds to the terminal condition described by the preserved Chugong-source audit. **Part 5 begins at p061.**

> **For this synthesis, V15 p061–p267 (Parts 5–6) is treated as manhwa-original relative to the supplied Chugong omnibus.**

This is stronger than the older project wording “the manhwa continues beyond the novel”: the collected-volume page boundary is now frozen.

## 6. Longitudinal adaptation pattern

Across the fifteen volumes, the adaptation does not merely “cut prose and add spectacle.” Four recurring operations are more precise:

1. **Continuous cognition → punctuated psychology.** The Korean novel distributes thought across prices, contracts, family chores, tactical calculation, embarrassment, resentment, institutional reasoning, and self-correction. The manhwa often compresses that continuum and substitutes one visually emphatic psychological tableau.
2. **Institutional/economic explanation → reaction and iconography.** Procedure, guild/state reasoning, and material incentives are routinely compressed while crowds, cameras, S-rank reactions, monster fear, kneeling, and sovereign imagery expand.
3. **Later information → earlier thematic confrontation.** The Architect material is the strongest example: later-source knowledge is brought forward so the Double Dungeon return becomes an explicit self-authorship scene.
4. **Epilogue plurality → recomposed closure and inheritance.** Volume 13 pulls aftermath material into the main ending; Volumes 14–15 braid individually titled external stories into long Parts; Volume 15 then creates an entirely new Suho continuation after the prose endpoint.
