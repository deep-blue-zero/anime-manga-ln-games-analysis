# 13 — Volume-by-Volume Evidence Ledger
## Audit layer for chronology, provenance, and retrospective control

## I. Purpose and evidentiary rule

This ledger records the fifteen-volume manhwa sequence before the thematic corpus is drafted. Its purpose is not to retell *Solo Leveling*. It preserves the changing interpretive horizon of the story:

> **what Jinwoo knows, what the reader can reasonably infer, and what remains unresolved at the close of each collected volume.**

Later revelations are recorded only in a distinct **Later correction** field. They do not overwrite the contemporary horizon.

The ledger follows these source rules:

- **Korean novel:** governing source for exact Korean wording, continuous interiority, motive, economics, institutions, side-story narration, and explicit metaphysics.
- **Official English manhwa:** governing source for visual form, collected-volume chapter order, panel sequence, adaptation structure, and manhwa-original material.
- **Original Korean manhwa text:** unavailable. Korean novel wording is therefore never represented as the original wording of a manhwa balloon.
- **Korean omnibus locator policy:** surviving headings are used through `전직 퀘스트`; later material is located by `index_split_XXX` region plus scene anchor. The digital splits are not treated as Korean print-volume or chapter boundaries.
- **Current source qualification:** the Korean omnibus boundaries and headings were established by the preserved raw-source audit. Exact paragraph micro-locators remain pending a current-session EPUB remount. No volume entry invents them.

### Evidence labels

- **TF** — explicit Korean-novel textual fact.
- **VF** — directly visible manhwa fact.
- **AF** — demonstrable adaptation fact.
- **KC** — Korean-language-dependent claim.
- **SI** — strong inference.
- **TI** — thematic interpretation.
- **VJ** — analytical value judgment.
- **UA** — unresolved ambiguity.

### Manhwa page convention

All page numbers are the logical `pNNN` identifiers recovered by natural sorting in Phase 1. Frontmatter and commercial/editorial backmatter are excluded from the narrative ranges. Title cards are included in chapter ranges.

Two collected-volume corrections matter:

- Volume 6 title cards occur at **p059, p143, and p231**, although the following archive segments begin at p061, p145, and p233.
- Volume 8 title cards occur at **p149 and p241**, although the following archive segments begin at p151 and p243.

The ledger uses the visually verified title-card pages rather than equating archive-segment boundaries with collected-volume chapter boundaries.

# Volume 01 — Constrained choice before autonomy

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Prologue, p007–p014; Chapter 1, “Double Dungeon,” p015–p122; Chapter 2, “Three Commandments,” p123–p252; Chapter 3, “Quest,” p253–p313.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p007–p313. Story panels begin at p008.
- **Archive segments:** `c000–c003`.
- **Korean correspondence:** `index_split_000.html`; surviving headings 1–7, from `프롤로그` through `페널티`, plus the onset of `일일 퀘스트`.
- **Correspondence confidence:** High.

## Volume thesis

> **A person can possess sound judgment, courage, and moral worth while lacking enough practical power to make any of them decisive.**

## Decisive events and contemporary horizon

At this point the Gates, ranks, and Hunter Association are accepted parts of the world. Awakening appears fixed. The Double Dungeon is unexplained. The System appears only as an impossible interface that has selected Jinwoo after the altar. Neither Jinwoo nor the reader can know that it is developmental machinery preparing a vessel.

## Jinwoo state at opening

- **TF:** Jinwoo is an E-rank hunter whose income is tied to his mother’s care and Jinah’s future.
- **TF/SI:** He continues work for which he is physically unsuited because the alternatives would transfer deprivation to his family.
- **TI:** His defining problem is not absence of will but **constrained choice**. He can choose among options whose material costs are coercive.
- **KC:** `인류 최약병기` is closer to **“humanity’s weakest weapon”** than merely “weakest hunter.” The nickname reduces him to failed utility.

## Jinwoo state at close

- **VF:** He survives the altar, awakens in a hospital, and alone sees the interface.
- **TF/VF:** The System can issue commands and penalties; refusal is already shown not to be costless.
- **Contemporary reading:** Jinwoo has gained a possible path of change, not yet autonomy. One structure of powerlessness may simply have been replaced by another structure of coercion.

## Development ledgers

### Power

- His principal capacity remains observational intelligence.
- He identifies the commandments while stronger hunters panic or die.
- The volume ends with potential power rather than demonstrated superiority.

### Autonomy

- The raid vote is formally voluntary but materially distorted by money, risk, and group pressure.
- Song Chiyul’s authority remains legitimate because he accepts responsibility and yields to better judgment.
- The temple god has overwhelming power and no legitimacy.
- The System has constitutive power over Jinwoo and has not sought meaningful consent.

### Ethics

- Jinwoo repeatedly directs attention toward preserving others before receiving any special power.
- He rejects Kim Sangshik’s attempt to move blame onto Song after the group’s own decision.
- **TI:** Moral formation precedes leveling. The later hero is not created ex nihilo by strength.

### Institutions

- The Association and hospital are not malicious; they are limited by a world model in which ranks do not change and the System does not exist.
- Woo Jinchul enters as competent institutional rationality facing an anomaly, not as an incompetent bureaucrat.
- The volume distinguishes **epistemic limitation** from institutional bad faith.

### Relationships

- Juhee sees the injured person rather than the rank.
- Song supplies accountable authority.
- Jinah’s future gives Jinwoo a concrete reason to keep taking risks.
- Kim teaches the negative lesson that kindness without boundaries can become self-erasure.

## Major visual evidence

- **Gaze:** the temple god’s eyes convert being seen into mortal threat.
- **Kneeling and standing:** hunters bow to coercive force; Jinwoo’s insight, not strength, organizes survival.
- **Scale:** his small damaged body is repeatedly placed beneath monumental statues.
- **Color transition:** blood-red sacrificial imagery gives way to the cold blue interface, exchanging religious horror for administrative coercion.
- **VF/TI:** The altar frames Jinwoo as disposable material before the System reframes him as usable material.

## Korean-language anchors

- `인류 최약병기` — “humanity’s weakest weapon.”
- `E급 헌터` — E-rank hunter.
- `페널티` — penalty; important because System participation is not initially free.
- `선택` — choice; a recurring word-family for the gap between formal decision and materially meaningful autonomy.
- `무력한 자의 용기` — “the courage of the powerless,” retained as a later interpretive anchor for conduct already visible here; exact paragraph backfill remains pending.

## Adaptation record

- **Preserved / Compressed:** family economics, embarrassment, privacy, and dry interior voice are substantially thinner in the manhwa.
- **Preserved / Expanded:** the Double Dungeon’s scale, gaze, dismemberment, and spatial terror are magnified.
- **Preserved / Compressed:** Korean narration gives more explicit weight to collective responsibility, scapegoating, and the legitimacy of Song’s authority.
- No major side-story insertion or narrative-level manhwa-original arc is identified.

## Later correction

- **Retrospective correction:** the System is not a neutral reward for courage. It is an apparatus designed for another purpose.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** Jinwoo’s moral judgment and family-directed care genuinely precede the System.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** rank never becomes equivalent to judgment, authority, or legitimacy.

## Question passed forward

> **Will leveling widen Jinwoo’s authorship of his life, or merely make him more useful to a power whose purpose he does not understand?**

# Volume 02 — Autonomy through leverage

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Chapter 3 continuation, p007–p062; Chapter 4, “Boss Round,” p063–p134; Chapter 5, “The Lizards,” p135–p297.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p007–p297.
- **Archive segments:** continuation `c003`; `c004–c005`.
- **Korean correspondence:** late `index_split_000` through approximately `001–002`; headings `일일 퀘스트`, `레벨 업!`, `도마뱀들`, `기브 앤 테이크`, `정리`, and `거래`.
- **Correspondence confidence:** High.

## Volume thesis

> **Power first matters because it gives Jinwoo leverage: the ability to refuse predation, preserve his own life, and make effort alter a future that had seemed fixed.**

## Decisive events and contemporary horizon

The System appears personalized, coercive, and astonishingly effective. Jinwoo knows that he alone can level, but not why. Hostile-human quests imply the interface may define enemies and authorize killing. Nothing yet establishes whether those rules are morally trustworthy.

## Jinwoo state at opening

- Newly selected Player.
- Physically weak relative to serious hunters, but capable of compounding growth.
- Still economically constrained and institutionally classified as E-rank.
- Learning through tests, farming, and controlled risk rather than blind confidence.

## Jinwoo state at close

- Has defeated Kasaka, killed hostile humans, and hidden the extent of his growth.
- Has entered a negotiated raid partnership with Yoo Jinho.
- Has acquired practical leverage against both monsters and exploitative hunters.
- Has begun articulating reciprocity as a moral grammar.

## Development ledgers

### Power

- Daily quests make effort reliably causal.
- Stat allocation, inventory, recovery, and instance access create a private economy of improvement.
- Kasaka is won through preparation and accumulated capability, not a spontaneous awakening.

### Autonomy

- The penalty zone shows that the System coerces even while it liberates.
- Hidden growth gives Jinwoo informational asymmetry and exit options.
- The Dongsuk betrayal is the first time he can prevent stronger people from treating him as disposable.
- Jinho’s offer becomes a bargain rather than dependency because Jinwoo can refuse.

### Ethics

- `기브 앤 테이크` becomes the explicit reciprocal frame: care and harm both call for return.
- **TF/TI:** The positive side is visible in protection and loyalty; the negative side appears in punitive retaliation.
- The System’s hostile-human condition complicates agency: Jinwoo acts to survive, but the interface also defines the permitted target.
- He does not hunt the Dongsuk party for pleasure. He recognizes their choice and refuses to die for their enrichment.

### Institutions

- Raid parties are revealed as weak institutions when private incentives, information asymmetry, and low accountability permit “lizard” predation.
- Hunter licensing authorizes entry but does not guarantee protection inside a Gate.
- Jinho seeks to build a guild through formal rules, but his family’s project initially treats hunters as organizational inputs.

### Relationships

- Jinho begins from fear and transactional need.
- `형님` carries an older-brother/kinship trajectory that “Boss” only partially preserves.
- Jinwoo’s refusal to exploit Jinho’s vulnerability is an early sign that leverage need not become domination.

## Major visual evidence

- **Predator reversal:** Kasaka coils around a solitary Jinwoo; later Jinwoo becomes the still center around panicking human predators.
- **Red lizard imagery:** the betrayers are visually reduced to a predatory type at the same moment Jinwoo must decide whether to treat them as persons responsible for choices.
- **Eyes and reflections:** Jinwoo increasingly sees himself as someone capable of returning another’s gaze rather than avoiding it.
- **Verticality:** dungeon descent becomes controlled self-testing rather than helpless entrapment.

## Korean-language anchors

- `일일 퀘스트` — daily quest.
- `레벨 업!` — level up; the world’s fixed-rank ontology is broken only for him.
- `도마뱀들` — “the lizards,” predatory hunters who shed companions to survive or profit.
- `기브 앤 테이크` — “give and take,” deliberately English inside the Korean prose.
- `형님` — respectful older-brother address, containing hierarchy and chosen kinship.

## Adaptation record

- **Preserved / Compressed:** routine experimentation, bodily discomfort, economics, and calculation are abbreviated.
- **Preserved / Expanded / Transformed / Manhwa-original:** the Kasaka sequence concentrates prior humiliation into a trauma tableau and gives manhwa Jinwoo a sharper proposition that knowledge without force cannot preserve consequences.
- **Preserved / Compressed:** the “lizards” sequence retains events but reduces ethical interiority and aftermath.
- **Preserved / Compressed:** Jinho’s relationship is intact, while the English address term narrows `형님`.

## Later correction

- **Retrospective correction:** the manhwa’s concentrated thought that overwhelming force may make knowledge or gratitude irrelevant is not Jinwoo’s stable doctrine. He continues to plan, test, ask, and learn.
- **Retrospective correction:** “ruthless but not cruel” cannot remain absolute, although Volume 2 itself supports a narrower defensive reading.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** his foundational desire is non-subordination, not control over the innocent.

## Question passed forward

> **Can a reciprocal ethic remain proportionate once Jinwoo no longer needs external restraint to survive?**

# Volume 03 — The burden and temptation of unilateral judgment

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Chapter 6, “B-rank Taesik Kang,” p007–p134; Chapter 7, “The Strange Raids,” p135–p186; Chapter 8, “Job-Change Quest,” p187–p313.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p007–p313.
- **Archive segments:** `c006–c008`.
- **Korean correspondence:** approximately `index_split_002–004`; headings `우연`, `B급 강태식`, `이상한 레이드`, `대박의 예감`, and `전직 퀘스트`, followed by the Igris/job-change continuation.
- **Correspondence confidence:** High.

## Volume thesis

> **Jinwoo becomes capable of judging and killing without institutional support, then receives a class whose solution to dependence is absolute command.**

## Decisive events and contemporary horizon

Taeshik proves that power can coexist with professional office, concealment, and enjoyment of murder. Jinwoo has reason to fear not merely becoming violent but becoming someone for whom asymmetry is pleasurable. The Shadow class appears as a reward and tactical miracle; its persons and consent problems are not yet legible.

## Jinwoo state at opening

- Hidden growth has made ordinary B-rank threat survivable.
- He still works through Jinho’s raids and keeps his anomaly private.
- His moral confidence is less developed than his combat confidence.
- Institutional authorities cannot see enough of the Gate to regulate his immediate judgment.

## Jinwoo state at close

- Has defeated Taeshik and survived the Job-Change Quest.
- Has defeated and recruited Igris.
- Has pronounced `일어나라` and created a first army.
- Has moved from private self-defense toward sovereign command over dependent beings.

## Development ledgers

### Power

- Taeshik shows the gap between measurable rank and lethal experience.
- The Job Quest tests endurance, improvisation, and refusal to surrender rather than simple output.
- Shadow extraction multiplies every victory into future capacity.

### Autonomy

- Jinwoo can now make the decisive judgment inside a closed Gate.
- The absence of immediate external review is both necessary and dangerous.
- The class change eliminates many logistical dependencies by making defeated enemies into assets.
- **TI:** autonomy begins converting into sovereignty because collective capability remains under one will.

### Ethics

- Taeshik is an immediate killer, but the scene also tests whether Jinwoo enjoys the same abyss.
- The manhwa’s “power rules,” abyss, and “broken inside” imagery make self-corruption a concentrated concern.
- Jinwoo’s protection of survivors differentiates him from Taeshik, but does not yet answer how he will govern punitive force.
- The first extraction raises no explicit consent debate in the contemporary narrative.

### Institutions

- Taeshik weaponizes institutional role and privileged information.
- Woo Jinchul and the Association can investigate afterward but cannot provide real-time oversight.
- The volume demonstrates why emergency individual judgment may be necessary without proving it legitimate in every case.

### Relationships

- Jinho’s loyalty grows through repeated chosen cooperation.
- Song and Juhee reconnect Jinwoo to his powerless past.
- Igris introduces a relationship structurally unlike Jinho’s: service is produced through conquest and class power, not free risk.

## Major visual evidence

- **Abyss imagery:** Taeshik and Jinwoo are framed as possible reflections, making moral difference a question rather than an assumption.
- **Old versus new self:** the Job Quest externalizes identity conflict through a visual confrontation between the wounded E-rank body and the increasingly cold combatant.
- **Throne and kneeling:** Igris and the new shadows establish monarchic iconography before the cosmic meaning of “Monarch” is known.
- **Black negative space:** psychological compression replaces much of the novel’s continuous thought.

## Korean-language anchors

- `B급 강태식` — B-rank Kang Taeshik.
- `살의` — killing intent; relevant to distinguishing danger, disposition, and pleasure in killing.
- `전직 퀘스트` — job-change quest.
- `일어나라` — “Arise,” an imperative that creates service.
- `군주` — monarch/lord/sovereign; its political force will grow beyond a class label.

## Adaptation record

- **Preserved / Expanded / Transformed / Manhwa-original:** the Taeshik scene intensifies moral self-recognition through abyss and corruption imagery.
- **Preserved / Compressed:** raid economics and ordinary cognition are reduced.
- **Preserved / Expanded:** Igris and the endless assault receive monumental visual weight.
- **Transformed / Expanded / Manhwa-original:** the old/new Jinwoo tableau externalizes a dispersed prose conflict.
- No side-story material is pulled forward.

## Later correction

- **Retrospective correction:** fear that Jinwoo is “broken” does not establish equivalence with Taeshik. His expanding protective circle and ability to release Byung-Gyu later provide strong counterevidence.
- **Retrospective correction:** shadow loyalty cannot be presumed consensual merely because affection later appears sincere.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** the Shadow Army is the decisive mechanism by which “solo” becomes collective capability without divided sovereignty.

## Question passed forward

> **What can correct a sovereign whose most reliable followers are structurally unable to withdraw allegiance?**

# Volume 04 — Autonomy becomes sovereignty

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Chapter 9, “The Red Gate,” p007–p192; Chapter 10, “Conquering the Demon’s Castle,” p193–p320.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p007–p320; standalone continuation page p321 excluded.
- **Archive segments:** `c009–c010`.
- **Korean correspondence:** approximately `index_split_005–007`; Red Gate, ice world, Baruka, Kim Chul/Iron, and the transition into the Demon Castle/Elixir quest.
- **Correspondence confidence:** High at scene-zone level.

## Volume thesis

> **Jinwoo learns to lead living people without treating superior force as automatic jurisdiction, while his Shadow Army turns independence into portable sovereignty.**

## Decisive events and contemporary horizon

The Red Gate appears to be a rare dungeon abnormality. Jinwoo’s class is understood mechanically, not cosmologically. Kim Chul’s conversion into Iron can be read as tactical retribution and asset acquisition; retained shadow subjectivity is not yet established clearly enough to settle the ethics.

## Jinwoo state at opening

- Newly formed shadow commander.
- Still officially E-rank and deliberately hidden.
- Testing the limits of extraction, storage, command, and mana.
- Oriented toward curing his mother rather than public heroism.

## Jinwoo state at close

- Has led survivors through a Red Gate and defeated Baruka.
- Has converted Kim Chul into Iron.
- Has shown that living survivors may follow his terms or refuse.
- Has begun the Demon Castle/Elixir project, turning sovereign power toward restoration.

## Development ledgers

### Power

- Shadows provide scouting, attrition, labor, and force multiplication.
- Iron demonstrates that a human enemy can be incorporated into the army.
- Demon Castle makes power a means toward healing rather than only survival.

### Autonomy

- Jinwoo offers the Red Gate survivors a real, if harsh, choice: accept his plan or leave.
- He does not claim authority over them solely because he is strongest.
- His shadows, by contrast, receive commands rather than negotiated terms.
- **TI:** the volume establishes a durable split between his treatment of living adults and constitutively subordinate subjects.

### Ethics

- Protection is conditional on following a survival plan, but not on personal submission after the crisis.
- Kim Chul’s aggression makes defensive force necessary; turning him into Iron adds punitive and instrumental dimensions after death.
- The mother-cure objective shows that sovereignty can be restorative and relational.

### Institutions

- White Tiger’s training structure becomes helpless once the Red Gate invalidates its assumptions.
- Baek Yoonho demonstrates expert perception and institutional concern, even though he cannot enter.
- The incident does not prove institutions useless; it proves they cannot act across every abnormal boundary in real time.

### Relationships

- Han Song-Yi allows Jinwoo to act as an older-brother protector beyond Jinah.
- The survivors see both his severity and reliability.
- Iron’s comic loyalty later obscures the coercive origin visible here.
- His mother remains the absent relational center directing the Demon Castle quest.

## Major visual evidence

- **White snow / black shadow:** Jinwoo’s darkness becomes protection inside an environment coded as lethal purity.
- **Red threshold:** the Gate marks separation from normal authority and accountability.
- **Standing / kneeling:** Iron’s recruitment repeats the reversal from Jinwoo beneath the temple god to subjects beneath Jinwoo.
- **Silhouette:** the cloak-and-army framing begins making him look less like a hunter and more like a ruler.

## Korean-language anchors

- `군주` — monarch/sovereign.
- `일어나라` — Arise.
- `그림자 병사` — shadow soldier.
- `선택` — choice; especially important in contrasting living survivors with shadows.
- Exact Red Gate and Iron paragraph locators remain pending EPUB remount.

## Adaptation record

- **Preserved / Compressed:** survival reasoning, group psychology, and prose logistics are tightened.
- **Preserved / Expanded:** environmental hostility, Baruka, and army scale are enlarged visually.
- No major side-story pull-forward or source-free arc is identified.
- The manhwa’s monarchic visual grammar makes the ethical discomfort of kneeling more immediate than the prose alone.

## Later correction

- **Retrospective correction:** Iron’s later affection does not retroactively establish free initial allegiance.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** later permission-seeking with Son Kihoon, Park Jongsoo, and the Association confirms that Jinwoo does not generally infer jurisdiction from superior force.
- **Retrospective correction:** “autonomy becomes sovereignty” is descriptive, not automatic moral endorsement.

## Question passed forward

> **Can Jinwoo preserve the distinction between commanding dependent shadows and respecting the jurisdiction of living people as the power gap becomes absolute?**

# Volume 05 — Recognition without ownership

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Chapter 11, “The Vanguard of the Excavation Team,” p005–p074; Chapter 12, “The S-rank Luggage Carrier,” p075–p228; Chapter 13, “The Tenth S-rank Hunter,” p229–p314.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p005–p314; continuation page p315 excluded.
- **Archive segments:** `c011–c013`.
- **Korean correspondence:** approximately `index_split_007–010`; Hunters Guild mining/porter material, High Orc dungeon, Son Kihoon, Cha Hae-In, remeasurement, and public S-rank recognition.
- **Correspondence confidence:** High at scene-zone level.

## Volume thesis

> **Public recognition corrects Jinwoo’s misclassification but immediately creates new attempts to recruit, price, own, and narrate him.**

## Decisive events and contemporary horizon

The reader can now know that Jinwoo is far beyond ordinary S-rank measurement, but the origin and ceiling of that growth remain unknown. Institutions can recognize the anomaly only through incomplete instruments. Cha’s response marks personal perception, but romance is not yet a settled endpoint.

## Jinwoo state at opening

- Officially E-rank, privately sovereign.
- Curious about elite raids and willing to enter through low-status labor rather than reveal himself.
- Focused on information, mother’s cure, and controlled exposure.

## Jinwoo state at close

- Has rescued the Hunters Guild strike team and acquired Tusk.
- Has requested formal reevaluation.
- Has become Korea’s tenth recognized S-rank hunter.
- Has gained visibility while refusing immediate absorption into an existing guild.

## Development ledgers

### Power

- Tusk and the High Orc force expand army scale and magical capability.
- Jinwoo’s strength exceeds the raid’s formal command structure, yet he waits before intervening.
- Public measurement confirms anomaly without defining it.

### Autonomy

- He accepts recognition because misclassification has become impractical.
- Recognition widens legal and economic options.
- Guild interest demonstrates that social freedom can shrink when exceptional value becomes visible.
- He seeks a status that prevents control without surrendering privacy entirely.

### Ethics

- He asks Son Kihoon for permission before taking over the Hunters Guild’s monsters.
- This is a crucial jurisdictional act: capability does not erase the raid leader’s recognized authority.
- He protects the team without demanding permanent loyalty.
- Shadow extraction of Tusk remains instrumental; the source has not yet fully developed shadow personhood.

### Institutions

- The Hunters Guild is competent but outmatched by bad information.
- The Association’s measurement process is procedurally reasonable inside known rules.
- Go Gunhee’s interest begins the contrast between stewardship and acquisition.
- Guild recruitment is not inherently illegitimate; presumed entitlement to Jinwoo is.

### Relationships

- Cha Hae-In detects Jinwoo differently from other hunters and becomes a counter-gaze to rank alone.
- Son Kihoon receives respect as a legitimate commander despite inferiority in force.
- Jinho remains the chosen partner positioned to accompany a public future.

## Major visual evidence

- **Uniform reversal:** the porter/miner body is visually coded as low-status while carrying overwhelming concealed force.
- **Gaze:** Cha, Baek, guild masters, cameras, and crowds begin constructing Jinwoo through reaction.
- **Scale:** Tusk and the High Orc temple allow the manhwa to stage the moment modern institutions encounter mythic force.
- **Public light:** the dark private Player is brought before cameras and measurement apparatus.

## Korean-language anchors

- `재각성` — reawakening; the institution’s best available category for an impossible case.
- `S급` — S-rank.
- `형님` — Jinho’s relational address.
- `군주` — sovereign/Monarch, still concealed beneath public hunter classification.
- `선택` — Jinwoo chooses recognition without accepting ownership.

## Adaptation record

- **Preserved / Compressed:** porter labor, institutional procedure, and internal calculation receive less prose-like continuity.
- **Preserved / Expanded:** Cha’s sensory recognition, Tusk’s scale, and public reaction are enlarged.
- **Preserved:** permission from Son Kihoon remains important evidence against the idea that Jinwoo treats strength as automatic authority.
- No side-story material is pulled forward.

## Later correction

- **Retrospective strengthening:** recognition becomes geopolitical commodification in Volumes 10–11.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** Cha becomes central to Jinwoo’s life beyond usefulness, but Volume 5 should not be rewritten as if that future were already certain.
- **Retrospective correction:** public S-rank status does not make Jinwoo institutionally accountable in proportion to his actual power.

## Question passed forward

> **Can Jinwoo use public status and institutions without allowing guilds, states, or audiences to convert recognition into ownership?**


## Synthesis destinations

Documents 03, 05, 07, and 10.

# Volume 06 — Power chooses what it serves

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Chapter 14, “Esil the Demon Noble,” p005–p058; Chapter 15, “The Alliance,” p059–p142; Chapter 16, “Showdown of the S-ranks,” p143–p230; Chapter 17, “The Jeju Island Raid,” p231–p302.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p005–p302; continuation page p303 excluded.
- **Archive segments:** `c014–c017`; collected title cards precede three segment boundaries by two pages.
- **Korean correspondence:** approximately `index_split_010–014`; Demon Castle/Esil/Elixir, mother’s recovery, Korea–Japan S-rank interaction, Jeju planning, and the opening operation.

## Volume thesis

- **Retrospective strengthening:** Go’s institutional legacy becomes the clearest contrast to power that cannot survive its sovereign.

# Volume 12 — Inheritance without erasure

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Chapter 34, “The Death of the Player,” p005–p070; Chapter 35, “At the Edge of Death,” p071–p222; Chapter 36, “The Truth,” p223–p299.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p005–p299; continuation page p300 excluded.
- **Archive segments:** `c034–c036`.
- **Korean correspondence:** approximately `index_split_026–029`; Monarch ambush, Jinwoo’s death, Ashborn’s history, the Architect and System, full inheritance, Bellion, and the reunited Shadow Army.
- **Correspondence confidence:** High at scene-zone level.

## Volume thesis

> **Ashborn’s inheritance becomes legitimate not because the power is benevolent, but because Jinwoo is finally offered a choice that preserves rather than replaces his selfhood.**

## Decisive events and contemporary horizon

The volume resolves the System’s immediate purpose and Ashborn’s identity. Jinwoo can now understand that “Player” was a developmental role, but the final cosmic war and reset remain unknown. The ethical difference between Architect and Ashborn becomes visible through how each treats the inheritor.

## Jinwoo state at opening

- Humanity’s strongest active protector, still mediated by a System he did not design.
- A target whose enemies understand more about his ontological role than he does.
- Personally sovereign in the human world, but not yet author of the machinery producing his power.

## Jinwoo state at close

- Has died, confronted the source history, and chosen to return.
- Possesses the full Shadow Monarch power without being erased by Ashborn.
- No longer needs the System as external command structure; Bellion and the full army recognize him directly.

## Development ledgers

### Power

- The Black Heart and accumulated leveling are revealed as preparation for full inheritance.
- System-mediated growth ends; power becomes internalized capacity.
- `일어나라` shifts from a class command supplied by an interface to Jinwoo’s own sovereign speech.

### Autonomy

- Architect design treated Jinwoo as a vessel and therefore threatened replacement.
- Ashborn’s decisive act is recognition: he allows Jinwoo’s `자아` and `의식` to remain and presents a genuine `선택`.
- Jinwoo’s return converts inherited force into self-authored obligation.

### Ethics

- The legitimacy of inheritance rests on consent and continuity of personhood, not merely beneficial outcome.
- Jinwoo chooses renewed danger because others remain exposed; the choice is protective without being externally compelled.
- **UA:** Bellion’s and the older shadows’ constitutive allegiance remains sincere but not demonstrably recusant.

### Institutions

- The System dissolves after fulfilling its developmental function.
- This is liberation from external rule, but also further concentration: no impersonal mechanism now stands between Jinwoo and absolute power.
- Cosmic orders—the Rulers and Monarchs—remain war structures rather than publicly accountable institutions.

### Relationships

- Ashborn becomes the first cosmic being to recognize Jinwoo as successor rather than replaceable material.
- Human attachments supply the reason to return from death.
- Bellion’s kneeling recognition inaugurates a court and society whose political status remains unresolved.

## Major visual evidence

- **Death and white void:** physical defeat opens an interior space of choice rather than simple resurrection.
- **Throne / kneeling army:** inherited kingship is monumentalized, but Jinwoo’s human silhouette remains continuous.
- **System dissolution:** interface fragments visualize appropriation ending in authorship.
- **Black and white duality:** Ashborn is framed as death-power capable of preserving a living self.

## Korean-language anchors

- `플레이어` — Player.
- `시스템` — System.
- `자아` — self/ego.
- `의식` — consciousness.
- `선택` — choice.
- `일어나라` — arise.

## Adaptation record

- **Preserved / Compressed:** Ashborn’s history and cosmological exposition.
- **Expanded:** throne, army, death-space, and inheritance spectacle.
- **Transformed / Expanded:** the visual return of sovereign command concentrates self-authorship.
- **Omitted / Compressed:** some prose distinctions among motive, cosmic history, and System operation.

## Later correction

- **Retrospective correction:** the System was never neutral gamification; it was coercive developmental machinery.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** power does not create Jinwoo’s values—the full inheritance magnifies choices already visible before leveling.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** Ashborn’s legitimacy contrasts with the Architect because continuity without erasure matters more than intended outcome alone.

## Question passed forward

> **Once power is fully his, what prevents Jinwoo’s chosen protective code from becoming another unreviewable system imposed on everyone else?**

# Volume 13 — Freedom beyond usefulness

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Chapter 37, “The Dragon King,” p005–p058; Chapter 38, “The Final War I,” p059–p158; Chapter 39, “The Final War II,” p159–p228; Chapter 40, “Solo Leveling,” p229–p288.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p005–p288; “To Be Continued” p289 and later backmatter excluded.
- **Archive segments:** `c037–c040`.
- **Korean correspondence:** `index_split_029–030`; Antares and the final war, Cup of Reincarnation, dimensional conflict, formal main-story ending, plus selected post-main-story material pulled into the manhwa’s Chapter 40 closure.
- **Correspondence confidence:** High for the main-war sequence; high for the boundary crossing, with micro-allocation of external stories pending.

## Volume thesis

> **Jinwoo achieves complete sovereignty, rejects possession of the world, then makes the most procedurally radical decision in the series before learning that life need not be justified by usefulness.**

## Decisive events and contemporary horizon

The cosmic structure is now known. Humanity cannot defeat the Monarchs independently. The Cup offers a replacement history, but neither humanity nor the erased persons participate in the decision. The manhwa’s Chapter 40 also moves beyond Chugong’s formal main-story endpoint to stage Jinwoo’s post-mission crisis.

## Jinwoo state at opening

- Full Shadow Monarch confronting Antares as an equal sovereign.
- Bound by chosen guardianship to humanity, not by species destiny or institutional command.
- Still oriented toward a mission whose completion appears to define his purpose.

## Jinwoo state at close

- Has rejected Antares’s ownership logic and fought the war outside ordinary history.
- Returns as the sole bearer of an erased world’s memory.
- Briefly wonders whether a world that no longer needs his power still needs him; Cha and ordinary relationship answer without assigning a new mission.

## Development ledgers

### Power

- Jinwoo coordinates a near-total shadow civilization and fights at planetary/cosmic scale.
- The Cup lets him alter history’s conditions rather than merely win within them.
- Power reaches its endpoint: no further strength is required to solve the external conflict.

### Autonomy

- Antares offers rule; Jinwoo refuses to infer ownership from victory.
- The reset is maximal self-authorship and maximal paternalism at once: he chooses the history everyone else will inhabit.
- Postwar freedom becomes the harder question of living when no emergency dictates identity.

### Ethics

- **TF/TI:** The reset is benevolent in intention and restores enormous numbers of lives.
- **VJ:** It remains procedurally radical: no public consent, review, or representation exists, and some identities and relationships are overwritten.
- The narrative persuades through restored persons rather than a theory of legitimate historical authority.

### Institutions

- Humanity is saved by an exceptional individual operating beyond every institution.
- The hunter order is not reformed; its ontological cause is removed.
- This resolves suffering while bypassing the political work of building durable structures for the old world.

### Relationships

- Cha’s promise and recognition become the manhwa’s strongest bridge from cosmic utility back to personhood.
- Jinwoo’s invisible sacrifice deprives most beneficiaries of the ability to recognize or reciprocate it.
- The restored dead demonstrate the value of the choice while also embodying the autonomy problem.

## Major visual evidence

- **Red dragon / black sovereign:** destructive possession and protective sovereignty receive opposing mythic palettes.
- **Global battlefield:** modern institutions become visually minute beside cosmic force.
- **Cup and temporal montage:** lives are restored through images of erased causality.
- **Ordinary daylight:** the final scale contracts from armies to human presence.

## Korean-language anchors

- `파멸의 군주` — Monarch of Destruction.
- `윤회의 잔` — Cup of Reincarnation.
- `선택` — choice.
- `나 혼자만 레벨업` — “Only I level up”; ontological singularity rather than mature social isolation.

## Adaptation record

- **Preserved / Expanded:** Antares and final-war spectacle.
- **Preserved / Compressed:** strategic and metaphysical prose reasoning.
- **Relocated / Recomposed / Expanded:** Chapter 40 incorporates post-main-story material into the numbered manhwa ending.
- **AF:** Chugong’s formal main story closes around Go Gunhee’s restored heartbeat; the manhwa closes around Jinwoo’s reason to remain after usefulness.

## Later correction

- **Retrospective correction:** the reset is not a clean moral eraser; later stories preserve memory scars, overwritten identities, and unequal restoration.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** “solo” ends as singularity without relational isolation.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** ordinary life is not decorative reward but the final test of selfhood beyond function.

## Question passed forward

> **Who is the strongest weapon in existence when the world is safe enough that no weapon is needed?**

# Volume 14 — The sovereign in ordinary life

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Side Stories Part 1, p005–p122; Part 2, p123–p216; Part 3, p217–p306.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p005–p306; continuation page p307 excluded.
- **Archive segments:** `c040#1–c040#3`.
- **Korean correspondence:** remainder of `index_split_030` and substantial material from `index_split_031`; multiple `외전` passages braided into larger collected Parts. Exact story-number allocation remains **Source-uncertain** pending Korean paragraph remount.
- **Correspondence confidence:** High at scene-zone level; medium for individual `외전` numbering.

## Volume thesis

> **Without an apocalypse to assign his role, Jinwoo voluntarily enters ordinary relationships and institutions while retaining an exceptional jurisdiction that those institutions cannot review.**

## Decisive events and contemporary horizon

The reset world is stable, but Jinwoo alone retains complete knowledge. Residual recognition appears in Woo and Cha. The volume tests whether stable personality survives changed history and whether extraordinary protection can coexist with ordinary law.

## Jinwoo state at opening

- Sole historical witness and hidden guardian.
- Free of hunter institutions, public worship, and strategic ownership.
- Potentially vulnerable to the belief that completed usefulness has made his continued existence unnecessary.

## Jinwoo state at close

- Reconnected to Woo, Cha, family, work, and a socially active shadow retinue.
- Demonstrates that he can choose ordinary institutional participation.
- Still reserves supernatural surveillance, memory control, and punishment to himself.

## Development ledgers

### Power

- Power is concealed rather than reduced: memory can be restored, shadows can police unseen threats, and punishment can exceed ordinary human limits.
- The absence of spectacle reveals stable habits more clearly than another escalation would.

### Autonomy

- Jinwoo enters policing and relationship voluntarily; ordinary structure is no longer experienced as ownership.
- Yet the constitution does not absorb the sword. He decides when supernatural action should supersede ordinary procedure.
- **TI:** This is institutional compatibility at its mature limit.

### Ethics

- The detective-era abuser receives prolonged supernatural suffering after Jinwoo’s private judgment.
- **VJ:** The act is deliberately cruel and extrajudicial even if the target is guilty and Jinwoo is not motivated by pleasure.
- Memory restoration and withholding continue the series’ unresolved protective-paternalism problem.

### Institutions

- Police work, Woo’s competence, and ordinary investigation are treated with respect.
- Woo’s personal trust in Jinwoo partly substitutes for formal accountability.
- Institutions supply continuity and social order; Jinwoo supplies impossible capability.

### Relationships

- Woo becomes a witness whose recovered memory restores reciprocal recognition.
- Cha is drawn toward Jinwoo without the old hunter hierarchy, strengthening person-to-person attraction.
- Shadows display humor, hierarchy, affection, and cultural life beyond combat function.

## Major visual evidence

- **Ordinary city / hidden shadow:** cosmic force inhabits daylight without demanding public centrality.
- **Residual gaze:** Woo and Cha recognize absence before recovering explanation.
- **Police framing:** uniform and procedure surround a figure whose ultimate power remains private.
- **Romantic scale:** races, meetings, and domestic spaces reduce Jinwoo from public myth to available person.

## Korean-language anchors

- `기억` — memory.
- `선택` — choice.
- `형` — older brother/hyung, emphasizing ordinary relational identity.
- `군주` — sovereign/Monarch, now coexisting with mundane roles.

## Adaptation record

- **Relocated / Recomposed:** several external stories are integrated into three manhwa Parts.
- **Expanded:** romance, ordinary-world visual continuity, and shadow comedy/sociality.
- **Compressed / Omitted:** some prose interiority and exact side-story separations.
- **Source-uncertain:** one-to-one `외전` numbering for every Part subsection.

## Later correction

- **Retrospective correction:** Jinwoo is not anti-institutional; he can voluntarily work inside an institution he respects.
- **Retrospective correction:** “not sadistic” cannot be converted into “incapable of cruelty.”
- **Retrospective strengthening:** stable personality is often clearer in ordinary decisions than in battle extremity.

## Question passed forward

> **Can exceptional private judgment coexist indefinitely with ordinary law without turning personal trust into the only form of accountability?**

# Volume 15 — Inheritance without repetition

## Source and page record

- **Manhwa chapter range:** Side Stories Part 4, p005–p060; Part 5, p061–p142; Part 6, p143–p267.
- **Narrative apparatus:** p005–p267; creator farewell and promotional backmatter p268–p272 excluded.
- **Archive segments:** `c040#4–c040#6`.
- **Korean correspondence:** Part 4 corresponds to late `외전`/`후일담` material in `index_split_031–032`. Chugong’s supplied prose ends after young Suho’s power and relevant memories are sealed and the shadows appear in his dream.
- **Hard source boundary:** **p061–p267 is manhwa-original relative to the supplied Chugong omnibus.**
- **Correspondence confidence:** High.

## Volume thesis

> **The final autonomy problem is generational: can Jinwoo preserve Suho’s right to become himself while transmitting power and judgment without demanding repetition of the trauma that formed his father?**

## Decisive events and contemporary horizon

Part 4 completes Chugong’s postscript logic. Parts 5–6 are an authored manhwa continuation and must not be treated as Korean-novel evidence. They extend the ending from heredity toward pedagogy, restraint, and distinct succession.

## Jinwoo state at opening

- Husband, father, hidden guardian, and sovereign whose life has acquired reasons beyond emergency.
- Confronted with a child whose inherited capacity could erase ordinary development if restored too early.

## Jinwoo state at close

- Has sealed and later structured Suho’s access to power rather than simply handing over sovereignty.
- Functions as designer and teacher, uncomfortably reusing System-like machinery while attempting a different ethical purpose.
- Allows the next generation’s decisive lesson to concern judgment—including retreat—not merely victory.

## Development ledgers

### Power

- **Heredity:** Suho possesses latent connection to Shadow Monarch capability.
- **Inheritance:** meaning, timing, and use remain open rather than genetically predetermined.
- Parts 5–6 transform power restoration into staged testing and pedagogy.

### Autonomy

- Sealing Suho’s memories and power is paternalistic, but aimed at preserving ordinary childhood rather than maximizing utility.
- Jinwoo risks reproducing the Architect’s environmental authorship; the ethical difference is that Suho is meant to remain distinct rather than become a replacement vessel.
- The final door leaves succession incomplete and therefore preserves future choice.

### Ethics

- The volume rejects suffering as credential: Jinwoo’s trauma need not be reproduced to make Suho worthy.
- The manhwa-original trials refine `무력한 자의 용기`: courage does not require prideful persistence when retreat is the wiser protection of self and others.
- **VJ:** Good parenting here means delaying capability until judgment can develop, while remaining alert to the danger of scripting the child’s identity.

### Institutions

- Family becomes the primary transmission structure.
- Repurposed System machinery remains private and unaccountable, but its stated aim changes from vessel production to developmental testing.
- No public institution governs the inheritance of Jinwoo’s world-altering capacity.

### Relationships

- Jinwoo and Cha provide an ordinary household around extraordinary heredity.
- Shadows’ farewell to the young master displays sincere affection while retaining constitutive hierarchy.
- Suho is positioned as successor without being reduced to copy.

## Major visual evidence

- **Home and family:** the strongest being is repeatedly framed through domestic availability.
- **Farewell dream:** ants, knights, and orcs turn the army into remembered childhood community.
- **Interface and arena:** inherited machinery echoes Jinwoo’s coercive origin while changing pedagogical intent.
- **Doors / thresholds:** succession is staged as approach and choice, not automatic possession.

## Korean-language anchors

- `기억` — memory.
- `자아` — self.
- `의식` — consciousness.
- `선택` — choice.
- `후일담` — postscript/after-story; source category distinct from the completed `외전` sequence.

## Adaptation record

- **Preserved / Expanded / Recomposed:** Part 4 integrates Chugong’s terminal family-and-sealing material.
- **Manhwa-original:** Parts 5–6, including the extended adolescent-Suho trials and final-door structure.
- **AF:** Claims about Suho’s class, the pedagogical System, retreat lesson, and later trials belong to the completed manhwa adaptation, not Chugong’s prose.

## Later correction

- **Retrospective correction:** heredity is not inheritance; capability does not determine identity or rightful role.
- **Retrospective correction:** Jinwoo’s suffering was not good merely because capacities emerged from it.
- **Retrospective strengthening:** mature courage includes refusing pride-driven battle when retreat better serves judgment.

## Question passed forward

> **Can Jinwoo transmit wisdom without converting love, protection, and superior knowledge into authorship of the person Suho is allowed to become?**
