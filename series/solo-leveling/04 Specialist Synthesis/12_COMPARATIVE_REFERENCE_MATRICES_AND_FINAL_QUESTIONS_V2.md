# 12 — Comparative Reference Matrices and Final Questions
## Portable conclusions for future analysis

## Scope and use

This document compresses the specialist arguments in Documents 01–11 and the chronological evidence in Document 13 for comparison with other characters, political orders, inheritances, and adaptations. The cited cases and specialist arguments set the scope of each comparison.

Capability, authority, legitimacy, attachment, and consent describe different relations. The same event can establish effective protection and raise a question about who decided, which choices were available, and which later evidence was already known.

> Sung Jinwoo learns to protect people and to recognize legitimate authority in particular settings. He also decides when to set procedure aside, who may keep certain memories, and how to punish outside public law. His restraint and attachments help explain why he makes some choices and provide grounds for criticizing others. They supply neither review of his exceptional decisions nor a repeatable way to authorize a successor.

### Developmental compression

The most portable form of Jinwoo's arc is therefore:

> **constrained agency → effective agency → leverage → negative liberty → sovereignty → infrastructure → self-authorship → chosen guardianship → historical sovereignty → life beyond usefulness → generational stewardship**

Each solution creates new choices. Power relieves helplessness and makes covert compulsion possible. Sovereignty reduces subordination and creates jurisdictional conflicts. Self-authorship defeats the Architect's replacement plan while leaving the inheritor responsible for what his new powers do to others. Parenthood exposes the same tension in the design of Suho's development.

### Comparative conclusion

Go and Woo can preserve an office, delegate roles, and coordinate under rules even where their institutions lack the strength to stop a powerful enemy. Jinwoo supplies that strength in emergencies, especially when time and reach defeat ordinary procedure. Thomas, Antares and the Architect show how power can also be used to claim entitlement over others. Jinwoo's retaliations and private punishment make that danger relevant to him even where his objectives differ.

The chief political problem is the mismatch between exceptional ability to intervene and defensible authority to decide for the people affected.

### Relationship conclusion

Jinho decides to oppose Dongsuk while exposed to danger, and later treats Jinwoo as family. In between, Jinwoo explicitly makes use of Jinho's fear. These changes distinguish fidelity from structural enforceability and show how one relationship can contain both.

Shadows can offer insight and disagreement despite compulsory allegiance. Human intimates can withhold or refuse in ways the army's ontology makes doubtful. Love, friendship, and public office thereby give Jinwoo reasons to reconsider in particular situations. None performs consistent review of his sovereign decisions.

### Shadow conclusion

The strongest final classification remains:

> **conscious dependent subjects with socially rich personhood under metaphysically embedded absolute monarchy.**

The narrator states compelled absolute loyalty and individually retained consciousness together in ch. 226 · s028¶1367–1397. Memory, preferences, attachments and advice give shadows independent moral standing. The text shows local resistance, divided orders, and personal initiative. It does not establish a freely revocable relation to Jinwoo.

The central unresolved distinction is therefore:

> Sincere affection, conscious initiative, continuing consent and a right of exit require separate evidence.

# 6. Final open questions

### Additional final analytical questions

Several broader questions remain useful for future comparative work even where this corpus has a stable answer:

1. **What does Jinwoo fear about weakness?** Exposure to exploitation and failure to care for others accompany the particular fear and guilt of the temple sequence. He already has causal agency through observation and persuasion while physically weak.
2. **Does Jinwoo adopt a comprehensive doctrine of rule by the strong?** He repeatedly protects the weak and acknowledges public roles, while in some settings treating strength as license to coerce, punish or control terms.
3. **Why seek sovereignty with limited interest in public rule?** Jinwoo explicitly values independence from others' demands and the pleasure of further growth. The fraudulent offer in ch. 241 fails through distrust. His desire for ordinary participation and family gives a separate ground for the stronger longitudinal reading.
4. **What can institutions do that Jinwoo cannot?** Preserve records, distribute authority, routinize coordination, and maintain offices through different occupants. A succession like Ashborn's can carry personnel forward without providing those public functions.
5. **What can Jinwoo do that institutions cannot?** Act at overwhelming speed and scale, rescue where existing capacity fails, and undertake the renewed war after the Rulers grant the Cup.
6. **How does the final succession differ from the Architect's design?** Ashborn chooses assimilation and allows Jinwoo to choose his reality. Jinwoo retains recognizable agency and relationships through a change that the narrator describes as joining personalities or consciousness.
7. **What does `solo` mean in this corpus?** The early System makes one Player's progression exceptional. It remains possible for him to depend on people, institutions, and an inherited army.
8. **What does `leveling` come to include?** Learning how to act with more power and deciding where to stop, release or return. Recurrent counterexamples keep judgment from replicating a steadily rising numerical stat.

# 7. Compact final propositions

The following propositions are intended as reusable comparison anchors. Each is compressed from arguments owned by Documents 01–11 and should be unpacked through those documents rather than cited as a substitute for them.

1. **Non-subordination is an explicit motive alongside provision, pleasure in growth, affection, and revenge.**
2. **Limited strength reduces Jinwoo's options. Observation and collective action already give some of his choices life-and-death force.**
3. **Power makes Jinwoo's judgment effective. It does not automatically make that judgment legitimate.**
4. **The System liberates Jinwoo from fixed rank while simultaneously trying to author what that liberation is for.**
5. **Self-authorship survives the System and Ashborn through continued agency within a genuinely inherited and assimilated power.**
6. **Jinwoo negotiates with public institutions and serves in one while retaining a private jurisdiction in some matters.**
7. **The sword–constitution problem survives because exceptional capability and legitimate public authority remain located in different places.**
8. **Go Gunhee's importance is that he translates personal stature into stewardship and succession rather than personal rule.**
9. **Woo Jinchul demonstrates the value of evidence, continuity, and revisable institutional judgment—and the danger of letting justified trust become exemption.**
10. **Thomas Andre uses fear to secure standing and fights partly for a subordinate. Surrender, mercy and later gratitude make the foil more than a difference of raw power.**
11. **Antares chooses destruction and deception, and Jinwoo chooses substantial human protection. Jinwoo's wartime and police choices still need their own account of authority.**
12. **The Architect's deepest wrong is not that it makes Jinwoo strong; it treats a developing person as replaceable substrate for a function.**
13. **Ashborn defers to an inheritor whose agency persists through personal assimilation.**
14. **A promise or expression of trust has different weight from structurally enforced allegiance.**
15. **Jinho's fidelity survives a change from a fearful and unequal bargain to freely renewed attachment.**
16. **Members of the Shadow Army can question choices. Their advice has no demonstrated authority over the final command.**
17. **The army combines genuine subjectivity with narratorial evidence of compelled absolute loyalty.**
18. **Benevolent treatment improves dependent life without automatically making dependence revocable.**
19. **Jinwoo deliberately extends fear or suffering in some scenes, with Kargalgan and Seo Gyu-nam offering distinct war and police-era examples.**
20. **Protection becomes paternalism when capability allows Jinwoo to replace another person's decision rather than merely expand that person's options.**
21. **The reset restores lives through a jointly enabled choice in which the affected human populations have no voice.**
22. **Ordinary life is not an anticlimax; it is the test of whether Jinwoo possesses a self after usefulness.**
23. **A person is not worthless because capability is insufficient, and does not become worthless when extraordinary capability is no longer needed.**
24. **The best generational inheritance does not reproduce the trauma that created the parent.**
25. **V15 gives the stated value of retreat to Jinwoo in a conversation with the shadows. The door leaves Suho's eventual judgment open.**
26. **`나 혼자만 레벨업` bears an ontological reading about exceptional progression alongside the story's chosen relationships and dependencies.**
27. **Judgment, including recurring failures of restraint, becomes a subject of development alongside increasing capacity.**
28. **In selected scenes prose and panels disclose different kinds of access to motive and fear.**
29. **Recomposition changes the audience's knowledge and sometimes the function of an adapted event.**
30. **Jinwoo's escape from imposed authorship makes his own control of other lives a central question of the later story.**

# Final comparative synthesis

The question that begins *Solo Leveling* is easy to state:

> **What if the weakest hunter alone could keep becoming stronger?**

Strength enlarges Jinwoo's options. Economically it lets him refuse exploitative work and provide for his family. Militarily it gives him an army whose dependence raises the stakes of each command. Metaphysically it creates an opportunity to resist the Architect and choose continued life with his friends and family. The reset transforms his freedom to act into a decision about conditions under which everyone else will act. Ordinary service and fatherhood keep that tension visible after the war.

Jinwoo repeatedly preserves the space for a life apart from combat and public recognition. He can also delay rescue to secure an advantage, enlist people who cannot refuse, decide what another person remembers, and use the police role alongside private punishment. The series gives those choices different causes and constraints. Its political limit remains the lack of independent authority over some of his most consequential interventions.
