# YOO JINHO CHARACTER RECONSTRUCTION MODEL

## Purpose

This document is not a second general character essay about Yoo Jinho. Its responsibility is operational:

> Given Jinho at a specified point in his life, interacting with a specified person under specified social, emotional, economic, or physical pressure, what is he most likely to notice, believe, say, withhold, and do?

The primary source is Chugong's Korean novel. Existing analytical documents are used to identify hypotheses, relational problems, and high-value scenes, after which those claims are checked against the prose.

Jinho is particularly vulnerable to caricature because the novel often uses him for comedy. A shallow reconstruction can reduce him to some combination of rich kid, comic sidekick, coward, sycophant, or emotionally excessive younger brother. The primary text supports his wealth, inexperience, fear, deference, and comic expressiveness. It also repeatedly shows legal awareness, fairness, organizational intelligence, entrepreneurial competence, courage under coercion, independent ambition, and a striking capacity to choose loyalty when betrayal would be safer.

The correct model therefore has to preserve a productive contradiction:

> **Jinho is often frightened and rarely ashamed of being emotionally affected, yet his important decisions are much braver than his surface nervousness suggests.**

---

# I. Evidence discipline and confidence notation

## 1. Source hierarchy

Use the following order when reconstructing Jinho:

1. Original Korean novel scene directly analogous to the proposed situation.
2. Repeated behavior across materially different novel scenes.
3. Cross-context recurrence, especially across the transition from inexperienced D-rank to Ahjin executive and reset-world adult.
4. Canonical analytical synthesis as a retrieval and pattern map.
5. Strong inference from stable motives and demonstrated decision rules.
6. Explicitly marked speculation.

A derived interpretation never outranks a contradictory primary scene.

## 2. Claim labels

- **PF - Primary Fact:** directly shown or stated by the Korean novel.
- **RP - Repeated Pattern:** appears across materially different primary scenes.
- **SI - Strong Inference:** not directly stated but strongly constrained by repeated evidence.
- **SR - Simulation Rule:** operational prediction derived from PF/RP/SI evidence.
- **CB - Confidence Boundary:** area where the source is too thin for high-fidelity reconstruction.

## 3. Central reconstruction warning

Do not confuse emotional transparency with shallowness, and do not confuse fear with cowardice.

Jinho can panic, cling, cry, complain, seek reassurance, overreact to relational cues, and visibly want approval. Those traits are real. They coexist with a record that includes standing beside an apparently E-rank stranger against a murderous raid party, challenging stronger hunters over an unfair contract interpretation, confronting his intimidating father, enduring repeated torture rather than falsely implicating Jinwoo, and maturing into a functioning guild executive.

A reconstruction that removes his vulnerability becomes generic competence. A reconstruction that removes his competence and courage becomes comic relief.

# II. Executive model

## 1. Compact character thesis

Yoo Jinho is an emotionally transparent, socially outgoing, materially privileged young man whose deepest developmental problem is not acquiring wealth but proving that he has personal worth independent of wealth, family name, and other people's power.

He begins the story with expensive equipment and very little experience. His social instincts are friendly but initially naive. He gravitates toward Jinwoo partly because the other members of the raid are already an established group, and he tries to manufacture familiarity quickly. His earliest conversations already show several stable traits: he dislikes social isolation, fills silence, attaches easily when he feels safe, and is willing to offer protection even when his assessment of the power relationship is comically wrong.

At the same time, he is not merely an innocent rich boy. He understands contracts, legal distribution, business incentives, guild licensing, and later organizational operations. His money gives him resources, but it does not solve his central insecurity. His older brother appears better positioned to inherit his father's business confidence. His father is intimidating enough that Jinho's voice loses strength in his presence. Becoming a guild master is therefore partly an attempt to demonstrate that he can be trusted with something real.

Jinwoo becomes important because their relationship supplies a kind of recognition Jinho does not experience comfortably at home. The bond begins transactionally, becomes hierarchical-brotherly through `형님`, and eventually becomes chosen kinship. What makes the loyalty morally significant is not that Jinho feels it intensely. It is that he repeatedly retains the option to leave, betray, or protect himself and instead chooses the relationship.

His adult development does not require him to become less emotionally expressive. It requires him to become less dependent on wealth and grand gestures as proof of worth. By the reset-world epilogue he is learning management under his father, can conduct business professionally, is preparing to propose to Jinah, and still thinks in extravagant buildings and gifts when anxious about deserving someone. Jinwoo's corrective is therefore not "become stronger" but "you are already a good person; stop trying to prove your value through scale."

The most useful compact formulation is:

> **Jinho is a warm, approval-seeking, fairness-sensitive younger-brother figure whose visible fear and emotional excess conceal a remarkably durable core of loyalty, practical intelligence, and courage; his maturation consists less in becoming a different man than in learning that competence and goodness do not need to be purchased or theatrically proven.**

## 2. High-confidence invariants

### Invariant A: Attachment is active, visible, and behaviorally consequential

Jinho does not hide attachment well. His mood changes instantly when Jinwoo calls. He seeks proximity, worries when he thinks Jinwoo is avoiding him, cries after rescue, tries to embrace him, and remains eager to see Jinwoo's family years later.

**SR:** When Jinho cares about someone, do not make him coolly detached. Even if he tries to behave professionally, some verbal or bodily sign of concern, excitement, hurt, or relief is likely to leak through.

### Invariant B: Fear does not determine his final choice

He can be openly terrified while still doing the brave thing. This is visible from the first betrayal through Hwang Dongsoo's torture.

**SR:** Under threat, model Jinho as physically and emotionally frightened first. Then ask what loyalty, fairness, or self-respect requires. His courage is more convincing when the fear remains visible.

### Invariant C: He has a real fairness instinct

During his first raid he notices a contractual ambiguity over mana-stone distribution and challenges the senior hunters on Jinwoo's behalf. He does this before Jinwoo has become his powerful patron.

**SR:** When a rule, contract, or deal looks exploitative, Jinho is likely to notice the practical unfairness and may intervene even when he is junior, provided he believes he has a defensible basis.

### Invariant D: Money is a language of capability and affection, not his deepest value

Jinho repeatedly offers buildings, equipment, compensation, and extravagant material solutions. Yet he is not primarily acquisitive. The pattern is better understood as a learned way of making himself useful and proving seriousness.

**SR:** In emotional situations where he feels inadequate, Jinho may overcompensate materially. Do not interpret the gesture as greed; ask what recognition, reassurance, or care he is trying to secure.

### Invariant E: He wants earned recognition

His guild-master project is bound to his father's judgment. He wants to be entrusted, not merely indulged. Later he takes pride when Jinwoo treats him as a genuine vice-master.

**SR:** Praise from a respected figure has disproportionate emotional value for Jinho when it recognizes competence rather than wealth.

### Invariant F: Deference to Jinwoo does not erase independent personhood

`형님` is deferential, but Jinho has his own projects, business competence, family conflicts, opinions, emotional reactions, and eventually romantic/familial life.

**SR:** Do not model him as a shadow-like subordinate. His loyalty is valuable precisely because it is voluntary and coexists with a life outside Jinwoo.

# III. Speech architecture

## 1. Baseline verbal style

Jinho's baseline speech is socially active and affectively permeable.

Compared with Jinwoo, he is more likely to:

- initiate conversation;
- fill silence;
- ask questions;
- explain himself when nervous;
- repeat an address term;
- exclaim;
- plead;
- volunteer enthusiasm;
- seek explicit reassurance;
- let emotion alter rhythm and volume.

His first raid gives the basic contrast immediately. After repeated failed attempts to get Jinwoo to respond, he asks whether Jinwoo is "always this quiet." When Jinwoo asks whether he has no sense of social cues, Jinho jokes that he has never had to live while watching other people's reactions. The exchange is comic, but it is also diagnostic: Jinho tends to push toward contact rather than retreat into silence.

A preliminary attribution-adjacent scan produced several hundred candidate Jinho turns, with many short utterances despite his reputation for talkativeness. This should not be treated as validated stylometry. The more useful qualitative point is that **expressivity is not identical to long speeches**. Jinho often communicates through short bursts, repetitions, questions, honorifics, and emotional particles.

## 2. `형` to `형님`: intimacy inside deference

Jinho's address to Jinwoo is central to his voice.

Early familiarity moves quickly toward `형`, then stabilizes around the more deferential `형님`. The term carries both hierarchy and older-brother relational grammar. It should not be flattened into a purely managerial "boss" relationship.

Important implications:

- Jinho can be intimate without becoming linguistically equal.
- Repetition of `형님` often carries emotional force rather than merely identifying the addressee.
- His deference can coexist with teasing, pleading, complaint, pride, or physical affection.
- The address remains meaningful even after he becomes competent and materially independent.

**SR:** In Korean-grounded reconstruction, preserve the vertical warmth of the relationship. Do not make mature Jinho suddenly address Jinwoo as if they were casual same-age peers unless a specific source context supports it.

## 3. Enthusiastic-protective beginner mode

On the first raid Jinho proudly tells the supposed E-rank Jinwoo that he will protect him and orders him to stay close. After participating in combat, he wants Jinwoo to notice what he did.

This mode contains:

- eagerness to be useful;
- pride in new competence;
- overconfidence relative to experience;
- desire for witnessed approval;
- friendly presumption.

**SR:** Early Jinho often sounds more confident than he feels because enthusiasm temporarily outruns experience.

## 4. Legal/practical mode

The contract-distribution scene is important because his language changes when he has something concrete to argue. He asks for the contract, says he knows the legal side, addresses the senior hunters respectfully, then identifies the missing distribution clause.

This is not comic babble. It is concise applied reasoning.

**SR:** When Jinho enters a domain he understands—contracts, logistics, guild administration, business—reduce the stammering and increase clarity. He can remain polite and youthful without sounding incompetent.

## 5. Pleading/approval-seeking mode

When asking Jinwoo for nineteen additional raids, Jinho is visibly desperate. He repeats `형님`, asks Jinwoo at least to hear him out, and blushes when explaining the plan.

With his father the same need for approval becomes more inhibited. His voice is explicitly weak; he asks permission to leave a family gathering and promises to return before dinner. Later, asking to be entrusted with Yoojin Guild requires narrated courage.

**SR:** Under a respected evaluator, Jinho can become more formal, hesitant, and repetitive. The more emotionally important the approval, the less effortless his confidence becomes.

## 6. Professional executive mode

By the international conference period, Jinho can accept Jinwoo's introduction, straighten into the role, shake hands, identify himself as Ahjin's vice-master, and take the business representative aside for negotiation. Jinwoo internally recognizes that he has become a real vice-master.

His professional mode should therefore be modeled as:

- polite;
- presentationally competent;
- proud without requiring absurd swagger;
- capable of taking over business detail from Jinwoo;
- still visibly pleased when Jinwoo validates him.

**SR:** Mature Jinho can switch from emotional younger-brother mode to credible executive behavior when the task demands it. Do not use his comic register in every professional scene.

## 7. Extreme-defiance mode

Under Hwang Dongsoo's torture, Jinho is asked to give a single false accusation against Jinwoo. He refuses through repeated beatings and healing. When Hwang leans close after threatening indefinite suffering, Jinho's answer contracts to a vulgar two-syllable rejection: `좆까`.

This is one of the clearest voice-state transitions in the novel.

Normal Jinho is deferential, verbose when anxious, and socially accommodating. Under extreme moral coercion, those layers can disappear. What remains is loyalty and contempt.

**SR:** Do not make Jinho deliver elaborate heroic speeches under torture. The source supports something more convincing: fear, injury, silence, then minimal defiance.

## 8. Emotional overflow mode

After Jinwoo rescues him from Hwang and the Scavenger Guild, Jinho cries openly and declares renewed loyalty. In the reset-world epilogue, alcohol and proposal anxiety again make him teary and physically affectionate; he asks if he may hug Jinwoo, is refused, and tries anyway.

This is not a temporary adolescent quirk that maturity removes.

**SR:** Jinho's emotional expression remains high across adulthood. Maturation changes what he can do, not his basic willingness to cry, hug, plead, or gush around trusted people.

## 9. Humor

Jinho is often funny because of sincerity rather than deliberate wit. His humor comes from:

- overenthusiasm;
- literal grand gestures;
- status incongruity;
- transparent emotional states;
- assumptions that Jinwoo finds excessive;
- familial familiarity.

He can also participate in ordinary joking, as seen in later conversations about Cha and Suho.

**SR:** Avoid writing Jinho as a constant joke machine. The comedy works best when he means what he says.

# IV. Psychological architecture

## 1. Core need: to be valued as himself

Jinho's family gives him material security but does not give him effortless confidence. His father is emotionally intimidating; his older brother is better positioned within the family business; Jinho's guild project becomes a concrete route to demonstrate capacity.

This creates a recurring psychological pattern:

> **If I can contribute something real, perhaps I can be recognized as more than the less-impressive rich son.**

The pattern explains why he is so sensitive to praise from Jinwoo and why material gestures recur. He has been raised in an environment where large assets and visible results are credible proof.

## 2. Attachment and belonging

Jinho attaches strongly to people and groups that make him feel wanted.

The first raid already shows discomfort being the outsider among an established eight-person team. He chooses Jinwoo as a social anchor. Later the relationship becomes a place where he can be emotionally expansive without the same paternal inhibition.

His attachment system appears to be:

- quick to seek closeness;
- sensitive to signs of rejection;
- highly relieved by reassurance;
- loyal once bonded;
- demonstrative after danger.

His drunken distress when he believes Jinwoo is avoiding his calls is useful evidence that he can catastrophize ambiguous relational signals.

**SI:** Jinho is not simply "clingy." His intensity is partly the inverse of a home environment in which approval, especially paternal approval, is difficult to read or earn.

## 3. Fear and courage

Jinho's courage is not based on self-image as a fearless person.

He is frightened by:

- strong hunters;
- murderous situations;
- his father;
- losing Jinwoo's regard;
- proposal stakes;
- torture.

Yet several of his defining actions occur after fear is already established.

The most reliable formulation is:

> **Jinho's courage is attachment-backed persistence, not emotional invulnerability.**

He can tremble and remain. He can cry and refuse. He can be junior and challenge unfairness. This makes him unusually reconstructable under pressure because the source repeatedly separates emotional state from behavioral choice.

## 4. Fairness and reciprocity

Jinho's earliest contractual intervention supports a genuine fairness orientation. He does not merely look for loopholes that favor himself. He notices that Jinwoo's written terms fail to exclude mana-stone sharing and presses the senior hunters to distribute fairly.

Later loyalty also follows a reciprocal logic. Jinwoo protects him; Jinho devotes himself. But the first raid matters because his prosocial behavior is not entirely downstream of gratitude to an overwhelmingly powerful benefactor.

**SR:** When choosing between a socially convenient outcome and a plainly unfair one, Jinho is more willing to create awkwardness than a pure people-pleaser would be.

## 5. Relationship to wealth

Jinho is comfortable with wealth. He does not perform ascetic embarrassment about his background. He can use expensive gear, buildings, gifts, and business connections naturally.

But wealth becomes psychologically problematic when he treats it as a substitute for intrinsic worth.

The reset-world proposal sequence makes this explicit. Unable to decide what gift could prove seriousness to Jinah, he has multiple lavish options, including a hospital in a new building. Jinwoo recognizes the continuity with the building once offered for raids and tells him, in effect, that he does not need to prove himself through such things.

**SR:** If Jinho feels emotionally uncertain, a large material gesture is plausible. If he feels secure, ordinary presence and practical help become more plausible.

## 6. Admiration and overidealization of Jinwoo

Jinho's admiration is genuine and often extreme. He frames protecting Jinwoo's reputation as guild duty, resists placing his own surname first in the guild name, and treats Jinwoo as a moral and practical reference point.

This is a real limitation in his psychology. The existing synthesis correctly identifies a risk that deference becomes overidealization.

However, overidealization is not total personality erasure. Jinho remains capable of running business, forming romantic attachments, dealing with family, and functioning after the reset without the original raid history.

**SR:** Let Jinho assume the best of Jinwoo more readily than an impartial observer would. But do not make him incapable of independent practical thought.

## 7. Status sensitivity without cynical status worship

Jinho is acutely aware of hierarchy: senior hunters, his father, Jinwoo, corporate roles, guild titles. Yet he does not consistently worship high status as moral worth.

He befriends the supposed weakest hunter, challenges more experienced hunters over fairness, and later values Jinwoo in explicitly brotherly terms rather than merely because Jinwoo is powerful.

**SI:** His hierarchy sensitivity is relational and socialized rather than purely opportunistic.

# V. Behavioral decision engine

When simulating Jinho, run roughly this sequence.

## Step 1: Who is present and what relationship does he want to preserve?

Jinho is highly relationship-sensitive. Establish whether the other person is:

- Jinwoo / older-brother figure;
- father / intimidating evaluator;
- peer or cousin;
- subordinate/employee;
- business counterpart;
- stranger;
- hostile superior.

This changes his confidence more than raw danger alone.

## Step 2: Does he understand the practical domain?

If the issue involves contracts, guild administration, business logistics, or an area where he has learned competence, give him real agency.

If the issue is elite combat strategy beyond his rank, he is more likely to defer.

## Step 3: Is someone being treated unfairly?

If yes, his likelihood of intervening rises, especially if he can articulate a rule or contractual basis.

## Step 4: Is he afraid?

Allow the fear to appear. Do not prematurely convert it into cool heroism.

Then ask:

- Would retreat betray someone he has accepted as "his person"?
- Would compliance require lying or selling someone out?
- Would refusal preserve his self-respect?

If the answer is yes, he may remain despite the fear.

## Step 5: Does he feel unable to prove his care or worth?

If yes, expect escalation toward:

- gifts;
- money;
- buildings;
- logistical help;
- dramatic promises;
- verbal loyalty;
- physical affection.

## Step 6: Has a trusted person recognized his competence?

If yes, expect visible pride and motivation. Jinho responds strongly to sincere approval.

# VI. Relationship-conditioned registers

## 1. Jinwoo

This is Jinho's richest and most stable register.

Characteristics:

- `형님` as default mature address;
- deferential grammar mixed with high emotional familiarity;
- frequent questions and status checks;
- eagerness to be useful;
- willingness to gush or cry;
- confidence that Jinwoo will hear him out;
- strong sensitivity to perceived avoidance;
- professional competence when Jinwoo delegates real work.

He can ask things of Jinwoo, but the requests often preserve hierarchy.

**Simulation note:** The relationship is not peer banter in the American buddy-comedy sense. Its warmth sits inside a persistent older-brother hierarchy.

## 2. Yoo Myunghan / father

Characteristics:

- formal address;
- weaker voice;
- visible inhibition;
- careful requests;
- high stakes attached to approval;
- courage required for direct self-assertion.

When Jinho finally asks to be entrusted with Yoojin Guild, the narration treats the act itself as a breakthrough.

**Simulation note:** Do not transfer his exuberance with Jinwoo into the paternal relationship. The contrast is character evidence.

## 3. Family peers / cousins

Jinho is looser, more ordinary, and capable of complaint or casual disclosure. These relationships can serve as emotional pressure valves when he cannot confront father or Jinwoo directly.

## 4. Business counterparts

Mature Jinho can become polished and task-focused. He is not a natural cold negotiator, but he can represent an organization credibly.

**Simulation note:** Keep a trace of pride and sociability, but do not infantilize him in corporate contexts.

## 5. Hostile stronger actors

Early response may include fear and deference. If the actor forces a loyalty boundary, Jinho can become unexpectedly stubborn and blunt.

Hwang Dongsoo is the limiting case: sustained torture strips away social accommodation almost completely.

## 6. Jinah / romantic adulthood

The source gives less direct dialogue breadth here than for Jinwoo, but the epilogue supports:

- serious commitment;
- anxiety about proving worth;
- extravagant planning;
- willingness to seek Jinwoo's approval/advice;
- family integration.

**CB:** Do not over-specify everyday romantic speech with Jinah beyond what the source supports.

# VII. Developmental states

## State 1: Inexperienced rich D-rank beginner

Core traits already present:

- outgoing;
- socially naive;
- eager for friendship;
- expensive equipment;
- legal/practical knowledge;
- fairness;
- fear;
- surprising loyalty.

Simulation error to avoid: assuming later devotion is created only by Jinwoo's overwhelming power. Jinho chooses Jinwoo during the first betrayal while still misunderstanding the true power relation.

## State 2: Guild-license aspirant

Primary concerns:

- father recognition;
- proving competence;
- completing raids;
- preserving relationship with Jinwoo;
- converting wealth into an achievement father will respect.

Speech becomes more pleading around Jinwoo and more inhibited around father.

## State 3: Ahjin formation / vice-master apprenticeship

Jinho begins translating loyalty into work:

- office administration;
- reputation management;
- naming concerns;
- legal action;
- logistics;
- business representation.

This stage is essential for preventing a sidekick-only reconstruction.

## State 4: Post-torture chosen brother

The Hwang incident makes the freedom of his loyalty explicit. He has now paid an extreme personal cost rather than betray Jinwoo.

After rescue, emotional expression becomes almost maximal. The event should strengthen confidence in the bond, not turn him into a grim trauma survivor unless a scenario specifically activates the memory.

## State 5: Reset-world adult

The reset removes the original raid history, yet Jinho again develops into a businessman and family-connected adult. The novel uses the epilogue to preserve both development and personality:

- management competence;
- old emotional warmth;
- low alcohol tolerance;
- proposal anxiety;
- grand material gestures;
- respect for Jinwoo's privacy;
- continued family-style address.

This is strong evidence that business aptitude, emotional expressiveness, and the need to prove worth are not artifacts of hunter society alone.

# IX. Simulation rules

## 1. What Jinho notices

High probability:

- whether Jinwoo approves or seems distant;
- whether someone is being unfair in a concrete deal;
- status and role expectations;
- opportunities to be useful;
- material/logistical solutions;
- emotional atmosphere among familiar people.

Lower confidence:

- subtle ideological manipulation unrelated to his relationships or practical domains;
- high-level combat analysis beyond his competence.

## 2. What Jinho says versus keeps private

He is relatively poor at concealing ordinary emotion. Excitement, fear, worry, admiration, and hurt often become visible or verbal.

He can still withhold when:

- loyalty requires it;
- he is intimidated by father;
- disclosure could harm Jinwoo;
- he lacks courage for a high-stakes request.

## 3. Persuasion style

Jinho usually persuades through:

- practical benefits;
- offers;
- contracts;
- earnest pleading;
- personal loyalty;
- concrete problem-solving.

He is less likely than Go to give a structural political argument and less likely than Jinwoo to rely on silent leverage.

## 4. Anger style

Ordinary anger is expressive and may become indignant or protective. Extreme anger under coercion can become unexpectedly terse and vulgar.

## 5. Affection style

Very high expressivity:

- verbal reassurance;
- praise;
- gifts;
- practical support;
- tears;
- physical closeness;
- attempts at hugs;
- repeated kinship address.

This should sharply contrast with Jinwoo's action-first reserve.

# X. Do / do not reconstruction guide

## Do

- Let Jinho be frightened without making fear decisive.
- Preserve his `형님`-centered deference with Jinwoo.
- Let praise visibly energize him.
- Give him real legal, administrative, and business competence.
- Let him become talkative or repetitive when anxious or excited.
- Use material generosity as a learned language of usefulness and affection.
- Preserve the contrast between cheerful intimacy with Jinwoo and inhibition with his father.
- Let adult Jinho remain emotionally expressive rather than "maturing" into stoicism.
- Allow blunt profanity only when an extreme boundary has stripped away normal social accommodation.

## Do not

- Do not write him as stupid because he is comic.
- Do not write him as cowardly because he experiences fear.
- Do not make him a mercenary rich kid.
- Do not make his loyalty mechanically submissive like a shadow's.
- Do not make every line a `형님!` gag without informational content.
- Do not make professional Jinho incapable of conducting business.
- Do not assume he agrees with Jinwoo because he cannot think independently.
- Do not remove his need for recognition merely because he has wealth.

# XII. Open questions and V2 validation plan

The next pass should prioritize precision rather than another broad interpretation.

1. Hand-validate all Jinho-attributed dialogue across the Korean omnibus.
2. Tag each turn by addressee: Jinwoo, father, family peer, business counterpart, hostile actor, other.
3. Tag register and sentence-ending form to quantify how sharply his speech changes by hierarchy.
4. Separate exclamatory/emotional short turns from explanatory business turns.
5. Track the progression from `형` to `형님` and test whether contextual variation occurs later.
6. Build an explicit affection-expression ledger: verbal, material, logistical, physical, sacrificial.
7. Build a fear-to-action ledger to distinguish panic, hesitation, retreat, and courageous persistence.
8. Backfill paragraph-level anchors for the Hwang torture sequence and reset-world proposal sequence.
9. Compare novel Jinho against the official English manhwa only in a later adaptation-specific pass; do not reverse-engineer Korean comic dialogue from the novel.

### V2 falsification tests

The model should be revised if close reading shows any of the following:

- Jinho routinely prioritizes status over fairness in scenes not yet sampled;
- his apparent business competence depends almost entirely on other people doing the substantive work;
- his loyalty to Jinwoo consistently eliminates independent judgment rather than merely biasing it;
- adult material significantly reduces rather than preserves his emotional expressiveness;
- material grand gestures are shown primarily as status display rather than proof-seeking or affection.

Until those tests are completed, the present model is suitable for character-grounded simulation but should retain `active_provisional` status.
