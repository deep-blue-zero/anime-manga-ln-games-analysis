## The medicine changes the decision environment, not the law of love

E08 makes the bodily deadline immediate. Lara's weakness is visible before Lisa speaks to Mari; Lisa's E06 account of the finite original and failing imitation has a practical sequel. She brings a vial and Mari gives it to Lara, who says she feels substantially better. Lisa explicitly calls this a temporary stopgap. Grace's answer to Mari's question about foaming—`誰から聞いた`—does not disconfirm the prognosis. A strong text/static conclusion is that a serious risk exists and the medicine provides temporary relief. Exact expiry, survival duration, efficacy after repeated doses, and whether foaming is inevitable without a particular form of love remain untested.

Lisa's visit is ethically double. She bypasses Grace's mediation and tells Mari a truth Lara had not been told in this direct form, and gives her a life-preserving resource. Yet she asks Mari to return Lara, predicts failure with humans, and initially treats the relationships sustaining Lara on land as obstacles to family recovery. Mari now bears information Lara lacks. Her command to go with Lisa is not a sudden discovery that friendship was false; it is a protective response to death risk with few trustworthy options. Conversely, Lara's wish to remain cannot be romanticized as informed freedom until she knows the stakes. Their waterfront conflict corrects both evasions.

Lara's list of an ordinary tomorrow is remarkably material: fold the futon, dress, attend Mari's bout, work again at Andeken, help with laundry, get closer to Luca, and perhaps one day find true love. These acts are not interchangeable with the royal mission. They are the content of the life the deadline threatens. Mari hears the fear beneath a confidence neither girl can sustain. Lara's `こわいに決まってるでしょ` and Mari's `死んでほしくない` turn the quarrel into mutually acknowledged vulnerability. Mari does not thereby gain authority to choose Lara's home; Lara does not abolish Mari's legitimate fear by promising to be fine. Her subsequent departure and note temporarily enact the return Mari urged, while the abandoned phone severs her most ordinary channels of contact.

## Three incompatible accounts of the light

Rowan's E06 formulation wants Lara's singular princess light to restore the castle and the family's glory; he preserves her and delegates waiting to Lisa. Grace repeatedly specifies true love with a human as the route to save the family. Lisa earlier forbade human-mermaid love and now tells Mari it cannot occur, but on the boat she says earlier princesses generated light from deep love for the *sea world*, without exception. Lara's E03 golden event occurred in connection with Mari and a land choice without an identified human romantic partner; E07 remembered light became a blade after rejection. In E08 she says it is on the *land* that she feels light (`この地上にこそ 光を感じる`). None of these statements can be fused into a proved universal mechanism.

The distinction between *feeling* light and *producing sufficient restorative light* is essential. Lara's declaration rejects Lisa's exclusive allocation of love to the sea and Rowan's sole claim to the benefit, but no castle awakening or cure follows onscreen. Lisa's sea-love claim may be a revision of the doctrine, a competing tradition, or strategic persuasion by someone who wants Lara alive near the family. Its `例外は無い` is especially categorical and should be tested against the prior historical failure and the unusual heart-to-blade testimony. Rowan's sacrificed light and current sleeping state give his demand both cost and coercive force; he remains a major ideological axis for a dedicated monograph, not a one-note villain inferred from absence.

Lara's recollection montage at 14:25-14:49 juxtaposes Rowan's anti-human judgment, the prince's rejection, Lisa's species prohibition, Luca's warmth, and Grace's command. `私って……なんなの?` asks who gets to define a person when mutually exclusive systems assign her a task, a body, a danger, and a prospective partner. Her answer is practical rather than taxonomic: she will return to people who helped her face fear. It resists both the demand to be a sea-world light producer and the fiction that the risk has disappeared.

## The tournament is a parallel choice, not a magical equivalence

Mari had framed her boxing trajectory as a search for increasingly strong opponents. Lara's banner makes her sporting future a shared public occasion, while Mari initially rejects that help after the separation. In the final, the coach says one must decide to fight before entering the ring. Luca's question to Lara—what does she really want?—is formally adjacent to Mari's contested resolve. The film crosscuts Lara's turn and statement on the boat with Mari's bout and later Lara's call at ringside. This parallel has real force: both choose a risky future rather than guaranteed safety, and both need others without surrendering the decision. It does *not* make punches a literal analogue of supernatural light, prove that one choice causes the other's athletic outcome, or settle either girl's ultimate path.

The first tournament day has an announced victory for Mari and a final scheduled for the next day. The final's participants are Mari Otsu of Zeze Minami and Homare Fukuchiyama of Bunei. The ring imagery at 18:50-20:10 shows hardship, cheers, renewed fighting, and Lara's arrival but no complete official result in the inspected evidence. A later coda image shows Mari with a trophy and Lara with a certificate at a boxing backdrop, immediately after Mari imagines reaching the world top and Lara pledges cake. The intervening steps, date, and whether this is literal future, wish-image, or formal promise-visualization are not established. It cannot be used as a transcript of the August 23 final.

Mari's mother's recalled words supply a counterreading of solitude: independence can be strength, but someone may stand alongside without taking away either person's forward gaze. The van shot of the two asleep and the balcony's paired ambitions realize an immediate companionship and future-directed promise. Neither the mother nor the shot names a lover; `横に並んで同じ方を見てくれる人` is relationally capacious. Lara's cake is not decorative: E05's failed, repaired, learned bakery work now becomes a specific gift she can promise from gained competence. Mari's world-title ambition is similarly hers, not merely a stage for Lara's survival story.

## Luca and Lisa remain in the relational field

Luca is not simply displaced by Lara's return to Mari. Lara names growing closer to him among the tomorrows she wants; after leaving her phone behind, she reaches him from the ferry, discloses that they may not meet again, and admits confusion. Wind makes the call difficult, yet he worries about her, watches the event expecting her, and asks her own desire. His question contributes to her turn but does not originate or own it. The episode neither forms a stable couple nor determines that Luca is Grace's true-love candidate. Her land choice includes human relationships in the plural.

Lisa likewise does not become a simple antagonist. She supplies treatment, affirms Lara may stay, discloses sleeping family, and fears a catastrophe she has lived through. But her sea-love formula and repeated impossibility claim seek to monopolize the meaning of Lara's light. Her language of allowing Lara to stay is softer than forced removal, though it remains housed inside a plan to save father and family by Lara's capacity. Lara refuses that exclusive future while not ceasing to care for her sister. No final reconciliation, assault, or magical verdict occurs in E08.
