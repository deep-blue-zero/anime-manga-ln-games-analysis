## The look-alike is an occasion for revision, not a returned prince

Luca resembles the original prince closely enough to trigger Lara's fear. The opening temporarily renders him in princely costume through her perception. Grace eagerly folds the involuntary reversion into a familiar formula: when Lara tried to unite with the prince two centuries earlier, her heart moved, and this youth might now be the person with whom she finds true love. Yet Grace says she might only have been shaken, and Lara insists on fear. The evidence thus supports an emotionally charged event, not an established causal law or a foretold partner. Luca introduces himself as an ordinary first-year high-school student and is observed in school, art, family, and local social networks. No identity continuity with the prince is supplied.

The title's scheduled place and time initially looks like a mechanized prince appointment. Its realization is awkward and contingent: Mari supplies the phone and teaches dictation, Lara struggles with the interface, the advertised panorama is lost in fog, and curry bread receives more urgent care than romantic scenery. Even the secret studio is interrupted by Leo. These comic failures matter because Luca is not validated by performing a fairy-tale role flawlessly. His personhood becomes legible through ordinary flaws, a brother's teasing, repeated messages, the incomplete painting, and his response to Lara's refusal.

## Beauty, art, and the risk of making someone an object

The original prince saw Lara's revealed mermaid body as `醜くおぞましい`—ugly and repulsive. Luca's `美しいもの` initially reactivates this wound, but his later account explicitly refuses the idea that beauty is exhausted by visible surface. He paints a sky, a wet dog, and a “real self” in abstract colors, then says he wants to discover Lara's beauty with his heart and leave it in a painting. The unfinished portrait looks nothing like a conventional likeness. Lara laughs, apologizes, and says the painting is good; Luca continues to seek colors. The moment permits her to be an interlocutor who evaluates an image of herself, not merely an exhibited body consumed by another's gaze.

That difference does not automatically absolve Luca's gaze. He first approached because Lara was a mermaid, and he initially wants to paint her. Lisa's E06 captivity warns that being looked at and rendered can become possession or spectacle. Here Leo punctures Luca's private-uniqueness performance; Lara laughs at the work rather than being compelled to admire it; later she can leave and articulate a refusal. The ethical test is whether Luca's avowed heart-seeing becomes responsive to Lara's own interpretation and boundary. His admission of initial novelty is more credible than a retroactive claim of pure motive, but only future conduct can prove durable respect.

The art room also complicates Grace's instrumental prince-hunt. Luca does not ask Lara to generate light, restore a polity, or satisfy his family. He asks to keep discovering what he does not yet see and to try colors. That open-ended, fallible practice is nearer to Lara's E05 desire to know humans and Seizō's E06 hypothesis-and-verification than Rowan's fixed extraction of princess light. Nonetheless desire to “discover” another person can itself become appropriative; this is an alternative possibility, not a certificate of innocence.

## The blade is Lara's remembered heart, not proof that love is harmless

E07 supplies a major correction to any simple light-equals-benevolence reading. Lara remembers the original prince rejecting her and a desire to eat his soul and pierce his heart. In her own explanation, the light in her chest changed into a blade. Static imagery renders a knife, a floating dark blade in the present, and an eye-shaped predatory weapon in the remembered underwater sequence. She calls that blade `私の心 そのもの`—her heart itself—and fears another person might be harmed. This is more than a symbolic association in her testimony, but the mechanics, agency, and whether the present dark image is a new manifestation or subjective representation are unresolved.

The memory does not make Lara innately monstrous. Rejection, shame, violence, and the wish to pierce the rejecting prince form the context of the transformation. Nor can the episode establish that a future attraction to Luca will repeat the weapon. Lara's fear is an intelligible protective motive, not a prediction guaranteed by the source. E05's fish-marked object and E06's bridge damage must not be automatically equated with this blade: design resemblance and thematic relation invite a comparison, while provenance and causal linkage remain unverified under continuous-video debt.

Lara's withdrawal follows this moral anxiety. She does not merely fear being deemed ugly; she fears hurting someone who regards her as beautiful. This reverses the earlier one-way injury and makes caution part of her agency. Her decision to tell Luca to leave her alone is not a puzzle that his declaration simply solves. The subsequent conversation changes her desire, but her prior refusal remains a real boundary and a meaningful ethical condition for any continued relationship.

## Mari, Luca, and hearing a second account

Mari facilitates the encounter twice without claiming its outcome. She lends a phone and teaches Lara how to use it; later she asks whether Lara will leave without hearing Luca's feelings. Lara misreads the second intervention as a command to make a definitive ending. Mari's question is about procedural reciprocity, not a vote for romance. She also invites Lara to the family fireworks and acknowledges that friends or someone close might share the event. Her own bond with Lara remains named friendship with practical and emotional attention, not displaced or downgraded by Luca's emergence.

At the bridge Lara states a genuine difference between becoming close to a mermaid and to another human, then asks for no further meeting. Luca accepts it. When he later speaks, he acknowledges the request and apologizes, rather than claiming a right to her. His distinction is exact: `はじめは 人魚やから興味持ってん`, but `今は ちゃう`; `ララが……ララやから 一緒にいたい`. Initial exoticizing interest is revised into a stated preference for Lara as a person. This is an avowal, not yet a tested guarantee. The scene supplies evidence of attraction and possible romantic orientation in the Luca/Lara direction; Lara says she may have waited for those words and later wants to get closer. It still does not pronounce a stable couple or certify the royal “true love” mechanism.

The fireworks close the episode on shared attention to beauty and Lara's desire for proximity. Her `もっと近づきたい` is significant because it names an aim she chooses, not an obligation to supply Rowan's light. The crowd, festival, and family invitation make the desired nearness social and situated rather than a sealed destiny. No castle awakening is shown, and visible fireworks must not be treated as evidence that the princess's magical light has fired.

## Lisa's clock and competing claims on Lara

Lisa and Kōta's brief E07 exchange keeps a second plot active. Lisa infers that Grace now knows of them and expects exclusion if they approach Lara. She stops Kōta's offer to try, says other means remain, and promises to recover Lara before time runs out. E06 established failing medicine, which gives this urgency material basis, but her claim about Grace's exact intention remains untested. `取り戻す` casts Lara as someone the family may reclaim, a sharper possession verb alongside Rowan's delegated vigil and Lisa's fear. The episode does not show the chosen alternative means, an attack on Luca or Mari, or a successful intervention.

Lara's own response to Luca also has a family-history dimension. Grace and Lisa disagree about whether love with humans is necessary or impossible, but both frame her future in advance. Rowan wants her light as restoration infrastructure. Lara instead confronts an uncertain person and an uncertain heart that can make either care or a blade. Her approach is not naive optimism: she remembers harm, can say no, listens, and later chooses tentative nearness. That sequence resists both mandatory true-love extraction and absolute species prohibition without yet resolving the world rule.

## Episode-level synthesis and E07 freeze

E07 makes “being seen” a variable ethical relation rather than a single event. The original prince's gaze disfigured Lara through disgust; human captors in E06 made Lisa a marketable spectacle; Luca begins with mermaid curiosity but offers an unfinished, contestable painting and a statement that Lara herself matters. Lara's laughter and refusal keep her interpretation in the scene. Whether his new account survives testing remains open.

The most consequential world-rule evidence is Lara's recollection that the light of her heart became a blade under rejection. Light is not guaranteed to be a gentle or politically useful substance. E07 gives the weapon subjective ownership and an apparent material history but leaves exact mechanics, present manifestation, and relation to E05's object unresolved. The bridge reversion remains similarly underdetermined: an emotionally moved heart is Grace's hypothesis, not a proved true-love detector.
