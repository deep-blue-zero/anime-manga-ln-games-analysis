## The prince is a contested form, not an answer

The episode does not merely make fun of Lara for treating `王子さま` literally. It repeatedly gives the term a different owner and function. Lara assembles a prince from romance-media surfaces; Mari imagines animal intensity and musculature; Yoshiya volunteers himself; schoolgirls describe idols, a comedian, and a VTuber as princes or favorites whose value does not require dating. The students' `付き合うだけがゴールちゃう` explicitly denies that coupling is the only legitimate endpoint.

Ouji then appears to fulfill the word by name, but not by role. His relationship with Himeka contains history, care, family intimacy, and asymmetry. Himeka's attraction is real; Ouji's importance and affection are also real; neither fact produces mutual romance. The episode thereby separates at least four things Lara still tends to compress: a symbolic prince, an admired favorite, a beloved important person, and a reciprocating romantic partner.

Lara learns from this plurality without abandoning the prince search. Himeka's final claim that one man is not the only prince becomes a hopeful maxim almost immediately. This is development, but it is not yet conceptual liberation: Lara broadens the candidate pool while continuing to imagine love through a prince-shaped slot.

## Himeka: concealment cannot preserve a relationship outside time

Himeka's central mistake is not simply cowardice. She correctly understands that naming desire can alter a relationship whose current form she values. Her wish that she and Ouji remain `ずーーっと今のまま` is a preservation strategy: suppress the difference in their feelings so that established intimacy can continue. Lara's blunt `そんなの 無理よ` introduces mortality and change into a school romance plot because Lara has already watched every supposedly permanent relation collapse.

The episode does not prove that earlier confession would have produced romance. Ouji's eventual answer gives no sign that timing caused nonreciprocity. What Himeka gains is therefore not the prince but truthful knowledge and the ability to stop organizing the relationship around concealment. Her relief and continued grief coexist. The statement about a new prince is an attempt to make a future after rejection, not evidence that the rejected attachment was trivial.

Lara's intervention is ethically mixed. She projects her own catastrophe onto Himeka and speaks despite being told not to. Yet her reason is specific: she could never say the necessary words to the original prince. The counsel is less “confession wins love” than “silence can preserve neither truth nor time.” Himeka's first angry refusal protects the difference between Lara's history and her own; her later choice remains Himeka's.

## Grace's protection removes Lara's agency

Grace's old rationale returns with new counterevidence. She says the land is so delicate that one word can justify exclusion and therefore takes Lara's voice. E01 already showed that the prince ultimately rejected Lara's body rather than an unfortunate phrase. E04 adds Lara's retrospective cost: wordlessness let her imagine mutual understanding but prevented her from answering when the relationship's truth mattered.

This does not prove that Grace intended the failure or that speech would have saved the romance. It does show that the “protective” intervention redistributed risk by taking away Lara's capacity to name herself, ask questions, or contest the prince's interpretation. Lara now converts that injury into an ethic of saying what matters while speech is possible. The lesson is valuable precisely because its source is coercive.

## Mari says “friend” after looking

Himeka, not either member of the dyad, supplies the category: `友達の まりちゃんも！` and then `友達 でしょ？`. The caption timeline leaves roughly fourteen seconds between the question and Mari's `うん`; the inspected static progression cycles through Himeka waiting, Lara's startled and visibly warmed reaction, Mari looking upward, and Himeka returning her gaze. No auditory account of the pause is made, but the frame/cue structure makes deliberation and mutual visibility part of the answer.

This is the first explicit relationship name accepted by Mari. It revises the E03 freeze from unnamed reciprocal commitment to named friendship. It should not be weakened into a meaningless label, nor inflated into proof that friendship is the final or exclusive ontology. Mari says only `うん`; the episode does not supply romantic attraction, mutual romantic recognition, or couple formation. The analytical consequence is exact: friendship is now text, while any further category remains a later evidentiary question.

The friendship is already materially productive. Mari has brought Lara into school space, retrieves her after a boundary violation, stays during Himeka's crisis, lets Lara participate in food selection, and delegates a small independent errand. Lara, in turn, enters Mari's peer world and affects someone outside the household. The bond expands both social fields rather than merely stabilizing Lara in a private refuge.

## Small autonomy and continuing asymmetry

Lara's competence remains uneven, but E04 supplies genuine increments. She seeks information from multiple humans, sustains a new acquaintance, recognizes a quality food item, volunteers for a task, completes a purchase, and returns toward the household. These are modest acts, but they move beyond the spectacular rescue/debt structure of E02-E03.

The asymmetry remains pronounced. Mari controls access to school and television, retrieves Lara, supplies local interpretation, and decides whether to trust her with the errand. Lara still conceals her identity under Grace's threat. Friendship therefore names emotional inclusion without eliminating dependence, unequal knowledge, or household authority.

## Family contact: love and managed information

Kōta's introduction ties the modern masked messenger directly to Rowan's castle and to Lisa's command. The phrase `今は コータと名乗っています` marks the name as a present identity, leaving earlier identity and transformation open. Lisa says that only she and Kōta are awake. This is a character assertion consistent with the dormant-shell account, not an independently demonstrated census of the family.

The call gives Lara something E03 could not: reciprocal confirmation that Lisa lives and speaks to her as a beloved sister. But Lisa also controls the conditions of reunion. She does not come in person, says that now is not possible, withholds location, repeats Lara's exceptional princess status, and ends on an unfinished `でも`. Affection and information management again coexist inside the royal family. Unlike Rowan's prohibition or Grace's trial, Lisa's present motive is not yet established; suspicion must remain proportional to the evidence.

## Episode-level synthesis

E04 moves the series from the question “who is the prince?” to “what kind of relation is this, and who gets to name it?” Its answer is plural. Admiration need not seek dating. Long intimacy can be family-like without romance. Romantic love can be one-sided without making the underlying bond false. Silence can protect a status quo while producing regret. A new friendship can be explicit while its future remains open.

Lara does not resolve these distinctions cleanly. She continues to chase a prince and to read other lives through her own loss. What changes is her capacity to encounter counterexamples and act among them. Mari's restrained assent gives Lara a human relationship that is neither royal mission nor prince fantasy. Lisa's call simultaneously restores a family relation and reactivates the language of exceptional duty. The episode's final happiness is therefore real but structurally unstable: Lara has more belonging, more speech, and more routes to others, while every route still carries an asymmetry she does not fully understand.
