## Refusal, death, and the changed light

The climax makes the old protocol both coercive and internally unstable. Rowan's paternal donation, visible bodily failure and grief are real; his remedy nevertheless requires the living daughter to wound another person and rationalizes that demand through contempt for human legs. Lisa transmits the doctrine but meets a human who explicitly does **not** claim supernatural sight. Mari's `近づかんと分からへん` (“I cannot know unless I come closer”) is an epistemic ethic, not a claim to read Lara's interior. Kōta's attachment to Lisa makes the cost of the requested act intelligible within their own camp.

Lara's first proposed alternative is not triumph but disappearing so nobody is hurt. Mari's answer is specific to both of them: Lara's disruptions matter because they broke solitude; separate lives and worlds are acknowledged rather than waved away. After Mari's apparent death, Lara briefly repeats that she should never have revived, then corrects herself: she wants this life despite injuries and shame. `本当の愛なんか要らない` rejects the externally administered quest/criterion at the very moment she says `あなたがいる世界にいたい`. It cannot fairly be paraphrased as “Lara loves no one.” The difference between love as lived attachment and “true love” as dynastic test is the episode's central distinction. Lara names Mari as the person whose world she wants to share, but the episode does not declare a mutual romantic partnership.

The visuals place the blade, predatory fish maw, a fallen weapon, white roses, the two bodies and a burst of light in causal proximity. Mari later accepts Lara's “yes” to the question whether she died. The result supports a revival and a changed form of royal light, corroborated by Lisa's later observation that the family gradually recovers. It does not isolate the contributing role of Lara's choice, Mari's care, the inherited donation, ambient magic or a precise victim-free mechanism. Rowan is not shown cured of hatred. Grace's E10 present fate is still not separately certified by this episode: weakening magic and the historical E11 childhood rescue are not proof of her living body six months later.

## Six months later: incomplete belonging

The news report preserves material consequences beyond private catharsis. Lisa measures a new light against prior princesses, admits fear of change, and returns Lara's ring; an object of royal control becomes an inheritance Lara may carry by choice. Lara offers repeated dialogue with the sleeping court after it wakes, not a promise that a single declaration resolves legislation, species prejudice or time. Her wish to live independently also does not mean Mari has ceased to matter. Luca receives a separate conversation about their dating and breakup; he remains a valued person and artist, not the magical answer or an erased rival.

The final Otsu scenes give Mari an independent trajectory—training continues and she speaks her loneliness plainly. Lara's move imposes an emotional cost on the friend who housed her, even while it tests whether a rescued mermaid can live by her own competence rather than perpetual shelter. `私と生きたい` is affirmed as true, yet its practical form is disputed. Mari's last smile cannot retroactively convert her loneliness into consent to any precise future arrangement. The scarred, still-painful legs are neither a curse simply purged nor proof she must return to an old body: Lara walks on them. The ending's durable settlement is a commitment to inhabit the world and keep negotiating relation, not a guaranteed romance, mortality cure, unscarred body or harmonious kingdom.
