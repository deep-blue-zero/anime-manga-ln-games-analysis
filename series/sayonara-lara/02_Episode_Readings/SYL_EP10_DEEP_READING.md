## Retrieval, spell, and conditional sovereignty

E09's disappearance is clarified but not fully assigned. Lisa directly says she came for Lara when a powerful entity began moving. Grace then confronts the dark creature within the chamber, while Lara watches a destructive magical exchange. Lisa declares the witch eliminated and Rowan momentarily wakes. Later Lara says Grace died. Those are consequential character beliefs and visual correlations, but there is no independently verified death, spell mechanism, or agent-by-agent physical sequence. The disappearance did not consist simply of Grace carrying Lara away: Lisa's retrieval, the creature, Grace's intervention, and Rowan's awakening have separate causal roles that remain partially unresolved.

Lisa says the castle's sleep, previously Grace's protective stasis, is failing as her power weakens; awakened relatives will disappear without light. Rowan's frail, partially blind awakening supports a changed bodily condition, not the precise expiration interval. He registers Lisa's human legs as ugly and painful to see even though those legs enabled her century-long survival and the research that retrieved Lara. His paternal sorrow and dynastic dependence coexist with an inability to honor the alteration that kept his daughter alive. He also discourages Lara's remorse as beneath a princess and assigns all catastrophe to “humans,” citing Rigmor's killing and wider maritime dispossession. His loss is particular; his species-wide indictment is a political generalization. E06's personal sacrifice cannot neutralize the present insult or command.

Rowan says the castle began dimming *before* Lara's disappearance. This revises any simple causal story that her love for a human alone destroyed it. It does not prove the real prior cause or Rigmor's precise death circumstances independent of his account. Against Lisa's E08 deep love of the sea world and Grace's human romantic true-love prescription, Rowan now identifies the blade as Lara's light and proposes that killing the beloved purifies corrupted love into its “proper” form. He claims the blade is only a temporary shape, produced by a heart searching without object, and that its power sustains unlimited life. The episode gives him a visual object to interpret; it does not prove that his homicidal prescription will restore anyone. His request makes the special princess a means to family survival through a moral injury he expects her to perform.

His final question—whom Lara truly loves?—is intercut with Mari's own crisis and later phone call. The intercut makes Mari an ethically urgent possible referent, not a demonstrated exclusive target or romantic revelation. Lara also has a mutually affirmed but interrupted lover intention with Luca, family loyalty, and attachments to land and household. A “loved one” cannot be chosen by montage alone. The future danger to Mari is real as a threat Rowan proposes; the heart's exact object and the blade's autonomous behavior remain open.

## Public catastrophe, secrecy, and the right to help

The private blade becomes a public environmental image: a vertical structure near Okinoshima and flower-like forms across the lake are reported alongside changed color, odor, plant growth and transport suspension. News voices describe observations and administrative response, not a scientifically tested derivation from princess magic. Mari recognizes the flowers from the bridge. Her urgency is constrained by mundane systems: police may find a missing person but Lara has no papers or ordinary identity; emergency lake restrictions block boat passage; her household sees broken property, missed school and invented kinship explanations. None of those institutions is trivial simply because Rowan calls humans incapable of seeing light. Their inability to see his claimed metaphysical source is not an inability to register ecological harm.

Mari finally tells her parents Lara is a mermaid. The disclosure is a major revision of her E06-E09 protective secrecy, though the adults' acceptance and response are not shown before the scene leaves them. This is not only a romance triangle problem. Mari bears knowledge that Luca lacks, but he has seen a tail and has an active interest in Lara's safety. He apologizes for his waterfront failure without being made responsible for Rowan's violence. Mari's brother obtains a small boat and insists on accompanying her; Luca objects on safety grounds. Their argument makes care plural and fallible, with life jackets and a lawful small-craft category unable to eliminate the danger of an altered lake.

Lara's call is an attempt to decide the relationship unilaterally under an external threat. She thanks Mari, says there may be no next meeting, forbids a visit, and reports Grace dead. Mari insists on coming and asks for truth. Lisa cuts across the contact. Lara may be trying to protect Mari from Rowan's proposed target, but the phone does not prove that this is her only motive, or that severance would save Mari. Mari's answer after the disconnection is painfully specific: troublesome people can still be wanted, because she is lonely and wanted Lara and Gon to stay. This revises any notion that Mari's E08 “I don't want you to die” was merely duty or coercion. It does not automatically rewrite their explicitly named friendship as romance. Nor does her rescue intention give her a veto over Lara's danger assessment. The two directions of care remain in tension.
