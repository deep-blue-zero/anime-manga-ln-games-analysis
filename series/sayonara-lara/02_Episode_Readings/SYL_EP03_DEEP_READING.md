# Episode 03 — do not look away

## Transaction boundary

This reading uses bundle `SYL-B03` (encode tag `BDA70D82`, duration 1440.078 seconds). All 399 aligned Japanese cues and all 44 contact sheets were inspected. Original-resolution frames were opened around Mari's childhood conflict, Lara's return of the cross pendant, Grace's manipulation proposal, the sparring interruption and apology, the lakeside commitment, the light/ring event, and the post-credit household settlement. English remained a secondary aid. No auditory performance or continuous-motion claim is made.

The entering E02 freeze established practical care and shared shelter without a named relationship. It did not establish Mari's boxing history, the source and failure mode of her strength ethic, Lara's response to being asked to exploit Mari, a durable housing decision, or any demonstrated relation between Lara's present heart and recoverable light. Later episodes are excluded.

## Reconstruction

Lara recaps her losses and concludes that she must find the “true love” she failed to attain before. She translates that demand into a concrete fairy-tale program: find a prince in Shiga. Mari's answering self-introduction establishes a different program. A first-year at Zeze Minami High School, she has boxed for ten years since beginning with her older brother, wants to be stronger than anyone, and refuses interference. Senior boxer Nagahama recognizes her undefeated record and title “the Lightning from Omi,” then judges her uncharming.

At breakfast, Mari's household continues calling Lara her friend while Lara enthusiastically discovers ordinary food. Mari worries about having brought a mermaid home and tells Lara to stay until she returns from school. Lara follows her to the door and mistakes “school,” with roughly six hundred humans, for the name of a kingdom.

At practice Mari's upward drive leads into childhood memory. After young Mari repeatedly fought other children, her mother converted confrontation into a staring contest: whoever looked away lost and had to obey. The playful rule stops another fight, but Mari retained an apparently contradictory lesson. When she later asks to box, she admits she withheld the request because her mother said she liked gentle children who did not fight. Her mother clarifies that she most dislikes children who look away at important moments, tells Mari to do what she loves, and repeats: if you look away, you lose.

In the present, Mari and Nagahama dispute equipment, seniority, and special treatment. Their coach rebukes both for failing to look around them, then schedules a full-contact spar to decide which same-weight boxer enters the tournament. At home Grace tells Lara that the original prince's rejection left pain and fear she must overcome. Grace narrows the task to finding a new human prince and calls this the only way to save the family. Lara admits she is afraid to go outside without Mari. Grace proposes deceiving Mari into helping.

Lara does not accept the proposal. She decides she must acclimate to the surface, touch its world, and learn about humans. Her first attempts are chaotic: water is a source of comfort and fascination; household processes are unfamiliar; online ordering produces stacks of cake without an understanding of money. Mari returns to the mess while worrying about the coming spar.

Another memory shows the child Mari learning boxing by watching it with her older brother and finally stating the desire to her mother. In the present, Mari studies her mother's cross pendant and repeats the lesson not to look away. Her grandmother separately insists that Lara's parents must be contacted because an unknown child cannot remain indefinitely, then asks whether the visibly tense Mari herself is all right.

Lara begins to keep pace with Mari's run and returns the cross pendant Mari usually carries. Mari says she does not bring it running, asks what Lara will do, states that the household cannot keep her much longer, and tells her not to follow. Lara goes home, submerges her face beneath running water to calm herself, and asks how Grace met Mari. Grace minimizes Mari's rescue as chance and claims command over the comfortable tank Mari built. Lara distinguishes herself from Grace: she cannot use someone, cannot keep depending on others, and does not want to burden Mari. She decides to leave.

Carrying a packed bag and a canned cocoa bought with Yoshiya's help, Lara goes to Mari's school to offer thanks. She finds the spar and mistakes it for an attempt to kill Mari. Lara enters the ring, declares that she will not permit Mari to be hurt, presents the gift, announces her departure, and bluntly asks whether everyone hates Mari. The intervention breaks the hostile fixation into shared laughter.

Mari then addresses Nagahama directly. She admits that she is bad at consideration and has caused trouble, but wants to face boxing straight on and “coolly.” She names her prior conduct uncool, apologizes for looking away, and asks for another round. Nagahama accepts. Mari turns the same language toward Lara: she apologizes for being on edge and tells Lara, `私から……目 そらすなよ`—do not look away from me. An observer calls them two of a kind.

Both boxers pass the coach's test. Walking home beside Lake Biwa, Mari asks what Grace meant by a mermaid who can live neither in sea nor on land. Lara says she still fears humans will again call her ugly, yet has decided to live on land. Mari answers that she will do something about it, with no guarantee of success, and allows Lara back into the household. Asked why, she says perhaps it is because of `あほ` and adds that she likes `あほで……おもろい奴`—foolish, interesting people. The exact elided subject should not be forced into an English confession formula, but the exchange is the first positive preference statement directed by context toward Lara. Lara's face visibly warms.

The scene cuts into a large golden light event on the lake. A ring matching Lara's lost royal ring rises and is recovered by Lisa's party. Lisa states that the girl's heart will become the light that saves them and asks Lara to find them—and true love—quickly. The episode therefore supplies its first event-level correlation among Lara's emotionally charged exchange with Mari, her heart, generated light, and the lost ring. It does not itself name Mari as true love or demonstrate the completed restoration mechanism.

After the ending sequence, the household's practical objection is answered through deception. Grace completes a telephone performance that supplies an absent-family story, after which Lara is allowed to stay if she does not expose the ruse. Lara and Mari share the ordered cakes. Mari receives peer messages and a photograph from the boxing-club incident, indicating that Lara's disruptive protection has entered Mari's social world rather than isolating her from it.

## I. “Do not look away” is both strength and danger

Mari's ethic begins as a mother's playful conversion of violence into attention. Its deeper formulation authorizes desire: when Mari finally says she wants to box, her mother values not gentleness as compliance but the refusal to avert one's eyes at an important moment. This helps explain Mari's directness, sustained discipline, and resistance to hierarchy.

The same maxim can harden into tunnel vision. Mari defines strength as allowing no interference and, during the spar, insists that only the opponent in front of her needs to exist. The coach's earlier rebuke—look around—supplies the corrective. Attention to one object becomes avoidance of teammates, context, and her own anxiety. Her breakdown is therefore not a betrayal of the ethic but an exposure of its incomplete form.

Mari repairs it relationally. She looks at Nagahama, names her own failure, apologizes, and asks for another round. She then asks Lara not to look away from *her*. The sentence changes the maxim from solitary endurance into a claim between people. Strength becomes the capacity to remain answerable while being seen.

## II. Lara distinguishes dependence from exploitation

Grace again translates a broad problem into a closed instruction: the failed prince must be replaced by a new prince, human love is the only family rescue, and Mari can be deceived into assisting. Lara's response marks real agency. She does not abandon the family or surface quest; she rejects Grace's proposed social method. She wants to learn the world directly and later leaves rather than knowingly use Mari.

Her decision is imperfect. She still relies on Yoshiya to buy the gift, cannot support herself, and ultimately remains because Grace fabricates a family account. Yet the moral distinction matters. Lara recognizes Mari as someone whose labor and consent cannot simply be instrumentalized. This extends the reciprocity begun when Mari insisted that her broken room mattered too.

Water visualizes the difficulty. Lara reaches for it in the household and submerges her head to calm herself, but E02 established that open water cannot now house her. The surface world is not yet home, and the old element is refuge only in bounded, temporary contact. Choosing land is therefore not proof of competence; it is a commitment made under continuing bodily and social dependence.

## III. Care becomes an uncertain commitment

Mari's E02 care could still have been emergency containment. E03 makes it a future-directed, if qualified, commitment. She asks what Lara's condition means, listens to the fear of being called ugly, offers to “do something,” admits she may fail, and permits return. The lack of a plan is important. Unlike Grace's `唯一の方法`, Mari does not claim exclusive knowledge or guaranteed rescue.

The episode also preserves boundaries. Mari tells Lara to stop following and correctly says the household cannot indefinitely shelter a child whose circumstances are unknown. Her later reversal is not unconditional ownership of Lara's problem; it is a decision to stay involved. Her preference statement—liking foolish, interesting people—is affectionate and contextually directed, while remaining less determinate than a declaration of friendship or romantic love.

Lara reciprocates through protection and restitution. The cocoa is a small attempt to repay care, and her ring invasion is disastrously misinformed but unambiguously opposed to Mari's injury. Her question about whether the club hates Mari is socially disruptive precisely because it sees the hostility Mari had normalized. The laughter that follows does not prove harmony, but the later messages show that the interruption opens rather than closes Mari's peer field.

## IV. The heart/light mechanism gains a test without resolving love's category

The cut from Mari's positive preference statement and Lara's visible response to the golden eruption, rising ring, and Lisa's explanation is the strongest evidence yet for a present-tense heart/light mechanism. Unlike E02's inherited doctrine, E03 supplies an event, a recoverable object, and an observer who recognizes the phenomenon.

The test remains partial. Lisa says Lara's heart becomes saving light, then separately urges her to find true love. This wording allows at least two readings: the exchange with Mari has already produced a form of restorative light, or Lara's awakening heart has begun a process whose “true love” condition is incomplete. The episode does not label Mari a prince, partner, friend, or true love. Treating the light as a final romantic verdict would collapse precisely the taxonomy the series keeps open.

The recovered ring also reverses E02. It no longer functions only as a dangerous substitute for home. Lara's choice to live on land and accept relation coincides with the object's return to the family search party, making it a connection across worlds rather than a route backward she must drown to pursue.

## V. Belonging is built through compromised social fictions

The Otsu household is neither boundless sanctuary nor hostile institution. Grandmother's demand for parental contact is reasonable care governance. The problem is that Lara has no living, reachable surface guardians and cannot safely narrate the truth. Grace solves that mismatch through a lie.

Lara's resulting stay is therefore ethically mixed. She has just rejected using Mari, but benefits from Grace using the household's trust. The episode does not erase that contradiction; Grace immediately makes the favor transactional. Belonging becomes materially possible before it becomes fully truthful.

Comedy performs the same work on a smaller scale. Lara mistakes school for a kingdom, online ordering for free wish fulfillment, a boxing spar for attempted murder, and purchased cakes for personal generosity. Each error creates a social correction, but none sends her back into exile. The modern world remains illegible, while now becoming learnable through repeated participation.

## Character and relationship changes

**Lara** converts passive fear into an explicit decision to learn humans and live on land. She rejects Grace's proposal to deceive Mari, tries reciprocal payment, and states her fear of renewed dehumanization. Her independence remains aspirational rather than achieved.

**Mari Otsu** is verified as an exceptional first-year boxer shaped by her mother and older brother. Her strength ideal contains a contradiction between courageous attention and isolating fixation. E03 shows self-critique, apology, renewed competition, and a qualified decision to help Lara.

**Grace/Gon** openly recommends manipulating Mari and later engineers the household deception that lets Lara stay. At the same time, her defensive description of the aquarium reveals attachment to the care she refuses to name straightforwardly.

**Nagahama** is verified as Mari's senior, captain, same-weight rival, and corrective peer. Her challenge is hostile at points, but she accepts Mari's apology and continues the round; both ultimately pass.

**Mari's mother** is present in childhood memory as the source of the staring rule and permission to pursue boxing. The cross pendant makes that relation active in Mari's present concentration. E03 does not textually establish the mother's current status.

**Lisa** recovers Lara's lost ring with Kota present and interprets the light as Lara's heart becoming the means of rescue. Her search now has physical proof but her intended treatment of Lara remains open.

## Comedy and form

Eyes organize the episode. Childhood staring, the coach's instruction to look around, Mari's narrowed boxing gaze, the cross pendant, Lara's intrusion, Mari's apology for looking away, and her request that Lara look at her turn vision into an ethical action. The static progression repeatedly isolates eyes and sightlines; motion and timing remain deferred.

The cross pendant and the brass time bell interrupt Mari's closed competitive loop. Each is an object carrying another person's claim: the mother authorizes desire without isolation; the bell makes the fight a shared rule-governed practice. Lara's canned cocoa becomes a comic third object. It is inadequate repayment, yet its ordinariness lets care enter the ring without becoming heroic sacrifice.

The golden lake sequence scales private exchange into magical consequence. Its visual abundance invites romantic inference, but Lisa's wording and the unfinished restoration keep the sequence prospective. The post-credit cakes shrink that abundance back to shared appetite and mundane co-presence.

## Counterreadings and unresolved causality

Mari's return invitation may be driven by pity, responsibility, comic interest, or the destabilizing risk Grace described, rather than affection. The episode gives direct inquiry, apology, preference, and future help, but still no named relationship.

Lara's refusal to exploit Mari may be less autonomous than it sounds because she immediately chooses self-removal and then accepts Grace's lie. Even so, the refusal changes what Lara believes is permissible; inconsistency does not erase the ethical recognition.

The light could be triggered by Lara's general decision to live, her relief at shelter, Mari's words, the recovery of social connection, or another unshown magical condition. Static cuts establish designed adjacency, not continuous causal mechanics. Lisa's statement supports heart/light causality but not a completed true-love identification.

Mari's mother's maxim may be a wholesome permission rather than the source of later tunnel vision. The episode nevertheless places the maxim, the cross, narrowed-opponent language, breakdown, and apology in a causal-formal chain. The safest conclusion is that Mari has made the lesson rigid, not that the mother taught rigidity.
