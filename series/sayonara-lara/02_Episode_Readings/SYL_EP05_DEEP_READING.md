## Work is learned participation, not pure willingness

The title's declaration that Lara can die for cake is deliberately inadequate as a labor ethic. Lara offers infinite enthusiasm because she has no scale for the finite practices a job requires. The interview language she borrowed from Mari gives her the surface of employability—philosophy, strengths, weaknesses, interpersonal lubrication—while the kitchen immediately tests whether she can accept unglamorous placement, repetition, instruction, and limits.

The episode does not simply punish naïveté. Dishwashing is named as important; peeling peaches and carrying eggs place Lara inside production before she is allowed to author it. Her work becomes meaningful through dependency among tasks. Even the manager's apparent creed is unstable. `仕事なんて苦行だよ` is later qualified by `今日はな`, when making a cake with others is enjoyable. Work is neither naturally fulfilling nor essentially torture. Its quality changes with the day, the task, competence, colleagues, and the relation between contribution and recognition.

The camera and oven buttons organize this lesson. Makoto says that pressing a camera button is easy but professional photography is not. Lara later assumes that access to a more consequential button means advancement. The oven mistake exposes the distinction between operating a control and understanding a system. Her error is not lack of effort: it is effort detached from knowledge, boundary recognition, and collective consequence.

Repair therefore matters more than expulsion. Lara offers to remake what she ruined; a coworker shares responsibility; the staff mobilizes around the damage; and the next morning colleagues answer her apology by disclosing their own worse mistakes. The manager neither sentimentalizes the failure nor makes it an essence. She tells Lara to change clothes and resume work. Competence here is social and cumulative: a person becomes reliable through ordinary support, errors, accountability, instruction, and renewed participation.

## Household law and the possibility of negotiated obligation

Ema's new `家のルール` comically echoes the royal law that once confined Lara. Both translate authority into a rule governing her movement and duty. The differences are as important as the rhyme. This rule applies to Mari as well as Lara; it is debated in a domestic setting; its goals are restitution and discretionary consumption rather than purity; and compliance produces wages that remain partly Lara's. Ema takes what the household claims and returns the rest instead of absorbing Lara's total labor.

The arrangement is not free of pressure. The quoted 100,000-yen window cost becomes Lara's private target and helps turn patience into dangerous haste. Lara initially accepts employment because Makoto's romantic anecdote lets her imagine work as a prince route. The household therefore still supplies a frame whose consequences Lara cannot fully calculate. But the rule creates a public site of learning and an income rather than closing the world around her. It is a useful contrast to Rowan's law, not an uncomplicated moral opposite.

## From acquiring a prince to knowing humans

E03 already gave Lara the sentence that she must touch the land and know humans more. E05 makes the method concrete. At first, work is an instrument: earn repayment, find a prince, return to the trial. By the welcome gathering, Lara describes a desire whose object has broadened. She says that even difficult acts such as washing dishes and peeling peaches let her encounter more aspects of humans; knowledge produces further desire to know, and she now believes that feeling connects to `本当の愛`.

This is not a completed redefinition of true love. Lara still says she must unite with a human to save her family, and she does not identify the workplace, Mari, or friendship itself as the magical answer. Yet the direction changes. Love is no longer only a correct person waiting to be selected. It may emerge through sustained attention to plurality, mundane dependence, and participation with people who exceed the roles Lara assigns them.

Her “at least today” matters. It refuses both the interview's death pledge and a grand permanent vow. Lara stays through a bounded decision that can be remade after experience. The line gives temporal modesty to someone whose previous choices were organized by destiny, forever, and catastrophe.

## Lara and Mari: practical accompaniment after naming friendship

E04 named Lara and Mari as friends; E05 tests what that name does. Mari supplies application literacy, enters the same labor market, works in a different part of the shop, recognizes Lara's effort, asks the question that separates failure from chosen departure, and accompanies her into the workers' social world. She also remains the person to whom Lara explains the change in her idea of humans and true love.

Lara's responses are materially small but relationally precise. She offers to use her wages for Mari's daily cocoa. After Lisa's hostile reference to “that girl” triggers fear for the household, Lara runs home calling Mari and embraces her when she finds her safe. This is strong attachment evidence. It does not establish romantic attraction, mutual couple formation, or a completed true-love mechanism. The disciplined conclusion is that named friendship has acquired shared labor, practical support, attempted giving, intellectual disclosure, and an urgent return toward Mari under perceived threat.

## Lisa: love as protection, protection as possession

Lisa's physical reunion ends the uncertainty around whether her pursuit is affectionate. She calls Lara beloved and special, waits for her revival, takes a potion to reach her, and wants the family's restoration. The same encounter makes the political content of that love explicit. Lisa calls her human body ugly, repeats Lara's exceptional productive role, makes the two-hundred-year disaster an absolute ban on mermaid-human love, and rejects the human attachments Lara has formed.

`私があなたを守る` therefore belongs beside Rowan's and Grace's protective language. All three genuinely act for Lara while controlling what futures count as safe. Lisa's version is possessive because it treats the house and Mari not as relations Lara may interpret but as objects she does not need. She uses historical injury as a closed proof: because the earlier human prince failed, human love is impossible. Lara's `明日は違うかもしれない` answers with neither evidence nor naïve denial of the past. It refuses to let catastrophe monopolize the future.

Lisa's disgust at her own legs also shows that the kingdom's beauty system has crossed into her self-perception. Lara once called her mermaid body ugly after the prince's rejection; Lisa now calls human embodiment ugly before Lara can reject it. Each sister has learned to treat the body associated with the other species as degradation. Lara's welcome surprise does not endorse Lisa's judgment, and her continued commitment to human contact contests it in practice.

The episode's visual menace must remain carefully scoped. After Lisa excludes Mari and the house, the edit makes Mari, a workplace group photograph, the fish-marked bladelike object, Lara's flight, and Kōta's drive part of one threatened field. Lisa's `動き出した` confirms that the object's movement or activation is significant to her, and `コータ！` directly implicates concern about Kōta. Static sampling cannot prove that Lisa launched it, that Mari was its target, that Kōta intercepted it, or even the exact spatial continuity among the images. What is established is an ideological-to-material escalation: Lisa's protective claim no longer remains only a family argument, and an unexplained mechanism places attachment and danger in the same sequence.

## Photographs: difficult permanence, belonging, and exposure

Makoto distinguishes professional photography from a disposable phone image by stressing that the result cannot simply be erased. Lara first uses the camera while looking for a cake-shop prince. Later, the welcome photograph makes the workplace relation into a durable image. The photograph can certify that Lara has entered a group even though she joined through fantasy and error.

Near Lisa's exclusion of Mari and the house, photographed faces and locations become part of an ominous observational field. The episode does not establish that Makoto's camera produced every later image or that Lisa acquired a specific physical print, so no causal chain should be invented. Formally, however, the contrast is clear: the medium that materializes belonging also makes relations fixed, visible, and vulnerable to someone who wants to sever them. “Cannot be erased” becomes both promise and risk.

## The post-credit false alarm narrows rather than cancels the threat

Lara's rushed return ends in comic relief. The house is intact, Mari is present, and Lara calls herself foolish for leaping to a conclusion. This corrects Lara's immediate causal inference. It does not convert Lisa's preceding language into a harmless misunderstanding. Lisa explicitly opposes mermaid-human love and says Lara does not need the house or Mari. The unknown object's mechanics remain unresolved. The correct freeze is therefore twofold: no demonstrated attack on Mari or the household in E05, and a newly explicit conflict between Lisa's protective family project and Lara's human attachments.

## Episode-level synthesis

E05 places two institutions around Lara: a workplace that turns boundaries and mistakes into learnable participation, and a royal-family relation that turns love into an argument for restricting participation. Neither is pure. Employment begins under household pressure and generates an unsafe rush toward competence; coworkers nevertheless allow repair and continued membership. Lisa's love is reciprocal and her historical fear is grounded in real catastrophe; she nevertheless treats that history as authority over Lara's future and her chosen relations.

Lara's development lies in how she responds to each. She learns that desire to help is not the same as knowing how, apologizes without disappearing, and remains for another day. Against Lisa, she neither renounces family rescue nor accepts that the old failure has settled every future relation. Her revised route toward true love is still speculative, but it is more ethically adequate than prince acquisition: know humans through ordinary life, keep learning when they disclose new aspects, and refuse the closure that says tomorrow can only repeat yesterday.
