## Warmth, declaration, and unequal clocks

The three-week interval matters. Luca and Lara are neither strangers nor a magically certified pair: they share meetings, painting, ordinary embarrassment, his brother's teasing, and physical proximity. Lara explicitly likes him and differentiates his imperfection from the original prince. Yet she rejects Mari's proposed equivalence between his warmth and true love: `ちがう`, then says she might reach the answer with him. This preserves her own interpretive delay rather than treating the E07 fireworks as completed destiny. Grace calls him a “new prince,” while Lara finds that label inaccurate even as she appreciates the warmth inside his awkward conduct.

Leo's practical question—what does Lara want, and will waiting on a timid Luca leave things unchanged?—is not a magical instruction. The household conversation says dating is *not required* to obtain love, but that dating can help discover its shape through mutual feelings. Himeka, having received a confession and now dating someone other than Ouji, encourages Lara from a distinct history of asymmetrical attraction. Lara compresses these fallible human observations into a procedure: give Luca her feeling, receive his, form love, and obtain the true love she needs before the medicine fails. Her reasoning is understandable under threat and still carries a category error. Reciprocal romantic commitment cannot be produced on demand as an instant dose or guaranteed family-restoration output.

The waterfront distinguishes at least three things. Lara asks to be lovers, and Luca answers that he wants this too. This is explicit mutual romantic intention, a substantial revision of E07-E08's tentative relation. Next, as she moves to physical intimacy, he stops, apologizes, and asks to go slowly because she matters. His consent to the first proposition does not entail consent to the second or to an immediate magical timetable. Lara hears “slowly” against a deadline he does not know; only after pressing does she say she lacks time. He is not withholding a known cure, and she is not merely impatient. His `本当の愛って……なに` is a legitimate question in the situation, not evidence he does not care. The episode closes this encounter with rupture and external violence before they can negotiate disclosure, pace, or a shared definition.

Luca earlier tells Mari that the more he seeks Lara, the further she seems; he suspects a hidden true face. He has seen her tail in E07, yet remains outside the danger of foaming, contested royal duty, and her remembered heart-blade. Mari says Luca has helped her return and that Lara advances slowly; when asked what Lara is to her, her `あほで おもろい奴 で……大事な……` breaks before completing a noun or predicate. It strongly signals importance but cannot be silently completed as “friend,” “lover,” or “family.” The intercut to Lara/Himeka is an interruption, not an answer. Mari's knowledge advantage makes her advice to Lara and reassurance to Luca both compassionate and incomplete.

## Multiple loves and the precarious household

The title's singular `本当の愛` is put under pressure by several actual attachments. Leo's sibling concern, Himeka's changed romantic partner, household advice, the church exchange about a grandmother's love, Mari's support, and Grace's odd domestic belonging all exist alongside Lara's attraction to Luca. The priestly/organ discussion especially insists that not being romantically desired does not exhaust being loved. None is individually certified as the princess-light trigger, but none should be erased because the royal mission demands one special form.

Grace is no longer merely a talking fish who controls the rule. Her unusual sleeping makes Lara and the household worry, while Makoto's account of constructing a tank and adjusting water, plants, sand, and temperature shows sustained care to which she has adapted—even while complaining. Her maxim about a second birth “to live” contrasts Rowan's preservation of Lara's mere existence for future light production. But Lisa's extraction of mansion traces and conjecture that Grace is no longer mermaid or human, the witch's weakness, and the late supernatural removal complicate any easy verdict of benevolent mentor. The evidence does not establish what the trace measures or complete the sentence about her body. Equally, Lisa's wish that the witch be gone is a character's hostile purpose, not proof that she causes each creature's movement.

## The interruption is not a verdict on the couple

At the bridge, pale flower-like objects and an enormous toothed dark form intrude just when Lara and Luca cannot reconcile pace with mortality. Mari and the masked Kōta are separately caught in an attack field, and a blue-haired Grace-like form appears with Lara in the mist. Post-credits dialogue explicitly frames Lara as carried away and Lisa predicts Grace's destination, Okinoshima. Thus disappearance and pursuit are certain enough for a sequential cliffhanger. Exact creature identity, whether the giant mouth and black-red creature are one being or related manifestations, who orders which action, whether Grace rescues, kidnaps, or both, and the descended blade's causal role require later evidence or continuous AV. E10 must be allowed to revise this provisional attribution.

The formal collision matters even with those limits. Lara has just exposed a vital secret to Luca too late for him to respond, and the bridge's ordinary public space becomes a supernatural jurisdictional battlefield. E05's fish-marked moving object, E06 bridge damage, E07 heart-blade memory, and E09 descending point make a repeating threat-image; visual recurrence alone does not prove shared physical provenance. Grace's earlier unqualified “true love with a human” prescription is also not vindicated by a mutually desired lover declaration: no restoring light appears, and Lara still fears death. The episode interrupts a potentially ethical conversation precisely where a magical shortcut would have pretended to settle it.
