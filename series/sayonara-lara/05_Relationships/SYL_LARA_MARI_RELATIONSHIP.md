# Lara and Mari: accompaniment with an unagreed distance

## Classification at the actual endpoint

The work explicitly names Lara and Mari friends in E04 and makes them the central reciprocal attachment by E12. Lara wants to continue living in the world Mari inhabits and confirms that wanting to live with her is true; Mari says Lara's trouble made her feel less alone and later calls the departure lonely (`SYL-C0020`, `SYL-C0059`-`SYL-C0062`). This is love language and shared-life desire, not merely gratitude for rescue. It is also not an explicit mutual romantic declaration, settled couple, or agreed household plan. The strongest source-bounded classification is an emotionally central, previously named friendship whose later intimacy exceeds a narrow instrumental friendship but whose romantic status remains unresolved. A romance reading is plausible; claiming either “only platonic” or “confirmed couple” is stronger than the inspected evidence.

## Phase I — rescue, hospitality and the right to say no (E02–E03)

Mari → Lara begins with a practical act before an agreed relationship exists. She intervenes after Lara's loss at the shore/station, then the household feeds and shelters a stranger who cannot explain money, kin, body or where she can return. Mari's initial `別に` minimizes the rescue; it does not undo it. Lara → Mari begins in gratitude and dependence: she gives a name and learns Mari's, but she does not yet know how much time, money, household legitimacy or risk this hospitality costs. The lost ring is not just a prop Lara wants recovered; she says “my ring, my family, my place to return” in sequence, so Mari meets someone whose home has become materially inaccessible before either girl can classify the other (E02 S06–S13; `SYL-L0012`–`L0013`, `SYL-R0007`–`R0008`).

The first durable conflict is not “does Mari care?” but who can set the terms of care. An Otsu adult asks for a parent's contact and rejects indefinite residence without a story. Mari insists Lara stop following her training route and says the family cannot keep her forever. Lara hears the burden correctly but over-answers it: she says she cannot exploit Mari the way Grace proposes and decides to leave, without testing whether Mari wants *that* outcome. Meanwhile Mari's own “do not look away” boxing rule has become narrow fixation; after a disrupted spar she apologizes to Nagahama and Lara, then tells Lara not to look away from her. Both corrections matter. Mari admits that irritation can be partly about her own pre-bout state, and Lara discovers that protecting someone by vanishing can itself deny them a response (E03 11:15–18:35; `SYL-C0013`–`C0015`, `SYL-L0017`–`L0020`; relationship ledger E03).

The resulting shelter is not unconditional possession. Grace and the household arrange a false guardian explanation, with a monetary cost and the requirement not to expose the lie. That makes Lara administratively legible but still dependent on secrecy and other people's labor. Mari's promise to “do something” and liking of foolish/interesting people is a positive preference, not a complete contract for every future imposition. Lara's fear of being ugly again and her declaration that she will live on land give Mari new information she did not have at the first rescue. Neither now has the full foaming or blade account (E03 S12–S16; `SYL-L0020`–`L0022`, `SYL-R0011`–`R0016`).

## Phase II — friendship becomes ordinary practice (E04–E07)

E04's naming is asymmetrical in origin and mutual in acceptance. Himeka prompts Lara to identify Mari as her friend; Mari answers yes after the socially public question. The word `友達` is actual source evidence, not a later analyst's consoling category. Its value lies partly in Lara's prior uncertainty: she has seen admiration, dating, family-like care and fairy-tale princes confused in human speech. A named friend can be chosen without the mermaid princess's light test. Yet a public label does not tell Mari how to handle every private disclosure or Lara how much she may assume; the relation remains in formation (E04 S05–S10; `SYL-L0023`–`L0030`, `SYL-C0020`).

Ordinary reciprocity accumulates in uneven increments. Mari corrects Lara's shopping and phone errors, lends a family phone for Luca contact, accompanies job search and teaches local routines. Lara does not only receive: she chooses a small purchase, endures bakery work, notices Mari's taste for cake, later makes a cheering banner and promises custard. Her E05 application takes a night of assisted writing; the workplace subsequently values real dishwashing. This matters for the dyad because Mari can increasingly relate to someone who contributes and can promise a concrete gift, not only to an emergency guest. The shop also gives Mari her own motive—earn for new earbuds while limiting shifts for club—and places the girls in distinct roles. Their shared life is not identical work or identical future (E04 S09–S10; E05 01:37–10:38; E07 S04; E08 S02/S16; `SYL-C0021`–`C0024`, `SYL-F0045`).

Luca's arrival changes the information geometry without erasing the friendship. Mari supplies the phone and invites Lara into family fireworks while explicitly leaving space for friends or someone close. When Lara wants to cut Luca off because the old blade might injure him, Mari asks her to hear his feeling; Lara initially misconstrues this as an instruction to end things decisively. Mari is thus a mediator of *procedure*, not the chooser of Lara's partner. Lara's later hope for Luca is no betrayal of Mari, and Mari's practical support need not conceal romantic jealousy. But the third person's presence does make Lara's autonomous relational life more visible, while Mari's knowledge of Lara's body and danger remains deeper than Luca's (E07 S04/S12–S16; `SYL-C0034`–`C0036`, `SYL-R0032`–`R0033`).

## Phase III — medicine, fear and incompatible protection (E08–E10)

Lisa changes Mari's decision environment by disclosing expiry/foaming and providing partial medicine. Mari knows enough to fear Lara's death, but not enough to certify Lisa's claim that human love is impossible. Lara knows the ordinary life she wants—futon, work, Mari's match, Luca, tomorrow—yet has not independently verified the full medical horizon. Mari gives the vial, urges her to return with Lisa and finally says `死んでほしくない`; Lara says she is afraid too. A clean “Mari supports Lara's choice” summary misses Mari's temporary attempt to redirect it. A clean “Mari controls Lara” summary misses that she discloses the risk and Lara subsequently leaves, returns and restates her own land choice. E08's van sleep and balcony promise follow a real disagreement, not the absence of one (E08 S03–S16; `SYL-C0038`–`C0042`, `SYL-L0062`–`L0067`, `SYL-R0035`–`R0040`).

E09 adds a quieter asymmetry. Luca asks Mari about the girl he is dating or hopes to date, and Mari knows about the mortal threat he does not. Her description of Lara as important stops at `大事な……`; it is dishonest to complete that phrase on her behalf. She can acknowledge his help and let him approach Lara without transferring every secret. Lara, in turn, makes an explicit lover request to Luca under a deadline, but does not tell him the deadline until after he asks for gradual physical pace. This is not a triangle in which Mari automatically loses or wins. It is a network of differently informed people whose forms of care are not interchangeable (E09 S07/S12–S13; `SYL-C0044`–`C0045`, `SYL-L0069`–`L0071`, `SYL-R0041`–`R0044`).

The E10 phone call reverses E03's withdrawal under greater stakes. Rowan has demanded a loved one's blood; Lara tells Mari not to come, thanks her and says they may not meet again. It is reasonable to infer protective intent, but Lara does not give Mari enough information to decide whether separation would work. Mari asks for an explanation and refuses a unilateral goodbye. Her private statement that she wanted even troublesome Lara and Gon to stay because she is lonely is an affirmative account of her own life, not an obligation Lara must obey. Mari then tells the Otsu household the mermaid truth and joins Luca and her brother's risky, materially planned rescue. This extends the relationship's cost to a family and a public lake, and it lets Mari act on a different assessment of risk than Lara's (E10 S07–S13; `SYL-C0050`–`C0052`, `SYL-L0076`–`L0078`, `SYL-R0047`–`R0049`).

## Phase IV — approach does not abolish difference (E11–E12)

E11's child-Mari/fish-Grace scene belongs to historical relation, not the present rescue. It nevertheless supplies an important prehistory: Mari could refuse to leave a depleted nonhuman creature to die before she met Lara, while Grace learned that human land can hold care. This supports a *bounded* pattern of approaching vulnerability; it does not license a claim that Mari has magical insight or never respects a request to be left alone (E11 S12–S13; `SYL-C0057`, `SYL-R0053`).

At E12 the two directions remain distinct. Mari → Lara: she admits she cannot see Lara's heart and therefore must come near enough to learn, brings back their professional-boxer/giant-cake promise, accepts `私とあんたは違う` and says Lara's difficult company made her feel less alone. Lara → Mari: she sees a potentially lethal blade, asks Mari not to approach, articulates inherited species hatred and initially chooses disappearance as a supposed way to prevent harm. Mari's approach neither supplies consent to Rowan's blood ritual nor invalidates Lara's fear; Lara's warning neither proves Mari's care unwelcome nor gives Lara a right to decide Mari's whole future. Their confrontation is a collision between self-protection, other-protection and another person's freedom to answer (E12 S05–S09; `SYL-L0085`–`L0087`, `SYL-R0054`–`R0055`).

Mari's reported death and revival are a huge event but a poor shortcut to relational classification. Lara's later avowal is person-directed: she wants tomorrow where Mari lives and refuses to say her own life should vanish. The new light and revival follow, but their exact mechanism and whether any single love category is necessary are not isolated. The death does not make the two a confirmed couple, and the revival does not automatically repair Lara's earlier withholding or settle how they will live. The line `本当の愛なんか要らない` rejects the prescribed test while affirming a particular attachment; it is not an anti-love confession (E12 13:06–18:14; `SYL-C0059`–`C0060`, `SYL-W0049`–`W0050`).

## Phase V — the six-month test of ordinary consent

After the time jump Mari still trains, the Otsu home still welcomes Lara and Lara plans an independent address reached by train. Lara → Mari includes a truthfully affirmed desire to live with her; Mari → Lara includes the question why that desire entails leaving and the unambiguous `さびしいわ`. Before the question, their easy teasing over whether Lara can live alone and their mutual concern about training and painful legs show ordinary intimacy has not simply vanished. Yet the planning conversation that could reconcile mobility with shared life is absent. The shot of Mari on the platform while Lara is aboard makes their current separation visible; Mari's later smile cannot supply a timetable, lease, visit agreement or a private romantic answer (E12 19:51–23:09; `SYL-L0089`, `SYL-R0057`–`R0058`, `SYL-F0064`–`F0065`).

This is the dyad's strongest counterexample to a reassuring model of reciprocity: Lara can state a wish for shared life and still decide its immediate form before Mari is satisfied. It is also a counterexample to the charge that Mari only holds Lara in place: she asks a pointed question and names loneliness rather than using rescue as ownership. Future closeness is possible, and final severance is not shown. The analyst must keep the *unagreed distance* as a relationship state, not replace it with either “romance confirmed” or “friendship ended.”

The phase account above is not a single intimacy score. These states distinguish information from inference and record which person's gift creates a cost for the other.

## Rescue, reciprocity and unequal knowledge

Mari's E02 help is costly before it is sentimental. Lara often presses beyond boundaries; Mari's blunt correction and household rules make care negotiable. Lara gradually brings more than an emergency: she joins work, interacts with Mari's family, attends boxing, learns to prepare a promised cake, and becomes someone whose company Mari says made her less alone. The exchange is not symmetric. Lara lives under Mari's roof and cannot initially survive without information or medicine; Mari has an independent school and athletic structure and later risks death without knowing the supernatural blade's trajectory. E10's one-sided farewell protects Mari in Lara's mind while depriving her of a say. E12 Mari's approach is an answer to that asymmetry, not an assertion she can read Lara's mind. Their E12 conflict preserves difference: `私とあんたは違う` is accepted before attachment is named. Reciprocity is a pattern of response and correction, not equal need at every moment.

## Romantic and nonromantic evidence, fairly compared

The case for romance cites narrative centrality, intimate address, concern beyond self-preservation, the loved-person blade crisis, Mari's loneliness, and Lara's wish to live in Mari's world. Some static framing and an E12 death/revival embrace heighten that reading. The countercase cites E04's explicit friendship name, no later mutual change of category, no unambiguous same-sex attraction or negotiated couple status, and Mari's final challenge to Lara's chosen distance. This countercase cannot dismiss person-directed love as “just” friendship; friendship is itself consequential here. Conversely, the E12 `本当の愛なんか要らない` rejects the inherited quest in the very context of wanting Mari's world, so neither “no love” nor “Mari definitively satisfies the royal true-love formula” fits the line. Magic is insufficient as a relationship classifier (`SYL-L0087`, `SYL-W0050`).

Lara and Luca are a necessary internal comparator. E07's individual recognition develops into E09 mutual lover designation with an explicit disagreement over pace and concealed mortality; E12 acknowledges dating and breakup. The show can name dating when it means dating. This does not impose a heterosexual norm that forbids Lara/Mari romance, but it makes the absence of a parallel category a real piece of evidence. The original prince is another, earlier romance script and must not be conflated with Luca. The story's yuri-adjacent focalization and a viewer's classification are distinct from a source-certified couple; reception is handled separately.

## Departure as a test, not an epilogue exemption

The end refuses an easy choice between “if they love each other Lara must stay” and “if Lara is free Mari cannot object.” Lara's long deprivation gives independent movement genuine value; Mari's shelter has also made that movement possible, and she is entitled to say the absence hurts. The six-month time jump shows routine, work and plans but not the decisive negotiation of a shared future. Mari's last smile can express hope or respect without answering her own question. This structural elision is the strongest criticism of the dyad's closure, regardless of whether a viewer wanted romance. Future visits are possible, not shown; loneliness is spoken, not magically cured.

The remaining performance/AV debt matters especially here: timing, intonation, gaze and train editing could sharpen a romance or friendship inference, but no such uninspected evidence is promoted. The current conclusion is precise about what the Japanese text and static progression do—and do not—establish (`SYL-D0001`-`SYL-D0003`).
