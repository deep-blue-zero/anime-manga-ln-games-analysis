# Grace/Gon: a rescuer who engineers the choice

## The double life

Grace is not merely a witch who imposes a plot on Lara. E11 reveals her as Rigmor's twin, an early dreamer of human land whose desire predates Lara's and Rowan's prohibition. She finds transformative medicine, asks Rigmor to travel together, experiences her sister's choice of Rowan as abandonment, and suffers rejection by a human beloved who names her monstrous. Rigmor returns through ring-light and dies during the consequent blade crisis; Grace's later confession of killing a loved person keeps her implicated even while human persecution remains real (`SYL-C0053`-`SYL-C0054`). Her comedy as the small tank-fish Gon rests on this history of lost scale and authority, not a simple mask that makes her harmless.

Grace gathers every drop of Lara's foam and uses Rowan's full donated light to revive her. She sleeps the court to prevent his planned use of Lara against humans and expends enough power to occupy a small fish vessel while waiting (`SYL-C0055`, E11 S09-S11). This is sacrifice and protection. It is also a unilateral suspension of others and an attempt to shape Lara's second life around Grace's own unfinished dream. Her E01-E02 rules about voice, cost, true love, family rescue and Lara-exclusive responsibility construct the available choices, even where she alone can provide a route past Rowan's ban (`SYL-C0003`, `SYL-C0010`). The E09 maxim that rebirth starts a new story gains literal force after E11, but does not authorize treating Lara as its instrument.

## Knowledge, omission and projection

The pivotal question is not whether Grace is secretly benevolent or malevolent. It is what she knows, tells, does and cannot control. She knows the medicine and castle's history and has seen a soul-hungry blade; Lara learns her foaming risk and her father's anti-human purpose through piecemeal testimony. Mari's E08 question about the medicine's horizon receives no fully disinterested mechanism. Grace admires Lara's earlier refusal to kill a beloved even at the cost of her first life, unlike Grace's own blade catastrophe (`SYL-C0056`). Yet she continues to imagine that Lara will find a human “true love” and realize the older land dream. This ideal partly emancipates Lara from dynastic vengeance and partly binds her to a different inherited purpose. Her epistemic advantage makes her silences consequential.

Young Mari's childhood rescue of a depleted fish-form Grace (E11 S12-S13) rebuts Grace's despair that land contains no light. Mari acts without recognizing a royal witch or demanding magical utility; Grace says she sees beautiful light. The scene explains the Gon household tie that later becomes water, food, temperature and tank maintenance (E09 S09-S10; `SYL-C0046`, `SYL-R0053`). It is **not** evidence that Grace survives the E10 fight. E10 characters say she died/gone; E12 says her spell loosens and shows later fishbowl imagery, but supplies no independently identified present Grace body or speech (`SYL-W0047`, `SYL-W0052`). The original-frame chronology correction is load-bearing: a sympathetic monograph may not invent a final reunion.

## The same dream changes its ethical owner

Grace's land experiment also changes the category in which she claims herself. In the E11 mirror account, her human beloved calls the revealed mermaid a monster without a human heart and orders her not to use human words. Grace answers `わたしは 人間だ`—a claim to the life and speech she has been practicing, not a biological fact the film independently certifies. The injury is therefore not only romantic rejection. A person who tried to make a human life is told she cannot belong to the class whose language she speaks. This helps explain both her later suspicion that a single word could exclude Lara and the ethical problem of removing Lara's voice preemptively: Grace protects against the form of violence she suffered by taking away one of the younger woman's means of answering it (E01 13:10–13:53; E11 11:54–12:12; `SYL-L0005`, `L0081`).

Grace begins as an explorer inside a court that wants princess light. She studies a forbidden transformative medicine because the land represents a new form of life; her E11 `私自身が` makes the wish first-person. Young Rowan initially defends the work, yet the court's dimming castle also makes it attractive as a possible source of usable light. Rigmor's decision to stay with Rowan does not invalidate Grace's dream, but Grace experiences the division as loss of the person with whom she meant to undertake it. The human beloved's disgust and soldiers later make land dangerous without proving that the dream was foolish. Rigmor's final visit offers a more generous rule—light can exist wherever one lives for love—while her death and Grace's confession of killing a loved person turn the dream into trauma (E11 S01–S08; `SYL-C0053`–`C0054`, `SYL-L0079`–`L0081`).

Afterward Grace's rescue impulse is real and not innocent. She can identify a different future for Lara because she has seen the cost of Rowan's anti-human solution and because Lara, in Grace's account, once rejected a hungry blade rather than kill a beloved. She gathers foam, receives Rowan's full light, revives the child, sleeps the obstructing court and sustains herself in a reduced vessel. Those are concrete, costly acts. But when Grace calls Lara “last light,” she also asks the child to prove her own lost thesis that the land contains love. She rescues Lara *from* one imposed role by offering a route that is partly another authored role. The question is not whether the witch “really meant well”; it is which costs she chose for herself and which she selected for people who could not answer (E11 S09–S11; `SYL-C0055`–`C0056`, `SYL-W0044`–`W0046`).

## Instruments of care and instruments of control

Grace's power is unevenly distributed. She understands medicine and the old catastrophe, commands a refuge built at Rowan's request, can alter bodies and suspend the court, and controls information about Lara's mission. E01's voice-taking has an explicit protective rationale—one human word could expose a mermaid—and a simultaneous cost: Lara loses an ordinary way to ask for recognition. Grace calls true love more than infatuation, but soon presents a human prince as the practical route, then E03 even proposes deceiving Mari to gain help. Lara's refusal `誰かを利用するなんてできない` is a direct ethical correction by the supposed pupil. In E04 Grace's remembered promise to return the voice “someday” leaves the duration in her hands. In E08 the serious foaming prognosis reaches Mari from Lisa, not a fully disclosed account from Grace (E01 12:37–13:53; E03 06:41–08:04, 14:06–14:25; E04 14:14–14:40; E08 03:43–08:16; `SYL-L0004`–`L0005`, `L0015`–`L0018`, `L0026`, `L0062`).

Her accounts should be tiered rather than accepted or dismissed together. She knows how to make a consequential medicine and can report her own role in Lara's revival; her claim that a particular human love is the *only* route to sustainable light is not demonstrated. She may know that the court's sleep depends on her spell, but E10 characters' “Grace is gone/dead” and E11's historical fish rescue do not verify her present body. Lisa's assertion that she is no longer mermaid or human is unfinished and attributed, not a settled taxonomy. A reconstruction of Grace confronting a new problem should use her demonstrated technical competence and impulse to secure the vulnerable, but abstain on unshown limits, exact dose or repeatability (`SYL-C0046`, `C0048`, `C0055`; `SYL-W0035`, `W0047`, `W0052`).

## Person-specific relations and ordinary vulnerability

With Rowan, Grace can mock royal helplessness while still maintaining the infrastructure that preserves his family. Each relies on the other and interprets Lara's second life against the other's plan. With Rigmor, Grace first wants a co-traveler and later receives a final affirmation she cannot keep without grief. With Lisa, medicine scarcity and spatial control create a long asymmetry; Grace's E06 dismissal of their foaming risk is callous even if Lisa's theft and threat are not harmless. With Lara, she offers both enabling instruction and nonconsensual narrative pressure. With Mari she is not only witch or teacher: child Mari finds a small creature who asks to die, insists she need not, and subsequently makes a place for her at home. The E09 tank's water, plants, sand, temperature and attention are mundane supports for a once-imposing maker of destinies. Grace complains about the container and nonetheless survives within it (E06 S05/S09; E09 S09; E11 S12–S13; `SYL-R0028`–`R0030`, `R0053`, `SYL-F0048`).

The Gon comedy has causal force because it reverses jurisdiction. A figure who can dictate Lara's trial has to be fed, housed, observed and perhaps rescued by the humans she uses as part of that trial. It does not prove she has abandoned manipulation: in E03 she still treats Mari as a resource. It also does not make her domestic adaptation fake. The more useful model is someone who can accept practical care without making herself transparent to the caregivers. A question that asks her to surrender a protective plan should therefore expect a possible mixture of honest fear, technical reasons, mockery and information retention. E11 supplies a counterexample to total control—she spends power to save someone and ends dependent—but not evidence that she would voluntarily cede every decision. Which danger she knows, whether the protected person has heard the alternatives, and whether a reversible safeguard exists are the variables needed before predicting a response.

## Comedy, image and reliability

In E02 the imposing witch reappears behind aquarium glass as a pet Mari casually calls Gon. This inversion lets Lara and Mari hold incompatible views of the same being; practical husbandry becomes a form of power over a figure who previously controlled Lara's mobility. E09 household recollection reveals unusual sleep and exact environmental needs, complicating any omnipotence model (`SYL-F0009`, `SYL-F0048`). But the joke cannot erase the witch's earlier control, just as her guilt cannot erase the rescue. E11's hidden mirror exposes history Rowan would rather compress and history Grace herself stages or selects; it is evidence with a frame, not a narrator whose every causal statement becomes world law.

Grace's best defense is that she saved Lara when Rowan could only imagine a weapon, protected the court from an anti-human war, and paid bodily cost. The strongest prosecution is that she manufactured Lara's dependence, withheld pertinent information and transferred her own dream to a child already carrying excessive duty. Both survive E12. Lara's final rejection of the prescribed `本当の愛` does not prove Grace never loved or helped her; it rejects Grace's authority to specify the form of Lara's light (`SYL-C0060`). Her present fate remains an explicit limit, not a hole to fill with an image of childhood rescue. No uninspected vocal delivery or precise fatal choreography is asserted (`SYL-D0001`-`SYL-D0003`).
