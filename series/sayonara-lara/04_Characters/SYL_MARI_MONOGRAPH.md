# Mari Otsu: care without clairvoyance

## Governing account

Mari is not merely a practical counterweight to Lara's fantasy. She has a boxing life, a household, ordinary irritations, a wish for company and a capacity to say no. Her characteristic care begins as action with boundaries: she rescues Lara and feeds and shelters her, while resenting the trouble and asking for restitution (E02 S06/S11-S13; `SYL-C0007`). At the climax she still refuses omniscience. `近づかんと分からへん` means she must approach to understand; it does not claim access to an invisible mermaid light (E12 S05; `SYL-L0085`). This makes her a relational agent rather than the magic key which simply unlocks Lara.

## A life beyond Lara

Mari's boxing emerges through family memory and training discipline, not only as an allegory for supporting the heroine. E03 pairs a childhood account of the mother, sparring, coach demands and her own refusal to avert her eyes. E08 gives competitive risk and a declared ambition toward professional and world-level fighting; the final result in that episode's sampled coda remains carefully bounded by the E08 checkpoint. E12 shows her still training, with a more demanding regimen (`SYL-C0039`, E12 S12). The mother-image of someone beside her looking in the same direction helps articulate desired accompaniment without identifying a single object of romance. Mari's father, mother and older brother also have claims on her attention. In E10 she confesses Lara's mermaid identity to them and the brother acquires a boat despite practical danger (`SYL-R0048`). Concealment, trust and a sibling's concern are costs of the rescue plot.

Her everyday preferences and comic irritation are not defenses against every attachment. She can complain about Lara's breakage, resist school or household disruption, share food and rest, enjoy an outing, give a rough-edged encouragement and articulate fear. E04's explicit `友達` is a relational name she chooses after receiving a confession that she distinguishes from romance (`SYL-C0020`). In E08 she urges Lara back toward Lisa and medicine because she fears death, even though that advice could separate them. Her care can constrain Lara, so it must be evaluated by outcome and response rather than assumed benevolent.

## Changing relation and asymmetric risk

The E09 unfinished `大事な……` cannot be completed by an interpreter with the desired category. E10 is clearer about a different issue: Mari says she wanted troublesome Lara and Gon around because she is lonely, and rejects a protective goodbye delivered without her consent. In E11 retrospect young Mari found fish-form Grace on shore and took her home, explaining a prior care relationship rather than rescuing Grace after E10 (`SYL-C0057`, `SYL-R0053`). Her history of tending a nonhuman precedes the present heroic crisis, but does not grant her a universal capacity to save anyone.

E12 lets Mari reject both species fatalism and false sameness. When Lara says they inhabit different worlds, Mari agrees they are different. She then names particular traits—foolish, troublesome, obstructive—and the specific change Lara made: she felt less alone in her company (E12 S07; `SYL-L0086`). This is stronger evidence of actual mutual life than a vague “everyone is the same” moral. Mari is reportedly killed in the ensuing blade crisis and later revived, but the physical path and magic remain uninspected in continuous video. Her death is not a transparent receipt for Lara's successful love test (`SYL-C0059`, `SYL-W0049`).

## The departure problem

After six months Mari still trains and the Otsu home remains an available welcome, while Lara plans to live elsewhere. Mari does not hide the cost behind a cheerful promise: Lara has said she wants to live with her, so why go, and `さびしいわ` (“I'm lonely”) (E12 S12; `SYL-R0058`). Lara says her wish is real, yet no terms of future cohabitation, visitation or commitment are established. Mari follows the train in the sampled sequence and finally smiles; the smile demonstrates neither painless acceptance nor a scheduled reunion. Calling Mari “cured” of loneliness would ignore her own last avowal. Calling her abandoned forever would ignore continuing invitations and Lara's affirmation. The ending gives her a voice to contest the heroine's self-authorship but not a jointly agreed resolution.

## Boxing is a chosen discipline, not a borrowed metaphor

Mari's earliest articulated desire is not to protect Lara but to box. Child Mari fears that saying so will violate her mother's preference for a gentle child; she turns the request into a mock fight and eye-contact challenge. Her mother refuses the performance and asks why Mari did not simply say what she wanted. The remembered maxim—do what you like, do not look away at an important time—authorizes an activity and a habit of attention, not a command to win every encounter. This distinction matters in E03, when present Mari has tightened “look at the opponent” into a defensive refusal of distracting voices. During sparring she rejects others' calls and even interprets Lara's intervention as an obstacle. Afterward she admits that she was uncool, apologizes to Nagahama for looking away, requests another round and apologizes to Lara for irritability. The principle becomes adult only when she can admit its own misuse (E03 09:52–11:07, 15:22–18:35; `SYL-C0013`–`C0014`, `SYL-L0017`–`L0019`).

Boxing gives Mari a peer field with its own status and risks. Nagahama is a senior whose fight Mari wants to meet properly, not an enemy defined by Lara's comfort. Coach criticism and the E08 final challenge her distracted readiness; Mari says she has decided to fight, but the sampled episode does not certify that final's score. Her later aim is professional competition and perhaps the world's best, and the E12 six-month coda says her practice menu has increased and today's work will be hard. She is hungry despite a meal because training has material bodily demands (E08 S09–S16; E12 21:18–21:32; `SYL-C0041`–`C0042`, `SYL-L0067`, `SYL-L0089`). The giant cake promise is reciprocal precisely because she keeps a future not reducible to keeping Lara alive. It is not evidence that Lara causes her boxing success.

Mari's competitiveness can become narrowness: E03 shows the gaze maxim isolating her from coach, peer and Lara; E08 shows Lara's mortality information intruding on a tournament. But it also supplies an ethical resource. She can return to an opponent after admitting fault, answer a coach's demand for a present choice, and keep training after the supernatural climax. “Cold boxer” misses how frequently Mari is moved by worry and how she must *work* to keep both sport and relation in view. “Born caregiver” misses that she is pursuing a difficult, chosen practice before and after Lara enters it.

## Household, peers and the texture of ordinary care

The Otsu home imposes limits through more than Mari. A household adult wants a parent's contact for an unidentified guest and later demands work from both girls; the window costs money. Mari's E05 job search is constrained by club priority—she asks for roughly one shift a week—and a wish for new earbuds, a small self-interested preference absent from a saintly caretaker model. She sits through applications with Lara, whose written form takes all night, but refuses the absurd interview vow to die for cake. Her “I cannot die” grounds an ordinary paid job in the life of the worker rather than heroic sacrifice. The store places Mari on the sales floor and Lara in the kitchen: shared employment is not identical experience (E03 11:15–11:44; E05 01:37–06:11; `SYL-L0033`–`L0035`).

This is why her irritability has to be read in context. She does not grant an indefinite right to her time, running route, household privacy or school life. In E03 she asks Lara why she keeps following, says the family cannot house her forever and tells her not to follow; after her own boxing failure she acknowledges that the sharpness was partly hers. In E04 she does not just silently tolerate Lara: she eventually accepts the public “friend” category. In E06 she compares a sister quarrel to a month without speaking to her brother, making a family idiom available to Lara without pretending their circumstances match. In E07 she lends the old phone, teaches use and offers the annual fireworks as a social space that can include family, friends and Luca. Mari can be terse and practically generous at the same time; neither quality cancels the other (E03 11:47–12:46, 18:26–18:35; E04 S09; E06 S15; E07 S04/S12; `SYL-C0020`, `SYL-L0049`, `L0052`, `L0058`).

Her ways of communicating care vary with audience. To Grace/Gon in E02 she demands that an apparently authoritative fish account for making Lara cry, recasting cosmic trial language as an interpersonal wrong; later the fish's tank needs real maintenance, and E11 reveals that child Mari once picked her up from shore before the present plot. To Lara she provides food, shelter, medicine, a phone and blunt correction, but also says she does not want her to die and later that she is lonely. To Luca she can acknowledge his help and advocate hearing his feeling, while refusing to complete a private definition of Lara for him in E09; in E10 she must break the household's cover story and use her brother's boating help. None of these are interchangeable performances of “practical realism.” Different audiences allow different amounts of information and tenderness (`SYL-L0010`, `L0057`–`L0059`, `L0063`, `L0069`, `L0076`–`L0078`; `SYL-R0042`–`R0049`).

## Knowledge, risk and refusal

Mari usually asks what an action means for someone concrete before accepting its grand label. She objects when Grace reduces Lara to a trial, asks who disclosed the foaming prognosis, checks whether the vial actually helps and tells Lara the feared death risk rather than hiding it for comfort. Yet she can also overreach. Her E08 instruction to return to Lisa is born of `死んでほしくない`, not proof that Lisa's sea-love theory is right or that Mari may settle Lara's home. Mari's own line that she knows nothing about mermaids is an important brake on a generic “she always sees the truth” account (E02 S06; E08 S03–S09; `SYL-L0062`–`L0065`).

The E07 Luca dispute shows a procedural norm before the finale: Mari tells Lara to hear the other person's feeling before declaring an end. She does not decree that Lara must accept Luca; indeed Lara first translates the advice into a more decisive goodbye than Mari intended. In E09 she does not know how to answer Luca's incomplete access to Lara's “real face” without disclosing mermaid danger. In E10 she tells the family the truth once secrecy itself blocks the rescue, while weighing police, identity documents, boating restrictions, a brother's license-free craft and life jackets. She is capable of urgent action and of material safety talk, neither wholly rule-bound nor heedlessly brave (E07 S13–S14; E09 S07; E10 S07–S13; `SYL-C0051`–`C0052`).

At E12 she explicitly says a human cannot see Lara's heart. `近づかんと分からへん` is continuous with the earlier wish to hear Luca's feeling and with her willingness to revise the household cover story, but it is not a doctrine of limitless access. She approaches despite an articulated killing risk and tells Lara that difference remains real. This is a high-stakes exception to ordinary safety caution, and she does not gain consent merely by loving someone. Lara's plea to stay away is a serious counterclaim. The model can explain Mari's choice—an incomplete account and a unilateral farewell have already failed—without certifying it as the only ethical response or a supernatural cure (`SYL-L0085`–`L0087`, `SYL-R0054`–`R0055`).

## Conditional reconstruction and abstention

With an awkward houseguest unrelated to Lara, Mari would likely ask for concrete terms—identity, length of stay, damage, work, her own training schedule—while possibly supplying immediate food or help before trust has been earned. E02–E05 support both responses; they do not establish how she treats every stranger. If someone close withheld dangerous information, E08's foaming disclosure and E10's broken cover story suggest anger and a demand for explanation, followed by practical mobilization if the person remains at risk. Whether she insists on disclosure to third parties would depend on the actual danger and whom it exposes. A pure “always tell everyone” rule would misread how long she keeps Lara's mermaid identity private.

If Lara wants a risky choice affecting only Lara, Mari may initially urge the safer course, as at the E08 waterfront, but can continue beside a choice she did not originate, as at the tournament and E12 shore. This is neither unconditional compliance nor permanent veto. If the risk directly includes Mari, her right to choose approach must be weighed against Lara's warning rather than assumed. Mari's reserve is not indifference, and explicit loneliness is not generic dependency: her mother, brother, peers, work and boxing remain live ties at the last station. No source certifies a private romantic attraction, exact post-train practice, performed stoicism, or a future date on which the pair will live together. Retrospective character reasoning should abstain where these unshown variables decide the answer (`SYL-C0039`, `C0059`–`C0062`; `SYL-D0001`–`D0003`).

## Affect, speech and her own social world

Mari's everyday sociability does not announce itself as a long reassuring speech. E02 she initially objects to a broken window, asks for repayment and has training to attend; she also notices Grace making Lara cry and demands that Gon answer for it. This is not a seamless caretaker instinct. She can delay chasing Lara when Grace orders it, because a fish's command and a claim about a dangerous princess are not yet persuasive to her. When she later helps, the act is her own, not proof Grace has enlisted her. The space between an irritated “why should I?” and a practical rescue is characteristic: she needs a person and situation rather than a grand label (E02 08:32–10:08, 19:20–20:14; `SYL-L0010`, `L0013`; `SYL-R0007`–`R0008`).

She is capable of pleasure that has nothing to do with Lara's safety. Boxing makes her proud enough to answer a childhood admirer who says she looks cool; she wants to be the coolest version of herself, though E03 exposes the danger of measuring that by never hearing another voice. She enjoys cake and considers new earbuds worth paid work while keeping her club schedule primary. E09 she scores well at karaoke and leaves to run, letting Luca spend time with Lara rather than treating her friendship as exclusive supervision. E12 she describes harder training, a larger appetite and a difficult afternoon. These details are modest but necessary: a hypothetical everyday scene must not render her permanently stationed beside a mermaid or allergic to enjoyment (E03 15:22–18:35; E05 01:59–03:07; E09 S01; E12 21:18–21:32; `SYL-C0013`, `C0023`, `C0062`).

Her textual register is locally marked and economical. The supplied Japanese frequently gives her `〜へん`, `〜や`, direct questions and compressed judgments; Lara often builds longer formal or declarative formulations. Mari can say “friend” when the category is publicly asked, “I don't want you to die” when evasion fails, and “I'm lonely” when Lara's plan becomes real. That trajectory is not a claim that she speaks in a flat, low or sarcastic *voice*: no heard performance has been admitted. It is evidence that she chooses explicit words when a short practical response no longer carries the relation. Her E12 `近づかんと分からへん` continues an earlier preference for confronting the person instead of accepting a story *about* them, but E08's command to return to Lisa shows she can also act on incomplete borrowed information when frightened (`SYL-L0019`–`L0020`, `L0029`, `L0063`, `L0085`–`L0089`; `SYL-D0001`).

The strongest predictive caution is that the same blunt action can mean different things. “Go back to your sister” at E08 is not indifference; it is fear of death. “Do not follow me” at E03 is a real boundary and partly a sign she is straining before a match. “I know nothing about mermaids” in E08 is an honest epistemic limit spoken amid a peer's pressure, not a permanent refusal to learn. “Why are you leaving?” at E12 is a claim that a friend should explain a life-changing choice, not a demand for title to Lara. To decide among those meanings, the model needs the current sporting stakes, household burden, mortality information, past disclosure and whether Mari has been permitted to answer. A stock tsundere translation supplies none of those state variables (E03 S10–S16; E08 S05/S09; E12 S12; `SYL-C0039`, `C0062`).

## Contradictory readings and limits

One reductive model treats Mari's reserve and boxing as emotional avoidance requiring Lara's rescue. The series instead shows a competent young athlete with inherited values, peers, family negotiation, care offered without mystical proof, and an explicit professional trajectory. Another model treats her as the obvious reciprocal romantic partner because the plot culminates in a loved person's danger. Emotional centrality, attachment and Mari's desire for shared presence are well supported; same-sex romantic attraction or couple formation is not explicitly named. Neither strict friendship-only certainty nor automatic romance is warranted by the text/static lane alone. Luca's actual dating relationship is a useful, non-exclusionary comparator (`SYL-R0057`).

The strongest criticism is that Mari is made to die for Lara's transformation and then left behind. The episode offsets this instrumentality with her independent ambition, direct accusation and final response, but does not wholly eliminate the asymmetry. Her monograph therefore keeps the ethical injury visible rather than invoking the character's resilience to excuse the structure. No diagnosis of grief or performed stoicism is made without a verified audio route (`SYL-D0001`-`SYL-D0002`).
