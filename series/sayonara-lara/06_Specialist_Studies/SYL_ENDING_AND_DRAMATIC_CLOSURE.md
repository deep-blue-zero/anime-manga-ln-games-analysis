# Closure by refusal, with a deliberately costly departure

## What converges in E12, and what each line closes

E12 is not a single “who does Lara love?” test. Its opening addresses assemble family and human acquaintances asking what she wanted, then Rowan offers a “proper” body and a lethal command. The crisis simultaneously asks whether Lara will endorse inherited species hatred, select a victim, let herself disappear to spare everyone else, accept a scarred land body, or permit another person to come near a danger she does not fully control. Mari's arrival adds a separate question: can concern respect an unknowable interior and still act? The climax resolves the first three through Lara's explicit refusal of stabbing and erasure; it gives Mari's answer in `近づかんと分からへん`, not in a claim of mind-reading. The text then makes Mari's reported death and revival part of the result. None of these outcomes by itself defines their post-crisis household relation (E12 S01–S10; `SYL-C0058`–`C0060`, `SYL-L0084`–`L0087`).

Lara's self-correction is carefully staged in Japanese words. First she says the princess should disappear so nobody is hurt. When Mari lies motionless she again says she should not have revived. Then she answers `ちがう`: she *was* glad to revive, still wants this world despite being foolish, troublesome, physically damaged and unable to save Mari by ordinary means. Only after that does she reject `本当の愛` as a compulsory answer and say she wants tomorrow in a world with Mari. A reading that extracts the “love” sentence and ignores the survival correction returns her to a new external validator. A reading that extracts the refusal and ignores Mari makes her choice falsely solitary (E12 10:19–16:27; `SYL-L0086`–`L0087`).

## Designed openness and absent information are different

Some openness is actively spoken. Lisa says the family may wake in one year or ten and still hate humans; Lara promises to talk “again and again.” Mari says she is lonely after Lara affirms wanting to live with her. These are not mysteries waiting for a single hidden shot: the work places future ethical practice beyond the final episode. Other absences are evidentiary or dramatic omissions. The precise sequence by which blade, fall, new light and resurrection act is not established by this text/static inspection; uninspected continuous motion and sound remain separate debts. The six-month interval does not show how Lara obtained independent housing, whether she found income, how the medicine risk was managed, or how she and Mari discussed leaving before the station. An intentional open future does not automatically justify those missing transitions (E12 S08–S13; `SYL-D0001`–`D0003`).

The final ordinary exchange makes the omission acute. Lara and Mari shop together; Mari describes increased training and hunger, and they tease one another about Lara living alone. Then Mari recalls the prior shared-life avowal and asks why Lara is going, saying `さびしいわ`. Lara answers that the avowal is true but no practical terms follow before the train. The platform shot leaves Mari outside and Lara aboard; the scar line gives Lara's departure bodily weight. This is a formal refusal to make freedom synonymous with a healed body or painless separation. It is also the strongest case that the drama underprovides a conversation it has itself taught the audience to value, especially after E07 Mari urged hearing another person's feeling and E10 rejected a unilateral goodbye (E07 S13; E10 S11–S12; E12 19:51–23:09; `SYL-L0089`, `SYL-F0065`).

The most charitable reading holds that leaving is a needed experiment: Lara's life should not be permanently defined by the home that first kept her safe, and the Otsu household explicitly leaves the door open. The strongest objection holds that the film makes Mari absorb the cost of that experiment with too little negotiation after she risked—and reportedly lost—her life. Both fit the source. A romantic demand that they must cohabit is not necessary to make the objection; a nonromantic friendship still owes response to stated loneliness. Conversely Mari's need does not create a veto on Lara's movement. The ending closes Lara's right to continue living and opens the work of relating without either person's disappearance. Whether its dramatic preparation is sufficient remains a legitimate evaluative disagreement, not a missing plot fact to invent.

The ending closes several different questions to different degrees. Lara explicitly refuses Rowan's proposed stabbing and her own erasure, says she wants to inhabit a world with Mari tomorrow, and remains alive on painful legs. Mari's death and revival are reported in direct dialogue; the burst of altered light and Lisa's six-month report make family restoration partial but real (`SYL-C0058`-`SYL-C0061`). These are substantive resolutions, not merely an ambiguous feeling. The exact magic transfer, Grace's present body, Lara's long-term lifespan and the sleeping court's later response are not shown. Treating them as solved by a visual flash would make causal closure too easy; treating everything as unknowable would ignore the actual coda.

The E12 structure first asks what Lara “really wanted” through a chorus of others' names, then lets the father provide a proper body and a terrible instruction. Lara's initial answer is refusal; Mari's answer is an approach without clairvoyance. Mari's fall drives Lara toward the earlier fairy-tale alternative of vanishing, but Lara corrects the death wish and claims a fallible life. That sequence connects the opening foam tragedy, E11 Rigmor's love-not-law counsel and E12 light without requiring the new light to be identical to either Grace's or Lisa's stated formula (`SYL-F0060`-`SYL-F0062`). The source of exact physical revival remains a channel debt, not an invitation to invent it.

The six-month coda prevents a no-cost reset. News says lake measurements recover while `何もなかったとは言えません`; Lisa says the family may wake years hence still hating humans; Lara offers to talk repeatedly (`SYL-W0051`). The ring's return is a partial sisterly acknowledgment, not a government transition. The coda then tests personhood on an ordinary axis: Lara will leave the home where Mari gave her refuge. Luca's completed portrait and their discussion of dating/breakup preserve a separate human bond. Mari maintains boxing ambition but says she is lonely and asks why Lara leaves if the shared-life wish is true. Lara affirms it without supplying a plan. The final painful scar and train position autonomy inside an unhealed body and an unnegotiated relationship (`SYL-C0062`).

This departure has a defensible rationale: the heroine who once needed permission, voice, medication and shelter tries to live beyond being a princess, daughter, romantic prize or permanent guest. It also has an intelligible objection: after Mari's lethal risk and the story's culminating love-language, a swift time jump and station exchange may underprepare the decision and give Mari less agency over its terms. The last smile cannot erase the objection. The most persuasive reading is neither “they must stay together” nor “freedom requires solitude”; a shared future remains possible but would need mundane negotiation the show does not dramatize. That is a real limitation of relational closure even if open form is artistically chosen.

One can also object that the family's slow recovery and hatred are deferred so Lara can depart without resolving the political cost of her refusal. The reply is not that the conflict is magically healed; she promises repeat engagement, and Lisa explicitly resists a final cure. The ending locates dramatic closure in a changed agent and a new method of engagement, not in resolved institutions. Whether that is satisfying is an evaluative judgment; it must keep the plot's partial solutions and remaining obligations distinct. Direct performance, precise strike choreography and final train editing remain under `SYL-D0001`-`SYL-D0002`.
