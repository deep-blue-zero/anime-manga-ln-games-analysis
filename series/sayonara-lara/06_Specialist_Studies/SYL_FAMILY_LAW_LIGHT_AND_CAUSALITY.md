# A resource, a doctrine and a choice are not the same rule

## A tiered causal chronology

The older court's shortage is already present when Grace and Rigmor dream of land. Its elders ask princesses for light before Lara exists. Rigmor and Rowan's love yields one celebrated, visible contribution to the castle. That is evidence that a relation and royal light can coincide; it is not a randomized test of every possible object or method of love. Grace's later human rejection, blade event and Rigmor's death supply a real injury but not Rowan's single-cause “humans alone destroyed us.” He then authors the anti-human rule and exiles Grace. The distinction between physical shortage, a successful historical example and a *legislated* prohibition is foundational (E11 S01–S08; `SYL-C0053`–`C0054`, `SYL-W0041`–`W0043`).

Lara's first dissolution and second life are another layered chain. The E11 mirror shows Grace gathering foam and Rowan giving his remaining light; Grace says she took it all, suspends the court and occupies a reduced fish body to wait. E01/E02 Lara experiences the outcome without knowing this history. Rowan can truthfully be a donor and still make an interested forecast that the revived daughter's future light will restore glory or become a weapon. Grace can truthfully have prevented immediate exploitation and still reserve medicine, chronology and route information to herself. Neither sacrifice automatically proves either guardian's later doctrine (E01 S11–S13; E06 S09; E11 S09–S11; `SYL-C0055`, `SYL-W0044`–`W0046`).

| Causal question | Demonstrated or reported result | Asserted extrapolation that remains unearned |
|---|---|---|
| What maintains the court? | Dimming, productive earlier light, sleep/depletion and later gradual recovery are staged or reported (`SYL-W0041`, `W0050`). | All princesses must replicate Rigmor/Rowan, or no nonroyal institution can aid survival. |
| What does medicine do? | Grace's substance transforms and sustains; Seizō's imperfect imitation delays foaming and helps Lara temporarily (`SYL-W0020`, `W0026`, `W0042`). | A fixed universal dose, an exact deadline, a guaranteed cure through romance, or a direct route from “available vial” to lasting safety. |
| What is the blade? | Lara remembers heart-light turning dangerous; Rowan observes a blade and commands blood; E12 altered light follows Lara's non-stabbing refusal (`SYL-C0035`, `C0050`, `C0058`). | Rowan's claim that a loved person's blood is necessary, that Lara freely chooses a target, or that all earlier threatening images are one physically identified object. |
| What happened to Mari? | Dialogue reports death and revival amid Lara's new light (`SYL-C0059`–`C0060`). | Exact strike path, a repeatable resurrection procedure, or the relationship category as a required magical input. |
| What happened to Grace? | E10 characters call her gone/dead, her spell weakens, E11 shows a historical child-Mari rescue (`SYL-W0047`, `W0052`). | Present survival from the flashback, or certified irreversible death from the battle stills. |
| What happened to lake and polity? | Public abnormalities/restrictions, then largely normalized measures and slowly recovering court (`SYL-W0040`, `W0050`–`W0051`). | Zero ecological residue, an already awakened/reconciled king, or a resolved constitutional relation with humans. |

This tiering explains why E12 can refute a *necessity claim* without delivering a replacement magic manual. Lara refuses to pierce a beloved, yet the feared no-light outcome does not simply follow: Mari lives after reported death and Lisa says the court is recovering. That is powerful counterevidence to Rowan's “must kill” prescription. It does not isolate Lara's words, Mari's approach, donor light, ambient magic or particular relationship ontology as the one sufficient cause. The story's ethical refusal and its physical mechanism have different confidence levels; neither should be used to erase the other.

## Three interested explanations of one endangered family

Rowan explains failure through impurity, human violence and a princess's incomplete duty. His account preserves a genuine shortage, a lost wife and his own donation, but makes the king arbiter of beauty, culpability and what victim is necessary. Grace explains the same family through an opportunity: a second birth can let Lara find human love and a future beyond the ban. Her technical acts are real, but the route is colored by her own abandoned land dream and her withholding of mortality information. Lisa explains it through guardianship and deep sea-world love. She has spent a century keeping the family alive, yet her human legs, Kōta bond, public museum life and Seizō's science are lived counterevidence to a rule that renders the human world only contamination. Each speaker knows something; none is a neutral narrator of the whole system (E06 S09–S14; E08 S03–S08; E11 S09–S11; `SYL-C0029`–`C0031`, `C0040`, `C0055`).

The ordinary human systems are causal too. A household makes temporary shelter plausible through a false guardian claim and work requirement; a bakery turns bodily effort into both wage and skill; medicine research buys time without omnipotence; museums and identity documents sustain decades of residence; police/boat rules and ecology measure costs of the island emergency. Rowan's claim that humans cannot see mermaid light does not mean human institutions cannot matter. It means that neither human science nor royal sight should be promoted into complete knowledge of a person or a crisis (E03 S16; E05 S01–S07; E06 S03/S10–S13; E10 S07–S13).

The future constitutional problem is intentionally harder than “love defeats the law.” Lisa expects Rowan and others to wake with hatred intact, perhaps years later; Lara says she will speak with them repeatedly about a shared future. Who controls princess light, whether a ban may be revised, who bears medicine risk and how nonhuman people live within human records are still questions for public negotiation. Rigmor's counsel and Lara's changed light discredit an absolute prohibition, not the need for a durable arrangement. A king who donated light is owed a truthful account of the new survival path; he is not owed a victim or unilateral control over the daughter who bears it (`SYL-C0061`, `SYL-W0050`–`W0052`).

The castle actually needs some form of sustaining light: E11 stages pre-Lara dimming, Rigmor and Rowan's productive example, Rowan's donation to Lara, and sleeping relatives; E12 Lisa reports gradual recovery after Lara's changed light (`SYL-W0041`, `SYL-W0044`, `SYL-W0050`). These observations justify taking family risk seriously. They do not establish that every princess must obtain light from the same object, that blood sacrifice is necessary, or that the king owns a daughter's future. The difference between material constraint and official explanation is the series' causal and ethical core.

| Speaker/rule | What is observed | What remains an assertion |
|---|---|---|
| Rowan: royal duty, ban, cleansing blood | Court's need, his light gift, Rigmor's loss, his explicit enactment of anti-human law and E12 order | Humans alone caused all catastrophe; only piercing a loved person produces proper light (`SYL-W0037`-`SYL-W0043`, `SYL-W0048`). |
| Grace: transformation, revival, human true-love route | Medicine and altered body, collected foam, donor light, court sleep, depleted fish form | Her route is uniquely sufficient or morally neutral; exact dosage, expiry and light formula (`SYL-W0042`, `SYL-W0044`-`SYL-W0045`). |
| Lisa: sea-world love, human blindness | Historical research, imitation medicine, her sight/interpretation of blade, later different light | No human can understand; all royal light must have a sea-world object or prior-princess pattern (`SYL-C0030`-`SYL-C0031`, `SYL-W0049`). |
| Lara/Mari: a future with care | Refusal of victim selection, Mari's approach without sight, reported death/revival, unusual light and partial recovery | That one relation alone is a reproducible magical cure or that an undetermined romance has been certified (`SYL-C0059`-`SYL-C0061`). |

Law is historically authored. Young Grace and Rigmor dream of the land before Rowan's prohibition; a royal elder pressures princesses while the castle is already dim. A human rejects Grace and orders soldiers; Rigmor arrives and dies amid a blade event; Grace later implicates herself. Rowan makes the ban and exiles her (`SYL-C0053`-`SYL-C0054`). Trauma explains why he seeks protection; a mixed causal chain and Rigmor's final counsel prevent the law from presenting itself as nature. Lisa's forced custodianship and hated human legs show how protection of the family can become domination of particular family members.

E12 is a strong falsifier of universal victim necessity, not a full magical manual. Lara does not execute Rowan's chosen stabbing; altered light appears, Mari reportedly revives, and the family gradually recovers. The weapon, fish-like maw, falling Mari and light are adjacent in sampled imagery, but exact strike order, transfer and sufficient conditions remain `SYL-D0002` debts. The same discipline applies to ecological effects: E10 public authorities restrict boating and E12 news measures improvement after six months, not a scientifically isolated supernatural cause or zero residue (`SYL-W0040`, `SYL-W0051`). Grace's spell weakening does not adjudicate her present body (`SYL-W0052`).

The political result is a future conversation, not abolition by miracle. Lara holds a returned ring and proposes repeated dialogue about human-mermaid life when the court awakens; Lisa expects the king's hatred to persist. A liberal reading may overstate Lara's individual autonomy if the material family need remains; a dynastic reading may overstate necessity if it reintroduces coerced blood despite counterevidence. The defensible synthesis holds survival and consent simultaneously: the family deserves aid and an account of its danger, while no person is merely its fuel.
