# Alienation is plural, and belonging does not mean staying put

Lara suffers at least four distinct estrangements. E01 removes her from the sea through a voice/body bargain and apparent death; E02 makes modern social norms, money and a missing ring hard to navigate. The two-century interval puts her beyond her original historical peers, while Rowan and Grace each claim the revived child for an inherited mission (`SYL-C0001`-`SYL-C0011`, `SYL-C0055`). An all-purpose “loneliness cured by Mari” thesis would hide the difference between learning a bus/food routine, recovering bodily survival, receiving affection and winning political permission to choose. Mari's own reserve, boxing discipline and desire for company are not the same displacement and should not be diagnosed as illness (`SYL-H0001`-`SYL-H0002`).

The small practical episodes change Lara's reachable world. Mari's E02 food and shelter keep her alive but initially make her dependent. E04's purchase and multiple viewpoints, E05's bakery labor and repair, E07's date/art encounter, and E08's preparation of custard for a future cake enlarge her competence. None abolishes medicine expiry or the castle's claim; competence is neither total independence nor a substitute for care (`SYL-C0021`, `SYL-F0026`, `SYL-C0039`). The E09 Luca conflict shows how desire can outrun another's pace when Lara conceals her time horizon. Alienation can make urgent contact feel necessary without authorizing it.

The E12 contrast is between two bad absolutes: Rowan's fixed “proper” mermaid body and Lara's own proposed disappearance. She refuses both. Her `卑しくて`, `アホで`, `迷惑` self-description accepts fallibility, while `まだこの世界にいたい` makes survival a chosen end, not a debt to repay by dying (`SYL-L0087`). The scarred legs still hurt after the light. Walking on them and carrying the returned ring makes personhood cumulative rather than an innocent restoration of either original species identity. The kingdom's law remains open to future speech, so reintegration is political as well as domestic (`SYL-C0061`-`SYL-C0062`).

Mari is changed too, but not in a mirror-image therapeutic arc. Lara's company diminishes solitude and pressures Mari's household boundaries; Mari keeps athletic ambition and says loneliness persists at departure. The final train is thus a formal argument that self-authorship need not erase attachment, and a criticism of any interpretation that makes independence costless. Strong rival: Lara's abrupt move repeats avoidant distance after Mari's sacrifice. The series answers partly by Lara's affirmation and future welcome, but does not show the material negotiation of return. This is an unresolved relational question, not evidence that alienation is “solved.”
