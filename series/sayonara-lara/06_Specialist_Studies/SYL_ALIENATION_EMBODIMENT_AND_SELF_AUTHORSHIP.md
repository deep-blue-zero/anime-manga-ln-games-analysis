# Alienation is plural, and belonging does not mean staying put

## Different estrangements require different remedies

| Form | Mechanism and diagnostic evidence | E12 position |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal | Lara's E01 apparent ending and two-century sleep sever contemporaries while money, schooling, phones and records change (`SYL-C0001`–`C0011`). | No return to her original historical peer world; new participation replaces rather than repairs the interval. |
| Bodily | Voice removal, painful legs, foam, medicine, involuntary reversion and blade make the body both vulnerability and supposed royal proof (`SYL-C0003`, `C0032`, `C0035`). | She rejects Rowan's “proper” form and walks on scarred legs; pain, lifespan and precise transformation rules remain open (`SYL-W0048`–`W0052`). |
| Cultural and social | Lara reads hospitality, money, work, phones, “prince,” “date” and “friend” through incomplete older categories (E03/E05/E07/E09; `SYL-L0023`, `L0033`–`L0036`, `L0052`). | Many errors become learnable routines, not a permanent comic incapacity; the time jump does not prove universal competence. |
| Familial and political | Rowan, Grace and Lisa preserve Lara while claiming authority over what her light should produce (`SYL-C0029`, `C0040`, `C0055`). | Lara keeps the ring and promises dialogue; court law and hatred have not been renegotiated (`SYL-C0061`). |
| Species and public | The prince's rejection and Lisa's exhibition show real anti-mermaid violence; Rowan's ban and Lisa's doctrine condemn humans collectively (E01/E06/E11). | Mari, Luca and Seizō are counterexamples to a universal verdict, not a completed public settlement. |
| Romantic | The first prince makes recognition existentially charged; Luca's regard is person-specific but his pace conflicts with her mortal clock (E07/E09; `SYL-C0034`, `C0044`). | Dating and breakup are acknowledged; neither relation supplies a certified magical cure. |
| Self-imposed withdrawal | Lara assumes she burdens Mari or may harm Luca/Mari, so leaving or disappearing appears morally clean (`SYL-C0015`, `C0035`, `C0052`). | E12 rejects disappearance; the later unilateral move leaves a lesser form of protective or autonomous distancing open to critique. |

These are not synonyms for loneliness. E05 labor addresses competence and public participation, not the two-century gap or medicine. Mari's E03 demand to stop following establishes a boundary, not Lara's species worth. Luca's E09 consent to lover status does not waive bodily pace. E12 light helps the court without settling distribution of its benefit. “Reintegration” is meaningful only when the analysis specifies *which* deprivation and *which* means of repair.

## Authorship remains materially dependent

E03's false guardian story makes shelter possible through an adult lie and the duty not to expose it. E05's assisted job application, dishwashing and later recognition make Lara a paid participant, not instantly independent. E08's medicine is supplied by Lisa and administered by Mari, with an unproved duration. E10 rescue depends on a brother's boat, Luca's apology, family disclosure and safety planning. Grace and Rowan have spent magic or light to keep Lara alive. Her E12 “I want to live” is more ethically mature when those dependencies remain visible, not when autonomy is imagined as needing nobody (E03 S16; E05 S01–S07; E08 S03–S16; E10 S07–S13; `SYL-C0055`).

The shift is partly jurisdictional. Early Lara receives a rule as a finished answer: a prophecy is forbidden, Grace defines love, Lisa predicts failure, Rowan defines beauty and blood. Later she compares these claims, asks what she herself wants, refuses a particular harm and offers repeated dialogue rather than unconditional separation. This is an epistemic development as well as a moral one. It cannot abolish the family's possible need for light, Mari's loneliness or bodily pain. The E12 move is a test of authorship under continuing relations, not its graduation certificate (`SYL-L0002`, `L0016`, `L0066`, `L0087`–`L0089`).

Lara suffers at least four distinct estrangements. E01 removes her from the sea through a voice/body bargain and apparent death; E02 makes modern social norms, money and a missing ring hard to navigate. The two-century interval puts her beyond her original historical peers, while Rowan and Grace each claim the revived child for an inherited mission (`SYL-C0001`-`SYL-C0011`, `SYL-C0055`). An all-purpose “loneliness cured by Mari” thesis would hide the difference between learning a bus/food routine, recovering bodily survival, receiving affection and winning political permission to choose. Mari's own reserve, boxing discipline and desire for company are not the same displacement and should not be diagnosed as illness (`SYL-H0001`-`SYL-H0002`).

The small practical episodes change Lara's reachable world. Mari's E02 food and shelter keep her alive but initially make her dependent. E04's purchase and multiple viewpoints, E05's bakery labor and repair, E07's date/art encounter, and E08's preparation of custard for a future cake enlarge her competence. None abolishes medicine expiry or the castle's claim; competence is neither total independence nor a substitute for care (`SYL-C0021`, `SYL-F0026`, `SYL-C0039`). The E09 Luca conflict shows how desire can outrun another's pace when Lara conceals her time horizon. Alienation can make urgent contact feel necessary without authorizing it.

The E12 contrast is between two bad absolutes: Rowan's fixed “proper” mermaid body and Lara's own proposed disappearance. She refuses both. Her `卑しくて`, `アホで`, `迷惑` self-description accepts fallibility, while `まだこの世界にいたい` makes survival a chosen end, not a debt to repay by dying (`SYL-L0087`). The scarred legs still hurt after the light. Walking on them and carrying the returned ring makes personhood cumulative rather than an innocent restoration of either original species identity. The kingdom's law remains open to future speech, so reintegration is political as well as domestic (`SYL-C0061`-`SYL-C0062`).

Mari is changed too, but not in a mirror-image therapeutic arc. Lara's company diminishes solitude and pressures Mari's household boundaries; Mari keeps athletic ambition and says loneliness persists at departure. The final train is thus a formal argument that self-authorship need not erase attachment, and a criticism of any interpretation that makes independence costless. Strong rival: Lara's abrupt move repeats avoidant distance after Mari's sacrifice. The series answers partly by Lara's affirmation and future welcome, but does not show the material negotiation of return. This is an unresolved relational question, not evidence that alienation is “solved.”
