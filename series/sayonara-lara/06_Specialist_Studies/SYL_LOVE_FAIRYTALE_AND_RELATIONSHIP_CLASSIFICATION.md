# Love is not one magical unit

The series credits Hans Christian Andersen and places a mermaid-princess sacrifice at the beginning rather than the end (E01 S15; `SYL-F0007`). This establishes an adaptation relation and a dramatized fairy-tale prehistory; it does **not** authorize detailed claims about a particular translated Andersen text not independently inspected. The repeated question is what the resurrected Lara can want after the failed prince-centered exchange. Her human curiosity precedes the prince (`SYL-C0001`), and E12's “what did you really want?” is answered by a world in which she wants to continue living, not the acquisition of a single person.

At least five loves must remain separate. Rigmor chooses Rowan and their light sustains the court, yet her twin bond with Grace and final counsel exceed that marriage. Rowan's paternal donation saves Lara while his kingly program demands her body and another's blood. Grace preserves Lara out of love and hope but projects her lost land dream. Lisa protects a sister through medicine and retrieval while fearing change and invoking sea-world duty. Mari and Lara name friendship and develop deep person-directed attachment; Lara and Luca explicitly date and part. Kōta names love in protesting the forced victim. Shared `愛` vocabulary or overlapping light imagery does not prove that these are equivalent magical inputs (`SYL-C0031`, `SYL-C0053`-`SYL-C0060`).

The E12 juxtaposition is decisive but not simplistic. Lara calls Mari the person whose world she wants tomorrow; she also says `本当の愛なんか要らない`. In context this rejects the prescribed royal/witch standard that would make a relationship earn a cure through a test, not the possibility of loving Mari. The novel light follows, and Lisa says it differs from previous princesses. Causal correlation supports rejection of universal blood and single-object doctrines, not proof that friendship or romance alone powers resurrection (`SYL-L0087`, `SYL-W0050`).

For Lara/Mari classification, distinguish female-dyad centrality, yuri as critical/marketing reception, Lara's possible attraction, Mari's possible attraction, mutual romantic recognition and couple formation. The first is beyond dispute; the earlier explicit friendship and later shared-life speech make love and companionship strong. Romance is a plausible reading of the intensity and climax, not source-certified mutual romantic status. E09's Luca lover designation and E12's dating/breakup provide an internal example of relationship language, without ruling out bisexuality or an unnamed later romance. The final train's unagreed distance prevents a confident couple claim and equally prevents reducing their bond to a trivial “just friends” foil (`SYL-R0054`-`SYL-R0058`).

The strongest romantic counterreading asks why a loved-person blade, revival embrace and wish to live in Mari's world are not sufficient dramatic confirmation. They may be sufficient for a viewer's interpretation; our narrower claim is about explicit mutual category and practice. The strongest friendship-centered counterreading cites E04's name and Mari's lack of overt desire. It must account for the E12 shared-life exchange and cannot treat male romance as exclusive by default. This calibrated outcome avoids demanding a kiss as proof while respecting the actual absent negotiation. Performance and uninspected continuous framing could materially shift the balance (`SYL-D0001`-`SYL-D0003`).
