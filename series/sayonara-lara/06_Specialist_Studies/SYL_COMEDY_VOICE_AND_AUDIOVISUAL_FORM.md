# Comic correction and the limits of an unheard soundtrack

## The joke's authority keeps changing hands

Lara's modern misunderstandings are not one repeated “mermaid is silly” beat. In E03 she can order cake before understanding money; Mari's correction is a practical limit, but Lara has remembered that Mari likes cake. In E05 the household's new work “law” sounds to Lara like royal decree, while Mari learns that her desired earbuds also require wages. Makoto's camera appears to be a job consisting of one easy button; the ensuing application sequence demonstrates that labor has qualifications and social language Lara cannot borrow successfully. Her claim to die for cake is answered by Mari's `私は死ねません`, then by a real kitchen that requires washing, waiting, failed recipes and eventually valued help. The comic premise changes as Lara acquires the ability to participate; the character is not frozen as the butt of the joke (E03 09:20–09:47; E05 01:37–10:38; `SYL-F0017`, `F0024`–`F0026`, `SYL-L0033`–`L0036`).

Mari's terseness is not automatically the straight-person position. E03 she makes “do not look away” so rigid that other people become noise; her apology, not Lara's correction alone, ends the sequence. E08 she is the one whose advice to return to Lisa narrows the choices under fear, while Lara's list of tomorrow's errands gives the practical content. E12 after a reported death Mari jokes that resurrection proves she is strongest; Lara's flat answer can carry the comic counterweight in the supplied wording. The source does not let us certify whether any of these lines is delivered deadpan, brightly or through a pause. It *does* show that comic authority and error are distributed rather than fixed in Lara/Mari roles (`SYL-C0013`–`C0014`, `C0039`, `SYL-F0043`, `F0064`).

Grace/Gon supplies a more political reversal. E01 Grace controls voice, potion, exclusion risk and the trial's terms. E02 puts her behind aquarium glass with a household pet name; E03 she mocks human dependence while admitting the tank Mari made is not wholly uncomfortable; E09 she needs carefully kept water, plants, temperature and a family's attention. The fish image punctures the witch's omnipotence but does not undo the information asymmetry. It also tests Rowan's anti-human claim through a dependent body: a human child once found the exhausted fish and refused to leave her to die (E02 S06; E03 13:14–14:04; E09 S09; E11 S12–S13; `SYL-F0009`, `F0048`, `F0059`).

## Static motifs as changing propositions, not fixed symbols

The ring passes through law, home-loss, family claim and, in E12, Lisa's returned inheritance. The same object can constrain Lara when others use it as authorization and become something she elects to carry; the image alone does not repeal the law. Foam begins as an apparent annihilation but becomes material Grace gathers for revival, making “second birth” literally dependent on another person's intervention. The shell can protect a sleeping court and hold Lara as a future resource. The heart-blade is an image of danger that Lara herself reports, then a royal object Rowan interprets, and finally part of a non-stabbing light event whose exact motion remains open. White flowers and lake color extend a private royal crisis into public ecology (E01/E06/E07/E10–E12; `SYL-F0001`–`F0007`, `F0039`, `F0051`–`F0065`).

Visual adjacency does not settle causation. E08 crosscuts Lara's return from Lisa's boat with Mari's final bout and a future-facing trophy/cake image; the structure aligns their decisions without certifying the bout score or a magical athletic effect. E11's mirror offers historical images while preserving Rowan's selection and Grace's self-implication; the frame prevents us from treating it as an impersonal court record. E12's platform places Mari outside and Lara aboard as the painful-leg statement occurs; two sampled stills can support separation and position, not duration of gaze, camera movement, exact smile timing or the felt musical closure. These remain D0001/D0002 work, not literary facts manufactured from metadata (E08 S11–S16; E11 S02–S13; E12 S12–S13).

The Japanese caption lane supports lexical contrasts—Mari's local `〜へん` and `〜や`, Lara's formal first-person declarations, royal `掟` and `さだめ`, work's `御社` borrowed too eagerly—without making a heard-voice profile. Koide's production comments can motivate an eventual listening test of fantasy/naturalistic acting contrast; they cannot certify that contrast in our uninspected soundtrack. The title retains “voice” as a scoped research question, and the present study's positive claims remain textual and static (`SYL-L0034`, `L0084`–`L0089`; `SYL-D0001`–`D0003`).

The show's most persistent joke is not that Lara is foolish and Mari sensible. It is a shifting mismatch of frames. A feared sovereign witch is a pet fish behind glass (E02, `SYL-F0009`); a fairy-tale heroine encounters school, checkout, workplace and phone protocols with cosmological expectations; Mari's blunt correction itself becomes tender when she continues to help. E05's work-as-torture claim is revised by a successful product, error and collective repair, then qualified “today” (`SYL-F0026`). E07's unfinished portrait lets Lara laugh while Luca attends to a person, not a stock princess. E12's Mari “I died?” exchange after revival and Luca's count of sudden dating/breakups bring comic elasticity back after genuine death and hurt (`SYL-F0064`). These are textual setups and static outcomes, not claims about audible comic timing.

Comedy is ethically ambivalent. Mari's irritation protects boundaries and gives Lara a chance to learn; repeated emergency dependence can also burden a household. Grace's fish humiliation makes authority contestable, yet cannot erase her long control over Lara's information or the body cost she paid. The small boat and life-jacket discussion in E10 introduces licensed, material danger into a rescue fantasy (`SYL-F0055`). E12's light does not make the lake's altered ecology disappear without trace. A comic mechanism matters when it changes what a person can refuse, repair or admit, rather than merely relieving tone.

Static form carries a linked argument across episodes: shell as origin, apparent tomb and suspended court; foam as erasure and later second-birth material; ring as royal identity then returned inheritance; repeated fish/rose/blade images that shift between danger and care (`SYL-F0001`-`SYL-F0007`, `SYL-F0058`-`SYL-F0065`). E08 boxing/ferry crosscut suggests parallel risk without proving Lara causes a bout result. E11's mirror gives a past with a visible selection frame and E12's train gives distance without a visual contractual reunion. The distinct local lake, school, shops and house make epic law answerable to mundane consequences. Color and composition can be described in sampled frames, but no static sequence alone proves a continuous camera move, cut rhythm, gaze duration or sound-image synchronization.

Japanese textual voice is legible in contrasts such as Mari's `近づかんと分からへん`, the royal `掟` and Lara's `本当の愛なんか要らない` (`SYL-L0085`-`SYL-L0089`). These are lexical/register observations from aligned captions; punctuation is not a pitch contour. The director's [Febri part 2](https://febri.jp/topics/sayonara_lala_interview_2/) describes a deliberate contrast between fantasy-leaning Lara acting and naturalistic Shiga-rooted Mari acting, local dialect direction, and removed exposition. That is a production account, not an auditory result of this inspection. It raises a useful countertest: the unexplained revival and departure may be purposeful subtraction or an under-motivated clarity cost; intention does not decide success.

`SYL-D0001` remains a hard boundary for actual performance, music, silence and humor timing. `SYL-D0002` bounds continuous motion and edit claims, and `SYL-D0003` exact disputed wording. This document fulfills the text/static comedy and form lane while explicitly leaving its named voice/AV responsibility provisional; it is not a completed soundtrack study.
