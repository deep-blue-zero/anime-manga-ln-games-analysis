# 『青春ブタ野郎はシスコンアイドルの夢を見ない』
## Volume 4 Deep Dive
### Differentiation without abandonment: sisters, borrowed bodies, public images, and the right not to become another person

**Primary source:** Original Japanese EPUB listed above.  
**Cumulative scope:** Volumes 1–4.  
**Spoiler boundary:** Strictly through Volume 4. The unstamped Shoko letter at the end remains unresolved; no Volume 5 or later explanations are imported.  
**Provenance note:** Migrated legacy reconstruction. Source object metadata are verified; claim-level locators are not retroactively invented.

---

# 1. Central thesis

Volume 3 asked whether contradictory parts of one person could be integrated without purifying the self.

Volume 4 asks the complementary question:

> **Can two people who have been forced into comparison become separate without losing the relationship between them?**

Toyohama Nodoka's life has been organized around Sakurajima Mai as impossible benchmark, desired sister, professional ideal, maternal weapon, and rival. Her problem is not merely envy. The chronology matters: **admiration comes first**.

As a child, Nodoka wanted an older sister. Her father pointed to the young actress on television and told her that Mai was her sister. Nodoka entered acting partly because she wanted to meet her. She sent letters. She admired Mai before adult family conflict and maternal comparison transformed that admiration into resentment.

The later comparison:

> 「麻衣ちゃんにできることが、どうしてのどかにはできないの」

turns love into a hierarchy.

The body phenomenon then literalizes the impossible demand:

> become Mai.

Yet once Nodoka actually inhabits Mai's visible body and social role, she learns that “being Mai” is not possession of a superior exterior. It means carrying accumulated professional expectations, practiced competence, reputation, and pressure.

The book's central identity thesis is therefore:

> **Selfhood does not require defeating the person to whom you were compared. It requires recovering the ability to choose what you want without needing your life to prove that someone else was wrong.**

Its relational companion is equally important:

> **Love does not require the abolition of anger, resentment, envy, or dislike. Mature attachment begins when contradictory feelings no longer have to be lied about in order for the relationship to continue.**

Mai can say:

> 「私ものどかのこと嫌いだったから」

and still treasure Nodoka's childhood letters.

Nodoka can say:

> 「お姉ちゃんなんて大嫌い」

and still look at Mai performing and involuntarily think:

> 「お姉ちゃん、すごい……」

Contradiction is not evidence that the relationship is fake.

It is the relationship.

The volume also pushes the series' recognition problem beyond memory.

Volume 1 showed:

> public visibility does not equal recognition.

Volume 4 makes the point literal in a different way:

> **The public can look directly at Mai's body and fail to recognize Mai because Nodoka is the person living inside the image.**

A camera records the visible body. It does not automatically record personhood, intention, or consent.

# 2. Source, structure, and narrated sequence

The EPUB contains twenty-five spine items and twenty-one image resources. Its principal chapters are:

1. **第一章　シスターパニック**
2. **第二章　冷たい戦争はじめます**
3. **第三章　シスコンではありません**
4. **第四章　コンプレックスこんぐらっちゅれーしょん**
5. **あとがき**

The opening directly answers the Volume 3 cliffhanger at the level of identity: the girl with Mai's appearance is Toyohama Nodoka, Mai's paternal half-sister.

The narrative then alternates between two forms of performance:

- Nodoka attempting to function as Sakurajima Mai;
- Mai attempting to function publicly inside Nodoka's idol body and career.

That structure permits an unusual reciprocal experiment. Each sister receives practical access to the other's social image.

The late movement shifts from career comparison to family history. Nodoka's apparent defeat at the Sweet Bullet live and her interpretation of her mother's reaction trigger a dangerous emotional collapse at Shichirigahama. The forbidden cabinet and childhood letters then reconstruct the sisters' relationship in a way neither woman's present resentment had fully represented.

The body restoration occurs only after Nodoka explicitly rejects the demand to become Mai:

> 「あたし、お姉ちゃんみたいにならなくていいんだよね？」

Mai tells her that she can become like Mai if she wants.

Nodoka answers:

> 「なりたくない」

The final sections then show that metaphysical resolution does not erase relational habits. Nodoka and her mother argue again. Nodoka moves in with Mai. The apartment itself changes to accommodate a second life.

The book closes with a new Shoko hook: an unstamped blue envelope addressed to Sakuta, signed `翔子さん`.

# 3. Causal synopsis: what changes and why

Nodoka and Mai find themselves in crossed visible embodiments while retaining their own memories, personalities, speech patterns, attachments, and professional histories.

For bookkeeping:

- **M→N:** Mai subjectivity / Nodoka embodiment.
- **N→M:** Nodoka subjectivity / Mai embodiment.

This is **not** the same structure as Rio's Volume 3 duplication.

- Rio: one autobiographical self → two simultaneous bodies.
- Mai/Nodoka: two autobiographical selves → crossed visible embodiment.

Sakuta quickly recognizes Mai despite Nodoka's body. That matters because his recognition is relational rather than purely visual.

Nodoka, meanwhile, discovers that Mai's professional life cannot be reproduced by simply having Mai's body. A CM shoot overwhelms her. The crew's tense attention initially looks like hostility, but Sakuta understands it as professional expectation: Sakurajima Mai has created a record that makes competence the assumed baseline.

His advice becomes one of the volume's most important identity lines:

> 「本番の瞬間を迎えても、お前はお前のまま」

Nodoka later performs more successfully when she stops attempting total imitation.

The reciprocal side of the experiment comes at the Sweet Bullet live. Mai, inside Nodoka's body, has actually rehearsed. She runs, sings, learns choreography, and performs with experienced professional composure. When leader Hirokawa Uzuki misses a vocal line after a baton error, Mai fills it. The crowd responds strongly to “Nodoka.”

Real Nodoka watches:

> 「お姉ちゃん、すごい……」

This is the admiration she cannot fully extinguish.

Yet the scene produces ethical residue. Professional opportunity for Nodoka—including center-related recognition—has partly been earned by Mai's labor while inhabiting Nodoka's body. Body restoration does not automatically solve the fairness problem.

Nodoka's mother attends and, believing Mai-in-Nodoka's-body to be Nodoka, holds her hands and cries:

> 「よかった、よかったね」

> 「がんばったわね」

Nodoka interprets this as proof that even her mother wanted Mai.

At the sea, she enters the dark water in a dangerous and self-destructive state. Sakuta physically stops her and tells her:

> 「こんなやり方で、自分の価値を試すなよ！」

and:

> 「助けてもらう前提で海になんて入んな」

His “value test” interpretation is plausible but should not be treated as omniscient diagnosis. Despair, self-erasure, and relational testing may coexist.

The childhood letters then change the relational model.

Mai had stored dozens of Nodoka's letters in a Hato Sabure tin. She explains that the letters mattered during her own difficult child-star years. Strangers already knew “Sakurajima Mai”; Nodoka's letters were different because they came from a little sister who admired her. The relation is therefore reciprocal.

Nodoka did not merely fail to become Mai.

> **Nodoka helped create the Mai she later believed existed independently of her.**

Her deepest maternal wish is finally articulated:

> 「あたしだって褒めてほしかったんだよ！」

> 「お母さんに笑ってほしかったの！」

Mai's response:

> 「いい加減、母親離れをしなさい」

is not a command to stop loving her mother. It is a demand for differentiation: choose a life first, then let the mother respond to the chosen person rather than treating maternal approval as the source of the choice.

# 4. The phenomenon: crossed embodiment and explanatory limits

Rio later makes an important first-pass distinction:

> 「そもそも、正確に言うのなら、体が入れ替わったわけじゃないでしょ」

The point is that ordinary “body swap” language may be too imprecise.

The visible bodies appear to have changed in accordance with the other sister's outward form, while autobiographical subjectivity remains continuous.

Observable features include:

- public social identity follows appearance;
- psychological identity follows memory/personality rather than visible body;
- bodily familiarity does not transfer perfectly;
- Mai notes differences in height/proportions;
- restoration changes visible embodiment while memories remain continuous;
- clothing remains materially situated at the moment of restoration, suggesting that the bodies themselves transform rather than minds merely jumping between fixed bodies.

Rio speculates about a:

> 「肉体の設計図」

and uses teleportation-style explanatory language.

Two broad hypotheses arise in the volume:

1. Nodoka's pressure/wish to become Mai alters embodiment.
2. Mai may possess some reciprocal envy toward Nodoka, making the exchange relational rather than unilateral.

A further “world consistency” idea is possible: two visible Mais may be unstable, so the transformation must be reciprocal.

None is proven.

The strongest termination model is psychological-relational rather than physical:

> **The phenomenon loses its function when Nodoka no longer equates loving/envying/resenting Mai with the requirement to become Mai.**

Her `なりたくない` is therefore a high-value causal correlate.

Still unresolved:

- the physical substrate;
- why Mai changes at all;
- whether Mai's reciprocal envy is real;
- whether a conservation/world-consistency rule exists;
- how proprioception works;
- which traits could or could not exchange.

# 5. Toyohama Nodoka: admiration before envy

Nodoka's rough speech matters to her characterization.

She uses forms such as:

- `あたし`;
- `あんた`;
- `ウザ`;
- `バカ`;
- `死ね`;
- `～だっつーの`.

The register creates emotional immediacy and distinguishes her sharply from Mai's controlled professional speech.

Her address for Mai also shifts in meaningful ways.

`麻衣さん` can create distance or professional defensiveness.

`お姉ちゃん` carries the older attachment and returns most strongly when affection escapes control.

Nodoka's idol career should not be dismissed as entirely her mother's project. The volume's development depends on recovering the parts she actually wants.

She discovers that she likes:

- performing lives;
- fans;
- the unit;
- the possibility of having her own center song.

Self-authorship therefore does **not** require rejecting every desire that originated under parental influence.

It requires the ability to endorse or refuse those desires now.

That distinction is more realistic than a clean “escape the mother and quit idols” ending.

Nodoka's final comparative habit also remains.

She can still say:

> 「だって、これこそ、お姉ちゃんに勝てっこないじゃん」

The complex has not been deleted. It has lost some of its power to define whether she deserves to exist as herself.

# 6. Mai: dislike, sisterhood, professionalism, and bodily jurisdiction

Mai is especially strong in Volume 4 because the novel refuses to sentimentalize her.

She says:

> 「私ものどかのこと嫌いだったから」

Her resentment is tied to her father, abandonment, and the existence of the new family.

Sakuta responds:

> 「嫌いにも、いろんな嫌いがありますし」

That is a useful relationship principle. `嫌い` does not erase the letters, admiration, or desire for a sister.

Mai later tells Nodoka:

> 「ありがとね」

> 「妹になってくれて、ありがと」

The line matters because it frames sisterhood as relationally produced rather than merely genealogically assigned.

Mai also once told young Nodoka:

> 「私も妹がほしいと思ってたの」

The desire for sisterhood was reciprocal before adult conflict hardened the relation.

## Professionalism as accumulated labor

Mai's ability to function inside Sweet Bullet is not presented as effortless genius. She trains. She rehearses. She uses years of performance experience. The body-swap device therefore debunks a crude visual idea of celebrity:

> looking like Mai is not what makes Mai professionally reliable.

Competence is historical labor.

## Bodily consent

The volume's most sophisticated ethical point may involve Mai refusing to treat her own personal consent as sufficient permission for Sakuta to sexualize/use Nodoka's body while Mai inhabits it.

She makes him wait until her own body is restored for the bunny-costume intimacy.

This separates:

- personal identity;
- physical embodiment;
- bodily jurisdiction;
- consent.

That is unusually careful for a romantic comedy built around a body-swap premise.

The publication's illustrations sometimes undermine that care by eroticizing the borrowed bodies anyway. The contradiction belongs in the critical record.

---

# 7. Sakuta and Mai: recognizing the person beyond the body

Sakuta's immediate recognition of Mai inside Nodoka's body is thematically important, but it should not be romanticized into mystical soul detection.

He has access to:

- speech;
- relational memory;
- habitual behavior;
- shared knowledge;
- tone;
- the way Mai responds to him.

The point is not “true love sees through any disguise” in a magical sense.

It is:

> **relationship provides a richer identity model than appearance alone.**

The couple also continues developing domestic boundaries.

Mai gives Sakuta an apartment key temporarily but refuses to frame it as permanent entitlement.

> 「あげてもいいって思えるようになったら、ちゃんとあげるから」

Domestic access remains chosen.

The later paparazzi scandal then repeats the body/person distinction in public form. Photographs show Mai's visible body with Sakuta during the period Nodoka was inside it. The camera records something real and still misrepresents the interpersonal event.

At the press conference, Mai refuses a shame ritual and confirms the relationship:

> 「はい、事実です」

At the same time, she asks the media not to circulate Sakuta's face or photos because he is not a celebrity.

Her own public career does not give the public jurisdiction over him.

The subsequent ordinary Kamakura date matters precisely because the series moves from spectacular identity crisis toward ordinary couple life.

Sakuta says:

> 「麻衣さんと普通にデートしたいなあ」

Mai later tells him:

> 「たぶん、咲太が思っているより、私、咲太のこと好きよ」

This is the strongest direct romantic affirmation from Mai so far.

---

# 8. Sakuta: increased respect for choice, persistent information-boundary violations

Sakuta's best interventions involve returning decisions to Nodoka.

He tells her to perform as herself.

He treats the decision to remain an idol as hers.

He recognizes that the final family relationship cannot be solved by the boyfriend from outside.

But the volume also gives one of his clearest privacy violations.

Mai explicitly tells him not to open the Japanese-room cabinet.

He opens it.

He reads the private childhood letters.

Those letters become crucial evidence that helps Nodoka. The good outcome does not erase the violation.

This produces the updated Sakuta thesis:

> **He now reliably recognizes that people must choose their own lives, but still often assumes he may cross informational boundaries in order to get them to the point where they can choose.**

That distinction should remain central to the intervention ledger.

---

# 9. Family, comparison, and parental incompleteness

Volume 4 significantly deepens the series' family model.

Nodoka's mother genuinely cares about her. That does not make comparison harmless.

The repeated use of Mai as benchmark turns maternal care into a structure in which Nodoka's achievements are always measured against someone else's body and career.

The volume's family thesis is:

> **Care can injure when the caregiver defines the recipient's life too completely.**

Mai's own reading of Nodoka's mother's trembling hands—suggesting anxiety over whether she has done right by her daughter—is plausible, but because the mother is not focalized deeply, it should remain strong/tentative inference rather than fact.

Sakuta's father also returns with useful ordinary evidence. He remains in contact, says Sakuta's mother is improving, expresses happiness about Kaede wearing her uniform, and offers university financial support.

When Sakuta asks what becoming a parent felt like, the answer emphasizes being overwhelmed rather than automatically competent.

Parenthood therefore does not confer moral omniscience.

Adults can love their children and still fail them.

Kamisato Saki provides a useful ordinary sibling mirror. She has a highly successful older sister and finds maternal comparisons irritating. Asked whether she likes or hates her sister, she says:

> 「別に」

This helps prevent Mai/Nodoka's relation from being reduced to a simple love/hate binary.

# 10. Kaede, Tomoe, Rio, and young Shoko

## Kaede

Kaede voluntarily wears her unused middle-school summer uniform.

> 「かえでも、そろそろだと思うんです」

> 「そろそろ、がんばろうと思うんです」

This is an important preparatory act, **not** evidence that she is already ready to attend school.

Sakuta encourages movement but asks for moderation. Their father is happy. Mai supports her.

The care network continues widening.

## Tomoe

Tomoe's ordinary scenes confirm that Volume 2 has not left her frozen inside her featured arc. Her Hakata speech can still leak; she remains self-conscious about it. Sakuta's response respects her choice to present herself in standard Japanese rather than treating dialect as a buried “true self.”

## Rio

Rio continues to function as scientist and friend. Because the reader has now seen her own Syndrome, her authority is easier to calibrate correctly: she is intelligent and useful, not omniscient.

## Young Shoko

She deliberately passes near Sakuta's home because:

> 「咲太さんに会えるかもしれないと思いまして」

She blushes and displays clear attachment.

The ontology remains unresolved.

# 11. Recognition: body, role, biography, person, boundary

Volume 4 allows a more precise recognition taxonomy.

A viewer can:

1. see a body;
2. identify a public role;
3. know a biography;
4. recognize a person;
5. respect that person's boundaries.

These are not the same achievement.

The paparazzi photograph proves the point.

It accurately records:

- a visible body;
- physical proximity.

It does **not** automatically reveal:

- who was subjectively inhabiting that body;
- what the contact meant;
- what either participant intended;
- what the audience is entitled to know.

The childhood letters provide a complementary medium.

A photograph captures visibility.

The letters preserve interiority across time.

That media contrast is one of the volume's strongest formal ideas.

# 12. Objects, spaces, and illustrations

## Hato Sabure tin

The tin functions as a private emotional archive. Its importance comes not only from the letters but from Mai having preserved them physically for years.

## Keys

The apartment key represents domestic trust with retained jurisdiction. Access can be given without becoming ownership.

## Dining table

After Nodoka moves in, the household acquires a larger dining table.

This may be the strongest material symbol in the volume:

> **home literally makes room for a second person.**

Reconciliation is not becoming identical.

The table gets bigger.

## Shichirigahama sea

The sea now hosts both romance/ordinary movement and dangerous self-erasure. It is not a single-symbol location.

## Illustrations

The publication includes several images that sharpen the theme of visible identity:

- Nodoka-body/Mai-subject early in the swap;
- the compromising beach contact later misread by photographs;
- Kaede in school uniform;
- borrowed-body bunny-costume framing;
- the live performance in which “Nodoka” is actually Mai;
- Mai's self-chosen autumn date presentation.

The borrowed-body erotics produce a real contradiction with the prose's careful consent distinction.

# 13. Ethics and philosophy

Volume 4's major philosophical contribution is **differentiation**.

V3 says:

> do not exile part of yourself.

V4 says:

> do not make another person responsible for completing your identity.

Together they produce:

> **integration without erasure; differentiation without abandonment.**

The family dimension makes this especially important. Nodoka's mother's love becomes harmful when it cannot allow Nodoka's life to cease functioning as an answer to Mai.

The career dimension adds another lesson:

> public identity is accumulated labor, not merely visible traits.

The consent dimension adds a third:

> personal identity does not create unlimited jurisdiction over whichever body one currently inhabits.

# 14. Counterreadings, limitations, and unresolved tensions

### Counterreading: the swap proves Nodoka simply wants to be Mai

Too simple. She loves, envies, resents, and competes with Mai. The final resolution depends on separating those feelings from compulsory imitation.

### Counterreading: Mai secretly hates Nodoka and the letters prove otherwise

The book explicitly permits both. Treasuring someone and resenting them are not mutually exclusive.

### Counterreading: the mother's emotional reaction proves she is harmless

Love does not retroactively remove comparative harm.

### Counterreading: Sakuta's cabinet violation is justified because it works

Outcome does not erase privacy jurisdiction.

### Unresolved: credit at Sweet Bullet

Nodoka receives opportunity partially through Mai's performance in Nodoka's body. The professional fairness problem remains.

### Unresolved: reciprocal cause

Mai's possible envy and the need for her to transform remain speculative.

### Publication tension

The art eroticizes bodies whose ownership/jurisdiction the prose treats with unusual care.

# 15. What Volume 4 changes in the series-so-far model

1. Adolescence Syndrome can cross visible embodiment between two people.
2. Body and autobiographical personhood can diverge without memory loss.
3. Recognition can survive radical appearance change through relational continuity.
4. V3 integration is complemented by V4 differentiation.
5. Family love can coexist with resentment and still require boundaries.
6. Nodoka's idol desire becomes more self-authored without becoming independent of her history.
7. Mai's professional competence is explicitly labor-based.
8. Bodily consent is distinguished from personal identity.
9. Public photography is shown to be true but semantically incomplete evidence.
10. Sakuta improves at respecting life choices but remains weak on information boundaries.
11. Kaede begins voluntarily preparing for school return.
12. Mai/Sakuta becomes more public and domestic.
13. The Shoko mystery is reactivated through an unstamped letter.

The identity ledger now contains:

- V3: one self divided across bodies;
- V4: two selves crossed across visible embodiment.

No universal mechanics should yet be inferred.

# 19. Cumulative archival note

Volume 4's most elegant material symbol is not the supernatural body transformation.

It is the larger table.

The sisters do not resolve their relationship by becoming the same person, proving who is better, or erasing the years of resentment.

They make enough room for both people to sit in the same home.
