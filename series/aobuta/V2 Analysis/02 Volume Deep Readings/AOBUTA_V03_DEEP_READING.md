# 『青春ブタ野郎はロジカルウィッチの夢を見ない』
## Volume 3 Deep Dive
### Divided selves, unwanted need, sexualized attention, and the right to remain among other people

**Primary source:** Original Japanese EPUB listed in metadata.  
**Cumulative scope:** Volumes 1–3.  
**Spoiler boundary:** Volume 3 only. The cliffhanger concerning Mai's lookalike is left unresolved. Later Shoko and body/identity revelations are not imported.  
**Provenance note:** Migrated legacy reconstruction. Source object metadata are verified; exact claim locators remain pending deliberate backfill.

---

# 1. Central thesis

Volume 3 asks the strongest identity question in the series so far:

> **What happens when you cannot tolerate that contradictory desires belong to the same self?**

Futaba Rio has built an identity around rationality, restraint, scientific competence, and apparent comfort with solitude. Yet she also wants to be noticed. She hates the sexual interest directed at her body, but uses sexualized self-presentation online to obtain attention. She judges that attention degrading, yet feels temporarily relieved by receiving it. She loves Kunimi, does not want to disrupt his relationship, and fears losing the friendship structure she already has. She once experienced being alone as normal; after friendship makes school meaningful, the prospect of future aloneness becomes frightening.

The volume refuses to solve these contradictions by revealing one Rio as “fake” and the other as “real.”

Both are Rio.

Both remember the same childhood and adolescence.

Both dislike themselves.

Both love Kunimi.

Both need Sakuta and Kunimi more than the self-image of solitary rationality can comfortably admit.

The strongest compact thesis is:

> **Integration does not require loving every part of yourself. It requires ceasing to treat an unwanted part of yourself as a stranger who has no right to exist.**

A broader formulation follows:

> **People do not become autonomous by ceasing to need others. They become more self-authored when they can acknowledge what they want, ask without automatically apologizing for existing, and allow other people to answer freely.**

That makes Volume 3 a book about the difference among:

- being looked at;
- being desired;
- being needed;
- being recognized;
- and being accompanied.

The resolution is not “Rio learns self-love.” The novel is more interesting than that.

Sakuta tells her:

> 「自分のことなんて、別に嫌いでいいんだよ」

Rio answers:

> 「最低だけど……なんか、ほっとする」

The ethical proposition is modest and powerful:

> **Self-love is not an entrance requirement for friendship, dignity, or continued life.**

# 2. Source, structure, and narrated sequence

The EPUB contains twenty-six spine items and twenty-three image resources. The main sequence is:

1. **第一章　不思議が不思議を呼んできた**
2. **第二章　青春はパラドックス**
3. **第三章　友情は時速四十キロメートル**
4. **第四章　大雨の夜にすべてを流して**
5. **あとがき**

The structure is notably dual.

The volume opens with an older-Shoko memory/dream at Shichirigahama, then moves into the present with the younger Makinohara Shoko introduced at the end of Volume 2. That unresolved identity problem runs in the background.

The main Rio plot then begins as a literal duplication problem: Sakuta can communicate by phone with one Rio while seeing another physical Rio elsewhere. The book spends its middle chapters alternating between investigation and ordinary cohabitation/friendship scenes. This matters because the phenomenon is not solved through abstract laboratory work. Sakuta, Mai, Rio, Kunimi, and Kaede create a temporary social environment in which the two Rios' different fears can become visible.

The late movement shifts from “which Rio is real?” to “what does each Rio believe would happen if she remained?”

The climactic request:

> 「私も、一緒に花火行きたい」

is therefore not simply a social invitation. It is a claim to continued membership in a world she had begun planning to leave.

The cliffhanger then introduces a new identity/embodiment anomaly: an apparent Mai who insists Sakuta cannot be her sister's boyfriend.

At the Volume 3 boundary, the book ends on question rather than answer.

# 3. Causal synopsis: what changes and why

Sakuta discovers that two physically distinct Futaba Rios exist simultaneously.

For analytical clarity, the original reading used neutral state labels:

### R-A — displaced / guarded Rio

- glasses;
- hair down;
- initially associated with net-café withdrawal;
- later stays with Sakuta/Mai/Kaede;
- disowns the attention-seeking Rio;
- assumes the other Rio is socially superior;
- increasingly concludes that she herself should disappear.

### R-B — socially active Rio

- hair tied up;
- contacts/no glasses in ordinary school presentation;
- continues attending school and watching Kunimi;
- connected to the anonymous sexualized social-media account;
- receives threatening attention;
- eventually deletes the account;
- learns through the crisis that she is not actually alone.

The key anti-purification fact is that the anonymous posting begins **before** the physical duplication.

Therefore the story cannot cleanly assign:

> sexualized attention-seeking = corrupt duplicate.

Both versions inherit the behavior.

The split is better understood as an intolerable conflict over what the behavior means and what kind of self is allowed to want attention.

The crisis escalates when threatening messages make the social-media activity dangerous. Sakuta intervenes, including by taking Rio's phone despite her refusal and calling Kunimi at 2:30 a.m. without her permission. The intervention is effective but ethically mixed.

Kunimi comes immediately.

Rio realizes:

> 「全然ひとりじゃなかったんだ……ひとりじゃなかった……」

The other Rio's problem is different. Seeing the social/photo evidence of friendship makes her feel replaceable. If the other Rio can occupy the friendships more successfully, she interprets her own continued existence as unnecessary.

Her shift occurs when she stops treating her desire for companionship as disqualifying and says:

> 「私も、花火行きたい」

Then, communicating with the other Rio by phone:

> 「私も、一緒に花火行きたい」

One body disappears. One Rio remains.

The strongest first-pass inference is:

> **The division loses its function when Rio no longer needs to assign the need-bearing version of herself to an illegitimate category and both states converge on the desire to remain with their friends.**

The exact physical mechanism remains unresolved.

# 4. The phenomenon: one autobiographical self, two bodies

Volume 3 differs sharply from the first two cases.

- Volume 1 altered collective perception and memory of one person.
- Volume 2 altered or multiplied experienced futures.
- Volume 3 places two bodily Rios in the same apparent world.

That makes it dangerous to assume a single mechanical template for Adolescence Syndrome.

## 4.1 Shared continuity

Both Rios possess the same pre-split history. Their memories diverge only after coexistence begins.

This argues against a simple “evil twin” or replacement model.

## 4.2 Communication without direct confrontation

The two Rios can communicate by phone. The series uses mediated communication to permit incompatible identity states to speak without requiring an immediate face-to-face collapse.

This will remain an important formal pattern to track.

## 4.3 Strong psychological correlation

Rio herself describes the problem through incompatible purpose and means.

Her need is:

> `構ってほしい` — she wants someone to pay attention, engage, respond.

Her means is a sexualized presentation she simultaneously experiences as degrading.

The anonymous account becomes a place where she can receive immediate reaction while preserving emotional distance from the people reacting.

## 4.4 The model is not “she secretly enjoys sexualization”

Rio says:

> 「この体が嫌いになった。自分が汚れたもののように思えてきて……」

and:

> 「私にはそれしかないんだよ」

She also explains:

> 「相手を選べるのは、選べるだけの魅力がある人間だけだよ。全員が望んだ形で必要とされるわけじゃない」

and:

> 「相手が誰であれ、反応があるだけで私は救われた気持ちになれた」

This is not sexual confidence.

It is a brutal market model of recognition: if the only attention one can reliably obtain is attention one dislikes, perhaps disliked attention is better than none.

## 4.5 Self-harm analogy

Rio says of the selfie behavior:

> 「一種の自傷行為なんだって私は思っている」

and:

> 「私は私が嫌いなんだよ」

The correct analysis is therefore not that the account *causes* the split in a simple mechanical sense. It is evidence of a preexisting conflict in which self-dislike, bodily exposure, need for response, and shame become mutually reinforcing.

## 4.6 Exact mechanics

Still unresolved:

- what physically duplicates;
- whether a single consciousness branches or two complete embodied states are instantiated;
- whether observer consciousness is causally necessary;
- what exact event triggers coexistence;
- why one state disappears rather than bodies merging visibly.

Rio's quantum-teleportation and observer language remains a heuristic rather than a verified world-law.

# 5. Futaba Rio: rationality as both competence and defense

Rio is not “the logical one who learns feelings matter.” She already has intense feelings and knows it.

Her rationality performs several functions:

- genuine intellectual pleasure;
- problem-solving competence;
- social role (`博士`);
- emotional regulation;
- defensive distance.

The volume reveals how painful it is for her that other people may value her through categories she did not author.

When classmates reduce her to `博士`, Kunimi's simple use of:

> 「双葉」

matters because it recognizes an individual rather than a role.

His chocolate cornet similarly becomes important because ordinary care can be more identity-forming than dramatic declaration.

Rio's family situation contributes to the architecture of solitude. Her father is associated with university-hospital work, her mother with fashion business and travel, and both are absent enough that the house feels emotionally uninhabited.

Rio describes it:

> 「体温を感じない家でしょ」

The phrase should not be over-literalized into “bad parents caused the Syndrome.” The stronger point is that solitude was normalized before friendship made its absence newly painful.

She says:

> 「学校でも、家でも、ひとりでいるのが当たり前だった」

After friendship, school becomes:

> 「少しだけ楽しくなった」

That produces the paradox:

> **attachment creates vulnerability to future loss.**

Rio was less afraid of being alone before she had evidence that not being alone could be better.

---

# 6. Sakuta: care requires participation, but his body still remains disposable to him

Volume 3 develops Sakuta positively in one important way.

He increasingly refuses to decide another person's identity for them.

When faced with two Rios, he does not select a “real” one. His position is:

> 「僕は双葉理央の味方だ」

He also says:

> 「そんなもん自分で決めろって」

and:

> 「自分のことは自分で決めるもんだろ」

This is a significant movement from rescue-as-solution toward supporting self-determination.

He also recognizes that he cannot simply force someone out of despair through argument.

However, the methods remain inconsistent.

He:

- takes Rio's phone after she says `ダメ`;
- summons Kunimi at 2:30 a.m. without her permission;
- continues sexual joking inside a body-shame crisis;
- bicycles through a typhoon;
- collapses with heat illness/dehydration.

The physical collapse is not a strategic self-sacrifice like the Volume 1 no-sleep plan; it is still evidence of how little jurisdiction Sakuta grants his own body when he believes someone needs him.

The updated Sakuta thesis is:

> **He is learning that care requires the other person's participation, but he still has not learned that his own body is not disposable property.**

---

# 7. Mai and Sakuta: from crisis bond toward negotiated partnership

Mai's importance in Volume 3 is easy to underestimate because Rio supplies the title.

When Rio may need a place to stay, Mai immediately recognizes a boundary problem in the assumption that Sakuta can simply house a female friend alone.

She says:

> 「だから、私も一緒に泊まるから」

This is not merely jealousy. It creates a safer and more socially legible care arrangement for Rio.

The couple also begins confronting career privacy as a joint problem.

Mai says:

> 「私個人で決めていい問題じゃないから保留にしてある」

and:

> 「ふたりの問題でしょ？」

When Sakuta tries to be rational and says the professional restriction is `仕方ない`, Mai pushes him to state the emotional truth:

> 「ほんとはすごい嫌ですよ」

That exchange is important because the relationship is beginning to require not only rational accommodation but honest desire.

Mai later says:

> 「仕事は仕事で大事だけど……私は、咲太が風邪を引いたら看病してあげたいし、オフの日にはデートだってしたいの」

and:

> 「咲太のおかげで、仕事を再開したのに、咲太に会えなくなるんじゃ意味がない」

The relationship has therefore moved toward a new structure:

> **Mai and Sakuta are becoming partners who must negotiate jurisdiction over a shared life.**

Physical intimacy likewise becomes more explicitly mutual; Mai is capable of initiating rather than existing only as recipient of Sakuta's desire.

---

# 8. Shoko: care-language transmitted across time without an explanation yet

The volume opens with a recurring memory/dream of older Shoko at Shichirigahama.

She tells Sakuta a horizon fact:

> 「人の目の高さから見える水平線までの距離は、約四キロメートル」

More important, she listens to the full story of the Kaede crisis without dismissing him.

She tells him:

> 「咲太君はがんばったね」

And she identifies three expressions she especially values:

> 「ありがとう」

> 「がんばったね」

> 「大好き」

The current younger Shoko is twelve, a first-year middle-school student, and does **not** remember Sakuta from two years earlier.

She is caring for a white kitten later named `はやて`.

The volume also reveals the seriousness of her heart condition. It is progressive and tied to growth; a transplant may be necessary, child donors are scarce, and she may not live long enough to graduate middle school without successful treatment.

This gives her future-anxiety extraordinary material force.

Her family dilemma over Hayate is subtle. Her parents love her enough that illness makes refusal difficult. Because she knows this, Shoko feels that asking for something may weaponize her illness against them.

Her mother says:

> 「病気の体に産んで、ごめんね」

Sakuta helps Shoko shift the family language away from apology and toward gratitude and affection.

Shoko tells her parents:

> 「お母さん、お父さん、いつもありがとう」

and expresses that she loves them and is glad they are her parents before asking to keep the cat.

The key formal recursion is therefore already visible:

> **Older Shoko → Sakuta → younger Shoko.**

Sakuta passes forward the care-language once given to him.

At the Volume 3 boundary, the ontology of older/younger Shoko remains unresolved.

---

# 9. Rio and Kunimi: recognition without entitlement

Kunimi's role is powerful precisely because he does not demand that Rio turn feeling into confession.

When Sakuta calls him in the middle of the night:

> 「双葉がピンチなんだよ。すぐに本鵠沼駅まで来てくれ」

Kunimi answers:

> 「おう、わかった」

> 「すぐ行くわ」

No interrogation comes first.

Rio's realization:

> 「全然ひとりじゃなかったんだ……ひとりじゃなかった……」

is about dependable presence.

Later, around the fireworks, the romantic feeling remains unresolved in a deliberately adult way.

Rio says:

> 「返事はいいよ」

> 「わかってるから」

and later:

> 「彼氏彼女になった場合、別れたらそれまででしょ」

> 「私は長くいたいから」

This can be read in two ways that should remain simultaneously live:

- a mature decision to preserve a valued friendship rather than demand an unavailable romance;
- a defensive attempt to control loss by choosing a relationship form that feels less fragile.

The text supports both.

# 10. Japanese voice, address, and translation-sensitive meaning

Rio's speech is usually controlled, dry, and analytically framed. The importance of moments such as:

> 「今は正論なんて聞きたくない」

comes partly from the fact that the “logical witch” explicitly rejects correct argument as emotionally mistimed.

This is not anti-rationalism. It is pragmatic recognition that truth and care have different timing requirements.

Address terms also matter. The `博士` label turns intellectual ability into a social role. Kunimi's `双葉` reduces that performative distance.

The three Shoko phrases—`ありがとう`, `がんばったね`, `大好き`—should be tracked verbatim because their later recurrence already has formal importance within this volume. They are not abstract themes; they are portable linguistic acts.

---

# 11. Phones, cameras, photographs, and the evolution of being seen

Volume 3 gives the phone/camera motif a particularly coherent progression.

For Rio, the camera first becomes a tool of self-surveillance and controlled exposure.

The anonymous account turns the body into:

- image;
- response generator;
- source of threat;
- temporary proof that someone is looking.

Later, a group photograph becomes something else entirely.

It is described as:

> 「ありのままの今を切り取った最高の一枚」

The camera moves from:

> **self-objectification → validation → danger → shared memory.**

Yet even the friendship photograph is not harmless for every Rio. It becomes evidence to R-A that the other Rio can occupy the social world without her.

The same object can therefore recognize one person and wound another state of the same person.

---

# 12. Illustrations and publication tension

The volume's illustrations create a genuine ethical contradiction.

One front illustration eroticizes Rio in a book centrally concerned with her distress over sexualized attention and bodily self-objectification.

Another image presents twelve-year-old Shoko in a wet-clothing/fanservice register despite the prose treating her as a seriously ill child whose privacy and vulnerability require care.

The correct critical position is neither:

> “therefore the prose's ideas are fake”

nor:

> “paratext does not matter.”

Instead:

> **The publication participates in the gaze the prose is sophisticated enough to analyze.**

That contradiction belongs to the work as published.

---

# 13. Ethics and philosophy

The volume's most important ethical contribution is a refinement of recognition.

After three books, the project can now distinguish:

> **attention ≠ visibility ≠ desire ≠ recognition ≠ care.**

Rio receives attention online.

It does not make her feel known.

She receives sexual desire.

It does not make her feel accompanied.

Kunimi coming in the middle of the night provides something the anonymous audience cannot:

> dependable personal response.

The book also rejects the idea that self-respect must precede relationship.

Sakuta's deliberately untherapeutic line—`別に嫌いでいい`—works because it removes another impossible performance requirement. Rio does not need to become a person who sincerely loves herself before she is allowed to continue existing among other people.

# 14. Counterreadings, limitations, and unresolved tensions

### Counterreading: one Rio is authentic and the other is fake

Contradicted by shared pre-split history and the fact that the online behavior predates duplication.

### Counterreading: sexual posting means Rio secretly enjoys sexual attention

The prose explicitly records body disgust and self-harm language. Relief at receiving response is not equivalent to enjoying the kind of response.

### Counterreading: the resolution teaches “confess your feelings”

Rio's handling of Kunimi explicitly resists that universalization.

### Counterreading: Sakuta saves Rio

His intervention matters, but Kunimi, Mai, Rio's own decisions, and the relation between the two Rio states are indispensable. Sakuta cannot choose integration for her.

### Limitation: Sakuta's boundary violations

Taking the phone and summoning Kunimi succeed. Success should not erase consent problems.

### Limitation: publication gaze

The art layer materially complicates the prose's critique of sexualized attention.

### Unresolved: exact duplication mechanics

The volume leaves this open.

# 15. What Volume 3 changes in the series-so-far model

1. Adolescence Syndrome can manifest as simultaneous bodily duplication.
2. A single autobiographical person can become divided into equally legitimate embodied states.
3. Identity integration need not mean self-love.
4. Need is not moral failure.
5. Sexual attention and recognition are decisively separated.
6. Sakuta increasingly returns identity decisions to the person affected.
7. His self-endangering embodiment problem remains.
8. Mai/Sakuta moves toward shared-life negotiation.
9. Rio/Kunimi demonstrates non-confessional care.
10. Older/younger Shoko now form a major unresolved continuity problem.
11. The three care phrases become a portable relational vocabulary.
12. The end introduces another unexplained body/identity problem involving Mai's apparent double.

V3 also establishes a useful identity proposition:

> **Contradiction can belong inside one person without requiring purification.**

# 19. Cumulative archival note

Volume 1 concerns a person whom the world will not acknowledge.

Volume 2 concerns a person who over-adapts to the world's anticipated judgment.

Volume 3 moves inward:

> **What if the intolerable judge is the self?**

Rio's resolution is not becoming unified because contradiction disappears.

She becomes unified because contradiction is finally permitted to remain hers.
