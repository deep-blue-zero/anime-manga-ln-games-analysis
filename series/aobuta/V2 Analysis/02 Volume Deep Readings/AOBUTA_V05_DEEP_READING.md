# 『青春ブタ野郎はおるすばん妹の夢を見ない』
## Volume 5 Deep Dive
### Recovery as bereavement: Kaede, Hanakaede, memory, ordinary life, and the right of a temporary self to have been real

**Primary source:** Original Japanese EPUB listed above.  
**Cumulative scope:** Volumes 1–5.  
**Spoiler boundary:** Strictly through Volume 5. Later explanations of Shoko, future-state mechanics, and subsequent Kaede/Hanakaede developments are not imported.  
**Provenance note:** This file reconstructs the established pre-v2 Volume 5 analysis and was partially re-opened during migration to verify several load-bearing Japanese anchors. It remains a legacy migration, not a newly reinterpreted retrospective reading.

---

# 1. Central thesis

Volume 5 is the first *AoButa* novel whose emotional catastrophe is produced not by the failure of recovery, but by its apparent success.

For two years, the Sakuta household has lived with `かえで`: a girl who emerged after the original Hanakaede's traumatic memory loss. She has the same body and name-history, but not the same autobiographical memory, habits, handedness, speech patterns, relationships, or experienced selfhood. Her family initially wants the earlier `花楓` back. `かえで` knows this. She looks at their faces and sees that they are searching through her for somebody else.

She tells Sakuta:

> 「かえではみんなの目がこわいです。みんな、花楓さんを見てるんです」

That is one of the most important recognition lines in the first five volumes.

Volume 1 asked whether a person can be socially erased.

Volume 4 asked whether a body can be seen while the person inside it is misidentified.

Volume 5 asks something harder:

> **What happens when everybody sees the correct body, remembers the correct history, and still fails to recognize the person presently living there because they are waiting for a previous continuity to return?**

The novel's central thesis is therefore:

> **Medical or mnemonic recovery can be genuine and still constitute bereavement.**

When `花楓` returns, Sakuta does not receive his sister “back” in a morally uncomplicated sense. He simultaneously loses `かえで`, the person who spent two years with him, depended on him, argued with him, wore panda pajamas, created goals, loved him, and built a future she knew she might not be permitted to keep.

The strongest personhood proposition is:

> **A person need not possess the oldest autobiographical continuity in a body to have been real.**

This does **not** require declaring `花楓` false or treating memory restoration as a mistake. The volume refuses that purification too.

Sakuta eventually has to hold both truths:

- `花楓` is his sister and her return matters;
- `かえで` was also his sister and her disappearance hurts like death.

The volume's broader ethical movement is from **protection** toward **supported re-entry**.

Sakuta has spent two years making the home safe enough for Kaede to survive. Kaede now begins choosing small encounters with the world. Her goals—answering a phone, buying pudding, riding a train, seeing pandas, reaching school—are not trivial therapy exercises. They are pieces of a life she authors for herself.

The tragedy is that she completes much of that work precisely as the autobiographical continuity called `かえで` approaches its end.

# 2. Source, structure, and narrated sequence

The EPUB contains twenty-eight spine items and twenty-five image resources. The chapter sequence is:

1. **第一章　あの日の続きに今日がある**
2. **第二章　かえでクエスト**
3. **第三章　覚めない夢の続きを生きている**
4. **第四章　明けない夜の夜明け**
5. **第五章　そして、また日は昇る**
6. **あとがき**

The titles themselves form a temporal argument.

The volume begins from continuation—today exists as the continuation of a traumatic earlier day.

It turns Kaede's self-chosen goals into a `クエスト`, giving ordinary activity the scale of adventure without mocking its difficulty.

It then questions waking, dreaming, night, and morning. The return of old memory looks like waking from one perspective and disappearance from another.

The final `また日は昇る` is therefore not a claim that everything is healed. Morning comes whether or not the people living through it are ready.

The volume also opens by following the unstamped Shoko letter from Volume 4, but the Kaede arc remains the principal structure. Older Shoko eventually becomes relevant again after Sakuta's collapse, yet her identity remains unresolved at the Volume 5 boundary.

---

# 3. Kaede's goals: ordinary life as self-authored future

Kaede announces:

> 「今日は、かえでから重大発表があります」

She presents a notebook headed:

> 「かえでの今年の目標です」

The timing is almost comic because the year is already far advanced. That is exactly why the list matters. Kaede has spent so long in emergency-survival mode that a future plan itself is new.

Her goals include:

- going outside with Sakuta;
- taking a walk with him;
- going to the sea;
- riding a train;
- buying pudding;
- going on a “date” with him;
- answering a phone call from someone other than Sakuta;
- going to school;
- later, seeing pandas.

The list is easy to read as dependency because many items involve `お兄ちゃん`.

Kaede herself partly frames the project as becoming independent:

> 「かえでが独り立ちをしないと、お兄ちゃんが一生結婚できなくなってしまいます」

But the goals also reveal something more subtle.

Independence is not defined as abruptly ceasing to need Sakuta.

It is defined as using a trusted relationship as a base from which a larger world becomes possible.

That is an important corrective to the idea that autonomy means self-sufficiency.

---

# 4. The phone test: progress, cost, and the persistence of the body

Mai helps Kaede practice answering calls. She has already been considerate enough to silence or reduce phone vibration around Kaede because networked communication is bound to traumatic history.

Kaede succeeds.

Mai praises her:

> 「おめでとう、かえでちゃん。よくできました」

Sakuta says:

> 「がんばったな、かえで」

The scene is an ordinary success and a warning.

The stress is followed by physical collapse and renewed bruising.

Sakuta recognizes:

> `かえで`'s Adolescence Syndrome had not simply ended.

Removing her from the hostile social environment prevented further acute damage, but isolation did not erase the underlying vulnerability.

This is clinically and ethically important.

The novel does **not** say:

> staying home was a failure.

The home protected her when protection was necessary.

It also does **not** say:

> because the home was safe, continued isolation is the final solution.

Kaede herself begins asking for something beyond survival.

---

# 5. The former school and the failure of punitive social repair

Kano Kotomi, a childhood friend, returns and gives one of the volume's most important institutional accounts.

After the bullying against Hanakaede was exposed, the students responsible became targets of collective hostility themselves. The school/social environment treated that reversal as justice.

Kotomi describes the atmosphere as:

> 「悪者退治して終わった感じの空気」

Then Hanakaede became a subject nobody was supposed to mention:

> 「もう誰もかえちゃんのことを口には出さないし、出したらいけない暗黙のルール」

This is a powerful continuation of the `空気` theme.

The community does not repair the person who was harmed.

It identifies villains, transfers aggression toward them, and then establishes silence as proof that the problem is over.

The social sequence is:

> victimization → exposure → punitive reversal → taboo → institutional closure.

The person at the center remains injured.

Volume 5 therefore makes an important distinction:

> **Punishing perpetrators is not identical to repairing a harmed person or the social environment that made the harm possible.**

The collective needs a clean ending before Kaede has one.

---

# 6. Medical language and Adolescence Syndrome must remain distinct

The volume provides the clearest explicit clinical framework so far.

A doctor says:

> 「梓川花楓さんの症状は、解離性障害の一種だと思われます」

The explanation continues:

> 「解離性障害とは、その同一性が失われてしまう症状を差します。つまり、今まで『自分』として捉えていたはずの感覚や意識、記憶といったものを、『自分』として認識できなくなる症状なんです」

The doctor also links dissociation in general to extreme stress and psychological trauma.

This material must not be collapsed into:

> dissociative disorder = Adolescence Syndrome.

The novel presents two related but analytically distinct layers:

1. a clinically legible dissociative/memory condition;
2. impossible bodily manifestations—such as the bruising—that exceed ordinary clinical explanation.

The doctor also gives the family an important personhood warning:

> 「花楓さんは、花楓さんでありながら、ご家族のよく知る花楓さんではなくなってしまっていることを、花楓さんのために理解してあげてください」

This is one of the most humane adult interventions in the series so far.

The family is asked not to turn the current person into a waiting room for the old one.

---

# 7. The naming of `かえで`: recognition as creation of social space

The current self writes `梓川花楓`.

Sakuta stops her:

> 「かえでは『花楓』じゃなくて、『かえで』だから」

The use of hiragana is not cosmetic.

It creates a social name for the currently living continuity.

The distinction lets Sakuta do something the rest of the family initially struggles to do:

> address the person in front of him without making every interaction a search for the lost past.

This does not mean he has rejected Hanakaede.

The later volume makes explicit that both matter to him.

But the naming act gives Kaede enough recognition to develop rather than merely await disappearance.

It also shows why names are so important in *AoButa*: naming can be an act of individuation.

---

# 8. Moving to Fujisawa: environmental care rather than cure

Kaede tells Sakuta:

> 「かえではみんなの目がこわいです。みんな、花楓さんを見てるんです」

Sakuta's move to Fujisawa can therefore be understood as an environmental intervention.

He cannot force everyone who knew Hanakaede to stop searching for her in Kaede's face.

He can give Kaede a place where strangers have no prior Hanakaede to project onto her.

This is care through social ecology.

It is not a cure.

The new environment allows a different self to accumulate experiences that belong to her.

This is one of the series' clearest examples of how place can change personhood by changing what other people expect to see.

---

# 9. School: ordinary life as extraordinary desire

The most important goal on Kaede's list is school.

Sakuta initially sees school through his own experience:

- boring classes;
- tests;
- tiring peer performance;
- mandatory atmosphere-reading.

Kaede sees something else.

For her, school means:

- ordinary friends;
- ordinary classes;
- ordinary after-school food;
- ordinary participation in an age-appropriate world.

The narration explicitly recognizes the asymmetry:

> school is not special to Sakuta;

but for Kaede, ordinary school life is precisely the special thing she lacks.

This is one of the volume's strongest thematic claims:

> **“Normal” is not inherently oppressive or inherently good; its value depends on whether a person is forced into it or freely wants access to it.**

The school counselor/teacher gives an unusually careful response.

She proposes gradual exposure rather than all-or-nothing attendance: walk part of the route, approach the school, use the gate as a goal, visit the infirmary.

More importantly, she challenges the institution itself:

> if the school treats people who cannot fit its required pace as the problem, perhaps the institution is also immature.

She tells Kaede that whatever she can genuinely manage may count as *her* version of:

> 「学校に行く」

and that simply coming to the infirmary would make her happy.

This is an anti-normalizing educational ethic.

The goal remains Kaede's.

The definition of success is allowed to adapt to the person.

---

# 10. Panda, pudding, and school: why the “quest” matters

Kaede's goal notebook turns ordinary life into visible evidence of authored continuity.

Each checked item says:

> `かえで` wanted this.

The zoo and pandas are particularly important because Kaede's panda pajamas have become part of her everyday self-image. Going to see real pandas makes the domestic motif into chosen outward movement.

Late in the volume she says:

> 「パンダと、プリンと、学校でコンプリートなんです」

The phrasing is playful.

The emotional structure is not.

She is trying to finish a life-project.

This becomes devastating once the reader realizes that Kaede has begun dreaming old Hanakaede memories and understands that the return of the previous continuity may erase her present one.

The quest is therefore not only exposure therapy.

It is an archive of a threatened self.

---

# 11. The return of Hanakaede: recovery and loss in the same morning

The morning after the school achievement, Sakuta sees a familiar expression and asks:

> 「お前……『花楓』なのか？」

The answer is embodied before it is theoretically explained.

The original Hanakaede appears to have returned.

For the father and the medical narrative, this is extraordinary recovery.

For Sakuta, it is also immediate bereavement.

He cries out:

> 「また、パンダを見に行くんじゃなかったのかよ！」

That line reveals the ethical center of the book.

If `かえで` had merely been a symptom or temporary shell, the panda promise would have no moral claim.

But Sakuta grieves because a person made the promise.

The body survives.

The promise-holder does not appear to.

This is where the series most decisively separates:

> body continuity from autobiographical continuity.

---

# 12. Kaede's diary: self-authored memory after disappearance

The diary prevents Kaede from being retroactively erased by recovery.

She writes:

> 「かえではかえでだから、花楓さんじゃないんです。」

She understands the relational problem:

> 「花楓さんを捜すお母さんとお父さんの目で見られるのは辛いんです。」

She also anticipates her own disappearance and Sakuta's grief:

> 「もし、このままかえでがいなくなったら、きっとお兄ちゃんはまた後悔すると思います。」

Then she states the memory she wants to leave:

> 「かえでがいなくなっても、お兄ちゃんにはかえでのことを笑いながら思い出してもらえたらうれしいです。」

This is extraordinary because it transforms Kaede from protected object into author of Sakuta's future memory.

She cannot control whether she remains.

She can still decide what kind of relationship she hopes survives her.

The diary is therefore an ethical testament without needing to be literal death writing.

It asserts:

> **If memory disappears, the person who lived it still had intentions.**

---

# 13. Sakuta: brotherhood beyond continuity preference

Sakuta's most important development is that he refuses to rank the sisters by authenticity.

`かえで` and `花楓` both matter.

When the doctor explains that the return of old memory may eliminate the current period's memories, Sakuta is forced to confront a choice he cannot control.

His mature position becomes:

> whichever self wakes—`かえで`, `花楓`, even some other `カエデ`—he will still be the brother.

This is a profound shift from trying to restore a particular identity state.

Brotherhood becomes a commitment that can survive uncertainty about the sister's autobiographical continuity.

But the return of Hanakaede shows that philosophical commitment does not eliminate grief.

Sakuta knows he should be happy that his original sister has recovered.

He cannot make the loss of Kaede disappear by reasoning correctly.

This is one of the series' first major demonstrations that:

> **two morally legitimate emotions can be mutually painful without one needing correction.**

---

# 14. Older Shoko and the second rescue of Sakuta

After Kaede disappears, Sakuta collapses emotionally.

The older Shoko—now apparently older than the high-school girl he remembers—appears and takes care of him.

Her presence is still unexplained at the Volume 5 boundary.

What matters locally is her function.

She does not tell Sakuta that he should be happy because Hanakaede returned.

She treats the loss of Kaede as real.

She tells him:

> 「咲太君はよくがんばりました」

and later insists that being the brother means receiving what has happened rather than choosing only the part of the outcome he can emotionally tolerate.

The care-language originally associated with older Shoko therefore returns precisely when Sakuta is unable to grant himself moral permission to grieve.

This is the second major occasion on which Shoko becomes a witness to Sakuta's suffering rather than a person he must rescue.

At this boundary, no temporal explanation is allowed.

---

# 15. Mai and the distributed care network

Mai remains essential even though Volume 5 belongs to Kaede.

She helps Kaede with practical exposure, especially the phone.

Her presence means Kaede's life has already expanded beyond a closed dyad with Sakuta.

This matters ethically because Sakuta's protectiveness can easily become total responsibility.

A larger network gives Kaede more than one route into the world.

Mai also becomes part of Sakuta's support structure while respecting the fact that sibling grief is not something she can simply solve for him.

The series' care model is becoming increasingly distributed.

---

# 16. Identity, memory, and personhood

Volumes 3–5 now form an unusually coherent identity sequence.

### Volume 3 — integration

One autobiographical self becomes two bodies.

The solution is not choosing which is real.

### Volume 4 — differentiation

Two people become crossed visible embodiments.

The solution is allowing each person not to be the other.

### Volume 5 — continuity replacement

One body carries radically discontinuous autobiographical/personality states across time.

The crisis is that the restoration of an earlier continuity may destroy the later one.

This produces a cumulative thesis:

> **Body, memory, name, personality, social recognition, and relational commitment can each carry identity, but no single element is sufficient in every case.**

Volume 5 also makes the ethical stakes of memory especially clear.

Calling Hanakaede's return a cure can be medically accurate and still incomplete as a description of what happens to the people involved.

---

# 17. Ethics and philosophy

The volume's deepest ethical question is:

> **Who has standing to define a recovered person?**

The family wants Hanakaede.

Kaede wants to remain Kaede.

Medicine can describe memory restoration.

Sakuta lives with the person produced by the intervening two years.

No single perspective can erase the others.

The school material adds another principle:

> a person's goals should not be judged solely against an institution's standard pace.

The counseling model respects Kaede's objective while refusing to turn objective into coercive deadline.

This is a strong autonomy model:

> **support the person's chosen direction; adapt the route; do not redefine the destination without her.**

The diary adds a final moral principle:

> **a person can possess interests in how she is remembered even if she cannot guarantee continued existence.**

---

# 18. Counterreadings, limitations, and unresolved tensions

### Counterreading: Hanakaede is the “real” sister and Kaede is a temporary illness personality

The novel's emotional and linguistic architecture strongly resists this. Sakuta names Kaede, lives with her, makes promises with her, and grieves her. The medical origin of a self does not make its experiences unreal.

### Counterreading: Kaede's school goal proves everyone should overcome school refusal by gradual exposure

Too universal. The novel explicitly individualizes pace and route. Kaede wants school; the intervention is legitimate because it follows her desire.

### Counterreading: the school handled the bullying once the perpetrators were punished

Kotomi's account shows the opposite. Collective punishment and taboo replaced meaningful repair.

### Counterreading: memory restoration is uncomplicated happiness

The father's joy is real. Sakuta's grief is also real. Neither cancels the other.

### Unresolved: what does Hanakaede remember?

At this boundary, the continuity between Kaede's two years and Hanakaede's restored consciousness is not fully resolved.

### Unresolved: older Shoko

Her reappearance and apparent age remain unexplained.

---

# 19. What Volume 5 changes in the series-so-far model

1. Clinical dissociation and Adolescence Syndrome must be analytically separated.
2. `かえで` is treated as a genuine person-state rather than a disposable temporary shell.
3. Memory restoration can constitute both recovery and bereavement.
4. Sakuta's sibling commitment begins to transcend preference for a particular identity continuity.
5. Kaede's ordinary goals become evidence of self-authored futurity.
6. Safe isolation is validated as emergency care but not universal endpoint.
7. School becomes both source of social injury and potential site of chosen ordinary life.
8. An adult educator provides one of the series' strongest institutionally humane responses.
9. Punishment of bullies is distinguished from social repair.
10. The diary becomes a material archive that preserves the threatened self's intentions.
11. Older Shoko returns as Sakuta's caregiver, deepening the unresolved mystery.

The cumulative identity formula becomes:

> **integration without purification; differentiation without abandonment; continuity without assuming that the oldest memory automatically monopolizes personhood.**

# 23. Cumulative archival note

Volume 5's cruelty lies in the fact that everybody can be correct about part of the outcome.

The doctor can call it recovery.

The father can cry with joy.

Hanakaede can be the sister who was lost.

And Sakuta can still mourn Kaede.

The series does not need to choose one of those statements as the only morally legitimate description.

That refusal is the volume's greatest strength.
