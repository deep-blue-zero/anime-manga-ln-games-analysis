# 00. README and Corpus Map
## Reader orientation for the definitive 『青春ブタ野郎シリーズ』 full-series synthesis

> **Complete spoilers follow for all fifteen numbered light novels and the two canonical bonus novels, *Animal Land* and *Spring Days*.**

This file is the reader-facing entrance to the complete AoButa analytical corpus. It explains:

- what the corpus includes and excludes;
- how publication order differs from story chronology;
- where the two bonus novels belong;
- how the specialist documents divide responsibility;
- how to move from a synthesis claim back to Japanese primary-source evidence;
- which terminology and state labels are controlled;
- what later volumes corrected in earlier readings;
- and which metaphysical questions remain legitimately unresolved after Volume 15.

The corpus is designed for two different uses.

A reader who wants one continuous argument can begin with:

> [`AOBUTA_FULL_SERIES_SYNTHESIS.md`](AOBUTA_FULL_SERIES_SYNTHESIS.md)

A researcher who wants source-traceable specialist analysis can use Documents 01–21, the Phase-1 ledgers, and the seventeen canonical volume/bonus readings.

The governing principle is:

> **The full-series synthesis may use hindsight, but it may not pretend hindsight was available to the first reader of an earlier volume.**

Accordingly, the package preserves both:

- **local historical readings** — what a volume established or reasonably suggested at its own publication boundary;
- **retrospective full-series readings** — what Volumes 1–15 and the analyzed bonuses ultimately allow the project to conclude.

# 1. Mature full-series thesis

AoButa begins with a girl in a public library whom almost nobody can see.

It ends with an adult trainee teacher who cannot see the person or ghost a student says she sees—and who believes the student enough to listen anyway.

The mature full-series thesis is:

> **AoButa begins by asking whether a person remains real when society stops recognizing her and ends by asking whether an adult can recognize another person's suffering even when he no longer shares her perception. Across that movement, the series develops a theory of personhood in which body, memory, name, public role, documentary history, relationship, social recognition, and present choice all matter, but none alone has the right to erase the others.**

Its principal recognition movement is:

> **seeing → remembering → recognizing → believing without shared sight**

Its principal care movement is:

> **exceptional rescue → reciprocal care → distributed support → ordinary institutional competence**

Its principal adulthood movement is:

> **immediate phenomenon → possible world → memory and ethical knowledge**

The final volume does not require Sakuta to say Adolescence Syndrome never happened. It gives him a third option between permanent supernatural actualization and crude denial:

> `思春期症候群を思い出に変える`

He turns Adolescence Syndrome into memory.

The experience changes form without becoming nonexistent.

That is why the final ethical closure is stronger than the final cosmological closure. The series leaves unresolved parts of:

- possible-world ontology;
- observer mechanics;
- Miori's cross-world condition;
- smartphone/network participation;
- R1 memory distribution;
- and the red-randoseru girl's literal identity.

But it reaches a much firmer conclusion about how people should respond under uncertainty:

> **Do not dismiss a person merely because you cannot verify her experience through your own senses. Listen before claiming authority over what the experience means.**

For the continuous argument, read:

- [Definitive Full-Series Synthesis](AOBUTA_FULL_SERIES_SYNTHESIS.md)
- [01 — Series Architecture, Phases, and Retrospective Progression](../04%20Synthesis%20Documents/01_SERIES_ARCHITECTURE_PHASES_AND_RETROSPECTIVE_PROGRESSION.md)

# 2. Complete corpus boundary

## 2.1 Included primary literary corpus

The definitive literary corpus contains seventeen analyzed Japanese prose sources:

1. the fifteen numbered light novels;
2. *Animal Land*;
3. *Spring Days*.

The numbered series runs from:

> 『青春ブタ野郎はバニーガール先輩の夢を見ない』

through:

> 『青春ブタ野郎はディアフレンドの夢を見ない』

The two analyzed bonuses are:

- 『青春ブタ野郎はアニマルランドの夢を見る』;
- 『青春ブタ野郎はスプリングデイズの夢を見る』.

The original Japanese EPUBs are the governing primary sources. Their filenames, SHA-256 hashes, EPUB spine counts, image counts, and integrity results are recorded in:

- [`SOURCE_INVENTORY.md`](../06%20Archive/SOURCE_INVENTORY.md)
- `SOURCE_CHECKSUMS.sha256` _(referenced artifact is not present in Git)_

The source EPUBs themselves are **not redistributed** in this analytical package.

## 2.2 Included paratext

When present in the supplied Japanese editions, the analyses may use:

- cover and title material;
- embedded illustrations;
- tables of contents;
- afterwords;
- colophons and edition metadata;
- official creator messages contained inside the analyzed edition.

Paratext is always separated from narrative fact. An illustrator or adaptation-staff farewell message may illuminate reception or emphasis, but it does not create post-story canon.

## 2.3 Excluded from the governing literary corpus

This package does not treat the following as primary evidence for the light-novel synthesis unless a specialist document explicitly identifies a narrow comparative use:

- television anime;
- theatrical anime adaptations;
- manga adaptation;
- game material;
- event scripts;
- drama-CD audio not physically present in the supplied source payload;
- official English translation;
- fan wikis, plot summaries, or fandom consensus;
- external interviews not included in the analyzed edition;
- general web reception.

Volume 15's supplied EPUB metadata identifies a special edition with drama-CD audio, but no audio payload was present inside the analyzed EPUB. No audio-performance claim is therefore derived from that metadata alone.

## 2.4 What “definitive” means here

“Definitive” means:

- complete for the supplied Japanese main-series and bonus corpus;
- grounded in controlled sequential analyses;
- retrospectively integrated through the final volume;
- auditable through evidence and locator ledgers;
- explicit about unresolved questions.

It does **not** mean:

- every interpretation is uniquely entailed;
- every metaphysical rule has been solved;
- the corpus replaces the original novels;
- adaptations have been comprehensively compared;
- every possible external publication or retailer extra has been audited.

# 3. Source hierarchy and analytical modes

## 3.1 Source hierarchy

The corpus follows this hierarchy:

| Priority | Source type | Use |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Original Japanese novel or bonus currently under analysis | Governing narrative, language, characterization, mechanics, and form |
| 2 | Earlier Japanese novels and canon extras already analyzed | Longitudinal comparison within the preserved spoiler boundary |
| 3 | Illustrations and paratext belonging to that edition | Visual and publication framing, kept distinct from prose fact |
| 4 | Official supplementary material | Used according to date, authorship, and narrative status |
| 5 | Official translation | Retrieval or translation comparison only; never overrides Japanese |
| 6 | Adaptations | Used only in a separately defined adaptation study |
| 7 | Interviews and external scholarship | Reception or production context; never substitutes for the text |
| 8 | Fan reference material | Claim discovery or reception mapping only, followed by primary-source verification |

The controlling protocol is preserved in:

- `reference/REFERENCE_AOBUTA_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2.md` _(referenced artifact is not present in Git)_

The corpus architecture is preserved in:

- `reference/REFERENCE_AOBUTA_MULTI_DOCUMENT_SYNTHESIS_ARCHITECTURE.md` _(referenced artifact is not present in Git)_

## 3.2 Four reading states

The final corpus distinguishes four legitimate knowledge states.

### Local first-pass truth

What a particular volume directly establishes or reasonably supports at its own endpoint.

Primary home:

> the corresponding `volumes/AOBUTA_VXX_DEEP_READING.md` artifact.

### Prospective signal

What an earlier volume suggests before later confirmation.

Examples:

- the nature of older Shoko;
- the relation among dreams, futures, and worlds;
- the original Kirishima Toko problem;
- the importance of smartphone non-participation.

Prospective evidence is marked `FP` and may not be converted into later certainty inside the earlier reading.

### Retrospective full-series truth

What later volumes confirm, correct, or substantially reframe.

Primary homes:

- [19 — Longitudinal Claim Revision and Retrospective Correction Ledger](../04%20Synthesis%20Documents/19_LONGITUDINAL_CLAIM_REVISION_AND_RETROSPECTIVE_CORRECTION_LEDGER.md)
- the relevant specialist document;
- the continuous synthesis.

### Residual ambiguity

What remains underdetermined after Volume 15.

These claims remain `UA` rather than being closed merely because the numbered series is complete.

## 4.1 Why publication order matters

Later-authored material can strengthen an earlier chronological period without proving that an earlier publication already contained every later implication.

This matters especially for the bonuses. Their events occur before Volume 10, but the supplied editions were released after Volume 13 and before Volumes 14–15.

The final synthesis therefore distinguishes:

- **story-position authority** — what canonically happened during the depicted period;
- **publication-history authority** — what a reader could actually know when earlier numbered volumes first appeared.

# 8. Evidence classes and confidence notation

The corpus uses eight evidence classes.

| Label | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `TF` | Textual fact | Directly stated or unambiguously shown in a retrievable source location |
| `SI` | Strong inference | Best explanation of converging evidence after alternatives are considered |
| `TI` | Thematic or tentative interpretation | Literary, psychological, ethical, or formal synthesis supported but not uniquely entailed |
| `CB` | Character belief | A proposition asserted or believed by a character; not automatically objective fact |
| `UA` | Unresolved ambiguity | Evidence remains incomplete, incompatible, or intentionally open |
| `FP` | Prospective or foreshadowing signal | Suggests later development but cannot be promoted to certainty early |
| `RC` | Retrospective correction | Later evidence changes an earlier working interpretation |
| `VJ` | Value judgment | Explicit normative or critical evaluation by the analysis |

## 8.1 Optional confidence axis

When ontological status matters, a second axis may be used:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `A` | directly established |
| `B` | strongly established |
| `C` | strongly implied |
| `D` | plausible inference |
| `E` | speculation or theory |

Example:

A Rio explanation may be:

- `TF/A` that Rio said it;
- only `CB/C` or `CB/D` as literal world physics.

This distinction is essential in a series where characters repeatedly use scientific language to model impossible events under uncertainty.

## 8.2 Claim-revision statuses

Document 19 additionally uses final claim statuses:

- `CONFIRMED`;
- `REVISED`;
- `CORRECTED`;
- `REJECTED`;
- `PARTIAL_UNRESOLVED`;
- `UA`.

These statuses describe the history of an interpretation, not the moral quality of the claim.

# 9. Controlled state notation

Do not use “timeline,” “dream,” “future,” “possible world,” and “rewrite” as interchangeable terms.

The principal full-series labels are:

| Label | Controlled meaning |
|---|---|

# 10. Naming, romanization, and lexical conventions

## 10.1 Japanese names

When full names are used, the corpus normally preserves Japanese order:

> family name → given name

Examples:

- 梓川咲太 / Azusagawa Sakuta;
- 桜島麻衣 / Sakurajima Mai;
- 牧之原翔子 / Makinohara Shoko;
- 美東美織 / Mito Miori.

After introduction, central characters are usually discussed by given name in English prose:

- Sakuta;
- Mai;
- Shoko;
- Rio;
- Tomoe;
- Nodoka;
- Miori.

## 10.2 Romanization

The corpus uses stable, search-friendly common romanization without mandatory macrons in filenames or ordinary English prose.

Accordingly:

- `Shoko`, not necessarily `Shōko`;
- `Toko`, not necessarily `Tōko`;
- `AoButa` as the project shorthand;
- `Seishun Buta Yarou` as the common romanized series form in metadata.

Japanese script remains the authority when pronunciation, orthography, or lexical ambiguity matters.

## 10.3 Kaede and Hanakaede

The project uses:

- **Kaede** for `かえで`;
- **Hanakaede** for `花楓`.

This is an analytical English distinction.

Both are pronounced *Kaede* in spoken Japanese. The page can distinguish the two orthographically even when the ear cannot.

Do not treat “Hanakaede” as a separate spoken name used by the characters.

## 10.4 Kirishima Toko

The corpus distinguishes:

### Human Toko

The deceased girl who:

- wrote and composed the songs;
- was Miori's lifelong friend;
- became Shoko's donor.

### Public Kirishima Toko

The composite artistic identity formed by:

- Toko's name;
- Toko's compositions;
- Miori's voice and performance;
- public recognition.

### Toko-identification states

Nene and the other Santa figures are not the originating human Toko. They occupy or imitate the socially valuable Toko role.

## 10.5 Adolescence Syndrome

The controlled English term is:

> **Adolescence Syndrome**

for:

> `思春期症候群`

The corpus does not reduce the term to a medical diagnosis or purely figurative label.

# 16. Residual ambiguity notice

The corpus intentionally leaves the following questions open.

## 16.1 Possible-world ontology

The novels do not fully establish whether all anomalous states are:

- independent worlds;
- simulations;
- dream interfaces;
- rewritten histories;
- observer-dependent possibility states;
- or mechanically distinct phenomena grouped under one social name.

## 16.2 Counterpart Sakuta's observer model

The final observer theory is the strongest in-world explanation of R1.

It remains a character theory, not narrator-certified physics.

## 16.3 Miori's cross-world singularity

Open questions include:

- whether one Miori literally spans all relevant worlds;
- how her memory persists;
- how Enoden transit implements world movement;
- what occurs in worlds that lose access to her;
- whether she returns physically after the epilogue.

## 16.4 Smartphone and network mechanics

Volume 14's unfinished phrase:

> `スマホを持たない思春期の`

is never completed into a formal law.

The evidence strongly links smartphones to networked social recognition and possibility circulation.

It does not establish:

> smartphone ownership causes Adolescence Syndrome.

## 16.5 Red-randoseru girl

The girl resembles child-actor Mai and guides Sakuta through the final memory pilgrimage.

# 20. Final orientation

AoButa is easiest to underestimate when remembered as a succession of supernatural girlfriend cases.

The corpus instead reads it as a long argument about what makes another person real when ordinary identity components stop aligning.

Across fifteen volumes and two bonuses, the series tests:

- body without memory;
- memory without public recognition;
- role without biography;
- biography in the wrong world;
- future persons without current bodies;
- dead persons whose art and organs remain;
- visible persons carrying the wrong public identity;
- possible lives that become morally significant without becoming destiny.

The final answer is not that one substrate wins.

It is that personhood is historical, relational, and presently active.

A person is not merely:

- the body;
- the name;
- the role;
- the memory;
- the document;
- the gaze of others.

She is the particular subject who lived this history, bears these consequences, stands within these relationships, and is still trying to choose what comes next.

That is why the series' final adult competence is so modest and so demanding.

Sakuta cannot see what Ebina sees.

He does not convert uncertainty into dismissal.

He says:

> `僕には見えない。でも、君を信じるよ`

Then he offers to listen.

The corpus begins from that same obligation.

Establish what the text supports.

Preserve what it does not settle.

Do not substitute confidence for evidence.

Do not mistake sight for recognition.

And when a person reports a reality that cannot yet be explained, begin by treating the person as real.

# 16. Interpretive cautions

## 16.1 Do not reduce Adolescence Syndrome to diagnosis or metaphor

The phenomena are literally real within the fiction.

They can also externalize:

- social pressure;
- grief;
- self-division;
- fear;
- recognition;
- possible life;
- public identity.

Literal event and thematic meaning should remain simultaneous.

## 16.2 Do not force one mechanical law across all cases

Tomoe's recursive future, Rio's duplication, Shoko's future states, Kaede/Hanakaede, W1 exchange, Nene/Toko, Miori, and Ebina need not be generated by one simple rule.

## 16.3 Do not collapse Kaede into Hanakaede

The distinction is foundational to the personhood argument.

## 16.4 Do not reduce Mai to “the girlfriend”

Her vocation, celebrity, sisterhood, public identity, ethical authority, and authorship of the final adulthood metaphor require independent treatment.

## 16.5 Do not reduce the title heroines to harem routes

The harem-market counterreading is legitimate and addressed in Document 18.

The analysis must also account for:

- independent adult futures;
- nonromantic relationships;
- refusal capacity;
- post-title-volume agency;
- later contributions to the ensemble.

## 16.6 Do not convert successful intervention into automatic legitimacy

Sakuta can help effectively while:

- invading privacy;
- overriding process;
- taking disproportionate bodily risk;
- interpreting urgency as jurisdiction.

Document 13 and the intervention ledger preserve those costs.

## 16.7 Do not equate organ donation with identity transfer

Toko's heart in Shoko creates bodily continuity and moral relationship.

It does not make Shoko Toko.

## 16.8 Do not treat objects as magical without evidence

Rings, trophies, diaries, photographs, passes, and songs can be powerful relational or mnemonic anchors.

The text does not automatically establish each object as an independent supernatural device.

## 16.9 Hashtag orthography is normalized for search

The original Japanese prose normally prints the dream hashtag as `＃夢見る` with a full-width hash. The analytical corpus commonly normalizes this to ASCII `#夢見る` for stable search and indexing. Exact quotations preserve the source typography. The two forms are not treated as different terms.

## 16.10 Do not invent a smartphone law

The no-smartphone cluster is important.

The final series does not complete it into a simple rule such as:

> “smartphone ownership causes Adolescence Syndrome.”

The strongest mature interpretation concerns networked participation, collective recognition, and chosen versus imposed connection.

## 16.11 Do not identify the red-randoseru girl beyond the evidence

She resembles young Mai and carries strong symbolic/liminal function.

The novels do not provide a final literal identity.

## 16.12 Do not call R1 morally meaningless

Its people and preferences matter even though the state is not preserved as RF.

## 16.13 Do not call adulthood disenchantment

The final solution is not:

> “The supernatural was childish nonsense.”

It is:

> **memory reclassification plus continued trust.**

## 16.14 Do not invent post-epilogue closure

Volume 15 does not establish:

- Sakuta and Mai's marriage;
- Rio's final answer to Toranosuke;
- Miori's physical return;
- a complete end to future Syndrome cases;
- the final ontology of Ebina's ghost.

# 19. Final orientation

The most useful way to hold the completed corpus is not as a list of fifteen supernatural cases.

It is a progression of recognition problems.

A girl can be visible to the nation and absent to her school.

A socially constructed self can be sincere.

Two contradictory desires can belong to one person.

Two sisters can share a body without becoming interchangeable.

One body can carry discontinuous autobiographies.

A future person can matter even if her future is overwritten.

A repaired family can be new rather than restored.

A helper can use care to avoid confronting herself.

A lover can want certainty badly enough to threaten the other's privacy.

A prestige role can erase the biography of the person performing it.

A crowd can recognize the wrong identity so strongly that the mistake becomes materially consequential.

A rewritten world can contain genuine goods without acquiring the right to replace every other history.

A dead friend can become memory without becoming nothing.

An adult can stop seeing the ghost without denying the pattern.

The series' final ethical achievement is therefore not a complete theory of supernatural physics.

It is the adult response to uncertainty:

> **I cannot see what you see.**
>
> **I believe that your experience matters.**
>
> **Tell me what happened.**

That is the endpoint toward which this entire corpus is organized.
