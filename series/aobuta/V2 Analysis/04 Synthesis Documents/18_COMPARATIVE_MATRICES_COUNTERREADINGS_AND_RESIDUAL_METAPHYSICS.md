# 18. Comparative Matrices, Counterreadings, and Residual Metaphysics
## Structured reference and adversarial synthesis

# 1. Function

This document is a comparative control layer.

It does not introduce a new master thesis.

Its functions are:

1. make the mature findings of Documents 01–17 reusable;
2. expose similarities without flattening differences;
3. preserve strong counterarguments;
4. distinguish final ethical conclusions from unresolved mechanics;
5. identify where the continuous synthesis must remain qualified.

The matrices are not numerical rankings.

They are structured comparisons.

# 8. Counterreading 1: AoButa is primarily metaphorical psychology, not literal speculative fiction

## Strongest case

Each Syndrome maps cleanly onto an adolescent conflict:

- invisibility;
- social repetition;
- split self;
- comparison;
- dissociation;
- future anxiety;
- identity performance;
- grief.

The scientific language is often loose and character-generated.

The emotional resolution usually matters more than mechanics.

## Strongest objection

The novels repeatedly insist upon literal impossible consequences:

- multiple bodies;
- organ-donor futures;
- record changes;
- observer-specific visibility;
- world-crossing messages;
- crowd physics;
- two separately embodied sister continuities.

A purely metaphorical reading cannot account for the plot's causal structure.

## Controlled conclusion

> **AoButa is literal speculative fiction whose phenomena also externalize psychological and social conflicts. Neither layer should erase the other.**

# 9. Counterreading 2: AoButa is fundamentally a harem centered on Sakuta

## Strongest case

- title-heroines cluster around one male protagonist;
- several express attraction;
- visual marketing foregrounds desirable young women;
- Sakuta remains narrative hub;
- some later futures are compressed.

## Strongest objection

- Mai relationship is canonically central and durable;
- Tomoe moves beyond the crush;
- Rio develops separate romantic possibility;
- Miori's endpoint is friendship;
- ensemble members gain vocation and agency;
- women help one another and Sakuta;
- title arcs are not interchangeable romance routes.

## Controlled conclusion

> **The series uses harem-market architecture and romantic clustering, but its mature narrative is better described as a central romance surrounded by an increasingly autonomous ensemble.**

# 10. Counterreading 3: Sakuta is an admirable rescuer

## Strongest case

He acts when others do not.

Without him, several characters may remain invisible, divided, isolated, or endangered.

He accepts social and bodily cost.

## Strongest objection

He also:

- violates privacy;
- manipulates;
- stages confrontations;
- treats his body as expendable;
- assumes jurisdiction.

## Controlled conclusion

> **Sakuta's courage is real and his paternalism is real. The arc is meaningful because the series gradually separates recognition from ownership.**

# 11. Counterreading 4: Mai is a recurring heroine rather than co-protagonist

## Strongest case

Sakuta narrates.

Each volume foregrounds another heroine.

Mai is sometimes absent due to work.

## Strongest objection

Mai provides:

- continuous romance;
- independent vocation;
- sister arc;
- sacrifice correction;
- family future;
- public identity crisis;
- final adulthood metaphor;
- shared-world thesis.

## Controlled conclusion

> **Mai is structurally a co-protagonist inside Sakuta's first-person series, even though narrative access remains asymmetrical.**

# 12. Counterreading 5: Adolescence Syndrome has one coherent hidden system

## Strongest case

The finale offers:

- observation;
- possible worlds;
- recognition;
- Miori overlap;
- Sakuta observer status.

Earlier cases can be retrospectively grouped around these concepts.

## Strongest objection

The cases differ radically in:

- bodies;
- records;
- memory;
- time;
- trigger;
- observer conditions.

The final explanation is character theory with explicit uncertainty.

## Controlled conclusion

> **The series has recurrent ontological principles but not a fully demonstrated single equation from which every case can be derived.**

# 13. Counterreading 6: The finale's adulthood is retreat from the supernatural

## Strongest case

Sakuta stops sustaining the impossible and cannot see Ebina's ghost.

The final world emphasizes conventional institutions and careers.

## Strongest objection

He refuses to deny Syndrome.

He says:

> `思い出に変える`

He believes Ebina despite lacking perception.

## Controlled conclusion

> **The finale is maturation through memory and epistemic humility, not disenchantment through dismissal.**

# 14. Counterreading 7: R1 resolution underexplores erasure of current persons

## Strongest case

R1 includes people with coherent preferences:

- Kaede;
- Rio;
- others.

RF does not preserve all of those lives.

The exact subjective fate of R1 Kaede is not explained.

## Strongest objection

- R1 itself overwrites biographies nonconsensually;
- Kaede receives information and chooses return;
- final world privileges ordinary authorship over magical optimization;
- the series explicitly preserves R1 moral significance.

## Controlled conclusion

> **The resolution is ethically defensible but not metaphysically or morally costless. Kaede's choice is essential and does not eliminate all residual concern.**

# 15. Counterreading 8: Metaphysical ambiguity is incompleteness rather than strength

## Strongest case

The finale leaves open:

- exact observer law;
- smartphone relevance;
- Miori travel;
- red-randoseru girl identity;
- R1 memory distribution.

Readers seeking hard speculative closure can reasonably find this incomplete.

## Strongest objection

The series' primary interest is how people should respond under uncertainty.

The ethical architecture closes strongly:

- remember without substitution;
- believe without seeing;
- prefer authored futures;
- distribute care.

## Controlled conclusion

> **The metaphysical incompleteness is partly a limitation and partly formally appropriate. The final synthesis should acknowledge both rather than celebrate ambiguity automatically.**

# 21. Final controlled conclusion

The strongest full-series reading is not the one that eliminates every contradiction.

It is the one that keeps the right contradictions visible.

AoButa is:

- literal speculative fiction and psychological externalization;
- a central romance and a harem-market serial structure;
- a celebration of Sakuta's courage and a critique of his jurisdiction;
- ethically resolved and metaphysically incomplete;
- committed to ordinary adulthood without treating adolescence as fake;
- willing to relinquish a desirable world without calling its people meaningless.

The final synthesis should not choose one side merely for neatness.

It should ask which distinction the series itself learns to make.

The most important is the final one:

> seeing is not the same as believing.

Sakuta once needed exceptional perception to recognize Mai.

He ends by recognizing Ebina without it.

That is the series' clearest ethical closure.

The remaining cosmology should be presented with equal clarity about what the books do and do not establish.
