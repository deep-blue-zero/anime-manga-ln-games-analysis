# 07. Title Heroines, Ensemble, Friendship, and Distributed Care
## From serial case structure to cumulative community

## Governing question

> **How does AoButa's apparent “one heroine per volume” structure gradually become an ensemble in which earlier title heroines retain agency, memory, friendship, and practical care beyond their own crisis?**


# 1. Executive thesis
AoButa initially resembles a familiar light-novel structure:

- one male narrator;
- one title heroine per arc;
- a supernaturalized personal crisis;
- intense emotional access;
- several girls who could plausibly be read as romantic alternatives.

The complete series does not abolish that structure. It **outgrows** it.

The decisive test is not whether multiple girls feel something for Sakuta. Several do. The decisive test is what happens after the title volume.

Do the characters remain:

- psychologically active?
- capable of disagreeing with Sakuta?
- connected to one another rather than only to him?
- able to help rather than only receive help?
- attached to independent work, family, and future?
- permitted to leave romantic attachment behind?

Across fifteen volumes and two bonuses, the answer increasingly becomes yes.

The serial heroine architecture therefore develops into a cumulative community.

> **Sakuta begins as the unusual person who can recognize each isolated crisis. He ends surrounded by people who recognize one another, provide practical support, enter professions, and preserve care even when he is no longer the indispensable center.**

That development is visible in the hospital sequence after the Volume-13 accident. Rio accompanies the ambulance. Kunimi drives professionally. Ikumi helps. Hanakaede comes. Tomoe and Sara visit during work break. Nodoka and Uzuki call. Mai arrives after public responsibilities. Then people leave because they have lives.

That is not a harem tableau.

It is a care network.


# 2. The title-heroine mechanism
A title heroine usually performs four functions.

1. **Local protagonist:** her problem supplies the volume's emotional center.
2. **Phenomenon carrier:** her crisis makes one aspect of Adolescence Syndrome literal.
3. **Sakuta mirror:** the case reveals something about his recognition or intervention style.
4. **Longitudinal node:** after resolution, she contributes to later characters, evidence, or care.

The fourth function becomes increasingly important.

Without it, the series would remain episodic.

With it, earlier arcs become infrastructure for later ones.


# 3. Tomoe Koga: from compulsory air-reading to ordinary separation
## 3.1 Initial crisis

Tomoe fears socially visible isolation.

Her phenomenon is built from:

- anticipated embarrassment;
- peer atmosphere;
- wish to rehearse or optimize a future before living it.

The repeated June is socially recursive rather than a simple clock rewind.

## 3.2 Sakuta's contribution

Sakuta recognizes that the self she built after moving is not therefore fake.

> `それが古賀だよ。前がどうだろうと、今のその姿が古賀`

He also participates in the fake dating scheme, despite knowing it is ethically dubious.

## 3.3 Tomoe's agency

Tomoe genuinely likes her remade self:

> `気に入ってる。すごく好き`

She eventually confesses even though she expects nonreciprocity.

That matters because her arc is not simply:

> stop caring what people think.

It is:

> accept social construction, risk embarrassment, and choose despite incomplete control.

## 3.4 Later role

Tomoe later becomes:

- one of the first people able to recognize future-state Sakuta;
- a coworker who helps Hanakaede;
- a source of `#夢見る` information;
- a university student who explicitly decides she needs to graduate from attachment to Sakuta.

Her final desire for an ordinary boyfriend is significant.

The series allows her to leave the protagonist-centered romantic possibility behind.


# 4. Rio Futaba: contradiction, friendship, and scientific humility
## 4.1 Initial crisis

Rio uses rationality as defense against:

- body shame;
- loneliness;
- desire for attention;
- love for Kunimi;
- fear of dependence.

Her duplicated bodies literalize wishes she cannot integrate.

## 4.2 Sakuta's contribution

He refuses to choose a “real” Rio:

> `僕は双葉理央の味方だ`

and says:

> `自分のことは自分で決めるもんだろ`

The stance preserves both contradictory continuities long enough for Rio to act.

## 4.3 Rio's agency

Rio recognizes:

> `全然ひとりじゃなかったんだ`

She asks to join friends rather than waiting to be rescued into belonging.

## 4.4 Later role

Rio becomes the series' most important model-builder.

Crucially, her science matures.

She increasingly distinguishes:

- evidence;
- hypothesis;
- speculation;
- unresolved anomaly.

She helps Sakuta without pretending each theory is law.

She also develops beyond the Kunimi triangle. R1 gives her the desired relationship, and she says she wants it to remain. RF does not preserve it. Three years later she is considering Toranosuke after actual dates.

The final relationship remains open.

That openness is appropriate: Rio's adult future becomes negotiated rather than magically assigned.


# 5. Nodoka Toyohama: comparison without substitution
## 5.1 Initial crisis

Nodoka's self is organized around comparison with Mai and maternal approval.

She says she hates her sister. Mai says she also hated Nodoka. The volume preserves multiple kinds of `嫌い` rather than treating dislike as absence of love.

## 5.2 Sakuta's contribution

He repeatedly insists that Nodoka should remain Nodoka even when performing inside Mai's body.

> `本番の瞬間を迎えても、お前はお前のまま`

## 5.3 Nodoka's agency

She rejects becoming Mai:

> `なりたくない`

Her real desire is not simply professional victory. She wants maternal praise and recognition.

## 5.4 Later role

Nodoka becomes:

- independent idol;
- sister capable of acknowledging what she cannot understand about Mai;
- caregiver to Hanakaede;
- archive holder for Kaede's photograph;
- participant in the final care network;
- teacher-license student;
- member of Sweet Bullet's earned final Budokan.

She no longer exists merely as the inferior sister.


# 6. Uzuki Hirokawa: from unreadability to overpermeability
## 6.1 Initial presentation

Uzuki's unusual literalism around `空気` first appears as comic/social difference.

> `空気って吸うものなのにおかしくないですか？`

Her mother articulates a durable autonomy rule:

> `卯月の幸せは、みんなに決めてもらうもんじゃなくて、卯月が決めるんだよ`

## 6.2 Volume-10 crisis

Uzuki gains social attunement.

The new awareness is initially useful. She understands:

- sarcasm;
- pressure;
- market reality;
- how others view her.

The problem begins when social information acquires total jurisdiction.

## 6.3 Sakuta and the ensemble

Sakuta tells her:

> `今こそ空気を読めよ、づっきー`

The instruction is not submission to abstract society. It means reading the concrete people still singing with her.

Sweet Bullet's dream becomes distributed rather than dependent upon Uzuki's inexhaustible optimism.

## 6.4 Final role

Uzuki gives the finale's clearest self-authorship statement:

> `今度は、現実にしよう`

> `自分で摑み取ってこその武道館だよ`

Sweet Bullet later achieves Budokan through ordinary history, and the event is also the group's ending.

Dream becomes motivation rather than destiny.


# 7. Ikumi Akagi: care without omnipotence
## 7.1 Initial crisis

Ikumi's identity depends on being:

- good;
- reliable;
- useful;
- the person who helps.

Her middle-school failure becomes a wound to the ideal self.

## 7.2 Sakuta's contribution

He recognizes that the atmosphere was never one girl's rightful burden.

He also recognizes the particular Ikumi behind the helper role:

> `赤城、見っけ`

## 7.3 Ethical complication

Sakuta exploits her helper compulsion with a false post. The case therefore does not present him as pure rescuer.

## 7.4 Later role

Ikumi:

- provides cross-world communication;
- helps with Nene/Toko;
- travels to Hokkaido;
- studies nursing;
- accepts gradual rather than heroic recovery;
- later recognizes Sakuta's lived happiness as `本当の一番`.

Her arc exits competitive goodness.

Care becomes a trained profession rather than proof that she never fails.


# 8. Sara Himeji: love, certainty, and the right not to know
## 8.1 Initial crisis

Romantic humiliation makes Sara distrust ordinary interpretation.

Clairvoyance offers certainty:

- attraction;
- rejection;
- thought;
- usefulness.

## 8.2 Sakuta's contribution

He distinguishes teaching from being needed. If another tutor can help more, he is willing to lose her as a student.

## 8.3 Sara's agency

Mai invites Sara to inspect her mind.

Sara refuses:

> `いやです……！`

The refusal accepts another person's opacity.

## 8.4 Later role

Sara later contributes to the Toko investigation through ordinary observation of Christmas motifs, not supernatural access.

She works at the restaurant, imagines Sakuta as teacher, and studies childcare.

Her value no longer depends on special usefulness.


# 9. Nene Iwamizawa: prestige identity versus authored vocation
## 9.1 Initial crisis

Nene expects Tokyo success to confirm that she is `何者か`.

When work fails, the viral Toko comparison offers a ready-made identity.

## 9.2 Ensemble contribution

Her recovery depends on more than Sakuta.

- Miori identifies the model contradiction.
- Sara notices the Christmas props.
- Rio revises the hypothesis.
- Ikumi travels.
- Takumi remembers through the trophy.
- Nene finally speaks her own humiliation.

This is one of the clearest distributed-care resolutions.

## 9.3 Final role

Nene applies for announcer work and later becomes a Hokkaido television announcer.

Her question `何者かであるって思いたい` receives an ordinary adult answer:

> build a vocation rather than occupy borrowed prestige.


# 10. Miori Mito: final heroine as friend rather than romance route
## 10.1 Initial position

Miori is difficult to classify, able to perceive Toko phenomena, and resistant to friendship.

## 10.2 Retrospective revelation

She is:

- Toko's lifelong friend;
- the singer behind public Toko songs;
- a serial reality observer;
- a person who has met many Sakutas without letting any become a friend.

## 10.3 Sakuta's contribution

Sakuta refuses to read the exchange diary because he wants Miori to tell him herself.

He offers friendship without proof of causal innocence.

## 10.4 Miori's agency

Miori:

- reverses/completes the song;
- grieves;
- restores Toko attribution publicly;
- accepts the diary;
- buys a smartphone for chosen reachability;
- accepts Sakuta's friendship;
- leaves for other worlds on her own mission.

The final title heroine does not become a final romantic challenger.

She becomes the friend whose absence can be sustained through memory and music.


# 11. Kunimi Yuma: reliable friendship and professional care
Kunimi begins as the socially competent friend who can respond immediately when Rio is in trouble.

He later becomes a firefighter.

The career is thematically important because it professionalizes emergency care:

- training;
- shifts;
- team;
- handoff;
- rest.

Kunimi contrasts with the adolescent fantasy that one person should always be available to rescue everyone.

He remains kind without becoming morally omnipotent.


# 12. Saki Uesato: from defensive hostility to adult coexistence
Saki's early hostility toward Sakuta is real.

She is also protective of her relationship with Kunimi and suspicious of Sakuta's chaos.

By university:

- she studies nursing;
- befriends Ikumi;
- can share social space with Sakuta without hostility organizing every encounter;
- seeks Sakuta when Ikumi disappears.

Her final future remains tied to care and to Kunimi, but she is not reduced to “the girlfriend blocking Rio.”


# 13. Takumi Fukuyama: memory, patience, and practical commitment
Takumi's relationship with Nene is erased from his memory.

When the trophy restores recognition, he does not demand that Nene immediately remember him.

He says:

- he will remember;
- he will wait;
- he still loves her.

His decision to step before the car is reckless, but his later development is more ordinary and sustainable.

He moves toward Hokkaido through employment at the television station where Nene works.

His adulthood definition is:

> `大切な人を大切にすること`

The phrase becomes practical geography and work.


# 14. Kotomi Kano: friendship after autobiographical rupture
Kotomi represents a different form of recognition.

She knew Hanakaede before the crisis.

When she returns, she does not demand that the current person reproduce the old relationship immediately.

She becomes:

- email contact;
- friend;
- university companion;
- scaffold for Hanakaede's self-authored future.

Her role helps prove that relationship restoration need not mean identity restoration.


# 15. Miwako Tomobe and the adult institutional model
Miwako is one of the series' strongest adults because she avoids two common failures.

## False reassurance

She can tell a student when something may not be realistic.

## Authoritarian closure

She also respects the student's desire and remains available afterward.

Her care model is:

> truth + options + autonomy + nonabandonment.

She also recognizes Sakuta's possible teaching aptitude.

The final Ebina scene can be read as Sakuta inheriting this institutional ethic.


# 16. The ensemble after each title arc
The series increasingly uses earlier title heroines to support later ones.

Examples:

- Nodoka preserves a Kaede photograph.
- Tomoe and Kunimi help Hanakaede at work.
- Uzuki models alternative education and later group reciprocity.
- Rio provides cautious mechanism analysis.
- Sara notices Toko's repeated objects without clairvoyance.
- Ikumi helps Nene.
- Shoko helps Miori interpret the song.
- Miori helps restore Mai's identity.

This creates a network in which solutions are distributed across prior experience.


# 17. Friendship as a full narrative category
AoButa is often discussed through romance because the titles are girl-centered and Sakuta's relationship with Mai is central.

But friendship becomes equally important.

- Sakuta/Rio survives unreciprocated love elsewhere.
- Sakuta/Tomoe survives rejected confession.
- Sakuta/Miori becomes the final title relationship.
- Mai/Nodoka becomes sisterly friendship inside family.
- Hanakaede/Kotomi is reconstructed without demanding old identity.
- Sweet Bullet turns a center's dream into group commitment.

The series' final adult network depends on relationships that do not require romance to remain meaningful.


# 18. Distributed care: the hospital as ensemble proof
After Sakuta is injured at the Volume-13 event:

- Mai catches him and calls emergency services;
- Rio accompanies him;
- Kunimi drives the ambulance;
- Ikumi helps;
- Hanakaede visits;
- Tomoe and Sara come during work break;
- Nodoka and Uzuki call;
- people leave to continue ordinary obligations.

The scene demonstrates the final care architecture:

> **many people contribute bounded support; no one person's love must become total availability.**


# 19. Vocation as character continuation
The epilogue gives earlier title heroines futures that correspond to their arcs without mechanically trapping them inside the original problem.

| Character | Crisis-linked danger | Adult direction |
|---|---|---|
| Tomoe | compulsory peer optimization | independent social life / ordinary romance |
| Rio | disembodied rationality / body shame | tutoring, academic work, negotiated intimacy |
| Nodoka | comparison with Mai | idol career, teaching credential, sisterhood |
| Uzuki | unreadable/overread social atmosphere | earned group achievement |
| Ikumi | compulsive helper identity | nursing with structured limits |
| Sara | certainty and special usefulness | childcare, ordinary observation |
| Nene | borrowed prestige identity | announcer vocation |
| Miori | unstable worlds / grief avoidance | friendship, art, chosen mission |

The futures are not neat therapeutic prescriptions. They are authored directions.

## 20.1 Care-flow map

Early care flows mainly:

> Sakuta → title heroine.

Later it becomes multidirectional:

- Mai → Sakuta, Hanakaede, family.
- Tomoe → Sakuta.
- Rio → Sakuta and ensemble.
- Nodoka → Mai, Hanakaede, ensemble.
- Uzuki → Hanakaede and Sweet Bullet.
- Ikumi → Nene case and volunteer students.
- Sara → Toko investigation and future children/students.
- Nene/Takumi → each other.
- Miori → public Toko correction and Sakuta's adult insight.
- Kunimi/Saki/institutions → emergency and professional care.

The network remains asymmetrical. It is no longer one-way.

## 20.2 Friendship types

- **Crisis friendship:** formed through hidden suffering.
- **Ordinary friendship:** sustained through work, food, school, and repetition.
- **Professional friendship:** support while retaining roles and obligations.
- **Continuing-bond friendship:** Miori/Toko through song and diary.
- **Future-oriented friendship:** Sakuta/Miori through reachability across absence.

The title heroines become an ensemble when relations move beyond the first category.

## 20.3 Adults and institutions

Miwako, parents, medical staff, donor organizations, managers, and workplace supervisors matter because the adult world is uneven rather than uniformly absent. The final institutional hope is that adults can respond without demanding certainty first.

## 20.4 Anti-harem test

For each former heroine ask:

1. Does she possess a desire not organized around Sakuta?
2. Does she sustain relationships independent of him?
3. Does she contribute to another person's development?
4. Can she refuse or move away?
5. Does her final future require romantic availability to him?

Across the major title heroines, the answer increasingly becomes yes. The series retains harem grammar and works against its own initial structural temptation.


# 21. Does the series outgrow the harem structure?
The strongest answer is:

> **substantially, but not completely at the level of genre presentation.**

## Evidence for the harem reading

- Most title heroines are attractive young women.
- Sakuta receives unusually intimate access.
- Several develop romantic feelings.
- The male narrator remains central.
- Visual/fanservice framing sometimes eroticizes vulnerability.

## Evidence against the strong harem reading

- Mai remains the continuous romantic partner rather than one route among equals.
- Tomoe's attachment is explicitly graduated from.
- Rio's future is not held in romantic suspension around Sakuta.
- Sara's attraction is bounded by teacher ethics.
- Miori becomes friend, not final challenger.
- Nene/Takumi and Kunimi/Saki have independent relationships.
- Earlier heroines form relations with one another and enter vocations.
- The final collective achievement is institutional listening, not romantic accumulation.

The most accurate description is:

> **AoButa uses harem-adjacent serial heroine grammar while progressively reorganizing its emotional economy around ensemble, friendship, and durable independent futures.**



# 22. Post-title autonomy test
A useful way to judge whether a title heroine remains an independent character is to ask what happens after Sakuta is no longer solving her central crisis.

| Character | Later self-authored action | Contribution not reducible to romance with Sakuta | Final ordinary direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tomoe | chooses university partly to graduate from attachment | recognizes Sakuta in V7; works; participates in ensemble | women's university, wider social life |
| Rio | builds university friendships; revises models | scientific caution, practical crisis help, honest disagreement | tutoring/research; open adult romance |
| Nodoka | supports Mai while admitting epistemic limits | idol work, family care, direct requests to Sakuta | teacher-license path / post-idol transition |
| Uzuki | chooses dream despite odds | gives Hanakaede an alternative school model; reframes Budokan | earned Budokan and group conclusion |
| Ikumi | returns from alternate life and faces failed self | volunteer work, nursing, world-state communication | nursing qualification |
| Sara | refuses total interior access | ordinary observation solves Christmas motif; encourages Sakuta teacher future | childcare study |
| Nene | abandons borrowed Toko prestige | warns of mass Toko phenomenon; resumes ordinary ambition | television announcer |
| Miori | publicly restores Toko/Miori authorship | gives final grief/world problem and accepts friendship | cross-world mission, song-based continuing relation |

Every one of these trajectories has a verb other than:

> wait for Sakuta.


# 23. Care-flow map
The ensemble's maturation can be represented as changing directions of care.

## Early direction

> Sakuta → title heroine

He sees, believes, confronts, and often takes excessive personal risk.

## Middle direction

> title heroine ↔ Sakuta

Examples:

- Tomoe recognizes socially invisible Sakuta;
- Rio models crises and treats injuries;
- Mai protects and corrects him;
- Shoko gives moral language;
- Nodoka and Uzuki contribute family/community support.

## Late direction

> ensemble ↔ ensemble, mediated by institutions

Examples:

- Uzuki helps Hanakaede imagine another school path;
- Nodoka helps Hanakaede with digital life;
- Sara notices the Christmas motif without supernatural access;
- Takumi restores Nene's relational memory;
- Shoko carries the diary to Miori;
- Kunimi transports Sakuta professionally;
- hospital, school, donor-support, and work structures share the burden.

The important development is not simply that Sakuta gets help. Care ceases to be protagonist-exclusive.


# 24. Friendship types across the ensemble
AoButa treats friendship as more than a holding category for women Sakuta does not date.

## 24.1 Witness friendship

Rio and Sakuta can tell each other difficult truths and tolerate disagreement. Their intimacy does not require romantic conversion.

## 24.2 Recovered friendship

Kotomi/Hanakaede demonstrates that a relationship can resume after autobiographical rupture without pretending the old person has been perfectly restored.

## 24.3 Post-crush friendship

Tomoe's future requires graduating from the romantic fixation while retaining social relation.

## 24.4 Friendship after failed recognition

Sakuta/Miori becomes possible only after Miori accepts that friendship can survive absence and does not require serial reality to stay provisional.

## 24.5 Professional friendship

Kunimi, Rio, Sakuta, Saki, and later adult roles show friendship compatible with schedules, work, and partial distance.

These forms help explain why “not endgame romance” is not narrative demotion.

# 27. Quiet ensemble evidence
The cumulative-community thesis depends on ordinary scenes as much as climaxes.

- Rio has university friends whom Sakuta does not organize.
- Tomoe works and talks with Sara.
- Hanakaede makes plans with Kotomi without Sakuta attending.
- Nodoka and Mai help with a laptop.
- Uzuki's mother researches schooling.
- Kunimi completes professional training.
- Saki and Ikumi become university friends before Sakuta understands the relationship.
- Nene applies for ordinary jobs.
- Takumi chooses a geographic future.

These details prove that the cast continues beyond the narrow moment when Sakuta is looking at them.

# 29. Primary-home boundary

# 30. Strongest counterreadings
## 30.1 Earlier heroines remain satellites around Sakuta

The narrator's viewpoint supports this concern. Many later appearances occur because Sakuta encounters them.

Yet the epilogue, jobs, relationships, and cross-heroine support show lives not reducible to his desire.

## 30.2 Vocation endings are too neat

There is some thematic neatness in matching care-oriented characters with care professions.

The series mitigates this by preserving uncertainty and friction:

- Shoko may not become a doctor;
- Rio's romantic answer remains open;
- Sweet Bullet's Budokan is also dissolution;
- Mai's career delays education;
- teaching is still a trainee role for Sakuta.

## 30.3 Distributed care is still coordinated by Sakuta

Often it is.

But later arcs increasingly require information, action, and recognition Sakuta cannot supply alone. Miori's song, Takumi's memory, Shoko's diary interpretation, and institutional emergency care are not extensions of his unique power.


# 31. Post-title-volume agency matrix
| Character | What Sakuta initially provides | What she later provides | Independent future | Why she is not reducible to rescue recipient |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tomoe | companionship, social cover, honest refusal | recognition of invisible Sakuta; coworker/friend normality | women's university, ordinary romance sought | she explicitly graduates from attachment to him |
| Rio | practical help integrating split self | scientific models, criticism, emergency support | university, tutoring, possible Toranosuke relationship | she repeatedly corrects Sakuta and retains epistemic independence |
| Nodoka | mediation during comparison/body exchange | family care, Mai insight, logistics, teacher-license path | idol conclusion and distinct post-idol life | sisterhood and vocation exceed Sakuta relation |
| Uzuki | distinction between concrete allies and abstract `みんな` | alternative-school model for Hanakaede; future-motivation ethic | earned Budokan and group ending | she converts possible success into self-authored effort |
| Ikumi | recognition of helper compulsion and world displacement | cross-world communication, travel/logistics, moral comparison | nursing | she becomes helper without sole-savior fantasy |
| Sara | tutoring and clarification of desire | observation, work, confidence in Sakuta-sensei | childcare study | her value survives loss of clairvoyance and romantic closure |
| Nene | archive preservation and reconnection to Takumi | public Toko clarification and adult vocational example | announcer | she builds the identity once borrowed from prestige through ordinary work |
| Miori | friendship offer, listening, autobiographical disclosure | final grief architecture, public song correction, cross-world task | open cross-world future | final heroine is explicitly friend rather than romance route |

The matrix makes visible a key structural change:

> **Sakuta often initiates recognition, but the completed ensemble returns recognition, care, knowledge, and future possibility to him and to one another.**


# 32. The social network after the case structure
Early AoButa can be diagrammed as a hub:

> title heroine → Sakuta → Rio/Mai as occasional support.

The later series becomes a network.

Examples:

- Mai and Nodoka care for Kaede/Hanakaede.
- Uzuki becomes a model for Hanakaede's alternative education.
- Rio interprets Ikumi, Nene, Miori, and world-state evidence.
- Sara notices the Christmas visual pattern without supernatural access.
- Ikumi travels with Sakuta and later continues nursing.
- Shoko becomes guide and donor-history witness.
- Tomoe finds invisible Sakuta.
- Kunimi provides professional emergency care.
- Miwako provides institutional educational care.

This matters because the series' ethical development requires Sakuta to cease being the only bridge among isolated women.

The characters increasingly know:

- one another;
- each other's histories;
- each other's work;
- each other's limits.

The network remains Sakuta-centered in narration.

It is no longer exclusively Sakuta-dependent in function.


# 33. Friendship as a full narrative endpoint
A strong harem structure tends to interpret female intimacy with the protagonist through romantic ranking.

AoButa certainly uses romantic possibility and attraction. It also builds friendships whose value is not inferior because romance does not occur.

## 33.1 Sakuta/Rio

Intellectual trust, criticism, long history, no need for romantic conversion.

## 33.2 Sakuta/Tomoe

Former romantic asymmetry becomes ordinary coworker/friend continuity.

## 33.3 Sakuta/Miori

The final title heroine becomes `ディアフレンド`, not final romantic challenger.

## 33.4 Female-female relations

- Mai/Nodoka;
- Hanakaede/Kotomi;
- Sweet Bullet members;
- Rio and other university friends;
- Saki/Ikumi.

The series' social world would be thinner if all meaningful relation had to route through romantic desire.


# 34. Distributed care matrix
| Care form | Representative character/institution | What it corrects |
|---|---|---|
| scientific/modeling support | Rio | Sakuta's tendency to treat intuition as sufficient |
| emotional/relational challenge | Mai | unilateral sacrifice and secrecy |
| peer recognition | Tomoe | assumption that Sakuta alone can restore visibility |
| institutional education | Miwako | school conformity as only legitimate route |
| professional emergency response | Kunimi | improvised heroism as sole care model |
| nursing trajectory | Ikumi, Saki | helping as trained, bounded, team-based work |
| family/friend continuity | Kotomi, Nodoka, parents | isolated caregiver model |
| public/professional correction | Miori, Mai, production staff | private insight alone cannot repair mass identity |
| teacher listening | adult Sakuta | exceptional sight as prerequisite for belief |

The hospital after the Volume-13 accident is the ensemble proof because people arrive and leave according to actual responsibilities.

They care.

They do not become permanently suspended around Sakuta.

That boundedness is a sign of maturity, not insufficient devotion.


# 35. Vocation is not decorative epilogue allocation
The final careers are sometimes so fitting that they can look overly neat.

Their analytical value lies in converting crisis capacities into ordinary roles.

## Tomoe

Social sensitivity becomes something she can manage rather than something that dictates every decision.

## Rio

Model-building and tutoring remain useful without requiring supernatural crisis.

## Nodoka

Idol work ends; teacher-license study opens another social role.

## Uzuki

Leadership and performance culminate in a real Budokan whose meaning includes group dissolution.

## Ikumi and Saki

Care becomes nursing rather than private moral omnipotence.

## Sara

Attention to others becomes childcare study rather than clairvoyant access.

## Nene

Prestige hunger becomes an actual announcer vocation.

## Sakuta

Recognition becomes teaching.

These endings are not rewards distributed according to archetype. They are the ordinary institutional forms into which earlier capacities can mature.


# 36. A stronger harem analysis
The harem interpretation should be neither dismissed nor accepted lazily.

## 36.1 Evidence supporting the reading

- male first-person narrator;
- multiple attractive title heroines;
- recurring sexualized observation;
- several girls develop romantic or quasi-romantic feelings;
- Sakuta becomes unusually central to their crises;
- covers and titles market one girl at a time;
- Mai is the stable winner from very early, leaving other emotional routes commercially legible but narratively closed.

## 36.2 Evidence against the strong reading

- Mai/Sakuta is established early rather than endlessly deferred;
- title heroines retain post-arc lives;
- Tomoe explicitly moves beyond Sakuta;
- Rio's final romantic possibility is elsewhere;
- Miori is framed as friend;
- Nodoka, Uzuki, Ikumi, Nene, and Sara acquire independent vocations;
- female-female relationships matter;
- earlier heroines later help Sakuta and one another;
- the final ethical endpoint is institutional listening, not accumulation of romantic devotion.

## 36.3 Controlled conclusion

AoButa uses the **surface grammar** of a harem-adjacent light novel.

It does not finally organize character worth by romantic access to Sakuta.

The strongest formulation is:

> **harem-adjacent presentation, ensemble-oriented longitudinal development.**


# 37. Unevenness inside the ensemble
The ensemble is real and still unequal.

## 37.1 Mai receives far more continuous development

This supports co-protagonist status and leaves some title heroines comparatively episodic.

## 37.2 Some later futures arrive through compact epilogue information

A vocation can feel more summarized than dramatized.

## 37.3 Sakuta remains the narrative hub

Even when care is distributed, readers mostly learn about the network through his movement.

## 37.4 Visual marketing recenters the title heroine as object of attention

The prose may grant autonomy while cover/illustration structures continue bishōjo serial consumption.

These limits should be acknowledged without losing the cumulative transformation.


# 38. Ensemble memory and the refusal of disposable arcs
The series repeatedly brings old evidence back through:

- jokes;
- jobs;
- places;
- phone calls;
- advice;
- hospital visits;
- dream outcomes;
- vocational futures.

This creates a social memory larger than Sakuta's private recollection.

Kaede is remembered through several people.

Toko is remembered through Miori, Shoko, family, song, and public identity.

Sakuta's own crises are held by Mai, Rio, Tomoe, Shoko, parents, and later students.

The ensemble therefore performs one of the series' deepest personhood functions:

> **no single observer owns the entire biography, and a person is harder to erase when memory is distributed.**


# 39. Cross-document boundary and controlled ensemble formulation
Document 07 is the primary home for:

- post-title-volume agency;
- friendship;
- ensemble topology;
- distributed care;
- harem counterreading;
- vocational continuation.

Sakuta/Mai romance belongs in Document 04. Individual Kaede/Hanakaede and Shoko analyses belong in Documents 05–06. Institutional care receives full treatment in Document 14. Japanese voice distinctions belong in Document 15.

The controlled Phase-4 formulation is:

> **AoButa begins with a title-heroine case structure in which Sakuta appears to be the exceptional bridge to each isolated girl. It gradually transforms that structure into a cumulative community. Earlier heroines retain agency after rescue, form relationships not reducible to Sakuta, acquire work and institutional roles, correct him, recognize him, and participate in care for one another. The series remains harem-adjacent in presentation, but its mature social world is an ensemble whose value is measured increasingly by friendship, vocation, and reciprocal support rather than romantic availability.**


# 41. Final ensemble thesis
The title-heroines do not become irrelevant after their Syndrome resolves.

They become:

- coworkers;
- scientists;
- sisters;
- idols;
- nurses;
- students;
- teachers;
- announcers;
- friends;
- witnesses;
- people capable of disagreeing with Sakuta and leaving him.

The series begins with isolated cases because each adolescent experiences the problem as uniquely private.

It ends with a community because the characters learn that:

> **recognition can be shared, care can be distributed, and another person's future does not have to terminate at the edge of the protagonist's romantic interest.**

That is how the serial heroine structure becomes cumulative literature rather than only recurring premise.
