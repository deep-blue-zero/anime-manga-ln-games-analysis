# 19. Longitudinal Claim Revision and Retrospective Correction Ledger

## 1. Function

This document is the reader-facing Phase-2 form of the Phase-1 claim/revision ledger.

Its purpose is not to produce forty new conclusions. Its purpose is to preserve **how the project came to know what it now claims**.

AoButa repeatedly encourages interpretations that are locally reasonable and later incomplete:

- public attention appears to cure Mai before later volumes separate attention, recognition, memory, and identity;
- older Shoko initially looks like a fixed time traveler before competing future states complicate the model;
- the visible Santa woman appears to be Kirishima Toko before Nene, the deceased composer Toko, and Miori's performance role are disentangled;
- Miori initially appears to be the direct author of reality rewriting before counterpart Sakuta reallocates the roles of cross-world locus and observer;
- the smartphone clue becomes too concentrated to dismiss, but the finale refuses to turn it into a simple technological law.

The architecture therefore requires four forms of truth to remain separately recoverable:

1. **local first-pass truth** — what the volume itself established;
2. **prospective truth** — what the volume reasonably invited the reader to suspect;
3. **retrospective full-series truth** — what the completed series makes most defensible;
4. **residual ambiguity** — what remains unresolved even after Volume 15.

The immutable `AOBUTA_VXX_DEEP_READING.md` files preserve the first category. This ledger governs the transition among the other three.

## 2. Phase-2 status vocabulary

Phase 1 used a few working labels with slightly different granularity. Phase 2 normalizes them into six reader-facing statuses while preserving the underlying prose and evidence route.

- `CONFIRMED` — later evidence substantially strengthens rather than alters the claim.
- `REVISED` — the early claim contained a defensible core but needs material reformulation.
- `CORRECTED` — later evidence overturns a concrete identification or causal/mechanical formulation.
- `REJECTED` — the completed series rejects the claim as a governing interpretation.
- `PARTIAL_UNRESOLVED` — an important part survives while causal or ontological details remain open.
- `UA` — the completed series deliberately leaves the point unresolved.

## 3. How to read the ledger

A `REJECTED` claim is not necessarily evidence that the original reading was careless.

Some rejected claims were **designed by the narrative to be tempting**.

For example:

- “older Shoko comes from the one true fixed future” is a reasonable early model;
- “the Santa woman is the original Toko” is a reasonable provisional identification;
- “R1 is simply another timeline” is a reasonable first response to coherent changed biographies.

The important analytical question is:

> **Was the earlier claim warranted by the evidence then available, and what exact later evidence forced it to change?**

Similarly, a `CONFIRMED` claim is not frozen at its first formulation. The recognition thesis begins in Volume 1 as a distinction between physical presence and social visibility. By Volume 15 it has expanded into a distinction between shared perception and ethical credibility.

The claim remains continuous because later evidence enlarges the same problem rather than replacing it.

## 6. Six correction chains that should structure later synthesis

### 6.1 Recognition: from seeing to believing

`CLM-001`, `CLM-002`, and `CLM-029` form the longest single argumentative line in the corpus.

Volume 1 demonstrates that:

> physical presence, fame, attention, memory, and recognition are not identical.

Later volumes repeatedly split those variables further:

- Sakuta becomes the unseen person;
- the wrong Ikumi can be socially accepted as the right one;
- Nene's body can disappear into a culturally legible prestige identity;
- institutions can fail to count physically present Santas;
- Mai can be recognized too strongly as the wrong person.

The final Ebina scene then removes the sensory privilege on which Sakuta's earliest helper role depended.

The mature claim is not:

> “believe every proposed mechanism.”

It is:

> **another person's testimony can deserve ethical attention before one's own perception verifies the object of that testimony.**

### 6.2 Personhood: from body mismatch to multi-axis continuity

`CLM-004` through `CLM-008`, together with `CLM-020`, `CLM-021`, and `CLM-032`, progressively prevent the synthesis from treating identity as one-variable continuity.

The series separates:

- body;
- autobiographical memory;
- learned/procedural memory;
- name;
- role;
- public recognition;
- documentary history;
- relationship;
- self-identification;
- current choice.

The Kaede/Hanakaede arc remains the most sustained test. Later identity cases do not replace it; they show why its distinctions were foundational.

### 6.3 Care: from effective rescue to constrained intervention

`CLM-003`, `CLM-009`, `CLM-010`, and `CLM-035` record a genuine normative development.

Early Sakuta repeatedly does something invasive that works.

The completed series does not retroactively declare those methods uniformly legitimate. Instead, later books supply contrasts:

- Mai rejects unilateral sacrifice;
- Hanakaede receives options rather than a predetermined school;
- Spring Days makes supported participation compatible with self-authored limits;
- Sakuta refuses Miori's private diary when information would damage the relationship he wants;
- the adult Sakuta begins with listening.

The specialist ethics document must therefore preserve both:

> the courage that makes Sakuta valuable

and:

> the paternalism that makes some of his interventions ethically costly.

### 6.4 Futurity: from prediction to morally significant possibility

`CLM-011`, `CLM-013`, `CLM-022`, `CLM-024`, `CLM-025`, `CLM-030`, `CLM-031`, and `CLM-040` should be read together.

The completed series increasingly refuses:

> possible = destined = morally rightful.

A possible life can be:

- experientially real;
- emotionally valuable;
- ethically relevant;
- worth grieving;

without thereby acquiring the right to overwrite the authored present.

The final preference is not “the timeline with the highest happiness score.”

It is:

> **continuity in which people can participate in making the future.**

### 6.5 Toko/Miori: from anonymous artist to distributed identity and grief

`CLM-019` through `CLM-023`, `CLM-033`, and `CLM-034` record the largest late-series identity correction.

The corpus must retain three levels:

1. **human Kirishima Toko** — deceased composer/lyricist, Miori's friend, Shoko's donor;
2. **Mito Miori** — singer/performer/uploader;
3. **public Kirishima Toko** — the composite artistic identity generated by Toko's works, Miori's performance, Toko's name, and audience recognition.

Nene's prestige-identity crisis then becomes a crisis of disappearing into an identity that was already distributed before she entered it.

### 6.6 Adulthood: from denial to transformed relation

`CLM-028`, `CLM-029`, and `CLM-039` guard against a shallow disenchantment reading of the finale.

Counterpart Sakuta offers denial.

Current Sakuta refuses.

The final formulation is:

> `思春期症候群を思い出に変える`

The decisive change is therefore not:

> “the supernatural was childish and false.”

It is:

> **the experience no longer has to remain immediately perceptible in order to retain autobiographical and ethical truth.**
