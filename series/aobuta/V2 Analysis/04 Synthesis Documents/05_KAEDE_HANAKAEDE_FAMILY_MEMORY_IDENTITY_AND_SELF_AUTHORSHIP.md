# 05. Kaede, Hanakaede, Family, Memory, Identity, and Self-Authorship
## One body, discontinuous autobiographies, and the ethics of inheritance

## Governing question

> **What does the Kaede/Hanakaede story establish about personhood when body continuity survives but autobiographical continuity does not?**



# 1. Executive thesis
The Kaede/Hanakaede movement is the series' most sustained challenge to any theory of personhood that relies upon bodily continuity alone.

One body carries:

- the pre-crisis autobiographical continuity `花楓`;
- a later post-amnesia continuity who calls herself `かえで`;
- learned abilities that can survive without remembered learning;
- social relationships formed by one continuity and inherited by another;
- trauma that remains embodied across the autobiographical break;
- documentary traces capable of speaking across the discontinuity.

The novels never provide a narrator-certified metaphysical answer such as:

> two souls occupied one body.

They do, however, make the ethical answer much clearer.

> **Kaede must be treated as a person because she forms a lived autobiography, relationships, goals, fears, habits, self-recognition, and a desired future. Hanakaede must be treated as a person because she returns as the pre-crisis autobiographical subject and must not be conscripted into completing Kaede's life.**

Neither continuity has the right to erase the other merely by claiming greater authenticity.

The complete arc therefore moves through four moral problems.

1. **Recognition:** Can Sakuta and the family recognize Kaede rather than look through her toward Hanakaede?
2. **Grief:** Can Kaede's disappearance be mourned even though the body remains alive?
3. **Inheritance:** Can Hanakaede receive the life Kaede helped create without experiencing that life as debt?
4. **Self-authorship:** Can both continuities be remembered without either becoming the other's compulsory future?

The bonuses and final volumes are essential because they show that the answer is not confined to one dramatic identity crisis. It must become ordinary life:

- school choice;
- work;
- photographs;
- zoo visits;
- family movement;
- friendships;
- university.



# 2. Family rupture before Kaede
The family crisis begins with Hanakaede's social injury.

Bullying and hostile digital communication produce:

- fear;
- withdrawal;
- bodily marks;
- institutional failure;
- maternal collapse;
- familial separation.

The school eventually treats the conflict through a punitive closure model:

> `悪者退治して終わった感じの空気`

The problem is not actually repaired. A simplified villain narrative permits the institution to stop asking what happened to the people inside it.

The Azusagawa family then reorganizes around the damage.

- Sakuta and Kaede live together.
- Their mother is unable to sustain ordinary relation.
- Their father carries contact and financial responsibilities but places daily care heavily on Sakuta.
- Hanakaede's prior social world becomes inaccessible to the post-amnesia continuity.

Kaede therefore emerges inside a family already defined by absence and redistributed roles.



# 3. Clinical language and Syndrome must remain distinct
The doctor identifies Kaede's condition as:

> `解離性障害の一種`

This is important because the series does not make every unusual experience reducible to Adolescence Syndrome.

At the same time, the bodily bruising exceeds ordinary clinical description inside the diegesis. Messages can produce literal marks:

> `メッセージが送信されてくれば、太ももに大きな痣が生まれた`

The correct model therefore preserves two layers.

## Clinical/autobiographical layer

- memory loss;
- discontinuity;
- dissociative diagnosis;
- family recognition problem.

## Syndrome/somatic layer

- social harm literally inscribed on the body;
- later stress-reactive bruising in Hanakaede;
- the body carrying trauma across discontinuous autobiography.

Neither layer should be allowed to erase the other.



# 4. Kaede is not merely a symptom
Several forms of evidence establish Kaede as a distinct autobiographical person.

## 4.1 Self-reference and orthography

Kaede says:

> `かえではかえでだから、花楓さんじゃないんです。`

The spoken names are homophones. The written text distinguishes:

- `かえで`
- `花楓`

The orthographic difference externalizes a difference the body cannot visually display.

## 4.2 Goals

Kaede creates a yearly goal list:

> `かえでの今年の目標です`

Her goals are not imposed clinical milestones. They belong to her sense of future.

## 4.3 Habits and voice

Her self-naming, polite little-sister register, panda attachment, clothing, gestures, and relationship with Sakuta form a recognizable voice.

## 4.4 Relationships

Sakuta's relation with Kaede is not identical to his prior relation with Hanakaede. Mai, Nodoka, and others also know Kaede specifically.

## 4.5 Fear of substitution

Kaede says people are looking past her toward Hanakaede:

> `みんな、花楓さんを見てるんです`

This fear only makes sense if she experiences herself as the person currently being replaced in others' attention.



# 5. Kaede's ordinary goals and the scale of recovery
Kaede's goals are deliberately small.

- go outside;
- see pandas;
- buy something;
- attend school;
- participate in ordinary public life.

The series refuses to define recovery solely as return to the previous Hanakaede biography.

Tomobe Miwako adapts the definition of school success to Kaede's pace. A brief, partial, supported presence can count.

This is one of the series' first strong institutional-care models:

> **the institution should expand the range of participation rather than make one standard the only proof of personhood.**



# 6. Sakuta as caregiver and the ethics of parentification
Sakuta's devotion is extraordinary.

He provides:

- daily structure;
- food;
- emotional regulation;
- public accompaniment;
- advocacy;
- reassurance;
- practical teaching.

He also becomes a child functioning in a parental role because the family cannot distribute care normally.

The arrangement has two truths.

## It saves Kaede's daily life

Sakuta recognizes her pace and treats her goals as real.

## It concentrates too much responsibility in Sakuta

His competence makes it easy for adults to continue relying on him. The family repeatedly assumes he can carry what he has already demonstrated he can carry.

The later series' distributed-care ethic is partly a response to this early burden concentration.



# 7. Kaede's disappearance and Hanakaede's return
The return occurs inside the same body.

Sakuta asks:

> `お前……『花楓』なのか？`

This is simultaneously:

- reunion with Hanakaede;
- loss of Kaede.

The world around Sakuta can describe the event as memory recovery.

Sakuta experiences bereavement.

That divergence is ethically central.

A body remaining alive can conceal the fact that one autobiographical relation has ended.

The series refuses to make Sakuta choose one interpretation.

Both are true.



# 8. Kaede anticipates disappearance and authors remembrance
Kaede's notebook proves that she understood enough of the risk to address the future.

Her desire is not:

> force Hanakaede to continue my exact life.

It is closer to:

> `笑いながら思い出してもらえたら`

She wants remembrance capable of containing warmth rather than only pain.

This becomes the mature standard later reached in *Animal Land*.



# 9. Hanakaede's inherited life
Hanakaede returns without ordinary first-person access to Kaede's two years.

She nevertheless inherits:

- learned school content;
- a changed body;
- Sakuta's caregiving history;
- Mai and Nodoka;
- Kotomi's return;
- a family reorganized by crisis;
- Kaede's notebook;
- Kaede's wish to attend Minegahara.

This makes inheritance ethically dangerous.

A person can receive benefits whose emotional history belongs to somebody else.

Hanakaede interprets the situation through debt:

> `全部くれた`

> `お返しできない`

The life surrounding her feels earned by Kaede and merely occupied by Hanakaede.



# 10. `もうひとりの私`: relation without settled ontology
Hanakaede calls Kaede:

> `もうひとりの私`

The phrase is analytically valuable because it preserves both:

- continuity: `私`;
- distinction: `もうひとり`.

It does not settle whether the series endorses:

- one self with discontinuous memory;
- two persons;
- two souls;
- one person and one illness-generated personality.

The ethics do not need the metaphysics fully solved.

Hanakaede must still respond to a life that was both hers bodily and not hers autobiographically.



# 11. Minegahara as inherited command
Kaede wrote:

> `お兄ちゃんと同じ高校に行きたいです。かえでの夢のひとつです`

Hanakaede's desire to attend Minegahara is genuine in part.

But it becomes entangled with repayment.

If Kaede gave her relationships and opportunity, then perhaps Hanakaede must complete Kaede's dream.

The entrance-exam collapse exposes the comparison:

> `もうひとりの私の方が、もっとがんばってた`

and fear:

> Sakuta preferred Kaede.

The problem is therefore not merely school anxiety. It is successor guilt.



# 12. Sakuta's decisive correction
Sakuta tells Hanakaede:

> `『かえで』の願いを、花楓に叶えてほしいわけじゃない`

and the narration clarifies:

> `決めるのは咲太じゃない。花楓が決めることだ`

He refuses two substitutions.

1. Hanakaede need not become executor of Kaede's unfinished life.
2. Sakuta must not use school choice to prove his own care was successful.

His most legitimate intervention is relational truth:

> `花楓が帰ってきてくれたから`

He wanted Hanakaede back.

That fact does not rank her over Kaede.

It removes the fear that Hanakaede is an unwanted replacement.



# 13. `どっちも普通だ`: refusing rank
Sakuta says:

> `どっちも普通だ`

The line does not mean both continuities are socially or clinically ordinary.

It means neither needs exceptional justification to count as his sister.

The word `普通` is repurposed from social norm into refusal of hierarchy.



# 14. Self-authored school choice
Hanakaede is accepted to Minegahara.

That plot choice is essential because it removes necessity from her later decision.

She says:

> `峰ヶ原高校にはいかない。行きたい学校は、自分で見つける`

Correspondence school is therefore not a consolation imposed after failure.

It becomes a positive choice.

The volume's educational thesis is:

> **participation should fit the person rather than forcing the person to prove worth by fitting one institutional form.**



# 15. `Animal Land`: remembrance after differentiation
The zoo bonus performs the next ethical step.

Volume 8 establishes that Hanakaede does not need to become Kaede.

Only then can she safely ask:

> `もうひとりの私のこと、もっと教えてほしい`

and clarify:

> `少しずつ話してほしい`

The past becomes biography rather than command.

## 15.1 The annual pass

The pass bears Kaede's name and crosses the autobiographical discontinuity.

Hanakaede uses it without being required to feel exactly what Kaede felt.

## 15.2 The zoo

The same place carries:

- Kaede's last major outing;
- Sakuta's grief;
- Hanakaede's new experience.

Place holds both histories without making one replace the other.

## 15.3 The photograph

Nodoka's candid image proves:

> `『かえで』がそこにいた`

The photograph does not explain Kaede. It preserves presence.

## 15.4 Distributed biography

Sakuta refuses to become the sole narrator of Kaede. Mai and Nodoka also tell stories.

Kaede's memory becomes polyphonic rather than owned by the primary caregiver.



# 16. `Spring Days`: self-authorship becomes work, money, and limits
Hanakaede's part-time job tests whether autonomy can remain supported.

She asks for:

- a familiar workplace;
- overlapping shifts with Sakuta;
- a bounded initial period.

These accommodations do not make the achievement fake.

They allow skill to develop.

## 16.1 The interview

The manager asks Hanakaede.

Sakuta realizes:

> `店長は咲太に質問をしてきたわけではない`

and the scene centers:

> `花楓本人に`

Sakuta's restraint is part of her autonomy.

## 16.2 The old school uniform

Hanakaede notices a customer wearing the old uniform.

The trigger remains meaningful.

It no longer controls the encounter.

## 16.3 The right to stop

After succeeding, she says:

> `でも、ずっとって思うと、今はまだ自信ない`

Sakuta does not countermand her.

A successful trial does not create permanent obligation.

## 16.4 Wages and gratitude

Hanakaede earns money and chooses gifts.

Care becomes reciprocity rather than existential debt.



# 17. Two homes and family reconstruction
Hanakaede later moves between:

- Sakuta's Fujisawa home;
- the parents' Yokohama home.

The repaired family does not require all members to return to one untouched household form.

This is consistent with Volume 9's larger thesis:

> family is remade through recognition and chosen relation, not restored by pretending the rupture never occurred.



# 18. Parents: failure, care, and distributed responsibility
## 18.1 Mother

The mother is neither monster nor merely misunderstood victim.

She collapses under Hanakaede's suffering and later cannot adequately recognize Sakuta.

Her diary records guilt and a family fantasy that initially excludes him.

Volume 9 permits new relation without declaring the damage unreal.

## 18.2 Father

The father preserves records, researches school options, supports finances, and mediates contact.

He also repeatedly relies upon Sakuta.

The family becomes healthier as responsibility becomes more distributed.



# 19. Kotomi: recovered friendship without recovered old self
Kotomi's return is important because she does not demand that Hanakaede become the girl she knew before the crisis.

Their relationship develops through:

- email;
- ordinary outings;
- university planning;
- friendship independent of Sakuta.

Kotomi becomes a scaffold for Hanakaede's future rather than a witness who insists upon restoring the past.



# 20. R1: the impossible mercy of simultaneous embodiment
R1 physically realizes both continuities.

Kaede:

- grows older;
- attends Minegahara;
- joins the animal club;
- cooks;
- has friends;
- wants to remain with Sakuta.

Hanakaede:

- exists separately at the parents' home.

This is the most seductive possible resolution of the entire sister arc:

> nobody must be lost.

It is also the strongest evidence that R1 is not a simple ordinary branch. Mutually exclusive autobiographical continuities become separately embodied persons.



# 21. R1 Kaede must not be treated as Sakuta's reward
The emotional temptation is obvious.

Sakuta has already mourned Kaede.

R1 gives him the ordinary future she never received.

If he preserves R1 merely because he wants her, he repeats the very error Volume 8 rejected:

> using another person's life to complete an unfinished wish.

R1 Kaede must therefore receive standing of her own.



# 22. The diary reverses direction
Kaede finds Hanakaede's later diary.

Earlier, Hanakaede inherited Kaede's record.

Now Kaede receives evidence of Hanakaede's future.

The archive becomes bidirectional.

It tells Kaede:

- the dissociative condition eventually resolves;
- Hanakaede returns;
- another continuing life exists beyond the present R1 state.

The diary informs.

It does not decide.



# 23. `帰りたい`: R1 Kaede's agency
Kaede says:

> `かえでは帰りたいです`

and:

> `かえでは、かえでの本当のお兄ちゃんのところに帰りたいです`

The narration explicitly acknowledges:

> `自分が消えることへの怯え`

Her fear does not invalidate her decision.

The text says her will remains stable and that she has chosen to move forward.

The language should not be flattened into ordinary suicide terminology.

Kaede understands the act as return.

The exact metaphysical fate remains unresolved.

Ethically, her stated direction prevents Sakuta from deciding solely from grief.



# 24. RF: Hanakaede's ordinary future
Three years later, Hanakaede:

- attends university;
- remains close to Kotomi;
- participates in family life;
- is not required to reproduce Kaede.

This is the final validation of Volume 8.

Kaede remains:

- remembered;
- loved;
- historically real.

Hanakaede remains:

- current;
- self-authoring;
- not a replacement required to justify her return.



# 25. Body, memory, identity, and substitution
The sister arc produces several durable rules.

## Rule 1 — bodily continuity is insufficient

The same body can sustain discontinuous autobiographical persons.

## Rule 2 — autobiographical memory is not the only continuity

Learned knowledge and embodied trauma can survive absent episodic recall.

## Rule 3 — relationships create evidence but not ownership

Sakuta, Mai, Nodoka, and others can recognize Kaede. They do not thereby own Hanakaede's future.

## Rule 4 — documentary archives preserve access

Notebook, photographs, zoo pass, diary, and place keep relation available when memory changes.

## Rule 5 — current choice matters

Hanakaede's school and work choices matter.

R1 Kaede's `帰りたい` matters.

## Rule 6 — remembrance is not substitution

The healthiest final structure permits:

> Kaede remembered;
>
> Hanakaede living.



# 26. Identity-continuity matrix
| Axis | Kaede | Hanakaede | Why the distinction matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| body | same R0 body at different autobiographical periods | same R0 body after memory return | body cannot settle personhood alone |
| episodic memory | post-amnesia life | pre-amnesia life, little/no first-person memory of Kaede period | autobiography is discontinuous |
| learned knowledge | acquired during Kaede period | partly retained after return | learning can survive loss of remembered learning |
| speech/self-reference | self-naming `かえで`, marked register | ordinary `私` | voice contributes to specific recognition |
| relationships | lived current relation with Sakuta, Mai, ensemble | inherits relations without remembering formation | social continuity can outlast autobiographical ownership |
| goals | public exposure, school, zoo, ordinary life | correspondence school, work, university | each continuity authors a different future |
| archive | writes notebook | reads notebook and later asks for biography | archive can preserve and pressure |
| R1 | separately embodied older Kaede | separately embodied at parents' home | rewrite gives both current standing |

## 26.1 Family-member perspectives

Sakuta recognizes Kaede strongly enough to grieve her and later must avoid turning that love into control over Hanakaede or R1 Kaede.

The father loves both children but delegates too much caregiving to Sakuta; later he becomes more active and available.

The mother is part of the family's trauma and later repair. Hanakaede's movement toward her matters because it is not imposed restoration.

Mai becomes family-adjacent caregiver without replacing the parents.

Kotomi gives Hanakaede peer friendship not permanently mediated by Sakuta.

The family rebuilds through several partial relationships rather than one repaired center.

## 26.2 Objects and places as continuity supports

- **Notebook:** preserves Kaede's voice and later threatens to become command.
- **Ueno Zoo:** allows Kaede memory and Hanakaede present experience to coexist.
- **Annual pass:** carries Kaede's name without dictating Hanakaede's feeling.
- **Photograph:** proves presence but cannot explain the person.
- **Fujisawa home:** shifts from protective enclosure to a place Hanakaede can leave and return to.
- **Parents' home:** represents reconstruction rather than return to one pre-trauma arrangement.
- **Workplace/bankbook:** make Hanakaede's participation and exchange materially bounded.

These form a distributed memory system. None is a complete person.

## 26.3 Supported autonomy versus isolation

Hanakaede's achievements are scaffolded by Miwako, Sakuta, Mai, Nodoka, Uzuki, coworkers, and family. Support does not make the choice false.

Autonomy is shown through:

- disclosure of need;
- selection among options;
- capacity to stop;
- ability to revise the future.

## 26.4 Recovery as range

Recovery here means expanded range:

- more places one can enter;
- more people one can address;
- more institutions one can choose among;
- more capacity to say no;
- more future one can imagine.

It does not require no fear, one normal school, or one correct identity.



# 27. Strongest counterreadings
## 27.1 Kaede is only a dissociative symptom

The clinical diagnosis supports describing the condition in dissociative terms.

But the ethical and narrative evidence gives Kaede:

- self-recognition;
- goals;
- relationships;
- fear;
- desired remembrance;
- current agency in R1.

Calling her “only a symptom” cannot account for the series' grief structure.

## 27.2 Kaede is metaphysically a second soul

The text never certifies this.

The ethical distinction between persons does not require a soul theory.

## 27.3 Hanakaede owes Kaede completion of her dreams

Volume 8 explicitly rejects this.

Gratitude does not create total jurisdiction.

## 27.4 Correspondence school is a retreat from normal life

Hanakaede chooses it after Minegahara becomes available.

The later bonus demonstrates work, friendship, public movement, and university aspiration.

The path expands life rather than narrows it.

## 27.5 R1 solves the sister problem perfectly

R1 grants both lives, but through a world rewrite affecting many other biographies.

The local mercy cannot settle the global ethics.

## 27.6 Kaede's consent removes all concern

Her agency makes the transition far more defensible.

It does not solve the metaphysical question of what happens to her current experiential continuity.



# 28. Identity-axis matrix
The Kaede/Hanakaede case should be evaluated across separate identity dimensions rather than through one “real self” test.

| Axis | Kaede | Hanakaede | Analytical consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| body in R0 | same body | same body | body alone cannot decide personhood |
| autobiographical memory | post-amnesia history | pre-crisis history restored | each continuity possesses a first-person life inaccessible to the other |
| learned knowledge | acquires school knowledge/skills | retains some learned content without episodic memory | procedural/semantic continuity can cross autobiographical break |
| self-reference | `かえで` | `私` / `花楓` | language helps mark continuity but is not metaphysical proof |
| relationships | Sakuta, Mai, Nodoka, Kotomi, others through Kaede period | inherits many relations and must renegotiate them | relationships preserve evidence without transferring ownership automatically |
| goals | panda/zoo, public exposure, school, brother-linked future | self-chosen school form, work, university | inherited wishes require consent before becoming current plans |
| documentary archive | notebook, annual pass, photograph | reads/receives the archive | records create access across discontinuity |
| trauma/body response | formed inside bullying aftermath | embodied triggers remain after memory return | the body remembers more than autobiography narrates |
| current choice | anticipates disappearance and authors remembrance | chooses correspondence school/work/university | current agency matters even where continuity is unstable |
| R1 embodiment | separately embodied older Kaede | separately embodied Hanakaede | R1 shows the ethical problem cannot be dismissed as one-body confusion |

The matrix supports a plural conclusion:

> **Kaede and Hanakaede are neither simple duplicates nor ordinary phases of one smoothly remembered self. The novels leave exact metaphysical number unresolved while making both continuities ethically non-disposable.**



# 29. Clinical explanation, supernatural residue, and the danger of category collapse
Volume 5 explicitly introduces `解離性障害` and explains loss of identity among sensation, consciousness, and memory.

That clinical frame must remain visible.

At the same time, the series gives literal phenomena not exhausted by the diagnosis:

- wounds appearing under social attack;
- injuries disappearing under changed recognition/emotion;
- R1 simultaneous separate embodiment;
- cross-world archive effects.

Two reductions should therefore be avoided.

## Reduction one: “It is all Adolescence Syndrome”

This erases the novel's explicit clinical and family-social treatment of dissociation.

## Reduction two: “It is only a realistic dissociative disorder”

This erases literal impossible events narrated as intersubjectively real.

The controlled formulation is:

> **Clinical dissociation organizes the autobiographical discontinuity; Adolescence Syndrome externalizes and later reconfigures aspects of embodiment, recognition, and world state around it.**



# 30. Family responsibility matrix
| Person | What they provide | What they fail to provide | Later development |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sakuta | daily care, safety, recognition, accompaniment, archive continuity | cannot replace parents/institutions without cost | becomes option-builder, asks for help, accepts son role |
| Father | financial support, information, later research/participation | delegates too much to Sakuta; family remains split | supports consultation and university costs; family contact increases |
| Mother | attachment and grief; later renewed recognition | breakdown and inability to remain with children during crisis | Volume 9 permits a new relation rather than restoration of untouched motherhood |
| Mai | independent care, practical support, emotional recognition | cannot decide Hanakaede's future or replace origin family | becomes stable family-adjacent adult and partner to Sakuta |
| Miwako | educational realism, institutional options, nonabandonment | cannot solve family trauma | models bounded adult care |
| Kotomi | returns friendship/history | cannot restore Hanakaede's missing memory | becomes ordinary continuing friend |

The family arc is therefore not:

> absent parents → heroic brother proves family unnecessary.

It is:

> **family systems fail; a teenager compensates; the compensation saves someone and damages the distribution of care; later adulthood requires responsibility to be redistributed without denying the brother's achievement.**



# 31. Parentification and Sakuta's hidden needs
`かえでのこと、頼むぞ` condenses the family problem.

The father's statement is understandable. Kaede trusts Sakuta most. Sakuta knows her routine best. The family is in crisis.

It is also an extraordinary burden to place on a teenage son.

Sakuta's competence then creates a feedback loop:

1. he performs the care;
2. the care works enough to stabilize daily life;
3. adults rely upon his competence;
4. his own need becomes less visible;
5. he interprets needing help as avoidable burden.

Volume 9 breaks the loop by making Sakuta the person who requires recognition. The family cannot mature if he remains only:

- caretaker;
- messenger;
- problem solver.

He must become:

- son;
- dependent person;
- future university student asking for financial support.



# 32. Archive map: how the two continuities speak without sharing memory
## Notebook

Kaede's own voice, goals, fear, and desired remembrance.

## Annual zoo pass

A future-oriented object that survives the continuity for which it was purchased.

## Photograph

Evidence that Kaede occupied social space even when she disliked camera attention.

## Other people's memories

Sakuta, Mai, Nodoka, Kotomi, places, and routines each preserve different parts of the biography.

## Hanakaede's later diary in R1

The archive reverses direction. R1 Kaede learns about Hanakaede's future and uses the information in making her own decision.

The archive system demonstrates:

> **No one observer owns the complete person. Biography is socially distributed.**

That distribution is protective because memory failure in one location does not annihilate every trace.

# 38. Final personhood thesis
The Kaede/Hanakaede arc rejects two symmetrical violences.

### Violence one

Treat Kaede as unreal because Hanakaede came first.

### Violence two

Treat Hanakaede as morally obligated to become Kaede because Kaede earned the current life.

The completed series offers a more demanding relation.

> **Recognize Kaede as a person without making Hanakaede her replacement. Recognize Hanakaede as current without making Kaede a mistake. Let inheritance enlarge choice rather than dictate it. Let memory preserve relation without conscripting the living.**

This is one of AoButa's most complete personhood arguments because it survives every scale:

- one body;
- two autobiographies;
- family rupture;
- school;
- work;
- objects;
- grief;
- possible worlds;
- final ordinary life.
