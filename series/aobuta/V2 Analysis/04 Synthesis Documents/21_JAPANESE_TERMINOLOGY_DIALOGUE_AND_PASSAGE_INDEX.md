# 21. Japanese Terminology, Dialogue, and Passage Index
## Phase-8 verified controlled version

## 1. Function and scope

This is the **Phase-8 source-verified controlled version** of the exact-language index for the full-series synthesis.

It remains **intentionally non-exhaustive**: its purpose is to control the Japanese passages and lexical targets that carry disproportionate interpretive weight, not to reproduce a full concordance of the novels.

Its immediate purpose is to lock down the Japanese phrases most likely to carry disproportionate interpretive weight in Documents 01–18:

- `空気`;
- recognition and observation vocabulary;
- the difference between seeing and believing;
- selfhood and ordinary/normal language;
- Kaede/Hanakaede naming and return;
- friendship and family;
- reciprocal protection;
- mutual happiness and `同じ景色`;
- time, possibility, and reality rewriting;
- grief, meeting, returning, forgetting, and memory;
- the final Toko song.

Each entry records:

1. source;
2. controlled evidence ID;
3. primary-source locator;
4. speaker or narrator where securely recoverable;
5. a short Japanese retrieval excerpt;
6. a working translation;
7. an ambiguity/usage note;
8. the future specialist document(s) most likely to depend on it.

The translations are deliberately **working analytical translations**, not claims about an official English edition.

## 1.1 Phase-8 verification status

Phase 8 rechecked all **90 controlled entries** directly against the original Japanese EPUB corpus. Because two entries contain two independently quoted source phrases, this corresponds to **92/92 individual Japanese excerpt phrases** verified both at source level and against the stated XHTML locator. No controlled passage remains unresolved at the exact-language level.

### Hashtag orthography

The source prose normally renders the recurring hashtag as `＃夢見る` with a **full-width hash**. The synthesis corpus commonly uses ASCII `#夢見る` as a normalized analytical/search label except when reproducing an exact source excerpt. This is an **orthographic normalization only**; it is not a textual variant, semantic distinction, or separate hashtag.

## 2. Control rules

### 2.1 Quote economy

The Japanese excerpt is kept short.

A specialist document that needs broader context should follow the evidence route back to the local volume artifact and then reopen the exact XHTML passage.

### 2.2 Speaker discipline

Speaker labels are given only where the local analysis and source context make attribution secure.

`Narration` means the first-person or third-person narrative voice rather than an externally omniscient authorial explanation.

### 2.3 Mechanical vocabulary discipline

Terms such as:

- `観測`;
- `認識`;
- `可能性`;
- `書き換える`;

must not be translated in a way that silently upgrades character theories into proven physics.

### 2.4 Identity naming

The project convention:

- **Kaede** = post-amnesia `かえで`;
- **Hanakaede** = restored `花楓`;

exists solely to keep two same-pronunciation continuities analytically distinct in English.

It is not a claim that the spoken Japanese contains two different names.

## 4. Recognition and sociality: controlled lexical conclusions

### `空気`

The index deliberately preserves both of the series' key uses:

- V1: `もう、空気なんて読んでやるか`
- V10: `今こそ空気を読めよ、づっきー`

This prevents an overly simple conclusion that AoButa is “against reading the room.”

The final distinction is closer to:

> **social awareness is valuable; anonymous social jurisdiction is dangerous.**

Sakuta's adult research question—

> `みんなってなんだろうね`

—then turns the ambient aggregate itself into an object of inquiry.

### `認識` and `観測`

The two terms overlap in final-arc discussions but should not be collapsed.

`認識` is used for:

- recognizing;
- apprehending;
- retaining a cognitive identification of a person/state.

`観測` is the more explicitly model-building term used in possibility/observer explanations.

The latter carries scientific resonance.

It does not eliminate the V2 requirement to label the relevant statements as character theory where appropriate.

### `見える` and `信じる`

The final Ebina line is the cleanest lexical endpoint:

> `僕には見えない。でも、君を信じるよ`

The structure is adversative:

> I cannot see it **but** I believe you.

The mature recognition ethic therefore cannot be translated as:

> “Sakuta sees invisible things better than everyone else.”

His final competence survives loss of that privilege.

## 5. Selfhood and personhood: controlled lexical conclusions

### `普通`

`普通` is heavily context-sensitive across AoButa.

In the V8 Kaede/Hanakaede conversation:

> `どっちも普通だ`

does not mean:

> both conform to the social average.

It means Sakuta refuses a hierarchy in which one sister is the normative “real” one and the other is deviation.

Later specialist documents should therefore translate `普通` locally rather than assigning the series one fixed technical meaning.

### `何者か`

Nene's:

> `何者かであるって思いたいの！`

is stronger than merely:

> “I want to know who I am.”

It concerns social and autobiographical **consequence**:

> being somebody;
> being a person whose life has a legible shape.

That is why the final vocational answer—announcer work acquired through ordinary labor—is thematically stronger than merely recovering her legal name.

### `帰る`

Kaede's:

> `かえでは帰りたいです`

must remain close to her own language.

The synthesis can analyze the metaphysical danger of the R1 collapse.

It should not erase Kaede's first-person framing by translating the scene entirely into an externally imposed death metaphor.

### `思い出に変える`

This is the final-series lexical hinge.

`思い出` is not simply stored data.

It is lived recollection.

`変える` means the relation changes form.

The line therefore resists both:

- permanent supernatural actualization;
- retrospective declaration that the experience never happened.

## 6. Care and relationship: controlled lexical conclusions

### `大丈夫`

AoButa repeatedly uses `大丈夫` in ways that exceed literal “fine.”

Shoko's Volume-14:

> `わたしが来たからもう大丈夫ですよ`

does not assert that the rewritten world has been solved.

It asserts:

> Sakuta is no longer alone inside it.

Future document 15 should therefore resist translating every `大丈夫` identically.

### `友達`

Miori's arc makes an ordinary noun structurally decisive.

The change is not from:

> stranger → lover.

It is from:

> endlessly provisional acquaintance → friend who can be expected to remain reachable across future absence.

### `家族`

The V9 narration:

> `この日、咲太たちは家族になった`

is translation-sensitive because the absence of “again” matters.

The family is not portrayed as an untouched original unit recovered after temporary illness.

It is made again under changed conditions.

### `守る`

The V13 pair should remain adjacent in later quotation:

> `麻衣さんのことは僕が守るから`

followed by:

> `じゃあ、咲太のことは私が守ってあげる`

Quoting only Sakuta's line would distort the final romance ethic by reproducing the hierarchy the reply immediately contests.

### `同じ景色`

Mai's language is spatial and perceptual.

She does not ask:

> become the same person as me.

She asks to inhabit:

> the same scenery.

This is one of the strongest Japanese formulations for the mature romance's combination of shared reality and preserved difference.

## 7. Time, dreams, and world vocabulary

### `夢`

`夢` remains deliberately polyvalent across the series:

- ordinary dream;
- desired aspiration;
- anomalous future information;
- interface with possible states.

A passage index should therefore never translate a later `夢` mechanically from one earlier case.

### `可能性の世界`

Rio's V9 formulation and counterpart Sakuta's later language make “possible world” the best controlled English label for many cases.

It still does not prove that every dream, loop, future, or rewrite belongs to the same ontology.

### `書き換える`

`現実を書き換える` is stronger than “the future changes.”

The image is revision of an already legible state.

This is why the corpus reserves `R1` as a **rewritten/composite state** rather than casually calling it another timeline.

### `現実`

Uzuki's:

> `今度は、現実にしよう`

contains the final positive use of reality.

Reality is not the boring alternative to imagination.

It is where a desired possibility becomes meaningful through action.

## 8. Grief and remembrance

### `会いたい`

Miori's:

> `透子に会いたい`

should remain relational.

She wants:

> Toko.

Not merely:

> a metaphysical branch condition labelled Toko-alive.

This distinction becomes crucial when the finale turns resurrection-like desire back into grief.

### `返して`

`透子を返して` is allowed to remain impossible.

Its importance comes precisely from no longer functioning as a solvable mechanical instruction.

### `忘れる`

Volume 11's `忘れるとはそういうこと` provides an important companion to Volume 15's memory solution.

Forgetting in that context means that an injury loses total jurisdiction over the present.

It does not require deletion of the event.

### `思い出`

Kaede, Miori, and final Sakuta all use memory differently.

The common full-series movement is:

> the absent person or past experience remains particular without requiring present reality to reproduce the lost state.

## 9. Final-song pair

These two lines should normally be indexed together:

> `君に出会わなければよかった`

> `君に出会えてよかった`

The final volume does not erase the first line.

It changes the interpretive architecture by placing regret and gratitude inside one completed song.

A synthesis that quotes only the ending gratitude would sentimentalize Miori/Toko.

A synthesis that quotes only the earlier regret would freeze the Volume-14 misreading.

The correct full-series treatment preserves both.

## 10. Title-language seed

The recurring title construction:

> `青春ブタ野郎は〜の夢を見ない`

should provisionally be rendered close to:

> **Rascal Does Not Dream of ~**

but `夢` should remain semantically open.

Across the series it can resonate with:

- literal sleep dreams;
- wishes;
- projected identities;
- future states;
- possible worlds;
- title-heroines as objects of Sakuta's encounter.

Document 15 should eventually examine whether the formula increasingly shifts from a character-specific title label into a statement about what Sakuta will or will not permit to remain only a dream.

This Phase-2 index does not yet treat every title individually.

## 12. Controlled-version limitations and Phase-4 use

This file is intentionally incomplete in three ways.

First, it is **selective**, not exhaustive. It indexes phrases that are already known to carry specialist-level interpretive weight.

Second, it prioritizes exact locators over broad lexical frequency counts. A later language document may add concordance-like statistics, but those statistics should never replace contextual reading.

Third, some terms—especially `観測`, `可能性`, and final world language—occur inside character theories. Exact Japanese wording does not convert epistemic status from `CB/SI` into `TF`.

During Phase 4 and later, any specialist document making a translation-sensitive claim should:

1. cite the relevant `JP-xxx` entry;
2. follow it to the volume evidence ID;
3. reopen the primary Japanese passage if the surrounding sentence structure matters;
4. add a new controlled entry here if the phrase becomes load-bearing.

The index should therefore grow **because the synthesis needs evidence**, not because every repeated word must be catalogued.
