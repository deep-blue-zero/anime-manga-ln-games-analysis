# 10. Memory, Body, Name, Role, and Personhood
## What must remain continuous for someone to remain the same person?


# 1. Governing question
> **When body, autobiographical memory, social name, public role, relationship, and historical record diverge, what does AoButa treat as the relevant unit of personhood?**

The completed-series answer is deliberately plural.

> **AoButa treats personhood as a historically and relationally continuous subject whose identity is carried through several partially independent axes. Body, memory, name, role, documentary record, relationship, self-identification, and current choice all matter. None alone has the right to erase the others.**

The series never provides one necessary-and-sufficient definition.

Instead, it repeatedly creates cases in which one proposed criterion fails.

- If body alone settles identity, Kaede/Hanakaede and Mai/Nodoka become unintelligible.
- If autobiographical memory alone settles identity, learned continuity and bodily history are lost.
- If social recognition settles identity, the wrong Ikumi or Toko role can replace the person.
- If name settles identity, Nene can call herself Toko while Nene's biography disappears.
- If public role settles identity, the composite artist Toko becomes one simple person when the text shows distributed authorship.
- If current preference alone settles identity, world rewrites can overwrite histories without consent from the continuities being replaced.

The series' mature ethic is therefore anti-reductionist.


# 2. Identity dimensions
The following axes should remain separately tracked.

| Axis | Governing question |
|---|---|
| Body | Which physical organism or appearance continues? |
| Autobiographical memory | Which lived history can the subject narrate as “mine”? |
| Learned/procedural continuity | What knowledge or skill survives even when episodic memory does not? |
| Name | What linguistic identity does self and society use? |
| Public role | What function or prestige identity does society recognize? |
| Documentary continuity | What records, objects, photographs, or writing preserve history? |
| Relational continuity | Who remembers and recognizes the person through shared history? |
| Self-identification | What does the subject currently claim to be? |
| Current choice | What future does the subject now authorize? |

No case preserves every axis equally.


# 3. Mai — presence without social personhood
Mai's body is initially present.

Her public name is famous.

Her career record exists.

Yet the school fails to recognize the current person.

This immediately disproves:

> body + fame + name = secure personhood.

Sakuta's recognition matters because it is directed toward the current subject, not the celebrity image.

The later Mai/Toko rewrite repeats the same problem in reverse. Society does not fail to recognize Mai. It recognizes her too confidently as the wrong identity.

The complete Mai arc therefore establishes:

> **Personhood can be threatened by both absence of recognition and excess misrecognition.**


# 4. Tomoe — constructed identity without inauthenticity
Tomoe deliberately changes presentation after moving.

The false essentialist model says:

> the pre-move self is authentic; the made Tokyo self is performance.

The volume rejects that hierarchy.

Tomoe likes the current self.

This gives the personhood document one of its foundational rules:

> **A self may be socially and deliberately constructed and still be genuinely owned.**

The danger is not construction.

The danger is loss of the ability to choose or revise the construction.


# 5. Rio — one autobiography, two bodies
Rio's duplication is the cleanest rejection of body singularity.

Two bodies exist.

Both share prior history.

Each carries one side of a contradiction.

If personhood were identical to one body, the narrative would require two independent Rios after the split.

Instead, the resolution treats the bodies as expressions of one autobiographical conflict.

The case supports:

> **Embodiment can multiply without autobiographical origin becoming irrelevant.**

It also warns against the opposite reduction:

> memory alone does not make the embodied experiences inconsequential.

Both bodies act, suffer, and relate.


# 6. Mai/Nodoka — public body and person come apart
The crossed embodiment case distinguishes:

- visible design;
- public role;
- autobiographical person;
- relational recognition.

The public follows Mai's body.

Sakuta identifies the person through voice and history.

The sisters' identities are not located wholly in:

- appearance;
- performance;
- desire to be the other.

Their resolution requires differentiation:

> one sister's value cannot depend upon becoming the other.


# 7. Kaede/Hanakaede — the hardest body-memory split
The same body carries two discontinuous autobiographical lives.

## 7.1 Body continuity

The organism continues.

## 7.2 Autobiographical discontinuity

`かえで` and `花楓` differ in:

- memory;
- self-reference;
- speech;
- habits;
- relationships;
- future goals.

## 7.3 Learned continuity

Hanakaede retains some learned content from the Kaede period despite lacking episodic memory.

This proves that memory itself is internally plural.

## 7.4 Relational continuity

Sakuta, Mai, Nodoka, Kotomi, and others retain Kaede biography after Hanakaede returns.

## 7.5 Documentary continuity

Notebook, annual pass, photographs, zoo, and testimony preserve Kaede.

## 7.6 Ethical conclusion

Kaede is not reducible to:

> false temporary personality.

Hanakaede is not required to:

> continue Kaede's script.

The mature formula is:

> **remember the discontinued person without conscripting the current person into replacement.**


# 8. `もうひとりの私`
Hanakaede's phrase:

> `もうひとりの私`

is structurally exact.

`もうひとり` treats Kaede as another countable person.

`私` retains bodily/historical relation.

The phrase refuses a clean metaphysical answer.

Translation should preserve both distance and continuity.


# 9. Shoko — future selves and bodily donation
Shoko's case separates several continuities.

- young/current Shoko;
- older future Shoko;
- future states with different donors;
- current body carrying Toko's heart.

The donor organ does not transfer personhood.

Toko's heart beats in Shoko.

Shoko remains Shoko.

This rejects `CLM-012`.

Likewise, future Shoko is not merely current Shoko aged in one fixed destiny. Multiple future continuities possess different histories.

The strongest claim is:

> **Possible-world autobiographical persons can be morally and relationally real without becoming one simple continuous body-line.**


# 10. Sakuta in W1 — a better life is still another person's life
Volume 9 gives Sakuta access to a family world that appears in some respects more desirable.

He refuses to treat another Sakuta's biography as property.

This establishes that personhood includes:

- authored commitments;
- relationships;
- history;
- responsibility.

The same external body/name does not make two world-specific lives interchangeable.


# 11. Ikumi — functional replacement is not personal continuity
The counterpart Ikumi can perform the current Ikumi role.

The university accepts her.

Friends accept her.

Volunteer work continues.

The substitution is socially successful.

The person experiences that success as erasure:

> `代わりの私でもいいなら、私ってなんなんだろう。`

This is one of the series' strongest anti-functionalist claims.

> **A person is not exhausted by the role she can successfully perform.**

Sakuta's `赤城、見っけ` restores recognition of particular traits and biography rather than merely confirming the role label.


# 12. Nene/Toko — name and prestige displace biography
Nene's case separates:

- legal/personal name;
- public prestige identity;
- voice;
- relationship;
- career aspiration;
- material archive;
- self-recognition.

As Toko becomes dominant:

- `岩見沢寧々` becomes inaccessible;
- Takumi forgets her;
- she discards the trophy carrying her name;
- society cannot perceive her body;
- she no longer recognizes her own name.

Recovery requires more than changing the label.

It requires:

- trophy;
- Takumi's memory;
- Nene's first-person confession;
- future vocational possibility;
- recognition by others.

The role Toko is socially valuable.

Nene becomes visible again by becoming more biographically specific, including failure.


# 13. Human Toko, Miori, and the public artist
The final series separates three entities.

## 13.1 Human Kirishima Toko

- deceased girl;
- composer/lyricist;
- Miori's friend;
- Shoko's donor.

## 13.2 Mito Miori

- performer;
- uploader;
- surviving friend;
- voice heard by the public.

## 13.3 Public `霧島透子`

- Toko's name;
- Toko's compositions;
- Miori's performance;
- public recognition.

The public artist is composite.

Calling the artist simply “Miori” erases Toko's authorship.

Calling the artist simply “the deceased Toko” erases Miori's voice and action.

This is one of the series' most sophisticated identity cases because composite authorship need not mean counterfeit identity.


# 14. R1 Mai/Toko — additive identity rewrite
Mai's Toko state differs from Nene's.

Mai retains:

- relationship with Sakuta;
- career behavior;
- ordinary personality;
- many memories.

The proposition:

> `私は霧島透子`

is added as sincerely experienced fact.

The phenomenon therefore shows identity can be partially rewritten without total biography replacement.

Mai's later:

> `私は霧島透子じゃない`

restores first-person authority after public reattribution.


# 15. R1 Kaede/Hanakaede — mutually exclusive continuities become separate persons
R1 is the hardest challenge to simple continuity theory.

Kaede and Hanakaede both exist physically in separate locations.

This suggests:

- R1 is not a simple continuation of one body;
- possibility realization can produce separate embodiments for formerly exclusive autobiographies.

The ethical consequence is decisive:

> R1 Kaede must be treated as a current person with standing, not merely a remembered wish.

Her final `帰りたい` matters because it is her choice.

Residual ambiguity remains about her subjective fate after RF stabilization.


# 16. Miori across worlds
Counterpart Sakuta proposes that Miori is one experiential person across multiple worlds.

This would explain:

- memory of many Sakutas;
- continuity across serial rewrites;
- observer-specific visibility;
- cross-world travel.

The model remains CB/SI.

If correct, Miori is the series' most radical challenge to world-specific personhood: one subject associated with many locally different realities.


# 17. The red-randoseru girl
The girl resembles young Mai.

The series never confirms:

- literal child Mai;
- daughter;
- counterpart;
- ghost;
- independent person.

Sakuta's mature response is important precisely because he does not force substitution.

He attends to her as the person/phenomenon present.

Her ontology remains UA.


# 18. The final Miori lookalike
At the station, Sakuta briefly thinks a stranger resembles Miori.

He checks:

- characteristic mole;
- presence;
- reciprocal recognition.

She is not Miori.

The scene provides the final anti-substitution test:

> **Resemblance is evidence, not identity.**

Sakuta turns to the actual artifact Miori left—the complete song—rather than making the stranger into a replacement.

# 20. Proposed final personhood model
The series supports a **relational-historical plural model**.

A person is not one substance identified by one test.

Personhood is most securely recognized where several axes converge:

1. first-person autobiographical continuity;
2. embodied experience;
3. self-identification;
4. shared relational history;
5. current agency;
6. material/documentary traces;
7. recognition by specific others.

When the axes diverge, the ethical response is not to choose the easiest one and erase the rest.

It is to ask:

- Which history is speaking?
- Who bears the consequences?
- Who remembers?
- What does the current subject choose?
- Which relation is being preserved or overwritten?


# 21. Current choice as a decisive late-series criterion
The finale increasingly gives weight to current choice.

- Hanakaede chooses school.
- Sara chooses not to know.
- Nene chooses announcer work.
- Miori chooses friendship and departure.
- Kaede chooses `帰りたい`.
- Sakuta chooses memory rather than denial.
- Mai chooses shared scenery.

Current choice does not erase prior history.

It prevents prior history from becoming total jurisdiction over the future.


# 22. Material archives and identity
Objects repeatedly preserve a person when memory or social recognition fails.

- Kaede notebook;
- annual zoo pass;
- photographs;
- Nene trophy;
- Toko/Miori diary;
- donor card;
- complete song;
- ring.

The objects are not usually magical identity containers.

They provide:

- retrieval cues;
- evidence;
- permission for biography to return;
- continuity outside unstable minds.

The series therefore treats personhood as materially distributed without reducing it to the object.

# 24. Current choice: decisive but not absolute
Late AoButa increasingly gives current choice strong normative weight.

- Hanakaede chooses correspondence school after being admitted to Minegahara.
- Sara refuses complete access to Mai's interiority.
- Nene chooses an announcer path after rejecting Toko substitution.
- R1 Kaede chooses `帰りたい` despite fear.
- Miori chooses friendship, a smartphone, and cross-world departure.
- Sakuta chooses to turn Syndrome into memory.

This does not mean any present statement automatically settles identity.

Current self-identification can itself be altered by:

- memory loss;
- public recognition;
- world rewrite;
- coercive role.

Mai sincerely says she is Toko inside R1. That sincerity deserves respect as a present state but is not treated as sufficient reason to preserve the rewrite indefinitely.

The mature rule is:

> **Current choice has special standing when the person has access to relevant history, meaningful alternatives, and enough freedom to answer as a particular subject rather than as an imposed role.**


# 25. Organ donation and the refusal of identity transfer
The donor architecture supplies a direct bodily challenge to personhood.

- Future Sakuta's heart can support future Shoko.
- Future Mai's heart can support another Shoko state.
- Toko's heart supports current Shoko.

The novels never treat the recipient as becoming the donor.

The organ transfers:

- biological function;
- causal history;
- gratitude and grief.

It does not transfer:

- autobiographical memory;
- name;
- relational identity;
- moral ownership of the donor's life.

This is especially important when Toko/Miori and Shoko meet. Shoko is not a substitute Toko whom Miori can recover through the transplant. Preserving both girls requires the body-person distinction.


# 26. Archives preserve routes, not complete persons
Notebooks, photographs, diaries, trophies, records, songs, and objects repeatedly stabilize identity under recognition failure.

But no archive equals the whole person.

- Kaede's notebook preserves goals and voice but not living first-person presence.
- Nene's trophy preserves a name and achievement but cannot by itself restore her.
- Toko/Miori's diary preserves a relationship but does not make Toko's final interior intention fully transparent.
- The complete song preserves distributed authorship and memory but does not resurrect Toko or physically return Miori.

The archive's proper function is:

> **to reopen a route to biography and relationship.**

It becomes dangerous when treated as total jurisdiction over the living successor.


# 27. Relation, history, and nonfungibility
AoButa's strongest personhood claim is nonfungibility.

Two persons can share:

- appearance;
- name;
- body;
- role;
- voice;
- or a large portion of memory

without becoming interchangeable.

What makes the person nonfungible is not a hidden soul variable the series proves. It is the particular conjunction of:

- lived history;
- remembered consequences;
- relationships;
- commitments;
- and current capacity to answer.

This explains why:

- alternate Ikumi cannot simply replace current Ikumi;
- Nene cannot become valuable merely by filling the Toko role;
- the final station woman is not Miori;
- Hanakaede cannot be conscripted into Kaede's unfinished future.


# 28. Refined personhood thesis
The mature model is neither purely psychological nor purely social.

> **A person in AoButa is a historically particular subject whose body, autobiography, social name, relationships, records, roles, and current choices jointly establish continuity. Any one axis may fail. No one axis may erase the rest.**

Recognition is therefore an obligation to track the particular life, not simply the most visible substrate.


# 29. Strongest counterarguments
## “The series ultimately privileges autobiographical memory above everything”

Kaede/Hanakaede strongly supports memory's importance.

Counterpoint:

Learned content, body, relationship, object, and current choice remain morally relevant when autobiographical memory fails.

## “Current self-identification should always settle identity”

Respect for first-person authority is crucial.

Counterpoint:

Nene sincerely identifies as Toko during a state that deletes Nene biography. Mai sincerely identifies as Toko after collective rewrite. The series treats self-identification as central but not infallible under phenomenon conditions.

## “Specific others create the person by recognizing her”

Recognition restores social existence in many cases.

Counterpoint:

Others do not own the person. Hanakaede, Sara, Nene, Miori, and Kaede require current choice, not merely external naming.

## “Kaede and Hanakaede are metaphysically two souls”

The text gives strong person-level distinction.

It does not certify a soul ontology.

The safest claim is autobiographical personhood with unresolved metaphysics.

## “The final composite Toko makes individuality irrelevant”

Opposite.

The composite identity requires more precise attribution:

- who wrote;
- who sang;
- whose name;
- whose body;
- whose grief.

# 32. Controlled personhood test
Later documents should evaluate identity claims through the following ordered questions rather than asking only “Who is the real one?”

1. Which body or bodies are present?
2. Which autobiographical history can the subject narrate as mine?
3. What learned/procedural continuity survives?
4. Which name and role are used by self and others?
5. Which relationships preserve shared history?
6. What records or objects preserve otherwise inaccessible biography?
7. What does the current subject claim?
8. What future does the current subject choose?
9. Which proposed identity would erase another axis if accepted as sole authority?

The final question is especially important. AoButa repeatedly presents plausible identity criteria that become destructive when universalized.

# 34. Stronger counterarguments
## 34.1 Why current choice cannot always settle everything

Current choice is decisive late in the series, especially for Hanakaede and R1 Kaede. But phenomenon states may distort access to biography—as with Nene's sincere Toko identification or Mai's R1 Toko belief. The correct principle is not “ignore current choice.” It is:

> **Treat current choice as morally central while investigating whether the conditions of choosing have erased relevant history or another person's standing.**

## 34.2 Why memory cannot always settle everything

Autobiographical memory is crucial, but learned continuity, body history, relation, and object archives remain. Hanakaede retains academic knowledge learned during Kaede's continuity. Shoko's future kindness remains causally active in Sakuta after the future state ceases to be current.

## 34.3 Why relational recognition cannot own the person

Sakuta's recognition often restores legibility. Yet no relationship may convert recognition into jurisdiction. Volume 8 rejects using Kaede's wishes to script Hanakaede; Volume 12 rejects total access as proof of love; Volume 15 respects Kaede's choice rather than Sakuta's desire.


# 35. Residual ontology lock
The corpus should not claim certainty about:

- Kaede as metaphysical soul;
- whether future Shokos are numerically identical persons;
- whether Miori is literally one consciousness across all worlds;
- the subjective fate of R1 Kaede after RF stabilization;
- the red-randoseru girl's identity.

The final personhood argument does not require these questions to be falsely closed.


# 36. Phase-3 personhood lock
