# 08. Adolescence Syndrome: Typology, Literal Rules, and Mechanical Models
## The impossible events as events

## Governing question

> **What literal phenomenological families exist across the series, what rules can actually be established, and where does the text intentionally stop short of a unified hard-magic system?**


# 1. Executive thesis
Adolescence Syndrome is diegetically real in AoButa. It produces bodily duplication, social invisibility, recursive time, future-state embodiment, cross-world displacement, observer-dependent records, mass dream access, prestige-identity substitution, and world-level rewriting. Treating those events as nothing more than metaphor would contradict the narrated evidence.

The opposite error is to infer one complete hard-magic law from the series' recurring vocabulary of `観測`, possibility, quantum analogy, and recognition. The characters repeatedly build models. Rio herself warns that her Volume-1 explanation is an `荒唐無稽で空想科学的な拡大解釈`. Counterpart Sakuta's final observer model is the strongest late-series synthesis, but he uses qualifiers such as `恐らく`. The novels never supply a narrator-certified equation from which every case can be derived.

The completed series supports a **family model**:

> **Adolescence Syndrome consists of literal impossible events in which conflicts over selfhood, recognition, futurity, embodiment, or social belonging become externally and intersubjectively real. The cases often depend on observation, recognition, memory, or possible-state access, but no single demonstrated rule is sufficient for all cases.**

Several cross-case regularities are strong:

1. the phenomenon is tied to a person whose self/world relation is under unusual pressure;
2. it often separates dimensions ordinary life keeps aligned—body, memory, role, name, public recognition, future;
3. it produces evidence accessible to more than one observer;
4. social recognition can sustain, suppress, or alter manifestation;
5. resolution normally requires a change in the person's relation to the conflict, not merely external attention;
6. resolution does not always erase knowledge, scars, memory, or consequences;
7. recurrence or variation is possible when an underlying vulnerability reappears in a new life stage;
8. the final ethical value of a case can survive after its immediate supernatural mode ends.

The best specialist approach is therefore typological rather than reductively unifying.


# 2. Evidence discipline
This document uses the final V2 categories:

- **TF** — textual fact;
- **CB** — character belief/model;
- **SI** — strong inference;
- **TI** — thematic interpretation;
- **UA** — unresolved ambiguity.

A sentence such as:

> `僕には霧島透子を観測できなかった`

is a textual fact concerning counterpart Sakuta's statement. Whether the world's ontology literally follows quantum-observation physics remains character theory.

Likewise, the recurring fact that social recognition and visibility change together does not prove that “attention” is always a sufficient cure. Volume 13–15 shows recognition producing both restoration and distortion.


# 3. Typology overview
| Family | Representative cases | Core literal disturbance |
|---|---|---|
| social nonrecognition | Mai V1; Sakuta V7/V9; returned Ikumi V11; Nene/Santas V13 | a present body/person is not perceived, remembered, counted, or correctly identified |
| recursive/prospective time | Tomoe V2; Shoko V6–7; dated dreams V12–14 | a possible/future state is experienced before or beside public history |
| multiplication | Rio V3; two Sakutas V7 | one autobiographical subject exists in multiple embodied states |
| crossed embodiment/role | Mai/Nodoka V4 | public body/appearance and autobiographical person diverge |
| somatic inscription | Sakuta scars; Kaede/Hanakaede wounds; Ikumi body messages | history, social injury, or cross-world information appears on the body |
| autobiographical discontinuity | Kaede/Hanakaede; counterpart Ikumi; Nene/Toko | body/name/social role continue while first-person biography changes |
| world-state access | V9 family world; W1 Ikumi; R1 | persons enter or remember alternate/revised histories |
| collective dream access | `#夢見る` | many people receive coherent possibility information from different viewpoints |
| prestige-identity substitution | Nene/Toko; mass Santas; Mai/Toko | a socially legible role overwrites a particular biography |
| collective possibility actualization | R1 | observed possibilities become current history across records and relationships |
| residual/next-generation case | Ebina | possible new impossible experience after Sakuta's own visibility has changed |

These families overlap. The point of the table is not to force every case into one bin, but to identify what the literal event does before asking what it means.


# 4. Family A — Recognition-state phenomena
## 4.1 Mai's social nonrecognition

### Observable event

Mai becomes progressively:

- unseen;
- unheard;
- forgotten;
- absent from the semantic resolution of records and conversation.

Sakuta and, for a period, Rio retain access longer than others. School-wide attention after Sakuta's public declaration coincides with Mai's restoration.

### Strongest model

Recognition-linked social erasure. The school had already treated Mai like `空気`; the literal event extends that relation into perception and memory.

Rio proposes an observation model. Her own wording marks it as speculative.

### What can be established

- social nonacknowledgment precedes literal erasure;
- observer access differs by person;
- sleep correlates with forgetting;
- mass attention is associated with restoration.

### What cannot be established

- one universal attention threshold;
- a complete rule for why Sakuta persists;
- whether public confession alone is sufficient.

The later series makes caution necessary. Collective recognition can restore Mai in V1 and overwrite her as Toko in V14.

## 4.2 Invisible Sakuta and returned Ikumi

V7 inverts the V1 structure: Sakuta becomes the socially unperceived person. Tomoe sees him first after dreaming him; perception later broadens.

V11 repeats recognition-linked visibility after native Ikumi returns. Sakuta's correct naming/recognition precedes collective visibility.

These cases strengthen the claim that recognition has literal consequence while weakening any simplistic “fame fixes invisibility” rule. What matters is often not volume of attention but whether the observer recognizes the correct person/continuity.

## 4.3 Nene and the mass Santas

Nene's case makes recognition failure biographical and infrastructural.

Society recognizes the role `霧島透子` while Nene's:

- name;
- relationship;
- past;
- and body

become inaccessible.

At the public event, unseen Santas still occupy physical space. Spectators and police misread crowd density. Recognition failure therefore changes institutional risk.

The epilogue says the Santas remember who they are and become recognizable again:

> `自分が誰なのかを思い出し、周りから認識されるようになった`

This couples self-memory and social recognition. It does not prove a one-directional causal equation.

## 4.4 Mai/Toko

Mass expectation does not erase Mai. It inserts a false autobiographical proposition:

> Mai experiences herself as Toko.

This is an important mechanical distinction.

- V1: socially known person becomes absent.
- V14: socially known person remains present but becomes identified as a different public identity.

Miori's unveiled performance and the completed song provide a counter-recognition event. Mai can again say she is not Toko.


# 5. Family B — Recursive and prospective time
## 5.1 Tomoe's repeated June

The early loop is best treated as recursively experienced possible future, not straightforward rewinding of one objective timeline.

Evidence includes:

- Sakuta and Tomoe retain accumulated experience;
- emotion accumulates rather than resets:
  > `繰り返した分だけ、積み重なっていく`
- Rio later describes the repeated days as calculated/experienced futures rather than time physically moving backward.

The phenomenon arises around Tomoe's fear of socially visible rejection. It ends when she accepts an outcome containing embarrassment and unreciprocated love.

Open questions:

- why Sakuta shares the state;
- exact reset boundary;
- relation to later dreams.

## 5.2 Shoko's future states

The Shoko architecture is more complex than a loop.

Older Shoko possesses memories and bodily evidence from futures in which:

- Sakuta dies and becomes donor;
- or Mai dies and her heart saves Shoko.

Young Shoko's inability to author a future she may not survive supplies the emotional center. Dreams function as a possible state interface:

> `常識を捨てられるのは、夢の中だけ`

The series later changes the fatal history. Current Shoko survives through Toko's donor heart.

Strong conclusion:

> future states are experienced enough to generate real memory, relationship, and moral standing.

Open conclusion:

> whether every future is a fully independent world, simulation, or observer-conditioned state.

## 5.3 Dated dreams and `#夢見る`

Volumes 12–14 increase evidentiary precision:

- dates;
- phone numbers;
- venues;
- multi-observer viewpoints;
- later partial realization.

The dreams are not reducible to ordinary symbolism because they supply externally valid novel information. They are not fixed prophecy because outcomes diverge.

The strongest final label is:

> **possible-state information.**


# 6. Family C — Multiplication, exchange, and continuity
## 6.1 Rio duplication

Rio exists in two complete bodies with shared pre-split history and divergent conduct. Both are recognizably Rio.

The case demonstrates:

- one autobiography can be distributed across simultaneous embodiments;
- the person can reject desire so strongly that it appears as an alien self;
- integration is relational and psychological, not selection of a “real body.”

No exact duplication substrate is confirmed.

## 6.2 Mai/Nodoka crossed embodiment

The public reads the visible body/role and therefore sees the wrong sister. Rio explicitly says it is not accurately described as a simple body swap and proposes a bodily-design explanation.

Established:

- autobiographical memory remains with each sister;
- social treatment follows appearance;
- Sakuta distinguishes them through biography/voice;
- restoration follows differentiation.

Unresolved:

- exact bodily substrate;
- extent of sensory/proprioceptive transfer;
- conservation rules.

## 6.3 Kaede/Hanakaede

The medical dissociative account and Syndrome must remain distinct.

Established:

- dissociative disorder is explicitly diagnosed;
- wounds/bruises literalize social trauma;
- one body carries discontinuous autobiographical lives;
- learned content can persist without episodic memory;
- Kaede's personhood is not reducible to medical “false self” language.

R1 later physically embodies both Kaede and Hanakaede separately. That outcome exceeds the earlier body-continuity model and is one of the strongest reasons R1 should not be described as a normal branch.

## 6.4 Ikumi counterpart displacement

Two Ikumi autobiographies inhabit opposite possibility worlds for months. Each world socially accepts the occupying counterpart. Bodily writing crosses states.

The case supplies the strongest direct evidence that:

- adjacent possibilities persist;
- counterparts can be displaced;
- biography matters even when function and social recognition remain stable;
- bodies/selves can remain coupled across worlds.

The text does not settle whether the proper noun is “mind transfer,” “soul exchange,” or another ontology. Neutral description is required.


# 7. Family D — Somatic inscription
## 7.1 Sakuta's scars

The scars are visible bodily evidence connected to impossible future histories and Shoko. They react and later disappear.

They establish that:

- counterfactual relation can be bodily;
- a body may preserve history inaccessible to ordinary records.

They do not establish one final physiological law.

## 7.2 Kaede/Hanakaede bruises

Social messages and fear become wounds. Later triggers produce renewed bruising even when autobiographical access differs.

The strongest careful conclusion is:

> trauma remains embodied beyond complete first-person episodic continuity.

The text does not provide a neurological theory of body memory.

## 7.3 Ikumi's body as cross-world message field

Words appear on the body and cross counterpart states. This case is more directly informational than the earlier scars.

Established:

- writing can cross;
- sensations/traces can cross;
- communication persists after return.

Unknown:

- latency;
- bandwidth;
- whether every injury crosses;
- what preserves the link.


# 8. Family E — World-state access and rewriting
## 8.1 Volume 9 adjacent family world

Sakuta enters a world containing another Sakuta's authored family life. The world is coherent and attractive. Sakuta rejects appropriation.

The case establishes that alternate biographies are not merely fantasy rewards. Another life can be real enough to impose ethical limits.

## 8.2 W1 Ikumi world

Volume 11 strengthens persistence. Counterparts can remain displaced for months and communicate.

This corrects any reading of Volume 9 as only one subjective dream.

## 8.3 R1

R1 rewrites:

- memories;
- relationships;
- institutions;
- records;
- embodied identities.

The simultaneous physical existence of Kaede and Hanakaede strongly resists a simple one-branch-transfer description. R1 appears synthetic or composite.

Most people accept R1 as ordinary. Sakuta and Miori retain prior-state memory.

The finale chooses RF rather than preserving R1, but the ethical standing of R1 persons remains real.


# 9. Family F — Collective dream access and possibility actualization
## 9.1 `#夢見る` as distributed observation

The tag becomes an unofficial possibility archive.

The reports vary:

- ordinary rehearsal-like dreams;
- specific future scenes;
- multiple viewpoints of one event;
- long Tomoe simulation;
- valid phone information.

Therefore the tag is a social container, not proof of one mechanism.

## 9.2 From observation to actualization

Many R1 outcomes correspond to prior dream possibilities. Counterpart Sakuta's final theory assigns roles:

- Miori as cross-world singular/overlap point;
- `#夢見る` as distributed recognition of possibilities;
- current Sakuta as observer whose recognition can rewrite reality.

This is the strongest internal model.

It remains CB/SI, not omniscient law.


# 10. Family G — Prestige-identity substitution
## 10.1 Nene/Toko

Nene adopts a prestigious identity with high public recognizability and low biographical specificity. The role supplies social value while erasing Nene's history.

The case is not “social influence is fake.” Socially made selves throughout the series can be genuine.

The failure condition is:

> the role cannot include the person's failed, ordinary, and relational biography.

## 10.2 Public Toko as composite identity

Volume 14 reveals:

- human Toko wrote/composed;
- Miori performed/uploaded;
- the public attached both to one name.

The public artist is distributed rather than one hidden performer.

This helps explain why later attempts to locate one body behind the name are unstable.


# 11. Cross-case rules that are strongly supported
## Rule 1 — ordinary reality can become literally dependent on recognition

Supported across Mai, invisible Sakuta, Ikumi, Nene, Santas, and Mai/Toko.

## Rule 2 — recognition is multidimensional

It includes:

- sight;
- memory;
- name;
- biography;
- role;
- public record;
- first-person authority;
- institutional counting.

## Rule 3 — phenomena often separate normally aligned identity axes

Body, memory, name, role, and history can diverge.

## Rule 4 — internal change alone is not always sufficient

A person may need:

- another witness;
- an archive;
- public correction;
- institutional support;
- ordinary time.

## Rule 5 — external recognition alone is not always sufficient

Nene requires both Takumi's recognition and her own first-person return. Mai's public attention can become misrecognition. Hanakaede's agency matters.

## Rule 6 — resolution can preserve residue

Characters may retain:

- memory;
- learned social awareness;
- grief;
- moral knowledge;
- changed relationships;
- vocational direction.

## Rule 7 — possible states have moral standing without becoming destiny

Shoko, W1, R1, and dreamed futures all support this.


# 12. Strongest final mechanical model
The completed series' strongest model is layered rather than singular.

## Layer 1 — person-centered conflict

A conflict in adolescence concerns:

- recognition;
- selfhood;
- future;
- belonging;
- or grief.

## Layer 2 — literal externalization

The conflict becomes bodily, temporal, social, or world-level.

## Layer 3 — observer/recognition dependence

Access and persistence vary by:

- who sees;
- who remembers;
- who recognizes the correct person/state;
- how widely the possibility is socially observed.

## Layer 4 — relational/institutional stabilization

Resolution often requires:

- correct recognition;
- self-recognition;
- changed relation to desire;
- bounded care;
- an archive;
- public attribution;
- or institutional support.

## Layer 5 — memory after manifestation

The final ethical value remains after the literal event no longer needs to remain current.

This layered model explains more than either “one quantum law” or “all metaphor.” It also preserves real uncertainty.


# 13. Controlled mechanical vocabulary
The following terms should remain stable across Documents 02–18.

| Term | Controlled use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **phenomenon** | the literal impossible event as narrated | using “metaphor” as though nothing literally happens |
| **trigger/context** | the interpersonal, social, bodily, or temporal condition associated with onset | claiming one clinical cause is proven when the text supplies only correlation |
| **observer condition** | who can perceive, remember, or socially register the event/person | treating “observer” as narrator-certified quantum physics |
| **recognition-state change** | altered social or personal legibility of an existing person | reducing all invisibility to lack of attention |
| **autobiographical continuity change** | change in first-person remembered life | equating automatically with body death or soul replacement |
| **possible state** | an experienced or observed life/event not necessarily current in public history | calling every state “the future” or “timeline” |
| **world-state rewrite** | current records, memories, and relationships become aligned to a different history | assuming ordinary travel into a preexisting branch |
| **resolution** | the active crisis ceases or becomes livable | assuming all residue, trauma, or ambiguity disappears |
| **residue** | memory, learned habit, bodily effect, relationship, or ethical knowledge that persists | treating resolution as reset |
| **actualization** | a previously observed possibility becomes current history | treating desirability as sufficient causal explanation |

# 15. Resolution taxonomy
“Resolution” should be specified rather than assumed.

## 15.1 Recognition restoration

The person becomes perceptible or socially legible again.

Examples:

- Mai;
- Sakuta in the future state;
- returned Ikumi;
- Nene and the Santas.

## 15.2 Identity integration

A disowned desire or role is accepted as belonging to the person.

Examples:

- Rio;
- Uzuki's socially informed but self-authored dream;
- Nene's failed biography.

## 15.3 Autobiographical transition

One first-person continuity ends or returns while body and social history persist.

Example:

- Kaede/Hanakaede.

This should never be mislabeled a neat “cure.”

## 15.4 World-state replacement

A current history changes.

Examples:

- Shoko fatal-future revision;
- R1/RF transition.

The new state does not retroactively make displaced states morally meaningless.

## 15.5 Memory reclassification

The phenomenon no longer needs to remain present, but its ethical and autobiographical effects persist.

Final example:

> `思春期症候群を思い出に変える`

This is the series' most mature resolution type.


# 16. Strong evidence boundaries
The refined mechanical document adopts five explicit limits.

1. **No diagnosis substitution.** A medical diagnosis such as dissociative disorder may coexist with Syndrome. One should not be used to erase the other.
2. **No automatic quantum literalism.** `観測`, Laplace, and possibility language are internal models and analogies unless the text establishes more.
3. **No causation from one successful intervention.** A person being recognized immediately before restoration does not prove recognition alone is universally sufficient.
4. **No single trigger rule.** Desire, stress, social atmosphere, bodily threat, grief, and networked observation appear in different combinations.
5. **No complete-system claim at finale.** Counterpart Sakuta's observer model is the strongest synthesis and remains CB/SI.


# 17. Refined mechanical synthesis
The strongest common description is not “adolescent emotions become magic.” It is:

> **AoButa literalizes failures of ordinary coordination among body, memory, social recognition, desire, and possible life. The phenomenon makes one unstable axis materially consequential until the person and the surrounding relationships establish a more livable configuration.**

This describes common structure without pretending every case shares one equation.

The final move then changes the aim of explanation. Adult Sakuta need not derive the new student's ghost mechanically before responding well. The system's ethical result is an adult who can preserve uncertainty and still refuse abandonment.


# 18. Model limits and residual ambiguity
The completed series does not finally settle:

1. whether all cases belong to one physical substrate;
2. why Sakuta shares Tomoe's state;
3. exact scar physiology;
4. exact counterpart transfer mechanics;
5. how dreams select states;
6. how Miori travels among possibilities;
7. whether smartphone non-participation affected observer resistance mechanically;
8. who retains R1 memory in RF;
9. the literal ontology of the red-randoseru girl;
10. whether Ebina's ghost is Syndrome.

These are not gaps that the specialist document should fill from general science or franchise speculation.


# 19. Strong counterreading I — all Syndrome cases are psychological metaphors
The metaphor-only reading captures genuine literary function. Each case corresponds closely to a social or emotional conflict.

It fails because:

- multiple observers share impossible events;
- records and bodies change;
- new information crosses states;
- institutions miscount bodies;
- possible worlds contain independent biographies.

The literal layer is not decorative.


# 20. Strong counterreading II — the series has one coherent hard-magic system
The hard-system reading gains support from repeated terms:

- `観測`;
- possible worlds;
- dreams;
- recognition;
- counterpart theory.

It fails if stated too strongly because:

- Rio labels early physics speculative;
- case rules differ;
- counterpart Sakuta remains uncertain;
- the smartphone clue remains incomplete;
- the finale privileges ethical resolution over mechanical closure.

The best final judgment is:

> **a coherent field of related phenomena, not a fully formalized magic system.**


# 21. Controlled terminology and classification lock
The typology now distinguishes five analytical levels that later documents must not collapse.

| Level | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **manifestation** | What impossible event occurs? | Mai becomes socially unrecognizable. |
| **trigger/context** | What pressure precedes or accompanies it? | withdrawal from public/school recognition and loss of authorship |
| **character model** | What explanation do Rio, Shoko, Sakuta, or others propose? | observation/possible-world theory |
| **resolution condition** | What changed when the event ended or transformed? | renewed recognition, self-integration, changed relation to conflict |
| **literary function** | What social/personhood problem does the case externalize? | visibility versus recognition |

A claim may be strong at one level and weak at another. For example, it is textual fact that public recognition and Mai's visibility change together; it is not textual fact that mass attention alone is a universally sufficient mechanical cause.

# 23. Counterevidence upgrades
## 23.1 Recognition cannot be the sole universal mechanism

Recognition is central, but several cases resist a simple formula.

- Tomoe's repeated futures involve recursive calculation and memory accumulation, not only social visibility.
- Shoko's possible selves carry novel information and donor histories.
- Rio's duplication persists while both bodies are visible.
- Kaede/Hanakaede's dissociative history includes clinically described memory discontinuity alongside supernatural bruising.
- Miori's cross-world condition remains active after public Toko recognition is corrected.

Recognition is therefore best treated as a recurrent dependency and organizing theme, not a complete physical substrate.

## 23.2 Internal acceptance is not a universal cure

Many resolutions involve changed self-relation, but external conditions remain necessary.

- Mai requires social recognition and Sakuta's intervention.
- Hanakaede requires school/workplace accommodation.
- Nene requires Takumi's relational memory and material archives.
- Ebina requires an adult institutional listener.

The series is not saying distress disappears when a person “accepts herself.”

## 23.3 External attention is not universally emancipatory

Attention restores Mai in one context but destroys autonomy in others.

- Rio receives sexualized attention she experiences as both relief and harm.
- Nene becomes trapped in a prestigious public identity.
- Mai's celebrity produces maximum attention and unstable authorship.
- the April 1 crowd helps impose an identity through expectation.

# 25. Phase-3 mechanical lock
The final controlled formulation is:

> **Adolescence Syndrome is a diegetically real family of impossible events that externalizes conflicts among selfhood, recognition, embodiment, social belonging, and possible futures. Observation and recognition recur across cases, but the books do not establish one complete hard-magic law. Resolution changes how persons and relationships hold the conflict; it need not erase memory, consequence, or all underlying vulnerability.**


# 26. Final synthesis
Adolescence Syndrome is the series' method for refusing minimization.

A person may say:

- nobody sees me;
- I feel divided;
- people see my body and not me;
- I woke inside a life that is not mine;
- everyone has decided who I am;
- I cannot accept that my friend is dead;
- another future feels more real than this one.

Ordinary society can answer:

> that is only a feeling.

AoButa answers by making the feeling intersubjectively consequential.

The body splits.

The school forgets.

The future arrives.

The wrong identity becomes socially real.

The crowd barrier falls because invisible bodies still occupy space.

Yet the series does not end by making the impossible permanent. Its final move is to preserve what the impossible taught.

The adult Sakuta no longer sees every ghost.

He has learned enough from seeing earlier ones that he can listen without sight.

That is the mechanical system's final literary purpose:

> **to produce an adult capable of ethical recognition after exceptional perception ends.**
