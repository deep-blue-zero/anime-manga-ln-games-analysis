# AoButa Recognition and Observer Ledger — Final

## 1. Governing distinction

Across the series, **recognition** is never merely visual detection.

It can mean:

- perceiving a body;
- remembering a person;
- naming the correct autobiographical continuity;
- respecting first-person authority;
- treating testimony as credible;
- allowing an institution to count a body;
- refusing substitution between lookalikes or roles.

The final-series movement is:

> **seeing → remembering → recognizing → believing without shared sight.**

## 3. Observer categories

### 3.1 Sensory observer

Can visually/audibly perceive the impossible person or state.

Examples:

- Sakuta seeing Bunny Girl Mai;
- Miori seeing Nene/Toko;
- Sakuta seeing Santas;
- Sakuta seeing the red-randoseru girl.

### 3.2 Memory observer

Retains a prior or alternate history when others do not.

Examples:

- Sakuta in W1/R1;
- Miori across Rn;
- Shoko across future states;
- Ikumi counterparts.

### 3.3 Relational recognizer

Identifies the correct person through biography and relation rather than surface appearance.

Examples:

- Sakuta with Rio/Nodoka/Kaede/Ikumi;
- Takumi with Nene;
- Mai trusting Sakuta against her own Toko self-perception.

### 3.4 Institutional recognizer

Creates a durable adult/social channel for testimony.

Final example:

- teacher-candidate Sakuta with Ebina.

## 4. Recognition and `空気`

Volume 1's `空気` is ambient consensus experienced as authority without an identifiable author.

The final series expands it into:

- rumor;
- celebrity image;
- follower attention;
- `#夢見る`;
- mass expectation;
- rewritten identity;
- “general opinion.”

Sakuta's final research question:

> `みんなってなんだろうね`

turns the force that once governed him into an object of inquiry.

## 5. Final ledger conclusion

The complete series does not define recognition as:

> seeing what is objectively there.

It defines mature recognition as:

> **taking seriously the historically particular person before you, including when body, role, memory, public consensus, or your own perception fails to provide certainty.**
