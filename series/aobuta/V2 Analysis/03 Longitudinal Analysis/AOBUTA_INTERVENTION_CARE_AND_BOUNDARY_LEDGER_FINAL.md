# AoButa Intervention, Care, and Boundary Ledger — Final

## 1. Purpose

This ledger separates **effective intervention** from **legitimate intervention**.

The series repeatedly gives Sakuta and others good outcomes through actions that remain ethically mixed.

Required distinctions:

- rescue is not automatically legitimate;
- success does not retroactively create consent;
- protection is not ownership;
- information access is not a right to know;
- self-sacrifice is not the purest form of love;
- support can preserve autonomy when the person names the support required.

## 3. Sakuta's longitudinal intervention pattern

### Phase I — force the crisis open

Typical method:

- pursue;
- investigate;
- invade privacy;
- use spectacle;
- accept bodily cost.

### Phase II — discover the violence of unilateral sacrifice

V6–7 forces Sakuta to admit:

> his own disappearance is not an ethically neutral resource.

### Phase III — build options and distribute care

V8 onward increasingly uses:

- counselors;
- workplaces;
- teachers;
- friends;
- medical systems;
- the person best positioned to recognize the subject.

### Phase IV — listen without owning the answer

Final examples:

- refusing Miori's diary;
- respecting Kaede's decision;
- believing Ebina without seeing.

## 4. Final care principle

The completed series' mature care sequence is:

> **notice → ask → create options → disclose relevant risk → preserve refusal capacity → involve the right people/institutions → accept that the final decision belongs to the person → remain available afterward.**

AoButa never makes Sakuta perfect at this sequence.

It makes him capable of teaching it.
