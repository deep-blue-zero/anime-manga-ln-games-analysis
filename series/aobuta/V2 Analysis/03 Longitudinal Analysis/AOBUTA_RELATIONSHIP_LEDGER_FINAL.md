- initial asymmetry;

- information asymmetry;
- protection;

- privacy;
- refusal capacity;
- reciprocity;
- ordinary-life continuity;

## 3. Relationship-development tests

### 3.1 Reciprocity test

A relationship becomes more reciprocal when both parties can:

- impose limits;
- disagree;
- protect;
- receive care;
- make future plans;
- continue ordinary life outside crisis.

Sakuta/Mai passes this test most clearly by the finale.

### 3.2 Substitution test

A relationship becomes ethically unstable when one person is treated as substitute for:

- another continuity;
- a desired role;
- an ideal self;
- a deceased friend;
- a possible-world partner.

The series repeatedly resolves relationships by restoring distinction.

### 3.3 Opacity test

Mature intimacy does not require complete access.

Key cases:

- Sara refusing Mai's mind;
- Miori/Toko misunderstanding;
- Mai trusting Sakuta despite contradictory self-perception;
- Sakuta believing Ebina without seeing.

## 4. Final relationship-ledger conclusion

AoButa's durable relationships are not those in which people know everything about one another.

They are those in which:

> **difference, uncertainty, grief, and ordinary absence can occur without the relationship requiring substitution, possession, or permanent crisis to remain real.**
