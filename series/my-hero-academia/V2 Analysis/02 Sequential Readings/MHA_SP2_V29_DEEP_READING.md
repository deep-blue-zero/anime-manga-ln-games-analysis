# My Hero Academia - Second-Pass Deep Reading, Volume 29

## 0. Status, method, and prospective lock

This reading follows `MHA_SP2_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2_1.md` and begins from the exact frozen Volume 28 endpoint at `V28:p180`. It therefore treats Volume 29 prospectively even though the full series is known elsewhere in the corpus. Later outcomes are admissible only when Volume 29 itself earns them.

The entering state is deliberately unstable. Shigaraki has awakened despite his interrupted procedure; the displayed stabilization state is around seventy-five percent rather than clean completion. He carries the original All For One Quirk, can use Search and other stored Quirks, and is being pressured by an AFO-associated internal drive toward One For All. Aizawa's Erasure is the field's unique brake, but its continued use depends materially on Manual, mobility, defense, and offense. Midoriya has identified himself as Shigaraki's target and re-entered the battlefield with Bakugo. Gigantomachia has been called toward Shigaraki with the League aboard him. The mansion, hospital, evacuation, Hawks/Mirko, Toga, Dabi, Best Jeanist, later OFA Quirks, and the war's wider casualty/escape outcomes remain unresolved at the boundary.

# 1. Governing transition: distributed containment becomes responsibility under irreversible triage

Volume 28's strongest institutional proposition was that catastrophic inherited power cannot be ethically answered by producing a stronger solitary inheritor. It requires a network that distributes knowledge, restraint, protection, and risk while preserving distinct agency. Volume 29 accepts that proposition and places it under catastrophic stress.

The question changes from **how a network contains power** to **who owns judgment when the network is breaking faster than authority can keep up**.

The volume stages three sharply different forms of responsibility transfer.

First is the failed social form embedded in Shigaraki's childhood memory. Child Tenko is visibly alone and distressed. Adults notice him, but noticing does not become ownership. Someone assumes that a hero, police officer, parent, or some other person will eventually handle the problem. Responsibility remains socially distributed in the worst sense: everyone can recognize need while nobody accepts that the need is theirs to answer.

Second is Midnight's emergency transfer to Yaoyorozu. The adult command chain is physically disrupted. Midnight gives the student a morally and legally difficult objective, names the emergency-law problem rather than pretending it does not exist, and says in effect that the decision is now entrusted to Yaoyorozu's judgment. This is not abandonment disguised as empowerment. Midnight specifies the desired end, identifies adult support if available, instructs evacuation where possible, and then transfers actual decision jurisdiction when communication and bodily capacity fail.

Third is Bakugo's endpoint rescue of Midoriya. Here responsibility has been internalized so deeply that it precedes conscious narration. He does not report weighing pros and cons before moving. The volume closes on the same bodily grammar that once defined Midoriya's earliest rescue reflex: `身体が勝手に動いてた`. The decision has become embodied before it becomes verbally owned.

These three cases define the governing distinction:

> **Distributed responsibility is ethical only when somebody actually accepts ownership of the next necessary act.**

This does not mean every act is correct merely because someone commits to it. Volume 29 is particularly interested in **correct action without guaranteed success**. Yaoyorozu's plan can be rational and morally serious while Gigantomachia continues moving. Aizawa's amputation can be the correct field decision while failing to secure permanent control. Midoriya's self-sacrifice can be strategically intelligible while still reproducing a dangerous failure to count himself among the people worth protecting. Bakugo can save Midoriya reflexively without the war being resolved.

The volume therefore introduces irreversibility as a major ethical pressure. Bodies can be permanently damaged. Buildings and cities cannot be restored by good intentions. A student can inherit a decision with no adult standing beside her to absorb blame. A rescuer may need to act before knowing whether the act will work. Under these conditions, heroism becomes less about choosing a clean good outcome and more about **owning the best available responsibility while knowing that consequence may exceed control**.

The V29 transition is therefore:

**`inherited burden as distributed containment -> responsibility under irreversible triage / delegated judgment and reflexive rescue`**

Compact formulation:

**A functioning hero network cannot promise control over outcomes. Its ethical work is to make responsibility legible, transferable, and actionable before crisis turns collective awareness into collective evasion.**

# 2. No.277 - `誰だよ`: Shigaraki contests succession from inside the inherited body

## 2.1 Erasure proves that Shigaraki's body is now an independent strategic problem

The opening continuation matters because it prevents the inherited-Quirk story from swallowing the body. Under Aizawa's Erasure, Shigaraki cannot simply answer every threat with his stored Quirk inventory. Yet the modified body remains physically overwhelming at a level compared on the page to All Might-like power and toughness (`V29:p013`).

This preserves a key V28 correction. Erasure is indispensable, but it is not a universal off switch. The field has to distinguish at least three layers:

1. Shigaraki's active Quirks;
2. the engineered body's baseline physical capacity;
3. the unstable relation between Shigaraki's agency and the AFO-associated inheritance inside him.

Aizawa can suppress the first. He cannot undo the second, and he cannot settle the third.

That is why Shigaraki immediately identifies Eraser as a priority target while the heroes organize around keeping Aizawa functioning (`V29:p013-014`). The fight is no longer intelligible as hero A versus villain B. It is a network trying to preserve the one local rule that makes every other role survivable.

## 2.2 `弟` reveals the AFO drive as somebody else's relational grammar

At `V29:p015`, the AFO-associated pressure toward One For All becomes more personal. The internal language addresses the first holder as `弟` and urges acquisition. Shigaraki notices that this is not his own ordinary thought or way of speaking.

That distinction is analytically important. AFO inheritance is not merely a set of usable powers and not merely a vague corruptive influence. It carries **relationship-specific desire** belonging to a history that predates Shigaraki. The drive toward OFA is partly an inherited conflict between brothers into which Shigaraki has been inserted.

Shigaraki's response refuses a simplistic possession reading. At `V29:p016`, he asserts the body/power as his and resists being moved around by the predecessor's phantom pressure. The most responsible V2 formulation remains layered:

- Shigaraki has self-authored destructive desire.
- AFO materially enabled and cultivated him.
- The original AFO Quirk brings predecessor-associated pressure and relationship grammar.
- Shigaraki recognizes that pressure as not simply identical to himself.
- Recognition of intrusion does not prove permanent control over it.

Volume 29 therefore strengthens the V28 rejection of both extremes: **pure puppet** and **perfectly autonomous destroyer** are each too flat.

# 3. No.278 - `災害歩行`: when adult command breaks, judgment has to move

## 3.1 Gigantomachia changes the mansion front from battle to moving disaster

The chapter title's `災害歩行` is a useful conceptual reclassification. Gigantomachia is not simply a large opponent advancing across a battlefield. His movement itself produces disaster geometry. Forest, buildings, roads, and later cities become part of the casualty problem simply because his route passes through them.

That matters for the student contingent because the problem cannot be solved by ordinary tournament logic. There is no neutral ring, no clear capture point, and no assumption that stronger attacks necessarily create safer outcomes. The heroes and students need to stop or alter a moving mass whose continued locomotion is itself catastrophic.

## 3.2 Midnight states the law problem instead of laundering it

The call to Yaoyorozu is one of the most institutionally important student scenes in the reread.

At `V29:p040-042`, the injured Midnight states what she wants: Gigantomachia must be put to sleep. She proposes anesthesia and explicitly acknowledges that this would normally violate the law: `法律違反になっちゃうけど…事態が事態よ`.

The wording matters because the series does not pretend emergency legality has disappeared. A prohibited act does not become magically lawful because the speaker is a hero. Instead, the source makes the normative conflict visible:

- the ordinary rule matters;
- the emergency creates a competing duty;
- the adult names the conflict;
- the student inherits a decision inside it.

Majestic is identified as the adult who can take over if the students can get the anesthetic into professional hands, and Midnight also tells Yaoyorozu to evacuate if possible. This is not an adult saying, "children, solve the war for us." The command is **bounded but real**.

Then communication collapses.

## 3.3 `あなたの判断に…委ねます`: entrustment is not the same as abandonment

Midnight's most diagnostic line is `あなたの判断に…委ねます` (`V29:p042`). The actor with formal authority is unable to retain tactical control and therefore names the recipient of judgment.

This offers an exact counter-model to the Tenko memory later in the volume.

The bystander failure says:

> Someone will deal with this.

Midnight's delegation says:

> You are now the person who must judge this, and I am telling you that openly.

The difference is **ownership visibility**.

Entrustment can still be burdensome, arguably unfair, and dangerous. Yaoyorozu is a student being asked to make a decision with lethal/public consequences. But the scene does not confuse that burden with neglect. Midnight has trained her, communicates the objective and constraints, and assigns jurisdiction because the normal command path has failed.

This becomes a major hero-society proposition for V29:

> **Institutions fail ethically not merely when authority decentralizes, but when responsibility becomes decentralized without a named owner.**

# 4. Yaoyorozu Momo: leadership becomes accountable judgment rather than correct-answer performance

## 4.1 Her first response is information architecture

Yaoyorozu's reaction at `V29:p044-046` is revealing because she does not begin by projecting confidence. She begins by reducing uncertainty.

She immediately gives roles to Jiro and Shoji to estimate Gigantomachia's location, distance, and arrival time. She turns sensory Quirks into a local information network, calculates approximate size and required dosing conditions, and reorganizes nearby students around preparation.

This is a mature extension of her earlier planning style. Earlier volumes repeatedly tested whether she could devise a good plan while under self-doubt, defeat, or comparison pressure. V29 changes the task. There is no teacher standing nearby who can grade the plan. Her plan determines what other people expose themselves to.

The cognitive burden is now:

**incomplete information -> technical model -> role assignment -> moral ownership -> action before certainty**.

## 4.2 `私は…戦います…！` is not merely courage rhetoric

Yaoyorozu's statement at `V29:p046` that she will fight and meet the threat here follows the emergency delegation. It should not be abstracted into generic inspirational courage.

She has already been told to evacuate if possible. She has already been told that adult help is preferable. She has already calculated why ordinary physical stopping is implausible. Choosing to hold the location is therefore a judgment made **after** considering alternatives, not evidence that she simply prefers combat.

Her statement that she was not taught to become a hero who shows the enemy her back is identity language under genuine professional consequence. It is also potentially dangerous if read without context: hero identity can motivate necessary action, but identity scripts can also push young people toward exposure. The volume avoids making the issue simple because her decision is later explicitly validated while the outcome remains bad.

## 4.3 The plan is technically specific rather than symbolically brave

At `V29:p052`, the anesthetic problem is given concrete constraints. Normal injection is impractical because of Gigantomachia's scale and skin. Yaoyorozu estimates the enormous dose a body that size would conventionally imply, then produces concentrated oral doses as the only plausible delivery route.

The detail matters for character reconstruction. Yaoyorozu under war stress should not be modeled as giving broad strategic speeches and then waiting for others. Her leadership remains **technical, distributive, and material**. She turns her Quirk into production infrastructure and her classmates into a delivery system.

## 4.4 Future belonging anchors student risk

Mina's `また皆で授業受けるんだもんね！` at `V29:p053` supplies the relational horizon of the operation. The students are not fighting because school has ceased to matter and war has replaced adolescence. They fight while imagining a return to school together.

This is important for the corpus's ongoing student-militarization question. V29 unquestionably exposes students to war. But the text does not depict them as fully transformed into soldiers whose civilian/student identity is obsolete. Their hoped-for endpoint is ordinary continuity.

# 5. Mina and Kirishima: courage is relational memory, not a permanent trait possession

## 5.1 Mina's earlier courage does not immunize her against trauma

When Mina hears Gigantomachia's voice around `V29:p073-075`, the war abruptly reconnects to the middle-school encounter that V16 used to explain Kirishima's shame and identity construction. The important new fact is not that Mina was secretly less brave than the flashback suggested. It is that the same person who once improvised effectively under fear can later freeze when the threat returns at far greater scale.

A stable character model must therefore reject trait essentialism:

- Mina displayed real courage before U.A.
- That courage influenced Kirishima deeply.
- Recognition of the same threat can still trigger hesitation.
- Hesitation does not retroactively falsify the earlier courage.

The scene offers a useful general model for trauma-sensitive simulation: **past success is evidence of capability, not a guarantee of identical response under recurrence**.

## 5.2 Kirishima's rescue is the repayment of an earlier borrowed model

Kirishima's response around `V29:p075-085` is one of the cleanest long-range character payoffs in the current reread. His V16 identity was partly built by looking backward at a moment when Mina acted and he did not. Crimson Riot gave him a doctrine for acting despite fear, but Mina supplied the personal shame/admiration comparison that made the doctrine urgent.

Now, when Mina's own action falters, Kirishima becomes the one who continues.

This is not a reversal in which "Kirishima is now brave and Mina is not." It is **reciprocal longitudinal enabling**:

`Mina's earlier action -> Kirishima's self-reconstruction -> Kirishima's later action when Mina falters`.

He protects her, continues through Dabi's fire, loses one dose, preserves another, takes the incoming attack himself, and delivers the anesthetic into Gigantomachia's mouth. His line `俺が受けとった!!` is both tactical and relational: he accepts the hit and the task.

## 5.3 `俺の後ろに!! 血はァ流れねえ!!!` preserves Kirishima's constructed heroic style

Kirishima's V29 behavior should not be modeled as calm courage. His hero identity remains vocally declarative, body-centered, and explicitly protective. `俺の後ろに!! 血はァ流れねえ!!!` converts bodily hardness into a social promise: behind him, others should not bleed.

That is a mature expression of the V16 distinction between being naturally fearless and **choosing a form of manliness/courage that can contain fear**. The source has now tested the model in a real war rather than a controlled internship.

Readiness should therefore remain `strong`, but V29 substantially increases confidence in the model's transferability across contexts.

# 6. Gigantomachia's Quirk stack: engineered simplicity is a design philosophy

The `V29:p089` interstitial identifies seven Quirks associated with Gigantomachia:

1. `耐久` - original;
2. `痛覚遮断`;
3. `巨大化`;
4. `犬`;
5. `エネルギー効率`;
6. `剛筋`;
7. `土竜`.

The important analytical point is not merely inventory accumulation. The profile explains a **selection principle**. Gigantomachia is not suited to finely controlling many complex Quirks, so the added abilities are comparatively simple, physically synergistic functions that can compound durability, endurance, sensory pursuit, musculature, size, and underground concealment.

This is villain-side systems engineering. AFO/Garaki do not treat "more Quirks" as automatically better. They optimize the stack around the user's cognitive/behavioral capacity and intended function.

That mirrors the hero side in a morally inverted form. Endeavor's V26 training taught decomposition and automaticity so a person could layer functions responsibly. Gigantomachia's stack is engineered so the **person need not exercise much interpretive complexity at all**. Design reduces agency cost while maximizing utility to the master.

The profile therefore belongs in the power-philosophy and villain-formation infrastructure as evidence for **capability architecture shaped around obedience and low-control complexity**.

# 7. No.281 - Shigaraki's social critique becomes explicit causal doctrine

## 7.1 Endeavor's `beliefless destruction` diagnosis is no longer sufficient

Endeavor challenges Shigaraki as someone who has gained enormous power without a legitimate conviction behind the destruction (`V29:p087-088`). Shigaraki's answer matters because it marks a real developmental change from earlier volumes in which hatred/destruction frequently exceeded articulate doctrine.

He now says, in effect, that conviction existed all along.

This should not be inflated into a coherent political program. Shigaraki is not presenting a workable alternative institution, distributive policy, or reparative social design. But V29 does establish that his destructive project is no longer analytically reducible to arbitrary nihilism.

## 7.2 Hero society's failure is framed as accumulated covering-over

At `V29:p091-094`, Shigaraki argues that heroes and society have repeatedly ignored what could not be protected, covered over pain, and built the present order on top of those wounds. The language is accusatory and generalized, but the visual return to child Tenko gives the accusation a specific experiential base.

The central image is not that nobody saw him. People did see him.

That is precisely the problem.

A person can be socially legible and still remain unrescued because each observer treats recognition as somebody else's jurisdiction. This substantially deepens the corpus's recognition/failed-rescue thesis:

> **Legibility is insufficient when recognition does not create responsibility ownership.**

That proposition also recontextualizes V28's Search analysis. Information about who needs help is not itself rescue. The social system must convert information into a named obligation.

## 7.3 `理解できなくていい`: Shigaraki gives up on mutual intelligibility

The line `理解できなくていい できないから “ヒーロー”と“敵”だ` at `V29:p094` is one of the strongest speech-model markers in the volume.

Shigaraki is not asking Endeavor to understand him so reconciliation can occur. He uses failed understanding as a category boundary. Hero and villain become mutually exclusive roles because one side cannot accept the other's reason for being.

This distinguishes him from villains whose moral argument seeks public conversion. Shigaraki's current rhetoric is closer to **negative reciprocal authorization**:

- society negated me;
- I negate society;
- you need not understand;
- inability to understand confirms the opposition.

The result preserves accountability. The diagnosis that social wounds can be ignored is real evidence. The conclusion that the answer is generalized destruction remains his chosen destructive escalation.

V2 should therefore represent the sequence as:

**failed rescue / accumulated grievance -> generalized social diagnosis -> self-authorized total negation**,

not:

**society failed him -> therefore destruction is justified**.

# 8. Nana, Kotaro, and Gran Torino: protective decisions can transmit injury

## 8.1 The source preserves Nana's protective rationale

Gran Torino's memory after Shigaraki catastrophically wounds him is crucial because it complicates any simple "Nana abandoned Kotaro" shorthand.

The flashback establishes that the severing of traceability around Kotaro was intended to keep AFO from locating and exploiting him. Records/links were deliberately made difficult to follow. Nana's statement that she had no child belongs to that protective strategy rather than to indifference.

This is morally relevant information.

## 8.2 Protective rationale does not erase the child's experienced injury

The same memory preserves Kotaro's pain. The child experiences the hero role through loss: heroes save strangers and, in his experience, injure their own families. The volume does not ask the reader to choose between the adult intention and the child's consequence as though one must be false.

Both can be true:

- Nana acted under a credible threat from AFO.
- Severing the family relation was intended as protection.
- Kotaro experienced the separation as abandonment/injury.
- That injury became part of the family environment inherited by Tenko.

Gran Torino's thought about whether "our choice" was correct is therefore not a retroactive confession that there was an obviously easy alternative. It is recognition of **moral residue**: a decision can be defensible under its original constraints and still produce harm that later actors inherit.

This is one of V29's strongest additions to the inheritance theme. Inheritance is not only Quirks or ideology. It includes the consequences of prior protective choices.

# 9. Aizawa and the Quirk-destroying bullet: rationality becomes irreversible body triage

## 9.1 The bullet converts Eri's past into a present decision constraint

When Shigaraki obtains and fires the remaining Quirk-destroying ammunition, the threat is unusually concentrated. If Erasure is lost, the field loses its unique suppression mechanism. The bullet therefore targets not simply Aizawa's personal future but the containment architecture around him.

The visual connection to Eri matters because the bullet is a material residue of her exploitation. A power that was taken from a child through abuse now returns as a weapon against the teacher who is helping that child build a future.

## 9.2 Aizawa amputates before uncertainty can spread

At the opening of No.282, Aizawa severs the affected lower leg before the Quirk-erasure effect can propagate. The action is violent, immediate, and precisely consistent with his established decision policy.

This is not self-sacrifice as theatrical virtue. It is **field-preserving triage**:

- the threat is time-sensitive;
- the affected body part is expendable relative to Erasure;
- hesitation would endanger everyone;
- therefore the body is altered irreversibly to preserve function.

Aizawa's earlier rationality could sometimes be mistaken for emotional distance. V26–V29 make that reading untenable. His rationality is not a refusal to care; it is one mechanism by which care survives situations in which sentiment cannot be allowed to delay action.

## 9.3 Eri makes the rational decision more personal, not less

The Eri memory accompanying the act is analytically important. Aizawa can make the severe decision because there is a future in which Eri exists, trains, and may help restore what was damaged. But the important point is not "Eri will fix it, therefore the loss costs nothing." At the V29 boundary, later restoration outcomes remain unknown.

Instead, the memory supplies a reason to preserve Erasure **now**. Aizawa's relationship to Eri and Class 1-A makes future continuity valuable enough to justify present loss.

This deepens the V28 readiness promotion to `specialist_ready`: rational evaluation, survival ethics, teaching, grief, friendship, trauma, and bodily sacrifice now all cohere under one decision policy without collapsing into a single affective register.

# 10. Shoto's arrival and the failure of perfect containment

Shoto's arrival around `V29:p119` is operationally useful but also structurally significant. The battlefield keeps gaining relationships and support functions precisely because no single containment layer is permanent.

Aizawa's Erasure can be maintained only until bodily injury and direct attack make that impossible. Once suppression breaks, Shigaraki's active Quirks return to the problem. The correct lesson is not that the prior support was futile. Erasure bought time, constrained damage, and created windows for everyone else.

Volume 29 repeatedly refuses outcome-only ethics:

- A measure can be correct and still eventually fail.
- Temporary containment can be valuable even if the threat later escapes it.
- A person's body can be permanently damaged without the act becoming pointless.

Shoto enters that logic as another layer, not as a replacement solution. The war has become a chain of actors accepting the next responsibility when the prior actor's capacity is exhausted.

# 11. The Gigantomachia route: local failure becomes geographic consequence

The public/emergency route information around `V29:p123-126` transforms the mansion-front failure into a civil-defense problem. Gigantomachia's movement toward Jaku now has named settlements and civilian geography in its path.

This answers an important question about the earlier student operation. The stakes are no longer hypothetical. Every minute that Machia continues moving translates the incomplete success of the sedative operation into destroyed urban space and evacuation pressure.

Uraraka's refusal to treat the operation as meaningless is therefore ethically important. The correct reaction to partial failure is not nihilistic self-dismissal. Heroes continue evacuating because what can still be saved remains morally relevant.

That distinction becomes one of the volume's repeated claims:

> **Failure to stop all harm does not release an actor from responsibility for preventable remaining harm.**

This also resists the hero-society collapse into Symbol-of-Peace logic. There is no single gesture available that makes the population safe. Civilian survival depends on dispersed evacuation labor even while the spectacular battle proceeds elsewhere.

# 12. No.283 - `75`: incomplete bodies reveal the cost of oversized power

## 12.1 Yaoyorozu confronts the possibility that correct action did not work

At `V29:p128-132`, the student group is battered and uncertain. Yaoyorozu's self-assessment is especially severe: their survival may be attributable not to success but to Gigantomachia not even regarding them as meaningful enemies.

This is an important failure state for her model. Earlier Yaoyorozu confidence problems often emerged from comparison or defeat. V29 gives her something harder: she made the decision, people followed it, and the world does not provide immediate feedback that she was right.

Majestic's validation is therefore diagnostic. He tells the students that their decision and action were unquestionably correct even though the outcome remains unresolved and the threat continues.

That is a major readiness threshold. Yaoyorozu can now be modeled across:

- technical planning;
- peer coordination;
- independent command;
- incomplete information;
- legal/moral emergency judgment;
- failure self-appraisal;
- external professional validation after an apparently unsuccessful result.

This justifies promotion from `moderate (very strongly strengthened)` to **`strong`**.

## 12.2 `75` becomes causal rather than merely informational

On the hospital front, Shigaraki's body begins failing under the load it now carries. The displayed `75%` state at `V29:p141` confirms that V28's interrupted-procedure correction is not a cosmetic qualification. The unfinished body is a real constraint.

Shigaraki can possess extraordinary Quirk abundance and still be unable to sustain the body's designed operating envelope. This parallels the series' long-running insistence that power without bodily integration is not equivalent to usable mastery.

The comparison with Midoriya is obvious but should be made carefully. Midoriya's body/OFA problem and Shigaraki's engineered-body/AFO problem arise through radically different ethics and processes. What they share is a systems principle:

> **Power that exceeds embodiment produces failure even when the power itself is successfully acquired.**

That principle links V01 Midoriya, V17 Eri/Rewind interactions, V22 Blackwhip loss of control, V26 automaticity training, and V28–29 Shigaraki stabilization into a long-running power-philosophy thread.

# 13. Float: Nana's inheritance becomes literal rescue geometry

## 13.1 The V28 OPEN register resolves inside V29

At `V29:p143-144`, Float becomes operational. Midoriya uses Blackwhip together with airborne movement to lift allies away from ground contact and keep Shigaraki from re-establishing a Decay route through the terrain.

Gran Torino recognizes the seventh holder's Quirk, and Midoriya names `「浮遊」` as he commits to stopping Shigaraki in the air.

This is more than a new combat ability.

## 13.2 A predecessor's Quirk is used to carry other people's weight

Nana's Quirk enters the present through a rescue function that is almost diagrammatically aligned with the volume's ethics. The inherited power is not first presented as a personal mobility luxury. It is used to change the **relationship between vulnerable bodies and dangerous ground**.

Float therefore literalizes distributed containment:

- Decay turns connected ground into lethal infrastructure.
- Blackwhip connects Midoriya to other people.
- Float removes the connected group from the dangerous substrate.

Inheritance becomes the ability to carry others while also making Midoriya more central to the field's burden.

That second half matters. The more people he can support, the easier it becomes for him to imagine that he personally should absorb the cost of keeping everyone safe. Float expands rescue capacity and thereby intensifies the self-disposability problem.

# 14. OFA training becomes distributed pedagogy rather than private succession

The flashback embedded in No.284 is one of the most important institutional answers to AFO-style inheritance.

## 14.1 Class 1-A helps Midoriya train a power they do not inherit

At `V29:p149-152`, Midoriya asks classmates to help with Blackwhip and preparation for expected future Quirks. The group uses existing abilities and training arrangements to simulate demands he has not yet fully mastered, including the imagined feel of aerial movement before Float manifests.

This is a major reversal of solitary-successor logic.

OFA may be transmissible to one bearer at a time, but **mastery is socialized**. Peers can contribute perception, movement, pressure, feedback, and embodied analogues without possessing OFA themselves.

This makes the hero-side inheritance system structurally different from AFO's concentration model. AFO's system gathers powers into bodies selected/modified to contain them. Midoriya's system increasingly gathers **people around the bearer** so the bearer does not have to solve every adaptation alone.

## 14.2 All Might teaches anticipation rather than waiting for disaster

All Might's training logic is also developmentally important. The goal is to establish an image of Float and weightlessness before the Quirk erupts unexpectedly. V22 taught the cost of inherited power appearing before the successor has a model for regulating it. V29 shows the institutional answer: prepare the cognitive and bodily pattern in advance.

The method is therefore:

**prospective information -> simulated approximation -> relational practice -> lower-cost manifestation**.

This belongs in the power-philosophy ledger as a maturation of OFA pedagogy.

# 15. Bakugo and All Might: concern becomes explicit without changing Bakugo's surface register

## 15.1 Bakugo reads the missing information in the OFA record

Around `V29:p153-155`, Bakugo notices that the information about the fourth OFA holder is discontinuous in a way the records for other holders are not. He asks why the cause/information trail breaks.

This is high-value evidence for Bakugo's attention. He is not merely following Midoriya's percentages or combat output. He is auditing the **knowledge architecture around Midoriya's inherited risk**.

# 16. Bakugo's self-analysis: bullying is named as his weakness, not Midoriya's defect

The conversation around `V29:p156-157` is one of the most important character-modeling passages in the V2 reread.

This is a major accountability transition.

The current V2 model can now say with source support:

- Bakugo recognizes that Midoriya's behavior triggered insecurity/incomprehension in him.
- He explicitly locates wrongdoing in his own response.
- He does not retroactively deny that he bullied Midoriya.
- His current training/support behavior is partly reparative.
- He still retains competition, aggression, and status sensitivity.

This avoids two bad models at once.

The first bad model is **unchanged bully Bakugo**, in which every prosocial behavior is treated as merely tactical. V29 explicitly contradicts that.

The second bad model is **softened redeemed Bakugo**, in which accountability erases rivalry, abrasiveness, and competitive selfhood. V29 contradicts that too.

The stronger model is:

> **Bakugo's moral development works by incorporating accountability into the same intensely competitive self rather than replacing that self with a new personality.**

# 22. Power philosophy: usable power is embodied, relational, and morally underdetermined

V29 substantially develops the power-philosophy ledger along five axes.

## 22.1 Power exceeds usefulness when embodiment cannot carry it

The seventy-five-percent Shigaraki condition confirms a recurring series rule: acquiring power and being able to sustain it are different achievements. An unfinished body can be overwhelmed by the very capacity it was built to host.

# 23. Hero society: from command hierarchy to accountable delegation

Volume 29 is the strongest source yet for distinguishing **decentralization** from **abdication**.

A rigid command hierarchy cannot survive the war intact. Communications fail. Adults are injured. Students become the nearest capable specialists. Battlefield information is partial. Evacuation zones change. The system has to let judgment move.

But the series does not therefore celebrate pure individual improvisation.

The better cases include:

- Midnight naming Yaoyorozu as judgment owner;
- Yaoyorozu immediately building an information/role network;
- Majestic later validating the decision independent of outcome;
- Bakugo coordinating Endeavor rather than attempting to replace him;
- Class 1-A participating in OFA preparation even though Midoriya alone bears the Quirk;
- Aizawa sacrificing a body part to preserve a function on which others depend.

The failed cases/contrasts include:

- Tenko's bystanders assuming somebody else will act;
- Shigaraki's totalizing conclusion that institutional failure invalidates the entire social order;
- AFO attempting to reclaim bodily jurisdiction instead of entrusting successor judgment.

The V29 hero-society proposition is therefore:

> **Healthy decentralization names who owns the next decision, gives them usable support, and permits later evaluation without pretending that a good decision guarantees a good outcome.**

This also refines the student-militarization analysis. V29 does expose U.A. students to lethal national conflict and gives them decisions with adult consequences. That pressure is real. But the source repeatedly preserves distinctions:

- adult heroes try to retain/retake tasks when able;
- the students' specialist abilities explain why they are useful;
- student identity and return-to-school desire remain active;
- professionals validate or support rather than simply treating them as expendable troops.

The proper claim is therefore **accelerated wartime delegation under institutional strain**, not uncomplicated conversion of students into soldiers.

# 24. Recognition and failed rescue: responsibility is the missing conversion layer

The Tenko bystander memory makes V29 one of the most important recognition volumes in the corpus.

Earlier stages established that people can fail when nobody sees distress, when victims are stigmatized, when institutions sort by inadequate metrics, or when rescuers themselves are not recognized as rescue subjects.

V29 adds a different failure:

**the need is seen, but recognition never converts into ownership.**

That yields a revised chain:

`perception -> interpretation -> jurisdiction -> action -> follow-through`.

Failure can occur at any link.

- Search can make someone perceptually legible.
- A bystander can interpret a child as needing help.
- Yet if everyone assigns jurisdiction elsewhere, no action follows.

Midnight's delegation makes the contrast explicit by naming jurisdiction. Bakugo's endpoint pushes even further: repeated relationship and moral development can internalize jurisdiction until action happens before articulated interpretation.

Recognition is therefore neither inherently good nor sufficient. It must be attached to a policy for **who acts next**.

# 25. Villain formation: valid diagnosis can coexist with self-authorized annihilation

## 25.1 Shigaraki

V29 strengthens Shigaraki's causal and ideological model substantially.

He can now articulate:

- how hero society invisibilizes or covers over unprotected pain;
- how his own childhood experienced responsibility diffusion;
- how the hero role and his own identity have become mutually negating categories;
- why he interprets accumulated injury as evidence against the current order.

This is more than V06's hatred without a mature doctrine.

But the developmental endpoint remains destructive. Shigaraki converts diagnosis into authorization to negate everything that negated him. There is no requirement in his current model that destruction discriminate between responsible institutions, passive bystanders, children, victims, or comrades of the future. His conclusion is universalized from personal/social injury.

The proper V2 formulation is therefore:

**social failure explains the architecture of grievance; it does not justify the chosen totalization of harm.**

## 25.2 AFO and successor capture

The AFO-associated `lend me your body` language demonstrates a different villain-formation mechanism: recognition and empowerment can be used to **colonize agency** rather than restore it.

AFO's relationship with Shigaraki has always involved validation, resources, and mentorship. V29 makes clearer that those goods can be supplied in a relation whose eventual logic claims bodily jurisdiction.

This is why "villains need recognition" cannot function as a complete ethical prescription. Recognition must preserve the other's agency and constrain harm, or it can become grooming/patronage infrastructure.

## 25.3 Gigantomachia

The in-volume profile shows formation by functional optimization. The ideal subordinate is not taught broad moral judgment; the body/Quirk stack is engineered around simple capacities and extraordinary durability while loyalty remains master-centered.

This is person-to-function conversion by design.

# 32. V29 major model change

Volume 28 argued that catastrophic inherited power requires distributed containment. Volume 29 discovers the failure mode inside that answer: networks can distribute responsibility so widely that nobody owns it.

That is the hidden connection between the war fronts and Shigaraki's childhood memory.

The heroes succeed when responsibility moves **with a named recipient**.

Midnight cannot continue directing the Machia response, so she tells Yaoyorozu that judgment is entrusted to her. Yaoyorozu converts that jurisdiction into information gathering, technical production, and role assignment. Majestic later evaluates the process without pretending that good judgment guaranteed success.

Aizawa cannot preserve his body intact and preserve Erasure at the same time, so he chooses what function he will own and accepts irreversible cost.

Midoriya cannot allow Shigaraki to touch the ground, so he uses inherited Float and Blackwhip to carry other people while taking increasing cost onto himself.

Bakugo sees the hidden failure inside Midoriya's solution. Midoriya keeps counting everybody except Midoriya. Bakugo's long development has made that flaw legible to him because he once responded to the same quality with fear, contempt, and bullying. He now responds by training with Midoriya, planning around him, naming his own wrongdoing, and finally placing his own body between Midoriya and the attack.

Shigaraki's childhood supplies the anti-model. An adult sees Tenko and imagines that another institution will eventually own the rescue. The social network contains perception but no jurisdiction transfer. That is how distributed capacity becomes distributed evasion.

AFO supplies a second anti-model. When Shigaraki's capacity falters, AFO does not entrust the successor with judgment. The internal voice asks to borrow the successor's body. The predecessor seeks to collapse jurisdiction back into himself.

The hero-side ideal that emerges between those failures is therefore neither isolated autonomy nor total collective ownership.

It is **accountable delegation**.

> **Responsibility can move between people without becoming nobody's responsibility, and support can constrain a person's self-destructive choices without taking away their agency.**

This becomes the volume's deepest institutional and relational proposition.

It also clarifies why Bakugo's endpoint matters beyond character redemption. `身体が勝手に動いてた` is what accountable responsibility looks like when it has become fully embodied. He does not need an external authority to tell him that Midoriya is a rescue subject. He does not need Midoriya to ask. He does not need time to convert recognition into jurisdiction. The relationship has already done that work.

V29 therefore completes a long movement from **being told who needs saving** toward **becoming the kind of person for whom another person's need automatically counts**.
