## 2. Governing transition


Volume 13 established **distributed professional trust / integrated responsibility**. Rescue became a network of triage, reassurance, handoff, and role distribution; the post-Symbol institution explicitly rejected another `一強` as its only answer; and Bakugo's private guilt finally acquired a receiver in Midoriya.


Volume 14 asks the next question: once authority is distributed, **what makes any particular claim to authority legitimate?**


All Might chose Midoriya, but Nighteye disputes that choice. Bakugo defeats Midoriya, but All Might refuses to convert that victory into a verdict about which boy has the superior hero philosophy. Mirio looks nearly invincible, but the source insists his apparent natural superiority is an artifact of relentless bodily learning and prediction. Overhaul has a plan and organizational resources, but his preferred form of order is subordination. The League is comparatively chaotic, but Magne insists that its members' `居場所` is theirs to choose. U.A. can understand why Midoriya and Bakugo fought and still punish them for violating rules. Nighteye can hire Midoriya and still refuse to recognize him as the rightful successor.


The governing transition is therefore:


> **distributed professional trust / integrated responsibility → earned legitimacy / reciprocal mastery**


The volume repeatedly distinguishes **designation** from **legitimation**:


- being chosen does not finish the work of becoming worthy of the choice;
- winning does not by itself determine the correct heroic standard;
- possessing a Quirk does not equal mastering it;
- holding institutional rank does not make every judgment uncontestable;
- having organizational force does not make coercive hierarchy relationally legitimate;
- being admitted into professional practice does not mean the evaluator has accepted one's self-conception.


V14's strongest compact claim is:


> **Authority becomes legitimate only when it can survive reciprocal testing—by rivals, mentors, institutions, bodies, colleagues, and the people who must live under its consequences.**


## 3. Ground Beta resolves V13 without turning the fight into a moral referendum


### 3.1 Bakugo wins the fight; the story refuses to make that the philosophical verdict


At `V14:p039`, Bakugo's local result is unambiguous: `俺の勝ちだ`. That matters because the analysis should not soften the competitive reality. Midoriya does not secretly win by having a purer heart, nor does Bakugo's disclosure make combat consequence-free.


But the sequence immediately refuses the more tempting inference that Bakugo's victory proves Bakugo's hero ideal superior. When All Might intervenes, his answer is not “Bakugo was right” or “Midoriya was right.” He identifies the incompleteness in both boys' inheritances.


At `V14:p043`, he explicitly pairs Bakugo's fixation on victory with Midoriya's desire to save people and states that if either side is missing, one cannot fully carry through one's own justice as a hero. The formulation culminates at `V14:p044`:


`救けて勝つ / 勝って救ける`


This is the most explicit source confirmation yet that the two boys have not inherited competing complete All Mights. Each has preferentially modeled one publicly visible dimension of the same hero.


### 3.2 The rescue/victory synthesis is not symmetrical flattening


The volume does not say that rescue and victory are identical or that the boys are interchangeable. Their attentional priorities remain different.


Midoriya has repeatedly moved toward people in distress before possessing a viable victory plan. Bakugo has repeatedly treated defeat as existentially contaminating and has often regarded help-receiving as status loss. V14 does not erase those histories. Instead, it gives each boy an external standard supplied by the dimension he has underweighted.


For Midoriya, “win” becomes a constraint against rescue that cannot actually secure the victim or ends in self-destruction. For Bakugo, “save” becomes a constraint against victory that cannot be used by frightened, injured, or dependent people.


The synthesis is therefore developmental rather than rhetorical:


> **Midoriya must make rescue reliably victorious; Bakugo must make victory reliably protective.**


### 3.3 All Might assumes adult responsibility for the burden Bakugo carried


One of the most important V2 additions is All Might's response to Bakugo's Kamino guilt. V13 froze Bakugo at `俺は……オールマイトを終わらせちまってんだ`, with Midoriya recognizing himself as the only available receiver. V14 adds the adult who should have been able to receive it.


At `V14:p043`, All Might apologizes to Bakugo and frames the failure as partly his own: he has been a hero for too long and did not adequately teach a child how to process the events surrounding Kamino. At `V14:p049`, he states that transferring power to Midoriya was **his choice** and reiterates that Bakugo is not responsible for the consequence.


This is not absolution by sentiment alone. It is causal redistribution. Bakugo had privately assigned himself responsibility for a chain of events whose decisive adult choices included All Might's own succession, the rescue operation, and All Might's decision to fight AFO. V14 has the adult actor explicitly reclaim responsibility from the child who had internalized it.


That is a significant extension of the V11–V13 All Might model:


- V11: he accepts Inko's demand that mentorship require survival rather than sacrificial promise;
- V13: `私は死なないぞ` shows behavioral uptake;
- V14: he accepts responsibility for the emotional consequences of his secrecy and hero role on Bakugo.


Post-Symbol All Might is learning that responsible mentorship includes **owning downstream burdens generated by adult decisions**.


## 4. `三人`: the One For All secret becomes a triangular relationship


### 4.1 Bakugo is no longer merely the outsider who inferred the pattern


V13 established Bakugo's causal reconstruction: Midoriya's Quirk, All Might's relationship to him, Kamino, and the cryptic phrase about receiving power all formed a pattern Bakugo could no longer ignore.


V14 changes the epistemic relationship. At `V14:p046`, Bakugo demonstrates that he understands the secrecy itself—`バレたくねェんだろ オールマイト`—and All Might confirms that the matter is to remain `ここだけの秘密だ`.


At `V14:p050`, Midoriya narratively recognizes the change: what had been “my and All Might's secret” has become a secret shared by three people.


That is a relationship-state change, not only an information transfer. Bakugo becomes an **authorized knower**.


### 4.2 Authorization does not make Bakugo a successor


The volume is careful not to confuse access to succession knowledge with succession itself. Bakugo does not receive OFA, does not become a co-holder, and does not acquire authority to revise All Might's original designation. His role is different: he becomes a witness, critic, technical peer, and rival who can now interpret Midoriya's development with knowledge that the rest of Class 1-A lacks.


This distinction matters for later character reconstruction. A Bakugo who knows the secret can speak more directly about Midoriya's power without becoming emotionally open in every other domain. V14 therefore predicts a specific communication asymmetry:


> Bakugo may become unusually precise and useful about Midoriya's development while remaining abrasive, competitive, and guarded in ordinary emotional language.


The volume immediately validates that prediction.


## 5. Institutional discipline survives emotional legitimacy


Aizawa catches the boys after the fight. All Might can explain that he has known there is a special history between them since before enrollment, but that does not erase the rule violation.


At `V14:p052–053`, Aizawa imposes concrete consequences: Bakugo receives four days of dorm confinement and Midoriya three, with common-space cleaning and written reflection requirements. Their injuries are separately routed to medical care.


The sequence continues a pattern established in V10–V13:


> **A morally intelligible motive and an institutionally legitimate action are not the same thing.**


The fight did relational work. It allowed Bakugo to externalize guilt and allowed Midoriya to receive it. It also violated rules, caused injury, and required adult intervention. V14 refuses to make either layer cancel the other.


Aizawa's legitimacy comes partly from this dual capacity: he can recognize context without surrendering procedure.


## 6. Rivalry becomes a reciprocal technical interface


The post-fight low-stakes sequence matters because it shows that the relationship change is not confined to confession.


During cleanup at `V14:p055–056`, Bakugo critiques Midoriya's Shoot Style in concrete tactical terms. He identifies predictability, reaction windows, and the problems Midoriya will face in close-range exchange. Midoriya listens rather than reading the criticism solely as hostility.


This is new behavioral territory for both characters.


Earlier Bakugo often observed Midoriya with intense precision, but that observation was filtered through status threat: “Why can he do this?”, “Why is he improving?”, “What does this imply about me?” V14 begins converting the same observational ability into **usable peer feedback**.


Midoriya likewise changes. His old pattern toward Bakugo combined admiration, fear, rivalry, and compulsive observation. He now accepts Bakugo as someone whose different hero grammar can expose blind spots in his own technique.


The relationship therefore shifts from:


`asymmetrical admiration / humiliation / threat → adversarial parity → burden reception → reciprocal developmental pressure`


It is not friendship in an ordinary low-temperature sense. It is an increasingly productive relationship whose native language remains competition.


## 7. Work-study extends professionalization beyond examination


### 7.1 The provisional license is not a graduation certificate


Chapter 122 introduces a deeper work-study architecture. The students have passed a licensing gate, but the institution does not treat that as completion. The next layer is longer-term contact with professional offices.


Aizawa distinguishes the internship/work-study structure from the earlier short workplace-experience model. The crucial change is duration and relationship continuity: students can remain tied to a professional office and accumulate field experience rather than merely sampling a job for one week.


Participation also depends on student initiative and professional connection. Sports Festival visibility and earlier contacts become social infrastructure for later training.


The professional model thus becomes:


`credential → access → apprenticeship → repeated field feedback → revised competence`


rather than:


`credential → competence presumed`.


### 7.2 This extends V13's `群` model into pedagogy


V13's Commission response to All Might's retirement was not “find one replacement immediately,” but strengthen a distributed population of heroes. V14 gives that population-development problem a pedagogical form.


A coordinated hero society cannot be generated by classroom instruction alone. It requires many offices, mentors, specialties, local practices, and repeated work relationships. Professional formation is itself networked.


This is important because it prevents the `群` thesis from becoming merely numerical. A group is not stronger just because it contains more licensed bodies. It becomes stronger when its members can **learn in relation to differentiated professional environments**.


## 8. Mirio Togata: mastery is something built into the body


### 8.1 The Big Three entrance initially looks like a talent hierarchy


Mirio's demonstration against Class 1-A is deliberately staged to make him appear almost absurdly superior. He is difficult to touch, predicts student behavior, moves through space at angles that initially seem impossible, and ends the exchange before the class can construct a stable model of him.


The title `無敵` captures the first-person experience of the students: he appears invincible.


But the explanatory sequence then undercuts the easiest interpretation.


### 8.2 Permeation is not presented as a convenient automatic advantage


Aizawa's explanation around `V14:p100–101` emphasizes that Mirio's Quirk is not simply an enviable, self-executing gift. The apparently seamless movement is the product of technical refinement. Aizawa identifies Mirio as extraordinarily close to No.1, but the explanation is inseparable from the work that made a difficult ability operational.


This is a direct thematic cousin of Midoriya's V12 Shoot Style correction.


Midoriya had to stop treating “inherit All Might's power” as “use All Might's bodily grammar.” Mirio has similarly learned that a Quirk becomes useful only when a person can translate its awkward physical rules into a stable action language.


The relevant philosophy is:


> **Power is not the capacity listed on the character sheet. Power is the repeatable interface between capacity, body, perception, timing, and practiced decision.**


### 8.3 Prediction is trained social cognition, not only a Quirk category


Mirio's combat looks predictive before Nighteye's literal Foresight appears. He reads where people are going, what they are likely to do, and how their bodies commit to an action. His advantage therefore depends on accumulated embodied expectations.


This creates a useful contrast when Nighteye's Quirk later makes future information literal. Mirio demonstrates that professional anticipation also exists in ordinary cognition: experience compresses recurring patterns into fast prediction.


### 8.4 Readiness implication


V14 gives Mirio several regimes rather than one showcase:


- joking, socially warm presentation;
- instructional contact with younger students;
- combat demonstration;
- explicit relation to difficult-skill mastery;
- guidance of Midoriya toward Nighteye;
- supportive reaction to Midoriya's stated hero goal;
- professional office affiliation and first patrol context.


That is enough for an **emerging (strongly grounded)** model, but not yet enough to promote him to moderate. The most important missing evidence is what happens when his professional confidence collides with irreversible moral or rescue cost. That remains OPEN at V14.


## 9. Midoriya's integrated hero goal becomes self-authored language


The Ground Beta lesson would be weaker if it remained only All Might's interpretation of the boys. Chapter 126 gives the held-out consequence.


Mirio asks Midoriya what kind of hero he wants to become. Midoriya's answer at `V14:p151` is no longer his earliest “save people with a smile” formulation in isolation. After reflecting on how difficult the path has proved, he says:


`必ず勝って / 必ず助ける`


and identifies that as the standard of the `最高のヒーロー` he wants to become.


This matters in three ways.


First, the language is **Midoriya's**, not All Might's. He has incorporated the rival's missing dimension into his own objective.


Second, he does not abandon rescue. “Win” is added as a reliability condition on rescue.


Third, the sequence occurs after Bakugo has won their fight and given technical criticism. Midoriya can absorb information from defeat without translating it into loss of identity.


That is a meaningful self-authorship advance.


## 10. Mirio is a counterfactual successor without being written as an antagonist


The volume could have made the successor question psychologically easy by making Mirio arrogant, resentful, or obviously unfit. It does the opposite.


Mirio listens to Midoriya's aspiration and supports it. He provides access to Nighteye rather than guarding status. His competence is visible, his social warmth is genuine, and his mastery has been earned through work.


That means the question “Would Mirio have been a better successor?” cannot be dismissed as villain rhetoric or rival jealousy.


For Midoriya, this is a different legitimacy challenge from Bakugo.


Bakugo asks, in effect: “Why you rather than me?” from inside a history of rivalry and wounded hierarchy.


Nighteye asks: “Why you rather than a candidate I can concretely demonstrate is more professionally complete?”


Mirio himself does not weaponize the comparison.


That separation is analytically valuable. It allows the corpus to distinguish:


- **candidate quality**;
- **evaluator judgment**;
- **designator choice**;
- **candidate self-worth**;
- **relationship behavior between candidates**.


They are not the same variable.


## 11. Nighteye: loyalty to All Might does not mean deference to All Might's every choice


### 11.1 The first impression is obsessive attachment plus exacting standards


Nighteye's first meeting with Midoriya is comic and severe at once. He recognizes tiny inaccuracies in Midoriya's All Might face imitation and demonstrates an almost archival level of attention to All Might's public history and image.


This prevents a simplistic reading of his later disagreement. Nighteye does not oppose Midoriya because he lacks attachment to All Might. He opposes him **from inside an intense attachment**.


The character model therefore begins with a useful tension:


> reverence for a person does not require surrendering evaluative independence from that person's decisions.


### 11.2 Foresight makes evaluation literally asymmetrical


Nighteye's test gives him information Midoriya does not possess. Once his Foresight is active, he can anticipate Midoriya's movements and make the student's normal adaptation loop appear inadequate.


But the test is not only about the Quirk. It is designed to expose what Midoriya does when an evaluator appears to know the outcome in advance.


Midoriya responds by trying to exceed the expected pattern rather than treating predicted failure as final identity.


This is an important contrast with earlier forms of authority. He is no longer the quirkless child waiting for All Might to tell him whether he can be a hero. He is a selected successor arguing that an external evaluator must eventually update.


## 12. Designation is a fact; legitimacy remains contestable


### 12.1 Nighteye states the counterfactual directly


At `V14:p178`, Nighteye says:


`やはりワン・フォー・オールは ミリオに継がせるべきだった`


The sentence matters because it is not a vague statement that Mirio is talented. Nighteye explicitly disputes the succession choice.


At `V14:p180`, he sharpens it:


`ワン・フォー・オールには もっとふさわしい人物がいる`


and:


`私は貴様を認められん`


All Might's choice is therefore neither erased nor treated as self-validating.


### 12.2 Midoriya does not answer with inherited entitlement


Midoriya could answer, “All Might selected me, therefore the argument is over.” He does not.


Instead, he anchors himself in the fact of All Might's choice while treating recognition as something he still has to win. At `V14:p182`:


`僕を選んでくれたんだ!!! / 絶対 認めさせてみせます!`


The distinction is crucial.


Midoriya does not surrender the designation, but neither does he confuse designation with completed proof.


The emergent legitimacy model is:


`designation → contested performance → demonstrated benefit → recognition`


not:


`designation → unquestionable authority`.


### 12.3 Nighteye's hire is explicitly not recognition


Chapter 128 makes the structure even clearer. At `V14:p194`, Nighteye announces Midoriya's hiring even though Midoriya did not accomplish the literal stamp objective. He reveals that he had decided to hire Midoriya before the test and never said failure to take the stamp would mean rejection.


At `V14:p195`, he says the test showed Midoriya is not yet a useful enough person and then states:


`だが 認めたわけではない`


He wants Midoriya to experience professional reality and understand who is actually fit to fill the post-Symbol gap.


At `V14:p196`, the narration makes Nighteye's intent even harsher: because OFA is transferred by the holder's will, the hire is partly `諦めさせる為の採用`—employment intended to make Midoriya confront Nighteye's evaluation rather than a grant of legitimacy.


This is one of the cleanest institutional distinctions in the reread:


> **Access to an institution can be a test of legitimacy rather than a declaration of legitimacy.**


## 13. Nighteye and Midoriya are both trying to make the future update


The Foresight motif creates a deeper structural irony.


Nighteye possesses a Quirk whose basic grammar is privileged future information. Midoriya's response is to try to produce action that exceeds the evaluator's prediction. The later succession argument repeats that structure at the social level.


Nighteye believes there is a more appropriate successor and creates professional conditions meant to make Midoriya confront that judgment.


Midoriya believes All Might's choice can be made justifiable through what he becomes.


Both are therefore future-oriented, but in different ways:


- Nighteye uses present evaluation to constrain the likely future;
- Midoriya uses present effort to make current evaluation obsolete.


V14 does **not** resolve who is ultimately right. It establishes the conflict as a longitudinal test.


## 14. Overhaul: organized power without reciprocal legitimacy


### 14.1 The underworld gap mirrors the hero-side post-Symbol gap


Overhaul's introduction is placed in the same historical vacuum that is restructuring hero society. With All Might retired and AFO removed from ordinary underworld command, the source presents an environment in which old hierarchies and new villain coalitions can compete to define the next order.


Overhaul recognizes the vacuum strategically. He is not introduced as a purely impulsive destroyer. He analyzes organizational weakness and asks who will become the next ruler.


This immediately differentiates him from V09 Muscular's predatory hedonism and from early Shigaraki's more reactive destruction.


### 14.2 `俺の傘下に入れ`: his preferred solution is subordination


At `V14:p132`, Overhaul's proposition is direct:


`俺の傘下に入れ`


followed by the claim that he will use the League and become the next ruler.


He has a plan, but the plan's social grammar is hierarchical instrumentalization. Other people are resources inside an order centered on his design.


That becomes especially visible because the League has just acquired a different kind of cohesion.


### 14.3 Magne defines the League as chosen habitat


Before Overhaul kills her, Magne frames the League in terms that echo V13 Twice's `居場所`. The League is not legitimate to her because Shigaraki possesses formal title. It is legitimate because its members have found a place in which imposed conventions no longer determine their right to exist.


Her formulation at `V14:p133–134` culminates in the claim that **they decide their own place**.


That does not make the League morally good. It makes its internal attachment structure analytically different from Overhaul's proposal.


The comparison is:


- League: heterogeneous motives, chosen belonging, uneven but real peer attachment;
- Overhaul: centralized plan, instrumental subordinates, order through vertical control.


The volume therefore extends the V13 `群` thesis into an ethical-neutral organizational question: **distributed organization can be protective or destructive, but its internal legitimacy depends on how agency is allocated.**


## 15. Magne's death and the cost of the hierarchy clash


Overhaul kills Magne almost immediately after the verbal conflict. Compress then loses an arm when he tries to attack.


This scene is important for more than establishing Overhaul's threat level.


First, it demonstrates that Overhaul's planning language is backed by willingness to convert people into disposable costs. He can shift from negotiation to lethal force without needing emotional escalation.


Second, the League's reaction shows that Magne is not an interchangeable asset. Toga and Compress react as members whose associate has been killed, while Shigaraki's anger is directed at accountability as well as dominance.


Third, the exchange exposes a weakness in both organizations. Overhaul wanted controlled recruitment and instead produces bloodshed; Shigaraki cannot simply destroy the intruder because Overhaul has prepared external support and because further mutual losses would be strategically wasteful.


At `V14:p141`, Overhaul explicitly argues that reducing each other's fighting strength is pointless and proposes talking again after both sides cool down. The proposal does not repair the injury. It converts violence into a new bargaining condition.


The most defensible V14 conclusion is therefore not “Shigaraki accepts Overhaul.” He does not. It is:


> **Shigaraki is now capable of remaining inside a conflict whose immediate emotional answer is destruction because organization-level survival requires delayed adjudication.**


That strengthens the longitudinal claim that his V08–V13 maturation is producing decision rules, but it does not prove stable alliance or independent strategic mastery.


## 16. Overhaul's contamination register is behavioral infrastructure


Throughout his introduction, Overhaul reacts strongly to touch, contamination, and bodily mess. The beaked mask and repeated disgust register create a stable surface behavior before the source fully explains its causal origin.


The correct V14 posture is to record the behavior without importing later diagnosis.


Supported now:


- touch is treated as contaminating or dirty;
- he avoids unnecessary bodily contact;
- his Quirk produces grotesque bodily destruction through contact;
- his organizational rhetoric treats people as things to be placed under a plan;
- he responds to disorder by imposing hierarchy.


OPEN at V14:


- complete causal origin of the contamination response;
- full mechanics and ideological purpose of later research;
- the complete meaning of his relationship to Eri;
- every later claim that requires evidence from the raid or its aftermath.


This boundary is important because the historical V1 reading jumped rapidly to “clinical instrumentalization” using V14–V15 together. V14 supports the **organizational and behavioral skeleton** of that interpretation, but not yet its full Eri-centered causal proof.


## 17. Hero-side and villain-side authority are now structurally comparable


V14 is useful because it does not reserve questions of authority for institutions coded as legitimate.


On the hero side:


- U.A. disciplines students while allowing contextual explanation;
- work-study offices decide who receives practical access;
- Nighteye challenges All Might's successor choice from within the hero profession;
- Mirio's status comes from demonstrable mastery, not merely school prestige.


On the villain side:


- Overhaul claims legitimacy through plan, force, and hierarchy;
- Shigaraki claims leadership inside a coalition whose members have chosen it as a place;
- Magne explicitly rejects externally imposed conventions;
- violence becomes part of bargaining over organizational sovereignty.


The series is therefore asking a general rather than faction-specific question:


> **Why should another person accept your authority?**


Possible answers include designation, expertise, coercion, belonging, demonstrated competence, institutional procedure, and reciprocal recognition. V14 does not treat these as morally equivalent, but it puts them in the same analytical field.


## 18. The first Eri encounter is an opening signal, not yet the moral crisis


### 18.1 The professional investigation begins before the child appears


After Midoriya is hired, Nighteye explains the first work-study task. His agency is monitoring the Shie Hassaikai and its young head, Overhaul. The office lacks sufficient evidence for direct action and therefore needs surveillance and information gathering.


That framing matters. Midoriya enters the street already inside a professional epistemic constraint: suspicion is not the same as proof.


### 18.2 Eri's body produces immediate concern


At `V14:p199–201`, a small bandaged child runs into the patrol. Midoriya notices her distress, apologizes for hurting her, and asks whether she can stand. Her body language changes sharply when the pursuing adult arrives.


The source supports the following at V14:


- Eri is a frightened child;
- she is visibly bandaged;
- she has fled into public space;
- she seeks/accepts proximity to Midoriya before recoiling at the arriving adult;
- Midoriya's attention immediately shifts toward her physical and emotional state.


The source does **not yet** support the complete later explanation of why she is bandaged, what Overhaul has done, or what professional decision Midoriya and Mirio will make next.


### 18.3 The endpoint is deliberately unresolved


At `V14:p202`, the pursuing man is revealed as Overhaul and says:


`帰るぞ エリ`


The page ends with `始まった`.


This is not the “failure to save Eri” scene yet. It is the collision point between three structures already built by the volume:

2. Nighteye's evidence-constrained professional investigation;

## 21. Visual and formal architecture


### 21.1 Ground Beta closes through triangulation


The fight is visually two-person, but its resolution requires a third figure. All Might physically and narratively enters the space after the boys have exhausted the logic available inside their dyad.


The composition therefore converts rivalry into triangle:


`Midoriya ↔ Bakugo` becomes `Midoriya ↔ Bakugo ↔ All Might`.


That triangle is then made literal through the shared secret.


### 21.2 Mirio's “invincibility” is drawn as missing information


The class experiences Mirio through discontinuity: attacks miss, angles change, his body disappears from expected trajectories. The page makes mastery look like magic because the observer lacks the procedural model.


The later explanation reverses the visual effect. What looked like supernatural ease becomes trained control over a difficult body/Quirk interface.


### 21.3 Foresight externalizes evaluator asymmetry


During Nighteye's test, Midoriya's movements are visually treated as already-known trajectories. The student experiences an evaluator who seems to have pre-written the result.


His response—accelerating, changing patterns, protecting All Might memorabilia while attempting the stamp—makes the scene formally about the attempt to create **surprise inside a predicted space**.


This visual grammar supports the volume's legitimacy thesis: a current judgment is powerful, but the candidate still acts toward a future in which that judgment must change.


### 21.4 Overhaul's mask and touch field mark relational distance


Overhaul's plague-mask silhouette, gloves, and disgust response produce a visual grammar of separation. He wants people organized beneath him but does not want them bodily close to him.


That is an effective formal contrast with the League's messier physical co-presence and with Eri's final movement toward Midoriya.


### 21.5 The volume ends on a collision rather than a conclusion


`V14:p202` brings Overhaul, Eri, and Midoriya into one frame and stops. The formal endpoint is therefore not “Midoriya failed to save Eri.” It is **competing claims of relation becoming simultaneously visible**:


- Eri has run toward the public/hero space;
- Midoriya is attending to her distress;
- Overhaul commands her return.


V15 must adjudicate what professional practice does with that collision.


## 22. Power philosophy and professional philosophy


V14 deepens the corpus's power model in four directions.


### 22.1 Heroic power must integrate outcome and recipient


`救けて勝つ / 勝って救ける` rejects both a rescue ethic that cannot secure the situation and a victory ethic that cannot protect people.


### 22.2 Power is embodied mastery


Mirio demonstrates that a technically awkward Quirk can become overwhelming through repeated bodily learning, prediction, and experience. This strengthens the V12 rule that professional capacity is not reducible to raw Quirk specification.


### 22.3 Designated power does not eliminate legitimacy work


OFA can be transferred by holder intent. That settles ownership, not social recognition. Nighteye's challenge makes succession ethically and professionally contestable even after the transfer is irreversible.


### 22.4 Organizational power has an internal social form


Overhaul and the League both want post-Symbol influence, but they distribute agency differently. A plan can be strategically sophisticated and still depend on coercive subordination. Belonging can be emotionally real and still serve destructive ends.


Power philosophy therefore now requires two separate questions:


1. **Can this actor or organization produce outcomes?**
2. **What kind of relationship to other persons makes those outcomes possible?**


## 23. Hero Society update


### 23.1 Post-Symbol society is becoming apprenticeship-heavy


The internship architecture turns professional offices into distributed training infrastructure. This is one practical mechanism by which V13's desired `群` can be produced.


### 23.2 All Might's authority becomes contestable without becoming irrelevant


Nighteye can revere All Might and still dispute his successor decision. This is a healthy structural change in one sense: the post-Symbol era cannot depend on one man's preferences being beyond review.


The series is therefore de-monopolizing not only combat power but **epistemic authority**.


### 23.3 Credentialing and recognition remain different


Midoriya has a provisional license, school approval, All Might's designation, and now Nighteye-office access. None of those alone answers whether he is professionally ready for the symbolic role others imagine.


The institution is becoming layered rather than singular.


## 24. Villain formation and organization update


V14 strengthens the argument that villain formation cannot be represented by one causal story.


Magne and Twice emphasize belonging. Shigaraki's development increasingly concerns leadership and strategic judgment. Overhaul arrives from a surviving yakuza structure with a domination/organization problem rather than Stain-like purity or Muscular-like pleasure.


This requires a new organizational distinction in the villain ledger:


- **grievance or desire explains why a person enters opposition**;
- **organizational form explains what kind of opposition becomes sustainable**.


The League's chosen habitat and Overhaul's proposed umbrella are different answers to that second question.

## 29. Closing synthesis


Volume 14 is a volume about what happens **after someone has been chosen**.


Bakugo has chosen All Might's victory ideal, Midoriya has chosen All Might's rescue ideal, and the boys discover that neither partial inheritance is enough. All Might has chosen Midoriya as OFA successor, and Nighteye demonstrates that the professional world does not have to treat that choice as the end of the argument. U.A. has chosen to license and train these students, but it still disciplines them when their meaningful private conflict violates institutional procedure. Mirio has a difficult Quirk, but what makes him look invincible is not possession; it is mastery constructed through experience. Overhaul has a plan for the underworld, but his plan's claim to authority is coercive. The League's members are criminals, but their internal belonging is chosen strongly enough that Magne dies asserting the right to define their own `居場所`.


The volume's repeated answer is that **designation is only a beginning**.


A successor must justify succession. A rival must become useful to the rival. A mentor must take responsibility for what his choices do to children. A Quirk must become a body-specific technique. A credential must open into apprenticeship. An organization must persuade, bind, or coerce people into accepting its authority. A prediction can describe the future, but a person can still act in an effort to make the evaluator revise what that future means.


This is why V14's best compact formulation is:


> **Legitimacy is designation tested by relationship. Mastery is capacity tested by reality.**


The first fourteen-volume conceptual sequence is now:


`recognition → judgment → public legibility → self-authorship → recognition without possession → enacted identity → aftermath → diagnostic resistance → emergency enactment / cost-bearing → contested legibility / protective authority → accountable succession / de-centered support → operational self-authorship / reliable coordination → distributed professional trust / integrated responsibility → earned legitimacy / reciprocal mastery`


And the volume stops at exactly the right place to make the next test dangerous.


Midoriya has just entered a professional environment whose senior evaluator believes he is the wrong successor. He has declared that the highest hero must `必ず勝って 必ず助ける`. His first monitored field day immediately produces a frightened, bandaged child who has run into him while his office is investigating the man who comes to retrieve her.


At `V14:p202`, Overhaul says `帰るぞ エリ`.


Nothing after that sentence is V14 evidence.


Volume 15 must determine what earned legitimacy looks like when the person in front of the hero appears to need rescue **before the professional system has finished knowing what it is allowed to do**.
