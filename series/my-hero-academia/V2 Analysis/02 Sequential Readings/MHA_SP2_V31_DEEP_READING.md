# My Hero Academia - Second-Pass Deep Reading, Volume 31

## 0. Status, method, and prospective lock

This reading follows `MHA_SP2_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2_1.md` and begins from the exact frozen Volume 30 endpoint at `V30:p192`. It treats Volume 31 prospectively. Later-series knowledge is not admissible as evidence for resolving a V31 question.

The entering state is unusually unstable. Midoriya has just perceived the AFO-overwritten Shigaraki as though Shigaraki were asking to be saved (`V30:p188-192`), but that perception is still Midoriya's inference rather than narrator-certified fact. Shigaraki's prior destructive will and killings remain fully authoritative. AFO's coexisting consciousness has begun operating Shigaraki's body. The war has not yet been administratively or socially resolved. Bakugo is alive but severely wounded; the later condition of Aizawa, Hawks, Endeavor, Gran Torino and other injured participants remains prospective. Dabi is now publicly known to be Todoroki Toya, but the family and social consequences of the broadcast remain OPEN. Danger Sense has manifested without demonstrated mastery. Best Jeanist has returned alive. Gigantomachia has fallen under the delayed sedative effect, but later custody and system-wide war results remain OPEN.

# 1. Governing transition: contested inheritance becomes chosen responsibility after structure fails

Volume 30 made inheritance morally contested. OFA and AFO no longer looked like two neutral mechanisms with different users. They became opposed theories of succession: chosen co-presence versus possessive jurisdiction, successor agency versus predecessor capture. The volume ended by asking whether rescue can remain meaningful when the possible rescue subject is also an accountable perpetrator.

Volume 31 keeps both problems and scales them outward. The war destroys the background structures that previously allowed the word "hero" to feel settled. Rescue capacity is overwhelmed. Prisons fail. Police are overloaded. Commercial support items move into civilian hands. Professionals resign. Public confidence collapses. The top-ranked heroes can no longer rely on prestige as a substitute for legitimacy. U.A., built to train future heroes, is converted into a civilian refuge. The question "who is a hero?" loses its occupational shortcut.

The same stripping-away happens inside several characters.

- Hawks loses the HPSC structure that trained, named and commanded him, yet still chooses to help.
- Endeavor loses the possibility of hiding family history behind professional achievement and must decide what responsibility remains after public disgrace.
- Shoto refuses both paternal exculpation and the idea that family responsibility belongs to one broken person alone.
- AFO tries to turn Shigaraki's earlier desire for power into retroactive consent to becoming `次の僕`.
- OFA reveals that succession is biologically narrowing and may terminate with Midoriya; "passing it on" can no longer function as an automatic future answer.
- Midoriya is asked whether the rescue principle survives when stopping Shigaraki may require killing him.
- The volume then ends by removing Midoriya himself from the institutional shelter of U.A.

The governing transition is therefore:

**`contested inheritance under public truth, agency, accountability and the demand to save -> chosen responsibility after institutional, familial and succession structures cease to supply an easy answer`**

Compact formulation:

> **Volume 31 converts heroism from a licensed identity into a choice made after legitimacy, command and inherited scripts begin to fail. But it ends by exposing the danger inside that freedom: Midoriya can reject an inherited command about whom he must kill while still reproducing the older error of excluding himself from the people his rescue ethic protects.**

This double movement matters. "Choose for yourself" is not a moral solution by itself. AFO's domination is wrong partly because it destroys successor authorship, but self-authorship can still select destruction, isolation or bad judgment. V31 values agency while continuing to judge what agency does.

# 2. No.296 - catastrophe turns the war result into a systems problem

## 2.1 The operation has measurable victories without producing social victory

The immediate aftermath prevents a binary "heroes won / villains lost" reading. At `V31:p009-012`, integrated hero forces finish three Near High-Ends and secure huge numbers of Liberation Front participants. The source gives a precise arrest/secure count of **16,929**, while **132**, including executives, escape in the disruption created by Gigantomachia's advance. Shigaraki/AFO escapes with **seven remaining Near High-Ends**. Gigantomachia and Mr. Compress are in custody.

These figures matter because they show that the operation was not tactically meaningless. The heroes dismantled a massive organized force. Yet the volume refuses to let that success settle the political meaning of the battle. Cities are devastated, civilians remain trapped, professionals are dead or wounded, Shigaraki remains free, and the prison system will collapse almost immediately afterward.

The proper V2 claim is therefore not:

`the heroes lost the war`.

Nor is it:

`the heroes won because most PLF members were arrested`.

It is:

`the operation achieved large-scale tactical suppression while imposing losses and escapes severe enough to trigger a national legitimacy and security crisis.`

## 2.2 Rescue labor continues after the spectacular battle has ended

Pages `V31:p013-019` shift attention from superhuman combat to ordinary disaster rescue. Uraraka and other heroes/students move through destroyed urban space hearing multiple simultaneous pleas. The relevant constraint is no longer defeating one opponent. It is the mismatch between the number of people needing help and the number of available rescuers.

That makes the small pro-hero line at `V31:p019` diagnostic. In the middle of active cries for rescue, one professional thinks about finding another job. The page does not need to declare him evil. The more interesting fact is that professional identity can fail under accumulated stress exactly when social need is highest.

Hero society is therefore entering a capacity spiral:

`mass casualty -> rescuer overload -> professional attrition -> lower rescue capacity -> greater public fear/anger -> further professional attrition`.

The later resignation wave does not come from one scandal alone. The social system is being depleted materially and psychologically.

## 2.3 Midnight's death resolves a personnel register without reducing her to a casualty statistic

The students reach Midnight's body/location and the volume confirms her among the dead (`V31:p020-021`). This closes the V29-V30 uncertainty around her condition.

Her death has a specific structural meaning because V29 had just shown her entrusting Yaoyorozu with genuine judgment under emergency law. The last substantial V2 evidence for Midnight is therefore not merely that she dies. It is that she transfers adult responsibility to a student when communication and hierarchy are breaking, and the student later learns that the adult who entrusted her is gone.

That should remain available to later synthesis as a mentorship consequence, not only a casualty ledger entry.

# 3. No.297 - Tartarus: institutional containment and personhood fail together

## 3.1 The prison chapter begins with a dangerous dehumanizing vocabulary

At `V31:p031-032`, Tartarus guards discuss Gigantomachia and the prisoners. One guard says a being like Machia should not be called human but a disaster or beast; another warns him to stop because the statement is being recorded. The conversation then broadens into language suggesting some prisoners merely have human shape.

The evidence must be bounded. This is **guard discourse**, not narrator-certified Tartarus doctrine and not proof that every worker shares it. But it is analytically significant because the institution designed to contain people with extreme powers contains personnel who are tempted to solve the moral difficulty by withdrawing personhood.

The series repeatedly distinguishes `dangerous person` from `non-person`. Tartarus shows how easily a security institution can collapse that distinction.

The counterargument is obvious: some inmates are catastrophically dangerous, and detention infrastructure must act on threat capacity. V31 does not deny that. The warning is narrower: describing a person as dangerous can be operationally necessary; describing them as no longer human changes what treatment becomes thinkable.

## 3.2 AFO turns desire for power into retroactive consent to possession

The prison assault develops the succession conflict from V30 into an explicit theory of consent. Shigaraki says `俺の体を返せ` and `駒にはならない` (`V31:p035-037`). AFO answers by pointing out that Shigaraki wanted his power and by claiming Shigaraki's hatred invited him.

The rhetorical move is crucial. AFO converts:

`I wanted power from you`

into:

`therefore you authorized me to own the body that received it.`

That is not ordinary mentorship gone too far. It is retrospective consent manufacture.

AFO then supplies the clearest current succession statement: Shigaraki is not a pawn because he is AFO's precious `次の僕`. "Not a pawn" sounds like recognition until the predicate arrives. Shigaraki is elevated from disposable instrument to replacement self. The relation still destroys successor separateness.

This materially revises any earlier model of AFO as a mentor who merely wants his ideology continued. His preferred continuity is much more literal: the successor becomes the site through which the predecessor continues.

## 3.3 Synchronized internal/external AFO turns one consciousness relation into institutional breach

The Tartarus escape works because the AFO consciousness inside Shigaraki and the imprisoned original AFO act on coordinated timing (`V31:p040-041`). All cells/floors open, and high-risk inmates become available to a new coalition.

The architecture is almost an inversion of OFA. OFA accumulates persons as memory/presence inside one bearer while insisting that the bearer remains the current agent. AFO distributes one predecessor's control project across bodies and institutions while calling the resulting subordination continuity.

The mechanical similarity - more than one consciousness relation across time - therefore should not obscure the moral difference in jurisdiction.

# 4. No.298 - collapse propagates, but relationship-specific recognition still works

## 4.1 Tartarus is not an isolated prison break

By `V31:p051`, the source reports attacks on **seven prisons**, with prisoners released from **six**. This moves the crisis from one extraordinary facility failure to national security-system contagion.

The timing matters. Police and heroes are already depleted by the Liberation War. The prison attacks do not simply add "more villains" to the street. They attack a system whose response capacity has already been damaged.

The resulting social crisis in later chapters should therefore not be attributed only to Dabi's broadcast or public ingratitude. Material state capacity has collapsed.

## 4.2 Spinner knows the difference between Shigaraki and the body currently speaking through him

At `V31:p052`, Spinner rejects the current speaker: the person he follows is not "you." This is one of the strongest controls on AFO's claim that Shigaraki's desires and AFO's desires can be merged into an undifferentiated "we."

Spinner is not a neutral observer. His attachment to Shigaraki is itself politically and morally compromised. But that is why the evidence is useful. He knows Shigaraki relationally enough to notice the discontinuity.

AFO, in the same sequence, claims to respect Tomura's feelings above all. The rhetoric/action gap is now stark:

- he says he respects Shigaraki's feelings;
- he speaks through Shigaraki's body over explicit refusal;
- he calls the successor `次の僕`;
- he uses Shigaraki's hatred as a tool for OFA acquisition.

Respect language does not function as evidence of respect when jurisdiction is being seized.

## 4.3 Hospital scenes convert war abstractions back into bodies and relationships

Bakugo wakes after three days and immediately asks about others, especially Midoriya (`V31:p054-056`). Aizawa is alive but gravely changed by injury; Present Mic shares the grief of Midnight's death (`V31:p057`). Shoto is hospitalized with severe throat damage and watches the public consequences of Toya's reveal (`V31:p058-062`). Midoriya remains unconscious.

These scenes matter for character reconstruction because high-stakes action is followed by diagnostic help-receiving states. Several characters whose heroic identity is defined through action are now unable to act normally. The story asks whether relationship networks can carry them when competence temporarily cannot.

Shoto's conclusion, `I have to deal with Toya` (`V31:p062`), should not be translated into a lethal intention. The Japanese construction marks responsibility/necessity, not a specified method of killing. V31 will shortly revise even the "I" in that statement into a family `we`.

# 5. No.299 - Hawks after command: institutional conditioning does not exhaust the person

## 5.1 Hawks's origin explains both the reality and the danger of his hero formation

The childhood material at `V31:p067-073` gives Hawks an unusually clear transition from fictional hero image to lived political institution. His father is abusive and criminal; Endeavor's arrest of that father makes "hero" real to the child. Later, Hawks's rescue capacity is recognized and the HPSC-linked system reorganizes his life and identity around professional utility.

A simplistic reading can go in two opposite directions:

1. `Hawks was manufactured by the HPSC, so his hero motive is artificial.`
2. `Hawks always wanted to help, so the HPSC's control does not matter.`

V31 supports neither.

The child has genuine rescue/help orientation. The institution identifies and exploits that orientation, gives it training and opportunity, and also binds identity, secrecy and command around it. Genuine motive and institutional ownership coexist.

## 5.2 The Best Jeanist deception used a real body, not a costless stage prop

At `V31:p074-076`, the Best Jeanist infiltration explanation specifies advanced medical intervention that put Jeanist into a death-like state before later revival. This matters because it changes the moral texture of the deception. Hawks did not simply carry a fake corpse. Jeanist accepted genuine bodily risk to create a credible intelligence artifact.

The scene strengthens two character models:

- Jeanist is willing to subordinate his own body/status to an operation whose success he cannot publicly explain in advance.
- Hawks's deception depends on cooperative trust and other people's consent, not solely on manipulative brilliance.

## 5.3 Freedom becomes diagnostic only after the command structure disappears

The most important Hawks material comes at `V31:p079-085`. His mother has fled after villains reached her through information pressure. The HPSC command structure is effectively nonfunctional. Hawks says `縛るもんがなくなった`: the thing that bound him is gone.

Jeanist observes that a person's nature appears when they are cornered or when they gain freedom. Hawks answers by returning to Twice. He calls Bubaigawara a good person because he was desperately trying to be useful to others (`人の役に立とうと必死だった`). Hawks then says he wants to be like that too.

This is a crucial held-out validation of the Hawks model. After surveillance, order and institutional constraint weaken, he does **not** default to escape, vengeance or retirement. He chooses to help Endeavor because Endeavor is in trouble, beginning from his own origin rather than from a received directive.

The scene does not erase what Hawks did to Twice. In fact, it makes the moral wound harder to flatten. Hawks can believe the lethal decision was necessary while preserving Twice's personhood and admiring a trait in him. Recognition after killing is not exoneration; it is evidence that threat-management did not completely consume the human model of the target.

**Model consequence:** Hawks reaches `specialist_ready` in V27 and V31 validates that tier under a new regime: post-command freedom. His hero behavior is institutionally shaped but not reducible to obedience.

# 6. No.300 - hero society loses the occupational shortcut

## 6.1 Civilian support-item use shows why trust collapse produces more than bad publicity

Pages `V31:p087-092` show frightened civilians using commercially available support equipment because licensed protection is no longer reliably present. The impulse is intelligible: people are under attack and formal capacity is insufficient. But untrained force produces collateral damage and creates more rescue burden.

This is a systems loop, not an anti-civilian morality tale:

`lower state/pro capacity -> civilian self-armament -> variable competence and collateral -> more crisis load -> still lower perceived state/pro capacity.`

The series' licensing system was never only about professional monopoly. It also distributed training, liability, role expectations and coercive legitimacy. V31 demonstrates what happens when the public loses faith in that infrastructure faster than a replacement can emerge.

## 6.2 Resignation converts hero status into a stress test of motive

The retirement of No.9 Yoroi Musha at `V31:p093` catalyzes/embodies a wider wave: heroes continue resigning. The narration then states that the meaning of "hero" is being asked again and that heroes are being `篩に掛けられている` (`V31:p094-095`).

The metaphor is not morally neutral. It implies that prestige, income, rank and public adoration had allowed heterogeneous motives to coexist under one occupational label. Crisis removes those rewards and increases cost. What remains is more diagnostic of why someone stands.

That does not mean everyone who resigns is a fraud. Burnout, injury, fear and family responsibility can be legitimate. The text is making a structural rather than universal character claim: when a profession's reward/cost ratio reverses, the role label stops telling us as much about motive.

## 6.3 `I AM NOT HERE` is an inversion of reassurance infrastructure

The defaced All Might statue is one of V31's strongest visual statements. The old phrase "I am here" worked socially because it converted one person's presence into public reassurance. `I AM NOT HERE` marks the absence of that stabilizing function.

This is not merely a hostile message about All Might as an individual. It records a public condition: the institution built around the Symbol cannot reproduce the feeling of guaranteed arrival after the Symbol's retirement.

The later question is therefore not how to create another singular All Might. The volume keeps moving toward distributed, chosen responsibility.

# 7. Endeavor in the hospital: public strength cannot solve a family jurisdiction problem

## 7.1 Endeavor survives, but survival creates a responsibility he cannot solve by fighting harder

Endeavor wakes physically recoverable enough to face the next stage (`V31:p096-103`), but Toya's reveal breaks the simple professional script. He can fight villains as Endeavor. He cannot easily process a son whose present mass violence is inseparable from a family system he created.

The emotional collapse should not be mocked as too late, but neither should it be treated as atonement. Natsuo's challenge - why cry now? - keeps timing and victim perspective visible.

Rei's arrival changes the scene from solitary guilt to a family accounting. She has not come to soothe Endeavor into innocence. She has come to talk about Toya.

## 7.2 V31 separates remorse from usable responsibility

Endeavor's guilt is real. The family nevertheless refuses to let guilt become another form of withdrawal. This will become explicit in No.302: even a broken heart does not terminate the obligation to act.

That distinction prepares the press conference later in the volume. Feeling terrible is not the same thing as repairing what can still be repaired.

# 8. No.301-No.302 - Toya formation: asymmetric causation, distributed repair

## 8.1 The family project begins with Endeavor's successor logic

The flashback at `V31:p105-113` grounds the family structure in Endeavor's attempt to produce a child capable of surpassing All Might. Toya inherits unusually strong fire but a body adapted poorly to heat. A doctor warns about the danger of this kind of Quirk marriage/project. Endeavor nevertheless remains organized around the surpass-All-Might goal.

Toya's early desire for his father's attention is therefore not independent of the status project. The father creates a value system in which fire performance is the privileged route to recognition, then attempts to tell the child that other parts of life should matter after the child's body makes the project inconvenient.

The instruction may be substantively reasonable - school, friends and a broader life do matter - but Endeavor has damaged his credibility as the person delivering it. He is asking Toya to stop believing the metric Endeavor taught him to treat as the basis of worth.

## 8.2 More children do not merely expand the family; they become evidence of replacement

The decision to have additional children is partly framed as an attempt to produce the desired hybrid and to make Toya give up the impossible path (`V31:p113-116`). From Toya's perspective, siblings become proof that the father has moved the project to a more suitable candidate.

This does not make Toya's later aggression morally involuntary. It explains the relational environment in which sibling existence becomes status displacement.

At `V31:p120`, child Toya lunges toward the infant Shoto/family in a burst of fire while screaming for his father to look at him. The act belongs to Toya. Causal injury does not erase agency at the moment he harms others.

The correct formation model is therefore neither "Endeavor made Dabi, full stop" nor "Toya was always dangerous." It is a layered causal chain in which paternal engineering, bodily mismatch, attention withdrawal, replacement interpretation, repeated self-training, family fear and Toya's own harmful choices interact.

## 8.3 Rei sees a broader self for Toya, but recognition arrives inside a damaged authority structure

Rei tells the adolescent Toya to look beyond his father, classmates and the hero path and ask what he actually wants (`V31:p127`). This is one of the family's clearest attempts to reopen self-authorship.

Toya rejects her authority partly by weaponizing the conditions of her arranged marriage and the family's economic/social arrangement (`V31:p128`). The attack is cruel, but it targets a real structural vulnerability: he sees himself as a product of adults whose choices were constrained and instrumental.

Again, truthful grievance and permissible action must remain separate.

## 8.4 Endeavor's Sekoto failure is not simply "he did not know"

As Toya's flames become blue and stronger, he asks Endeavor to come to Sekoto Peak (`V31:p129-135`). Endeavor does not go. In retrospective discussion he cannot even articulate what he should have said. Rei states that she also could not stop the child.

The key causal asymmetry becomes explicit at `V31:p138`: Shoto tells Endeavor `全部 あんたが始めた事で あんたが原因だ` - all of this was something Endeavor started and he was the cause - **but** the family conversation continues from there.

That "but" prevents two flattenings.

- It prevents shared family responsibility from erasing primary causal authorship.
- It prevents primary causal authorship from becoming an excuse for every other family member to deny any present agency they still possess.

## 8.5 `Responsibility is not yours alone` means repair coalition, not equal blame

At `V31:p139`, Rei says `責任はあなただけのものじゃない`. In context, this cannot responsibly be translated as "we are all equally to blame for Toya." The immediately preceding pages explicitly locate the initiating cause in Endeavor. Rei, Fuyumi, Natsuo and Shoto instead identify narrower failures: not seeing enough, not confronting, withdrawing, not being able to stop the escalation.

The family's present policy is collective because the present problem exceeds one person's capacity. It is **distributed repair responsibility after asymmetric causation**.

This formulation is important beyond the Todorokis. The series repeatedly asks whether acknowledging structural or interpersonal causation destroys individual agency. V31's answer is no. Causal responsibility can be highly unequal while current repair responsibility is still shared.

## 8.6 Shoto becomes the family hero through relationships outside the family

Rei says Shoto made friends at U.A., kept the family connected and became `焦凍が轟家のヒーローになってくれた` (`V31:p140`). This is a major payoff to the series' relationship architecture.

Shoto does not repair the closed family system by becoming strong enough inside it. External relationships make new forms of selfhood available to him; those changes then flow back into the family. The family is repaired, to the degree it is being repaired at all, by a member who learned relation elsewhere.

Shoto's subsequent line, `皆で燈矢兄を止めに行こう` (`V31:p141`), revises his earlier hospital conclusion that **he** had to handle Toya. Responsibility becomes plural.

The method remains OPEN. "Stop Toya" is not yet a settled kill/capture/save policy. Later volumes must determine how the family operationalizes the commitment.

# 9. No.303 - Top 3: rank becomes coalition rather than hierarchy

## 9.1 Hawks and Best Jeanist enter the Todoroki-family crisis without claiming family authority

Hawks and Best Jeanist arrive at the hospital during/after the family decision (`V31:p143-149`). Hawks explicitly compares his own difficulty facing family with Shoto's capacity to do so, strengthening his recognition of Shoto rather than treating the Todoroki problem as a purely professional scandal.

The professionals then move toward a **Top 3 team-up**. The label once implied ranking. V31 turns it into burden-sharing because none of the three can restore social order alone.

Endeavor's raw power, Hawks's intelligence/mobility and Jeanist's stabilization/public credibility become complementary resources inside a damaged institution.

## 9.2 OFA secrecy crosses the threshold from protective secret to operational liability

Hawks investigates the "One For All" references that became audible/public during the battle (`V31:p151-153`). The question is no longer confined to Midoriya, All Might and a small trusted circle. Shigaraki's target behavior makes the secret relevant to national threat management.

This sets up All Might's later decision to disclose to the necessary hero/government group. The secrecy policy has not become morally wrong in retrospect. Its cost-benefit conditions have changed.

# 10. No.304 - OFA succession becomes biologically finite

## 10.1 The fourth holder resolves the Danger Sense biography enough to change the succession model

Inside the vestige space, the fourth holder is identified as Shinomori Hikage and Danger Sense is tied to him (`V31:p164-169`). He held OFA for **18 years** and died at only forty, initially recorded as death from old age.

The new explanation is that holding OFA alongside an already-existing Quirk consumed the vessel's life. The problem is not moral worth or willpower. It is capacity.

This adds an embodiment limit to the power-philosophy ledger:

`more inherited power` is not universally better. A power can be benevolently transferred and still be physically incompatible with the recipient.

## 10.2 All Might's Quirklessness becomes constitutive rather than merely biographical

All Might held OFA for **40 years**, far longer than other holders, because he was Quirkless - an "empty vessel" in the explanatory metaphor (`V31:p171-172`). OFA could fit and become truly his rather than overloading an already occupied vessel.

This changes how earlier succession should be read. Quirklessness was not an unfortunate lack that OFA simply corrected. It became the condition that allowed All Might to cultivate OFA for decades.

The series therefore reverses one of its opening status assumptions. A trait socially framed as absence turns out to have unique compatibility value in a mature inherited system.

## 10.3 Midoriya may be the last successor

The current Quirkless population is described as increasingly rare. The vestiges conclude `ワン・フォー・オールはもう譲渡できない` in practical terms and tell Midoriya he may become the `最後の継承者` (`V31:p173-174`).

This does not establish metaphysical impossibility of any future transfer under every conceivable condition. The responsible V31 claim is narrower:

- transfer to an ordinary Quirk-holder now carries severe lifespan/capacity danger;
- compatible Quirkless recipients are becoming rare;
- the present holders therefore believe Midoriya may be the last successor.

The old serial solution - "another person can inherit later" - can no longer bear the ethical burden of current decisions.

Midoriya must answer what OFA is **for** when he may not be able to pass the question to someone else.

# 11. No.305 - `Can you kill Shigaraki Tomura?`

## 11.1 Nana's question tests resolve; it does not issue an automatic execution order

Nana asks `君 死柄木弔を殺せるか?` (`V31:p175-177`). She clarifies that this is a question rather than a command. The distinction matters because the entire volume is testing what inherited authority can legitimately demand from the current bearer.

The predecessors have more history than Midoriya. They do not therefore own his judgment.

## 11.2 The vestiges explicitly deny that Midoriya's rescue perception is shared objective fact

At `V31:p178`, the vestiges address the V30 endpoint directly. They know Midoriya thought Shigaraki looked as though he were asking for help. They say they did **not** see it that way. Shigaraki was suffering AFO's invasion, but his eyes still carried hatred and he did not literally issue a rescue request.

This is one of the most important epistemic safeguards in the second pass.

The V2 analysis must not say:

`Shigaraki secretly asked Midoriya to save him.`

The source supports:

`Midoriya perceived rescue-relevant distress inside an enemy whose conscious expressed position remained hateful and destructive.`

That makes Midoriya's decision more morally interesting, not less. Rescue judgment sometimes begins from interpretation under uncertainty.

## 11.3 OFA transfer mechanics formally protect bearer will

The vestiges explain that AFO has twice failed to force OFA away because `所有者の意思でしか動かせない` (`V31:p180`): OFA can be moved by the holder's will, and AFO's own emotional force has not been sufficient to override that structure. He therefore uses Shigaraki's extraordinary hatred as leverage.

Mechanically, OFA's anti-possession architecture encodes a consent principle. This does not make every OFA inheritance perfectly consensual or burden-free, but it sharply distinguishes the transfer system from AFO's successor-body seizure.

## 11.4 Midoriya does not answer with pacifist certainty

Midoriya says he knows there are people he cannot save (`V31:p183`). He acknowledges that fighting Shigaraki may remain necessary and that killing him may ultimately be the only way to stop him (`V31:p184-191`).

This prevents a common flattening of his ethics. V31 Midoriya is not saying:

`I will never kill under any circumstances.`

He is saying:

`I refuse to make killing the governing aim before I have exhausted the rescue problem I perceive.`

The difference is substantial. It combines enforcement with epistemic humility.

## 11.5 The crying-child perception is causal understanding, not innocence manufacture

Midoriya says that behind Shigaraki's hatred he felt `泣いている子どもを感じたんです` (`V31:p186`). He then reflects that he has fought people he could not reach before and often did not know why they became what they were (`V31:p187`). He does **not** infer that knowing a cause would erase the need to fight.

The model is:

`causal understanding may change what rescue options are imaginable even when it does not remove containment or accountability.`

That is the same architecture V31 applies to Toya. Understanding how a person formed does not undo what the person did.

## 11.6 OFA's moral meaning has changed through historical use

Midoriya recalls All Might's teaching that `OFAは殺す為の力じゃなく 救ける為の力`. The vestige discussion then makes a deeper claim: OFA began as power organized against AFO, but the holders' repeated acts of protecting/supporting people caused the inherited power to accumulate another meaning (`V31:p189-190`).

This is an unusually explicit statement that the telos of inherited power is historically revisable.

Origin does not dictate final meaning.

That proposition applies far beyond OFA:

- Endeavor's inherited family fire can be used outside Endeavor's status project.
- Hawks's institutional training can support self-authored helping after the institution fails.
- Shoto can inherit technical knowledge without inheriting paternal jurisdiction.

## 11.7 `I want to save that child` is a governing aim under acknowledged uncertainty

Midoriya finally says `僕は…あの子を救けたい` (`V31:p191`). He has no concrete method. He does not deny the killings. He does not claim that Shigaraki consciously wants rehabilitation. He does not promise success.

The claim is therefore best represented as a **policy orientation**, not a solved plan.

Nana accepts the answer and says this is why they follow him (`V31:p192`). The predecessors do not disappear, and Midoriya does not simply submit. The relation becomes endorsement of current-bearer judgment.

This is the strongest V31 formulation of successor authorship:

> **Inheritance can supply memory, warning and power without owning the moral decision of the present bearer.**

# 12. No.306 - public truth, public fear, and atonement as work

## 12.1 All Might decides the secrecy boundary has changed

Hawks tells All Might that they have passed the point where continued silence about OFA is operationally sustainable (`V31:p197-200`). All Might answers `全てを話そう` to the necessary actors.

This is not a repudiation of all earlier secrecy. It is a context transition:

- earlier secrecy reduced targeting and protected a young successor;
- current secrecy can obstruct coordination because Shigaraki/AFO is already visibly targeting Midoriya and the term OFA has entered public evidence.

A rigorous secrecy ethic has to be dynamic rather than absolutist.

## 12.2 The public's demand for spotless heroes is framed as fear seeking reassurance

At `V31:p201`, narration says people want heroes to be `清廉潔白`, to say they made no mistakes, because people are afraid and want reassurance.

This is more sympathetic than a simple "fickle public" reading. The demand is unrealistic, but its emotional cause is intelligible. People have lost homes, family members, security and confidence in institutions. Perfect hero morality becomes a fantasy of restored control.

The danger is that such a demand makes honest accountability harder: if admitting error destroys legitimacy, institutions are incentivized to hide error. V31 instead has the Top 3 move toward partial truth-telling under hostile scrutiny.

## 12.3 Endeavor's confession does not purchase forgiveness

Endeavor publicly confirms the disgraceful family past (`V31:p202`). Hawks similarly separates true and false components of Dabi's presentation (`V31:p203-204`), including the Best Jeanist deception and his father's criminal history.

A reporter whose mother was injured in Gigantomachia's traversal rejects apology as reversal. The dead, injured and destroyed homes remain. She asks what the heroes will actually do (`V31:p204-206`).

The chapter therefore distinguishes three things:

1. **truth** - what happened;
2. **remorse/apology** - what the actor feels/says about it;
3. **atonement/repair** - what the actor now does under persistent consequence.

None substitutes for the others.

## 12.4 U.A. becomes civilian infrastructure

Because the remaining hero system cannot protect every dispersed household, the response concentrates civilians into fortified refuge zones, including U.A. (`V31:p206-207`).

This is a major institutional transformation. A hero school becomes civil-protection infrastructure. Education, shelter, security and professional hero capacity collapse into the same site.

The move also changes the meaning of "academia" just before Midoriya leaves it. U.A. is no longer merely where students learn to become heroes; it is part of what heroes are trying to keep alive.

## 12.5 `Everyone, watch me` reverses Endeavor's old gaze hunger

Endeavor asks the public `みんなで俺を見ていてくれ` (`V31:p208`). Earlier Endeavor sought recognition as proof of rank, achievement and superiority. In V31 the gaze is recoded as accountability. He asks anxiety/criticism to be concentrated on him while other heroes continue the work.

The parallel to Toya's repeated demand to be seen is deliberately uncomfortable. Both father and son organize crucial parts of identity around gaze, but V31 changes what being watched can mean:

- Toya wants recognition of injury and uses public attention as punitive weapon;
- Endeavor accepts public attention as surveillance and responsibility rather than reward.

This is a state transition, not absolution.

## 12.6 The occupational collapse leaves `people who still stand`

The narration returns to the sifting metaphor and says that even after heroes disappeared, when someone seeks a hero `まだ立ち上がる人はいる` (`V31:p209`). Stain watches the transformed landscape.

This is the cleanest series-level distinction in the volume:

`hero as profession/status` can contract while `hero as action/responsibility` remains possible.

The statement does not abolish institutions. V31 has just shown why training and coordinated infrastructure matter. Rather, it denies that certification alone is sufficient to explain heroic conduct.

# 13. Midoriya's departure: moral maturation and old self-exclusion coexist

## 13.1 Midoriya tells Class 1-A the secret by letter

At `V31:p210-211`, Midoriya informs classmates that his power came from All Might and that AFO/Shigaraki are targeting him. The disclosure acknowledges that peers have a legitimate stake in the risk surrounding him.

Yet the communication mode matters. He tells them in a letter after the operative decision to leave rather than building the decision with them.

This produces a mixed state:

- greater truthfulness about the burden;
- continued unilateral ownership of the burden.

## 13.2 Uraraka's narration asks who saves the heroes

Immediately before the departure image, Uraraka frames a question: if people who do not shrink from hard roads are called heroes, then when those heroes are in pain, who helps them? (`V31:p211`).

That juxtaposition is not accidental. The next pages show Midoriya alone.

The formal sequence makes his departure legible as a **problem**, even if the operational logic is understandable. He is a high-value target; separating himself from civilians and classmates can reduce immediate targeting risk. But the decision also reactivates the self-disposability mechanism Bakugo identified in V29-V30.

## 13.3 V31 does not permit a simple "Deku grows up and becomes independent" reading

At `V31:p212-213`, April begins; Midoriya stands alone in damaged gear over a ruined city. Narration states `デクくんがヒーローアカデミアを去った` and marks `終章開幕`.

The image can look like maturation because he is more powerful, informed and morally self-authored than V01 Midoriya. But the behavioral continuity is equally important:

- he again treats himself as the movable cost-bearing boundary;
- he makes a protective decision that isolates him from people who would share the risk;
- he accepts a world in which his own support needs are secondary to everyone else's safety.

The correct V31 model is therefore not "growth" or "regression" alone.

It is **matured values operating through a still-dangerous decision heuristic**.

Midoriya has become better at deciding *what* his power should mean. He has not yet proven he can include himself among the people that meaning is supposed to protect.

# 15. Hero-society model after V31

V31 is the first volume in the second pass where the institutional model changes at national scale after the war.

## 15.1 Capacity, legitimacy and trust fail on different timelines

Three distinct failures should not be collapsed:

1. **capacity failure** - injured/dead/resigning heroes and overloaded police cannot cover all threats;
2. **security containment failure** - Tartarus and five other prisons release inmates;
3. **legitimacy failure** - the public no longer assumes heroes are competent, truthful or reliably present.

The three reinforce each other but are not identical.

## 15.2 Civilian self-defense is both rational response and systems hazard

Support-item use is understandable under immediate threat. It also creates collateral when training/coordination is absent. The series therefore preserves a serious argument for professionalization even while criticizing professional hero culture.

## 15.3 Hero resignation is evidence about system incentives, not a universal moral sorting hat

The narration's "sifting" language is strong, but analysis should resist saying every remaining hero is pure and every retiree false. V31 gives too little biography for that. What can be said is that crisis changes the incentive structure and makes motive more behaviorally legible.

## 15.4 Public purity demands arise from fear

The public's unrealistic desire for spotless heroes is explicitly connected to anxiety. This makes public anger socially intelligible while still exposing a governance problem: a society that requires mythic perfection from protectors may punish the truth-telling necessary for institutional repair.

## 15.5 U.A. becomes a public good beyond education

The refuge transformation makes school infrastructure part of national resilience. Later synthesis should treat U.A. not only as pedagogical setting but as land, security, housing and trust infrastructure.

# 16. Power philosophy after V31

## 16.1 Power has a vessel problem

The Hikage/All Might explanation establishes that physical compatibility conditions inherited power. Moral worth cannot overcome a body that is overfilled.

## 16.2 Power has a jurisdiction problem

AFO asks who controls the successor body. OFA protects holder will more strongly, though predecessors still exert moral pressure through memory and argument.

## 16.3 Power has a historical-meaning problem

OFA began as anti-AFO resistance but accumulated "saving" as a meaning through repeated use. Power's origin is not its complete telos.

## 16.4 Power has a termination problem

If Midoriya may be the last successor, stewardship can no longer mean merely preserving the asset for the next bearer. He must decide what responsible completion looks like.

# 17. Recognition / failed rescue after V31

V31 adds four high-value recognition patterns.

### Toya: recognition demanded through the wrong channel

Toya's repeated "look at me" demand is grounded in a childhood where paternal attention was tied to performance. The desire to be seen is real; the later violence used to force recognition is his own harmful policy.

### Shigaraki: rescue recognition without requested rescue

Midoriya sees a crying child where the predecessors see hatred and invasion. The series explicitly preserves the inferential gap. Recognition can motivate rescue without proving the target's consent to rehabilitation.

### Hawks: self-recognition after institutional release

When the command structure disappears, Hawks discovers/affirms a helping motive that remains his. Freedom functions as diagnostic evidence.

### Heroes: the rescuer becomes rescue-relevant

Uraraka's endpoint question asks who helps heroes when they are in pain. The series' early one-directional rescue grammar is becoming reciprocal.
