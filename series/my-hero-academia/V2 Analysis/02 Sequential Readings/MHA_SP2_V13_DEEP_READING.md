## 2. Governing transition


Volume 12 established **operational self-authorship / reliable coordination**. Midoriya translated inherited OFA into a body-specific Shoot Style, support gear became a legitimate interface between capacity and role, and Class 1-A cleared Stage 1 by converting individual strengths into distributed coordination. But V12 deliberately stopped before those capacities were tested against civilians who had needs, fear, injuries, and the right to judge how a hero treated them.


Volume 13 therefore changes the test. It asks not merely whether a candidate can perform a useful action, but whether other people can **rely on that action inside a social system**.


The governing transition is:


> **operational self-authorship / reliable coordination → distributed professional trust / integrated responsibility**


The volume repeatedly demonstrates that competence becomes socially trustworthy only when it is integrated across four levels:


1. **the person being helped** — triage, reassurance, communication, and recognition of actual need;
2. **the surrounding network** — role distribution, handoff, parallel processing, and cooperation;
3. **the institution** — credentialing, delegated authority, remediation, and public accountability;
4. **the actor’s own unresolved history** — resentment, guilt, inheritance, and identity must be integrated rather than merely suppressed.


That fourth layer prevents V13 from becoming a simple “professional exam” volume. Todoroki and Inasa fail operationally because private history becomes public interference. Bakugo can correctly read physical rescue priority while still being professionally abrasive, then later reveals that his obsession with strength has become entangled with guilt over All Might’s retirement. All Might, now unable to fight, confronts AFO and explicitly refuses to die. Twice presents the villain-side inverse: a person whose very certainty that he is one self has collapsed finds functional belonging in the League.


The Commission’s own institutional answer is the clearest system-level statement. All Might was an exceptional `一強`; society cannot simply wait for another person like him. The gap should instead be filled by a more consciously coordinated **`群` of heroes** (`V13:p047`). The post-Symbol problem is therefore answered not by identifying a replacement monolith, but by distributing trust across a network.


A concise Volume 13 thesis is:


> **Heroic reliability is not isolated strength. It is the capacity to become trustworthy across persons, teams, institutions, and one’s own inherited history. The post-All-Might world therefore moves from singular reassurance toward distributed professional trust, while individual characters learn that self-authorship requires integrating—not erasing—the relationships, wounds, and responsibilities that shaped them.**


The first thirteen volumes can now be described compactly as:


`recognition → judgment → public legibility → self-authorship → recognition without possession → enacted identity → aftermath → diagnostic resistance → emergency enactment / cost-bearing → contested legibility / protective authority → accountable succession / de-centered support → operational self-authorship / reliable coordination → distributed professional trust / integrated responsibility`


## 3. Rescue is not a moral impulse alone


### 3.1 HUC turns “save them” into a professional process


The Help Us Company setup immediately changes the epistemic status of the civilian. HUC participants are not inert objectives. They are trained evaluators playing victims and judging whether provisional-license candidates behave as people who can safely be entrusted with emergency authority (`V13:p008-021`).


Midoriya’s first error is instructive because his moral instinct is not wrong. He moves quickly toward someone apparently trapped. HUC still deducts points. At `V13:p020`, the actor explains what Midoriya failed to establish first: can the person walk, are they breathing, is the visible head bleeding superficial, is there a more dangerous injury elsewhere? A provisional-license holder is expected to make this assessment almost immediately.


The distinction is important:


`desire to rescue ≠ correct rescue prioritization`.


Earlier volumes repeatedly validated Midoriya’s tendency to move before conscious calculation when someone is endangered. V13 does not reverse that moral foundation. It professionalizes it. The rescue-first impulse must now become a sequence of reliable judgments that does not waste time, worsen injury, or monopolize attention that belongs to someone in greater danger.


At `V13:p021`, the exam explicitly introduces `トリアージ`. Rescue also extends beyond the individual extraction. Heroes are expected to establish evacuation, treat or stabilize as appropriate, and continue until fire/police services can take over, performing a smooth `橋渡し`. The hero’s job is therefore partly **interoperability** with other institutions.


This is a major extension of the V09 “networked rescue” finding. V09 showed that Mandalay, Aizawa, Shoji, Kendo and others could distribute information and function under attack. V13 now formalizes that logic as professional doctrine: the hero is not a complete emergency-services system alone and should not try to become one.


### 3.2 Civilian-facing speech is operational, not cosmetic


HUC also makes communication a safety function. At `V13:p021`, candidates are warned that their first words matter. A frightened or injured person cannot be expected to interpret a hero’s uncertainty charitably. The professional must give the other person enough reassurance to follow instructions and enough clarity to know what happens next.


Midoriya consciously activates the model he inherited from All Might. He remembers `もう大丈夫 私が来た`, then at `V13:p023` tells the victim that things will be all right and that they will be saved. This is not a return to copying All Might’s combat form. It is a selective retention of an inherited social function: **reassurance as part of rescue**.


That distinction is analytically useful after V12. Midoriya’s increasing self-authorship does not require purging All Might from his repertoire. It requires deciding which inherited practices solve genuine human problems and translating them into his own behavior.


### 3.3 Midoriya’s rescue cognition becomes parallel rather than tunnelled


By `V13:p037`, Midoriya is no longer simply correcting his opening mistake. He is doing several things at once: distinguishing visible bleeding from deeper danger, directing mobile victims, prioritizing a severely burned person, estimating arrival of additional rescue support, and planning what infrastructure will be needed.


The progression is therefore:


`run toward victim → assess victim → triage multiple victims → organize network and handoff`.


This is one of the strongest professional-development sequences in the first thirteen volumes because the source shows Midoriya updating inside the exercise rather than merely receiving a pass/fail judgment afterward.


### 3.4 Momo and Uraraka show different rescue cognition


V13 also gives comparatively clean non-combat evidence for Yaoyorozu and Uraraka.


Momo checks pulse/breathing and creates a treatment environment (`V13:p031`). The behavior fits her established planning/logistics strengths but applies them to civilian care rather than a combat exercise.


Uraraka notices a secondary-collapse problem and prioritizes structural support before extraction (`V13:p032`). HUC explicitly treats the judgment as good. This matters for her model because her rescue value is not reducible to emotional warmth or a useful anti-gravity Quirk. She is attending to the environment as a causal system and preventing the rescue itself from creating another casualty.


### 3.5 Large-scale rescue requires role distribution


At `V13:p033`, the manga states the principle directly: a large disaster cannot be handled by one rescuer; trying to do everything oneself wastes time. Candidates must understand one another’s Quirks and distribute tasks for `効率化`.


This is the local operational version of the Commission’s later `群` thesis. The volume first teaches distributed trust from the ground up—among students at a disaster site—then later gives it system-level political/institutional articulation.


The fact that cooperation crosses school boundaries (`V13:p034`) matters too. Professional coordination cannot depend entirely on preexisting friendship. The exam tests whether people can plug into a wider civic network with unfamiliar partners.


## 4. Bakugo: correct triage, unusable reassurance


### 4.1 The first-pass model needs a precise correction


The historical combined V12–V13 reading summarized Bakugo’s rescue failure as though he lacked the civilian-facing side of heroism in a broad sense. V13 supports only part of that formulation.


At `V13:p035-036`, Bakugo tells lightly injured HUC victims to stop complaining and save themselves. Kirishima understandably worries about the interaction. But HUC’s internal evaluation immediately distinguishes **judgment** from **manner**: Bakugo has accurately and instantly recognized that these people are mobile, lightly injured, and capable of self-evacuation. His triage is good.


The deduction is therefore not:


> Bakugo cannot tell who needs help.


It is:


> Bakugo can make an efficient rescue judgment but communicates it in a way that fails the profession’s civilian-facing social standard.


That is a much more useful character model. Bakugo’s problem is not generic incompetence around vulnerable people. It is that his compressed, contemptuous imperative register assumes the recipient can absorb the correct operational instruction without also requiring reassurance, explanation, or emotional regulation from the hero.


### 4.2 “Bearable to the weak” remains useful only after revision


The V1 phrase “power can defeat villains, but heroism has to be bearable to the weak” still captures something real, but V2 should make it more exact. Bakugo is not being asked to fake tenderness or become a fundamentally different personality. He is being asked to make his correct judgment **socially usable by the person whose safety depends on it**.


Professional communication is thus analogous to support gear in V12. Both are interfaces. Kaminari’s equipment translates raw electricity into selective precision. Bakugo needs to translate rapid situational judgment into a form civilians can act on without being destabilized by the rescuer.


### 4.3 The later Ground Beta sequence reveals the concealed cost beneath the same surface


The rescue exam alone could tempt an analyst to stabilize “abrasive but competent” as a sufficient Bakugo model. The volume’s final chapters prevent that. His aggression is carrying an unspoken responsibility structure that neither peers nor adults have been allowed to see.


This later evidence does not excuse his communication failure. It does, however, show why a reconstruction system must distinguish surface register from internal state. Bakugo’s loudness is stable; what that loudness is carrying can change radically by context.


## 5. Uraraka: attraction becomes explicit self-knowledge


V12 preserved Uraraka’s romantic state as OPEN because `恋だ` was Ashido’s external label. V13 resolves that ambiguity.


At `V13:p024`, Uraraka recalls the question about whether she likes Midoriya and thinks directly:


> `恋だ`


She then immediately asks herself what she is doing and concludes:


> `この気持ちは しまっておこう`


At `V13:p025`, she watches Midoriya working with complete vocational focus. His lack of spare attention is part of what appears admirable to her. The sequence links attraction to aspiration: liking someone can make a person want to become more like the quality they admire or to work harder themselves.


This is not denial. Uraraka has moved through three distinct epistemic states:


`others can see attraction → she recognizes attraction → she deliberately defers acting on it`.


The decision is therefore self-authored suppression, not ignorance. She chooses to place the feeling away because she wants to remain oriented toward her own hero development.


This also avoids a simplistic romance-versus-career reading. Midoriya’s striving is itself part of what attracts her. Romance and vocational admiration are entangled, but the behavioral consequence is increased self-regulation rather than abandonment of her independent goal.


The post-exam interaction at `V13:p112` is also useful low-stakes evidence. Uraraka remains warm and ordinary with Midoriya after making the internal decision. `しまっておこう` does not produce cold withdrawal. The modeled state is better described as **conscious private attraction under deliberate behavioral containment**.## 6. Todoroki and Inasa: history that is denied returns as operational blindness


### 6.1 The exam turns private resentment into a public-safety variable


Volume 5 established that Todoroki could temporarily de-center Endeavor. His use of fire against Midoriya was prospectively described through `お前を忘れた`: in that moment he stopped organizing himself entirely around rejection of his father. V13 supplies an important held-out correction. Temporary de-centering was not completed integration.


At `V13:p059`, Todoroki admits this to himself. He thought he had gotten past the problem, but `あいつへの嫌悪はまだ……` remains. The suppressed residue matters because Inasa’s presence turns it into behavior under professional pressure.


Inasa’s own history is equally important. At `V13:p057`, he explains heroism through `熱さ`: a hero’s power to ignite passion and hope in other people. His first contact with Endeavor therefore created not simply personal dislike but ideological revulsion. The hero he approached with genuine enthusiasm responded with cold rejection. Later, young Todoroki appeared to reproduce that same coldness.


The source prevents a caricature of Inasa as someone who arbitrarily hates Todoroki. He was originally interested in Todoroki’s strength and even wanted to understand or befriend him. The relationship becomes hostile through accumulated encounters in which Inasa reads Todoroki as the continuation of the exact affective quality he rejects in Endeavor.


### 6.2 Todoroki literally failed to see another person


The decisive self-diagnosis occurs around `V13:p070`:


> `何ですぐ思い出せなかった…!?`
>
> `…見てなかったんだな本当に`


This is unusually valuable recognition evidence. Todoroki’s anti-Endeavor fixation did not merely make him rude. It narrowed perception so strongly that a person who had directly engaged him barely registered as an individual.


The causal chain is therefore:


`inherited family injury → rejection strategy → attentional narrowing → interpersonal misrecognition → professional coordination failure`.


The manga then refuses the fantasy that self-authorship means forgetting where one came from. At `V13:p071`, Todoroki thinks:


> `過去も 血も 忘れたままじゃいられねえんだな`


The point is not that blood determines him. It is almost the opposite. If he wants blood and history **not** to determine his behavior unconsciously, he has to acknowledge them consciously.


This revises a common simplistic opposition between inheritance and individuality. V13 argues that disowned inheritance can exercise more control precisely because it is not being integrated into the self-model.


### 6.3 `嫌だったモンに 自分がなっていた`


At `V13:p074`, Todoroki reaches the volume’s clearest responsibility statement:


> `嫌だったモンに 自分がなっていたよ!!`
>
> `俺のしてきた事が この事態を招いた`


This is stronger than regret. He recognizes a structurally humiliating irony: resistance to Endeavor has reproduced an Endeavor-like interpersonal effect.


The response is also properly professional:


> `俺が……取り返さねえと!!`


He does not ask the exam to excuse him because his history is painful. He treats history as causal explanation while retaining responsibility for correction.


### 6.4 Inasa must perform the same correction


Inasa is not merely the victim of Todoroki’s blindness. His own negative motivation has become similarly distorting. The Commission discussion at `V13:p030` provides the conceptual frame before the personal conflict fully unfolds: when denial or hatred becomes the motive force, vision can become clouded and action can go too far.


The line applies to Stain, but V13 immediately uses it on non-villains. This is analytically important. The manga is not constructing a moral binary in which only villains are warped by resentment. Hero candidates can suffer the same attentional failure without thereby becoming villains.


Todoroki and Inasa’s fire/wind collision threatens the actual rescue/combat operation (`V13:p061-062`). Midoriya’s `何をしてんだよ` is blunt because the professional issue is now obvious: whatever the legitimacy of their feelings, civilians and allies cannot be made to pay for them.


### 6.5 Repair becomes operational cooperation


The repair is not complete because the two exchange apologies. It becomes meaningful because they alter action.


At `V13:p075-078`, Todoroki and Inasa begin reasoning from one another’s capabilities. Heat makes wind rise; wind can circulate and sustain the heat. Their formerly interfering powers become complementary and produce a combined containment strategy against Gang Orca.


The visual/formal reversal is elegant:


`fire + wind as collision → fire + wind as circulation`.


The relationship has not suddenly become intimate, and V13 should not overstate reconciliation. But the professional transition is real: they can now treat the other person as a causal partner rather than an object inside private resentment.


Gang Orca still criticizes them at `V13:p088` for failing to prepare the next move. The manga therefore does not reward emotional breakthrough with immediate professional perfection. Repair restores the possibility of cooperation; it does not erase the need for continued skill.


## 7. The post-Symbol institution chooses a network, not another monolith


### 7.1 `一強` is named as a structural condition


Chapter 111 moves from student-scale coordination to explicit policy. The Public Safety Commission discusses the state of hero society only days after All Might’s final battle (`V13:p046-047`).


The key diagnosis is that All Might was overwhelmingly exceptional in both power and charisma. The social order had therefore become organized around a practical `一強`.


The Commission does **not** respond by saying the task is to discover a replacement All Might immediately. Instead, it argues that waiting for another `“次の彼”` is unrealistic. The gap should be filled by a more consciously connected:


> `“群”のヒーロー`


This is one of the most important institutional statements in the first thirteen volumes.


The post-Symbol problem is not framed merely as:


`Who is the next strongest person?`


It becomes:


`How can many heroes collectively produce the reliability previously concentrated in one person?`


### 7.2 The license exam is therefore system design


Seen in that light, the exam’s emphasis on rescue knowledge, coordination, judgment and cooperation is not bureaucratic ornament. It is an attempt to change the architecture of hero society.


The institutional project is:


`singular reassurance → distributed competence → coordinated trust`.


That does not mean the Commission has solved the problem. V13 itself shows public unease around Endeavor and rising villain activity. Nor does the existence of a policy document prove institutions can manufacture genuine solidarity. But the source establishes that at least some officials **recognize** the single-point-of-failure problem and are deliberately designing credentials around a networked answer.


### 7.3 Licensing delegates real public authority


The results sequence makes the stakes concrete. At `V13:p106`, the provisional license is not described as a school achievement alone. A holder may be required to make decisions and take actions equivalent to hero activity during emergencies even without explicit instructions from a professional hero. Those choices can affect human lives.


Credentialing therefore creates a legal/ethical transition:


`student competence → delegated emergency authority`.


This strongly extends the V06–V11 legitimacy framework. MHA has consistently refused the equation “good intention + useful power = legitimate coercive authority.” V13 adds that society can conditionally delegate such authority when training and assessment make a candidate sufficiently trustworthy.


### 7.4 The scoring system is imperfect by design, not a test of purity


Candidates begin from a maximum score and lose points. No passing student is flawless (`V13:p094-106`). This matters because the institution is not demanding an abstract perfect hero. It is deciding whether observed errors remain below a threshold compatible with conditional public trust.


Midoriya passes despite opening mistakes. Momo scores strongly. Kaminari passes despite meaningful deductions. The system can therefore recognize development during the exercise rather than treating an early error as dispositive.


### 7.5 Failure is remediable


Todoroki, Bakugo and Inasa fail, but the institution immediately offers a corrective route. At `V13:p108-109`, a special course and later individual test can still produce a provisional license. The stated goal is better-quality heroes, not merely preserving a fixed exclusion quota.


This is a significant institutional contrast with punitive sorting. The exam says:


> you are not yet trustworthy enough **in these dimensions**,


not:


> you have been revealed as permanently unfit for heroism.


Todoroki’s `当然` acceptance is especially useful character evidence. Once the result is known, he does not dispute the legitimacy of remediation or demand that raw power outweigh the failure.


## 8. Stage 2 answers the V12 closing question


V12 asked whether reliable technique and coordination could survive when the recipient became a civilian who actually had to rely on the hero.


V13’s answer is qualified:


- **Midoriya:** yes, after an initial adjustment. He learns to triage, reassure, prioritize, and plan across multiple casualties.
- **Momo:** yes in the observed rescue sequence; her procedural/logistical strengths translate effectively.
- **Uraraka:** yes in the observed sequence; she identifies structural risk before extraction.
- **Bakugo:** partially. His assessment can be correct while the communication interface remains professionally defective.
- **Todoroki/Inasa:** no at first. Private resentment contaminates coordination badly enough to fail the credentialing threshold, even though both later correct and cooperate.
- **Class/network:** increasingly yes. Cross-school task distribution and parallel processing are treated as essential professional behavior.


The important conceptual change is that **trustworthiness is divisible by domain**. A character can be excellent at combat and fail communication. Another can have strong rescue instinct and initially fail triage. A third can have excellent judgment in ordinary conditions and become distorted by a specific relational trigger.


This multidimensional structure is exactly what the V2.1 modeling method needs. “Good hero” and “bad hero” are too coarse to predict behavior.


## 9. Toga/Camie: behavioral detection becomes canonical identity resolution


Volume 12 deliberately left the identity of the person presented as Utsushimi Camie OPEN. Midoriya had already noticed that the behavior did not match the Uraraka he knew. V13 resolves the source-side identity question.


At `V13:p115-116`, the manga reveals Toga Himiko and gives the direct Quirk explanation: `“個性”「変身」`; ingesting another person’s blood allows her to transform into that person. Toga also states that she obtained Midoriya’s blood.


This creates a useful two-volume epistemic sequence:


`visual plausibility → behavioral inconsistency → later identity confirmation`.


The V12 model was therefore right to preserve uncertainty while still treating Midoriya’s relationship knowledge as evidence. He did not need the hidden identity to know that the action policy was inconsistent with the Uraraka he knew.


For Toga, V13 adds a technical constraint to her previously emerging blood/intimacy model. Blood is not only an object of fascination or violence; it is also an input to identity transformation. The exact psychological relationship among attraction, blood desire, imitation, identity, and Quirk remains broader than a single scene can establish, but the mechanism is now source-explicit.


The real Utsushimi Camie’s condition should remain OPEN at this boundary except where V13 directly states it. Later explanation must not be retroactively imported.


## 10. Twice: identity collapse and the League as habitat


### 10.1 The mask is psychological infrastructure


Chapter 115 introduces Twice in a way that strongly rewards V2.1’s distinction between costume surface and behavioral function.


At `V13:p127`, Twice explains that covering himself makes him `一つ`. His mask is therefore not merely visual branding or concealment. It is an external stabilizer for a fractured self-model.


His Quirk, `二倍`, can turn one into two. Repeated self-duplication once gave him abundance and a criminal “king” position. But the copies rebelled. Because sufficiently damaged copies disappear and all copies were behaviorally/visually similar, their violent mutual destruction left the survivor unable to know with certainty whether he was the original (`V13:p127-128`).


The result is not adequately modeled as generic “madness.” It is a specific epistemic injury:


> **Twice lacks stable confidence that the self speaking now is ontologically the original self.**


That uncertainty reorganizes behavior. The mask helps produce continuity where internal certainty cannot.


### 10.2 `イカレちまった人間に居場所はねえ`


At `V13:p129`, Twice provides one of the series’ clearest villain-belonging statements so far:


> `イカレちまった人間に居場所はねえ`


He contrasts social abandonment with the League, which accepted him. He then seeks other damaged or “crazy” people who might likewise need a place.


This is strong villain-formation evidence, but it must not become a universal theory. V09 already established motive pluralism. Muscular’s predatory enjoyment, Stain’s purification ideology, Shigaraki’s strategic hatred, Toga’s affiliative/predatory entanglement, and Twice’s belonging need are not interchangeable.


Twice instead supplies a high-quality **belonging route**:


`identity destabilization + social exclusion → acceptance by deviant peer group → loyalty/recruitment toward that habitat`.


### 10.3 The League is a coalition because its members want different things


The surrounding sequence reinforces organizational heterogeneity.


Dabi rejects weak or purposeless recruits, even killing/discarding them, with the severe formulation `ゴミならせめて燃えて俺の薪となれ` (`V13:p126`). His recruitment behavior is filtering rather than indiscriminate inclusion.


Compress remains more materially pragmatic. Twice is looking for damaged people who need belonging. The League’s capacity is therefore not that everyone shares one psychology; it is that a political/criminal formation can coordinate people with partly incompatible motive structures.


This strengthens the V09 coalition model.


### 10.4 Identity becomes the chapter’s shared hero/villain problem


The narration around `V13:p132` broadens the point: heroes, sidekicks, and villains are all changing, and knowing who one is and what one wants matters.


This creates a deliberate structural mirror with the license-exam material. Hero students are converting identity into professional form; villains are converting damaged or ideological identities into organizational form.


The moral difference is not that heroes possess identity while villains do not. It is what kinds of action, relationship, and institution those identities authorize.


## 11. AFO in Tartarus: succession as a general response to finitude


### 11.1 AFO’s ideal is more than opportunistic domination


The Tartarus conversation substantially strengthens the AFO model because it gives direct self-report rather than only opponent interpretation.


At `V13:p141`, AFO says that he and All Might resemble one another in one respect. All Might admired heroes of justice; AFO admired the `悪の魔王`. Each held an ideal and possessed power capable of embodying it. AFO’s own formulation is that if he can continue living within that ideal, he does not begrudge the effort required.


This does not rehabilitate him morally. It does, however, correct an analytically thin model in which he is only spite, appetite, or tactical manipulation. He possesses a self-conscious aesthetic/normative ideal of domination and has organized extraordinary resources around its embodiment.


### 11.2 Why pass anything on if one wants eternal control?


All Might asks the obvious succession question. AFO’s answer at `V13:p142` is one of the volume’s most important philosophical statements.


If people know there is an end, they `託す`. Cities, buildings, food, and human development persist because things are passed from person to person. AFO says he is trying to do what everyone else does.


This revises the earlier hero/villain succession contrast. V11 showed that AFO values making Shigaraki stand independently and can use mentor withdrawal to intensify hatred. V13 now reveals that AFO also understands succession as a **general human continuity technology produced by finitude**.


The difference between One For All and All For One succession therefore cannot be reduced to:


`good side passes on / bad side hoards`.


Both sides pass on. The important questions become:


- what value is being transmitted;
- what relationship to the successor is cultivated;
- whether the successor gains agency or becomes an instrument;
- what affect fuels continuity;
- what kind of social order the inheritance tries to reproduce.


### 11.3 AFO remains a sophisticated social-system modeler


Even cut off from ordinary information, AFO predicts the post-All-Might situation with considerable accuracy (`V13:p143-145`): the media will diminish/retire the former Symbol, Endeavor will inherit leadership under anxiety, reassurance will turn toward unease, villains will move in the shadows, and citizens will seek organizations or collectives to stabilize themselves.


This is not omniscience. It is structural reasoning from incentives and dependence. AFO understands that removing an overwhelmingly central trust node changes the behavior of the whole network.


That makes him a conceptual counterpart to the Commission. Both diagnose a society destabilized by singular dependence. The Commission answers with a coordinated `群` of heroes. AFO anticipates fragmentation, underground mobilization, and organizational conflict.


### 11.4 All Might’s most important action is refusing sacrificial death


AFO then attacks Toshinori at the point of deepest identity vulnerability: now he cannot personally save people; his disappearance from the field causes enemies to multiply; he has to watch from the side.


The response at `V13:p147` is crucial:


> `私は死なないぞ`
>
> `死柄木に私は殺させない`
>
> `私は殺されないぞ！`


This is the cleanest behavioral payoff yet to Inko’s V11 correction. All Might had promised to protect Midoriya `この命に代えても`; Inko explicitly rejected that grammar and told him to live, protect, and raise the boy rather than spend his life as proof of care.


V13 shows that Toshinori has incorporated the correction strongly enough to assert survival against AFO’s desired future.


This matters because “I will not die” could superficially sound less heroic than “I will give my life.” Within the accumulated V2 framework, it is the more mature heroism. Toshinori’s continued life has relational and pedagogical value. Refusing death is not cowardice; it is accepting an obligation to remain present when spectacular self-sacrifice would once have seemed like the purest expression of duty.


At `V13:p149-150`, Midoriya’s message announcing the provisional license arrives as a perfect counter-image to AFO’s taunt. Toshinori cannot act as the old Symbol, but the successor is advancing. De-centered support is producing continuity.## 12. Bakugo reconstructs the secret: antagonistic attention as high-resolution recognition


### 12.1 Ground Beta is a deliberate return to the first defeat


Chapter 117 brings Midoriya and Bakugo back to Ground Beta, the site of their first formal combat-training confrontation (`V13:p154-156`). The spatial callback matters because the original fight exposed Bakugo’s inability to assimilate the contradiction represented by Midoriya: someone he had categorized as powerless could act with courage, strategy, and now suddenly possess force.


By V13, Bakugo has not solved the emotional contradiction, but he has been collecting evidence for months.


### 12.2 The OFA inference is a real causal reconstruction


Bakugo’s conclusion is not a lucky guess. Across `V13:p157-161`, he assembles a multi-source chain:


- Midoriya was Quirkless and then suddenly developed a powerful Quirk;
- Midoriya once said the power had been entrusted/given to him;
- All For One visibly possessed multiple Quirks and demonstrated a world in which `“個性”の移動` is possible;
- a Wild, Wild Pussycats member lost a Quirk;
- All Might and the enemy leader knew one another personally;
- Midoriya’s transformation began after contact with All Might;
- All Might subsequently lost his own power;
- the public `次は君だ` affected Midoriya in a way inconsistent with the ordinary public interpretation.


Bakugo therefore concludes directly at `V13:p159`:


> `オールマイトから貰ったんだろ その“個性”`


This materially strengthens his character model. Bakugo’s surface interaction style is impulsive, abrasive, and emotionally explosive, but his opponent/peer observation is not shallow. He tracks anomalies, remembers statements, notices relational reactions, and revises causal models when enough evidence accumulates.


The V13 lesson is:


> **Bakugo can be emotionally dysregulated about Midoriya while simultaneously being one of the most attentive observers of Midoriya.**


Those properties coexist rather than cancel one another.


### 12.3 The secret is inseparable from the license result


At `V13:p158`, Bakugo frames the license outcome in direct comparative terms: Midoriya passed and he did not. For Bakugo, this is not an abstract institutional inconvenience. It threatens the model through which he has organized self-worth since childhood.


If Midoriya’s All-Might-oriented path receives institutional validation while Bakugo’s does not, then the problem is not merely that Midoriya gained an extraordinary power. Bakugo has to consider whether his **theory of heroism** was wrong.


This becomes explicit at `V13:p165`.


Bakugo’s admired All Might is the hero who, no matter how bad the situation, **wins in the end**. Midoriya’s admired All Might is the hero who **saves people with a smile**. Both boys built themselves from the same public figure while extracting different governing features.


The conflict can therefore be modeled as:


`victory-centered inheritance ↔ rescue-centered inheritance`.


Neither is wholly false. All Might did both. Their developmental problem is that each boy has over-weighted one half.


### 12.4 `てめェの何が オールマイトにそこまでさせたのか`


Bakugo’s demand to fight is also an epistemic demand:


> `てめェの何が オールマイトにそこまでさせたのか`
>
> `確かめさせろ`


He wants to know what Midoriya had that caused All Might to choose and invest in him.


This is not yet a verbal request for comfort. Bakugo’s habitual method is embodied comparison. He makes the question testable in the grammar he trusts most: direct contest.


The fight is unauthorized, and the manga does not erase that. Aizawa’s monitoring around `V13:p170` preserves the institutional boundary. But the psychological function of the fight is more specific than “Bakugo wants to prove he is stronger.” He is trying to interrogate the meaning of All Might’s choice and his own failure through the medium in which he knows how to be maximally honest.


## 13. `意味のない戦い`: guilt that had no legitimate receiver


### 13.1 The hidden proposition: “I ended All Might”


The final chapter reveals the affect that Bakugo’s earlier behavior could not make legible.


At `V13:p180`, after describing how the person who had always been behind him began making him chase from behind, Bakugo reaches the concealed thought:


> `俺は……オールマイトを 終わらせちまってんだ`


At `V13:p181`, he explains the causal self-accusation. If he had been strong enough not to be captured, the Kamino crisis would not have taken that form. All Might was trying to keep the secret, and Bakugo could tell no one. Even when he tried not to think about it, the thought kept returning.


This is not a rationally complete causal account of Kamino. The League chose to target him; professional heroes and villains made their own decisions; All Might’s remaining power was already finite. But Bakugo’s internal responsibility model is real and behaviorally consequential.


The crucial V2 distinction is:


> **causal guilt does not require accurate total causation to reorganize a person’s behavior.**


Bakugo has personalized a systemic chain into a private verdict: his insufficient strength caused the fall of the Symbol he worshipped.


### 13.2 Strength has become morally contaminated


Earlier Bakugo’s pursuit of strength was comparatively straightforward: strength validated superiority and victory. V13 shows that after Kamino, strength is carrying another burden.


He says that if he had been stronger, he would not have been taken. This converts future strength into an attempted defense against guilt. The desire to become strong is therefore no longer only aspirational competition; it is partly an effort to ensure that his weakness never again forces the person he admires to pay a catastrophic cost.


This helps explain why the provisional-license failure is psychologically explosive. A credential telling Bakugo that he is not yet professionally trustworthy lands on someone already privately accusing himself of having caused the greatest hero’s retirement.


### 13.3 Midoriya recognizes a burden, not merely an opponent


At `V13:p182`, Bakugo admits he does not know what to do. Midoriya realizes that Bakugo has been carrying, thinking, and suffering with this problem `僕なんかより…ずっと…`.


This is an important Midoriya recognition event because it reverses their usual interpretive asymmetry. Bakugo has spent years reading Midoriya through contempt and rivalry; Midoriya has spent years admiring and fearing Bakugo. Here Midoriya finally identifies a hidden vulnerability that Bakugo has not been able to place anywhere else.


Midoriya asks himself whether they truly need to fight. The title, `意味のない戦い`, preserves that doubt.


### 13.4 A fight can be institutionally wrong and relationally meaningful


At `V13:p183`, Midoriya’s narration concedes that the fight may have no meaning in victory or defeat. He still decides that he must participate.


At `V13:p184`, the reason is explicit:


> `かっちゃんのこの気持ちを今受けられるのは`
>
> `僕しかいないんだから`


and:


> `やるなら…全力だ`


This is not a general endorsement of violence as therapy. The source keeps the rule violation visible. It is a highly relationship-specific response to a person whose available language for unbearable comparison/guilt is still combat.


The best modeling formulation is:


> **Midoriya accepts an illegitimate form because he recognizes a legitimate relational need that currently has no other receiver.**


That is ethically messy, and the manga leaves it unresolved at the V13 boundary. Chapter 118 ends before the fight’s result. The next volume must adjudicate what adult intervention, secret disclosure, punishment, and relational change follow. None of those outcomes should be backfilled here.


### 13.5 Ground Beta converts rivalry into burden-sharing without completing reconciliation


The location now holds two versions of their relationship.


The first Ground Beta fight was structured by Bakugo’s panic at a former subordinate becoming unreadable. The second begins after Bakugo has read too much: enough to reconstruct the secret, enough to question his hero ideal, and enough to blame himself for All Might’s end.


The transformation is not:


`enemy → friend`.


It is closer to:


`misrecognition through hierarchy → antagonistic observation → causal inference → involuntary vulnerability / burden disclosure`.


That is a much more useful longitudinal relationship state.

## 16. Visual and formal architecture


### 16.1 Rescue staging multiplies simultaneous centers of attention


The HUC chapters visually resist single-duel composition. Victims, structural hazards, rescue partners, villains and evaluators occupy competing attention channels. The reader is repeatedly asked to process the environment as a network rather than simply follow the strongest combatant.


This formal density supports the `並行処理` requirement around `V13:p042`. Professional heroism is visually represented as **attention allocation**.


### 16.2 Fire and wind reverse from interference to circulation


Todoroki and Inasa’s powers first collide because the users are psychologically colliding. Later, the same elemental relation becomes useful because each begins treating the other as part of the system. The powers do not need to change; the relational model changes.


This is a strong visual analogue for the volume thesis:


`same capacities + different coordination = different civic value`.


### 16.3 The `一強` / `群` contrast is social composition


The Commission’s language names a composition problem that has existed visually throughout the series. All Might often occupied pages as the overwhelming center around which civilians, villains and heroes oriented. V13 explicitly proposes a distributed composition: many heroes consciously linked strongly enough that public reliability no longer depends on one body.


### 16.4 Twice’s mask externalizes an internal boundary


Twice’s costume boundary becomes a visual technology of self-coherence. Removing it exposes a face tied to uncertainty about whether the person exists as the “original”; replacing it helps restore one behavioral unit. This is unusually direct embodiment evidence for a villain character.


### 16.5 Ground Beta as relational recursion


Returning to Ground Beta makes space itself a callback ledger. The earlier arena contained Bakugo’s outrage that Midoriya could no longer be placed beneath him. The new arena contains Bakugo’s recognition that Midoriya has been chosen, has passed him in at least one institutional measure, and may embody a side of All Might that Bakugo did not understand.


The same architecture therefore holds a different problem:


`hierarchy crisis → legitimacy/guilt crisis`.


## 17. Power philosophy and professional philosophy


V13 expands the series’ power philosophy in five directions.


First, **power must be recipient-compatible**. Correct triage delivered in a professionally unusable social form is still deficient.


Second, **power is networked**. The rescue system explicitly values role distribution, handoff, and cooperation across unfamiliar partners.


Third, **power becomes authority only through legitimacy**. A provisional license conditionally authorizes emergency decisions that affect lives; strength alone does not grant that jurisdiction.


Fourth, **inheritance is not the opposite of authorship**. Todoroki must integrate blood/history rather than erase them; Bakugo and Midoriya inherit different aspects of All Might; AFO describes succession as a general response to finitude.


Fifth, **survival can be a heroic obligation**. All Might’s refusal to die shows that preserving one’s continued relational/pedagogical value can supersede spectacular self-sacrifice.


## 18. Hero Society update


The post-Kamino society model now contains three interacting levels.


### 18.1 Public affective vacuum


All Might’s retirement creates not only a combat-capability deficit but a reassurance deficit. Endeavor’s new position does not automatically inherit public emotional legitimacy. The news/Tartarus material depicts a society in which symbolic continuity is uncertain.


### 18.2 Institutional redesign


The Commission recognizes the danger of waiting for a new singular Symbol and deliberately prioritizes cooperative/quality criteria. The `群` is the strongest explicit institutional answer so far to the V07–V11 single-point-of-failure problem.


### 18.3 Adversarial adaptation


AFO and the League also understand the transition as opportunity. AFO predicts organization/faction formation; League members disperse and recruit; Twice seeks people for whom mainstream society offered no place; Dabi filters for purpose.


The same weakened center therefore generates competing network formation:


`hero coordination ↔ villain coalition / underground organization`.


V13’s system conflict is not “one new hero versus one new villain.” It is increasingly **network against network**.


## 19. First-pass correction ledger transitions


The historical V1 combined V12–V13 reading contains several strong insights, but V2 requires boundary and mechanism corrections.


### 19.1 “Heroism becomes professionalized” — STRENGTHEN / REVISE


V12 established repeatable technique and plural competence. V13 provides the direct civilian-facing evidence: triage, reassurance, rescue logistics, inter-service handoff, simultaneous threat processing, external credentialing, and delegated authority.


The V1 thesis is therefore strongly supported, but V12 and V13 should no longer be treated as one undifferentiated professionalization block.


### 19.2 “Bakugo lacks the civilian-facing side of heroism” — REVISE


Bakugo does **not** fail because he cannot recognize rescue priority. HUC explicitly recognizes his rapid accurate judgment that the victims can self-evacuate. The professional deficit is communication/reassurance/social usability.


Current formulation:


> **Bakugo can make efficient rescue judgments while failing to translate them into a civilian-compatible interaction.**


### 19.3 “Todoroki fails because unresolved Endeavor history interferes” — STRENGTHEN / REVISE


Strongly confirmed, but V2 makes the mechanism more precise: unresolved resentment narrows perception, causing Todoroki to admit that he had not really seen Inasa. Repair begins when he integrates rather than tries to erase past/blood and accepts causal responsibility for the present failure.


### 19.4 “All Might and AFO embody competing inheritances” — STRENGTHEN / REVISE


Confirmed, but AFO is more philosophically explicit than a simple corruption-through-dependency model. He frames succession as what finite beings do and compares it to ordinary civilization passing things forward. The moral difference lies in content, relationship, affect and intended social order—not in whether succession occurs.


### 19.5 “Bakugo’s suspicion brings the secret inward” — STRENGTHEN


V13 turns suspicion into a source-grounded causal inference. Bakugo explicitly reconstructs Quirk transfer and identifies All Might as the donor.


### 19.6 “Mirio/Nighteye challenge Midoriya’s legitimacy” — DEFER TO V14+


That material is **not** part of the V13 tankōbon narrative boundary. The historical combined reading’s later successor-legitimacy material must remain deferred until the sequential V2 source reaches it.


### 19.7 New V2 propositions absent or underdeveloped in V1


- Uraraka explicitly self-recognizes `恋だ` and deliberately contains the feeling.
- Commission policy explicitly replaces waiting for another `一強` with a coordinated `群`.
- rescue is institutional interoperability, including `橋渡し` to police/fire services.
- failure is remediable; licensing is a developmental quality gate, not pure elimination.
- Twice’s mask functions as self-coherence infrastructure; League belonging is a direct motive.
- All Might explicitly refuses sacrificial death, paying off Inko’s V11 correction.
- Bakugo’s OFA inference demonstrates high-resolution causal observation under emotional antagonism.
- Bakugo’s hidden Kamino guilt is the immediate affective engine of the Ground Beta confrontation.
- Midoriya accepts the fight because he recognizes himself as the only current receiver of Bakugo’s burden, not because he believes the fight is institutionally legitimate.

## 23. Closing synthesis


Volume 13 begins by asking whether a hero candidate can be trusted by an injured stranger and ends with one boy realizing that he is the only person another boy can currently trust with an unbearable feeling.


That scale shift is the volume’s real unity.


HUC makes trust operational: assess me correctly, speak to me in a usable way, move me according to real need, cooperate with other responders, and do not make your private emotional state my emergency problem. The Commission makes trust institutional: no new All Might can simply be assumed, so society must build a coordinated `群` whose members can be conditionally granted authority. Todoroki and Inasa demonstrate what happens when private resentment contaminates that trust. Uraraka demonstrates that even a newly recognized intimate feeling can be deliberately regulated in service of a chosen professional direction. Twice shows the villain-side consequence of having no stable social place to trust at all. AFO and All Might debate how ideals persist beyond finite bodies. And Bakugo finally makes visible the private burden that his public aggression had concealed.


The deepest Volume 13 proposition is therefore not merely “heroes need teamwork.” It is:


> **Reliability requires integration. A person becomes trustworthy not by severing dependence, history, emotion, or inheritance, but by integrating them well enough that they no longer hijack judgment, erase other people, or force one body to carry the entire system alone.**


That is why the `群` matters socially, why Todoroki must remember what he tried to forget, why All Might must live rather than spend himself, why Twice’s lack of `居場所` matters, and why Midoriya’s final decision is relational rather than competitive. He fights because he recognizes that Bakugo’s burden has reached him in a form no one else can presently receive.


The danger remains obvious: integration is not the same thing as legitimacy. Midoriya and Bakugo are still breaking rules. The Commission’s network may still fail. AFO also knows how to build succession and organizations. V13 does not promise that distributed structures are automatically good.


It establishes a harder standard:


> **What matters is whether the relationships and institutions through which power is distributed make people more capable of seeing others, sharing responsibility, correcting failure, and remaining present for what comes next.**


Volume 14 must begin at that unresolved Ground Beta confrontation, without importing its outcome into this volume.
