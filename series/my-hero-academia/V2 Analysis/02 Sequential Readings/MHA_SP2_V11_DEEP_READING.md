# 1. Central thesis and volume role


Volume 10 ended by asking whether successful protection leaves the protected person more able to author the next step, or more dependent on the protector who made survival possible. Volume 11 answers by forcing almost every major relationship in the Kamino sequence to confront the same structural problem: **how does protection continue after the protector can no longer remain the center?**


The answer is not withdrawal alone, and it is not indefinite possession. Volume 11 repeatedly converts singular heroic action into forms of **accountable succession and de-centered support**.


Midoriya saves Bakugo without becoming the hand Bakugo must take. The actual reaching hand belongs to Kirishima, because Midoriya recognizes that Kirishima has built a relationship Bakugo can accept as relatively equal (`V11:p020-025`). All Might wins his final battle, but victory does not preserve the old arrangement: the remaining embers of One For All disappear, his combat career ends, and the public `次は君だ` becomes a private succession message that Midoriya hears as `私はもう 出し切ってしまった` (`V11:p096-102`). All For One produces a deliberately mirrored handoff to Shigaraki, but his theory of mentorship is different: a teacher exists to make the student stand alone, and the mentor's removal can be used as fuel for hatred and independent leadership (`V11:p105-107`).


The most important correction comes later, away from the battlefield. All Might initially promises Inko that he will protect and raise Midoriya `この命に代えても`. Inko rejects the self-sacrificial grammar itself. All Might is Midoriya's `生きがい`; therefore she tells him `命に代えないで / ちゃんと生きて / 守り育てて下さい` (`V11:p159-162`). This is not merely a mother asking a hero to be careful. It is a direct ethical correction to the series' inherited idea that the highest proof of care is willingness to die.


The volume gives Midoriya a parallel individuation. When Inko asks whether U.A. is truly necessary, he does not abandon heroism. He shows Kota's letter and says that even if it is not U.A., even if it is somewhere else, he will become a hero (`V11:p154-155`). All Might then explicitly realizes that Midoriya is no longer merely someone following his back and concludes that, as teacher, he must support him (`V11:p156-158`). The successor becomes more self-authored precisely as the mentor stops treating imitation and access to U.A. as the only valid route.


U.A. undergoes the same transformation institutionally. Mitsuki Bakugo grants trust because Aizawa's press conference proved that the school actually sees her son rather than merely praising his visible talent (`V11:p136-138`). Inko withholds trust because U.A.'s prestige and All Might's greatness do not erase repeated attacks and injuries (`V11:p143-151`). Her consent is conditional, not ceremonial. And after the students move into the dorm, Aizawa does not let a successful rescue retrospectively legitimate their rule breach. He names the five who went to Kamino, says that absent the extraordinary crisis created by All Might's retirement he would have expelled almost the entire informed class, and requires them to restore trust through regular procedure and legitimate activity (`V11:p173-175`).


The closing Tsuyu sequence completes the volume's logic on the smallest scale. She had condemned the covert rescue as behavior resembling villains, then learned the group went anyway. Rather than suppressing the resulting hurt to preserve harmony, she tells the class that she cannot simply return to cheerful conversation while those feelings remain. The group acknowledges the fear and deliberately restores ordinary sociality (`V11:p201-205`). Continuity is not created by pretending rupture never occurred. It is created by making the rupture speakable enough that relationship can resume.


A concise Volume 11 thesis is therefore:


> **Volume 11 is about accountable succession: how a value, relationship, or institution survives when its previous center can no longer carry everything. Durable protection requires de-centering the protector without abandoning the protected, giving successors room to author action, accepting consent and criticism from the people who bear the costs, and repairing trust through future conduct rather than treating victory as retroactive justification.**


The first eleven volumes can now be described compactly as:


`recognition → judgment → public legibility → self-authorship → recognition without possession → enacted identity → aftermath → diagnostic resistance → emergency enactment / cost-bearing → contested legibility / protective authority → accountable succession / de-centered support`

# 2. Narrative architecture


## 2.1 Chapter 90 resolves V10 through relational routing, not greater force


V10 deliberately stopped before the famous Bakugo extraction. Volume 11 opens by validating the structure of the plan rather than replacing it with a stronger attack.


Midoriya insists there is a way to extract Bakugo that `決して戦闘行為にはならない`, allows the students themselves to leave, and still saves him (`V11:p020`). The plan distributes propulsion, route creation and breakthrough across Midoriya, Iida, Todoroki and Kirishima (`V11:p021-023`). Its decisive component, however, is not physical capacity. It is the answer to **who should call Bakugo**.


Midoriya explicitly rules himself out. He also rules out Iida, Todoroki and Yaoyorozu. Kirishima is different because, since entering U.A., he has built what Midoriya calls a `対等な関係` with Bakugo. Midoriya predicts that Kirishima's invitation will have the highest chance of being accepted (`V11:p024`). Bakugo then takes Kirishima's hand (`V11:p025`).


The solution therefore contains four layers:


1. no direct student-villain combat is required;
2. the plan uses complementary physical roles rather than a single strongest actor;
3. Midoriya models the protected person's likely response rather than centering his own desire to rescue;
4. rescue succeeds because the chosen relationship preserves Bakugo's agency and status enough for him to cooperate.


This is one of the cleanest early examples of character knowledge functioning as operational knowledge.


## 2.2 Kamino makes the Symbol's body publicly falsifiable


The All Might/AFO battle is not only a power contest. AFO attacks the public epistemology that sustains the Symbol of Peace.


He deliberately exposes All Might's emaciated true form and demands that `平和の象徴` show the world a miserable body (`V11:p053-057`). The attack is strategic because hero society has treated All Might's visible arrival as a compressed promise of safety. If the body behind the image looks fragile, then the public may reinterpret the entire promise.


All Might's response is not that the body is secretly fine. He instead separates bodily condition from normative commitment: `私の心は 依然 平和の象徴!!` (`V11:p058`). V08 had already established that Toshinori privately knows he cannot save everyone. V11 now adds a second correction to crude Symbol criticism: he also does not believe Symbolhood is identical with physical invulnerability. The role is something he continues to enact after the public disguise collapses.


That does not mean image never mattered. AFO is correct that public trust is vulnerable to what people see. The point is that the manga tests whether the meaning of the image can survive the revelation that the image was never the whole person.


## 2.3 Nana's smile becomes an inherited practice rather than a brand slogan


AFO's revelation that Shigaraki Tomura is Shimura Nana's grandson (`V11:p059`) destabilizes All Might precisely because it fuses his public mission with a private predecessor wound.


The flashback around `V11:p060-061` matters equally for All Might's smile. Nana teaches that even when frightened, one says one is all right and smiles because the person smiling is strongest. The smile therefore enters the V2 model as **inherited affective pedagogy**. It is not adequately described as PR, denial, or spontaneous Toshinori personality.


This inheritance has an ethical ambiguity. The practice can reassure people under danger; it can also conceal cost and teach successors to equate care with suppressing visible fear. V11 does not resolve that ambiguity. It instead shows both sides: the smile helps sustain public hope, while later Inko explicitly rejects a culture of self-destruction disguised as protection.


## 2.4 The public becomes reciprocal support infrastructure


As All Might weakens, civilians do not merely consume reassurance. They call for him, plead for rescue, demand that he win, and continue recognizing him as All Might after the body beneath the Symbol has been exposed (`V11:p062-096`).


The direction of support therefore becomes partially reciprocal. Earlier volumes often modeled the Symbol as a one-way emitter of reassurance. Kamino reveals that social recognition can also return energy to the person performing the role. The public cannot provide OFA output, but it can sustain the meaning structure inside which Toshinori continues acting.


This should not be romanticized into a claim that public dependence is harmless. V07–V10 established the system risk of overconcentration in one figure. V11 instead adds a subtler proposition: **dependence and reciprocity can coexist**. A society may over-rely on a symbol while still becoming a real source of motivation and recognition for that symbol.


## 2.5 Victory ends the old arrangement rather than restoring it


The `UNITED STATES OF SMASH` climax defeats AFO (`V11:p090-096`), but the scene refuses restoration. The old All Might cannot return after winning.


By `V11:p113`, Toshinori states directly: `私の中の残り火は消えた / “平和の象徴”は死にました`. Later, to Midoriya, he calls himself effectively retired and says he is no longer a body capable of fighting (`V11:p124-125`).


This wording matters. The battle does not simply reveal that All Might is weaker than before. It creates a categorical status transition:


`active Symbol with dwindling embers → final expenditure → combat retirement / post-Symbol mentor`.


The narrative question after Kamino is therefore not “can All Might get his strength back?” It is “what can Toshinori still be once the role that organized his adult life can no longer be performed through combat?”


## 2.6 `次は君だ` creates two audiences and two successions


All Might's gesture toward the camera is interpreted publicly as warning: future villains are next. Midoriya receives the same gesture as the opposite message. The narration explicitly distinguishes the public reading from his private one, and Midoriya understands it as `私はもう 出し切ってしまった` (`V11:p099-102`).


The message therefore transfers more than encouragement. It transfers **burden under conditions of loss**.


The mirrored AFO sequence then sharpens the architecture. From confinement, AFO tells Shigaraki that All Might made a mistake by remaining beside his pupil. A teacher, he says, exists to make the student stand alone. When the relied-upon mentor goes somewhere unreachable, Shigaraki's hatred can increase, he can walk at the front, he already has allies, and he has learned how to gain more (`V11:p105-106`). AFO ends with the same phrase: `次は君だ` (`V11:p107`).


This is the clearest formalization so far of dual succession, but it also prevents a simplistic “good mentor supports / bad mentor controls” contrast. AFO explicitly values successor independence. His manipulation operates through what he chooses to make independent, what affect he cultivates, and what resources and causal stories he supplied before withdrawal.


## 2.7 The second half turns succession into a consent problem


The home visits are not a light epilogue to Kamino. They are where heroic institutions face the people who bear their externalized risk.


Mitsuki Bakugo accepts U.A.'s continued custody of Katsuki because she believes the school accurately reads him. Inko initially refuses because U.A. has not delivered the safety that institutional prestige implies. All Might's status as the world's greatest hero is explicitly irrelevant to her evaluation (`V11:p136-162`).


This is the volume's essential shift from symbolic legitimacy to **consented legitimacy**.


## 2.8 Dorms do not erase the rule breach


U.A. moves students into Heights Alliance as a safety and supervision adaptation (`V11:p169-172`). But Aizawa immediately reopens the Kamino rescue issue. The institutional redesign is not a clean slate.


He identifies the five participants and says the rest of the class largely knew what they intended. Only the extraordinary instability following All Might's retirement prevents the full disciplinary response he otherwise would have imposed. He says their reasons do not change that they betrayed the teachers' trust and asks them to restore it through regular procedure and regular achievement (`V11:p173-175`).


Thus the manga denies two easy moral shortcuts:


- success does not prove unauthorized action was legitimate;
- rule violation does not prove the rescuers lacked heroic motive.


The conflict remains genuinely dual.


## 2.9 Ordinary life becomes a repair mechanism


The room-tour material is not analytically disposable comedy. After kidnapping, terror, public institutional crisis and rule conflict, the students need ordinary knowledge of one another again.


The sequence accumulates low-stakes data—taste, embarrassment, domestic habits, aesthetic identity, class teasing—and culminates in Tsuyu refusing to let the class simply act cheerful without resolving her hurt (`V11:p189-205`). The return to `日常へ!!` on `V11:p205` is therefore earned through disclosure rather than amnesia.

# 3. Midoriya: rescue through someone else's hand


## 3.1 V11 supplies a held-out test of the V10 bodily-risk update


V10 ended with Midoriya possessing explicit medical knowledge that repeated comparable arm damage could permanently destroy function. That knowledge mattered only prospectively: would it change action when another person was again in immediate danger?


V11 gives the first meaningful answer. Midoriya still refuses passivity. He still designs a rescue under lethal conditions. But he does **not** convert urgency into direct bodily expenditure. He identifies a noncombat route, distributes physical roles, and chooses Kirishima as the relationship-compatible rescuer (`V11:p020-025`).


All Might later explicitly notices the result: `君が初めて 怪我せず 窮地を脱したこと / すごく嬉しい` (`V11:p126`). This makes the event stronger evidence than a retrospective reader inference. The mentor himself marks it as a behavioral discontinuity.


The V09 formulation therefore survives but becomes more precise:


> Midoriya's low threshold for catastrophic self-cost is not immutable. When he can perceive a rescue route that preserves the other person's agency and distributes action across allies, explicit sustainability knowledge can alter his method without extinguishing rescue urgency.


## 3.2 Decentering is relational rather than merely tactical


The first-pass claim that Midoriya learns “he does not have to be the hand” is correct but underspecified.


He does not choose Kirishima because delegation is abstractly mature. He chooses him because Bakugo's response depends on relationship history. `僕じゃダメだ` is not self-abasement; it is an operational judgment. The same physical hand extended by a different person changes the rescue probability.


This is one of the strongest pieces of evidence yet for Midoriya's character-analysis habit becoming genuinely interpersonal. Earlier notebooks and combat observations sometimes risked reducing people to capabilities. Here, understanding a person's pride, equality needs and relationship map directly determines the ethical form of rescue.


## 3.3 Midoriya can distinguish vocation from institution


The Inko confrontation creates another form of decentering. U.A. has enormous symbolic weight because it is All Might's alma mater and the path Midoriya imagined while Quirkless. Yet when his mother says she cannot currently entrust him to U.A., Midoriya does not make U.A. synonymous with becoming a hero.


Kota's letter is decisive (`V11:p154-155`). Midoriya describes how a child who hated heroes allowed him, for one moment, to become a hero in that child's eyes. He then says that even if it is not U.A., even if it is somewhere else, he will become a hero.


This is a major self-authorship transition. The hero vocation has become portable across institutions.


## 3.4 Mentor loss does not collapse his goal


Midoriya experiences `次は君だ` as loss and burden rather than simple praise (`V11:p099-102`). He cries. Later, All Might tells him directly that the embers are gone and he is effectively retired (`V11:p124-125`).


Yet Midoriya's goal does not disappear when the ideal becomes mortal and non-combatant. That stability matters for model readiness. The V01 boy wanted proximity to All Might; the V11 student can continue after All Might's heroic body is gone and can even imagine an alternate school route.


The longitudinal model is therefore now strong enough to distinguish:


- admiration as an origin condition;
- imitation as an early developmental strategy;
- inherited value as a continuing commitment;
- self-authored hero identity that survives loss of the original route.


## 3.5 Readiness consequence


V11 materially changes multiple previously missing dimensions at once: held-out behavior after explicit bodily-risk knowledge, relationship-specific rescue judgment, mentor-loss adaptation, institutional independence and ordinary relational repair. Midoriya therefore advances from `moderate (very strongly grounded)` to **`strong`** at the V11 boundary.


This does not imply completeness. Adult identity, sustained command and later extreme bodily-risk tests remain valuable future evidence. `strong` here means the accumulated model can responsibly predict across several qualitatively different regimes without relying on fandom shorthand.

# 4. Bakugo and Kirishima: accepting help without accepting subordination


## 4.1 The rescue validates a relationship-specific help model


Bakugo's V10 recruitment refusal already validated the value structure beneath his aggressive surface. V11 tests a different question: can he accept rescue?


The answer is yes, but the route matters. Kirishima has built an equal-enough relationship that taking his hand does not require Bakugo to accept Midoriya's symbolic superiority. Bakugo's `…バカかよ` on accepting the hand preserves his ordinary register rather than turning the moment into grateful softness (`V11:p024-025`).


This is important negative evidence against a common simplification. Bakugo does not need to become verbally tender before his relationships can become cooperative or reciprocal.


## 4.2 Repayment makes reciprocity concrete


Later Bakugo calls Kirishima over and gives him money. He states that remaining indebted feels bad: `いつまでも借りっぱなしも こっちも気分悪ィんだ` (`V11:p176-177`). The page identifies the night-vision goggles as ¥49,800.


This is not merely a gag. It shows Bakugo converting received help into an account he can actively settle. The mechanism is consistent with his broader autonomy structure: he can receive something important, but leaving the relationship in a passive debt state is uncomfortable.


That does not mean every future form of help will be accepted if repayable. But it gives the model a more precise hypothesis:


> Bakugo is more comfortable with help when the relationship can be represented as reciprocal, equal, earned or repayable rather than as unilateral caretaking from above.


## 4.3 Mitsuki confirms the distinction between praise and recognition


At the Bakugo home, Mitsuki explains that Katsuki could do many things and was blessed with ability, so other people praised him and brought him this far. But, in her view, they often praised only the shallow visible part. Aizawa's press-conference language pleased her because it showed that U.A. `勝己を見てくれてる` (`V11:p136-138`).


This is unusually valuable triangulation. The manga does not only tell us Aizawa read Bakugo accurately; a parent with long-term knowledge independently identifies the same distinction.


Bakugo's strong-readiness state is therefore further strengthened by family evidence and by help-receiving under a relationship-compatible condition.

# 5. All Might: the end of embodiment is not the end of responsibility


## 5.1 The Symbol survives bodily exposure long enough to complete its final act


AFO's most psychologically sophisticated attack is to expose the body behind the public image. All Might does not respond by restoring illusion. He keeps acting after the illusion is gone.


`私の心は 依然 平和の象徴!!` (`V11:p058`) should therefore be read as a claim about role commitment under bodily falsification. It is neither proof that image never mattered nor evidence that Toshinori thinks willpower can erase limits.


V08 already gave `悔しいが 私も人だ`. V11 now shows what a consciously finite person does when the social role built on apparent invulnerability becomes publicly impossible to maintain.


## 5.2 Nana's inherited smile complicates the Symbol critique


The Nana flashback (`V11:p060-061`) reclassifies All Might's smile. It is part of a teacher-transmitted rescue practice: frightened people need an image of confidence, and the rescuer deliberately performs `大丈夫` even under fear.


The smile is therefore simultaneously:


- sincere commitment;
- inherited technique;
- public affect regulation;
- concealment of vulnerability;
- a practice capable of helping others while potentially isolating the person performing it.


Future analysis should preserve all five functions rather than choosing one total explanation.


## 5.3 The final victory produces retirement, not restoration


All Might defeats AFO, but `V11:p113` makes the cost explicit: `私の中の残り火は消えた / “平和の象徴”は死にました`.


He later tells Midoriya that he is effectively retired and can no longer fight (`V11:p124`). The end state is not ambiguous enough to keep “maybe the Symbol can return” as the operative model. Combat-symbol All Might has ended.


Gran Torino immediately prevents the character model from collapsing into “All Might is over.” He tells Toshinori to remain at U.A. and fulfill what he must; even if he cannot remain the Symbol of Peace, All Might is still alive (`V11:p116`).


That is one of the cleanest identity-separation statements in the series so far:


`role loss ≠ person loss`.


## 5.4 Public support becomes part of his last performance


The crowd's calls during Kamino matter because they reveal a two-way relationship between symbol and audience. All Might has trained society to look to him; at the moment his bodily weakness becomes visible, society returns recognition and expectation.


This does not solve the structural fragility of concentrated heroism. It does show why simple condemnation of public reliance misses part of the relationship. Toshinori is not merely exploited by expectation; expectation is also part of what lets the role remain meaningful to him.


## 5.5 Post-Symbol mentorship requires a new competence


The beach conversation with Midoriya begins awkwardly. All Might still scolds, hits, worries and uses the language of a mentor who has repeatedly watched his successor ignore bodily limits (`V11:p124-127`). But he also says he is happy that Midoriya escaped a crisis without injury and that he will now devote himself to Midoriya's development.


The second half of the volume then exposes a flaw in All Might's first attempt to perform that new role. Faced with Inko, he promises to protect Midoriya even at the cost of his life (`V11:p159-160`). Inko rejects precisely that promise.


All Might has lost the ability to solve every problem through combat, but his instinctive moral grammar still reaches for self-sacrifice. He must learn that being a teacher means **staying alive enough to support**.


## 5.6 Readiness consequence


V10 kept All Might at `moderate` specifically because post-battle/post-Symbol identity and successor adaptation were still unknown. V11 supplies both at high density across public battle, private mentorship, colleague correction, parental accountability and institutional petition.


All Might therefore advances `moderate → strong` at V11.


Open evidence remains—later grief, long-term helplessness, broader help-receiving and the durability of his teacher identity—but the model now spans heroic performance, private limitation, pedagogy, failure, retirement and re-authored purpose.

# 6. All For One and Shigaraki: withdrawal as engineered individuation


## 6.1 AFO does not merely want a dependent successor


V10 had already shown that AFO could validate Shigaraki's self-direction inside structural dependency. V11 makes that architecture more explicit and forces a revision to any model that treats dependency as the endpoint.


From confinement, AFO says All Might mistimed separation from his student. `先生というのは 弟子を一人立ちさせる為にいる` (`V11:p105-106`). He predicts that removal of a relied-upon teacher will intensify Shigaraki's hatred and make him walk at the front. He notes that Shigaraki already has allies and has learned how to gain more.


AFO therefore values **independent successor performance**, provided the successor's affect, worldview and resource environment have been shaped toward AFO's desired future.


## 6.2 Manipulation and autonomy are not opposites


This requires a more sophisticated coercion model. A person can make real decisions, lead real allies and acquire real competence while the space of valued outcomes has been engineered by someone else.


AFO's method now includes:


- interpreting pain;
- absolving failure;
- providing rescue and retry infrastructure;
- selecting powers suited to temperament;
- validating initiative;
- cultivating hatred;
- then accepting or even welcoming physical separation when it can force leadership growth.


The right contrast with All Might is therefore not “independence versus dependence.” Both mentors want a successor capable of standing on their own. The ethical contrast lies in **what kind of person the successor is being enabled to become, how much truth and choice structure the mentorship supplies, and whether the mentor's support expands or captures the successor's future**.


## 6.3 The mirrored `次は君だ` is formally symmetric but psychologically asymmetric


All Might's `次は君だ` is received by Midoriya as depletion: the mentor has spent everything and the burden now reaches him. AFO's `次は君だ` is delivered as strategic confidence: the mentor's removal is itself material for Shigaraki's next stage (`V11:p099-107`).


Both messages de-center the mentor. One emerges from physical exhaustion and public duty; the other from a theory that frustration, hatred and absence can accelerate succession.


The symmetry is therefore narratively real without implying moral equivalence.


## 6.4 Shigaraki remains prospectively incomplete


V11 does not yet provide enough direct Shigaraki behavior after AFO's incarceration to prove mature independence. AFO believes the student can lead; that belief is evidence about AFO and a prospective prediction about Shigaraki, not proof that the prediction has already succeeded.


Shigaraki therefore remains `moderate (very strongly grounded)`. The next high-value test is how he behaves when AFO cannot immediately solve operational failure for him.

# 7. Inko Midoriya: parental consent as an independent moral jurisdiction


## 7.1 Inko's objection is not anti-heroic


The home-visit sequence becomes much clearer when Inko's claims are separated from a generic “worried mother” archetype.


She explicitly tells Midoriya that she has always admired him and recalls telling him she would support him; she also reminds him that support never meant the absence of worry (`V11:p143-145`). Her objection is not to heroism as a vocation. It is to the combination of recurrent severe injury and an institution that has repeatedly failed to prevent attacks.


Her thought that the Quirkless child happily watching heroes might have been happier than a son headed toward a `血みどろの未来` (`V11:p144`) is one of the series' strongest counterclaims to achievement teleology. Obtaining power and entering the dream institution are not automatically proof that the child's life became better.


## 7.2 U.A. prestige does not override delivered safety


Inko says explicitly that however wonderful a hero All Might is, it is irrelevant to her evaluation of U.A. She points to an institution attacked by villains, unable to continue ordinary classes, and unable to stop students from suffering severe injuries (`V11:p147-151`).


The parent therefore applies an outcome-sensitive legitimacy test:


> **If an institution asks for custody and trust, its ideals and prestige do not cancel the obligation to make the entrusted child's continued life reasonably survivable.**


This adds a civilian/parental jurisdiction that the hero system cannot simply absorb into professional judgment.


## 7.3 `モンスターペアレンツでいい` rejects shame as a consent mechanism


Inko anticipates that her refusal may be labeled unreasonable: `モンスターペアレンツかもしれません でも モンスターでいいです` (`V11:p151`). She accepts the social cost of appearing obstructive because the underlying responsibility—to protect her child's life and dream—matters more than deference to institutional authority.


This is character-modeling evidence about conflict behavior. Under high-stakes institutional pressure, Inko can be deferential in surface register without surrendering the substantive boundary.


## 7.4 She separates Midoriya's dream from U.A.


Crucially, Inko does not say that Midoriya must stop pursuing heroism. She proposes another hero course if necessary. The distinction is:


`I support your vocation` ≠ `I consent to this institution under current conditions`.


That separation allows Midoriya's response to become self-authored rather than oppositional. He does not need to choose between mother and dream.


## 7.5 She corrects All Might's sacrificial promise


All Might tries to answer institutional mistrust with total personal commitment. He says his own path was bloody and that he wants to walk beside Midoriya so the boy need not follow the same route; he asks Inko to look not only at present U.A. but at the U.A. trying to change (`V11:p159`). Then he promises `この命に代えても 守り育てます` (`V11:p160`).


Inko's immediate answer is `…やっぱり 嫌です`.


The reason is exact and ethically decisive: All Might is Midoriya's `生きがい`; she does not hate U.A.; she wants Midoriya to be happy; therefore `命に代えないで / ちゃんと生きて / 守り育てて下さい` (`V11:p161`).


She refuses to purchase her son's safety with the mentor's death.


This is a major series-level correction to sacrificial logic. The parent demands **continuing care rather than substitutive death**.


## 7.6 Consent is conditional trust, not capitulation


At `V11:p162`, Inko says that if All Might will promise that, she too will yield. All Might answers `約束します`.


Her consent therefore has an explicit condition: the institution and mentor may continue only under a revised understanding of care. She is not persuaded by status; she is persuaded by accountability, Midoriya's demonstrated self-authorship, and a promise oriented toward continued life.


## 7.7 Readiness consequence


Inko advances from `insufficient/emerging (strengthened)` to **`emerging (strongly grounded)`**. V11 supplies sustained conflict behavior, institutional evaluation, explicit values, conditional consent and interaction with All Might beyond private mother-son worry.


A promotion to `moderate` would still be premature because nearly all evidence remains organized around Midoriya and crisis. Ordinary independent life, broader relationships and non-parental identity remain undersampled.

# 8. U.A. and the Bakugo family: trust is earned by accurate recognition


## 8.1 Mitsuki's approval does not contradict Inko's refusal


The home visits deliberately show that “parents” are not one social actor.


Mitsuki Bakugo knows that Katsuki has been praised for competence and advantageous ability. Her complaint is not lack of recognition in the everyday sense; it is **shallow recognition**. People praise what is easy to see and can thereby reinforce the worst parts of his self-concept.


Aizawa's public statement after the kidnapping matters because Mitsuki hears in it evidence that U.A. sees the person rather than the surface: `この学校は 勝己を見てくれてる` (`V11:p136-138`). She therefore trusts U.A. despite the attack.


Inko, by contrast, sees a son whose body has repeatedly been damaged under U.A.'s watch. Her refusal is not evidence that she understands U.A. less. The two parents apply different child-specific risk models.


## 8.2 Recognition can generate institutional legitimacy


The Bakugo scene strengthens a central V2 thesis that has been building since V01: recognition is not merely emotional niceness. Accurate recognition can become an institutional asset.


U.A. wins Mitsuki's consent because Aizawa publicly refuses the villain interpretation of Bakugo without denying his difficult surface behavior. That is **epistemic trust**: she believes the school has a model of her son adequate enough to teach him.


## 8.3 The school must answer both recognition and safety claims


The two home visits together yield a stronger institutional standard:


1. understand the student accurately;
2. admit failures honestly;
3. redesign when the threat environment changes;
4. provide credible safety infrastructure;
5. accept that parental consent is not automatic.


A school can meet one condition and still be challenged on another. This prevents “U.A. good” or “U.A. negligent” from functioning as sufficient analysis.

# 9. Aizawa and the dorm: crisis does not erase procedure


## 9.1 Dorm implementation is adaptive protection, not merely control


The move into `ハイツアライアンス` responds to an environment in which dispersed student homes and ordinary school routines have become exploitable vulnerabilities (`V11:p169-172`). It increases supervision and concentrates students in a protectable space.


There is a real control dimension, but the source does not support reducing the dorm to disciplinary enclosure. It is an institutional adaptation to a demonstrated targeting problem.


## 9.2 Aizawa refuses outcome-based moral laundering


Once students arrive, Aizawa identifies Todoroki, Kirishima, Midoriya, Yaoyorozu and Iida as the five who went to rescue Bakugo (`V11:p173`). He also states that the rest of the informed class bears responsibility for knowing and not stopping them.


His strongest line is counterfactual: if All Might's retirement had not created a situation in which U.A. cannot afford to eject people amid continuing villain uncertainty, he would have expelled everyone except Bakugo, Jiro and Hagakure (`V11:p174-175`).


This means successful rescue does not retrospectively legalize or institutionalize the act.


## 9.3 Discipline is adjusted to system conditions without changing the judgment


Aizawa's actual punishment is moderated because the institution is operating under extraordinary strategic pressure. But he says the reasons do not change that students betrayed the teachers' trust.


This is a valuable example of **context-sensitive sanction without norm abandonment**.


The school does not say:


- “you succeeded, therefore you were right”; or
- “you broke the rule, therefore your rescue motive was false.”


It says the motive and the breach can both be real.


## 9.4 Trust repair must be enacted through legitimate procedure


Aizawa asks students to restore the lost trust by following `正規の手続き` and performing legitimate activity (`V11:p175`). This directly connects V06–V07 hero-law analysis to classroom development.


Legitimacy is longitudinal. One bad act can damage trust; one good intention cannot instantly restore it. Repair requires future conduct.


That mechanism mirrors the interpersonal structure of the volume: Tsuyu also cannot simply pretend the rescue conflict is over because the outcome was good.

# 10. Tsuyu and Class 1-A: relationship repair requires making hurt speakable


## 10.1 Tsuyu's earlier condemnation remains meaningful after success


At the hospital, Tsuyu had said that breaking the rules in this way made the action resemble that of villains. V11 does not quietly erase that position once Bakugo is recovered.


She recalls the statement directly (`V11:p202`). She also says she forced herself to use harsh language and then learned the group went anyway.


This preserves the emotional cost of ignored dissent.


## 10.2 Her distress includes self-critique, not only accusation


Tsuyu says she thought she had stopped them and felt inadequate when she learned she had not. Several unpleasant feelings overflowed at once, and she became unable to figure out what to say or to chat happily with everyone (`V11:p202`).


This is important for her model. She does not simply moralize from outside. When her warning fails, she directs part of the disappointment inward.


## 10.3 She chooses disclosure over false harmony


Tsuyu's core decision is that the sadness of not being able to speak normally is itself intolerable. Even if her thoughts are not neatly organized, she wants to talk properly so she can again enjoy ordinary conversation with everyone (`V11:p203`).


This gives Tsuyu a strong conflict-repair heuristic:


> when unresolved moral hurt makes ordinary intimacy feel false, she prefers explicit disclosure even if the disclosure is emotionally difficult and imperfectly organized.


## 10.4 Uraraka acts as relational bridge


Uraraka gathers the relevant classmates and explains that Tsuyu wants to speak. She then reframes the room-contest frivolity as an attempt by the class to clear anxiety rather than assign blame and says the goal is to laugh together and keep going (`V11:p201-204`).


This is meaningful ordinary-life leadership. Uraraka is not solving a battle; she is helping convert dispersed guilt and hurt into a setting where relationship can resume.


## 10.5 `日常へ!!` is a relational achievement


`V11:p205` explicitly returns the class to `日常へ!!` only after Tsuyu cries, classmates apologize and reassurance is exchanged.


The volume therefore treats ordinary life as something that can be damaged by crisis and rebuilt through communication. This supports a larger MHA theme: hero development is not only increased capacity to survive abnormal events; it is increased capacity to return to shared life afterward.


## 10.6 Readiness consequence


Tsuyu advances `emerging (strengthened) → moderate` at V11.


The promotion is justified not by scene prominence but by a material coverage expansion: the model now spans USJ danger, principled rule judgment, friendship conflict, self-directed disappointment, vulnerable disclosure, ordinary-life reintegration and multiple class relationships. Future evidence should still broaden her family, professional and later leadership regimes.

# 11. Kirishima: heroic self-conception becomes relationship infrastructure


V10 established that Kirishima's `動けなきゃ` concern is an identity threshold: failing to move when he believes action is required threatens his idea of what a man/hero should be.


V11 gives this motive a more sustainable form. He does not prove courage by independently charging AFO. He accepts a role inside a noncombat plan and becomes the person whose relationship makes Bakugo's rescue possible (`V11:p021-025`).

# 12. Japanese-language and conceptual evidence


## 12.1 `手を`


Chapter 90's title is literal and relational. The important question is not only whether a hand can physically reach Bakugo, but **whose hand can be accepted**. The title retrospectively converts Midoriya's rescue obsession from reach-as-distance into reach-as-relationship.


## 12.2 `対等な関係`


Midoriya's description of Kirishima and Bakugo as having built a `対等な関係` (`V11:p024`) is the semantic key to the extraction. Equality is not claimed as perfect emotional symmetry. It names the social condition under which Bakugo can accept help without interpreting it as humiliating subordination.


## 12.3 `平和の象徴`


V11 uses the phrase in at least three distinct senses:


- AFO's target: a public image whose credibility can be damaged;
- All Might's internal commitment: `私の心は 依然 平和の象徴`;
- All Might's declared ended status after the embers disappear: `“平和の象徴”は死にました`.


The phrase therefore shifts from person-image fusion toward a role that can be inhabited, attacked and relinquished.


## 12.4 `残り火`


`残り火` makes OFA's post-transfer persistence finite and consumable. It gives a concrete temporal form to succession: the predecessor can remain active after transmission, but not indefinitely.


## 12.5 `次は君だ`


The same phrase is formally assigned to both successor lines (`V11:p099-107`). Its meaning depends on relationship context.


For Midoriya, it is heard as depletion and burden. For Shigaraki, AFO frames it as individuation after mentor removal. Identical grammar carries different succession ethics.


## 12.6 `一人立ち`


AFO's statement that a teacher exists to make a pupil `一人立ち` is crucial because it blocks a simplistic captivity model of mentorship. His preferred successor is not a puppet who never acts independently; it is a person whose independent action has been developmentally shaped toward AFO's world.


## 12.7 `これからの雄英`


All Might asks Inko to look not only at current U.A. but `これから` U.A.—the institution in the act of changing (`V11:p159`). This is an institutional claim of revisability, not a request to ignore failure.


## 12.8 `命に代えないで`


Inko's imperative is one of the most important ethical phrases in the volume. It directly reverses All Might's `この命に代えても` promise. Protection is redefined from willingness to substitute one's life into a commitment to **live and continue care**.


## 12.9 `正規の手続き`


Aizawa's trust-repair language places formal procedure inside moral development. The term does not mean rules are intrinsically infallible; it means licensed public power depends on routes that make action accountable and socially legible.


## 12.10 `日常`


The return to `日常へ!!` after Tsuyu's disclosure makes ordinary life an achieved condition rather than empty downtime. Crisis resolution is incomplete until people can inhabit shared everyday space without suppressing unresolved rupture.

# 13. Visual grammar and motif


## 13.1 The hand changes from reach to fit


The rescue sequence is built around bodies that can already reach the physical space but cannot all produce the same relational result. Kirishima's extended hand is visually simple because the complexity has been moved into the prior model of Bakugo.


## 13.2 The exposed body breaks iconographic fusion


AFO's exposure of All Might's skeletal form places the public heroic image and the fragile body in the same broadcast frame (`V11:p053-058`). The composition asks whether social meaning survives visual contradiction.


## 13.3 The raised fist survives where the body does not


After AFO falls, All Might's raised arm becomes the compressed image of victory (`V11:p096-099`). The gesture is legible even though the body beneath it no longer supports future combat. Symbolic form momentarily outlives its material basis.


## 13.4 Pointing creates an asymmetric double address


The `次は君だ` gesture is staged through camera mediation. The crowd sees a public warning; Midoriya experiences a private handoff (`V11:p099-102`). The same image simultaneously organizes two audiences.


## 13.5 The AFO mirror converts visual succession into structural comparison


AFO's own `次は君だ` (`V11:p107`) makes the parallel impossible to dismiss as thematic coincidence. The visual repetition authorizes direct comparison while the surrounding dialogue preserves ethical difference.


## 13.6 Dogeza relocates heroic scale


All Might's bow before Inko (`V11:p157-162`) is almost the inverse of Kamino spectacle. The man who just occupied national broadcast space lowers his body before one parent and asks for permission. Heroic legitimacy becomes visually subordinate to accountability.


## 13.7 Dorm rooms externalize ordinary selfhood


The room-tour sequence uses private space as low-stakes character evidence. Students become knowable through decoration, tidiness, embarrassment, hobbies and aesthetic preference rather than combat output. This matters for character modeling because it enlarges behavior beyond crisis-only samples.


## 13.8 Tears are allowed after the battle is over


Tsuyu's tears (`V11:p202-204`) appear not in the lethal crisis but in the work of restoring friendship. Emotional intensity is therefore not reserved for action spectacle; relationship maintenance receives its own visual seriousness.

# 14. Hero Society and institutional model updates


## 14.1 Kamino is the end of a social operating assumption


The most consequential institutional fact of V11 is not simply that All Might retires. It is that the public now knows the body behind the Symbol, has seen the Symbol reach its limit, and must continue living after the assumption of effectively unlimited singular rescue has been publicly falsified (`V11:p053-113`).


The institutional system therefore loses an informal guarantee that had been doing work beyond any written rule. Police, schools, agencies and civilians now have to operate in a world where “All Might can arrive” is no longer a valid terminal contingency.


## 14.2 Public trust is neither blind nor unitary


V10 established public trust as a strategic casualty. V11 shows different trust responses rather than one national attitude.


Some civilians continue supporting All Might even after his weak body is visible. Mitsuki grants U.A. trust because it accurately understands Katsuki. Inko withholds trust because U.A. has not protected Midoriya adequately. Aizawa then says students themselves damaged teacher trust by acting outside procedure.


“Trust” must therefore be disaggregated into at least:


- public-symbol trust;
- parent-to-school custodial trust;
- teacher-to-student procedural trust;
- peer relational trust;
- epistemic trust that another person/institution understands the person correctly.


V11 shows all five operating simultaneously.


## 14.3 Parental authority is a real check on hero institutions


U.A. does not merely announce the dorm plan and take students. Teachers visit homes and seek consent. Inko can say no. All Might has to argue, apologize and promise changed conduct.


That matters because hero education could otherwise be analyzed as a closed professional pipeline whose internal values settle all disputes. V11 explicitly places family authority outside that loop.


## 14.4 Accountability includes the ability to be corrected by civilians


All Might's dogeza is not public-relations theater for an audience. He is privately accountable to a parent whose child has paid the bodily cost of his mentorship. Inko corrects his promise, and he accepts the correction (`V11:p159-162`).


This is a stronger form of institutional legitimacy than merely apologizing after failure: the powerful actor allows the less institutionally powerful stakeholder to change the terms of future care.


## 14.5 U.A. adapts without declaring emergency rule permanent


Dorms increase control, but Chapter 99 immediately points toward provisional licensing. Aizawa says the provisional license is a serious qualification directly connected to human life and that the examination is appropriately severe (`V11:p206-207`).


The response to crisis is therefore not “children can now fight however they need to.” It is accelerated but still formal preparation for accountable authority.

# 15. Recognition / failed rescue model updates


## 15.1 Rescue can fail when the right action is offered through the wrong relationship


The Kirishima-hand solution provides a new recognition category. Midoriya can physically participate in saving Bakugo while recognizing that **his own direct offer may reduce acceptance probability**.


This is recognition without possession in operational form: understanding the other person well enough to give up the emotionally central role.


## 15.2 Protected-person perspective continues to travel through Kota


Kota's letter returns during the Inko dispute (`V11:p154-155`). In V09, Kota's direct experience of rescue changed his ability to understand his parents' sacrifice. In V11, his response becomes evidence for Midoriya and then indirectly for Inko and All Might.


The rescue therefore has longitudinal social afterlife. A successful intervention can become information that helps other people interpret the rescuer's vocation.


## 15.3 Inko recognizes the difference between dream and route


Inko's challenge would be a failed recognition if she treated Midoriya's hero desire as a childish fixation to be abolished. Instead she proposes another hero course. She recognizes the value while rejecting the current path.


This is a strong civilian parallel to the series' recurring distinction between goal and method.


## 15.4 All Might recognizes Midoriya's individuation


After hearing Midoriya say he will become a hero even outside U.A., All Might explicitly thinks `君はもう ただ私の背を追うだけの人間ではない` and concludes that a teacher must support him (`V11:p156`).


Recognition changes mentorship behavior. That is exactly the test established by earlier volumes: seeing another person matters only if the perception changes what one does next.


## 15.5 Tsuyu makes failed prevention part of repair


Tsuyu believed she had stopped the rescue group and is distressed to learn she had not (`V11:p202`). She does not respond by escalating condemnation. She communicates the hurt because she wants relationship restored.


This produces a new failed-rescue subtype: **failed prevention of a friend's dangerous action can create responsibility feelings even when the friend's action succeeds**.

# 16. Power philosophy updates


## 16.1 OFA's `残り火` makes succession temporally overlapping


One For All is not transferred as an instantaneous replacement of predecessor by successor. The predecessor retains finite embers after transfer, allowing an overlap period in which both mentor and successor act.


V11 ends that overlap. This explains why succession can feel sudden socially even though it was gradual privately.


## 16.2 Power is not identical with symbolic authority


All Might can retain symbolic authority after losing reliable combat power; conversely, the loss of power eventually forces explicit retirement. The relationship is neither identity nor independence.


A better model is:


`power enables repeated enactment of role → repeated enactment builds social authority → authority can persist briefly after power collapses → long-term role structure must then change`.


## 16.3 AFO's sensory stack demonstrates compensatory accumulation


In confinement, AFO explains that he can perceive through small air movement/vibration and infrared rather than relying on ordinary sight (`V11:p103-104`). This is useful power-model evidence because it shows multi-Quirk accumulation functioning as compensatory systems architecture rather than merely additive attack output.


## 16.4 Mentor power includes shaping the successor's future option space


AFO's most important power in the succession sequence is not a Quirk. It is the capacity to preconfigure Shigaraki's allies, emotional interpretation, resources and developmental expectations before physical separation.


Similarly, All Might's post-combat power becomes institutional and relational: access, teaching, public authority, responsibility and the ability to support Midoriya's training.


V11 therefore broadens “power” beyond bodily output without dissolving the importance of bodily power.

# 17. Villain formation and succession updates


## 17.1 Shigaraki's origin boundary advances only one step


V11 canonically establishes that Shigaraki Tomura is Shimura Nana's grandson (`V11:p059`). It also establishes that Nana separated her child from herself as part of protecting the family from AFO-related danger (`V11:p115`).


That is enough to alter the inheritance model. It is **not** enough to import later childhood causation, household dynamics or Tenko's full biography. Those remain OPEN prospectively.


## 17.2 AFO weaponizes genealogy as psychological force


The revelation is timed to destabilize All Might during combat. Family history becomes an attack vector.


This strengthens the AFO model as someone who treats knowledge about relationships as operational weaponry. He does not merely overpower bodies; he identifies which truth, revealed at which moment, can damage decision quality and affect regulation.


## 17.3 Nana's protective separation has unintended longitudinal consequences


The source shows Nana separating her child for safety; V11 does not yet establish the full path from that decision to Shigaraki. Still, it opens a legitimate prospective question:


> Can protection that removes relationship in order to prevent immediate harm create vulnerabilities that later protection systems fail to address?


The question should remain OPEN rather than converted into causal certainty.


## 17.4 AFO's capture is also a succession operation


AFO calls his own defeat real (`負けたよ オールマイト`) yet immediately interprets it through successor development (`V11:p105-107`). This ability to convert personal loss into a future-oriented plan distinguishes him from villains whose identity collapses when immediate victory fails.


His readiness remains `moderate`, strongly strengthened by defeat response and explicit mentorship theory.

# 18. First-pass comparison and claim revision


The historical V1 artifact bundled Volumes 10 and 11. The second pass can now route each claim to its actual source boundary.


## 18.1 “Kamino turns inheritance into burden”


**PRESERVE / STRENGTHEN / RELOCATE.**


The broad claim remains correct, but its strongest evidence is V11, not the undifferentiated V10–V11 unit. V10 opens Kamino and exposes succession structures; V11 supplies the final expenditure, `次は君だ`, declared death of the Symbol role and post-combat mentor transition.


The revised claim is stronger:


> Kamino turns inheritance into **accountable succession**. Burden transfers, but the series simultaneously asks what support, consent and institutional change are required so the successor is not simply consumed by the predecessor's role.


## 18.2 “Midoriya learns he does not have to be the hand”


**PRESERVE / STRENGTHEN.**


The first-pass claim was right but too generic. V11 grounds the mechanism in `対等な関係`: Midoriya chooses Kirishima because Bakugo's acceptance depends on who reaches.


This is relational routing, not abstract delegation.


## 18.3 “Bakugo becomes the person villains misread”


**PRESERVE, already adjudicated primarily in V10; STRENGTHEN relationally in V11.**


V10 contains the clean recruitment falsification. V11 adds that Bakugo also cannot be modeled from pride alone: he can accept help when the relationship preserves sufficient equality and can later settle the debt explicitly.


## 18.4 “Shigaraki and Midoriya are parallel heirs”


**STRENGTHEN / REVISE.**


V11 makes the parallel textual and visual through mirrored `次は君だ` scenes. However, the old “one successor gets trust, the other manipulation” contrast is too simple.


AFO also wants Shigaraki to stand on his own. All Might also exerts enormous shaping pressure through admiration and inherited role. The better comparison examines transparency, ends, support structures, emotional cultivation and the successor's freedom to redirect the inherited project.


## 18.5 “The doctor warning and Inko objection change the ethics of heroism”


**SPLIT / STRENGTHEN.**


The arm warning belongs to V10. Inko's institutional confrontation belongs to V11.


Together they form a longitudinal sequence:


`body supplies medical limit → rescue method changes → parent supplies relational/institutional limit → mentor promise changes`.


## 18.6 “All Might bowing to Inko accepts accountability”


**STRENGTHEN substantially.**


The source goes beyond a generic apology. All Might separates his apology as the former Symbol from his petition as a U.A. teacher (`V11:p157-159`), and Inko directly corrects his sacrificial promise. Accountability changes the future terms of mentorship.


## 18.7 “U.A. dorms are safety, control and institutional escalation”


**PRESERVE / REVISE.**


The safety/control description is grounded, but “wartime preparation” is broader than the source requires at this boundary. V11 more specifically supports:


- protective concentration;
- increased supervision;
- consent-driven institutional redesign;
- continued formal licensing;
- internal discipline and trust repair.


## 18.8 “Midoriya's heroism is becoming less self-destructive”


**STRENGTHEN with held-out validation.**


V11 supplies the first source-marked case in which All Might explicitly celebrates Midoriya escaping a crisis without injury. This is better evidence than an interpretive trend claim.


## 18.9 New V11 claim: the series distinguishes dying for someone from staying alive for them


**NEW.**


Inko's `命に代えないで` directly challenges the idea that maximal sacrifice is maximal care. For All Might's new teacher role, survival becomes a duty.


## 18.10 New V11 claim: ordinary life is part of post-crisis repair


**NEW.**


The dorm/room/Tsuyu sequence makes return to everyday sociality an explicit endpoint. The class cannot simply leap from trauma to the next exam; emotional and relational repair are part of hero development.

# 23. What this volume changes in the current MHA model


## 23.1 Succession is no longer equivalent to replacement


All Might stops fighting but remains teacher. AFO is removed but deliberately treats absence as successor development. Mentorship can continue through presence or through engineered withdrawal; the analytical question becomes what each mode does to the successor's agency.


## 23.2 Midoriya's rescue ethic achieves its first explicit sustainability success


The V10 arm warning is not ignored. V11's Bakugo extraction uses a distributed, no-combat, relationship-sensitive method, and All Might explicitly marks the lack of injury as new.


## 23.3 Relationship knowledge becomes operational infrastructure


Kirishima's hand succeeds because equality history matters. Character modeling is not an interpretive luxury inside the story; it changes the rescue plan.


## 23.4 All Might's Symbol is historically inherited and materially finite


Nana supplies the smile practice; OFA supplies finite embers; public recognition sustains meaning; Kamino ends the body-based role. The Symbol is a constructed social practice, not a mystical essence.


## 23.5 AFO's mentorship must be modeled as engineered autonomy


He wants Shigaraki capable of leading without immediate mentor presence. Manipulation operates by shaping motives, support conditions and future option space, not by eliminating all independent choice.


## 23.6 Parents become constitutional actors in hero education


Mitsuki and Inko show different child-specific judgments. U.A. cannot simply declare safety sufficient; it must obtain consent from people whose children bear the risk.


## 23.7 Self-sacrifice receives an explicit relational limit


`この命に代えても` is not allowed to stand as the highest form of promise. Inko replaces it with `命に代えないで / ちゃんと生きて / 守り育てて下さい`.


This may be one of the most important ethical corrections in the first eleven volumes.


## 23.8 Institutional rule remains morally real after successful disobedience


Aizawa's response prevents outcome bias. The students' rescue worked; the trust breach still existed. Legitimate heroism requires a future route back through accountable procedure.


## 23.9 Ordinary life is part of resilience


The class does not recover by defeating AFO. It recovers enough to proceed when Tsuyu can say what hurt, classmates can respond, and daily social life becomes possible again.


## 23.10 Volume 11's governing question for the next reading


> **Once inherited purpose is no longer carried by one overwhelming protector, can characters turn that purpose into repeatable skill, legitimate authority and shared everyday practice without rebuilding the same dependence in a new form?**


That question should remain active through Volume 12 as Class 1-A moves toward special moves and provisional licensing in a world where All Might can no longer function as the universal final answer.
