# My Hero Academia - Second-Pass Deep Reading, Volume 38

## 0. Status, method, and prospective lock

This document is the canonical Japanese-primary second-pass reading of **My Hero Academia Volume 38**, conducted prospectively from the frozen Volume 37 boundary at `V37:p195`.

The reading does **not** use Volume 39 or later outcomes to adjudicate unresolved states. In particular, this volume is allowed to establish only what its own pages establish about:

- Bakugo and Edgeshot's still-unresolved repair state;
- Midoriya versus the Shigaraki/Tenko/AFO identity system;
- AFO's Rewind body and attempt to reach Shigaraki;

# 1. Governing transition: propagation becomes plural authorship

Volume 37 ended by making propagation explicit. Wounds, loyalties, powers, weather, institutional choices and inherited ideals had become coupled: local actions no longer remained local. Volume 38 asks what happens **after those coupled effects arrive at the same battlefield**.

The governing transition is therefore:

> **causal inheritance under propagation -> plural authorship under convergence: once the fronts recouple, no single actor can own the future; heroism consists in choosing how inherited power, injury, love, expertise, memory and public attention will be used so that other people remain able to act.**

This is the volume's strongest unifying claim.

AFO repeatedly seeks ownership:

- ownership of Quirks;
- ownership of successors;
- ownership of Machia's loyalty;
- ownership of Shigaraki's body and hatred;
- ownership of the story itself;
- ultimately, ownership of other people's possible futures.

The counter-system is not simply “the heroes are stronger together.” It is **anti-ownership through differentiated agency**. Gentle acts without an audience. La Brava makes sure his real action can nevertheless be known. Business Course students define recording and connection as hero activity. Reporters decide that witnessing is also personal responsibility. Inasa chooses the future made by people he supports over the old terror AFO represents. Mina converts fear into mutual-support doctrine. Machia's loyalty ceases to be mechanically available to AFO. Iida's legs carry Shoto because their inherited identities have become functions for helping somebody else act. And the volume closes by returning to the earliest question in the manga: whether someone with no Quirk can still be a hero.

The answer at `V38:p203` is not yet an outcome. It is an action: **Quirkless Toshinori Yagi places himself in AFO's path and says `私が / AM HERE`.**

# 2. No.375 `滅茶苦茶` — Toga treats love, replication and timing as strategy

## 2.1 The Twice blood is a finite temporal resource

At `V38:p007-013`, Toga's remaining Twice blood is not an infinite army button. Its useful window is measured in tens of minutes. That changes her problem from “can she reproduce Twice?” to **where and when should she spend a finite replication interval?**

The heroes' segmentation plan matters here. A Sad Man's Parade released only on remote Okuto would be devastating locally but strategically underused. Toga therefore reasons around the enemy's attempt to isolate her.

That is important character evidence. Her late-war behavior cannot responsibly be modeled as pure affective impulse. Love, grief and rage are dominant motives, but she can still perform operational reasoning under them.

## 2.2 `スピナーくんに賭ける！私は私の為すべき事を！`

Toga's choice to stake the extraction problem on Spinner/Kurogiri and perform “what I must do” herself separates roles inside the villain coalition. She does not need to control the whole system personally.

The narration's movement toward Toga being completed as an `敵` should not be flattened into “she finally becomes evil.” The local point is harder: accumulated hostility and conviction have stabilized a **self-authored enemy role**. She is not merely being swept along by the League.

## 2.3 Uraraka refuses to let the conflict remain purely tactical

Uraraka's `私まだ あなたと恋バナしてない！` is intentionally almost absurd against the scale of the battlefield. That is why it matters.

She is not saying combat can be replaced with friendliness, nor that Toga's killings disappear under empathy. She is insisting that the unresolved relation includes a conversation Toga wanted and Uraraka once could not give. The rescue attempt becomes dialogic without becoming exculpatory.

This continues V35's distinction:

> **understanding another person's `当たり前` is not the same as granting that person's actions moral permission.**

# 3. No.376 `花に嵐` — segmented battle becomes coupled battle

Kurogiri's reactivation converts Volume 37's “Butterfly Effect” from metaphor into battlefield topology. The heroes designed the second decisive battle around controlled separation. Kurogiri restores connectivity faster than the separated teams can safely absorb it.

The result is not merely chaos. It is **causal recombination**:

- Toga/Twice pressure can migrate;
- Dabi reaches the Endeavor region;
- Erasure and U.A. infrastructure are disrupted;
- prior local victories become temporary rather than final;
- different characters inherit the consequences of decisions made far away from them.

The volume therefore begins by taking away the comforting idea that competent planning produces independent, closable problems.

# 4. No.377 `ここに至るまでの連なり` — information infrastructure is part of the battlefield

## 4.1 Skeptic attacks coordination rather than bodies

Skeptic's intervention demonstrates that hero infrastructure has an informational attack surface. The war is not only physical. Communications, control systems and the ability to know what is happening are combat resources.

This follows the floating-U.A. argument from V35-V36: hero society is a maintained system whose material and informational layers can fail independently.

## 4.2 La Brava's return preserves skill continuity

La Brava's rehabilitation does not require her hacking talent to become a different kind of talent. The same technical capacity that once served Gentle's criminal spectacle becomes defensive infrastructure.

That is a recurring late-series moral grammar:

> **reform does not necessarily mean becoming a different person; it can mean placing durable capacities inside a different relationship to other people.**

The parallel with Endeavor is structurally useful even though the moral histories are radically different. The series repeatedly rejects purification metaphors in favor of responsibility, changed use and changed relation.

# 5. No.378 `皆がヒーローになるまでの物語④` — Gentle proves heroism when nobody is watching

## 5.1 Gentle's decisive test is the absence of an audience

The most important Gentle evidence is not the scale of Elasticity. It is his internal state when he believes there are **no cameras and no audience**.

At `V38:p081-083` later framing makes the logic explicit, but the rescue sequence establishes it first: Gentle continues because the action itself matters. Reward, punishment and spectacle no longer determine whether he acts.

That is an unusually strong held-out validation of his rehabilitation.

Early Gentle wanted history to remember that Gentle Criminal existed. V38 gives him the opportunity to do something historically important under the belief that history may not see it. He still does it.

## 5.2 Rehabilitation does not erase theatrical personality

Nothing about the scene requires Gentle to become sober, plain or emotionally muted. His manner, partnership with La Brava and desire to do something grand remain continuous.

The repaired model is therefore not:

> attention-seeking criminal -> modest normal person.

It is:

> **person who once needed spectacle to establish self-worth -> person whose theatrical self can now serve rescue even without recognition.**

This continuity is why the change is reconstructible rather than merely symbolic.

# 6. No.379 `hopes` — Lady Nagant returns because remembered light becomes usable action

## 6.1 Hospitalized Nagant chooses re-entry under known bodily cost

At `V38:p063-068`, Lady Nagant is not magically restored. Medical staff explicitly frame movement/firing as dangerous. Her body still carries the consequences of AFO's booby-trapped gift.

She asks for the battlefield location anyway because Midoriya asked her to fight with them.

This matters because the decision is neither simple obedience nor an attempt to erase her past. It is **a new answer to what her trained capacity is for**.

## 6.2 `生きた意味` is prospective, not absolving

Nagant's remembered “gray sky” and Midoriya-associated light do not retroactively make her HPSC killings good. The volume instead attaches `生きた意味` to a present act whose value is still being made.

Her model therefore becomes much more longitudinally stable:

- institutional service;
- moral collapse/disillusionment;
- violent opposition;
- encounter with Midoriya;
- survival;
- voluntary re-entry on a different basis.

That is enough to move her beyond a single-encounter reconstruction regime.

# 7. Shigaraki's internal recovery — the origin was hidden, not erased

## 7.1 AFO's project is finally named from inside its target

At `V38:p070-075`, Shigaraki understands that AFO exploited the Shimura/All Might relation, fed hatred and intended eventually to take mind and body.

The key correction is that Shigaraki does **not** describe the fusion as unreal. He says, in substance, that they genuinely mixed and that he became part of AFO.

But he also says he hid his `原点` deep inside where AFO would not notice it, and that this origin recovered him from within.

This is stronger than the V36-V37 evidence for nonuniformity. The source now authorizes **internal self-recovery**.

It still does not authorize a simplistic binary in which “Tenko” is the good true self and “Shigaraki” is the bad false shell.

## 7.2 `死柄木弔の 志村転弧の 望みは唯一つ`

The grammar is decisive. Shigaraki joins both names when stating his one desire.

That desire remains the destruction of everything extending from “that house.” He then says that alone will save him.

This is why Midoriya's rescue problem becomes more difficult, not easier. The child he wants to save is not simply waiting under an alien personality to accept heroic extraction.

The current person says:

- AFO tried to own me;
- I recovered myself;
- I do not need OFA;
- my desire remains destruction;
- I call that salvation.

Any mature rescue interpretation must preserve all five facts simultaneously.

## 7.3 Midoriya's answer is anti-erasure

Midoriya's response — `泣いていた君を 見なかった事には しない` — is among the clearest statements of his late rescue doctrine.

He does **not** say “your present desire is not really yours.”

He says, effectively:

> I will not act as though I did not see you crying.

Recognition becomes an evidentiary obligation. Once Midoriya has witnessed a state of suffering, he refuses the convenience of deleting that evidence merely because the present adult is dangerous, hostile and resistant to rescue.

# 8. No.380 `カンイチ` — unseen action and accurate public recognition are both necessary

## 8.1 Gentle does not need the camera; La Brava still wants the truth seen

Gentle's internal heroism no longer depends on recognition. La Brava's response is not therefore redundant.

Her insistence that such a wonderful Gentle should not remain unknown establishes a second legitimate function:

- **Gentle:** act even if nobody sees;
- **La Brava:** make sure what actually happened can become socially legible.

The series refuses the false choice between pure private virtue and public narrative. Public understanding matters because hero society itself is mediated.

## 8.2 Business Course students define mediation as hero activity

The Business Course students' `だから 撮って 今度は 繋ぐ!!` and `それが 経営科の ヒーロー活動なんだ!!` are not throwaway meta-commentary.

They identify a specific institutional failure: society previously concentrated responsibility and symbolic burden on a few visible heroes, especially Endeavor, while the systems responsible for connecting professional action to public understanding failed.

Their answer is not “make better propaganda.” It is:

- record;
- preserve process;
- connect people to what is actually happening;
- make invisible labor causally visible.

This is a major expansion of the corpus's hero-society model.

# 9. AFO versus Hawks — singular ownership versus plural future

AFO's earlier `これは僕の物語なのさ` crystallizes his preferred ontology: other people are roles, resources, replacements and factors inside his authored story.

Hawks's answer in this phase — `これは俺たちの物語だ` and the future of “those kids” — is the exact countermodel.

The conflict is not merely protagonist versus antagonist. It is **narrative sovereignty**:

- AFO: one owner, everyone else consumed;
- hero coalition: plural authorship, differentiated roles, future people whose actions cannot be fully scripted by predecessors.

That argument explains why seemingly minor V38 actors matter structurally. A reporter, Business Course student, Gentle, La Brava, Mineta or Shiketsu student can alter the causal system without becoming the central protagonist.

# 10. No.381 `闇` — historical knowledge can defeat the monopoly of lived terror

## 10.1 AFO claims experiential authority over fear

AFO invokes the `黄金の闇` of an older social order: suspicion, resentment, exploitation and discrimination.

His argument is epistemic as well as threatening: younger heroes do not know what that world felt like, so their courage is shallow ignorance.

## 10.2 Inasa rejects the idea that only direct victims can know history

Inasa's answer that they studied it in modern history is comic in register and serious in implication.

He rejects AFO's attempt to monopolize historical knowledge through personal memory.

A younger generation can:

- learn what happened;
- inherit information without inheriting submission;
- judge predecessors;
- choose a different future.

His `あんたの古い栄光より 応援してる方のつくる未来が見たい` places hope in **future-making by chosen people**, not reverence for old power.

That is high-value evidence for Inasa's reconstruction because it shows that his emotional intensity can be organized around historical judgment rather than only rivalry.

## 10.3 Shishikura refuses hatred as an AFO-controlled response

Shishikura has unusually personal reason for hatred through his father. Yet he explicitly refuses to drown in it because doing so would play into AFO's hands.

This is another V38 example of anger governance rather than suppression.

Shoji, Koda, Mirio, Midoriya and now Shishikura all support a broader series rule:

> **anger can carry moral information, but letting an enemy dictate its target or form transfers agency to that enemy.**

# 11. Tokoyami — darkness is not owned by the Demon Lord

AFO recognizes Dark Shadow as “darkness,” but the volume refuses his implied ownership of that semantic field.

Tokoyami's maximum action is possible because:

- the storm creates environmental darkness;
- Hawks supplies professional coordination;
- Inasa contributes wind;
- other actors keep AFO occupied;
- Tokoyami uses the role for which he was selected.

This is not a hidden chosen-one revelation. It is a **situational convergence of differentiated capacities**.

Tokoyami's prior V36 statement that he cannot simply replace Endeavor remains the better model. He does not become someone else. He becomes maximally effective as Tokoyami inside a network that understands what he specifically can do.

# 12. No.382 `行かせない!!` — Toga's love grammar stops being mechanically simple

## 12.1 Transform's Quirk-use condition is relationship-sensitive

The heroes reason that Toga can reproduce Quirks only under a `好き` condition. But V38 immediately complicates any attempt to treat that condition as a simple conscious label.

Toga loves Toya and Jin. Yet while wearing Twice, she cannot reproduce their capacities the way Jin himself could.

Her own question — why can she not become Jin? — is therefore a crisis in self-understanding.

Love is not functioning as a switch she can command by declaration.

## 12.2 `好きな人の喜ぶ顔が好きだった`

Toga remembers that she liked the happy faces of the people she loved.

The point is easy to miss if her “love” is reduced only to blood/violence. Her affective system has always included a desire for another person's visible joy. The tragedy is that her body, socialization, suppression history and later ideology attached love to forms that harmed the people whose closeness she wanted.

## 12.3 Uraraka sees the tears

The narration's `だから彼女は 涙を見逃さない` returns recognition to the center.

Uraraka does not merely see a tactical opening. She sees that the person producing mass danger is crying.

That does not settle what rescue should mean. It makes refusal to notice impossible.

Toga remains `strong` rather than `specialist_ready` at this boundary. V38 greatly deepens affective mechanics and strategic behavior, but the terminal Uraraka/Toga outcome and wider reciprocal-life evidence remain beyond the volume.

# 13. Damage accelerates AFO's Rewind expenditure

Repeated hero attacks visibly force AFO into further youth.

This resolves an important operational point: damage is not meaningless merely because Rewind restores tissue. It consumes the remaining temporal/body budget faster.

The heroes therefore have a coherent strategy even when individual attacks do not permanently injure the visible body:

> **make AFO spend himself.**

This also reframes “support” later in No.385. The coalition's many contributions are not decorative because no one contributor lands the final blow. They collectively change the rate at which AFO's current existence can continue.

# 14. Gigantomachia's arrival is not AFO's recovered possession

The cliff at `V38:p129-130` initially looks like AFO's contingency working: Machia arrives.

Then Machia attacks AFO.

V38 deliberately waits until No.384 to explain why. That delay matters because it lets the reader initially reproduce AFO's own assumption — that a long-conditioned subordinate remains an owned resource — before showing the error.

# 15. No.383 `小さな心` — Mina turns trauma into a support ethic

## 15.1 Gigantomachia is explicitly her psychological wound

V38 does not imply that Mina's earlier freeze simply disappeared through training. Machia is framed as her `心の傷`.

This is high-value reconstructive evidence because it establishes recurrence. A model built after V16/V29 must predict that the same cue can still trigger fear.

Growth is not the absence of the old reaction. It is what she does **with the reaction now**.

## 15.2 `アシッドマン・ALMA` is training applied to a remembered failure

Mina's strengthened Acidman is not a generic power-up detached from biography.

She explicitly remembers that the last time she could not act as she wanted, people died and everything was destroyed. She sought training input from Bakugo and Todoroki because she refuses to repeat that helplessness.

The causal chain is unusually clean:

> trauma/failure -> specific learning -> technical adaptation -> later re-entry under the same trigger.

That is exactly the kind of evidence behavioral reconstruction needs.

## 15.3 `大事なものを守れないのは もう嫌だから!!`

Mina's motive is not abstract glory. She hates being unable to protect what matters.

This gives her cheerful, socially proactive surface an explicit deeper policy without contradicting it.

## 15.4 `弱いから…寄り添い合っていたい`

Mina's most important V38 line is not about strength. It is about weakness.

She says she knows what it is to be afraid because she is weak, and because people are weak she wants them to remain beside/support one another.

That is a mature relational philosophy:

- weakness is not shame;
- fear is not disqualification;
- mutual dependence is not a temporary defect to outgrow;
- community is a response to shared vulnerability.

It directly opposes both AFO's predatory hierarchy and the local villain's “might makes right” language.

## 15.5 Kirishima refuses the debt ledger

Kirishima's answer to Mina's “debt repaid” framing is decisive: she did not need to repay him; she had always been his hero.

Their relationship is therefore not a balanced rescue ledger in which favors cancel out. Earlier inspiration remained real even after later failure, and later support did not retroactively make the first help a debt.

This makes the Mina/Kirishima relationship strongly reciprocal without making it transactional.

# 16. Mina readiness adjudication

### Context breadth

Now includes:

- ordinary/social leadership and peer interaction;
- middle-school encounter with Machia;
- fear/freeze;
- training response;
- team exercises;
- large-scale war recurrence;

### Relationship breadth

Now includes:

- Class 1-A training dependencies;
- Midnight-related class attachment;
- Shoji's anti-revenge example as a moral reference;
- enemy-facing persuasion rather than only combat.

### Language/values

V38 supplies unusually direct statements of:

- what she fears;
- what she refuses to repeat;
- why protection matters;
- why weakness makes mutual support desirable.

### Adjudication

**Ashido Mina: `insufficient/emerging (strongly grounded) -> moderate (very strongly grounded)`.**

Do not jump directly to `strong`. The volume solves values/stress/relationship causality but ordinary adult/professional breadth and repeated independent leadership remain thinner than the strong tier.

# 17. Shinso — influence has an explicit boundary: the heart

Shinso's V38 role is technically sophisticated. He uses recorded AFO voice and Persona Chords to trigger Brainwashing in Machia.

But No.384 gives the more important conceptual limit:

> `俺の“個性”で 心までは操れない!!`

His Quirk can govern action under conditions. It does not manufacture the emotional grievance that persists after control breaks.

This line matters far beyond mechanics. MHA repeatedly distinguishes:

- action from motive;
- coercion from authorship;
- externally imposed behavior from retained relationship.

Shinso understands that distinction from inside a power whose social stigma has always depended on fear of mind control.

His existing `strong` model is materially strengthened but not promoted.

# 18. No.384 `It's A Small World` — Machia's loyalty produces betrayal grievance

## 18.1 Brainwashing breaks, hostility does not

AFO initially assumes Machia is attacking because Shinso controls him. V38 then supplies the falsification: light impact breaks Brainwashing, yet Machia continues speaking and acting against AFO.

That is the clearest evidence yet that Machia possesses relationship-conditioned agency beyond simple command execution.

## 18.2 AFO's old reassurance becomes the source of the wound

Machia remembers AFO's prior language of affection and promised succession:

- “my cute Machia”;
- AFO will disappear;
- a successor will someday need him;
- separation is presented as regrettable.

Machia waited on that basis.

His anger now comes from discovering that the relationship as he understood it was disposable from AFO's side.

This does not suddenly make Machia psychologically broad. It does, however, reject the earlier over-simple model of him as pure command machinery.

## 18.3 AFO mistakes possession for loyalty

AFO repeatedly builds systems in which another person's devotion is treated as capital he owns.

Machia shows the failure mode:

> **a relationship can be manipulated without becoming property.**

Once abandonment is recognized, the same intense loyalty can generate equally intense grievance.

No forced broad readiness tier is assigned to Machia at V38; the evidence remains heavily concentrated in service/combat/AFO-attachment domains.

# 19. Media and witness become part of the hero system

## 19.1 The reporter goes because “telling” is her work — and also personal

The reporter does not pretend detached neutrality. She explicitly says that journalism alone cannot fully describe why she is going; `これも私事なんです`.

That is not presented as corruption. The ethical standard is instead truth-bearing commitment:

> `勝つにせよ / 負けるにせよ` — whether they win or lose, she will deliver what happened.

The witness is committed without making the outcome conditional on what she wants to report.

## 19.2 `過程とその結果`

The public needs more than a heroic result. V38 emphasizes process and result.

That directly answers earlier hero-society failures in which:

- labor disappeared behind symbols;
- impossible expectations concentrated on individual heroes;
- publics judged from fragments;
- invisible support systems were treated as secondary.

Business Course students and professional reporters now perform a corrective function: **making causal structure visible**.

## 19.3 The world becomes smaller through connection, not homogenization

`It's A Small World` does not mean all people become the same. The visual montage does the opposite: distinct civilians, officials, children, reporters and national contexts remain visibly different while information links them.

This is the benign form of global connection, opposed to AFO's model of global unity under one will.

# 20. Gentle readiness adjudication

Gentle entered V38 at `moderate` with the major missing evidence explicitly identified as later rehabilitation and longitudinal recurrence/change.

V38 supplies exactly that held-out regime.

He:

- returns under institutional trust;
- uses the same core Quirk/personality style in rescue rather than self-mythologizing crime;
- believes no audience is present;
- acts anyway;
- experiences joy in being alive/acting rather than in becoming famous;
- remains recognizably theatrical rather than personality-replaced.

**Gentle Criminal: `moderate -> strong`.**

Broader relationships outside La Brava remain a relative weakness, so `specialist_ready` would be premature.

La Brava remains `moderate`, very strongly strengthened. V38 gives independent technical hero labor and a public-recognition philosophy, but her relationship evidence remains heavily Gentle-centered.

# 21. Lady Nagant readiness adjudication

Nagant's previous `moderate (strongly grounded)` tier named “later recovery trajectory” as a principal missing axis.

V38 supplies it in a particularly diagnostic form:

- bodily damage remains real;
- she receives medical warning;
- she asks to re-enter voluntarily;
- Midoriya's request is remembered as an invitation rather than a command;
- she converts prior professional marksmanship into chosen protective action;
- she frames present action as a way to make survival meaningful.

Together with HPSC history, disillusionment, Chisaki interaction, Midoriya confrontation and ideological revision, this gives enough longitudinal and stress breadth for:

**Lady Nagant / Tsutsumi Kaina: `moderate (strongly grounded) -> strong`.**

Ordinary private-life evidence remains limited, so no specialist promotion is warranted.

# 22. Inasa and Shishikura readiness

## Inasa

The top snapshot lagged behind the V13 longitudinal update that had already moved Inasa to `emerging`. V38 adds:

- historical reasoning rather than raw affect;
- explicit future preference;
- Todoroki/Endeavor support after earlier hostility;
- AFO-class stress behavior;
- role-specific cooperation with Tokoyami and Hawks.

That is enough for a cautious promotion:

**Yoarashi Inasa: `emerging -> moderate`.**

His ordinary/private breadth remains thin.

## Shishikura

V38 adds a direct family-causal stake through his father and an explicit refusal to let hatred become AFO's behavioral instrument.

**Shishikura Seiji: `insufficient/emerging -> emerging (strongly grounded)`.**

Do not force `moderate`; ordinary baseline and relationship breadth remain narrow.

# 23. No.385 `若き衝動` — “support” is not secondary labor

## 23.1 AFO dismisses the coalition as support

AFO's contempt for the actors surrounding him is consistent with his hierarchy: only power that can be owned, stolen or recognized as central seems fully real to him.

Tokoyami's answer is structurally important. The `支え` AFO dismisses is precisely what accelerates the coalition's `光明`.

The series is now explicit:

> support is not what happens around the real action; support changes what real action is possible.

## 23.2 Young AFO becomes more powerful and less restrained

Rewind produces a paradox. As AFO becomes younger, raw power rises, but negative affect becomes harder to suppress.

This is valuable AFO-model evidence because it falsifies the image of him as purely cold and affectless. His mature manipulative composure is partly a regulatory achievement, not absence of emotion.

The younger body exposes:

- impatience;
- contempt;
- rage at resistance;
- stronger susceptibility to provocation and inherited Shigaraki hatred.

That vulnerability becomes central to the volume endpoint.

## 23.3 Mineta's protest is genuinely prosocial

Injured and terrified, Mineta tells AFO not to take Dark Shadow and offers Pop Off instead.

This is important because his old model's largest weakness has been the tendency for prosocial action to remain entangled with self-interest or comic libido.

V38 supplies a clean counterexample: he is trying to protect Tokoyami's Quirk at cost to himself.

Readiness remains `emerging`, strongly strengthened. One scene does not create broad reconstructibility, but the “prosocial behavior beyond self-interest” gap is no longer wholly empty.

# 24. AFO leaves the battlefield — tactical success can coexist with strategic failure

AFO eventually escapes the Gunga suppression field.

That does **not** retroactively make the coalition's effort meaningless.

They:

- forced further Rewind/youth expenditure;
- delayed travel;
- denied Quirk theft opportunities at multiple moments;
- exposed emotional dysregulation;
- changed his available time and body state;
- forced him to treat supposedly minor actors as real obstacles.

The late war repeatedly rejects binary evaluation in which only final closure counts as meaningful action.

# 25. Civilian shelters — the war's causal field includes people who cannot fight AFO

The stalled evacuation blocks place civilians inside the predicted Dabi blast radius.

This is not merely an extra hostage device. It reconnects professional combat to the ordinary population whose survival is the reason the battle has moral meaning.

The volume also shows people inside the evacuation system acting under uncertainty rather than waiting as passive cargo. The social field continues to broaden beyond licensed heroes.

# 26. No.386 `I AM HERE` — doubt, chosen action and the return of the first question

## 26.1 Dabi turns the family problem into a mass-casualty systems problem

Command analysis predicts a roughly five-kilometer-scale catastrophic blast if Toya's stored heat releases.

Endeavor is present, but his fire-based Quirk is not the appropriate tool for stopping that thermal event.

The answer is role complementarity again:

> `エンジン` + `半冷半燃`.

Iida and Shoto are needed because **their specific bodies and histories create the relevant combination**.

## 26.2 All Might does not tell Shoto to stop doubting

All Might's guidance is one of the strongest late-series statements about decision under moral injury:

`心と体は一元だ`.

He recognizes that Shoto's mind and body have traveled through the same conflict. He then tells him that fighting while carrying unresolved doubt is not enough by itself, but the answer is not purification.

The crucial statement is that the thought a troubled young person finally fixes in his heart is especially precious.

This fits Shoto's entire arc:

- he did not become free by deleting inheritance;
- he did not become mature by never feeling ambivalence;
- he becomes self-authored by making choices **through** inherited conflict.

`君が君である為に` is the endpoint of the self-authorship line that began when Midoriya told him the fire was `君の力`.

## 26.3 Iida's legs return to the “lost child” ideal

Iida's `迷子を導く為にあるんだから` is a major callback to the Ingenium ideal preserved from Tensei.

His speed is not framed as personal achievement. It exists to guide/carry somebody who needs to reach the place where only that person's power can matter.

This is mature inherited identity:

- not imitation of Tensei's exact life;
- not mere preservation of the Ingenium name;
- a functional rule that can be re-applied in a novel crisis.

Iida remains `specialist_ready`, strengthened.

## 26.4 All Might identifies the remaining “helper” without naming another hero

When Shoto worries about AFO, All Might says a powerful helper remains.

The page sequence then cuts to Toshinori himself.

This is important because the manga has spent dozens of volumes teaching both Toshinori and the reader to separate his worth from OFA. V38 turns that proposition into a live behavioral test.

# 27. The Quirkless-hero question returns as action, not exposition

At `V38:p201`, the manga explicitly recalls Midoriya's first-volume question:

> `“個性”がなくても ヒーローは出来ますか!?`

Then it shows the now-Quirkless All Might preparing to face AFO.

The callback does **not** yet establish whether he can win, survive or even meaningfully damage AFO. Those are V39+ questions.

What V38 can establish is more philosophically important:

> **the series no longer treats “can a Quirkless person be a hero?” as a hypothetical asked by a powerless child; it becomes a choice made by the former Symbol after losing the power that once defined his public legitimacy.**

That distinction matters. Hero status is being separated from possession of OFA at the exact point where acting is most dangerous.

# 28. `私が / AM HERE` — the Symbol becomes a first-person commitment again

All Might's famous arrival phrase once functioned socially: people were safe because the Symbol had arrived.

At `V38:p202-203`, the phrase is stripped of its former guarantees.

There is no OFA aura guaranteeing dominance. There is an aging, injured, Quirkless man, an engineered support system, and AFO approaching at terrifying speed.

So `I AM HERE` changes meaning.

It no longer means:

> the strongest person has arrived, therefore the crisis is solved.

It means:

> **I am still a person who can choose to stand here.**

That is an enormous maturation of the series' power philosophy.

The support vehicle/armor visible at the endpoint does not weaken this claim. It reinforces the distributed-agency model: Quirklessness need not mean technological or social isolation. The important distinction is between **having no innate Quirk** and **having no resources, relationships or tools**.

# 29. All Might readiness update

All Might was already `specialist_ready`; V38 therefore does not create a new tier.

It provides an exceptionally strong held-out validation of the post-Symbol model developed since V11:

- he can accept that he is no longer the old combat Symbol;
- he can coordinate rather than personally solve every battlefield;
- he trusts Iida and Shoto with role-specific tasks;
- he distinguishes a young person's chosen commitment from simply obeying him;
- and when he personally re-enters combat, he does so without pretending OFA still defines whether his action counts as heroism.

The exact tactical capability and consequences of the endpoint remain V39+ OPEN.

# 30. AFO readiness and affective model

AFO remains formally `moderate`, but V38 strongly deepens a domain that earlier volumes under-sampled: **loss of emotional regulation**.

The mature AFO persona has often made affect look subordinate to strategy. Rewind-induced youth reveals that:

- negative emotion can become harder to suppress;
- Shigaraki-derived hatred can influence the original body;
- humiliation and obstruction increase impulsive pressure;
- his belief that minor actors are beneath him becomes a tactical blind spot.

Do not read this as “young AFO is a different person.” The volume instead shows how age/body state changes the expression of already-present traits.

# 31. Hero society: V38 expands the category of hero labor

By the end of the volume, hero activity includes at least:

- direct combat;
- rescue;
- technical systems defense;
- infrastructure maintenance;
- transport;
- medical repair;
- battlefield coordination;
- emotional regulation of allies;
- historical learning;
- reporting;
- recording;
- public connection;
- preserving accurate memory;
- choosing to witness;
- making another person's action possible.

This is not metaphorical inflation in which “everyone is a hero” means everyone is morally wonderful.

It is a **division-of-labor thesis**. A society that relies on one Symbol is brittle. A society in which more people can identify and perform their own responsibility is harder to collapse through one point of failure.

# 34. Japanese language and register notes

## `恋バナ`

Uraraka's casual `恋バナ` is deliberately ordinary and adolescent. Translation that makes the line solemn (“we have not discussed romantic feelings”) loses the social register. The force comes from inserting an everyday-girl conversation category into a lethal battlefield without trivializing either one.

## `原点`

Shigaraki's `原点` is not simply “innocent self.” It is origin/source point: a retained core from which self-recovery proceeds. The surrounding grammar explicitly joins this recovered origin to present Shigaraki/Tenko destructive desire.

## `見なかった事にはしない`

Midoriya's phrase is epistemically strong. It is not only “I won't forget.” It rejects treating witnessed suffering **as though it had not been seen**. The moral act begins in refusal to falsify perception.

## `寄り添い合う`

Mina's language is reciprocal. She does not frame support as a strong person carrying a weak person. Weakness itself is why people should remain beside one another.

## `繋ぐ`

Business Course/media usage gives `繋ぐ` institutional weight: to connect people, events, process and public understanding. It continues the series' wider vocabulary of chains, inheritance and connection without reducing those links to bloodline.

## `私事`

The reporter's `これも私事なんです` acknowledges personal stake rather than claiming impossible neutrality. The adjacent win-or-lose reporting commitment prevents the line from meaning “I will propagandize for my side.”

## `迷子を導く`

Iida's “guide the lost” line carries the Ingenium inheritance in functional rather than titular form. `迷子` is deliberately modest relative to war-scale heroism, preserving Tensei's low-status service ideal.

## `AM HERE`

The English phrase is now separated visually from the earlier full-power Symbol. Its semantic constant is presence; what changes is what that presence can guarantee.

# 36. First-pass / earlier-claim revision opportunities

- heroism is not reducible to raw combat output;
- MHA's strongest mature social model is distributed rather than Symbol-dependent;

- Shoto's mature identity is self-authored integration rather than rejection of inheritance;
- Endeavor's late responsibility does not erase prior abuse;

- ordinary/public mediation is part of hero society, not decorative world-building;
- Quirk function is often less morally important than present purpose/use;
- relational inheritance can propagate beyond formal bloodline or OFA succession;
- weakness and dependence are becoming positive design features of mature heroism rather than temporary defects.

- **Gentle's motive:** from “he becomes a hero once society recognizes him” to “he becomes behaviorally heroic before recognition; later recognition makes the truth socially legible.”
- **Machia:** from command-bound monster to narrow but genuine relationship-conditioned agent whose loyalty can become abandonment grievance.

- any reading in which Toga's `好き` condition can be predicted from conscious verbal labels alone;
- any reading in which AFO's mature calm proves low emotionality rather than high regulation;
- any reading in which “support” characters are analytically secondary because they do not deliver final blows.

- hero reform requires personality purification;
- media visibility is inherently superficial while unseen action alone is authentic;
- Machia continues attacking AFO only because Shinso controls him;
- Mina's growth means she is no longer afraid;
- Mineta has no demonstrated capacity for self-endangering prosocial action;
- Quirklessness at the V38 endpoint means All Might has no meaningful role available.
