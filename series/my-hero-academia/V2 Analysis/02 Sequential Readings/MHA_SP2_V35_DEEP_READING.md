# My Hero Academia - Second-Pass Deep Reading, Volume 35

## 0. Status, method, and prospective lock

This reading begins strictly from the frozen V34 boundary at `V34:p190`. No V36+ information is used to settle V35 ambiguities. The governing method remains `MHA_SP2_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2_1.md`: Japanese-primary evidence, prospective truth freeze, page-bounded claims, language/register attention, visual and institutional analysis, explicit uncertainty, cumulative character/relationship state routing, and correction of earlier overclaims only where V35 itself licenses the correction.

The strongest volume-level transition is:

> **distributed agency under coercion, contingency and loss -> engineered interdependence confronting weaponized grievance, with rescue becoming a contested relationship inside a segmented war.**

V34 established that AFO and the hero side both distribute function, but in morally different ways. V35 turns that contrast into literal battle architecture. AFO distributes himself through planted agents, symbolic figureheads, coercive dependency, stolen capacities and fallback routes. The heroes distribute necessary functions across Aoyama, Shinso, Monoma, Aizawa, Support Course, Power Loader, Cementoss, Ectoplasm, Momo, Kaminari, Manga, professional heroes and the student combatants. The source itself states the operation's governing premise: `脇役なんていない` and `この作戦は 誰か欠けても成り立たない` (`V35:p084-085`).

The crucial qualification is that hero-side coordination is not morally clean simply because it is heroic. Aizawa explicitly admits that the heroes are also going to `利用する` Aoyama. V35 therefore refuses a simple opposition between coercion and non-coercion. Its sharper distinction is between systems that **consume people into functions** and systems that **ask people to bear functions while preserving a claim on their future personhood, relationships, refusal, accountability and life after the operation**.

# 1. No.340 — Aoyama: accountability without lifelong moral foreclosure

## 1.1 The war plan begins with a compromised student, not a superweapon

The volume opens after Aoyama's disclosure rather than by immediately escalating battlefield spectacle. At Central Hospital/police coordination space, Aoyama is still being examined and the heroes remain under severe secrecy constraints (`V35:p007-009`). Ragdoll volunteers because her stolen Search is being used by the enemy. That opening already places the operation inside a web of involuntary dispossession: the heroes are planning around powers, information and bodies AFO has previously appropriated.

All Might identifies the strategic necessity as `分断`: AFO and Shigaraki cannot be allowed to operate together, and Dabi alone can consume enough hero attention to break a unified response (`p010-012`). The plan is therefore not primarily “beat the strongest villain.” It is **jurisdictional decomposition**: isolate enemy command/power nodes far enough apart that specialized coalitions can operate against them.

Aoyama matters because he is a live channel into the enemy's trust architecture. Tsukauchi does not erase what he did; All Might nevertheless says he wants to believe in the boy (`p013-014`). The volume's ethical problem is thus set before the tactical mechanics are revealed: what does it mean to use a coerced wrongdoer as bait without simply reproducing the logic that made him useful to AFO in the first place?

## 1.2 Aizawa refuses both innocence and sacrificial disposal

The medical/security check finds no Lady Nagant-style implanted physical bomb. The more important AFO implant is terror: fear for Aoyama's parents and a learned expectation that resistance means death (`p015`).

Aizawa's response is among the clearest teacher-ethics passages in the second pass. He does **not** promise that Aoyama's crimes disappear. He says circumstances may mitigate punishment, but `罪は消えない`; after the war Aoyama may not remain a hero (`p016-017`). That is explicit prospective restraint: V35 itself refuses to settle Aoyama's eventual legal/school outcome.

At the same time, Aizawa rejects a punishment model in which accountability becomes total identity. He recognizes that Aoyama now distrusts himself more strongly as others express trust, and that he is tempted to leave the “stage” by accepting punishment, disappearance or death. Aizawa's task is to peel away the fear AFO planted strongly enough for Aoyama to act (`p017`).

The most difficult line comes when Aizawa admits that the hero side is also going to use him: `俺たちとAFO何も違いはないかもな おまえを利用するって点では` (`p018`). V35 does not let heroic intent launder instrumentality. The distinction emerges in what the system does with Aoyama **after and beyond** the function. AFO's model makes the granted Quirk a permanent debt and converts the family into expendable leverage. Aizawa's model says the student remains inside teacher jurisdiction, remains among friends, retains a future after the operation, and will not be taught to live permanently in moral debt.

`先生は生徒に この先一生 負い目を抱える生き方など 教えない` (`p019`) is therefore load-bearing. It is not absolution. It is a rejection of **indebtedness as a permanent social ontology**.

## 1.3 “Fight” as restorative obligation

Aizawa tells Aoyama to walk with the friends who reached toward him and says the adults will protect him (`p019-020`). When Aoyama asks what a sinner like him can do, the answer is not “be forgiven first.” It is to participate in the shared road. The chapter closes by revealing Shinso and the line that this is a path everyone walks (`p020-021`).

Restoration here is action under unresolved guilt. Aoyama is neither purified nor discarded. He is given a dangerous responsibility precisely because a future self can still be authored through what he does next.

# 2. No.341 — Toga and Spinner: grievance turned into usable political material

## 2.1 Toga's childhood home: “normality” as imposed self-erasure

Toga returns to her abandoned childhood home, now marked by public condemnation (`V35:p023-028`). The visual structure is important: adult Toga moves through rooms in which remembered parental and peer speech overlays the emptied domestic space. The home is not a nostalgic refuge. It is an archive of attempted normalization.

The remembered injunctions include claims that her desire will never be accepted, that nobody gets to live however they like, that she must endure/control herself, and that this is being said “for her sake” (`p026`). The sequence therefore sharpens an earlier distinction from V24: the problem is not merely that Toga had a dangerous Quirk-related desire. It is that the surrounding adults' response framed acceptability as suppression until the suppressed self became socially unlivable.

Her recurring dream makes attraction, blood, beauty and bodily incorporation inseparable (`p027-028`). V35 does not ask the reader to declare that phenomenology harmless. It asks the reader to recognize that her category of “ordinary” was never congruent with the category imposed around her.

## 2.2 Dabi offers companionship in the language of use

Dabi appears during this sentimental return and burns the house (`p029-031`). He tells Toga that tomorrow comes equally whether people cry or laugh and suggests they laugh. She calls him kind. His answer undercuts the sentiment: he has something she can use.

He supplies Twice's preserved blood and explains the evolved condition of Toga's Transformation: she can reproduce the Quirk of a transformed person only when she genuinely loves that person (`p031-033`). The gift is simultaneously relational and instrumental. Dabi correctly recognizes that Twice matters to her, but he also routes that grief toward mass battlefield utility. He wants the “sad man's parade” to continue.

This is the villain-side counterpart to the Aoyama question. Both coalitions ask emotionally wounded people to become operational nodes. The difference is not that the League lacks real affection; Twice/Toga affection is already strongly established. The sharper question is whether grief is being given a future or converted into destructive capacity.

## 2.3 Spinner becomes a symbol larger than his own motive

AFO explains that Shigaraki is recovering and says his body is approaching an adaptive threshold beyond the `個性` singularity problem (`p034-035`). Spinner immediately asks whether the being before him is still Shigaraki (`p035`). V35 therefore preserves V34's identity uncertainty; it does not resolve the fused self simply because AFO answers affirmatively.

AFO then shifts to heteromorph discrimination. He identifies historical and current resentment and positions Spinner as `異形を導く星` (`p036-038`). Spinner's own orientation remains much narrower: he cares about Shigaraki. AFO scales that personal loyalty into a political icon.

The chapter-ending claim `誰もが誰かのヒーローになれるんだ` (`p039`) is a dark mirror of the series' distributed-heroism thesis. AFO can appropriate the vocabulary of “hero” because symbolic recognition is politically powerful regardless of moral direction. V35 thereby distinguishes **being someone's hero** from being ethically protective. Recognition can mobilize liberation, resentment, revenge or domination.

# 3. No.342 — Quiet before battle: recognition without exculpation

## 3.1 Civilian space remains penetrated by AFO's contingency network

The U.A. refuge contains dormant AFO-aligned civilians who receive instructions and note that AFO anticipated movement restrictions (`V35:p041-042`). The sequence prevents the fortified refuge from becoming a morally or tactically sealed space. AFO's contingency doctrine is social as well as Quirk-based.

Class 1-A returns exhausted from unsuccessful search work (`p043-046`). The adults interpret the repeated failed search, declining hero numbers and the students' visible fatigue as part of the information environment AFO will read. The “quiet” is therefore already operational performance.

## 3.2 Leaving U.A. converts dependence into mutual responsibility

The students learn that Shigaraki is estimated to move in four days (`p048`). This sharpens V34's at-least-one-week reprieve into a current operation countdown; it does not prove the medical estimate was exact in an omniscient sense.

When the class leaves U.A., Midoriya says they have had enough time to wash away the mud (`p049`). The refugees are asked to use the evacuation system if needed. One planted agent privately concludes that the students' departure has achieved AFO's intended condition anyway (`p049-050`).

The class relocates to the rapidly built temporary fortress `トロイア` roughly 30 km away (`p050-051`). This matters structurally: hero society is no longer protected by a single institution. It is becoming a network of movable, redundant, specialized sites.

## 3.3 Uraraka and Midoriya: rescue cognition as epistemic humility

Midoriya finds Uraraka looking over the ruined city and finally thanks her for the U.A. speech (`p052-053`). Before understanding why she calls herself “strange,” he reflexively lists positive traits. She interrupts the reassurance and names Toga.

Uraraka's formulation is unusually precise. She does not retreat from the damage Toga has caused. People have been killed and lives/happiness destroyed; the issue has moved beyond a simple permission/forgiveness binary (`p054`). Yet Uraraka also says she does not know Toga's `当たり前`. What arrests her is the sad face she saw when Toga's question was answered from the hero side.

That is not exculpation. It is **epistemic incompleteness recognized under justified hostility**.

Midoriya answers with the crying child he perceived inside Shigaraki. Even if battle cannot be avoided, he does not want to ignore what lies behind the enemy's present violence (`p055`). This exchange joins their rescue ethics without making them identical. Uraraka's problem is a person whose love ontology she does not understand; Midoriya's is a destructive self in whom he has perceived a child-state asking, or seeming to ask, to be saved.

## 3.4 Shoto's ordinary future claim toward Toya

Iida tells Shoto that having an older brother does not let him pretend he understands Shoto's situation (`p056`). Shoto says he does not even know what Toya likes to eat. The joking possibility of hot udon becomes a serious future-oriented line: if that is what Toya likes, Shoto would eat it with him.

The importance is not culinary. Shoto imagines a sibling relation **outside crisis, guilt adjudication and combat** before the battle begins. V35 therefore supplies low-stakes relational evidence inside the war: he is not only preparing to “stop Dabi”; he can still imagine Toya as someone with preferences that could be learned.

The chapter ends on `必ず止めよう` and then operation day (`p057`). Rescue and stopping are not treated as opposites.

# 4. No.343 — `Let you down`: deceiving the deceiver

## 4.1 AFO's lie-detection is powerful, bounded and exploitable

AFO says that a Quirk in his possession lets him detect lies or harmful intent toward himself and that he has combined/enhanced it with other capacities (`V35:p059`). V35 does **not** establish omniscient truth reading. The operative input is speaker state—lie/malice—as AFO understands it.

Aoyama's parents contact him through an innocuous whisky code. AFO judges the communication `真っ白`: no lie or malice is detectable (`p059-060`). The later reveal is therefore not that AFO's power simply “failed.” It was presented a communication chain from which the relevant speaker intent had been removed.

## 4.2 AFO's end state is infrastructural sovereignty

In the parking-structure meeting, Aoyama explains a projected AFO strategy: Japan's collapse destabilizes currency and firms; global villain activation and security crises force states inward; under resource scarcity, someone possessing the necessary Quirks can monopolize infrastructure and become the world's `裁定者` (`p063-065`).

This is more specific than “rule the world.” It is sovereignty through dependency: control the capacities without which states and populations cannot function.

AFO later indicates that Aoyama's account is not perfectly exact (`p069`). Therefore the correct claim is bounded: **V35 presents infrastructural monopolization as a substantially relevant description of AFO's project, but not as a fully exhaustive or verbatim statement of his final goal.**

## 4.3 Aoyama succeeds because he chooses to act, not because guilt disappears

Aoyama performs renewed betrayal and calls AFO into the open (`p066-069`). He then fires Naval Laser at AFO and states that there was no betrayal of Midoriya: he will fight AFO for his parents, everyone, and himself (`p070-071`).

This closes one V34 open question: **Aoyama successfully participates in deceiving/luring AFO.** It does not close his legal or school status.

AFO checks Search and concludes that tracked heroes are dispersed, making a mass immediate reinforcement unlikely (`p072`). He brings the villain force into the apparent kill zone (`p073`). Hero portals then appear (`p074-075`). The reversal is less “AFO got careless” than “his information model was deliberately fed a coherent pattern of fatigue, dispersion and speaker states.”

# 5. No.344 — `主役`: the operation belongs to indispensable supporting functions

## 5.1 Failed searching was part of the performance

The flashback reveals that the broad hero search was useful precisely because AFO would interpret repeated failure, exhaustion and bad-looking decisions as evidence that the heroes were cornered (`V35:p077-079`). Aizawa's plan turns AFO's paranoid information gathering against him by making the observable world reinforce his prior expectations.

The danger is the voice-state check. If Aoyama or his parents consciously lie, AFO can detect it (`p079`). The solution is not technological encryption. It is Shinso.

## 5.2 Shinso's growth resolves an old limitation without erasing its ethics

Shinso says he has trained to make brainwashed subjects speak, something he could not do during the Joint Training battle (`p080-081`). If he controls Aoyama and the parents, their statements contain neither their own lie nor their own harmful intent. The plan therefore uses Brainwashing as anti-surveillance infrastructure.

This is a direct payoff of a Quirk repeatedly coded by others as “villain-like.” V35 makes the normative point concrete: an affordance is not an ethic. Coercive capacity can rescue, deceive, dominate or protect depending on relation, consent/jurisdiction and purpose.

The Class asks whether Shinso will join A or B; his delayed Hero Course future remains institutionally present even while normal advancement is suspended (`p081`). The exchange adds low-stakes affiliative context to an otherwise function-heavy Shinso model.

## 5.3 Monoma: from “side role” stigma to load-bearing protagonist function

Aizawa identifies Monoma as the operation's most important role: copy Kurogiri's Warp Gate and master functional use within days (`p082-083`). Monoma initially calls it impossible.

Aizawa's own condition matters. He has lost an eye and cannot reliably sustain Erasure as before; he also failed to reach Kurogiri/Shirakumo through voice (`p084`). Monoma responds by recalling how indirect/Copy-like Quirks are told they cannot produce a conventional “super hero.”

Aizawa's answer is the volume's operation thesis: `この作戦は 誰か欠けても成り立たない`; there is no `脇役`, and Monoma is a `主役` here (`p085-086`).

This is not motivational decoration. The subsequent battle mechanically proves it. The front-line icons cannot reach their assignments unless a student with a “supporting” Quirk reproduces another person's spatial function.

# 6. No.345 — `DIVISION`: battle as logistics and timing

AFO, Shigaraki, Dabi, Toga and the rest of the force emerge as expected (`V35:p087-088`). Dabi immediately seeks Endeavor; Shoto intercepts him (`p089-090`). Temporary cages close around villain groups (`p091`).

AFO correctly recognizes that the cages are too weak and too expensive to be conventional containment. Their value is time: roughly the few seconds needed to restrict movement, align groups and push them toward separate Warp Gates (`p092-099`).

The operation therefore treats seconds as a jointly manufactured resource. Steel, construction labor, prediction, positioning, Copy, Brainwashing, Aoyama's performance and individual combat pressure all converge to buy a tiny temporal window.

The plan succeeds at its primary strategic objective: **the villain force is segmented into distinct battlefields.** But success is immediately imperfect. Toga catches Midoriya with a line and pulls him through the wrong portal (`p100`). The hero system is robust enough to continue, but not omniscient enough to prevent contingency.

At the central staging point, remaining heroes manage stragglers, protect Aoyama and move Monoma toward Aizawa (`p101-103`). AFO at Gunga also identifies the limitation of his own Mud Warp: it is inferior to Kurogiri's original, has limited range and cannot warp the user in the same way (`p104`).

The battlefield assignments visible in V35 include:

- Gunga: AFO separated into a hero coalition including Endeavor/Hawks-side forces;
- Kamino: Dabi versus Shoto with Endeavor-side sidekick support;
- floating U.A.: Shigaraki versus the anti-Shigaraki team centered on Erasure/Monoma and the U.A. trap;
- Okuto Island: Toga and associated villains against Uraraka, Tsuyu, Gang Orca-side forces, with Midoriya accidentally pulled there before departing.

Do not infer every V36+ battlefield participant or result from V35 alone.

AFO calls Endeavor's delegation cruel: the father fights AFO while the abused youngest son is sent against Dabi (`p104-105`). Dabi makes the same accusation. The source therefore marks the assignment as morally contestable even though Shoto himself accepts it.

# 7. No.346 — the floating U.A. as anti-single-protagonist engineering

The floating U.A. battlefield reveals itself as a purpose-built `天空の棺` for Shigaraki (`V35:p111-118`). When he attempts Decay, a floor module is ejected before propagation can consume the whole structure (`p113-114`). Electromagnetic systems create brief bodily interruption windows that Jeanist/Edgeshot exploit (`p115-116`).

The point is not merely clever technology. V35 explicitly inventories the social production behind the trap. Hatsume and the Support Course, Momo's fabrication, Power Loader, construction labor, replacement plates, evacuation logic, electricity from Kaminari and Manga, Aizawa/Monoma's Erasure chain, Nezu's planning and accumulated data from prior Shigaraki battles all become one combat organism (`p118-123`).

The governing narration says `これまでに積み上げられた全てが この棺を動かしている` and emphasizes people who are not seeking honor or glory but protecting the next day (`p121-122`).

This is V35's clearest materialization of distributed heroism. The “heroic body” fighting Shigaraki is an institution made of people, knowledge, machines, supplies and substituted functions.

The design nevertheless assumed OFA/Midoriya at the center. Midoriya's displacement therefore creates a genuine structural deficit (`p123-124`). Distributed systems can reduce single-point dependency without eliminating it.

# 8. No.347 — `INFLATION`: Shigaraki exceeds the anti-Quirk model

## 8.1 Finger proliferation is presented as body adaptation, not an active Quirk

Monoma is visibly sustaining Erasure when Shigaraki's body produces a massive proliferation of fingers (`V35:p127-129`). The AFO/Shigaraki-side explanation is that this is `成長`, “just the body”: a new bodily form acquired to keep pace with deepening Quirks and the endpoint anticipated by Garaki's singularity thesis (`p128`).

Prospective caution is necessary. V35 establishes that the phenomenon **persists under active Erasure** and is explained by the enemy as bodily adaptation rather than an active Quirk. It does not authorize a universal rule that every future mutation-like change is non-Quirk bodily growth.

The anti-Shigaraki system was engineered around known instant-death, speed and Quirk threats. The growth exploits a category boundary: Erasure can stop Quirk activation but cannot necessarily return the body to an earlier morphology.

## 8.2 Mirko/Bakugo/Jeanist preserve baseline style under lethal pressure

Even as the battlefield becomes grotesquely unmanageable, Mirko and Bakugo retain abrasive competitive register; Jeanist has to pull them back toward mission focus (`p129-130`). This is valuable character-model evidence because crisis does not erase their ordinary interaction grammar.

## 8.3 Monoma cannot be both portal and Erasure at once

Aizawa receives Midoriya's message from Okuto (`p130-131`). The immediate solution—use copied Warp Gate to retrieve him—is unavailable because Monoma cannot simultaneously sustain the copied Erasure and switch to the portal function. A ten-second release could expose the battlefield to Decay through the expanded hands (`p131-132`).

Aizawa therefore tells Midoriya to return under his own power (`p133`). The U.A. combat group answers that it will win even without OFA until then.

The scene prevents the “everyone is a protagonist” thesis from becoming magical equivalence. Roles are indispensable **because they are different and constrained**. Monoma cannot do everything. Midoriya still matters. The team continues because distributed agency means absorbing a missing node rather than pretending the node was unnecessary.

# 9. No.348 — `失恋`: love, danger and the failure of shared categories

## 9.1 Danger Sense fails because Toga's attack does not occupy her own category of harm

At Okuto, Midoriya realizes that Toga can cut or attack him without Danger Sense warning him in the expected way (`V35:p137-142`). Toga tells him not to leave because she loves him and asks him to become her lover (`p138-142`).

The important mechanic is inseparable from the psychology. Toga's love does not cleanly distinguish intimacy from injury. For her, blood-taking, becoming the loved person and approaching them with a blade can belong to the same affective category. Danger Sense is therefore not a universal physical-risk radar detached from human intention/meaning.

V35 does not fully formalize every Danger Sense rule. The bounded conclusion is that **Toga's sincere love/desire makes her hostile actions poorly legible to the inherited threat-sensing mechanism**.

## 9.2 Midoriya cannot translate admiration into Toga's ontology of becoming

Toga says she has liked Midoriya since first seeing him and links him to an earlier bloodied boy she loved (`p142-144`). Midoriya tries to find common ground: he too once wanted to become like All Might. But his imitation/admiration model does not include wanting to injure the loved person (`p144-145`).

That response is ethically reasonable and phenomenologically incomplete. Midoriya can recognize the desire to “become like” another person but cannot inhabit the Toga-specific fusion of attraction, blood and incorporation.

Toga then asks the key recognition question: `ねえヒーロー 君は私をどうしたい?` (`p144`). It reprises her V30 demand that heroes answer what they will do with a person like her.

## 9.3 Toga interprets the answer as exclusion from the category “person”

Midoriya says he understands wanting to become like someone but cannot accept hurting someone one loves (`p145`). Toga's expression shifts from romantic excitement toward grief. She asks, in effect, why the other person cannot simply live “normally” (`p146`), echoing the normality language once imposed on her.

She then makes the political inference that heroes and the people heroes protect are the ones counted as people, and that she cannot cross into that category (`p147`). This is not proof that the heroes actually hold a doctrine of non-personhood. It is **Toga's interpretation of repeated failed recognition**.

That distinction matters. The series can take the experience of exclusion seriously without endorsing her conclusion or her violence.

## 9.4 Uraraka takes over the relationship-specific responsibility

Toga says she had continued thinking about Uraraka after their prior encounter; when the hoped-for understanding does not come, she declares that if the world rejects her, she will reject the world (`p149`). She still calls Uraraka's sadness “sad,” preserving a thin relational line even in rejection.

When Toga attacks, Uraraka holds her and Toga says she thought Uraraka would understand because they are the same (`p150`). Tsuyu helps break the engagement and tells Midoriya to leave for Shigaraki (`p151-153`). Uraraka sends him onward and the quiet-scene callback resolves: both she and Midoriya are “strange” in the sense that each is unable to ignore the inner/relational problem inside the enemy they must stop (`p153`).

## 9.5 Toga reasserts self-authorship through destructive means

After Midoriya leaves, Toga says she loves Midoriya, Ochako and Tsuyu but is done asking for the world's permission. She wants to become the self she wants to be and live her own `当たり前` (`p160-162`). She addresses Twice and holds the preserved blood.

Uraraka answers from the opposite side: she too will fulfill her own ordinary way of being, `麗日お茶子として` (`p162`). The conflict is therefore no longer “normal hero versus abnormal villain.” It is **two people claiming incompatible forms of self-authorship under conditions where one form includes nonconsensual harm**.

The Twice-blood use itself remains OPEN at `V35:p162`; V35 establishes possession, intent and readiness, not the later outcome.

# 10. No.349 — `BATTLE FLAME`: preserving judgment under emotional displacement

## 10.1 Midoriya is warned against emotional overactivation

Midoriya attempts to cross roughly 200 km of ocean back toward the Shigaraki battlefield using Float/Fa Jin/Air Force-style propulsion (`V35:p157-159`). Over open water he cannot rely on Blackwhip anchors in the same way. A predecessor warns that he is agitated and that a Quirk previously reserved as a final option has been dramatically intensified by OFA and is no longer something to handle casually (`p159-160`).

The important character point is not the still-open exact later capability. It is that Midoriya's rescue urgency can cloud tactical judgment even after V32-V33 taught him to accept distributed help. The predecessor's advice is relational regulation inside OFA itself: trust comrades and look toward his assigned battlefield rather than trying to solve all theaters at once (`p160`).

## 10.2 Toga and Uraraka convert “ordinary” into opposed vows

At Okuto, Toga rejects heroes and says goodbye; Uraraka answers that she will also fulfill her own ordinary life as Ochako Uraraka (`p162`). This is a powerful revision to any simplistic “Uraraka wants Toga normalized” reading. By this point Uraraka is not demanding sameness. She is insisting that **her own** identity and protective commitments also count.

## 10.3 Kamino reopens the Todoroki family through role assignment

Kamino is already a sea of fire (`p163-164`). Dabi treats the environment as nostalgic and says the Hosu-era conflict helped set him in motion (`p164`). His heat prevents ordinary approach and forces the Endeavor sidekicks to use specialized control (`p165-166`).

Dabi sees his matchup—Shoto plus three sidekicks—as Endeavor's answer to him. He mocks the father for not coming personally (`p165`). The accusation cannot simply be dismissed as villain rhetoric because V35 has already established that the family history is real and that the assignment genuinely delegates confrontation to the abused younger son.

The sidekicks make a narrower defense: Endeavor's family conduct may be terrible, but the public hero work they have witnessed was not fake, and they will support Shoto in the responsibility entrusted to him (`p166`). The source again refuses totalization. Domestic abuse does not become unreal because public service was real; public service does not absolve domestic abuse.

## 10.4 Shoto distinguishes inherited history from chosen present purpose

Shoto tells Dabi not to misunderstand why he stands there. He has not simply fled crying from Toya; he has thought about how to stop him (`p167-169`). The confrontation is not framed as Shoto paying Endeavor's debt on Endeavor's behalf. Shoto's pre-battle wish to learn what Toya likes and share an ordinary meal remains the relational counterweight.

Dabi in turn emphasizes individual collision: this battle is ultimately person against person, the result of separate people reaching points of eruption (`p168-169`). That claim should not erase structural causality; V35 immediately proceeds into a biography filled with family failure and AFO/Garaki intervention. But it does foreground Dabi's own authorship of the present destructive policy.

He offers to tell Shoto how Toya became Dabi (`p169`).

# 11. No.350 — `エン`: Toya was exploitable, but not successfully authored by AFO

## 11.1 Toya survived Sekoto Peak and was taken into an AFO/Garaki facility

The chapter begins with police questioning Garaki about children born with “distortion” and the events around Toya (`V35:p171-174`). The flashback returns to Sekoto Peak eleven years earlier. Toya is burning, terrified and saying he does not want to die because he has not yet shown what he wanted to show (`p173`).

AFO's organization recovered the burned child. Toya later wakes in an institution after **three years** (`p175-176`). The child residents/caretakers have treated him as a long-sleeping boy; he does not initially understand where he is or what happened.

## 11.2 The replacement-family offer fails because Toya still wants his original family

A flower-faced caretaker tells Toya he can live there and become part of a new family (`p177`). Toya immediately tries to return home. He rationalizes that Endeavor may simply have been too busy to come. He wants to apologize to his mother and be seen by his father again (`p177-178`).

This is decisive causal evidence. The post-burn child does **not** wake as Dabi already committed to revenge. His first future orientation is return, apology and renewed paternal recognition.

The AFO-side system has reconstructed him but cannot restore him fully. V35 states that burned/lost tissue was supplemented with regenerative material; his appearance is altered, organs are damaged, pain/somatic sensation is blunted, his body is weakened and he is told he cannot produce power as before (`p178`).

The caretaker offers another possibility: if Toya stays and receives their education, they may be able to restore his flames (`p179`). Toya refuses to learn from another person. He remains attached to Endeavor as the authority whose recognition matters.

Garaki calls the project a failure (`p179-181`). The facility is connected to AFO's contingency architecture: children like Toya can function as possible vessels/backups if something happens to Shigaraki. Toya is one such attempted “insurance” route, but he is not successfully converted into the intended product.

## 11.3 AFO/Garaki are causal exploiters, not the sole authors of Dabi

V35 substantially revises any interpretation in which AFO simply “made Dabi.” He and Garaki do intervene in Toya's survival, reconstruct his body and attempt to redirect him into their system. But the attempted re-education fails because Toya's emotional jurisdiction remains elsewhere.

The moral implication is not exculpatory for AFO. Saving a child in order to cultivate a future vessel is itself exploitative. The analytical correction is causal: **their manipulation encounters a pre-existing, family-produced recognition wound that they cannot successfully replace.**

## 11.4 The homecoming is the true Dabi-formation hinge in V35

Toya returns to the Todoroki home after three years and sees a scene that, from his perspective, has not changed: Endeavor is still focused on Shoto's training, while Toya himself has become a memorial photograph/shrine presence (`p182-184`).

Adult Dabi tells Shoto that he returned because he hoped something had changed. He wanted his father to see him again despite his weakened body (`p182-183`). Instead, he reads the unchanged training regime and his own memorialization as proof that he had no meaning beyond being a failed project and that the family had placed him in the past (`p184`).

The source then gives the transformation its clearest sentence: `燈矢が死んで 荼毘が生まれたあの時からずっと` (`p185`). “Toya died / Dabi was born” is not a literal biological death claim; it is the character's identity narration of the failed homecoming.

## 11.5 Self-destruction becomes the medium of continuing attachment

Dabi says that after crossing enough limits, what formed a person can invert into black/white (`p184-185`). He continued observing Endeavor's techniques and public activity. His body could not reliably feel its own burning, but the sight of Endeavor's continued hero life kept his emotional “heart” beating (`p185`).

Garaki says that once Toya's body woke and began moving, it should not have lasted even a month (`p181`). Yet Dabi survives for years. The chapter attributes that persistence not to AFO's successful life-preservation design but to a death-directed `熱`: a fixation AFO, who seeks enduring existence, cannot possess in the same way (`p185-186`).

This is not medical realism; it is the manga's explicit moral/affective physiology. Dabi is alive through a drive aimed toward his own destruction and Endeavor's loss.

The final attack language is correspondingly annihilative. Dabi frames the destruction of everything important to Endeavor as proof that he existed (`p187`). Shoto rejects the program and confronts him directly.

# 12. AFO's system versus hero-side interdependence

V35 supplies unusually clean comparative evidence because both sides use deception, delegation and specialized bodies.

## AFO-side organization

AFO's network is built from:

- powers stolen from people and retained as inventory;
- Search as surveillance inherited through theft;
- lie/malice detection as distrust infrastructure;
- planted civilians and sleeper agents;
- Aoyama's family dependency converted into coercive access;
- Spinner's personal loyalty converted into a mass political symbol;
- Toga's grief supplied with Twice's blood as battlefield replication capacity;
- Toya's damaged child-body treated as possible backup/vessel material;
- multiple contingency routes so failed pieces can be abandoned.

The system's resilience comes from **fungibility**. A route can fail because another exists.

## Hero-side organization

The hero coalition also distributes function, but the architecture attempts to preserve differentiated subjects:

- Aoyama is used but told guilt is not his permanent identity;
- Shinso's coercive Quirk is used under an explicitly bounded operation;
- Monoma's Copy becomes load-bearing without erasing his own status;
- Support Course labor is named hero activity without pretending support and front-line combat are identical;
- Momo/Kaminari/Manga/Power Loader/Cementoss/Ectoplasm provide resources the “main” fighters cannot substitute;
- Aizawa admits his own impairment and delegates rather than pretending the Symbol model remains intact;
- combat teams keep acting after Midoriya is displaced instead of collapsing into one-body dependence.

The system's resilience comes from **non-fungible interdependence**. The operation works because people are different, not because anyone can be discarded.

That distinction is not absolute: military operations can still instrumentalize. V35's ethical claim is narrower and stronger when stated this way: a legitimate coalition must build ways for necessary function to coexist with continuing personhood and relationship.

# 13. Rescue, recognition and the “ordinary” problem

Three V35 arcs revolve around `当たり前` / normality:

1. Aoyama fears he has forfeited the ordinary future of being a student/hero/friend. Aizawa refuses to teach permanent indebtedness.
2. Toga's childhood normality was imposed as suppression; adult Toga claims the right to her own ordinary desire, even when that desire violates others' bodily autonomy.
3. Uraraka answers not by demanding Toga become normal, but by asserting that she too will live her own ordinary self `麗日お茶子として`.

This matters for later synthesis because “recognition” cannot mean unconditional ratification of another person's self-conception. V35 distinguishes at least three levels:

- **person recognition:** the opponent is still a person whose inner world exists;
- **causal recognition:** their present violence has a history worth understanding;
- **normative endorsement:** their chosen conduct is acceptable.

Uraraka/Midoriya increasingly commit to the first two while explicitly refusing the third.

# 14. Power philosophy

## 14.1 Quirk affordance is not moral essence

Shinso's Brainwashing—socially coded as villain-like—becomes the mechanism that defeats AFO's lie-detection system. Monoma's Copy—previously stigmatized as a supporting/borrowed ability—becomes the spatial backbone of the entire operation. V35 therefore decisively strengthens the corpus rule:

> **what a Quirk can do does not determine what kind of relation its user builds with other people.**

## 14.2 Power as infrastructure versus power as possession

AFO's global project is described through monopoly of capacities needed for social infrastructure (`p063-065`). The floating U.A. answers with the opposite architecture: infrastructure produced by many people whose capacities remain distributed and socially embedded (`p118-123`).

## 14.3 Erasure reveals the body/Quirk boundary is incomplete

The Shigaraki finger-growth sequence shows that anti-Quirk power cannot simply restore a pre-Quirk human baseline. The body has adapted to power. This is a major ontological complication to any model in which Quirks are detachable “abilities” sitting on top of otherwise unchanged bodies.

## 14.4 OFA itself is now a distributed deliberative system

The second predecessor's warning to Midoriya in No.349 shows that OFA's internal predecessors are not merely a power library. They can function as a deliberative brake when Midoriya is emotionally overdriven. In V35, even the most protagonist-centered inheritance becomes internally plural.

# 15. Hero-society and institutional findings

V35's hero society is not recovering by restoring the old Symbol hierarchy. It is operating through emergency pluralism.

- Police/hospital/security systems still perform custody, examination and planning around Aoyama.
- Refuge/evacuation systems remain important while also penetrable by sleeper agents.
- U.A. becomes a mobile/engineered combat infrastructure rather than just a school.
- Support Course, construction Quirks, logistics and electricity production are direct battlefield dependencies.
- Students whose institutional advancement is formally disrupted by collapse (Shinso) can still be emergency-authorized into critical roles.
- Professional legitimacy is no longer reducible to public image: Endeavor's sidekicks can condemn his domestic history while affirming that the public work they personally witnessed was real.

This is a post-Symbol society attempting to preserve institutions through **role differentiation and mutual dependency**, not through restoration of one flawless protector.

# 17. Visual grammar and motifs

## 17.1 Empty homes and memorial spaces

Toga's abandoned childhood home and Toya's memorialized place in the Todoroki home form a strong visual pair. Both villains encounter domestic spaces that tell them the social world has moved on without a living version of them. The causes differ, but the visual grammar is shared: **absence has been organized into architecture**.

## 17.2 Portals and cages

The battle begins through frames within frames—cages, portals, assigned zones. V35 visually converts the narrative into segmented jurisdictions. The “final war” is not one battlefield; it is a set of artificially separated relationship problems.

## 17.3 Hands

AFO/Shigaraki's proliferating hands literalize uncontrolled reach at the moment Erasure is supposed to constrain him. In contrast, Aizawa's/Aoyama's earlier repair language is about accepting offered hands from friends. The hand motif again distinguishes grasping/expansion from reciprocal assistance.

## 17.4 The floating coffin

U.A. as `天空の棺` reverses the school-as-shelter image. The institution becomes a weaponized container built by students and adults together. It is simultaneously protective infrastructure and execution/containment architecture.

## 17.5 Fire as family memory

Kamino is already a fire-scarred symbolic location. Dabi's return makes public urban fire, Hosu memory, Endeavor's techniques and private family grievance occupy the same visual field.
