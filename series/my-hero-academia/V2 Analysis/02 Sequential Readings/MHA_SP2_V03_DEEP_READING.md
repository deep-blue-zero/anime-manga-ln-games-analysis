# 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 1 asks whether heroic worth can be recognized before conventional power makes it legible. Volume 2 asks whether power can be governed by judgment once it is acquired. Volume 3 asks the next social question:

> **How does governed power become publicly legible, selected, cultivated, and transformed into a symbol?**

The volume's deepest organizing concept is **symbol production**.

This is not merely because the Sports Festival is televised. The volume deliberately places two mentorship scenes on opposite sides of the USJ aftermath.

After Shigaraki's defeat, his unseen `先生` does not tell him that failure proves he is worthless. The mentor reframes the loss as usable information, proposes gathering stronger allies over time, and then explains why Shigaraki himself matters: `我々は自由に動けない！ だから君のようなシンボルが必要なんだ`. The command that follows is explicit: `次こそ君という恐怖を世に知らしめろ！` — make the world know the fear that is you. At the prospective V03 boundary, this speaker is only Shigaraki's unseen teacher. Retrospectively, he is All For One, but that identity must not be imported into the prospective reading before the manga supplies it.

Soon afterward, All Might tells Midoriya why he entrusted him with One For All: `君に“力”を授けたのは “私”を継いで欲しいからだ！`. He describes Midoriya as the next All Might, an egg of the Symbol, and uses the nationally watched Sports Festival as a stage on which he wants Midoriya to announce himself: `君が来た！ってことを 世の中に知らしめてほしい!!`.

The parallel is too exact to treat as accidental.

Both sides now possess:

- a damaged or constrained older figure;
- a younger successor whose current form is incomplete;
- an intergenerational transfer of mission;
- a theory that private capacity is not enough;
- and a demand that the successor's existence become socially visible as a **symbol**.

The moral opposition does not lie in one side understanding symbols and the other not understanding them. Both understand that social power depends on public imagination. The difference lies in the kind of relation they want the successor to create with the public.

All Might wants a successor whose presence can mean rescue, reassurance, possibility, and continued social confidence. Shigaraki's teacher wants a successor whose presence can mean fear, destabilization, recruitment, and the public visibility of suppressed malice.

This substantially strengthens the series-level model of One For All and All For One as relational opposites. The opposition is already broader than “one power is given and the other steals.” It is becoming:

> **two rival architectures for reproducing power across generations and turning a person into a socially consequential symbol.**

The Sports Festival then supplies the institutional machinery through which heroic symbols are produced. U.A. explicitly describes it as a national mega-event that has replaced the old Olympics in Japanese popular importance. Professional heroes watch to scout talent. Office affiliation and sidekick placement can open a career. Students get only three such annual chances. Performance can even move someone from the General Studies course into Heroics — or contribute to movement in the other direction. The festival therefore converts bodies, Quirks, judgment, charisma, and competitive performance into **publicly legible career value**.

That creates an ethical contradiction the volume does not solve. Midoriya's model of heroism is rescue-oriented, but the Sports Festival explicitly requires him to defeat peers and make them fail. In his own thought, the event is `露骨にそこに相反する 他を蹴落として競う場`: a place where the rescue hero's social ideal is placed against an overt competition built on knocking others down.

Yet the volume does not condemn competition as inherently anti-heroic. It repeatedly shows competition producing growth, information, ambition, and unexpected forms of excellence. Aizawa's comment from the stands is telling: when someone asks how he trained such a class, he says `俺は何もしてねえよ 奴らが勝手に火ィ付け合ってんだろう` — he did not manufacture their intensity; they are setting one another alight.

The emerging thesis is therefore not “competition is corrupt.” It is more difficult:

> **Hero Society needs public competition to discover and develop talent, but the same machinery can turn heroic capacity into rank, market value, brand, spectacle, and exclusion.**

Midoriya's obstacle-race victory beautifully holds the tension together. All Might asks him to tell the world he has arrived, but Midoriya wins without using One For All at all. He scavenges robot armor, reads the course, weaponizes the minefield, and converts being hunted by Todoroki and Bakugo into a chance to pass them. The stadium commentary then announces `緑谷出久の存在を!!` — the existence/presence of Midoriya Izuku — before the crowd.

He becomes publicly legible not by reproducing All Might's visible power, but by revealing **Midoriya's own mode of competence**.

That is the volume's decisive answer to inherited identity:

> **To inherit a symbol is not merely to imitate its surface. A successor has to become legible in his own way.**

A compact formulation is:

> **Volume 3 is about the deliberate production of successors and symbols: villains and heroes both understand that power becomes historically consequential only when it is cultivated, publicly recognized, and embedded in a social audience.**

# 2. Narrative architecture

## 2.1 USJ ends by distributing defense of the Symbol

Volume 2 ends with All Might's reassuring arrival. Volume 3 refuses to let that arrival reduce everyone else to spectators.

Midoriya continues analyzing even while All Might fights Nomu. He wonders whether Shigaraki retains another killing method and recognizes that, regardless of what he can personally accomplish, he cannot simply stop watching the field. Bakugo attacks Kurogiri to obstruct villain movement. Todoroki, Kirishima, Bakugo, and Midoriya converge around the remaining threat. Todoroki explicitly tells the villains that `平和の象徴はてめェら如きに殺れねェよ`.

This is a subtle but important inversion. All Might is the Symbol who protects society, but his students now participate in **protecting the Symbol**.

That does not make All Might weak or unnecessary. His victory over Nomu still depends on spectacular overperformance. The scene intensifies the existing contradiction: the Symbol is powerful enough to justify extraordinary public confidence, but preserving him increasingly requires distributed support from people he is meant to protect.

The series' later move from `私が来た` toward collective responsibility therefore has a genuine early seed here. The students do not replace All Might; they create the conditions under which the exhausted All Might can continue functioning.

## 2.2 Defeat becomes pedagogy on both sides

The next architectural movement is more unusual. The manga processes USJ through two different systems of post-failure learning.

### Shigaraki's side

Shigaraki returns injured and openly calls the operation a complete defeat: both arms and legs were shot, Nomu was defeated almost instantly, the children were stronger than expected, and the Symbol of Peace was still healthy enough to overcome the plan. His first response is accusatory: the situation was not what his teacher had promised.

The teacher does not mirror Shigaraki's agitation. He says the problem was poor foresight, asks what happened to Nomu, treats the defeat as data, and proposes a longer-term strategy: gather an elite force and take time.

Most importantly, failure is not merely corrected tactically. It is incorporated into **successor construction**. Shigaraki is told that the older organization cannot move freely, so it needs a symbol like him.

### Hero-side institutional reflection

Police and U.A. then analyze Shigaraki behaviorally rather than mythologizing him. All Might notes the strange mixture of boldness and immaturity: he undertakes an attack most people would never attempt, delivers an improvised-seeming ideology, hides his own Quirk while bragging about Nomu's, and becomes visibly upset when reality does not follow expectation.

The conclusion is the memorable formulation:

`幼児的万能感の抜け切らない “子ども大人”だ`

He is an adult-child who has not outgrown an infantile sense of omnipotence.

This is immediately tied to socialization. The discussion references childhood Quirk counseling and people who fail to manage power. All Might then performs the crucial conceptual inversion:

`“子ども大人”…逆に考えれば 生徒らと同じだ`

If thought of another way, he is like the students. He has room to grow. And if an excellent instructor were to appear, the consequences could become much more dangerous.

The reader has just seen that such an instructor already exists.

This creates one of the first explicit structural parallels between hero education and villain formation. The question is not simply whether a person has dangerous impulses or dangerous power. It is **who teaches that person what their power means, what goals it should serve, and how failure should be interpreted**.

Volume 3 therefore complicates the later failed-recognition thesis. It does not yet supply Shigaraki's origin and cannot support a Tenko-specific causal account. But it does establish that:

- power without socialization can remain developmentally immature;
- institutions attempt to discipline Quirk use from childhood;
- unsocialized or alienated people with power can become socially dangerous;
- grievance can attract followers;
- and mentorship can convert immature destructiveness into organized ideological power.

## 2.3 The Sports Festival converts education into public sorting

The transition from terrorism to festival looks tonally abrupt, but institutionally it is exact.

After a real attack raises fears about U.A.'s security, Aizawa insists that holding the Sports Festival is itself a demonstration of crisis-management capacity. Security will be increased dramatically. Cancellation would concede that U.A. can be intimidated.

The festival therefore serves several functions simultaneously:

1. **Public reassurance:** U.A. demonstrates that it can continue operating after attack.
2. **Talent discovery:** professional heroes scout future sidekicks.
3. **Labor-market sorting:** placement into prestigious offices can materially alter careers.
4. **Internal school mobility:** strong results can support movement into Heroics from other courses.
5. **Brand production:** the public begins associating names, faces, Quirks, styles, and reputations.
6. **Pedagogy:** students learn to perform under mass scrutiny and against motivated peers.
7. **Spectacle:** the event functions as national entertainment.

The series thus makes hero education inseparable from public culture and labor-market formation. U.A. is not only a school. It is an elite credentialing institution, televised showcase, recruitment platform, and reputation market.

## 2.4 Publicity changes the meaning of power

At USJ, power matters because people may die. At the Sports Festival, the same power also becomes information for spectators.

This shift is repeatedly explicit.

The crowd watches whether students can produce maximum performance under observation. Top heroes watch for recruitment. General-course students watch for openings. Business-course students imagine how a promising student might be marketed and how an agency might present him. Support-course students can use equipment publicly to appeal directly to companies.

Power therefore acquires a second layer of meaning:

> **what can you do?** becomes **what can others see, recognize, price, recruit, and narrate about what you can do?**

That distinction is essential for later hero rankings and celebrity culture.

## 2.5 The obstacle race makes public recognition contingent and unstable

The first Sports Festival event compresses the volume's themes into a single structure.

Todoroki initially dominates by freezing the starting bottleneck and moving first. Bakugo pursues. Midoriya is repeatedly behind and has no safe conventional way to use OFA. He therefore survives by improvisation:

- avoids or repurposes obstacles rather than simply overpowering them;
- scavenges a robot plate;
- uses the plate as protection and mobility tool;
- observes the minefield rather than panicking;
- deliberately triggers collected mines to launch himself forward;
- converts the Todoroki/Bakugo contest into a temporary opening;
- and reaches the stadium first.

The visual logic is important. The race begins with Midoriya literally trapped inside the crush of bodies at the starting gate. It ends with a full-page public announcement of `緑谷出久の存在を!!`.

The transformation is from **obscured body in the crowd** to **named individual under collective gaze**.

But the success is immediately made precarious. In the next event, first place receives ten million points and therefore becomes the universal target. The title of Chapter 26 — `立て追われる身` — captures the reversal: standing at the top means becoming the pursued body.

Public recognition is thus never purely reward. Visibility creates opportunity and attack surface simultaneously.

# 3. Character-state ledger

## 3.1 Midoriya Izuku

### Current state

Volume 3 makes Midoriya consciously enter a public competitive order without converting him into a rank-for-rank's-sake personality.

In Volume 2 he learned that power must become manageable and that authority should follow function. Here he is asked to do something psychologically harder: **want first place on purpose**.

Todoroki tells him that, objectively, Todoroki believes himself stronger and that he intends to beat Midoriya because All Might appears to favor him. Midoriya does not deny the power gap. He looks at the whole field and recognizes that everyone present is aiming upward. His answer is:

`僕も本気で獲りに行く！`

This requires a refinement of the first-pass model. Midoriya is not indifferent to victory. He does not seek superiority as a primary moral good, but when competition is attached to professional opportunity, obligation to All Might, recognition by others, and proof of growth, he can intentionally pursue first place.

The better model is:

> **Midoriya is rescue-first, not competition-averse. He can become highly competitive when winning serves a value he understands as larger than status itself.**

### Mentor obligation and public pressure

All Might's request produces a new form of burden. He has not simply been given power. He has been told that he is expected to inherit a public role.

Midoriya understands that All Might's time as Symbol is shortening. He can also infer that hostile actors are beginning to notice All Might's weakness. The Sports Festival therefore feels less like ordinary school competition and more like an early succession test.

This does not immediately energize him. In the pre-festival flashback he admits that, despite understanding All Might's expectation, he cannot fully enter the mood so soon after USJ and while his own physical metrics remain poor. This is important modeling evidence: **Midoriya can feel duty before he feels confidence or excitement**.

### Attention and improvisation

The obstacle race strongly strengthens the analytic model.

Midoriya does not win because he has a superior base body or because OFA solves the course. He wins through opportunistic recombination of environment and information. The discarded robot plate is not designed as a mobility aid; the mines are not designed as propulsion; Todoroki and Bakugo's pursuit is not designed to create an opening. Midoriya turns all three into tools.

This is a higher-order form of his notebook habit:

> **he does not only analyze fixed abilities; he sees temporarily available relationships among objects, opponents, timing, and terrain.**

### Self-attribution after success

His response to winning is just as important as the win.

When Uraraka praises him, Midoriya insists internally that he was merely lucky: `運が良かっただけ！`, that the things he thought might work happened to work, and that the real test of ability begins now.

This is partly healthy calibration. The course did contain contingency. But the degree of self-discounting is notable because the successful combination required repeated decisions that were not random.

Current model implication:

> **Midoriya is much quicker to attribute failure to his own inadequacy than to attribute success to his own competence.**

That asymmetry may protect him from arrogance while also delaying a stable sense of earned ability.

### Public recognition

The crowd and announcer make his achievement socially real. All Might asked him to let the world know `君が来た`. By the end of the obstacle race the event literally calls attention to `緑谷出久の存在`.

Yet Midoriya does this without One For All. The successor's first major national recognition is not a clean imitation of All Might. It is evidence that Midoriya's pre-OFA adaptive intelligence remains central even after inheritance.

### Current modeling delta

`STRENGTHEN / REVISE`

- Competition is available as a deliberate mode under meaningful stakes.
- Public duty can motivate him even when confidence lags.
- Environmental improvisation is a stable competence, not merely exam-specific luck.
- Self-credit remains abnormally low.
- Hero identity is becoming partially public rather than only interpersonal/institutional.

## 3.2 Bakugo Katsuki

### Current state

Volume 3 confirms the Volume 2 correction: Bakugo's aggression toward Midoriya is unusually relationship-conditioned, but his broader competitive drive is not.

When students from other courses crowd Class 1-A's doorway, Bakugo correctly identifies the visit as reconnaissance. When Kirishima becomes angry at the challenges, Bakugo responds with a more generalized competitive horizon:

`関係ねえよ`

`上に上がりゃ 関係ねえ`

He no longer needs every opponent to be individually humiliated before he can proceed. If he reaches the top, the hierarchy answers them collectively.

This is a meaningful development from Volume 2's collapse. His ambition is still extreme, but it begins to shift from **defending inherited superiority** toward **proving superiority through actual competition**.

### Public self-binding

Bakugo's athlete pledge is one of his clearest early behavioral signatures:

`俺が一位になる`

He knows the statement will provoke hostility. The crowd immediately boos. Kirishima asks why he would deliberately inflame resentment. Bakugo's answer treats the field as future material for his victory: `せめて跳ねの良い踏み台になってくれ`.

This is more than generic arrogance. Public declaration functions as **self-binding**. Bakugo converts the audience into pressure and every competitor into evidence that must be overcome. He removes the socially comfortable possibility of quiet underperformance.

A good predictive rule at this stage is:

> when Bakugo strongly values a competition, he is likely to make the standard maximally explicit and then force himself to live under it.

### Rivalry broadening

Todoroki now matters. Bakugo pursues him in the obstacle race and angrily resists being left behind. Midoriya's unexpected surge then converts the race into a three-way hierarchy problem.

Bakugo is therefore no longer modeled adequately by “Midoriya obsession.” Midoriya remains a uniquely destabilizing relationship, but credible high-level rivals rapidly enter the same status field.

### Peer perception of change

Other classmates notice that his response to the pre-festival challengers is different enough to seem changed after the battle trial. This does not mean he becomes socially gentle. It means humiliation has already altered the **form** of his competitiveness.

### Current modeling delta

`STRENGTHEN`

- Public commitment and deliberate provocation are stable competitive tools.
- Status drive generalizes beyond Midoriya.
- Evidence from V02 defeat has not reduced ambition; it has redirected ambition toward earned top position.
- Tactical and competitive cognition remain distinct from interpersonal dysregulation.

## 3.3 Uraraka Ochako

Volume 3 gives Uraraka's hero aspiration its first major motivational foundation.

When she becomes intensely serious about the Sports Festival, Midoriya is surprised to learn that money matters to her. Uraraka is embarrassed because she thinks the motive sounds less noble than Iida's. The family context immediately changes its meaning.

Her parents run a construction business with limited work. As a child she wanted to use Zero Gravity to help them. Her father refused to turn her dream into family labor and told her that, as parents, they would be far happier if she achieved her own dream. Her current resolve is therefore:

`私は絶対ヒーローになってお金稼いで 父ちゃん母ちゃんに楽させたげるんだ！`

This is not a mercenary counterexample to idealistic heroism. It is a form of **care translated into economic security**.

Important consequences:

- hero work is explicitly labor, not only vocation;
- family class/economic conditions shape reasons for entering the profession;
- Uraraka's care ethic includes reciprocal material support;
- she is willing to state a practical motive that the heroic culture might code as less “pure.”

Her Volume 1 point-sharing behavior and Volume 2 tactical support now gain a common structure: she repeatedly tries to use what she has to reduce burdens on others.

### Current modeling delta

`STRENGTHEN`

Primary motive can now be modeled as a combination of personal hero aspiration and filial economic care rather than generic friendliness.

## 3.4 Iida Tenya

Iida's principal contribution here is comparative. His already articulated high-minded family/professional ideal becomes the benchmark against which Uraraka initially judges her own financial motive. The volume thereby uses Iida to show that hero motives exist on more than one socially legitimate axis.

He also remains part of the class's professionalizing discourse: the Sports Festival is understood as an event where differences in ambition and preparation can later matter in society.

No major contradiction to the V02 model appears.

## 3.5 Todoroki Shoto

Volume 3 establishes Todoroki as more than a high-capability classmate and does so while still preserving substantial mystery.

### Competitive self-assessment

He tells Midoriya:

`客観的に見ても 実力は俺の方が上だと思う`

This is not phrased like Bakugo's global status entitlement. Todoroki presents it as an objective comparison and then directly declares that he will beat Midoriya.

He has also noticed the All Might–Midoriya relationship: `おまえ オールマイトに目ぇかけられてるよな`. He does not demand the secret. He says he has no intention of prying, but the connection makes Midoriya competitively salient.

### Family pressure becomes textually visible

The volume explicitly identifies him in public commentary as Endeavor's son. More importantly, during the obstacle race he thinks:

`クソ親父が見てるんだから`

The detailed family history is not yet available and must not be prospectively reconstructed from later knowledge. What V03 does support is:

- father is the high-ranked flame hero Endeavor;
- Todoroki is conscious of his father's gaze during national competition;
- his father relationship is hostile enough for `クソ親父`;
- and that gaze is motivationally active during performance.

That is already enough to make “inherited heroic identity” a second major family case alongside Midoriya's chosen inheritance from All Might.

### Competitive style

Todoroki begins the obstacle race by exploiting the mass bottleneck, freezing the field, and taking the lead. He is willing to create a large environmental constraint and trust his own control over its consequences. He also deliberately defeats the large robots by freezing them in unstable posture so they collapse behind him, turning solved obstacles into problems for pursuers.

This is efficient, impersonal, and positional.

### Current modeling status

`OPEN / emerging`

Do not yet infer full internal ideology from later Sports Festival material. Current evidence supports controlled confidence, strategic environmental manipulation, hostile father salience, and deliberate interest in Midoriya as All Might's apparent chosen student.

## 3.6 Shigaraki Tomura

Volume 3 is the first time the manga subjects Shigaraki to sustained **developmental interpretation**.

### Failure response

Immediately after USJ he is physically injured, emotionally agitated, and willing to call the operation a complete defeat. He blames the mismatch between expectation and reality, but he is not incapable of reporting useful information. He identifies that the students were stronger than expected and reports a child whose speed appeared comparable to All Might's from his perspective.

### Mentor dependence

The unseen teacher supplies what Shigaraki currently lacks:

- emotional regulation after failure;
- long time horizon;
- recruitment strategy;
- interpretation of setback;
- and symbolic mission.

This does not remove Shigaraki's agency. He willingly tried to murder children and All Might. But it makes his villain development relational rather than solitary.

### Adult-child model

All Might and police infer from behavior that Shigaraki remains developmentally immature: impulsive grandiosity, bragging, frustration when reality resists him, and a simplistic omnipotence structure.

The crucial point is not the insult `子ども大人` by itself. It is that All Might immediately sees the danger of **growth**. If Shigaraki is developmentally incomplete, then he is not at a fixed endpoint. The appearance of a skilled mentor can make him worse in organized, durable ways.

The reader knows that this process is already underway.

### Symbol construction

The mentor's request `君という恐怖を世に知らしめろ` gives Shigaraki a public task. Like Midoriya, he is being asked to become more than an individual combatant. He must become a recognizable social sign.

### Current modeling delta

`STRENGTHEN / REVISE`

- Immaturity and tactical perception coexist.
- Failure can produce dysregulated complaint, but a trusted superior can reframe it into continued action.
- He is susceptible to identity construction by mentorship.
- Public fear/reputation is now an explicit assigned objective.
- Origin causation remains unresolved at this chronology.

## 3.7 All Might

Volume 3 moves All Might from recognizing a worthy child to **actively engineering succession**.

### Symbolic self-awareness

He knows his remaining time is shrinking and that villains may notice. He no longer treats Midoriya merely as a student who should become a good hero. He says explicitly that he gave him power because he wants Midoriya to succeed him.

This is both generous and burdensome.

All Might gives Midoriya opportunity, but he also projects a civilization-scale role onto a first-year student whose body still cannot safely use OFA at normal output. The moral beauty of entrustment therefore contains a new risk: even voluntary inheritance can become psychologically heavy when the inherited ideal is enormous.

### Mentor style

Unlike Shigaraki's unseen teacher, All Might does not respond to Midoriya's weakness with contempt. He treats the first no-recoil attempted strike as progress. He can be comically overanxious about Midoriya's performance. His core expectation is demanding, but his immediate pedagogy includes reassurance and celebration of incremental improvement.

### Current modeling delta

`STRENGTHEN`

- increasingly explicit succession planning;
- tendency to translate personal trust into very high expectations;
- care remains emotionally expressive and sometimes anxious;
- symbol role is something he consciously believes must be reproduced.

## 3.8 Aizawa Shota

Volume 3 adds a valuable low-intervention teaching datum.

From the stands, when asked how he made the class so capable, he says:

`俺は何もしてねえよ 奴らが勝手に火ィ付け合ってんだろう`

The line should not be taken literally as absence of teaching; V01–V02 already show intensive intervention. It does indicate that Aizawa recognizes **peer competition and self-driven response** as forces of development he does not need to micromanage.

This fits his earlier pedagogy: he creates demanding evaluative conditions, states consequences bluntly, and expects students to adapt rather than constantly supplying emotional scaffolding.

## 3.9 Hatsume Mei — baseline

Hatsume's first meaningful appearance establishes a markedly different relationship to the Sports Festival.

She is Support Course, not Hero Course. She treats the national spectacle as a technology exhibition and openly says support students can appeal their ideas and development skills to companies. Her equipment is framed as her `ベイビー`.

This is important institutionally and behaviorally:

- intense product attachment;
- entrepreneurial publicity orientation;
- little embarrassment about self-promotion;
- ability to reinterpret a “hero” competition as an innovation market.

Readiness remains `emerging`/low because evidence is brief, but the baseline is unusually clear.

# 4. Power philosophies

## 4.1 Power becomes socially reproducible through mentorship

V03 makes power philosophy explicitly intergenerational.

All Might and Shigaraki's teacher both understand that raw ability does not reproduce itself. A younger person needs:

- interpretation;
- training;
- identity;
- strategic direction;
- and a relationship to a public.

The ethical question is therefore no longer only “what is power for?” It becomes:

> **what kind of person does an older power-holder try to create through a successor?**

## 4.2 All Might: power as entrusted continuity

All Might's formulation `“私”を継いで欲しい` is striking because Midoriya is asked to inherit more than the Quirk. He is asked to inherit a social function.

The risk is obvious: a person can be entrusted without being possessed, yet still be burdened by the predecessor's expectations. This is an early reason to distinguish ethical entrustment from complete autonomy. OFA's relational virtue does not mean every consequence of succession is automatically benign.

## 4.3 Shigaraki's teacher: power as cultivated fear

The unseen teacher wants Shigaraki to become a public symbol because the older organization cannot move freely. The successor is therefore partly a **proxy for constrained power**.

This is already a different relational structure from All Might's. Shigaraki is being shaped to project fear and attract/organize destructive social energies. The teacher's patience after failure may look supportive at the interpersonal level while serving an anti-personhood project at the social level.

## 4.4 Todoroki: power under inherited family gaze

Todoroki introduces another inheritance problem. He possesses exceptional power, but the available V03 evidence makes the father relationship hostile rather than aspirational. He competes while aware that Endeavor watches.

This means inherited capacity can be experienced as pressure even before the exact mechanism of that inheritance is revealed.

## 4.5 Midoriya: power not used can still shape victory

The obstacle race is philosophically important because Midoriya wins while largely **not using OFA**.

The manga refuses the idea that receiving a great power should erase the virtues developed without it. His analytical improvisation remains constitutive of hero competence.

Power therefore becomes one resource inside a larger practical intelligence rather than the whole self.

## 4.6 Support power and commercial power

Hatsume broadens the concept of meaningful power again. Her value lies in equipment, invention, and the ability to improve or extend other people's capabilities. Business-course students introduce another noncombat form: the capacity to position, market, and operate a hero office.

Hero Society is increasingly shown as dependent on multiple forms of competence that do not look like frontline combat Quirks.

# 5. Hero Society / institutional analysis

## 5.1 The Sports Festival is national labor-market infrastructure

The festival is not merely school spirit.

The manga explicitly states that it has become a Japanese mega-event comparable to/replacing the once-dominant Olympics in popular importance. Top heroes attend for scouting. The standard path into professional work runs through agencies and sidekick experience. Being noticed by a strong office can open a future.

Students receive only three such annual opportunities.

This means public adolescent performance can have durable labor-market effects.

## 5.2 U.A. answers terrorism with institutional continuity

Holding the festival after USJ is framed as a demonstration of confidence in U.A.'s crisis management. Security is intensified rather than the event abandoned.

This can be read in two ways simultaneously:

- legitimate refusal to let violent actors dictate institutional life;
- and evidence that the school is willing to expose students to enormous public pressure because the event is too structurally important to cancel.

Neither reading alone is sufficient.

## 5.3 Sorting remains permeable but harsh

Shinso explains that Sports Festival results can be used to consider transfer from General Studies into the Hero Course, and that movement in the other direction is also possible.

This is important because V01 showed hero-track selection as metric-dependent. V03 shows that the selection is **not entirely final**, but the second-chance mechanism is itself another public competition.

The system therefore permits mobility without abandoning ranking.

## 5.4 Hero performance is branding

The festival turns Quirks into reputational assets.

Public observers speak of Midoriya's rising “stock.” Business-course students practice thinking about how they would market a hero and run an office. Support students display inventions to companies. Students themselves understand that memorable performance affects future recruitment.

The hero profession is therefore already simultaneously:

- public service;
- dangerous labor;
- credentialed occupation;
- entertainment;
- personal brand;
- and market organization.

## 5.5 Economic motives are not outside hero ethics

Uraraka's family story makes this institutional picture personal. Becoming a hero is also a route to income and economic mobility.

The series refuses to reserve legitimacy for abstract motives. Wanting to support one's parents financially can coexist with genuine rescue ethics.

## 5.6 Public visibility produces vulnerability

The Sports Festival offers opportunity precisely by making students visible to everyone.

That includes:

- recruiters;
- peers;
- family;
- general public;
- other courses;
- and potentially hostile observers.

The same public legibility that creates a career can expose weakness, secrets, affiliations, and pressure points. This extends V02's finding that fame creates attack surface.

# 6. Recognition and failed rescue

V03 shifts recognition from intimate rescue recognition toward **mass recognition**.

## 6.1 Recognition becomes industrialized

V01 recognition often occurred one person at a time: All Might sees Midoriya act; Uraraka sees his rescue value; Iida revises his judgment.

V03 builds an institution designed to make recognition happen at scale. Scouts, agencies, spectators, commentators, business students, and support companies all watch the same bodies.

This creates opportunity, but also distorts the object of recognition. What becomes visible in a stadium may not equal what matters most in actual rescue.

## 6.2 Midoriya's public visibility is earned through non-obvious competence

The obstacle race is a good case because his first-place result reveals strategic improvisation that earlier standardized physical testing did not capture well.

Public competition can therefore reveal hidden strengths as well as flatten people into metrics.

## 6.3 Shinso represents a recognition problem inside the institution

Shinso appears from outside the Hero Course and explicitly uses the Sports Festival as a route toward reevaluation.

At this point the manga has not yet explained the full reason his Quirk fits poorly with entrance-exam structure, so that later analysis must wait. But V03 already establishes him as someone who believes the first institutional classification need not be final.

## 6.4 Uraraka recognizes family burden

Her hero aspiration is shaped by seeing economic strain at home and wanting to relieve it. This is recognition in a non-crisis register: she sees people she loves carrying burdens and wants her future work to change their material condition.

## 6.5 Villain recognition becomes recruitment risk

All Might's police discussion suggests that suppressed malice in a hero-saturated society may be attracted to Shigaraki's naive evil. Here recognition has an inverted form: harmful people may recognize themselves or their grievance in a public villain symbol.

A symbol does not merely tell others who to admire. It can tell them **where they belong**.

# 7. Villain formation, rescue, and accountability

## 7.1 V03 materially advances villain formation without yet revealing origin

This volume is the first strong evidence that villain development is socially mediated.

Shigaraki's present crimes remain fully accountable. Nothing in the police diagnosis excuses attempted child murder or the USJ attack. But the manga now asks what happens to a powerful, developmentally immature person when power is not adequately socialized and a skilled mentor gives destructive impulses organization.

That is a causal question without being an exculpatory answer.

## 7.2 Childhood Quirk socialization matters

The discussion of `個性カウンセリング` is easy to underweight. It implies that superhuman society understands Quirk management as a developmental problem from childhood, not merely a licensing problem at adulthood.

This strengthens the institutional thesis:

> Hero Society must teach people how to live with power before it ever has to police them for misusing it.

The later series' concern with earlier intervention has a genuine conceptual seed here.

## 7.3 The adult-child formulation is double-edged

Calling Shigaraki a `子ども大人` could merely infantilize a dangerous adult. All Might avoids that trap by using the diagnosis to predict **growth potential** rather than diminished responsibility.

He is dangerous now and may become more dangerous through guidance.

This is one of the cleanest early distinctions between:

- explaining an offender's developmental state;
- recognizing future plasticity;
- and denying current agency.

The manga does the first two without doing the third.

## 7.4 Mentor asymmetry

The unseen teacher's post-failure behavior is psychologically effective. He remains calm, does not discard Shigaraki, gives him a longer horizon, and transforms failure into renewed mission.

This is important because destructive mentorship does not need to look interpersonally abusive at every moment. A relationship can provide patience, validation, and structure while orienting the recipient toward catastrophic ends.

Later analysis should preserve this distinction.

# 8. Japanese language, character voice, and key vocabulary

## 8.1 `シンボル`

The most important V03 word.

It now belongs explicitly to both sides:

- `平和の象徴` — Symbol of Peace;
- Shigaraki as the required symbol of fear.

The term denotes not just fame but a person whose public existence organizes collective expectation.

## 8.2 `継ぐ`

All Might says `“私”を継いで欲しい`.

This extends earlier OFA transfer vocabulary. The inheritance is no longer merely receipt of `力`; it is succession to a person-defined social role.

## 8.3 `世に知らしめる`

This phrase becomes a deliberate bridge between the two successor projects:

- Shigaraki must make his fear known to the world.
- Midoriya must make his arrival known to the world.

The grammar is outward-facing and public. Both mentors want a successor to become legible beyond the private relationship.

## 8.4 `子ども大人` / `幼児的万能感`

The police/U.A. analysis uses quasi-developmental vocabulary to describe Shigaraki's behavior. These words should be preserved because “immature villain” is weaker than the source. The specific claim is that adult agency coexists with an unoutgrown childlike omnipotence structure.

## 8.5 `本気で獲りに行く`

Midoriya's response to Todoroki is active and competitive. `獲る` gives winning a deliberately seized quality. This matters against readings that make him purely reactive in competition.

## 8.6 `俺が一位になる`

Bakugo's pledge is unusually stripped down. No explanation, no social smoothing: he publicly declares the outcome he intends to produce.

## 8.7 `クソ親父`

Todoroki's private label for Endeavor is already highly charged. It establishes hostility before the source explains why.

## 8.8 `運が良かっただけ`

Midoriya's immediate self-explanation after first place. The phrase is useful for modeling his asymmetrical attribution style: success is externalized toward luck more readily than failure is.

## 8.9 `存在`

The announcer's call to show `緑谷出久の存在` is nearly a literal fulfillment of All Might's symbolic assignment. The Sports Festival makes existence itself socially visible and nameable.

## Voice updates

### Midoriya
- can make direct competitive declarations under meaningful stakes;
- remains prone to anxious self-discount after success;
- internal language under competition is increasingly strategic rather than purely fearful.

### Bakugo
- public speech is deliberately provocative and self-binding;
- competitive contempt remains broad, but not every challenger receives Midoriya-level emotional fixation.

### Todoroki
- concise, controlled, comparative assertions;
- minimal interest in explaining himself;
- father-directed hostility appears privately.

### Uraraka
- embarrassment when discussing money contrasts with forceful determination when explaining family motive;
- warmth coexists with hard practical ambition.

### Shigaraki
- post-failure speech is fragmented, grievance-heavy, accusatory, and still game-adjacent;
- the teacher supplies calmer long-range discourse.

# 9. Visual and formal analysis

## 9.1 Mirrored successor compositions

The strongest formal relationship in the volume is not a single repeated panel but a repeated **mentor/successor scene structure**.

Shigaraki lies wounded beneath/within the authority of an unseen older voice and is given a future symbolic mission. Midoriya sits beneath the overwhelming visual presence of All Might and is told to succeed him. In both cases, the younger figure is visually smaller than the role being projected onto him.

The compositions make succession look burdensome before either successor can fully embody it.

## 9.2 The stadium as machine of visibility

The Sports Festival repeatedly frames students as tiny bodies beneath stands full of spectators, screens, commentary, and institutional signage. The visual field says that they are not simply fighting one another; they are being **observed into professional identity**.

## 9.3 Bottleneck → singular emergence

At the obstacle-race start, Midoriya is physically compressed among other bodies. Todoroki weaponizes that congestion. By the end, Midoriya receives a full-page singularizing announcement.

This is an elegant visual metaphor for institutional sorting: many candidates enter together; a few names emerge.

## 9.4 Bakugo on the pledge platform

Bakugo is visually isolated above the student mass when he declares first place. The composition turns arrogance into staged spectacle. His self-concept is not private; he performs it against a crowd that immediately pushes back.

## 9.5 Todoroki and paternal gaze

His `クソ親父が見てるんだから` thought is placed alongside decisive use of ice. The father's gaze is not shown as comforting recognition. It darkens what would otherwise be a straightforward display of excellence.

## 9.6 Midoriya's robot plate

The plate is visually important because he repeatedly carries an object others have discarded. It becomes shield, sled, mine-collection tool, and launch surface. The motif is consistent with his broader analytical habit: value is not identical to an object's intended function.

## 9.7 First place becomes target

The ranking page and ten-million-point reveal immediately reverse the visual meaning of `1`. A huge first-place marker initially signifies success; the next event converts it into a target painted on Midoriya.

Recognition and vulnerability are visually joined.

# 10. Body, embodiment, family, and inheritance

## 10.1 Midoriya's body still cannot embody the inherited ideal normally

All Might wants national symbolic emergence while Midoriya's ordinary physical tests remain unimpressive and full OFA use remains dangerous. The succession project is therefore ahead of the body.

That mismatch matters. Midoriya is being socially asked to become visible before he is physically ready to perform the role in the obvious All Might-like way.

His obstacle-race solution is consequently significant: he produces symbolic visibility with the body and cognition he actually has.

## 10.2 All Might's finite body drives succession urgency

The mentor's expectation is not abstract. His activity limit has fallen to roughly fifty minutes. Succession pressure is produced by bodily decline.

## 10.3 Todoroki introduces biological/familial hero inheritance

Midoriya's inheritance is chosen. Todoroki's hero identity is already publicly attached to biological parentage: he is known as Endeavor's son, and his father's gaze matters during competition.

The exact family project is not yet available, but V03 already sets up a major contrast:

> chosen inheritance from a beloved mentor versus family inheritance experienced under hostility.

## 10.4 Uraraka's family turns hero labor into care

Her desired future body at work is imagined as a means of making her parents' lives easier. Family inheritance here is not power but economic obligation and gratitude.

## 10.5 Shigaraki's development is relational without biological context

At this boundary, no biological family history is available. The meaningful relation supplied by the text is mentor/student. Preserve that limit.

# 11. Continuity, callback, and payoff ledger

## 11.1 V01 `人は生まれながらに平等ではない` → V03 public sorting

The opening inequality proposition is now institutionalized through a national showcase that visibly ranks uneven bodies, talents, and opportunities.

The manga does not pretend merit competition abolishes unequal starting conditions. It makes difference legible and allocates opportunity through performance.

## 11.2 V01 `君はヒーローになれる` → V03 `君が来た！`

All Might's recognition has changed scale.

V01: one adult tells one child that he can become a hero.

V03: the mentor tells the child to make an entire society recognize his arrival.

Private recognition becomes public succession.

## 11.3 V02 `僕の力` → V03 winning without OFA

Midoriya wanted to make inherited power his own. The first major national-stage result after that declaration comes through his preexisting analytic competence rather than raw OFA.

This strengthens the interpretation that self-authorship cannot mean being reduced to inheritance.

## 11.4 V02 Bakugo `こっからだ!!` → V03 generalized competition

The recommitment after defeat immediately pays forward. Bakugo now ignores lesser provocations in favor of reaching the top, declares first place publicly, and treats Todoroki/Midoriya as real competitive threats.

## 11.5 V02 All Might social dependency → V03 succession urgency

The system's dependence on All Might now produces an explicit need for a next symbol.

## 11.6 V02 Shigaraki `コンティニューだ` → V03 mentor-backed continuation

The “continue” framing becomes organizational reality. USJ failure does not end the villain project; the teacher converts it into recruitment, patience, and public-symbol strategy.

## 11.7 V01/V02 noncombat competence → V03 support/business routes

The early insistence that hero value exceeds combat expands into whole educational tracks whose contribution lies in equipment and management rather than frontline fighting.

# 13. Prospective versus retrospective reading

## Prospective V03 reading

A reader at Volume 3 can securely know:

- Shigaraki has an unseen `先生` who guides him after failure.
- That teacher wants Shigaraki to become a public symbol of fear.
- Police and All Might interpret Shigaraki as developmentally immature but capable of growth.
- All Might fears what a capable mentor could do with such a person.
- All Might explicitly wants Midoriya to inherit him as next Symbol.
- Midoriya is asked to reveal his arrival to the nation.
- Todoroki is Endeavor's son and privately calls him `クソ親父`.
- the Sports Festival is a public recruitment and ranking mechanism.
- Uraraka's motive includes financially supporting her parents.
- Shinso treats the festival as a possible course-transfer route.

A prospective reader **cannot yet securely know**:

- the unseen teacher's complete identity/history;
- Shigaraki/Tenko's childhood origin;
- the exact Todoroki family project or abuse history;
- the complete mechanism/history of OFA/AFO opposition;
- how Sports Festival relationships will develop in later rounds.

## Retrospective full-series reading

With hindsight, V03 becomes far more architecturally important than a transitional early volume.

The paired symbol commands anticipate the entire OFA/AFO inheritance conflict. Later material makes clear that both older figures are engaged in successor projects, but V03 already supplies the shared grammar: constrained predecessor → developing successor → public symbol.

Todoroki's father-directed hostility is a compressed seed for the series' larger critique of treating children as family projects and powers as inherited instruments.

The police discussion of Quirk counseling and child/adult development anticipates the series' eventual insistence that intervention must occur before crisis hardens into villain identity.

But hindsight should **strengthen these connections, not rewrite V03 as though all later causes were already explicit**.

# 14. What the first pass missed or underweighted

The first pass's early Volumes 1–3 synthesis correctly identified heroism-before-power, Midoriya's analytical intelligence, Bakugo's status orientation, All Might's burden, and the emerging professional world. The V03 close read substantially expands that model.

## 14.1 The paired successor/symbol architecture was underweighted

This is the largest correction.

V03 contains explicit mirror instructions:

- villain mentor: become the symbol we need; make your fear known to the world;
- All Might: inherit me; make the world know you have arrived.

This should become a load-bearing series-level finding.

## 14.2 Shigaraki is already analyzed as a developmental subject

The first pass emphasized later villain origins. V03 deserves more credit for establishing much earlier that dangerous power, socialization, counseling, growth, and mentorship are causally important.

## 14.3 The Sports Festival is labor-market infrastructure immediately, not only later thematic color

The source explicitly ties it to scouting, sidekick routes, limited career opportunities, course mobility, company appeal, and business strategy.

## 14.4 Uraraka's economic motive deserves much more weight

Money is not an incidental joke. It is the first major grounding of her hero aspiration and connects care to labor/class reality.

## 14.5 Midoriya is more competitively agentic than “rescue-first” can imply

He deliberately tells Todoroki he will seriously go for first place. Rescue-first must not become competition-denying.

## 14.6 Midoriya's first national-stage victory is achieved without OFA

This materially matters to the inheritance/self-authorship thesis.

## 14.7 Todoroki's father problem is already textually visible in V03

Detailed hindsight must be withheld, but `クソ親父` and public identification as Endeavor's son mean the family conflict is not merely later backfill.

## 14.8 Bakugo's post-defeat development begins immediately

His V02 `こっからだ` is already changing how he handles social challengers and public competition.

# 16. Revised interpretation

Earlier early-series formulation:

> Midoriya is a rescue-first moral idealist whose worthiness precedes power and who must learn to control inherited strength.

After Volume 3:

> **Midoriya is becoming a publicly legible successor inside a social system that converts heroic capacity into symbol, reputation, labor-market value, and competition. His task is not only to control One For All, but to inherit without disappearing into inheritance: to become visible as Midoriya rather than merely a smaller All Might.**

And the broader series model now needs a second axis:

> **Hero Society and villain organization are both reproductive systems. They do not only use existing people; they train, narrate, recruit, and symbolically reproduce themselves through younger successors.**

This is a major expansion of the V01–V02 framework.

## New predictive constraints

### Midoriya
Do not predict that he will avoid competition because he is altruistic. Ask instead whether winning is connected to rescue, professional growth, duty, recognition, or a person he values. If yes, he can become intensely competitive without becoming dominance-oriented.

Do not automatically model success as increasing confidence. He may reinterpret successful adaptive judgment as luck and move the standard upward immediately.

### Bakugo

### Uraraka

### Todoroki
At V03 chronology, father-related hostility is known but motive detail is not. Simulations frozen here must not import later family revelations.

### Shigaraki
A trusted mentor can regulate and redirect him after failure. His dysregulation should not be modeled as making him incapable of learning; it makes the quality of guidance especially consequential.

### All Might
He can impose enormous expectations through sincere belief in another person's potential. The expectation should not be modeled as manipulative ownership, but neither should its psychological weight be erased.

# 21. What this volume changes in the current MHA model

Volume 3 changes the project model in five major ways.

## 21.1 Inheritance is now explicitly symbolic, not merely biological or Quirk-based

All Might wants Midoriya to inherit **him**. Shigaraki's teacher wants Shigaraki to become a symbol of fear. The series is already asking how identities and social functions reproduce across generations.

## 21.2 Hero Society is now visibly a reputation market

The professional system does not merely license people after training. It watches adolescents, ranks them, recruits them, brands them, and converts visibility into career opportunity.

## 21.3 Villain formation is now a pedagogical problem

Shigaraki is dangerous now, but he is also still developing. The manga explicitly recognizes that guidance can change the trajectory of a powerful immature person. The presence of a destructive mentor makes this a structural, not merely psychological, problem.

## 21.4 Midoriya's moral idealism includes genuine competitive ambition

The rescue-first model must not flatten him into someone who does not care about winning. He can pursue first place deliberately when victory serves a meaningful obligation. What distinguishes him from Bakugo is not absence of ambition but **the role ambition occupies in the value hierarchy**.

## 21.5 Public recognition is both emancipation and danger

The Sports Festival can reveal a student whom prior metrics underread. It can open careers and second chances. It can also turn children into brands, expose them to national judgment, intensify ranking, and make first place into the most aggressively targeted position.

The resulting current series model is:

> **MHA is not only about what makes a person heroic or how power should be used. It is increasingly about the social machinery that teaches people what power means, chooses who becomes visible, converts visibility into opportunity, and reproduces rival moral orders through successors.**

At the end of Volume 3, Midoriya has fulfilled All Might's request in an unexpectedly revealing way. The world begins to notice him — not because he looks like All Might, but because under pressure he behaves unmistakably like Midoriya Izuku.
