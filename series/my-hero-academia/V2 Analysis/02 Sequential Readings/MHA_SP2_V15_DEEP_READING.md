## 2. Governing transition

Volume 14 moved the series from distributed professional trust toward **earned legitimacy / reciprocal mastery**. Midoriya could no longer rest on All Might's designation; Mirio made the alternative-successor question serious; Bakugo and Midoriya began exchanging usable developmental information; and mastery was shown as something built through difficult bodily integration rather than conferred by a powerful Quirk or prestigious mentor.

Volume 15 changes the problem. The principal actors now possess **too little certainty rather than too little power**.

Midoriya knows a child is frightened but does not know enough to remove her without risking the investigation. Mirio correctly reads the immediate danger but must choose whether to preserve a longer rescue path after the child returns. Nighteye has more information about the future than anyone else and has learned that this can make action ethically harder rather than easier. Police and heroes possess fragments—an unauthorized drug, a strange projectile, Hassaikai affiliates, blood/cell evidence, a child's existence—but must decide when those fragments justify a coordinated raid. Kirishima cannot know in advance whether his new hardening will hold; he has to become reliable through a commitment enacted under threat.

The governing transition is therefore:

> **earned legitimacy / reciprocal mastery → responsible action under uncertainty / contested futurity**

Two related claims organize the volume.

First, **professional judgment is not the elimination of uncertainty**. A hero sometimes has to delay, investigate, coordinate, or accept limited action precisely because certainty is unavailable. But delay does not become morally clean simply because it is procedurally defensible. Midoriya and Mirio remain wounded by Eri's return because professional restraint can be correct and still leave a person suffering.

Second, **future knowledge does not cancel agency**. Nighteye's Foresight is the extreme case: prediction can become so authoritative that it tempts people to treat the future as already completed. Midoriya, All Might, and eventually the operational logic of the volume refuse that surrender. They cannot prove the future can be changed; they choose to act as though responsible effort still matters.

A compact Volume 15 formulation is:

> **Professional heroism is not certainty before action. It is the disciplined conversion of incomplete knowledge into accountable action while refusing to let either guilt or prediction erase the possibility of a different future.**

The first fifteen volumes can now be described compactly as:

`recognition → judgment → public legibility → self-authorship → recognition without possession → enacted identity → aftermath → diagnostic resistance → emergency enactment / cost-bearing → contested legibility / protective authority → accountable succession / de-centered support → operational self-authorship / reliable coordination → distributed professional trust / integrated responsibility → earned legitimacy / reciprocal mastery → responsible action under uncertainty / contested futurity`

## 3. Eri: recognition succeeds before rescue does

### 3.1 The street scene is not a simple case of heroes ignoring obvious abuse

The historical combined V14–V15 reading was directionally right that Eri forces Midoriya's rescue ethic into a harder professional problem, but the exact mechanics matter.

Chisaki immediately offers a socially legible explanation. He presents Eri as his daughter and her distress as a private parenting matter. When Mirio notes that the child is frightened, Chisaki says he has just scolded her. He then invokes a boundary familiar to ordinary social life: do not impose one's own normality onto another family (`V15:p009-014`).

That framing is powerful because hero institutions cannot simply treat every uncomfortable domestic interaction as grounds for violent extraction. At this point Midoriya and Mirio do not possess the later evidence about blood, cells, drugs, or the Hassaikai laboratory.

Yet the manga does not let procedural ambiguity erase embodied evidence. Eri continues gripping Midoriya. At `V15:p015`, Midoriya makes the moral recognition explicit:

> `ヒーローが怯えた子どもを やり過ごすわけがない`

He asks Chisaki directly what he is doing to her. Mirio joins him with `俺たちはヒーローだ`.

The important revision is therefore:

`insufficient legal/investigative knowledge ≠ failure to recognize distress`.

Both students recognize that something is wrong. The problem is what action remains justified and effective once Chisaki controls the information and Eri herself becomes the point at which coercion is exercised.

### 3.2 Chisaki weaponizes anticipated violence rather than physically dragging Eri away

The crucial turn is Eri's behavior. Chisaki does not need to seize her in front of the heroes. His body language and threat state make the danger legible to her; she releases Midoriya and returns.

Mirio identifies the mechanism after the pair leaves. At `V15:p019`, he stops Midoriya from following and explains that Chisaki displayed killing intent and used it to draw Eri back. Her compliance is therefore not evidence of safety or preference. It is **coercion mediated through her anticipation of what Chisaki might do to other people**.

This matters for Eri's character model. Even with extremely limited autonomy, her response is socially oriented: she can choose self-return when she believes resistance endangers others. That should not be inflated into mature altruistic doctrine; the evidence is a traumatized child's learned danger response. But it is already more informative than treating her as a passive package transferred between adults.

### 3.3 Professional restraint leaves moral residue

Mirio says `追わないよ`. The logic is not indifference. Pushing deeper now risks alerting the target and making Eri harder to locate later. Midoriya reaches the same investigative conclusion: deeper intrusion could make the Hassaikai more difficult to track, so they should report to Nighteye (`V15:p019-020`).

This is the first clean series case where Midoriya's immediate recognition of someone who needs help does **not** produce immediate physical rescue.

The result is not that his earlier reflex has been disproven. The source validates his perception. Eri really is in danger. The complication is temporal and institutional:

`recognize now → cannot safely complete rescue now → preserve a path to later rescue`.

That middle term is ethically costly. Volume 15 refuses the convenient fiction that a correct professional decision feels correct from inside the person making it.

## 4. Chisaki and Eri: private authority becomes industrial possession

### 4.1 The family claim collapses almost immediately for the reader

Once Eri is back inside the Hassaikai environment, Chisaki's parental framing gives way to ownership language and laboratory imagery. At `V15:p022`, he tells her:

> `おまえは 計画の“核”なんだ`

The room is full of medical/industrial equipment. At `V15:p023`, he asks her not to make him dirty his hands again.

The contrast is deliberate. Publicly, Chisaki invokes household privacy and paternal difficulty. Privately, Eri is a core component of a plan. Domestic legitimacy is being used as a shield for instrumental control.

This is stronger than the V14 evidence, where Chisaki was already hierarchical and coercive but Eri's place in the system was still unknown.

### 4.2 The Quirk-destroying projectile turns personhood into supply chain

The volume gradually assembles the evidence rather than explaining the entire scheme at once. Fat Gum's team encounters a projectile that interferes with Amajiki's Quirk. Police later connect the Hassaikai to an unapproved drug network. At the multi-agency meeting, the recovered material is found to contain `人の血ィや細胞` (`V15:p136`).

The conclusion is horrifying because it converts the earlier laboratory image into an economic/organizational model. At `V15:p141`, Fat Gum states the implication bluntly:

> `つまり 娘の身体を 銃弾にして 捌いてんじゃね？って事だ`

The source preserves an important epistemic boundary. Nighteye says they do **not** yet know whether completed rounds are actually being sold. The current effect is incomplete/temporary. The possibility of a finished product that completely destroys a Quirk is still an inference from prototype trajectory, not an already demonstrated fact (`V15:p141`).

The ethical fact, however, is already established: Eri's body is being treated as raw material for an organizational technology.

### 4.3 Overhaul is anti-relational power

Earlier antagonists often seek recognition, ideological purification, revenge, liberation, belonging, or domination. Chisaki's V15 form is colder. He repeatedly reduces persons and powers to controlled inputs:

- Eri is `計画の“核”`;
- her blood/cells become ammunition;
- Hassaikai affiliates become distribution/investigative nodes;
- League cooperation is negotiated as organizational utility;
- his own Quirk decomposes and reassembles matter/persons through touch.

This makes the hand motif structurally important. Midoriya's hand is what Eri later remembers as gentle. Chisaki's hand is the medium of disassembly, threat and imposed order. The opposition is not merely “good touch / bad touch”; it is **relationship versus possession**. Midoriya's touch recognizes another person as someone to be protected. Chisaki's system treats the body as something whose components can be reassigned to his plan.

## 5. Failed rescue becomes a relationship, not only a guilt state

One of V15's most important corrections to the first pass is that the alley scene does not end when Eri returns.

Later, inside the Hassaikai base, Eri remembers Midoriya's hand. The page isolates hand against hand and then her crying face; the governing word is `優しかった` (`V15:p163`). Her thought contrasts that touch with what she has previously known.

This means the failed immediate rescue has two simultaneous consequences:

1. **for Midoriya/Mirio:** guilt, responsibility, and a future rescue commitment;
2. **for Eri:** evidence that a different kind of human contact exists.

The relationship exists before either side can secure the other. Midoriya has not saved Eri physically, but his behavior has altered her comparative model of how another person can touch/respond to her.

This is a recurring MHA mechanism at a more painful stage of development: recognition can precede rescue, and recognition can matter even when rescue initially fails.

The distinction protects the reading from two errors. It would be wrong to romanticize the failure as “good enough”; Eri remains captive and exploited. It would also be wrong to treat the alley encounter as analytically null because she was not extracted. The encounter creates relational evidence that later action can build upon.

## 6. Midoriya: from rescue reflex to burdened professional judgment

### 6.1 He does not lose his rescue-first perception

Midoriya's opening behavior confirms continuity. He notices Eri's fear, refuses to dismiss her, and directly challenges Chisaki despite being a first-day intern (`V15:p013-015`). His recognition system remains extremely sensitive to embodied distress.

The growth lies elsewhere: he can now accept a situation in which continuing to pursue the target may reduce the eventual chance of rescue. This is painful precisely because the source does not let him convert “professionalism” into emotional insulation.

### 6.2 Guilt does not become permission for reckless unilateral action

Once the larger scheme is explained, Midoriya and Mirio are visibly devastated. Other adults could easily turn them into scapegoats for having let Eri return. Nighteye explicitly refuses that move: he assumes responsibility for the operation and asks that the two students not be blamed for what they did not yet know (`V15:p142`).

Midoriya nevertheless does not answer guilt by abandoning the institution. He and Mirio declare:

> `今度こそ必ず エリちゃんを…!! 保護する!!` (`V15:p143`)

The noun matters. `保護` places the goal inside a protective/legal/professional operation, not merely a private revenge-rescue mission.

### 6.3 Confidentiality becomes a new stress regime

By Chapter 137, Midoriya is carrying two heavy burdens he cannot freely discuss: Eri and All Might's predicted death. He says the internship information is under nondisclosure and he cannot simply talk to Kirishima or the others; the combination leaves his heart at its limit (`V15:p169`).

This is useful modeling evidence because Midoriya's earlier stress responses often externalized as movement, analysis, muttering, or self-sacrificial action. Here he is constrained from action and disclosure. His appetite and ordinary social functioning deteriorate enough for Iida to notice.

The scene therefore adds a new Midoriya condition:

`high responsibility + confidentiality + inability to act immediately → visible withdrawal / somatic-social strain`.

That is materially different from his combat crisis profile.

## 7. Iida and the reciprocity of friendship

Iida's response at `V15:p169-171` is a small scene with unusually high longitudinal value.

He notices Midoriya is not eating and that his expression has remained troubled since the internship began. He does not demand classified information. He offers a boundary-respecting fallback:

> `本当にどうしようもなくなったら 言ってくれ 友だちだろ` (`V15:p170`)

Midoriya immediately recalls the earlier words he himself had offered Iida during the Hosu crisis. He cannot explain the current problem, but the care breaks through his isolation and he cries while thanking him (`V15:p171`).

This is a clean payoff to Iida's V06–V10 repair trajectory. He has learned from being the person who concealed a private burden until it became dangerous. Now he recognizes a similar closed-off state in a friend and intervenes without taking control of that friend's agency.

The relationship has become reciprocal rather than one-directional:

`Midoriya reaches Iida under concealed crisis → Iida later reaches Midoriya under concealed crisis`.

Todoroki's deadpan observation that heroes cry too (`ヒーローも泣く時ゃ泣くだろ…多分`, `V15:p172`) keeps the scene from becoming sentimental abstraction. Ordinary peer presence is itself part of the support system.

## 8. All Might's predicted death: knowledge can become fatalism

### 8.1 Nighteye's prediction externalizes an endpoint All Might had already begun to accept

Midoriya learns that Nighteye used Foresight on All Might and saw a future in which All Might dies. All Might explains that the predicted time was roughly six or seven years ahead, with greater timing error at long distance, and that Nighteye had never observed a predicted scene successfully changed (`V15:p047-050`).

This turns mortality from an abstract fact into an apparently pre-authored event.

The revelation also clarifies All Might's prior psychology. At `V15:p052`, he admits that after hearing the prediction he accepted it relatively smoothly. If an end had been shown to him, he decided to run until that endpoint. At Kamino he even thought he had reached that goal.

That is an important retrospective revision. All Might's martyr tendency was not only generic self-sacrifice or Symbol-of-Peace ideology. For years, it was reinforced by a specific epistemic structure: **he believed death was already waiting at a fixed point on his path**.

### 8.2 Midoriya refuses the completed-future frame

Midoriya's reaction is immediate and relational rather than metaphysical. He asks whether Nighteye's prediction cannot be changed, invokes the promise from the Sports Festival, and tells All Might:

> `「僕が来た」って言うところ 生きて見ててよ オールマイト!` (`V15:p051`)

The line is structurally important. Earlier succession threatened to make All Might's disappearance the condition for Midoriya's emergence. Midoriya instead imagines a future in which the predecessor survives long enough to witness the successor publicly become himself.

Succession is therefore redefined as **coexistence across generations**, not ritual replacement through death.

### 8.3 All Might's desire to live becomes explicit

All Might's answer is one of the strongest V15 model changes. He says Midoriya's existence changed him: watching the boy run toward strength made him want to stand beside him. The page then recalls Nana Shimura's declaration:

> `運命など この腕で 好きな形にねじ曲げてやるさ!!` (`V15:p054`)

Midoriya answers:

> `僕も 一緒にねじ曲げます` (`V15:p055`)

This strongly extends V13's `私は死なないぞ`. V13 established a policy-level refusal to die. V15 provides the underlying causal and relational transformation: All Might had once accommodated himself to a predicted ending, but Midoriya and Inko's demands have made continued life itself an ethical commitment.

The strongest claim is not “fate can definitely be changed.” The source refuses to prove that here. The claim is:

> **Even when a future is presented as highly authoritative, responsible agents may refuse to treat that prediction as a reason to stop trying to alter it.**

## 9. Nighteye: Foresight is a burdened professional instrument

### 9.1 More information does not mean unrestricted use

Chapter 136 finally supplies the operational limitations V14 left open. Once Foresight is activated, Nighteye needs a 24-hour interval before using it again, effectively limiting him to one target per day. He describes the future as film-like frames shown from near the target's perspective, with only the person's behavior and limited surrounding environment visible (`V15:p149`).

This sharply reduces any temptation to model him as omniscient.

### 9.2 He treats Foresight as a finishing tool, not a substitute for investigation

At `V15:p151`, Nighteye explains his doctrine:

> `この“個性”は 行動の成功率を最大まで引き上げた後に 勝利のダメ押しとして使うものです`

He adds that it should not be used blindly while many variables remain uncertain.

This is a significant power-philosophy statement. The strongest information advantage in the volume is deliberately subordinated to ordinary evidence, planning, teamwork and judgment. Foresight is not permission to skip causality.

### 9.3 The refusal to predict death is not cowardice reducible to sentiment

Fat Gum challenges Nighteye: if death is information, shouldn't they use it to plan around the death? He even volunteers himself. Nighteye replies that Foresight is not fortune-telling and there is no guarantee the outcome can be avoided. His answer to the offer is simply `ダメだ` (`V15:p150-151`).

The emotional history with All Might obviously matters. But the policy is also epistemically coherent. A prediction that cannot be proven reversible can impose psychologically and strategically distorting certainty on the people who receive it. Nighteye therefore treats certain information as **dangerous to acquire before it is operationally necessary**.

That gives him a more complex relationship to truth than “always maximize information.” His actual heuristic is closer to:

`increase ordinary success probability first → use Foresight when the information can close a decision rather than paralyze one`.

## 10. Past, future, and the volume's theory of agency

Near the operation's start, the text articulates its temporal thesis directly. At `V15:p181`, the heroes acknowledge that whether the future can be changed is unknown, while the past cannot be changed. What can change is how the past is interpreted and what one does because of it.

That formulation links several seemingly separate storylines:

- Midoriya and Mirio cannot undo handing Eri back, but can make that failure the basis of a more informed rescue attempt;
- All Might cannot undo years of bodily damage or the prophecy already seen, but can reinterpret survival as a duty rather than an indulgence;
- Iida cannot undo Hosu, but can make that history into a resource for recognizing another friend's concealed distress;
- Kirishima cannot know that his hardening will hold, but can make the person he wants to be operational through repeated commitment;
- the task force cannot obtain perfect certainty before acting, but can accumulate enough evidence to cross an action threshold.

Volume 15's anti-fatalism is therefore not magical optimism. It is an **action theory**:

> the future is not guaranteed to be alterable, but treating agency as already null is itself an avoidable failure of responsibility.

## 11. Kirishima: courage becomes a professional construction

### 11.1 V15 supports the constructed-courage thesis, but not yet the full historical backstory

The first-pass combined V14–V15 artifact was broadly right that Kirishima offers a different model of heroism from Midoriya. But it overran the source boundary by importing the later detailed freeze/Crimson Riot history.

V15 itself already supports a narrower and strong claim: **Kirishima consciously constructs reliability through a chosen courage vocabulary and bodily technique**.

During Fat Gum's street work, an assailant threatens civilians and uses an enhancement drug. Kirishima pursues because allowing the offender to escape would endanger others. When his ordinary Hardening proves insufficient, he does not discover that he is secretly invulnerable. He raises the commitment level of an already trained capacity.

### 11.2 `安無嶺過武瑠` is a self-authored operational identity

The internal sequence around `V15:p100-103` is explicit:

- `倒れねーってのは クソ強ェだろ`
- `上げる もっとだ!! 身も!! 心も!! 硬く!!`
- `絶対倒れぬ壁となれ!!`
- `烈怒頼雄斗 安無嶺過武瑠!!`

The stylized kanji reading of “Red Riot Unbreakable” matters because Kirishima is not merely reporting a physical condition. He is **naming a rule for how he intends to behave**.

His strength is therefore not best modeled as fearlessness. The source gives us a self-command: become the wall that does not fall when other people need the route blocked.

### 11.3 Fat Gum converts performance into recognized professional identity

Fat Gum's mentorship matters because Kirishima does not validate himself alone. The pro arrives, contains the situation, and tells him that the people were helped and that he will become a great hero (`V15:p105-115`). Classmates later react to his publicized work (`V15:p117`).

This is the same legitimacy problem V14 raised, now seen from a different character. Kirishima's chosen self-image becomes professionally meaningful because it survives a real public-risk context and is recognized by mentor, civilians and peers.

The detailed causal origin of his courage language remains OPEN until a later volume supplies it directly.

## 12. Fat Gum: mentorship as compression and confidence transfer

V15 gives Fat Gum enough evidence to establish a bounded professional model.

He is socially loose, physically comic in presentation, and quick to praise, but his field behavior is structurally serious. He understands the criminal's threat, uses `脂肪吸着` to contain attacks, monitors his interns, distinguishes temporary Quirk interference from wider threat implications, and carries Kirishima's successful action back into a professional-development frame.

His mentoring style is notably different from Nighteye's adversarial evaluation or Aizawa's diagnostic pressure. Fat Gum **compresses complex professional judgment into confidence his student can use immediately**. He tells Kirishima what worked, why it mattered, and what that says about the hero he can become.

This does not make him permissive. He remains the adult who finishes containment and participates in the later multi-agency investigation. Warmth and rigor are not opposites in his professional style.

## 13. Mirio: the cost of correct restraint

V14 introduced Mirio as an apparently complete candidate whose mastery makes Nighteye's preference rational. V15 supplies the first serious crack in that surface—not incompetence, but **moral injury from a defensible professional decision**.

Mirio reads Chisaki's threat to Eri faster than Midoriya does and stops the pursuit because pressing the encounter could destroy the larger investigation. Later, once Eri's exploitation becomes clearer, he does not protect himself with “we followed procedure.” At `V15:p142`, his internal reaction attacks his own heroic identity: what kind of “best hero” or “Lemillion” fails to save the child in front of him?

This is important character evidence because it shows that Mirio's optimism does not depend on shallow emotional insulation. He can experience intense guilt while remaining functional inside the team.

By `V15:p143` and `p181`, the response is prospective: `今度こそ必ず`. The mistake/failure is neither denied nor allowed to define the future as already lost.

That breadth is enough to move Mirio beyond a strong introductory baseline into a genuinely moderate reconstruction state.

## 14. Aizawa: supervision instead of exclusion

Aizawa's V15 role is compact but consistent with his increasingly precise governance model.

Once the work-study students become entangled in a dangerous Hassaikai operation, his response is not “students must never be exposed to risk” and not “licensed students can do whatever they want.” He inserts himself as an adult supervisory condition. Around `V15:p155-158`, he tells Midoriya that he will watch him and that the student must conduct legitimate professional activity rather than repeating unsanctioned action.

This extends the earlier distinction between recklessness and meaningful risk. Aizawa's preferred correction mechanism is **structured participation under accountability**, not automatic removal from the field.

It also matters that he is present in the raid coalition. U.A.'s responsibility for students does not end at the school gate once those students enter professional systems.

## 15. Ryukyu, Nejire, Uraraka and Tsuyu: field professionalism broadens

The Ryukyu-team segment gives useful, if comparatively brief, evidence for the female students and pro-hero ecosystem.

Ryukyu is introduced as the No.9-ranked hero and operates in direct field response with Nejire, Uraraka and Tsuyu. Their work is not framed as a side lesson separate from “real” hero work; it is part of the same distributed professional network that later converges on the Hassaikai case.

For Uraraka and Tsuyu, the important addition is **licensed/internship field behavior under an unfamiliar professional authority**. Both remain recognizably themselves, but their rescue/combat capacities are being embedded in a wider agency team rather than Class 1-A-only coordination.

Nejire's V14 social curiosity now gains the beginning of a professional-action layer. Ryukyu enters as a bounded pro-hero baseline whose leadership style can be expanded later.

These scenes are not individually sufficient for large readiness promotions, but they prevent character models from being overfit to school exercises.

## 16. Amajiki: vulnerability does not erase professional usefulness

Amajiki's V14 introduction emphasized severe audience/social discomfort. V15 places him in field work under Fat Gum and exposes him to the Quirk-disabling projectile.

The result gives two useful modeling corrections.

First, his anxiety is not equivalent to non-function. He is sufficiently competent to operate in real patrol work and to be part of the later Hassaikai response network.

Second, temporary loss/interference with his Quirk creates a new vulnerability regime. The projectile turns a character whose professional identity depends heavily on a highly technical Quirk into direct evidence that heroic systems must plan for **capacity removal**, not merely capacity overload.

Amajiki remains too sparsely observed for a strong reconstruction, but V15 materially broadens him beyond introduction-stage social anxiety.

## 17. The hero system: evidence thresholds, interoperability and lawful force

### 17.1 The task force is the institutional answer to the Eri alley problem

The eventual raid is not a repudiation of the earlier restraint. It is what that restraint was trying to make possible.

Police and heroes gather multiple independent pieces of information:

- Hassaikai organizational structure;
- suspicious affiliate activity;
- the Quirk-disabling projectile;
- unauthorized drug evidence;
- blood/cell contents;
- Eri's connection to Chisaki;
- prospective evidence from carefully timed Foresight use;
- location information sufficient to justify coordinated action.

The moral urgency existed in Chapter 129. The institution's job is to turn urgency into **action that can actually reach the target without simply driving the target deeper underground**.

### 17.2 Inter-agency work is conflictual rather than frictionless

The meeting includes skepticism about student involvement, disagreements about evidentiary sufficiency, different risk tolerances and blunt criticism. That friction is analytically useful. Distributed professional trust does not mean everyone agrees or likes one another. It means disagreement can be processed into a shared operation.

Nighteye assumes responsibility for the students rather than sacrificing them to preserve his own authority (`V15:p142`). Fat Gum contributes field evidence. Aizawa provides Quirk-control and student supervision. Ryukyu's agency, police and other pros contribute personnel and jurisdictional capacity.

The `群` answer from V13 is becoming concrete bureaucracy.

### 17.3 Confidentiality is part of professional cost

The nondisclosure rule that strains Midoriya (`V15:p169`) is not merely narrative convenience. Investigations require information boundaries. The cost is that ordinary friendship cannot always be used as a full emotional processing channel.

The series therefore complicates the idea that “talking to friends” always solves isolation. Mature professional responsibility may require not telling them. The healthier alternative is not breaking confidentiality automatically; it is preserving relationships in which care can be offered **without demanding protected content**. Iida does exactly that.

## 18. Power philosophy: the volume repeatedly limits power

V15 contains several distinct power-limitation models:

- **Midoriya/Mirio:** moral recognition does not grant unlimited intervention authority;
- **Kirishima:** hardening has to be trained and can be exceeded;
- **Amajiki:** a Quirk can be temporarily removed by hostile technology;
- **Chisaki:** a Quirk can be industrialized into coercive biotechnology;
- **Nighteye:** extraordinary information is rate-limited, perspective-limited and ethically constrained;
- **All Might:** symbolic power is gone and the future cannot be defeated by force alone.

The common principle is that power becomes ethically meaningful through the constraints governing its use.

This makes the Quirk-destroying drug especially significant. It attacks the social assumption that Quirks are stable personal capacities. In a hero society where vocation, identity and institutional classification are built around Quirks, a technology that can remove them is not merely another weapon. It threatens the continuity between body, identity and social role.

V15 does not yet establish the full future consequences of that technology. It does establish the conceptual threat.

## 19. Japanese-language evidence

Several V15 terms and constructions deserve longitudinal preservation.

### `怯えた子どもを やり過ごす`

Midoriya's `ヒーローが怯えた子どもを やり過ごすわけがない` (`V15:p015`) is stronger than generic “helping.” `やり過ごす` frames the unacceptable act as letting the visible situation pass by—continuing onward while a frightened child remains. It is a recognition verb as much as a rescue statement.

### `計画の“核”`

Chisaki's label for Eri (`V15:p022`) reduces a person to the core of a project. `核` makes her structurally central while erasing her agency.

### `“個性”を壊すクスリ`

The briefing phrase (`V15:p132`) is deliberately blunt: a “drug that destroys Quirks.” The vocabulary frames the threat as destruction of a constitutive capacity, not ordinary suppression.

### `保護`

Midoriya and Mirio's `今度こそ必ず エリちゃんを…!! 保護する!!` (`V15:p143`) is institutionally important. `保護` means protection/custody rather than simply “win her back.” Their guilt is translated into a lawful protective objective.

### `予知` and `運命`

Nighteye's `予知` is empirical/technical within the setting; `運命` is the interpretive category characters place around it. The manga does not equate them automatically. A prediction can be accurate without proving a metaphysical doctrine that agency is meaningless.

### `ねじ曲げる`

Nana's and Midoriya's repeated `ねじ曲げる` (`V15:p054-055`) is physical language applied to fate: twist/bend it into another shape. It preserves MHA's tendency to make abstract ethics bodily and kinetic.

### `安無嶺過武瑠`

Kirishima's stylized phonetic kanji for “Unbreakable” turns a foreign hero-name concept into an aggressively self-authored visual inscription. It functions as identity performance and operational command simultaneously.

## 20. Visual-form evidence

### 20.1 Hands: control, recognition, memory

The strongest V15 visual motif is the hand.

Eri grips Midoriya rather than merely standing near him. Chisaki's Quirk and threat are inseparable from touch. The laboratory imagery turns bodily material into ammunition. Later, `V15:p163` isolates Eri's memory of Midoriya's scarred hand and labels it `優しかった`.

The manga therefore makes touch a test of what power thinks another body is **for**.

### 20.2 Film strips and the visual ontology of prediction

Foresight is visualized as frames/film. That formal choice matters because film appears fixed: once recorded, the sequence seems already complete. The page design gives intuitive force to Nighteye's fatalism even before anyone proves whether prediction is metaphysically immutable.

The medium metaphor is doing philosophical work.

### 20.3 Kirishima's body becomes typography

The Unbreakable sequence uses increasingly dense blacks, sharp contours and oversized text to make “hardness” not just a Quirk property but a page property. His body becomes visually difficult to penetrate, and the name itself becomes part of the armor.

### 20.4 Network diagrams versus the isolated child

The task-force briefing uses charts, names, branches and organizational connections. Eri's scenes, by contrast, repeatedly isolate her in rooms or close frames. The rescue project is structurally a network trying to reach a person who has been socially and spatially isolated by another network.

## 21. First-pass comparison and claim revision

The historical V1 `MHA_V14-V15_DEEP_READING.md` is useful but combined two tankōbon objects and therefore blurred which volume actually established several major claims. V15 permits the following adjudications.

### STRENGTHEN / REVISE — “saving someone now” versus “saving someone correctly”

The thesis is substantially correct, but V2 makes it less slogan-like. Midoriya and Mirio **do** recognize Eri correctly in the alley. Their restraint follows from incomplete legal/investigative knowledge and the risk of losing her location entirely, not from a professional doctrine that immediate rescue is inherently naive.

The revision is:

> **Professional rescue can require preserving a later intervention path when immediate extraction is not reliably achievable; the fact that restraint is defensible does not erase the moral injury of leaving the person in danger.**

### STRENGTHEN — Nighteye as external legitimacy challenge

V14 established the designation/legitimacy distinction. V15 strengthens Nighteye by giving the judgment a causal history: his conflict with All Might is shaped by having foreseen a death he cannot prove avoidable and by watching All Might continue toward it.

### STRENGTHEN — Overhaul as clinical instrumentalization

V15 provides the decisive evidence the V1 reading previously imported too early. Eri is `計画の“核”`; human blood/cells are found in the projectile; the working hypothesis is that her body is being converted into ammunition. “Instrumentalization” is therefore no longer merely thematic analogy.

### STRENGTHEN / BROADEN — Eri as the wound of failed rescue

V1 focused primarily on Midoriya's guilt. V2 broadens the claim: the failed rescue also changes Eri because she remembers Midoriya's hand as `優しかった`. The relationship has two evidentiary directions.

### REVISE / BOUND — Kirishima as constructed courage

V15 strongly supports courage as actively constructed through self-command, training, named technique and mentor recognition. However, the detailed “he froze in the past / Crimson Riot origin” causal narrative is **not yet V15 evidence**. Preserve the current behavioral thesis and defer that backstory until its own tankōbon establishes it.

### STRENGTHEN / REVISE — Midoriya from moral instinct to moral judgment

The V1 developmental direction is correct, but “instinct becomes judgment” should not imply that instinct is discarded. V15 repeatedly validates Midoriya's first perception. Growth consists of integrating that perception with timing, evidence, institutional constraint, survival and long-horizon rescue.

### NEW — future knowledge can itself become a responsibility hazard

V1 did not sufficiently isolate the epistemic ethics of Foresight. V15 shows that knowing more can constrain agency psychologically. Nighteye therefore withholds prediction until ordinary success probability is high enough for the knowledge to become actionable rather than merely burdensome.

### NEW — friendship can function without information access

Iida's cafeteria intervention is a new relational proposition: care does not require entitlement to the concealed facts. This is especially important in a professionalizing cast where confidentiality will increasingly coexist with friendship.

## 25. Major V15 propositions

1. **V15 shifts the series from earned legitimacy / reciprocal mastery to responsible action under uncertainty / contested futurity.**
2. Midoriya and Mirio correctly recognize Eri's distress before they possess enough evidence to complete a safe extraction; restraint preserves a later rescue path but produces genuine moral injury.
3. Eri's return to Chisaki is coercive compliance mediated through anticipated violence, not evidence that she is safe with him.
4. Failed rescue is relationally consequential: Eri later remembers Midoriya's hand as `優しかった`, meaning recognition can alter a victim's model of human contact before physical rescue succeeds.
5. Chisaki's public family claim functions as cover for private instrumentalization; Eri is named `計画の“核”` and her blood/cells are connected to Quirk-destroying ammunition.
6. The prototype status must be preserved. V15 has not yet demonstrated a fully commercialized permanent Quirk-destroying round as an accomplished fact.
7. Nighteye's Foresight does not remove uncertainty. Its 24-hour interval, limited perspective and film-like output make it a constrained professional instrument.
8. Nighteye's doctrine explicitly subordinates extraordinary prediction to ordinary investigation: maximize success probability first, then use Foresight as `勝利のダメ押し`.
9. All Might reveals that he had internalized Nighteye's predicted death as an endpoint; Midoriya's demand that he live to witness `僕が来た` converts succession from replacement-through-death into intergenerational coexistence.
10. `僕も 一緒にねじ曲げます` is not proof fate can be changed. It is an ethical refusal to treat uncertain inevitability as a reason to surrender agency.
11. Kirishima's V15 courage is explicitly constructed through bodily training, self-command and professional commitment. The detailed later Crimson Riot/freeze backstory remains outside the V15 boundary.
12. Iida's cafeteria intervention demonstrates relationship learning: his own history of concealed crisis becomes the basis for recognizing and supporting Midoriya without demanding confidential details.
13. The Hassaikai operation is the institutional completion of V13's `群` logic: police, multiple agencies, teachers, sidekicks and licensed students convert distributed evidence into coordinated protective force.
14. Professional confidentiality has interpersonal costs. Mature friendship must sometimes support a person without entitlement to the information they are carrying.
15. The volume's temporal ethic is neither fatalism nor naive optimism: the past cannot be changed; whether the future can be changed is unknown; responsible action consists in changing what one does with that uncertainty.
