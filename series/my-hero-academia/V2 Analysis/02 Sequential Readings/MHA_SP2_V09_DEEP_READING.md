# 1. Central thesis and volume role


Volume 8 asked whether resistance can be read correctly enough to produce growth. Volume 9 removes the educational safety rail and asks a harsher question:


> **What remains of that learning when the wall is no longer designed for you, nobody guarantees a path to victory, and the cost of misreading the situation is another person's death?**


The forest attack does not simply make the series “darker.” It changes the **jurisdiction of action**. The students had spent the previous volumes learning inside environments whose constraints were authored by adults: entrance tests, combat exercises, the Sports Festival, internships, and the final practical. Even Hosu, despite becoming a lethal real-world crisis, ultimately triggered an explicit institutional reckoning over unauthorized student force. Volume 9 creates a different emergency. Villains deliberately enter the students' training space, professional containment begins failing in multiple locations at once, communications become fragmented, and Aizawa finally authorizes students to use their Quirks in self-defense.


That authorization matters because the manga does not simply announce that the children are now full heroes. Aizawa's order is narrower: survival has become the immediate condition of every other pedagogical or legal value. The professional institution changes its rule because the threat environment has changed. This is emergency delegation, not abolition of professional boundaries.


Midoriya's Muscular encounter gives the volume its clearest individual version of the same problem. V08 had established that Kota's rejection of hero culture was coherent from inside bereavement and could not honestly be argued away. V09 therefore does not resolve Kota by giving Midoriya a better speech. Midoriya meets the exact situation that made Kota's parents legible to the outside world and unbearable to Kota: a person with power places their own body between lethal violence and someone else.


This does not make Kota discover that his grief was mistaken. The causal sequence is more precise:


`heroic sacrifice as abstract adult claim → distrust / disgust → direct experience of being the protected person → recognition of what the claim meant from the protected side`.


That distinction is essential. Kota does not learn that losing his parents was good. He acquires access to the perspective of the lives their action saved.


The Muscular fight simultaneously changes Midoriya's bodily-risk model. The first pass was broadly right that Full Cowling has not eliminated self-destructive rescue. But “Midoriya still breaks himself” is too coarse. Earlier injuries frequently came from insufficient control over inherited power. Here he knows more, controls more, understands the bodily stakes better, and still crosses the damage threshold because the alternative is allowing Kota to die. The self-endangerment is therefore increasingly **value-conditioned rather than merely technically immature**.


That does not make it automatically virtuous. The volume deliberately shows the downstream cost. Midoriya survives Muscular but leaves the encounter badly injured and immediately attempts to re-enter a distributed crisis. His rescue ethic produces extraordinary local effectiveness while degrading his capacity to respond elsewhere. V09 therefore sharpens the sustainability problem from “learn not to hurt yourself” into a genuine command problem:


> **When is catastrophic bodily expenditure the only available rescue action, and when does repeated willingness to pay that cost begin to reduce the total rescue capacity of the person who keeps paying it?**


The villain side develops in parallel. Shigaraki's V08 `信念` does not remain private rhetoric. In V09 he articulates a strategic theory: create a crack in superhuman society, exploit people whom law and social order have repressed or excluded, and make the villains' mere arrival damage the assumption that hero-controlled spaces are safe. The Vanguard Action Squad is consequently not a unit unified by one coherent ideology. It is a **coalition of differently motivated marginal or violent actors organized by a shared operational target**.


That heterogeneity matters. Muscular is not Stain. Toga is not Muscular. Spinner is explicitly Stain-oriented enough to police conduct against what he thinks Stain would approve. Mustard's resentment and status language differ again. Dabi's interior motive remains underdetermined prospectively. Shigaraki's organizational growth is therefore visible not because everyone believes what he believes, but because he can increasingly make differently motivated people function inside one attack.


A concise Volume 9 thesis is:


> **Volume 9 converts learned heroism into emergency action under broken guarantees. It asks whether recognition, restraint, teamwork, professional rule, and bodily discipline survive when no one has designed a fair win condition. The answer is neither simple triumph nor collapse: characters reveal which principles they can carry into genuine danger, which rules institutions must alter to preserve life, and which forms of rescue create new costs even when they succeed.**


The first nine volumes can now be described compactly as:


`recognition → judgment → public legibility → self-authorship → recognition without possession → enacted identity → aftermath → diagnostic resistance → emergency enactment / cost-bearing`

# 2. Narrative architecture


## 2.1 The training camp begins by separating capacity from spectacle


The opening continuation of camp training matters because the attack does not interrupt a vacation. U.A. is deliberately trying to enlarge the students' physical Quirk capacities after earlier growth had been more technical, tactical, or cognitive. The exercises are repetitive, uncomfortable and individualized rather than glamorous (`V09:p006-025`).


This reinforces V08's distinction between knowing what to do and possessing enough embodied capacity to do it repeatedly. The attack will immediately test that distinction. Students whose tactics are sound may still be limited by output, endurance, injury, visibility, fear or environmental mismatch.


The mundane camp material also continues to provide baseline social evidence. Cooking, complaints, peer observation and ordinary group coordination matter precisely because the same children are about to be redistributed into crisis roles. V09 should not be modeled as if their only available selves are battle selves.


## 2.2 The Vanguard's arrival destroys the assumption that educational space is insulated


Chapter 73 gives the villains an entrance structured around intrusion rather than formal challenge (`V09:p026-047`). The group does not wait for students to consent to a contest. It arrives after dark and treats the training environment itself as a targetable vulnerability.


The effect on Hero Society analysis is larger than “villains found the camp.” U.A. had already changed the final exam because the threat environment was changing. The camp attack shows that adaptation does not guarantee control. Institutions can learn and still be penetrated.


The correct inference is therefore not that U.A. is static or incompetent. V06–V08 established repeated evidence of institutional learning. V09 supplies a different proposition:


> **Adaptive institutions can still be outpaced by adversaries who choose the time, terrain and objective.**


This will matter later when evaluating institutional failure. Failure after adaptation is analytically different from refusal to adapt.


## 2.3 Shigaraki's V08 conviction becomes an organizational theory


In the bar/remote-command material around Chapter 77, Shigaraki explains that the operation is meant to put a `ヒビ` — a crack — into superhuman society (`V09:p112-114`). He explicitly frames contemporary society as one in which violent or deviant people are suppressed by law and can therefore become recruitable companions.


This is a material development from V08.


V08 gave him a named conviction: All Might is the organizing object of his hatred. V09 begins turning that conviction into **strategy**:


- attack the credibility of protected hero space;
- target Bakugo for reasons tied to his publicly legible aggression;
- recruit heterogeneous actors who can be directed toward the same operation;
- treat public/social effects as part of success rather than measuring success only by immediate kills.


This is not yet mature political theory. It remains grievance-driven and dependent on AFO's infrastructure. But the V08 watch question — does `信念` create decision rules or only rhetoric? — receives its first strong positive evidence. The conviction now changes target selection, recruitment logic and operational meaning.


## 2.4 The Vanguard is coalition, not ideological uniformity


The attack repeatedly prevents “League ideology” from being treated as one mental state.


Muscular openly presents violence as pleasure and choice. His challenge to Midoriya is not a sophisticated social critique; it is an anti-moralizing defense of acting on desire. Toga combines lethal behavior with attraction, curiosity and an idiosyncratic intimacy grammar. Spinner identifies with Stain strongly enough that Stain's imagined standard constrains his behavior. Mustard expresses resentment toward students he sees as privileged or celebrated. Moonfish is dominated by violent appetite/compulsion. Dabi operates with discipline and hostility, but his deeper personal history remains unavailable at V09.


Shigaraki's organizational achievement is therefore **coordination across motive pluralism**.


That is more important than pretending the group has ideological consensus.


## 2.5 The attack turns student location into a rescue-network problem


Once the forest becomes fragmented by fire, gas, hostile Quirks and poor communication, action depends on who can transmit information to whom. Midoriya repeatedly becomes a moving information node after defeating Muscular: carrying Kota, trying to reach Mandalay, conveying what he has learned, and reorienting toward the villains' target.


This extends his V07 recognition sequence beyond noticing emotional distress. In V09, perception must become **routing**. Knowing something matters only if the information can alter other people's action in time.


The same principle applies institutionally. Mandalay's telepathy becomes infrastructure. Aizawa's orders become infrastructure. Kendo's reading of the gas pattern becomes infrastructure. Shoji's sensory and protective capacities become infrastructure. Heroism in the attack is increasingly distributed through communication and role differentiation rather than reducible to who wins the strongest duel.

# 3. Midoriya and Muscular: rescue without a designed win path


## 3.1 The encounter is prospectively different from an exam


The Muscular encounter is the most important correction to any reading that treats all MHA fights as tests of combat development.


There is no pedagogical author, no escape gate, no teacher intentionally preserving `勝ち筋`, and no reason to assume Muscular has been calibrated to Midoriya's current level. V08's “wall” metaphor becomes dangerous here because this wall does not care whether Midoriya grows from it.


That changes what success can mean.


In an exam, Midoriya can learn from failure and try again. Against Muscular, Kota dies if Midoriya's interpretation is wrong.


## 3.2 Muscular is a strong control case against over-socialized villain explanation


Muscular gives unusually clear direct evidence of his own motivational structure (`V09:p074-108`). He treats combat as `遊び`, enjoys overpowering people, and states that he was acting with a play-like attitude toward killing. He does not need Shigaraki's social critique to want violence.


His argument with Midoriya is revealing because he rejects rescue language as hypocrisy or impossible prettification. Where Midoriya says he will save Kota, Muscular asks how that can actually be done and mocks beautiful claims that cannot be realized.


The surface resemblance to Stain should not mislead. Both can accuse heroes of false language, but their governing commitments differ radically.


Stain uses lethal violence in service of a warped purification standard. Muscular uses anti-hypocrisy language to strip away moral constraint and legitimize appetite.


This is important for the villain-formation ledger:


> **V09 supplies direct evidence that some villainy in MHA cannot be adequately explained as failed recognition, social stigma, institutional hypocrisy or ideological radicalization. Predatory enjoyment and voluntary domination must remain available causal categories.**


## 3.3 Midoriya's analysis remains active under catastrophic bodily stress


Midoriya does not become a pure berserker when the fight turns desperate. He continues trying to identify Muscular's Quirk mechanics, evaluates his own failed output, considers distance and escape, asks about the League's purpose, and keeps Kota's location central.


That is character-modeling evidence. Under extreme stress, his cognition narrows around rescue but does not disappear.


The decision structure is roughly:


1. identify immediate victim and lethal threat;
2. test available force;
3. update when the first maximum attempt fails;
4. consider whether retreat can preserve the victim;
5. reject retreat when it would expose Kota;
6. accept catastrophic bodily cost to maintain the barrier between threat and victim.


This is not efficient self-preservation. It is also not thoughtless.


## 3.4 `1000000%` should not be literalized without evidence


The climactic attack is named `ワン・フォー・オール 1000000%` (`V09:p107-108`). Prospective analysis should resist converting this typography into a calibrated physical claim that Midoriya produced exactly ten thousand times his ordinary “100%.”


The scene establishes desperate overdrive, emotional extremity and a named escalation beyond his declared limit. It does **not** provide measurement infrastructure capable of validating the number as a literal multiplier.


Therefore the current model should record:


- direct: Midoriya names the attack `1000000%`;
- direct: it follows repeated 100%-named efforts and extreme injury;
- direct: it defeats Muscular;
- inference: the naming marks a desperate beyond-limit effort;
- OPEN: exact quantitative relation between the label and OFA output.


This prevents later power-scaling convention from replacing source epistemology.


## 3.5 Midoriya's bodily sacrifice is now value-conditioned


The first-pass claim that Midoriya's danger is martyrdom is directionally strong but needs refinement.


V09 does **not** establish a generalized desire to suffer or die. Midoriya does not seek injury as moral proof. He accepts injury when he sees no other route that preserves the person in front of him.


The better model is:


> **Midoriya has an exceptionally low threshold for accepting severe personal cost once he believes another person's survival is contingent on his continued action.**


That distinction predicts behavior better than “martyr complex.” It also preserves room to test whether he can improve at finding alternatives before reaching that threshold.


## 3.6 The victory exposes a rescue-capacity paradox


Defeating Muscular is an extraordinary rescue success. It also leaves Midoriya physically degraded inside a still-active multi-front attack.


This creates a recurring structural paradox:


`local maximum rescue commitment → victim survives → rescuer's later capacity decreases`.


The series can therefore criticize Midoriya's self-damage without claiming the immediate decision was obviously wrong. The problem is not solved by saying “he should value himself more.” The harder question is whether a hero system can give rescue-first actors enough technique, backup, communication and institutional support that they are less often forced into mutually exclusive choices between bodily integrity and another person's life.

# 4. Kota: from counter-narrative to experiential recognition


## 4.1 V08's anti-hero stance is preserved, not erased


Kota enters V09 already holding a coherent grief-grounded interpretation: hero society praised the conduct that took his parents away from him.


Nothing in the Muscular sequence retroactively makes that grief irrational.


The volume instead changes his **available evidence**.


## 4.2 Kota first becomes the object of the sacrifice he resented


During the Muscular fight, Kota watches Midoriya refuse to yield the route to him even as Midoriya's body is failing. This gives Kota a first-person position he did not have when interpreting his parents' deaths.


Mandalay's remembered explanation then becomes newly legible: his parents left him behind, but lives were genuinely preserved because of what they did (`V09:p109-110`).


The ordering is crucial:


- explanation existed before;
- explanation did not persuade;
- direct rescue changes the experiential frame;
- earlier explanation can then be reprocessed.


This strongly supports the project's recognition thesis:


> **Information is not always unavailable because nobody stated it. Sometimes a person's lived position makes the information unusable until another experience changes what the statement can mean.**


## 4.3 `僕のヒーロー` is relational, not institutional conversion


The chapter title and climax establish Midoriya as “my hero” from Kota's position. That should not be inflated into immediate endorsement of Hero Society as a whole.


What Kota has encountered is one specific person whose conduct makes heroic protection intelligible.


Current safe claim:


> Kota's categorical anti-hero stance is breached by a concrete relational counterexample.


Unsafe claims at V09:


- Kota now approves of professional hero culture generally;
- Kota has resolved grief over his parents;
- Kota now thinks their deaths were simply good;
- Kota has adopted Midoriya's entire heroic ideology.


The character model should preserve the smaller but more psychologically meaningful transition.

# 5. Emergency legitimacy: Aizawa's self-defense authorization


## 5.1 V06 and V07 make the V09 order more meaningful


The Hosu material established that hero coercive authority is historically regulated through licensing, supervision, accumulated trust and institutional control. Student action outside those rules remained a legal/professional problem even where it saved lives.


V09 does not discard that framework.


Aizawa changes the applicable rule because the situation has become an immediate survival emergency. His broadcast authorizes the students to fight (`戦闘を許可する`) under conditions where the villains are actively attacking them (`V09:p130`).


That is not the same as saying “rules no longer matter.” It demonstrates that **legitimate authority can contain emergency exceptions whose purpose is preserving the people the rule normally protects**.


## 5.2 Aizawa accepts command responsibility for delegated risk


The panel is especially important for Aizawa. He is not physically beside every student. His pedagogical role therefore becomes command judgment under incomplete information.


He must choose between two dangers:


- students using dangerous Quirks in uncontrolled combat;
- students withholding effective self-defense against lethal attackers.


He chooses the second risk as worse.


This strengthens his model as pragmatic rather than doctrinaire. His earlier hostility to wasteful self-harm and unauthorized conduct does not become inconsistency. The governing variable is whether the restriction still serves survival and professional purpose under current conditions.


## 5.3 The authorization remains bounded


The students are not deputized to pursue revenge, conduct punishment, or redefine themselves as autonomous law enforcement. The context is defense against active attackers and protection of others inside the attack zone.


This distinction should remain active in later Hero Society synthesis. Emergency permission is evidence **for** rule-governed flexibility, not evidence that professional law is cosmetic.

# 6. Shigaraki, Bakugo and symbolic target selection


## 6.1 Bakugo becomes target because public behavior is interpretable


The villains' interest in Bakugo develops from the way his aggression was publicly visible. That produces an inversion of the Sports Festival's public-legibility mechanism.


V03 showed that spectacle creates opportunity and vulnerability. V09 turns that thesis literal: a student's televised public identity becomes actionable intelligence for an enemy organization.


Public recognition therefore has at least three audiences:


1. institutions/pro heroes who may recruit;
2. peers/public who form reputation;
3. adversaries who interpret visible traits for exploitation.


## 6.2 Villain recognition can itself be misrecognition


The League reads Bakugo's anger and aggression as evidence that he may be recruitable or convertible.


Prospectively, V09 does not yet adjudicate whether that inference is correct. It does establish a useful distinction:


> **Being accurately observed at the level of surface behavior does not guarantee accurate inference about governing values.**


Bakugo's hostility, profanity and competitive violence are real. Whether they imply ideological compatibility with villains remains OPEN at the V09 endpoint.


This is a high-value character-model watch item for Volume 10.


## 6.3 Shigaraki is increasingly capable of strategic patience


The Bakugo objective also develops Shigaraki beyond immediate impulse. He can organize an attack around capture and symbolic damage rather than demanding indiscriminate destruction from every participant.


That does not erase his volatility. It gives the volatility a larger structure.


The V08 `信念` question therefore moves from OPEN toward STRENGTHENED:


`private anti-All-Might conviction → target selection → coalition operation → attempted symbolic appropriation of a visible U.A. student`.

# 7. Spinner: ideology as internal restraint inside a villain coalition


Spinner is one of V09's most analytically useful new antagonists because his Stain adherence is not merely aesthetic branding.


He invokes Stain as a governing standard and reacts to conduct according to whether he believes it fits Stain's project. This supplies an early example of ideology operating as **constraint on an ally**, not only justification for attacking an enemy.


That makes Spinner different from Muscular.


Muscular's relevant question is “what do I want to do?”


Spinner's relevant question is increasingly “what would the ideal I have adopted permit?”


The model should remain narrow. V09 does not yet provide enough context for a full Spinner reconstruction. But it is enough to establish:


- Stain's post-capture symbolic reach has recruited at least one actor who treats the ideology as normative rather than decorative;
- League coalition does not erase ideological disagreement;
- Shigaraki can currently tolerate heterogeneous internal motives when they remain operationally useful.


This is an important continuation of V07's `受け皿` model: a movement can aggregate people first and resolve ideological coherence later, or never.

# 8. Toga: intimacy, attraction and violence become behaviorally linked


V08 gave Toga only one strong direct causal signal: life feels difficult (`生きにくい`). V09 materially expands behavior but still does **not** explain the origin of that condition.


Her encounter with Uraraka and Tsuyu supplies several distinct observations (`V09:p176-181` region):


- she approaches social contact with conspicuous curiosity and playful affect;
- she asks/names people in a way that creates rapid pseudo-intimacy;
- blood, cutting, attraction and “cute” evaluation occupy the same conversational field;
- violence does not require an emotionally cold register; excitement and affiliative language coexist with threat.


This is crucial for modeling because “Toga is cheerful” and “Toga is violent” should not be stored as contradictory separate traits. V09 suggests that her affiliative and predatory systems may be behaviorally entangled.


What remains OPEN:


- why blood has this relational meaning;
- whether her attraction language maps cleanly onto ordinary romantic categories;
- what developmental history produced the pattern;
- how she distinguishes desired intimacy from possession or harm.


Do not backfill any answer from later volumes.

# 9. Kendo, Tetsutetsu and Mustard: Class B becomes behaviorally useful


## 9.1 Kendo converts environmental inference into a counter-operation


The gas confrontation is valuable because Kendo does not merely display her Quirk. She reads how the gas is distributed, reasons toward the source, and coordinates action around environmental control (`V09:p152-171`).


This is early but substantial evidence for a leadership/problem-solving model:


- notices system behavior rather than only visible opponent;
- uses communication to align partner action;
- treats terrain/airflow as part of the tactical problem;
- remains functional under poison/gas pressure.


Kendo should advance beyond a purely background Class B baseline.


## 9.2 Tetsutetsu turns persistence into protective pressure rather than empty stubbornness


Tetsutetsu's durability allows him to continue through Mustard's gunfire and gas long enough to collapse the enemy's control. His behavior is simple but contextually meaningful: he accepts bodily punishment to keep pressure off others and reach the source.


This should not yet be generalized into a full stable “self-sacrificing” trait. The evidence supports high persistence and directness under combat pressure.


## 9.3 Mustard's resentment is socially comparative


Mustard is not Muscular. His dialogue repeatedly frames U.A. students through resentment, status and contempt for their celebrated position. That gives the Vanguard another motivational route: social-comparative grievance attached to a dangerous competence set.


Again, the coalition is analytically heterogeneous.

# 10. Shoji and Tokoyami: protection through knowledge of another person's dangerous state


The Dark Shadow material provides unusually useful relationship-conditioned evidence.


Tokoyami's Quirk becomes far harder to control in darkness and under emotional escalation. The attack therefore turns his own power into a local hazard. Shoji, already injured, remains oriented toward Tokoyami's condition rather than simply maximizing his own escape.


Midoriya's group then solves the problem by **redirecting** dangerous capacity toward an external threat rather than trying to dominate it directly. Dark Shadow's uncontrolled force can destroy Moonfish, after which the group must again manage Tokoyami rather than treating the output as uncomplicated victory (`V09:p161-176`).


This extends V08's diagnostic-growth idea into emergency conditions:


> Sometimes the correct response to an unstable power is not suppression or maximal release but environmental redirection plus trusted-partner containment.


For Tokoyami modeling:


- darkness materially changes control;
- emotional/stress conditions interact with Quirk risk;
- power output and agency are not identical;
- trusted peers can become external regulation infrastructure.


For Shoji modeling:


- injury does not collapse his attention exclusively onto self;
- he can prioritize another person's dangerous vulnerability;
- sensory/protective role behavior is emerging strongly enough to merit a more serious baseline.

# 11. Bakugo/Todoroki and the difference between combat competence and capture vulnerability


Bakugo and Todoroki perform effectively against a lethal attacker, but the volume increasingly reframes the villains' objective around Bakugo's **capture**, not merely defeating him in a duel.


That matters because combat competence does not automatically solve an extraction problem. A target can be strong and still be vulnerable when:


- allies are dispersed;
- objectives are asymmetric;
- attackers need only create a brief opening;
- one side is willing to disengage after capture rather than win the battlefield.


The emerging “Bakugo escort” logic in Chapter 80 makes this explicit. The students begin organizing around preserving a person rather than defeating every enemy they encounter.


This is another step toward distributed hero cognition: threat neutralization is subordinate to the actual rescue/protection objective.

# 12. Japanese language and voice observations


V09 gives several high-value register observations.


## Midoriya


Under extreme injury, his language compresses toward urgent imperatives directed at Kota and functional reporting directed at adults. He does not become rhetorically heroic for its own sake. The strongest rescue speech is operational: move, stay back, survive.


His internal/near-internal apologies to Inko and All Might during the Muscular crush are relationally revealing. Under perceived death pressure, the people activated in his cognition are not abstract “society” but concrete attachment/obligation figures.


## Muscular


Muscular's register is casual, taunting and recreational even around lethal violence. `遊ぶ` vocabulary is not ornamental; it encodes the mismatch between his affective frame and the victim's stakes.


## Shigaraki


Compared with the earlier fragmented irritability, V09 gives more declarative strategic language. He can now speak in terms of society, suppression, cracks and recruitment. The linguistic change supports — but does not by itself prove — increased organizational cognition.


## Toga


Toga's light, intimate, curiosity-heavy register coexists with cutting/blood language. A reconstruction that makes her continuously sinister in tone would miss the evidence. The danger partly lies in how little conventional tonal separation exists between social approach and predatory intent.


## Aizawa


His emergency order is characteristically compressed and functional. He does not moralize the exception; he changes the rule necessary for survival.


## Bakugo


Bakugo remains aggressive and abrasive under crisis. V09 does not supply a generalized softening after the All Might exam. Functional alliance and changed behavior should continue to be modeled independently from speech politeness.

# 13. Visual grammar and motif


## 13.1 Designed walls become uncontrolled forest occlusion


V08's barriers were often legible: exam arenas, capture conditions, known opponents, teacher-authored difficulty. V09 replaces these with trees, smoke, gas, darkness and broken sightlines.


The visual environment itself therefore represents loss of informational control.


## 13.2 Bodies become maps of accumulated cost


Midoriya's limbs after Muscular, Shoji's injury, poison exposure, and repeated impact imagery make bodily condition a persistent tactical fact rather than a reset between scenes.


This is especially important for character modeling: later decisions inside the same attack must be read from the injured state actually carried forward.


## 13.3 The `僕のヒーロー` composition reverses Kota's observer position


Kota had viewed hero culture from outside as a child forced to live with its cost. In the Muscular climax he is visually positioned as the protected observer of a sacrifice being made **for him**. The manga does not need a long ideological speech because the panel relation itself changes his epistemic position.


## 13.4 Darkness amplifies internal power instability


Tokoyami/Dark Shadow converts environmental darkness into both strength and loss of control. The visual field becomes not simply spooky atmosphere but a causal component of character state.

# 14. Hero Society and institutional model updates


## 14.1 U.A. is adaptive but penetrable


The attack should revise simplistic readings in both directions.


It is wrong to describe U.A. as an institution that never learns: prior volumes show adaptation.


It is also wrong to treat adaptation as proof of adequate control: the camp is penetrated and students are exposed to lethal threat.


Current model:


> U.A. is a learning institution operating inside an adversarial environment whose threat actors also learn.


## 14.2 Professional legitimacy includes emergency rule modification


Aizawa's combat authorization strengthens, rather than destroys, the V06–V07 legitimacy model. Authority is not legitimate because rules are immovable. It is legitimate partly because accountable professionals can alter operational permissions when the purpose of the standing rule would otherwise be defeated.


## 14.3 Student heroization is becoming structurally unavoidable


Even though the institution tries to preserve adult command, the students possess real power and are attacked as strategic actors. The society can no longer pretend that they are only classroom participants.


This does **not** mean their premature exposure is normatively good. It means the distinction between “student” and “front-line actor” is being forced open by adversarial reality.

# 15. Villain-formation model updates


V09 requires the villain model to remain plural.


### Shigaraki


Grievance becomes strategic organization and symbolic target selection.


### Spinner


Adopted ideology creates behavioral constraint and an evaluative standard.


### Muscular


Predatory enjoyment / domination provides a control case against explanatory reduction to social injury.


### Toga


Affiliation, attraction, blood and violence are behaviorally entangled, while causal history remains OPEN.


### Mustard


Social-comparative resentment/status grievance enters as another route.


### Dabi


Operational participation is visible; identity and deep motive remain OPEN.


The resulting proposition is:


> **The League's growth depends less on ideological homogeneity than on its capacity to aggregate distinct grievance, appetite, identification and alienation structures into common operations.**


This substantially strengthens V07's “movement infrastructure / receptacle” thesis.

# 16. First-pass comparison and claim revision


The historical V1 `MHA_V08-V09_DEEP_READING.md` contained several useful intuitions, but V2 separates the two tankobon and tightens causal claims.


## 16.1 Forest camp as “children carrying hero society's war”


**REVISE / STRENGTHEN.**


The first pass correctly recognized the move from bounded training into direct targeted violence against students. But “children forced to carry the war” risks flattening professional command and emergency authorization.


V09 supports a more precise claim:


> The adversary collapses the protected educational boundary faster than professional institutions can preserve it, forcing students into genuine self-defense while adults continue attempting to retain command responsibility.


## 16.2 Midoriya's danger as martyrdom


**REVISE / STRENGTHEN.**


The first pass correctly identified recurrent bodily self-expenditure. V09 supports a narrower behavioral mechanism: Midoriya accepts extreme bodily cost when he believes another person's survival is directly contingent on his continued action.


Do not infer that he values suffering or seeks death.


## 16.3 Kota changes because Midoriya protects him rather than argues


**STRENGTHEN.**


This is one of the strongest first-pass claims. V2 makes the mechanism more exact: direct rescue changes Kota's experiential position enough that Mandalay's earlier explanation of his parents' sacrifice becomes newly usable.


## 16.4 Muscular as power without moral imagination


**REVISE.**


The phrase is evocative but too abstract. V09 gives stronger direct evidence: Muscular frames killing as play, foregrounds desire, mocks rescue claims as impossible prettification, and accepts domination as self-honest enjoyment.


The better current category is **predatory hedonism / voluntary domination**, not merely absence of moral imagination.


## 16.5 Full Cowling solves control but not self-damage


**STRENGTHEN / REVISE.**


Technical control has improved materially. V09 shows that catastrophic self-damage can recur for a different reason: Midoriya knowingly overrides sustainability constraints when rescue stakes cross his threshold.


## 16.6 “Heroism becomes real when someone who distrusts the word hero is saved anyway”


**STRENGTHEN with constraint.**


Strong as a relational formulation. It should not be generalized into “rescue automatically legitimizes Hero Society.” Kota's change is toward Midoriya and toward the intelligibility of protective sacrifice, not yet wholesale institutional conversion.

## 22.1 Heroism is tested without a pedagogical guarantee


The forest turns learned principles into action where no teacher has designed a fair path to success.


## 22.2 Midoriya's self-damage changes causal category


Technical incompetence is no longer an adequate explanation. Severe self-expenditure can now be knowingly value-conditioned by immediate rescue stakes.


## 22.3 Kota demonstrates experiential recognition


An argument he could not use becomes meaningful after he occupies the position of a person saved by another's sacrifice.


## 22.4 Emergency authority is flexible but still institutional


Aizawa's combat authorization is an emergency delegation inside professional responsibility, not a collapse of legitimacy into “anything goes.”

The V08 `信念` now affects recruitment, symbolic target selection and the desired social meaning of an operation.

The Vanguard combines ideology, appetite, resentment, alienation and idiosyncratic intimacy structures. League organization matters because motives differ.


## 22.7 Public identity becomes enemy intelligence


Bakugo's public aggression is not merely reputation. An adversary interprets it as a potential recruitment/capture opportunity.

Shoji, Tokoyami, Kendo and Tetsutetsu gain behaviorally useful evidence under conditions that cannot be dismissed as school-game performance.


## 22.9 The rescue network matters as much as individual power


Telepathy, information transfer, environmental inference, partner regulation and escort logic show that the crisis is solved — or failed — through distributed coordination.
