# 1. Governing transition: visibility under consequence

Volume 19 established **ordinary joy as heroic labor / recognition as a contested social good**. It separated several ways of seeing another person: Aoyama's imperfect but genuine peer recognition, Jiro's peers recognizing value in a private skill without possessing it, Eri's need for ordinary happiness after extraction, and Gentle's desire to be seen and remembered through criminal performance.

Volume 20 forces each form of visibility to answer a harder question: **what obligations follow once one has been seen?**

The governing transition is:

> **ordinary joy as heroic labor / recognition as a contested social good -> visibility under consequence / responsibility before multiple witnesses**

Compact formulation:

> **Recognition becomes ethically meaningful only when visibility can be converted into responsibility. Gentle and La Brava must face the consequences of a bond that genuinely saved them but also sustained crime; Class 1-A turns public attention into shared joy rather than possession; and Endeavor's new No.1 visibility places public hero performance under the judgment of a family whose private injuries rank cannot erase.**

The volume repeatedly stages acts of watching:

- La Brava once watches Gentle's videos when she has withdrawn from ordinary social life, then becomes the person who sees and supports him most completely.
- Gentle wants history and the public to remember him, but his decisive act is to prioritize La Brava over the audience he sought.
- Midoriya sees enough of Gentle's relational motive to recognize him without treating recognition as absolution.
- Class 1-A performs before a crowd but organizes visibility around the audience's pleasure rather than the performers' fame.
- Eri moves from being the object of rescue and surveillance to becoming an audience member capable of spontaneous delight.
- the Hero Billboard Chart formalizes public visibility into ranking metrics;
- Hawks treats public attention as an operational resource rather than merely fame;
- Endeavor says `俺を見ていてくれ` and voluntarily places himself under future judgment;
- Rei, Fuyumi, Natsuo and Shoto then literally watch Endeavor's public battle while carrying private knowledge that the wider public does not share.

V20 therefore unifies the School Festival and High-End material more tightly than their tonal difference initially suggests. Both ask what public performance is for. One performance restores ordinary life. Another criminal performance seeks memory. The final professional-hero performance is an attempt to inhabit public responsibility under an inherited deficit of trust.

# 2. No.178 — La Brava: love as power, attachment after social withdrawal

## 2.1 V19's Quirk lock resolves literally

La Brava's Quirk is named `愛` / Love. Its mechanism makes an already visible relational fact bodily consequential: she can temporarily strengthen the person she loves, and greater love produces greater amplification.

This should not be reduced to a romantic power-up joke. MHA has repeatedly treated Quirks as capacities that become ethically meaningful only through use and relationship. Here the Quirk does not prove that Gentle's cause is just. It proves that **La Brava's attachment is real enough to become measurable force**.

The distinction matters:

`authentic love -> real power` does not imply `authentic love -> legitimate action`.

That preserves a core V19 insight while making it sharper. Bonds can empower conduct without morally authorizing the conduct.

## 2.2 La Brava's earlier origin becomes admissible

V20 now establishes why Gentle's first-fan relationship carried such extraordinary weight for La Brava. Earlier romantic attachment was mocked as excessive, heavy or creepy. Rather than moving easily into another peer relationship, she withdrew from ordinary social life.

In that isolated state, she encountered Gentle's uploaded videos. What he offered was not conventional social status. He offered an object around which her attention, editing ability, research, devotion and later hacking competence could organize.

The sequence is therefore more specific than `rejected girl finds villain boyfriend`:

`intense attachment -> ridicule/rejection -> social withdrawal -> mediated encounter with Gentle -> first-fan recognition -> technical collaboration -> shared identity/project`.

Her relationship-specific behavior follows naturally from this history. She does not merely admire Gentle's persona. She has rebuilt social participation through him.

## 2.3 La Brava as collaborator, not battery

The Quirk reveal could encourage a weak model in which La Brava exists only to make Gentle stronger. V19-V20 source evidence argues against that simplification.

She:

- films and edits;
- researches routes and targets;
- monitors online response;
- hacks U.A.'s systems;
- chooses when to deploy Love;
- interprets the fight and understands when Gentle will not withdraw;
- attempts to preserve the relationship under imminent failure.

Her attachment is dominant, but her function inside the dyad is agentic and technically competent.

The risk is not absence of agency. It is **agency narrowed around one relationship**.

# 3. No.179 — Gentle's failed hero route: incompetence, harm, shame and fear of erasure

## 3.1 The failed-recognition thesis requires revision

Historical V1 analysis described Gentle as a failed hero aspirant whose lack of recognition curdled into criminal notoriety. V20 keeps recognition in the model but rejects monocausal victimization.

At eighteen, Gentle still wants to become a hero and a `大いなる偉大な男`, someone important enough to enter history. But his actual institutional record is poor. He has repeated a school year and failed the provisional-license exam four times. A teacher recommends withdrawal. A peer, Takeshita, is already attracting agency attention.

This is not a case where the institution simply fails to detect obvious competence. At the observed boundary, Gentle has not demonstrated the professional readiness required by the licensing system.

## 3.2 Unauthorized rescue causes real harm

Gentle then encounters a falling civilian and acts without authorization. The motive is recognizably heroic-shaped: he wants to save someone. The outcome is not.

His intervention interferes with a licensed hero's rescue and contributes to severe injury requiring roughly six months of recovery.

This is one of MHA's strongest controls against the idea that sincere rescue impulse alone constitutes professional heroism.

Compare several earlier cases:

- Midoriya's unlicensed movement repeatedly requires later adjudication because motive and outcome do not automatically erase law;
- Iida's Hosu conduct showed that even an accurate moral target does not legitimize private coercive jurisdiction;
- the provisional-license material emphasized triage, communication, lawful role and public trust;
- Gentle demonstrates the inverse danger: **a person can sincerely try to save and still make rescue worse through poor judgment and insufficient competence**.

The consequences then spread. He leaves school, his family is pulled into disgrace, and his home/social position deteriorates.

## 3.3 Being forgotten reactivates the dream

By twenty-two, Gentle is living much more modestly and his original hero dream has largely collapsed. The decisive psychological event is not another institutional rejection. It is an encounter with Takeshita, now a functioning hero, who no longer remembers him.

Gentle confronts a feared future in which his life disappears from social memory.

The causal sequence is therefore:

> **heroic aspiration + inadequate professional competence -> unauthorized intervention causing harm -> institutional/family disgrace -> abandoned dream -> encounter with social erasure -> terror of being forgotten -> criminal-performance project aimed at historical visibility**

This is a far stronger villain-formation model than `society did not recognize him`.

Recognition remains central, but as an existential desire to leave evidence that he mattered—not as proof that the hero system unjustly denied a competent candidate.

## 3.4 Love amplifies a dream Gentle can no longer treat as private

La Brava's Love makes the present fight physically harder, but the more important effect is moral/psychological. Gentle now experiences the dream as something another person has invested her life in.

That raises his persistence while also creating the condition for later surrender: because La Brava has made his dream non-private, he can no longer pretend that only his own future is at stake.

# 4. No.180 — Midoriya and Gentle as non-zero-sum mirrors

## 4.1 Both are carrying another person's hope

The fight becomes most revealing when Midoriya and Gentle recognize that neither is fighting for himself alone.

Midoriya's immediate reason for stopping Gentle includes the fragile ordinary event Eri is about to attend, as well as his class's work and the institution's precarious ability to hold the festival at all.

Gentle is carrying La Brava's belief that he gave her life direction and light.

The mirror is therefore not:

`successful hero vs failed villain`.

It is:

`two actors whose self-authored projects have become relational obligations, making withdrawal psychologically difficult`.

The ethical difference lies in what those obligations authorize.

Midoriya's obligation requires stopping an intrusion without exposing the school to unnecessary panic. Gentle's obligation has been used to continue a criminal project whose success would destroy the event other people depend on.

## 4.2 Gentle's decisive choice is protective surrender

Once he can no longer win without exposing La Brava to greater harm, Gentle changes objective.

The public-memory project gives way to relationship protection.

He tries to reduce her culpability, separate her hacking from his authorship where possible, and choose surrender rather than force the intrusion to its terminal point.

This is not redemption by one gesture. It is, however, an important decision-policy transition:

`preserve performance/dream -> preserve La Brava's future`.

The fact that this requires abandoning the act meant to make him historically visible gives it analytical weight.

## 4.3 La Brava refuses self-preservation through abandonment

La Brava identifies Gentle as her only `光` / light. She does not interpret abandoning him as a meaningful future, even when legal exposure becomes imminent.

Gentle answers that he too was happy.

Their relationship is therefore genuinely reciprocal. Earlier shorthand such as `La Brava saves Gentle / Gentle exploits devotion` is not adequate.

A better model is:

> **two socially wounded people create a relationship that genuinely restores meaning to both, then build an unlawful public project around that relationship. The bond is real; the project remains answerable to people outside the bond.**

That distinction is crucial to MHA's relational ethics. Intimacy cannot become a closed moral universe.

# 5. No.181 — Recognition without absolution; hidden hero labor

## 5.1 The conflict ends quietly

The resolution avoids triumphal hero-villain staging. Midoriya does not turn Gentle into a public trophy. Gentle does not demand that Midoriya validate his criminal identity. The interaction is comparatively subdued.

Gentle recognizes that Midoriya is carrying other people's hopes. Midoriya, in turn, understands that Gentle is acting for La Brava as well as for himself.

This is **recognition without moral equivalence**.

Midoriya can understand why Gentle cannot simply yield and still insist that he must be stopped.

That is a useful reconstruction principle for Midoriya: empathy does not necessarily lower his action threshold against a threat. It can instead change **how** he interprets and contains the threat.

## 5.2 Heroic causality can remain invisible

Midoriya's success is deliberately anti-spectacular. The festival audience does not need to know that an intrusion was stopped nearby. Protecting the event includes protecting its ordinariness.

This is an important reversal of Gentle's media logic.

Gentle performs crime so that people will see and remember it.

Midoriya performs protection so that people **do not have to know protection was necessary**.

That gives V20 a strong hidden-labor thesis:

> **some successful hero work is successful precisely because the beneficiary gets to experience an ordinary day rather than a story about being endangered.**

## 5.3 From conflict to stage

The transition to the School Festival is almost immediate. Midoriya returns just in time. The performance starts on schedule. Eri and Mirio are in the audience.

The formal contrast is deliberate:

- Gentle: performance whose primary object is the performer's persistence in public memory;
- Class 1-A: performance whose primary object is what happens **inside the audience**.

# 6. No.182 — The School Festival: distributed restoration and Jiro's integrated identity

## 6.1 Entertainment becomes legitimate hero work without becoming identical to rescue

The performance combines:

- Jiro's music and singing;
- Bakugo's drumming;
- instrumental support from classmates;
- dance;
- lighting;
- Quirk-assisted visual effects;
- crowd movement and timing;
- months/weeks of rehearsal and support work.

The result is not a battle translated into spectacle. The objective function has changed.

Instead of neutralizing an enemy, the class wants to change the audience's affective state.

That is still heroically relevant because V19 established that U.A.'s other students have lived under repeated disruption and resentment generated around the Hero Course. The class is attempting to return something rather than demand admiration.

## 6.2 Jiro's private competence becomes chosen public service

Jiro's family flashback resolves the identity problem opened in V19.

Music is not an accidental hobby she happens to be good at. It belongs to her family history and self-conception. Her parents support her choice to pursue heroism rather than treating musical inheritance as an obligation.

The important synthesis is not `Jiro chooses both careers`.

It is that she discovers a common social function:

`hero skill -> can reach/help people`

`music -> can reach/move/make people happy`.

This preserves differentiation between domains while allowing them to coexist inside one self.

V19 already showed that Jiro becomes assertive once she has chosen a technical responsibility. V20 strengthens that model under actual performance pressure: embarrassment about personal exposure does not imply inability to lead or perform publicly when the role is self-authored.

## 6.3 Eri's smile is distributed causality

Historical V1 analysis correctly identified Eri's smile as a major rescue payoff but over-centered Midoriya as the direct causal agent.

V2 evidence supports a distributed model:

- Midoriya and the raid team physically extracted Eri;
- Aizawa/U.A. maintain medical and Quirk-safety supervision;
- Mirio sustains a trusted relationship and brings her into ordinary space;
- Midoriya protects the festival opportunity without burdening her with the hidden conflict;
- Class 1-A collectively creates the performance;
- Jiro's music and the class's staging organize the immediate emotional environment;
- Eri herself responds.

The payoff is not `Deku makes Eri smile`.

It is:

> **a rescue network finally creates conditions safe and joyful enough for Eri to generate spontaneous positive affect of her own.**

That distinction is consistent with V17's shift from rescue-as-extraction toward restored agency.

## 6.4 `笑ったよ!!` completes an earlier OPEN claim

Eri's inability to smile was explicit in V19. V20 now gives the first source-grounded payoff: she visibly laughs/smiles during the performance, and the adults/companions around her register it.

The important state transition is:

`physical safety + persistent learned guilt + inaccessible ordinary smile`

->

`supervised ordinary participation + collective performance + spontaneous delight`.

This does not mean trauma is cured. It does mean the model can no longer treat Eri's current state as exclusively fear/guilt/avoidance.

Positive preference and positive affect are now observed behaviors.

# 7. No.183 — Ordinary life after the climax; rehabilitation after crime

## 7.1 The festival continues after Eri's big moment

V20 does not freeze Eri's smile into a symbolic tableau and stop. The festival continues through mundane experiences: food, booths, peer interaction, thanks and low-stakes movement through school space.

Eri's curiosity about a candy apple and experience of sweetness matter precisely because they are narratively small.

For reconstruction, they provide evidence that she can now:

- ask about unfamiliar pleasurable objects;
- express curiosity without immediate fear framing;
- receive an ordinary answer;
- try something because she wants to know what it is like;
- experience pleasure that is not tied to rescue debt.

This is the kind of evidence V2.1 explicitly treats as first-class modeling material.

## 7.2 Jiro receives thanks rather than possession

Jiro's aftermath is also important. Her talent is publicly recognized, but the social response is gratitude for what she helped create rather than a demand that she convert herself into a permanent entertainer.

This validates the V19 distinction between recognition and possession.

A skill can be made socially legible without the group claiming ownership over the person who has it.

## 7.3 Gentle and La Brava are processed as people with futures

The custody/interrogation material is one of the volume's strongest institutional correctives.

La Brava continues to say she wants to help Gentle. Gentle rejects an easy fiction that she was merely brainwashed; the investigators can tell her affection is genuine.

Gentle himself can now articulate the twisted route from former hero-course student to criminal video poster: he remembered a dream, became afraid of disappearing, and ran toward a distorted way of making that dream visible.

The investigators do not erase responsibility. They also do not define his current offense as proof of immutable criminal essence.

The atmosphere permits the possibility that a person who took the wrong route can still choose another route later.

That produces a useful hero-society finding:

> **MHA's lawful institutions can distinguish accountability from ontological condemnation. Criminal conduct creates consequences; it need not make rehabilitation conceptually impossible.**

This is especially important after arcs dominated by villains whose choices produced escalating lethal harm.

# 8. No.184 — Recovery becomes institutional; ranking becomes visibility infrastructure

## 8.1 The Wild Wild Pussycats return after capability loss

The Pussycats' return, including Ragdoll after the loss of Search, creates another capability/identity control case.

V17-V18 established that Mirio's hero identity survives catastrophic Quirk loss. Ragdoll now reinforces the broader principle from another professional context: loss of a specific capability does not automatically erase group belonging, role identity or social value.

The cases are not identical. Mirio is a student whose future field role is uncertain; Ragdoll is part of an established professional team. But both resist the idea that person = power.

## 8.2 Tartarus preserves threat without restoring dominance

All For One remains physically contained but psychologically present. The important V20 point is not later plot mechanism; it is that institutional containment has not made his ideological/relational influence irrelevant.

This keeps the post-Kamino order unstable even as ordinary school life resumes.

## 8.3 Hero Billboard Chart JP formalizes the post-All-Might order

The new Hero Billboard Chart makes visible what V18 had begun to establish: No.1 is now a measured social position rather than a role automatically fused with one universally trusted person.

The chart uses multiple metrics rather than simple combat output. Public support, contribution and broader professional performance enter the ranking environment.

The top order includes Endeavor at No.1 and Hawks at No.2, followed by other established heroes.

This is an institutional attempt to quantify hero visibility after a Symbol whose social meaning exceeded ordinary ranking.

That mismatch is important:

`ranking can identify the top-ranked hero`

is not the same proposition as

`ranking can manufacture a Symbol of Peace`.

V20's second half exists inside that gap.

# 9. No.185 — Hawks and Endeavor: competing public styles, one post-symbol problem

## 9.1 Hawks interrupts ceremonial reassurance

Hawks is introduced not merely as a high-ranked combatant but as someone highly comfortable manipulating public attention.

His informality, age, media fluency and irreverence sharply contrast with Endeavor's severe presentation.

At the ranking event, Hawks disrupts the expected sequence of conventional top-hero remarks. He pushes the room toward the actual post-All-Might problem rather than letting the ceremony pretend continuity.

This supplies a useful decision heuristic:

> **when Hawks thinks conventional reassurance obscures an important instability, he is willing to violate professional decorum in order to force the instability into public speech.**

## 9.2 `俺を見ていてくれ`: No.1 as future evidentiary burden

Endeavor's response is short:

`俺を見ていてくれ`.

This should not be translated analytically into `trust me` or `I will become All Might`.

It is closer to a request for continued observation.

That makes it unusually compatible with the V18 family scene. Endeavor has already told Shoto he will try to become a hero Shoto can be proud of. He cannot compel Shoto to accept that declaration. Now, at public scale, he similarly offers future action as evidence.

The model becomes:

`rank has been assigned -> trust has not -> future conduct must carry the burden`.

This is accountability-oriented language, but it is not yet proof that accountability will succeed.

## 9.3 Hawks converts publicity into intelligence partnership

Hawks then moves rapidly from ceremonial disruption into operational concern: Nomu rumors/sightings.

His logic is important. Rumors can be false and still become socially dangerous through diffusion. A credible, highly visible No.1 hero is useful not only for fighting the threat but for stabilizing public understanding of whether the threat is real.

This means Hawks treats visibility as **infrastructure**.

His No.2 status, fan relations and media ease are not separate from intelligence work. They are part of how he navigates information and social response.

# 10. No.186 — High-End turns public symbolism into emergency operations

## 10.1 Hawks's casual public persona is operationally continuous

Moving through public space, Hawks is recognized constantly and responds with easy familiarity. The surface style is loose, but his attention is not.

He tracks rumor patterns, social transmission and threat plausibility. He recruits Endeavor because credibility and combat capacity are both relevant.

The contrast with Endeavor should therefore not be simplified to `fun Hawks / angry Endeavor`.

They solve different problems with different social temperatures.

## 10.2 High-End attacks before the partnership can remain theoretical

The High-End Nomu attacks a populated commercial area, immediately forcing the new No.1/No.2 arrangement into public crisis.

Their initial division of labor is highly diagnostic:

- Endeavor intercepts and contests the principal threat;
- Hawks begins distributing protection across civilians and the built environment.

This is not hierarchy as `No.1 does everything and No.2 assists`.

It is complementary specialization.

## 10.3 High-End is not an ordinary Nomu control case

High-End speaks, seeks strong opponents and demonstrates adaptive combat behavior. V20 establishes a threat capable of responding dynamically to Endeavor rather than functioning as a passive durability test.

That makes the confrontation a genuine stress test of the post-All-Might ranking order.

The new No.1 is not being evaluated against an abstract public expectation. He is being evaluated live, in a populated environment, against a threat that can learn and persist.

# 11. No.187 — Endeavor's family as differentiated witnesses; Hawks as distributed rescuer

## 11.1 The Todoroki family cannot be represented by one response

Rei, Fuyumi and Natsuo discuss Endeavor from different positions.

Rei can perceive signs that he is trying to face what he did. Fuyumi is comparatively oriented toward family connection. Natsuo remains substantially more resistant.

The analytical rule must therefore be:

> **there is no unitary “Todoroki family forgiveness state.”**

Each relationship has its own history, fear threshold, evidentiary standard and willingness to engage.

This is especially important for later character simulation. Endeavor cannot infer one child's or Rei's response from another's.

## 11.2 The flower is evidence of memory, not absolution

Rei notes that Endeavor has been sending a flower she mentioned liking only once around the time they first met.

This matters because it demonstrates remembered personal detail and an attempt to communicate without direct contact.

But the same scene protects the boundary against sentimental overreading. Direct visitation remains frightening and medically/psychologically inappropriate at this point.

Thus:

`remembered flower + repeated sending -> evidence of attempted responsibility / attention`

not

`flower -> reconciliation / forgiveness`.

The distinction should remain explicit in the relationship ledger.

## 11.3 Hawks's Fierce Wings reveal rescue-oriented micro-control

Hawks's Quirk, `剛翼`, is shown as a distributed control system rather than merely wings for flight.

Individual feathers can be controlled for sensing, movement, extraction and attack. During the crisis he evacuates the people trapped in the damaged building—reported as all 76 occupants—while Endeavor continues to hold High-End's attention.

This supplies unusually rich modeling evidence in one sequence:

- environmental attention;
- parallel task management;
- high-speed civilian triage;
- precise micro-control;
- willingness to sacrifice feather resources;
- rapid transition between rescue and combat support.

Fire also damages the feather resource, so his capacity is neither unlimited nor independent of Endeavor's operating environment.

Their teamwork therefore contains friction and cost, not magical compatibility.

# 12. No.188 — Endeavor's body limit returns the public battle to private history

## 12.1 The heat limit is not merely a combat weakness

During the High-End fight Endeavor's accumulated body heat becomes a central constraint:

`俺の体は 熱が篭り続ける`

The narration/internal sequence immediately reconnects this limit to his children:

`だからおまえを`

`だからおまえたちを`.

This is one of the most important retrospective payoffs in the volume.

Earlier `個性婚` material established that Endeavor engineered a successor project partly to obtain a body/power combination that could overcome his own thermal limitation.

V20 places that same bodily limitation inside the current No.1 battle.

The public hero's physical problem and the father's abusive instrumental project are therefore causally continuous.

He cannot narratively become a clean new symbol by simply performing well enough in public; his present strength is inseparable from the history of what he did in response to perceived bodily insufficiency.

## 12.2 High-End refuses the easy decisive finish

Endeavor attempts a decisive high-temperature attack, including Prominence Burn. High-End survives through extreme regeneration/adaptation and bodily separation tactics.

The opponent therefore turns Endeavor's signature escalation into another cost cycle rather than allowing one spectacular move to settle the new-symbol question.

## 12.3 V20 ends at apparent failure, not victory

High-End lands a devastating facial strike. Endeavor appears down in the rubble.

The volume then cuts to Shoto and the family watching the live broadcast.

This is the correct prospective endpoint.

Historical V1 analysis already discussed Endeavor's eventual victory and its public meaning, but that outcome is not available here.

At `V20:p199`, the only admissible conclusion is:

> **Endeavor has asked the public to watch him; his family is watching; the first major live test of that claim appears, at this instant, to have broken him.**

The moral and symbolic experiment is unresolved.

# 13. Character-modeling updates

## 13.1 Midoriya Izuku

**State delta**

Midoriya's rescue-first model expands from emergency extraction and technical precision toward **protection of ordinary conditions** and **recognition without absolution**.

**New context coverage**

- one-on-one confrontation with a comparatively nonlethal criminal mirror;
- conflict in which secrecy/ordinariness is itself part of the rescue objective;
- rapid transition from combat to scheduled peer performance;
- post-conflict discretion rather than public victory behavior.

**New decision/behavior evidence**

- refuses to yield because Gentle's success would destroy an event carrying other people's hopes;
- recognizes that Gentle is also relationally motivated;
- does not equate that understanding with permission to continue;
- returns immediately to the communal obligation once the threat is contained.

**Relationship-specific update**

Midoriya and Gentle achieve adversarial mutual recognition without alliance or moral equivalence.

**Speech/register update**

No major stable register shift; urgency remains direct and earnest rather than contemptuous.

**Contradictions / uncertainty**

V20 does not establish how Midoriya would respond to Gentle after longer rehabilitative change.

**Model-readiness change**

Remains `strong`; materially strengthened.

## 13.2 Jiro Kyoka

**State delta**

Private musical identity becomes publicly enacted on self-authored terms and integrated with her hero identity.

**New context coverage**

- family reflection;
- live performance under large audience attention;
- creative leadership payoff;
- receipt of post-performance gratitude.

**New decision/behavior evidence**

- performs confidently once role is chosen;
- tolerates personal visibility when it serves a purpose she endorsed;
- accepts peers' thanks despite embarrassment.

**Relationship-specific update**

Parents support choice rather than prescribe musical inheritance. Classmates treat her skill as valuable without permanently claiming it.

**Speech/register update**

Embarrassed/private register remains distinct from decisive technical/performance leadership.

**Contradictions / uncertainty**

Professional future relationship between music and hero work remains OPEN.

**Model-readiness change**

Remains `moderate`, strongly strengthened.

## 13.3 Eri

**State delta**

Eri crosses a major reconstruction threshold from a model dominated by trauma/guilt to one that now includes **spontaneous positive affect, ordinary curiosity and self-motivated pleasure**.

**New context coverage**

- large but safe audience environment;
- entertainment;
- food/novel sensory experience;
- low-stakes curiosity after the performance.

**New decision/behavior evidence**

- laughs/smiles spontaneously;
- remains engaged with ordinary festival space;
- asks about unfamiliar pleasurable experiences;
- tries a candy apple and responds to sweetness.

**Relationship-specific update**

# 15. Power-philosophy updates

## 15.1 Love: relational power does not equal relational legitimacy

La Brava's Quirk literalizes the idea that relationship can generate power. The stronger the love, the stronger the amplification.

But MHA refuses the inference that power proves righteousness.

The ethical equation is:

`bond -> capacity`

not

`bond -> moral authorization`.

This provides a useful counterpoint to One For All's voluntarily transferred relational inheritance. Both are relational powers; their ethics depend on what relationships and actions they sustain.

## 15.2 Music and Quirks as noncoercive force

The School Festival broadens the power ledger beyond emergency utility. Quirks and trained non-Quirk skills can be organized to change affect, attention and participation without coercion.

This is not metaphorical rescue replacing literal rescue. It is a broader account of what socially useful capability can do once immediate danger is absent.

## 15.3 Fierce Wings as distributed micro-control

Hawks demonstrates a power model almost opposite Endeavor's concentrated thermal escalation.

Fierce Wings supports:

- parallel sensing;
- individual transport;
- mass evacuation;
- distributed rescue;
- attack;
- rapid allocation across many small tasks.

Its weakness under fire and finite feather resource prevent reading it as costless omnipresence.

## 15.4 Endeavor's heat limit and the ethics of compensating for bodily constraint

The return of `俺の体は 熱が篭り続ける` is not a technical footnote. It exposes the moral history of one attempted solution to embodied limitation.

Endeavor did not merely train around a weakness. He attempted to solve it through reproductive instrumentalization of spouse and children.

V20 therefore strengthens a major series distinction:

> **having a real physical constraint does not justify treating other people as components in a solution architecture.**

# 26. Final synthesis

Volume 20 is a volume about what happens after someone becomes visible.

Gentle spends years trying to become visible enough that history cannot forget him. La Brava's love gives him the first audience whose recognition is intimate rather than statistical, and that same bond ultimately makes him abandon the public act he thought would prove his existence. Midoriya defeats him but does not need the victory to become spectacle.

Class 1-A then demonstrates another use of public attention. Jiro steps onto a stage not because the crowd's gaze validates her existence, but because she has chosen to use a private skill for other people's experience. The performance succeeds when the audience becomes participant rather than consumer of heroic greatness. Eri's smile is the most important result precisely because she is not being asked to thank a rescuer. For a moment, she is simply a child enjoying something.

The second half scales the same problem up to national hero society. The Billboard Chart can rank visibility. Hawks can manipulate and operationalize visibility. Endeavor, newly No.1, can ask people to watch him. None of those acts can force trust.

And the people watching are not interchangeable.

The public sees the top-ranked hero fighting a monstrous threat. Hawks sees a partner whose combat presence can anchor a wider rescue field. Rei sees a man who has caused profound harm and may be trying to face it. Natsuo sees a father for whom attempted change does not erase injury. Shoto sees both father and No.1 hero at once.

That is why V20 must end where it does.

At `V20:p199`, there is no victory yet to simplify the problem. Endeavor has made a public promise of observable conduct and then appears to fall under the first major test. The manga freezes the experiment before validation.

The V20 governing result is therefore:

> **Visibility is not legitimacy. What matters is what a person does once other people have reason to watch: whether attention becomes possession, performance, rescue, accountability, or responsibility to people whose judgments cannot be commanded.**
