# 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 4 asked how a person can become the author of a body, power, opportunity, and identity whose starting conditions were not freely chosen. Volume 5 asks the relational question that follows:

> **What does it mean to recognize another person's agency without possessing, patronizing, or overwriting it?**

The volume's governing concept is **recognition without possession**.

That formulation unifies material that can otherwise look like three separate dramatic peaks: Uraraka versus Bakugo, Midoriya versus Todoroki, and Bakugo versus Todoroki.

In the opening match, the stadium crowd thinks Bakugo's repeated explosions against Uraraka prove cruelty toward an obviously weaker girl. Aizawa rejects that reading so sharply that he tells anyone who sincerely believes it to go home. Bakugo is not prolonging the fight because he does not recognize Uraraka. He is doing the opposite: `ここまで上がってきた相手の力を認めてるから 警戒してんだろう`. He recognizes the danger of her touch, refuses both gendered patronization and tactical complacency, and forces her to reveal the strategy she developed independently.

After the match, Bakugo initially attributes that strategy to Midoriya. Midoriya corrects him: `全部……麗日さんが君に勝つ為に考えて組んだんだよ`. If Bakugo found the plan troublesome, then it was Uraraka herself who made him so. Midoriya therefore performs a small but important act of recognition: he returns authorship to the person who actually produced the plan rather than accepting reflected credit for helping her earlier.

The Todoroki material enlarges the same problem from a school relationship into a family system. Endeavor's language is saturated with possession and instrumentality. Shoto has an `義務` to surpass All Might. Midoriya is a useful `テストベッド`. Shoto is the successor through whom Endeavor's ambition should be realized. When Midoriya answers `轟くんも あなたじゃない`, he states the ethical proposition Volume 5 will spend the rest of the Sports Festival testing:

> **Another person's power, achievement, pain, and future do not become yours merely because you contributed to their conditions of existence.**

This is why Midoriya's later `君の!! 力じゃないか!!` carries more weight than generic encouragement. He is not telling Todoroki that family history does not matter. He is telling him that Endeavor's causal role does not exhaust the meaning of Todoroki's body.

Todoroki's childhood memory then supplies the positive alternative. His mother does not tell him to deny inheritance. She tells him `血に囚われることなんかない` and `なりたい 自分に なっていいんだよ`. Blood can be acknowledged without becoming destiny. The fire can remain historically entangled with Endeavor while becoming ethically and psychologically Todoroki's own.

Endeavor's response demonstrates the opposite form of recognition. When Shoto finally ignites, Endeavor immediately interprets the act as acceptance of him: `やっと俺を受け入れたか!!`. He sees the same physical event Midoriya sees, but reads it through ownership. Where Midoriya says *that is your power*, Endeavor says *that proves my project was right*.

Todoroki corrects that interpretation after the match. He tells Endeavor that nothing has been overturned so easily. In the decisive instant, `お前を忘れた`. The liberation was not reconciliation with the father. It was a moment in which the father ceased to occupy the center of Todoroki's consciousness.

Bakugo's final match creates a third version of the recognition problem. He wants Todoroki to use his full power, but not because Bakugo has become therapeutically concerned with Todoroki's family trauma. He explicitly says he does not care about Todoroki's family circumstances or feelings. His demand is competitive: a victory against an opponent who withholds a meaningful part of himself cannot satisfy Bakugo's internal criterion of victory. When Todoroki's fire falters, Bakugo reads the outcome not as a glorious first place but as contamination of the thing he wanted to prove.

The medal ceremony makes that philosophy explicit:

`こんな一番…何の価値もねえんだよ`

and, more importantly,

`世間が認めても俺が認めてなきゃゴミなんだよ!!`

That is an extreme, ego-driven standard, but it is not empty vanity. Bakugo distinguishes **public rank** from **internally legitimate achievement**. All Might even identifies this as a rare capacity to maintain an `不変の絶対評価` inside a world of constant relative evaluation, while telling Bakugo to accept the unwanted medal as a `傷` that should prevent him from forgetting the imperfection of the win.

The volume therefore refuses to make recognition synonymous with kindness. Aizawa's professional recognition can sound harsh. Bakugo's recognition can take the form of dangerous seriousness. Midoriya's recognition becomes intrusive `余計なお世話`. Uraraka's father recognizes her pain precisely by refusing to treat one defeat as a closed future. Todoroki begins recognizing his mother as someone with whom he must speak, not simply a source of trauma or a person from whom he must stay away.

All Might gives the most explicit hero-side formulation after Midoriya apologizes for allowing concern for Todoroki to interfere with the succession task:

`余計なお世話ってのは ヒーローの本質でもある`.

Heroism is, in part, meddling where one has not been formally invited.

But Volume 5 immediately limits the romantic appeal of that idea. Recovery Girl refuses to let Midoriya and All Might turn bodily destruction into heroic proof. Midoriya's mother later makes visible another cost: a body destroyed in public does not belong only to the person destroying it. It terrifies people who love and watch him. The volume ends with Midoriya reaching a new criterion:

`誰も心配することのない 僕なりのやり方が要る`.

Only then can `“僕が来た”って言えるようになる`.

This is the key development from self-authorship to mutual recognition. Midoriya's body must become *his* way of being a hero, but “his way” must also recognize that he exists in relationship with people whose fear for him is morally relevant.

The volume's concise formulation is therefore:

> **Volume 5 argues that heroism, competition, inheritance, and care become legitimate only when they take the other person's agency seriously: recognition must neither patronize the person nor convert them into someone else's instrument, and even self-sacrifice must eventually recognize the rescuer as a person others are allowed to care about.**

# 2. Narrative architecture

## 2.1 Uraraka–Bakugo completes V04's autonomy setup

Volume 4 ended before the substantive fight, after Uraraka declined Midoriya's prepared plan because Iida's earlier decision to challenge rather than depend on a friend made her reconsider her own reliance. Volume 5 now tests whether that independence produces meaningful competence.

It does.

Uraraka repeatedly rushes Bakugo at close range, appearing at first to be consumed by his superior firepower. The crowd interprets the visual asymmetry as proof of sadistic overkill. The fight's real structure is hidden from them. Every low blast breaks the arena into more rubble. Uraraka deliberately lets Bakugo's attacks create the material for her eventual meteor shower. She conceals the accumulating resource until the field above him is filled with debris.

Momo is among the people who understand the plan once it becomes visible. Uraraka has been directing Bakugo's attention downward and building a second attack surface overhead. When the debris falls, she charges him simultaneously.

The plan fails because Bakugo possesses enough instantaneous output to destroy the entire debris field and defend against the rush. But its failure is a **capacity failure**, not a thought failure. Uraraka reaches her Quirk's bodily `許容量` and collapses still trying to continue.

That distinction matters for the model. V05 does not say “Uraraka tried hard despite being outclassed.” It says she independently designed a plan capable of making Bakugo say `危ねぇな`.

## 2.2 Public spectatorship misreads professional seriousness

The opening match also revises the Sports Festival's public-legibility thesis. Visibility does not guarantee accurate recognition.

The crowd has access to the same spectacular surface everyone sees: Bakugo is standing while a smaller girl is repeatedly blasted backward. Without access to the tactical model inside Bakugo's head or to Uraraka's concealed plan, the public supplies an easier moral story.

Aizawa's intervention is important because he defends neither Bakugo's manners nor violence in the abstract. His point is narrower: anyone who reached this tournament stage deserves to be evaluated as a dangerous competitor until evidence says otherwise.

The volume therefore distinguishes:

- public emotional interpretation;
- professional threat assessment;
- opponent-to-opponent recognition;
- and actual hidden capability.

Those layers can diverge badly.

This becomes an early warning for later hero celebrity culture. Being watched does not mean being understood.

## 2.3 Defeat reveals Uraraka's private urgency

After losing, Uraraka returns quickly to an upbeat peer-facing register. She says Bakugo was simply very strong and that she needs to work harder. `負けたからって負けてられんよ` is genuine resilience.

But the subsequent phone call with her father prevents that resilience from being mistaken for emotional invulnerability.

Her father refuses to let one tournament loss become existential. Professional scouts evaluate more than one matchup; there will be other opportunities and another year. He asks why she is trying to live so urgently: `何を生き急いどんのや`.

Uraraka's answer returns to her family. She wants to help them soon. Her economic motive is therefore not merely a stable prosocial value; it can create **self-imposed time pressure**.

Her father answers by releasing her from that pressure. She does not need to rescue the family immediately. Her kindness itself convinces him she can become a good hero.

The sequence creates a useful private/public split:

> **Uraraka can stabilize peers with cheer and forward motion while reserving the full expression of frustration and dependency for family.**

That is much richer than modeling her as simply cheerful or emotionally transparent.

## 2.4 Endeavor reduces two students to functions

The corridor meeting between Midoriya and Endeavor is a compact demonstration of instrumental cognition.

Endeavor immediately evaluates Midoriya's Quirk through power comparison. He notes that the wind pressure produced by a finger resembles All Might-class output. But the apparent compliment quickly becomes functional placement. Midoriya is useful because Shoto must exceed All Might, and a match against a student with an All Might-like Quirk will provide data.

`テストベッド` is the decisive word.

Endeavor is not currently interested in Midoriya's personhood at all. Midoriya is an experimental environment for Shoto. Shoto, in turn, carries the `義務` to fulfill Endeavor's comparative ambition.

Midoriya's answer rejects both substitutions simultaneously:

- `僕は オールマイトじゃありません`
- `轟くんも あなたじゃない`

Neither student is reducible to the older hero through whom Endeavor interprets him.

## 2.5 Midoriya–Todoroki turns competition into intrusive rescue

The Midoriya/Todoroki fight begins as a tactical problem. Midoriya notices that Todoroki's ice has a bodily limit. The colder he becomes, the slower and weaker his output grows. Midoriya infers that the unused fire side would solve the thermal problem.

But the match then becomes morally unstable. Midoriya understands that everyone in the tournament is seriously pursuing a future and first place. Todoroki's claim that he can win while withholding half his power therefore feels disrespectful to the stakes of the people he defeats. Midoriya demands `全力でかかって来い!!`.

This is not pure altruism. After the match, he explicitly says that although Todoroki looked so sad that Midoriya began thinking about meddling, something else was also true: `それ以上にあの時僕はただ…悔しかった`.

That admission is vital.

Midoriya's intervention combines:

- desire to rescue;
- frustration at being unable to win;
- resentment toward being defeated by a deliberately incomplete opponent;
- identification with everyone else who is competing seriously;
- and a belief that Todoroki is harming himself by defining half his body as another person's possession.

The scene is therefore stronger than a simple “Midoriya sacrifices victory to save Todoroki” reading. He is simultaneously rival and rescuer.

## 2.6 Todoroki's `Origin` restores an earlier desire

Chapter 39 does not create Todoroki's heroic desire. It retrieves it.

As a child he tells his mother that he does not want to become like his father and, specifically, does not want to become a person who hurts her. His mother asks the crucial question: `でも ヒーローには なりたいんでしょ？`

Todoroki's desire for heroism therefore predates the mature strategy of winning with ice to negate Endeavor.

That distinction matters enormously. His current anti-Endeavor identity is not the deepest available layer of self. Beneath it is a prior wish to become a hero without reproducing the father's abuse.

The remembered message `なりたい 自分に なっていいんだよ` gives Todoroki a criterion that does not require either obeying Endeavor or living as Endeavor's permanent negative image.

When he ignites and thinks `俺だって ヒーローに…!!`, the fire is temporarily detached from paternal ownership and reconnected to his own desire.

## 2.7 Endeavor immediately tries to repossess the moment

The victory of that moment is made ethically visible by Endeavor's failure to understand it.

From the stands he shouts that Shoto has finally accepted him and should now fulfill his ambition. Later he calls Shoto an `俺の上位互換` — an optimized version of himself.

The irony is exact. Todoroki has just used fire because Midoriya helped him think *this is mine*. Endeavor interprets the same event as *this proves you are mine*.

Todoroki's later correction — `あの時 あの一瞬は お前を忘れた` — therefore becomes one of the strongest self-authorship lines so far. The opposite of being owned by Endeavor is not necessarily hating Endeavor harder. It is being able, even briefly, to act without organizing the self around him.

## 2.8 Recovery Girl makes sustainability an adult responsibility

Midoriya's match is tactically more controlled than his earliest OFA use. Aizawa explicitly observes that Midoriya is not merely flailing destructively: he is beginning to calibrate output, and the ugly finger-breaking strategy is his current best available route under severe constraints.

But “current best” is not allowed to become “acceptable long term.”

Recovery Girl refuses that conversion. She tells All Might not to praise the behavior and later says she will not continue healing this kind of injury. Midoriya's distorted hand should remain an admonition. All Might must help him find a different method rather than continuing a destructive loop simply because the loop can win or save someone.

This is a crucial institutional correction to the heroic-sacrifice motif:

> **professional care is not impressed merely because self-destruction produces morally admirable outcomes.**

## 2.9 All Might moves from self-replication toward successor differentiation

The hospital conversation contains one of the most important early revelations about All Might.

Midoriya sees classmates with powerful Quirks and asks the question his insecurity naturally produces: might someone else have been a better successor? One For All layered onto an already powerful Quirk could create an extraordinary hero.

All Might reveals that he, too, was `無個性`.

That initially appears to explain Midoriya as identification: All Might saw his younger self in a Quirkless boy. All Might confirms that at first he did overlap the two. But he then revises the relation. Midoriya has already exceeded All Might's expectations many times. All Might believes there is `君にしか導き出せないもの` — something only Midoriya can derive.

Succession therefore advances from:

**I recognize myself in you**

toward:

**I recognize that you must become someone I cannot pre-script.**

That is the benevolent successor architecture becoming more fully anti-Endeavor.

## 2.10 The final turns public first place into a legitimacy crisis

Bakugo's Sports Festival objective was never simply “have the number 1 written next to my name.” His final makes the distinction explicit.

Todoroki's emotional uncertainty causes him to suppress the fire he briefly begins using. Bakugo experiences this not as strategic luck but as degradation of the competition itself. `俺じゃあ力不足かよ` reveals the status wound inside the demand: withholding full power can feel like a judgment that Bakugo is not worthy of it.

He insists that a person standing before him in the final should be there to win. He wants `完膚なきまでの一位` — first place produced through unambiguous superiority over an opponent who is genuinely trying.

He receives the official championship and rejects its meaning.

The podium visualizes the contradiction almost grotesquely: the institutional winner is literally chained and restrained on the number-one platform because he refuses the symbolic interpretation the institution wants to confer.

## 2.11 The epilogue turns “rescue” back onto relationships

The volume's final movements distribute consequences across three families.

Iida leaves the festival after learning that his admired brother has been attacked by the Hero Killer. Tensei wakes and apologizes to Tenya for losing despite being the older brother Tenya admired. The prospective V05 boundary gives us acute injury, grief, and destabilization — but **not yet** the revenge behavior that follows. That must remain V06 evidence.

Todoroki chooses to visit his hospitalized mother. He explains that avoiding her was itself shaped by the belief that his existence drove her suffering. If he is to use his whole body and become a hero again, they need to meet and talk extensively. Even if she does not want it, he decides to `救け出す` her. This is potentially caring and potentially intrusive; the text does not yet resolve the mother's perspective. What matters now is that Todoroki understands his own recovery as relational rather than purely internal.

Uraraka returns home to find that her parents traveled to see her after watching the tournament. The family she has been trying to hurry herself into rescuing crosses distance to care for her instead.

Finally, Midoriya returns to Inko. She has been repeatedly fainting from fear while watching him destroy himself. Midoriya realizes that “supporting him” never meant “not being worried.” His body is part of other people's emotional world.

That recognition produces the final line of development:

`誰も心配することのない 僕なりのやり方が要る`.

The solution to self-destruction is no longer only better efficiency. It is a form of heroism compatible with remaining a person inside a network of care.

# 3. Character-state ledger

## 3.1 Midoriya Izuku

### Current state

Volume 5 substantially strengthens Midoriya's model while also correcting any tendency to idealize his intervention in Todoroki's trauma.

His core rescue disposition remains stable, but the volume demonstrates that his `余計なお世話` can emerge from mixed motives: compassion, competitiveness, frustration, identification, and moral conviction can operate simultaneously.

### Recognition policy

Midoriya repeatedly attributes agency away from himself when evidence requires it.

After Uraraka loses, Bakugo assumes the sophisticated strategy must have come from Midoriya. Midoriya corrects him and gives the plan back to Uraraka. With Endeavor, Midoriya rejects substitution in both directions: he is not All Might, and Todoroki is not Endeavor.

This suggests a stable recognition tendency:

> **Midoriya is unusually sensitive to the difference between helping constitute another person's success and owning that success.**

### Competition and rescue are not separable systems

Against Todoroki, Midoriya's rescue impulse does not replace his desire to win. It tangles with it.

He is angry that everyone is giving everything while Todoroki intends to defeat them with half of himself. He is frustrated by his own inability to land meaningful damage. He simultaneously sees Todoroki as profoundly sad.

This makes his heroic psychology more credible. He does not stop being a competitive adolescent when he recognizes another person's suffering.

### Successor insecurity

After seeing Todoroki and other powerful students, Midoriya asks whether someone else ought to have inherited OFA. This is not simple modesty. He can imagine the utilitarian argument: give the stockpiled power to an already exceptional Quirk holder and create a far stronger result.

All Might's answer begins to stabilize an important counter-model. Midoriya was not chosen solely because of missing power; his conduct already exceeded expectation. More importantly, All Might now expects Midoriya to derive something that All Might himself could not prescribe.

### Embodiment and sustainability

V05 is the largest early change yet in Midoriya's body model.

Aizawa validates that his finger-based destruction is tactically reasoned rather than meaningless chaos. Recovery Girl nevertheless draws a hard medical boundary. Inko then supplies the relational consequence of the same behavior.

Midoriya's resulting thought changes the reason for self-preservation:

- V02: preserve an arm because it remains tactically necessary.
- V05: preserve the self because people who care about him are morally entitled not to be repeatedly terrorized by his self-destruction.

The emerging sustainable-hero criterion is therefore both **operational and relational**.

## 3.2 Bakugo Katsuki

### Current state

Volume 5 sharply strengthens the evidence that Bakugo's aggression cannot be modeled as simple contempt for weaker opponents. He is capable of recognizing an opponent precisely by refusing to patronize them.

### Uraraka: threat recognition rather than gendered leniency

Bakugo warns Uraraka to withdraw because once the match begins the pain will be real. During the fight, he maintains distance and repeatedly blocks her approach because being touched creates an immediate loss condition.

Aizawa's interpretation is explicit: Bakugo is cautious because he recognizes the strength of a person who reached this stage.

When Uraraka's concealed plan is revealed, Bakugo calls it dangerous. Afterward he seeks causal attribution. His first guess — Midoriya supplied the plan — shows how highly he rates Midoriya's analytic influence. When Midoriya corrects him, Bakugo receives new evidence that Uraraka herself constructed the strategy.

### Full-power opponent requirement

The Todoroki final clarifies a pattern already visible in V02 when Bakugo was enraged by Midoriya's refusal to use his full power.

For Bakugo, an opponent who withholds capacity can make victory feel like status humiliation. `俺じゃあ力不足かよ` exposes the inference: *do you not think I am enough to require everything?*

He explicitly does not care about Todoroki's family psychology in that moment. His demand is competitive rather than therapeutic.

### Internal legitimacy versus public rank

Bakugo's statement at the podium is one of the strongest early keys to his value system:

`世間が認めても俺が認めてなきゃゴミなんだよ!!`

This means his status orientation is not reducible to social applause. He desperately wants No.1, but the No.1 label must satisfy an internal causal story: he must have defeated the best version of the relevant opponent through his own superiority.

That is why the official championship produces rage rather than satisfaction.

### Reprocessing Midoriya

When Todoroki asks whether Midoriya has always been like this, Bakugo remembers the childhood/Sludge-era rescue grammar — the boy who reaches because someone looked like they needed saving.

He does not verbalize a mature reinterpretation yet. But the memory itself matters: new testimony from Todoroki activates old evidence that Midoriya's meddling was not a one-off status maneuver aimed specifically at Bakugo.

**Readiness:** remains `moderate`, substantially strengthened. Major missing dimensions remain care behavior, ordinary friendship, adult relations, and later explicit moral revision.

## 3.3 Uraraka Ochako

### Current state

V05 converts V04's autonomy intention into demonstrated independent competence and adds a much richer private emotional model.

### Tactical authorship

Uraraka independently creates a layered plan using Bakugo's own explosions as resource generation. She hides the accumulation until the meteor attack can be combined with a close-range rush.

The plan fails because of Bakugo's output and her body's limit, not because she simply charges without thinking.

### Persistence and body

Even after reaching `許容量`, she attempts to stand and continue. This creates an early parallel with Midoriya's action bias, although the frequency and degree of self-destruction are not comparable enough yet to treat them as identical traits.

### Public resilience / private vulnerability

With peers she quickly turns defeat into future-directed effort: `負けたからって負けてられんよ`.

With her father, the emotional load emerges. Her desire to help the family has become urgency. Her father de-escalates that urgency and validates her character rather than demanding immediate success.

This suggests a conditional emotional pattern:

> **Uraraka tends to metabolize pain into upbeat forward motion socially, while family relationships permit more direct expression of frustration and dependency.**

### Recognition by others

The volume repeatedly protects Uraraka from being interpreted as Midoriya's extension:

- she refuses his prefabricated strategy at the V04 boundary;
- she executes her own plan;
- Aizawa defends the seriousness with which Bakugo treats her;
- Midoriya returns authorship to her after the match;
- her parents travel to care for her after she tried to hurry herself into caring for them.

**Readiness:** `moderate (strengthened)`.

## 3.4 Todoroki Shoto

### Current state

Volume 5 does not “solve” Todoroki. It changes his problem from **negative certainty** to **productive uncertainty**.

At the V04 boundary he knows what he refuses: being Endeavor's tool. After Midoriya's intervention he can no longer be sure that refusing fire is equivalent to being himself.

### Original heroic desire

The childhood memory establishes a pre-Endeavor-negation aspiration. Shoto wants to become a hero without becoming someone who hurts his mother.

This is decisive for simulation. His deepest available motive at V05 is not hatred of Endeavor. Hatred became a strategy wrapped around an earlier heroic desire.

### Fire and self-authorship

Using fire against Midoriya does not mean forgiving Endeavor. Shoto later says the decisive feature of the moment was that he forgot his father.

The best current formulation is:

> **Todoroki can use the inherited fire as his own only when its meaning is no longer monopolized by Endeavor — either through obedience or through refusal.**

### Reflective uncertainty

Afterward he says he no longer knows what he ought to do or whether he is right. This is growth, not regression. A rigid identity has loosened enough to become examinable.

### Family repair as heroic work

By the end he concludes that simply freeing himself is insufficient. There are things that must be settled. To use his whole body and begin pursuing heroism again, he must meet his mother and talk.

His `救け出す` formulation is important but should not yet be romanticized. He is still deciding on intervention partly from his own need, and the mother's response is not yet available. Preserve her agency as an open variable.

**Readiness:** `moderate (materially strengthened)`.

## 3.5 Iida Tenya

V05 first shows Iida using peer competition exactly as he said he wanted to use it: as developmental information.

He tells Midoriya that the Midoriya–Todoroki match will become `糧`. Against Todoroki he constructs a plan around the opponent's newly visible mixed power and uses Recipro Burst to force a close-range decision. Todoroki explicitly recognizes that Iida's fine-grained approach escaped his earlier planning.

Then the volume removes competition from the center entirely. News arrives that Tensei has been attacked. Iida leaves the Sports Festival rather than remain for the ceremony.

The hospital scene establishes intense attachment and idealization: Tensei apologizes for losing despite having an excellent younger brother who admired him.

**Prospective constraint:** V05 does not yet show what Tenya will *do* with this injury. Do not import revenge, concealment, or Hosu conduct from V06.

**Readiness:** `moderate (strengthened; major stress transition now open)`.

## 3.6 All Might / Yagi Toshinori

V05 adds three important dimensions.

First, All Might explicitly frames intrusive concern as part of hero essence: `余計なお世話ってのは ヒーローの本質でもある`.

Second, he reveals that he too was Quirkless. His predecessor had a Quirk but believed in and raised him. This makes OFA's relational inheritance more explicit: the line is not a genetic or natural-power elite reproducing itself.

Third, All Might recognizes the danger of simply reproducing himself through Midoriya. He initially overlapped Midoriya with his own past, but now says Midoriya has repeatedly exceeded his imagination and may derive something only Midoriya can.

That is a major mentoring distinction from Endeavor:

> **All Might increasingly treats successful succession as divergence, not duplication.**

At the medal ceremony he also shows skill in converting Bakugo's rejected victory into developmental meaning, telling him to carry the medal as a wound/reminder.

**Readiness:** `moderate (strengthened)`.

## 3.7 Endeavor

V05 substantially strengthens the emerging model.

### Comparative cognition

Endeavor interprets people through hierarchy and utility. Midoriya is interesting because his power resembles All Might's and therefore provides a useful test for Shoto.

### Ownership grammar

- Shoto has an `義務` to surpass All Might.
- Midoriya is a `テストベッド`.
- Shoto's fire use is interpreted as accepting Endeavor.
- Shoto is called an `俺の上位互換`.
- Shoto is expected to fulfill Endeavor's ambition.

The consistency of this language matters. Endeavor does not merely pressure Shoto harshly; he systematically reduces the child's independent trajectory to the elder's comparative project.

### Recognition failure

Endeavor's most important V05 error is interpretive. He sees a real developmental breakthrough and gets its meaning almost exactly backward. Shoto's fire use emerged when Endeavor briefly disappeared from the center of his self-conception. Endeavor treats it as confirmation of paternal ownership.

**Readiness:** remains `emerging`, strongly strengthened but still relationship-concentrated.

## 3.8 Recovery Girl

Recovery Girl becomes an increasingly important adult counterweight to heroic romanticism.

She refuses to celebrate Midoriya's destructive method, directly tells All Might that he must search for another route, and establishes a medical consequence: she will not endlessly repair the same category of avoidable catastrophic damage.

Her ethic can now be summarized:

> **care sometimes requires refusing to make self-destruction consequence-free.**

**Readiness:** `emerging (strengthened)`; behavior remains concentrated in medical/pedagogical contexts.

## 3.9 Stain / ステイン

V05 establishes an `emerging` prospective baseline.

His initial ideology is already legible:

- fame and money are signs of degraded hero motivation in his rhetoric;
- most people calling themselves heroes are false;
- All Might alone is treated as the legitimate ideal;
- he murders or cripples people in the name of that purification;
- he believes society is covered in vanity/hypocrisy and must be forced to notice.

The League's approach to the notorious `ヒーロー殺し` shows that his symbolic reputation already extends beyond individual crimes.

**Accountability constraint:** ideological critique does not soften the fact that V05 presents him as an attacker who has catastrophically injured Ingenium and uses lethal violence as purification.

**Readiness:** `emerging`, with ideology and voice much stronger than ordinary-life or relational breadth.

## 3.10 Ingenium / Iida Tensei

V05 provides the first direct enough material to open an `insufficient/emerging` entry.

He is seen only in catastrophic injury, so ordinary behavior must not be inferred. What is available is relationally powerful: while barely conscious, he apologizes to Tenya because the younger brother admired him and he believes losing has betrayed that admiration.

This suggests that his heroic self-conception includes responsibility toward the people for whom he serves as an example.

# 4. Power philosophies

## 4.1 Power belongs to the user even when its history does not

Midoriya's `君の!! 力じゃないか!!` does not erase inheritance. It changes ownership grammar.

Todoroki's fire came through Endeavor's reproductive project. That historical fact remains. But causal origin does not grant permanent moral title to the parent.

Volume 5 therefore strengthens H7:

> **legitimate inheritance permits re-authorship; illegitimate power relations insist that origin determines ownership.**

## 4.2 Full power and full personhood are related but not identical

Both Midoriya and Bakugo demand Todoroki's fire, but for different reasons.

Midoriya sees the refusal as psychological captivity and competitive disrespect. Bakugo sees it primarily as corruption of the victory test.

Neither demand means “maximum output is always morally superior.” The point is that Todoroki's current restriction is not simply a tactical preference. It is an identity constraint imposed by unresolved trauma.

## 4.3 `余計なお世話` defines a risk-bearing theory of care

All Might's statement that meddling is part of hero essence formalizes a pattern visible since V01. Midoriya acts on perceived need before receiving permission.

The series now has to solve the ethical limit of that principle. Uninvited intervention can rescue someone who cannot yet articulate need; it can also become paternalism. Todoroki's mother visit leaves that tension open deliberately.

## 4.4 OFA inheritance becomes non-genetic social reproduction

All Might's Quirkless origin is a major ontology/ideology datum.

The OFA line can reproduce heroic capacity through recognition and entrustment across people who do not naturally possess the relevant power. The predecessor chose All Might despite his lack, and All Might chooses Midoriya under a structurally similar condition.

But V05 refuses simple replication: Midoriya must become something only he can derive.

## 4.5 Bakugo's power philosophy distinguishes rank from validity

Bakugo wants No.1 but rejects No.1 when the causal route to the title does not meet his internal criterion.

This complicates the first-pass shorthand “power = status.”

A better formulation is:

> **Bakugo seeks status as public proof of an internally validated superiority; status without the proof condition is worthless or humiliating.**

## 4.6 Midoriya's body becomes a moral object of care

V01–V02 mostly frame bodily destruction through feasibility and professional sustainability. V05 adds another principle: the hero's body matters because other people have legitimate attachments to it.

This is an early move away from martyrdom as private property.

# 5. Hero Society / institutional analysis

## 5.1 Public evaluation can be morally confident and professionally wrong

The Uraraka–Bakugo crowd is a clean case. Spectators believe they are defending a vulnerable girl from excessive violence, yet their interpretation erases Uraraka's actual competence and Bakugo's threat assessment.

Aizawa's response establishes a professional norm: evaluate the opponent who actually reached the field, not the social role spectators project onto them.

## 5.2 The Sports Festival measures more than victory

Uraraka's father explicitly understands that one loss does not exhaust professional evaluation. Scouts can observe tactics, adaptability, matchup behavior, and potential even when a student does not win the bracket.

This complicates the apparent tournament meritocracy. The festival creates rank, but the labor market consumes richer information than rank alone.

## 5.3 Gendered protection can become misrecognition

The crowd's desire for Bakugo to end the match gently is not treated as harmless courtesy. In context it assumes Uraraka is not a competitor worthy of serious vigilance.

The manga's position here is not “violence against girls is good.” It is that **professional equality can require taking a female competitor's capability seriously enough not to substitute protective condescension for evaluation.**

## 5.4 U.A. contains adult checks against student self-destruction

Recovery Girl's refusal to heal repeated destructive behavior matters institutionally. U.A. is not uniformly a machine that rewards sacrifice. At least one senior medical authority is willing to place a hard limit on the prestige logic surrounding an exceptional student.

## 5.5 Competition produces data that students use on one another

Iida explicitly converts the Midoriya–Todoroki match into `糧`. Todoroki learns from Iida's unexpectedly fine close-range tactics. Bakugo studies Todoroki's earlier match and demands the power he knows exists.

The Sports Festival is therefore not only sorting students for outsiders. It is a **peer-learning information environment**.

## 5.6 The winner's podium reveals institutional-symbolic coercion

Bakugo is the official winner and refuses the meaning of the award. U.A. physically restrains him and places the medal on him anyway, played as dark comedy.

The scene literalizes a recurring series problem: institutions can confer a public identity that the person does not experience as legitimate.

This does not make Bakugo's behavior reasonable. It makes the gap between external title and internal recognition visible.

# 6. Recognition and failed rescue

## 6.1 Uraraka is misrecognized by the crowd and correctly recognized by opponents/peers

The crowd sees victimhood. Bakugo sees a dangerous touch-based win condition. Aizawa sees Bakugo's vigilance as rational recognition. Momo recognizes the concealed plan. Midoriya later restores authorship to Uraraka.

This is one of the clearest early demonstrations that **recognition quality depends on what the observer is capable of perceiving**.

## 6.2 Uraraka's father recognizes the person behind the career objective

Uraraka experiences family support as a reason to succeed quickly. Her father recognizes that this reciprocity can become pressure and explicitly tells her she does not need to rush.

The care relationship refuses instrumentalization in both directions: the parents once refused to use the child's Quirk as family labor; now the father refuses to let the child turn herself into an emergency solution to their economic difficulty.

## 6.3 Midoriya recognizes Todoroki's captivity but risks overreach

Midoriya sees what spectators largely cannot: Todoroki's refusal of fire is not merely a strategic handicap but an imposed meaning structure.

His intervention succeeds in opening a possibility, but the volume itself labels the behavior `余計なお世話`. Recognition does not automatically grant perfect authority to intervene.

## 6.4 Endeavor recognizes output while failing to recognize agency

Endeavor is not blind to Shoto's ability. He may be the person most obsessed with it. His failure is precisely that **capability recognition without person recognition remains instrumentalization**.

## 6.5 Bakugo's recognition is real but ethically narrow

Bakugo recognizes capability, threat, and competitive seriousness very well. V05 does not yet show equivalent competence at recognizing another person's emotional conditions as morally relevant.

His response to Todoroki is almost a control case:

> he accurately perceives that Todoroki is withholding a meaningful capacity, but explicitly refuses the family/trauma context that explains why.

## 6.6 Midoriya finally recognizes himself as someone others may rescue from his own method

Inko's fear forces a reversal of perspective. Midoriya is accustomed to treating his body as the price *he* chooses to pay. He begins to understand that the choice is socially distributed because people who love him experience the damage too.

This is an early seed of the later proposition that the rescuer can also become someone who needs saving.

# 7. Villain formation, rescue, and accountability

## 7.1 Stain introduces ideological purification

Shigaraki's V02–V03 violence is symbolically anti-All-Might but initially developmentally immature. Stain enters with a much more articulated moral filter.

He distinguishes “true” and “false” heroes, treats fame and money as corruption, and elevates All Might as the singular person worthy even to kill him.

This gives villain formation a new route:

> **villainous violence can grow not only from failed recognition or grievance, but from absolutized moral judgment.**

## 7.2 Stain's critique and method must remain separate

The social observation that hero incentives can include fame/money may later overlap with legitimate institutional criticism. V05 does not authorize the inference that Stain is therefore morally correct.

His method is already catastrophic violence against people, including Ingenium.

## 7.3 Shinso remains an important control case

V04 showed stigma + mis-sorting without villainy. V05 now adds another non-deterministic path: Stain appears ideologically committed to violence despite recognizing an authentic heroic ideal in All Might.

The developing villain model therefore cannot be reduced to a single “society failed them” causal pipeline.

## 7.4 The League recognizes Stain's symbolic value

Someone associated with the emerging villain organization seeks the notorious Hero Killer. At the prospective boundary, the important fact is not future alliance detail but **recruitment logic**: villain organizations also scout publicly legible ideological capital.

This parallels V03's hero-side scouting market in a darker form.

# 8. Japanese language, character voice, and key vocabulary

## 8.1 `認める`

Aizawa's `相手の力を認めてるから` is a load-bearing V05 usage. Recognition means taking capability seriously enough to alter behavior around it.

Bakugo's later `世間が認めても俺が認めてなきゃ` turns the same verb inward: public recognition does not automatically create subjective legitimacy.

The volume therefore uses recognition in both interpersonal and self-evaluative registers.

## 8.2 `テストベッド`

Endeavor's loanword is unusually cold. Midoriya is not described as a rival, student, or person but as a test environment for Shoto. It is among the clearest early lexical markers of instrumentalization.

## 8.3 `義務`

`ウチの焦凍には オールマイトを超える義務がある` frames Shoto's future as obligation owed to Endeavor's comparative project.

## 8.4 `君の力`

`君の!! 力じゃないか!!` is simultaneously possessive correction and identity intervention. The emphatic `君の` reassigns the fire from father-symbol to current user.

## 8.5 `血に囚われる`

Rei's remembered `血に囚われることなんかない` does not deny kinship. `囚われる` frames blood as something that can imprison interpretation if allowed to dictate identity.

## 8.6 `なりたい 自分`

`なりたい 自分に なっていいんだよ` gives Todoroki positive authorship language. The desired self is not defined solely through rejection.

## 8.7 `お前を忘れた`

Todoroki's later `あの時 あの一瞬は お前を忘れた` is one of the volume's most exact liberation statements. The key is not acceptance, victory over, or forgiveness of the father; it is temporary de-centering.

## 8.8 `余計なお世話`

Ordinarily “unwanted/unnecessary meddling.” All Might's `ヒーローの本質でもある` elevates a socially awkward phrase into heroic doctrine while preserving its ethical friction.

## 8.9 `不変の絶対評価`

All Might's description of Bakugo's inner standard distinguishes absolute self-evaluation from the Sports Festival's constant `相対評価`. This is unusually explicit psychological vocabulary for Bakugo's status system.

## 8.10 `傷`

All Might tells Bakugo to receive the medal as a `傷` so he will not forget. The award is re-signified from proof of victory into memory of insufficient victory.

## 8.11 `僕なりのやり方`

Midoriya's final phrase is crucial. He does not conclude “I need to become All Might's way but safer.” He needs **his own** method.

That directly pays off V02's `僕の力` and V05 All Might's statement that Midoriya can derive something only he can.

## 8.12 Speech-model consequences

- **Midoriya:** polite/deferential baseline can sharpen into direct declarative moral correction when personhood is at stake (`轟くんも あなたじゃない`).
- **Bakugo:** insults remain constant, but threat language often coexists with precise competitive attribution; do not equate rudeness with inattention.
- **Uraraka:** socially buoyant phrasing can mask much stronger private frustration; family speech opens emotional permeability.
- **Todoroki:** sparse declaratives become halting/reflexive when self-conception destabilizes; he is capable of direct intimate disclosure to rivals when the subject is load-bearing.
- **Endeavor:** imperative/comparative language repeatedly encodes ownership and hierarchical evaluation.
- **All Might:** public grandiosity coexists with unusually explicit mentoring abstraction when discussing development.

# 9. Visual and formal analysis

## 9.1 Uraraka–Bakugo is staged as a problem of visible asymmetry

The early pages repeatedly show a small figure being driven backward by black explosive bursts. The surface image encourages the same mistake the crowd makes: visible force appears to equal total control.

The meteor reveal changes the meaning of prior panels retroactively. Rubble that looked like mere evidence of Uraraka being overwhelmed becomes accumulated tactical material. The fight's visual grammar therefore teaches the reader to distinguish **damage seen** from **agency unseen**.

## 9.2 Aizawa's commentary interrupts the stadium's moral camera

The crowd's condemnation occupies the same public space that is supposed to make students legible. Aizawa's intervention functions almost like an editorial cut into that interpretation. The manga refuses to let audience sentiment determine what the panels mean.

This is formally important for later public-opinion material: spectatorship is itself something the narrative can place under judgment.

## 9.3 Todoroki's split body becomes a contested field of authorship

V04 made the half-red/half-white body an inherited division. V05 repeatedly overlays past and present so that the left side changes meaning in real time.

The scalding memory, the mother's words, Midoriya's broken fingers, Endeavor in the stands, and the returning flame all occupy the same fight architecture. The fire is never “just” an attack effect. It is the visible site where family history and present choice collide.

## 9.4 Endeavor is visually positioned as a reader who gets the scene wrong

When Shoto ignites, Endeavor's ecstatic reaction follows immediately. The sequence deliberately gives the reader enough interior access to know the meaning Endeavor cannot see.

This produces dramatic irony through montage:

- reader: Shoto temporarily remembered his own desire;
- Endeavor: Shoto finally accepted paternal design.

The same spectacle therefore supports opposite interpretations depending on whether the observer recognizes interior agency.

## 9.5 The Bakugo winner's podium is institutional contradiction made physical

Bakugo occupies the highest platform while chained, muzzled, and furious. The visual joke is also conceptually exact.

The institution says **winner**.

Bakugo's body says **I do not consent to what this win means**.

The scene turns V04's concern with procedurally valid but subjectively illegitimate advancement into a grotesque public emblem.

## 9.6 The volume exits spectacle through increasingly private spaces

After the stadium climax, the manga moves through:

- a hospital room with Tensei and Tenya;
- the psychiatric hospital where Shoto meets Rei;
- Uraraka's home;
- Midoriya's kitchen with Inko.

The movement is from maximum public visibility to intimate relational consequence. The tournament's meaning is not settled by the scoreboard; it is settled partly in what the students carry home.

## 9.7 Midoriya's damaged hand becomes a social image

The final self-reflective image centers Midoriya's bandaged hand while faces of people worried about him accumulate around the thought. The body is visually recoded from personal weapon into **shared object of concern**.

That is a major formal step toward distributed heroism: even the protagonist's own body is no longer treated as belonging only to his private will.

# 10. Body, embodiment, family, and inheritance

## 10.1 Uraraka's body sets the limit her strategy cannot

Uraraka's plan is cognitively successful enough to threaten Bakugo, but Zero Gravity's nausea/capacity limit ends the match. This is useful modeling evidence because it separates tactical intelligence from bodily throughput.

Her refusal to stop until the body stops her also creates an early self-pressure warning. Family urgency may increase willingness to push past comfort even when she is not a Midoriya-style habitual self-destroyer.

## 10.2 Midoriya's hand becomes durable consequence

Recovery Girl's refusal to erase the meaning of repeated injury changes the role of healing in the story. Medical technology cannot be a narrative license for infinite martyrdom.

The injured hand is retained as `戒め` — an admonition. Embodiment begins accumulating memory.

## 10.3 Todoroki's fire is simultaneously biological inheritance and social wound

The fire is genetically his, socially produced through Endeavor's reproductive project, emotionally associated with paternal abuse, rejected as contaminated, and then partially reclaimed.

No single one of those descriptions is sufficient.

This is why “use your full power” is psychologically different for Todoroki than for Bakugo or Midoriya. Full bodily integration requires a reinterpretation of inheritance, not merely technique.

## 10.4 Rei's remembered breakdown cannot be reduced to a symbolic mother-side

V05 provides enough childhood material to complicate any simplistic equation:

- mother = good half;
- father = bad half.

Rei is a victim of coercive family conditions and also the person who scalded Shoto during psychiatric collapse. Shoto attributes the causal environment to Endeavor, but the injury and mother's agency remain real.

The family model must therefore preserve trauma without flattening persons into symbolic functions.

## 10.5 All Might and Midoriya make Quirklessness part of the inheritance chain

The revelation that All Might was also Quirkless creates a striking anti-eugenic contrast with Endeavor's project.

Endeavor attempts to engineer superior hereditary combination.

OFA reaches major successors through **recognition of people who naturally lacked the desired power**.

This does not make OFA meritocratically pure; it does make its reproductive logic relational rather than reproductive-biological.

## 10.6 Inko makes the hero's body familial

Inko's repeated fainting is played comedically, but the underlying point is serious. Midoriya's bodily risk is experienced by his mother as fear.

His final insight therefore broadens the body ethic:

> a heroic body is not merely a tool whose owner may rationally spend it; it is also the embodied person to whom others are attached.

# 11. Continuity, callback, and payoff ledger

## 11.1 V04 Uraraka autonomy setup → V05 tactical authorship

V04 ends with Uraraka choosing not to use Midoriya's prefabricated Bakugo plan. V05 immediately validates the choice by giving her a plan that genuinely threatens Bakugo.

This is a clean payoff from stated autonomy to demonstrated competence.

## 11.2 V01 rescue-before-permission → V05 `余計なお世話`

The sludge-villain scene established a body that moves because another person looks like they need saving. V05 finally gives the pattern explicit professional language: unrequested meddling can be part of hero essence.

## 11.3 V01 `君はヒーローになれる` → V05 All Might was also Quirkless

The succession relation becomes biographically recursive. All Might once occupied the position of a Quirkless aspirant recognized by a predecessor who already had power.

The V01 recognition scene is now part of a lineage of recognition.

## 11.4 V02 `僕の力` → V05 `君の力` → `僕なりのやり方`

The self-authorship sequence is increasingly explicit:

1. Midoriya wants OFA to become `僕の力`.
2. Midoriya tells Todoroki the fire is `君の力`.
3. Midoriya concludes he needs `僕なりのやり方`.

Power ownership develops into method ownership.

## 11.5 V03 `君が来た` public-arrival burden → V05 sustainable arrival criterion

Midoriya is frustrated that losing to Todoroki means he failed All Might's request to announce himself publicly.

The volume ends by revising the criterion. Being able to say `僕が来た` cannot mean merely producing All Might-scale spectacle. It requires a mode of action that does not repeatedly terrify the people who care about him.

## 11.6 V03–V04 Bakugo evidence sensitivity → V05 Uraraka attribution correction

Bakugo initially assumes Uraraka's plan is Midoriya's, which is itself evidence of how seriously he takes Midoriya's analysis. Midoriya supplies contrary evidence. Bakugo does not receive a speech proving instant emotional revision, but the attribution question is now explicitly reopened.

## 11.7 V02 Bakugo full-power demand → V05 Todoroki full-power demand

The same trigger recurs across different opponents: withheld capacity threatens the legitimacy of Bakugo's desired victory.

This strongly supports a stable decision/value rule rather than treating the V02 reaction as purely Midoriya-specific.

The **intensity** remains relationship-conditioned; the **full-effort legitimacy standard** is broader.

## 11.8 V01 Bakugo rescue memory → V05 Todoroki asks whether Midoriya was always like this

Todoroki's question activates Bakugo's memory of Midoriya reaching toward him because he looked like he needed help. This is a high-value recontextualization node.

Bakugo has not yet completed the moral revision, but the story has placed old evidence back into active cognition.

## 11.9 V04 `お前を完全否定する` → V05 `お前を忘れた`

This is perhaps Todoroki's most important directional shift.

V04 autonomy is defined negatively: defeat/refute father.

V05's liberating instant is defined by absence: father temporarily ceases to organize the self.

## 11.10 V03 All Might wants a successor → V05 successor divergence

The successor project becomes less like role replacement. All Might says Midoriya has exceeded his imagination and can derive something unique.

A healthy inheritance is beginning to require difference.

## 11.11 V02 Recovery Girl rebuke → V05 hard medical boundary

The earlier warning becomes policy. She will not continue repairing the same kind of catastrophic self-destruction and tells All Might to search for another method.

## 11.12 V02 Iida admiration for Ingenium → V05 ideal wounded

The admired older brother becomes physically vulnerable and apologizes to the younger sibling who looked up to him. Iida's heroic ideal is no longer abstractly available; it has been injured in front of him.

# 12. Secondary-character findings

## 12.1 Momo continues to function as process recognizer

Momo is among the students who understand Uraraka's hidden tactical structure once the meteor plan reveals itself. This coheres with her V02 tendency to evaluate how a result was produced rather than only who won.

The evidence remains too narrow to advance readiness, but it strengthens an existing cognition pattern.

## 12.2 Tokoyami gains a useful defeat-response marker

Peers describe Tokoyami as visibly frustrated after Bakugo exploits Dark Shadow's light weakness. This is limited evidence, but it prevents a future model from treating his restrained presentation as absence of competitive investment.

No readiness promotion yet.

## 12.3 Kirishima/Bakugo peer normalization continues

Kirishima remains comfortable enough around Bakugo to engage him as a peer rather than treating his aggression as untouchable prestige. V05 does not yet provide enough new independent Kirishima motive to promote him, but the relationship remains important for later low-stakes reconstruction.

## 12.4 Rei Todoroki becomes a prospective person, not only a flashback function

Rei has not yet supplied enough present-tense speech for a robust model. But V05 moves her from purely remembered trauma into a current relationship endpoint. Shoto chooses conversation and rescue as future tasks, making her future response an essential variable rather than an assumed resolution.

## 12.5 Uraraka's parents reverse the rescue direction

The parents physically travel to see Ochako after she has been trying to become financially useful to them. The family dynamic is therefore reciprocal in action, not merely in stated values.

# 13. Prospective versus retrospective reading

## 13.1 What V05 legitimately establishes about Todoroki

A prospective V05 reader knows:

- Endeavor deliberately shaped his birth/childhood around surpassing All Might;
- his mother experienced severe psychological deterioration and scalded him;
- young Shoto wanted to be a hero without becoming someone who hurt his mother;
- his mother told him blood need not imprison him and he could become the self he wanted;
- Midoriya helps him reclaim fire for one moment;
- using fire does **not** equal forgiving Endeavor;
- after Midoriya, Shoto becomes uncertain rather than resolved;
- he chooses to meet Rei and talk as part of restarting heroism.

Do not import later Todoroki-family developments, Dabi identity, reconciliation outcomes, or Endeavor's later self-revision into this state.

## 13.2 What V05 legitimately establishes about All Might's past

The reader now knows All Might was Quirkless and inherited OFA from a Quirk-bearing predecessor who believed in and raised him.

The full predecessor identity/history and later vestige ontology remain outside the current boundary unless supplied elsewhere prospectively.

## 13.3 What V05 legitimately establishes about Stain

The reader knows:

- he is called the Hero Killer;
- he has attacked Ingenium;
- he condemns fame/money/status-driven heroism;
- he treats All Might as uniquely legitimate;
- he uses catastrophic violence in pursuit of purification;
- villain actors seek him because of his notoriety.

Do not import his full origin, later ideological speech, Quirk mechanics, or Hosu resolution from V06.

## 13.4 What V05 legitimately establishes about Iida

Iida receives the news, leaves the festival, reaches his injured brother, and experiences the collapse of an admired figure.

The volume ends before it gives us a decision rule for vengeance. Therefore:

- grief = supported;
- destabilized ideal = supported;
- future revenge behavior = not yet V05 evidence.

## 13.5 What V05 establishes about Bakugo's Sports Festival victory

The reader can already reject two simplistic readings:

1. “Bakugo only wants public first place.”
2. “Bakugo goes easy on weaker/female opponents when socially expected.”

V05 shows the opposite on both points.

It does **not** yet justify the later claim that Bakugo has integrated rescue/care into victory.

## 13.6 Retrospective significance

With full-series hindsight, V05 is an early hinge for several later developments:

- Midoriya must discover sustainable, distributed heroism rather than solitary bodily expenditure;
- Bakugo's internal legitimacy standard will later be morally reworked rather than erased;
- Todoroki's liberation requires family-facing integration rather than simple anti-Endeavor negation;
- Stain's symbolic critique will prove socially contagious;
- Iida's procedural heroism will shortly be tested by personal vengeance.

Those trajectories may guide questions, but must not overwrite V05's prospective uncertainty.

The first pass correctly anticipated Stain as ideological stress test. V05 already shows something important before the full arc: villainous violence can emerge from moral absolutism/purification, not only from deprivation or failed recognition.

# 16. Revised interpretation

The first five volumes now form a coherent sequence:

1. **V01 — Recognition before power:** can heroic worth be seen before conventional ability makes it legible?
2. **V02 — Judgment under power:** once power exists, can it be governed, allocated, and used professionally?
3. **V03 — Public legibility:** how does competence become socially visible, ranked, recruited, and symbolized?
4. **V04 — Self-authorship:** how can people make inherited bodies/powers/opportunities genuinely their own?
5. **V05 — Recognition without possession:** can other people acknowledge that self-authorship without patronizing, appropriating, or rewriting it?

Volume 5 is therefore the relational completion of the V04 problem.

Self-authorship is not enough if every surrounding observer insists on supplying the meaning.

- Uraraka authors a plan; the crowd sees victimhood, Bakugo sees threat, Midoriya returns credit.
- Todoroki authors one moment of fire; Endeavor sees paternal acceptance, Midoriya sees reclaimed personhood.
- Bakugo wins officially; the institution sees champion, Bakugo sees a failed proof.
- Midoriya tries to author heroic sacrifice; Recovery Girl and Inko insist that his body exists inside relationships he cannot ethically ignore.
- All Might begins by recognizing himself in Midoriya, then develops toward recognizing Midoriya's difference.

The most important revised series proposition after V05 is:

> **MHA increasingly treats recognition as an ethical act of accurate attribution: seeing what another person can do, what belongs to them, what they actually chose, and where one's own causal involvement stops.**

That helps unify heroism, mentorship, rivalry, family, and institutions without collapsing them into the same moral role.

# 21. What this volume changes in the current MHA model

## 21.1 Recognition becomes more important than visibility alone

V03 made public visibility central. V05 demonstrates that being visible can still produce severe misrecognition. The crowd sees Uraraka and Bakugo clearly and understands the match poorly.

The analytical model must therefore separate **legibility** from **accurate recognition**.

## 21.2 Personhood-versus-toolhood receives its clearest relational formulation

`轟くんも あなたじゃない` and `君の力` now directly oppose Endeavor's `テストベッド`, `義務`, and `上位互換` grammar.

Hypothesis 7 is no longer merely a broad thematic abstraction. It is embedded in dialogue.

## 21.3 Self-authorship requires observers who can stop claiming ownership

Todoroki's internal liberation is not complete when he uses fire. It must survive Endeavor immediately claiming the act as proof of himself.

V05 therefore adds an external condition to V04's authorship thesis: the surrounding relationship must eventually learn to recognize the other person as separate.

## 21.4 Bakugo's hierarchy model needs a major refinement

He seeks No.1, but not any No.1.

His desired status is **evidentiary**. It must prove something to his own internal standard. Public recognition without that proof is `ゴミ`.

This makes him psychologically harsher but more coherent than a simple approval-seeking model.

## 21.5 Midoriya's rescue ethic becomes both more intrusive and more accountable

The manga openly validates `余計なお世話` as heroic essence. At the same time, Recovery Girl and Inko deny Midoriya the right to treat every successful intervention as justification for self-destruction.

The mature form of his ethic will have to solve both problems:

- intervene when someone cannot yet fully ask;
- remain a person rather than becoming disposable rescue infrastructure.

## 21.6 All Might's succession becomes generative rather than reproductive

The Quirkless parallel explains why All Might recognized Midoriya, but the mentor's own statement refuses to end there. Midoriya has exceeded his imagination and must derive something unique.

A healthy succession does not reproduce the predecessor unchanged.

## 21.7 Hero Society's public contest produces both insight and distortion

The Sports Festival successfully surfaces:

- Uraraka's tactical intelligence;
- Shinso's earlier institutional mismatch;
- Iida's close-range speed strategy;
- Todoroki's overwhelming potential;
- Bakugo's adaptive combat ability.

But the same audience misreads Uraraka–Bakugo and the winner's podium confers a meaning Bakugo rejects.

The institution is productive without being epistemically perfect.

## 21.8 Villain formation is now explicitly plural

By V05 the corpus contains at least three different warning structures:

- Shigaraki: immature destructive grievance shaped by mentorship;
- Shinso: stigma/misrecognition without villainy;
- Stain: moral purification ideology converted into violence.

The later synthesis must preserve causal plurality.

## 21.9 The Sports Festival ends by returning students to family

The arc begins as national spectacle and ends in family rooms.

This is not accidental tonal cleanup. It reasserts that hero students are not merely public assets, rankings, or future brands. They remain children embedded in relationships that competition cannot supersede.

## 21.10 Midoriya's new start line is sustainable distinctiveness

V01's start line was being recognized as someone who could become a hero.

V05's start line is harder:

> **develop a way of being a hero that is distinctly his, effective enough to inherit the Symbol's responsibility, and sustainable enough that the people who love him do not have to experience his heroism primarily as fear.**

That is the most consequential forward change produced by Volume 5.
