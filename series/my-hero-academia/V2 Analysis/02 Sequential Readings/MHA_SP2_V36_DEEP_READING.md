# My Hero Academia - Second-Pass Deep Reading, Volume 36

## 0. Status, method, and prospective lock

This reading begins strictly from the frozen V35 boundary at `V35:p187`. No Volume 37 or later event is used to settle a V36 ambiguity. The governing method remains `MHA_SP2_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2_1.md`: Japanese-primary evidence, prospective truth freeze, page-bounded claims, language/register attention, visual and institutional analysis, explicit uncertainty, cumulative character/relationship-state routing, and character-model updates based only on observed canonical behavior.

The strongest volume-level transition is:

> **engineered interdependence confronting weaponized grievance -> interdependence under disproportionality, where maturity means continuing after the plan works but the expected decisive blow does not.**

Volume 35 proved that the hero coalition could distribute indispensable functions without reducing everyone to interchangeable parts. Volume 36 stress-tests that architecture. Shoto successfully stops Toya's immediate offensive, yet the Todoroki problem is not thereby morally or relationally solved. Jiro, Tokoyami and Hawks break AFO's mask, yet AFO remains capable of contingency. Endeavor lands the kind of apparently decisive attack the operation was built to create, yet the enemy has prepared something beyond the expected endpoint. Bakugo, the Big Three, Erasure, Support Course, Business Course, power-generation students, professional heroes and U.A.'s moving structure all function as designed, yet Shigaraki's body still exceeds their calculated damage model.

V36 therefore refuses the simple equation **good coordination = clean victory**. Its mature heroes are defined by what they do when scale remains unfair after they have done the right preparatory work.

AFO/Shigaraki offers the ideological countermodel. Difference, in his account, produces incomprehension; incomprehension produces fear and exclusion; the answer is to place everyone beneath one hand as equally exploitable subjects. The hero coalition answers not by erasing difference but by making differentiated capacities mutually necessary. Jiro's sound is not Endeavor's fire. Tokoyami cannot replace Endeavor but can coordinate with Hawks. Tamaki cannot imitate Mirio or Nejire, but their trust and energy enlarge what only he can do. Bakugo's strongest movement is literally produced by an embodied side effect of accumulated strain while he recalls the road Midoriya walked and the infrastructure his classmates are maintaining around him.

The volume's deepest antithesis is therefore:

> **AFO seeks equality by making everyone equally subordinate. The hero side seeks solidarity by preserving unequal, non-substitutable persons inside a shared operation.**

# 1. Governing transition: when the correct plan is still not enough

V35 made distributed heroism concrete as an engineered system. V36 asks what happens when that system performs correctly but encounters bodies, contingencies and histories larger than its predictions.

Three repeated structures organize the volume.

First is **successful action without final closure**. Shoto lands the move he built specifically for Toya and stops the immediate Dabi offensive. Jiro/Tokoyami/Hawks create the exact opening the anti-AFO plan needs. Endeavor follows through with overwhelming force. Bakugo's new equipment and team coordination allow the heroes to attack Shigaraki's real body. Tamaki creates an attack whose scale is deliberately without a fixed upper ceiling. None of these accomplishments is fake; none alone closes the war.

Second is **relation as power without relation becoming absolution**. Shoto tells Toya to stop while explicitly rejecting the idea that abuse or birth condition makes current destruction inevitable. Endeavor accepts that his past does not disappear and refuses the fantasy of being reborn as an innocent man. Jiro acts because her friends cried. Tamaki and Nejire convert received recognition into combat capacity. Bakugo's late-volume breakthrough is inseparable from Midoriya, All Might and the people sustaining U.A., yet the source does not dissolve his distinct ambition into sentimental dependence.

Third is **the enemy's repeated attempt to turn relationship into vulnerability**. AFO needles Endeavor with Toya. Shigaraki/AFO calls Bakugo Midoriya's hanger-on and plans to make his corpse a present for Midoriya. The villain worldview treats attachment as leverage because it assumes all dependence is domination. The hero-side answer is not to stop caring. It is to continue caring while developing enough reciprocal structure that no one person's capture automatically ends everyone else's agency.

# 2. No.351 `二つの赫灼` - two forms of fire, two theories of inheritance

## 2.1 Shoto accepts kinship without accepting Toya's causal fatalism

The Todoroki confrontation begins with Toya wielding Endeavor-derived Flashfire techniques at heat beyond what his body was built to tolerate (`V36:p007-013`). Dabi's technical competence matters. He is not merely flailing with an inherited flame. He has studied the hero whose recognition he wanted, reproduced techniques and pushed them into a body-destroying regime.

Shoto's response is explicit: `お前は俺が止める` (`p008-009`). The line is simpler than a rescue speech because the immediate problem is simpler: Toya is trying to kill people and must be stopped.

Yet Shoto does not solve the moral problem by declaring Toya incomprehensible. He places them inside the same family history. Both are sons formed under Endeavor's reproductive/hero project; both inherited bodies and expectations they did not choose. That shared causality is exactly why Shoto rejects Dabi's move from **cause** to **inevitability** (`p012-018`). Endeavor's abuse, Toya's birth condition and hero society's failures explain; they do not exhaustively author the current choice to burn others.

This is a mature continuation of the recognition-without-exculpation model from V35. Shoto can say, in effect, “I know why this wound exists” and “you still have to stop” in the same confrontation.

## 2.2 Dabi's inheritance is imitative and consumptive

Dabi's fire philosophy remains oriented toward an absent judge. His mastery is organized around proving something to Endeavor and making Endeavor's world suffer. His body is therefore not simply a resource he risks. It is part of the accusation.

Visually, Dabi's flames increasingly consume the body that produces them (`p009-022`). This is the opposite of Shoto's V36 technical project. Toya turns inherited technique into a proof that can end with the prover. Shoto builds a bodily circulation system intended to sustain two inherited capacities without letting either become the whole self.

The chapter title's “two Flashfires” thus names more than two hot attacks. It identifies two answers to inheritance:

- **Dabi:** reproduce the father's fire so intensely that body and world become evidence against the father;
- **Shoto:** re-engineer inherited fire and ice into a self-authored body capable of affirming a life beyond the father's design.

# 3. No.352 `必殺技` - Shoto reverses Endeavor's reproductive logic

## 3.1 `赫灼熱拳 “燐”` makes bodily integration a self-authored practice

The pre-battle training explanation is one of the clearest power-philosophy scenes in the Todoroki line (`V36:p023-031`). Shoto describes circulating hot and cold blood simultaneously and centering the balance around his heart. The move is not “use fire, then cool down.” It requires both thermal inheritances to operate as one regulated system.

He explicitly contrasts his aim with Endeavor's original reproductive logic. Endeavor wanted cold as a mechanism for lowering the heat generated by a superior flame. Shoto says he is going the opposite way (`p027-029`). Ice is not an accessory to maximize the father's desired fire; fire and ice become coequal parts of Shoto's own embodied control.

The identity claim is more important than the technical one. Shoto wants to be able to affirm for himself the meaning of his own birth/existence within that family (`p029-031`). He does not retroactively validate Endeavor's project. He takes a body produced instrumentally and makes its meaning non-instrumental.

The resulting state, `赫灼熱拳 “燐”` / Phosphor, is therefore a technique of **reappropriation**. It is not reconciliation with Endeavor and it is not a denial of maternal/paternal inheritance. It is the act of refusing to let either parent, either element or the eugenic purpose of his conception monopolize what the body can mean now.

## 3.2 Class 1-A has become internal infrastructure

Shoto's retrospective explicitly credits his classmates with making this development possible (`p030-034`). The class did not leave people behind. Their repeated presence gave him a kind of reassurance from which he could work on himself.

That matters for behavioral reconstruction. Shoto's autonomy should no longer be modeled as independence from others. His mature state is **relational autonomy**: he makes the decision and authors the technique, but he can identify the social scaffold that made self-authorship possible.

This is also a clean counterexample to the idea that help-receiving diminishes individuality. The more Shoto can internalize Class 1-A's support, the more singular his technique becomes.

## 3.3 `交わるよ 無理にでも` - forced intersection without forced forgiveness

In the final exchange, Shoto tells Toya they will intersect even if he has to force the encounter, and then asks him to stop (`p034-038`). The phrasing preserves both agency and coercion. Shoto is not waiting for Toya to consent to relationship before preventing murder. He is also not treating restraint as the end of relation.

`大氷海嘯` transforms the battlefield and drops Dabi (`p036-038`). At this V36 point, the immediate offensive is stopped. The source does not yet authorize a final statement about Toya's survival, future conduct or whether any ordinary sibling relation can become real.

# 4. No.353 `ENDEAVOR` - peer success becomes distributed obligation

## 4.1 Shoto's result travels across the operation as morale, not magical rescue

News of Shoto's successful stop moves across multiple battlefields (`V36:p039-044`). The montage matters because it does not say “Shoto won, therefore everyone is saved.” It says one node's success changes what other nodes believe is possible.

Kirishima's reaction at the Gigantomachia/Jakku defense is especially diagnostic. He converts Shoto's effort into a collective commitment: a member of Class 1-A showed what he could do, so their own defensive line must hold (`p042-043`). This is familiar Kirishima behavior at larger scale. Admiration becomes a prompt to act rather than passive hero worship.

Bakugo's reaction is characteristically compressed: Shoto succeeding is treated as expected (`p044`). The lack of overt sentiment is not lack of recognition. For Bakugo, respect often appears as refusing to be surprised that a peer met the standard.

## 4.2 Iida rejects Shoto's reflexive over-responsibility

Shoto apologizes because Iida's engines were affected by the heat. Iida answers that Shoto has no reason to apologize (`p043-045`). This small exchange is valuable precisely because it occurs after a major victory.

Shoto's tendency to absorb excessive responsibility has not vanished. Iida's care behavior is to correct the allocation of guilt rather than simply praise him. Their relationship has matured into reciprocal regulation: Shoto can act at extreme scale; Iida can still tell him which burden is not his.

## 4.3 Spinner's front reveals grievance as mass mobilization

The heteromorph front is shown rallying around the recovery of Kurogiri and around accumulated discrimination (`p046-049`). The crowd language combines Shigaraki loyalty, group grievance and an instruction to destroy those associated with oppression.

V36 does not yet adjudicate the political legitimacy of every grievance claim or the later outcome of the mobilization. It does establish that AFO's network has succeeded in converting structural resentment into operational manpower. Spinner's symbolic role from V35 is therefore no longer merely preparatory.

## 4.4 AFO targets Aoyama as a dangerous exception

Another front places Kunieda against Aoyama and treats Aoyama's defection as something that cannot be allowed to become an example (`p050-052`). This strengthens V35's restorative-action reading. Aoyama's value is not just tactical. A person successfully leaving AFO's debt architecture threatens the ideology that granted power creates permanent ownership.

# 5. No.354 `ここだ!!` - the anti-AFO plan recognizes both Quirk theft and psychological warfare

## 5.1 Numerical superiority can be a liability against a thief

Hawks identifies AFO's life-support mask as the available physical weakness (`V36:p057-060`). Kamino/Tartarus observation suggests sustained activity without it is not viable. The plan therefore focuses damage on a specific dependency rather than assuming AFO can be worn down by generic volume.

The heroes also avoid simply sending a huge crowd at him. AFO can steal Quirks. More bodies can mean more resources delivered to the enemy (`p059-062`). This is an important institutional correction to conventional superhero arithmetic: sometimes “everyone attack” is not solidarity but bad systems design.

Endeavor and Hawks are selected because their combination can apply high-output pressure while minimizing the number of stealable combatants exposed at once.

## 5.2 Hawks' professional modeling remains probabilistic rather than omniscient

Hawks continuously tracks distance, mask condition, AFO's sensory capacities and his own diminished wing performance (`p061-065`). AFO, in turn, notices that Hawks is slower than before. Both are reading damaged bodies in real time.

Hawks does not assume prior intelligence guarantees the result. His behavior remains a model of adaptive professional cognition: carry a working hypothesis, attack the dependency it identifies, and revise when the enemy behaves outside expectation.

## 5.3 AFO describes himself as a planter of seeds in existing wounds

AFO's taunting of Endeavor is not random sadism. He identifies the Todoroki wound and presses it because it is already fertile (`p063-067`). His language frames his own behavior as planting into “good soil”: he exploits a vulnerability rather than needing to invent the underlying harm from nothing.

That distinction matters for causality. AFO is not the author of Endeavor's abuse. He is highly skilled at finding injuries other people created and converting them into control surfaces.

Hawks recognizes the consequence immediately. The current Endeavor, emotionally destabilized by Toya, could not have fought Dabi effectively; he must stay on the assigned problem and defeat AFO (`p066-069`). This does not excuse Endeavor for not facing Toya. It clarifies why the operation's family-role allocation has a tactical as well as emotional logic.

AFO's reminder that Toya's body was never recovered deepens the destabilization, and Endeavor takes a major wound (`p068-072`).

# 6. No.355 `EXTRAS` - the allegedly ordinary people become the decisive disturbance

## 6.1 Jiro and Tokoyami enter without pretending to replace Endeavor

Jiro and Tokoyami arrive into a battlefield whose scale visibly frightens them (`V36:p073-076`). Their first exchange still sounds like them. Jiro complains about the difficulty of maintaining flight; Tokoyami's response preserves his awkwardness rather than converting the scene into generic solemnity.

Hawks objects because this is not supposed to be their “turn” and they can die here (`p075-077`). Tokoyami's answer is crucial: he cannot become Endeavor, but he can coordinate with Hawks (`p076-078`).

This is V36's distributed-agency thesis in one sentence. Tokoyami does not justify participation by claiming equivalence with the missing No.1 hero. He identifies the function he can perform **because he is different**.

## 6.2 Jiro's fear is first-class character evidence

AFO's presence terrifies Jiro. She recognizes that she cannot comfortably read the incoming attacks or even process the malice quickly enough (`p078-081`). Rather than erase the fear, the chapter uses it to revise her understanding of other people.

She thinks of Midoriya and Aoyama: both have lived under this kind of threat for much longer than she has (`p080-082`). This is high-value empathic updating. Jiro does not need to share their biographies to recognize the scale of fear they have been carrying.

AFO attempts to classify Hawks and the students as diminished or ordinary. His error is categorical. Jiro's answer is not “actually I am secretly top-tier.” She rejects the relevance of his prestige taxonomy. Her friends were made to cry because of him; that is enough reason to act (`p082-084`).

That line should materially change Jiro reconstruction. Under extreme threat, her governing motive remains relational and concrete rather than abstractly heroic. She can be frightened, perceive herself as outmatched, and still escalate because a friend has been harmed.

## 6.3 Heartbeat becomes a systems disturbance rather than a raw-power victory

Jiro's attack does not suddenly overpower AFO's body. Instead, the vibration interacts with a system already damaged by New Order and accumulated Quirk-factor strain (`p083-087`). The vestiges associated with stolen Quirks begin resisting him.

AFO himself regards this as abnormal. The individual vestiges should not ordinarily possess enough strength to revolt (`p085-087`). V36 therefore supports a bounded causal chain:

1. AFO's internal Quirk system has prior damage/instability;
2. Jiro's emotionally driven sound/vibration creates an additional perturbation;
3. the vestiges respond in an unprecedented way;
4. Hawks and Tokoyami exploit the resulting opening.

This is not “friendship magically beats AFO.” It is **a small force becoming decisive because it reaches the right failure mode in a damaged system**.

Hawks' own summary, effectively “dust accumulates,” matches the chapter's title. “Extras” matter because systems fail through accumulation as often as through a single superior blow.

Hawks/Tokoyami then concentrate repeated damage on the same point and break AFO's mask (`p086-088`).

# 7. No.356 `敵について` - AFO's sensory architecture and Endeavor's refusal of moral rebirth

## 7.1 AFO's perception is engineered and therefore can malfunction

With the mask damaged and internal vestige disturbance continuing, AFO explains that much of his ordinary sensory function was lost long ago. He compensated by acquiring vibration, infrared and other sensing Quirks (`V36:p089-094`). Under normal conditions that composite system gives him extremely high recognition capacity.

The current delay is therefore diagnostically meaningful. His perception has not simply become “bad.” An engineered multisensory system is producing abnormal latency because the Quirk factors themselves are rebelling (`p091-095`).

This sharpens the broader AFO model: his apparent godlike perception is technologically/Quirk-mediated, layered and contingent. It can have dependencies and synchronization failure.

## 7.2 Endeavor's internal critic does not offer forgiveness

Endeavor re-enters while AFO continues to attack the family wound (`p095-099`). The most important material becomes internal rather than tactical. A younger/self-critical image tells him he has become even weaker and forces him back toward the origin of his hero fixation (`p099-103`).

The sequence does not culminate in “I am a better man now.” It explicitly rejects rebirth as a fantasy of moral cleanliness. Endeavor's governing insight is that he should not imagine he can become another person and leave the past behind. The weakness, ugliness and harm remain part of what he must continue confronting.

His defining trait is reframed as persistence: `俺は…しつこい` (`p102-103`). Obsessiveness was part of what distorted him. V36 does not magically convert the trait into virtue. It shows the same persistence being redirected from defeating All Might toward bearing responsibility and finishing the present task.

# 8. No.357 `焼身照命!! 手負いのヒーロー` - atonement as future-clearing labor

## 8.1 Endeavor fights through damage without converting bodily cost into moral payment

Endeavor's counterattack is extreme (`V36:p105-113`). He drives AFO through repeated high-output fire, loses bodily capacity and continues applying force to the precise physical vulnerabilities AFO exposes.

The important ethical distinction is that the source does not frame his injury as payment that cancels abuse. Endeavor does not say, “I have suffered enough.” Bodily cost is tactical cost, not moral currency.

AFO accuses him, correctly, of having used his own hands to destroy other people's futures (`p112-113`). Endeavor agrees. The past does not disappear because the accuser is a worse person.

## 8.2 `未来とは若者の進む道だ`

Endeavor's resulting future philosophy is unusually explicit (`p113-115`). The future is the road younger people move along, and the number of roads available to them matters. He therefore has to win so that adults' pasts do not become obstacles that block those roads.

This is the most mature formulation yet of his atonement project. It does not ask the children to validate his improvement. It defines adult responsibility as **removing obstacles from futures one has already damaged**.

He also accepts continued anger, resentment and punishment rather than imagining one heroic act closes the account (`p114-115`). His intention to keep looking at Toya belongs inside the same logic: responsibility persists after the spectacular battle.

## 8.3 AFO also possesses a self-sacrificial contingency

Endeavor's attack leaves AFO's current body in an apparently catastrophic state (`p115-119`). Yet AFO explicitly says the body is already disposable to him and that he has prepared something he can still test/use. The imagery directly associates the contingency with Eri/Quirk-erasure-bullet research (`p117`).

V36 does **not** yet provide enough direct explanation to name the exact mechanism, duration or final effect with confidence. What is canonical at the boundary is narrower:

- AFO anticipated possible destruction of this body;
- he does not regard bodily self-preservation as an absolute constraint;
- a prearranged contingency begins after Endeavor's apparent kill-level attack;
- the contingency is visually linked to the Eri-derived research chain;
- the later state/outcome remains V37+ OPEN.

This also corrects a simple moral contrast in self-sacrifice. Recklessness is not hero-exclusive. AFO can spend his own body because he treats even himself as an instrument of the larger AFO project.

# 9. No.358 `ちょっと進んだ男` - Bakugo's progress is social without becoming submissive

## 9.1 Shigaraki's body turns singularity into adaptive morphology

At floating U.A., Shigaraki's hand/finger proliferation continues under Erasure (`V36:p121-126`). The enemy explanation frames the phenomenon as bodily adaptation: as the accumulated Quirk factors settle into the body under a wide range of external conditions, the body searches for forms able to accommodate the load.

The source continues to distinguish this from an ordinary activated Quirk. Do not generalize this into a universal biological law beyond the observed Shigaraki body. V36 supports **adaptive morphology under singularity-scale load**, not a complete theory of every future Quirk body.

## 9.2 AFO/Shigaraki offers equality through universal exploitation

The fused enemy articulates a political theory (`p126-127`). Large differences create incomprehension; incomprehension creates fear and exclusion; a fragmented world heads toward rupture. His proposed solution is to bring everything beneath his hand, making everyone equally subject to exploitation.

The rhetoric is important because it superficially recognizes a real coordination problem: radically different people can fail to understand one another. The proposed cure is authoritarian flattening. If everyone is equally beneath one sovereign, social difference no longer needs to be negotiated.

Bakugo's answer is not a philosophical essay. He has already absorbed the reality of incomprehension and difference and finds AFO/Shigaraki's monologue intolerable (`p127-131`). He then points to the actual counterexample around him.

## 9.3 Bakugo's attack is literally made by other people's work

Bakugo names the distributed functions sustaining his opportunity (`p130-131`): Monoma and Aizawa are maintaining Erasure; Kaminari and others are keeping the moving stage operating; Momo and the support/logistics teams are restoring damaged footing and structure. His own Strafe Panzer equipment exists because Support Course built it.

This is a major Bakugo modeling atom. Earlier Bakugo often experienced dependence as humiliation or competitive threat. V36 Bakugo can perceive a network of other people's labor as part of the conditions for his own maximum agency without reducing his self-conception to “I am helpless without them.”

His instruction is still aggressively Bakugo: everyone together targets the real body (`p128-131`). The personality surface remains stable while the cooperation heuristic has changed.

Best Jeanist's chapter-title judgment follows: Bakugo has advanced “a little” (`p131`). The understatement is appropriate. Growth is not a personality replacement.

## 9.4 `Howitzer Impact: Cluster` and the rejection of instant social perfection

Bakugo's large attack closes the chapter (`p132-133`). Just before it, he recognizes that people are trying to move forward even when progress takes time. This is the deeper answer to the enemy's impatience with incomprehension.

AFO/Shigaraki says difference should be administratively crushed. Bakugo's current model accepts that people can remain difficult, unequal and imperfect while still moving incrementally toward one another.

That is why “a little advanced” is not a joke at his expense. It is the volume's social theory.

# 10. No.359 `学び舎` - U.A. is a school because noncombat labor counts

## 10.1 The floating battlefield is visibly maintained by students

The enormous U.A. structure is losing altitude under Shigaraki's unexpected body mass and battle damage (`V36:p134-139`). Students replace motors, shafts and damaged components while power-generation and transmission teams keep the platform moving.

V35 established that the battlefield had been engineered by a distributed coalition. V36 shows the ongoing maintenance cost. Infrastructure is not something heroes “have.” It is a continuous social process performed by people who can become exhausted, injured or overloaded.

## 10.2 Business Course turns documentation into hero-society infrastructure

Business Course students are filming inside the moving structure (`p137-139`). Other students understandably ask why they are recording instead of doing more obvious mechanical labor.

Their answer is institutionally significant. Hero education includes producing heroes and connecting heroes to society. Because the electromagnetic barrier prevents ordinary communication, their documentation is one of the few ways the outside world can later know what happened. They intend to preserve the record of how the coalition won (`p138-139`).

This is not combat power, but it is not trivial. V36 explicitly recognizes **memory, mediation and public legibility** as infrastructure. A heroic act that cannot be socially understood can be politically misremembered, mythologized or detached from the collective labor that made it possible.

The Business Course therefore functions as a small answer to MHA's longstanding Symbol problem. Instead of one spectacular body standing for heroism, they are trying to preserve evidence of the network.

## 10.3 Bakugo's maximum attack still does not solve the scale gap

Bakugo's Howitzer Impact produces enormous damage but does not incapacitate Shigaraki (`p140-147`). Shigaraki breaks Bakugo's arm and dismisses his personal growth as irrelevant (`p143-145`).

This is important for Bakugo because the humiliation target has changed. Shigaraki does not merely say “you are weak.” He says Bakugo's development matters only insofar as Bakugo is attached to Midoriya.

The enemy then identifies the one thing about Bakugo that interests him: Bakugo is the person most closely connected to Midoriya (`p147`). Relationship is immediately converted into leverage.

# 11. No.360 `それでも` - Bakugo is attacked through Midoriya, and the Big Three choose continuation

## 11.1 Shigaraki weaponizes Bakugo's oldest insecurity in updated form

Shigaraki plans to use Bakugo's suffering/body as a psychological weapon against Midoriya and calls Bakugo a hanger-on to Midoriya (`V36:p153-156`). The insult is unusually precise.

Early Bakugo organized much of his identity around being ahead of Midoriya and was enraged by the possibility that Midoriya could surpass or pity him. By V36, he has already apologized, accepted Midoriya's strength and learned reciprocal team behavior. The enemy therefore attacks the residue: perhaps all that remains of Bakugo's significance is his relation to the person who became stronger.

The scene does not show Bakugo reverting into denial. That is the behavioral payoff. Relationship can wound him without forcing him back into the old superiority script.

## 11.2 Aizawa's hope for Bakugo is future-oriented, not merely tactical

The hero side tries to extract/protect Bakugo while Aizawa's communication is disrupted (`p148-156`). The adult expectation around him is strikingly ordinary: Bakugo is supposed to graduate and continue toward No.1. The operation is not permitted to redefine the student as disposable simply because the war is existential.

This repeats V35's Aoyama principle in a different register. The young person has a future beyond the function being demanded now.

## 11.3 The Big Three recover an interrupted school future

Mirio, Nejire and Tamaki re-form as a unit (`p157-163`). Their dialogue includes a desire to have the graduation ceremony the war interrupted. This is low-stakes evidence embedded in catastrophe: they are not fighting because combat has become their only imaginable life. They are fighting for the continuation of ordinary institutional time.

Tamaki asks whether they can win; Nejire answers with confidence. Tamaki then reduces the immediate task to something bounded: keep everyone alive until Midoriya arrives (`p150-151`). This is good anxiety regulation. He does not need certainty about the war to identify the next responsible action.

# 12. No.361 `異変` - a joke about friendship exposes the unstable Shimura layer

## 12.1 Mirio tries social provocation and immediately recognizes the wound

Mirio observes that his close-range attacks are not doing enough and tries to disrupt Shigaraki verbally (`V36:p162-170`). His remark that Shigaraki seems not to have friends is partly tactical improvisation.

The response is disproportionate. A childlike/Tenko-linked state erupts and insists that Mikkun and Tomo-chan called Tenko kind, that Mon mattered, and that he did have friends (`p169-170`). Mirio immediately realizes that he struck a genuine wound and apologizes.

This is valuable Mirio modeling. His optimism/social fluency does not mean perfect empathic accuracy. He can make a bad inference under pressure; what distinguishes him is rapid correction rather than defensive insistence that the joke “should not” have hurt.

## 12.2 `志村` re-enters the fused identity problem

The chapter visually names `志村` while childhood memory breaks through the AFO/Shigaraki battle state (`p169-172`). This is stronger evidence than V34's abstract “new personality” description for the persistence of Tenko/Shimura material.

But the correct prospective conclusion remains bounded. V36 supports:

- the fused/current entity is **not psychologically seamless**;
- early-childhood Shimura/Tenko memories can intrude under relational triggers;
- the intrusion includes specific friends, pet/family attachment and affect;
- AFO/Shigaraki's current self-description does not exhaust what remains inside the body/identity system.

V36 does **not** yet settle which self has final control, whether the selves can be cleanly separated or how Midoriya's rescue intention will interact with the fracture.

## 12.3 Tamaki's “weight” changes meaning

While Mirio buys time, Tamaki prepares his large-scale attack (`p172-180`). His earlier insecurity is recalled through the words other people gave him—Mirio, Kirishima, Fat Gum and others who recognized ability he had difficulty carrying himself.

The governing metaphor changes. Other people's expectations once felt like a weight capable of crushing him. At V36's crisis point, that same received recognition becomes something that makes him stronger (`p181-182`, continuing into No.362).

This is not generic confidence. Tamaki's model remains anxious and self-critical. Growth means he can carry relational expectation without interpreting it solely as a threat of disappointing everyone.

# 13. Nejire Hado: curiosity, rejection and relational strengthening

The V36 flashback supplies the most important standalone Nejire reconstruction evidence in the corpus so far (`V36:p176-179`). Her persistent questioning/curiosity had caused peers to distance themselves; other people interpreted her interest negatively rather than reciprocating it.

Tamaki and the Big Three relationship gives that curiosity a place to land. Because people answered and asked her questions in return, she was able to know them and be known by them. She explicitly links having learned so much about her friends to becoming stronger (`p176-179`).

This corrects a shallow model of Nejire as merely cheerful, talkative or inquisitive. Her questioning has a relational history:

- attention is her default route toward people;
- failed reciprocity can turn that route into alienation;
- successful reciprocity converts curiosity into attachment rather than social performance;
- under extreme danger she remains highly expressive, but her confidence is anchored in specific people and shared history.

V36 therefore closes enough of the prior context/relationship gap to justify a substantial readiness promotion.

# 14. No.362 `Light Fades To Rain` - received belief becomes force, then force remains insufficient

## 14.1 Tamaki's ultimate attack is explicitly relational

The opening of No.362 continues Tamaki's internal accounting (`V36:p181-183`). He remembers praise and confidence from people whose words he previously had difficulty accepting. He no longer treats those words only as a burden that exposes his inadequacy.

His Manifest has unusual scaling potential because what he can manifest depends on what he has consumed; Fat Gum's earlier framing is recalled as a belief that Tamaki's capacity lacks the kind of fixed ceiling many other Quirks have (`p178-182`). Nejire's energy is incorporated into the attack.

`混成大夥 波動砲` is therefore a particularly literal image of relational individuality. Tamaki's body produces the attack, but it is assembled from food/animal capacities, training, Nejire's energy and the confidence of people around him.

## 14.2 Shigaraki survives the scale test

The attack is enormous and still fails to produce decisive incapacitation (`p182-185`). Shigaraki invokes prime All Might as the scale comparison: would a body on that level die from this?

This is not narrative invalidation of Tamaki. The chapter has already established why the attack matters. The failure diagnoses the enemy's body as outside the coalition's expected damage model.

The operation now faces the central V36 problem in its purest form: **what do you do after the best available action was genuinely excellent and still insufficient?**

# 15. Bakugo's final V36 state: progress through pain, relation without surrender of self

## 15.1 Cluster produces a whole-body side effect

Bakugo returns despite severe injury (`V36:p186-190`). V36 provides an explicit mechanical explanation. Cluster's concentrated sweat handling has overloaded the usual palm-gland pathway. Accumulated explosive output begins seeking other exits, producing explosions across his body (`p188-189`).

The effect gives him a further speed increase but is painful. This is not a free “awakening.” Growth is embodied as accumulated strain changing what the body can do.

## 15.2 `俺まだ おまえに追いつけるかな 出久`

Inside that pain, Bakugo thinks about Midoriya's path and asks whether he can still catch up (`p189`). This is a decisive inversion of the childhood rivalry without erasing rivalry itself.

He still wants comparative excellence. He has not become indifferent to rank or to Midoriya's strength. What has changed is the direction of the comparison. Midoriya's progress no longer needs to be denied in order for Bakugo to possess a self.

The question “can I catch up?” is compatible with cooperation, apology and admiration. That compatibility is one of the clearest marks of Bakugo's mature model.

## 15.3 AFO/Shigaraki is disturbed by a person without OFA

Bakugo's new movement is fast enough to force genuine surprise/panic from the current Shigaraki/AFO entity (`p189-190`). The enemy explicitly wonders why he is feeling pressure from someone who does not possess OFA.

That reaction has thematic weight. AFO's worldview is organized around exceptional powers, accumulation and possession. Bakugo's threat at this moment is the result of training, body cost, equipment, social infrastructure and a self-authored use of an ordinary inherited Quirk rather than possession of the story's singular legacy power.

The “supporting” rival can disrupt the sovereign without becoming the chosen inheritor.

# 16. Bakugo, All Might and the autograph card: the private child remains inside the competitor

Before the catastrophic final exchange, V36 gives Bakugo a quiet internal image centered on an All Might card and the autograph he never received (`V36:p191-192`). The childhood fan remains legible beneath the aggressive hero persona.

This is unusually valuable low-stakes reconstruction evidence because Bakugo's relationship to All Might is often expressed through victory, professional standard and rivalry with Midoriya. Here the desire is small and almost embarrassingly ordinary: he wanted a signature.

The scene prevents his ambition from being modeled as pure dominance. The original hero attachment included admiration and childish fandom before the social comparison hardened around it.

That private desire immediately precedes the terminal-looking injury sequence, making the card function as a bridge between the boy who admired a hero and the student currently trying to become one.

# 17. The V36 endpoint: apparent terminality is not yet a closed life-state

The final sequence (`V36:p193-197`) escalates from Shigaraki's attack through Bakugo's catastrophic chest/cardiac state. The text explicitly draws attention to the heart (`心臓が—`, `p195`). The final spread shows Bakugo motionless with blood, severe chest damage and his All Might card beside him (`p196-197`).

The staging is intentionally terminal-looking. A responsible prospective reading should preserve that fact rather than softening it.

But the volume ends **there**. There is no V36 medical pronouncement establishing irreversible death, no completed rescue attempt and no later bodily-status observation. Therefore the canonical V36 claim is:

> **Bakugo ends V36 in a catastrophic, apparently terminal cardiac/chest state; exact life/death and recoverability remain OPEN.**

Do not import V37+ resolution into V36. “Bakugo dies in V36” is too strong as a frozen-boundary statement; “Bakugo is merely injured” is too weak for the visual and textual evidence.

# 18. Endeavor's V36 ethical state: responsibility without the fantasy of purification

V36 materially clarifies why Endeavor is unusually reconstructible compared with many atonement-arc characters.

His model does not depend on a single mood of guilt. It now contains a stable decision rule:

1. the harm he caused is real and does not disappear;
2. being hated or punished is not evidence that repair has failed;
3. present usefulness does not purchase forgiveness;
4. the future belongs especially to younger people whose available paths adults can obstruct;
5. therefore his task is to keep clearing space for those futures while continuing to bear the past.

This is not self-forgiveness. It is also not self-annihilation. The internal instruction not to imagine rebirth prevents him from escaping into either “I am redeemed now” or “I am irredeemable, so action is meaningless.”

His old obsessiveness survives as persistence. That continuity is psychologically more convincing than personality replacement and should be preserved in simulation.

# 19. Jiro's V36 readiness jump

Jiro enters V36 with a solid but still middle-resolution model: family/creative context, Class 1-A social behavior, performance, embarrassment/banter and some combat teamwork. V36 adds precisely the missing diagnostic regime.

Observed V36 evidence includes:

- direct physiological/attentional fear in the presence of AFO;
- explicit recognition that her own prior resolve did not prepare her for the sensory reality of that fear;
- empathic updating toward Midoriya and Aoyama based on newly sampled threat;
- refusal to let prestige/power taxonomy decide whether she is entitled to act;
- friend-centered escalation under severe risk;
- continued recognizable informal register while frightened;
- persistence after bodily injury;
- causal contribution to an elite battlefield through a capability nobody else present substitutes exactly.

This crosses the responsible threshold from `moderate` to **`strong`**. It does not make Jiro specialist-ready; adult professional breadth, broader independent leadership and additional non-Class relational states remain comparatively thin.

# 21. Tokoyami and Hawks: coordination without substitution

Tokoyami's line that he cannot replace Endeavor but can coordinate with Hawks should be treated as a core modeling atom (`V36:p076-078`). His mature power philosophy no longer needs to imitate his mentor or fill the No.1 slot. He identifies a relation-specific affordance.

V36 also adds high-threat entry, tactical coordination with diminished Hawks, response to Jiro's presence and repeated exploitation of a tiny opening. His current `strong` state is substantially strengthened.

A conservative method should still hold `specialist_ready` until ordinary/relationship breadth is comparably rich. V36 is mostly one extreme combat regime, however excellent the evidence within it.

Hawks remains `specialist_ready`. His most important new evidence is command adaptation: he initially tries to protect students by excluding them from lethal risk, then changes the plan when their distinct capabilities become necessary. Protection does not harden into paternalistic refusal to use available agency.

# 22. Shigaraki/AFO identity state: V36 strengthens fracture, not final separability

V34 established a fused/new-personality state rather than simple possession. V35 showed bodily adaptation under Erasure. V36 adds a qualitatively different kind of evidence: relationally triggered childhood memory.

When Mirio invokes friendship, the current entity produces Tenko/Shimura-specific memories and affect (`V36:p169-172`). The response is not a calm AFO strategic narration. It is immediate, child-linked insistence that he had friends and loved Mon.

This supports three strong conclusions:

1. the current fused state remains internally nonuniform;
2. Shimura/Tenko autobiographical material is not erased;
3. relationship language can reach material that the governing AFO/Shigaraki persona does not ordinarily foreground.

It does **not** yet support:

- that Tenko is fully independent inside the body;
- that AFO is about to lose control;
- that Midoriya can necessarily rescue/separate him;
- that childhood memory cancels adult Shigaraki agency;
- that the “new personality” description from V34 was false.

The better model is dynamic contested integration whose exact topology remains OPEN.

# 23. Power philosophy: two answers to difference

V36 makes the ideological contrast unusually explicit.

AFO/Shigaraki begins from a recognizable social fact: people with radically different bodies, capacities and perspectives often fail to understand one another. His chain is:

> difference -> incomprehension -> fear/exclusion -> fragmentation -> collapse.

His answer is universal subordination. If everyone becomes a subject under one sovereign hand, difference no longer needs to be negotiated.

The hero-side operation answers each link differently:

- Shoto does not erase Toya's difference; he meets it with a technique built from his own incompatible inheritances.
- Jiro does not become Endeavor; her sound reaches a failure mode Endeavor's fire does not.
- Tokoyami explicitly refuses the fantasy of replacing Endeavor.
- Monoma copies others yet remains valuable precisely because Copy is his own relational affordance.
- U.A.'s power, support, business and hero students do different kinds of work.
- Tamaki's power becomes stronger by incorporating heterogeneity.
- Bakugo recognizes all the other functions maintaining the conditions for his attack.

The alternative chain is therefore:

> difference -> partial incomprehension -> reciprocal dependence -> iterative adjustment -> plural agency.

Bakugo's “a little progress” is the right scale for this theory. Plural society does not become harmonious all at once.

# 24. Hero society: memory and maintenance join combat as first-class labor

V36 advances the institutional model in two underappreciated ways.

First, the floating U.A. sequence makes **maintenance** visible. The glamorous anti-Shigaraki battle is only possible while students replace machinery, generate power, repair surfaces, carry material and preserve Erasure infrastructure. Heroism becomes a continuous service system rather than a set of isolated interventions.

Second, Business Course makes **documentation** visible. Their task is to preserve and mediate what happened so hero action can remain connected to society (`V36:p137-139`). This is especially important in a series where public legitimacy has repeatedly depended on images, symbols, broadcasts and selective visibility.

A mature institutional reading should therefore distinguish at least four kinds of V36 hero labor:

1. direct combat;
2. technical/logistical sustainment;
3. command/information coordination;
4. social memory/mediation.

The operation fails if any of these is treated as decorative.

# 25. Visual grammar and recurrent motifs

## 25.1 Hearts and circulation

The volume begins by centering Shoto's technique around regulated circulation at the heart and ends by centering Bakugo's catastrophic injury on the heart. The visual rhyme is not evidence of identical meaning, but it creates a strong bodily frame around selfhood.

Shoto's heart is a site where incompatible inheritances are deliberately integrated. Bakugo's heart becomes the point at which accumulated bodily strain and external violence threaten to terminate a still-developing self.

## 25.2 Fire as relation to history

Dabi's fire consumes; Shoto's heat/cold circulates; Endeavor's fire becomes both weapon and visible continuation of a past he refuses to erase. “Heat” in V36 is never merely elemental power.

## 25.3 Hands

Shigaraki's proliferating hands literalize the sovereign fantasy of putting everything beneath one hand while simultaneously making the body grotesquely plural. The visual contradiction is productive: the entity seeking total centralization is physically becoming a mass of uncontrolled multiplicity.

## 25.4 Cards, photographs and records

Bakugo's All Might card and Business Course documentation share a quieter motif: mediated memory. The hero image exists as card, recording, public story and private object. V36 is interested not only in what heroes do but in what traces of heroism people carry.

## 25.5 The school in the sky

U.A. as a floating battlefield is both fortress and school. Students continue practicing the social roles their education prepared them for even after ordinary school time has collapsed. The war does not abolish education; it reveals what different kinds of education were for.

# 29. First-pass / earlier-claim correction opportunities

## PRESERVE

- Shoto's power development is fundamentally an identity/family project, not merely elemental optimization.
- Endeavor's atonement does not entail a right to forgiveness.
- Jiro's social/creative identity remains central to her hero behavior.
- Bakugo's development preserves abrasive register while changing decision policy.
- AFO repeatedly exploits wounds he did not necessarily originate.

## STRENGTHEN

- **Distributed heroism:** V36 proves maintenance, documentation and “extra” student capacities can be causally decisive.
- **Recognition without exculpation:** Shoto/Toya continues the same ethical structure established in Midoriya/Shigaraki and Uraraka/Toga.
- **Endeavor anti-absolution:** the adult explicitly accepts continued anger/punishment and rejects rebirth fantasy.
- **Bakugo relational growth:** he can now consciously depend on others and use Midoriya as a benchmark without collapsing into inferiority panic.
- **Shigaraki identity plurality:** childhood Shimura material is directly triggerable in the fused state.

## REVISE

- Any claim that Jiro is only moderately reconstructible because her crisis behavior is under-sampled. V36 supplies the missing extreme-threat regime and justifies `strong`.
- Any portrayal of Nejire's questioning as context-free “airheaded curiosity.” V36 gives peer-rejection and reciprocal-friendship causality.
- Any reading of Tamaki's insecurity as simple lack of confidence. The important variable is how he processes the weight of other people's belief; V36 shows that relationship changing.
- Any claim that Endeavor's mature atonement is “becoming a new man.” V36 explicitly rejects that formulation.

## DOWNGRADE

- “Shigaraki/AFO is one seamless new personality” should be downgraded. V36 shows direct Shimura/Tenko memory intrusion.
- “Bakugo's V36 maximum is purely self-generated power growth” should be downgraded. Equipment, infrastructure, class labor and accumulated bodily side effects are all explicit.
- “AFO's current senses are naturally superhuman” should be downgraded. V36 describes a composite acquired sensory architecture.

## REJECT

- “Jiro beats AFO through raw sound power.” Her attack catalyzes a pre-damaged internal-system disturbance; Hawks/Tokoyami exploit the opening.
- “Endeavor's suffering pays for his abuse.” The source rejects moral cancellation.
- “Bakugo is definitively dead at the V36 boundary.” The staging is terminal-looking, but the exact life-state is not adjudicated until later evidence.
- “Tamaki's attack failing means it was narratively meaningless.” Its failure is the scale diagnostic around which the chapter is structured.

## OPEN

- exact mechanism and consequence of AFO's post-body-destruction contingency;
- Toya's post-Shoto bodily/life state and later family relation;
- Bakugo's life-state/recoverability after `V36:p197`;
- whether Shimura/Tenko identity intrusion can materially change control of the fused body;
- Midoriya's arrival and response to floating-U.A. conditions;
- Spinner/Toga/other battlefield outcomes.
