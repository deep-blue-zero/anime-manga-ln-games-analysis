# 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 3 asked how heroic capacity becomes publicly legible: who is seen, recruited, ranked, and turned into a symbol. Volume 4 asks the moral question underneath that public process:

> **When power, identity, opportunity, and even one's body are partly given by other people or by birth, what makes an achievement — or a life — one's own?**

The governing concept of Volume 4 is therefore **self-authorship under unequal inheritance**.

The volume repeatedly places characters in situations where the thing that gives them social possibility is not something they freely chose.

- Todoroki Shoto was deliberately produced through `個性婚` as Endeavor's attempt to create a child capable of surpassing All Might. He experiences his left-side fire not simply as an ability but as the bodily mark of another person's project. His competitive vow is therefore a desperate authorship claim: win using only his right side, and thereby prove he is not `そんな屑の道具` — not that scum's tool.
- Shinso Hitoshi was born with Brainwashing, an exceptionally powerful ability that other children immediately code as suitable for crime and villainy. The U.A. practical entrance exam, built around robots, cannot properly display what his Quirk can do. He enters the Sports Festival already delayed by an ability he never chose and by an institution whose metric does not fit him.
- Midoriya was born Quirkless and once thought exactly as Shinso does about people who were `恵まれた`. Yet Volume 4 forces him to acknowledge that his present position has changed. He has been entrusted with One For All, supported by All Might and his peers, and literally carried forward by other people's help. His internal answer to Shinso is not denial: `でも…そうだ 僕は恵まれた`.
- Ojiro and Shoda reach the tournament through a cavalry-battle process they cannot fully remember or regard as self-authored because Shinso controlled them. Both voluntarily relinquish advancement rather than accept a place produced by actions they cannot call their own.
- Iida refuses the tactically attractive choice of joining Midoriya's team because friendship and repeated reliance have begun to feel developmentally limiting. He chooses to oppose Midoriya so that his own growth remains something more than proximity to a stronger friend.
- Uraraka, near the end of the volume, makes the same move in a gentler register. Midoriya has prepared an anti-Bakugo strategy for her. She thanks him, but Iida's earlier declaration that he would `挑戦する` has made her reflect on how easily she has leaned toward familiar people. She chooses to enter the match through her own judgment.
- Hatsume Mei treats a different kind of inheritance — public attention — as something to appropriate deliberately. She openly tells the first-place Midoriya that she wants to use his position for visibility. For her, authorship is not purity from outside conditions; it is the ability to repurpose those conditions toward one's own professional project.

These variations prevent the volume from offering a simplistic individualist answer such as “true achievement is whatever you do alone.” Midoriya's most important counterclaim is precisely the opposite:

`誰かに救けられてここにいる`

He is here because other people saved and supported him.

Self-authorship in MHA therefore does **not** mean self-creation from nothing. It means being able to participate as a person in what one's inheritance becomes.

This is why All Might's succession project and Endeavor's succession project are now distinguishable at a much deeper level than “good father figure versus bad father.” Both older heroes want the next generation to exceed what presently exists. Both place a younger person into relation with the overwhelming standard represented by the Symbol of Peace. But the relational structures are opposite.

All Might first recognizes Midoriya's already-existing heroic disposition, offers him a choice, entrusts power, trains him, and repeatedly treats the inheritance as something Midoriya must eventually make into `僕の力`. Endeavor, by contrast, explains Shoto as a child created for his own failed ambition: `いずれ貴様をも超えるヒーローにする / そうするべく つくった仔だ`.

The distinction is therefore:

> **entrustment invites another person to author an inheritance; instrumentalization authors the other person for one's own ends.**

That formulation substantially strengthens the project hypothesis that legitimate power preserves personhood whereas illegitimate power converts people into tools.

Volume 4 also complicates the idea of disadvantage. Midoriya and Shinso are both genuine outsiders to the default Quirk hierarchy, but not at the same time or in the same way. Midoriya's old deprivation does not entitle him to pretend he is still positioned identically to Shinso. The manga makes him acknowledge present privilege without denying past exclusion. That is unusually important for the series' moral epistemology: having suffered can create empathy, but it does not erase later asymmetry.

Finally, Volume 4 offers its first major non-villain test of Quirk stigma. Shinso has an ability repeatedly described as villain-like. He is institutionally under-read and socially stereotyped. Yet he wants to become a hero. His existence therefore qualifies any deterministic version of the later villain-formation thesis:

> **being feared, stigmatized, or poorly recognized because of one's Quirk can be part of a harmful social environment, but it does not mechanically produce villainy.**

What matters is how the person, relationships, institutions, and available routes of recognition interact with that condition.

A concise volume formulation is:

> **Volume 4 asks whether people can become authors of powers, bodies, opportunities, and identities they did not choose — and exposes the moral difference between inheritance that can be made one's own and inheritance imposed as someone else's project.**

# 2. Narrative architecture

## 2.1 The cavalry battle turns public visibility into a negotiation problem

Volume 3 ended by making Midoriya first place and therefore giving his headband ten million points. Volume 4 begins by making that visibility socially expensive.

The structure immediately reverses the apparent reward of first place. Students avoid Midoriya because joining him means inheriting his risk. He recognizes that maintaining the lead from the beginning is unrealistic and that other classes' Quirks remain unknown. The “No.1” position is no longer simply a mark of achievement; it is a condition that reorganizes everybody else's incentives around him.

This makes team formation an unusually revealing low-stakes modeling environment. The students must answer several questions at once:

- Do I choose friends or tactical complementarity?
- Do I seek safety or visibility?
- Do I attach myself to a high-value person or challenge them?
- Do I treat teammates as people, functions, or opportunities?
- How much uncertainty about another person's Quirk can I accept?

The resulting choices expose more character than a simple fight would.

Uraraka initially chooses Midoriya even after he warns that his ten-million value will make her a target. Her `良い！` and preference for fighting with someone she knows are not irrational; they reveal a low threshold for relational commitment. Midoriya, in turn, is relieved by the friendship but had already been thinking about her tactically as part of a maneuverable team. Affection and functional reasoning coexist.

Iida makes the opposite choice. He recognizes that joining Midoriya and Uraraka would produce excellent mobility, but explicitly refuses because repeatedly standing beside Midoriya has begun to feel like continued defeat and immaturity. He chooses Todoroki's team in order to become Midoriya's rival rather than simply his friend-supporter.

Hatsume chooses Midoriya for yet another reason: publicity. She states with striking directness that she does not know him but intends to use his position. Her Support Course identity means national visibility is itself a professional resource.

The Sports Festival therefore stops being merely a competition among Quirks and becomes a negotiation among **relationships, incentives, ambitions, and institutional opportunities**.

## 2.2 Todoroki's cavalry team foreshadows his central problem

Todoroki's team construction is highly functional. He assigns Kaminari, Yaoyorozu, and Iida by capability and gives them defined tactical roles. His leadership is competent, concise, and instrumentally clear.

There is no evidence here that he dehumanizes teammates in the way Endeavor dehumanizes him. But the formal rhyme matters. Todoroki has grown up as a person whose abilities were selected and arranged toward another man's objective; in competition, he naturally thinks in strong functional terms about what each body can contribute.

That should be held as a **plausible structural parallel**, not a causal diagnosis. Volume 4 does not yet show enough ordinary Todoroki leadership to say that Endeavor's instrumentalism has made him generally instrumental toward others. What the volume does show is that Todoroki is extremely comfortable converting differentiated abilities into a planned system.

The cavalry battle also establishes that his “right side only” restriction is not a lack of tactical discipline. He can operate with considerable planning while maintaining the self-imposed power constraint. The restriction is ideological and autobiographical, not evidence that he does not understand what would maximize raw output.

## 2.3 Advancement becomes an authorship problem

The most philosophically important consequence of Shinso's cavalry participation is not his ability to control people. It is Ojiro and Shoda's response afterward.

Ojiro knows that the Sports Festival is a rare national opportunity for professional recognition. He nevertheless withdraws because he cannot remember enough of the cavalry battle to regard the qualification as his own. His language is explicitly personal:

`こんなわけわかんないまま そこに並ぶなんて…俺は出来ない`

and then:

`プライド`.

The pride involved is not Bakugo's hierarchy pride. It is closer to integrity: **I need to be able to stand behind the path by which I received this recognition**.

Shoda independently reaches essentially the same conclusion. If people who did not meaningfully act through their own agency advance while others are excluded, the legitimacy of the festival itself is damaged.

This introduces an important distinction that will matter later throughout the series:

> **an outcome can be procedurally valid and still feel illegitimate to the person who receives it.**

No rule forces Ojiro or Shoda to withdraw. Their refusal comes from an internal standard of authorship and fairness.

## 2.4 Todoroki's family revelation reorganizes the Sports Festival

Chapter 31 transforms what had looked like an elite student's arbitrary refusal to use half his Quirk.

Todoroki tells Midoriya that Endeavor is his father and explains `個性婚`: a practice of choosing partners to preserve or strengthen desired Quirks. Endeavor, unable to surpass All Might himself, uses status and resources to pursue a next-generation solution. Shoto describes himself as born to satisfy that desire.

This changes the meaning of every previous Todoroki competition scene.

His public excellence is not psychologically experienced as simple evidence of personal talent. The very combination that makes him extraordinary is evidence of being designed for someone else's goal. His refusal of fire is therefore a strategy of **negative self-authorship**:

`使わず“一番になる”ことで 奴を完全否定する`

He will become number one by using only the side he associates with his mother, thus disproving the father's claim over what his body means.

The tragedy is built into the method. If Todoroki defines himself primarily by denying Endeavor, then Endeavor still determines the terms of the self-definition. He is no longer obeying the father's project, but he remains organized around it.

The manga makes this visible again during the Sero match. Todoroki releases an enormous ice attack far beyond what the situation appears to require. Afterward, as the crowd reacts to the scale and he uses his left-side heat to melt the ice he himself created, Midoriya thinks the sight looks `ひどく悲しく` — terribly sad. The left side is physically available. The conflict is over what using it would mean.

## 2.5 Midoriya answers Todoroki with relational authorship

Midoriya does not respond to Todoroki's confession by claiming a comparable tragedy or by offering generic pity. Instead, Todoroki's account forces him to articulate his own position.

He remembers All Might, Uraraka, Iida, and others who enabled him to reach U.A. and the Sports Festival. The key formulation is:

`誰かに救けられてここにいる`.

This is a major conceptual development from V01's “heroism before power.” Midoriya's identity is not independent because he did everything himself. It is his because he can acknowledge the people who helped constitute it and choose what obligation follows from that help.

His competitive answer to Todoroki is therefore:

> I also cannot lose, because becoming stronger and reaching the top is part of how I answer the people who saved me.

The contrast is exact:

- Todoroki competes to **deny** the author who tried to write his life.
- Midoriya competes to **respond** to people whose help he voluntarily recognizes as part of his life.

Both are relational. Only one currently has a relationship to inheritance that can support positive self-authorship.

## 2.6 Shinso turns institutional sorting into a visible contradiction

Chapter 33 shifts the same question from family to institution.

Aizawa says plainly that the U.A. entrance exam was `合理的じゃねぇ`. Shinso's Brainwashing is powerful, but the practical exam's robot opponents made its usefulness difficult or impossible to demonstrate. The mismatch is not that Shinso lacks heroic potential. The test measures a form of performance that does not fit what he can do.

The Sports Festival now functions as a corrective mechanism to U.A.'s own selection process. Shinso can demonstrate his ability against human opponents, and after he loses, the crowd still recognizes that he appears Hero Course-capable.

This matters because Hero Society is becoming more institutionally complicated than “bad system versus good outsider.” U.A. contains a flawed entrance mechanism, adults capable of recognizing that flaw, and a later mechanism capable of generating new evidence. The institution can mis-sort people **and** contain routes for self-correction.

That makes reform a question of metric design, second chances, and alternate evidence rather than only individual virtue.

## 2.7 Volume 4 ends before Uraraka–Bakugo resolves

The final chapter deliberately prepares a new autonomy test without resolving it inside this volume.

Midoriya has prepared a strategy for Uraraka based on his long observation of Bakugo. She initially appears tempted by the practical value. But Iida's earlier declaration that he would challenge rather than simply remain beside Midoriya causes her to reconsider. She admits that in the cavalry battle she may have chosen the easier path of relying on someone familiar. She chooses to face Bakugo through her own thinking.

The class also rejects the suggestion that Bakugo should reduce his effort because Uraraka is female. Kirishima's reaction is immediate: everyone is trying to become number one for their future, so holding back would misunderstand the contest.

Volume 4 then ends on the match introduction.

This prospective boundary is important. The famous tactical content and Bakugo/Aizawa recognition dynamics of the actual fight belong to Volume 5 and must not be credited to Volume 4. V04's contribution is the **ethical setup**: Uraraka chooses independent challenge, and the surrounding peer norm treats full competitive seriousness as a form of respect rather than cruelty.

# 3. Character-state ledger

## 3.1 Midoriya Izuku

### Current state

Volume 4 forces Midoriya to revise the story he can tell about his own disadvantage.

He still remembers being Quirkless. When Shinso says that `恵まれた人間` cannot understand being delayed by the Quirk one was given, Midoriya's immediate thought is that he once believed the same thing. But he does not stop there:

`でも…そうだ 僕は恵まれた`.

That is a major self-model transition.

Midoriya can now hold two facts simultaneously:

1. he genuinely experienced exclusion and powerlessness;
2. he is now genuinely advantaged by extraordinary entrusted power and unusually consequential relationships.

For modeling, this means later empathy should not be explained only as permanent outsider identity. Increasingly, he may recognize asymmetry from the position of someone who has received help and power.

### Relational self-conception

The Todoroki conversation gives the clearest statement so far that Midoriya does not experience achievement as solitary self-production:

`誰かに救けられてここにいる`.

This does not reduce agency. It reorganizes it. His response to being helped is not passive gratitude but reciprocal obligation: become stronger, answer the trust, and make the received opportunity meaningful.

That is highly predictive. When Midoriya receives significant care, sacrifice, or entrustment, he is likely to convert gratitude into duty and self-demand.

### OFA ontology and body

The Shinso match introduces the first unmistakable suggestion that One For All carries something of prior wielders beyond abstract accumulated power.

While Brainwashed, Midoriya sees dark human figures. His body then produces finger movement against conscious control. After breaking the effect, he interprets the experience through the image of OFA as a torch passed down:

`聖火の如く引き継がれてきたもの`

and wonders whether he felt the `気配` of the people who spun/carried this power forward.

Prospectively, this is a question, not an established metaphysical answer. The V04-frozen model may know that Midoriya experienced apparent predecessor traces and involuntary aid; it may not know later vestige mechanics.

### Competitive cognition

Midoriya remains highly analytical and generous with that analysis. Before Uraraka's Bakugo match, he has independently prepared a countermeasure because he has spent years observing Bakugo and feels indebted to Uraraka for repeatedly helping him.

The important modeling complication is that his instinct to help can unintentionally reduce another person's opportunity for independent authorship. Uraraka's refusal is not a rejection of Midoriya; it is a boundary around growth.

Midoriya's response is surprise rather than resentment. That suggests his care is not contingent on being needed.

### Relationship with Todoroki

Todoroki's confession substantially raises the intimacy and moral complexity of this relationship. Midoriya is no longer merely a target of competitive challenge. He is entrusted with deeply private family information and responds by articulating his own relational philosophy rather than treating Todoroki as an object of sympathy.

This relation should now be modeled as **competitive, confessional, and philosophically catalytic**.

**Readiness:** remains `moderate`, materially strengthened.

## 3.2 Todoroki Shoto

### Current state

Volume 4 is Todoroki's first genuinely deep character volume and justifies a move from `emerging` to `moderate` model readiness.

His central psychological problem is not simply “hates his father.” It is **ownership of a body deliberately produced for another person's ambition**.

### Family and self-model

He describes Endeavor as extraordinarily upward-striving and explains that being unable to surpass All Might directly led him to a reproductive strategy. The phrase `つくった仔` is especially important because it is Endeavor's own formulation in direct conversation with All Might: Shoto was made for this.

Todoroki's response is correspondingly object-centered:

`そんな屑の道具にはならねえ`.

He fears not only resemblance to his father but reduction to an instrument that vindicates his father.

### Power philosophy

His refusal to use fire is best understood as a moral-symbolic constraint on power rather than a tactical preference.

The Quirk itself is not morally contaminated in any objective sense. Todoroki has assigned his left-side fire the meaning of Endeavor's ownership. Winning with only ice would prove that the father's eugenic project was unnecessary and therefore deny his claim.

This is psychologically coherent and strategically self-limiting.

The model should therefore predict that Todoroki can voluntarily sacrifice efficiency when using a capacity would feel like accepting another person's definition of him.

### Trauma-conditioned stress

The Sero match demonstrates that his outwardly controlled combat style is not globally equivalent to emotional calm. After the Endeavor/Midoriya material, he produces a colossal ice response. Sero is immobilized almost immediately, and the scale becomes comically excessive to the crowd.

The post-match image is more revealing than the win. Todoroki uses his left-side heat to melt the ice while Midoriya experiences the image as deeply sad. He can physically use the fire. The prohibition is tied to competition, identity, and symbolic ownership.

This gives us the first strong evidence of **relationship-conditioned overcontrol and overreaction around Endeavor**.

### Speech and social behavior

His speech remains sparse and declarative, but Volume 4 demonstrates surprising willingness to disclose intensely private material once he has decided another person is relevant to his conflict. He does not ask Midoriya to comfort him. The disclosure is explanatory and competitive: here is why I will beat you in this way.

That suggests emotional privacy is not simple inability to articulate experience. He can articulate it with precision when it serves a chosen objective.

### Current contradiction

Todoroki wants autonomy so intensely that his chosen method remains organized around Endeavor. His attempt to reject the father risks preserving the father as the central reference point of his identity.

**Readiness:** `emerging → moderate`.

## 3.3 Shinso Hitoshi

### Current state

Volume 4 provides enough material to move Shinso from `insufficient/emerging` to `moderate` for this early chronology.

### Desire

He wants entry into the Hero Course. This is not vague status envy; the Sports Festival is a concrete second-chance mechanism after the entrance exam failed to let his Quirk demonstrate its value.

### Social stigma

His childhood/peer memory establishes a repeated reaction to Brainwashing:

- it would be useful for crime;
- it is `敵向き`;
- people associate the capacity to control others with villainy.

Shinso says he is accustomed to this response. His apparent resignation should not be confused with indifference. The desire to reach the Hero Course remains strong enough that he uses the Sports Festival aggressively to create another route.

### Decision policy and speech

Brainwashing requires verbal response, so Shinso's combat style is inseparable from social provocation. He studies what can make another person answer and uses emotionally loaded language strategically.

His speech therefore has a dual function:

- genuine grievance about unequal starting conditions;
- tactical bait designed to trigger the Quirk.

Do not assume every provocative statement in a combat context is a transparent disclosure of affect. In Shinso's case, speech is itself part of the weapon system.

### Envy and recognition

When Midoriya breaks the Brainwashing effect through OFA, Shinso sees the visible output and says that having such force from a finger is enviable. He frames himself as delayed by the Quirk he received and Midoriya as one of the blessed people who can go where they want.

This perception is incomplete but not absurd. Midoriya now possesses an extraordinarily powerful inherited Quirk and elite mentorship. The ethical strength of the scene comes from Midoriya admitting the part Shinso gets right.

After losing, Shinso receives applause and explicit comments that he appears suitable for Hero Course. This is important evidence about his response to recognition: defeat does not erase the opportunity because the festival generates a different kind of evaluative evidence from the entrance exam.

### Current contradiction

Shinso has a capacity associated with involuntary control, but he seeks entry into the profession whose normative purpose is protection. His existence is therefore a direct challenge to Quirk essentialism.

**Readiness:** `insufficient/emerging → moderate`.

## 3.4 Uraraka Ochako

### Current state

Volume 3 grounded Uraraka's family/economic motive. Volume 4 adds **individuation**.

She initially gravitates toward Midoriya in the cavalry battle because she likes and trusts him. By the end of the volume, she reflects that this tendency can become reliance. Iida's choice to challenge Midoriya acts as a social model for her own development.

When Midoriya offers an anti-Bakugo plan, she says in effect that she will do it herself. Her internal reasoning is not rejection of friendship. She explicitly recognizes how impressive Midoriya is. That admiration is precisely why she worries about leaning on him.

This gives a very useful predictive distinction:

> **Uraraka seeks closeness readily, but she does not want closeness to erase competence or self-respect.**

Her friendliness therefore should not be modeled as dependency.

The actual Bakugo fight belongs to V05. At the V04 boundary, the reliable evidence is her decision to face the challenge through her own preparation.

**Readiness:** remains `moderate`, strengthened.

## 3.5 Iida Tenya

Volume 4 strengthens Iida's model through deliberate competitive separation.

He recognizes that Midoriya/Uraraka/Iida would make a highly functional cavalry team. He chooses not to join anyway because he believes he has repeatedly been behind Midoriya since the entrance exam. His language frames continued alignment as remaining `未熟者`.

This is important because V02 showed that Iida can defer to function and accept indirect rescue roles. V04 shows the complementary side: he is not content to become a permanent support appendage to another protagonist's development. Friendship itself can create a reason to compete.

Iida's version of rivalry is therefore developmental rather than dominance-based. He challenges in order to improve himself, not to prove another person illegitimate.

**Readiness:** remains `moderate`, strengthened.

## 3.6 Bakugo Katsuki

Volume 4's direct Bakugo development is smaller than the culturally famous material that follows in V05, and chronology discipline matters here.

Two points are nevertheless valuable.

First, the cavalry battle continues to show fast tactical adaptation, including pursuit and retrieval behavior under chaotic conditions. His general competition model remains broader than Midoriya-specific rage.

Second, Chapter 35 explicitly establishes the surrounding peer norm before his match with Uraraka: when the possibility of holding back against a female opponent is raised, the answer is that everyone is competing for their future and trying to become number one. The match introduction positions Bakugo and Uraraka as serious competitors.

Do **not** credit V04 with Bakugo's later recognition of Uraraka's tactics or Aizawa's defense of him; those occur after the volume boundary.

**Readiness:** remains `moderate`.

## 3.7 Ojiro Mashirao

Volume 4 gives Ojiro his first genuinely model-relevant independent evidence.

He voluntarily gives up tournament qualification despite understanding its career value because he does not remember the cavalry battle as self-authored participation. His choice is explicitly tied to pride/integrity rather than fear of competition.

This is enough to establish an `emerging` baseline:

- high concern for procedural and personal legitimacy;
- discomfort receiving recognition produced by impaired agency;
- willingness to sacrifice opportunity to preserve self-respect;
- relatively direct, non-theatrical expression of principle.

**Readiness:** `insufficient → emerging`.

## 3.8 Hatsume Mei

Hatsume's V03 entrepreneurial baseline is strongly confirmed.

She does not hide instrumental social reasoning. She tells Midoriya openly that she intends to use his first-place visibility because companies are watching. For her, publicity is not vulgar contamination of technical craft. It is part of professional engineering practice.

That candor is useful for modeling. She may violate ordinary expectations about social tact not because she is unaware of instrumental motives but because she sees little reason to conceal motives she considers legitimate.

**Readiness:** remains `emerging`, strengthened.

## 3.9 Endeavor

Volume 4 finally provides enough direct behavior and speech for an `emerging` Endeavor model.

### Status orientation

His relationship to All Might is intensely comparative. He has remained unable to surpass the Symbol and treats Shoto's future as the route through which that failure can be overcome.

### Instrumental parenthood

His own language is devastatingly clear:

`いずれ貴様をも超えるヒーローにする`

`そうするべく つくった仔だ`

Shoto is framed as a produced solution to Endeavor's ranking problem.

### Interaction style

With All Might, Endeavor is brusque, hostile, and status-sensitive. He does not treat fatherhood as an independent relational good in the evidence available here; it is bound to achievement and succession.

This remains an early model. Later complexity must not be backfilled. At V04, the source strongly supports upward-striving obsession, comparison with All Might, and instrumentalization of Shoto.

**Readiness:** `insufficient → emerging`.

## 3.10 Aizawa Shota

Volume 4 adds one of the most important institutional judgments in his model:

`あの入試は合理的じゃねぇ`.

The entrance exam's robot structure failed to display Shinso's powerful human-targeted Quirk. Aizawa's rationalism is therefore not loyalty to existing metrics. He is capable of calling a U.A. evaluation mechanism irrational when it predicts professional usefulness poorly.

That sharply improves the interpretation of his V01 severity. His commitment is to usable evidence and professional forecasting, not institutional procedure for its own sake.

**Readiness:** remains `moderate`, strengthened.

# 4. Power philosophies

## 4.1 Power can be inherited without being owned

Todoroki's problem demonstrates that possession and ownership are psychologically distinct.

He undeniably has fire. The fire is part of his body. Yet because the body was deliberately produced through Endeavor's project, he experiences the ability as carrying another person's claim.

This creates a central series question:

> **When does inherited power become “mine”?**

Midoriya asked the positive version in V02 when he promised to make OFA `僕の力`. Todoroki lives the negative version: a power can be biologically his while still feeling like somebody else's authorship.

## 4.2 Entrustment versus manufacture

Volume 4 gives the clearest contrast so far between two ways of producing successors.

### All Might → Midoriya
- recognizes conduct before transfer;
- offers power rather than creating a child for it;
- requires preparation;
- expects eventual autonomous mastery;
- attaches public expectation, which can still burden Midoriya.

### Endeavor → Todoroki
- uses marriage/reproduction strategically;
- selects for a desired Quirk combination;
- creates a child to accomplish the father's failed objective;
- treats the child's success as continuation of the father's rank struggle.

The moral difference is not that one inheritance contains pressure and the other does not. All Might absolutely pressures Midoriya. The difference is whether the successor is recognized as a person whose agency can transform the inheritance.

## 4.3 Brainwashing rejects Quirk moral essentialism

Shinso's Quirk makes power's moral underdetermination explicit from another angle.

The ability sounds villainous because it directly affects another person's agency. Peers repeatedly tell him so. But a capacity's intuitive moral symbolism is not its user's moral identity.

This echoes Thirteen's V02 doctrine that many Quirks can kill and must be directed toward rescue. Volume 4 adds:

> **some Quirks may be socially classified as morally suspicious before the user has done anything wrong.**

Hero ethics therefore requires judging use, purpose, restraint, and personhood rather than simply reading morality off the shape of the power.

## 4.4 One For All is becoming relationally inhabited

The mysterious figures during Brainwashing transform OFA from an abstract succession principle into something more materially relational.

At V04, the exact ontology is OPEN. But Midoriya's interpretation is source-grounded: the power was carried like a torch, and he may have felt the presence of those who carried it.

This strengthens the hypothesis that OFA is structurally about relation, inheritance, and entrustment rather than simple accumulation.

## 4.5 Refusing power can itself be power philosophy

Todoroki's rejection of fire demonstrates that power philosophy includes **non-use**.

He is not merely asking “what can my Quirk do?” He is asking “what does using this part of my Quirk signify?”

The danger is that symbolic refusal can become self-imposed mutilation of possibility. The manga has not yet resolved whether reclaiming power from its abusive origin is possible, but the problem is now explicit.

# 5. Hero Society / institutional analysis

## 5.1 U.A.'s entrance exam has a measurable blind spot

V01 showed that U.A. contained hidden rescue points that could recognize heroic behavior beyond combat score. Volume 4 prevents that from becoming a simple story of an enlightened institution.

Shinso demonstrates an opposite case. The practical exam's robot design privileges Quirks capable of affecting machines or creating conventional physical effects. Brainwashing is extremely powerful in human conflict but cannot easily demonstrate value against robots.

Aizawa explicitly calls the exam irrational.

The important institutional model is therefore:

> **U.A. contains sophisticated recognition mechanisms and significant metric-design blind spots at the same time.**

## 5.2 The Sports Festival functions as institutional correction

The same school that mis-sorts Shinso also provides a mechanism through which he can generate new public evidence.

General Studies students can use Sports Festival performance to seek reconsideration for Hero Course. Shinso's loss is therefore not purely a failure. The crowd and observers now possess evidence the entrance exam never generated.

This is an early model of reform through **multiple evaluation surfaces** rather than a single gate.

## 5.3 Public evaluation is not identical to institutional evaluation

Shinso's applause after losing is important because it separates rank from recognition.

The bracket says he lost. The audience says his performance revealed something worth noticing.

Hero Society's public spectacle can therefore correct one institutional blindness — even while V03 established that spectacle creates other distortions such as branding, ranking pressure, and attack surfaces.

## 5.4 `個性婚` exposes the family as part of the Quirk institution

Hero Society is not only agencies, licenses, schools, and rankings. Quirk value can enter intimate reproductive decisions.

Todoroki describes `個性婚` as a previous-generation practice in which partners were selected for desired Quirk inheritance. Endeavor's rank obsession therefore colonizes family formation itself.

This dramatically widens the institutional analysis. The social valuation of strong Quirks can shape:

- marriage;
- reproduction;
- childhood expectations;
- bodily identity;
- parental authority;
- professional aspiration.

The hero ranking system does not stay outside the home.

## 5.5 Competition can cultivate autonomy as well as hierarchy

Iida and Uraraka both use competition to separate themselves productively from Midoriya. This matters because the Sports Festival is not simply a neoliberal sorting machine in textual function. It can also force students to discover where friendship has become reliance and where independent judgment is needed.

The institutional effect is therefore ambivalent:

> competition can commodify talent and intensify hierarchy, while also generating individuation, evidence, and growth.

# 6. Recognition and failed rescue

## 6.1 Shinso is the volume's clearest recognition case

Shinso's ability is visible to peers primarily through a villain schema before U.A. gives him a route to demonstrate heroic usefulness.

This is not yet a catastrophic failed-rescue narrative. He has not been abandoned into criminality. He is in school, seeking institutional mobility, and eventually receives public recognition.

That distinction matters. Volume 4 shows **recognition failure that remains correctable**.

## 6.2 Midoriya recognizes present privilege

Recognition is not only about seeing another person's pain. It is also about accurately locating oneself relative to them.

Midoriya's `僕は恵まれた` is an act of self-recognition. His past exclusion does not prevent him from seeing that Shinso currently lacks things Midoriya now possesses.

That makes empathy less possessive: “I suffered too” does not have to become “therefore our positions are the same.”

## 6.3 Todoroki is recognized by Midoriya as sad rather than merely strong

The public sees overwhelming ice output. Midoriya, after hearing the family history, sees the post-match act of melting that ice with the rejected side of Todoroki's body and experiences the image as `ひどく悲しく`.

This is precisely the project's recurring interest in recognition beneath performance. The same body that looks spectacular to the stadium carries a private conflict invisible to most spectators.

## 6.4 Ojiro and Shoda recognize illegitimacy in their own success

Most recognition analysis asks whether society sees a deserving person. Here the characters themselves refuse recognition they believe they did not earn through their own agency.

This adds an inward direction to the concept: responsible recognition can include refusing to let public status overwrite private knowledge of how the result occurred.

# 7. Villain formation, rescue, and accountability

Volume 4 contains little direct villain action, but Shinso is extremely important for the villain-formation model because he is a **counterexample**.

He has several conditions that could be overread retrospectively as proto-villainous:

- an ability other children label villain-like;
- resentment about unequal starting position;
- institutional misrecognition;
- willingness to manipulate speech tactically;
- envy toward the visibly powerful.

Yet his desired destination is the Hero Course.

Therefore:

> **Quirk stigma, grievance, and failed institutional fit are neither necessary nor sufficient explanations of villainy on their own.**

The series-level failed-recognition hypothesis must remain relational and contingent. Suffering matters, but so do available institutions, moral commitments, mentors, social routes, choices, and subsequent recognition.

Todoroki also reinforces the accountability/personhood distinction on the non-villain side. Severe parental instrumentalization can produce trauma and maladaptive self-limitation without making the harmed child morally equivalent to a future villain. The analytical framework must never treat suffering as destiny.

No major villain-formation revision beyond this qualification is warranted from V04.

# 8. Japanese language, character voice, and key vocabulary

## 8.1 `個性婚`

Todoroki's term for Quirk marriage condenses the volume's reproductive politics. `個性` is not merely a personal superpower; it becomes a heritable trait around which families can be strategically constructed.

The compactness of the compound makes the practice sound almost administrative, which increases the horror of Shoto's account.

## 8.2 `つくった仔`

Endeavor's description of Shoto is among the most load-bearing phrases in the volume.

The verb `つくる` frames the child as something deliberately made toward a purpose. Combined with his statement that Shoto will be made into a hero who surpasses All Might, the wording strongly supports instrumentalization rather than merely demanding parenting.

## 8.3 `屑の道具`

Shoto's refusal to become the `道具` of a `屑` makes his self-conception explicit. “Tool” is not analyst metaphor imposed on the scene; it is the character's own language for the threat to personhood.

## 8.4 `完全否定`

Shoto wants to become number one without using his father's side so as to `完全否定` Endeavor completely.

This is stronger than dislike. His competitive method is organized as total negation. The absolutism helps explain why a tactically costly restriction can feel necessary.

## 8.5 `誰かに救けられてここにいる`

Midoriya's line is one of the clearest early formulations of relational identity.

He is not embarrassed that others enabled him. Being helped creates obligation and connection rather than invalidating achievement.

## 8.6 `恵まれた`

Shinso uses `恵まれた人間` accusatorily for people blessed with abilities that let them advance. Midoriya then accepts the term for his current self.

The semantic pivot is crucial. Midoriya does not answer grievance by proving he has suffered more. He acknowledges receipt.

## 8.7 `敵向き`

The description of Shinso's Brainwashing as suited to villains demonstrates how everyday vocabulary can moralize a Quirk before conduct is known.

## 8.8 `合理的じゃねぇ`

Aizawa's critique of the entrance exam is perfectly consistent with his speech model: clipped, evaluative, and oriented toward whether a system actually predicts usable capability.

His “rationality” is not bureaucratic conformity. It can be used against U.A.'s own procedure.

## 8.9 `辞退` and `プライド`

Ojiro's withdrawal language places integrity inside the vocabulary of competition. The important pride is not pride in rank but pride in being able to claim one's own advancement.

## 8.10 `挑戦する`

Iida's framing of his refusal to join Midoriya becomes socially contagious. Uraraka later cites the idea of `挑戦する` as what made her reconsider dependence.

Competition is linguistically reframed from hostility to developmental challenge.

## 8.11 Speech-model consequences

- **Todoroki:** sparse, declarative speech can carry highly private autobiographical disclosure when he has chosen a reason to explain himself.
- **Shinso:** provocation is partly strategic because answering is the trigger for Brainwashing; combat dialogue cannot be read transparently as confession.
- **Hatsume:** unusually candid instrumental language about using another person's visibility; low concern for disguising professional self-interest.
- **Ojiro:** direct and comparatively untheatrical language around principle.
- **Iida:** formal self-development language; rivalry framed as duty to overcome immaturity.
- **Midoriya:** internal self-correction remains rapid; he can revise from “I know exclusion” to “I am now blessed” without erasing either proposition.

# 9. Visual and formal analysis

## 9.1 Todoroki's split body becomes narrative grammar

The half-cold/half-hot design has always visually advertised duality. Volume 4 finally gives the split autobiographical meaning.

His right/ice side is the side he permits himself to use competitively. His left/fire side is experienced as Endeavor's claim. The body therefore acts like a diagram of contested authorship.

The later image of Todoroki melting his own excessive ice with the forbidden left side is especially rich. Fire is literally used to undo the consequences of the one-sided identity he is trying to maintain.

## 9.2 The OFA silhouettes interrupt conscious control

During Shinso's Brainwashing, the human silhouettes appear in a visual space not reducible to the immediate arena. They precede involuntary finger movement and establish prior wielders as image before explanation.

Formally, the manga lets the inheritance act before either Midoriya or the reader can classify it.

## 9.3 Crowd visibility reclassifies Shinso

The Sports Festival audience is often drawn as mass spectacle, but Shinso's post-loss sequence reverses its function. Instead of being swallowed by the crowd's judgment, he receives recognition the original institutional gate denied him.

The public gaze is therefore not consistently punitive or superficial. It can generate an alternate reading of a person.

## 9.4 Midoriya's remembered helpers fill the self

During the Todoroki conversation, Midoriya's recollection of people who helped him visually populates the claim that he is here through rescue.

The self is represented not as an isolated heroic silhouette but as a network of remembered interventions.

## 9.5 Todoroki's giant ice is emotional excess rendered spatially

Against Sero, the ice extends far beyond the opponent's body and dominates the stadium architecture. The scale externalizes something his restrained face does not.

This gives visual evidence against equating muted expression with muted affect.

## 9.6 The volume ending freezes Uraraka–Bakugo at the threshold

The final page presents them as opposing serious competitors and then stops. This is formally useful for prospective analysis because the source boundary itself preserves uncertainty.

V04 can establish Uraraka's autonomy decision and the no-handicap peer norm. It cannot yet establish how either competitor will actually execute or interpret the fight.

# 10. Body, embodiment, family, and inheritance

Volume 4 is the first volume where embodiment becomes inseparable from reproductive politics.

## 10.1 Todoroki's body was socially planned

`個性婚` makes the body a site of deliberate intergenerational engineering. Shoto's ice/fire combination is simultaneously:

- natural to him as lived embodiment;
- inherited from parents;
- intentionally sought by Endeavor;
- professionally valuable;
- psychologically divided by trauma.

This is a radical complication of “your Quirk is part of you.” A body can be yours and still carry another person's project.

## 10.2 The mother/father division is not a clean moral anatomy

Shoto attempts to assign ice to the self/mother side and fire to the father side. But both are physically his.

The story has not yet resolved whether this partition can hold. At V04, the important point is that bodily capacities acquire relational meaning through family history.

## 10.3 Midoriya's body becomes a lineage site

The OFA silhouettes suggest that his body is not simply housing transferable energy. It may be participating in a continuity among prior bearers.

Prospectively this remains uncertain, but embodiment has become genealogical in a non-biological way.

## 10.4 Shinso's Quirk demonstrates social embodiment

Brainwashing affects how other people imagine his moral possibilities before they know his conduct. His body/ability enters social interpretation as a stigma category.

The moral burden of embodiment is therefore not only what a power does to one's own body, but what others decide that body means.

# 12. Secondary-character findings

## 12.1 Ojiro is more philosophically consequential than his screen time suggests

His withdrawal establishes a clean ethics of earned participation and self-authored achievement. This is exactly the kind of evidence the V2 architecture is designed to preserve rather than dismiss as tournament bookkeeping.

Shoda does not yet have enough breadth for a substantial independent model, but he should receive an `insufficient/emerging` watchlist note.

Her inventions, not combat ability, are what she wants evaluated. The Support Course expands the series' model of useful power beyond hero bodies.

He challenges Todoroki directly and is overwhelmed. The evidence remains too narrow for more than an early watchlist entry, but it helps establish ordinary competitive courage without a grand personal philosophy.

The class discussion rejects gendered handicapping in serious competition. This should be preserved as social-environment evidence without attributing the later fight's full meaning backward into V04.

# 13. Prospective versus retrospective reading

## 13.1 What a V04 reader legitimately knows about Todoroki

A prospective reader now knows:

- Endeavor deliberately pursued a Quirk marriage;
- Shoto was produced to surpass All Might;
- his mother suffered and scarred him with boiling water while rejecting the father's side of his face;
- Shoto experiences his fire as Endeavor's claim;
- he wants to win using only ice to negate the father;
- father-related activation can destabilize his otherwise controlled competitive behavior.

Do not import later Todoroki family developments, later Endeavor reinterpretation, or later reconciliation into this state.

## 13.2 What a V04 reader knows about OFA

The reader has seen predecessor-like silhouettes and Midoriya has inferred the possible `気配` of people who carried OFA.

The exact vestige system remains OPEN. Do not import later rules, identities, or Quirks.

## 13.3 What a V04 reader knows about Shinso

He is a General Studies student whose Brainwashing Quirk was poorly served by the practical entrance exam, who wants Hero Course admission, who has experienced villain-coded stereotyping, and whose Sports Festival performance earns public recognition.

Do not import later training, mentorship, costume, or role developments.

## 13.4 What V04 does **not** yet know about Uraraka–Bakugo

The volume ends at the introduction of their match.

Prospective V04 evidence supports:

- Uraraka chooses independent challenge rather than simply use Midoriya's prepared plan;
- peers expect Bakugo not to hold back on gender grounds;
- both are entering as serious competitors.

The actual tactics, audience reaction, Aizawa commentary, result, and post-match states belong to V05.

## 13.5 Retrospective significance

With full-series hindsight, Volume 4 is clearly foundational for later questions of inherited burdens, family authorship, social stigma, and reclaiming power. But the deep reading should preserve how radical the open questions still are here: whether Todoroki can ever make fire his own, what exactly OFA contains, whether Shinso can cross institutional boundaries, and how Uraraka's independent competitive identity will develop.

# 16. Revised interpretation

The first four volumes now form a coherent progression:

1. **V01 — Recognition:** can heroic worth be seen before conventional power makes it legible?
2. **V02 — Judgment:** once power exists, can it be governed professionally and ethically?
3. **V03 — Public legibility:** how does society select, rank, recruit, and turn powerful people into symbols?
4. **V04 — Self-authorship:** when power and opportunity come from inheritance, institutions, family, friendship, or birth, what makes the resulting identity genuinely one's own?

The fourth question prevents the series from collapsing into meritocratic celebration.

The Sports Festival publicly ranks people who did not begin from equal biological, familial, institutional, or relational positions. Yet the manga does not answer by declaring all achievement meaningless. Instead it asks whether characters can **take responsibility for what they have been given without pretending they created the starting conditions**.

Midoriya currently gives the healthiest answer. He acknowledges being blessed, remembers being saved, and treats support as a reason to act rather than proof that his achievement is fraudulent.

Todoroki gives the most wounded answer. Because his inheritance was imposed as another person's project, he tries to author himself through negation.

Shinso gives the institutional answer. A starting condition can be real power and still be badly measured or morally stereotyped; alternate routes of recognition matter.

Ojiro and Shoda give the integrity answer. A publicly valid outcome may not be personally claimable when agency was absent.

Hatsume gives the entrepreneurial answer. Outside conditions do not have to be pure; they can be appropriated and redirected deliberately.

Iida and Uraraka give the developmental answer. Supportive relationships are valuable, but a person sometimes has to stop choosing the safest relational path in order to discover independent capacity.

The volume's unifying proposition is therefore:

> **Personhood is not freedom from inheritance. It is the capacity to participate in the meaning and direction of what has been inherited.**

# 21. What this volume changes in the current MHA model

Volume 4 produces six major revisions.

## 21.1 Personhood versus toolhood becomes explicit text

This is no longer only an abstract analyst framework. Todoroki describes the danger in exactly those terms: he refuses to be Endeavor's `道具`, while Endeavor describes him as a child deliberately made to surpass All Might.

The series-level power-legitimacy hypothesis is therefore very strongly strengthened.

## 21.2 Inheritance is now a problem of authorship

OFA, Todoroki's body, Quirk inheritance, friendship support, family ambition, and institutional opportunity all ask versions of the same question: what does it mean to make something received into one's own?

The emerging answer is not independence from others. It is agency within relation.

## 21.3 Hero Society can misrecognize without being uniformly malicious

Shinso is not excluded because an adult hates him. He is under-read because a test is poorly designed for his Quirk. Aizawa can recognize that flaw, and the Sports Festival can produce corrective evidence.

This shifts reform analysis toward systems, metrics, and second chances.

## 21.4 Stigma is not destiny

Shinso's villain-coded Quirk and grievance do not make him a villain. This is an essential control case for later villain analysis and prevents causal overreach.

## 21.5 Midoriya's identity is explicitly relational and presently privileged

He can truthfully say both “I was once excluded” and “I am now blessed.” More importantly, he does not imagine receiving help as invalidating agency. He understands himself as someone made possible through rescue and therefore obligated to answer it.

## 21.6 Todoroki becomes the first major case of liberation through integration rather than simple opposition

Volume 4 does not resolve this case. It establishes the trap: if Todoroki can define himself only through negating Endeavor, Endeavor still determines his horizon.

That unresolved problem becomes the next major analytical hinge.

The current four-volume model can therefore be stated as:

> **MHA begins by asking who deserves power, then how power should be used, then how society makes power visible, and now whether a person can become the author of powers and identities shaped by inheritance, institutions, family, and other people's expectations.**

Volume 4's deepest contribution is to reject both isolated individualism and deterministic inheritance. A person is neither self-created nor reducible to what others gave, designed, measured, or called them. The ethical struggle is over whether relation leaves room for the person to answer back.
