## 1. Governing transition

Volume 17 moved the series toward **identity beyond power / rescue as restored agency**. Volume 18 asks what remains once the rescue attempt succeeds, a power is lost, a prediction is contradicted, a ranking changes, and a battle ends.

The governing transition is:

> **identity beyond power / rescue as restored agency -> aftermath responsibility / plural heroic legitimacy**

Compact formulation:

> **Victory does not finish rescue, and inheritance, rank, power, or prediction do not by themselves settle heroic worth. Legitimacy is tested by what a person carries forward after the decisive moment: consequence, grief, responsibility, trust, and the capacity to make others safer without reducing them to instruments.**

V18 repeatedly converts apparently decisive outcomes into new responsibilities:

- Infinite 100% wins the local confrontation, but Aizawa is still needed to stop Rewind safely.
- Nighteye's forecast is visibly contradicted, but Nighteye still dies from wounds already received.
- Mirio loses his Quirk, but refuses to convert Midoriya's inheritance into compensation and instead commits to his own future.
- Chisaki loses the battle, then loses the product, the organization, and the hands through which he normally exercises Overhaul.
- Bakugo, Todoroki, Inasa, and Camie cannot pass their remedial exercise through superior combat output; they have to become socially legible to children.
- Endeavor has the No.1 ranking but recognizes that ranking is not the same thing as occupying All Might's social function.
- Aoyama's closing behavior creates a low-stakes interpersonal mystery rather than a solved character explanation.

The volume is therefore less about who possesses the strongest answer than about whether people can inhabit a role after the old answer stops being sufficient.

# 2. No.158 — Infinite 100% resolves as a coupled rescue system

## 2.1 V17's hypothesis becomes an observed result

V17 ended before the counter-rate hypothesis had a result. V18 now shows it working in the immediate battle. Midoriya sustains otherwise self-destructive 100% output while Eri's uncontrolled Rewind continuously reverses the resulting bodily damage.

This validates the local mechanics without authorizing a broader claim that Midoriya has independently mastered 100% One For All. The configuration is exceptional and relational:

`Midoriya high-output damage <-> Eri continuous Rewind`

Neither participant alone produces the observed state. Midoriya supplies a body he is willing and able to drive at catastrophic output; Eri supplies a dangerous rate of temporal-biological reversal that would otherwise continue past safety. Their powers temporarily solve one another's immediate failure modes.

That is why Infinite 100% matters beyond spectacle. It is the first moment in which Midoriya's maximum current output is not represented as solitary self-destruction. But neither should it be sentimentalized into perfect mutual control: Eri still cannot regulate Rewind. The system works because Midoriya adapts himself around her uncontrolled condition.

## 2.2 Midoriya's rescue standard remains universalizing

Midoriya's attack language explicitly prevents the scene from becoming only revenge against Chisaki. He refuses to let Chisaki restore himself again (`V18:p020`) and frames the immediate demand through the standard that a hero who cannot save the people present cannot become the kind of hero he seeks to be (`V18:p021`).

The volume therefore preserves the rescue-first core while changing its implementation. Early Midoriya often spent his body because he had no other route. Here he spends it inside a temporary feedback system whose cost is being continuously reversed. The difference is not that sacrifice disappears; it is that power, victim agency, and bodily consequence have been recombined into a more complex rescue mechanism.

## 2.3 Chisaki's `恩情` is attachment without respect for another person's authorship

The chapter title itself directs attention toward Chisaki's abnormal gratitude. His flashback with the Hassaikai elder sharply revises a simple “Chisaki cannot care about anyone” model.

Chisaki remembers being taken in and says, in substance, that he wants only to repay the debt of being picked up. Yet the elder rejects Chisaki's project and tells him that if he will not follow the house's principles he should leave. Chisaki's response is not to revise the project around the elder's expressed values; it is effectively to tell him to stay quiet and watch while Chisaki pursues what Chisaki has decided repayment must mean (`V18:p013-016`).

This is a major relational distinction:

- Chisaki can experience gratitude and attachment.
- He can organize years of labor around an imagined obligation to a benefactor.
- But he substitutes his own theory of what the benefactor needs for the benefactor's actual stated will.

His care therefore remains possessive/instrumental at the level of authorship even when it is emotionally real. The elder is valued, but the elder's agency can be overridden by Chisaki's project of restoration.

That pattern sits in direct continuity with Eri. Eri is far more radically instrumentalized, but the deeper rule is similar: Chisaki decides what another person's body, future, or organization must mean and then treats resistance as failure to see the `大局`.

## 2.4 The decisive visual opposition

Chisaki's fused body has become enormous, grotesque, and spatially dominant, while Midoriya's victory is organized around carrying a small child. The visual contrast refuses a simple equation of scale with legitimate power. Chisaki becomes physically larger as his project becomes more totalizing; Midoriya's maximum power is literally burden-bearing.

The final strike at `V18:p023` is therefore a battle result, but not yet the whole rescue result.

# 3. No.159 — A changed future, distributed completion, and the limits of victory

## 3.1 Nighteye's forecast is empirically contradicted

V17 preserved Nighteye's forecast as credible evidence: he had seen himself and Midoriya die while Chisaki escaped. V18 now gives the first direct adjudication.

Nighteye explicitly recognizes that the unfolding future differs from what he saw. He says he does not know the logic by which the difference occurred, describes the outcome as the realization of his own wish, and tells Midoriya that he has twisted the future (`V18:p038`).

This is a genuine revision of the epistemic state.

What V18 establishes:

- Nighteye's observed future is not perfectly inevitable.
- A visible deviation has occurred.
- Nighteye himself accepts the deviation as real.

What V18 does **not** establish:

- a general physical law explaining how Foresight branches;
- that Midoriya's will alone caused the branch;
- that belief always defeats prediction;
- that every component of Nighteye's forecast is avoidable;
- that Nighteye's earlier fear was irrational given his prior evidence.

Nighteye says directly that he does not know the mechanism. The correct update is therefore from **observed non-branching history** to **at least one observed branch/deviation under conditions not yet causally isolated**.

## 3.2 Anti-fatalism becomes empirical without becoming magical voluntarism

Midoriya's V17 statement that he would twist the future was a commitment. V18 turns the commitment into an observed discrepancy, but the manga distributes the causal field across many people:

- Mirio reaches and protects Eri before Midoriya arrives.
- Kirishima, Amajiki, Fat Gum, Ryukyu's team, police, and other pros keep the operation moving.
- Eri chooses toward rescue and her Quirk participates in the final mechanism.
- Midoriya supplies the 100% counter-rate and decisive attack.
- Aizawa later suppresses Rewind so the coupled state does not become lethal.

The strongest source-grounded formulation is not “Midoriya wills fate away.” It is that a forecast formed under one model of the future is exceeded by a dense network of actions, choices, and interactions whose combined effect Nighteye had not treated as a manipulable branch.

## 3.3 Aizawa proves that winning and safely ending the rescue are different tasks

After the battle, Eri's Rewind continues. The same power that made Infinite 100% possible becomes an immediate danger because Eri still cannot stop it deliberately.

Aizawa's Erasure is required to shut down the uncontrolled process.

This produces one of V18's cleanest distributed-heroism propositions:

> **The person who defeats the antagonist is not necessarily the person who completes the rescue safely.**

Midoriya can solve the Chisaki problem while being unable to terminate the Eri/Midoriya power interaction. Aizawa's role is not visually climactic, but it is causally indispensable.

This also protects Eri from being recoded as a convenient power-up. Her Quirk remains dangerous to herself and others because control is undeveloped; professionals treat that as a medical/training problem rather than moral contamination.

## 3.4 The narration refuses clean triumph

Midoriya immediately turns concern toward Nighteye and toward All Might's promise to live (`V18:p039`). The narration then frames the operation as a battle to save one girl while emphasizing the injuries incurred.

The tonal effect matters: V18 grants victory without allowing victory to erase cost. This becomes the template for the rest of the volume.

# 4. No.160 — Shigaraki converts grievance into strategic appropriation

## 4.1 The League/Hassaikai coalition was never assimilation

The convoy attack resolves the political structure V16 and V17 had already established. Toga and Twice's secondment did not create Hassaikai loyalty. The League cooperated while retaining Magne's death, Compress's injury, and broader sovereignty grievances.

Once the Hassaikai operation leaves Chisaki weakened and the valuable ammunition mobile, the League converts inside knowledge into an interception opportunity.

This is not a sudden betrayal of a harmonious alliance. It is the payoff to a coalition that had always contained `遺恨`.

## 4.2 Shigaraki's revenge is designed around the target's value system

When Chisaki asks whether Shigaraki came to kill him, Shigaraki answers that he has thought about what Chisaki would hate most (`V18:p055`). That line is diagnostic.

Shigaraki does not choose the shortest route to elimination. He chooses symbolic deprivation:

- Compress acquires the ammunition Chisaki spent the arc producing.
- the League takes the future leverage of the Quirk-destroying project;
- Chisaki is deprived of his arms/hands, the bodily interface through which he normally uses Overhaul;
- Shigaraki forces him to survive and witness the appropriation.

At `V18:p058`, Shigaraki states the logic explicitly: the effort Chisaki spent has become Shigaraki's.

This is strategic revenge in a mature sense. The punishment has three functions simultaneously:

1. **emotional:** answer Magne/Compress grievances;
2. **symbolic:** force Chisaki into the kind of helpless observation he imposed on others;
3. **organizational:** transfer scarce technological leverage to the League.

## 4.3 Shigaraki's development is not “becoming calm”

His speech remains taunting, cruel, and emotionally charged. Maturation does not mean affective flattening. It means that emotion can now be converted into delayed, target-specific, materially useful action.

This is important for reconstruction. A “mature Shigaraki” should not sound like an unemotional strategist. V18 supports something more specific:

> grievance remains hot, but the response can be patient, theatrical, and instrumentally sequenced.

## 4.4 Chisaki's loss is structurally tailored

Chisaki defined himself through hands-on control: disassemble, restore, purify, reorganize. Shigaraki's punishment leaves him alive but unable to enact that mode normally.

The scene thus converts villain-on-villain conflict into a contest over authorship. Chisaki tried to make the League a subordinate component in his restoration project; Shigaraki instead converts Chisaki's project into League property and Chisaki himself into a spectator.

# 5. No.161 — Nighteye's final revision: from future as sentence to future as gift

## 5.1 The operation's casualty accounting preserves institutional scale

The hospital chapter begins by widening the frame beyond the central duel. Heroes, police, local damage verification, wounded participants, and medical treatment all remain active. The outcome is not represented as “Deku won; arc over.”

This supports the Hero Society ledger's growing distinction between spectacular causality and actual professional completion. Arrest, evacuation, damage control, medical transfer, victim monitoring, and chain-of-command continuity all remain part of heroic labor.

## 5.2 Eri is rescued but not operationally “resolved”

Eri is separated for treatment/monitoring because her Quirk and physical state remain unstable. Her horn changes, she develops a fever, and the adults do not treat Rewind as something she can immediately deploy on request.

That is crucial for both ethics and modeling. V18 does not convert her from abused child into healer-resource. The same story that just used her Quirk in a spectacular rescue immediately restores the knowledge boundary:

- she lacks fine control;
- continued activation can be dangerous;
- future training/understanding is needed;
- her post-rescue state includes medical and psychological instability.

Physical extraction is real. Recovery is unfinished.

## 5.3 All Might and Nighteye recover enough relation for final closure

Nighteye and All Might's earlier rupture came from different responses to a predicted death. V18 does not pretend years of conflict disappear, but All Might reaches Nighteye before death and the two are no longer organized around the old argument.

The scene's emotional center then shifts to Mirio.

## 5.4 Mirio's grief reveals the cost of mentor-derived identity without collapsing him into dependence

Mirio begs Nighteye not to die because Nighteye taught him how to become strong and because Mirio still wants more guidance (`V18:p074`). The plea is intensely dependent in the ordinary human sense: a student does not want the relationship to end.

Nighteye answers not by demanding stoic detachment but by protecting Mirio's future self-conception.

He tells Mirio he will become a finer hero than anyone (`V18:p075`). He then uses the language of a bright future and tells him to keep smiling (`V18:p076`).

The important modeling point is that Nighteye's last use of future-language no longer functions as a sentence imposed on another person's agency. After witnessing an apparent deviation in his own forecast, he uses the future as reassurance and commitment.

## 5.5 Nighteye's death refines, rather than negates, the future-change thesis

The historical V1 claim that “the future can change, but not without cost” broadly survives, but V2 can state it more precisely.

Nighteye had forecast a configuration including his/Midoriya's deaths and Chisaki's escape. V18 visibly deviates from that configuration: Chisaki does not escape and Midoriya survives the immediate battle. Nighteye nevertheless dies because he is already mortally wounded.

So:

- **REJECT:** “the prediction was simply false.”
- **REJECT:** “belief guarantees everyone survives.”
- **STRENGTHEN:** a predicted trajectory can deviate.
- **STRENGTHEN:** deviation does not retroactively remove injuries or consequence already incurred.
- **OPEN:** the causal mechanism by which Foresight deviates.

This makes Midoriya's optimism less magical and more ethically demanding. A future may be changeable, yet action still has irreversible cost.

# 6. No.162 — `ふさわしき者`: worth without winner-take-all succession

## 6.1 Midoriya's legitimacy anxiety returns after victory

The arc does not let the Chisaki victory erase Nighteye's earlier succession challenge. Midoriya still remembers that Nighteye regarded Mirio as the superior One For All candidate.

He therefore raises a hypothetical: if transferring his Quirk to Mirio were possible, what then? (`V18:p087-089`).

This is not evidence that Midoriya wants to abandon heroism. It is evidence that legitimacy remains relationally vulnerable even after an extraordinary performance. Midoriya is capable of interpreting Mirio's catastrophic loss as a reason to question whether his own inherited advantage should be reallocated.

## 6.2 Mirio refuses compensation through inheritance

Mirio's answer is immediate: `いらないです` (`V18:p088`).

The refusal matters for several reasons.

First, he does not treat Quirk loss as proof that someone owes him a replacement identity.

Second, he does not interpret Midoriya's possession of One For All as an injustice merely because Nighteye once preferred Mirio.

Third, he explicitly recognizes Midoriya's successful heroic action. On `V18:p089`, he tells Midoriya in effect that he did well and acted like a hero, then redirects attention to Eri's condition and his own possible future.

## 6.3 Mirio chooses a conditional path that preserves Eri's personhood

Mirio says that if Eri eventually becomes able to handle her Quirk properly, he may ask whether she can return him to the state in which he had a Quirk (`V18:p090`).

The conditional grammar matters:

- **if** she becomes able to handle it;
- **ask** her;
- try rather than assume success.

V18 therefore refuses to turn Eri into a restoration machine for Mirio. The possibility exists, but her control and consent are prerequisites.

Mirio also grounds himself in Nighteye's prediction of his future and returns to the policy `だからさ!! 笑っていようぜ`.

## 6.4 The “better candidate” problem resolves into plural heroic fitness

Volumes 14–17 treated Mirio as a serious challenge to Midoriya's inherited legitimacy. V18 does not settle that question by proving Mirio unworthy. It settles it in the opposite direction.

Mirio is worthy **without needing Midoriya's role**.

Midoriya can be All Might's successor while Mirio remains a great hero on his own trajectory. Heroic legitimacy becomes plural rather than winner-take-all.

That is one of V18's most important anti-zero-sum moves:

> **Another person's excellence does not automatically delegitimize your own path, and another person's loss does not automatically entitle them to your inheritance.**

# 7. No.163 — returning to ordinary systems after extraordinary violence

## 7.1 U.A. re-entry is itself evidence

The manga deliberately returns the work-study students to classmates, schedules, school rules, meals, lessons, teasing, and ordinary concern. This is not analytically empty transition material.

V2.1 treats low-stakes behavior as first-class modeling evidence because crisis-only characterization overstates dramatic modes. V18's return to U.A. shows that the characters must carry raid knowledge and grief into ordinary institutional time rather than remaining permanently in climax register.

## 7.2 Classmates provide care without possessing the trauma

The returning students are met by peers who know something serious occurred but do not share complete firsthand knowledge. The social atmosphere mixes concern, surprise, awkwardness, and normal classroom rhythm.

That supports a recurring MHA principle: care does not require identical epistemic access. The class can reabsorb people without demanding full ownership of what happened.

## 7.3 The narrative changes scale rather than abandoning stakes

The next problem is the remedial provisional-license course for Bakugo and Todoroki. Moving from organized crime and a dying mentor to difficult elementary-school children is a deliberate stress-regime change.

The question becomes whether heroic competence remains legible when force is the wrong instrument.

# 8. No.164–166 — remedial training as social-function heroism

## 8.1 The task is to capture `心`, not bodies

Bakugo, Todoroki, Inasa, and the real Camie are given a group of difficult children and told to win/capture their hearts. The exercise explicitly changes the success condition from combat dominance to social leadership.

The children are not helpless. They are confident, clever, Quirk-aware, and resistant to adult authority. The participants therefore cannot earn compliance merely by being stronger.

This is post-All-Might professional training in miniature: heroes must create conditions under which people **choose to orient toward them**.

## 8.2 Bakugo's abrasive perceptiveness is separated from simple cruelty

Bakugo remains verbally harsh. V18 does not sanitize him into a gentle child-whisperer. But his harshness is increasingly paired with accurate structure-reading.

He recognizes that merely flattering or submitting to the children would leave the underlying hierarchy/arrogance intact. His own history makes him unusually sensitive to inflated self-conception.

This is valuable for reconstruction because Bakugo's developmental change is not “he becomes nice.” It is:

- increasingly accurate recognition of what domination does to a person's self-model;
- growing ability to direct that recognition toward a functional outcome;
- still-hostile surface register.

His social growth therefore preserves voice continuity.

## 8.3 Todoroki and Inasa convert prior conflict into coordination

The licensing exam exposed mutual attentional failure: both became so focused on the other's symbolic meaning that they damaged a shared rescue objective. The remedial exercise requires them to coordinate powers and attention around a third party—the children.

V18 therefore strengthens the V13 correction. Their relationship is no longer merely “they apologized.” They can now produce a shared environment together.

## 8.4 Camie makes social flexibility operational

The real Camie enters with a markedly casual, socially permissive register and uses Glamour in a context where spectacle is not deception for predation but a tool for engagement.

Her evidence remains narrow, but V18 establishes a different personality surface from Toga's earlier impersonation. The corpus must not back-project Toga-Camie behavior into the real Camie.

## 8.5 The solution is participatory spectacle

The cohort ultimately uses Quirks to create a controlled, exciting environment rather than a battlefield:

- Todoroki's ice becomes structure/play-space;
- Inasa's wind becomes controlled motion;
- Bakugo's explosions become calibrated spectacle;
- Camie's Glamour becomes imaginative/social framing.

This is one of the clearest demonstrations so far that Quirk ethics cannot be reduced to “dangerous power restrained.” The same capacities can be re-authored toward play, awe, communication, and communal experience.

The children are not conquered; they become participants.

## 8.6 Social legitimacy is performative in the non-deceptive sense

All Might's Symbol of Peace was not only a combat record. It was a socially legible performance of reassurance. The remedial course trains an analogous competency at a tiny scale: if the people present cannot interpret you as someone worth following, raw output is insufficient.

This becomes the bridge to Endeavor.

# 9. Endeavor at the No.1 starting line

## 9.1 Rank transfers faster than symbolic function

All Might's retirement automatically changes the ranking structure, but it does not automatically transfer the emotional relation society had to the Symbol of Peace.

V18 makes Endeavor confront the distinction between:

- being ranked No.1;
- being the strongest active hero by conventional measures;
- being someone other people can organize trust around.

The chapter title calls this a **starting line**, not an arrival.

## 9.2 Endeavor's developmental move is self-revision, not absolution

The most important family statement occurs when Endeavor tells Shoto:

`ならば俺も おまえが胸を張れるようなヒーローになろう` (`V18:p173`)

The grammar is future-oriented. Endeavor does not claim that his past conduct has already been corrected or that Shoto should now admire him. He states an intention to become the kind of hero Shoto could be proud of.

Shoto answers `勝手にしろよ`.

That reply prevents premature reconciliation. It neither endorses Endeavor nor prohibits change. Shoto refuses responsibility for managing Endeavor's improvement.

Current relationship state:

> **Endeavor initiates a self-revision project; Shoto withholds absolution and leaves responsibility with Endeavor.**

## 9.3 “No.1” becomes a social-ethical problem

Endeavor has spent years treating rank as an objective measure by which All Might could be surpassed. V18 forces the realization that the position he wanted includes obligations the ranking cannot quantify.

This does not erase his competitiveness. It expands the object of competition: he now has to ask what kind of person can actually carry the role.

For the Hero Society ledger, this is a major transition from **ranking as professional ordering** to **ranking as incomplete proxy for legitimacy**.

# 10. No.167 closing movement — Aoyama as low-stakes unreadability

## 10.1 The volume refuses to end only on grand institutional themes

After the No.1/remedial material, the manga returns to U.A. several days later. The closing movement focuses on Aoyama and Midoriya in an ordinary school context.

This is methodologically important. V18 has spent much of its length resolving huge claims about fate, death, organized crime, and succession. It then ends with a classmate behaving strangely around food, study, and nighttime dorm life.

That shift reminds the reconstruction project not to model the cast only through ideological climax.

## 10.2 Aoyama's outreach is socially intrusive but not yet causally explained

Aoyama approaches Midoriya through eccentric cheese-centered behavior. He feeds him cheese in the cafeteria, comments on taste, continues his theatrical self-presentation, and later appears outside/near Midoriya's room at night.

Midoriya does not understand what Aoyama is thinking (`V18:p182`). That epistemic limit is the point to preserve.

At this boundary we can say:

- Aoyama is deliberately directing unusual attention toward Midoriya;
- food/taste becomes a medium of attempted contact;
- he is willing to violate ordinary interpersonal distance in eccentric ways;
- the behavior is impactful enough that Midoriya keeps thinking about it;
- Midoriya does not yet possess a causal explanation.

We cannot yet say why Aoyama is doing this in any deeper sense.

## 10.3 Exact prospective endpoint

At `V18:p183`, Midoriya decides he needs to sleep; Aoyama is visible outside/at the window, and the narration states:

`彼が本性を現し始めたんだ`

This is the final admissible narrative fact.

Do **not** backfill any later explanation of `本性` into V18. The chapter intentionally produces uncertainty.

# 11. Character-modeling updates

## Midoriya Izuku

**State delta:** remains `strong`; V18 strongly expands aftermath, legitimacy, and exceptional-power evidence.

**New context coverage:**
- successful maximum-output rescue;
- immediate post-battle concern for wounded mentor;
- return to U.A. ordinary routine;
- post-succession anxiety toward Mirio;
- confusion in low-stakes Aoyama interaction.

**Decision/behavior evidence:**
- sustains 100% inside the Rewind counter-rate rather than treating pain as accidental failure;
- keeps rescue language foregrounded during combat;
- after victory, redirects attention toward Nighteye and All Might rather than self-celebration;
- interprets Mirio's Quirk loss through a legitimacy/compensation lens and raises hypothetical OFA transfer;
- accepts Mirio's refusal rather than insisting on martyr-compensation;
- admits inability to infer Aoyama's internal state rather than fabricating certainty.

**Relationship-specific:**
- Eri: cooperative rescue becomes physically successful; Midoriya still cannot independently shut down her Quirk.
- Nighteye: forecast dispute becomes empirical deviation and then grief.
- Mirio: rival-successor framing begins resolving into plural legitimacy.
- Aoyama: receives eccentric attention but remains epistemically uncertain.

**Speech/register:** urgent rescue declaratives remain high-intensity but post-battle speech becomes solicitous and self-questioning.

**Contradiction/uncertainty:** Infinite 100% cannot be treated as independent mastery; later Aoyama interpretation remains OPEN.

**Readiness:** remains `strong`.

## Togata Mirio

**State delta:** remains `strong`; V18 adds grief, post-loss future planning, and explicit refusal of compensatory succession.

**Decision heuristic:** catastrophic capability loss does not create entitlement to another person's inherited role. If Eri eventually gains safe control, Mirio may ask for help; until then he continues from his current state.

**Relationship-specific:**
- Nighteye: intense grief and desire for continued guidance; final future reassurance becomes a stabilizing inheritance.
- Midoriya: rejects OFA transfer and validates Midoriya's actual hero conduct.
- Eri: any future restoration request is conditional on her safe agency/control.

**Readiness:** remains `strong`, substantially broadened.

## Sir Nighteye

**State delta:** `moderate -> strong`.

V18 supplies the missing adjudication and terminal relationship breadth:

- directly recognizes a future different from his forecast;
- explicitly says he does not know the causal mechanism;
- reorients from fatalistic epistemic caution toward acknowledging a realized wish;
- receives Midoriya's All Might-survival message;
- achieves terminal contact with All Might;
- responds to Mirio's grief by using future-language as reassurance rather than prohibition;
- dies after passing forward a bright-future/smile injunction.

This now provides sufficient breadth across office authority, humor/fandom, mentor conflict, field command, epistemic fear, catastrophic failure, relationship repair, death, and future-oriented care.

**Readiness:** `moderate -> strong`.

## Eri

**State delta:** remains `moderate`, strengthened by successful rescue and post-rescue instability.

**New evidence:**
- her power can function cooperatively in the Infinite-100% configuration;
- she still cannot shut it off safely on her own;
- Aizawa must terminate activation;
- medical instability/fever and horn change persist after extraction;
- adults frame future control/training as uncertain rather than treating her as an available restorative tool.

**Modeling consequence:** rescue has changed her environment, not instantly normalized her. Ordinary baseline remains too sparse for `strong`.

## Chisaki Kai / Overhaul

**State delta:** remains `strong`.

**New causal depth:**
- gratitude toward the elder is genuine enough to organize his identity/project;
- he nevertheless overrides the elder's stated wishes in the name of repayment;
- after defeat he is subjected to Shigaraki's personalized symbolic punishment;
- loss of the ammunition and his arms leaves him alive with the project stripped away.

**Modeling consequence:** his relational pathology is better described as **attachment without respect for autonomous authorship** than total incapacity for attachment.

## Shigaraki Tomura

**State delta:** remains `strong`, very strongly strengthened.

**New context:** delayed post-coalition retaliation, logistical interception, symbolic punishment, technology appropriation, subordinate coordination.

**Decision heuristic:** when grievance and strategy can be combined, Shigaraki can delay immediate violence until he can impose a punishment tailored to the target's values and simultaneously strengthen the League.

**Speech/register:** taunting informality and cruelty remain intact inside increasingly strategic behavior.

# 13. Visual and formal observations

## 13.1 Scale collapse after the giant fight

The volume begins with gigantic fused Chisaki and maximum-output Midoriya, then progressively shrinks its spaces:

battlefield -> hospital room -> school/classroom -> primary-school exercise -> cafeteria -> dorm window.

The formal contraction is ethical. Heroism is tested again at progressively less spectacular scales.

Chisaki's Quirk and identity have been visually hand-centered. Shigaraki's highway punishment turns loss of hands into an attack on the grammar of Chisaki's agency, not merely damage.

## 13.3 Nighteye's eye and monitor imagery

## 13.4 Remedial spectacle reverses combat grammar

Ice, wind, explosions, and illusions retain high visual energy while being reorganized into play. The manga reuses battle-page kinetic grammar for nonviolent social connection.

## 13.5 The final window

The last page turns the ordinary dorm window into an uncertainty frame. Aoyama watches; Midoriya is inside; narration announces `本性` without explaining it. The visual relation is intentionally observational and unresolved.

# 14. Cross-cutting ledger propositions

## Power philosophy

1. **Exceptional power can be relationally stabilized without becoming independently mastered.** Infinite 100% works because Rewind and OFA temporarily counterbalance.
2. **Capability loss does not erase acquired personhood.** Mirio remains a hero after Permeation loss.
3. **Restorative power is not automatically socially available.** Eri's control and consent remain prerequisites to any future use.
4. **The same Quirk can be socially re-authored.** Remedial participants turn combat-capable abilities into play and connection.

## Hero Society

1. Victory requires aftermath institutions: medicine, police, local damage control, containment, schooling, and recovery.
2. Rank is an incomplete proxy for symbolic legitimacy. Endeavor becomes No.1 before becoming someone who can carry All Might's social function.
3. Provisional licensing evaluates relational/social competence, not merely force.
4. Heroic systems require role diversity: Midoriya defeats Chisaki; Aizawa safely terminates Rewind; medical/institutional actors carry the aftermath.

## Recognition / failed rescue

1. Eri is physically extracted, but recovery remains incomplete.
2. Nighteye's forecast is contradicted without erasing the fatal consequence of prior injury.
3. Midoriya's legitimacy anxiety persists even after success; Mirio's refusal becomes corrective recognition.
4. Aoyama's low-stakes behavior is noticed without being understood—recognition can precede interpretation.

## Villain formation

1. Chisaki's pathology includes real gratitude combined with disregard for the loved authority's self-authorship.
2. Shigaraki converts unresolved grievance into symbolic, material, and organizational gain.
3. League coalition behavior is strategic and non-assimilative.
