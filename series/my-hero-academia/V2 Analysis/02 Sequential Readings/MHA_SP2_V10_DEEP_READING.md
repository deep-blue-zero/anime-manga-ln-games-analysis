# 1. Central thesis and volume role


Volume 9 asked what survives when learned heroism is forced into a lethal environment without a teacher-authored `勝ち筋`. Volume 10 begins with the answer **`完全敗北`**. Bakugo is gone, the camp is wrecked, students and pros are injured or missing, and the event has damaged not only bodies but the public credibility of U.A. and hero society.


The volume's organizing problem is therefore no longer merely whether someone is willing to rescue. It is:


> **Who has understood the person or crisis correctly, who has the authority to act on that understanding, and what kind of protection preserves another person's agency rather than replacing it?**


That question links four apparently separate threads.


First, Bakugo becomes a test of **contested interpretation**. The League saw the same aggressive public behavior everyone else saw and inferred recruitability. Aizawa, by contrast, publicly argues that the behavior must be read together with Bakugo's relentless pursuit of the top-hero ideal. Volume 10 then gives the rare luxury of a prospective validation result: the V01–V09 Bakugo model predicted that public aggression would not imply moral alignment with villains, and Bakugo himself answers the League by attacking Shigaraki, citing All Might's winning image, and refusing even strategic false compliance.


Second, the hospital/rescue debate becomes a problem of **jurisdiction**. Kirishima's inability to accept passive inaction, Midoriya's `手が届く` trigger, Todoroki's covert plan, Yaoyorozu's self-designation as `ストッパー`, and Iida's post-Stain insistence on responsibility are not interchangeable forms of “teenagers disobeying adults.” They represent competing attempts to preserve rescue impulse while deciding what students who remain `保護下` may legitimately do. All Might himself states the tension precisely when he tells Yaoyorozu that wanting to act for endangered friends is evidence that she can be a hero, **and** that the adults must now take over.


Third, U.A.'s response makes public legitimacy an operational problem. The school accepts that the forest incident is a grave institutional failure, investigates the possibility of an `内通者`, coordinates with police, and stages a public press conference. Yet accountability does not require surrendering interpretive authority. Aizawa apologizes for his educational responsibility while simultaneously rejecting the League's reading of Bakugo as shallow. The institution must answer for failure without pretending that hostile observers therefore understand its students better than it does.


Fourth, All For One complicates the opposition between protection and possession. He tells Shigaraki that nobody saved him, that others looked away while expecting “a hero” to arrive, and `君は悪くない`. When Shigaraki's plan fails, AFO does not simply issue commands. He validates the request for help, retrieves allies, guarantees the possibility of another attempt, praises Shigaraki for beginning to think and lead for himself, and says `僕がいる` and ultimately `全ては君の為にある`. V08–V09 had already warned that subjective self-authorship can exist inside structural manipulation. V10 turns that proposition into a full mentorship mechanism: **dependency can be engineered precisely by becoming the infrastructure that makes another person's self-authored attempts survivable.**


The volume's professional side offers a contrasting model of protective authority. The police/U.A. operation converts concern into intelligence, simultaneous targets, role assignment and bounded coercive action. Best Jeanist then becomes an unexpectedly important power-philosophy control case: AFO recognizes that Jeanist's extraordinary performance comes from training and field experience and rejects the Quirk as unsuitable for Shigaraki. The scene establishes that stolen capacity is not the same thing as appropriated expertise.


A concise Volume 10 thesis is:


> **Volume 10 turns rescue after defeat into a problem of interpretation, jurisdiction and protective authority. It asks who actually knows the person being protected, which forms of action are authorized by that knowledge, and whether protection can sustain another person's agency without converting care into possession.**


The first ten volumes can now be represented as:


`recognition → judgment → public legibility → self-authorship → recognition without possession → enacted identity → aftermath → diagnostic resistance → emergency enactment → contested legibility / protective authority`

# 2. Narrative architecture


## 2.1 `完全敗北`: the attack becomes institutional aftermath


Chapter 83 does not allow the forest operation to end as an exhilarating student battle. It names the outcome `完全敗北` (`V10:p045-046`) and immediately counts the cost. Of forty students, fifteen were rendered unconscious by gas, eleven suffered serious or minor injuries, only thirteen escaped injury, and one student—Bakugo—is missing. Among the six professional heroes, one is seriously injured and Ragdoll is missing with substantial blood evidence (`V10:p047-048`).


That accounting matters because V09's local victories did not add up to institutional victory. Midoriya saved Kota; Kendo and Tetsutetsu stopped Mustard; students survived individual engagements. Yet the adversary achieved a strategically meaningful objective and escaped.


MHA therefore refuses a simple additive model in which enough heroic moments automatically equal systemic success.


The U.A. staff discussion makes that point explicit. The League's action is treated as the beginning of something closer to a war against hero society, and staff self-critique includes the possibility that All Might's long suppression of organized villainy allowed institutions to become `平和ボケ`—habituated to peace and the expectation of preparation time (`V10:p049`). This is not evidence that U.A. never adapted. V07–V09 already established adaptation. The sharper proposition is:


> **An institution can be adaptive and still discover that its threat model was too slow for an adversary choosing timing, terrain and symbolic objective.**


The loss is also explicitly reputational. The League did not only abduct Bakugo; it took public trust in heroes with him (`V10:p050`). That wording advances the Hero Society model from “media can distort causality” to “public trust itself can be captured as an operational target.”


## 2.2 Failure creates competing claims over the next rescue


The hospital chapters redistribute the forest crisis into a quieter but more difficult moral field. Midoriya can no longer simply act because the threat is physically in front of him. He is injured, the adults have an active investigation, and Bakugo is beyond immediate reach.


Kirishima nevertheless experiences inaction as identity-threatening. His `ここで動けなきゃ俺ァ ヒーローでも男でもなくなっちまうんだよ` (`V10:p062`) is not a general theory of law. It is a personal threshold produced by having been unable to stop the kidnapping. His proposed next move is correspondingly shaped by the need to regain agency.


Iida occupies the opposite position but for equally personal reasons. He explicitly names his own Stain episode as `私的暴走` and asks why the classmates who stopped him and shared the official aftermath would now repeat his error (`V10:p079`). His objection includes institutional externality: they are still `保護下`, U.A. is already in crisis, and their actions impose responsibility on other people.


The disagreement therefore cannot be reduced to emotion versus reason. Both positions are memory-conditioned forms of care:


- Kirishima fears becoming the person who failed to move.
- Iida fears watching friends reproduce the self-destructive unauthorized conduct that nearly destroyed him and injured the people around him.


Todoroki and Yaoyorozu introduce a third route: covert movement without frontal combat. Todoroki rejects the assumption that they intend to storm the enemy openly; Kirishima describes the plan as `戦闘無しで救け出す` and `隠密活動`, a method he believes stays barely on the acceptable side of the rules (`V10:p082`). Yaoyorozu accompanies them specifically as a `ストッパー` in case the plan exceeds its own boundary.


The group is therefore not abolishing jurisdiction. It is **contesting where the jurisdictional line lies while trying to build internal brakes against escalation**.


## 2.3 Bakugo becomes a controlled test of surface legibility


The League's recruitment attempt gives V10 its cleanest character-model validation. Shigaraki and the others have seen Bakugo's aggression, anger, public tournament conduct and willingness to fight. They infer that a boy who “likes winning” and appears alienated from polite heroic comportment may share their grievance against the system (`V10:p100-105`).


The inference is plausible from surface evidence and wrong at the level that matters.


Bakugo attacks Shigaraki almost immediately after gaining an opening (`V10:p102`). His explanation is not newly invented for the scene:


`俺は オールマイトが 勝つ姿に 憧れた` (`V10:p104`).


He then states that no matter what anyone says, `そこァもう曲がらねえ` (`V10:p105`). Later, when Dabi observes that someone with no intention of joining could at least pretend to be persuaded, Bakugo refuses even that instrumental deception: `したくねーモンは嘘でもしねんだよ俺ァ` (`V10:p113`).


V07 had established the All Might-victory origin; V08–V09 had preserved aggressive speech and relational difficulty without equating them with value collapse. V10 now tests that model under kidnapping, social isolation and direct hostile recruitment. It passes strongly.


## 2.4 U.A. converts public crisis into accountability and counter-operation


The press conference around `V10:p109-112` is not mere public-relations texture. Aizawa's formal `行動については私の不徳の致すところです` accepts responsibility for Bakugo's problematic public conduct as an educator. But he refuses the next inference. Bakugo's actions, he argues, show someone who pursues the top hero more fiercely than anyone; if villains read his roughness as a moral opening, their interpretation is `浅はか`.


The school therefore performs two actions simultaneously:


1. **accept responsibility for conditions and conduct within its educational role**;
2. **reject a false moral reading of the student produced by that conduct**.


Nezu then states that the school is not standing idle, the police investigation is underway, and `我が校の生徒は必ず取り戻します` (`V10:p111`).


This is institutionally important. Public accountability in MHA is not modeled here as ceremonial shame followed by passivity. It is paired with intelligence gathering, police partnership and an operational promise.


## 2.5 The professional raid shows care translated into system design


The adult rescue operation around `V10:p120-139` contrasts productively with the students' covert group. The pros have many of the same emotions—Tiger wants Ragdoll back, All Might wants Bakugo recovered—but their action is organized through intelligence, role differentiation and simultaneous assault on multiple identified locations.


The incident is explicitly described as something that could trigger `ヒーロー社会崩壊` (`V10:p120`). Edgeshot tells Endeavor to see the larger situation; police define abductee recovery and suspect arrest as dual priorities; timing is designed to deny escape (`V10:p120-123`).


All Might's habitual reassurance formula is then altered by collective operation. The bar raid culminates not in a solitary `私が来た` but a spread organized around `我々が` / `来た！` (`V10:p138-139`). The visual and grammatical plural matter. The Symbol of Peace remains central, but successful institutional rescue is being staged as **we**, not only **I**.


## 2.6 AFO transforms defeat into protected retry


The professional raid appears to close the crisis quickly. League members are immobilized, Bakugo is reassured, and Shigaraki's plan looks defeated. AFO changes the meaning of that defeat.


The critical prior moment occurs before the raid when Shigaraki asks `先生 力を貸せ` (`V10:p116`). AFO answers that this is a good judgment. Dependence is not framed as humiliating failure; asking for the mentor's infrastructure is rewarded.


When AFO intervenes, the volume repeatedly links protection to the narrative he has built around Shigaraki. In the remembered/grooming material he tells the child that nobody saved him, people looked away while saying a hero would eventually come, and `君は悪くない` (`V10:p151`). In the present he supplies the reassurance `もう大丈夫` / `僕がいる` (`V10:p152-153`).


The language is structurally parasitic on heroic reassurance. AFO becomes the person who claims to arrive where hero society did not.


But his mentorship is more sophisticated than simple substitution. He says Shigaraki has begun `自身で考え` and `自身で導き始めた`, and that he would prefer outsiders not interfere with that development (`V10:p162`). Later, after the operation fails, he tells Shigaraki not to be discouraged, to begin again as many times as necessary, and makes himself the guarantee of retry: `その為に僕がいるんだよ` (`V10:p180`). The volume closes this mentorship formulation with `全ては君の為にある` (`V10:p181`).


This is the strongest evidence so far for a paradoxical successor system:


> **AFO does not need to erase Shigaraki's experience of agency. He can cultivate independent thought while controlling the material and interpretive environment that makes repeated self-authored action possible.**


## 2.7 Volume 10 ends before Kamino resolves


Chapter 89 proceeds into All Might's confrontation with AFO and ends at `V10:p185` with the conflict unresolved. All Might demands `全て返してもらうぞ オール・フォー・ワン!!`; AFO answers `また僕を殺すか オールマイト`.


That source boundary is analytically decisive.


Volume 10 does **not** yet establish:


- the final outcome of All Might versus AFO;
- the eventual method by which Bakugo is removed from the battlefield;
- any public `次は君だ` succession message;
- All Might's final post-battle power state;
- Inko's definitive response to continued U.A. enrollment;
- implementation of the dorm system.


The V1 `V10-V11` grouped reading bundled those consequences into one Kamino interpretation. The second pass must leave them prospectively OPEN for Volume 11.

# 3. Bakugo: held-out validation of value beneath hostile legibility


## 3.1 The League's inference is not irrational—but it is shallow


V09 deliberately separated “selected as recruitable” from “actually recruitable.” Bakugo's public behavior gives an adversary data that can be overinterpreted: explosive anger, contemptuous speech, intense victory orientation, poor conventional likability and visible resistance to social correction.


The League's error is therefore analytically useful precisely because it is not random. It demonstrates the limits of trait inference from public presentation.


A person can be:


- aggressive without admiring predation;
- status-driven without rejecting professional legitimacy;
- cruel in interpersonal conduct without regarding villain violence as an acceptable path;
- obsessed with winning while remaining morally bound to a heroic model of what winning is for.


The V2 method's insistence on relationship-conditioned behavior and value evidence prevents “angry = villain-adjacent” from becoming a model.


## 3.2 The V07 `Origin` prediction survives direct recruitment


The decisive source is Bakugo himself. He does not ground refusal in abstract civic virtue or suddenly polite morality. He returns to the same origin value already established:


`俺は オールマイトが 勝つ姿に 憧れた` (`V10:p104`).


That continuity is exactly what makes the scene a useful validation rather than a new trait insertion. His All Might model is the hero who wins. V07 showed that Midoriya-specific hierarchy injury could temporarily override the practical use of that value; V10 shows that hostile recruitment cannot rewrite the value itself.


The conditional prediction frozen at V09 was therefore substantially correct:


> Under villain recognition/recruitment pressure, Bakugo's aggression should not be treated as evidence of villain alignment; his established All Might-victory ideal should create strong resistance to recruitment.


Observed result:


- immediate violent rejection of Shigaraki when an opening appears;
- explicit citation of All Might's winning image;
- explicit statement that the core will not bend;
- refusal even to fake compliance for instrumental advantage.


**Validation classification: strong PASS.**


No post hoc repair is necessary to explain the direction of his response.


## 3.3 Refusing false compliance clarifies Bakugo's integrity structure


`したくねーモンは嘘でもしねんだよ俺ァ` (`V10:p113`) is not automatically admirable strategy. Feigned compliance might have been safer. What it reveals is that Bakugo's self-conception resists certain forms of strategic self-alienation.


This connects with earlier evidence:


- he rejects tournament outcomes he regards as causally illegitimate;
- he prefers defeat to using Midoriya's help when that help feels identity-threatening;
- he can cooperate when he can preserve authorship of the cooperative structure;
- under villain captivity he refuses to perform an affiliation he experiences as false.


The stable tendency is not “always honest.” The better rule is:


> **Bakugo is unusually resistant to publicly enacting a self-position that violates the identity standard by which he measures victory and superiority.**


That can produce principled resistance and strategically costly stubbornness from the same mechanism.


## 3.4 Aizawa's public defense is recognition rather than sanitization


Aizawa does not say Bakugo behaves well. His `不徳の致すところ` accepts educational responsibility for the conduct. What he rejects is the League's inference about motive.


This is a high-value recognition scene because it keeps two truths together:


- Bakugo's public aggression is real and institutionally consequential;
- the aggression does not exhaust the student's value structure.


Aizawa therefore models the distinction the analysis itself must preserve: **recognizing Bakugo accurately is not the same thing as excusing Bakugo.**

# 4. Midoriya: when `手が届く` becomes an epistemic trigger


## 4.1 V09 victory becomes V10 incapacity


Midoriya begins the post-camp volume in a hospital bed. Kirishima's blunt summary—Midoriya saved one person and then became effectively unusable—turns the V09 sustainability issue into peer-observed operational evidence (`V10:p058`).


This does not downgrade the Muscular rescue. Kota would likely have died without it. It changes the question from whether the local act was justified to whether Midoriya can build a heroic decision system that distinguishes:


- catastrophic cost that is genuinely the only rescue path;
- catastrophic cost that merely feels morally pure or immediate;
- situations where preserving his body permits more total rescue.


The distinction will matter more now because V10 supplies new medical knowledge.


## 4.2 The doctor's warning creates an explicit future-capacity constraint


The doctor says this injury is substantially worse than Midoriya's previous damage (`V10:p074-075`). His explanation of `火事場の馬鹿力` frames extreme output as a condition in which normal bodily limiting systems are overridden even though the body cannot safely sustain the load.


The practical warning is unusually concrete: if comparable injury occurs another two or three times, Midoriya should expect a life in which his arms may no longer function normally.


This changes the epistemic state of all later self-damage decisions.


Earlier, Midoriya knew that injury hurt him and frightened others. After V10 he has been told that repetition threatens permanent loss of a major part of his future rescue capacity.


Therefore:


> **Later catastrophic arm use cannot be analyzed as if he merely lacks information about cumulative bodily cost.**


Whether later risk is justified remains scene-dependent, but the knowledge state is now materially different.


## 4.3 `手が届く` is both rescue philosophy and activation hazard


Midoriya remembers All Might's principle that he cannot save places his hands cannot reach, so he must save the range he can reach (`V10:p058`). Kirishima later tells him that Bakugo is still within reach: `まだ手は届くんだよ!` (`V10:p063`).


Midoriya admits that hearing this leaves him unable to remain still (`V10:p082`).


The phrase is therefore doing more than expressing benevolence. It functions as a cognitive trigger. Once Midoriya categorizes another person as both endangered and still reachable, disengagement becomes extremely difficult.


That mechanism helps explain his consistency without turning every rescue into the same scene:


- if the person is unreachable, he may accept professional delegation;
- if the person is reachable and immediate survival depends on him, his cost threshold collapses;
- if someone reframes distance as still “within reach,” rescue compulsion can reactivate even when institutional conditions differ.


This is a more predictive model than “Midoriya always rushes in.”


## 4.4 The V10 rescue coalition is not yet the V11 relational solution


The historical V1 reading correctly anticipated that Kamino would develop Midoriya toward rescue through planning and others. But Volume 10 itself stops before the decisive extraction sequence.


At V10, the evidence is narrower:


- Midoriya joins a covert group rather than launching a frontal solo assault;
- he accepts Iida and Yaoyorozu as constraint-bearing participants;
- the plan is explicitly formulated as no-combat extraction/scouting;
- he remains bodily limited and aware of that limitation.


This is **positive evidence toward distributed rescue**, but the stronger V1 claim that Midoriya learns he need not personally be “the hand” belongs to Volume 11 when the source actually stages the rescue method.


---# 5. Iida: accountability becomes reciprocal protective authority


## 5.1 V10 validates the V07 repair rather than resetting it


Iida's response to the proposed rescue is among the best longitudinal tests of a repaired moral failure in the first ten volumes.


V06 showed him concealing vengeance, overriding professional/legal warning and narrowing heroism into private retribution. V07 showed apology, retained bodily consequence, distinction between Stain's `信念` and Stain's illegitimate methods, and a future-prevention doctrine. V10 places him beside friends who now feel the same emotionally compelling pressure to act outside the adult operation.


His response is not merely “rules say no.” He names his own past conduct:


`俺の私的暴走をとめてくれた…共に特赦を受けたハズの君たち二人が…っ!!! 何で俺と同じ過ちを犯そうとしている!?` (`V10:p079`).


The repair has generalized. Iida now uses his failure as a model for recognizing danger in someone else.


**Longitudinal validation: strong positive evidence.**


## 5.2 Institutional responsibility is part of his moral reasoning


Iida explicitly reminds the group that they remain `保護下` and that U.A. is already under severe pressure. He asks whether they understand whose responsibility their conduct would become (`V10:p079`).


This shows that his post-Stain growth is not merely “I should personally avoid revenge.” He has learned to reason through **externalized responsibility**:


- unauthorized conduct can expose supervisors and institutions;
- good motives do not privatize consequences;
- being willing to accept one's own risk does not authorize imposing risk on others.


That is an important maturation from the pre-Hosu Iida, whose proceduralism could look like rule observance for its own sake.


## 5.3 The punch is care conditioned by trauma memory


Iida physically strikes Midoriya during the argument (`V10:p080`). The act should not be romanticized; it is an escalation. But his subsequent explanation prevents it from being interpreted as detached authoritarian anger.


He says he is worried because he is class representative and because Bakugo is his classmate—but not only Bakugo. Midoriya's injured body makes him see his brother Tensei lying incapacitated (`V10:p081`).


The causal chain is:


`Tensei injury → Iida's revenge failure → repair doctrine → Midoriya's visible injury → fear of repeated loss → attempt to stop friend`.


V10 therefore turns Iida's family trauma into **protective recurrence detection**. He is trying to interrupt a pattern before another person becomes what his brother became.


## 5.4 Joining the group does not mean surrendering his objection


When the debate reaches `平行線`, Iida chooses to accompany them (`V10:p083`). A shallow reading could call this capitulation. His later explanation shows otherwise.


He recognizes that once Midoriya decides to act he is difficult to stop. He respects that rescue drive as friend and rival, but states that he will not keep yielding to it. His conclusion is `今度は俺が 守るんだ` (`V10:p119`).


This is a major relationship-state change.


In Hosu, Midoriya and Todoroki entered Iida's crisis and redirected him. In V10, Iida assumes the reciprocal role: **he accompanies rather than abandons, but treats accompaniment as a means of preserving a boundary.**


The more mature Iida model is not “always obey the rule” or “eventually joins his friends.” It is:


> **After experiencing his own morally compelling rule breach, Iida is increasingly willing to stay physically close to a friend whose rescue impulse he cannot extinguish so that he can constrain escalation from inside the relationship.**


That is far more reconstructable than generic class-president seriousness.

# 6. Kirishima, Todoroki and Yaoyorozu: three different ways of making rescue actionable


## 6.1 Kirishima converts helplessness into a personal identity threshold


Kirishima's `ここで動けなきゃ` line (`V10:p062`) is one of his first high-information motive disclosures. His problem is not abstract fascination with danger. The forest kidnapping left him with intolerable inaction, and continued passivity now threatens both heroic self-conception and masculine self-conception.


The phrase should be handled carefully because `男` here is part of Kirishima's own value language, not an analyst endorsement of gendered moral worth.


Behaviorally, V10 establishes:


- he can experience failure-to-act as identity damage;
- that damage produces action pressure;
- he nevertheless tries to design a route that avoids direct combat;
- he recruits others because rescue is not framed as a solo test of toughness.


That combination begins moving Kirishima beyond “hardening / manly enthusiasm” shorthand.


## 6.2 Todoroki operationalizes the distinction between rescue and combat


Todoroki is the one who most directly rejects the assumption that action means frontal attack. `俺たちだって何も正面きってカチ込む気なんざねえよ` (`V10:p082`) narrows the proposed mission.


This matters for his model because earlier volumes established that Todoroki can default toward self-reliant competence. V08 corrected that with Momo. V10 shows him participating in a group plan whose legitimacy depends on **limiting what the group will attempt**, not merely maximizing its power.


His contribution is therefore jurisdictional as much as tactical: define the mission in a way that preserves a distinction between locating/extracting a friend and initiating unauthorized combat.


## 6.3 Yaoyorozu becomes a self-appointed brake rather than passive intelligence source


Momo's tracker is central to the professional counter-operation, and All Might praises what she managed to provide. Her own reaction is telling: she says she cooperated because classmates were endangered and regrets the circumstances, while All Might identifies that desire as evidence of heroic potential and tells her to leave the rest to the adults (`V10:p060`).


Her later decision to join Todoroki and the others is not framed as unqualified endorsement. She says she trusts Todoroki **but** accompanies them so that, in the worst case, she can become the `ストッパー` (`V10:p082`).


This is excellent post-V08 evidence. V08 restored Momo's confidence in her own judgment after excessive deference to Todoroki. V10 now shows:


- she can trust Todoroki without surrendering independent risk evaluation;
- she can participate while explicitly preserving a dissent/restraint role;
- her support competence feeds both official and unofficial action without erasing concern about boundaries.


The V08 question “does confidence restoration persist under incomplete-information leadership?” receives meaningful positive evidence.


## 6.4 The rescue group is deliberately internally heterogeneous


The covert group is analytically valuable because its members do not agree for the same reason.


- **Kirishima:** inaction threatens heroic identity.
- **Midoriya:** `手が届く` reactivates rescue obligation.
- **Todoroki:** a bounded no-frontal-combat route appears possible.
- **Yaoyorozu:** support is acceptable only with an internal stopper.
- **Iida:** the rescue drive is real, but someone must prevent another Hosu-like escalation.


Their coordination is therefore not ideological consensus. It is a temporary coalition whose internal disagreements become safety architecture.


That is a useful positive counterpart to the V09 Vanguard coalition. Heterogeneous groups can coordinate in both hero and villain domains; what differs is not heterogeneity itself but the constraints, goals and methods through which disagreement is managed.

# 7. U.A., police and the public: accountability without epistemic surrender


## 7.1 Public trust becomes a strategic casualty


The staff explicitly recognizes that Bakugo's kidnapping has taken something beyond the student: public trust (`V10:p050`). This is the institutional analogue of the Sports Festival's earlier public-legibility problem.


Public visibility creates professional opportunity, but it also creates exploitable narratives. Bakugo's televised aggression helps villains select him. U.A.'s inability to prevent his kidnapping lets those villains threaten the school's legitimacy. The same visibility economy can therefore produce recruitment, vulnerability and reputational attack.


## 7.2 The `内通者` hypothesis must remain prospective OPEN


Present Mic raises the possibility of an insider because knowledge of the camp location was tightly restricted (`V10:p050-051`). Vlad resists converting suspicion into indiscriminate mistrust, while Nezu states that trust in students and staff does not justify ignoring the possibility.


At V10 the evidence supports only:


- restricted information makes insider access a rational hypothesis;
- staff explicitly consider it;
- no identity is established.


No later reveal may be backfilled into the V10 epistemic state.


This is also institutional modeling evidence: Nezu can combine affiliative trust with non-zero-probability threat assessment instead of treating them as mutually exclusive.


## 7.3 Aizawa distinguishes conduct accountability from character judgment


The press conference is one of Aizawa's strongest non-combat scenes so far.


His formal register and bow accept public-facing responsibility. Yet when asked about Bakugo, he supplies a behavior-based interpretation rather than sentimental reassurance: the same Sports Festival record being used against Bakugo shows someone who is desperately pursuing the top-hero position.


That is consistent with Aizawa's earlier perceptual role:


- V05: sees Uraraka's agency where the crowd sees victimhood.
- V08: identifies student-specific bottlenecks and preserves `勝ち筋`.
- V10: sees Bakugo's aspirational structure where villains/public commentary can see only aggression.


Aizawa's pedagogy and public defense share one core capability: **he is unusually attentive to the difference between visible conduct and the decision structure generating it.**


## 7.4 Professional action converts concern into coordinated jurisdiction


The subsequent raid shows why “leave it to the professionals” is not synonymous with passivity.


Police and heroes:


- use Yaoyorozu's tracker-derived intelligence;
- identify multiple likely locations;
- prioritize abductee recovery and suspect arrest;
- coordinate simultaneous action to reduce escape routes;
- distribute roles across specialists;
- accept that the incident could become a trigger for hero-society collapse (`V10:p120-123`).


Tiger's personal desire to recover Ragdoll is present, but it is embedded in the joint plan. This provides a clean contrast with Iida's V06 vengeance: personal care is not erased by professional structure; it is **disciplined through shared information and authorized coordination**.

# 8. Shigaraki and AFO: self-authorship inside engineered dependency


## 8.1 Strategic failure tests the V09 leadership model


V09 supplied the first strong evidence that Shigaraki's V08 `信念` was generating actual decision rules. V10 then gives that strategy a meaningful failure. Bakugo refuses recruitment, the bar is raided, and League members are immobilized.


The important character question is not whether Shigaraki ever fails. It is what failure does to his action system.


His request `先生 力を貸せ` (`V10:p116`) shows that under strategic defeat he reactivates mentor dependency. But it is not an involuntary puppet response. He recognizes that the situation exceeds his available resources and asks.


AFO immediately validates the judgment.


This is exactly the kind of scene that requires the V2 model to hold two truths simultaneously:


- Shigaraki can exercise real situational judgment;
- his ability to survive the consequences of that judgment remains structurally dependent on AFO.


## 8.2 AFO supplies a causal story of abandonment


The grooming/remembered language around `V10:p151` is unusually important for villain-formation analysis.


AFO tells Shigaraki that nobody saved him, that people looked away while expecting heroes to handle the problem, names him `志村転弧くん`, and says `君は悪くない`.


The scene establishes an interpretive intervention:


1. identify suffering and non-rescue;
2. frame bystander reliance on heroes as culpable abandonment;
3. externalize blame away from the child;
4. position AFO as the person who sees what others refused to see.


At V10 we should not infer the complete underlying biography or adjudicate every causal claim in AFO's explanation. What is direct is that **AFO provides Shigaraki with a socially explanatory narrative for pain and a relationship in which that narrative is coupled to relief.**


That is grooming by meaning, not only by command or material reward.


## 8.3 `僕がいる` is an anti-hero reassurance formula


AFO's `もう大丈夫` / `僕がいる` (`V10:p152-153`) is deliberately legible against heroic reassurance language. The emotional offer is: where society relied on absent heroes, AFO is actually here.


This does not make his care morally equivalent to All Might's. It reveals why dependency can feel like rescue from inside the relationship.


The danger is strongest when manipulation does not feel like manipulation.


## 8.4 AFO explicitly values Shigaraki's independent thinking


The line `せっかく弔が自身で考え 自身で導き始めたんだ` (`V10:p162`) decisively rules out a simplistic model in which AFO wants Shigaraki to remain incapable of autonomous thought.


His stated project includes Shigaraki:


- thinking for himself;
- beginning to lead for himself;
- experiencing plans as his own;
- learning through failure.


Yet AFO controls the protective environment in which those failures do not become terminal. He retrieves allies, moves pieces, supplies force and reframes defeat as retry.


This gives the project a more precise formulation:


> **AFO engineers dependency not by preventing self-authorship but by monopolizing the conditions under which self-authorship can survive failure.**


## 8.5 Protected retry is a form of successor infrastructure


At `V10:p180-181`, AFO tells Shigaraki not to be discouraged and to try again as many times as necessary. `その為に僕がいるんだよ` makes the relationship functional: the mentor exists to make repeated attempts possible.


`全ては君の為にある` then expands the frame toward totalization. Resources, allies and perhaps the surrounding structure are offered as if organized around Shigaraki's development.


Prospectively, we must keep AFO's ultimate hidden objective OPEN. V10 proves what he **says and does** as mentor; it does not prove that the apparent successor-centered framing exhausts his motive.

# 9. AFO, Best Jeanist and the limits of appropriation


## 9.1 Best Jeanist separates Quirk possession from professional mastery


During the confrontation, Best Jeanist uses fiber control with extraordinary speed and precision to move/protect multiple people (`V10:p175-177`). AFO's reaction is analytically sharper than simple admiration.


He attributes Jeanist's strength to `相当な練習量と 実務経験`—a great amount of training and practical experience—and concludes `君のは…いらないな`.


This yields a major power-philosophy proposition:


> **AFO can appropriate a Quirk, but the source distinguishes appropriating the capacity from appropriating the trained person who made that capacity exceptional.**


The point is not that skill is literally impossible to reproduce after theft; the scene does not establish that. It establishes that AFO himself evaluates some power as unattractive because its exceptional effectiveness depends heavily on acquired mastery.


## 9.2 Quirk selection is psychologically successor-specific


AFO then says the Quirk is `弔とは 性の合わない“個性”だ` (`V10:p177`).


This is important because it rejects a simple “steal the strongest available ability” model. AFO curates power against Shigaraki's disposition.


His successor engineering is therefore at least partly **person-specific**:


- what capacities fit this successor;
- what capacities require kinds of discipline that do not fit him;
- what powers can become useful within the successor's existing behavioral style.


That is another way AFO simultaneously instrumentalizes and recognizes Shigaraki. He treats the successor as a person whose temperament constrains what gifts will actually function.


## 9.3 Appropriation has epistemic limits


The Best Jeanist scene also prevents the OFA/AFO contrast from collapsing into “giving good, taking bad.” AFO's system has a practical epistemology: he assesses not only abilities but their dependence on tacit expertise.


Power is not fungible.


That proposition should remain active throughout later Quirk-transfer material.

# 10. All Might: from singular reassurance toward coordinated rescue


## 10.1 Guilt becomes sanctioned counter-action


The forest aftermath hits All Might personally, but his response is not an unauthorized solo hunt. He joins a coordinated rescue/suppression operation and explicitly asks for professional assistance.


This is a useful contrast with the students' debate. All Might's rescue instinct is enormous, but in V10 it is embedded in police intelligence and team structure.


## 10.2 `我々が来た` modifies the Symbol grammar


The bar raid spread around `V10:p138-139` uses `我々が` / `来た！`.


The phrase matters because All Might's iconic reassurance is singular: *I am here.* Here, the rescue arrives as a plural professional formation.


The manga is not yet declaring the end of the Symbol of Peace—that would import V11. But V10 already supplies a visual-linguistic counterpoint:


> **The most symbolically singular hero enters one of the series' largest rescue operations as part of an institutional “we.”**


## 10.3 All Might recognizes Bakugo's endurance without requiring vulnerability performance


After the raid reaches Bakugo, All Might tells him he must have been frightened and praises how well he endured (`V10:p140`). Bakugo responds `こわくねえよクソッ!!`.


The exchange is small but useful.


All Might offers permission to have experienced fear; Bakugo rejects the vulnerable self-description while accepting the rescue context. The scene therefore adds to Bakugo's receiving-help model without pretending that rescue makes him suddenly comfortable with being read as frightened or dependent.

AFO's intervention destroys the clean closure the raid seemed to promise. The students experience his presence as enough to produce a bodily hallucination/anticipation of death (`V10:p164-165`), and Midoriya identifies the figure All Might had warned him about.


By `V10:p185`, All Might and AFO are again in direct conflict. The correct V10 state is **confrontation reactivated**.


Any claim about the final transfer of symbolic burden, All Might's post-battle status, or the public meaning of the outcome must wait for V11.

# 12. Visual grammar and motif


## 12.1 Failed reach becomes missing space


V09 ended with bodies converging to protect Bakugo. V10 turns that geometry into absence. Bakugo's `来んな デク` (`V10:p042`) and subsequent disappearance create a visual/relational failure of reach before the hospital chapters repeatedly verbalize `手が届く`.


The hand motif is therefore not only rescue aspiration. It now includes:


- a hand that cannot reach in time;
- a person who rejects one rescuer's approach;
- injured arms that constrain future reaching;
- later attempts to redesign what “reaching” may legitimately mean.


## 12.2 Bandaged arms make accumulated cost persistent between action scenes


Midoriya's hospital body remains visually present during the rescue debate. His injuries are not cleared away so that the next moral problem can begin. The body is part of the argument.


This strengthens a recurring MHA visual principle:


> **Past decisions remain on bodies as state information.**


Iida's remembered brother and Midoriya's bandaging make that continuity relational as well as individual.


## 12.3 Iida's strike and protective posture reverse Hosu geometry


The physical confrontation with Midoriya initially resembles coercive interruption: Iida stops movement with force. Later he positions himself as the person who will protect the group (`今度は俺が守る`).


The visual function is reversal. In Hosu, friends entered Iida's violent narrowing. In V10, Iida inserts himself into friends' narrowing so that he can become a brake.


## 12.4 The press conference stages institutional vulnerability


U.A.'s representatives bow before cameras while Bakugo watches remotely. The composition puts public judgment and the absent student's face in one interpretive circuit.


Aizawa's lowered posture accepts responsibility; Bakugo's defiant reaction retains agency. The institution's apology does not visually reduce the student to an object of shame.


## 12.5 AFO is first experienced as environmental death


Before the students can conceptually process AFO, his presence is staged as a bodily false perception of death (`V10:p164-165`). The threat is not initially a list of powers. It is an environmental condition that collapses imagined survival.


This gives AFO a different visual ontology from Shigaraki's earlier close-range irritation or Stain's ideological pressure. He enters as **overwhelming field dominance**.


## 12.6 `僕がいる` inverts rescue iconography


AFO's reassurance appears amid destruction and the forced relocation/protection of League members. The visual effect is an anti-hero arrival: the person who says “I am here” is the figure enabling the villains to survive professional defeat.


The rescue grammar itself is morally neutral at the level of form; what changes is whom the rescue serves and what dependency it builds.


## 12.7 Best Jeanist's fibers visualize expertise as networked rescue


Jeanist's fibers physically redistribute bodies through space. The scene makes practical experience visible as controlled multi-person coordination, then immediately has AFO interpret that performance as the product of training rather than an intrinsically spectacular Quirk.


This is one of the clearest places where visual virtuosity and explicit textual interpretation reinforce each other.


## 12.8 `我々が来た` visually pluralizes reassurance


The bar raid uses multiple heroes and simultaneous restraint around the arrival text. The image does not erase All Might's prominence; it embeds it in a coordinated formation.


That pluralization should be tracked into later Symbol-of-Peace analysis without prematurely treating it as completed succession.


## 12.9 `全ては君の為にある` expands AFO's gift frame


AFO's successor-centered language is given large visual space near the volume's end. The scale matters: protection is presented not as one favor but as a world/resources orientation around Shigaraki.


That is precisely why the relationship can feel empowering while becoming structurally totalizing.

# 13. Hero Society and institutional model updates


## 13.1 Public trust is now explicitly part of the battlefield


V10 is the first volume to state so directly that a villain operation can seize institutional trust along with a person. The kidnapping becomes a reputational weapon because the victim is a U.A. student whose behavior was already publicly legible.


Hero Society therefore operates in at least three simultaneous spaces:


- physical rescue/security;
- legal/professional legitimacy;
- narrative/public interpretation.


A failure in one can amplify the others.


## 13.2 U.A. shows self-critique, suspicion control and active adaptation


The school does not respond with one institutional personality. Staff disagree about insider risk, publicly accept responsibility, protect a student's moral reputation, and work with police on a raid.


This continues the V07–V09 correction against static-U.A. caricature. The new V10 complication is that adaptation now happens **under public legitimacy loss**, not only within pedagogy.


## 13.3 Protection of minors creates real jurisdictional limits


Iida's `保護下` language and All Might's instruction to Yaoyorozu make explicit that students' heroic dispositions do not automatically authorize self-deployment.


The series is therefore not simply narratively “unlocking” the children into hero status. It continues to distinguish:


- possessing heroic motive;
- having useful information or competence;
- being legally/professionally authorized to conduct a dangerous operation.


The tension is increasingly difficult precisely because the students are becoming capable enough to matter.


## 13.4 Professional legitimacy includes planning, not only power differential


The adult raid is not legitimate merely because the participants are stronger. Its professional form includes:


- police intelligence;
- simultaneous targeting;
- abductee recovery priority;
- arrest objectives;
- specialist role assignment;
- coordination designed to prevent escape.


This is evidence for what “professional” means in MHA beyond licensure as a bureaucratic badge.


## 13.5 Accountability need not mean epistemic surrender


Aizawa's press performance becomes a general institutional proposition:


> **An institution can acknowledge that it failed to protect or educate adequately while still contesting an inaccurate external interpretation of the person harmed by that failure.**


This will matter later whenever public distrust is treated as if it automatically makes hostile critiques accurate.

The League attempts to convert public aggression into evidence of moral affinity. The attempt fails because Bakugo's core aspiration was not generated by the resentment frame the League offers.

- absolution;

## 14.5 Temperament-tailored power is part of successor engineering


The Best Jeanist assessment shows AFO curating abilities based on Shigaraki's temperament and the amount/type of mastery required.


Villain succession is therefore not only ideological grooming. It includes technical design around the successor's behavioral profile.

# 15. Recognition / failed rescue model updates


## 15.1 V10 distinguishes successful rescue from successful interpretation


The League physically reaches Bakugo but understands him poorly. Aizawa is physically distant yet interprets his values accurately. This separation is crucial.


Recognition is not identical to proximity or possession.


## 15.2 `手が届く` can create both moral clarity and dangerous narrowing


For Midoriya, reachable suffering becomes morally salient with unusual force. V10 reveals the downside: “reachability” can become an activation condition that pushes him toward action even when he is injured and professionals are already operating.


A rescue ethic therefore needs not only sensitivity to suffering but calibration of jurisdiction and future capacity.


## 15.3 Iida becomes a second-order rescuer


Iida is not primarily trying to rescue Bakugo himself. He is trying to rescue the rescuers from reproducing a failure mode he knows intimately.


That expands the series' rescue vocabulary. Sometimes the person one must protect is the would-be hero whose moral urgency is becoming dangerous.


## 15.4 AFO weaponizes the memory of failed rescue


AFO's story to Shigaraki takes non-rescue as foundational evidence about society: people looked away because they expected heroes to handle it. He then offers himself as the corrective presence.


The sequence demonstrates that recognition failure does not inherently produce humanistic rescue politics. It can be appropriated into dependency and destructive grievance.

# 22. What this volume changes in the current MHA model


## 22.1 Rescue now requires jurisdiction, not only willingness


The story no longer lets “I want to save someone” settle the question of action. Heroic impulse, professional authority, institutional responsibility and relationship knowledge can point in different directions.


## 22.2 Bakugo becomes one of the strongest early character-model validation successes


Surface aggression predicted badly; longitudinal value evidence predicted well. His recruitment refusal explicitly cites the same All Might-victory origin already in the model.


## 22.3 Iida's moral repair becomes transferable


Hosu is no longer an isolated lesson. Iida recognizes the structure of his own former failure in friends and becomes a protective constraint inside their action.


## 22.4 Midoriya's sustainability problem becomes informed rather than merely experiential


He now has explicit medical knowledge that repeated comparable arm damage threatens permanent disability. Future cost acceptance must be read against that knowledge state.


## 22.5 Momo's V08 confidence repair survives a real crisis


She trusts Todoroki without becoming epistemically subordinate and defines herself as the person who may have to stop the group.


## 22.6 U.A. demonstrates accountability without accepting hostile misrecognition


The school can admit institutional failure while arguing—correctly, in Bakugo's case—that villains have misunderstood the student's underlying aspiration.


## 22.7 Public trust becomes a resource villains can target


The forest attack proves that kidnapping a highly visible student can injure the institution symbolically as well as operationally.


## 22.8 AFO's mentorship model becomes far more psychologically specific


He recognizes pain, supplies causal explanation, absolves, protects, enables retry, praises self-direction and tailors power to temperament. Dependency and subjective agency are not opposites in his system.


## 22.9 Best Jeanist establishes the skill residue of power


Quirk possession alone does not explain professional excellence. Training and practical experience can be so central that AFO judges the underlying Quirk unattractive to steal for Shigaraki.


## 22.10 Collective rescue enters All Might's own symbolic grammar


`我々が来た` puts the most singular hero inside an institutional plural before the source has yet resolved what happens to the Symbol itself.


## 22.11 The V1 V10–V11 bundle is analytically too coarse


Volume 10 supplies the kidnapping aftermath, student-jurisdiction debate, Bakugo recruitment validation, U.A. public response, professional raid, AFO/Shigaraki dependency architecture and the opening of the All Might/AFO confrontation.


The famous Kamino resolution, public succession message, final All Might status, Inko decision and dorm transition remain Volume 11 evidence.


## 22.12 Volume 10's governing question for the next reading


> **When protection succeeds or fails, does it leave the protected person more able to author the next step—or more dependent on the protector who made survival possible?**


That question should remain active through Volume 11 for Bakugo, Midoriya, Shigaraki, All Might, AFO, U.A. and the families/institutions around them.
