# 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 6 asked whether a claimed identity survives contact with actual conduct. Volume 7 asks the next question:

> **What survives after conduct has exposed the truth?**

Its governing structure is therefore not simply “the conclusion of the Stain arc” followed by school material. Across apparently separate chapters, the volume repeatedly shows **crisis residue becoming future structure**.

Iida's revenge fails morally before it fails tactically. Volume 7 does not allow the experience to disappear once Stain is defeated. His shame becomes apology, an embodied reminder in his damaged left hand, a more discriminating account of Stain's ideology, and a renewed commitment to prevent others from repeating his mistake. `Reインゲニウム` is therefore not a magical reset. It is the beginning of turning a compromised inheritance into a revised practice.

Stain loses the physical confrontation, but his influence expands after capture. His violence becomes image, footage, language, news, internet circulation, and recruitment stimulus. Gran Torino explicitly compares the pressure generated by Stain's strong ideology or obsession to the same *kind* of symbolic force produced by All Might's `平和の象徴観念` (V07:p081-082). The comparison does not morally equate them. It does something more important: it shows that **symbolic power is a social technology whose moral direction depends on what it asks people to become**.

The villains then supply an organizational answer. Diffuse grievance is individually small. A charismatic frame gives it language; media gives it reach; a group such as the League gives it a `受け皿`, a receptacle in which separate malice can aggregate into a larger collective will (V07:p082). Villain formation therefore becomes not only psychological but infrastructural.

The police aftermath performs the same conversion on the hero side. The Hosu police chief explains that hero coercive authority is historically conditional: early police deliberately prioritized standardized public order and refrained from treating Quirks as ordinary weapons; heroes emerged into that gap, and the privilege of public Quirk use remains legitimate only because predecessors accumulated trust through rules and restraint (V07:p063-067). The students' rescue was morally consequential, but that does not erase the institutional problem of unlicensed force. The compromise that follows — suppressing the students' direct role and allocating public credit to licensed adults — creates another form of residue: **actual causal truth and official public narrative diverge**.

One For All and All For One then reveal that the same problem operates across generations. All For One is defined as a power that can take another person's Quirk, make it one's own, and give Quirks to others (V07:p117 onward). Its social history is appropriation joined to unilateral distribution and dependency. One For All originates when forced power-giving unexpectedly combines with an inherited/passing ability in All For One's resistant younger brother (V07:p121). The powers are not merely opposites because one is used by heroes and one by villains. Their **relational grammars** are opposite:

- All For One takes, owns, assigns, and builds dependency around a central possessor.
- One For All passes forward, accumulates through successors, and makes the present bearer dependent on a chain of prior wills rather than sovereign over them.

Volume 7 therefore gives the project its strongest early confirmation that inheritance itself is a theory of power.

Even All Might's mentorship shows that inheritance can fail at the point of emotional transmission. Midoriya tells him that with All Might beside him he feels capable of anything. All Might internally knows he may no longer be beside Midoriya when the inherited conflict reaches its next stage, tells himself to say so, and cannot complete the disclosure (V07:p124-126). The mentor can transmit history and power more easily than he can transmit the pain of his own likely absence.

U.A. converts social threat into pedagogy. Faculty change the practical final from the expected robot exercise to student pairs fighting teachers because the villain environment has changed. Their education must now test cooperation, judgment, escape, weakness-management, and adversarial adaptation rather than only destructive output.

Finally, the volume turns this logic inward to Midoriya and Bakugo. The two have inherited different aspects of All Might. Midoriya has organized himself around rescue and reverence; Bakugo's childhood `Origin` establishes that he loved All Might because, no matter how bad the situation looked, `最後は絶対勝つ` — he always won in the end (V07:p181-188). Yet Bakugo's relationship injury becomes so severe that he says losing would be preferable to borrowing Midoriya's strength (V07:p185). His deepest heroic value — refusal to surrender — is momentarily overridden by the relational condition through which help would have to arrive.

Midoriya's response is significant because he does not ask Bakugo to become gentler or more like himself. He calls Bakugo back to **Bakugo's own standard**:

`負けていいなんて 言わないでよ！`

`勝つのを諦めないのが 君じゃないか――!!` (V07:p186-187)

The volume then ends before reconciliation. Bakugo and Midoriya begin coordinated action against All Might, and Midoriya literally uses Bakugo's stored explosive equipment at Bakugo's order to fire (V07:p189-190). The closed circuit has been breached, not healed.

A concise volume thesis is therefore:

> **Volume 7 argues that recognition matters only if what has been recognized changes what comes next. Pain can become doctrine, injury can become memory, ideology can become a movement, crisis can become institutional redesign, inheritance can become obligation, and rivalry can become cooperation — but none of those transformations are automatic or morally neutral. Aftermath is where an event becomes a future.**

# 2. Narrative architecture

## 2.1 `Reインゲニウム` begins with ordinary care rather than grand heroism

The title immediately promises some kind of return to Ingenium, but the chapter deliberately grounds the inherited ideal in Tensei's remarkably modest hero philosophy.

In memory, Tensei says the person he finds coolest is someone who sees a lost child and simply takes the child's hand to the lost-child center: `迷子を見かけたら迷子センターへ手を引いてやれる / そういう人間が一番かっこいいと思うんだよな` (V07:p011).

This matters because Iida's revenge in Volume 6 made the hero name enormous. `Ingenium` became family honor, injury, inherited duty, and the desire to defeat the man who crippled his brother. Tensei's own philosophy shrinks the name back to human scale. Heroism is not primarily avenging Ingenium. It is seeing a person who needs help and taking their hand somewhere safe.

The formal irony is exact: Iida can honor his brother most faithfully by acting less like an avenger of a famous hero and more like an ordinary person who notices the person beside him.

## 2.2 Iida's recovery is enacted before it is verbally completed

The Stain fight resumes with Iida attempting to move again after Todoroki's V06 challenge. His return is not instantaneous mastery. He remains injured, emotionally compromised, and dependent on the others. But his attention begins shifting from Stain-as-object-of-hatred toward the shared task of protecting the injured and ending the threat.

That is why `Reインゲニウム` should be treated as a **process title**, not proof of completed moral restoration. The heroic identity is being rebuilt through redirected action.

Midoriya, Todoroki, and Iida win through complementary contributions. No one character monopolizes the rescue. This continues the distributed structure already visible at Hosu: relational correction and tactical victory are collective.

## 2.3 Endeavor's simultaneous Nomu response preserves role differentiation

While the students confront Stain, Endeavor is handling the wider Nomu crisis. He rapidly identifies the significance of an enemy exhibiting multiple Quirks and adjusts his tactical response rather than behaving as a one-note brute (V07:p030 onward).

This is important for the character architecture. V04–V05 established Endeavor as an abusive father whose family project converted Shoto into an instrument of ambition. V07 does not soften that judgment. It adds a second role state: **professional competence can coexist with grave private moral failure**.

Later analysis must resist flattening him in either direction. Being an effective pro hero does not excuse the household. Being an abusive father does not mean his combat/professional cognition is fictitious.

## 2.4 Friendship becomes explicit after the intervention

Todoroki's reason for following Midoriya's location message is straightforward: `友だちがピンチかもしれねえ` — a friend might be in trouble (V07:p035-036 region). His action is no longer explainable only by debt for the Sports Festival intervention or abstract hero duty.

After the fight, Iida apologizes directly for causing both friends to be injured (V07:p042). Midoriya then does something crucial: he apologizes too.

`君があそこまで思いつめてたのに 全然見えてなかったんだ`

`友だちなのに…` (V07:p043)

This is one of the clearest early statements of the project's recognition thesis. Midoriya did notice that Iida was wrong in V06. What he now condemns in himself is the insufficiency of that perception. He did not see **how far** Iida had gone.

Recognition therefore becomes graded rather than binary:

1. see an abnormal signal;
2. infer its depth;
3. decide whether social boundaries should be pressed;
4. intervene early enough to change trajectory.

V06 failed at stages two through four. V07 turns that failure into explicit friendship knowledge.

## 2.5 Stain saving Midoriya prevents ideological simplification

After the nominal fight is over, a winged Nomu seizes Midoriya. The badly injured Stain attacks it and saves Midoriya (V07:p053-055).

This does **not** redeem Stain. It does establish behavioral consistency in his classification system. He does not merely hate the category “hero” or opportunistically murder whoever is nearby. Midoriya's prior rescue-directed conduct has placed him in a different moral category from Stain's `粛清対象`.

This is useful modeling evidence because it prevents the easy caricature “Stain attacks all heroes.” His worldview is lethal precisely because it is **discriminating** and then self-authorizing. He sees distinctions and believes he alone may enforce them through violence.

## 2.6 Stain's final stand turns ideology into affective force

Stain's last conscious display is formally disproportionate to his physical condition. He is grievously injured, his Quirk is not the source of the surrounding heroes' paralysis, and yet his declaration that only a genuine hero may kill him freezes the group momentarily (V07:p055-058).

The manga darkens and enlarges his face/body until the pressure seems almost supernatural. Later Gran Torino supplies the diegetic interpretation: the force likely comes from a strong ideology or obsession, the same *kind* of thing as Toshinori's Symbol-of-Peace conviction (V07:p081).

The point is not “Stain equals All Might.” It is:

> **conviction can become socially perceptible power before it becomes institution, law, or Quirk.**

All Might's conviction reassures and coordinates. Stain's terrifies and purifies. Both demonstrate how a person can become more than an individual actor by embodying an idea other people recognize.

## 2.7 The police aftermath makes hero legitimacy historical rather than natural

The Hosu police chief's hospital explanation is among the most important institutional passages in the early manga (V07:p063-067).

In the chaotic early superhuman era, police prioritized `統率と規格`; public order required standardization. They did not simply decide that anyone with a useful Quirk could deploy it as a weapon. Heroes emerged to occupy a special social space, and their exceptional public use of potentially lethal powers is tolerated because earlier heroes built a record of morality and rule-observance.

The argument means that hero authority is not metaphysically contained inside a license. It is **borrowed from institutional history and public trust**.

That makes the students' position difficult. Todoroki correctly points out the counterfactual human costs: without their actions, Native or the students themselves could have died, and the licensed system had not yet reached Stain. `人を……救けるのがヒーローの仕事だろ` (V07:p065) is morally powerful.

The police chief's answer is not “then let them die.” He asks whether a good result is sufficient grounds to let rules dissolve. If every powerful individual privately decides when extraordinary violence is justified, the hero system loses the very restraint that makes public Quirk use tolerable.

The volume therefore refuses a cheap choice between:

- procedural legalism indifferent to rescue; and
- outcome-based vigilantism indifferent to authorization.

The actual response is an ethically messy institutional compromise.

## 2.8 The cover-up separates actual causality from public heroic narrative

Because eyewitnesses are limited, the police can `握り潰す` the violation. The students' achievement will not be publicly known; licensed heroes receive official credit (V07:p066-067).

This produces a major worldbuilding proposition:

> **public reputation in hero society is not a transparent ledger of who actually caused a rescue.**

The distortion here is not celebrity fraud for its own sake. It is generated by law, age, supervision, institutional accountability, and the desire not to permanently damage promising students over a morally complicated violation.

The same action can therefore be:

- causally indispensable;
- morally understandable or admirable;
- legally/professionally unauthorized;
- institutionally suppressed;
- publicly credited to someone else.

That is a much richer model of hero society than a simple ranking economy.

## 2.9 Iida chooses to retain injury as embodied memory

The hospital doctor explains that Iida's left-arm nerve damage could potentially be treated through surgery/transplant. Iida says he wants to leave the hand as it is until he becomes a true hero: `俺が本当のヒーローになれるまで / この左手は残そうと思う` (V07:p077-078).

This is one of the series' strongest early examples of moral memory being inscribed into the body.

The decision is not unambiguously healthy. It has at least two simultaneous meanings:

- **accountability mnemonic:** the body prevents him from abstracting his mistake into a lesson already mastered;
- **self-punitive risk:** guilt becomes attached to continued impairment as proof that he has not earned restoration.

The longitudinal ledger should preserve both. Later evidence must decide whether the damaged hand functions mainly as disciplined memory, self-punishment, or some mixture.

## 2.10 Ordinary humor becomes part of post-trauma repair

The hospital trio scene gives Todoroki a small but valuable low-stakes behavior. He awkwardly observes that people whose hands become involved with him seem to get injured; Iida laughs, and the tension loosens (V07:p079-080).

This is not disposable comedy. It provides baseline friendship evidence after acute danger. Todoroki's care is not only battlefield competence or blunt moral command. He can also participate in awkward social decompression.

For character reconstruction this scene is unusually valuable because it shows how the trio sounds and behaves after the crisis rather than only during it.

## 2.11 Stain's defeat becomes more socially powerful than his freedom

The chapter explicitly titled for Stain's `余波` shifts from body to circulation.

News coverage, video, internet discussion, and criminal intermediaries turn a captured murderer into a symbolic object. Kaminari later gives the mechanism from the receiving side: watching the video, one can find Stain's single-minded conviction or persistence `かっこよくね？` even while fearing him (V07:p099).

Gran Torino supplies the structural model. The current age is `抑圧された`; people will be influenced. Media distribute the frame, and the League becomes the `受け皿` into which inspired or resentful actors can flow (V07:p082).

The crucial line is that individually small malice can expand many times over when gathered under one will.

This is villain formation as **aggregation**, not only biography.

## 2.12 Shigaraki experiences narrative displacement as status injury

Shigaraki's immediate reaction is not admiration. He is angry that Nomu and the League are being treated as secondary while Stain becomes the story (V07:p070 onward).

This confirms the attention/status sensitivity already visible in V06. Shigaraki wants destruction to possess social meaning. Being relegated to “the extra” is itself frustrating.

That creates an important distinction between him and Stain at this boundary:

- Stain already has a coherent moral narrative capable of propagating without him.
- Shigaraki has destructive desire and mentor infrastructure but has not yet generated an equally coherent public meaning.

Do not import later Shigaraki ideology backward.

## 2.13 Iida's classroom judgment shows repair has become doctrine

When Kaminari admits that Stain can look cool, Iida does not demand that everyone deny the charisma. He says he understands why someone might see Stain's conviction that way (V07:p099-100).

Then he draws a precise boundary:

`ただ奴は信念の果てに「粛清」という手段を選んだ`

`どんな考えを持とうとも そこだけは間違いなんだ`

This is much more mature than simply reversing from “I hate Stain” to “Stain is bad.” Iida can now separate:

- conviction from method;
- understandable moral appeal from legitimate action;
- ideological content from the right to purify persons.

He then converts his own failure into a forward obligation: `俺のような者を もうこれ以上 出さぬ為にも!! / 改めて ヒーローへの道を 俺は歩む!!` (V07:p100).

That is the strongest evidence so far that the revenge crisis has generated **stable explicit learning**, even though longitudinal proof remains incomplete.

## 2.14 Full Cowling becomes socially legible rather than private technique

During U.A.'s rescue-training race, Midoriya moves with substantially greater continuity and speed. His classmates notice. Uraraka reacts; Todoroki observes; Bakugo is especially agitated by the visible rate of Midoriya's growth (V07:p101-108).

This matters because V06 Full Cowling was primarily a private conceptual breakthrough between Midoriya and Gran Torino. V07 turns it into a social fact.

Bakugo's perception is particularly important. Midoriya's development is not only changing Midoriya's body. It is changing the comparative field through which Bakugo understands himself.

## 2.15 OFA/AFO history moves the succession conflict from biography to political history

All Might's explanation in Chapter 59 reframes the central powers.

All For One's ability to remove Quirks from others, possess them, and give them to other people lets him convert bodily difference into dependency and political organization (V07:p115-120). The early superhuman social crisis is not merely chaotic because powers exist. It is vulnerable to a central actor who can decide who gets what power.

His younger brother resists him morally but appears physically powerless. The forced stockpiling Quirk combines with the brother's hidden passing ability, producing One For All (V07:p121).

The origin is therefore founded on a paradox:

> coercive bestowal creates the material from which voluntary succession eventually resists the coercer.

One For All is not pure because every element of its origin was consensual. Its ethical direction comes from what the brother and later successors do with the inherited capacity: pass it onward as unfinished resistance rather than build possession around themselves.

## 2.16 All Might's incomplete disclosure reveals a mentorship blind spot produced by love

After hearing the history and the possibility that he may eventually confront a great inherited evil, Midoriya responds with absolute trust in All Might's presence. All Might internally recognizes the problem. He intends to say that he probably will no longer be beside Midoriya by then — and cannot say it (V07:p124-126).

This is a subtle but important failure of guidance.

All Might has improved since V02, when Recovery Girl criticized his weakness as a technical mentor. By V07 he can provide historical truth. But emotional protection still interferes with complete preparation.

The resulting principle is:

> **care can become a form of withholding even when possession/control is absent.**

That will matter later whenever the project evaluates whether mentors preserve another person's agency by telling them enough to make informed choices.

## 2.17 U.A. converts villain escalation into curriculum redesign

The final-exam redesign is direct institutional learning. Students expect the familiar robot structure; staff decide that worsening villain conditions require a practical exam against teachers instead (V07:p137 onward).

The students are paired in ways intended to expose weaknesses and force adaptation. The win conditions include either restraining the teacher or escaping through the gate.

That matters because U.A. does not merely respond to crisis by increasing difficulty or combat output. It changes **what competence means**:

- cooperation;
- role complementarity;
- escape judgment;
- exploiting constraints;
- overcoming relational or tactical weaknesses.

This is a rare positive institutional-learning case in a series often interested in institutional blind spots.

## 2.18 `最悪のふたり` is a relationship exam disguised as combat

All Might explicitly tells Midoriya and Bakugo to cooperate and come win: `協力して 勝ちに来いよ お二人さん!!` (V07:p151 onward).

Bakugo immediately treats All Might as an opponent to crush; Midoriya treats escape as a viable victory condition. Their disagreement is not merely tactical. It reflects different inherited meanings of All Might.

More importantly, the conflict reveals how relationship conditioning can override otherwise competent decision-making. Bakugo's hostility toward Midoriya makes collaboration itself feel like defeat.

Midoriya, meanwhile, explicitly rejects the old conversational asymmetry. When Bakugo yells and hits him, Midoriya answers: `怒鳴らないでよ!! / それでいつも会話にならないんだよ!!` (V07:p166-167).

This is a major state change from early childhood/middle-school dynamics. Midoriya is no longer only trying to survive or appease Bakugo's anger. He names the communicative mechanism as dysfunctional.

## 2.19 Bakugo's `Origin` grounds his victory ethic in childhood admiration

Bakugo's `Origin` is not “he likes winning” as an abstract trait. The chapter shows the formative object.

As a child, he watches All Might appear to be losing against multiple opponents and delights in the reversal: no matter how bad the pinch, All Might ultimately wins. `どんだけピンチでも 最後は絶対勝つんだよなぁ!!` (V07:p181-183).

The memory gives Bakugo's hero philosophy a relational source:

> **All Might taught Bakugo that the proof of the greatest hero is the refusal to let crisis determine the final outcome.**

This makes Bakugo's present refusal to use Midoriya especially contradictory.

## 2.20 The deepest Bakugo contradiction is exposed by `負けた方がマシ`

Bakugo says that rather than borrow Midoriya's power, losing would be better (V07:p185).

This is not evidence that Bakugo does not care about winning. It is evidence that **Midoriya-conditioned status injury can temporarily outrank winning**.

That is a much stronger and more precise behavioral model than “Bakugo is irrational around Deku.” The relation activates a hierarchy threat so intense that Bakugo becomes willing to betray the very heroic value through which he understands himself.

Midoriya sees the contradiction and uses it as leverage.

## 2.21 The final image makes cooperation materially visible

Midoriya's answer culminates in `勝つのを諦めないのが 君じゃないか` (V07:p186-187). The two then begin acting together.

The volume closes with Midoriya wielding Bakugo's stored explosive equipment while Bakugo orders him to fire (V07:p189-190).

The formal reversal is almost literal:

- Bakugo says he would rather lose than borrow Midoriya's strength.
- The chapter ends with **Midoriya borrowing Bakugo's strength** inside a plan Bakugo accepts.

The asymmetry matters. The first breach in their relationship is not yet Bakugo calmly receiving help from Midoriya. It is a form of cooperation that allows Bakugo to remain an active author of the shared tactic.

That is exactly why the endpoint must remain prospective:

> cooperation is now behaviorally possible; reciprocal trust and genuine relational repair remain OPEN.

# 3. Character-state ledger

## 3.1 Midoriya Izuku

### Current state

V07 strongly deepens Midoriya without changing his readiness tier. His important development is not a new moral value; it is the increasing sophistication with which he applies existing rescue/recognition values across law, friendship, inheritance, and conflict.

### Friendship failure becomes explicit self-critique

Midoriya does not simply accept Iida's apology. He identifies his own failure to understand how deeply Iida was suffering, despite being his friend (V07:p043).

This adds a corrective to the “Midoriya always sees people” model. He is unusually attentive, but not omniscient. Distress can be visible and still be underestimated, especially when the other person closes the door.

### He can accept moral achievement without needing public credit

The police arrangement erases the students from the official narrative. Midoriya accepts that bargain because preserving people and their futures matters more than public authorship of the rescue (V07:p065-067).

This is useful contrast with the Sports Festival/public-legibility arc. Midoriya values recognition when it enables professional opportunity or another person's agency, but he is not primarily motivated by being publicly credited for heroic causation.

### Full Cowling becomes ordinary movement

At school, the technique is no longer framed only as a breakthrough. It is becoming his baseline bodily grammar. The important modeling shift is from “can use Full Cowling” to “begins to think/move from a continuously powered body.”

### All Might's presence remains a psychological support dependency

After the AFO/OFA history, Midoriya says that if All Might is there, he feels he can do anything (V07:p124-125). This shows that technical individuation has advanced faster than emotional individuation.

Midoriya can make OFA more bodily his own while still relying heavily on All Might as a guarantee that the future is survivable.

### Midoriya becomes more assertive toward Bakugo

His `怒鳴らないでよ!! / それでいつも会話にならないんだよ!!` is high-value relationship-specific evidence (V07:p166-167).

He is not globally dominant or newly fearless. The change is conditional: when the shared goal requires real communication, he is increasingly willing to challenge Bakugo's interaction pattern directly.

### He reaches Bakugo through Bakugo's values rather than imposing his own

`勝つのを諦めないのが 君じゃないか` is recognition-based intervention. Midoriya's best leverage is not “help me because teamwork is good.” It is “your present refusal contradicts the person I know you to be.”

This extends the pattern from Todoroki and Iida: Midoriya often rescues agency by identifying a person's own deeper value and returning it to them.

## 3.2 Iida Tenya

### Current state

V07 resolves the major V06 OPEN question about whether Iida's first reorientation becomes substantive moral learning. It does.

### Tensei's ideal is lower-status than Iida's revenge made it

The lost-child memory reframes what Iida has inherited. The brother he wanted to avenge defines admirable heroism through ordinary handholding care (V07:p011). This gives Iida a concrete alternative to honor-through-punishment.

### Apology is direct and responsibility-taking

Iida does not blame Stain, grief, or circumstances for the injuries his friends received. He explicitly says they were hurt because of him and apologizes (V07:p042).

### The damaged hand becomes chosen memory

His decision to retain the injury until he becomes a “true hero” suggests unusually strong self-monitoring through bodily symbol (V07:p077-078). Preserve both constructive-accountability and self-punitive interpretations.

### He can now separate conviction from method

The classroom discussion is the clearest evidence of moral repair. Iida acknowledges that Stain possessed conviction and understands why someone could find it impressive, but categorically identifies `粛清` as the point that remains wrong regardless of belief (V07:p099-100).

This is not post-trauma slogan reversal. It is analytical differentiation.

### Failure becomes prevention obligation

`俺のような者を もうこれ以上 出さぬ為にも` turns his own moral collapse into a future-facing purpose (V07:p100).

This matters because it moves guilt away from purely private self-condemnation toward responsibility for other people who might enter the same state.

# 4. Power philosophies

## 4.1 One For All and All For One become explicit relational opposites

V07 gives the clearest early textual foundation for Hypothesis 2.

All For One is not merely “more powers.” His ability changes social relations by making the possessor the gatekeeper of bodily capability. He can take what a person has and decide what another person receives. The political temptation is obvious: people whose Quirks burden them can be relieved; people who desire power can be supplied; both paths point back toward the same central distributor.

One For All develops from that coercive history but moves in the opposite relational direction. Its bearer does not maintain power by preventing succession. The power becomes itself through accumulation across successors.

This is why the project's best formulation remains:

> **AFO makes other people's power orbit the possessor; OFA makes the possessor part of a chain larger than the self.**

## 4.2 Coercive legitimacy depends on restraint, not strength

The police chief's historical account confirms Manual's V06 warning. The right to use powerful Quirks publicly against other people is not self-justifying. It exists inside a tradition of moral restraint, supervision, and public trust.

Strength therefore creates a need for governance rather than a claim to sovereignty.

## 4.3 Rescue can be right while its method remains institutionally problematic

The students' Hosu action is a hard case. The manga refuses to say:

- they were wrong because unlicensed; or
- they were fully right because people lived.

Instead it preserves competing truths. This is an important maturation of the power philosophy: **good ends do not automatically purify all uses of force, but rules also require discretion when literal compliance would fail the persons the system exists to protect.**

## 4.4 Stain's power is partly non-Quirk symbolic authority

Stain's social force after defeat makes a strong distinction between:

- physical/Quirk power;
- moral conviction;
- symbolic authority;
- media amplification;
- organizational capacity.

A person can lose the first and gain the latter three.

## 4.5 Iida's hand makes power ethics bodily and mnemonic

Unlike Midoriya's early self-injury, Iida's retained impairment is not payment during rescue. It is chosen afterward as memory of moral failure.

That means bodily cost can perform different ethical functions:

- reckless self-sacrifice;
- unavoidable protective cost;
- disciplinary feedback;
- self-punishment;
- embodied memorialization.

Do not collapse them into one “heroic injury” category.

## 4.6 Bakugo's core philosophy is victory as refusal of final defeat

V07's Origin chapter clarifies that his ideal is not fundamentally “the strongest person is morally best.” The childhood All Might scene is about **outcome reversal under pressure**.

Bakugo's unhealthy superiority complex grows around a genuine heroic admiration: the greatest hero refuses to let bad odds become the final answer.

That makes the later contradiction with Midoriya more psychologically powerful.

## 4.7 Cooperation can preserve difference rather than erase it

Midoriya and Bakugo do not become interchangeable. Their first cooperation works because the plan begins to use their different capacities and values rather than demanding emotional harmony first.

This is a miniature of distributed heroism: coordination does not require identical personality.

# 5. Hero Society / institutional analysis

## 5.1 Hero authority is an inherited public trust

The police chief's explanation supplies a genealogy of exceptional Quirk use. A license is therefore not merely a technical credential. It sits on top of prior generations' accumulated moral and regulatory record.

Hero society is institutionally vulnerable whenever individuals treat that inherited trust as a personal permission slip.

## 5.2 The Hosu cover-up reveals a gap between public reputation and actual causality

Public news cannot be read naively as a precise record of who did what. Here the distortion is intentional and protective. That makes later ranking/media claims analytically more complex: visibility, causality, legality, and public credit are related but non-identical dimensions.

## 5.3 Accountability is distributed upward as well as downward

The police chief does not frame the students as isolated offenders. Supervising adults bear responsibility too. This is an important institutional alternative to Stain's individual purification model.

Where Stain isolates a person and sentences them, legitimate institutions can ask what network of supervision, rule, crisis, and decision produced the action.

## 5.4 U.A. demonstrates adaptive institutional learning

The practical-final redesign is a positive institutional finding. U.A. responds to an altered threat environment by changing assessment design rather than repeating an obsolete test because tradition says so.

This should remain an important control case against any later overbroad claim that hero institutions are uniformly inert.

## 5.5 Teacher exams make escape a legitimate heroic outcome

Students can win by restraining a teacher **or escaping**. This is directly important for Bakugo. The institution explicitly defines survival/withdrawal as legitimate success under the right objective.

Heroic competence therefore includes knowing when “defeat the opponent” is the wrong problem definition.

## 5.6 Professional identity can be instrumental rather than existential

Gran Torino's account of why he obtained a hero license shows that “licensed hero” need not be a person's total identity. The credential can be a lawful operational tool.

This complicates both fandom archetypes and Stain's purity categories.

# 6. Recognition and failed rescue

## 6.1 V06 recognition failure becomes explicit V07 repair

Midoriya's `友だちなのに` is crucial because it admits that caring and noticing are not enough. The friend relation creates a stronger retrospective standard: he wishes he had perceived depth, not merely signal.

## 6.2 Tensei's lost-child ideal makes recognition deliberately ordinary

The series' intervention thesis does not require dramatic intuition. The model hero sees a lost child, recognizes a simple need, and takes the child's hand.

This is perhaps the clearest early articulation that ordinary intervention belongs inside hero ethics rather than beneath it.

## 6.3 Stain recognizes Midoriya but turns recognition into classification

Stain's decision to save/spare Midoriya is evidence of perception. But his framework treats recognition as sorting people into those permitted to live and those designated for purge.

Heroic recognition keeps another person's future open; Stain's recognition can close it.

## 6.4 Iida learns from being recognized by multiple people

His repair does not come from a single speech. Tensei memory, Midoriya intervention, Todoroki challenge, friends' injuries, police aftermath, and bodily consequence all contribute.

This is an important anti-protagonist-monopoly finding.

## 6.5 Midoriya recognizes Bakugo's deeper value beneath abusive behavior

Midoriya's admiration remains morally complicated. He does not deny Bakugo's cruelty. Yet he can identify a real trait worth admiring — refusal to surrender — and use it to interrupt Bakugo's current self-defeating behavior.

This is recognition without absolution.

## 6.6 All Might fails to fully recognize what Midoriya needs to know

He recognizes Midoriya's dependence and his own likely absence, but cannot act on that knowledge through disclosure. This extends the project's recognition model:

> seeing the truth is not the same as successfully communicating it.

# 7. Villain formation, rescue, and accountability

## 7.1 Stain's route is ideology plus self-authorized violence

V07 strengthens the need to separate social critique from villain causation. Stain is not presented here as someone who became a killer because nobody recognized his pain. His salient mechanism is purificatory ideology combined with willingness to sentence persons.

## 7.2 Shigaraki's route remains developmental and attention-sensitive

He is still being taught how to turn destruction into broader meaning. His anger at Stain's publicity is evidence of comparative status injury, not yet proof that he shares Stain's worldview.

## 7.3 Villain recruitment becomes an ecosystem

Gran Torino's model is one of the most important additions to the villain-formation ledger:

`individual grievance/malice` → `charismatic symbolic frame` → `media circulation` → `organizational receptacle` → `collective escalation`.

This is not reducible to any single recruit's psychology.

## 7.4 AFO represents active manufacture of dependency

His early social rise demonstrates that villain formation can be deliberately organized by someone who solves bodily/social problems in ways that bind recipients to him.

The concept of “failed rescue” must therefore include the possibility of a predatory actor arriving **where legitimate institutions fail**, offering relief with strings attached.

## 7.5 Accountability in V07 is restorative in structure even when imperfect

The police compromise, Iida's apology, and his revised doctrine all assume that a serious failure need not define the person permanently.

This is the opposite of Stain's logic. The system is willing to conceal some public truth partly because it believes young offenders can learn and because adults share responsibility.

That discretion is ethically risky, but it is also evidence of a non-purificatory accountability model.

# 8. Japanese language, character voice, and key vocabulary

## 8.1 `Reインゲニウム`

The English `Re` prefix plus inherited hero name encodes restart/reconstruction. The title should not be translated analytically as “Ingenium restored” in a completed sense.

## 8.2 `手を引いてやれる`

Tensei's lost-child formulation makes physical guidance/hand-taking the image of admirable heroism. The verb phrase is modest, practical, and relational.

## 8.3 `友だちなのに`

Midoriya's hospital line places his recognition failure inside friendship obligation. The final `のに` carries the sense that “being a friend” should have implied better seeing.

## 8.4 `粛清対象`

Stain's language converts persons into targets of purification. The noun phrase is bureaucratically categorical despite his anti-institutional position.

## 8.5 `正しき社会`

Stain's abstract correct society remains the object for which persons may be sacrificed. The phrasing matters because the abstraction precedes the individual's continued personhood.

## 8.6 `統率` / `規格`

The police chief's language frames early legal order around coordination and standardization. These words are foundational for understanding why uncontrolled individual Quirk use is institutionally dangerous even when motives are good.

## 8.7 `規則違反`

The students' actions are explicitly named as rule violation rather than retroactively legalized by outcome.

## 8.8 `握り潰す`

The blunt idiom for suppressing the incident should not be sanitized. The institution is knowingly burying a violation/public causal truth for a perceived larger good.

## 8.9 `受け皿`

Gran Torino's use of “receptacle” for the League is conceptually important. An organization can become a container for grievances it did not create.

## 8.10 `感化`

The media discussion centers ideological influence rather than formal recruitment alone. People can be affected by a figure before joining anything.

## 8.11 `信念` and `粛清`

Iida's post-crisis distinction between conviction and purification method is central. `信念` is not condemned as such; `粛清` is.

## 8.12 `仮初の平和`

All For One's description of current peace as temporary/false foreshadows a conflict over whether All Might's symbolic order has actually produced durable institutions.

## 8.13 `怒鳴らないでよ`

Midoriya's imperative toward Bakugo is relationship-specific growth. He names the volume/intimidation mechanism rather than merely enduring it.

## 8.14 `会話にならない`

This phrase is stronger than “we disagree.” Midoriya says the interaction does not become a conversation at all under Bakugo's habitual shouting.

## 8.15 `負けた方がマシ`

Bakugo's phrasing makes the contradiction explicit: the person whose identity is organized around victory temporarily ranks relational nondependence above winning.

## 8.16 `勝つのを諦めないのが君じゃないか`

Midoriya's line is an identity appeal, not moral preaching. `君じゃないか` invokes the person as Midoriya recognizes him.

# 9. Visual and formal analysis

## 9.1 The volume moves from bodies in alleys to ideas in circulation

The first chapters remain dense with bodies, blood, wounds, and narrowed urban space. The Stain-aftermath chapters progressively widen into hospital rooms, television screens, internet/news discourse, organizational conversation, and school classrooms.

Formally, the threat changes scale from physical encounter to social propagation.

## 9.2 Stain's final face converts human injury into near-monster iconography

At V07:p055-058, black fields, enlarged eyes/teeth, blood, and extreme contrast make Stain visually exceed his damaged body. The visual excess explains why witnesses experience him as force even when his Quirk cannot account for the effect.

This is exactly the kind of panel grammar by which an individual becomes symbol.

## 9.3 Iida's damaged hand becomes a repeated isolated object

The hospital sequence repeatedly frames the hand/arm separately from the whole person before Iida decides to retain the damage (V07:p077-078). The body part becomes a mnemonic object, analogous in a different register to Midoriya's repeatedly injured hands/arms.

## 9.4 Media panels multiply Stain after his capture

Screens, reports, recordings, and discussion reproduce a single embodied villain across many social surfaces. The visual form participates in the thesis: physical containment does not contain a symbol.

## 9.5 AFO/OFA origin uses bodily transfer to diagram political relation

The history sequence is not abstract exposition only. Hands, bodies, Quirk transfer, a frail younger brother, and the act of forcing power into another person make domination physically legible before the inherited resistance is named.

## 9.6 The final-exam city is a manufactured miniature of hero society

Students must navigate an environment in which overpowering the central threat is only one possible route; escape is equally legitimate. The built environment converts decision philosophy into spatial choice.

## 9.7 Bakugo's Origin is visually mediated through All Might footage

His formative heroic ideal comes from watching a public image of All Might reverse bad odds. This connects Bakugo's private psychology to hero society's media-symbol infrastructure.

The same society that now spreads Stain once spread All Might.

## 9.8 The final gauntlet image literalizes shared power

The last pages make cooperation visible through equipment. Midoriya's body becomes the bearer of Bakugo's stored explosive resource while Bakugo remains the directing partner.

The manga ends the volume on an **interpersonal transfer of usable force**, a fitting formal echo of the chapter's larger inheritance structures.

# 10. Body, embodiment, family, and inheritance

## 10.1 Iida's body becomes a moral archive

Where V06 showed grief capturing inherited identity, V07 gives the resulting damage a memorial function. Iida's left hand is not merely medical aftermath; he chooses to preserve it as evidence against self-forgetting.

The most important thing Iida inherits from Tensei in V07 is not a name, costume, or engine style. It is a standard of ordinary attentiveness.

The origin prevents a simplistic “good power was always purely good” myth. AFO forcibly modifies his brother. The ethical reversal occurs because the resulting power enters a different relational chain.

His statement that All Might being there makes anything feel possible shows the successor has not emotionally separated the inherited mission from the mentor's continued availability.

## 10.5 Bakugo inherits no Quirk from All Might but inherits a model of heroic temporality

What he receives is a rule about endings: being cornered does not decide the final outcome. This is a non-biological, non-OFA inheritance that nonetheless organizes his whole heroic self.

## 10.6 The Midoriya-Bakugo pair demonstrate competing inherited fragments

Midoriya is oriented toward saving; Bakugo toward winning. At V07 neither alone is an adequate reproduction of All Might's complete social function.

The final exam begins the process of forcing those partial inheritances into contact.

# 16. Revised interpretation

Through Volume 7, the early series can now be read as a sequence of increasingly durable transformations:

1. **V01 — recognition:** heroism exists before power and must be seen.
2. **V02 — judgment:** good intent must become disciplined use.
3. **V03 — public legibility:** heroism enters spectacle, recruitment, and symbolic politics.
4. **V04 — self-authorship:** inherited bodies/powers/opportunities must become one's own.
5. **V05 — recognition without possession:** another person's agency must be acknowledged without patronizing or appropriating it.
6. **V06 — enactment:** names and ideals are tested by conduct under stress.
7. **V07 — residue:** the truth exposed by conduct must be converted into future structure or it remains only an event.

That last move is what makes V07 unusually important despite its mixed arc content.

Iida is the clearest personal case. The Stain encounter could become trauma, shame, and a damaged arm. Instead the manga asks whether it becomes a different way of being Ingenium. Tensei's lost-child ideal, Iida's apology, the decision about his hand, and his classroom distinction between `信念` and `粛清` show the first answer: yes, but through active reinterpretation rather than automatic suffering-as-growth.

Stain is the inverse case. Defeat does not produce reflection; it produces symbolic reproduction. His body is contained while his image becomes more mobile. This is the first major demonstration that hero/villain conflict is not only about who controls the street. It is about who controls the language through which other people interpret social dissatisfaction.

AFO then reveals the long historical version. Power can be used to make people dependent on the distributor, and succession can be engineered. OFA's history answers that structure not with individual autonomy but with **counter-succession**: no single bearer is sufficient, so resistance survives by being handed forward.

That makes the All Might/Midoriya scene especially poignant. The heroic inheritance is ethically healthier than AFO's possession system, but healthy inheritance still requires difficult truth-telling. All Might cannot yet fully perform that part.

The U.A. final exam brings the same question down to pedagogy. Institutions can learn from crisis instead of merely commemorating it. Pairing Midoriya and Bakugo is effectively an attempt to turn years of relational dysfunction into a competence problem that must be practiced rather than merely discussed.

Bakugo's Origin then supplies the final key. Both boys have inherited All Might without fully understanding him. Midoriya can mistake the ideal for something too sacred; Bakugo reduces the ideal toward victory. Volume 7 does not fuse those interpretations into one correct doctrine yet. It forces the boys to discover that neither can pass the immediate test while refusing the other.

The closing gauntlet image is therefore not just a clever teamwork beat. It condenses the volume's theory:

> **a future can be different when what one person carries becomes usable by another without erasing either person's authorship.**

That is a healthier micro-model of inheritance than possession, and a healthier micro-model of relationship than either worship or domination.

# 21. What this volume changes in the current MHA model

## 21.1 Aftermath becomes an analytical category in its own right

The meaning of a crisis is not exhausted by who won. V07 tracks what the event leaves behind in bodies, institutions, media, ideology, and relationships.

## 21.2 Iida's repair becomes substantive rather than assumed

He apologizes, keeps a bodily reminder, analytically distinguishes conviction from purification, and turns his own error into a prevention obligation. His recovery is now evidence-based, though not longitudinally complete.

## 21.3 Ordinary intervention is explicitly heroic doctrine

Tensei's lost-child example strongly strengthens the project thesis that the deepest form of social repair may be earlier, mundane human action rather than spectacular professional force.

## 21.4 Symbolic power is morally neutral in mechanism

Gran Torino's comparison between Stain's conviction and All Might's Symbol conviction establishes that charisma/symbolhood can stabilize society or mobilize destructive purification depending on the attached ethic.

## 21.5 Villain formation becomes infrastructural

Media and organizations matter. A person can be moved by a symbol, find language for grievance, and then enter a group capable of magnifying otherwise diffuse hostility.

## 21.6 Hero coercive legitimacy becomes historically grounded

The right to use Quirks publicly against people rests on inherited trust, restraint, supervision, and regulation. Strength alone creates no private right to punish.

## 21.7 Public hero narrative is not identical to truth

The Hosu cover-up proves that rankings/news/credit can reflect institutional necessities and legal boundaries rather than exact causal contribution.

## 21.8 OFA/AFO opposition becomes explicit at origin level

The series' central powers now encode rival relational models: centralized taking/assigning versus accumulated succession beyond the self.

## 21.9 Mentorship can fail through protection as well as neglect

All Might's inability to tell Midoriya about likely future absence demonstrates that love can reduce epistemic agency when it becomes withholding.

## 21.10 Institutions can learn

U.A.'s exam redesign is positive evidence that organizations in MHA are not merely static targets of critique. They can absorb threat information and alter practice.

## 21.11 Bakugo's heroism is grounded more deeply than aggression

His `Origin` proves that the victory obsession began as admiration for All Might's refusal to let crisis dictate the ending. The superiority complex is a deformation around a real heroic value, not the total origin of the value.

## 21.12 Relationship-specific dysfunction can outrank stable traits

Bakugo's willingness to prefer defeat over relying on Midoriya is the clearest early example. Character simulation must preserve relational conditions rather than average them into generic traits.

## 21.13 Midoriya increasingly rescues through recognition of another person's own values

Todoroki, Iida, and now Bakugo form an emerging pattern: Midoriya often helps by returning a person to a self-conception they still value rather than making them adopt his own personality.

## 21.14 The first Midoriya-Bakugo cooperation is a breach, not a reconciliation

The final gauntlet exchange is deliberately limited but real. Cooperation has become possible under extreme pressure; mutual trust, apology, and ordinary reciprocity remain prospective.

## 21.15 Volume 7's governing question for future readings

The series model should now carry forward:

> **When a crisis exposes the truth about a person, institution, or ideal, what structure is built from the residue — and who gets to decide what that residue means?**
