# 1. Governing transition: inheritance becomes jurisdiction

Volume 22 established **distributed competence / the rescuer as a legitimate rescue subject**. Its strongest people were not self-sufficient: good teams preserved one another's options; Uraraka and Shinso had to stop Midoriya; Iida and Todoroki inherited techniques without surrendering authorship; Bakugo's aggression became more effective when incorporated into reciprocal rescue.

Volume 23 takes that anti-solitary insight and asks a harder question:

> **If powers, techniques, institutions, ideologies, roles, obligations, and wounds arrive through other people, who has the right and capacity to decide what those inheritances become?**

The governing transition is therefore:

> **distributed competence / reciprocal rescue -> inheritance as jurisdiction: received power must be governed, earned, redirected, or resisted.**

Compact formulation:

> **Volume 23 repeatedly separates possession from authorship. Midoriya can inherit six additional Quirks without becoming a passive vessel; Shinso can earn entry into the Hero Course without pretending he already possesses its rescue reflexes; Bakugo and Todoroki can receive licenses only for those credentials to become immediate obligations; Re-Destro can inherit Destro's doctrine and convert it into corporate-political infrastructure; Shigaraki can inherit AFO's retainers and weapons only to discover that inheritance does not compel their allegiance. Succession matters only when the successor can govern what has been received.**

This gives the volume a much tighter internal unity than a simple “school arc ends / villain arc begins” division suggests.

The first half concerns inherited heroic power and institutional qualification. The second half concerns inherited villain power and ideological succession. Both halves refuse automatic legitimacy.

- One For All contains predecessor factors, but Banjo tells Midoriya to **control his heart** and **use/master us**.
- Shinso passes the transfer evaluation, but his own self-critique correctly distinguishes tactical competence from spontaneous rescue orientation.
- Bakugo and Todoroki receive provisional licenses and are almost immediately forced to convert certification into civilian protection.
- Re-Destro's political movement survives its founder by disappearing into normal companies, products, data systems, media, and more than one hundred thousand latent supporters.
- Gigantomachia was hidden for AFO's successor, yet he does not recognize Shigaraki merely because AFO named him heir.
- Ujiko has prepared an enormous technical inheritance for AFO's project, yet explicitly demands that Shigaraki show him something worthy of support.
- Shigaraki's own rage has been interpreted and cultivated by AFO, but V23 does not leave him as a passive product of that grooming: he articulates a goal in his own voice and defines a meaningful exception for his comrades' desires.

The volume is therefore about **jurisdiction over inheritance** rather than inheritance alone.

# 2. No.213 — `魂の所在`: One For All becomes plural without erasing Midoriya

## 2.1 The vestige is no longer merely dream-like residue

Volume 22 ended with incomplete contact. No.213 immediately sharpens the ontology.

The predecessor identifies the phenomenon as something more definite than a vague dream or emotional impression. At `V23:p008` he tells Midoriya:

> `おまえが今出した力は俺の“個性”さ`

The new tendril is therefore not an unexplained mutation of Midoriya's base strength. It is a predecessor Quirk.

At `V23:p009` the predecessor explains that the predecessors' factors were mixed into the core of One For All and remained there:

> `俺たちの因子は“力”の核に混ざって ワン・フォー・オールの中にずうっと在った`

The core that once seemed small has swelled through accumulated power and is now entering a period of `胎動`—a stirring or quickening.

This significantly strengthens the relational-inheritance model established in V04/V07/V21. OFA is not merely a quantity of force transferred across named owners. It contains durable predecessor traces capable of manifesting as power and communication.

But V23 does **not** authorize the stronger claim that Midoriya is simply becoming an aggregate consciousness. The predecessor's instructions move in the opposite direction.

## 2.2 Blackwhip is not “anger makes Midoriya lose control”

The first-pass formulation requires an important revision here.

The predecessor links Blackwhip's emergence to Midoriya's immediate intent: he wanted to capture/seize Monoma, and the Quirk suited that intention. At `V23:p010-012` the predecessor explains that Blackwhip has become dramatically stronger through OFA accumulation.

Then he says:

> `怒りのままに力を振るえば 力は応える`
>
> `肝心なのは心を制する事さ`
>
> `怒るのは良い 怒りは力の源さ`
>
> `なればこそ最も慎重にコントロールしてかねばならん`

The nuance matters.

The manga does **not** establish a simple equation:

`anger = corruption / dangerous power`.

Instead:

`emotion/intention = power input`;

`anger = legitimate source of force`;

`ethical/operational maturity = governing the heart strongly enough that force answers intention without overrunning judgment`.

This is a better fit for MHA's larger power philosophy. The series rarely asks heroic characters to become affectless. Iida's Hosu failure, Bakugo's anger, Todoroki's resentment, and Midoriya's rescue compulsion are not solved by deleting emotion. They are solved by changing what emotion is allowed to govern.

The V22 phrase `雑念マシマシ` therefore should not be generalized into “strong feeling is unsafe.” The V23 correction is **disciplined affect**.

## 2.3 Six additional Quirks: multiplicity becomes explicit

At `V23:p012`, the predecessor tells Midoriya:

> `おまえにはこれから6つの“個性”が発現するさ`

This is the first admissible V2 point for the six-additional-Quirk claim.

The narration should remain exact about what is and is not established:

**Established in V23:**
- predecessor Quirk factors persisted within OFA;
- Blackwhip is one such predecessor Quirk;
- OFA accumulation has amplified Blackwhip relative to the predecessor's own era;
- six additional Quirks are expected to manifest in Midoriya;
- psychological/intention control matters to safe use.

**Not yet established in V23:**
- the identities and complete mechanics of all six Quirks;
- their precise manifestation order;
- whether every vestige is equally communicative;
- later mastery techniques;
- later physiological/ontological implications not stated here.

## 2.4 `俺たちを使いこなせ`: plural inheritance serves successor authorship

The most important line for the volume's governing thesis arrives at `V23:p013`:

> `心を制して`
>
> `俺たちを使いこなせ`
>
> `俺たちがついてる`
>
> `ワン・フォー・オールを完遂させるのはおまえだ`

These sentences preserve two truths simultaneously.

Midoriya is **not alone**. The predecessors are present, their powers matter, and their support is real.

But Midoriya is also **not being dissolved into them**. The imperative is for *him* to master/use them. The task of completing OFA belongs to the present successor.

This sharpens a recurring MHA distinction:

> healthy inheritance enlarges agency; it does not replace the inheritor's judgment.

The contrast with AFO's later succession structure is deliberate enough to become one of the volume's central formal comparisons.

# 3. No.214–216 — Match 5: competence after destabilization

## 3.1 Midoriya's first restored attention is relational

When Midoriya returns to ordinary consciousness, one of his first observations is Uraraka's injury (`V23:p015`). He recognizes that she was hurt while stopping him and reacts with immediate guilt/concern.

This is useful character-model evidence because the scene occurs immediately after a frightening self-control failure. Midoriya does not remain absorbed in the metaphysical discovery. His attention reorients toward the person who incurred cost to stabilize him.

The pattern is consistent with his established rescue-first attention, but V23 adds a new role reversal: Midoriya is learning to perceive harm produced **by others rescuing him**.

That is the interpersonal consequence of V22's rescuer-as-rescue-subject thesis.

## 3.2 The match resumes before anyone receives emotional closure

Monoma attacks before the group can fully process the incident (`V23:p016-019`). This matters because the exercise tests whether participants can return to task under disturbed internal state rather than after a clean reset.

Midoriya must continue without using the new Quirk confidently. Uraraka and Shinso must shift from emergency stabilization back into competition. Monoma must exploit the temporary disorder rather than wait politely for a narrative explanation.

The resulting `乱戦` is valuable modeling evidence precisely because it is messy.

## 3.3 Shinso's development remains deliberately incomplete

Shinso's late-match behavior is competent and purposeful. He uses Persona Chords, capture cloth, positioning, and the habits developed under Aizawa's instruction. At `V23:p051`, Aizawa's `常に原点を心に!!` remains an active training anchor.

But the volume refuses a sentimental shortcut after the match.

When the exercise ends with Class A's victory (`V23:p057`), Shinso does not reinterpret his own emergency role as proof that he has already become a rescue-driven hero. At `V23:p059-063` he says, in substance:

- alone he could do very little;
- the exercise format and teammates gave him usable structure;
- during the Blackwhip emergency he moved because Uraraka told him what to do;
- he fought Midoriya because he wanted to win;
- while the Hero Course students showed rapid concern for others, his own mind was still largely occupied by himself.

That self-report should be preserved rather than corrected by analyst enthusiasm.

Shinso's strongest V23 evidence is not “he secretly had the same rescue instinct all along.” It is **accurate self-assessment without self-excusing**.

## 3.4 Aizawa distinguishes qualification from completion

Aizawa's response is equally important.

He does not tell Shinso that motive does not matter. Nor does he use Shinso's admitted self-focus to reject him.

Instead, at `V23:p064-066`, Aizawa explains that Hero Course students train continuously to develop the ability to save others; expecting Shinso to have already internalized every rescue reflex before entering the course would be absurd. His movement and decoy performance are above a passing level even though his second-match decision-making still included competitive tunnel vision.

Then the verdict is explicit:

> `心操は2年からヒーロー科に入ってくる`

Shinso will enter the Hero Course from the second year. Which class he will join remains undecided.

The institutional logic is strong:

> **admission recognizes demonstrated capacity and trainability; it does not certify moral completion.**

This directly opposes both deterministic stigma (“his Quirk looks villainous, therefore he cannot be a hero”) and sentimental exceptionalism (“his aspiration is sincere, therefore standards should disappear”).

## 3.5 Uraraka's rescue behavior becomes observable professional evidence

At `V23:p061-062`, Midoriya and the teachers explicitly recognize Uraraka's rapid intervention. She noticed the runaway state early, physically entered the hazard, stabilized Midoriya, and recruited Shinso's unique affordance.

Her own first response is not triumph. She worries that she might have done more, then permits herself relief that the intervention mattered.

This reinforces V22's upgrade to `strong` character-model readiness. The evidence now spans childhood motive, competitive agency, ordinary peer interaction, professional aspiration, acute rescue behavior, and reflection after action.

# 4. No.217 — OFA after the crisis: secrecy, comparison, and practical regulation

## 4.1 Bakugo becomes part of the OFA regulation environment

No.217 opens not with reverence but with Bakugo aggressively testing whether Blackwhip will reappear (`V23:p069-070`). His presentation remains abrasive, but the function is practical: he does not want Midoriya freezing or panicking merely because an unknown power might emerge.

This is a useful relationship-conditioned development.

Bakugo is not Midoriya's gentle reassurance partner. He contributes through:

- adversarial pressure;
- comparison;
- refusal to mythologize the new power;
- competitive insistence that Midoriya keep moving upward.

The OFA secret now has an unusual three-person working environment: All Might supplies historical interpretation, Midoriya supplies direct embodied observation, and Bakugo supplies skeptical/competitive stress-testing.

## 4.2 All Might is no longer a complete technical authority over the inheritance

All Might can explain his own use of OFA, but the multi-Quirk manifestation did not happen to him. V23 therefore changes the mentorship relation subtly.

The mentor remains indispensable, but his experience no longer maps one-to-one onto the successor's condition. Midoriya's inheritance has crossed into a state that even All Might must research.

At `V23:p073-077`, All Might resolves to investigate predecessor information. This is good epistemic discipline: authority responds to anomalous evidence by widening inquiry rather than pretending old expertise already contains the answer.

## 4.3 Midoriya's secrecy produces ordinary social friction

Todoroki notices the anomaly and asks Midoriya about apparently having another Quirk (`V23:p075`). Midoriya must answer inside OFA's secrecy constraints.

The scene is useful because it shows that secret inheritance has mundane relational cost. Midoriya can be socially sincere and still be unable to give a straightforward account to a peer who has accurately noticed something strange.

The knowledge-state problem is therefore not restricted to high politics. It enters friendships.

## 4.4 Monoma's Copy provides a stockpile control case

The post-match discussion of Eri and Monoma gives a useful mechanical distinction (`V23:p078-080`). Copy can reproduce a Quirk's form but cannot automatically reproduce accumulated stockpiles that the original user's power has built over time. Monoma describes such cases as effectively producing a `blank`.

This is relevant beyond mechanics because it reinforces MHA's resistance to treating Quirk labels as complete descriptions of capability. A power's usable state can depend on history, stored resources, training, body, and accumulated conditions.

## 4.5 Midoriya reframes Eri's self-blame

Eri worries about her power being troublesome or causing difficulty. Midoriya responds by reminding her that her power also saved/helped him (`V23:p080`).

This continues an important anti-essentialist thread:

> a dangerous affordance does not make its bearer dangerous in moral essence.

The line links Eri to Shinso and, now, to Midoriya himself. Brainwashing, Rewind, Blackwhip, Decay, and other dangerous Quirks cannot be morally classified from surface affordance alone. What matters is control, purpose, context, and social infrastructure.

# 5. No.218–219 — hero credentials become immediate public obligation

## 5.1 Bakugo and Todoroki complete the provisional-license process

By early December, Bakugo and Todoroki reach the final stage of the remedial course (`V23:p083-084`). If they pass, all of Class 1-A will hold provisional licenses.

The key analytical point is not the credential itself. The volume immediately refuses to let the license function as a ceremonial reward.

No.219 places them into an actual villain incident shortly after completion.

The structure is almost didactic:

`qualification -> authority -> responsibility`.

The institution grants limited coercive/rescue legitimacy, and the narrative immediately asks whether the students can operationalize it around civilians rather than simply enjoy status.

## 5.2 Bakugo's V22 rescue integration survives outside the training match

Bakugo and Todoroki respond with complementary abilities. The aftermath includes an understated but significant Bakugo detail at `V23:p107`: he returns a civilian's belongings in a condition designed not to ignite or be lost during the intervention, and the civilian thanks him.

This is not a personality replacement. Bakugo is still Bakugo. The behavioral development is procedural:

- detect civilian stakes;
- incorporate rescue/property protection into aggressive action;
- finish the threat quickly;
- handle aftermath without needing a soft public persona.

That matters more for reconstruction than simply calling him “nicer.”

At `V23:p108`, All Might praises both boys. Todoroki's understated `あっす` and Bakugo's rough `ンだよ` preserve distinct speech identities even under shared approval.

## 5.3 Institutional legitimacy is validated in use, not merely possession

This mini-incident parallels Shinso's transfer result.

A credential can open access to a role. It cannot substitute for behavior inside that role.

Shinso earns entry by demonstrating enough capacity to be trained further. Bakugo and Todoroki's licenses become meaningful because they can act under real public conditions.

Volume 23 thus repeatedly treats institutions as **provisional trust mechanisms** rather than moral absolution machines.

# 6. No.218–219 — the Meta Liberation Army: ideology hidden inside ordinary infrastructure

## 6.1 Detnerat collapses the boundary between consumer life and political preparation

The volume's villain turn begins through ordinary economic surfaces rather than a hideout.

Detnerat is introduced as a lifestyle-support manufacturer serving a society in which individualized bodies and Quirks create individualized product needs (`V23:p085-090`). The company looks like a plausible participant in normal hero society.

Its president, however, is Re-Destro.

This is one of the most important hero-society developments since the formal licensing material because it shows that political insurgency can inhabit respectable institutions rather than standing visibly outside them.

At `V23:p109`, distributed products are described as monitored, data-producing, and destructible in ways that can erase traces. Consumer technology, battle data, and liberation preparation are therefore integrated.

The Meta Liberation Army is not merely “a villain group with an ideology.” It is a **parallel social system**.

## 6.2 Re-Destro's corporate normalcy is not moderation

At `V23:p089-090`, an employee makes a slighting or insufficiently reverent remark about Destro. Re-Destro kills him while framing the conflict as failed mutual understanding:

> `君とは わかりあえなかった`

The scene is important because the violence is not presented through stereotypical villain theatricality. Re-Destro can occupy executive normalcy, speak in managerial/social language, and still enact ideological absolutism lethally.

This gives him an immediately useful behavioral contradiction:

- polished public/corporate register;
- intense doctrinal identification;
- low tolerance for perceived desecration of inherited ideology;
- capacity to redescribe elimination in the language of failed understanding.

## 6.3 Destro's doctrine is explicitly political

Historical exposition at `V23:p091-093` establishes the first version of the liberation doctrine admissible to V2.

Its key formulation is:

> `抑圧ではなく解放を。異能の自由行使は人間として当然の権利である`

Rather than suppression, liberation; free exercise of meta-abilities is framed as a natural human right.

The historical Meta Liberation Army fought the state, was defeated, and was dismantled. Destro died in prison. Yet the ideology survived.

The modern movement therefore constitutes **ideological succession**. Re-Destro is not simply copying an old criminal brand. He is converting inherited doctrine into a more deeply embedded form adapted to contemporary society.

## 6.4 The movement's scale changes the social model

By `V23:p171-175`, the movement's latent membership is described at a scale exceeding one hundred thousand supporters (`11万人余り`) distributed through ordinary society. Named leadership spans politics, media/communications, corporate support technology, and other respectable positions.

This matters for the V2 hero-society model:

> apparent social normalcy is not equivalent to ideological consensus.

The public hero system is therefore more politically plural and internally contested than earlier street-level villain encounters could show.

# 7. Giran — criminal professionalism as relational loyalty

Giran's capture gives the volume a small but unusually diagnostic character study (`V23:p110-111`).

The Meta Liberation Army has intelligence advantages and subjects him to severe torture. He nevertheless refuses to sell out clients.

His language is explicitly relational/professional rather than abstractly moral:

> `商売ってのよ 面見てやるもんだよ`
>
> `俺ぁ 他人が気に入った人間としか取引しねえ`
>
> `客売る売人がどこにいるってんだ`

Giran is still a broker in a criminal ecosystem. The scene should not be sentimentalized into conventional heroism.

But it provides strong evidence that villain/criminal relationships are not reducible to interchangeable self-interest. Giran has a professional identity, a selective-affiliation rule, and a loyalty norm capable of surviving torture.

This becomes important when the League later chooses to enter an enormous hostile city partly because Giran is there. Their relationships possess real binding force even in a group organized around destruction.

# 8. No.220 — `僕のヴィランアカデミア`: the League as a depleted social unit

## 8.1 The League is materially weak before it becomes politically large

No.220 deliberately begins from degradation rather than triumph.

The League attacks a Creature Rejection Clan group, a heteromorph-hating organization, and takes what resources it can (`V23:p115-120`). Equipment is damaged. Money is scarce. Food and logistics matter. Kurogiri has been captured. AFO is imprisoned. Dabi complains about recruitment quality. Toga looks for usable or appealing objects among wreckage. Compress complains about the group's circumstances.

The League at this point is not yet an all-powerful revolutionary army. It is a small, exhausted, under-resourced network.

That material condition matters because it prevents later growth from being read as inevitable.

## 8.2 Spinner's heteromorph background materially advances the villain-formation model

Spinner becomes the chapter's central interpretive consciousness.

At `V23:p117`, he is sensitive to the language around heteromorphic appearance and notes the social charge around `異形`. At `V23:p124-125`, he gives the strongest direct account of his pre-League formation yet.

He grew up in a backward/rural environment where he was mocked as a `トカゲやろう`. Crucially, he says he had treated this social condition as normal. His description is not simply “discrimination made me evil.” It is closer to habituated emptiness:

> `俺の心にはずっと何も無かった`

Stain's public ideological action produced a rupture. Spinner says he was inspired by Stain and, through seeing one person try to change the world, recognized the suffocating quality of the world around him and could no longer remain still.

This strengthens a plural villain-formation model:

`social marginalization / normalized humiliation` + `lack of self-directed purpose` + `ideological encounter` -> `mobilization`.

It does **not** establish that every heteromorph subjected to discrimination follows the same path, nor that social exclusion removes Spinner's agency.

## 8.3 Spinner becomes an internal accountability pressure on Shigaraki

Spinner physically confronts Shigaraki and asks where the League is going (`V23:p122-125`).

This is important relationship evidence. Membership is not blind obedience. Spinner joined through an ideological route connected to Stain; he therefore expects direction with meaning.

His challenge forces Shigaraki to articulate a project rather than relying indefinitely on AFO's inherited authority.

The League can contain loyalty and dissent simultaneously.

# 9. No.221 — AFO's bequest: succession does not compel allegiance

## 9.1 Gigantomachia is an inheritance prepared in advance

Gigantomachia is identified as an extraordinarily durable AFO loyalist/bodyguard (`V23:p131-136`). The narration explains that AFO anticipated the possibility of defeat by All Might and concealed resources for a successor years in advance.

This makes AFO's villainy institutional rather than merely personal.

He does not plan only to survive encounters. He builds succession infrastructure:

- retainers;
- hidden assets;
- medical/technical support;
- Nomu research;
- communication/warp access;
- a successor project.

But the inherited infrastructure is not self-executing.

## 9.2 Gigantomachia recognizes AFO, not the title “successor”

Machia responds powerfully to AFO's recorded voice, yet he does not automatically submit to Shigaraki (`V23:p136-161`). His standard is strength/worthiness.

This is a critical difference from a simplistic inheritance model:

> AFO can nominate an heir; he cannot simply transfer another person's recognition by fiat.

Shigaraki must become someone Machia can acknowledge.

## 9.3 Ujiko is a gatekeeper to inherited technical power

The Doctor/Ujiko encounter intensifies the same problem.

The laboratory at `V23:p140-145` visually reveals that AFO's power system includes industrialized biological infrastructure: multiple Nomu bodies, higher-grade experiments, Quirk research, and controlled access to capabilities the League itself does not command.

Ujiko's provisional alias `氏子達磨` is explicitly improvised and should not be treated as a stable true identity at this boundary.

More important is his judgment of Shigaraki. Ujiko says, in effect, that he devoted his life and technology to AFO while Shigaraki has so far merely been the beneficiary of AFO's favor. He asks what a roughly twenty-year-old social castoff who has accomplished little can show him (`V23:p144-145`).

The scene creates a villain-side analogue to institutional qualification:

- Shinso is not admitted because he wants the Hero Course; he demonstrates enough ability to earn entry.
- Shigaraki is not granted AFO's entire infrastructure because AFO liked him; he must demonstrate enough capacity to win over AFO's retained specialists.

The moral content differs radically. The structural problem of **succession legitimacy** is shared.

# 10. No.222 — Shigaraki `Distortion`: inherited interpretation and self-authored negation

## 10.1 The family hands function as an anger-induction ritual

At `V23:p148`, Shigaraki says that his memories from before meeting AFO remain fragmentary, but attaching the family hands makes anger rise:

> `皆を身につけると怒りが沸々湧いてくる`

The hands are therefore not neutral memorials. They are an embodied regulation technology oriented toward preserving a painful affective state.

This makes AFO's mentorship look even less like simple rescue and more like affective grooming.

## 10.2 AFO's account must remain attributed to AFO

The flashback at `V23:p149-152` is analytically dangerous because AFO supplies a powerful causal narrative:

- no one helped Tenko;
- everyone assumed some hero would eventually intervene;
- society looked away;
- Tenko accidentally killed his family when an unknown Quirk manifested;
- the severed hands are family remains;
- the emotion encoded in the event should be preserved and directed.

Some of these claims may later be corroborated, revised, or contextualized. At the V23 boundary they must remain properly attributed.

AFO's words are **evidence that AFO taught Tenko a particular interpretation of his suffering**. They are not automatically omniscient narration.

The key grooming sequence is rhetorically elegant:

> `もう大丈夫 僕がいる`

AFO occupies the rescue position after explaining the surrounding world as a universal failure to rescue. He becomes both interpreter and substitute attachment.

This is highly relevant to the recognition/failed-rescue ledger, but it must be preserved as an intervention into Tenko's meaning-making rather than treated as a neutral diagnosis.

## 10.3 Shigaraki still develops agency inside the grooming structure

The volume does not stop at “AFO made him this way.”

Shigaraki explicitly identifies the question as where to direct the accumulated emotion (`V23:p152-154`). He says the family hands produce anger without relief; the leaden mass in him persists.

Then he articulates a goal broader than tactical anti-hero hostility:

> `俺はきっと“全部”嫌いなんだ`
>
> `息づく全てが俺を苛つかせるんだ`
>
> `じゃあもう壊そう`

He reasons that even if hero society collapsed or he ruled from the shadows, the internal burden would remain. Destruction becomes less a route to conventional political replacement than an attempt to answer a self that experiences the living world as intolerable.

This is not yet a complete philosophy. It is a self-authored negative horizon.

## 10.4 The comrades are an explicit exception to indiscriminate abstraction

At `V23:p156`, Toga asks whether Shigaraki would destroy even what she likes. Shigaraki answers:

> `仲間の望みは別腹さ`
>
> `好きに生きてろ`

The colloquial `別腹` is revealing. His universal negation has a social exception carved out by comradeship. The League members' desires are allowed independent space.

This prevents an overly tidy reading of Shigaraki as pure indiscriminate nihilism.

His current structure is contradictory but intelligible:

- generalized hatred/destructive horizon toward the wider world;
- strong tolerance for comrades' divergent wants;
- willingness to preserve their freedom of desire;
- personal need to remove the “lead” in himself;
- continuing dependence on inherited AFO infrastructure that he does not yet control.

That contradiction is central character-model evidence.

## 10.5 Ujiko responds to demonstrated orientation, not lineage alone

Ujiko sees enough in Shigaraki's declaration to continue the test and prepare support (`V23:p157-161`). But Machia remains the gate.

The succession problem therefore becomes behavioral: Shigaraki must survive and alter Machia's judgment.

# 11. No.223 — prolonged Machia combat: leadership under depletion

## 11.1 The test is endurance, not a single boss fight

By mid-December (`V23:p163`), the League has been fighting Gigantomachia in repeated cycles.

The narration at `V23:p166` gives the scale: Machia can continue attacking for roughly **48 hours and 44 minutes** without rest, then sleeps for only a few hours before resuming. The League uses those sleep windows for survival and recovery.

This is not simply power-scaling spectacle. It creates an extended deprivation environment in which personality and group cohesion become observable.

Shigaraki is physically exhausted and increasingly adapted to the rhythm. Toga, Twice, Spinner, Compress and others remain present. Dabi's participation is more selective. The group functions as a social unit under extreme attrition rather than a set of isolated villain monologues.

## 11.2 Shigaraki's leadership remains permissive rather than command-heavy

The V23 League does not resemble a military bureaucracy. Shigaraki's earlier `好きに生きてろ` matches the group's actual organization: members retain distinctive motives, interpersonal bonds, complaints, and tactical autonomy.

His leadership claim is therefore not “I centrally control all of you.” It is closer to:

> I can provide a destructive direction broad enough that your separate wants can continue inside it.

That makes Spinner's demand for purpose especially consequential. Shigaraki must provide enough direction to hold together people whose motives are not identical.

# 12. No.223–224 — the Meta Liberation Army invitation: distributed ideology becomes a city

## 12.1 The MLA weaponizes information and hostages rather than simply challenging strength

The Meta Liberation Army contacts the League through Giran's captured network (`V23:p169-177`). The call demonstrates extensive surveillance and coordination capacity. Re-Destro's side can identify League movements, present Giran as leverage, and choose the terrain of confrontation.

By `V23:p174`, the movement claims more than **110,000 (`11万人余り`) latent liberation soldiers**.

This number should not be treated as independently audited census data; it is the movement's represented organizational strength. But even as claimed strength, it changes the scale of the conflict from gang warfare to political mobilization.

## 12.2 MLA leadership is embedded in normal institutions

The named leadership reveal spans respectable social positions rather than costumed criminal archetypes. Corporate executives, political/media figures, and technology actors form the movement's visible top layer (`V23:p174-176`).

The visual grammar matters: ordinary offices, suits, products, communication systems, and urban space become villain/insurgent infrastructure.

This extends the volume's core theme of inheritance into institutional form. Destro's defeated movement survives because successors have translated doctrine into the ordinary operating systems of contemporary life.

## 12.3 Shigaraki's plan is opportunistic but relationally motivated

The League is exhausted and radically outnumbered. It does not enter Deika because the situation is tactically fair.

Giran's captivity matters. So does Shigaraki's attempt to use one hostile force against another: the Meta Liberation Army's massed combatants can potentially become the pressure source that draws or exhausts Gigantomachia.

This is characteristic Shigaraki adaptation at this stage: destructive rather than institution-building, but increasingly capable of using system collisions instead of attacking one obstacle at a time.

# 13. No.224 — Deika City: ideology as civilian mass action

## 13.1 The city defeats the civilian/combatant expectation

When the League enters Deika, the apparent absence/normalcy of ordinary civilians proves deceptive (`V23:p184-190`). Residents themselves become the attack surface.

The Meta Liberation Army is therefore not merely hiding soldiers among civilians. Its politics have produced a population in which ordinary social actors can become organized combatants.

This is one of the volume's strongest visual/social-system images:

> **a whole city can look like ordinary civil society until ideology activates its latent coordination.**

The hero-villain binary is now inadequate to describe every political actor in the setting.

## 13.2 `再臨祭` frames violence as ideological ritual

No.224's title, `再臨祭`, casts the confrontation as a festival of return/reappearance. The MLA is not merely attempting to kill a rival villain group. It is staging the re-emergence of Destro's political project.

The battle therefore functions as propaganda, internal movement proof, and ideological theater.

## 13.3 Curious treats Toga as a story before treating her as a person

The volume ends with Curious isolating Toga (`V23:p191-193`). Her interest is explicitly media-narrative. She connects Toga to disappearance/death cases and asks why a school-age girl became “mad,” seeking a shocking interview/story before death.

The exact causal history is not yet supplied. The important V23 evidence is the **frame** Curious brings:

- Toga is content;
- biography is political material;
- personal abnormality can be converted into ideological explanation;
- the journalist claims interpretive jurisdiction over how the subject's life will mean.

This creates a sharp parallel to AFO's treatment of Tenko. In both cases another actor offers to turn a wounded/aberrant life into a causal narrative useful to a larger project.

The difference is that Toga's response and the ultimate truth of her history remain outside the V23 boundary.

# 14. Hero and villain succession as parallel structures

Volume 23's strongest full-volume construction is the parallel between OFA succession and AFO/MLA succession.

## 14.1 OFA: inheritance that explicitly returns agency to the successor

Banjo says:

- the predecessors remain present;
- their Quirks can emerge;
- their accumulated power exceeds what they individually held;
- Midoriya must control his heart;
- Midoriya must `俺たちを使いこなせ`;
- they are with him;
- he is the one who must complete OFA.

This is relational inheritance without passive possession.

## 14.2 AFO: inheritance that includes retainers, tools, and imposed interpretation

AFO leaves Shigaraki:

- Gigantomachia;
- Ujiko and his research infrastructure;
- Nomu production;
- communication/warp access;
- a narrative about Tenko's past;
- a successor identity.

Yet the retainers do not automatically obey. Shigaraki has to prove himself.

This makes AFO's succession paradoxical. AFO is intensely controlling, but the scale of his project requires other agents whose allegiance cannot be fully transferred as property.

## 14.3 MLA: ideological inheritance adapted through institutions

Re-Destro inherits not a Quirk succession but a doctrine/name/project. His achievement is adaptation:

- corporate embedding;
- political contacts;
- consumer products;
- data collection;
- media influence;
- mass latent membership.

The movement shows how ideology can survive defeat by changing container.

## 14.4 Shinso/Bakugo/Todoroki: institutional succession is conditional trust

Hero society offers a fourth succession type: professional role transmission.

- Shinso gains access to the Hero Course because current evidence justifies further trust.
- Bakugo and Todoroki gain provisional licenses and immediately incur duties.

Professional inheritance is therefore neither bloodline nor mystical transfer. It is institutional authorization tied to continuing performance.
