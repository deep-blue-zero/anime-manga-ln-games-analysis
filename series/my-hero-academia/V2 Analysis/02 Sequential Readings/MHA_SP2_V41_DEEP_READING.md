### Entering state from Volume 40

Volume 40 established **inheritance without possession**. AFO's external Rewind body vanished before reaching Shigaraki. Bakugo survived and defeated/interrupted the external AFO trajectory while explicitly accepting plural victory. All Might survived the predicted death point with longer medical status unresolved. Shigaraki rejected AFO's summons and continued a destruction program he called his own. Midoriya and Shigaraki remained engaged, and the final explicit uncertainty was `危機感知` / Danger Sense.

The critical V41 questions are therefore not merely “who wins?” They are:

- What happens when a power inheritance can be deliberately relinquished rather than merely received?
- Can Midoriya recognize Shigaraki's personhood without annexing Shigaraki's life into a preferred rescue narrative?
- How much of Tenko/Shigaraki was engineered by AFO, and what does that do — or not do — to agency and accountability?
- Can a person altered by imposed identity retain continuity without being reducible either to the imposed self or to a supposedly recoverable pure original?
- What replaces the singular Symbol-of-Peace model at the level of actual action, not only slogan?

# 1. Governing transition: inheritance without possession becomes agency without self-sufficiency

Volume 40's central distinction was between **transmission** and **ownership**. Power, survival, technique, ideals and roles can pass between people without making the recipient property of the giver. Volume 41 applies that distinction to the final identity struggle and then radicalizes it.

Midoriya is asked to give away One For All — the thing he calls a `宝物`, the treasure given by the hero he admired. Shigaraki is shown to have been manipulated more deeply than the prospective reread had previously authorized: AFO shaped the Shimura family environment, removed an inherited Quirk factor before manifestation, supplied an engineered destructive factor, and cultivated hatred as a tool for breaking OFA's will. Kurogiri's present identity is described by Aizawa not as pure Shirakumo restored, but as something mixed and altered. Eri voluntarily contributes a severed horn while explicitly retaining a future ordinary desire: she wants to sing. Midoriya reaches the final attack after losing OFA's transferable factors, losing both arms temporarily, being rescued by classmates, receiving Eri's bounded help, and accepting a battlefield architecture in which his success depends on everyone else's.

The volume therefore cannot be summarized as “Midoriya becomes strong enough to win.” Its final formal proposition is almost the opposite. AFO identifies something All Might did not have in the same form: Midoriya's **weakness**, the fact that he is visibly incomplete and in need of others, repeatedly causes people to stand back up and enter the causal chain.

The governing transition is:

**`inheritance without possession -> agency without self-sufficiency`**

Compact formulation:

> **Volume 41 repeatedly strips ownership claims from power, identity, rescue and history. Midoriya can relinquish OFA without losing the hero-self it formed; Shigaraki can be deeply engineered without becoming merely AFO's authored object; Kurogiri can retain a Shirakumo-origin without reverting to a pure prior self; Eri can contribute without surrendering her ordinary future; and the final heroic form is not an invulnerable singular symbol but a network whose members can need and enable one another.**

Two subordinate propositions organize the volume.

### 1.1 Rescue without annexation

Midoriya's rescue ethic is morally serious only if “saving Shigaraki” does not mean replacing Shigaraki's own account of his life with a cleaner story in which the adult villain is discarded and only the crying child counts. Chapter 418 is explicit counterevidence to that simplification. Shigaraki insists that he chose to join hands, that the hands he carries mean something, and that he must be the `ヒーロー` of the villains/outcasts whose lives attached to his.

A rescuer may judge Shigaraki's destruction wrong and still have to recognize that his adult attachments are real.

### 1.2 Designed conditions are not total authorship

Chapter 419 greatly expands AFO's causal responsibility. It would be a methodological failure to protect an older “Shigaraki was independent” reading by minimizing the new evidence. But the opposite failure is equally serious: accepting AFO's `全部だ!!!` as neutral omniscient narration.

AFO's concrete claims establish extensive **engineering of conditions**. His total claim — that tragedy, trial, negation, affirmation and choice were all his — is also the culminating expression of the ownership heuristic V40 had already established. The volume itself supplies counterevidence: League relationships AFO did not simply script into existence, Shigaraki's repeated rejection of AFO, Spinner's recognition of Shigaraki as distinct, and adult choices that remain intelligible as Shigaraki's even after the origin manipulation is disclosed.

Causal control is not identical to total authorship.

# 2. No.411 — Danger Sense theft resolves the V40 cliffhanger and immediately returns to personhood

## 2.1 `危機感知` is actually stolen

The first substantive V41 resolution is mechanical and clean. Shigaraki has taken the Fourth holder's Danger Sense. The stolen factor becomes operationally available to him, and the fourth vestige separates from Midoriya's interior cohort (`V41:p005-010`).

This matters beyond battle tactics. One For All's inherited interior community is no longer only an accumulating resource. A hostile recipient can selectively tear pieces away. “Inheritance” becomes physically vulnerable to involuntary redistribution.

The correct V40 cliffhanger resolution is therefore:

`Danger Sense consequence OPEN -> Danger Sense stolen by Shigaraki; Fourth separates; Shigaraki can use it.`

Do not generalize this into the final OFA outcome yet.

## 2.2 Shigaraki challenges the rescue narrative before Midoriya can stabilize it

Shigaraki immediately pushes on the interpretive weak point in Midoriya's mission. The crying child Midoriya perceived is, in Shigaraki's telling, not the current person standing in front of him. He continues to articulate destruction rather than asking for rescue.

Midoriya's answer is `人だよ` (`V41:p019`).

That line should be preserved as a **moral classification**, not transformed into narrator-certified proof that Shigaraki is secretly redeemable. Midoriya refuses the category move that would make catastrophic danger equivalent to non-personhood. It is continuous with earlier MHA distinctions:

- dangerous Quirk is not moral essence;
- villain-coded Quirk is not villain identity;
- prisoner is not non-human;
- perpetrator status does not erase the fact that a person has history, relationships and reasons.

The line does not answer what justice requires. It determines what kind of being justice is being done *to*.

## 2.3 Tactical asymmetry grows while Midoriya's ethical objective becomes harder

Losing Danger Sense means Midoriya is materially worse positioned against a hyper-mobile destructive opponent. V41 therefore does not create a convenient path where choosing rescue unlocks superior tactical advantage. The ethical choice increases difficulty.

That is important because the series repeatedly distinguishes moral commitment from evidence that the world will reward it. Midoriya's rescue project is about to require the loss of the very inheritance that made him capable of reaching Shigaraki at all.

# 3. No.412 — rescue becomes an explicit wager rather than a certainty

## 3.1 Blackwhip becomes bodily scaffolding

With Gearshift blowback and accumulated damage constraining movement, Blackwhip is used to reinforce the body rather than simply bind an external target (`V41:p021-027`). The Quirk has become infrastructure: an inherited tool incorporated into Midoriya's own improvised embodiment.

This extends the long Full Cowling trajectory. Midoriya's mature relation to OFA is not “fire the strongest inherited power.” It is “reconfigure inherited capacities around the actual body and problem.”

## 3.2 Shigaraki identifies the cost of having things to protect

Shigaraki's `守るもんが多くて大変だなァ 緑谷` is not only taunting (`V41:p028`). It names a real asymmetry. Midoriya is fighting while preserving people, terrain, future possibility and the possible rescue subject. Destruction has fewer constraints.

The hero-villain difference is therefore not simply one of output. Protection creates a burden of **multiple simultaneous goods**.

Midoriya answers that this does not permit him to give up on the crying boy he perceived. The key wording remains a statement of commitment, not certainty. He does not say he now knows how to save Shigaraki or that Shigaraki will accept rescue.

## 3.3 Midoriya's anthropology is a value commitment

The chapter connects Midoriya's position to a broad belief that beneath difference there is equally a human heart (`V41:p033`). Because the line is connected to his own Quirkless formation, it has autobiographical force: he learned early what it means for social capability classification to become a judgment about human possibility.

Methodological caution: this is **Midoriya's moral anthropology**. The source does not require us to treat it as a measurable empirical law that every person contains an accessible benign core. Muscular remains a control case; AFO will remain a control case. Midoriya's claim is that personhood should not be forfeited merely because moral rescue is uncertain.

## 3.4 Kudo turns rescue into a gamble

Kudo's decisive shift is not “the vestiges reveal the correct answer.” He says he is willing to bet on Midoriya and instructs him to let go of OFA (`V41:p035-036`). His earlier dislike of pretty ideals does not disappear. He accepts a risk because Midoriya has generated enough evidence to justify wagering on a route Kudo himself would not naturally privilege.

That preserves epistemic humility inside the heroic side. The plan is dangerous, technically speculative and morally uncertain.

# 4. No.413 — relinquishing OFA is meaningful precisely because it is treasured

## 4.1 Star-and-Stripe's encounter becomes evidence of an interior wound

Kudo interprets the earlier Star-and-Stripe confrontation as evidence that Shigaraki's interior has a damaged seam or wound that can be attacked from within (`V41:p037-043`). The exact metaphysics remain story-specific, but the analytical point is clear: a person who appears physically overwhelming can contain an internally damaged structure produced by prior relation/conflict.

This is not a generic “trauma makes him weak” message. The volume will later explicitly reject tragedy as a necessary strength source. Here, injury creates an exploitable condition. It does not become morally ennobling.

## 4.2 The OFA-transfer plan reverses ordinary succession

The plan is to force the inherited factors into Shigaraki in a way that breaks/shatters them against the interior wound (`V41:p041-048`). OFA, historically protected through voluntary succession, is converted into a deliberately expended intervention.

This creates an ethical inversion without erasing the original inheritance grammar:

- Midoriya received OFA through recognition and consent;
- he now chooses to relinquish it;
- the vestiges themselves participate in the plan;
- the destination is hostile and resists.

The process is therefore neither simple gift nor simple theft. It is chosen self-dispossession used as a combat/rescue tactic.

## 4.3 `宝物` blocks the “power is disposable” misreading

Midoriya calls OFA the `宝物` given to him by the hero he admired and then agrees to the plan (`V41:p049-051`).

The order matters. Sacrifice has moral/psychological weight because the object is valued. A reading that says “Midoriya never cared about power, so giving it away costs nothing” would flatten the longitudinal evidence. He has spent the entire series turning received power into an embodied, relationally inhabited part of his life.

The stronger claim is:

> Midoriya's ownership of OFA becomes mature enough that he can choose not to preserve possession when preservation conflicts with the rescue objective for which the inheritance mattered.

That is self-authorship, not detachment.

## 4.4 Blackwhip becomes a literal life line

As the other factors are prepared for expenditure, Blackwhip is treated as the support that will keep Midoriya operational (`V41:p049-052`). The inheritance is being disassembled by function. This blocks a romantic “give everything away at once” reading. Midoriya and the vestiges are managing a resource transition under combat constraints.

# 5. No.414-No.415 — overlay, deception, transfer, and the ethical friction of refusal

## 5.1 `オーバーレイ` makes a heroic body look monstrous without making it morally monstrous

Midoriya wraps and reinforces himself through Blackwhip, creating a visually distorted/monstrous silhouette (`V41:p053-058`). This is a useful control on the manga's grotesque body imagery. Visual monstrosity is not a reliable moral classifier.

Across the series, bodies have been:

- admired because spectacular;
- stigmatized because heteromorphic;
- engineered into Nomu instruments;
- weaponized through family projects;
- altered by trauma/Quirk overuse;
- possessed or overwritten.

V41 adds a self-authored grotesque form used to preserve agency and pursue rescue.

## 5.2 Midoriya's old Nagant decoy returns as anti-omniscience evidence

Midoriya reuses the decoy logic from the Lady Nagant fight; Shigaraki recognizes the precedent (`V41:p059-064`). Search/Danger Sense/accumulated combat information create enormous perception, but perception remains model-based and spoofable.

This matters to the recognition framework. More information does not automatically create perfect knowledge. AFO's later claim to have designed everything should be read under the same epistemic discipline: extensive causal access is not omniscience.

## 5.3 The transfer is opposed by `拒絶`

Chapter 415 names the reaction directly: rejection/refusal (`V41:p069 onward`). The vestige-transfer tactic encounters active resistance.

This is ethically important. MHA has long treated `余計なお世話` — unwanted intervention — as part of heroism. V41 does not remove the friction. Midoriya's intervention may still be justified by imminent catastrophic harm, but Shigaraki is not converted into a passive rescue object whose resistance is narratively irrelevant.

A careful rescue model must therefore preserve three propositions simultaneously:

1. Shigaraki is committing mass lethal harm and can legitimately be forcibly stopped.
2. Midoriya has reason to attempt rescue/understanding beyond simple killing.
3. Shigaraki's refusal is still evidence of agency and must not be redescribed as consent merely because the rescuer's motive is benevolent.

This is **rescue without annexation** in its hardest form.

## 5.4 Memory crossover is evidence, not total interior possession

As the factors break through, memories cross between Midoriya and Shigaraki (`V41:p073-080`). The source is showing unprecedented experiential access, but it still does not license “Midoriya now knows all of Shigaraki.” What becomes available is selective, trauma-salient and Quirk-mediated.

The later AFO reveal is likewise a memory/causal disclosure, not permission to erase every previously observed autonomous behavior.

# 6. No.416 — the social answer begins before the battlefield crowd arrives

## 6.1 Eri cannot simply solve the battle

The chapter checks Eri's Rewind capacity and finds it insufficient for the obvious fantasy of immediate full restoration (`V41:p081-084`). This preserves the long-standing rule that Eri is not a convenient reset device.

Her later contribution will be meaningful precisely because it is bounded.

## 6.2 Kota converts received rescue into a desire to participate

Kota openly calls himself a crybaby and explains that watching the older person he admires fight makes him think he has to do something (`V41:p085-087`).

This is a powerful longitudinal return to V08-V09. Kota once inherited hero sacrifice primarily as abandonment and resentment after his parents died. Midoriya's rescue gave him an experiential counterexample. V41 shows that the result is not blind hero worship. The boy knows his own weakness and still develops a participation impulse.

The causal chain is:

`received protection -> recognition of another's struggle -> desire to contribute within one's capacity`.

That is the network logic V41 will scale up.

## 6.3 “Everyone thinking a little about everyone” is not occupational flattening

The chapter proposes that if everyone could think just a little about everyone else, something changes (`V41:p090`).

The phrase should **not** be turned into “everyone literally becomes a professional hero.” The volume is careful about differentiated roles. Evacuation defenders remain at their posts. Some people fight. Some transport. Some give equipment. Some guide. Some cheer. Eri can contribute only within a small temporal window. Aizawa explicitly asks for currently usable positions rather than demanding identical sacrifice from all.

The stronger institutional reading is:

> Hero society becomes less fragile when concern and responsibility are distributed more broadly than the licensed profession while specialist roles and limits remain real.

## 6.4 `勧善懲悪` is explicitly insufficient for Midoriya's chosen route

An observer identifies Midoriya's refusal to perform a clean good-punishes-evil script as a thorny road (`V41:p090-093`). The source does not say simple moral opposition is always wrong. It says Midoriya's particular evidence — the crying child perception, AFO/Tenko entanglement and Shigaraki's history — has made a purely classificatory ending insufficient for him.

That distinction matters. Muscular did not require the same route; stopping him was enough. Rescue ethics remains case-sensitive.

## 6.5 The Shimura house becomes the actual interior threshold

Midoriya arrives at the remembered family house by `V41:p095`. The physical battle has turned into a threshold problem: will he enter another person's formative memory and what will he do there?

The volume's answer begins with a small social form in the next chapter: he enters with the grammar of visiting another person's home rather than conquering territory.

# 7. No.417 — the Shimura family wound is admitted without becoming a single-cause explanation

## 7.1 Nana's exclusion is relationally specific

Nana is uniquely unable to pass normally into the deepest family memory (`V41:p097-101`). This is not simply power mechanics. The separation dramatizes her special causal/relational position in the wound.

Nana calls the decision her weakness (`これは 私の弱さだ`). That is an accountability statement. She does not deny the reasons she had for leaving her child in order to fight AFO. She recognizes that a choice made under threat still wounded a family line.

The series is again distinguishing **explanation** from **exemption**.

## 7.2 `おじゃまします` is a formal recognition of entering someone else's interior space

Midoriya's approach to the house carries the etiquette of entering another person's home (`V41:p101-103`). The scene is not evidence that mental invasion has become consensual. It is a formal motif: Midoriya conceptualizes what he is doing as entering a space that is not his.

This small phrase supports the rescue-without-annexation reading. He is not acting as though another person's interior is automatically his property because he wants to help.

## 7.3 Midoriya protects Tenko before exploiting the memory

At the core abuse scene, Midoriya blocks Kotaro's strike (`V41:p104-108`). He had entered with a strategy designed to break through Shigaraki's interior. When confronted with the child being hit, his immediate rescue reflex overrides purely instrumental use of the memory.

This is a held-out validation of the V01 behavioral rule:

`visible person in danger -> rescue action can precede complete strategic calculation`.

The context has changed from a sludge villain to the interior of the series' central antagonist. The action policy remains recognizable.

## 7.4 Kotaro's grievance is inherited and partially true without legitimizing abuse

The family wound contains a harsh proposition: heroes hurt their families in order to save strangers. Nana's history gives the statement real causal content. Kotaro's pain is not fabricated.

But the source does not make truth in grievance equivalent to legitimate conduct. Kotaro's abuse of Tenko remains abuse. The broader pattern mirrors Stain and other villains: accurate diagnosis of a social wound does not grant moral jurisdiction to harm.

## 7.5 Tenko/Shigaraki has “never been able to throw it away”

The memory establishes persistence of the family wound rather than a clean separation between child Tenko and adult Shigaraki. Nana recognizes that the wound she created/left spread into Shigaraki and apologizes for not returning to take her child home (`V41:p109-112`).

This materially strengthens failed-recognition/failed-rescue analysis. Yet the next chapter immediately prevents the wound from swallowing the adult identity.

# 8. No.418 — Shigaraki rejects the pure-victim interpretation and names a chosen relational role

## 8.1 Midoriya stops trying to solve Shigaraki only through childhood

Midoriya's decisive move is not a speech saying “you were innocent, therefore the villain is fake.” He says he will collide with / bring himself against Shigaraki because Shigaraki is crying (`V41:p113-117`). The intervention becomes self-disclosure/action rather than diagnostic superiority.

That is a better rescue posture than simply telling another person what they “really are.”

## 8.2 `僕が選んだ` is the chapter's agency claim

Shigaraki answers the victim-only reading explicitly:

- `僕は…僕が 選んだんだ`
- `僕の意志で この手を繋いだんだ`

The exact phrases matter because Chapter 419 will attack them directly. At this point, the adult Shigaraki is asserting that his League bonds and villain path are not merely things done to him.

This does not make the claim metaphysically complete. V41 is about to reveal that AFO manipulated foundational conditions. But it does establish the object of the later dispute: whether manipulation of conditions nullifies all later authorship.

## 8.3 The hands are not only trauma props; they have become chosen relationship symbols

Shigaraki asks, in effect, if his choices do not matter, then what are these hands (`V41:p118-121`). Earlier, the hands began as grotesque imposed family remains and control objects. Across the series, the League creates additional relational meaning around the identity built from that history.

The adult cannot be reduced to the child without losing what happened afterward.

## 8.4 “Villains' hero” is morally compromised but relationally real

Shigaraki says he is a villain who destroys with malice and that even if hatred is broken and he becomes empty, he has to be the `ヒーロー` of the villains/outcasts who attached themselves to him (`V41:p121-123`).

This is one of the most important character-model lines in the final arc.

It establishes:

- destruction remains chosen/claimed;
- League/outcast attachment is not erased by origin rescue;
- hero vocabulary has been reappropriated inside an anti-social coalition as responsibility to one's people;
- Shigaraki's prosocial capacity is **selective**, not generalized moral rehabilitation.

Calling him a “hero to villains” is not the same as calling him morally heroic overall. It is evidence that the capacity to recognize and carry others can exist inside a destructive ethical boundary.

## 8.5 The unknown memory opens the AFO causation problem

Midoriya sees a memory of a seemingly ordinary man around the Shimura family (`V41:p124-125`). The page is prospective evidence that the causal history contains an external actor not previously admitted at this depth. It should not be pre-solved until AFO identifies his role.

At `V41:p127`, AFO reappears and says Tenko has never chosen anything.

The volume has deliberately placed two claims adjacent:

`Shigaraki: I chose.`

`AFO: You chose nothing.`

The responsible reading must adjudicate evidence rather than automatically privileging the more powerful narrator.

# 9. No.419 — `DESIGN`: extensive engineering does not make AFO the sole author of a human life

Chapter 419 is the most important causal-revision chapter in the V2 reread since Shigaraki's original origin material. It requires two corrections at once.

Any earlier model that treated Shigaraki's formative path as largely autonomous after generic social/family failure is now **too weak on AFO's direct engineering**.

Any new model that accepts AFO's `全部だ` and concludes Shigaraki never possessed meaningful agency is **too strong on total authorship**.

## 9.1 AFO's reemergence is causally linked to the collapse of Tenko's interior resistance

AFO explains that the vestige assault let him regain consciousness because Tenko's interior heart/mind was overwhelmed in the unconscious domain (`V41:p131`). This resolves the immediate final-internal-control uncertainty from V40 in a specific way: AFO's return is not evidence that Shigaraki had secretly remained obedient. It occurs after a catastrophic interior assault weakens/disrupts Tenko's state.

That distinction preserves the V40 evidence that Shigaraki had independently rejected AFO while functional.

## 9.2 AFO explicitly says felt free choice was part of the hatred-engineering design

The exchange at `V41:p132-133` is unusually explicit.

Tenko insists this was his choice. AFO answers that Tenko needed to **believe** that in order to generate the hatred force required to overcome OFA's will, and that the key was to guide while avoiding overt free-will interference.

This adds a new manipulation model:

`coercion is not always direct command; a controller can design environments so the target experiences self-authorship while desired affect/behavior emerges.`

That is more sophisticated than the earlier mentor/dependency picture.

It also creates an important epistemic limit. Because AFO *wanted* Tenko to experience choice, the existence of subjective choice cannot by itself prove absence of manipulation. But the reverse is also true: evidence of manipulated conditions does not prove every downstream relationship or decision was exhaustively authored by AFO.

## 9.3 OFA acquisition was the design objective

AFO says that through earlier OFA encounters he concluded that stealing OFA required surpassing the inherited holders in will, so he needed a vessel/soul with sufficiently concentrated hatred (`V41:p132-133`).

This changes the causal hierarchy of the Shigaraki project.

AFO did not merely notice a damaged child and opportunistically raise him as a successor. At minimum, V41 establishes deliberate long-horizon cultivation of a person whose hatred could function as an anti-OFA instrument.

The successor relation is therefore even more extractive than V40 had shown.

## 9.4 Kotaro's family environment was deliberately nudged

AFO says he avoided obvious interference and instead applied subtle prompts around Kotaro's existing family/hero grievance (`V41:p133-134`).

This is analytically crucial because it preserves causal plurality.

Kotaro's grievance about Nana did not need to be fabricated from nothing. AFO exploits and amplifies a real wound. The pattern is consistent with his broader method:

- identify an existing vulnerability;
- provide a “solution,” story or nudge;
- convert the vulnerability into dependence/desired action;
- later claim ownership over the result.

AFO's agency increases without making Nana/Kotaro/social conditions causally irrelevant.

## 9.5 Tenko's original Quirk trajectory was directly altered

The chapter says AFO removed the still-unmanifest inherited factor Tenko had received from his parents and gave him a destructive Quirk copy from Garaki's facility (`V41:p134-135`). That copy had reversibility stripped away and was driven only toward destruction.

This is a major factual revision to the origin model.

The responsible current claim is:

> **Decay as Tenko manifested it was not simply his naturally inherited Quirk emerging under stress. AFO deliberately altered the child's Quirk inheritance and supplied an engineered destructive factor.**

This sharply increases AFO's responsibility for the physical mechanics of the Shimura catastrophe.

It does **not** by itself answer every question about Tenko's affective state, later League bonds, adult ideology, or all killings committed after years of development.

## 9.6 `全部だ!!!` is both a causal boast and a character-model tell

AFO escalates from specific evidence into totality: tragedy, trials, negation, affirmation — all were given by him; `全部だ!!!` (`V41:p135-136`).

Some components are now source-confirmed. The total claim is not.

Why resist taking it literally?

1. **The speaker has a demonstrated ownership heuristic.** V40 showed him treating Yoichi, successors and stolen powers through possession grammar.
2. **The claim overreaches the specific causal evidence.** Manipulating Kotaro and supplying Decay do not logically entail authorship of every later interpersonal event.
3. **The series has repeatedly shown emergent relation beyond the mentor's design.** The League developed internal attachments and disagreements; Spinner distinguishes Shigaraki from AFO; Shigaraki rejects AFO's summons; Shigaraki's “villains' hero” role is relationally conditioned by people whose subjectivities matter.
4. **AFO's own later grief contradicts the fantasy of total control.** Yoichi remains a unique loss he cannot metabolize into replaceable possession.

The best V2 rule is:

`Record the specific manipulations as facts. Record “I authored everything” as AFO's totalizing interpretation of those facts.`

## 9.7 The body-possession objective becomes explicit again

AFO intends to erase Tomura/Tenko's remaining heart and take the body/control state (`V41:p136-140`). Midoriya is simultaneously left without the transferred factors and loses both arms in the physical struggle.

The parallel is severe:

- AFO tries to turn another person's life/body into continuation of self;
- Midoriya has just relinquished his inheritance in order to reach another person.

One side treats possession as victory. The other accepts dispossession as the cost of relation.

# 10. No.420 — identity after forced alteration: Aizawa refuses both pure-restoration fantasy and abandonment

## 10.1 Present Mic's grief is adult, not nostalgic wish fulfillment

The chapter returns to Aizawa, Present Mic and Kurogiri/Shirakumo (`V41:p147-150`). Mic reacts with anger and grief. They are no longer U.A. second-years. The friend they remember died long ago; relying on a corpse/constructed remnant is morally and emotionally intolerable to him.

This does not make Mic less attached. It shows attachment constrained by accumulated loss.

## 10.2 Aizawa's Kurogiri model is explicitly mixed

Aizawa reasons from observed behavior: Kurogiri did not simply act as a clean enemy in every relevant moment, but neither has “Shirakumo” returned as an uncomplicated original. His black/white metaphor says that pouring white into black does not produce pure white; what remains is mixed and damaged/altered (`V41:p149`).

This is one of the clearest anti-essentialist identity statements in the series.

It rejects two false binaries:

- `Kurogiri is only an artificial villain, so Shirakumo continuity is meaningless.`
- `Shirakumo is the true self, so successful rescue means restoring the pre-death teenager and discarding Kurogiri history.`

Aizawa looks instead for an **origin point still capable of relation** inside the changed person.

Important constraint: Aizawa's model is an operational interpretation, not narrator-certified metaphysics. Final Kurogiri/Shirakumo status remains open.

## 10.3 Teacher identity overrides nostalgia as the current duty

Aizawa says they are Hero Course teachers whose job is to get U.A. students through graduation (`V41:p149-151`).

That line is a major longitudinal payoff. Shirakumo grief partly shaped Aizawa's harsh survival-focused pedagogy; now the old friendship is mobilized not to recreate the past for its own sake but to protect the future students the adults became responsible for.

The relation is generative rather than purely restorative.

## 10.4 Kurogiri responds enough to become a route, not enough to resolve identity

Kurogiri's response and Warp Gate activation permit Aizawa to reconnect battlefields and gather fighters (`V41:p150-156`). The correct state is:

`relational response + renewed operational function -> continuity materially strengthened; final personhood/identity outcome still OPEN.`

## 10.5 Aizawa preserves role differentiation while assembling reinforcement

Aizawa does **not** order every remaining person to abandon evacuation or containment and rush the central battlefield. He looks for currently available positions while leaving necessary defenders at their assigned functions (`V41:p151-154`).

This is the institutional form of V41's network thesis. Distributed heroism is not an undifferentiated mob. It depends on role allocation.

## 10.6 Returning and unnamed heroes make legitimacy behavioral again

Death Arms appears among those asking to re-enter the fight, followed by an unnamed hero who says that despite being unknown, he is a hero and wants to fight (`V41:p153`). Civilians provide practical support/equipment.

The scene should route primarily to Hero Society rather than force a high-readiness Death Arms model. Its broader proposition is that public/occupational identity can be re-earned or reactivated through action after prestige and confidence collapse.

# 11. Eri — bounded agency tied to an ordinary future

Eri's V41 material is one of the strongest late-series validations of the project's insistence that rescue should produce personhood rather than permanent protected-object status.

## 11.1 The severed horn is a deliberate contribution, not an accidental power donation

Aizawa reveals that Eri gave him her horn for Midoriya (`V41:p157-159`). The scene explicitly frames this as something she chose after learning enough about her Quirk to understand what she could contribute.

The power is limited. The stored Rewind only reaches back a short period. It can restore Midoriya's just-lost arms because the injury falls within that window; it cannot erase the entire accumulated war.

This preserves both agency and constraint.

## 11.2 Eri's future desire is part of the ethical meaning of the contribution

Eri says she wants to sing later — for Midoriya and the people who made her happy (`V41:p158-159`).

That future ordinary desire is not decorative. It prevents the horn act from being interpreted as the endpoint of a child learning heroic self-sacrifice.

The more responsible model is:

`I have a bounded capacity; I want to use it to help; I also expect a future in which I do something joyful and ordinary.`

This is exactly the kind of post-rescue personhood the Overhaul arc needed to produce.

## 11.3 Eri now moves from rescue subject to reciprocal participant without becoming a frontline adult

She is still a child. Aizawa mediates the delivery; adults assess the time window; she is not sent into direct battle. Her agency is therefore **supported and bounded**, not equated with unrestricted adult jurisdiction.

This is a useful model for autonomy generally: respect for another person's choice does not require pretending age, expertise, risk or capacity differences do not exist.

# 12. No.421 — `WE ARE HERE`: the Symbol grammar becomes plural

## 12.1 Friends arrive because another friend is still fighting

The returning fighters say, in substance, that they cannot watch a friend continue fighting and simply stay away (`V41:p163`).

This is not the old Midoriya pattern in which love becomes a reason to exclude others from danger. The relationship now runs reciprocally:

`your danger matters to me -> therefore I may accept risk alongside you rather than only asking you to accept risk for me.`

## 12.2 `WE ARE HERE` is the structural answer to `私が来た`

The chapter title spread converts the series' most famous singular reassurance grammar into plural form (`V41:p164-165`).

The point is not that All Might's singular symbol was false. Earlier V2 volumes established that it genuinely saved, reassured and deterred. The problem was concentration: one finite body became too much infrastructure.

`WE ARE HERE` is therefore a **successor architecture**, not a denunciation of the predecessor.

It says:

- reassurance can be plural;
- no one actor needs causal exclusivity;
- contribution can remain differentiated;
- vulnerability in one node does not collapse the whole network if others can enter.

## 12.3 AFO's world loses color with Yoichi

AFO says that when he heard Yoichi shatter, his world lost color (`V41:p166`). This is crucial attachment evidence.

It would be wrong to infer healthy love. AFO's relation to Yoichi has been possessive, coercive and violent. Yet the unique affective salience is real. Yoichi is not interchangeable with other assets.

The contradiction strengthens the AFO model:

> He is capable of profound attachment but lacks a non-possessive grammar for attachment. Loss therefore becomes emptiness and control-seeking rather than recognition of another person's separateness.

This also falsifies a simplistic “AFO feels nothing” model.

## 12.4 Sero rejects tragedy as a necessary engine of strength

AFO proposes that tragedy is what makes people strong (`V41:p167`). Sero answers that he is not sure; his own motivations are ordinary, and sad things are better absent than present (`V41:p167-168`).

This is a major series-level correction.

MHA contains enormous trauma. Many characters grow after suffering. It would therefore be easy to slide into a compensatory thesis:

`pain was necessary because it produced heroic strength.`

Sero explicitly rejects that teleology.

His evidence is low-stakes and therefore analytically valuable. A person can develop prosocial commitment through ordinary embarrassment, school competition, friendship, wanting not to be left behind, tomorrow's test — not only through catastrophe.

The correct synthesis is:

`People can make meaning and strength after tragedy; tragedy is not thereby justified, necessary, or preferable.`

## 12.5 AFO's mass perception collapses people into gray bodies

Against the entering crowd, AFO increasingly experiences everyone other than Yoichi as low-salience bodies. The world is gray/empty (`V41:p166-171`).

This is the visual-cognitive inverse of hero-society distributed recognition. Midoriya's side becomes stronger by correctly using more people's distinct capacities. AFO becomes weaker as plural persons blur into an undifferentiated mass because the one person he tried to possess is gone.

Plurality is operational strength for one side and perceptual noise for the other.

# 13. OFA after relinquishment — loss of the inheritance does not reset Midoriya to an empty pre-hero self

## 13.1 Midoriya explicitly says he has lost OFA

By `V41:p173`, Midoriya states that One For All is gone/lost. At the same time, the immediate Rewind only restores a short recent interval; earlier accumulated damage remains materially relevant.

At this prospective boundary, do **not** leap from “OFA lost” to a permanent final Quirk status. V42 remains excluded.

## 13.2 `無個性` is no longer the same social/psychological state it was in Volume 1

Midoriya says what he inherited has soaked/stained into his Quirkless self, comparing the situation to All Might, and that embers remain (`V41:p173-174`).

The phrase is one of the series' strongest longitudinal answers to Volume 1.

At V01, `無個性` functioned as a social exclusion category: no assigned heroic capability, ridiculous aspiration, need for external recognition before possibility became legible.

At V41, Midoriya can face the loss of inherited factors while retaining:

- years of trained movement and judgment;
- a hero identity recognized by others;
- relationship networks;
- accumulated tactical knowledge;
- bodily habits shaped by OFA;
- remaining embers at the current boundary;
- a history of choices that power enabled but did not wholly author.

Even if the power can be lost, the person is not reset.

This is **inheritance becoming irreversible biography without requiring permanent possession**.

## 13.3 Aizawa states reciprocal dependence directly

Aizawa tells Midoriya: `僕らは君の力が必要だし 君も僕らの力が必要だ` (`V41:p175`).

This may be the cleanest explicit formula for the volume's governing transition.

The statement does not shame dependency or sentimentalize equality. It says both directions are true:

- the group genuinely needs Midoriya's unique contribution;
- Midoriya genuinely needs the group's contribution.

Neither need cancels agency.

# 14. No.422 — `緑谷出久：ライジング`: weakness becomes relationally generative

## 14.1 The final path is built rather than individually traversed

Aoyama tells Midoriya to run; the coalition's objective is literally to make a path to Shigaraki/AFO (`V41:p179-184`). Hagakure refracts Aoyama's light; Momo and many others contribute their own functions; surrounding people continue to watch and encourage.

The final attack is therefore not causally assignable to Midoriya alone even though Midoriya throws the punch.

This is not dilution of protagonist agency. It is a mature distinction between **authorship of one's act** and **causal exclusivity**.

## 14.2 `頑張れ` replaces passive spectatorship with relational force

The repeated `頑張れ` across classmates, civilians and people linked to the series is not itself a combat power (`V41:p185-194`). Its causal role is motivational/relational. The series does not pretend words substitute for specialized action; they exist alongside Quirk coordination, portals, support gear, physical shielding and battlefield tactics.

The public has changed from the V01 sludge crowd that watches a crisis to a distributed field of people who increasingly understand themselves as implicated, even when their contribution is not direct fighting.

## 14.3 AFO cannot understand why damaged people keep standing

AFO looks at people he regards as little more than dead meat/broken bodies and asks why they do not die/stay down (`V41:p186`).

This is an epistemic failure produced by his person model. If people are assets ranked by individual power, a damaged low-output person has little reason to remain causally important. If people are relational agents, damaged actors can still provide information, openings, shielding, transport, morale, equipment, or a handoff.

AFO is not merely outnumbered. He is using the wrong ontology of cooperation.

## 14.4 `弱き強さ` is not “weakness is good”; it is strength made possible by visible incompleteness

The formal realization at `V41:p187-188` says Midoriya has a weakness All Might did not possess in the same way, a `弱き強さ`, and that it causes these people to rise again as long as he keeps moving.

This needs careful handling because a cheap reading would romanticize weakness or suffering.

The volume has just given Sero an explicit anti-tragedy line. Therefore the intended proposition cannot coherently be “damage makes you better.”

The stronger reading is:

> **Midoriya's visible non-self-sufficiency makes reciprocal participation structurally possible. He is strong enough to matter and weak enough to need others; because he does not close the causal field around a singular invulnerable body, other people can recognize a place for their own agency.**

All Might inspired mass heroism too, so this is not an insult to him. The difference is systemic form. The Symbol reassured through extraordinary sufficiency; Midoriya increasingly mobilizes through **legible insufficiency plus persistence**.

## 14.5 The volume ends on impact, not outcome

`V41:p195` is the decisive punch image and Chapter 422 endpoint. The responsible prospective freeze is:

- Midoriya has reached the strike through a fully distributed causal chain;
- AFO is under attack;
- Tenko/Shigaraki's final interior state is unresolved;
- final victory/defeat is unresolved;
- OFA's permanent endpoint is unresolved;
- survival and postwar consequences are unresolved.

Do not read V42 backward into this page.

### Modeling consequence

Midoriya's mature heroism should no longer be modeled as a trajectory toward maximal self-sufficiency. His agency is stable enough to survive dependence, inherited-power loss and another person's refusal.

**Readiness:** remains `specialist_ready`.

# 16. Power philosophy update — power can leave the body without leaving the self

V41 makes several power propositions unusually explicit.

## 16.1 Ownership is not permanence

Midoriya makes OFA “his” over the series by embodying it, interpreting it and acting through it. In V41 he can choose to give it away. Mature ownership therefore includes the ability to relinquish possession.

## 16.2 Power transfer does not transfer complete authorship

AFO tries to prove the opposite. He gave/manipulated powers and conditions, therefore he claims the resulting person. V41 rejects that inference at the structural level even while confirming extensive manipulation.

## 16.3 Skill/identity can outlast the factor

Midoriya's claim that what he inherited has soaked into his Quirkless self makes training/history non-fungible. A Quirk factor is not the only locus of what was inherited.

## 16.4 Bounded power is still meaningful power

Eri's small Rewind window, Aizawa's damaged Erasure-dependent operations, Kurogiri's unstable portal function, wounded heroes' limited actions and civilians' noncombat support all matter. V41 is hostile to the assumption that only maximal output counts as agency.

## 16.5 The strongest power architecture is plural and role-differentiated

The final assault is not “everyone's powers combine into one superpower.” Distinct functions remain distinct. That is ethically relevant: cooperation need not erase personhood to create collective capacity.

# 17. Recognition and failed rescue update — seeing the person is necessary but not sufficient

## 17.1 Midoriya recognizes personhood without acquiring certainty

`人だよ` establishes the category. The rest of the volume preserves uncertainty, resistance and accountability.

## 17.2 Childhood recognition cannot erase adult recognition

The crying Tenko is real. So is the adult who says he chose League relationships and must be their hero. Complete recognition requires both temporal layers.

## 17.3 AFO is an extreme case of recognition without respect

AFO knows Tenko's wounds, Kotaro's grievance, OFA psychology and Yoichi's significance with exceptional precision. His failure is not lack of information. It is what he thinks knowledge authorizes: design, possession and substitution.

This strongly validates a long-running V2 proposition:

> **Recognition quality requires a theory of what the recognized person is owed. Accurate perception without limits on jurisdiction can become more dangerous, not less.**

## 17.4 Aizawa recognizes continuity without claiming identity certainty

His Kurogiri/Shirakumo model is a mature recognition posture: enough continuity to attempt relation, enough uncertainty to avoid pretending the person is simply restored.

## 17.5 Midoriya's weakness becomes legible to others

The final inversion is that the rescuer becomes a rescue subject at system scale. Classmates, Eri, Aizawa, civilians and other heroes respond because Midoriya's need is visible.

The V01 rule “see the person asking for help” has become reciprocal.

# 18. Villain formation update — AFO engineering sharply increases causal responsibility but defeats monocausal models rather than restoring one

The project had already rejected a simple `society failed them -> villain` thesis. V41 does not replace it with `AFO programmed him -> villain`.

The new causal map is richer.

## 18.1 Established AFO interventions

- exploited the Shimura family wound;
- nudged Kotaro/family conditions;
- removed Tenko's latent inherited factor before normal manifestation;
- supplied an engineered destruction-only Quirk factor;
- cultivated hatred because it was instrumentally useful against OFA;
- attempted to make the process feel self-authored rather than visibly commanded.

## 18.2 Independent/other causal layers remain

- Nana's abandonment decision and family wound are real prior conditions;
- Kotaro's abuse is his own conduct, even if nudged/exploited;
- bystander/social failure around the post-catastrophe child remains a social condition;
- AFO's grooming/mentorship supplies containment, naming and destructive direction;
- Shigaraki later learns from Stain, Midoriya, League peers and actual political events;
- League members possess their own motives and shape Shigaraki relationally;
- Shigaraki makes adult choices under conditions that are manipulated but not shown to be moment-by-moment remote control.

## 18.3 The right causal language is segmented agency

A useful current model is:

`designed vulnerability + manipulated developmental environment + engineered Quirk catastrophe + grooming + social failures + self-authored learning/relationships + chosen harms`.

Different acts may have different agency distributions.

This is much more defensible than either:

`Shigaraki is solely self-created`

or

`Shigaraki never chose anything.`

# 19. Hero Society update — distributed heroism becomes executable architecture

## 19.1 The singular-symbol critique reaches an action-level answer

Earlier volumes diagnosed All Might concentration as both enormously effective and systemically fragile. V41 finally shows the replacement architecture working at full scale:

- Aizawa coordinates;
- Kurogiri transports;
- specialists remain at other fronts where needed;
- Eri contributes a limited restorative resource;
- students create openings/pathways;
- pro heroes return or volunteer;
- civilians supply equipment and social support;
- Midoriya carries a unique final action without owning the whole causal chain.

## 19.2 “Everyone can help” is not “expertise does not matter”

The volume repeatedly preserves expertise. Aizawa chooses available positions. Rewind's exact window matters. Quirk matchups matter. Wounded capacity matters. Evacuation roles matter.

Distributed legitimacy therefore does not mean anti-professional populism.

## 19.3 Public legitimacy becomes participatory rather than merely spectatorial

Earlier hero society often converted public into audience: stadium spectators, rankings viewers, news consumers, scandal judges. V41 retains watching but increasingly adds participation: supplies, encouragement, volunteering, movement toward shared stakes.

The public is not magically competent at frontline combat. It becomes less external to the system it asks heroes to sustain.

## 19.4 Retirement/failure need not be permanent moral essence

Returning heroes matter because institutional collapse previously produced attrition. V41 allows renewed choice without pretending earlier withdrawal did no damage. Identity can be revised through later conduct.

A body part associated with dangerous Quirk accumulation is severed and converted into bounded aid, while the child verbally projects herself into a future defined by singing. Power/body sacrifice is visually subordinated to future personhood rather than becoming her whole identity.

## 22.6 `WE ARE HERE` plural composition

The title spread formalizes a shift from singular page-scale arrival to collective arrival. The series does not need to erase All Might's image; it pluralizes the function.

## 22.7 Color loss around AFO

AFO's world losing color after Yoichi and the later gray massing of other people makes his recognition failure visual. Singular possession has impoverished perception of plurality.

## 22.8 Repeated path-making toward Midoriya's strike

The final chapter repeatedly shows other bodies/Quirks constructing the corridor through which Midoriya can act. Composition assigns final agency to Midoriya without pretending the attack is self-caused.

# 24. First-pass / common-reading adjudications

## 24.1 “AFO created Shigaraki, therefore Shigaraki had no agency”

**REJECT AS TOO STRONG.**

V41 confirms far more AFO engineering than earlier prospective evidence allowed. It does not convert AFO's total-authorship boast into narrator authority. Use segmented agency.

## 24.2 “Shigaraki was always fully self-authored and AFO merely exploited him later”

**REJECT / MAJOR REVISION.**

Chapter 419 directly contradicts this. AFO altered the child's Quirk inheritance, engineered Decay, manipulated family conditions and cultivated hatred for OFA acquisition.

## 24.3 “Saving Shigaraki means revealing that Tomura was fake and Tenko was the true person”

**REJECT.**

Chapter 418 explicitly gives adult Shigaraki chosen bonds and a relational role he refuses to discard. Childhood rescue cannot require adulthood erasure.

## 24.4 “The ending is that everyone becomes a hero”

**REVISE.**

V41 distributes concern and causal participation but preserves differentiated roles, professional expertise, evacuation duty and capacity limits. The stronger claim is broad civic participation in protection rather than occupational flattening.

## 24.5 “MHA says trauma makes people stronger”

**REJECT AS A NORMATIVE THESIS.**

AFO voices the compensatory-tragedy argument and Sero directly rejects it. Characters can grow after suffering without suffering becoming justified or necessary.

## 24.6 “Midoriya's final growth is becoming strong enough not to need anyone”

**REJECT.**

V41's explicit formulation is reciprocal need. The final action is enabled by a network.

## 24.7 “Kurogiri is Shirakumo restored”

**REJECT AS CURRENT V41 STATE.**

Aizawa explicitly conceptualizes the remaining person as mixed/altered. Final identity remains unresolved, but pure restoration is not the current working model.

## 24.8 “Eri's horn proves her heroic development through self-sacrifice”

**REVISE / QUALIFY.**

The act is chosen and costly, but its ethical meaning depends on bounded deployment, adult mediation and Eri's preserved future desire to sing. She is becoming an agent, not a sacrifice resource.

## 24.9 “AFO cannot love or care about anyone”

**REJECT AS TOO SIMPLE.**

Yoichi's loss drains color from AFO's world. The problem is not absence of attachment; it is possessive attachment that cannot recognize separateness.

## 24.10 “Weakness itself is the final virtue”

**REJECT.**

The `弱き強さ` proposition is relational. Midoriya's incompleteness creates room/need for other agents. The volume has already denied that suffering is intrinsically good.

# 26. Frozen Volume 41 endpoint and current synthesis

## Resolved in V41

- Danger Sense was stolen by Shigaraki and operationally used.
- Midoriya/vestiges deliberately chose to transfer/expend OFA factors against Shigaraki's interior wound.
- Midoriya reached Shimura/Tenko memory and directly protected the child in the memory-space encounter.
- Shigaraki explicitly claimed adult choices/League bonds and a “hero to villains” relational role.
- AFO reemerged after Tenko's interior collapse.
- AFO directly manipulated the Shimura family environment, removed Tenko's latent inherited Quirk factor, and supplied an engineered destructive Quirk copy stripped of reversibility.
- AFO's hatred-cultivation project was designed to overcome OFA's collective will.
- Midoriya temporarily lost both arms; Eri's short-window Rewind contribution restored the recent loss.
- Aizawa/Kurogiri enabled distributed reinforcements.
- Aizawa explicitly modeled Kurogiri/Shirakumo as mixed/altered rather than simply restored.
- Eri deliberately contributed her horn and expressed a future desire to sing.
- Sero explicitly rejected tragedy as a desirable/necessary route to strength.
- AFO's attachment to Yoichi was shown as singular enough that Yoichi's loss drained color from his world.
- Midoriya stated OFA had been lost while `残り火` remained at the current boundary.
- Aizawa explicitly stated reciprocal need between Midoriya and the group.
- The final attack was constructed through collective path-making and encouragement.

## Revised / complicated

- **Shigaraki agency:** substantially more AFO-engineered at the developmental/origin level than previously authorized; not reduced to zero.
- **Villain formation:** AFO becomes a much more direct causal engineer, but monocausal “AFO made everything” remains inadequate.
- **Rescue:** recognizing child Tenko is insufficient if it erases adult Tomura's chosen relationships.
- **OFA identity:** factor possession and inherited identity separate; loss can coexist with retained biography/skill/embers.
- **Kurogiri:** continuity with Shirakumo is better modeled as mixed identity than pure restoration.
- **Hero society:** distributed heroism is role-differentiated participation, not abolition of expertise.
- **Strength:** tragedy can be metabolized into strength but is not thereby good/necessary; visible weakness can be relationally generative without weakness itself becoming the goal.

## Current synthesis

> **Volume 41 completes the move away from heroic self-sufficiency. It does so without abandoning individual agency. Midoriya remains the author of the final punch precisely because other people make the punch possible; Shigaraki remains accountable precisely because explaining how AFO designed the conditions of his life does not turn every later relation into AFO's property. The volume's deepest opposition is therefore not individual versus collective. It is possession versus relation: AFO tries to make causal influence total ownership, while the heroic coalition becomes stronger by letting agency remain distributed, bounded, reciprocal and incomplete.**
