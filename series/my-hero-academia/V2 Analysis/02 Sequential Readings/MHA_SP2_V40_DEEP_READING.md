# 1. Governing transition: reciprocal agency against extraction -> inheritance without possession

Volume 39 established **reciprocal agency against extraction**. V40 asks what makes reciprocity structurally different from AFO's own language of giving, taking, succession and inheritance.

The answer is not that receiving power is good while taking power is bad in the abstract. The volume gives Yoichi the decisive formulation: `与えるも奪うも 世界一優しい力になったかもしれないのに`. Giving and taking could have become the world's kindest power if AFO had possessed even a little regard for another person.

The governing transition is therefore:

> **reciprocal agency against extraction -> inheritance without possession: power, survival, technique, ideals and narrative roles can pass between people without making the receiver an extension or property of the giver; AFO fails because he repeatedly mistakes transmission for ownership and plurality for defective control.**

This is visible at every scale:

- All Might fights with functions modeled on the students but does not become them.
- Aoyama receives light/encouragement and turns it into self-authored rescue labor.
- Hagakure refracts Aoyama's laser without appropriating his action.
- Stain assists All Might without becoming an institutional hero.
- Midoriya launches Bakugo but cannot perform Bakugo's rescue for him.
- Edgeshot repairs Bakugo but says he only `繋いだ`; Bakugo's cultivated Quirk wakes him.
- All Might gives Bakugo equipment while recognizing Bakugo's own hero identity.
- Kudo's hand lets Yoichi's future continue outside AFO's ownership.
- OFA itself emerges from factor fusion and voluntary onward transfer.
- Bakugo answers AFO's `僕の物語` with `俺たちの物語` and then explicitly denies solitary victory.

AFO's countermodel is consolidation: all Quirks, all persons, all stories, all inherited selves, all objectives under one proprietor. His defeat is therefore not merely that Bakugo hits harder. It is that a system built to absorb factors under one will cannot reliably model a causal field produced by distinct wills that remain distinct.

# 2. No.399 — All Might turns received relationships into equipment

## 2.1 Mortality does not terminate transmitted light

Armored All Might continues the V39 confrontation without pretending his body or equipment is inexhaustible. His language shifts from his own persistence to transmission: people flicker; even if his light goes out, someone who received it can illuminate afterward.

That claim is important because it opposes AFO's criterion for victory. AFO repeatedly treats a person's death or incapacity as proof that their project has failed. All Might treats mortality as compatible with continued causal efficacy if other people remain able to act.

The point is not mystical immortality. It is social inheritance.

## 2.2 Aoyama: compromised identity becomes deliberate rescue labor

The Kunieda front gives V40 a smaller-scale control case for the same structure.

Aoyama is not purified of betrayal history. He knows his future at U.A. is uncertain and still chooses to use the remaining present to free captured heroes and oppose Kunieda. His desire to `輝く` is therefore no longer merely self-presentation. It becomes a service action performed under the possibility that his institutional status will end.

This strengthens V34's reparative model:

> **debt does not disappear, but neither does debt erase the possibility of self-authored prosocial action.**

## 2.3 Hagakure makes support additive rather than substitutive

Hagakure's refraction of Aoyama's Navel Laser produces `集光屈折 ネビルレーザー!!`. The two Quirks are complementary rather than interchangeable.

This is a compact version of the volume's larger inheritance argument. Aoyama supplies one capacity; Hagakure alters its propagation; the result is jointly enabled without requiring either person to become the other's tool.

# 3. No.400 — tools have limits; inherited technique still matters

V40 does not retreat from V39's explicit limit on support equipment. AFO continues to insist that tools do not exceed their limits, and the suit continues to fail component by component.

The correct inference is therefore not:

> technology proves Quirks unnecessary in every tactical sense.

It is:

> **Quirklessness does not erase hero agency, while technology remains finite and dependent on design, knowledge, timing and the people whose techniques inspired it.**

The armor's student-named functions make the social archive literal. `ENGINE`, `URAVITY`, `EARPHONE JACK`, `CREATI` and related functions do not transfer the students' personhood into Toshinori. They are learned/engineered affordances placed under Toshinori's own judgment.

# 4. Stain's return — blood equalizes bodies without equalizing ethics

## 4.1 `等しく赤い`

Stain locates the fight and makes blood itself the point of intervention. His `等しく赤い` is visually and conceptually sharp: beneath costume, reputation, Quirk status and social role, blood is equally red.

This does not mean All Might and AFO are morally equivalent. It means Stain's own Quirk operates through a bodily commonality that precedes those categories.

That matters in a volume preoccupied with ownership and inherited status. Biological commonality does not determine moral use.

## 4.2 Stain's final imperative is survival, not purification

The strongest late Stain evidence is `生きて勝て`.

Earlier Stain made death and purification central to his moral jurisdiction. Here, confronting the person who most closely embodies his hero ideal, his imperative is not “die properly” or “prove the ideal through martyrdom.” It is **live and win**.

This is a meaningful longitudinal strengthening/revision of how his absolutism relates to the object of admiration. He remains ideologically severe; V40 does not turn him into a pluralist. But his hero ideal is compatible with survival and future action.

## 4.3 AFO learns from defection

Stain's intervention also produces strong AFO modeling evidence. AFO had considered the possibility of Bloodcurdle after Tartarus and prepared a blood-condition counter. He then overwhelms Stain and takes `凝血`.

AFO is not defeated because he cannot learn. He learns very well from prior defections and hostile capacities.

His later failure must therefore be located more precisely: **he can model tools and tactics better than he can model independent agency once it ceases to behave as an owned factor.**

# 5. No.402 — Shigaraki refuses to answer AFO's summons

The separated fronts begin producing closure reports: Shoto's operation succeeds, the Toga/Twice proliferation stops, and AFO approaches a range where reaching Shigaraki becomes tactically plausible.

AFO attempts to call Shigaraki toward him.

Shigaraki answers:

`先生…邪魔するなよ`

This is a major separability datum.

At the V40 boundary, Shigaraki is not an available extension waiting for the original AFO body to issue a command. He identifies AFO as interference with his own present fight.

This strongly supports the V38 hidden-origin recovery and V39 self-authored destruction model. It still does **not** prove that every AFO factor/vestige inside Shigaraki is gone. Final internal separability remains OPEN.

# 6. Nana's death and AFO's criterion of failure

As Toshinori approaches the death Nighteye predicted, AFO remembers Nana Shimura's end.

His evaluation is revealing. He remembers Nana as someone whose ideals ran ahead of her ability and whose death therefore looked pathetic. The contempt is not only personal. It is philosophical:

> if you cannot personally complete the project, your death demonstrates the project's failure.

Nana and Toshinori instead operate through entrustment. Nana asks Gran Torino to carry forward a future beyond herself. Toshinori's entire late identity increasingly accepts that he may not be the final actor.

The disagreement is therefore about what counts as causal survival.

AFO's model recognizes persistence through possession: something remains his because he retains or reinserts himself.

The OFA-side model recognizes persistence through **difference**: what is handed forward can cease to belong to the predecessor and still fulfill something the predecessor valued.

# 7. No.403 — AFO denies All Might control over his own martyrdom

All Might's willingness to die is not equivalent to wanting death. V39 already established explicit win orientation. V40 sharpens the point by having AFO deny Toshinori even the ability to choose a narratively useful heroic death.

AFO wants the execution to serve AFO's story: kill the man who gave the world a dream and force that dream back into “reality.”

This is narrative possession again. AFO wants not merely Toshinori's body but ownership over the meaning of Toshinori's death.

The surviving Star and Stripe-linked pilots interfere because Toshinori was Star's object of admiration. Another inherited relation therefore interrupts AFO's attempt to dictate the scene's meaning.

# 8. No.403-404 — Bakugo returns as an answer to `誰か`

## 8.1 The rescue is staged as successor intervention

Midoriya sees All Might moving toward death and internally rejects it: `嫌だ`.

The visual grammar then asks for `誰か` — somebody.

Bakugo appears conscious and standing.

The scene does not restore the old Symbol to autonomous self-sufficiency. It makes the next actor possible because prior actors kept Bakugo alive and because Bakugo himself can now choose what to do with that survival.

## 8.2 Midoriya and Bakugo co-author the rescue

Midoriya and Bakugo coordinate physically. Midoriya uses `変速` to launch Bakugo toward All Might.

That is one of the strongest longitudinal payoffs in the reread.

The early Midoriya/Bakugo problem was not simply dislike. It was whether they could communicate, recognize one another's capacities and act toward a shared objective without hierarchy injury destroying cooperation.

V40's answer is highly mature:

- Midoriya supplies acceleration.
- Bakugo accepts the enablement.
- Bakugo performs the rescue.
- neither action becomes the other's authorship.

# 9. The Butterfly Effect is causal metaphor, not yet magic

No.404 explicitly invokes the butterfly-effect image: a small wingbeat elsewhere can contribute to a hurricane far away.

The sequence pairs this with distributed wishing, watching and action.

The correct prospective reading is bounded:

- **FACT:** the manga deliberately frames many distant small states/actions as part of a causal field converging on the rescue.
- **STRONG INFERENCE:** this extends V37-V38 propagation/plural-authorship grammar.
- **NOT ESTABLISHED:** ordinary hope itself has acquired a free-floating supernatural force that directly alters physics.

The volume gives enough material causes — Edgeshot, Bakugo's Quirk, Midoriya's Gearshift, pilots, All Might's delay, AFO's Rewind — that no magical causation is required.

# 10. No.405 — `運命が捻じ曲がった`

The manga explicitly identifies this location as the death point Nighteye foresaw and then states:

`運命が捻じ曲がったんだよ`

This is stronger than saying the prophecy was merely misunderstood. The text treats the expected future as having been bent.

But the volume also supplies the bending mechanism as accumulated action. Nighteye's old future does not change because a prophecy decides to be kind. It changes after years of relationships, preparation, repair, intervention and cooperation.

The strongest current formulation is therefore:

> **MHA treats fate as a high-probability causal trajectory that can be bent when enough independent agency changes the path feeding it.**

That remains an interpretation, not a general metaphysical law proven beyond this sequence.

# 11. Edgeshot and Bakugo — repair without authorship transfer

Edgeshot gives one of the cleanest statements in the entire V2 project about supported agency.

He repaired internal damage and sustained life functions. Yet he tells Bakugo:

`俺は…繋いだだけだ`

and:

`目覚めさせたのは お前が磨いてきた“個性”だ`

Bakugo's own sweat/Quirk participates in the final awakening process.

The distinction matters:

- Edgeshot's intervention is indispensable.
- Bakugo did not “save himself” in the ordinary sense.
- Bakugo's cultivated body/Quirk remains causally necessary.
- receiving rescue does not make the rescued person a passive authored product of the rescuer.

Bakugo's `生かしてくれてありがとう先輩` also matters for reconstruction. He accepts life as received help without converting gratitude into humiliation.

# 12. All Might recognizes Bakugo's authored hero identity

All Might addresses Bakugo by the full `大・爆・殺・神ダイナマイト` hero name and gives him the remaining equipment he can provide.

This is a late-series correction to early adult responses to Bakugo's self-presentation. Recognition no longer requires sanitizing his identity into something aesthetically comfortable before assistance can be offered.

All Might gives; Bakugo decides what to do with the gift.

Again, inheritance without possession.

# 13. No.406 — post-revival Bakugo is continuity, not a new person

Bakugo's extraordinary movement after revival is routed back to `Cluster` and the whole-body sweat/explosion state he had been approaching before collapse.

The volume therefore blocks a lazy “death gave him a new power” model.

His current combat logic is embodied learning:

- pain tells him where sweat beads are distributed;
- he maps position and timing;
- he triggers chain explosions through the body;
- the performance grows from a prior trained Quirk rather than replacing it.

This is useful behavioral modeling evidence because Bakugo remains analytic under severe pain. Excitement and aggression coexist with precise proprioceptive control.

# 14. Kudo — the hand AFO cannot forgive

AFO initially processes Bakugo as an irritating `モブ`. The depth of his anger only becomes legible when Bakugo evokes Kudo, the second OFA bearer.

The decisive Kudo act is not maximum combat output.

It is extending a hand to Yoichi.

AFO's hatred is therefore relational. Kudo's offense was enabling Yoichi to become causally available to a future AFO did not own.

This gives the extended-hand motif exceptional weight:

> **the hand does not create possession; it creates an exit from possession.**

Bakugo then refuses literal identity collapse. The manga can establish an analogy of will/role across generations without saying Bakugo is Kudo reborn.

# 15. No.407 — AFO/Yoichi origin: abandonment is real, monocausal trauma is not

V40 finally supplies a direct birth/origin layer for AFO and Yoichi.

Their mother dies at a riverbank. The infants are left with her body and then separated from it by flooding. AFO takes her abnormal factor/ability; prenatal resource asymmetry leaves Yoichi tiny and frail.

This is severe origin adversity.

It is **not** enough to establish:

> AFO became AFO because nobody cared for him.

The narration and behavior give more specific evidence:

- possessive orientation appears extremely early;
- AFO treats surrounding things as property;
- even retaining Yoichi is framed through ownership rather than ordinary sibling care;
- he kills/uses power while still young;
- later he consciously selects an idealized narrative identity.

Formation is therefore interaction among early deprivation, bodily asymmetry, extreme possessiveness, power opportunity, relational fixation and chosen ideology.

# 16. Comics produce divergent aspirations

Yoichi reads comics because pictures let another person's thought reach him. His response is aspirational: he wants to resemble the hero.

He also has not fully given up on his brother. He interprets a small remembered gesture as evidence that AFO might contain kindness.

AFO reads the same broad cultural form differently. He finds the Demon King model attractive because everyone exists for him and the world can be ordered around his centrality.

`僕にも夢ができた`

This is crucial villain-formation evidence.

AFO is not merely an instinct machine. He develops a **dream** and chooses a symbolic role that makes possession meaningful to him.

The series therefore refuses both extremes:

- “he was born evil, so history is irrelevant”; and
- “trauma made him evil, so later choice is irrelevant.”

# 17. No.408 — OFA begins as factor fusion, not moral essence

V40 provides unusually precise early mechanics.

AFO forcibly gave Yoichi a stolen ability/power. Yoichi, whom AFO had regarded as Quirkless, actually possessed a tiny underdeveloped factor associated with transfer. The factors merged, producing the basis of One For All.

This means OFA is not originally “good” because of a morally pure substance.

Its moral trajectory emerges from relation, consent, purpose and onward use.

Yoichi's line is therefore the volume's load-bearing power-philosophy statement:

`与えるも奪うも 世界一優しい力になったかもしれないのに`

Giving and taking are capacities. Kindness or domination depends on what the user recognizes other people to be.

# 18. AFO's manipulation heuristic becomes explicit

V40 also gives direct decision-policy evidence for AFO.

He can use tears/display to unsettle people, attends to what others hate and desire, and uses those vulnerabilities to bring people under his control.

That is stronger than simply calling him manipulative.

The working heuristic is:

> **identify affective leverage -> distort the target's available choices -> convert dependence/desire into possession -> reinterpret the resulting relation as proof that the target belongs in AFO's story.**

This supports the readiness promotion later in this document.

# 19. `全因解放` — maximum consolidation under one will

AFO's late tactic is `全因解放`: release/concentrate accumulated factors toward one objective.

It is the combat expression of his philosophy.

Many Quirks become one project because one owner says so.

The opposing side is almost the inverse architecture. It is not centrally optimized in the same way. Its strength is that Bakugo, All Might, Midoriya, Edgeshot, Stain, the pilots, students and viewers do not need to become one mind before their actions can converge.

# 20. No.409 — `僕の物語` versus `俺たちの物語`

AFO repeats:

`これは僕の物語だ!!`

Bakugo answers:

`俺たちの物語だ!!`

The pronoun shift is the philosophical climax of V40.

This is not a modesty scene in which Bakugo discovers that he personally matters less. He remains intensely self-authored, competitive and decisive.

What changes is the causal ontology of winning.

He then says, in substance:

`俺一人で…勝てるワケねーーンだよ`

That is a major Bakugo longitudinal payoff. The character who once experienced reliance as hierarchy injury can now state that solitary victory is impossible without experiencing the statement as self-erasure.

# 21. AFO's affect disrupts the control system

As AFO regresses to a younger body, his factor integration begins failing.

The manga explicitly links this to both bodily fragility and negative emotion. The internal explanation is that being jerked around by `負の感情` weakens `支配権`.

This strongly preserves V38-V39's correction:

> mature AFO calm was not evidence that he lacked emotion; it was evidence that he normally regulated emotion well enough to maintain control.

V40 makes dysregulation mechanically consequential. The owner loses control of the factors precisely while trying to force all factors into one objective.

# 22. No.410 — AFO fails to transfer himself back into Shigaraki

At the start of No.410, AFO's remaining plan is explicit: progress far enough to transfer his own Quirk/self-linked factor to Shigaraki, reinforce the “other me” he believes has been swallowed, and regain control of Shigaraki's heart/body.

This is the purest possible statement of succession-as-repossession.

He does not want Shigaraki to inherit and differ.

He wants to **reinstall ownership**.

He fails to reach him.

Bakugo's intervention consumes the remaining Rewind trajectory until the external AFO body disappears.

# 23. AFO's external-body endpoint — death without restitution

V40 closes the external Rewind-body question.

AFO regresses to infancy and disappears. The subsequent Shigaraki scene explicitly says `先生が死んだな`.

One visual sequence imagines stolen Quirk factors returning to their original holders, but Hawks's vestige immediately rejects that as too generous a `サービス`.

Therefore the correct adjudication is:

- **FACT:** the external AFO/Rewind body dies/disappears in V40.
- **FACT:** AFO fails to reach Shigaraki and fails to execute the planned self-transfer.
- **REJECT:** V40 establishes that all stolen Quirks literally return to their original owners after AFO dies.
- **OPEN:** the final state of any AFO-derived vestige/control architecture already inside Shigaraki.

# 24. Bakugo's endpoint — victory ends in handoff, not ownership

After AFO disappears, Bakugo is physically spent.

His final orientation is toward Midoriya:

`出久 頑張れよ`

That matters because Bakugo does not attempt to make defeating AFO equivalent to owning the war's final resolution.

He completes the action only he can complete, then relinquishes the remaining fight to another actor.

This is the mature form of plural authorship.

# 25. No.410 returns to Midoriya/Shigaraki — AFO's death does not end Shigaraki

The volume deliberately refuses to let AFO's disappearance collapse Shigaraki into a solved remainder.

A pre-battle discussion had already recognized the danger that Shigaraki's destructive wish could turn toward the land itself. The current Shigaraki now describes destruction at national scale: not merely defeating heroes or destroying one city, but leaving no `再興の芽` — no seed of reconstruction.

This strongly confirms that Shigaraki's present destructive agency cannot be dismissed as “AFO made him do it.”

He then recognizes AFO's death:

`先生が死んだな`

and gives AFO bounded credit for making him stronger.

This is neither loyalty nor total disavowal. It is a differentiated retrospective relation:

- AFO exploited him;
- AFO strengthened him;
- AFO tried to repossess him;
- AFO is now dead externally;
- Shigaraki continues his own chosen destructive project.

# 26. Prospective endpoint: `危機感知` becomes unstable/contested

# 28. Relationship-state updates

## Midoriya <-> Bakugo

The relation reaches a new operational maturity. Midoriya supplies Gearshift acceleration; Bakugo accepts it; Bakugo performs the rescue. The pair can now use one another as high-trust causal infrastructure without either collapsing into the other's role.

## Bakugo <-> All Might

All Might receives rescue from a student whose admiration has long been partly hidden beneath abrasive behavior. All Might then addresses Bakugo by his chosen hero name and gives him equipment. Recognition is bidirectional and non-paternalistic.

## Bakugo <-> Edgeshot

Edgeshot's repair is indispensable, but he refuses ownership of Bakugo's return. Bakugo answers with direct gratitude. This is unusually clean evidence for help-receiving without debt/humiliation conversion.

## AFO -> Yoichi

The sibling relation is now strongly specified as possession fixation rather than ordinary care. AFO wants Yoichi, retains him, gives him power without respecting refusal, chases him after escape and cannot tolerate the future Kudo enables.

## Yoichi -> AFO

Yoichi does not merely fear or hate his brother. He continues hoping there is kindness in him and imagines a counterfactual in which AFO's capacity could have become gentle. That hope does not validate AFO; it establishes Yoichi's recognition policy.

## AFO -> Kudo

Kudo's enduring offense is the hand extended to Yoichi. AFO's retaliation against his line/associates converts relational exit into a grievance that persists across generations.

## AFO -> Shigaraki

V40 confirms the endpoint of AFO's succession theory: inheritance was never meant to produce an autonomous successor. AFO's final plan is to transfer himself back in and regain `支配`.

## Shigaraki -> AFO

Shigaraki calls him `先生`, rejects his interference, later recognizes that he made Shigaraki stronger, and continues independently after AFO's external death. This is a differentiated relation, not simple loyalty or simple external possession.

# 29. Power-philosophy updates

## Power is morally underdetermined without relation

Yoichi's `与えるも奪うも` statement prevents an overly simple moral ontology of Quirk mechanics. Transfer, theft, inheritance and support have to be analyzed through consent, recognition and use.

## OFA is mechanically composite and socially re-authored

The first OFA state emerges through factor fusion: Yoichi's small transfer factor plus a power AFO forcibly gave him. What begins through coercive action is later re-authored through voluntary entrustment.

## AFO's strongest attack embodies his weakest philosophy

`全因解放` maximizes incorporated power under one objective. Its failure is accompanied by weakened `支配権` under negative emotion. The combat system and ethical system mirror one another: central ownership produces extraordinary output but also a single point of affective/control failure.

## Cluster is cultivated embodiment, not resurrection magic

Bakugo's post-revival output is continuous with a trained Quirk state. Edgeshot's rescue makes future action possible; it does not manufacture Bakugo's competence.

# 30. Hero-society updates

## Global spectatorship becomes participatory without becoming magical

People previously able to call Japan distant begin watching, praying and identifying with the outcome. V40 continues V38's argument that mediation changes who understands themselves as implicated.

The effect is socially real even if the manga does not establish prayer as a literal new force.

## Evacuation planning is future-oriented hero labor

The pre-battle planning in No.410 makes a simple institutional priority explicit: `人命と未来を第一に行動する`.

This is not victory spectacle. It is contingency design around civilians, buildings, underground shelters and what remains after battle. Hero society is being reconstructed in the text as infrastructure for preserving a future, not merely defeating a current enemy.

# 31. Recognition / failed-rescue updates

## All Might — the rescuer becomes rescue recipient

The Symbol's predicted death is bent because other people recognize that Toshinori himself is someone whose survival matters. This is an important reversal of the old one-way rescue hierarchy.

## Bakugo — receiving life does not erase agency

Edgeshot's repair and Midoriya's launch demonstrate that receiving rescue can expand future authorship rather than create permanent debt ownership.

## Yoichi — recognition without control

Yoichi's tragedy is partly that he recognizes a possible brother AFO never chooses to become. His hope is not rewarded with reform, but the capacity to see possibility still produces the relation that later allows OFA's history to exist.

# 32. Villain-formation updates

## AFO — formation is deprivation + possession + chosen ideal

V40 supplies a much stronger formation model than “born evil” or “abandoned child becomes evil.”

The evidence supports:

- extreme prenatal/birth deprivation;
- early extraction of resources from mother and twin;
- very early possession orientation;
- overwhelming power asymmetry;
- Yoichi as retained property/desired object;
- exposure to narrative models;
- conscious selection of Demon King centrality;
- reinforcement through successful manipulation and accumulation;
- enduring retaliatory hatred toward people who enable exit from his ownership.

This is enough for a strong behavioral model without pretending every motive is explained.

## Shigaraki — AFO's death does not dissolve chosen grievance

Shigaraki's national-scale destruction claim after AFO's external death is a decisive control against blaming all destructive desire on active AFO command. Manipulation remains part of his formation; current destructive agency remains his.

# 34. Visual grammar and motifs

## Extended hands

Kudo's hand to Yoichi is the historical anti-possession image. Midoriya/Bakugo physical coordination and Bakugo's reach toward All Might replay the gesture as enabling action rather than claiming a person.

## Light and flicker

All Might's flickering light, Aoyama's desire to shine and Hagakure's refraction form a single visual grammar: light can pass through another person and change without being owned by the origin.

## Infancy and regression

AFO's childhood origin and Rewind regression collapse beginning and end together. The visual return to infancy does **not** restore innocence. It strips away accumulated adult regulation while leaving the possessive affect exposed.

## Eyes, screens and spectators

V40 repeatedly cuts from battlefield eyes to screens and distant viewers. The war's public meaning is no longer carried by one Symbol's face; recognition is distributed.

## Story text

`僕の物語` / `俺たちの物語` externalizes the competing ontologies into literal narration language.

The abstract torch metaphor gains a concrete relational origin: escape, hand-extension, factor transfer and voluntary continuation.
