# 1. Governing transition: distributed heroism becomes distributed agency under coercion, contingency and loss

Volume 33 broke the single-point-of-failure model of heroism by showing that rescuers themselves can be rescue subjects and that civilians, classmates, mentors and institutions can carry bounded pieces of the work. Volume 34 asks a more difficult follow-up question: **what kind of network is being built when responsibility is distributed?**

The answer is not that every distributed network is morally equivalent. V34 places two network architectures beside one another.

AFO's architecture is redundancy through **fungibility**. He accumulates Quirks, creates multiple routes toward the same objective, cultivates people as backups, weaponizes dependencies, and treats identities as things that can be occupied, overwritten, replaced or discarded. His boast that he has many `友達` is deliberately perverse in a volume whose other relationships depend on treating persons as ends rather than interchangeable means. His network is resilient precisely because no subordinate needs to remain fully protected as a person.

The hero-side architecture also distributes capability, but its redundancy is **person-preserving rather than person-displacing**. Star cannot equal All Might's raw body and does not need to. Her pilots extend her information, targeting and survival capacity. Even after her death, their recordings and her final rule create strategic time for people she will never meet. Class 1-A's members train distinct capacities rather than becoming identical soldiers. Aoyama is offered a future role not because his betrayal never happened, but because the class refuses to reduce him permanently to the function AFO assigned him. Hatsume makes the same distinction institutionally: Support Course students do not fight on the front line, but invention is still their `ヒーロー活動` because it sustains the people who do.

This produces V34's strongest contrast:

> **AFO builds redundancy by making people disposable. The hero side builds redundancy so that people do not have to be disposable.**

The contrast also prevents an easy celebration of sacrifice. Star dies. The source treats that as a grave loss to her pilots, the United States and the international hero system. Her death is not vindicated simply because it produces useful consequences. What matters analytically is that she preserves agency inside loss: when her body and Quirk are about to be taken, she converts the theft mechanism itself into damage to AFO's stockpile and a delay for others. Her value is not retroactively reduced to dying usefully.

Aoyama is the corresponding moral test on the noncombat side. He has actually done wrong. He transmitted the U.S.J. and forest-camp information under coercion. V34 refuses both simplifications: coercion does not make those acts unreal, and those acts do not make `敵` an exhaustive identity from which no further agency is possible. Midoriya, Iida, Aizawa and the authorities differ in tone, but the resulting policy is coherent: preserve accountability, preserve security controls, and preserve the person's remaining capacity to choose.

# 2. No.329 - foreign intervention exposes the strategic value of one exceptional person

## 2.1 AFO evaluates Star as both threat and resource

The opening enemy-side discussion makes international hero intervention part of AFO's planning problem rather than an uncomplicated cavalry arrival (`V34:p007-013`). Other countries are experiencing instability and have domestic reasons to retain their own strongest heroes. AFO therefore understands Star and Stripe in two registers at once:

1. as an opponent whose presence could disrupt the completion schedule; and
2. as a Quirk asset whose theft could radically lower the difficulty of everything that follows.

This is an important AFO modeling atom. He does not encounter exceptional people primarily as persons with whom he must negotiate stable reciprocal relationships. He evaluates their capabilities for incorporation into his own architecture. New Order's value is not only that it would make him stronger; stealing it would convert the enemy's strongest national deterrent into his own reusable property.

The same scene broadens the remaining villain network. Skeptic continues information operations, Dabi remains oriented toward destructive culmination, and Spinner is being positioned by surviving liberation actors as a rallying figure for heteromorphic discontent (`V34:p010-016`). Spinner's own affect still includes concern for Shigaraki rather than clean devotion to AFO. The enemy network is therefore not psychologically uniform even when AFO can exploit its members as strategic routes.

## 2.2 Star arrives as a relational actor, not merely a power benchmark

Star's encounter with the target offshore immediately makes her All Might connection relevant. She has come because her mentor is in danger, but she arrives embedded in a U.S. Air Force formation whose pilots communicate with her as long-standing comrades rather than distant support personnel (`V34:p017-022`).

The fight therefore begins by undermining the most obvious comparison. Star is the U.S. No.1 and is visually staged as an All Might-scale symbol, but the volume will not define her by whether she can reproduce All Might's solitary image. Her effective combat body includes pilots, targeting information, military authorization structures she sometimes disobeys, and later missile/data infrastructure. That distributed form of power is not a consolation for failing to be All Might. It is what she actually has.

## 2.3 `俺って何なんだろ？` opens the identity problem before New Order explains why it matters

When Star asks whether the figure before her is AFO, the response is not a stable alias correction. The target asks, `俺って何なんだろ？` (`V34:p021-022`).

At this point, V34 does **not** support the shorthand that “AFO has simply possessed Shigaraki” if that phrase implies a clean replacement of one intact subject by another intact subject. Nor does it support treating Shigaraki as wholly autonomous. The correct entering model for the battle is **contested and transforming identity**, with exact mechanics still to be supplied by the next chapter.

# 3. No.330 `俺と僕` - New Order turns identity recognition into combat mechanics

## 3.1 New Order is powerful because it can impose rules, not because it has no limits

V34 gives New Order explicit operational boundaries (`V34:p023-034`). Star must:

1. touch a target;
2. call/name that target; and
3. impose a rule.

She can maintain two rules simultaneously. One is ordinarily spent on her own body, giving her superhuman physical capacity. Crucially, the text also says that this self-enhancement has a ceiling: even with New Order, Star cannot make herself physically equal to All Might (`V34:p031-033`).

This matters beyond power-scaling trivia. Star's self-conception contains a long-standing comparison to an ideal she cannot literally reproduce. The volume does not resolve that gap by secretly declaring her stronger. It resolves it by showing that hero capacity is not exhausted by one body's maximum output.

The air-vacuum rule and later giant-air-avatar techniques also show that New Order's apparent reality alteration remains recognition-dependent. Star must formulate and maintain a target concept. The Quirk is extraordinary, but it is not omnipotence detached from perception.

## 3.2 Living targets introduce a self/recognition constraint

Star touches the opponent, calls him `死柄木弔`, and imposes the condition that if Tomura Shigaraki moves, his heart will stop (`V34:p033-034`). The rule does not take hold as expected.

The failure is not random resistance and is not proof that New Order simply “doesn't work on strong people.” V34 connects the failure to identity mismatch.

The target explains that his boundaries have changed. He was able to say that he was Shigaraki Tomura, but the AFO/Shigaraki fusion has advanced to the point that `僕らはもうじき1つとなる`; they are `融け合い新たな人格となりつつある`, becoming a new personality neither simply AFO nor simply Shigaraki (`V34:p035-036`).

The important distinction is epistemic and ontological at once. New Order can name a living person only insofar as Star's recognition and the target's current identity condition line up sufficiently. Shigaraki's destabilized self is therefore not merely psychological flavor. It has immediate causal consequences in battle.

## 3.3 The “98%” state does not settle later separability

AFO-side narration frames fusion/completion as approximately 98 percent (`V34:p035-038`). That is strong evidence that the transition is advanced, but V34 still does not answer several later questions:

- whether the merged identity will stabilize;
- whether Shigaraki's prior self remains recoverable;
- whether AFO's original body and the Shigaraki-side consciousness share unrestricted cognition;
- whether the two minds can diverge after completion;
- how this fusion interacts with Tenko/Shigaraki's rescue-relevant interior state.

Those remain OPEN. The V34 correction is narrower: **identity is already sufficiently transformed that “Tomura Shigaraki” cannot function as a fully reliable naming predicate for New Order.**

# 4. No.331 `アメリカ` - Star's strength is explicitly relational

## 4.1 The pilots reject a biological-essentialist explanation of her limit

Star's frustration with the physical gap between herself and All Might is not hidden. She has spent years aware that her self-enhancement cannot reach his raw ceiling. Her Air Force comrades answer this without telling her the limitation is imaginary and without reducing it to “because you're a woman” (`V34:p039-046`).

Their response is relational: if one person cannot do everything, other people cover what she cannot. This is one of the cleanest V34 bridges from V33's distributed heroism into V34's network contrast. The pilots are not lesser bodies orbiting a real hero. They are part of the hero's operational capacity.

Star reciprocates that structure. She refers to the pilots in family-like terms—`お兄ちゃん達`—and makes tactical choices around their safety (`V34:p039-056`). Their relationship is neither abstract military hierarchy nor sentimental decoration. It changes the decisions available to her.

## 4.2 Institutional disobedience is bounded, not romanticized

U.S. command recognizes that Star moved before formal deployment authorization was fully settled. Yet the same institutional voice also recognizes that her independent action and prior conduct have repeatedly protected the country (`V34:p039-056`).

This adds useful behavioral nuance. Star is willing to violate procedure under a high-confidence rescue imperative, but the text does not frame all procedure as meaningless. Her pilots, military command and later missile authorization remain necessary to what she can do. She is not an anti-institutional lone wolf. She is a high-agency institutional actor whose judgment can exceed the timing of bureaucracy while still depending on institutional capability.

## 4.3 New Order externalizes imagination through recognition

Star's giant air construct and laser manipulation visually enlarge the distinction between body and effective reach (`V34:p047-056`). Her physical body does not become All Might's. Instead, New Order lets her create an enormous recognized extension of action in coordination with external fire.

This is a visual argument for the volume's broader power philosophy: **capacity can be extended without pretending that all capacity belongs inside one heroic body.**

# 5. No.332 - Tiamat and the failure of individual-supremacy readings

The hypersonic intercontinental cruise missiles known as Tiamat enter the battle as a deliberately external resource (`V34:p057-074`). Star does not “own” their destructive power in the same sense she owns New Order. She receives, redirects and integrates them into a joint operation.

The fight therefore becomes difficult to summarize meaningfully as “Star vs Shigaraki.” It is Star + pilots + U.S. command + missile infrastructure versus an AFO/Shigaraki entity using multiple accumulated Quirks, Nomu resources and anticipatory tactics.

Shigaraki/AFO survives the missile strike not simply by having a bigger durability number. The enemy side anticipates the attack, uses terrain/Nomu positioning and creates a survival route through the blast sequence (`V34:p065-074`). This preserves AFO's core strategic characterization: power is valuable, but contingency planning is what prevents a single failed assumption from ending the plan.

Star correspondingly analyzes New Order as recognition-bound. The more the target escapes a stable recognized category, the harder it is to produce a decisive rule. The battle's final approach then forces the conflict into the contact range that AFO wants: if he can touch Star and steal New Order, her most distinctive capability enters his accumulation system.

AFO also exploits the fact that Star will not casually sacrifice her pilots. This is not presented as a moral flaw to be corrected. It is a real tactical vulnerability generated by attachment. V34's hero-side network is stronger because persons matter, but exactly because persons matter, enemies can create dilemmas around them.

# 6. No.333 `亡霊` - theft becomes a channel for resistance

## 6.1 Star loses the Quirk transfer contest

At the level of immediate possession, AFO/Shigaraki succeeds: Star is touched and New Order is stolen (`V34:p075-083`). V34 does not undo this fact by declaring the theft fake.

AFO understands New Order and OFA as exceptionally valuable “crystals” of power. His accumulation logic is consistent: obtaining a capability means converting another person's singular power into a component of himself. New Order appears, for a moment, to vindicate that architecture.

## 6.2 Star's final rule denies AFO passive ownership

Star's response is the decisive reversal. Before the transfer fully resolves, she has already imposed a rule on New Order itself:

`新秩序は他の個性と反発する` — New Order will revolt against other Quirks (`V34:p082-085`).

This is not a resurrection device and not a hidden victory that leaves Star unharmed. It is an agency-preserving use of the last rule available to her. The Quirk AFO wanted as an obedient asset enters his system as an antagonistic condition.

Inside the Quirk-factor/vestige representation, Star's figure attacks the accumulated Quirks. Reflect is explicitly shown among the factors destroyed (`V34:p079-088`). Other losses are visible, but V34 does not provide a complete authoritative inventory sufficient to claim that every unknown ability has been identified.

The thematic importance is larger than the exact casualty list. AFO's system assumes that successful acquisition converts difference into ownership. Star makes the acquired difference **refuse assimilation**.

## 6.3 `亡霊` is legacy as active interference

The chapter title `亡霊` is exact. Star's residual figure becomes a “ghost” inside AFO's architecture, but the volume does not use haunting as passive memory. Her afterimage acts. It attacks the assumption that a stolen power must become a compliant extension of the thief.

That makes Star a sharp continuation of V33's ember model. Legacy is not valuable only because people remember a dead hero. Legacy can materially alter the choices and capacities of survivors.

# 7. No.334 `置き土産` - Star's death creates a reprieve without making death the ideal

## 7.1 The source treats Star's death as loss

Star disintegrates and dies (`V34:p087-094`). Her pilots survive. The United States loses its No.1 hero and the wielder of its strongest Quirk. International intervention capacity contracts because governments now have even stronger incentives to protect remaining domestic assets (`V34:p099-103`).

This matters for interpretation. V34 does not say that because Star's final action produced strategic benefit, her death was therefore “worth it” in a simple utilitarian sense. Her death closes possibilities as well as opening time. The hero network must continue despite a real missing person.

## 7.2 AFO tries to convert the problem into another disposable route

As New Order destroys Quirk factors, AFO/Shigaraki searches for a nearby person into whom he can dump the Quirk (`V34:p091-099`). The intended host is valuable precisely as an expendable externalization target. This is AFO's network logic in miniature: when an acquired component becomes dangerous to the center, transfer the danger into another body.

He fails to preserve New Order. The Quirk ultimately disappears rather than becoming a stable AFO asset.

## 7.3 `英雄の意志を継いだ誰かが` opposes inheritance to possession

Star's residual will asserts that someone who inherits a hero's will will continue the struggle (`V34:p099-101`). AFO answers from his own philosophy: whoever that inheritor is, he will wear them down.

The confrontation distinguishes two senses of inheritance:

- **possession:** acquire the power and make it part of oneself;
- **succession:** another subject independently carries forward a value, practice, warning or opportunity.

AFO is extraordinarily good at the first and repeatedly underestimates the second.

## 7.4 The pilots and combat data are part of Star's `置き土産`

The pilots preserve X-66 combat recordings and biological/Quirk-response data for analysis and transmission (`V34:p099-106`). Star's “parting gift” is therefore not only the damage New Order caused internally. It includes information produced by the fight and carried by surviving partners.

The next-day briefing makes the strategic effect explicit. Two days have passed since Midoriya returned to U.A. The heroes had expected Shigaraki to become fully operational by the following day. Star's damage has changed that: the enemy is now expected to be unable to move properly for **at least one week** (`V34:p103-106`).

This is a direct revision of the V33 countdown. Star has purchased `猶予`—a reprieve, not a victory.

Midoriya's response is appropriately bounded. He resolves to use the time and defeat Shigaraki/AFO. V34 does not yet show whether that plan succeeds or whether the estimate remains exact.

# 8. No.335 `有精卵` - growth is distributed, and AFO names his own contingency philosophy

## 8.1 The enemy inventory prevents premature “AFO is crippled” conclusions

Star's damage is major, but the hero-side briefing still treats the enemy force as formidable (`V34:p107-113`). The currently relevant structure includes:

- damaged/immobile Shigaraki/AFO-side body;
- original-body All For One;
- Dabi;
- Toga;
- approximately six remaining Near High-End Nomu;
- liberation/escaped-villain and AFO-linked remnants.

Hero manpower has also contracted severely after the war and postwar collapse. The one-week window is therefore valuable because the protagonists need preparation time, not because the enemy has become trivial.

## 8.2 Class 1-A has been training rather than waiting for the plot

The students have continued technical development during the refuge crisis (`V34:p107-117`). Bakugo is integrating `Cluster`. Shoto is working toward a new solution within his fire/ice inheritance problem. Midoriya and classmates are preparing for the fact that the next conflict cannot be won by repeating prior output.

This is where the chapter title `有精卵` becomes a callback rather than a joke. All Might once described the students as fertilized eggs: people with latent possibility who had not yet hatched into professional capacity. Hawks now sees how far that development has accelerated (`V34:p113-117`).

The callback is important because growth remains plural. There is no single replacement All Might. Multiple students are becoming more capable in different ways.

## 8.3 AFO's own network principle is redundancy through routes and spares

The chapter's enemy-side ending provides one of the clearest statements of AFO's planning philosophy (`V34:p117-121`). He tells Toya that for one objective he prepares many possible routes and backups, sometimes over years or decades, and connects whichever route remains usable to the goal.

This is strategically sophisticated. It is also morally revealing.

AFO does not merely have Plan A and Plan B. His social world is organized so that people can function as route components. The line that he has many `友達` is grotesquely compatible with V33's friendship language because his “friends” are valuable as prepared means.

V34 therefore does not oppose centralized planning to distributed planning. **Both sides distribute capability. The moral distinction is whether distribution preserves or consumes personhood.**

# 9. No.336 `敵（ヴィラン）` - Aoyama turns the network contrast inward

## 9.1 The chapter first establishes active preparation

Class 1-A's training continues across `V34:p122-128`. Bakugo's aggressive coaching remains recognizably Bakugo; technical cooperation still does not require personality flattening. Shoto, Midoriya and others continue improving. The class also identifies a strategic problem: finding AFO reactively is insufficient. They need a way to **induce him to move on terms they can prepare for**.

That strategic need is important because the Aoyama reveal will immediately become more than a retrospective moral crisis. It will create a possible information channel back toward AFO.

## 9.2 Hagakure is the observer who notices the anomaly

Hagakure follows Aoyama because his behavior does not fit the current class baseline (`V34:p128-132`). This is a meaningful character-model addition. She is not merely present for the reveal; she independently notices social irregularity, investigates, and later intervenes physically.

Her low-information/invisibility-centered characterization gains three distinct pieces in this volume:

- social attention;
- moral anger when betrayal becomes clear;
- tactical use of light refraction under danger.

That is enough to strengthen an emerging model substantially, but not enough by itself to support a broad `moderate` reconstruction tier.

## 9.3 Aoyama's parents did not seek villainy; they sought normalization for a suffering child

The family history is precise enough to reject simplistic motive labels (`V34:p132-140`). Aoyama was born Quirkless. His parents saw his distress at being different in a Quirk-dominant childhood environment and wanted him to live like other children. Through a dangerous hidden route, they reached AFO, who gave Yuga a Quirk.

The parallel to Midoriya is immediate but not identical. Both were Quirkless children in a social world where that status produced exclusion and pain. Midoriya encountered All Might, who transferred power under a mentorship relation. Aoyama's parents encountered AFO, who converted the transfer into a dependency chain.

This is one of V34's strongest power-philosophy contrasts: **the same surface act—giving power to a powerless child—can have radically different moral content depending on agency, consent, future claims and the giver's relationship to the recipient.**

## 9.4 AFO turns beneficence into debt and debt into coercion

AFO does not need the Aoyama family to love him. The granted Quirk creates a relation he can later reactivate through fear. When demands arrive, the parents understand that failure may mean death. During AFO's imprisonment they briefly believe the coercive relation has ended and are able to experience U.A. life as ordinary happiness. His return restores the trap (`V34:p132-140`).

This does not remove Aoyama's agency. It defines the conditions under which that agency operated.

Aoyama explicitly admits that he provided the routes for both the U.S.J. attack and the forest training-camp attack: `USJも 合宿も 僕が手引きした` (`V34:p139-140`). The verb `手引き` matters. He is not saying only that he knew what his parents did or that AFO independently discovered the locations. He acknowledges his own participation in enabling access.

At the same time, his visible history contains resistant/prosocial acts that do not fit an enthusiastic-traitor model. V34 does not retroactively erase them as meaningless camouflage. It asks how contradictory behavior emerges when someone is acting under a lethal coercive relationship.

# 10. No.337 `使い捨ての人生を` - coercion changes culpability without deleting facts

## 10.1 Hagakure's anger gives betrayed peers moral standing

Midoriya intercepts the family, but Hagakure's response is essential (`V34:p141-150`). She demands to know how Aoyama could sit in the same classroom and live in the dorms with classmates after enabling attacks against them.

Her anger prevents the scene from becoming a purely dyadic Midoriya/Aoyama rescue conversation. People harmed by Aoyama's conduct are allowed to experience betrayal as betrayal. A rigorous account of coercion cannot require those peers to skip that recognition.

Hagakure also refracts Aoyama's laser and becomes visibly legible in the process (`V34:p147-149`). The moment is mechanically useful and visually symbolic: the previously invisible classmate becomes visible while exposing the hidden classmate. The visual device should not be over-allegorized, but the coincidence is too structured to ignore.

## 10.2 Aoyama's fear is not post-hoc invention

Aoyama describes his parents as loving him and trying to make him happy, but says the difference between himself and others became terrifying and the AFO relationship became unbearable (`V34:p143-150`). He did not want his parents killed. His compliance was therefore shaped by an explicit threat regime rather than ordinary self-interest.

This supports **coercive mitigation**, not innocence.

The correct claim is:

> Aoyama knowingly performed harmful enabling acts under a dependency and lethal-threat structure created by AFO.

The incorrect extremes are:

> Aoyama was really a willing villain all along.

and

> Aoyama did nothing wrong because AFO forced him.

V34 itself refuses both.

## 10.3 Authorities preserve the distinction between coercion and exculpation

Once the Aoyama family is taken into custody, Tsukauchi/police reasoning remains security-sensitive (`V34:p151-158`). AFO has used lethal implanted contingencies in other people, so the family must be checked. Current evidence does not indicate that Aoyama's granted Quirk carries the same explosive mechanism, but absence of that mechanism does not mean the testimony can simply be accepted without verification.

The adults also preserve the legal/moral fact that the acts do not vanish because the family was threatened. This is exactly the accountability layer the analysis needs to retain.

## 10.4 Midoriya refuses identity totalization

Midoriya's intervention is not “you are innocent.” He explicitly recognizes that AFO exploited Aoyama's heart and that Aoyama despaired at himself. He also recognizes the common Quirkless-child origin (`V34:p153-158`).

The key move is future-facing. Midoriya argues that the known wrongdoing does not exhaust the person's remaining agency. He extends his hand and tells Aoyama that he can still become a hero.

That sentence should not be flattened into absolution. In context, becoming a hero now means **doing something with the remaining choice**—specifically, helping create a route to deceive AFO—while custody, investigation and past facts remain in place.

V34 therefore gives a strong operational definition of restorative possibility:

> personhood can remain larger than the worst coerced act without requiring the act to be renamed harmless.# 11. No.338 `皆がヒーローになるまでの物語①` - friendship does not erase crime; it changes what to do next

## 11.1 Aoyama remains a security problem even after disclosure

The authorities initially restrict Aoyama's contact and speech while checks are performed (`V34:p159-162`). This is analytically important because the class's relational response does not control the entire institutional response. AFO is a deception specialist; Aoyama has already functioned as an intelligence channel; and the family has been under coercive contact. Verification remains reasonable.

Tsukauchi's position is not emotionally generous, but it protects an essential distinction: acknowledging coercion does not require the state to treat every subsequent claim as automatically trustworthy. The story can preserve compassion and adversarial security at the same time.

## 11.2 Midoriya converts the liability into a bounded strategic affordance

Midoriya identifies the same fact from the opposite direction: if Aoyama has been AFO's coerced channel, he may now be uniquely positioned to deceive AFO (`V34:p160-164`).

This is not a magical redemption mechanism. It is a proposal to use the relationship AFO created against him. The strategic value emerges specifically because AFO believes he owns the channel.

That creates a structural rhyme with Star. AFO tries to acquire New Order and finds that the acquired channel has been weaponized against his stockpile. He coerces Aoyama into becoming an information route and now risks having the route reverse direction.

In both cases, the weakness in AFO's model is not that he lacks contingency. It is that he tends to treat incorporated agents as if ownership will permanently settle their alignment.

## 11.3 Iida defines friendship as present-tense intervention, not retrospective denial

Aoyama asks whether classmates can truly trust him now. Iida's answer preserves the volume's accountability logic (`V34:p163-166`). He does not say the U.S.J. and forest-camp facts are irrelevant. He instead argues that when a classmate is crying in despair in the present, friendship gives them a reason to reach for his hand.

The direction of the argument matters:

- friendship is not proof that the betrayal never happened;
- friendship is not a guarantee that future betrayal is impossible;
- friendship is a reason not to make despair and exclusion the only available future state.

This continues Iida's V33 reciprocal-rescue model. Rescue jurisdiction extends to a classmate whose conduct has created legitimate reasons for anger and caution.

## 11.4 Kirishima rejects permanent moral quarantine

Kirishima responds in his characteristic high-intensity register. He refuses to treat Aoyama as permanently outside the class and links this to the class's recent intervention toward Midoriya (`V34:p163-168`).

The comparison must be bounded. Midoriya's V32 self-isolation and Aoyama's coerced betrayals are not morally identical. Kirishima is not saying they are. The shared structure is that hidden suffering can produce behavior the group cannot correct if it treats disclosure as the endpoint of relationship.

His role is therefore less juridical than relational: after truth, what kind of person does the group permit the disclosed person to become?

## 11.5 Bakugo's participation preserves post-apology register continuity

Bakugo supports the class's forward movement without shifting into gentle therapeutic speech (`V34:p164-168`). His abrasive, violent-sounding competitive language continues to function alongside willingness to cooperate with Aoyama's possible role.

This is a useful held-out validation for the V33 model. Bakugo's apology did not predict permanent softness. V34 immediately confirms that accountability can change relational policy while leaving core speech temperature intact.

# 12. Aizawa - no expulsion now does not mean no accountability

Aizawa appears remotely from the hospital and says that, as homeroom teacher, he currently has **no intention of expelling Aoyama** (`V34:p167-170`). He is visibly emotionally affected, but his decision is not presented as emotion replacing procedure.

Three layers coexist:

1. **relational:** Aoyama remains one of his students;
2. **accountability/security:** the family remains subject to custody, questioning and checks;
3. **operational:** Aizawa wants a concrete plan that can convert the disclosure into advantage against AFO.

This is consistent with Aizawa's broader decision style. He can use harsh exclusion language when he believes it produces growth, but in a genuine crisis he often distinguishes whether removal will improve the student's capacity or merely discard a problem. Here, expulsion would accomplish little besides ratifying AFO's reduction of Aoyama to a used-up tool.

The source does not establish what long-term legal or school consequences will follow. Those remain OPEN.

# 13. No.339 `皆がヒーローになるまでの物語②` - the Support Course makes distributed heroism materially literal

## 13.1 `p175` is publication paratext, not story evidence

Before the visible chapter opening at `V34:p176`, the volume inserts a `超絶裏話` note stating that the Aoyama matter had originally been planned to become clear around the forest-training-camp period and was substantially changed in publication planning (`V34:p175`).

This is useful evidence about authorial production history. It is **not** evidence that characters possessed earlier knowledge, that earlier ambiguity must be read as deliberate current-version foreshadowing in every panel, or that the in-universe timeline differs from the published sequence.

It should remain in a paratext register rather than contaminate character epistemics.

## 13.2 Hatsume is operating inside wartime logistics, not ordinary invention fantasy

Midoriya and Iida visit the Support Course for equipment work (`V34:p176-184`). Hatsume is intensely occupied with U.A. barrier/refuge systems. Her apparent ignorance of some outside developments is not apathy; she has been absorbed in support labor.

The source adds a significant constraint absent from earlier school-festival/internship-style equipment scenes: overseas supply chains and component availability are disrupted. Hatsume cannot simply reproduce every requested foreign-spec part on demand (`V34:p178-182`). She adapts with available material and rapid prototyping.

This strengthens her character model beyond eccentric invention enthusiasm. She is now observed under infrastructure pressure, scarcity and urgent user need.

## 13.3 `発明がサポート科のヒーロー活動なんです`

Hatsume gives V34 one of its clearest role-pluralization statements:

`発明がサポート科のヒーロー活動なんです` — invention is the Support Course's hero activity (`V34:p183-184`).

She immediately distinguishes role from function. Support students do not fight the way Hero Course students do, but their work is `同じ` in the relevant sense: it sustains rescue and protection.

This is not metaphorical praise detached from material contribution. The surrounding pages show:

- U.A. barrier development and maintenance;
- equipment repair/replacement;
- prototype fabrication under disrupted supply;
- Power Loader's supervision;
- upgrades that allow Midoriya and other students to return to field operations.

V33's claim that civilians/peers can participate in sustaining hero work therefore becomes institutional labor in V34. Distributed heroism now has workshops, shortages, prototypes and maintenance schedules.

## 13.4 Hatsume reaches `moderate` model readiness

Across V04, V12, V19 and now V34, Hatsume has repeated evidence in:

- public/promotion contexts;
- iterative user-centered design;
- collaborative technical explanation;
- failure/constraint response;
- institutional support work;
- crisis scarcity;
- stable high-energy technical register.

Relationship breadth remains limited, so `strong` would overstate the model. But V34 supplies enough context diversity to move her from `emerging (strengthened)` to **`moderate`**.

# 14. The volume closes on preparation, not the decisive battle itself

The final movement shows upgraded equipment, renewed search/deployment teams, civilian gratitude, and coordinated information gathering (`V34:p184-190`). Midoriya trusts that Aoyama will return. Search teams combine Hero Course students and professionals. The strategic direction is now toward a **second decisive battle**, but V34 does not reveal the full plan or execute it.

The final-page language is precise: necessary items for the next strategy have been assembled and the protagonists are moving toward the second decisive battle (`V34:p190`).

Therefore the V34 prospective endpoint must not import:

- the final lure mechanics;
- deployment locations;
- who fights whom;
- whether Aoyama successfully deceives AFO;
- whether Shigaraki remains immobile for the full predicted week;
- any later separation/fusion result;
- any battle outcome.

Those are V35+ evidence.

# 15. Star and Stripe - a bounded character reconstruction

V34 is unusually efficient at turning Star from a narrow endpoint cameo into a reconstructable secondary character.

## 15.1 Desire hierarchy

Her dominant visible priorities are:

1. intervene when All Might/Japan is in immediate danger;
2. protect comrades while completing the mission;
3. stop AFO/Shigaraki even at enormous personal cost;
4. live up to an All Might-derived hero ideal without denying her own limits.

The order is inferred from repeated decisions, not from a complete autobiographical monologue.

## 15.2 Self-conception and insecurity

Star is confident, theatrical and willing to command, but she carries a specific comparative insecurity: New Order cannot make her body equal All Might's (`V34:p031-046`). She does not generalize this into global inadequacy. The pilots' long-term response helps stabilize a relational self-conception in which limitation can be covered by comrades.

## 15.3 Decision heuristics

Repeated V34 evidence supports:

- high willingness to act before bureaucratic timing is settled when rescue urgency is high;
- rapid rule experimentation rather than rigid technique repetition;
- protection of named comrades as a binding constraint;
- willingness to use lethal force against an existential target;
- when loss is unavoidable, preference for converting the loss into downstream strategic value rather than purely self-preserving retreat.

## 15.4 Relationship-specific behavior

With pilots, her register is commanding but familial; she uses `お兄ちゃん達` and trusts them with high-risk synchronized action. With the AFO/Shigaraki target, she is declarative, adversarial and willing to impose lethal rules. With All Might as mentor-symbol, she is emulative but not merely imitative.

## 15.5 Readiness

V33's `insufficient/emerging` was appropriate because only one mentor-conditioned deployment decision existed. V34 adds:

- combat improvisation;
- institutional conflict;
- teammate relationships;
- self-comparison/insecurity;
- tactical hesitation;
- sacrifice/loss behavior;
- command register;
- Quirk philosophy;
- downstream consequence.

That supports **`moderate (strongly grounded)`**. Ordinary low-stakes behavior, family/private life and repeated noncombat relationships remain too thin for `strong`.

# 16. Aoyama Yuga - from stylistic eccentricity to a causal model

Aoyama's prior corpus already contained useful behavioral breadth: conspicuous self-presentation, equality/belonging anxiety, bodily mismatch with his Quirk, projection-based care, odd Midoriya-directed outreach, battlefield contribution and ordinary class presence. V34 supplies the missing causal history that had been explicitly listed as a model gap.

## 16.1 Desire hierarchy

At the childhood/family level:

1. belong / not be excluded as Quirkless;
2. preserve parents and family;
3. participate genuinely in U.A./hero aspiration;
4. avoid AFO's punishment;
5. under crisis, seek some way to resist without causing parents' deaths.

These desires conflict rather than forming a clean stable preference ordering.

## 16.2 Stress response

Under AFO pressure, Aoyama becomes secretive, frightened and compliant enough to transmit dangerous information. Under peer discovery, he collapses into shame and self-totalizing language. When offered a bounded future route, he does not instantly become confident; he questions whether trust is still possible.

That sequence is better reconstruction evidence than a simple trait label like “cowardly” or “kind.” It shows **coercion-sensitive compliance plus persistent prosocial aspiration**.

## 16.3 Self-conception

Aoyama's self-judgment becomes harsher than the class's. He interprets his own history as proof that he is fundamentally on the enemy side / morally worthless. Midoriya's intervention therefore targets not factual memory but identity totalization.

The character-modeling implication is important: when shame is activated by exposure, Aoyama is likely to over-compress multiple states—coercion, fear, wrongdoing, care—into one condemning identity statement.

## 16.4 Speech/register

His ornamental, stylized surface speech in ordinary contexts should not be discarded now that the cause of some anxiety is known. V34 reveals that flamboyant self-presentation coexisted with severe concealed fear. A reconstruction that makes post-reveal Aoyama permanently solemn would be as inaccurate as one that ignores the coercion history.

## 16.5 Readiness

The prior readiness index listed Aoyama at `moderate` with missing causal history, broader relationship evidence and later stress/failure decisions. V34 directly fills all three categories:

- causal childhood/family/AFO history;
- parents, Midoriya, Hagakure, Class 1-A, authorities and Aizawa relations;
- exposure, pursuit, custody, shame and proposed repair under extreme stress.

Aoyama therefore advances **`moderate -> strong`**.

`specialist_ready` would still be premature. The model remains comparatively thin on autonomous adulthood/professional identity, sustained post-disclosure ordinary behavior and repeated future decisions after the immediate crisis.

# 17. Hagakure Toru - visibility, attention and moral anger

V34 gives Hagakure her strongest concentrated evidence block so far.

- She notices Aoyama's anomaly and investigates without being instructed.
- She witnesses the confession and immediately frames the harm from the classmates' perspective.
- She physically intervenes by refracting Aoyama's laser.
- Her face becomes visible under the light interaction.
- She later remains present in the class's collective processing rather than being reduced to the reveal device.

The important modeling distinction is that her anger is **relationally specific and harm-aware**. It does not prove she is generally unforgiving. Nor does the class's later forward movement erase her initial betrayal response.

The readiness index should add/clarify Hagakure at **`emerging (strongly grounded)`** rather than promote her to moderate. V34 supplies crisis and moral-conflict breadth, but independent ordinary goals, broader relationships and repeated longitudinal decisions remain sparse.

# 18. All For One - contingency without reciprocity

V34 materially strengthens the current AFO model without justifying a readiness promotion beyond `moderate` yet.

## 18.1 Acquisition heuristic

When AFO encounters a powerful difference, his default question is whether it can be possessed. New Order is evaluated as a Quirk to steal; Star's death is acceptable if the capability survives as his property.

## 18.2 Social heuristic

AFO creates relationships that are difficult to exit:

- gifted Quirks create debt/dependence;
- fear creates compliance;
- hidden contingencies punish defection;
- identity fusion can transform a successor into a vehicle;
- multiple subordinate routes ensure no one piece must be trusted as an autonomous partner.

## 18.3 Planning heuristic

His chapter-335 explanation establishes **contingency multiplication** as a stable decision rule. For one objective, build many routes, cultivate them long before need, and connect whichever remains viable to the goal.

This is not mere improvisational cleverness. It is an architecture.

## 18.4 Blind spot

V34 repeatedly attacks the assumption that controlled channels remain inertly controlled. New Order revolts after theft. Aoyama can become a deception channel. Star's pilots carry information after her death. Heroic will can propagate outside the owned power itself.

The blind spot should therefore be phrased carefully:

> AFO understands contingency among **assets** exceptionally well but repeatedly discounts agency that re-emerges when an “asset” still functions as a person or relationship node.

This is stronger than saying “AFO cannot understand friendship,” which is too broad and psychologically vague.

# 19. Shigaraki/AFO identity - new-personality evidence without premature finality

V34 provides the strongest mechanical identity evidence since V31 but still demands terminological caution.

At the battle boundary:

- Shigaraki's prior body and memories remain relevant;
- AFO's consciousness/jurisdiction is active;
- the source explicitly describes melting/fusion;
- the emerging subject is said to be neither simply AFO nor simply Shigaraki;
- New Order's name rule fails because `死柄木弔` no longer maps cleanly to the living target.

For current ledgers, preferred language is:

**AFO/Shigaraki merged or emerging fused personality**, with the exact durability of each prior identity unresolved.

Avoid:

- “AFO completely controls Shigaraki” as a total statement;
- “Shigaraki is gone”;
- “they are already one perfectly stable person”;
- “the fusion is only metaphorical.”

All go beyond V34.

# 20. Hero-society revision: role pluralism becomes infrastructure

V33 revised civilians from passive `客` toward bounded co-production of public safety. V34 gives that model organizational depth.

## 20.1 Military pilots

Star's pilots are not licensed Japanese heroes, but their flight, targeting, communication and data preservation directly affect the hero operation.

## 20.2 National institutions

U.S. command can constrain or enable intervention; domestic instability changes whether a nation will release its strongest hero asset. International heroism is therefore politically/institutionally bounded rather than free-floating moral will.

## 20.3 Support Course

Hatsume/Power Loader/U.A. engineering convert shelter and equipment into maintained systems. Hero action depends on technical labor invisible from the battlefield.

## 20.4 Police/security

Tsukauchi's skepticism around Aoyama is also part of rescue infrastructure. A society that values rehabilitation still needs adversarial verification against AFO.

## 20.5 Students

Class 1-A's role is no longer “children waiting for professionals.” Their accumulated skills, relationships and new techniques are part of the force structure.

The result is not role collapse. Pilots do not become Hero Course students; Support Course students do not become front-line fighters; police do not become therapists. The commonality lies in **contributing distinct professional/social functions to a shared protection system**.

# 21. Recognition / failed-rescue ledger implications

## 21.1 Star recognizes her own limitation without accepting disposability

The All Might comparison could have become a failed-recognition trap in which Star treats inability to equal him as proof she must compensate by dying. V34 does not support that simplistic reading. She operates for years through pilots and institutions, acknowledging relational supplementation before the fatal battle.

Her final action occurs after lethal contact has already made survival extraordinarily constrained. It is better classified as **agency preserved within forced loss** than as an initial preference for self-sacrifice.

## 21.2 AFO weaponizes failed recognition around Quirklessness

The Aoyama family's original vulnerability emerges from a social environment in which a Quirkless child's difference is painful enough that parents seek an illicit miracle. AFO recognizes the desire accurately and exploits it.

Recognition itself is therefore morally neutral. AFO is often perceptive. The ethical question is what the perceiver does with the vulnerability recognized.

## 21.3 Midoriya recognizes wrongdoing and rescue eligibility simultaneously

Midoriya's strongest V34 rescue judgment is not “Aoyama is actually good.” It is the ability to hold:

- the betrayal facts;
- coercive context;
- shared Quirkless origin;
- prior prosocial acts;
- present despair;
- future strategic agency

without forcing them into one total identity.

This is an advanced version of the recognition ethic developed around villains and harmed people earlier in the corpus. Recognition must be specific enough to avoid both demonization and sentimental erasure.

# 22. Power philosophy: giving power versus owning the empowered person

V34 creates a deliberate contrast among All Might/OFA, AFO's gifted Quirk to Aoyama, New Order and Support Course invention.

## 22.1 Power transfer can be gift, chain, theft or tool

- All Might gives OFA to Midoriya within a mentorship relation that increasingly has to learn reciprocity.
- AFO gives Aoyama a Quirk, then converts the gift into an enduring claim over the recipient/family.
- AFO steals New Order, attempting to turn another person's capacity into property.
- Hatsume gives/prototypes equipment that remains oriented toward the user's autonomous mission rather than toward owning the user.

The material object “power” is therefore insufficient to classify the moral relationship.

## 22.2 New Order's failure exposes the limit of sovereign naming

Star's Quirk can impose rules, but living personhood introduces a limit: naming must map onto the actual target's identity condition. This makes New Order a striking power-philosophy object. It looks sovereign—speak a rule and reality obeys—but even its sovereignty is mediated by recognition.

AFO's entire project seeks to overcome such relational limits by accumulating more capacities inside one center. V34 repeatedly demonstrates why that still does not produce total control.

## 22.3 Support technology is power without heroic possession

Hatsume's `発明` moves power outward. A piece of equipment increases Midoriya's or another hero's capacity while leaving the inventor and user distinct. That is the opposite architecture from Quirk theft.
