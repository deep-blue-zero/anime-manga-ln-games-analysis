# 1. Governing transition: distributed competence and the rescuer as a rescue subject

Volume 21 established **plural adjudication / legitimacy as situated trust**. People and institutions did not share one universal court: Endeavor's public victory did not compel family forgiveness; Hawks had to sustain incompatible credibility regimes; Shinso's professional worth had to be demonstrated under constraints rather than granted by sympathy.

Volume 22 asks what happens after legitimacy has to be demonstrated in practice. Its answer is strikingly anti-solitary.

The governing transition is:

> **plural adjudication / legitimacy as situated trust -> distributed competence / the rescuer as a legitimate rescue subject**

Compact formulation:

> **Capability is neither solitary nor fully self-authored. Good teams preserve one another's options after local defeat; inherited techniques and powers arrive through other people; mature victory integrates rescue into tempo and aggression; and when a hero's own power becomes dangerous, peers must be able to support, restrain, or rescue the person normally cast as rescuer.**

This transition unifies what otherwise looks like a tournament volume plus an unrelated OFA cliffhanger.

- Yaoyorozu loses the formal Match 2 result, but her planning continues to shape the battle after capture.
- Iida's speed upgrade is literally inherited professional-family knowledge and bodily discipline.
- Todoroki can appropriate Endeavor-taught technique without granting Endeavor moral reconciliation.
- Bakugo's strongest performance is not solitary domination but an aggressive team system in which he both rescues and expects rescue.
- Monoma's philosophy makes every participant protagonist of one life and supporting actor in another, undermining protagonist-centered measures of relevance.
- Midoriya's inherited power exceeds his conscious authorship and becomes an environmental danger.
- Uraraka's rescue ethic explicitly expands from saving civilians to asking who protects heroes when heroes suffer.
- Shinso's Brainwashing, once socially coded as villainous, becomes the mechanism that safely interrupts Midoriya's runaway power.

V22 therefore makes mutual regulation a heroic competency. The strongest person in a scene may still require intervention. The person doing the intervening may possess the apparently coercive power. The person who loses a match may still have demonstrated superior planning. The person who leads may need teammates empowered to save them.

# 2. No.201 — Match 2: scoreboard truth and process truth

## 2.1 Class B wins 4-0; V22 does not erase that result

The first obligation of the volume is to resolve the V21 lock honestly. Class B wins Match 2 **4-0** (`V22:p019`). Yaoyorozu's team does not receive a retroactive moral or tactical victory simply because the narrative respects her planning.

That distinction matters because MHA increasingly refuses to use outcome as a totalizing measure of personhood.

The scoreboard answers one question:

`Who satisfied the match's capture conditions?`

It does not automatically answer:

- who made the best individual decision under pressure;
- whose actions preserved teammates' options longest;
- who improved most relative to prior weakness;
- whose model of the opponent was most accurate;
- who would perform better under a different objective function.

The formal defeat must therefore remain intact while the character inference becomes richer.

## 2.2 Yaoyorozu's action persists after Yaoyorozu is removed

Kendo's plan succeeds at its first priority: deny Momo time and isolate her from the rest of Class A. But the chapter's title, `先を見据えて`, is paid off by the fact that Yaoyorozu does not plan only for the duel immediately in front of her.

Her creations are selected to continue producing value after she is captured. Equipment and information reach teammates who must now solve the rest of the field without her direct command.

This is a meaningful maturation of the V08 Yaoyorozu model.

Earlier confidence trouble often made her tactical cognition appear dependent on obtaining enough internal certainty before acting. V22 shows a more robust policy:

1. accept that local defeat is possible;
2. model what teammates will need after that defeat;
3. convert remaining time/material into future options for them;
4. allow the plan to outlive the planner.

That is not merely intelligence. It is **distributed leadership**.

## 2.3 Kendo's `勝った気しないな` is opponent recognition, not score denial

At `V22:p019`, Kendo evaluates Momo after the 4-0 win:

> `創造するもの 全部 先を見据えてて`
>
> `勝った気しないな`

The line should not be mistranslated analytically into “Momo really won.” Kendo knows Class B won. What she denies is the *felt completeness* of the victory.

That gives Kendo unusually useful post-success evidence. She does not need to preserve self-esteem by flattening a defeated opponent. She can hold two propositions simultaneously:

- `we won decisively under the rules`;
- `the opponent's planning quality was sufficiently strong that I do not experience this as clean superiority`.

This is good calibration evidence for Kendo and a strong confidence-stability result for Momo.

## 2.4 Recognition can survive competitive hostility

V21 established that Kendo's respect appeared as suppression: because Momo was dangerous, Kendo denied her thinking time. V22 completes that relationship logic. After winning, Kendo can verbalize the exact competence she had been trying to suppress.

The sequence is therefore:

`accurate respect -> targeted denial -> formal victory -> continued respect`.

This is valuable for modeling competitive relationships because praise is not the only sign of esteem. Sometimes the strongest evidence that one character respects another is the amount of tactical architecture devoted to preventing that person's preferred game state.

# 3. No.202-203 — Match 3 and the value of flexibility

## 3.1 Class A's experience does not collapse Class B into lesser copies

Match 3 begins with Todoroki, Iida, Ojiro and Shoji against Tetsutetsu, Honenuki Juzo, Pony Tsunotori and Kaibara Sen.

The encounter continues V21's correction of protagonist-class bias. Class B does not exist merely to reveal Class A's new moves. Its members have developed around different affordances and team structures.

Honenuki is the clearest early example. `柔軟` in the chapter title describes more than Softening's material effect. He reads changing terrain and objectives rapidly, changes target priority, and converts his Quirk between mobility denial, environmental restructuring and rescue/capture support.

That gives him a strong first substantial model:

- low attachment to a single opening plan;
- high willingness to alter terrain rather than chase direct damage;
- attention to the whole team's evolving state;
- calm verbal/tactical presentation under pressure.

The match therefore tests **adaptation rate**, not only raw output.

## 3.2 Tetsutetsu is a pressure test with a personality attached

Tetsutetsu's value is similarly easy to reduce to “Kirishima-like steel guy.” V22 gives more specific evidence.

His battle policy is deliberately direct: close distance, accept bodily cost, keep pressure on a superior-output opponent, and make durability itself a strategic resource. Against Todoroki he treats the obvious elemental disadvantage as a challenge to be physically endured rather than a reason to disengage.

At `V22:p060`, his claim that Half-Cold Half-Hot is not working on him is partly tactical intimidation and partly an accurate report of what his trained steel body can currently tolerate.

The important character distinction from Kirishima is not simply Quirk material. Tetsutetsu's register is more openly pressure-seeking and contest-oriented. His self-conception is strengthened by finding the boundary and forcing himself through it.

## 3.3 Todoroki is forced away from comfortable dominance

Todoroki's normal battlefield advantage is immediate environmental control: broad ice can deny movement before opponents establish their preferred range. Honenuki and Tetsutetsu destabilize that assumption.

Softened terrain undermines fixed footing and large surfaces; Tetsutetsu can enter the close-range zone that many opponents cannot survive. Todoroki is therefore pushed into the part of his power history he has the most complicated relationship with: escalating fire.

This sets up one of the volume's most important distinctions between **technical inheritance** and **moral reconciliation**.

# 4. No.204 — Iida's tuning: inheritance as craft rather than destiny

## 4.1 Recipro Turbo is family knowledge embodied through pain

Chapter 204 `チューニング` temporarily moves from tournament spectacle to Iida-family technical inheritance.

Tensei congratulates Tenya on his provisional license and explains an Ingenium-family method for changing Engine performance. The method is not a magical upgrade and not a metaphorical pep talk. It requires pulling out the existing mufflers and training through the replacement process so that new mufflers grow capable of bearing greater load (`V22:p047-048`).

The narration is explicit that this comes with:

> `激痛と忍耐`

The result is Recipro Turbo: increased horsepower with minimized fuel expenditure, but with a bounded operating duration:

> `10分だ!!`

This is an unusually concrete example of inherited professional knowledge.

Tenya is not merely wearing his brother's name. He is receiving a family craft tradition, deciding whether he wants greater speed, accepting the bodily cost, and then integrating that knowledge into his own field method.

## 4.2 `おまえがもっと走りたいと思うなら` preserves agency

Tensei's condition matters:

> `おまえがもっと走りたいと思うなら——`

The inheritance is offered conditionally on Tenya's desire rather than imposed as proof that he deserves the Ingenium lineage.

This strengthens the post-Hosu Iida model. Inheritance is healthiest when it supplies resources without replacing authorship.

Compare several inheritance structures now visible in the series:

- Iida receives family technique and chooses to embody it;
- Todoroki receives Endeavor's technical teaching while retaining the right to reject Endeavor's moral authority;
- Midoriya receives OFA and increasingly discovers properties he did not knowingly choose.

V22 is quietly constructing a spectrum from **chosen craft inheritance** to **partially non-consensual informational/power inheritance**.

## 4.3 Speed becomes rescue bandwidth

Tenya's higher speed is not meaningful simply because he can move faster. Its heroic value is the number of problems he can reach and re-prioritize inside the ten-minute window.

The match uses Recipro Turbo for interception, transport and rescue/capture repositioning. The upgrade therefore follows the project's power-philosophy rule:

`capability -> affordance -> responsibility`.

More speed creates more reachable obligations.

# 5. No.205 — Todoroki: appropriating technique without surrendering judgment

## 5.1 Tetsutetsu forces the fire question

As Tetsutetsu stays inside Todoroki's preferred exclusion zone, Shoto cannot solve the match through moderate output. The physical contest pushes him toward the higher heat Endeavor has insisted he can produce.

The manga then cross-cuts current pressure with two memories that must be held together.

One is Rei's earlier refusal to let Shoto define himself solely through his father. The other is Endeavor's technical recognition:

> `自覚しろ おまえは誰よりも強い力を秘めていると!!`

At `V22:p061`, Todoroki answers the returning memory with frustrated recognition rather than filial warmth.

## 5.2 Useful knowledge does not cleanse the source relationship

This scene is easy to misread as “Shoto finally accepts Endeavor.” That would contradict the volume sequence and flatten the family's moral architecture.

The better model is:

> **A person can appropriate useful knowledge from someone whose past authority over them was abusive without thereby validating the abuse or granting reconciliation.**

Todoroki's power is biologically and technically entangled with Endeavor. Ethical individuation cannot mean pretending the entanglement never existed. It means deciding what to do with what has already been inherited.

That makes this scene a sophisticated form of self-authorship:

`this capacity came through him` does not imply `therefore he owns my use of it`.

## 5.3 The body remains a limiter on symbolic decisions

Todoroki's willingness to use greater fire does not erase heat, concentration or situational cost. Likewise, Tetsutetsu's durability is not infinite. Match 3 is built around bodies that can be trained beyond previous limits but not abstracted away.

That continuity matters for MHA's power ethics: moral conviction does not suspend physiology.

# 6. No.206 — Match 3 ends in a draw

Match 3 resolves as a **draw** (`V22:p086`).

That result is structurally appropriate. Neither side's development is reduced to a hierarchical answer.

- Iida demonstrates a major mobility upgrade and rescue-oriented use of inherited craft.
- Todoroki accesses greater heat under pressure but does not receive a clean triumph that would make the Endeavor-memory sequence a reward token.
- Tetsutetsu proves that trained durability can force a high-output opponent out of comfortable patterns.
- Honenuki demonstrates flexible environmental control and late-state problem solving.
- Ojiro, Shoji, Pony and Kaibara contribute to a match where capture, rescue, terrain and time interact rather than collapsing into a duel.

The draw reinforces the same epistemic lesson as Match 2: **outcome is evidence, not total explanation**.

A training system is doing useful work when a draw can expose more about capability than a clean win would.

# 7. No.207-208 — Bakugo's aggression becomes a cooperative operating system

## 7.1 The surface has not softened

Match 4 is one of the strongest tests in the reread of whether Bakugo's growth has been accidentally equated with becoming nicer.

It has not.

At `V22:p099`, Sato and Jiro explicitly note the continuity:

- he still tells people to follow him;
- his register remains commanding and abrasive;
- he still expects aggressive forward tempo.

Jiro's qualification is equally important: despite the surface, he **does cooperate**.

Bakugo then gives Jiro a clear sensory role, tells the others how to support the advance, and treats teammate information as operationally valuable.

The character change is therefore not:

`hostile -> polite`.

It is:

`self-centered victory architecture -> mutual-support victory architecture while retaining hostile/competitive surface style`.

## 7.2 `勝って救ける` becomes observable behavior

Midoriya recognizes the change during the match:

> `勝って救ける`

That phrase matters because it is no longer aspirational synthesis spoken by adults about Bakugo and Midoriya. Bakugo operationalizes it.

When a teammate is threatened, he interrupts his own line to rescue. The action is not framed as altruistic self-erasure. He rescues because keeping the team intact preserves the route to complete victory.

This is precisely why the growth is convincing for him: the new prosocial behavior is integrated into his existing value architecture instead of replacing it with somebody else's personality.

He tells Midoriya, through action and then assertion:

> `俺はもっと強くなってんぞ`

The “more” is not merely blast output. It is a broader definition of what victory requires.

## 7.3 Mutual rescue is built into the plan

Bakugo also expects teammates to rescue/support him if the tactical position reverses. That is a major help-receiving development.

Earlier Bakugo often experienced dependence as humiliation, threat or evidence that somebody had misread his capability. Match 4 turns mutual rescue into architecture established in advance.

That makes help less identity-threatening because it is no longer interpreted as a verdict of weakness. It is part of the system that produces the desired outcome.

This is useful character-model evidence beyond the match itself:

> **Bakugo accepts interdependence most readily when it is framed as competence-enhancing reciprocity rather than pity or unilateral caretaking.**

## 7.4 The 4-0 result is specifically team-shaped

Class A wins Match 4 **4-0 without injury/capture loss** (`V22:p109-111`).

The observers' reaction centers the completeness of the team rather than a single spectacular finishing move. Bakugo himself describes the result as what complete victory by genuinely strong people looks like.

The causal structure includes:

- Jiro's sensing;
- Sero's mobility/capture assistance;
- Sato's physical support;
- Bakugo's tempo, damage and rapid teammate rescue;
- a standing expectation of reciprocal intervention.

Bakugo leads, but leadership no longer means making everyone else irrelevant.

# 8. Class B Match 4: creative capability can lose to tempo

Tokage Setsuna, Awase, Bondo and Kamakiri give Class B a tactically creative setup. Tokage's split-body scouting/feint affordances and the team's capture mechanisms are not incompetent.

What defeats them is tempo.

Bakugo's team refuses to grant enough stable time for Class B's distributed deception to become decisive. Every local threat is met quickly enough that the B-team cannot turn information advantage into sustained control.

This matters for Tokage's character model. A special recommendation/admission status or visually elaborate Quirk does not protect a plan from an opponent whose operating speed is higher than expected.

Her loss should therefore be preserved as **failure under compressed decision time**, not evidence that the plan had no intelligence.

# 9. No.209 — Match 5 and Monoma's anti-protagonist philosophy

## 9.1 `誰もが他人の人生の脇役であり / 自分の人生の主役なんだ`

Monoma supplies one of the volume's most useful meta-character lines at `V22:p131`:

> `誰もが 他人の人生の脇役であり`
>
> `自分の人生の主役なんだ`

This is funny coming from a character whose social style repeatedly tries to seize narrative attention, but it is not merely a joke.

The statement is structurally accurate. Class B has lived and trained outside the camera position naturally occupied by Class A. Joint Training is valuable partly because it reveals that less narrative exposure is not the same thing as less internal development.

For character reconstruction, the line is a direct warning against protagonist bias:

> **Every character experiences their own motives as central even when the story uses them as contextual support for somebody else.**

## 9.2 Monoma and Shinso recognize a family resemblance in stigmatized utility

Chapter 211 later makes Monoma's relation to Shinso more specific. He recalls being told that his Quirk would not fit the image of a “superhero” and identifies a similarity between powers whose best use often requires deception, appropriation, or another person's action.

This is not proof that either is victimized in the same way or has the same ethics. It is evidence that Monoma understands the social problem of possessing a capability whose usefulness is indirect and whose optics are easy to code as villainous.

That helps explain why his competitive hostility toward Class A coexists with a strong need to make Class B's less obvious capabilities legible.

## 9.3 Match 5 is engineered around uncertainty

Class B's fifth team—Monoma, Yanagi Reiko, Kodai Yui, Shoda Nirengeki and Shinso—creates uncertainty through indirect, technical and communication-sensitive Quirks.

Midoriya's team—Uraraka, Mineta and Ashido—cannot simply maximize output because Shinso makes verbal response itself dangerous.

The match is therefore primed to test controlled attention before the new OFA event destroys the assumption that Midoriya himself is a stable source of controllable force.

# 10. No.210 — OFA resonance becomes externally legible

## 10.1 All For One hears `弟の声がする`

Before Midoriya's new manifestation becomes intelligible, V22 cuts to All For One in Tartarus.

He reflects on hidden League pieces and the value of not over-stimulating the situation, then states at `V22:p139`:

> `弟の声がする`

This is a major epistemic change.

V21 established that Midoriya could experience predecessor history internally. V22 now establishes an **external resonance**: All For One perceives something associated with his younger brother while imprisoned elsewhere.

The mechanism remains unknown at this boundary. V22 does not authorize a complete telepathic system, distance rule or vestige ontology.

What it does establish is that the OFA/AFO historical relation is not confined to Midoriya's private dream state.

## 10.2 The new power is not intentionally selected

When the tendril-like inherited power erupts, Midoriya has not chosen a new technique from a menu. He is in the middle of competitive pressure, trying to win and manage Monoma/Shinso's tactical threats.

The visual grammar shifts rapidly from controlled industrial training-space geometry to thick black lines that cross panel boundaries, grab surfaces and overwhelm Midoriya's body (`V22:p155 onward`).

This is a reversal of his recent power-development trajectory.

From Full Cowl through Shoot Style, support gear and ranged pressure, Midoriya has been increasing:

- percentage control;
- bodily sustainability;
- optionality;
- repeatability;
- separation between maximum output and ordinary action.

The new manifestation abruptly introduces a capability with almost none of those properties.

## 10.3 Emotion matters, but V22 does not establish a simple trigger law

Historical V1 described the eruption as heroic emotion directly triggering Blackwhip. V22 supports a more bounded claim.

The eruption occurs amid agitation, competitive urgency and intrusive thought. At the endpoint, the prior holder explicitly says the inherited power has grown beyond something safe to use while full of stray/noisy thoughts:

> `雑念マシマシで使っていいモノじゃなくなってる`

That establishes **mental/emotional state as a control variable**.

It does not yet establish a deterministic rule such as “anger activates this power” or “desire to save always triggers it.” The safer V22 model is:

> **OFA's newly manifested inherited capability is sensitive enough to the holder's mental state that ordinary competitive agitation can become a safety hazard.**

# 11. No.211 — Midoriya's old fear returns in a new form

## 11.1 `止まれ` replaces “how much can my body survive?”

Midoriya's immediate response is not excitement at acquiring more power. It is repeated command toward the power itself:

> `止まれ`

At `V22:p167`, the memory chain links the current event to prior situations where other people were harmed, frightened or forced to care for him because his power exceeded his control.

The crucial internal concern is:

> `誰にも心配させたくないのに`

That line updates the self-sacrifice model.

Earlier Midoriya could interpret self-damage as acceptable if somebody else was saved. Accumulated experience has taught him that uncontrolled self-damage is not private. It transfers fear, labor and responsibility onto peers and mentors.

The new OFA danger therefore activates not merely fear of injury but fear of becoming a burden/threat to the people around him.

## 11.2 More power creates a new rescue obligation toward the holder

The scene destroys any simple equation between “more inherited power” and “more heroic capacity.” At this moment, additional capacity makes Midoriya less able to satisfy his own rescue ethics because the field around him becomes dangerous.

The ethical task changes from:

`use power to save others`

to:

`allow others to intervene so the power does not harm them`.

That is a difficult reversal for a character whose identity is organized around moving toward danger first.

# 12. No.212 — Uraraka's rescue ethic expands to heroes

## 12.1 Childhood attention was directed toward faces around the hero

Chapter 212 gives Uraraka one of her most important causal-history sequences so far.

As a child, she remembers her parents as routinely tired. Their exhausted faces hurt to see (`V22:p172`). When she first watched hero activity, her attention did not remain fixed on the spectacle itself:

> `活動よりも 周りの人々の表情に目が行った`

She liked seeing people happy:

> `人の喜ぶ顔が好きだった`

And because of that orientation:

> `困ってる人を助ける事は当たり前だった`

This substantially deepens the earlier economic/family motive. Uraraka did want to help her parents financially, but V22 demonstrates a pre-professional attentional habit: she reads the emotional state of the people around an event.

Her heroism is not produced only by poverty or admiration of individual heroes. It is rooted in **relational affect monitoring**.

## 12.2 The object of rescue changes when she sees heroes suffer

The childhood model then encounters experiences the younger Uraraka did not yet have: heroes themselves can be overwhelmed, injured, desperate and afraid.

The question at `V22:p173` is explicit:

> `ヒーローが辛い時 誰がヒーローを守ってあげられるだろう`

This is not a decorative shipping beat attached to Midoriya. It is a revision of the scope of rescue.

Earlier schema:

`hero -> person in trouble`.

V22 schema:

`person in trouble -> rescue target, regardless of whether that person normally occupies the hero role`.

That is a significant professional-ethical maturation because it breaks role essentialism. Heroes are not inexhaustible rescue infrastructure. They are human participants who can become rescue subjects.

## 12.3 Uraraka acts before she has a complete explanation

Uraraka physically enters the uncontrolled tendrils and grabs Midoriya (`V22:p169`). She does not know the new power's mechanics. Her decision is based on immediate recognition that Midoriya is suffering and losing control.

She then calls to Shinso because his Brainwashing supplies a way to interrupt Midoriya from inside the behavioral control loop.

Her rescue is therefore not solitary either. She provides physical grounding and relational contact; Shinso provides the precise stopping interface.

The scene is one of the clearest examples yet of distributed rescue:

`recognize distress -> enter danger -> recruit the capability that can actually stop the hazard -> maintain contact while the intervention occurs`.

## 12.4 Readiness consequence

On accumulated V2 evidence, this closes a major gap in Uraraka's model. We now have:

- family/economic motive;
- independent competitive planning against Bakugo;
- attraction and deliberate emotional self-regulation;
- crisis rescue/support behavior;
- low-stakes peer interaction;
- recognition of other people's emotional states;
- explicit causal history for why smiles matter;
- a mature rescue principle that includes rescuers themselves;
- willingness to physically intervene when Midoriya's power makes him dangerous.

This supports promotion from `moderate` to **`strong`**.

# 13. Shinso: a coercive affordance becomes rescue infrastructure

## 13.1 V22 pays off the ethical professionalization argument

Shinso's Brainwashing has always carried socially threatening optics because it directly alters another person's agency after a response trigger.

V21 showed professionalization through:

- training;
- Persona Chords;
- tactical trigger discipline;
- supervision;
- team integration;
- post-performance self-calibration.

V22 supplies a more morally diagnostic use than tournament capture.

Midoriya is becoming dangerous without intending to be. Ordinary force risks escalating the situation. Aizawa and the students recognize that Shinso can interrupt him by getting him to answer.

The supposedly “villain-like” capability becomes the safest available rescue tool.

## 13.2 Shinso's motive is now explicit in service terms

At `V22:p177`, Shinso recalls the Sports Festival loss and the path since then. His current goal is not merely entrance into the Hero Course as status recovery.

The important formulation is:

> `人の為に使いたい`

He wants to use his Quirk for people.

Then he addresses Midoriya:

> `俺と戦おうぜ`

The invitation is tactically designed to elicit a response. When Midoriya responds, Brainwashing interrupts the runaway state (`V22:p178-180`).

The ethical meaning of Brainwashing therefore depends on context, consent structure, emergency necessity and purpose. V22 gives the strongest evidence yet for the project rule:

> **A Quirk is not an ethic. Its affordances acquire ethical meaning through use, constraints and responsibility.**

## 13.3 Do not overpromote Shinso yet

This is major evidence, but it remains concentrated in hero-training and emergency-use contexts. Ordinary life, stable friendships and broader longitudinal professional failure/repair remain relatively sparse.

Shinso therefore remains **`moderate (very strongly grounded)`**, strengthened rather than promoted to `strong`.

# 14. The inherited-power endpoint: more than strength, less than a complete roster

## 14.1 V22 confirms a prior holder is causally present

After Brainwashing interrupts the runaway manifestation, Midoriya re-enters a vestige-like state. A broad, goggle-wearing predecessor appears (`V22:p181-183`).

The figure explicitly identifies himself as:

> `歴代継承者の一人…!!`

This moves the V21 model forward. V21 showed historical encounter. V22 establishes that a prior holder is connected to a newly manifest capability in the present holder.

That is enough to say:

> **OFA contains inherited capacity whose operation cannot be reduced to All Might-style stockpiled strength.**

## 14.2 The power has changed through accumulation

The predecessor emphasizes that the power is no longer in the same state in which he used it:

> `その力はもう!`
>

This is important for inheritance theory.

# 15. Character-state synthesis

## 15.1 Midoriya Izuku — from self-control to acceptance of external control

Midoriya remains `strong`, but V22 adds a qualitatively new failure regime.

His prior OFA problem was primarily percentage mismatch between inherited output and body capacity. The V22 problem is different:

- he does not know the new capability exists;
- he does not intentionally activate it;
- it affects the environment and other people;
- his own command `止まれ` is insufficient;
- he must be physically grounded by Uraraka;
- he must be behaviorally interrupted by Shinso.

For simulation, this matters because the correct Midoriya response to dangerous loss of control is not prideful refusal of help. He panics, tries to stop the power, worries about others, and becomes reachable through peers.

His fear is relational: `誰にも心配させたくない`.

The long-term challenge is no longer only “master strength.” It is “remain morally accountable when inherited capacity exceeds conscious authorship.”

## 15.2 Uraraka Ochako — strong readiness

Uraraka advances `moderate -> strong`.

The most important new evidence is not romantic proximity to Midoriya. It is a causal theory of her attention and rescue motivation that begins with tired parents, moves through attention to bystanders' faces, and culminates in the explicit question of who protects heroes when they suffer.

Her intervention in the runaway OFA scene is consistent with that theory and therefore highly diagnostic.

## 15.3 Bakugo Katsuki — cooperative aggression without personality replacement

Bakugo remains `strong`.

V22 validates a key character-model prediction: greater prosocial maturity does not produce gentle speech or consensus-seeking temperament. It produces more accurate integration of teammate capability, reciprocal rescue and complete-victory requirements.

His leadership remains high-pressure and command-forward. The behavioral change occurs under the register, not instead of it.

## 15.4 Iida Tenya — family craft integrated into self-authored professionalism

Iida remains `strong`.

Recipro Turbo adds a new form of inheritance distinct from merely adopting the Ingenium name. He accepts painful family technical knowledge because he wants to run farther/faster, then uses that capability inside team rescue/capture priorities.

This strengthens the post-Hosu model in which inherited ideals are healthiest when converted into deliberate present policy.

## 15.5 Todoroki Shoto — technical inheritance without moral surrender

Todoroki remains `strong`.

The Tetsutetsu pressure forces him to reuse Endeavor's teaching, but the manga preserves his resistance and prior Rei-centered individuation. V22 therefore strengthens the ability to model Shoto as someone who can acknowledge that Endeavor was correct about a technical fact without converting that fact into forgiveness or paternal authority.

## 15.6 Yaoyorozu Momo — leadership that survives capture

Yaoyorozu remains `moderate (very strongly strengthened)`.

V22 resolves the V21 open result with formal defeat, but her model improves rather than collapses. Her plan continues after capture, and Kendo explicitly recognizes its forward-looking quality.

The remaining gap for `strong` is not intelligence. It is broader repeated independent crisis leadership and longer confidence/failure stability across contexts.

## 15.7 Kendo Itsuka — calibrated winner

Kendo remains `moderate`, strengthened.

V22 supplies a valuable post-success state: she can win 4-0 and still distinguish scoreboard dominance from the opponent's underlying planning quality.

This makes her more than the sensible Class B spokesperson archetype. She shows opponent modeling before, during and after success.

## 15.8 Monoma Neito — protagonist hunger with real meta-awareness

Monoma receives an `emerging (strongly grounded)` modeling baseline.

His recurring hostility toward Class A, strong Class B identification, Copy-dependent tactical thinking, and V22 philosophy about everybody being protagonist of their own life now form a coherent pattern. His later self-comparison with Shinso also shows awareness of socially awkward/indirect Quirk utility.

Ordinary relationships and noncompetitive identity remain too narrow for `moderate`.

## 15.9 Honenuki Juzo — flexible systems thinker

Honenuki enters `emerging (strongly grounded)`.

One match provides high-quality evidence of calm adaptation, terrain thinking, target reprioritization and rescue/capture integration. It does not yet provide enough ordinary or relationship breadth for `moderate`.

## 15.10 Tetsutetsu Tetsutetsu — pressure-seeking endurance

Tetsutetsu advances to `emerging (strongly grounded)` on accumulated V09/V22 evidence.

The forest-camp crisis and V22 match now show persistence under genuine danger and competitive bodily-limit pressure. His model is still disproportionately stress/combat-centered, so `moderate` would be premature.

## 15.11 Tokage Setsuna and other Class B additions

Tokage enters `emerging (strongly grounded)` from distributed-body scouting, deception, tactical leadership and failure under unexpectedly compressed tempo.

Pony Tsunotori and Kaibara Sen gain useful tactical baselines from Match 3. Yanagi Reiko, Kodai Yui and Shoda Nirengeki gain Match 5 evidence, but their current models remain narrow and should not be inflated by visually distinctive Quirks.

# 16. Relationship-state synthesis

## 16.1 Kendo <-> Yaoyorozu: adversarial esteem survives the result

V21 established esteem as targeted suppression. V22 adds the post-result state.

Kendo does not patronize Momo after beating her. She names the quality she found difficult: every creation looked ahead.

Relationship state:

`rival recognition -> deliberate denial of opponent's strength -> formal win -> continued esteem without score denial`.

This is a useful non-intimate relationship model built around competence rather than affection.

## 16.2 Iida <-> Tensei: inheritance as optional technical mentorship

Tensei's role remains important even after his frontline career is over. He transmits knowledge while preserving Tenya's choice:

`if you want to run more -> here is what our family learned -> the body cost is real -> decide and embody it yourself`.

This is a healthy inheritance relation precisely because it does not require Tenya to become a replica.

## 16.3 Todoroki <-> Endeavor: knowledge uptake without reconciliation

V22 adds a new relationship state rather than resolving the old one.

Shoto uses knowledge Endeavor tried to teach him because the present situation requires it. That is evidence that the paternal relationship no longer has absolute veto power over Shoto's own capabilities.

It is not evidence that Endeavor's past conduct has been forgiven.

## 16.4 Bakugo <-> teammates: reciprocal rescue becomes expected

Bakugo's Match 4 relation to Jiro/Sero/Sato is particularly diagnostic because they are not his deepest emotional relationships. He still constructs mutual rescue/support with them.

That means the behavior is becoming portable professional policy rather than an exception limited to Midoriya or emotionally loaded partners.

## 16.5 Uraraka <-> Midoriya: concern becomes role-generalizable

Uraraka's intervention is directed at Midoriya, but the chapter's explicit causal language generalizes beyond him: `ヒーローが辛い時`.

The relationship therefore matters without reducing the ethical development to romance. Midoriya is the immediate person who makes a broader principle actionable.

## 16.6 Uraraka <-> Shinso: rapid capability trust

Uraraka does not need an intimate relationship with Shinso to recruit him. She recognizes that he possesses the right interface to interrupt Midoriya and calls him into the rescue.

This is professional trust formed through capability recognition under time pressure.

## 16.7 Shinso <-> Midoriya: rivalry language becomes rescue mechanism

`俺と戦おうぜ` works because Midoriya recognizes Shinso as an opponent and responds.

The same competitive relation that once structured Sports Festival defeat is re-used as an emergency stopping pathway. Rivalry is not displaced by care; it becomes the language through which care can operate.

# 17. Power philosophy: control is relational infrastructure

V22 is one of the strongest volumes yet for the power-philosophy ledger.

## 17.1 Power is trainable through inherited craft

Iida demonstrates that a biological Quirk can be modified through embodied family technique. “Natural ability” is therefore not a fixed raw parameter.

## 17.2 Power can be reclaimed from a morally compromised teacher

Todoroki demonstrates that technical knowledge can be separated from the teacher's claim to moral authority.

## 17.3 Power becomes stronger when it incorporates teammates

Bakugo's Match 4 demonstrates that maximizing personal output can be inferior to maximizing team uptime, information and rescue redundancy.

## 17.4 Power can exceed conscious authorship

Midoriya demonstrates the opposite failure mode: inherited capacity may appear before the holder has a usable conceptual model.

## 17.5 Control may legitimately come from outside the holder

Uraraka and Shinso establish the most important new rule:

> **Self-control is not the only morally acceptable control. In an emergency, ethical power use may require trusted others to interrupt the holder.**

This is especially significant for Brainwashing. A capacity to suspend another person's voluntary action is frightening in the abstract; under V22 conditions it prevents a larger uncontrolled hazard.

## 17.6 Mental state is part of the power system

The predecessor's warning about `雑念マシマシ` makes cognition/affect operationally relevant. Emotional regulation becomes not merely good sports psychology but physical safety infrastructure for inherited power.

# 18. Hero-society and institutional implications

## 18.1 Joint Training is a diagnostic institution

The arc works because U.A. permits outcomes that do not flatter its protagonists.

- Class A can lose 4-0.
- a match can draw;
- Class B can reveal superior preparation in specific domains;
- Shinso can be evaluated without guaranteed success;
- students can discover new power problems inside a controlled environment rather than only in lethal villain encounters.

The training system's value is therefore partly **epistemic**: it produces information about capability and failure before society asks students to bear full professional consequences.

## 18.2 Team evaluation counters celebrity individualism

The ranking/public-symbol chapters of V20-21 foregrounded individual visibility. V22's training structure repeatedly demonstrates that professional success is often distributed.

This is an institutional corrective to hero-celebrity mythology. The visible finisher may depend on sensing, transport, rescue, restraint, terrain control or decision support supplied by less visible teammates.

## 18.3 Shinso shows institutional remediation of an assessment mismatch

Shinso's original problem was partly that the U.A. entrance test rewarded direct robot-destruction affordances poorly matched to Brainwashing. V21-22 demonstrate that the institution can partially correct that mismatch through later observation, training, equipment and supervised trials.

The correction is incomplete; his final transfer status is still outside V22. But the system is capable of revisiting an earlier filter rather than treating it as metaphysical proof of worth.

## 19.1 Momo: recognition after defeat

Kendo's `勝った気しないな` is a recognition event that does not erase failure. This is important because MHA repeatedly treats accurate recognition as more useful than generic reassurance.

## 19.2 Bakugo: rescue is no longer antithetical to victory

The Match 4 system is a callback/payoff to the long-running split between Midoriya's rescue orientation and Bakugo's victory orientation. Bakugo now makes rescue part of the mechanism of complete victory.

The chapter 212 question formalizes a major revision:

A hero in pain is still a person in trouble.

That is a valuable anti-perfectionist rule for the series' ethics:

> **Needing rescue is not disqualifying evidence against being a rescuer.**

# 25. Volume-level synthesis

Volume 22 begins by resolving a competition that Class B wins 4-0 and ends by refusing to resolve a competition at all.

That structural choice is revealing.

The volume is not primarily about determining which class is stronger. It is about making competence legible in forms that scoreboards, rankings and protagonist focus can miss.

Yaoyorozu loses, yet her decisions remain active after capture and Kendo explicitly identifies the future-oriented quality of what she created. Iida becomes faster, but the important fact is that speed arrived through family craft freely taken up and then used to expand rescue bandwidth. Todoroki uses Endeavor-taught power, but the act does not transfer moral authority back to Endeavor. Match 3 draws because both sides force adaptations that cannot be reduced to a clean hierarchy.

Bakugo then produces the volume's most visible success: a 4-0 win. Yet even this apparently perfect victory is written against solitary hero mythology. His aggression has become a team operating system. He expects others to support him, rescues them when their line breaks, uses their information, and treats mutual rescue as part of what “complete victory” means. He has become more prosocial without becoming lower-temperature.

Monoma's statement that everyone is a supporting character in somebody else's life and protagonist of their own supplies the meta-rule underneath these matches. Class B's development exists whether Class A or the reader has been watching it. Narrative centrality is not ontological importance.

Then the OFA event makes the same lesson physically unavoidable for Midoriya.

He has spent much of the series turning inherited strength into authored technique. Full Cowl, Shoot Style, percentages, support equipment and ranged pressure all increased the degree to which he could say, in practice, “this power is mine because I know how I am choosing to use it.” V22 interrupts that progress with inherited capacity he did not knowingly select and cannot stop by will alone.

The first heroic response is therefore not mastery. It is **being rescued**.

Uraraka enters the hazard because her rescue ethic has expanded beyond the category “civilian.” Her childhood memory shows that she has always watched the faces around hero activity; adult training lets her formulate the harder question: if a hero is suffering, who protects the hero? Shinso then provides the capability that turns this moral insight into successful stabilization. Brainwashing—an ability easy to imagine as villainous coercion—becomes precisely the tool that prevents uncontrolled inherited power from harming people.

The predecessor's appearance at the endpoint deepens rather than closes the problem. OFA now contains more than All Might-like stockpiled force, and at least one prior holder's capacity has become amplified enough that his own former use model is no longer sufficient. Inheritance is not static. It changes while being carried.

The volume's deepest rule is therefore:

> **Heroic competence includes the ability to participate in systems where agency, knowledge, rescue and control are distributed. A mature hero is not somebody who never needs help; it is somebody whose strength can remain accountable even when others must support, correct, restrain or rescue them.**

This produces several important reconstruction rules:

- do not infer competence solely from win/loss;
- do not infer moral reconciliation from technical inheritance;
- do not infer personality softening from prosocial policy growth;
- do not infer ethical danger solely from coercive-looking Quirk affordance;
- do not infer weakness from being a rescue subject;
- do not infer mastery merely because new power has manifested;
- do not treat protagonist status as proof that other characters' development exists only in relation to the protagonist.

V22 is therefore much more than the volume where a new OFA power appears. It is the volume where the series makes **mutual regulation** central to heroism.
