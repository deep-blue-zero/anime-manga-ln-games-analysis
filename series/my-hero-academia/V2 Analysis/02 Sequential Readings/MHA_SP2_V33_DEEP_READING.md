# My Hero Academia - Second-Pass Deep Reading, Volume 33

## 0. Status, method, and prospective lock

This reading follows `MHA_SP2_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2_1.md` and begins from the exact frozen Volume 32 endpoint at `V32:p215`. It treats Volume 33 prospectively. Volume 34 and later material are excluded from evidentiary use even where older first-pass analysis or general series knowledge would make later outcomes familiar.

The entering state is not simply “Dark Deku is exhausted.” Volume 32 established that Midoriya has become technically formidable and ethically more discriminating while preserving his oldest structural error: he treats reciprocal protection as a privilege for other people and unilateral burden ownership as his own obligation. He can ask Muscular whether another path exists without presuming an affirmative answer; rescue Chisaki without forgiving him; recognize Lady Nagant's institutional victimization without erasing her agency; and use inherited Quirks compositionally rather than worshipfully. Yet he increasingly refuses food, rest, accompaniment and help. Dictator is able to exploit precisely that accumulated depletion. At `V32:p215`, Bakugo and Class 1-A have arrived and neutralized Dictator's immediate control position. They have not yet persuaded Midoriya, returned him to U.A., apologized, reconciled, or resolved the civilian shelter problem. Those are V33 questions.

# 1. Governing transition: responsibility becomes reciprocal, and heroism becomes distributed

Volume 32's most important failure was not Midoriya's exhaustion by itself. Exhaustion was the symptom of a policy: he treated every additional relationship as another person to protect from himself and therefore converted care into justification for separation. The arrival of Class 1-A at the endpoint did not yet prove that policy wrong. Volume 33 does.

The class does not defeat Midoriya's argument by pretending AFO is harmless or by claiming that proximity to him creates no danger. They accept the danger. They reject the inference that danger gives Midoriya exclusive jurisdiction over everyone else's willingness to share it.

That distinction propagates outward through the whole volume.

- Bakugo knows Midoriya well enough to treat `僕は大丈夫` as diagnostically unreliable under this stress regime.
- Iida turns Midoriya's own `余計なお世話` rescue grammar back onto him.
- Bakugo apologizes for his own past conduct without using apology as a claim on forgiveness.
- Uraraka tells civilians that OFA is a special power while Midoriya is not a special category of person exempt from ordinary human protection.
- The evacuees are not required to deny their fear. They are asked to move from spectators/customers of hero labor into limited participants who permit a hero to rest beside them.
- The narration itself shifts from a singular greatest-hero trajectory toward `みんなが最高のヒーローになるまでの物語`, a story in which heroic participation becomes plural.
- Stain rejects All Might's conclusion that powerless post-Symbol existence makes him merely an obstruction. The “ember” All Might left has survived precisely because other people carried it.
- A nameless Tartarus guard preserves evidence to death; Stain, a criminal who despises false heroism, carries it to the only person he trusts with it; All Might carries it back into collective planning.
- The endpoint then widens the relay beyond Japan: Star and Stripe moves because her mentor is in trouble.

The governing transition is therefore:

**`relational responsibility versus unilateral burden ownership -> reciprocal/distributed heroism in which heroes are rescue subjects and civilians, peers, mentors and even morally compromised outsiders can become bounded participants in sustaining rescue`**

Compact formulation:

> **Volume 33 turns “save everyone” against the hero exception. Midoriya cannot coherently insist that everyone is eligible for rescue while treating himself as the one person whose fear, exhaustion and need do not authorize intervention. OFA may be exceptional power, but exceptional power does not create exceptional expendability. The volume then scales that correction into a social thesis: heroism fails when one Symbol, one successor, one profession or one class of workers is expected to carry public safety alone.**

This is not egalitarian flattening. V33 does not say everyone has the same power, expertise or responsibility. Midoriya remains the OFA holder. Pro heroes remain professionals. Nezu's infrastructure matters because expertise and preparation matter. What changes is the jurisdictional model: unequal capacity does not justify one-way care.

# 2. No.319 `友だち` - friendship becomes an evidentiary relationship, not sentimental decoration

## 2.1 Class 1-A does not begin by denying the threat

The class's intervention is grounded in information. They understand that Midoriya carries OFA, that AFO is pursuing him, that All Might is accompanying him, and that professional heroes have been attempting to manage the hunt. Their objection is not that adults have misunderstood the existence of danger. Their objection is that the adults cannot reliably stop the specific failure mode produced by the Midoriya-All Might dyad.

Bakugo provides the sharpest diagnosis. Both Midoriya and All Might have a self-sacrificial tendency that makes them poor brakes on one another. If the person assigned to restrain an over-self-sacrificing successor shares the same core pathology, supervision becomes structurally weak. This is not a generic insult. It is a relationship-specific model accumulated over years.

The class therefore seeks Midoriya's location and forces the issue with Endeavor rather than accepting professional reassurance.

## 2.2 `緑谷くんは友だちです` establishes a different kind of authority

Iida's formulation is simple: Midoriya is their friend (`V33:p015-016`). Friendship here is not presented as superior to professional competence in every domain. It supplies a kind of longitudinal evidence professionals do not possess.

The class knows:

- how Midoriya behaves when frightened for someone else;
- how he minimizes his own condition;
- how he talks when trying to distance people for their protection;
- how ordinary school life changes his baseline;
- which past rescues he will remember when addressed relationally;
- which claims of `大丈夫` are inconsistent with observable embodiment.

This is precisely the kind of context-specific knowledge the V2.1 character-modeling architecture is designed to preserve. V33 makes it canonically useful inside the story.

## 2.3 `僕は大丈夫だから` becomes a failed self-report

After the class secures the Dictator-controlled civilians, Midoriya insists that he is fine and asks everyone to leave (`V33:p019-023`). The source simultaneously shows a body that is visibly filthy, exhausted and degraded.

Bakugo does not treat verbal self-report as dispositive because he has higher-priority behavioral evidence. This matters epistemically. Respecting autonomy does not require pretending a person's stress-conditioned self-assessment is infallible.

The class's intervention remains morally bounded because they are not trying to seize OFA or decide Midoriya's life for him. They are contesting a specific emergency policy that assigns all risk to one deteriorating person.

# 3. No.320 `デクvsA組` - Midoriya understands their care and still excludes himself from it

## 3.1 The central conflict is not misrecognition

A weaker reading of this confrontation would say Midoriya needs to “realize his friends care.” The text forecloses that interpretation.

As classmates pursue, restrain and address him through remembered ordinary bonds, Midoriya explicitly understands that they are worried about him. Danger Sense does not activate against them because they are not approaching him with hostile intent (`V33:p041`). Yet he still asks them to separate from him and repeats that he is fine.

The error is therefore not:

`Midoriya cannot perceive love.`

It is:

`Midoriya perceives love but treats it as operationally inadmissible whenever receiving it could expose the giver to danger.`

That is a much stronger and more stable character claim.

## 3.2 Ordinary memories are tactical evidence

Jiro, Ojiro, Sato, Tokoyami, Momo, Tsuyu and others do not need unique philosophical speeches. Their force comes from accumulated mundane relation. Study help, school interactions, meals, training, ordinary kindness and prior rescues become the reasons they refuse his attempt to reduce the relationship to threat management.

V33 thus gives low-stakes material retrospective strategic significance. The class can act as a rescue network because it was a social network first.

## 3.3 Strength is not a unilateral-rights argument

Midoriya can break restraints and outrun most classmates. The class never accepts the premise that greater capability grants exclusive moral authority to decide who bears shared risk.

This directly revises a recurring power fantasy assumption:

`If I am strongest, I should carry the most danger alone.`

V33 answers:

`Greater capability can increase responsibility, but it does not erase the agency or responsibility of everyone around you.`

# 4. No.321 `A組からOFAへ` - the class turns Midoriya's own rescue ethic back onto him

## 4.1 The relay is the argument

Class 1-A's pursuit culminates in a literal chain of handoffs. No single student matches Midoriya's present mobility. They combine propulsion, capture, support, timing and trust until Iida can reach him.

The formal contrast with V32 is exact:

- V32 Midoriya composes inherited Quirks inside one body to approximate otherwise impossible output.
- V33 Class 1-A composes independent people without collapsing them into one owner.

Both are plural systems. Only the latter gives each component continuing agency.

## 4.2 Iida's `同じ意志で 同じ歩調で`

Iida does not ask Midoriya to abandon his objective. He says they challenge him with the same will and the same stride (`V33:p061`). The disagreement concerns implementation and burden allocation, not the value of saving people.

That is why Iida is the correct final hand in the relay. His own Hosu failure taught him what happens when private pain converts shared professional purpose into solitary action. His V07 repair was not merely remorse; it became a prevention doctrine. V33 is a strong held-out validation that the lesson generalizes beyond his own revenge scenario.

## 4.3 `余計なお世話` returns to its originator

The decisive callback appears when Iida invokes the principle that unwanted meddling is part of heroism (`V33:p063`). Midoriya has repeatedly used that logic to justify intervening when another person is unable or unwilling to articulate need.

V33 exposes the hidden exception in his policy:

`Unasked-for help is heroic when I give it, but dangerous/improper when others give it to me.`

The class does not invent a new ethic. It applies Midoriya's existing ethic consistently.

# 5. No.322 - Bakugo's apology: responsibility without possession

## 5.1 The apology is explanatory without becoming exculpatory

Bakugo finally gives Midoriya an explicit account of the childhood relation. His prior aggression is tied to what Midoriya exposed in him: vulnerability, inadequacy and an unwanted moral comparison. Bakugo recognized something in the Quirkless child that unsettled the superiority structure on which his self-concept depended, and he responded by pushing Midoriya down.

This explanation matters for character reconstruction because it identifies a mechanism. It does not make the conduct acceptable.

The key line is direct:

`今まで ごめん` (`V33:p072`).

He bows.

The scene avoids several cheap repair devices:

- Bakugo does not say the bullying made Midoriya stronger.
- He does not say he was secretly helping.
- He does not demand recognition for later battlefield sacrifice.
- He does not ask Midoriya to pronounce him forgiven.
- He does not make his current insight retroactively erase the past.

The apology therefore belongs under **accountability**, not “redemption payment.”

## 5.2 Bakugo preserves rivalry while changing jurisdiction

Immediately around the apology, Bakugo does not become generically soft. He continues to regard Midoriya as rival and remains invested in becoming No.1. Later bath/dorm humor confirms that explicit repair does not overwrite baseline abrasiveness.

This is high-value reconstruction evidence: prosocial moral growth and aggressive conversational style are not mutually exclusive variables.

## 5.3 `おまえが拭えねぇもんは 俺たちが拭う`

Bakugo's practical argument is more important than emotional catharsis. What Midoriya cannot handle, others will handle. The class will fill gaps. Saving and winning are not one person's two obligations; they can be distributed across a network.

Bakugo's V29 diagnosis of Midoriya's self-exclusion is therefore prospectively validated by V33 action. He did not merely understand Midoriya in theory. He organizes himself and the class around correcting the failure mode.

## 5.4 Midoriya's reciprocal apology matters

Midoriya later apologizes for saying the others could not keep up (`V33:p074`) and collapses into Bakugo's support.

This is the first direct behavioral movement away from unilateral burden ownership: not a finished doctrine, but an actual surrender of weight into another person's body.

# 6. U.A. gate crisis - credible fear is not the same as legitimate exclusion

## 6.1 The civilians' objection is not irrational

When Midoriya reaches U.A., evacuees object to admitting him because Shigaraki/AFO may pursue him (`V33:p079-093`). The risk is real. V33 does not require the reader to treat the crowd as stupid or morally corrupt.

This continuity with V32 is important. Public fear became evidence-sensitive after catastrophic institutional failure. U.A. is not just a school; it is refuge infrastructure containing people with limited alternatives. They have standing to care about correlated risk.

## 6.2 Credible fear does not automatically produce correct causal attribution

The crowd moves from “his presence may attract danger” toward treating Midoriya as though exclusion can detach them from the social crisis that already exists. V33 pushes back on that inference.

They are already living inside a system attacked by AFO, mass prison breaks and institutional collapse. Sending the exhausted OFA bearer elsewhere may relocate visible risk but does not restore the public order on which their own safety depends.

The appropriate adjudication is therefore dual:

- **PRESERVE:** civilian fear is grounded and must be recognized.
- **REJECT:** grounded fear gives civilians unlimited moral license to make an exhausted minor permanently external to shelter.

# 7. No.323 `一歩` - infrastructure and legitimacy are both forms of protection

## 7.1 Nezu treats refuge as an engineering problem

U.A.'s defenses are not improvised rhetorical reassurance. Nezu describes extensive segmentation, underground movement, anti-villain countermeasures and links with other educational/refuge sites (`V33:p083-088`).

This matters because “trust us” is not the only response to public fear. Institutions can answer distrust with material design.

V33 therefore distinguishes two kinds of legitimacy repair:

1. **technical legitimacy** - demonstrate that risk has been anticipated and mitigated;
2. **relational legitimacy** - acknowledge fear, distribute responsibility and allow affected people to participate in the decision.

Nezu supplies the first. Uraraka supplies the second.

## 7.2 Security planning does not eliminate uncertainty

The defenses are sophisticated, not omnipotent. Uraraka later explicitly refuses to guarantee absolute safety. This keeps the scene from becoming infrastructure-as-magical-proof.

The ethical ask is not “believe nothing can happen.” It is “given unavoidable uncertainty, what obligations do we owe one another?”

# 8. No.323-324 - Uraraka's public argument: special power is not special personhood

## 8.1 She takes responsibility for bringing Midoriya back

Uraraka's first major rhetorical move is jurisdictional:

`連れ戻したのは私たちです!!` (`V33:p095`).

Midoriya did not deceive the civilians into accepting him. The class chose to bring him. That means responsibility for the decision cannot be dumped back onto the already exhausted rescue subject.

This is a mature leadership behavior: she owns the controversial action before asking others to bear its consequences.

## 8.2 `見えてますか!?` demands perception before judgment

Uraraka points to Midoriya's body and asks whether the crowd can see him (`V33:p096-097`). The argument is not “you should love Deku because he is good.” It begins with perceptual correction.

The public sees:

`OFA bearer -> AFO target -> threat concentration`.

Uraraka asks them also to see:

`mud-covered exhausted high-school boy -> person who left to protect them -> body presently unable to continue indefinitely`.

Both are true. Recognition fails when only the first remains legible.

## 8.3 `特別な力はあっても!! 特別な人なんていません!!`

At `V33:p098`, Uraraka separates exceptional capacity from exceptional ontological status.

A clean analytical formulation is:

`special power != special personhood != special expendability`.

Midoriya's Quirk gives him unusual capability and unusual obligations. It does not create a class of human being who does not need food, sleep, shelter, emotional support or consent from peers before assigning them permanent passivity.

This is one of the strongest anti-Symbol claims in the series so far because it does not diminish All Might's greatness. It denies that greatness should be interpreted as exemption from human needs.

## 8.4 Uraraka does not ask for blind trust

The speech explicitly acknowledges that civilians are afraid and that absolute safety cannot be guaranteed. She therefore does not reinstall the old hero-society contract in which civilians hand over fear and professionals promise control.

Instead she asks for a bounded contribution:

- allow Midoriya to rest beside them;
- give the heroes time to remove the “mud” accumulated while fighting;
- lend strength so everyone can reach tomorrow together.

The limited scope is ethically important. Civilians are not conscripted into combat. Participation begins with refusing to externalize the exhausted rescuer.

## 8.5 `普通の高校生`

Uraraka identifies Midoriya as an ordinary high-school student who is trying to fulfill the responsibility of his power (`V33:p111`). The phrase does not deny his extraordinary role. It prevents role from swallowing personhood.

This should become a load-bearing synthesis proposition for MHA:

> The more exceptional a heroic role becomes, the more important it is to preserve the ordinary person who performs it.

# 9. No.324 - narration itself pluralizes the hero story

## 9.1 Kota and the heteromorphic woman reverse the direction of rescue

Kota, once a child Midoriya rescued, runs toward him. The heteromorphic woman Midoriya recently protected also physically embraces/reaches him (`V33:p118-123`).

These are not combat-capable substitutes for OFA. Their value is different: rescued people become agents capable of returning recognition and support.

The relation is therefore no longer:

`hero -> passive beneficiary`.

It becomes:

`person A rescues person B in one context; B may later become part of A's rescue ecology in another`.

This is reciprocity without debt accounting.

## 9.2 The narration changes from singular to plural

The decisive narrative line at `V33:p119` is:

`みんなが 最高のヒーローに なるまでの物語だ`.

Whatever later volumes do with the phrase, V33 itself has already revised the frame. The story is no longer adequately described as the isolated ascent of one greatest hero. “Everyone” enters the grammar of greatness.

This does not mean every civilian becomes a licensed professional or every character equals Midoriya's combat power. It means the causal production of the future “greatest hero” state is collective.

The distinction matters:

`plural causation` is not `identical roles`.

# 10. No.325 `つながるOFA` - civilians stop being only customers

## 10.1 The crowd's moral movement remains negotiated

The civilians do not instantly become a cheering mass. They continue to reason through risk, prior disappointment and what accepting Midoriya means. That preserves the credibility of the shift.

A recurring civilian with an All Might shirt formulates the deeper problem:

`俺たち いつまで 客でいる つもりだ?` (`V33:p126`).

“How long do we intend to remain customers?”

This is one of the most consequential hero-society lines in the reread.

Old hero society encouraged a service relation:

`heroes provide safety -> civilians consume safety -> public evaluates heroes as performers/providers`.

V33 does not abolish professional duty. It rejects the idea that public order can be sustainably produced while everyone outside the profession remains only a consumer.

## 10.2 Civilian participation is bounded civic responsibility

The volume is careful about scale. The civilians are not told to pick up weapons and become vigilantes. V32 already showed the danger of overconfident self-defense. The new role is more modest and therefore more plausible:

- look accurately;
- permit shelter;
- tolerate uncertainty;
- support exhausted professionals;
- refuse scapegoating;
- accept that public safety is relational infrastructure rather than a purchased spectacle.

This is a political theory of co-production rather than privatized vigilantism.

## 10.3 OFA becomes relational in meaning, not merely mechanism

Hawks and the visual/narrative chain around `V33:p129-132` connect successive acts of influence: All Might to others, Midoriya to classmates, Uraraka to civilians, civilians to one another.

One For All has always been mechanically cumulative. V33 makes its ethical meaning cumulative too. The relevant inheritance is not only stockpiled force. It is the transmission of reasons to act.

The title `つながるOFA` therefore supports a dual reading:

- OFA connects bearers across generations;
- the social meaning of OFA connects people who do not possess it.

This expands rather than dissolves Midoriya's special responsibility.

# 11. Aizawa and Kurogiri - recovery can remain faint without becoming meaningless

At `V33:p136-137`, Aizawa reports that Kurogiri was secretly transferred for restoration/research after responding to Aizawa and Present Mic. Mirko destroyed the copied Warp Gate factor available to the enemy. Aizawa characterizes the remaining possibility through a restrained image: even if the thread is faint and indistinct, a thread of reversal is being spun.

This should be bounded carefully.

V33 does **not** prove:

- full Shirakumo restoration;
- reliable Kurogiri cooperation;
- a future successful reversal.

It does establish that Aizawa does not equate uncertain recovery with zero-value recovery. His professional style remains anti-sentimental but not nihilistic.

The same conversation also records continued Toga countermeasures: blood-consumption / transformation-time limits have been analyzed, and evacuees undergo an isolation period before U.A. entry (`V33:p137`). Again, institutional response combines relational trust with technical precaution rather than choosing one.

# 12. No.326 `お前は誰だ` - All Might's post-Symbol identity is corrected by an enemy

## 12.1 All Might reaches the same self-exclusion trap as Midoriya

Alone at the vandalized All Might statue, Toshinori judges himself by what he could not do for his disciple. He remembers wanting to improve a world in which ordinary life is irrationally taken from people and recognizes that he once moved even when powerless. Yet after reaching the end of his great run, he sees the ruined present and concludes that he could not even tell the disciple he would protect with his life to rest (`V33:p141-146`).

The structural parallel with Midoriya is exact:

- Midoriya: “If I cannot protect everyone by staying near them, I should leave.”
- All Might: “If I cannot protect my disciple through power/action, I only hold him back.”

Both convert reduced effectiveness into reduced entitlement to relationship.

## 12.2 Stain refuses the identity claim

Stain tells him, in effect, that the self-erasing man before him is not the All Might he recognizes. He is not making a secret-identity mistake. He is making an ideological classification.

For Stain, All Might was never fundamentally validated by Quirk output. All Might smiled and gave himself to people in every circumstance; this came from the person, not merely from `個性` (`V33:p150`).

That is why Stain's intervention is analytically useful despite his murderous ideology. He is a hostile witness with an unusually strict model of authentic heroism.

## 12.3 `これは「結果」だと!? これは「過程」だ!`

All Might looks at social collapse as the result of his failed project. Stain rejects the temporal finality: this is process, not finished result (`V33:p150`).

The correction is not optimism by assertion. Stain supplies evidence.

A woman All Might rescued at Kamino continues coming from a nearby shelter to clean the vandalized statue. The action is tiny relative to the national crisis. That is the point. All Might's legacy exists in independent agents acting without command.

## 12.4 The ember is passed among people

Stain's most important formulation describes All Might's remaining ember as something carried among people and becoming a larger fire (`V33:p151`). As long as people live, he argues, the flame must continue to be fed.

This is a direct anti-single-point-of-failure revision of the Symbol of Peace model.

The mature formulation should be:

> All Might's greatest success is not that people can continue to depend on All Might. It is that motives formed through All Might can survive him inside people who act without him.

That makes V33's pluralization of heroism compatible with rather than hostile to All Might's legacy.

# 13. Stain and the Tartarus guard - morally compromised relays can carry legitimate evidence

## 13.1 The dying guard preserves a record without knowing who will receive it

No.328 returns to Tartarus approximately one month earlier (`V33:p173-178`). Amid institutional collapse, a dying guard protects a recording medium. Stain finds him and recognizes that the object mattered enough to preserve at the cost of life.

The guard does not need Stain to become morally clean. He needs the information to reach someone who will use it properly.

## 13.2 Stain calls himself a beast, not a hero

When asked whether he is person or beast, Stain's answer is bounded and self-classifying: a beast that merely contributes to a righteous world (`V33:p177`).

This is not redemption. Stain does not renounce his prior killings or become a licensed hero. The scene instead complicates role ontology:

`villain/criminal status` does not imply `every action performed by the person is useless or malicious`.

The corpus must preserve that distinction without laundering Stain's violence.

## 13.3 Trust is narrow, not generalized

Stain later explains that All Might is the only person he trusts enough to receive the record (`V33:p179`). This is highly relationship-specific. It should not be generalized into new social trust or broad reform.

For reconstruction, the stable rule is sharper:

- Stain remains extremely selective;
- ideology continues to dominate affiliation;
- when someone satisfies his heroic criterion, that recognition can motivate real cooperative conduct even without friendship or institutional alignment.

# 14. No.327 `REST!!!` - rest becomes positive hero infrastructure

The bath/dorm material is not tonal filler after the “serious” arc. It is the practical answer to V32.

Midoriya is washed, fed, sheltered and put back into ordinary peer space (`V33:p157-169`). The class jokes, argues, teases and discusses next steps. Bakugo remains Bakugo. Midoriya remains worried. No one needs to become emotionally transformed into a different personality for reciprocal care to work.

This low-stakes chapter supplies important reconstruction evidence:

- Bakugo's apology does not erase abrasive rivalry; he still intends to be No.1 and treats classmates as rivals.
- Midoriya can acknowledge that he treated All Might cruelly by abandoning him and remains concerned about repairing that relation (`V33:p161`).
- All Might returns and apologizes for having decided for himself that he was no longer an active participant; he commits to fighting even if he has to crawl (`V33:p162-164`).
- Uraraka's public leadership is followed by exhaustion, sleep and peer care rather than superhuman emotional invulnerability.
- Shoto continues to frame family/Toya responsibility as something he must prove through action rather than words alone.

The volume therefore literalizes its thesis:

`rest is not time outside heroism; rest is one of the conditions under which heroism remains possible.`

# 15. All Might and Midoriya repair by changing behavior, not by demanding a scene of absolution

Midoriya says he did something cruel to All Might and left things unresolved. All Might returns rather than waiting for the student to perform emotional repair alone. He apologizes for having self-classified himself as no longer useful and states that he too will fight.

Neither side is required to erase the preceding failure.

The relation becomes more reciprocal because:

- Midoriya can name his own hurtful distancing;
- All Might can name his own failed self-conception;
- both remain oriented toward the shared problem;
- post-powerless All Might accepts a role that is neither Symbol nor absence.

This is the mature continuation of V32's food/support failure.

# 16. No.328 - enemy synchronization and the corrected countdown

## 16.1 The Tartarus record changes the completion clock

Prior hero-side estimates, based on Nomu cadavers and Kurogiri/Central research, suggested a substantially longer stabilization/completion period. The preserved Tartarus record contains electromagnetic-wave signaling data associated with the synchronized AFO/Shigaraki operation (`V33:p170-183`).

Analysis of the waveform yields an enemy-side message:

`38日で完成させる` (`V33:p182`).

At the current V33 point, hero-side analysis concludes that **about three days remain** (`V33:p183`).

This is a major prospective correction. Do not retain the earlier longer timetable as current authority after V33.

## 16.2 AFO synchronization is not automatically identical to OFA vestige relation

The heroes compare the apparent coordination of AFO's original body and the consciousness inside Shigaraki with OFA's own consciousness relation. All Might notes meaningful similarity but also differences in experienced thought flow (`V33:p180-182`).

The correct adjudication is therefore:

- **STRENGTHEN:** AFO original/vestige synchronization is operationally real enough to coordinate attacks/signals.
- **REJECT:** V33 proves OFA and AFO consciousness mechanics are identical.
- **OPEN:** complete ontology, bidirectionality and later control state.

This preserves the series' continuing moral/mechanical contrast between inheritance and possession without inventing unsupported metaphysics.

# 17. Overseas heroes - distributed responsibility expands beyond national jurisdiction

V33's last story movement shows international debate about whether heroes can be sent to Japan while their own countries face crime and instability (`V33:p184-187`). This prevents overseas aid from being treated as costless.

Star and Stripe nevertheless moves ahead. Her rationale is relational: her mentor is in trouble (`師が困ってる!`).

The volume therefore ends on the same structure with which it began:

`relationship can create responsibility across institutional boundaries.`

V33 does not yet adjudicate whether Star's choice is strategically wise, legally authorized, successful, or sustainable. Those are V34+ questions.

# 18. Hero-society model revision

V33 permits a stronger model of why prewar hero society was brittle.

The problem was not merely that some heroes were corrupt or that civilians were ungrateful. The system concentrated too many functions into a professional class and one apex Symbol:

- physical protection;
- reassurance;
- legitimacy;
- crisis interpretation;
- public confidence;
- individual moral exemplarity.

Civilians were structurally encouraged to become `客` - customers/spectators. That arrangement works while providers appear inexhaustible. It fails catastrophically when capacity drops.

V33's repair model is not abolition of professional heroism. It is **distributed support around differentiated expertise**:

- heroes still fight threats civilians cannot;
- engineers/administrators build refuge infrastructure;
- peers intervene in rescuer self-neglect;
- civilians provide shelter/tolerance/recognition;
- information custodians preserve evidence;
- international allies may share external burden.

This is more resilient because failure of one node does not imply disappearance of all heroic agency.

The volume resolves a long-running asymmetry. Midoriya has been exceptionally good at seeing need before it is verbalized. His peers now do the same to him.

# 20. Power philosophy revision: OFA is special power without sovereign personhood

V33 changes the philosophical status of One For All again.

Earlier stages:

1. inherited power to be made `僕の力`;
2. plural vestige inheritance;
3. opposed succession model to AFO possession;
4. possible terminal succession in a Quirkless bearer;
5. compositional toolset in V32;
6. **socially connected responsibility in V33**.

The most important constraint is Uraraka's:

`特別な力` does not make `特別な人`.

OFA therefore cannot serve as a sovereignty token granting the bearer unilateral authority over all risk attached to it.

The bearer has special capability and special exposure. Those facts create duties. They do not erase the rights/duties of others in the network.

The image changes from epitaph to relay marker.

The ember metaphor shifts from finite OFA remainder in All Might to transmissible social motive. That is a significant semantic expansion. Physical power can expire while normative influence compounds.
