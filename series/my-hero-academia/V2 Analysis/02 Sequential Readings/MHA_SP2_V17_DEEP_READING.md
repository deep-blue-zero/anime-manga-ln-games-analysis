# My Hero Academia SP2 - Volume 17 Deep Reading

## 0. Reading contract and frozen entry boundary

This reading begins only from the frozen end of Volume 16. The coordinated Hassaikai raid is still in progress; Chisaki is attempting to move Eri through the underground complex; Toga and Twice have been seconded into the Hassaikai operation under an explicitly strategic, grievance-bearing relationship; and the raid team has already been divided by Mimic's spatial control and the Eight Bullets encounters. No later outcome is imported into the reading.

The specific facts held OPEN at entry are therefore: whether Mirio reaches Eri; whether Eri is extracted; what the completed Quirk-destroying projectile does to a named target; whether Nighteye uses Foresight during the decisive phase and what it shows; whether Chisaki is defeated; whether Midoriya's attempt to alter a predicted future succeeds; the full mechanics of Eri's Quirk; and the institutional/personal consequences of the raid.

The V16 governing state was `role-bearing courage / strategic trust under pressure`. V17 tests what remains of a role when the instrument that normally carries it is removed, and what it means to rescue someone whose conditioning has taught her that accepting rescue injures other people.

**Governing transition:**

`role-bearing courage / strategic trust under pressure -> identity beyond power / rescue as restored agency`

Compact formulation:

> **Power can carry a role without constituting the person who bears it. Rescue becomes ethically reciprocal when the endangered person is no longer treated as cargo or material, but can begin choosing, trusting and acting within the rescue itself.**

This formulation is deliberately bounded. V17 does not finish the Chisaki battle and therefore does not yet prove that the predicted future has been overturned or that the raid has succeeded in full.

## 2. Volume-level structural argument

V17 is not best described simply as "the volume where Mirio loses his Quirk" or "the beginning of Infinite 100%." Those are event labels. Structurally, the volume performs three related tests.

First, it separates **hero identity from the possession of a particular instrument**. Mirio has spent years converting Permeation from a dangerous, difficult ability into a highly reliable professional system. Chisaki therefore assumes that destroying the Quirk will retroactively nullify the years that produced Lemillion. The manga rejects that logic in action before it explains it in abstraction: Mirio continues to read opponents, protect Eri, use ordinary movement and striking, absorb bodily cost, and preserve the rescue target after Permeation is gone.

Second, it separates **being rescued from being passively moved by someone stronger**. Eri has been trained to believe that her presence causes injury to others. This belief makes returning to Chisaki look like moral responsibility from inside her conditioned model. V17 therefore cannot resolve her situation through stronger arms alone. Mirio and Midoriya have to oppose the moral premise Chisaki has installed: that other people's pain is proof she should surrender herself. The decisive development in V17 is not a speech proving this premise false; it is Eri's movement back toward the people trying to protect her, followed by the discovery that her own power can participate in the rescue.

Third, it separates **prediction from obligation**. Nighteye's Foresight has become psychologically dangerous to him precisely because he has never observed a future branch after looking far enough ahead. In V17 he sees a disastrous long-form outcome. Midoriya does not possess evidence that the vision is false. His response is not epistemic denial but normative refusal: even if that future is the strongest available forecast, he will continue acting to save Eri and keep people alive. V17 ends before the empirical adjudication of that refusal.

These three tests share one question: **does a description of what a person is - Quirkless, doomed, dangerous, unwanted, useful, weak - determine what that person may become next?** V17's characters answer by acting against reduction, not by pretending limits do not exist.

## 3. Chapters 148-149 - strategic trust is not organizational merger

The opening chapters immediately validate V16's reading of the League-Hassaikai arrangement. Toga and Twice are inside the Hassaikai operation, but they have not psychologically or politically joined Chisaki's organization.

Shigaraki's earlier assignment is recalled in explicitly relational terms. He frames the move as being for himself and his members and tells them that he trusts them. That matters because he is not giving a conventional command whose success depends on perfect obedience. He delegates under conditions of unresolved grievance and expects Toga and Twice to preserve League interests inside a hostile temporary coalition.

Mimic's attempt to regularize the relationship exposes the limit. He asks for information exchange so that cooperation will be easier. Toga refuses full disclosure unless circumstances require it because she still does not like them. This is a highly diagnostic social behavior atom. Her opacity is not random. Information access is relationship-conditioned. She can be tactically cooperative while withholding intimacy, identity-relevant information and trust.

Twice responds differently. He supplies operational detail about Double: duplication depends on sufficiently clear image/data; copied bodies lose durability as reproduction proceeds; and his inability to copy himself is tied to the psychological consequences of his earlier identity fracture. His openness is not evidence that he trusts the Hassaikai more than Toga does. The scene explicitly distinguishes their coping styles: he has reasons to explain the limitation, while Toga makes her dislike and withholding visible.

This gives the relationship ledger a useful asymmetry:

- Shigaraki -> Toga/Twice: high strategic trust, delegated agency, shared League interest.
- Toga/Twice -> Hassaikai: conditional cooperation, unresolved hostility, no merger of identity.
- Toga -> Hassaikai: disclosure is gated by liking/trust.
- Twice -> Hassaikai: relatively direct tactical disclosure can coexist with political hostility.

The later sabotage therefore should not be described as a sudden betrayal of an alliance that had become sincere. It is a predictable consequence of the V16 arrangement: captured pieces can be placed on another side's board without ceasing to belong to their original player.

## 4. Chapters 150-152 - Mirio reaches the child before he reaches victory

Mirio's pursuit is easy to flatten into reckless heroic speed, but the page sequence gives it a more professional logic. The raid group has identified that Chisaki and Eri are moving. Mirio has the mobility and route-reading capacity to close distance fastest. When he reaches them he states the operative purpose plainly: he came to protect the child.

Chisaki tries to control the meaning of the encounter. He insists that Eri does not want Mirio's protection and that Mirio is not a hero to her. Mirio's response is structurally important: the absence of established hero status is not a reason to withdraw; it is precisely why he came. Hero legitimacy is not treated as something the rescuer possesses in advance. It must be made true in relation to the person at risk.

### 4.1 Eri's return is re-read as altruistic self-erasure

Mirio explicitly understands what V15 could only begin to infer. Eri had enough awareness of the situation to choose a return to what he calls hell rather than allow Midoriya and Mirio to be hurt. He describes this as evidence of how kind she is. That wording matters because it refuses Chisaki's interpretation without romanticizing the behavior.

The act is altruistic in motive and catastrophic in result. Eri's care for other people has been weaponized against her. Her moral sensitivity does not protect her from coercion; it supplies the coercer with leverage.

This is a central model distinction for Eri:

- desire to reduce harm to others: directly observed;
- belief that surrendering herself is the appropriate way to reduce that harm: abuse-conditioned current state;
- stable preference for self-destruction: **not** established and should not be inferred as personality.

### 4.2 Mirio's self-model makes weakness a premise, not an exception

Mirio's self-understanding is clarified before the Quirk-loss event. His weaknesses were not hidden from him, and because he accepted them he could continue training. The key formulation is that weakness is a premise. Lemillion is therefore not an identity built on feeling invulnerable.

This retrospectively strengthens V14. His elite Permeation use did not demonstrate that a good successor is someone without weakness. It demonstrated that an acknowledged weakness can become a training problem rather than a verdict on identity.

His Eri-facing goal is equally specific. He cannot accept a future in which she remains unable to smile. This is broader than extraction but narrower than later full recovery. At V17, smiling functions as a relational marker of a life no longer organized exclusively around fear and pain.

### 4.3 Herohood becomes a promise under observation

Mirio's language during the fight makes professional identity performative in the strongest sense. He says he will become Eri's hero and promises not to let Chisaki touch her again. The sentence is not a claim that Eri already recognizes him as a symbol. It binds his future behavior to the protection she can observe.

The volume then attacks the instrument through which that promise has normally been carried.

## 5. Nemoto - loyalty formed by recognition inside an abusive organization

Shin Nemoto becomes unexpectedly important to the V2 correction of Chisaki. His Confession Quirk has made ordinary social trust difficult: people distance themselves because he can expose concealed truths, and he recalls simply wanting a friend he could trust.

Chisaki's recruitment gives him a form of recognition. Chisaki asks him to become a companion/subordinate and tells him that having him present is reassuring. Nemoto interprets this as being specifically needed by the young head and binds himself to that recognition.

This does not redeem Chisaki. It does, however, reject a crude model in which every Hassaikai subordinate follows only through fear and every Chisaki relationship is emotionally empty.

The more precise model is:

1. Chisaki can identify a person's utility and address that utility in personally meaningful language.
2. A recipient starved for trust or purpose can experience that address as genuine recognition.
3. The resulting loyalty can be psychologically sincere even when the larger organization is violent and the leader's moral model is radically instrumental.
4. Chisaki's capacity for selective recognition does not generalize into respect for personhood. Eri remains the clearest counterexample: he reduces her body, pain and power to project resources.

This distinction will matter in later villain modeling. "Capable of attachment-producing behavior" is not equivalent to "care-centered." Instrumentality and relationship can coexist.

## 6. The completed projectile - bodily personhood converted into permanent loss

V17 crosses the evidentiary threshold that V15 and V16 approached. The completed Quirk-destroying round is no longer a prototype hypothesis.

The text distinguishes temporary earlier shots from the completed product. Earlier effects could wear off; the completed ammunition is described as capable of fully and permanently eliminating a Quirk. Only five completed rounds have been refined, and the process is slow enough to require roughly a month plus a controlled laboratory environment.

This matters in three ways.

First, Chisaki's project is not abstract biotechnology. It has moved into scarce, high-value production with explicit tactical use.

Second, the bullet embodies his broader reduction of people. Eri's biological state is not merely researched; her bodily property is converted into a portable effect that can be imposed on someone else's identity and career.

Third, the attack on Mirio is not simply an attack on combat effectiveness. Chisaki recognizes that Eri can be targeted to force a protective hero into the line of fire. He uses care as a control surface.

## 7. Eri's pain response is learned bodily prediction

One of V17's strongest behavioral observations occurs immediately before the shot. Mirio notices that Eri's reaction to imminent violence does not resemble a normal child's attempt to avoid danger. Her body prepares to endure pain she expects she cannot escape.

The narration interprets this as habit: she braces for pain she believes must simply be accepted and endured. Chisaki knows this pattern because the environment that produced it is his.

For later character reconstruction, this should not be converted into the trait "Eri is passive." It is a state-conditioned sensorimotor expectation:

- threat cue -> anticipate unavoidable pain;
- anticipated pain -> brace/endure rather than expect rescue;
- other people endangered -> prefer self-surrender if it may stop their injury.

These are acquired policies. V17 simultaneously begins to provide counter-evidence that they can change.

## 8. Mirio after Permeation - identity beyond power

The projectile hits Mirio because he protects Eri rather than allowing the shot to reach her. The critical analytical fact is what happens afterward.

Chisaki taunts him that the power and years he cultivated have been reduced to nothing. The manga then refuses the premise through Mirio's body.

He continues to predict movement, fight at close range, interpose himself between Eri and attack, and use accumulated combat timing. A later narration gives the elapsed structure: after the completed round enters him, he fights for minutes in an overwhelmingly unfavorable condition, including a period without his Quirk at all.

His declaration is therefore not empty morale. He insists that everything before this has not become meaningless and that he is still Lemillion.

This is one of the clearest power-philosophy results in the reread so far:

> **A Quirk can be constitutive of a practiced technique without being constitutive of moral identity.**

V14 showed that Permeation mastery required enormous integration. V17 now shows that the resulting person has capacities that outlive the mechanism: observation, timing, pain tolerance, target priority, courage, tactical commitment and a relationship-specific promise to Eri.

The result should not be overstated into "Quirks do not matter." Losing Permeation is catastrophic. Mirio becomes less capable and is gravely injured. The stronger claim is that the loss does not retroactively erase the self authored through training.

This is sufficient to promote Mirio from `moderate` to `strong` model readiness. V17 supplies childhood aspiration, family context, skill acquisition, mentor/peer relationship, victim-facing behavior, catastrophic loss, pain/exhaustion response and action without his defining Quirk.

## 9. Chapter 153 - rescue arrives through distributed persistence

When the separated lines converge, the volume avoids a single-savior structure. Mirio has held the target, Midoriya's group has continued through spatial obstruction, Aizawa contributes Erasure and professional control, Nighteye brings prediction/command experience, and the larger operation continues to route bodies and information.

Mirio's achievement is therefore not "he nearly soloed the arc." He preserved the rescue state long enough for a distributed operation to reconnect with him.

Midoriya's arrival also matters relationally. He sees what Mirio has paid, but the scene does not reduce that payment to a succession score. The immediate obligation remains Eri.

This maintains the V13-V16 institutional thesis: mature heroism increasingly looks like a network of incomplete capacities that must arrive in the right sequence.

## 10. Chapter 154 - Nighteye's Foresight becomes an epistemic wound

V15 established the formal limits and professional doctrine of Foresight. V17 reveals why Nighteye treats long-range prediction as dangerous.

He recalls trying to act differently after seeing a future. The observed sequence would accommodate local action as though extra frames had been inserted, but the terminal path did not branch in the way he hoped. After seeing All Might's predicted death, he stopped casually looking far into other people's futures.

This creates an unresolved epistemic possibility in Nighteye's own mind: perhaps seeing a future does not simply report it; perhaps the act of observation is somehow entangled with fixing the person's path. V17 does not establish that theory as fact. It establishes that **Nighteye fears it enough to govern his use of Foresight around it**.

During combat he restricts himself to very short tactical prediction - roughly the next second - because this gives usable action information without forcing him to confront a long fixed endpoint. Under escalating pressure he looks farther.

The future he sees is disastrous: he and Midoriya die while Chisaki escapes. This vision becomes the volume's strongest apparent constraint.

### 10.1 Midoriya does not refute the evidence

Midoriya cannot disprove Nighteye's vision. He has neither a better predictive instrument nor prior evidence of a branched Foresight outcome. His response is instead a commitment: he will twist that future.

This is an important correction to a loose "Midoriya believes fate is fake" reading. He does not have epistemic warrant for that statement in V17. His heroism here is action under a terrible forecast.

The distinction is:

- Nighteye: forecast is highly credible and emotionally burdened by prior non-branching observations.
- Midoriya: credible forecast does not cancel present rescue obligation.
- V17 outcome: not yet adjudicated.

Whether the forecast is actually broken belongs to V18 unless V18 independently establishes it.

## 11. Chapters 155-156 - Chisaki weaponizes Eri's morality against her

Chisaki's most effective control over Eri is not physical possession alone. He repeatedly tells her that other people will die because of her and asks whether that is what she wants.

The pressure works because it attaches to a value Eri actually has: she does not want other people hurt.

When she says she will go back and asks for everyone to be restored to normal, the scene must not be read as evidence that she freely prefers Chisaki. It is the same coercive decision policy V15 and early V17 have established: if accepting pain herself seems to reduce harm to others, she chooses herself as the sacrifice.

Chisaki recognizes and names the mechanism with chilling accuracy: it is easier for her to be hurt than to watch others be hurt because of her.

### 11.1 "Not wanted" versus observable pursuit

The rescue conflict then pivots on demand. Chisaki insists, in effect, that Eri is not asking for this rescue. That claim has superficial force because her conditioned speech/action has repeatedly been surrender-oriented.

But Midoriya and Mirio do not treat coercively manufactured refusal as a complete account of desire. They also do not erase her agency by declaring a hidden preference without evidence. Instead, the manga gives Eri new evidence: people keep returning despite cost; Mirio fought after losing Permeation; Midoriya explicitly says he will save her; the rescuers do not interpret her existence as contamination.

Eri's movement toward Midoriya becomes the critical behavioral delta. Rescue begins to become something she participates in rather than something others attempt over her objections.

This justifies promoting Eri from `emerging` to `moderate`, but no further. V17 now gives multiple coercive contexts, relationship-conditioned behavior, pain expectation, altruistic motive, early family/Quirk history, flight/return choices and active movement toward rescue. Ordinary life, broad language baseline and post-trauma recovery remain too narrow for `strong`.

## 12. Eri's origin - the Quirk is mutation, not a moral defect

V17 provides the first substantial causal history of Eri's Quirk.

Her maternal family history contains no comparable power. The Quirk is framed as a mutation unlike either family line. As a very young child, without control or understanding, she causes her father to disappear when he reaches toward her. Her mother interprets this through curse language and abandons her to the Hassaikai elder.

Several later abuse structures become legible from this origin.

1. Eri begins with an event she could not understand or control.
2. An adult moralizes the event as evidence that the child is cursed.
3. Chisaki later converts the unexplained ability into a technical object of study.
4. Repeated bodily experimentation teaches Eri that her body causes harm and belongs under external control.

The manga therefore separates causal danger from moral blame. Her Quirk can be extraordinarily dangerous while the child remains non-culpable for its uncontrolled manifestation.

## 13. Rewind - from destructive label to process model

The Quirk is explicitly named Rewind. The text distinguishes it from Chisaki's disassembly/restoration. It does not merely heal in the ordinary sense. It returns a living body toward an earlier state.

That model explains both the danger and the possibility.

Eri cannot yet control the rate. Chisaki describes this lack of control as a reason she is dangerous. Midoriya experiences the process directly: after using 100% and expecting catastrophic fracture, he discovers that the injury has already been returned toward an earlier state. He realizes the body is continuously being rewound.

The key ethical and tactical reversal is that the same power Chisaki extracted into anti-Quirk ammunition can be understood relationally as an ongoing process whose rate can be interacted with.

Midoriya's reasoning is not "Eri will heal me for free." It is a dangerous rate-matching hypothesis: if Rewind continuously returns his body, then continually damaging his body through 100% OFA may prevent Rewind from carrying him too far backward.

Chisaki calls the Quirk frightening and uncontrollable. Midoriya, after experiencing it, calls attention to its gentleness because it returned his broken body before pain could fully register. Both observations concern real properties; the difference lies in what kind of relation each speaker constructs around the power.

Chisaki asks: how can this be controlled and converted into a project?

Midoriya asks: can I coordinate with the person who has it so we can survive the present rescue?

## 14. Chapter 157 - Infinite 100% begins as coordination, not victory

The chapter title announces Infinite 100%, but the volume's actual endpoint must be preserved exactly.

Midoriya rejects Chisaki's demand that he stop. He infers the approximate rate of Rewind from the speed with which his new injuries disappear and proposes maintaining catastrophic OFA output continuously so that Rewind has damage to reverse instead of rewinding him beyond a safe bodily state.

This is an inversion of the long-running OFA body problem.

Earlier:

`OFA output > Midoriya's bodily capacity -> self-destruction`

At the V17 endpoint:

`Rewind rate > stable present body unless countered`

Midoriya proposes:

`continuous 100% self-damage <-> continuous Rewind`

The important point is not that this is safe. It is not. The point is that Midoriya turns two individually dangerous processes into a provisional coupled system.

This extends the series' power philosophy away from solitary mastery. His current maximum is possible only because another person's power is present, and he explicitly addresses that person rather than treating her as an object. The final line asks Eri to lend him her power.

The wording matters. The V17 endpoint is a request for cooperation.

It is **not yet** evidence that:

- Chisaki is defeated;
- the Foresight future has been disproven;
- Eri controls Rewind independently;
- Infinite 100% is sustainable outside this paired emergency;
- the raid is complete.

All five remain OPEN for V18.

## 15. Midoriya - model update

### State delta

Midoriya remains `strong`. V17 strengthens him in three directions: anti-fatalistic action under credible negative prediction, relationship-sensitive reading of coerced refusal, and real-time coupling of his bodily-risk model with another person's uncontrolled Quirk.

### Decision evidence

When Nighteye reports a future in which they die and Chisaki escapes, Midoriya does not switch from rescue to resignation. This is not ignorance of the forecast. He acts after being told.

When Eri offers to return, he does not interpret her self-sacrificial choice as dispositive evidence that rescue is unwanted. He uses the accumulated context - her fear, prior return, Chisaki's threats and Mirio's sacrifice - to distinguish expressed surrender from uncoerced preference.

When Rewind begins affecting him, he analyzes bodily evidence under pressure and forms a rate-matching solution rather than merely increasing force.

### Relationship-specific update: Eri

The relation moves from failed first contact and renewed rescue commitment toward active cooperation. The final request for her power is important because Midoriya's plan depends on Eri without reducing her to a tool.

### Contradiction / uncertainty

The 100% maneuver still uses self-damage as a resource. V17 therefore does not resolve Midoriya's martyr tendency. It temporarily makes self-damage functionally compatible with another person's power. Whether this teaches sustainability or merely enables a new extreme remains OPEN.

## 16. Mirio - model update

### State delta

Mirio advances `moderate -> strong`.

### New context coverage

V17 provides direct evidence across:

- childhood aspiration;
- parental/family caution;
- difficult Quirk acquisition;
- long-term training identity;
- solo pursuit under command conditions;
- victim-facing reassurance;
- catastrophic permanent-seeming capability loss;
- combat without his Quirk;
- severe injury/exhaustion;
- mentor/peer rescue convergence.

### Decision heuristic

His central policy is not "always go alone." It is preserve the rescue target and use his comparative movement advantage when delay threatens extraction. Once separated, he continues until the network can reconnect.

### Stress response

Loss of Permeation does not produce role collapse. He experiences the loss but continues applying non-Quirk capacities.

### Speech model

His Eri-facing language is declarative but future-binding: he will become her hero, will not let Chisaki touch her, and will not let her remain unable to smile. This differs from status assertion toward peers; it is promise language toward a frightened child.

## 17. Eri - model update

### State delta

Eri advances `emerging -> moderate`.

### Motivational hierarchy

The best-grounded current motive is harm minimization for others, even at severe cost to herself. This is not a healthy stable value hierarchy; it is care filtered through coercive conditioning.

### Threat response

She braces for unavoidable pain rather than expecting escape. This is explicitly treated as learned habit.

### Relationship response

Repeated non-exploitative return by Mirio and Midoriya begins changing the action space available to her. She moves toward rescue even after Chisaki reactivates the "people will die because of you" frame.

### Quirk/embodiment

Rewind is dangerous, mutation-derived and uncontrolled. It can return living bodies toward earlier states. V17 does not establish conscious fine control.

### Knowledge boundary

Eri does not possess a technical model of Rewind comparable to Chisaki's research or Midoriya's emergency inference. Do not simulate her as understanding the rate-matching mechanism analytically merely because the reader does.

## 18. Chisaki - model update

Chisaki advances `moderate -> strong`.

V17 supplies breadth that was previously missing:

- public/private organizational behavior;
- selective recruitment capable of producing sincere loyalty;
- tactical adaptation against Mirio;
- willingness to target Eri specifically to exploit a protector's care;
- permanent Quirk-destruction logistics;
- body fusion/disassembly as combat escalation;
- explicit victim-conditioning language;
- Eri origin/Quirk knowledge;
- a worldview that labels Quirk-driven aspiration itself as a pathology;
- direct interaction with Nighteye/Midoriya under existential project threat.

The important refinement is that Chisaki's dehumanization is **selective and project-governed**, not a total inability to recognize anyone. He can tell Nemoto he is needed and create attachment. With Eri, however, recognition is subordinated to function. Her kindness, fear and bodily pain become control surfaces.

## 19. Nighteye - model update

Nighteye remains `moderate`, strongly strengthened rather than promoted automatically.

V17 adds a new high-stakes regime and a major epistemic contradiction: he values evidence and probability, yet his long-range Foresight experiences have created a fear that observation may harden fate. He therefore self-limits prediction use even when the Quirk would appear powerful.

This is rich evidence, but ordinary relational breadth remains narrower than Mirio's. Hold `strong` until the aftermath clarifies how he processes the prediction/outcome and his relationship to Midoriya/Mirio after the decisive result.

## 20. Toga, Twice and Shigaraki - coalition modeling update

Shigaraki remains `strong`. His trust in Toga/Twice is strengthened as delegated agency rather than command-and-control obedience.

Toga remains `insufficient/emerging (strongly grounded)`. V17 adds a high-value rule: information disclosure is conditioned by liking/trust. Her later tactical manipulation inside the Hassaikai space is consistent with League allegiance.

Twice advances from `emerging (strongly grounded)` to `moderate`. V17 adds direct Quirk mechanics, self-duplication limitation, trauma-conditioned disclosure, coalition behavior and coordinated deception/sabotage. Combined with V12 origin/self-coherence evidence, this materially broadens context and language coverage.

## 21. Nemoto - bounded new model

Nemoto enters `emerging (strongly grounded)`.

Supported dimensions:

- social wound created by Confession-mediated truth exposure;
- desire for trustworthy friendship;
- strong responsiveness to being specifically needed;
- loyal interpretation of Chisaki recruitment;
- tactical willingness to attack Eri as leverage against Mirio;
- language of duty toward the young head.

Do not infer broad social warmth, ordinary baseline or independent moral philosophy beyond these contexts.

## 22. Power philosophy - four reductions and four refusals

V17 can be read as a sequence of reduction attempts.

### Reduction 1: Mirio = Permeation
Chisaki: remove the Quirk and the cultivated self becomes nothing.

**Refusal:** Mirio continues as Lemillion through learned capacities and role commitment.

### Reduction 2: Eri = dangerous body / production source
Chisaki: her uncontrolled power and biological material justify external control.

**Refusal:** Midoriya interprets the same power as something that can be coordinated with through Eri rather than extracted from her.

### Reduction 3: predicted future = completed moral decision
Nighteye's fear: if the future does not branch, action may only move inside an already fixed film.

**Refusal:** Midoriya treats prediction as relevant evidence without granting it authority to cancel rescue obligation.

### Reduction 4: surrender = authentic preference
Chisaki: Eri says she will return; therefore rescue is unwanted.

**Refusal:** the manga contextualizes the surrender as coercively produced and then shows Eri moving toward the rescuers when given new relational evidence.

The volume does not pretend material constraints are unreal. Mirio really loses his Quirk. Eri really lacks control. Nighteye really sees a disastrous future. Eri really says she will return. What changes is the inference from those facts to personhood and possibility.

## 23. Visual/form analysis

### 23.1 Black fields and future-as-film

Nighteye's Foresight material repeatedly uses isolated black fields and film-strip imagery. The visual grammar makes prediction feel like already-developed footage: events are not hypothetical diagrams but apparently completed sequences available for viewing. This helps explain why his fatalism is not merely intellectual pessimism.

### 23.2 Mirio after Quirk loss - visual continuity of posture

The pages after Permeation is destroyed retain Mirio's protective body positioning and forward movement. The costume is damaged and the body is increasingly bloodied, but the spatial relation to Eri remains recognizable. The role survives visually before it is declared verbally.

### 23.3 Eri between frames of command and approach

Eri is often isolated in vertical or central panels while Chisaki's large speech balloons define what she "should" do. As she begins moving toward Midoriya, the page grammar changes from confinement by instruction toward directional motion across intervening space. Agency is small in scale but formally legible.

### 23.4 Rewind as reversal without cleanliness

Rewind is not drawn as simple medical healing. Midoriya's injuries appear to disappear while energy/line effects make the body unstable. The visual result preserves ambiguity: restoration is occurring, but control is not.

### 23.5 Final page - paired bodies, separate agency

The final 100% composition places Eri physically with Midoriya but gives her face and attention separate visibility. The endpoint line is a request, not narration over an inert object. This is the visual/linguistic completion of V17's move from extraction toward cooperation.

## 24. Japanese language and register observations

The volume supplies several high-value register patterns even where the present artifact paraphrases them in English for compactness.

- Shigaraki to Toga/Twice uses direct trust language (`ore wa omaetachi o shinjiteru`) while framing the mission as serving both himself and them. This is leader-to-member coalition intimacy, not Chisaki-style subordinate command.
- Toga tells the Hassaikai she will disclose more only if needed because she still does not like them. Her childlike/casual surface voice can therefore carry precise social boundaries.
- Mirio repeatedly uses promise/declaration forms with Eri: becoming her hero, not letting her be touched, not letting her remain unable to smile.
