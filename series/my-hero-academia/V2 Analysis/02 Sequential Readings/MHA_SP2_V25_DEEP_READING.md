# 1. Governing transition: recognition becomes mobilization

Volume 24 treated personhood as contested jurisdiction. Toga, Twice, Spinner, Re-Destro, Shigaraki, and the Shimura household were all caught between external stories about what they were and their own attempts to define themselves.

Volume 25 asks what happens **after a person, danger, desire, or social fact has been recognized**.

The governing transition is:

> **contested personhood -> recognition as mobilization / identity converted into organized capacity**

Compact formulation:

> **V25 repeatedly shows recognition allocating people, power, and institutions. Ordinary adults see Tenko but fail to convert perception into rescue. AFO recognizes Tenko's violent impulse and converts it into an authored successor identity. Re-Destro recognizes Shigaraki as the realized liberation his movement idealized and converts defeat into institutional transfer. Ujiko recognizes Shigaraki as a successor worth investing in and converts allegiance into technical augmentation. Hawks recognizes the scale of the new threat and converts covert access into coded warning. The Commission converts partial threat recognition into mass student internships, and Endeavor converts that warning into responsibility for the next generation. Recognition is therefore ethically underdetermined: what matters is what system it mobilizes.**

This extends one of the strongest V24 findings. Giran had already demonstrated that genuine recognition can route a vulnerable person into a criminal community. V25 generalizes the principle from interpersonal intervention to whole institutions.

The volume contains several sharply different conversions:

`seeing -> passing by`;

`seeing -> grooming`;

`seeing -> surrendering authority`;

`seeing -> intelligence transmission`;

`seeing -> training / preparation`.

The moral distinction is not whether someone was “recognized.” It is what the recognizer does with that knowledge, whose agency the response preserves, and what capacities become possible afterward.

The structural symmetry between villain and hero systems is therefore real without being morally equivalent. Both sides are building distributed capacity. Shigaraki acquires army, money, ideology, operators, Machia, and Ujiko's procedure. Hero society responds through covert intelligence, Commission coordination, internship networks, professional supervision, and student growth. But the legitimacy structures differ radically: one side consolidates around coercive/destructive command and charismatic abdication; the other attempts distributed preparedness inside a regulated public institution, even while secrecy and surveillance create their own ethical costs.

# 2. No.236 — the Shimura catastrophe: accident, intent, and the first chosen killing

## 2.1 The volume immediately resolves the V24 boundary without flattening the sequence

No.236 opens on the exact image V24 withheld: Mon disintegrates under Tenko's touch (`V25:p006-008`). Tenko's first response is confusion and panic, not articulated murderous purpose.

Hana approaches him, apologizes for exposing him to Kotaro, and is caught by the spreading Quirk (`V25:p009-011`). Tenko's mother and grandparents enter the expanding catastrophe as he remains overwhelmed and does not yet understand the full mechanics of what his body is doing.

This distinction is essential because later Shigaraki narration contains relief, hatred, and retrospective speculation about what he may have wished for. Those later feelings must not be projected backward to make every death equally intentional.

The most defensible V25 sequence is:

1. Decay manifests catastrophically on Mon;
2. the Quirk propagates through family contact/environment while Tenko is panicked and uncomprehending;
3. the household is destroyed through a mixture of uncontrolled power and escalating emotional state;
4. Kotaro confronts/strikes Tenko;
5. **only then does the narration explicitly mark a deliberate homicidal transition.**

## 2.2 Kotaro's death is source-explicit intentional patricide

At `V25:p017`, after Kotaro attacks and Tenko cries out to his father, the narration says:

> `明確な殺意をもって`
>
> `父に触れた`

This is unusually unambiguous language: Tenko touches his father **with clear murderous intent**.

That line should govern all later causal summaries. It rejects two opposite simplifications.

First, the entire family catastrophe cannot be described as a single deliberate massacre. The earlier deaths occur under a different knowledge and control state.

Second, the entire catastrophe cannot be described as pure accident. By the time Tenko reaches Kotaro, the manga deliberately marks a change from uncontrolled disaster to chosen lethal action.

The formation model therefore requires **state transition inside the event itself** rather than one label applied to the whole scene.

## 2.3 Relief after violence is real evidence, not retroactive proof of prior intent

After Kotaro's death, Tenko reports:

> `途方もない快感が`
>
> `全身を貫いた`

and then that he no longer feels the itching that had accompanied the household pressure.

The narration also allows the disturbing possibility that somewhere inside him he had wanted a catastrophic release like this.

This is strong evidence that the killing of Kotaro is experienced as release from an intolerable structure. It is also direct evidence that destructive action becomes somatically reinforcing.

But retrospective recognition of desire is not the same thing as proving conscious intent for every previous death. V25 is careful enough to let both truths coexist:

> **Tenko did not begin the event as a fully self-aware killer, and he does cross a threshold into intentional killing before the event ends.**

That is a much stronger villain-formation model than either “innocent child accidentally kills everyone” or “he always wanted to kill his family.”

## 2.4 `痒み` becomes psychologically legible without becoming fully mechanistic

V24 had associated Tenko's itching strongly with household stress but kept physiological explanation open. V25 now shows the itching disappear after the violent release.

This strengthens a stress/affect association, but the source still does not warrant a simple biomedical equation in which itching is nothing more than suppressed Decay or hatred. The safer current formulation is:

`domestic stress / suppressed desire -> recurrent itching`;

`violent release / removal of household restraint -> itching ceases`.

The precise Quirk-physiology relation remains only partly specified.

# 3. No.237 — the child who is seen but not claimed

## 3.1 “No one reached out” is finally admissible V2 evidence

After killing his family, Tenko wanders through public space visibly injured, bloodied, dirty, and disoriented (`V25:p022-024`).

Importantly, the manga does **not** depict a magical invisibility in which nobody notices him. People do notice.

One woman initially addresses him as a distressed child. Other adults look. But perception does not mature into sustained responsibility. The social response collapses into hesitation, avoidance, and the expectation that some appropriate person or institution will deal with him.

The counterfactual is then stated directly:

> `あの時もし`
>
> `誰か手を差し伸べてくれてたら`

This is the first admissible V2 point for the famous failed-rescue thesis.

The precise failure is more interesting than “society ignored him.” It is:

> **recognition without ownership of responsibility.**

A child is legible as distressed, but ordinary observers do not convert recognition into intervention.

This belongs beside V07's Hosu public-trust infrastructure, V18's ordinary intervention problem, and V22's “who rescues the rescuer?” question. Hero society can possess specialized professional rescue institutions while simultaneously weakening the felt obligation of ordinary people to become the first responsible actor.

## 3.2 The rescue vacuum is not itself a complete cause of Shigaraki

The bystander scene is morally damning, but causal discipline matters.

By this point Tenko has already:

- lived under Kotaro's anti-hero household rule;
- experienced delayed adult intervention;
- manifested Decay catastrophically;
- killed several family members unintentionally/uncontrollably;
- deliberately killed Kotaro;
- experienced intense relief from that intentional violence.

The public failure does not create all of those conditions. What it does is leave the post-catastrophe child **unclaimed at a decisive routing point**.

That makes the next adult who takes responsibility disproportionately powerful.

And the next adult is All For One.

# 4. No.237 — All For One: recognition engineered into grooming

## 4.1 AFO succeeds at the exact action ordinary society failed to perform

At `V25:p025`, an adult hand reaches Tenko.

Formally, the image is brutal because the act itself resembles rescue. Someone stops. Someone touches him. Someone takes him in.

The manga therefore refuses a simplistic recognition ethic in which “reaching out” is automatically salvific. AFO performs the missing intervention and then uses the resulting asymmetry to author the child's moral world.

The correct contrast is not:

`nobody saw Tenko -> AFO saw him`.

It is:

`others saw distress but did not assume responsibility -> AFO assumed responsibility in order to control what the rescued child would become`.

## 4.2 AFO's pedagogy explicitly dismantles external moral authority

At `V25:p026-027`, AFO tells Tenko that he does not need to endure or suppress what he feels. Then the language becomes philosophically explicit:

> `善悪・道徳・倫理`
>
> `全部誰かが作ったモノだ`

and:

> `君の中にある衝動が`
>
> `何より大切なんだよ`

followed by:

> `縛られるな`
>
> `恐れるな`

and the decisive question:

> `君はどうしたい`

Tenko answers that he wants to kill the two men who attacked him.

This is not neutral self-expression counseling. AFO first delegitimizes moral norms as external inventions, then elevates internal impulse above them, then asks the child to formulate desire, then gives him permission and material conditions to enact it.

The pedagogical sequence is:

`hurt / unclaimed child -> validation -> external morality discredited -> impulse sacralized -> desire verbalized -> violence enacted -> violence affirmed`.

This is much more specific than “AFO groomed Shigaraki.”

## 4.3 AFO does not erase agency; he trains the conditions under which agency is exercised

Tenko's answer `殺したい` is his own stated desire. AFO does not puppeteer the words.

That matters because grooming analysis should not convert Shigaraki into a passive automaton. AFO's power lies precisely in **selective affirmation**: he finds a child whose violent impulse is already real, removes competing normative restraints, rewards destructive enactment, and constructs a social world in which that impulse becomes the privileged index of authenticity.

The resulting agency is therefore both real and conditioned.

The series is again refusing monocausal innocence and monocausal evil.

## 4.4 The hands function as anti-healing technology inside this pedagogy

V24 established AFO's instruction to keep the family hands close so grief and rage would not naturally fade. No.237 places that practice beside the explicit moral pedagogy.

The hands do two jobs:

- they preserve bodily/emotional access to the traumatic past;
- they keep the new identity from stabilizing through ordinary forgetting, reinterpretation, or distance.

AFO is therefore not merely giving Shigaraki a costume motif. He is controlling memory ecology.

The successor is kept close to the emotional state from which AFO wants him to act.

## 4.5 `死柄木弔` is an authored identity inside a dependency relationship

At `V25:p033-034`, AFO names Tenko.

He explicitly identifies `死柄木` as **his own surname** and supplies the new given name `弔`.

This makes “Shigaraki Tomura” qualitatively different from a hero name chosen through self-authorship, aspiration, peer history, or public role. It is an identity granted by the adult who has just taught Tenko how to understand desire, violence, and morality.

AFO also promises a form of total acceptance:

> `僕は全てを肯定してあげよう`

paired with:

> `壊すことに臆病になるな`

Acceptance and destructive conditioning are fused.

That gives V25 one of its strongest recognition paradoxes:

> **AFO gives Tenko the unconditional affirmation his prior environment lacked, but makes that affirmation contingent on a worldview that converts destructive impulse into destiny.**

This is why “Midoriya was seen by All Might / Shigaraki was seen by AFO” remains a useful formal contrast only if the asymmetry is preserved. All Might's recognition is imperfect and institutionally complicated, but it ultimately asks Midoriya to develop judgment beyond imitation. AFO's recognition claims to free Tenko while narrowing the meaning of authenticity around violence.

# 5. No.238–239 — Deika: liberation recognizes its own radical endpoint

## 5.1 Shigaraki's expanded Decay changes the scale of political conflict

The resumed Deika fight turns Shigaraki's Quirk from local contact lethality into city-scale connected destruction (`V25:p038-062`). Buildings, streets, clustered bodies, and connected material become propagation surfaces.

The visual field makes the change unmistakable. Re-Destro's stress output and specialized support technology can still oppose Shigaraki physically, but the political movement's carefully organized city is being destroyed by a capability that spreads through the infrastructure it controls.

This matters thematically because the MLA has spent two volumes arguing that Quirks are authentic expressions of personhood that should not be suppressed. Shigaraki confronts them with an expression whose unrestricted form destroys the institutional environment needed for their politics to exist.

## 5.2 Re-Destro's defeat is interpretive before it is administrative

The V1 phrase “Shigaraki defeats the MLA because his emptiness is more absolute” is too blunt for the actual source.

Re-Destro does not merely lose a power contest. He **recognizes Shigaraki through his own ideology**.

By `V25:p063-066`, physically maimed and facing death, Re-Destro returns to the movement's core language of free ability-use without suppression. What he sees in Shigaraki is not simply nihilism. He sees a person acting with a degree of unrestrained freedom that the liberation movement has elevated as an ideal.

That produces a paradoxical surrender:

> the ideological leader submits because the enemy appears to instantiate the ideology more completely than the leader himself.

This is why the transfer is not reducible to conquest.

## 5.3 `異能解放軍は お前の後についていく`: allegiance is offered, not merely seized

At `V25:p067`, Re-Destro states:

> `異能解放軍は`
>
> `お前の後についていく`

The language matters. The Liberation Army will follow Shigaraki.

Shigaraki's immediate response then prevents the scene from becoming mystical succession theater. He asks the practical question:

> `おまえ 社長だから 金あるよな!`

This is extremely diagnostic Shigaraki behavior.

He can accept symbolic elevation without becoming reverent toward the symbolism. What interests him immediately is usable infrastructure: money, resources, manpower, mobility, and the capacity to fulfill comrades' desires while pursuing destruction.

The character model therefore needs both components:

- **charismatic/ideological recognition from others**;
- **instrumental irreverence toward the ideological apparatus he receives**.

## 5.4 Gigantomachia's reaction closes an earlier succession question

Gigantomachia's arrival allows AFO's intended successor structure to confront Shigaraki's now-demonstrated power and following.

V23 had established that AFO could designate an heir without automatically transferring Machia's loyalty. V25 materially changes that state: Shigaraki has now become powerful enough, persistent enough, and institutionally consequential enough to satisfy the inherited-retainer problem to a new degree.

The important succession principle is preserved:

> **designation created the opportunity; demonstrated successor capacity earns functional recognition.**

# 6. No.240 — the Paranormal Liberation Front: identity becomes organization

## 6.1 The public story immediately diverges from actual causality

One week after Deika, the public narrative is already being normalized around a simplified account of villain attack and citizen resistance (`V25:p070-073`).

The actual causal structure—the Meta Liberation Army's prepared city, its mass membership, its ideological project, its fight with the League, and the subsequent transfer of allegiance—is not visible to ordinary public audiences.

This strengthens a long-running MHA institutional distinction:

> **publicly legible hero/villain narratives can be operationally useful while remaining incomplete descriptions of causality.**

Hosu supplied one version through official suppression of student involvement. Deika supplies another through a covert political movement that can actively manufacture the public account of its own transformation.

## 6.2 Re-Destro publicly frames surrender as `必然の譲位`

At `V25:p075-079`, Re-Destro addresses the surviving movement and announces its rebirth.

He calls Shigaraki a true liberator and describes the transfer as:

> `必然の譲位である!`

This is not presented to followers as humiliating collapse. Re-Destro gives the transfer ideological legitimacy by interpreting Shigaraki as the movement's fulfilled principle.

The act does three things simultaneously:

1. preserves follower continuity;
2. transfers command legitimacy;
3. narratively reconciles defeat with Destro's inherited mission.

Succession is therefore achieved partly by **rewriting defeat as doctrinal completion**.

## 6.3 `超常解放戦線`: fusion without ideological conversion

The League and Liberation Army are fused under a new name:

> `超常解放戦線`

The new command structure mixes League and former MLA personnel rather than simply dissolving one group into the other.

Shigaraki's response remains revealing:

> `まあ 名前なんて これと同じ 飾りだ`
>
> `好きにやろう`

Names and ideological labels are ornaments to him in a way they were not to Re-Destro.

This should not be read as proof that ideology is irrelevant. Re-Destro's ideology is exactly what allows tens of thousands of adherents to accept the transfer. But Shigaraki himself does not need to become a doctrinal liberation theorist in order to command the resulting infrastructure.

The organization therefore contains a deliberate mismatch:

`institutional legitimacy inherited from liberation ideology`

+

`supreme leader whose own horizon remains generalized destruction and comrades' freedom`.

That mismatch is analytically more important than treating the PLF as a single coherent political mind.

## 6.4 Ujiko's offer resolves the successor-support gate

V23 introduced Ujiko as a keeper of AFO's technical inheritance who refused to support Shigaraki merely because AFO named him successor.

After Deika, that condition changes.

At `V25:p083-085`, Ujiko offers:

> `力を授けよう`
>
> `おまえがそれを望むなら`

Shigaraki has now produced the evidence Ujiko required.

This is a clean payoff to V23's “inheritance is not entitlement” thesis. The successor receives access only after demonstrating a capacity that the inherited institution recognizes as worthy of investment.

# 7. No.241 — hero public communication and controlled Blackwhip

## 7.1 Media competence is operational hero competence

The hero-side return begins with interview training rather than combat. Mt. Lady is brought in to teach students how to answer questions, present their abilities, and communicate reassurance under public scrutiny (`V25:p086-098`).

This is not merely celebrity satire.

Hero work is public coordination. Civilians, colleagues, media, and institutions need to understand what a hero can do, what is happening, and what role the hero is assuming. Public speech therefore becomes another form of professional interface.

Mt. Lady's lesson advances a theme present since the Sports Festival: public legibility can be spectacle, branding, recruitment, and market value, but it can also carry genuine emergency utility.

The mature hero system needs both competence and communicability.

## 7.2 Bakugo's public-speech deficit survives his tactical growth

Bakugo's interview performance remains abrasive, impatient, and hostile to presentational smoothing. His underlying tactical and rescue development from V22 has not magically generalized into media warmth.

This is important reconstruction evidence.

Character growth has been domain-specific:

- team coordination improved;
- rescue compatibility improved;
- reciprocal cover improved;
- public-facing verbal regulation remains poor.

The model should therefore reject a single global “Bakugo got nicer” variable.

## 7.3 Todoroki retains deadpan literalism under low stakes

Todoroki's interview responses preserve a low-temperature, literal social register that can read as unintentionally funny. Nothing in the high-stakes family material has erased this ordinary behavioral baseline.

V25 is valuable because the Christmas/interview chapters place dramatic characters back into mundane interaction before the four-month threat consumes the structure.

## 7.4 Midoriya has converted Blackwhip panic into a selectable small output

At `V25:p099-101`, the Blackwhip question receives its first post-eruption practical answer.

Midoriya explains that for approximately three weeks he has practiced an internal image of a locked mechanism. His reasoning is explicitly constructive: if the power can be “locked,” then opening and closing the lock should mean output can be selected rather than simply suffered.

He produces only a tiny, brief amount of Blackwhip and calls it a first step in control.

This is the first admissible V2 point for **deliberately controlled Blackwhip use**.

The progression is now:

`V22 uncontrolled manifestation -> V23 mechanism/inheritance explanation -> V24 no major Midoriya advance -> V25 deliberate micro-output through image-based regulation`.

That progression should replace hindsight summaries that jump directly from first eruption to later combat mastery.

## 7.5 Midoriya's learning style remains model-building rather than brute repetition

The important evidence is not only that control improves.

Midoriya constructs a conceptual model—lock, unlock, chosen output—and then rehearses against it. This matches his long-running tendency to externalize complex power into analyzable components, borrow metaphors, compare predecessors/peers, and make an otherwise overwhelming task cognitively manipulable.

The new Quirk does not change his basic learning architecture.

# 8. No.242 — Christmas under a quiet mobilization order

## 8.1 The Commission request is abnormal enough that U.A. staff notice

The preceding conversation at `V25:p104-107` makes the institutional background explicit.

Midnight asks whether the Commission is really anticipating a manpower shortage in a society already saturated with heroes. Aizawa remarks that a sudden mass mobilization of students is not normal. Cementoss says this kind of order is unprecedented in his experience. Nezu frames crisis preparedness as ordinary hero responsibility while still recognizing that the scale of the request is unusual.

The result is a subtle mobilization regime:

- the public has not been told the true threat;
- students have not been given complete intelligence;
- institutions nevertheless begin distributing them to experienced heroes as practical preparation.

Hero society is therefore acting on **asymmetric knowledge**.

This is neither ordinary education nor full wartime mobilization. It is an intermediate preparedness state.

## 8.2 U.A. explicitly considers the insider-threat problem without inventing a culprit

Nezu notes that if there is no League spy among the students, then the next meaningful movement must come from the League itself. The statement preserves an unresolved possibility rather than naming a traitor.

This is useful epistemic discipline inside the source: suspicion remains institutionally live, but the manga does not authorize analyst certainty about identity from this scene.

## 8.3 Christmas is not filler because baseline behavior matters

No.242 then turns into a Christmas party (`V25:p108-120`).

That tonal shift is analytically useful. The students are not continuously presented as mobilized assets. They remain teenagers with food, gifts, bad jokes, costumes, teasing, embarrassment, and mundane social preferences.

The page-wide party composition at `V25:p116` is especially important as ensemble baseline evidence: the class has enough accumulated trust to inhabit chaotic shared space without the scene needing to prove a crisis function.

High-stakes hero systems are being built around people whose ordinary social lives still matter.

## 8.4 Eri's holiday confusion becomes evidence of safe participation, not a trauma speech

Eri appears in a Santa outfit and mixes holiday formulas, prompting gentle correction rather than alarm. The scene does not need to announce “recovery” abstractly. Its value lies in ordinary participation: she can enter a noisy class ritual, make a harmless mistake, be corrected, and remain inside the group.

That is a different evidentiary category from her earlier rescue-centered scenes.

## 8.5 Bakugo's unresolved hero name becomes relational obligation to Best Jeanist

At `V25:p111-112`, Bakugo recalls Best Jeanist's earlier lesson:

> `名は願い`

Jeanist had told him that a name expresses how one wants or ought to exist, that Bakugo had not yet truly looked at the wider world, and that when he returned after gaining provisional qualification Jeanist would ask him again.

Bakugo now has a name in mind but says, in effect, that it is not settled for public disclosure while Jeanist is missing.

This materially changes the hero-name thread.

Bakugo is no longer simply rejecting reflective naming. He has accepted the premise that the name should carry self-definition, and he assigns Best Jeanist a privileged witness role in that process.

The relationship state is therefore:

> **Bakugo can internalize an adult's symbolic lesson without adopting the adult's surface mannerisms.**

This is another case where influence should not be measured by personality imitation.

## 8.6 Todoroki's invitation creates the Endeavor trio without pretending the relationships are simple

Near the end of the party, Todoroki asks Midoriya and Bakugo whether they will come to the No.1 hero's internship (`V25:p119-120`).

The invitation is operationally straightforward, but relationally dense.

Todoroki is inviting peers into a professional environment controlled by the father whose household conduct remains unresolved. He is not declaring reconciliation. He is treating Endeavor's current technical/professional value as usable while preserving his own independent judgment.

Bakugo and Midoriya accept for their own developmental reasons rather than because they share Todoroki's family problem.

The trio is therefore formed by **overlapping utility, not one unified emotional meaning**.

# 9. No.243 — home, reassurance, and entry into Endeavor's professional field

## 9.1 Inko's fear state changes because she reads Midoriya's present face

The New Year home sequence at `V25:p123-126` provides unusually valuable low-stakes Midoriya/Inko evidence.

Midoriya shows her Eri's handwritten letter and emphasizes that Eri worked hard to write it. The exchange returns to his childhood pattern of trying to help people even when he had no power adequate to the task.

Inko then says that she had always felt she needed to protect Izuku:

> `出久を守らなくちゃって…`

But after looking at his current face she says:

> `なんだか安心しちゃった`

This is not complete parental relief. Nor does it erase her earlier fear about bodily self-destruction.

It is a relationship-state transition from **protective alarm toward conditional trust in visible maturation**.

Midoriya's growth is becoming legible to the person who has suffered the most sustained fear about the costs of his hero path.

## 9.2 Hawks's return is governed by surveillance before the internship plot reaches him

At `V25:p128-131`, Hawks returns to his covert role and reports renewed contact with Dabi. He explicitly understands that his interactions will be monitored and that future communication with the Commission must therefore be coded.

This strongly advances his behavioral model:

- he maintains casual surface affect;
- he compartmentalizes operational knowledge;
- he anticipates adversarial monitoring;
- he accepts severe personal exposure in order to identify the source and structure of the threat;
- he constructs communication channels around surveillance rather than acting as though secrecy is binary.

Hawks's social fluency is therefore not merely charm. It is part of his covert tradecraft.

## 9.3 Endeavor's first reaction to the trio preserves his relational asymmetry

When Midoriya, Bakugo, and Todoroki arrive (`V25:p132-137`), Endeavor's reception is not warm universal mentorship.

His strongest prior relational investment is still Shoto. He is surprised by the other two, and Bakugo immediately punctures any possibility of ceremonial deference.

It does **not** support:

> if you want to learn, watch me from behind.

The three attempt to follow and are struck by how completely his movement speed exceeds their expectation (`V25:p137-142`).

`observe the expert under real conditions -> discover the actual performance gap -> later decompose it`.

The lesson is not yet a full technical curriculum. It is a benchmark.

# 10. No.244 — public rescue, Hawks's coded approach, and the first warning channel

## 10.1 Endeavor's professional field is simultaneous combat and civilian risk management

The Starservant incident forces Endeavor and the students into a crowded city environment (`V25:p142-155`).

Endeavor moves before the students can keep pace, treats civilian evacuation as part of the same operational problem as villain suppression, and coordinates with surrounding professionals/police rather than performing heroism as solitary spectacle.

This reinforces the mature pro-hero model established across earlier volumes: speed is valuable because response windows are social, not merely because speed wins fights.

## 10.2 Hawks appears casual precisely because he cannot speak casually

Hawks arrives after the incident and begins talking in his familiar light register. The content appears almost promotional: he recommends a liberation text, discusses the spread of its ideas, and imagines a society in which heroes might become unnecessary or idle.

But Endeavor catches a mismatch.

At `V25:p158-169`, Hawks's eyes, timing, repeated phrasing, and the text itself make the performance legible as coded communication. Endeavor realizes that the surface message is **not Hawks's true position**.

The scene is a strong relationship/voice example because it relies on partner-specific interpretation. The code works not simply because letters can be marked in a book, but because Endeavor knows enough of Hawks's ordinary performance to recognize abnormality within it.

This is conditional voice modeling at its best:

> the same smile and casual register that normally communicate ease become evidence of coercion because the partner knows what Hawks usually means when he uses them.

## 10.3 Hawks weaponizes a public ideological text as a covert channel

Hawks distributes/recommends the liberation book widely, including to top heroes, while placing information in a form surveillance can mistake for ideological promotion.

This is structurally elegant:

- the enemy expects him to circulate liberation material;
- the book is therefore safe-looking contraband;
- marked or patterned text carries the opposite practical purpose: warning heroes about the movement.

The same political artifact operates simultaneously as propaganda and counterintelligence medium.

This reinforces V24's thesis that political communication is a power technology, now redirected against the movement that produced it.

# 11. No.245 — Hawks under total surveillance and Endeavor's decoding

Endeavor notices that Hawks's overt statements are strangely inverted relative to what Endeavor believes Hawks actually wants. At `V25:p168-171`, he works through the text and repeated cues rather than receiving a clean exposition.

# 12. No.246 — Quirk singularity, Shigaraki's procedure, and the synchronized countdown

## 12.1 Ujiko turns successor recognition into bodily engineering

The villain-side system returns at `V25:p179-184`.

Ujiko treats the Deika experience as evidence that Shigaraki's Decay has “bloomed” or expanded. He then begins the next stage: a procedure designed to increase Shigaraki's total capacity beyond what his present body can safely support.

This is not simply training.

It is institutionalized technical transformation performed by the custodian of AFO's research infrastructure.

The inheritance pathway has become:

`AFO designation -> Machia/Ujiko testing -> Shigaraki demonstrated capacity -> institutional allegiance -> bodily augmentation`.

V23's successor problem is therefore fully converted into a material system.

## 12.2 Quirk singularity reframes power escalation as a body-system problem

Ujiko explains that across generations, Quirks mix and become stronger, more complex, and harder for bodies to contain (`V25:p181-183`). The theory had been dismissed, but the present generation increasingly exhibits the underlying problem.

This is the first major V25 use of **Quirk singularity** as a system-level explanatory framework.

Thematically, it fits a longstanding MHA principle:

> power is never just output; it is output inside a body, family, institution, and generation capable—or incapable—of governing it.

The theory therefore belongs in the power-philosophy ledger even before later volumes reveal its full consequences.

## 12.3 The four-month operation is deliberately hellish

Ujiko tells Shigaraki that the procedure will last four months and describes it as severe suffering.

Shigaraki accepts.

This is important character-model evidence because his risk tolerance has changed from the earlier League leader who often depended on AFO/Kurogiri infrastructure. He is now willing to subject himself to prolonged bodily transformation for future destructive capacity.

His earlier statement that he will no longer underestimate heroes also matters: growth has not made him less destructive, but it has made him less dismissive of opposition.

## 12.4 One For All re-enters the villain succession horizon

The operation is explicitly framed as giving Shigaraki power sufficient to place previously unreachable things within his grasp. One For All is named inside that future horizon.

V25 therefore reconnects the villain institutional buildup to the OFA/AFO succession conflict without revealing the later operational outcome.

The appropriate prospective state is:

> Ujiko intends the procedure to create an unprecedented successor-level capacity, and OFA is a target/reference point; whether the procedure completes as intended remains OPEN.

## 12.5 Hero preparation and villain transformation now run on the same clock

The volume then returns to Hawks/Endeavor and explicitly aligns the threat with **four months**.

This produces the strongest formal architecture in V25:

`Shigaraki: four months of bodily augmentation`

versus

`hero system: four months of distributed preparation`.

The students' internships, Hawks's covert message, the Commission's unusual mobilization request, and Endeavor's decision to oversee the trio all become parts of one countdown structure.

The manga has moved from isolated character growth to **system race**.

# 15. Power-philosophy updates

## 15.1 Power expression and moral authorship must remain separate

Tenko's origin shows why Quirk determinism is inadequate. Decay manifests amid overwhelming affect, but the chapter distinguishes uncontrolled propagation from a later deliberate killing. Power can create possibilities and pressures without deciding intent uniformly.

## 15.2 AFO turns authenticity into moral exemption

AFO's pedagogy makes a dangerous move:

`internal impulse is real -> therefore external morality is merely imposed -> therefore acting on impulse is authentic freedom`.

The first proposition can be true without the latter two following. V25 therefore supplies an internal villain-side critique of simplistic authenticity politics.

## 15.3 Liberation reaches the contradiction exposed in V24

Re-Destro's ideology praises unrestrained Quirk expression. Shigaraki demonstrates what unrestricted expression can mean when the power itself is massively destructive. Re-Destro responds by recognizing Shigaraki rather than revising the doctrine.

The movement therefore resolves its contradiction by subordinating itself to the most radically unrestrained actor.

## 15.4 Power can become organizational before it becomes individual mastery

Shigaraki's decisive V25 growth is not only Decay output. It is access to followers, money, companies, political networks, intelligence, lieutenants, Machia, and Ujiko.

“Power” now includes institutional capacity.

## 15.5 Quirk singularity is a governance problem, not merely a power-scaling rule

Ujiko's theory frames intergenerational Quirk complexity as a mismatch between ability and bodily capacity. That extends earlier body-cost themes from individual training to population-level inheritance.

The key question becomes:

> what happens when inherited capability grows faster than the biological/social systems meant to govern it?

## 15.6 Blackwhip control demonstrates regulation through abstraction

Midoriya's lock/unlock image shows a non-suppressive model of power governance. He does not deny the new Quirk or allow it unrestricted expression. He builds a mental interface that lets intention select output.

This is almost the exact opposite of AFO's lesson to Tenko.

AFO: remove restraint so impulse can rule.

Midoriya: understand the new capacity so judgment can govern expression.

# 19. Japanese-language and register findings

## `明確な殺意をもって`

This is the controlling phrase for the Kotaro death. `明確な` removes ambiguity; `殺意` specifies homicidal intent; `もって` marks the intentional state in which Tenko touches him. Any V2 summary that calls the whole family sequence either “accidental” or “deliberate” loses the source's internal transition.

## `誰か手を差し伸べてくれてたら`

The counterfactual centers not professional rescue machinery but a generic `誰か`—someone. `手を差し伸べる` is literally/idiomatically to extend a hand. In a volume saturated with hands as destructive, controlling, inheriting, and rescuing objects, the wording is formally loaded.

## `善悪・道徳・倫理`

AFO's triad is broader than “rules.” He attacks good/evil, morality, and ethics as socially fabricated categories before elevating impulse. His pedagogy is therefore philosophical delegitimation, not only “don't be afraid.”

## `縛られるな / 恐れるな`

The parallel imperatives frame restraint as bondage and fear as the obstacle to authenticity. In context, “freedom” is being defined by the manipulator who controls Tenko's shelter, interpretation, name, and future resources.

## `君はどうしたい`

AFO's question mimics agency-respecting language. Its context matters: by the time he asks it, he has already narrowed the acceptable interpretive frame. This is useful for modeling manipulative dialogue—open grammar can carry highly constrained choice architecture.

## `僕は全てを肯定してあげよう`

`肯定してあげよう` offers affirmation from a superior/provider position. It is emotionally potent precisely because Tenko has lacked stable affirmation. AFO's promised acceptance is not neutral care; it is paired directly with `壊すことに臆病になるな`.

## `異能解放軍は お前の後についていく`

Re-Destro's choice of `ついていく` makes the transfer relational and directional: the movement will follow behind Shigaraki. It is stronger than a purely procedural “we surrender.”

## `必然の譲位`

`譲位` is abdication/transfer of position, language with ceremonial succession weight. Modified by `必然`, Re-Destro reframes defeat as necessary historical succession rather than contingent military loss.

## `名前なんて…飾りだ`

Shigaraki's dismissal of the new organization's name as ornament sharply contrasts with both AFO's careful naming of `死柄木弔` and hero society's treatment of names as aspirational/public identity. Shigaraki understands names can organize others without treating them as sacred to himself.

## `名は願い`

Best Jeanist's formulation returns through Bakugo. A name is an aspiration/wish, not merely a label. Bakugo's decision not to publicly spend that answer before the intended mentor hears it shows the phrase was internalized.

## `本心じゃない`

Endeavor's recognition that Hawks's surface speech is not his `本心` is a language-modeling warning: lexical content cannot be interpreted without discourse conditions, prior speaker model, and coercion context.

## `3人は俺が見る`

Endeavor's closing commitment is terse, role-centered, and specific: he will watch/oversee the three. The manga has not yet supplied the full later curriculum, so this is best treated as assumption of responsibility rather than proof of later teaching detail.

## `次の桜が散る頃に / 皆が笑っていられるように`

The final line converts the four-month countdown into seasonal/social imagery. The target is not merely “win in four months” but preserve the possibility that everyone can still smile when the next cherry blossoms fall.

The contrast with Shigaraki's `全部` destruction horizon is deliberate.

# 23. V25 major model change

Volume 25 shifts the project from **contested personhood** to **recognition as mobilization / identity converted into organized capacity**.

The core propositions are:

1. The Shimura catastrophe contains different intent states. Mon and several family members die during uncontrolled/panicked Decay propagation; Kotaro's death is explicitly marked `明確な殺意をもって`.
2. Tenko experiences profound bodily relief after deliberately killing Kotaro, and the prior itching disappears. This is genuine reinforcement evidence but does not retroactively make all preceding deaths intentional.
3. The post-catastrophe public sees Tenko without assuming responsibility. `誰か手を差し伸べてくれてたら` establishes a failed-rescue counterfactual centered on ordinary intervention.
4. The failure is not literal invisibility but recognition without ownership of rescue.
5. AFO occupies the rescue vacuum by doing the missing act—reaching out—then weaponizes the dependency it creates.
6. AFO explicitly delegitimizes `善悪・道徳・倫理` as other people's constructions and elevates Tenko's internal impulse as the most important thing.
7. `君はどうしたい` operates inside a constrained interpretive frame; Tenko states `殺したい` and enacts that desire.
8. AFO's grooming works through selective affirmation rather than elimination of agency: he teaches which part of Tenko should count as “authentic.”
9. `僕は全てを肯定してあげよう` and `壊すことに臆病になるな` fuse acceptance with destructive conditioning.
10. AFO gives Tenko his own surname and the name `弔`; Shigaraki Tomura is therefore an authored identity inside the grooming relationship, not merely an independently selected alias.
11. Shigaraki's Deika power expands to environmental/city scale, forcing the MLA's liberation doctrine to confront unrestricted destructive expression.
12. Re-Destro's defeat is interpretive as well as physical. He recognizes Shigaraki as a truer realization of liberation and offers the movement to follow him.
13. `必然の譲位` turns defeat into ideologically legitimate succession.
14. Shigaraki immediately treats Re-Destro's corporate money/resources as useful, preserving his anti-ceremonial instrumentalism.
15. The League and MLA fuse as `超常解放戦線`; Shigaraki calls the name ornament, showing organizational adoption without doctrinal reverence.
16. Ujiko's earlier gatekeeping resolves: he now offers power because Shigaraki has demonstrated successor-worthy capacity.
17. Controlled Blackwhip use becomes canonical in V25: Midoriya uses a lock/unlock image and produces a deliberately tiny output after weeks of practice.
18. Hero media training expands professional competence into public communicability and reassurance.
19. Bakugo's tactical growth does not erase public-speech abrasiveness; growth remains domain-specific.
20. Best Jeanist's `名は願い` has been internalized by Bakugo enough that he reserves the hero-name answer for a meaningful mentor relationship.
21. Commission-requested internships are abnormal enough that U.A. staff interpret them as quiet preparation for a major crisis.
22. Christmas provides first-class low-stakes ensemble evidence inside that hidden mobilization state.
23. Inko's `なんだか安心しちゃった` marks a parent-child transition toward conditional trust in Izuku's visible maturation.
24. Hawks accepts monitored covert life and builds communication around surveillance rather than pretending secrecy can be total.
25. Endeavor recognizes Hawks's surface message as `本心じゃない`; partner-specific behavioral knowledge becomes an intelligence channel.
26. Hawks uses liberation propaganda itself as a covert warning medium.
27. The threat receives a four-month timetable, converting vague danger into a strategic clock.
28. Ujiko's Quirk-singularity explanation generalizes bodily power-governance into an intergenerational system problem.
29. Shigaraki begins a four-month augmentation procedure while hero society begins a four-month preparedness race.
30. `3人は俺が見る` establishes Endeavor's responsibility for the trio, but detailed later training mechanics remain V26+.
31. The final ensemble montage answers villain centralization with distributed hero preparation: many students across many agencies become the unit of future resilience.

The volume's larger proposition is:

> **Recognition only becomes historically consequential when it is converted into action, relationship, or institution. V25's tragedy is that ordinary society recognizes Tenko weakly while AFO recognizes him aggressively; its escalation is that Re-Destro converts recognition into an army; its hero-side answer is to convert hidden threat recognition into distributed preparation before the next spring.**
