# 1. Central thesis and volume role

Volume 5 asked what it means to recognize another person's agency without possessing or overwriting it. Volume 6 moves from recognition to **enactment**:

> **What makes a heroic identity real is not the name, ideal, inheritance, conviction, or power one claims, but the direction and discipline of one's action under stress.**

The governing problem is therefore **claimed identity versus enacted identity**.

That problem appears first in the apparently light chapter about hero names. Midnight tells the students that a name is not a decorative label. Naming helps solidify an image of who one intends to become; `名は体を表す` becomes almost programmatic for the volume (V06:p014). Tsuyu arrives with a childhood-tested identity, `フロッピー`; Kirishima openly adopts `烈怒頼雄斗 / Red Riot` in homage to Crimson Riot; Todoroki uses the plain `ショート`; Bakugo initially proposes names whose content is almost entirely explosive victory. Midoriya chooses `デク`, an insult whose meaning Uraraka changed for him and which he now chooses to carry forward himself (V06:p017-024).

But Iida refuses the most obvious inherited name at precisely this point. In a memory of Tensei's hospital words, his brother permits him to take `インゲニウム`; in the classroom Iida instead writes `天哉` and says he is not yet taking the name (V06:p021-023). By the end of the volume he will finally announce `僕はインゲニウム` — but he does so while hunting Stain for private vengeance (V06:p124-125).

This is one of the most exact identity reversals in the early manga. The inherited heroic name is not automatically heroic because Iida says it. Indeed, the first moment in which he publicly claims it is also the moment when his conduct has moved furthest from the ideal represented by his brother.

Stain sees this contradiction clearly. He tells Iida to save the already-injured Native first, commands `自らを顧みず他を救い出せ`, and condemns `己の為に力を振るう` (V06:p142-143). His diagnosis is substantially correct: Iida has allowed grief and hatred to narrow his field of moral attention until the person immediately needing rescue becomes secondary to punishment.

Yet Stain converts that correct diagnosis into `だから死ぬんだ` and describes human beings as `正しい社会への供物` (V06:p143-144). The volume therefore draws a crucial distinction:

> **moral perception does not confer moral jurisdiction.**

Stain can correctly identify heroic hypocrisy or motive failure and still be radically illegitimate because he appoints himself executioner over those judgments.

Manual states the institutional version earlier. Heroes do not possess a free-standing right to arrest, punish, or use coercive force merely because they are heroes. Their exceptional powers exist within a social/legal structure built through accumulated trust and record; acting from `私怨` and turning hero activity into `私刑` can make the hero himself a criminal (V06:p110-111). Stain's critique and Manual's warning converge on the same problem from opposite directions: **power becomes corrupt when private conviction substitutes for accountable public purpose**.

Midoriya's development supplies the positive counterpart. Gran Torino identifies a different form of capture: Midoriya's reverence for All Might. `オールマイトへの憧れや責任感が足枷になっとる` — admiration and responsibility have become shackles (V06:p057). Midoriya still conceptualizes One For All as an extraordinary external thing he switches into a body part for a decisive technique. That is why his analysis and his body remain temporally separated.

His breakthrough is deceptively ordinary: `個性は体の一部`. Quirk is part of the body. Using a microwave and taiyaki as analogy, he realizes he has been thinking of “switching on” power only at the necessary point instead of distributing it through the whole body from the beginning (V06:p078-084). The resulting Full Cowling prototype is therefore not merely a new combat technique. It is an act of **de-idealization and embodiment**.

The power becomes more truly Midoriya's not when he denies its inherited history, but when he stops handling it as a sacred imitation of All Might and integrates it into his own continuous movement, perception, and bodily limit.

This is the technical analogue of Volume 4's self-authorship and Volume 5's recognition-without-possession. Inheritance becomes legitimate when the successor can **inhabit** it rather than merely perform the predecessor.

Todoroki's role continues the same movement. Midoriya helped him stop defining his fire exclusively through Endeavor. In Volume 6, Todoroki begins using his own experience of resentment as a way to perceive someone else. He tells Iida that he became worried after Tensei's attack because he knows the face of a person moving through `恨みつらみ` (V06:p184-185). His family wound does not disappear; it becomes usable knowledge in the service of another person.

This is a small but extremely important shift:

> **pain becomes more heroic when it enlarges perception of other people rather than narrowing the world around the self.**

Iida's pain initially does the latter. Todoroki's increasingly does the former.

Shigaraki provides another negative comparison. Stain asks him for purpose and conviction. Shigaraki can presently offer hatred: All Might, Midoriya, things he dislikes, things he wants destroyed. Stain asks what significance there is in murderous intent without conviction (V06:p061-063). Later, Shigaraki releases Nomu in Hosu partly because Stain is receiving attention and Shigaraki wants that attention displaced; his unseen mentor explicitly treats the event as another opportunity to learn.

So Volume 6 places several people beside an ideal or inherited structure and asks what they actually do with it:

- Midoriya has All Might and One For All; he must stop imitation from becoming a shackle.
- Iida has Tensei and the name Ingenium; grief temporarily turns inheritance into revenge.
- Todoroki has family trauma; he begins converting it into recognition of another person's danger.
- Shigaraki has a mentor and growing villain identity; his destructive impulse still lacks a coherent governing purpose.
- Stain has a demanding theory of heroism; he converts it into private lethal jurisdiction.
- U.A. students choose hero names, but internships immediately test whether those identities correspond to actual professional conduct.

A concise volume formulation is therefore:

> **Volume 6 argues that identity becomes ethically meaningful only in practice. A heroic name, inherited ideal, or powerful conviction is not proof of heroism; heroism is the disciplined conversion of power and pain into other-directed action under conditions where private desire could seize control.**

# 2. Narrative architecture

## 2.1 Hero names create an explicit self-authorship laboratory

The volume opens by turning identity into an institutional exercise. U.A. asks students to choose names they may eventually carry as professionals. Midnight's explanation matters because the naming process is future-oriented: giving the future self a name fixes an image and helps the student move toward it (V06:p011-020).

The sequence exposes different identity logics in compressed form.

Tsuyu's `フロッピー` is socially tested and relationally accepted. Kirishima's `Red Riot` is an aspirational inheritance from a hero he admires. Todoroki's `ショート` is almost aggressively non-symbolic: simply himself. Bakugo's rejected `爆殺王`-type proposals externalize how thoroughly his heroic self-image is still saturated by conquest. Uraraka's `ウラビティ` combines self and Quirk. Midoriya's `デク` is the most explicitly revised identity: an externally imposed term of diminishment becomes a chosen future name because another relationship altered its meaning (V06:p017-024).

Names can therefore be inherited, invented, repaired, relationally transformed, or rejected.

Iida creates the chapter's crucial unresolved contrast. Tensei has offered him `インゲニウム`, but Iida does not use it in class. He is not yet prepared to make the inherited symbol his own. The manga then waits until the Stain confrontation to make him claim it under morally compromised conditions.

The chapter's apparently comic exercise is thus a miniature of the entire volume: **the gap between the identity one names and the identity one's action proves.**

## 2.2 Sports Festival visibility becomes internship sorting

The transition from tournament to workplace experience makes the Sports Festival's institutional function concrete. Students with professional nominations can choose among agencies that scouted them; students without nominations choose from forty pre-approved offices organized by region and specialty (V06:p029-031).

Todoroki and Bakugo possess thousands of nominations while most students receive dramatically fewer. The tournament's public visibility has become labor-market opportunity.

But the students' choices are not passive. Uraraka chooses Gunhead because she wants to expand close-combat capability. She is not simply converting Sports Festival performance into a predictable rescue-oriented placement; she identifies a weakness and selects an environment that can alter it (V06:p030-031).

Midoriya's invitation to Gran Torino is similarly nonstandard. All Might explains that Gran Torino taught him for a year and was connected to his predecessor. Midoriya's professional development therefore becomes another generational bridge — but, as the training will show, a bridge that exists partly because All Might himself is a poor technical fit for Midoriya's learning problem.

## 2.3 Iida's distress is visible but not successfully entered

Before the students separate, Midoriya and Uraraka both notice Iida's abnormal affect. He minimizes the situation and provides almost no opening. Midoriya's retrospective narration says that at the time he should have spoken more strongly; the memory becomes a later regret (V06:p037-038).

This is an important refinement of the project's failed-recognition thesis.

No one here fails to notice Iida. No one is indifferent. The failure is **intervention depth**.

Iida's normal procedural openness contracts under grief, and his friends accept a boundary that in hindsight concealed escalating risk. Recognition therefore has at least three stages:

1. notice that something is wrong;
2. correctly infer the nature/severity of the problem;
3. act strongly enough to alter the trajectory.

V06 shows stage one succeeding while stages two and three fail.

## 2.4 Gran Torino attacks the successor's conceptual bottleneck

Gran Torino's pedagogy is deliberately abrasive and indirect. He does not begin with a lecture on power distribution. He destabilizes Midoriya, attacks without warning, asks him to strike back, and lets the gap between analysis and embodied action become impossible to ignore (V06:p040-057).

Midoriya can predict, observe, and reason. The problem is latency: he thinks, decides where One For All is needed, activates it there, and then acts. Gran Torino identifies the deeper psychological source — Midoriya's reverence for All Might has preserved OFA as a special object rather than a bodily condition.

The breakthrough occurs through an ordinary household analogy rather than heroic grandeur. Cold taiyaki, microwave timing, and distributed heat help Midoriya conceptualize what hero worship had obscured.

That formal choice matters. The inherited superpower becomes livable through mundane embodied reasoning.

## 2.5 `個性は体の一部` turns technical growth into self-authorship

Midoriya's key realization is not “use a lower percentage.” It is **change the ontology of use**.

Previously:

`need → select limb → activate OFA → attack`

After the realization:

`OFA continuously integrated through body → perceive/decide/move from that embodied baseline`

At V06:p082-084 he imagines turning all the switches on from the beginning and distributing force through his entire body at the bodily permissible limit.

Gran Torino then asks whether he can move that way. Midoriya honestly says he does not know and asks to try (V06:p085). This is a useful character-model marker: conceptual certainty immediately becomes experiment rather than performance of confidence.

The subsequent three-minute test joins Midoriya's analytical cognition to continuous movement. Gran Torino later has to evade him seriously, showing that the gain is not raw output but **response integration**.

Gran Torino connects this directly to rescue: with the old response speed, `救えるもんも救えんぞ` (V06:p089). Embodiment is therefore not a neutral combat optimization. It expands the time window in which Midoriya can reach people.

## 2.6 Internships pluralize “professional heroism”

Volume 6 deliberately distributes classmates across different professional cultures.

- Best Jeanist treats Bakugo's uncivil public presentation as something that itself requires correction. He says reforming a violent person like Bakugo can be part of his hero activity (V06:p067-069).
- Gunhead exposes Uraraka to close-quarters martial training and the legal/procedural realities surrounding hero action (V06:p069-071).
- Uwabami exposes Momo and Kendo to celebrity, advertising, and media-facing professional work (V06:p070).
- Manual provides Iida with ordinary local hero work and, critically, an explicit account of the legal limits governing coercive authority (V06:p109-111).
- Gran Torino provides Midoriya technical retraining and then real field exposure.

The professional system is therefore not a single pipeline toward stronger combat. It contains rescue, patrol, arrest support, public communication, branding, pedagogy, specialized technique, and local civic work.

This matters when Stain later collapses the category “hero” into his own purity test. The manga has just shown that actual professional heroism is heterogeneous, institutionally embedded, and frequently mundane.

## 2.7 The Nomu investigation makes instrumentalized bodies explicit

The police investigation into the USJ Nomu finds a body whose structure has been deliberately altered and whose DNA/Quirk factors cannot be explained by an ordinary single-Quirk person. The investigators infer the probable existence of an ability capable of granting Quirks to others (V06:p047-049).

Prospectively, the exact identity and full mechanism remain unavailable. What Volume 6 does establish is enough for the personhood/toolhood ledger:

> **Nomu is not merely a powerful villain combatant; it is an engineered body configured around externally assigned function.**

This is the darkest possible inversion of self-authorship. Todoroki struggles to reclaim the meaning of a body shaped by someone else's project; Nomu appears to have been physically reconstructed into a project.

## 2.8 Stain forces Shigaraki to confront the absence of purpose

Kurogiri brings Stain into contact with Shigaraki. Shigaraki's account of motive remains primitive: All Might, Midoriya, and things he dislikes should be destroyed. Stain asks `目的は何だ` and, more sharply, what significance exists in murderous intent without conviction (V06:p061-063).

The men are not allies in worldview. Stain's ideology is rigid; Shigaraki's current destructive impulse is personal, affective, and under-theorized.

Yet Stain identifies a common directional desire to destroy the present order, and the encounter becomes pedagogically useful to Shigaraki's unseen mentor. Later the mentor allows Shigaraki to deploy several Nomu and explicitly treats the episode as an opportunity to learn.

Shigaraki's actual behavior shows how far he remains from Stain's conviction. He is irritated that the Hero Killer is receiving attention and releases Nomu partly to displace that attention. His development is occurring, but Volume 6 does not authorize the claim that he has internalized Stain's ideology.

## 2.9 Manual states the legal theory Iida is violating

Manual is unusually important because he perceives Iida's real purpose before Iida admits it. He tells him directly:

`私怨で動くのはやめた方がいいよ`

and explains that heroes do not independently possess unlimited power to arrest or punish. Exceptional force has been socially permitted within structures of trust, record, and law; private punishment (`私刑`) would collapse the distinction between hero and criminal (V06:p110-111).

This is one of the strongest early institutional statements in MHA.

Hero legitimacy is not reducible to benevolent intention or licensed identity. It depends on:

- accountable purpose;
- legal authorization;
- restraint;
- social trust;
- and direction toward public rather than private ends.

Iida hears this warning and still cannot release the grievance.

## 2.10 Iida takes the name Ingenium at the moment he violates its meaning

When Iida finds Stain over Native, he does not first rescue Native. He identifies himself through Tensei's injury and attacks.

Stain asks who he is. Iida answers:

`僕はインゲニウム`

`おまえを倒すヒーローの名だ!!` (V06:p124-125).

The formal irony is severe. Earlier, he would not take the name despite explicit familial permission. Now he claims it precisely when vengeance has overwhelmed the values the name represented to him.

Tensei's remembered hero philosophy reinforces the contradiction. Hero work is valuable because one's actions serve many people and make them happy; Iida remembers his brother as the hero who gave him a dream (V06:p139-141).

Stain then identifies the gap between symbol and behavior.

## 2.11 Stain's correct diagnosis exposes his illegitimate cure

Stain tells Iida to save Native first. He articulates principles that could almost appear in a U.A. lesson:

- disregard yourself to rescue others;
- do not wield power for yourself;
- private hatred and gratification are far from heroism (V06:p142-143).

The manga allows those claims to land because Iida's conduct visibly supports them.

Then Stain declares that this means Iida should die.

That transition is the ideological failure.

Stain's model does not merely identify moral deviation. It abolishes proportionality, due process, rehabilitation, growth, and the victim's status as a person with a future. People become `正しい社会への供物` (V06:p144).

His ideology therefore reproduces the toolification he claims to purify society against: persons become material for the society he wants to create.

## 2.12 Midoriya operationalizes `余計なお世話`

The citywide Nomu attack creates noise and spectacle. Midoriya does not simply follow the biggest visible threat. He remembers Iida's internship location and emotional state, links the Hero Killer to Hosu, reasons about where Stain might operate, and searches away from the central chaos.

This is a particularly mature combination of V01 rescue reflex and accumulated analytical ability. The logic is essentially:

`visible crisis + hidden friend risk + known villain pattern → search for the person least likely to be found by following the crowd`.

When Iida protests that Midoriya is unrelated, Midoriya invokes All Might's earlier lesson:

`余計なお世話はヒーローの本質なんだって` (V06:p147 onward).

A statement from V05 has become a decision heuristic under lethal conditions.

## 2.13 Full Cowling changes how Midoriya occupies a fight

In Hosu, Midoriya's new movement is continuous, multidirectional, and responsive. Iida himself compares it to Bakugo-like mobility; Midoriya has not copied All Might's combat body so much as recombined observations from multiple peers into a form usable by his own body.

His exclamation `通じた！通じたぞ フルカウル!! 僕 戦える!!` marks a change in self-efficacy: sustainable action is beginning to replace sacrificial proof.

The percentage is still low and the body can still be cut or injured. But the governing relationship to OFA has changed from ammunition to environment.

## 2.14 Todoroki converts resentment knowledge into relational rescue

Todoroki interprets Midoriya's location-only message as a distress signal, arrives, and immediately defines his objective as keeping Stain from killing the others.

The deeper payoff arrives near the end. Todoroki tells Iida he had been worried since Tensei's attack because he knows `恨みつらみで動く人間の顔` (V06:p184-185).

This is an inversion of his own earlier state. The knowledge produced by resentment now helps him perceive resentment in another person.

His command to Iida — `なりてえもんちゃんと見ろ!!` (V06:p189) — echoes the language of positive self-authorship that helped Todoroki himself. It does not erase Iida's grievance. It redirects attention from the hated object toward the desired self.

## 2.15 The volume ends at reorientation, not repair

Volume 6 closes before the Stain conflict is resolved.

Iida is still injured, emotionally destabilized, and not yet restored to his earlier procedural identity. But after seeing Midoriya and Todoroki protect him and Native, he begins trying to stand. He claims Ingenium again — now as a heroic name directed toward stopping Stain rather than merely announcing a brother's vengeance (V06:p187-189).

This is the beginning of a state transition, not its completion.

Prospectively, Volume 6 supports:

> vengeance state → shame/recognition → first attempt to reorient toward heroic identity

It does **not** yet support:

> vengeance fully processed → stable moral repair.

That distinction must be preserved into Volume 7.

# 3. Character-state ledger

## 3.1 Midoriya Izuku

### Current state

Midoriya's major V06 transition is **embodied individuation**. He still organizes himself around rescue, All Might, and OFA succession, but he begins to stop treating All Might's power and movement as the template through which all heroic action must pass.

### Admiration as a functional blind spot

Gran Torino identifies the contradiction precisely: Midoriya's admiration for All Might, which helped make him worthy of OFA, now limits technical learning (V06:p057).

This is not a rejection of admiration. It is a state transition in how admiration functions.

Earlier:

- All Might = proof the dream is possible;
- All Might = moral standard;
- All Might = physical model to imitate.

V06 begins separating the third from the first two.

### Embodied reasoning

The `個性は体の一部` realization shows Midoriya at his best cognitive pattern: abstract technical difficulty is solved through ordinary analogy and then immediately converted into experiment.

His reasoning remains recursive rather than purely intuitive:

1. notice response delay;
2. compare to an everyday heating/switching process;
3. reformulate causal model;
4. test whole-body distribution;
5. observe movement result;
6. refine in live combat.

The difference is that cognition and body are becoming continuous rather than sequential.

### Rescue under uncertainty

In Hosu, Midoriya does not have direct proof Iida is in danger. He assembles several weak signals and chooses to search:

- Iida's abnormal affect;
- Hosu internship location;
- Stain's presence in Hosu;
- citywide villain disturbance;
- likelihood that Iida might separate from official response.

This reinforces a stable pattern: under rescue-relevant uncertainty, Midoriya's threshold for investigating a plausible endangered person is low.

### `余計なお世話` as adopted doctrine

He now has explicit language for an old behavior: formal noninvolvement does not end responsibility when someone is in danger. This is not simply impulse anymore. It is a consciously adopted heroic heuristic.

## 3.2 Iida Tenya

### Current state

V06 resolves the V05 OPEN state into a severe but context-specific breakdown:

> **family harm narrows Iida from public/procedural responsibility toward concealed private vengeance.**

This is not evidence that his proceduralism was fake. It is evidence that his strongest existing identity structure can fail under personally charged grief.

### Concealment and withdrawal

Iida does not process grief through his usual public clarity. He minimizes the problem with classmates, chooses Hosu with a concealed purpose, researches Stain's pattern, and hopes Stain will reappear so `この手で始末してやる` (V06:p037-038; p103-105).

This is the strongest relationship-conditioned contrast in his model so far.

### Authority response under grievance

Manual gives him exactly the kind of explicit rule-based warning that normally fits Iida's cognitive style. Under ordinary conditions one would expect such a warning to carry great weight.

It does not.

That negative evidence is highly valuable. Under acute family grievance:

- legal/procedural information remains understood;
- but emotional priority overrides it.

The correct model is therefore not “Iida is rule-bound.” It is:

> Iida strongly relies on legitimate procedure until a sufficiently intimate moral injury convinces him that personal action is required.

### Ingenium as unstable inheritance

His use of `インゲニウム` has three V06 states:

1. inherited name offered by Tensei;
2. classroom refusal/deferral;
3. revenge-era claim against Stain.

The third is deliberately contradictory. Iida wants to honor his brother while acting contrary to the brother's remembered other-directed heroism.

### Stain's indictment and Iida's shame

Stain's demand that Iida save Native first creates an undeniable external comparison between stated identity and actual priority. Iida cannot answer it by procedure or family honor because the wounded person is physically present.

The later arrival of Midoriya and Todoroki deepens the shame: they enact the rescue orientation Iida claimed to inherit.

### Beginning of repair

Todoroki's `なりてえもんちゃんと見ろ` redirects Iida toward desired identity rather than hated target. Iida tries to stand and reclaims Ingenium as the name of a hero who will stop Stain (V06:p187-189).

The repair is incomplete at the volume boundary.

V06 adds a crucial grief/revenge stress regime, concealment, authority override, shame, and early repair. This is high-value depth, but longitudinal post-crisis recovery and broader ordinary-life evidence are still needed before `strong` is justified.

## 3.3 Todoroki Shoto

**Revision:** `STRENGTHEN` — prior trauma knowledge becomes externally useful social perception.

- Receives a location message from Midoriya and interprets it as likely distress rather than mere data.
- Arrives using both ice and fire without the V04 binary restriction dominating the decision.
- Prioritizes keeping Iida and Native alive rather than winning a formal contest.
- Explicitly recognizes Iida's resentment state because he knows that facial/behavioral pattern from himself: `恨みつらみで動く人間の顔なら よく知ってたから` (V06:p184-185).
- Redirects Iida with `なりてえもんちゃんと見ろ!!` (V06:p189), applying a self-authorship lesson analogous to his own recent transition.

### Modeling consequence

Todoroki's family trauma is no longer only a vulnerability. It is becoming a source of relationship-specific empathy and threat detection. He may notice resentment-driven self-narrowing earlier than classmates without comparable experience.

**Readiness:** remains `moderate (strengthened)`.

## 3.4 Bakugo Katsuki

V06 adds mostly low-stakes/professional evidence rather than a major state transition.

- His first hero-name attempts remain saturated with violent victory grammar and are rejected by Midnight (V06:p019-020).
- Best Jeanist explicitly chooses him partly to correct his violent public presentation (V06:p067-069).
- Bakugo resists the framing and appearance discipline, but does not simply leave; he remains inside the professional training environment.

### Modeling consequence

Bakugo's internal competence standards do not automatically produce a viable public professional identity. Adult mentors can identify conduct/presentation as part of hero work, creating a future tension between his internal absolute standard and external civic legibility.

**Readiness:** remains `moderate`.

## 3.5 Uraraka Ochako

- Chooses Gunhead's office to gain combat skill, reinforcing active weakness-targeted self-development rather than passive career sorting (V06:p030-031).
- Receives formal martial training and appears comfortable being repositioned as a learner rather than relying on her Sports Festival identity.

**Readiness:** remains `moderate`.

## 3.6 Asui Tsuyu

V06 supplies useful identity/voice evidence:

- `フロッピー` is not an imposed brand but a hero name she had already considered in childhood because friends found it easy and approachable (V06:p017).

This fits her established low-friction/direct social style: the name is functional, friendly, and already socially tested.

**Readiness:** remains `emerging`.

## 3.7 Kirishima Eijiro

- Chooses `Red Riot` in explicit admiration of Crimson Riot, making heroic identity openly aspirational and predecessor-linked (V06:p017-018).

## 3.15 Stain

**Revision:** `STRENGTHEN` — ideology, motive discrimination, opponent classification, and coercive moral structure become explicit.

### Current doctrine

Stain distinguishes:

- false heroism as self-interested use of the role;
- All Might as exceptional legitimate hero;
- Midoriya as someone worth allowing to live;
- Iida as corrupted by private vengeance.

He is unusually good at reading the immediate motivational direction of action. His diagnosis of Iida is substantially supported by the scene.

He converts the diagnosis into unilateral execution. His society has no meaningful room for:

- proportional response;
- rehabilitation;
- procedural judgment;

- high physical competence and mobility;
- weapon fluency;
- rapid exploitation of blood-based paralysis;
- willingness to spare someone who fits his hero criterion;
- persistent ideological speech during combat;
- does not appear motivated by personal enrichment or fame at this boundary.

# 4. Power philosophies

## 4.1 `個性は体の一部`: power becomes legitimate through embodiment

Midoriya's Full Cowling insight gives the manga a new formulation of mastery. Power should not remain an external miracle or a special sacrificial event. It must become part of the ordinary body.

This strengthens V02's `僕の力` trajectory. “My power” now means more than psychological ownership; it begins to mean **continuous embodied integration**.

## 4.2 Admiration can become domination without coercion

All Might does not coerce Midoriya the way Endeavor coerces Todoroki. Yet Gran Torino shows that even loving admiration can constrain a successor if the predecessor becomes too total a model.

This is an important refinement of the project's inheritance thesis:

> benign inheritance still requires differentiation.

The successor must eventually exceed imitation.

## 4.3 Hero names do not confer hero ethics

Iida's first spoken claim to `Ingenium` while pursuing private vengeance demonstrates that symbolic inheritance can be ethically inverted even without malicious intent.

The name is a claim. Conduct determines whether the claim is currently warranted.

## 4.4 Stain and Manual define opposed theories of legitimate force

Manual:

- exceptional force is institutionally limited;
- legitimacy is social/legal;
- `私刑` is impermissible;
- private grievance cannot authorize coercion.

Stain:

- personal conviction supplies authority;
- his own hero criterion determines who deserves life;
- institutional title is irrelevant if his moral judgment rejects it.

The conflict is therefore not “rules versus morality.” It is **accountable morality versus self-authorized moral sovereignty**.

## 4.5 Stain's ideology weaponizes personhood

Stain claims to restore the meaning of heroism, but the phrase `正しい社会への供物` exposes the contradiction. People are reduced to sacrifice for an abstract social correction.

This directly strengthens Hypothesis 7: power becomes illegitimate when people become tools for another person's project.

## 4.6 Iida demonstrates that prosocial identity can be captured by private ends

Iida is not selfish in baseline character. Precisely because he is normally public-minded, the revenge state is analytically valuable. It proves that moral identity does not immunize someone against motive capture.

The relevant question is not “is this a good person?” but:

> **what is this power serving in this moment?**

## 4.7 Full Cowling makes sustainability and rescue the same technical problem

Gran Torino's `救えるもんも救えんぞ` ties response speed and embodied integration directly to rescue. Sustainable power is not a moderation of heroism; it increases effective rescue capacity.

This revises any reading in which restraint appears opposed to heroic commitment.

# 5. Hero Society / institutional analysis

## 5.1 Names are professional infrastructure

U.A. treats hero naming as an early professional act because names become persistent public interfaces between person, brand, reputation, and future role.

Midnight's `名は体を表す` is therefore not merely aphoristic. The institution recognizes that public identity can shape self-development.

## 5.2 The Sports Festival is confirmed as a labor-market allocation system

The nomination numbers and workplace-choice rules make V03's public-market thesis concrete. Performance produces uneven professional opportunity.

The system is not fully deterministic because:

- non-nominated students still receive structured placement options;
- students can choose among available offices;
- students use placements to compensate for weaknesses.

## 5.3 Workplace experience reveals professional heterogeneity

Best Jeanist, Gunhead, Uwabami, Manual, and Gran Torino show that professional hero work spans:

- public image;
- combat technique;
- media labor;
- local patrol;
- legal restraint;
- mentorship;
- emergency response.

This resists Stain's tendency to judge a heterogeneous institution through a singular purity criterion.

## 5.4 Hero coercive authority is explicitly conditional

Manual's explanation is one of the most important Hero Society statements so far. Hero power is not sovereign. Its exceptional social permissions are conditional on trust, record, law, and role.

This creates an institutional answer to the problem Stain represents:

> the more dangerous a Quirk/user is, the more important accountable authority becomes.

## 5.5 Public emergency response is distributed

The Hosu attack mobilizes many professionals and students around different tasks. No single hero contains the situation alone. The narrative simultaneously tracks:

- central Nomu response;
- local rescue/protection;
- Gran Torino;
- Endeavor;
- Manual;
- Midoriya searching for Iida;
- Todoroki reading Midoriya's message and joining.

Distributed heroism is not abstract here; it is the only way the city can respond to multiple simultaneous threats.

## 5.6 Professional competence and private morality can diverge

Endeavor's Hosu response prevents the corpus from collapsing character ethics into professional incompetence. A person can function impressively in a public role while committing serious relational harm elsewhere.

This is crucial for later Hero Society analysis because public institutions often evaluate visible role performance more easily than private relational ethics.

# 6. Recognition and failed rescue

## 6.1 Iida is noticed but insufficiently reached

Midoriya and Uraraka see distress. Iida withholds. They do not press hard enough. Later Midoriya regrets that choice.

This is a more sophisticated failed-rescue pattern than simple neglect:

> **recognition can fail after perception succeeds.**

## 6.2 Gran Torino recognizes the problem beneath the technique

He does not merely see that Midoriya is slow. He recognizes why Midoriya is conceptually slow: All Might exists in Midoriya's mind as a model too sacred and external to inhabit naturally.

Good mentorship here is causal diagnosis, not generic encouragement.

## 6.3 Manual correctly recognizes Iida's motive before the crisis

Manual identifies `私怨` while Iida is still outwardly performing internship. This shows that the tragedy is not caused by total adult blindness. The warning exists; Iida's current state prevents it from becoming corrective.

## 6.4 Stain recognizes motive but refuses personhood

Stain accurately distinguishes Iida's revenge from Midoriya's rescue. Yet his own rigid ideology prevents recognition from becoming care, correction, or proportional judgment.

This is recognition stripped of mercy and institutional humility.

## 6.5 Midoriya searches for the hidden person inside the public crisis

His Hosu reasoning is a direct descendant of V01's ability to notice the face asking for help while others are focused on the visible event. The skill is now more analytic, but the attention structure is consistent.

## 6.6 Todoroki recognizes resentment through autobiographical knowledge

Todoroki's V06 empathy is not sentimental generalization. He recognizes a pattern because he has inhabited it.

The source of suffering becomes a perceptual resource.

# 7. Villain formation, rescue, and accountability

## 7.1 V06 confirms causal plurality

The corpus now contains at least three visibly different pathways:

- **Shigaraki:** destructive grievance, immaturity, mentor-mediated development, increasing exposure to ideological frameworks.
- **Shinso:** stigma/misrecognition without villainy, showing disadvantage is not destiny.
- **Stain:** ideological absolutism and moral purification, not primarily a story of being socially rejected into crime at the current boundary.

A single villain-formation theory is now untenable.

## 7.2 Stain is not merely “society's critique made villain”

The first-pass emphasis on fame, money, rankings, and celebrity is too broad if treated as Stain's complete V06 argument.

His direct moral test in this volume is about:

- motive;
- rescue priority;
- self-interest;
- sincerity;
- and whether the hero role is used for the self.

Broader institutional critique exists, but V06's strongest evidence is ethical-purification language, not socioeconomic analysis.

## 7.3 Shigaraki is not yet ideologically converted by Stain

Stain exposes Shigaraki's lack of conviction. Shigaraki responds with hostility and later attention rivalry. His mentor sees developmental value.

This is an **exposure event**, not an ideological merger.

## 7.4 Accountability must separate diagnosis from sentence

Stain's critique of Iida is one of the clearest examples in the series where an antagonist can be correct about the hero's immediate moral failure while radically wrong about what follows from that correctness.

This distinction should become a governing rule for later ideological antagonists:

> validate accurate diagnosis without laundering illegitimate remedy.

# 8. Japanese language, character voice, and key vocabulary

## 8.1 `名は体を表す`

Midnight's phrase links naming to embodied/public identity. In this volume, the phrase becomes ironic as Iida claims a heroic name while acting contrary to its ideal.

## 8.2 `デク`

Midoriya explicitly says the meaning has changed. The name is now voluntary, relationally re-authored identity rather than only Bakugo's diminishment.

## 8.3 `足枷`

Gran Torino describes All Might admiration/responsibility as a shackle. The word is important because it reframes a morally positive relation as a functional constraint without invalidating the relation itself.

## 8.4 `個性は体の一部`

The phrase grounds Full Cowling in embodiment rather than technique naming. Quirk is not detachable equipment.

## 8.5 `私怨`

Manual uses a compact legal/moral diagnosis for Iida's state: private grievance. It names the problem before Iida enters the Stain alley.

## 8.6 `私刑`

Manual's warning explicitly names private punishment/vigilante coercion as impermissible. This is foundational vocabulary for the legitimacy ledger.

## 8.7 `インゲニウム`

The name changes function across the volume:

- available inheritance;
- deferred inheritance;
- revenge identity;
- emerging reclaimed hero identity at the endpoint.

It should never be cited without chronology.

## 8.8 `自らを顧みず他を救い出せ`

Stain's hero criterion sounds recognizably close to the series' rescue ethic. Its presence in a murderer's mouth is deliberate ideological tension.

## 8.9 `己の為に力を振るうな`

This identifies direction of power as the key moral variable. The line supports a major series-level distinction between possession of strength and legitimate use.

## 8.10 `正しい社会への供物`

The sacrificial vocabulary reveals Stain's personhood failure. Individuals are subordinate to a purified abstraction.

## 8.11 `余計なお世話`

Midoriya operationalizes All Might's V05 phrase in V06. What was mentor commentary becomes behavior under danger.

## 8.12 `恨みつらみ`

Todoroki uses the phrase not only to describe emotion but as a recognizable behavioral state. It becomes part of his relationship-specific perception model.

## 8.13 `なりてえもんちゃんと見ろ`

Todoroki's rough imperative redirects Iida from offender-centered attention toward self-authored aspiration. It is the social/ethical counterpart to Midoriya's earlier `君の力` intervention.

# 9. Visual and formal analysis

## 9.1 Hero-name boards turn identity into visible text

Chapter 45 repeatedly frames students holding written names toward classmates/readers. Identity is made literally displayable before it is professionally lived.

Iida's `天哉` board is therefore visually meaningful beside the later shouted `インゲニウム`.

## 9.2 Full Cowling changes Midoriya's panel grammar

Earlier OFA use is often staged around a decisive limb, impact, and bodily rupture. V06 increasingly surrounds Midoriya's whole body with distributed energy and movement lines. The visual center shifts from **weaponized body part** to **continuous moving body**.

## 9.3 The microwave/taiyaki sequence deliberately de-spectacularizes power

One of the series' largest technical breakthroughs is discovered through domestic food and appliance imagery. The formal joke is philosophically productive: heroic mastery comes from making the extraordinary ordinary enough to inhabit.

## 9.4 Iida's revenge panels narrow the field

His private planning, clenched posture, and later alley confrontation reduce the social field around him. This contrasts with earlier Iida scenes in which his body becomes a visible organizing point for crowds/classes.

Grief visually converts a public coordinator into a tunnel-visioned pursuer.

## 9.5 Stain is repeatedly framed above immobilized bodies

The vertical relation supports his ideological structure. He is not merely fighting opponents; he positions himself as judge over who counts as hero and who may continue existing.

## 9.6 Todoroki's endpoint command reverses the visual direction

Rather than leaving Iida fixed on Stain, Todoroki's face and imperative point back toward Iida's desired future. The volume ends not on victory but on redirected sight: `ちゃんと見ろ`.

# 10. Body, embodiment, family, and inheritance

## 10.1 Midoriya's body becomes the site where inheritance is individualized

V04–V05 established psychological ownership of inherited power. V06 makes that ownership physiological.

He does not become less connected to predecessors by distributing OFA through the body. He becomes less dominated by a single predecessor's body as model.

## 10.2 Iida's inherited name is more fragile than his inherited engines

The family legacy appears both physically and symbolically. His body supports the same lineage of speed/engine heroism, but the moral meaning of Ingenium cannot be biologically inherited. It must be enacted.

## 10.3 Tensei's injury transforms family admiration into a stress test

Earlier, Tensei is an ideal/guiding figure. V06 proves that idealization can produce not only aspiration but unbearable demand when the ideal is harmed.

Iida's revenge partly arises because he experiences attack on Tensei as attack on the structure that gave his own life direction.

## 10.4 Todoroki's trauma begins to become transferable knowledge

He has not “healed” in any simple sense. But his experience now enlarges recognition of Iida rather than only constraining his own power choices.

This is the first major evidence that self-authorship can convert trauma from identity prison into relational competence.

## 10.5 Nomu is the extreme anti-self-authored body

The engineered multi-Quirk body remains function-first and externally configured. V06 makes its artificiality more explicit while exact later ontology remains OPEN.

V06 establishes:

- it operates at a low sustainable percentage relative to maximum output;

Nomu investigation supports the existence/probability of a mechanism capable of giving multiple Quirks. Do not import the complete later All For One identity/history merely because the full-series analyst knows it.

We know:

- he holds a purification doctrine of heroism;
- All Might is privileged in his ideal;
- he can classify Midoriya as worth sparing;

- he kills/attempts to kill as self-authorized correction;

## 13.5 What V06 establishes about Shigaraki

- successor differentiation from All Might;
- hero legitimacy as conditional public trust;
- plural villain causation;
- pain either narrowing or enlarging recognition;
- private conviction as a threat when it becomes sovereign.

These can guide what we notice without changing what V06 characters know.

# 16. Revised interpretation

Through Volume 6, the first six volumes form a particularly coherent conceptual progression:

1. **V01 — recognition:** can heroism be recognized before power makes it obvious?
2. **V02 — judgment:** can heroic intention become disciplined power use?
3. **V03 — public legibility:** how does capability become visible, recruited, and symbolically reproduced?
4. **V04 — self-authorship:** what makes inherited body/power/opportunity one's own?
5. **V05 — recognition without possession:** can others acknowledge agency without patronizing or claiming it?
6. **V06 — enactment:** does the identity one claims survive the pressure of actual conduct?

Volume 6 is therefore less a transition from Sports Festival to “real villains” than a test of whether the preceding language of identity survives professional and moral reality.

The hero-name chapter supplies names.
The internship system supplies roles.
Gran Torino supplies technique.
Manual supplies legal legitimacy.
Stain supplies ideological judgment.
Hosu supplies danger.

Only conduct can integrate them.

Midoriya's Full Cowling and Iida's Ingenium claim are mirror images in this sense.

Midoriya moves from inherited power as external ideal toward internally embodied practice.
Iida moves from inherited heroic ideal toward externally asserted name while his actual practice has been captured by revenge.

One inheritance becomes **more congruent** between symbol and action; the other becomes temporarily **less congruent**.

Todoroki then begins performing the relational function Midoriya performed for him: he detects when a friend's identity has become trapped by grievance and calls attention back to the self that friend wants to become.

This is distributed moral development rather than protagonist monopoly.

Stain's presence is crucial because he refuses that developmental logic. He can see discrepancy but believes discrepancy warrants elimination. MHA's heroes, by contrast, increasingly operate through intervention that assumes the person can still become otherwise.

That difference may be more foundational than the surface hero/villain binary:

> **heroic rescue preserves the possibility of another person's future; purifying violence closes that future because the judge believes the person's current failure is final.**

# 21. What this volume changes in the current MHA model

## 21.1 Heroic identity becomes behavioral rather than nominal

The volume begins with names and ends with a name under moral stress. The difference between calling oneself Ingenium and acting like the hero Ingenium represents becomes explicit.

## 21.2 Inheritance requires embodiment, not imitation

Midoriya's OFA growth is now inseparable from differentiating himself from All Might's body and learning style.

## 21.3 Pain can either narrow or enlarge moral attention

Iida's grief narrows him toward offender and vengeance. Todoroki's recent processing allows him to recognize the same narrowing in a friend. This becomes a major longitudinal distinction to track.

## 21.4 Legitimacy is explicitly institutional

Manual establishes that hero force is socially conditional rather than privately sovereign. Hero license is not a moral blank check.

## 21.5 Stain becomes the clearest early example of correct critique plus illegitimate jurisdiction

The manga does not require us to choose between “Stain is right” and “Stain is a murderer, therefore every observation is false.” The rigorous reading is harder: his perception can be accurate while his moral system remains lethal and dehumanizing.

## 21.6 Midoriya's rescue ethic becomes inferential and embodied

He no longer only reacts to a visible face in front of him. He uses contextual evidence to find a person who may be hidden from the main crisis, then has enough sustainable movement to intervene.

## 21.7 The secondary cast increasingly carries the series' ethical infrastructure

Manual articulates legitimacy; Gran Torino articulates successor differentiation; Todoroki articulates resentment recognition; Tensei supplies a service-centered ideal; Midnight supplies identity formation. The philosophical architecture is clearly distributed.

## 21.8 Villain formation is decisively plural by this point

Stain, Shigaraki, and Shinso cannot be explained by one social-failure mechanism. Future synthesis must preserve causal specificity.

## 21.9 The project hypothesis about early ordinary intervention strengthens

Midoriya explicitly remembers a moment when he saw something wrong with Iida and later wished he had pressed harder. The manga is already teaching that the decisive rescue window may occur **before** the spectacular crisis.

## 21.10 Volume 6's new governing question

The current series model should carry forward this test:

> **When a character claims a hero name, inherited ideal, moral conviction, or professional role, where does their attention go when pain and power make private desire easier than public responsibility?**

That question now links character psychology, power philosophy, institutional legitimacy, and villain ideology in one framework.
