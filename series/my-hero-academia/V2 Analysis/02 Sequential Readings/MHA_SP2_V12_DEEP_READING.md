## 2. Governing transition


V11 asked whether inherited purpose could survive the loss of a single overwhelming protector by becoming repeatable skill, legitimate authority, and shared everyday practice.


V12 supplies a substantial but deliberately incomplete answer:


> **accountable succession / de-centered support → operational self-authorship / reliable coordination**


The volume repeatedly turns values into **forms that can be reproduced under pressure**.


- Cementoss defines useful technique through stable action that is not easily derailed by circumstance.
- Aizawa situates combat inside a plural professional competency model rather than treating strength as the whole profession.
- All Might refuses to hand Midoriya the answer and instead gives a diagnostic prompt that forces self-authored discovery.
- Midoriya converts inherited OFA from an unconsciously All-Might-shaped fist grammar into a leg-centered style responsive to his own body.
- Hatsume's support logic makes equipment a means of translating bodily limits and functional goals into reliable affordances.
- Kaminari uses support equipment to convert an indiscriminate electrical field into selective precision compatible with allies.
- Class 1-A's final Stage-1 success depends not on one ace defeating everyone, but on distributed signaling, searching, regrouping, and complementary abilities.


The strongest compact V12 formulation is therefore:


> **A heroic value becomes professionally meaningful when it can survive translation into a repeatable technique, a teachable decision process, a cooperative protocol, or an institutionally legible form without losing the agency of the person who carries it.**


V12 does **not** yet prove that these students are fully professionally trustworthy in civilian rescue. The volume stops at the announcement of Stage 2. That distinction is essential because the historical V1 reading combined V12 and V13 and therefore blurred Stage-1 professionalization with later rescue-stage evidence.


## 3. No.100 — `編め必殺技`: from instinct to stable form


### 3.1 “Ultimate move” is a reliability concept before it is a spectacle concept


The phrase `必殺技` initially sounds like shonen branding, and the chapter knowingly plays with that register. But Cementoss immediately supplies a more operational definition. At `V12:p009`, the faculty frames learned moves/forms as bodily patterns that can become difficult for others to follow or disrupt. Midnight adds that techniques symbolize the self: `技は己を象徴する`.


The crucial refinement arrives at `V12:p012`. Cementoss explains that if one can take `安定行動` without being swayed by circumstances, that itself constitutes meaningful combat capability. He also explicitly notes that an ultimate move does **not** have to be an attack; Iida's high-speed movement can count as a reliable professional asset.


This matters because the series is moving away from a crude equation:


`powerful attack = professional heroism`


and toward a richer one:


`reproducible, situation-resistant capability + appropriate judgment = professional utility`.


That logic links backward to several earlier volumes. Midoriya's original OFA use was powerful but not reliably repeatable because it destroyed the body carrying it. Full Cowling improved continuity. V10 then made future rescue capacity an explicit bodily resource. V11 supplied the first held-out injury-free crisis success. V12 now turns that lesson into curriculum: a future hero needs action forms that can be repeated, controlled, and deployed without improvisational self-destruction every time.


### 3.2 Licensing evaluates a portfolio, not one heroic essence


At `V12:p011`, Aizawa defines hero work broadly: incidents, accidents, disasters, and other forms of trouble from which people must be rescued. He then lists multiple kinds of aptitude tested across professional evaluation: `情報力`, `判断力`, `機動力`, `戦闘力`, `コミュニケーション能力`, `魅力`, `統率力`, among others.


The page is especially important because Aizawa simultaneously says combat capability will be increasingly emphasized under current conditions. The point is therefore **not** that combat is unimportant. The point is that combat sits inside a plural competency ecology.


This is the cleanest V12 answer to the first-pass phrase “heroism becomes professionalized.” Professionalization here means that good intentions must be made observable through dimensions that institutions can assess:


- information acquisition;
- decision quality;
- mobility;
- controlled force;
- communication;
- leadership;
- public/social presence;
- and repeatable technical execution.


The series is not saying the institution perfectly measures heroism. Earlier volumes have already shown blind spots, narrative management, and metric distortions. Instead, V12 shows why **some** standardization is unavoidable if society is going to grant adolescents legal authority to act as heroes.


### 3.3 Emergency compression does not eliminate pedagogical structure


At `V12:p013`, the remaining summer period becomes a compressed training block: students develop their Quirks while constructing special moves. This happens because the post-Kamino environment has accelerated the timetable.


The institutional pattern is consistent with V07–V11. U.A. does not respond to danger by saying rules no longer matter. It adapts the training schedule and methods while retaining an educational structure. This supports the model of U.A. as an institution capable of changing operational form without abandoning its protective/professional purpose.


That is different from both complacency and reckless militarization. The school is clearly placing first-years under unusually advanced demands, but it is doing so through supervised training, support-development infrastructure, and later an external standardized credentialing exam.


## 4. All Might after the Symbol: teaching by refusing to be the answer


### 4.1 `君はまだ 私に倣おうとしてるぞ`


Midoriya is stuck. He has OFA, Full Cowling, the doctor warning, and a general need to protect his arms, but he has not yet converted those facts into a new action grammar.


At `V12:p020`, All Might gives him a deliberately minimal diagnosis:


> `君はまだ 私に倣おうとしてるぞ`


The important word is not only “imitating.” It is the pedagogical structure around the statement. All Might does **not** say “use your legs” and thereby install a new answer from above.


At `V12:p021`, his internal principle is explicit:


> `答えを教えるだけでは 教育とは言えない`


and:


> `正誤は重要じゃない 自分で考え…気付くことが重要なんだ！`


This is a direct continuation of V11's post-Symbol transition. All Might's continued value is no longer that he can physically solve the emergency. His value increasingly lies in creating conditions under which the successor can solve a problem without reproducing the mentor.


This is **de-centered support in action**.


### 4.2 All Might has learned something about succession too


Earlier All Might often embodied the answer so completely that Midoriya's admiration became a developmental constraint. Gran Torino called that admiration a `足枷` in V06. V12 is the first especially clean scene where All Might himself behaves as though he understands the pedagogical danger.


The teacher's problem is now analogous to the successor's problem:


- Midoriya must inherit purpose without copying form.
- All Might must support growth without remaining the epistemic center of every solution.


The scene therefore improves both character models at once. Midoriya is not simply “becoming independent,” and All Might is not simply “stepping back.” They are renegotiating what successful mentorship looks like after overwhelming predecessor authority has become impossible and undesirable as the sole model.


## 5. No.101 — Hatsume, Support logic, and embodied self-authorship


### 5.1 Hatsume functions as a different epistemic culture


The Development Studio sequence is not merely a gear-shopping interlude. Hatsume represents a different problem-solving culture from the hero course. She treats devices as prototypes, calls inventions her “babies,” responds enthusiastically to functional constraints, and is far less bound by inherited ideas about what a hero's body or costume should look like.


Midoriya's breakthrough emerges after contact with this design logic. The key shift is from asking:


> “How do I perform All Might's kind of move without ruining my arms?”


to asking:


> “Why did I assume OFA must be organized around that bodily pattern at all?”


This is a change in problem representation, not just a new attack.


### 5.2 `無意識の内に決めつけてた`


At `V12:p044`, Midoriya finally states the mistaken assumption:


> `オールマイトの必殺技は基本的に拳`
>
> `ワン・フォー・オールを授かった僕もそうあるべきだと 無意識の内に決めつけてた！`


The source is more precise than the V1 shorthand “Midoriya stops trying to become All Might's body.” He had **unconsciously decided** that inheriting OFA implied inheriting a fist-centered combat grammar.


At `V12:p045`, the answer appears embarrassingly simple once the assumption is visible:


> `腕が不安なら 脚をメインに…!!!`
>
> `ワン・フォー・オール フルカウル シュートスタイル!!`


All Might's response is simply `正解だ`.


This is a major self-authorship event because the “correct” move is not a rejection of inheritance. Midoriya does not discard OFA, All Might's values, or All Might as mentor. He changes the **interface between inherited power and his own body**.


The best model is therefore:


> **authorship through adaptive translation, not authorship through severance.**


Midoriya becomes more himself by changing the form in which inherited purpose enters his embodiment.


### 5.3 The solution is relationally assembled, not individually invented ex nihilo


It would be a mistake to convert Shoot Style into a myth of solitary independence. The solution depends on several relational inputs:


- the doctor's warning makes the arms a future-capacity constraint;
- All Might identifies imitation as the hidden block;
- Hatsume's design environment destabilizes the assumption that established form is fixed;
- Iida provides an existing lower-body movement reference;
- Midoriya performs the actual synthesis and practice.


This matters for the wider series thesis. Self-authorship is repeatedly relational in MHA. The alternative to possession is not isolation. A person can be deeply shaped by others while remaining the author of the resulting configuration.


### 5.4 Support gear is an agency amplifier, not an embarrassment


V12 also advances the status of equipment. Gear is not treated as proof that the hero's “real” Quirk is insufficient. It is a legitimate means of making a power safer, more precise, and more compatible with the user's body and role.


That logic will later become especially explicit through Kaminari's equipment in this same volume. V12's broader proposition is:


> **professional power includes engineered interfaces between body, Quirk, equipment, and social responsibility.**


This revises any power philosophy that treats “natural output” as a purer form of strength.


## 6. No.102 — refinement, role reversal, and low-stakes emotional evidence


### 6.1 Shoot Style is a direction, not mastery


At `V12:p049`, All Might treats Midoriya's new direction as meaningful, but the surrounding dialogue makes clear that Midoriya has not suddenly finished his development. He still needs more refinement and a more reliable named-move level of execution.


This is important for model discipline. V12 supports:


- a changed default bodily grammar;
- a new decision route when arm risk matters;
- increased authorship;
- a foundation for sustainable technique.


It does **not** support:


- full mastery of Shoot Style;
- permanent elimination of self-injury risk;
- proof that Midoriya will always choose sustainable action under every rescue trigger.


The earlier catastrophic-cost model therefore remains constrained rather than erased.


### 6.2 `もう私は 守られる側か`


At `V12:p050`, Bakugo warns All Might to watch out during training, and All Might realizes:


> `皆の意識下では もう私は 守られる側か`


This is one of the most economical post-Kamino status markers in the volume.


The change is not merely that All Might is weaker. The students' **automatic social model** of him has changed. He is now someone whose safety must be considered inside their action planning.


V11 established that All Might could remain valuable after combat retirement. V12 shows the reciprocal side: the people he once protected have incorporated his vulnerability into their own protective reflexes.


This is a genuine role reversal without humiliation. The former protector is not devalued by becoming protectable.


### 6.3 Uraraka: affect is grounded; self-label remains open


The dorm/training interlude provides exactly the kind of low-stakes evidence required by Method V2.1.


At `V12:p056`, Uraraka says that recently her heart has been needlessly unsettled more often. Ashido immediately diagnoses the state as `恋だ`.


But that diagnosis belongs to Ashido.


Uraraka's own response at `V12:p057` is denial/uncertainty rather than explicit self-recognition. Her body then involuntarily floats under the embarrassment, and she continues to watch Midoriya's training at `V12:p058`.


The correct state is therefore:


> **Midoriya-linked romantic affect is increasingly behaviorally legible to peers and to the reader; Uraraka has not yet cleanly adopted “love” as her own explicit self-description.**


This distinction matters for later relationship modeling. External legibility is not identical to internal articulation.


## 7. Licensing as institutional translation


### 7.1 First-year licensing is explicitly exceptional


Before the exam, Aizawa explains that obtaining a provisional license as a first-year is uncommon nationally. U.A.'s students are being pushed into an accelerated path because of changed conditions and the intensity of their existing training.


This strengthens the “adaptive institution under emergency” model. U.A. is not simply pretending normal conditions continue. At the same time, the students must enter an **external** credentialing environment with shared rules. U.A. cannot simply declare its own first-years professionally ready.


This is significant because it prevents the school from becoming entirely self-authorizing. Legitimacy requires recognition outside the institution's own internal pedagogy.


### 7.2 Stage 1 mechanics


At `V12:p075`, the examiner states that 1,540 applicants are present. Stage 1 will pass only the first 100 people to meet the condition.


At `V12:p077`, the condition is made concrete:


- each examinee attaches three targets to approved body positions;
- participants use issued balls;
- when all three targets have been hit, that person is eliminated;
- the person responsible for the third target hit receives the elimination credit;
- the first 100 people to record the required eliminations advance.


The design creates a high-information, multi-agent environment rather than a simple duel bracket. It rewards mobility, deception, observation, targeting, alliance management, and tactical timing.


### 7.3 Public recognition becomes an information disadvantage


At `V12:p081`, examinees discuss an annual custom nearly equivalent to a ritual of targeting U.A. Midoriya immediately connects the problem to the Sports Festival: many schools lack reliable information about one another's Quirks, but U.A.'s most visible first-years have had their abilities publicly broadcast.


At `V12:p082`, the exam starts and the surrounding field immediately converges on U.A.


This is a major continuation of the public-legibility ledger. The Sports Festival's publicity previously produced:


- recruitment opportunity;
- social recognition;
- celebrity/fame;
- villain intelligence;
- symbolic targeting.


V12 adds a new conversion:


> **institutionally legitimate competition turns fame into asymmetric tactical intelligence.**


Public visibility is neither simply good nor simply dangerous. It changes the information economy around the person.


### 7.4 Aizawa's response is not protective concealment


At `V12:p083`, Aizawa observes the U.A. targeting without treating it as a reason the school should have hidden the students from the exam. The implicit curriculum is now adversarial adaptation: students must act under a known condition in which opponents have more information about them than they have about many opponents.


This is consistent with Aizawa's established pedagogy. He values accurate diagnosis under meaningful constraints. The exam is no longer a controlled U.A. practical, but his students' preparation is being tested against an external environment where the school cannot set the `勝ち筋` for them.


## 8. No.104 — refined technique in a chaotic field


Chapter 104 matters less because any one move is decisive than because it shows many students converting earlier training into **stable affordances**.


The exam field forces abilities to function amid:


- incomplete information;
- hundreds of moving opponents;
- multiple schools;
- rapidly changing local alliances;
- limited pass slots;
- public knowledge of U.A. techniques.


In other words, this is the first immediate proof-of-use environment for the chapter-100 pedagogy.


The result is not that Class 1-A becomes tactically uniform. Students retain very different operational profiles. What changes is that those profiles become more legible as things teammates can plan around.


This is the volume's bridge from individual “ultimate moves” to team coordination. Reliable individual forms reduce coordination uncertainty because peers can predict what one another can do.


## 9. Inasa and Shiketsu: intensity, prestige, and an open contradiction


Shiketsu enters as a nationally prestigious peer institution, conventionally framed against U.A. as the western elite counterpart. Inasa Yoarashi immediately distinguishes himself through extreme energy, formal courtesy, and almost physically excessive enthusiasm.


At `V12:p064`, he declares that he loves U.A. and wants to compete with its students. At `V12:p065`, however, the source reveals that he had performed exceptionally in U.A.'s recommendation entrance process the previous year and nevertheless declined admission.


That creates a prospective contradiction:


- stated enthusiasm for U.A.;
- elite ability compatible with admission;
- deliberate refusal to enroll.


The reason remains OPEN in V12. Later explanations must not be backfilled here.


For character modeling, the source supports a narrow early Inasa model:


- high-arousal sincerity;
- unusually direct praise;
- intense competitive appetite;
- respectful/formal surface register even when physically overwhelming;
- unresolved hidden reason for declining U.A.


It does not yet support a mature motive model.


## 10. No.105 — presented-Camie and recognition through behavioral mismatch


### 10.1 The identity boundary must remain strict


The person operating under the identity of Shiketsu's Utsushimi Camie behaves strangely toward Midoriya, isolates him, and uses deception/disguise during the exam.


V12 does not authorize importing later identity knowledge into this reading. The correct notation is therefore something like:


> **presented-Camie / the person Midoriya encounters under the Camie identity**


until the source itself resolves more.


### 10.2 Midoriya detects the false Uraraka through a relationship model


The most important scene is not the disguise itself but how Midoriya detects it.


At `V12:p123`, the apparent Uraraka gives an explanation for her exposed state and behavior. At `V12:p124`, Midoriya concludes:


> `僕の知ってる麗日さんじゃない`


He explains that the behavior does not match the Uraraka he knows. The judgment is not based merely on costume or appearance. He uses a learned model of how Uraraka behaves under danger and how she uses her Quirk.


This is high-value character-reconstruction evidence because it demonstrates **relationship-conditioned prediction** inside canon. Midoriya has accumulated enough behavioral knowledge of Uraraka to reject a visual imitation when the action policy is wrong.


The scene gives a concrete example of why character modeling cannot be reduced to catchphrases or appearance:


> identity becomes legible through conditional behavior.


That is directly relevant to the project's reconstruction method.


### 10.3 Recognition remains fallible and bounded


Midoriya does not instantly understand the deceiver's true identity or entire mechanism. His success is narrower: he detects that “this is not how the Uraraka I know would act.”


That is a useful epistemic distinction. Strong relational recognition can detect mismatch without supplying a complete causal explanation.


## 11. No.106–107 — Shishikura, Kaminari, and precision as professionalization


### 11.1 Shishikura's prestige logic


Seiji Shishikura's behavior gives V12 a foil for professionalization. He is not arguing that standards are unnecessary. He argues from a highly hierarchical idea of professional status and selection.


At `V12:p154`, he interprets current conditions as evidence that indiscriminate masses should be filtered so the hero profession can become a higher-order occupation. His rhetoric treats selection as purification and visibly despises people he regards as lowering the profession's standing.


This is important because V12 otherwise gives many positive reasons for licensing and standards. Shishikura demonstrates that **professionalization itself is not automatically humane**. Standards can be organized around public safety and reliable service, or around status purification and contempt.


The volume therefore distinguishes:


- professional competence;
- professional legitimacy;
- professional elitism.


They are not the same concept.


### 11.2 Kaminari's problem is coordination, not raw voltage


Kaminari's chapter is especially important under the V2.1 modeling amendment.


At `V12:p154`, Shishikura warns that indiscriminate discharge would hurt Kaminari's transformed allies because their pain sensation remains active.


Kaminari's own earlier reasoning is shown at `V12:p157`:


> `俺 電気操れるワケじゃないんで… 共同がムズいンスよ`


His development problem is therefore explicitly social-operational: his Quirk's broad discharge is difficult to use in cooperation.


This is a textbook example of the difference between output and professional usability.


### 11.3 Support gear converts indiscriminate power into selective action


At `V12:p158`, Kaminari's new support system is explained. He can place pointer discs, and when a target is within the relevant range, his electrical discharge can be directed along that target relationship rather than merely saturating the environment.


Kaminari summarizes the value simply:


> `これなら 周り巻き込まずに個性使えるんスね`


That line could stand as a thesis for V12's power philosophy.


The upgrade does not make Kaminari's Quirk “more authentic.” It makes his power **more socially usable**.


The professional improvement is not maximum electricity. It is the ability to discriminate among targets while protecting allies.


### 11.4 Kaminari also demonstrates independent social judgment


Chapter 107 is titled `上鳴電気の思うこと` for a reason. Kaminari does not merely fire a new gadget. He interprets other people.


He recalls Bakugo explaining how his gauntlet/grenade system works, recognizes that Bakugo had supplied him with an explosive option before being transformed, notices Bakugo's intentionally useful positioning, and rejects Shishikura's contemptuous reading of his classmates.


At `V12:p159`, Kaminari's defense is blunt:


> `こいつらをディスってんじゃねえよ!!`


At `V12:p160`, after the successful counter, Kirishima thanks Kaminari directly: `ありがとな 上鳴`. Bakugo's corresponding register remains abrasive: `遅えんだよ アホ面!!`


The interaction is valuable because it preserves Bakugo's register while showing functional trust. Bakugo can prepare another student with equipment and rely on him without suddenly becoming verbally gentle. Kaminari, meanwhile, can read the pro-social function under the aggressive surface.


This materially advances both Kaminari and Bakugo modeling.


## 12. No.108 — Iida, Aoyama, and distributed success


### 12.1 Bakugo recognizes Midoriya's authorship


At `V12:p172`, after seeing the changed Midoriya, Bakugo asks:


> `“借り物”… 自分のモンになったかよ`


This line is structurally important.


Earlier volumes repeatedly described OFA as inherited/received power, while Midoriya worked toward making it `僕の力`. V12 has Midoriya consciously break an All-Might-shaped bodily assumption. Bakugo then independently recognizes that transformation from the outside.


The result is a recognition chain:


- Midoriya identifies his unconscious imitation;
- All Might validates the self-authored solution;
- Bakugo recognizes that the “borrowed” power is becoming Midoriya's own.


That is much stronger evidence than a solitary internal declaration.


### 12.2 Iida's leadership is no longer merely procedural


As Stage 1 approaches its limit, most of Class 1-A has passed, but Iida remains outside with Aoyama.


At `V12:p175–176`, Iida refuses the logic that he should simply secure his own pass. He says he came looking for the others and frames this through his class-president role. Most importantly, he says:


> `兄さんならそうする`


and:


> `俺の行動は 俺の夢の形でもある`


This is an important maturation of inherited aspiration. Tensei remains a model, but Iida does not present his behavior as mere imitation. The elder brother's ideal is being translated into Iida's present role and choice.


That parallels Midoriya's V12 process at a different scale:


- inherit an admired model;
- refuse passive copying;
- translate it into current responsibility;
- make the action one's own.


### 12.3 Aoyama's hidden desire is equality


Aoyama initially appears prepared to sacrifice his own pass. At `V12:p178`, he fires a conspicuous Navel Laser beacon despite being one target from elimination.


At `V12:p179`, he tells Iida `君に譲っちゃう` and explains the tactical idea: attracting opponents can expose them to Iida's speed.


Then the scene gives a highly consequential motivation line:


> `僕はずっと 対等になりたかったのさ`


This dramatically upgrades Aoyama's model.


His flamboyance is not adequately described as attention-seeking alone. In this crisis, conspicuousness becomes a tool for giving another person a chance, and his internal motive is framed through a desire for equality with classmates.


The chapter also briefly opens an unresolved self-difference question through the parental memory at `V12:p177`: `パパン ママン… どうして僕は皆と違うの？`


V12 does not explain the cause. The correct state is:


- Aoyama experiences himself as different;
- the difference has a family-linked history;
- he desires equality/belonging;
- under pressure he is willing to make himself conspicuous and risk elimination for another student;
- the underlying cause remains OPEN.


### 12.4 The beacon converts visibility from vulnerability into coordination infrastructure


Aoyama's laser is immediately visible across the field. That visual conspicuousness could have been purely suicidal. Instead, classmates recognize the signal and converge.


This creates a powerful volume-level inversion.


Earlier in the exam:


> **visibility = exposure**


U.A.'s Sports Festival visibility lets opponents target them.


At the end:


> **visibility = coordination**


Aoyama deliberately makes himself visible so allies can find one another.


Public legibility is therefore not intrinsically vulnerability. Its effect depends on who can read the signal, what relationship exists between them, and whether the visible person controls the terms of exposure.


### 12.5 All twenty pass Stage 1


At `V12:p184`, the remaining-pass counter reaches zero. At `V12:p185`, U.A. celebrates that the entire class passed the first stage.


The accomplishment is not evidence that every student has identical professional competence. It is evidence that the class can survive an information-disadvantaged, multi-agent competitive filter without atomizing entirely into solitary optimization.


That is a meaningful answer to V11's distributed-support question.


But the next page prevents overclaiming.


At `V12:p187`, the examiner announces the next test: participants will perform a rescue exercise as bystanders in the simulated disaster site.

## 13. V12 synthesis — five forms of translation


V12 can be organized around five translation problems.


### 13.1 Value → technique


Students need actions that embody purpose in repeatable form. `必殺技` is valuable when it creates stable behavior under pressure, not merely when it looks impressive.


### 13.2 Inheritance → authorship


Midoriya's OFA becomes more genuinely his when he stops assuming All Might's bodily grammar is compulsory. Iida likewise translates Tensei's example into his own class-president conduct.


### 13.3 Limitation → design


Arm risk, indiscriminate electricity, and other constraints are not simply weaknesses to overpower. They become design inputs for new techniques and support equipment.


### 13.4 Visibility → information


U.A.'s fame becomes tactical exposure; Aoyama later converts deliberate visibility into an ally beacon. Legibility is a resource whose ethical/tactical meaning depends on control and audience.


### 13.5 Individuals → coordinated class


The first exam stage begins by exploiting U.A. as a collectively visible target and ends with Class 1-A using its own internal knowledge and signals to secure all twenty passes.


The class is becoming an information network, not merely twenty separate combatants.

## 17. Visual and formal motifs


### Body re-routing


Shoot Style reorients the visual line of Midoriya's body from arm-centered inherited imitation toward lower-body movement. The body itself becomes the page on which authorship is redrawn.


### Targets on bodies


Stage 1 literally makes each examinee's vulnerability visible as three attached targets. This concretizes the volume's wider information theme: professional action happens while one's own exploitable surfaces are known.


### Convergence on U.A.


The mass opening attack visually compresses the cost of public fame. U.A. is recognizable before individual students have learned much about their opponents.


### False appearance / true behavior


The false-Uraraka sequence separates visual likeness from behavioral identity. The manga makes recognition a problem of action continuity rather than face alone.


### Pointer / circuit logic


Kaminari's support equipment visually turns diffuse electricity into routed connection. It is an unusually literal image of the volume's thesis: power becomes professional when it can be directed through relationships without indiscriminately harming the surrounding network.


### Beacon reversal


Aoyama's vertical laser transforms spectacle into a coordination signal. The same basic property—being conspicuous—that produces vulnerability elsewhere becomes a resource when deliberately authored.

## 21. Volume thesis


Volume 12 is not a post-Kamino cooldown. It is the first sustained demonstration of what the series means by **surviving succession structurally**.


All Might cannot remain society's overwhelming physical answer. The solution is not to locate a new identical center as quickly as possible. Instead, the volume distributes competence across several layers:


- a teacher who gives diagnostic prompts rather than answers;
- a successor who changes inherited power to fit his own body;
- support engineers who convert constraints into usable interfaces;
- students who make techniques reliable enough for others to coordinate around;
- an external licensing system that asks whether those capacities survive outside U.A.'s own controlled tests;
- classmates who use knowledge of one another to detect deception, route aid, share tools, search for stragglers, and turn visibility into a signal.


This makes V12 the natural successor to V11.


V11 established that succession should not require the mentor's death and that institutions/relationships must hold people accountable while keeping them alive. V12 asks what that principle looks like when translated into practice.


Its answer is neither “become independent” nor “work as a team” in the abstract.


It is more precise:


> **inherit what is worth inheriting, identify the form that no longer fits, redesign the interface, make the result repeatable, and become legible to others in ways that permit coordination rather than possession.**


That is the volume's strongest model change.
