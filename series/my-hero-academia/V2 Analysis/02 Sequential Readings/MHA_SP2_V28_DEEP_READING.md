# My Hero Academia - Second-Pass Deep Reading, Volume 28

## 0. Status, method, and prospective lock

This reading follows `MHA_SP2_ANALYTICAL_METHOD_V2_1.md` and begins from the exact frozen Volume 27 endpoint at `V27:p180`.

The admissible state entering Volume 28 is intentionally incomplete. The preemptive operation has already become open war; Twice is dead; Hawks is critically burned but was extracted alive by Tokoyami; Mirko is catastrophically injured but still operating; Garaki's Nomu infrastructure is exposed; and Shigaraki remains inside the interrupted augmentation process. V27 did **not** establish the actual outcome of that procedure, the result of the hospital or mansion operations, the later status of Hawks or Mirko, Best Jeanist's true status, the content of Dabi's reader-withheld identity disclosure, later Toga development after Twice's death, or later One For All mechanics.

Volume 28 is therefore read prospectively. Familiar later knowledge about Shigaraki, All For One, the war, Dabi, Float, later casualties, or later battle outcomes is inadmissible until the pages themselves establish it.

# 1. Governing transition: inherited burden becomes distributed containment

Volume 27 converted affiliation into action. Volume 28 makes inheritance itself operationally dangerous.

The governing transition is:

> **relational commitment under wartime triage / affiliation converted into action -> inherited burden as distributed containment**

A compact formulation is:

> **V28 turns inherited power into a pressure network. AFO gives Shigaraki an original Quirk and a succession claim; OFA warns Midoriya through predecessor relation; Search makes Midoriya trackable; Decay converts geography into an attack surface; and no inheritor can responsibly contain the resulting danger alone. Survival depends on distributed information, evacuation, Erasure, bodily support, extraction, pursuit management, and relationship-conditioned judgment.**

The title `継承` is therefore more than a lore chapter. The volume places several forms of succession beside one another and then asks what they do under stress.

- Garaki inherits and preserves an intellectual project rejected by ordinary society.
- All For One prepares succession by retaining a duplicate while transferring the original All For One Quirk to Shigaraki.
- Shigaraki inherits not only power but an intrusive AFO-shaped desire directed toward One For All.
- Midoriya receives a warning from the first OFA holder before he possesses enough information to understand the full mechanism.
- Aizawa's relationship to Class 1-A becomes a reason to remain alive and maintain Erasure.
- Bakugo refuses to allow the OFA bearer to disappear into unilateral burden, while still describing his choice through rivalry, competence, and unfinished defeat rather than sentimental confession.
- Gran Torino converts OFA secrecy into tactical protection rather than letting Midoriya treat inherited responsibility as permission for suicidal engagement.

The ethical center of V28 is not “great power creates great responsibility” in the abstract. It is more specific: **when inherited capacity becomes systemically consequential, responsibility has to be distributed among people who can perceive different parts of the danger.** The volume repeatedly punishes single-point failure.

Aizawa can neutralize Shigaraki's Quirks, but only if Manual maintains his eyes, other heroes move his body, and the field prevents Shigaraki from reaching him. Midoriya can serve as bait, but Bakugo recognizes the decision immediately, Endeavor must receive the private communication, Gran Torino must rescue them when their plan exceeds their capability, and the surviving professionals must form the actual containment ring. The heroes' strength lies less in possessing one answer than in preventing any indispensable person from becoming the only answer.

That makes V28 a major step in the reread's long-running critique of solitary heroism.

# 2. No.268 - `SCRAMBLE!!`: prevention survives only as coordinated specialization

## 2.1 The hospital battle is already a system, not a collection of duels

The opening does not reset after V27. It resumes a broken operational network and asks whether the network can still function while its components are injured.

The High-End fight requires simultaneous roles:

- Aizawa suppresses Quirks rather than attempting to overpower the targets;
- Endeavor and the other frontline heroes exploit the suppression window;
- Mirko continues trying to reach Shigaraki's capsule despite severe bodily damage;
- support heroes reposition people and manage access;
- the objective remains prospective prevention: **do not let Shigaraki wake.**

At `V28:p020`, Aizawa and Present Mic make the operation's purpose explicit: `絶対に 死柄木を 起こさせるな!!` The important object is not a retrospective victory score. It is a future state that must never be allowed to occur.

That extends V27's preventive ethics but changes its scale. Hawks's problem with Twice involved whether one person could be safely permitted to re-enter battle. Here the entire hospital team is trying to prevent a successor-state from becoming operational. Prevention is no longer controversial because of uncertainty about whether the danger is real; the danger is materially visible. The moral question moves to **how much bodily and institutional expenditure prevention justifies**.

## 2.2 Mirko remains an agent after bodily catastrophe

V27 established Mirko's willingness to treat injury as a changing constraint rather than a command to stop. V28 preserves that model.

Her attacks are not represented as simple rage. She keeps pursuing the relevant objective. `踵半月輪` at `V28:p016` is directed into the capsule fight rather than into a vanity contest with whichever enemy hurt her. She is still choosing means relative to mission state.

This matters for character reconstruction. A generic “reckless battle maniac” model would predict attraction to the most exciting opponent. The source instead shows a woman whose risk threshold is extreme **and whose attention remains task-bound**. Her body can be expendable to her without the task becoming arbitrary.

The distinction also prevents moral romanticization. The volume makes the costs of this policy grotesquely visible. Competence does not make bodily expenditure harmless.

## 2.3 Erasure is valuable because others know how to use its window

Aizawa's Quirk becomes increasingly important as enemy power scales, but the narrative refuses to turn that importance into solo supremacy. Erasure has no value if allies do not exploit it, if Aizawa cannot maintain sight, or if the field cannot keep lethal threats away from him.

V28 therefore begins establishing the model that will dominate its final chapter: **a high-leverage individual ability creates a support obligation around the individual rather than proving that individual self-sufficiency.**

# 3. No.269 - `三人で`: old relationships become present operational infrastructure

## 3.1 `Three of us` turns memory into action

The chapter title and `V28:p033` deliberately reactivate the Aizawa/Present Mic/Shirakumo triad. Present Mic's `三人で— 真贋確認!` does not undo Kurogiri's present identity or claim that Shirakumo simply returned. Instead, old relationship history supplies the motivation and language through which Aizawa and Mic approach the present crisis.

This is a continuation of V26's layered-personhood finding. The old bond is real evidence; so is the transformed person who exists now. V28 uses the bond as **operational memory** rather than as metaphysical proof that the current identity is false.

The “three” motif also quietly anticipates the rest of the volume. Major problems repeatedly become survivable only when a relation adds a third term to an apparent duel:

- Aizawa + Shigaraki requires Manual/support;
- Midoriya + Shigaraki requires Bakugo and then Gran Torino;
- Endeavor + Shigaraki requires Erasure and the wider hero ring;
- AFO -> Shigaraki inheritance is haunted by the Shimura family and opposed by OFA's inherited voices.

Binary confrontation is repeatedly broken open by networked relation.

## 3.2 Shigaraki's clinical death state is an engineered procedure, not a final outcome

At `V28:p035`, the text gives unusually concrete information: Shigaraki is not breathing and his heart is stopped. Garaki explains that the procedure used a provisional death-like state to reduce the burden of stabilization while the capsule maintained necessary life functions.

That means two tempting readings are both incorrect at this point:

1. “Shigaraki was simply dead and resurrected by unexplained villain magic.”
2. “The heroes safely succeeded because the monitor shows no ordinary vital signs.”

The state is intentionally engineered and incomplete.

Garaki's panic at `V28:p036`—the fear that everything will end—shows that the interruption matters. But it does not yet tell us what fraction of the intended outcome has been lost. The later `75%` stabilization/settling figure will become important precisely because V28 refuses a binary success/failure model.

## 3.3 Garaki's grief is organizational evidence

Garaki's language about the end of the “Demon King's dream” demonstrates that his project is not merely paid technical labor. His investment is ideological, relational, biographical, and intellectual.

This strengthens the earlier AFO/Garaki “friend” evidence. AFO built durable villain infrastructure partly because people around him experienced recognition, patronage, or purpose through him. Malignant systems can produce authentic commitment.

That is important to the series' broader recognition thesis: recognition is not inherently moral. It can rescue, bind, exploit, recruit, or radicalize depending on the structure into which it is inserted.

# 4. No.270 - `継承`: the Quirk Singularity theory moves from mocked thesis to governing infrastructure

## 4.1 Garaki's theory is now source-grounded history

At `V28:p041-42`, Garaki identifies the old thesis the world mocked: successive generations produce increasingly complex and powerful Quirks, eventually creating a future whose power cannot be cleanly controlled. The text places his argument roughly seventy years in the past and notes that the scholar disappeared from ordinary public life.

The important V2 correction is not “Garaki was right about everything.” The source supports something narrower and stronger:

- he articulated the Quirk Singularity problem early;
- social rejection helped sever him from legitimate institutions;
- AFO's recognition provided a patron and operational home;
- the resulting research later became part of AFO/Nomu/Shigaraki infrastructure.

This is another villain-formation pathway different from Shigaraki, Spinner, Toga, Twice, or Dabi. Garaki is not primarily formed through social abandonment after a visible crisis. He is an ideologue/researcher whose rejected explanatory model finds a patron willing to let him pursue it without ethical constraint.

## 4.2 `摂生`: Garaki's own body is already an inheritance experiment

Garaki names his own Quirk `摂生` at `V28:p043`: roughly doubled life force/lifespan in exchange for reduced motor ability. He describes giving the Quirk to AFO and later reveals that the version now inside himself is a duplicate.

This makes Quirk duplication more than a convenient explanation for repeated combat abilities. It is part of the relationship architecture between AFO and Garaki.

AFO's regime can separate:

- original from copy;
- body from capability;
- owner from user;
- present bearer from intended successor.

That technical separation makes Quirks into movable institutional resources. The moral problem is not the existence of duplication itself. It is the way AFO/Garaki treat powers—and often people—as inventories to be allocated toward a succession project.

## 4.3 AFO's original Quirk is explicitly transferred to Shigaraki

`V28:p053` resolves one of the largest OPEN registers from V27.

Garaki explains that AFO anticipated defeat and prepared succession. AFO retained/used a duplicate while the **original** All For One Quirk was designated for Shigaraki Tomura.

This matters at several levels.

First, Shigaraki is not merely “the next leader of the League.” The succession is embodied. AFO's defining acquisition/transfer power becomes part of Shigaraki's body.

Second, the original/copy distinction prevents careless conflation of prison-era AFO and Shigaraki's current power state. The two men are connected by a technological succession architecture rather than by simple replacement.

Third, the transfer problematizes autonomy. If a predecessor can pass not merely tools but also tendencies, stored Quirks, vestigial impulses, or target-fixations, then inheriting power also means inheriting pressure from another person's history.

The later pages make that pressure visible without yet resolving who ultimately governs whom.

# 5. Shigaraki's inheritance dream: family memory and AFO pressure compete for jurisdiction

## 5.1 The family does not disappear from Shigaraki's interiority

The dream sequence at `V28:p047-56` revisits Hana, his mother, his grandparents, Kotaro, Nana, and AFO-shaped imagery. This is not a neutral biographical replay. It stages competing claims on Tenko/Shigaraki's identity.

Several details resist a simplistic “he has transcended his past” interpretation.

- family members still appear as people capable of touching, calling, and restraining him;
- Hana's prior betrayal/apology remains affectively legible;
- his mother's question about whether he still wants to be a hero remains part of the interior archive;
- Nana's `忘れないでね` preserves the demand of memory;
- Kotaro's disciplinary presence still appears as an intrusive command.

The family is neither healed nor irrelevant. It remains active material inside his self-model.

## 5.2 Shigaraki rejects restraint, but the text does not equate rejection with freedom

The visual action is clear: family figures reach for him; he moves away; their forms break down. The sequence can support the proposition that Shigaraki rejects a return to the family identity that preceded Tomura Shigaraki.

It cannot yet support the stronger proposition that he becomes fully autonomous.

The reason is the final intrusion: AFO-associated presence tells him `俺を否定するな`—do not deny/reject me. That is not the voice of self-authored liberation. It is another external/internal claim upon him.

The dream therefore creates a jurisdiction problem:

- rejecting family restraint may be genuinely Shigaraki's choice;
- AFO's demand may also be operating within the inherited structure;
- the reader does not yet know how separable those impulses are.

V28 consequently **strengthens Shigaraki's agency evidence while simultaneously making his autonomy less simple**.

## 5.3 Seventy-five percent is narratively important because incompletion remains causal

The monitor's `75%` at `V28:p052` is not just a power-scaling statistic. It preserves the heroes' partial success.

They did not prevent awakening. They did interrupt the process. The result is neither full victory nor total failure.

That partiality becomes a recurring V28 structure: the operation repeatedly produces **damaged containment rather than clean control**. Shigaraki wakes, but incompletely. Hawks survives for now, but is incapacitated. Mirko survives for now, but is gravely injured. Aizawa can suppress Quirks, but must be physically maintained. Midoriya can redirect pursuit, but cannot safely fight the target alone.

The war is governed by partial states.

# 6. OFA answers AFO succession with warning rather than command

At `V28:p058`, the first OFA holder reaches Midoriya with a simple warning: `来るよ`.

This is structurally parallel to AFO's inherited pressure inside Shigaraki but ethically different in presentation.

The first holder does not seize Midoriya's body or dictate a total plan. He alerts Midoriya to an approaching danger. In No.272 the warning develops into `止めるんだ` and the recognition that a `超越者` is coming.

The distinction should not be overdrawn into “OFA is pure freedom / AFO is pure possession.” OFA still carries inherited voices and obligations. But V28 gives a meaningful difference in **mode of inheritance**:

- AFO's transfer is accompanied by desire to possess OFA and an internalized predecessor demand;
- OFA's predecessor relation supplies warning and shared situational awareness to a present bearer who must still decide what to do.

This strengthens the reread's inheritance-as-jurisdiction thesis from V23. The ethical quality of inheritance depends partly on whether predecessors treat successors as instruments or as agents.

# 7. No.271 - Tokoyami, Hawks, and Dabi: trust can act before complete information

## 7.1 Dabi converts Hawks's secrecy into an accusation against the profession

Dabi's attack on Tokoyami is rhetorical as well as physical. He points to the hero who killed a League member and mocks the “pro” image students admire.

The accusation is strategically potent because Tokoyami does not know everything the reader now knows about Hawks and Twice. The student cannot give a complete courtroom defense of Hawks's decision.

That makes his response important.

## 7.2 Tokoyami's answer is relationship-specific, not ideological

At `V28:p066`, Tokoyami's operative response is essentially: `俺は ただ師を 案じただけだ`—I was simply worried about my mentor.

He does not need to establish that every professional hero is morally clean before rescuing Hawks. He acts from a specific relationship under incomplete information.

At `V28:p077`, this becomes explicit trust: `信じてる! 皆信じてる! 正しいことをしたんだと! だから! 死ぬな!!`

The line is analytically useful because it contains uncertainty. Tokoyami says he **believes** Hawks did the right thing. Belief is not knowledge. He supplies relational trust where full epistemic access is impossible.

That is both humane and risky. The volume does not declare relationship trust universally correct. It shows why institutions composed of opaque actors still require forms of warranted interpersonal confidence to function at all.

## 7.3 Hawks remains alive at this boundary

Hawks loses consciousness during Tokoyami's extraction, but V28 does not establish his later recovery state. The correct boundary is therefore:

- critically burned;
- extracted alive by Tokoyami;
- unconscious during/after escape;
- later medical/operational outcome OPEN.

This prevents a terminal-state overread while preserving the severity of the injury.

# 8. Gigantomachia: succession becomes legible through scent and command

At the end of No.271, Gigantomachia recognizes `主の匂い`—the master's scent.

No.273 makes the command relationship explicit. At `V28:p109`, Shigaraki calls: `おいで マキア / みんなと 一緒に`.

This resolves another V23-V27 uncertainty. AFO's nomination of Shigaraki had not automatically guaranteed Gigantomachia's allegiance. The My Villain Academia material required Shigaraki to prove himself. V28 shows the result becoming operational: the awakened successor can now mobilize Machia.

The command is also relationally revealing. Shigaraki does not say only “come to me.” He says to come **together with everyone**. This preserves the comradeship exception established earlier even as his large-scale objective remains destruction.

The simultaneous `今ここから / 全てを壊す` at `V28:p110` therefore contains the contradiction that has defined mature Shigaraki:

- generalized destruction toward the world/system;
- selective preservation and incorporation of chosen comrades.

His ideology is not indiscriminate in the literal sense. It contains an attachment-defined exclusion zone.

# 9. No.272 - `おはよう!`: Shigaraki's awakening turns geography into a weapon

## 9.1 The first OFA holder identifies the coming threat before Midoriya can

At `V28:p083-84`, Midoriya recognizes the voice as the first holder of One For All. The warning describes a transcendent force and power released from ordinary human fetters.

This is not tactical omniscience. Midoriya still does not know the precise battlefield state. What inheritance provides is **early warning**.

That matters because the rest of the chapter converts seconds of information into lives.

## 9.2 Decay becomes an attack on connectivity itself

The awakened Decay is more than a stronger touch attack. At `V28:p088`, the emergency warning states that Shigaraki's `崩壊` propagates through things in contact and that people must not touch the spreading cracks.

The power therefore weaponizes the built environment.

Roads, buildings, rubble, ground, and connected structures stop being passive setting. They become transmission media.

This is a major visual and conceptual escalation. Earlier rescue scenes could often assume that the environment was the stable space through which rescuers moved. V28 makes **space itself hostile**. Heroism must respond by breaking contact chains, taking people airborne, transmitting warnings, and moving populations faster than destruction propagates.

## 9.3 Information is rescue infrastructure

The chapter's most important hero action is not a punch. It is the rapid transmission of a correct causal model:

- this is Shigaraki's Decay;
- it propagates through contact;
- evacuate;
- do not touch the cracks.

This is a strong hero-society proposition. Mature rescue systems depend on information quality. A powerful individual who knows the answer but cannot transmit it is less useful than a network that can transform observation into coordinated behavior.

The later communications blackout will therefore be strategically important: Shigaraki attacks not only bodies but the network's ability to share state.

## 9.4 Crust's last act is reciprocal rescue

Around `V28:p090-91`, Crust uses his Shield to save Aizawa while Decay reaches him. His final visible gesture is not framed as self-display; he spends his remaining action preserving the person whose Erasure is now systemically indispensable.

This is an unusually clean instance of the reread's reciprocal-rescue thesis. Aizawa is a professional rescuer who must himself be rescued because his survival protects everyone else's ability to survive.

The sacrifice also reinforces a painful institutional truth: distributed systems are more resilient than solitary systems, but they are not costless. Redundancy does not mean nobody dies. It means one person's death can preserve the network long enough for others to continue.

# 10. No.273 - destruction becomes Shigaraki's chosen operational program

## 10.1 Awakening resolves the procedure-outcome OPEN register, but not as full completion

By No.273, Shigaraki is awake and acting. The V27 question “does the procedure actually produce an operational Shigaraki?” is therefore resolved: **yes**.

But V28 simultaneously preserves that the process was interrupted at roughly 75% stabilization. The correct formulation is not “the augmentation completed successfully.” It is:

> Shigaraki awakens operationally with major augmentation and inherited AFO capacity despite an interrupted/incomplete stabilization process.

That distinction matters later because any instability must be allowed to emerge from future evidence rather than retroactively assumed here.

## 10.2 `All of it` remains a chosen destructive horizon

Shigaraki's `全てを壊す` is not presented as a momentary berserk response to pain. It is a deliberate command horizon associated with Machia and the League.

This strengthens V24's destructive self-authorship. AFO's internal pressure complicates autonomy, but the text still gives Shigaraki an articulated objective in his own speech and leadership behavior.

The mature model must therefore hold both:

- Shigaraki has genuine destructive commitments and leadership agency;
- Shigaraki now carries inherited AFO structures that generate their own pressure.

Reducing him either to pure puppet or pure sovereign would discard evidence.

# 11. Toga after Twice: grief converts directly into a world-judgment

V27 ended with immediate grief. V28 gives the first subsequent ideological conversion.

At `V28:p114`, Compress reports that Twice has probably been killed. Dabi describes trying to stop him and frames what followed through his own strategic lens. Toga is visibly altered; the group notices the loss of her ordinary affective equilibrium.

At `V28:p115`, Toga formulates the consequence:

> `生きやすい世界に— / 好きなものだけの世界に— / 邪魔です ヒーロー`

The important point is not a generic “Toga hates heroes more.” Her grief is organized through her long-standing desire for a world in which she can live as herself among what/whom she loves. Twice's death becomes evidence, from her perspective, that heroes are an obstacle to that habitable world.

This is a villain-formation update occurring **inside** an already-formed villain. Formation is not only childhood origin. Institutions continue generating meaning through later encounters.

Toga's relationship to Twice consequently becomes political without ceasing to be personal. Her ideology is downstream of attachment.

# 12. No.274 - `サーチ`: stolen recognition becomes predatory legibility

## 12.1 Ragdoll's Quirk turns another person's history into targeting infrastructure

Shigaraki's use of Search is one of V28's strongest power-philosophy scenes.

Ragdoll's Quirk was originally a rescue/intelligence capacity: locating people and reading relevant information about them. After AFO's theft and Shigaraki's inheritance, the same informational affordance becomes predatory targeting.

At `V28:p132`, Ragdoll herself wishes `サーチ` could again be useful to everyone. The juxtaposition makes the moral point explicit without claiming powers have fixed ethics.

**A Quirk's moral meaning is not contained in its mechanics. It emerges from the relationship between capability, bearer, target, and institutional purpose.**

## 12.2 Search makes Midoriya's inherited role externally legible

Shigaraki does not initially know Midoriya's location by ordinary social knowledge. Search permits him to identify relevant people across distance, and the AFO-linked desire tells him what to seek: One For All.

This transforms OFA secrecy.

Previously, secrecy mainly protected the social fact that Midoriya possessed inherited power. V28 creates an enemy who can locate the bearer through stolen/inherited capability. The burden is no longer only “keep the secret.” It is “manage what happens when the secret bearer becomes a moving beacon.”

The result is a rescue paradox: the person whose power is meant to protect society now threatens civilians merely by being near them when the enemy is pursuing him.

## 12.3 AFO's desire is experienced by Shigaraki as internal pressure

At `V28:p127-29`, Shigaraki experiences the stored Quirks with an almost native familiarity and then notices that something is missing. The missing object is One For All. He describes something inside himself driving him toward it.

This is critical evidence for the autonomy ledger.

The desire is inside Shigaraki, but the text associates it with AFO's historical obsession. Internal location therefore does not equal self-authorship.

A responsible model should distinguish:

- Shigaraki's own destruction-oriented values;
- AFO-originated acquisition pressure toward OFA;
- areas where the two motives align tactically;
- areas where future evidence may show conflict.

V28 establishes the distinction but does not resolve it.

# 13. Midoriya and Bakugo leave evacuation: secrecy becomes a burden-allocation problem

## 13.1 Midoriya recognizes that public disclosure would redirect protectors toward him

At `V28:p135`, Midoriya understands a practical consequence of telling everyone the truth in the middle of evacuation: heroes would divert personnel to protect a student.

This is a mature form of his old self-sacrificial logic. He is no longer merely thinking “I should take the hit because my body is expendable.” He is thinking systemically about scarce personnel and civilian safety.

But the old risk remains. System awareness can become a more sophisticated justification for unilateral self-expenditure.

## 13.2 Bakugo recognizes the decision before Midoriya explains it

Bakugo's response is one of the volume's most important relationship beats.

He observes that heroes try to protect everyone and says, in effect, that from the beginning there was only one immediate choice. He grasps why Midoriya must move away from the evacuation crowd without needing the full plan verbalized first.

This is not mind-reading. It is accumulated relationship competence.

Years of comparison with Midoriya, knowledge of OFA, and recent shared training make Bakugo one of the few people able to infer what Midoriya will do under this particular pressure.

The scene therefore converts intimacy into tactical prediction.

## 13.3 They lie lightly to classmates because secrecy and urgency outrun deliberation

When Todoroki/Uraraka ask where they are going, Midoriya and Bakugo use the “forgotten item” cover and promise to return quickly.

The lie should not be romanticized as proof that secrecy is good. It is evidence that OFA secrecy constrains their options in crisis.

The volume repeatedly shows the cost of information asymmetry:

- Tokoyami must trust Hawks without full knowledge;
- Midoriya cannot tell his classmates why he is a target;
- Endeavor receives private communication instead of a general explanation;
- Gran Torino knows enough about OFA to interpret the danger and intervene.

Trust networks partly compensate for missing public information.

# 14. Midoriya's decoy plan: rescue becomes controlled self-targeting

At `V28:p138`, Midoriya tells Endeavor that Shigaraki may be targeting him and that he may be able to guide Shigaraki toward an area without people.

This is a meaningful development from early-series self-sacrifice.

Midoriya is not simply rushing at the enemy. He is attempting to **change the enemy's path** so the threat vector points away from civilians. His own body/identity becomes bait in a rescue geometry.

The action combines:

- high self-risk;
- analytical inference;
- concern for civilian concentration;
- communication with professional command;
- intentional manipulation of enemy attention.

That is more strategically mature than V01's sludge-villain rush.

It is not yet fully sustainable. The later Gran Torino intervention proves that Midoriya still overestimates what he and Bakugo can safely contain once the bait succeeds.

V28 therefore records both growth and continuity:

> **Midoriya's self-endangerment has become more intelligent, but intelligence has not eliminated the tendency to make himself the disposable center of the rescue solution.**

# 15. Bakugo's accompaniment: relational protection remains encoded as rivalry and self-claim

## 15.1 `Only I could have understood immediately`

At `V28:p146`, Midoriya asks why Bakugo came with him. Bakugo's answer is not “because I was worried about you.” He says that in that situation he was the only person who could immediately understand what was happening and move.

This is both boastful and true enough to matter.

The voice model should preserve the double function:

- Bakugo asserts unique competence and rejects sentimental framing;
- his unique competence is relationship-produced, because he knows Midoriya and OFA unusually well.

The scene demonstrates why “softened Bakugo” is an inadequate reconstruction. Prosocial behavior does not require prosocial surface language.

## 15.2 Bakugo explicitly refuses the `came for me` frame

When Midoriya thanks him, Bakugo rejects the wording: don't get conceited; don't treat yourself like the protagonist; he has his own business with Shigaraki.

The content is defensive, competitive, and status-conscious. Yet it does not undo the action of accompanying Midoriya into danger.

This is precisely the kind of contradiction the character-modeling schema is meant to preserve:

> **Bakugo can materially reduce Midoriya's isolation while verbally refusing to define himself as Midoriya's helper.**

## 15.3 The unresolved Kamino debt remains part of Bakugo's motivation

At `V28:p147-49`, Bakugo frames Shigaraki through the old Kamino wound: he sees himself as the man whose capture contributed to All Might's end and wants a complete victory that answers that humiliation.

He also tracks Midoriya's One For All development with extraordinary precision. At `V28:p148`, he knows Midoriya now uses roughly 30% Full Cowl normally and can reach about 45% at the instant of impact to limit bodily burden.

The rivalry is therefore increasingly informed rather than fantasy-based. Bakugo is measuring himself against Midoriya's actual rate of growth.

# 16. Shigaraki's multi-Quirk combat: inherited abundance threatens coordination itself

# 19. Aizawa's survival motive: teacher identity becomes a containment mechanism

# 20. The endpoint: students return to the teacher's battlefield

Succession is not replacement.

The older generation does not disappear once the younger inherits power. The younger generation does not remain passive because adults are present. Mature inheritance means overlapping responsibility.

That is the opposite of AFO's succession model, in which a predecessor's original power and desire are installed into a successor as a vehicle for continuation.

# 21. Power philosophy: Quirks do not carry ethics; systems assign them functions

Volume 28 provides unusually dense evidence for the power-philosophy ledger.

### AFO/Garaki model: power as transferable inventory

Quirks can be duplicated, stored, transferred, selected, and embedded. Bodies can be modified to host larger inventories. The original/copy distinction is logistical. Power exists to increase strategic reach and succession capacity.

### Shigaraki model: power as destruction plus acquisition pressure

Shigaraki consciously wants broad destruction and selectively preserves comrades. The inherited AFO structure adds a pressure to obtain OFA. His power model is therefore internally plural rather than reducible to one doctrine.

### OFA model: power as inherited warning and protected burden

The first holder communicates risk. Gran Torino treats the bearer as someone who must not be lost. Bakugo understands the bearer well enough to share the tactical burden. OFA increasingly functions as a relationship-bound responsibility rather than a private superweapon.

### Hero-system model: power as composable specialization

Aizawa's Erasure, Manual's water support, Gran Torino's mobility, Endeavor's offense, Ryukyu's transport/body scale, and evacuation personnel all become valuable through combination.

### Ragdoll/Search model: capability is morally recontextualizable

The same information Quirk can support rescue when used by Ragdoll and predation when stolen and used to hunt OFA. Mechanics do not fix ethics.

The deepest V28 proposition is therefore:

> **The moral unit is not the Quirk. It is the capability-in-relation: bearer, target, purpose, institution, and constraints.**

# 22. Hero society: abundance matters only when it can coordinate

V28 contains a pointed line about the `ヒーロー飽和時代`—an age saturated with heroes. The speaker's challenge is effectively: if this many heroes cannot defeat one enemy, what was the point of such abundance?

That line can be read as swagger, but the volume's structure gives it analytical weight.

A large hero population is not automatically resilience. It becomes resilient only through:

- role assignment;
- communication;
- shared threat models;
- evacuation routing;
- specialist support;
- willingness to rescue one another;
- the ability to preserve high-leverage nodes like Aizawa without making them solitary saviors.

Shigaraki's Radio Waves attack exposes the vulnerability. When communications fail, numerical abundance becomes harder to convert into coordinated capacity.

This modifies earlier first-pass “hero saturation” critiques. The problem is not simply that society has too many professional heroes or that professionalism produces complacency. V28 shows why institutional density can be an enormous public good under crisis **if the network can coordinate**.

The series' institutional critique therefore needs two sides:

- hero society can produce blind spots, commodification, secrecy, abusive structures, and dependency on symbols;
- hero society can also produce a thick distributed workforce whose specialized capacities save lives in combinations no singular symbol could reproduce.

V28 is strong evidence for the second proposition.

# 23. Recognition and failed rescue: becoming legible can itself become dangerous

The recognition ledger gains a new form of failure.

Earlier arcs often treated failed recognition as **not seeing someone who needed help**. V28 introduces the inverse: being seen by the wrong system can create danger.

Search makes people legible as targets. Midoriya's relation to OFA becomes detectable. Shigaraki can identify and pursue the bearer through information stolen from another hero.

This produces a useful distinction:

- **care recognition** — seeing need in order to assist;
- **administrative recognition** — identifying a person for institutional handling;
- **predatory recognition** — identifying vulnerability/location in order to capture or destroy.

Ragdoll's Quirk can participate in more than one mode depending on bearer and purpose.

The rescue problem therefore cannot be summarized as “more recognition is always good.” Ethical recognition includes control over **who gets to know what about whom, for what purpose**.

OFA secrecy becomes ethically intelligible through that lens even when secrecy also creates coordination costs.

# 24. Villain formation: succession can reproduce injury while magnifying agency

V28 substantially advances the villain-formation model for Shigaraki and Garaki.

## 24.1 Garaki: rejected knowledge + patron recognition -> unbounded research institution

Garaki's old theory is mocked; ordinary legitimacy closes around him; AFO recognizes him and gives him a place. That recognition does not heal him into social participation. It creates an alternative institution without ethical constraints.

The pathway is therefore:

> intellectual rejection / isolation -> charismatic patron recognition -> long-duration technical loyalty -> dehumanizing infrastructure

This is distinct from grievance alone.

## 24.2 Shigaraki: inherited trauma + cultivated identity + self-authored destruction + predecessor pressure

V28 makes any monocausal Shigaraki model untenable.

His present state contains at least four causal layers:

1. Tenko's family history and traumatic memory;
2. AFO's grooming and imposed interpretive structure;
3. Shigaraki's later self-authored destructive and comradeship commitments;
4. the newly transferred AFO Quirk and its associated acquisition pressure.

The family dream proves that earlier layers remain psychologically available. `俺を否定するな` proves that AFO's presence is not reducible to a historical memory Shigaraki has already mastered.

This is not less agency. It is **agency under inherited occupation pressure**.

## 24.3 Toga: bereavement becomes renewed formation

Twice's death shows that villain formation remains longitudinal. A person can acquire new reasons for hostility after joining a villain group. Toga's “heroes are in the way” judgment is a post-belonging injury, not merely a childhood-origin echo.

# 31. V28 major model change

The most important change produced by Volume 28 is that **inheritance ceases to be mainly a question of legitimacy and becomes a question of containment architecture**.

Earlier volumes asked:

- who deserves inherited power;
- whether succession grants legitimacy;
- how a successor interprets a predecessor's will;
- whether inherited relationships can be self-authored.

V28 asks what happens when inheritance can endanger everyone around the bearer.

AFO's original Quirk inside Shigaraki produces massive asymmetric capability and predecessor-originated pressure. OFA inside Midoriya makes him a target whose location can reshape civilian danger. Neither inheritance can be treated as a private relation between two generations anymore.

That forces the series toward network ethics.

Midoriya cannot responsibly “own” the OFA problem alone because his capture would harm others. Aizawa cannot responsibly “own” Erasure alone because keeping it active requires Manual, mobility, and defense. Endeavor cannot simply beat Shigaraki because the communications network, evacuation geometry, and Erasure state determine what attacks are possible. Students cannot simply be kept outside the war because their inherited relationships and specialist competence already affect the field; yet adults still attempt to bound their exposure.

The volume's answer is not that everyone shares equal responsibility. It is that responsibility must be **differentiated and linked**.

This yields the V28 synthesis proposition:

> **The ethical opposite of catastrophic inherited power is not a stronger solitary inheritor. It is a network capable of distributing knowledge, protection, restraint, and risk without erasing the agency of the person at its center.**

That proposition also clarifies the contrast between AFO and the hero system.

AFO succession concentrates capabilities inside a successor while preserving the predecessor's pressure within him. Hero containment spreads functions across people whose distinct agency remains visible. Both are networks, but one network tends toward incorporation while the other—at its best—tends toward coordination.

This is one of the strongest institutional contrasts the reread has produced so far.
