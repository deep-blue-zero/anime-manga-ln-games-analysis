This document is the canonical Japanese-primary second-pass reading of **My Hero Academia Volume 39**, conducted prospectively from the frozen Volume 38 boundary at `V38:p203`.

The reading does **not** use Volume 40 or later outcomes to adjudicate unresolved states. Volume 39 may establish only what its own pages establish about:

# 1. Governing transition: plural authorship becomes reciprocal agency against extraction

Volume 38 ended with **plural authorship under convergence**. Once Kurogiri recoupled the fronts, the war could no longer be narrated as a set of isolated duels or as the work of one savior. Volume 39 keeps that distributed causal structure but adds a sharper ethical distinction.

The governing transition is:

> **plural authorship under convergence → reciprocal agency against extraction: once people have become causally entangled, the decisive question is whether they answer one another through freely chosen listening, restraint, gift, support and cost-bearing, or instead through ownership, erasure, assimilation and use.**

“Reciprocal” here does **not** mean mutual affection, reconciliation, forgiveness or moral symmetry. The volume repeatedly refuses those shortcuts.

- Toya is finally looked at by the whole family and still says he hates them.
- Endeavor apologizes without claiming that apology repairs the damage or obligates forgiveness.
- Uraraka refuses to erase Toga's killings while also refusing to erase Toga's smile.
- Toga remains unwilling to submit to the institutional identity offered to her and nevertheless makes a radical life-giving choice for Uraraka.
- All Might fights AFO through capacities other people gave him, while AFO's characteristic relation to power remains acquisition, possession and forced incorporation.

The strongest literalization appears in the blood arc. Toga's history is organized around wanting to **take** the blood of those she loves. V39 does not simply suppress that desire into normality. It brings her to an act of **giving her own blood** to Uraraka. The direction of relation reverses without requiring Toga to become a different person.

The same structure governs Toshinori's origin. Before he has OFA, he tells Nana that what he cannot tolerate is a world where `奪った者が 得をする` and the sorrow of those deprived becomes hatred. His project is explicitly to break an extractive grievance spiral. Present-day Quirkless All Might then confronts the most extractive person in the series using `私が与えられてきたもの 全て` — everything he has been given.

V39 therefore moves beyond the claim that many people matter. It asks what kind of relation between many people can interrupt the production of further grievance.

# 2. No.387 `煮凝り` — inherited power can remain latent inside inherited harm

## 2.1 The Himura history ties Quirk inheritance to property and status

Geten's Himura account at `V39:p007-010` places Quirk lineage inside a family history of property, status and blood-boundary preservation. The family is not merely “traditional.” Its resistance to mixing is connected to material decline and a former landlord position after social change.

This matters because MHA's reproductive politics are now broader than Endeavor's individual `個性婚` project. Quirk inheritance can become embedded in:

- class/status preservation;
- family continuity;
- fear of dilution;
- property history;
- deliberate reproductive choice.

**Bounded inference:** the manga is not giving a complete demographic theory of Quirk society here. It is showing one family in which biological inheritance, social hierarchy and historical decline reinforce one another.

## 2.2 Toya's cold factor revises the body model

Toya reaches another near-death threshold while calling for Endeavor to look at him: `おとうさん 見て 見てえ`.

The volume distinguishes ordinary life-or-death Quirk development from a capacity that emerges only at an extreme boundary: `死に瀕した時のみ顕現する力`.

Cold/ice appears from Toya's inherited body at that threshold. This requires a correction to the prior simplified model.

**REVISE:** Toya should no longer be modeled only as a fire-Quirk child whose body inherited cold tolerance incompatible with his fire. V39 establishes a latent Rei/Himura-side cold factor capable of manifestation at the edge of death.

It does **not** establish:

- stable routine dual-Quirk mastery;
- ordinary voluntary use under nonterminal conditions;
- a clean Shoto-equivalent physiology;
- that the newly manifested cold could have solved Toya's childhood problem if discovered earlier.

The evidence is narrower and more tragic: inheritance contained another unrealized capacity, but it becomes legible only when the body is already at catastrophic failure.

## 2.3 Endeavor recognizes the limit of solitary atonement

Endeavor's `全部 俺の責任だ` continues his refusal to displace causal responsibility. But V39 adds an important correction to the form that responsibility takes.

He had imagined surviving and carrying the burden himself. Toya, however, had spent years directing his demand toward a father who would not look at him. A self-punitive plan in which Endeavor alone dies with Toya risks becoming another unilateral decision centered on Endeavor's own theory of what responsibility should look like.

The arrival of Rei interrupts that closure.

This is not a redistribution that makes everyone equally culpable. Endeavor's responsibility remains asymmetrically large. The change is relational:

> responsibility cannot be completed merely by deciding how much suffering the responsible person deserves; it also requires receiving the harmed person's speech and allowing other affected people to exercise their own agency.

# 3. No.388 `燈矢` — the family becomes causally present without becoming morally reconciled

Rei, Fuyumi and Natsuo enter the thermal crisis rather than remaining narrative objects inside Endeavor and Toya's confrontation. Each adds cooling. Each also enters as a person with a different history of harm, avoidance and continuing attachment.

The most important moment is Toya's recognition:

> `みんな…見てる`

For much of Toya's development, “look at me” has been a demand aimed almost exclusively at Endeavor. V39 answers that demand in a form Toya never designed: the entire family is now physically present and looking.

That produces a major recognition payoff, but the text refuses the tempting false conclusion that recognition automatically creates forgiveness or emotional regulation.

Toya's hatred remains real. His body remains catastrophic. The family is not restored to an innocent earlier state.

The idealized family image at the end of the sequence is therefore best read as a visual measure of deprivation and counterfactual possibility, not a claim that the problem was easy or that the present family has become whole. The cruel simplicity is that being looked at was one of Toya's basic unmet needs; satisfying it now cannot undo the years during which that need metastasized inside abuse, rivalry, bodily injury and chosen violence.

# 4. No.389 `安心と祈り` — assurance is a social function, not a private feeling

The Gunga crisis remains larger than the immediate family. Evacuation systems have failed or been sabotaged; civilians and responders remain inside the causal radius. The family can contribute materially without guaranteeing success.

The chapter's crosscutting between fighters, evacuees, witnesses and prayer continues V38's claim that apparently secondary social roles are part of the war's infrastructure.

The title's pairing of **assurance** and **prayer** is useful. Neither is a substitute for physical action. But MHA repeatedly treats social confidence, visibility and emotional orientation as things that can determine whether people remain capable of acting.

That concern will become explicit again in Toshinori's childhood definition of the Symbol of Peace: rebuilding structures is not enough when people's hearts remain covered in fear.

# 5. No.390 `轟焦凍：ライジング` — Shoto's rising is relational rather than solitary

## 5.1 Iida's service ends where Shoto's distinct agency begins

Iida gets Shoto to the crisis. Shoto tells him that the guidance is complete and thanks him.

This is a clean example of V38's plural authorship principle carried into V39. Iida does not become a lesser participant because Shoto performs the decisive cooling technique. His action changes whether Shoto can act at all.

The war system identified `Engine` + `Half-Cold Half-Hot` as a complementary combination. The volume then preserves their distinction rather than collapsing them into a team attack whose ownership is ambiguous.

## 5.2 Shoto explicitly articulates a learned relationship policy

Shoto's internal language to Rei supplies unusually direct reconstruction evidence:

- `たくさん話すと 衝突することも あります。`
- `でも ぶつかり合うと より気持ちを 理解できる時も あります。`
- `俺の友だちは 自分の為だけじゃなく 他人の為にも 怒ったり 泣いたりします。`
- `俺も そうなりたいです。`

This is not merely a thematic statement that friendship helped him. It is a decision-policy statement.

Shoto now expects that:

1. talking can create conflict;
2. conflict need not mean relational failure;
3. collision can sometimes produce better understanding;
4. mature care includes feeling and acting for others, not only controlling oneself;
5. relational competence is effortful and something he still has to practice.

That is a major refinement to the specialist-ready Shoto model. His early emotional sparsity should not be reconstructed as an essential preference for isolation. V39 gives evidence that he has deliberately learned to tolerate relational friction because understanding matters more than avoiding discomfort.

## 5.3 `大氷海嘯` is a plural causal achievement

Shoto's large-scale cooling is indispensable. The text also indicates that his ice **alone** might not have been enough against Toya's continuing temperature rise. Rei, Fuyumi and Natsuo have already been cooling Toya.

Therefore:

**REJECT:** “Shoto finally solves Toya by himself.”

The better attribution is:

> Shoto supplies the uniquely decisive capacity at the end of a family-scale causal chain that includes Iida's transport and prior family cooling.

This makes `轟焦凍：ライジング` consistent with the volume's distributed heroism rather than an exception to it.

## 5.4 Being seen does not erase hatred

After the immediate thermal catastrophe is stopped, Toya can still say he hates his father and family.

That matters enormously. The volume does not use recognition as a magic therapeutic switch.

Endeavor's response is not a demand that Toya appreciate the rescue. He asks Toya to keep telling him and then gives concrete apologies to Rei, Fuyumi, Natsuo and Shoto.

This is a stronger atonement model than generalized self-condemnation. Endeavor names differentiated failures:

- what he forced onto Rei;
- what he left Fuyumi to carry;
- how he neglected Natsuo;
- what he did to Shoto.

**STRENGTHEN:** Endeavor's mature atonement is becoming receptive rather than merely punitive. He must hear hatred without converting listening into a claim on forgiveness.

# 6. No.391 `拒んだ世界` — Toga's battlefield state is love, grief, hatred and abandonment at once

The Sad Man's Parade remains a mass-casualty threat. Transform's Twice interval is finite. Toga is not operating inside a pure emotional state.

Uraraka explicitly says that her own thinking has changed during the short period she has known Toga. That self-report matters for reconstruction: she is not pretending her current position existed unchanged at first contact.

Uraraka notices that Toga is unable to reproduce Twice in the fully stable form expected from a simple love model. Tsuyu had previously proposed one explanation involving who Toga loves. Uraraka instead reads the current state as affectively mixed: love is entangled with murderous intent and accumulated anger.

That is best preserved as **Uraraka's informed interpretation**, not an omniscient mechanical law of Transform.

The key correction remains:

**DOWNGRADE:** `好き` cannot be treated as a transparent conscious switch that mechanically predicts whether Toga can use a copied person's Quirk.

Toga's response — `あなたが 私の 何を 知ってるの!?` — then turns the conflict from external diagnosis to the politics of being known.

# 7. No.392 `ヴィラン名` — normalization failure is not the same thing as causal absolution

## 7.1 Quirk counseling is shown as correction toward `普通`

Toga's childhood material is among the strongest villain-formation evidence in the reread because it shows a repeated social response to a child whose desire does not fit ordinary norms.

The counseling grammar is correction: `矯正していきましょう “普通”に`.

Her parents' response escalates from horror to dehumanization. Toga cannot understand why others do not experience love as she does and asks why everyone suppresses what feels obvious to her. She is told, in effect, that her difference means she is not properly human.

This supports a precise institutional failure model:

> the child is trained to suppress visible difference and perform normality before anyone has built a relational language for understanding, containing and ethically redirecting the underlying desire.

It does **not** support the claim that later homicide is simply society's fault. Toga's later intentional killings remain intentional and are explicitly preserved as such by Uraraka.

## 7.2 Tsuyu revises a governing belief

Tsuyu's V39 statement is one of the strongest late additions to her character model:

> `私は… “ルール”を守ることが…ヒーローだと思ってた…!! 外れる事が敵だと思ってた…!`

She then identifies Uraraka's attempt to face Toga beyond simple elimination or capture as harder than either.

This is a genuine longitudinal belief transition. Earlier Tsuyu's moral directness and rule sensitivity were stable strengths. V39 does not discard them. It differentiates them:

- rules can structure legitimate conduct;
- rule violation is not a sufficient ontology of enemy personhood;
- heroism can require confronting a person whom existing categories are insufficient to understand;
- that harder relational work does not eliminate containment or accountability.

This closes enough breadth to promote Tsuyu from `moderate (strongly strengthened)` to **`strong`**.

## 7.3 Toga refuses pity as another form of assimilation

Toga insists that her emotional structure is different and rejects being made into a pitiable person by the heroes' scale:

> `そっちの尺度で 私を 可哀想な人間にするな!!`

This is important because a weaker rescue story could merely relabel her “sick” or “sad” and preserve the rescuers' normative authority intact.

V39 lets Toga attack that move directly. If Uraraka's understanding only says “you are actually like us once properly interpreted,” then Toga has not really been recognized.

# 8. No.393 `少女のエゴ` — self-naming, accountability and the refusal of neutral rescue

## 8.1 `私は トガヒミコとして 生きるのです`

The discussion of villain aliases historicizes hero/villain naming as a social classification that can later become self-authored identity.

Toga refuses to create a separate villain self. Her declaration — `私は トガヒミコとして 生きるのです` — is a strong identity claim:

- she does not split “real Himiko” from “villain Himiko”;
- she does not accept that reform would require abandoning her own name;
- she does not treat criminal conduct as evidence that a different hidden person is the only one worth recognizing.

This parallels V38's Shigaraki/Tenko correction. Rescue becomes harder when the person to be rescued insists that the dangerous present self is still genuinely theirs.

## 8.2 Toga's institutional skepticism is morally serious even when incomplete

Toga asks what hero kindness actually amounts to if the endpoint is prison and execution, or if dangerous people are simply killed as Twice was.

The manga does not have to endorse her entire equivalence for the challenge to matter. Her question exposes a gap between **recognition rhetoric** and the institutional outcome available after recognition.

Uraraka does not claim neutral moral authority. When Toga calls the attempt `エゴ`, Uraraka accepts the charge.

That is a key V39 move:

> rescue is not presented as an objective, costless view from nowhere. Uraraka chooses a relational obligation she knows exceeds what can be cleanly justified as simple capture procedure.

## 8.3 Zero Gravity is explicitly governed by a non-harm philosophy

Toga points out that Uraraka's Quirk only makes people float and does not directly injure them. Uraraka responds that Zero Gravity is not a power for hurting people and that she does not find happiness in dropping them.

This is a power-philosophy statement, not only combat technique.

The late-war escalation of Zero Gravity therefore preserves rather than overwrites its normative direction.

At the same time, Zero Gravity itself changes. Observers note that people float without direct Uraraka touch and infer propagation through contact. Toga explicitly compares this to her own near-death awakening against Curious.

**STRENGTHEN:** Quirk awakening can alter a capacity's operational topology under death-edge stress. In Uraraka's case, effect can propagate through a contact chain rather than requiring each target to be touched by her personally.

Do not generalize this into an unrestricted rule that extreme emotion produces any desired new ability.

## 8.4 Accountability and recognition are stated in the same breath

Uraraka says:

> `故意に人を殺めた事…!! なかった事にはしてあげられない…!!`

Then she also says:

> `私は あなたの笑顔を 見なかった事には したくない!!`

This is the clearest V39 rescue formulation.

It directly echoes Midoriya's V38 `泣いていた君を 見なかった事には しない`, but Uraraka's version adds explicit criminal accountability.

The rescue doctrine is therefore not:

> suffering erases agency.

It is:

> **once another person's suffering, joy or personhood has been seen, the rescuer refuses the convenience of deleting that evidence; harmful agency remains part of the same person and must also remain visible.**

## 8.5 `血なんて 一生くれてやる` is restorative, not a policy blueprint

Uraraka's offer of lifelong blood and ordinary `恋バナ` is radical because it imagines a relation in which Toga's need is neither criminalized by definition nor simply allowed to consume others without consent.

It is a personal restorative proposition:

- Toga's acts cannot be made nonexistent;
- Uraraka will not affirm everything;
- Toga's underlying desire need not be treated as proof that she is unfit for human relation;
- a consensual future relation is imaginable.

This should not be overpromoted into a complete institutional reform program. V39 gives Uraraka's commitment, not a worked-out legal/medical social system for Toga.

# 9. No.394 `麗日お茶子VS渡我被身子` — ordinary conversation becomes evidentiary repair

Uraraka asks Toga to tell her what she has been thinking. The resulting dialogue adds multiple forms of evidence that were previously inferred only indirectly.

Toga describes attraction across species, gender and social category because everyone contains beautiful blood. She explains that being told not to smile and not to express blood desire produced shame. She could not tell the boy she liked that she wanted his blood because she expected the desire to make her appear monstrous or unlovable.

The League is then named as a positive belonging structure:

> `私が “好き”に 生きられる場所だったの!`

This does not purify the League. It establishes why membership had affirmative value for Toga rather than functioning only as tactical recruitment or shared grievance.

Uraraka answers childhood Toga with another recognition failure:

> `ずっとサインを 出してくれてたのに / 気づくのが 遅くなっちゃった`

The key word is **late**, not nonexistent. Signs were present. Adults and institutions either interpreted them only as deviance or failed to build a usable relation around them.

When Toga asks whether she is cute, Uraraka answers `世界一`.

That line matters because Uraraka does not tell Toga she can become normal. She affirms the evaluative identity Toga actually wants recognized.

# 10. No.395 `幸せの上に` — blood taking becomes blood giving

## 10.1 Toga recognizes her own motive transition

Toga distinguishes the knife once held because she wanted to become what she loved from the knife later held with anger and hatred.

This is valuable reconstruction evidence because she does not narrate her entire history as one undifferentiated essence.

Her desire for blood was always ethically dangerous when enacted without consent. But V39 distinguishes:

- attraction and identification;
- suppression/shame;
- aggression;
- accumulated hatred;
- grief after losing the League;
- chosen enemy action.

That layered account is one reason her model reaches specialist readiness here.

## 10.2 Toga accurately models Uraraka's restraint

As Zero Gravity propagates, Toga observes that Uraraka still does not drop people destructively.

This is reciprocal recognition. Toga is not merely the object of Uraraka's psychological insight; she is capable of noticing Uraraka's consistent ethical policy under stress.

The volume therefore gives the relationship bidirectional explanatory force.

## 10.3 `私の血 全部あげる`

Toga realizes that Uraraka will die from the wound unless someone intervenes. The survival-competition frame she asserted earlier fails at one specific point:

> `お茶子ちゃんが いなくなるの “だけ”は やっぱり嫌`

She then chooses:

> `私の血 / 全部あげる`

Her Transform knowledge provides a mechanism: by becoming Uraraka, she can make her own blood/body compatible enough to transfuse her.

This is the volume's most literal reversal of extraction.

Toga's formative desire was to take blood from loved persons. Her V39 terminal decision is to give her blood to a loved person.

The important point is not moral purification. She simultaneously says she will continue to live `好き` and will not simply let herself be captured. She then gives specific apologies:

- `刺してごめんね`
- `怒鳴ってごめんね`
- `本当よ`

This is accountability without assimilation.

## 10.4 Destruction cannot make injury nonexistent

Toga recalls how Dabi destroyed the hated house associated with enforced normality. She was happy because it seemed to make the hated place disappear.

But:

> `なくなっても 心には残るの`

By contrast:

> `お茶子ちゃんは なかった事に しなかった`

This is one of V39's strongest corrections to destructive salvation.

Burning the external site of injury does not remove the internalized injury. Uraraka's contact hurts because it does not deny the past, but that same recognition makes Toga's heart feel lighter.

This directly challenges the logic that salvation can be achieved by destroying everything connected to the wound — a logic that remains central to Shigaraki at the V38 boundary.

The comparison should remain structural, not predictive: V39 does **not** establish how Shigaraki will respond later.

## 10.5 Toga's final V39 status must remain prospectively precise

Toga narrates the act in explicitly self-elegiac terms and chooses to give **all** her blood. The intended fatality of that choice is overwhelmingly clear.

However, V39 does not provide a separate medical confirmation after the act.

Therefore:

- **FACT:** Toga intentionally performs a total-blood transfusion to save Uraraka while understanding the lethal implication.
- **STRONG INFERENCE:** the volume presents the act as fatal self-sacrifice.
- **OPEN:** formal post-act medical/death confirmation remains V40+.

The project should not use later knowledge to collapse that distinction.

## 10.6 The counterfactual remains Toga's hypothesis

Toga imagines that if someone had noticed earlier, and if she had met someone she wanted to **give** blood to as much as she wanted to drink it, perhaps the world would have been easier to live in.

That is a meaningful counterfactual, but it is not experimental proof.

**Do not convert it into:** “one kind friend in childhood would certainly have prevented all later violence.”

The source supports the weaker and stronger-to-evidence claim that early relational accommodation was a missing developmental possibility.

# 11. No.396 `“個性”無き戦い` — support technology is inherited agency, not a substitute ontology

## 11.1 All Might enters the fight knowing Nighteye's prophecy

All Might recognizes that this confrontation looks like the predicted site of his gruesome death. The volume immediately presents observers asking whether he has chosen a place to die.

His own behavior rejects that interpretation.

He is willing to risk death. He is **not** fighting in order to die.

His explicit statement is:

> `私は 過去一度たりとも 負ける気で 戦った事はない!!!`

Therefore:

**REJECT:** Armored All Might is a suicide scene in which Toshinori seeks death as his final heroic proof.

A better model is extreme-risk delay/contest under a genuine intention to win or at least alter the causal path.

## 11.2 The tactical objective is time

All Might cannot allow AFO to reach Midoriya. The immediate objective is to take minutes and seconds from an enemy whose Rewind body is already temporally finite.

This gives the fight a different metric from ordinary knockout victory.

If All Might:

- holds AFO's attention;
- forces expensive Quirk use;
- damages the Rewinding body;
- accelerates youth regression;
- prevents AFO from reaching Shigaraki/Midoriya for additional time,

then he changes the war even without defeating AFO outright.

## 11.3 All Might's strongest weapon is a behavioral model of AFO

All Might recognizes a pattern in AFO's combat behavior: broad attacks, weakening, then Quirk theft. That pattern assumes an opponent who has a Quirk worth stealing.

Quirkless All Might is outside one of the behavioral assumptions around which AFO has optimized combat.

More importantly, All Might knows that AFO's emotional fixation can be baited. He deliberately attacks the relationship:

- their old history;
- AFO's resentment;
- the insult of being delayed by a Quirkless opponent;
- the contradiction between “Demon Lord” self-image and petty personal obsession.

This is not merely trash talk. It is a tactical use of longitudinal character knowledge.

AFO's model should therefore preserve an important failure mode:

> high strategic intelligence can coexist with an exploitable relationship-conditioned compulsion.

## 11.4 The suit literalizes social inheritance

Armored All Might's functions repeatedly invoke techniques associated with students and successors: Blackwhip, Chargebolt, Cellophane, Sugar Man, Red, Tsukuyomi, Pinky, Uravity, Ingenium, Shoot Style and related borrowed design language.

The suit is not a generic Iron-Man answer to Quirklessness. It is a physical archive of what Toshinori has watched others become capable of doing.

This is why his governing line matters:

> `私が与えられてきたもの 全てをぶつける!!`

Quirkless agency is socially composed. All Might acts himself, but the range of actions now available to him exists because of engineers, friends, students and accumulated observation.

# 12. No.397 `ゴミ拾い` — tools break; relationship knowledge remains

## 12.1 V39 explicitly rejects infinite support-gear equivalence

All Might's equipment is repeatedly destroyed. His body takes fractures and contusions. He remembers his own warning about relying too heavily on support tools:

> `壊れて失うだけだ / 道具は 限界を超えない`

This requires a strong correction to any technology-determinist reading of the Quirkless hero callback.

**REJECT:** V39 proves that sufficient equipment makes a Quirkless person functionally equivalent to a top Quirk holder.

What V39 actually shows is narrower and more interesting:

- equipment can expand agency;
- equipment can translate observed techniques into tools;
- equipment can let a knowledgeable actor participate in an otherwise impossible fight;
- equipment is finite, fragile and replaceable only while a support network continues supplying it;
- the user still needs judgment, timing, pain tolerance and a model of the opponent.

## 12.2 AFO's face-saving is part of the failure model

AFO insists he has not been baited even while continuing to spend time on All Might.

This is useful because it distinguishes self-image from behavior. His strategic narration attempts to preserve the identity of an omnipotent planner even when his current actions are relationship-conditioned.

V38 had already shown that youth/Rewind reduced affective regulation. V39 **strengthens** that adjudication with an observable tactical consequence: All Might can convert AFO's hatred into delay.

# 13. No.398 `八木俊典：ライジングオリジン` — the Symbol of Peace begins as anti-grievance infrastructure

## 13.1 Toshinori's origin is not personal revenge

Teenage Toshinori tells Nana that his family was killed. Nana refuses to take another person's revenge project as a reason for apprenticeship.

Toshinori then clarifies what he cannot accept:

> `奪った者が 得をする 世の中が`

and

> `奪われた人の 悲しみが憎悪に 変換される… この螺旋が…`

The target is a **social spiral**:

1. people are deprived;
2. takers benefit;
3. victims retain grief;
4. grief converts into hatred;
5. hatred produces further taking and harm.

That formulation retroactively clarifies the Symbol of Peace at a level deeper than “be strong enough that villains fear committing crime.”

## 13.2 `平和の象徴` is a psychological-public role

Toshinori says he wants a world in which everyone can live smiling and that such a world requires a `平和の象徴`.

Reconstruction of buildings after disorder is insufficient because people still live afraid and their hearts remain covered in darkness.

The Symbol of Peace is therefore conceived as:

- public assurance;
- a reason to believe predation will not be rewarded indefinitely;
- an interruption to the conversion of fear/grief into further hatred;
- a social expectation that someone will act.

This strengthens, but also complicates, the corpus's long-running critique of Symbol overcentralization. Toshinori's original theory addresses a genuine psychological coordination problem. The later institutional weakness is not evidence that the original problem was imaginary.

## 13.3 `“無個性”には 役割が ないから` contains both heroism and overcentralization risk

Toshinori tells Nana that everyone is consumed by the three meters immediately around them. He will take on the missing wider role because a Quirkless person has no assigned role.

**FACT:** young Toshinori experiences Quirklessness as rolelessness and self-assigns a system-level responsibility.

**Bounded interpretation:** the same move that produces extraordinary heroism also contains the seed of the later single-point-of-failure problem. Because others are busy with local responsibilities, Toshinori tries to become the person who carries the unfilled whole-society function.

That should not be flattened into “All Might was wrong from the beginning.” The tension is more productive:

> a socially necessary assurance function can become dangerously concentrated when one person makes himself its almost exclusive carrier.

## 13.4 Present All Might fights with received power after giving OFA away

The present sequence returns to Armored All Might. Acid-based `Pinky` support forces AFO to expend more power/body to purge damage, accelerating Rewind. AFO becomes younger faster.

All Might is badly injured but continues to call for more equipment.

The visual-history collage culminates in a simple motive:

> `人の役に立てて 嬉しい`

This is crucial specialist-level evidence. Toshinori's service is not only duty, guilt or Symbol performance. Being useful to other people is intrinsically joyful to him.

He deploys `ウラビティ`, `インゲニウム` and other inherited/support functions and says `勝とうぜ!`.

The chapter therefore answers V38's Quirkless-hero callback only prospectively and operationally:

- a Quirkless Toshinori can still choose hero action;
- he can make consequential use of support infrastructure and learned relationships;
- he can remain committed to victory while accepting catastrophic bodily risk;
- the actual battle outcome is still unresolved at `V39:p179`.

# 15. Relationship-state transitions

Uraraka learns Toga's childhood/self-description without pretending intentional killings disappear. Toga in turn recognizes Uraraka's stable restraint and becomes willing to save her at lethal personal cost. Neither fully adopts the other's normative framework.

## Toya ↔ Endeavor

**Transition:**

> demanded gaze / punitive obsession → received hatred with concrete apology, without forgiveness closure.

Endeavor finally makes hearing Toya part of atonement rather than deciding alone how atonement should end.

## Todoroki family ↔ Toya

**Transition:**

> fragmented responsibility and imagined family → physically convergent family recognition under catastrophe.

The family jointly contributes to stopping the thermal event. Emotional unity is not inferred.

## Shoto ↔ Iida

Iida's `迷子を導く` function completes in transport; Shoto explicitly thanks him when the route reaches the point at which Shoto's distinct agency must take over. This is differentiated reciprocity rather than dependency.

## All Might ↔ AFO

## All Might ↔ students/support network

The relationship becomes materially embodied. Student techniques/names appear as suit functions. Toshinori's current agency is not possession of their powers; it is a technological translation of what he has learned from and received through others.

# 16. Power-philosophy updates

## Received power versus extracted power

AFO's dominant relation to Quirks is taking/owning/using. All Might's V39 relation is explicitly `与えられてきたもの` — what has been given to him.

This makes the All Might/AFO conflict a philosophical contrast between **extraction** and **reception** as much as between power levels.

## Support technology extends agency but remains finite

V39 gives both sides of the support-gear question:

- tools let Quirkless All Might enter an otherwise impossible fight;
- tools translate accumulated social knowledge into action;
- tools break and do not exceed their own limits;
- losing equipment can sharply reduce professional function.

Technology is therefore neither trivial nor ontologically equivalent to a Quirk.

## Zero Gravity awakens through propagation

Uraraka's effect can pass through contact chains without her direct touch to every target. The awakening preserves her Quirk's non-harm orientation while changing its topology.

## Toya's latent cold complicates inheritance

Quirk inheritance can contain unrealized factors that become visible only under exceptional bodily states. Genetic/inherited potential is therefore not fully legible from ordinary phenotype or earlier performance.

## Rewind becomes adversarially accelerable expenditure

All Might's acid/support attacks force AFO to spend body/Quirk resources in ways that accelerate rejuvenation. V38's “finite temporal expenditure” model is strengthened.

# 17. Hero-society updates

## Rule compliance is insufficient as a complete hero/villain ontology

Tsuyu explicitly says she once equated rule-following with hero status and departure from rules with enemy status. Her revision does not abolish rules; it rejects their sufficiency for understanding personhood.

## Quirk counseling appears as normalization infrastructure

Toga's childhood counseling aims to make her `普通`. Combined with parental dehumanization, it supplies evidence for a hero-society failure mode in which social safety is pursued through suppression of visible difference without constructing a consensual, ethically bounded accommodation for the underlying drive.

## Institutional kindness has an endpoint problem

Toga's challenge — that kind words can still end in cages, execution or killing — identifies a gap between interpersonal recognition and available institutional response. V39 does not solve that policy problem. It makes it explicit.

## The Symbol of Peace began as assurance infrastructure

Toshinori's pre-OFA theory is not merely deterrent combat dominance. He believes a society remains unhealed while people continue to live in fear even after buildings are rebuilt.

The Symbol is designed to change public expectation.

**Revision:** later overcentralization should not erase the original social problem the Symbol solved. The mature analysis must hold both causal effectiveness and systemic fragility.

# 18. Recognition / failed-rescue updates

## Toya — recognition arrives too late to function as erasure

`みんな…見てる` resolves the narrow absence of family gaze. It cannot reverse the accumulated developmental history or current hatred.

Recognition can be necessary and still radically insufficient when delayed.

## Toga — signs were visible but translated only as deviance

Uraraka's `気づくのが 遅くなっちゃった` marks delayed recognition. Toga's childhood scenes show that adults noticed behavior but interpreted it through correction/dehumanization rather than relational understanding.

This is a useful distinction:

> seeing behavior is not the same as recognizing the person who is trying, however maladaptively, to communicate through it.

## Uraraka — recognition plus accountability

`故意に人を殺めた事` cannot be made nonexistent. Toga's smile likewise cannot be unseen.

This is the strongest current MHA formulation of restorative recognition because neither side of the ledger is permitted to cancel the other.

## Toga — destruction fails where recognition partly succeeds

Dabi's destruction of the house cannot remove the internal wound. Uraraka's refusal to erase the wound does not cure it, but Toga reports feeling lighter.

This is a direct anti-erasure result.

# 19. Villain-formation updates

## Toga — formation is interaction, not monocause

V39 provides a much richer formation chain:

- atypical blood/love desire;
- family horror and dehumanization;
- counseling toward performed normality;
- shame around telling a desired person what she wants;
- eventual violent expression;
- League belonging as affirmative permission to live through `好き`;
- grief/anger after League losses;
- chosen criminal/enemy agency.

No single link is sufficient. The model should reject both “she was born evil” and “society mechanically made her kill.”

## League belonging is real social repair with dangerous moral content

The League gives Toga a place where her self-definition is not immediately corrected out of existence. That relational success coexists with a group environment that licenses lethal violence.

Villain communities can therefore satisfy genuine recognition needs while directing them into destructive practice.

## AFO — emotional extraction becomes self-defeating

AFO's attempt to own relations as utilities leaves him vulnerable to relations he cannot reduce to utilities. His All Might hatred becomes time loss; V38 Machia showed a different version when owned loyalty produced abandonment grievance.

AFO's failure mode is not lack of social intelligence. It is the assumption that understanding another person's lever is equivalent to owning the relation itself.

# 21. Visual grammar and motifs

## Looking / being seen

V39 turns “looking” into a recurring visual-verbal structure:

- Toya's `見て`;
- whole-family `みんな…見てる`;
- Toga's childhood signs and Uraraka's late noticing;
- Uraraka's refusal to unsee Toga's smile;
- All Might asking what he has seen and what has protected them.

The motif moves from **demanded gaze** to **evidentiary responsibility**: seeing something can create an obligation not to falsify it later.

## Taking blood / giving blood

Toga's tubes, knives and blood imagery are compositionally reversed by the transfusion line and body-to-body rescue. The same material substance changes ethical direction through consent and purpose.

## Family convergence without sentimental closure

The family image appears only after the real family physically converges around Toya. The continued hateful language prevents the composition from becoming a simple reconciliation tableau.

## Armored All Might as social archive

The suit reconstitutes an All-Might-like silhouette while continually breaking underneath. Named student-derived functions make the body a visual archive of people who have contributed to him.

The old Symbol image is thus reconstructed as **networked and maintained**, not as proof that Toshinori's individual body secretly remained self-sufficient.

## Youth versus origin

AFO becomes younger biologically as Rewind consumes him; All Might becomes “young” narratively through origin memory.

The contrast is bounded but productive:

- AFO's regression intensifies fixation and strips time away;
- Toshinori's return to origin recovers purpose and the relational reasons for acting.

# 22. Callback and payoff structure

## V01 Quirkless hero question → V38/V39 action test

The early claim that inherited fire is still Shoto's own power matures into a person who can use inherited capacities inside a relational policy he himself has chosen.

## Todoroki-family dream imagery → actual family convergence

Endeavor's recurring image of a family from which he is absent is answered not by his disappearance but by the family members themselves entering the crisis and making separate choices.

## Toga's blood desire → reciprocal blood gift

This is one of the clearest long-horizon inversions in the reread: the same blood-centered identity can generate taking or giving depending on relation, consent and motive.

## Early Symbol of Peace → origin theory

The series has long treated All Might as a social Symbol. V39 finally places the phrase inside teenage Toshinori's pre-OFA political/moral reasoning: he wants to interrupt fear and grievance, not merely become strongest.
