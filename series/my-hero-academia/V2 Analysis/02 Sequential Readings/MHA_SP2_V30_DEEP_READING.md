# 1. Governing transition: responsibility under irreversible triage becomes contested inheritance under public truth and the demand to save

Volume 29 asked who owns judgment when a distributed hero network begins breaking faster than formal authority can keep up. Volume 30 keeps the irreversible bodily consequences of that question, but changes the axis. The central conflict is now about **what inheritance means when multiple people claim the right to define another person's body, history, name, guilt, future, or public meaning**.

Five forms of inheritance are placed beside one another.

1. **AFO -> Shigaraki:** inheritance as bodily and psychological capture. AFO does not merely teach or entrust. He attempts to reoccupy the successor's body, calls his own coexisting consciousness stronger as Shigaraki weakens, and finally operates the body while describing every sacrifice as acceptable `僕の為に`.
2. **OFA -> Midoriya:** inheritance as voluntary co-presence. The vestiges explicitly say `僕たちはこの子の中にいる事を選んだ` and `この力はこの子についていく`. OFA does not become morally pure merely because its bearers consent, but its succession grammar is decisively different from possession.
3. **Endeavor -> Toya/Shoto:** inheritance as family engineering and damaged lineage. Endeavor's status project shaped reproduction, training, attention, rivalry, abandonment wounds and the techniques his sons now use. Toya converts inherited fire and family truth into a punitive public spectacle; Shoto uses inherited power while refusing Toya's conclusion that family history licenses further harm.
4. **Mentor/community -> Bakugo, Mirio, Eri and others:** inheritance as relationally mediated agency. Bakugo discloses the hero name he had deliberately withheld until Best Jeanist could hear it. Mirio returns because Eri's controlled Rewind restores Permeation. The agency produced here is not ownership by the helper; another person's contribution expands the actor's future capacity.
5. **Social grievance -> public narrative:** Dabi shows that true inherited injury does not guarantee truthful public framing. He reveals facts that are real and morally devastating while also curating evidence for maximum institutional collapse, including a Best Jeanist claim whose public implication is immediately destabilized by Jeanist's living return.

These strands make Volume 30 unusually important for the corpus's ethics of recognition. Recognition, truth, inheritance and rescue are all morally underdetermined. It is possible to recognize someone's wound and weaponize it; to tell the truth and manipulate; to inherit a power and resist the predecessor's will; to condemn past abuse while protecting the abuser's present attempt at repair; to see a murderer as someone in need of rescue without treating his murders as erased.

The endpoint makes this last distinction explicit. Midoriya thinks of the people Shigaraki has killed and hurt and says in effect that he cannot simply forgive him. Yet when AFO swallows Shigaraki's agency, Midoriya sees Shigaraki's face and retrospectively reads it as `救けを求めたように見えた` (`V30:p188-192`). The new question is no longer whether Shigaraki is dangerous. That is settled. The question is whether the hero's rescue perception remains operative **after** accountability has become maximally difficult.

The V30 governing transition is therefore:

**`responsibility under irreversible triage / delegated judgment and reflexive rescue -> contested inheritance under public truth, agency, accountability and the demand to save`**

Compact formulation:

> **Volume 30 separates saving from absolving: a person may remain accountable for chosen harm while still being perceptible as someone whose agency is being violated and whose rescue can become a legitimate heroic problem.**

# 2. No.286 - `僕らの中の人`: Bakugo survives, Midoriya loses emotional control, and succession becomes bodily jurisdiction

## 2.1 Bakugo's first V30 act preserves the V29 critique of solitary victory

V30 immediately resolves the narrowest V29 OPEN register: Bakugo is alive after the interception, though badly injured. His speech at `V30:p008-009` matters more than the mere survival fact. He tells Midoriya not to try to win alone: `一人で…勝とうと…してんじゃねェっ`.

The line is a direct continuation of V29's explicit diagnosis that Midoriya does not count himself among the people who need protection. Bakugo is not simply asking Midoriya to calm down because Bakugo has been hurt. He is challenging the hero model in which Midoriya converts every increase in stakes into a reason to make himself the exclusive cost-bearing surface.

This is important for the Bakugo model after `specialist_ready`. His growth is not reducible to "becoming nicer." He has become able to model Midoriya's pathological strength accurately enough to intervene against it. His aggressive register remains intact; the content of what he protects has changed.

## 2.2 Midoriya's rage shows that care can degrade judgment

Bakugo's injury triggers a violent Midoriya response (`V30:p009-014`). The volume explicitly warns that anger can move power while destabilizing the heart/control needed to use it. Midoriya's emotional intensity is morally intelligible: someone has just been grievously injured protecting him. But intelligibility does not make dysregulation strategically sound.

This complicates a common flattening of Midoriya's compassion. His care is not always a clean decision advantage. Under relational injury it can narrow attention, amplify self-harm and make him easier to manipulate. The same attachment system that enables rescue can produce tunnel vision.

The current model therefore needs both:

- Midoriya's rescue sensitivity is a major virtue;
- Midoriya's inability to integrate himself into the protected set, plus intense relational rage, can make that virtue strategically dangerous.

## 2.3 AFO's intrusion is no longer merely a pressure; it becomes an attempt to occupy

The Shigaraki/AFO conflict intensifies as the body deteriorates. AFO-associated language and action increasingly operate through Shigaraki rather than merely advising or tempting him. The inherited relation is therefore a jurisdiction fight over who gets to author bodily action.

Shigaraki does not disappear as a subject. At `V30:p021-022`, he explicitly insists `俺の意志だ 俺の夢だ…!!`. This is one of the volume's key protections against a retrospective absolution-by-possession model. AFO's control is real; Shigaraki's prior destructive will is also real.

The correct V2 model is not:

`AFO made Shigaraki do everything`.

It is:

`Shigaraki developed and chose a destructive project under catastrophic grooming/support, then inherited a power whose predecessor increasingly attempts to seize jurisdiction over the successor body.`

Agency can be both morally accountable and presently violated.

# 3. No.287 - `間違い`: OFA defines itself as chosen co-presence rather than ownership

## 3.1 The vestige confrontation makes succession philosophy source-explicit

The OFA/AFO interior confrontation (`V30:p027-039`) is not merely lore delivery. It supplies an explicit comparison between the series' two inheritance systems.

AFO treats people and Quirks as things that can be accumulated, possessed, recombined and taken. The first OFA holder answers through relationship and choice. At `V30:p034` the vestige position is explicit:

- `僕たちはそっちへは行かない`
- `僕たちはこの子の中にいる事を選んだ`

At `V30:p038` the conclusion becomes:

- `この力はこの子についていく`

The significance is not that the previous holders control Midoriya for his own good. The text frames their presence as **choosing to remain with him**. That makes OFA a relational inheritance whose prior persons retain presence without claiming sole jurisdiction over the current bearer.

## 3.2 AFO's mistake is partly a theory-of-persons mistake

AFO's failure to seize OFA cannot be reduced to insufficient force. The encounter suggests a deeper misapprehension: he understands attachment primarily through possession, utility and acquisition, while OFA's resistance depends on people whose wills remain relationally aligned with the bearer.

This does not make OFA simple consent utopia. Midoriya still carries enormous inherited burden, secret history, bodily risk, public danger and vestige influence. But the operative moral grammar differs:

- AFO: **your power/body can become mine**;
- OFA: **we choose to remain with you**.

This distinction should be preserved in the power-philosophy ledger as one of the clearest series-level contrasts yet.

## 3.3 Shigaraki's resistance and AFO's resistance coexist

Shigaraki's explicit `俺の意志` assertion and the OFA vestiges' choice language are structurally parallel in one respect: both defend successor agency against predecessor capture. Yet Shigaraki's chosen project remains mass destruction, while Midoriya's chosen project is rescue. Agency itself does not determine moral legitimacy.

Volume 30 is therefore careful not to equate "self-authored" with "good." It values agency as a condition of personhood while separately judging what the person chooses to do with it.

# 4. No.288-No.289 - Toga and Uraraka: recognition without impunity

## 4.1 Toga exploits the rescue reflex because she understands it

Toga's decision to seek Uraraka (`V30:p044-060`) grows directly out of Twice's death and her V28-V29 grief. She uses disguise as an elderly woman in apparent danger to pull Uraraka away from mass evacuation work. This is tactically important because it shows that Toga understands the hero's rescue grammar well enough to weaponize it.

The act also creates an ethical asymmetry the later conversation cannot erase. Toga genuinely wants an answer about whether heroes regard people like her as disposable. But she creates the private conversation by diverting a rescuer during a mass-casualty disaster and by deception that exploits the rescuer's obligation.

Desire for recognition and harmful means coexist.

## 4.2 The question is personal: what do heroes intend to do with someone like Toga?

Once the disguise falls (`V30:p055-060`), Toga's question is not an abstract policy seminar. Twice's death has made it urgent. She wants to know what Uraraka, specifically, thinks about a person whose love, blood desire and violence do not fit the hero world's acceptable categories.

Toga explains the phenomenology of her love: she wants the blood of those she loves and wants to become them. She recalls the happiness associated with becoming Uraraka. This is valuable character-model evidence because it distinguishes her internal positive affect from the external harm generated by her behavioral expression.

A good reconstruction therefore cannot reduce Toga to sadism, nor can it convert felt love into moral innocence.

## 4.3 Uraraka gives an accountability answer, not an annihilation answer

Uraraka's response is firm. At `V30:p074`, the source states:

- `やっちゃった事は消えない!!`
- `その責任は受け入れなきゃいけない`

What one has done does not disappear; responsibility must be accepted.

That wording is crucial because it prevents two opposite misreadings.

It is not:

`Your feelings are fake and you are only a monster.`

It is also not:

`Your feelings explain the harm, therefore the harm should be excused.`

Uraraka's position is **recognition plus accountability**. When Toga obstructs rescue or harms people, Uraraka will stop/arrest her. But Uraraka has listened to what Toga says about love and loss.

## 4.4 Toga's quiet `ありがとう` is a state change, not reconciliation

Toga's response around `V30:p075` is strikingly soft: `うん そうだね`, `ありがとう`, `ばいばい`. Uraraka remains visibly unsettled after Toga leaves.

This should not be overclaimed as friendship, agreement or redemption. The safer reading is that Toga received a clear answer from someone she had invested with unusual personal significance. The answer did not grant impunity, but neither did it erase her subjectivity.

The encounter therefore advances both character models:

- Toga can seek moral/relational information from an adversary rather than only prey on her.
- Uraraka can maintain enforcement boundaries while being emotionally affected by the person she must oppose.

Later consequences remain V31+.

# 5. No.290-No.291 - `ダビダンス`: Toya turns true family injury into punitive public dramaturgy

## 5.1 The identity register resolves: Dabi is Todoroki Toya

At `V30:p090-093`, Dabi publicly identifies himself as **Todoroki Toya / 轟燈矢**, Endeavor's son. This resolves the identity register that V27 deliberately withheld and V28-V29 correctly kept OPEN.

The disclosure is not private reunion. It is a prepared mass-media event. Dabi has recorded testimony, synchronized it with the battlefield confrontation, and selected a moment when Endeavor is the No.1 hero under national scrutiny. His truth is staged for maximum institutional and personal damage.

That staging does not make the underlying family history false.

## 5.2 Dabi's testimony confirms the family project already established from other sources

The broadcast recounts Endeavor's reproduction/training project around surpassing All Might and situates Toya inside it (`V30:p092-104`). Earlier volumes already independently established that Endeavor treated Shoto as a deliberately produced path to surpassing All Might and that the family suffered severe harm. V30 therefore does not introduce these facts solely through an untrusted narrator; it adds Toya's location inside the already-grounded structure.

The childhood material gives Toya a highly diagnostic desire: **to be seen by his father**. His expanding fire becomes entangled with recognition hunger. The Sekoto Peak disaster and apparent death are not merely villain-origin spectacle; they are the catastrophic endpoint of a child continuing to organize worth around paternal attention.

The source preserves Endeavor's grief and belief that Toya died. It also preserves that grief inside a causal system Endeavor helped create. Remorse does not retroactively remove causation.

## 5.3 `過去は消えない`: the past becomes both ethical truth and weapon

Dabi's `過去は消えない` at `V30:p097` is one of the volume's key formulations. The past cannot be deleted by present hero ranking, public service or later remorse.

But Dabi uses that valid proposition to support an explicitly punitive goal. At `V30:p098`, his rhetoric culminates in a hell/dance image directed at Enji. At `V30:p110-113`, he says he could not forgive, thanks Endeavor for remaining alive until this moment, and reveals that the timing itself was part of the punishment.

Dabi is therefore not simply a whistleblower whose only aim is truth. He is a harmed person using truth as an instrument of revenge and institutional destabilization.

## 5.4 Best Jeanist demonstrates why truthful grievance and truthful presentation are separate questions

Dabi's broadcast also incorporates Hawks and Best Jeanist. It uses Hawks's hidden history and Twice's killing to construct a broader indictment of heroes. Some of this material is source-grounded and morally serious. But the Best Jeanist component is immediately destabilized when Jeanist appears alive (`V30:p108-118`).

This gives the corpus a powerful epistemic rule:

> **A speaker can be truthful about the central wound while strategically arranging adjacent evidence to create a false total picture.**

Dabi's family history should therefore not be dismissed because part of his broadcast is manipulative; nor should every broadcast implication be elevated to neutral narrator authority because the family history is true.

## 5.5 Endeavor freezes again where family history enters professional action

The Toya reveal produces another family-conditioned operational freeze (`V30:p110-115`). Endeavor's elite automaticity cannot immediately absorb the fact that the son he believed dead is alive, has become Dabi, and has constructed his life around destroying Endeavor's public and familial legacy.

Shoto again has to bridge the gap between family catastrophe and current physical danger. His urgent `親父!!! 来るぞ!! 後にしてくれ!!!` (`V30:p113`) is not emotional indifference. It is emergency compartmentalization by the child who has repeatedly had to function while the father's past invades the present.

# 6. Shoto and Midoriya: refusing both family erasure and Dabi's ownership of family meaning

## 6.1 Shoto becomes the active mediator of a father who cannot act

Dabi's attack on Shoto is explicitly relationally instrumental. He repeatedly frames Shoto's injury/death as something that will make Endeavor suffer. Shoto is not treated primarily as a sibling to be understood; he is an affective weapon aimed at their father.

That makes Shoto's resistance especially important. He is not fighting to prove Endeavor innocent. He is preventing Toya from using the family's already-real injury to authorize new injury.

## 6.2 Midoriya states the volume's strongest repair-without-erasure formulation

At `V30:p150-151`, Midoriya intervenes despite catastrophic bodily damage, using `フロッピースタイル` Blackwhip through his mouth/tongue. His speech is analytically central:

- Shoto is an important friend.
- Endeavor is the mentor who made Midoriya stronger.
- `過去は消えない`.
- Therefore Midoriya is watching the Endeavor who is trying now.
- Dabi is **not** Endeavor.

This is not a defense of past abuse. The sentence explicitly preserves the past. It is also not a claim that repair guarantees forgiveness. V26 already separated atonement from entitlement to forgiveness.

What Midoriya rejects is Dabi's attempt to monopolize the meaning of Endeavor's history. A harmed son can truthfully identify the harm without becoming identical to the father or gaining unlimited authority over what everybody else must do with the father's later change.

## 6.3 Dabi's self-definition makes the conflict explicit

Dabi answers that he is `正義の味方が犯した罪`—the sin committed by a champion of justice (`V30:p151`). He says the point is not that evil flourishes but that justice collapses.

This is a much richer Dabi model than the earlier `hero hypocrisy` shorthand. His project is symbolic and genealogical: he wants the hero institution's apex representative to become evidence against the institution by making the hidden family cost publicly inseparable from the public hero image.

But Dabi also chooses continued harm as the vehicle. His critique therefore remains morally discriminating in object while indiscriminate in acceptable victimization.

# 7. Best Jeanist and Bakugo: the withheld hero name becomes relational self-authorship

## 7.1 Best Jeanist returns as both combatant and epistemic event

Best Jeanist's return is strategically important because his Fiber Master restrains Gigantomachia/League members and buys time (`V30:p108-129`). It is also a public credibility event. His living presence visibly contradicts the implication that Hawks murdered and disposed of him as Dabi's presentation suggests.

His return therefore works simultaneously as:

- physical restraint;
- morale reinforcement;
- evidence correction;
- restoration of a mentor relationship whose status had been intentionally kept uncertain.

## 7.2 Bakugo waited for the mentor whose judgment mattered

At `V30:p144-146`, Bakugo finally announces his hero name:

`大・爆・殺・神ダイナマイト`

The comic payoff depends on the long delay. Bakugo had withheld the name because Best Jeanist was the person to whom he wanted to report it first. The absurd/aggressive surface remains completely Bakugo. The relational act underneath is mature: he honors the mentor relationship by waiting for the mentor's presence rather than treating the name as a publicity artifact to deploy whenever convenient.

This is ideal character-model evidence because development preserves idiolect. Bakugo does not become generic, soft or elegant to demonstrate gratitude. He performs gratitude **as Bakugo**.

## 7.3 Jeanist's V30 field leadership supports a moderate independent model

V30 also broadens Jeanist beyond Bakugo's mentor and Kamino survivor. He re-enters duty while still physically constrained, coordinates large-scale restraint, communicates with injured fighters, and explicitly frames the coming future as likely chaotic rather than cleanly heroic (`V30:p152-153`).

The responsible readiness consequence is a promotion from `emerging` to **`moderate`**, not `strong`: professional/mentor evidence is now substantial, but ordinary/private and broader relational regimes remain comparatively narrow.

# 8. Yaoyorozu's V29 judgment receives V30 causal confirmation

## 8.1 The sedative works, but not on the timeline the students could guarantee

The Gigantomachia plan finally receives causal adjudication in V30. Around `V30:p125-129`, his body begins losing function and the earlier oral sedative is identified as taking effect. Best Jeanist's fibers and the accumulated combat pressure become more effective as the drug acts.

This resolves an important V29 uncertainty. Yaoyorozu's plan was not a symbolic failure. It was technically correct and materially consequential even though it did not stop the catastrophe immediately.

This strengthens the V29 distinction:

**good judgment != guaranteed immediate outcome**.

## 8.2 Professional validation is now joined by causal validation

Majestic had already treated Yaoyorozu's command decision as worthy even before the final effect was known. V30 adds the second layer: the mechanism actually mattered.

The series therefore avoids two bad leadership models:

- outcome-only judgment, in which a good plan is bad if it does not work instantly;
- intention-only judgment, in which courage makes the technical result irrelevant.

Yaoyorozu's V29 promotion to `strong` is further strengthened rather than re-adjudicated.

# 9. Eri and Mirio: restoration as agency-expanding inheritance

## 9.1 Mirio's return resolves a long-held Quirk-loss register

Mirio returns to the battlefield in No.292-293 and uses Permeation (`V30:p132-145`). The source then explicitly explains that Eri's Rewind restored his Quirk after a period of training/control (`V30:p138-140`).

This resolves two separate OPEN questions:

- Mirio can use Permeation again.
- Eri has achieved at least one **specific controlled restorative application** of Rewind.

The second must be bounded. V30 does not establish unlimited, frequent or risk-free mastery. It establishes enough control to reverse Mirio's Quirk loss in a supervised relational context.

## 9.2 Eri's power restores another person's future without owning it

This is one of V30's cleanest contrasts with AFO inheritance. Eri's intervention changes Mirio's body dramatically, but the effect is to return Mirio's own professional agency. She does not become the author of what Mirio must do afterward.

The causal chain is:

`Eri develops control -> Eri chooses/participates in restoration -> Mirio regains Permeation -> Mirio re-enters action as himself`.

That is an agency-expanding use of power over another body rather than a possessive one.

## 9.3 Mirio's return remains Mirio-shaped

Mirio's battlefield conduct immediately returns to role recognition, movement, rescue and the need to keep others functioning. Restoration is not represented as a new personality or miraculous reset of grief. It returns a technical capability to an already-established heroic identity.

His readiness remains `strong`; V30 deepens embodiment, gratitude, Eri relationship and post-loss return rather than filling the still-limited long-horizon adult/ordinary breadth required for `specialist_ready`.

# 10. No.294 - Mr. Compress: performance, lineage and coalition attachment converge

## 10.1 The mask/persona finally opens into causal biography

Mr. Compress has long been readable as a theatrical operational specialist. V30 gives that performance a lineage. At `V30:p168`, he identifies Harima Oji / 張間歐児, a notorious thief/criminal associated with exposing injustice during the formation of modern institutions, and describes himself as carrying a bloodline of justice directed against corruption.

This is not enough to validate the ancestor's self-description as objective justice. It is enough to establish Compress's self-model: crime/performance is not merely aesthetic amusement to him. It is connected to a family myth of rectifying an unjust social order.

## 10.2 Coalition belonging is more important than completing his own performance

The most diagnostic Compress material is not the genealogy but the escape decision. He recognizes that the League's plans and each member's ambitions remain unfinished (`V30:p162`). He uses his own body and Quirk to create space, compress/release allies, and accepts catastrophic bodily injury to give Shigaraki and Spinner time.

At `V30:p178`, his delegation is explicit: he asks Spinner to carry Shigaraki because Spinner is the one who admired/cared for Shigaraki most, and calls himself a collaborator while treating the others as the protagonists.

This is a striking inversion of the stage persona. The man who frames action theatrically does not insist on being the protagonist of the final act. His last-stage performance exists to preserve other people's next act.

## 10.3 Readiness consequence

V30 provides enough combined evidence—performance register, operational planning, ideology/family myth, League attachment, self-sacrifice and role relinquishment—to justify **`moderate`** as the first responsible broad reconstruction tier for Mr. Compress / Sako Atsuhiro.

It does not justify `strong`: ordinary/private-life breadth, non-League relationships and repeated low-stakes regimes remain sparse.

# 11. Spinner: follower identity becomes custodial responsibility

Spinner's V23-V25 model established that he knows himself partly as a follower and that Stain/Shigaraki give a previously aimless person a direction. V30 makes that follower identity operationally responsible rather than merely dependent.

Compress entrusts Shigaraki to Spinner because Spinner's attachment is the most directly Shigaraki-centered (`V30:p178`). Spinner then searches for something that can reach Shigaraki's own consciousness. Around `V30:p179-180`, he recalls Shigaraki's relationship to the severed hands/family residue and places a surviving hand against him.

The action matters because Spinner is responding not to AFO's strategic utility but to **Shigaraki-specific memory**. He is trying to reach the person through a personally meaningful object.

This does not mean the act permanently defeats AFO's control; it does not. But it establishes a relationship-conditioned care policy:

`when Shigaraki's agency appears absent -> use remembered Shigaraki-specific anchors -> attempt to restore/activate Shigaraki rather than accept the predecessor as equivalent`.

Spinner remains `moderate`, substantially strengthened. Independent leadership and ordinary breadth remain insufficient for `strong`.

# 12. No.295 - `しつこい`: Danger Sense and AFO control move the rescue problem inside the enemy

## 12.1 `危機感知` resolves the fourth-user Quirk register only partially

At `V30:p175`, Midoriya experiences a head-stabbing danger sensation and identifies it from All Might's records as the **fourth user's Quirk, `危機感知` / Danger Sense**.

The manifestation is uncontrolled. Midoriya explicitly compares it to the earlier Blackwhip overflow: it is not yet included in practiced training, and the pain/load contributes to his loss of consciousness.

The V30-authorized statement is therefore:

- fourth user's Quirk = Danger Sense;
- Midoriya manifests it;
- first manifestation is not mastered and carries severe sensory/cognitive load.

The fourth user's full identity/biography and later controlled use remain V31+ unless already independently established by earlier source, which they are not at this boundary.

## 12.2 AFO gains control as Shigaraki weakens

At `V30:p183`, the AFO-associated consciousness explicitly says `共生する僕の意識も強くなる` as the shared/coexisting predecessor consciousness strengthens. He instructs Shigaraki/Tomura not to let hatred die: `憎しみを絶やすな 弔`.

This is not merely mentorship. By this point the predecessor is operating Shigaraki's body enough to direct Nomu through Radio Waves, organize escape, and speak to the heroes through the successor's physical form (`V30:p183-187`).

At `V30:p185`, the possession grammar becomes morally naked: the costs/sacrifices can be offered because `全ては僕の為に`.

AFO's theory of relationship is thus sharply different from Compress's immediately preceding self-sacrifice. Compress spends himself so that collaborators can continue their own projects. AFO spends bodies because all projects are finally subordinated to himself.

## 12.3 Shigaraki loses this round without being narratively erased

AFO describes Tomura as having lost to OFA/Endeavor and treats the damaged body as something to finish/complete later. The escape therefore closes the immediate battle without closing the successor conflict.

Importantly, Spinner's resistance to AFO and Midoriya's later perception both keep Shigaraki legible as a distinct person inside the capture process. The text does not allow AFO's use of the body to retroactively turn Shigaraki into an empty shell.

# 13. Midoriya's endpoint perception: `救けを求めたように見えた`

## 13.1 The volume refuses the easy binary between enemy and rescue subject

The last pages are some of the most consequential material in the reread to date. Midoriya reviews the moral facts:

- Shigaraki has killed many people.
- Shigaraki has injured many people.
- Midoriya cannot simply treat that as forgivable.

This matters because the subsequent rescue perception is not built on forgetting accountability.

As AFO pulls Shigaraki away, Midoriya looks at the face/body being used and later formulates that Shigaraki **looked as if he was asking to be saved** (`V30:p188-192`).

The visual endpoint is a ruined city overlaid by:

- `救けを求めたように`
- `見えた`

The word is perceptual, not omniscient. Midoriya does not possess a narrator guarantee that Shigaraki consciously asked him for help. The evidence is that **Midoriya saw him that way**.

## 13.2 This is the V01 rescue reflex returning at maximum moral difficulty

The callback is not `Bakugo = Shigaraki`. It is:

`visible distress / threatened agency -> Midoriya perceives a rescue request before explicit invitation`.

The moral difficulty has changed radically. Bakugo in V01 was an endangered peer with a history of cruelty toward Midoriya but not a mass murderer. Shigaraki in V30 is responsible for catastrophic violence. The rescue perception therefore tests whether Midoriya's principle was ever merely tribal.

## 13.3 Saving is not forgiving

The endpoint gives the corpus a necessary distinction:

**rescue eligibility != moral innocence**.

Midoriya can perceive Shigaraki as someone being violated by AFO while still condemning Shigaraki's murders. A future rescue attempt, if one occurs, need not imply release from accountability, endorsement of ideology, or denial of victims.

This is exactly why the wording `見えた` matters. The next phase begins as a heroic **question**, not a completed moral verdict.

# 16. Hero society: public truth can collapse legitimacy faster than institutions can adjudicate it

Dabi's broadcast makes information itself part of the war. Physical hero performance and public trust are now coupled in real time.

> **A legitimacy system dependent on heroic public images becomes exceptionally vulnerable when genuine suppressed harm and strategically misleading framing arrive in the same package.**

# 26. No.292-No.293 as the volume's hinge: small hopes become a network rather than a miracle

The title `一縷の希望たち` is analytically precise. The battlefield does not receive one miraculous reversal. It receives several narrow advantages that only matter because they accumulate.

Best Jeanist's fibers constrain Gigantomachia. The student's sedative begins to affect the giant. Mirio returns with restored Permeation. Iida, Nejire, Shoto, Bakugo, Midoriya and the professional heroes continue contributing while carrying serious injuries. Each intervention is individually insufficient; together they prevent total collapse.

That is a direct continuation of V28's distributed-containment thesis, but V30 adds a new feature: **the component hopes arrive from different temporal layers of the story**.

- Yaoyorozu's contribution is the delayed result of a judgment made on another battlefield.
- Mirio's return is the delayed result of Eri's recovery and training after the Shie Hassaikai rescue.
- Best Jeanist's presence is the delayed payoff of his hidden status and Bakugo mentorship.
- Shoto's action carries years of family conflict into present rescue.
- Bakugo's name and rescue behavior carry earlier identity/mentorship development into war.

The network is therefore not only spatially distributed. It is **temporally accumulated**. Earlier care, teaching, repair, failure and training become latent capacity that can re-enter a crisis much later.

This helps explain why the series' repeated attention to apparently non-combat recovery is not analytically peripheral. Eri learning control, Bakugo's internship relationship, Yaoyorozu's earlier failures, Shoto's family negotiations and Mirio living heroically without his Quirk all eventually affect what is possible in a battlefield emergency.

## 26.1 Best Jeanist's `私たちは紡ぐ` is a systems claim

`私たちは紡ぐ!!` at `V30:p119` works literally through Fiber Master and metaphorically through the volume's structure. Jeanist is not declaring that one hero will restore order. `私たち` matters. The heroes, students and prior relationships weave a temporary line strong enough to prevent immediate collapse.

The image also preserves fragility. Fibers can snap. Sedative can arrive late. Mirio can be overwhelmed. Endeavor can freeze. Aizawa can be disabled. The value of the network lies precisely in not requiring any one component to be infallible.

## 26.2 `ヒーロー飽和社会` becomes ironic under scarcity

Chapter 293 is titled `ヒーロー飽和社会`—a society saturated with heroes—while the actual battlefield depicts acute scarcity of usable bodies, time, information and attention. Nearly every available actor is injured, overloaded or forced into multiple roles.

That creates a useful institutional inversion. A society can be numerically saturated with licensed heroic labor and still be **functionally under-resourced when catastrophe arrives at system scale**. Hero abundance at normal times does not imply resilient capacity under correlated failure.

The lesson is not that the profession is pointless. V30 repeatedly shows professionals saving lives. The lesson is that an ecosystem built around plentiful specialized actors can still require redundancy, succession, civilian trust and distributed support when many specialists fail simultaneously.

# 28. Source-boundary cautions for later synthesis

V30 is unusually easy to overread because several famous later-series trajectories become visible here. The following cautions should travel with every later retrieval of this volume.

1. **`救けを求めたように見えた` is Midoriya's perception, not a narrator declaration of Shigaraki's conscious request.** It licenses the rescue question, not a completed answer.
2. **AFO has substantial bodily control by the endpoint, but V30 does not settle the final ontology of AFO/Shigaraki identity.** Later possession, resistance, fusion or separation claims remain future evidence.
3. **Dabi's identity and family history are canonical; his broadcast remains a strategic rhetorical object.** Use independently corroborated claims as facts and treat uncorroborated broadcast implications as testimony until verified.
4. **Eri has controlled Rewind evidence, but one successful restoration does not prove unrestricted mastery.** Preserve scale/frequency uncertainty.
5. **Danger Sense has manifested, not been mastered.** Later combat fluency must not be backfilled.
6. **Gigantomachia's sedative has worked physiologically and contributed to collapse, but complete custody/aftermath is not established by `p192`.**
7. **Compress's self-sacrifice and escape facilitation are canonical; later survival/custody is not.**
8. **Best Jeanist's living return falsifies a key public implication of Dabi's package but does not falsify Endeavor's abuse history.** Do not use one correction to erase the other truth.
9. **Shoto's opposition to Toya does not equal defense of Endeavor's past.** His model consistently differentiates present emergency, professional use, family harm and forgiveness.
10. **Midoriya's defense of Endeavor is not victim-proxy forgiveness.** He speaks from his own mentor relationship and current observation, not on behalf of Toya, Shoto, Natsuo, Fuyumi or Rei.
