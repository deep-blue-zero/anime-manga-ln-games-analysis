# 1. Governing transition: from inheritance as jurisdiction to contested personhood

Volume 23 asked who has jurisdiction over what has been inherited. A successor could receive power, doctrine, credentials, retainers, wounds, or institutional machinery without automatically possessing the right or capacity to govern it.

Volume 24 shifts the problem one level inward:

> **When Quirk, family, trauma, ideology, social norms, political movements, and relationships all claim to explain a person, who gets to define what that person is?**

The governing transition is:

> **inheritance as jurisdiction -> contested personhood / self-definition under coercive normalization and ideological capture**

Compact formulation:

> **Volume 24 treats identity as an active jurisdictional struggle. Curious wants Toga's biography to become proof of MLA doctrine; Toga insists that her own violent way of loving is nevertheless what “normal” means to her. Twice's deepest terror is that he cannot know whether he is himself, and his breakthrough comes through bodily evidence plus relational belonging rather than abstract reassurance. Spinner accepts that he is an ordinary follower but rejects the shame attached to dependence by exposing the MLA's own follower structure. Re-Destro argues that Quirk and personality are directly linked and that power should determine status; Shigaraki's returning memories complicate that determinism by showing family history, AFO's deliberate preservation of resentment, present training, and his own destructive choices interacting. Child Tenko ends the volume inside a household whose loving adults repeatedly yield to a father's anti-hero rule, making “normal family life” itself a contested system of recognition and denial.**

This is not a simple “be yourself” volume.

Toga's self-definition does not make her killing harmless. Twice's solidarity makes him more capable of mass criminal violence. Spinner's rejection of shame does not make his chosen movement ethically benign. Shigaraki's recovered self does not become morally exculpatory merely because it is psychologically intelligible. The MLA's rhetoric of liberation becomes especially suspect because it claims to release people from suppression while simultaneously ranking social worth by Quirk strength.

The volume therefore separates four questions that are often collapsed:

1. **What desires, capacities, and vulnerabilities are actually present in a person?**
2. **How have family and society responded to those traits?**
3. **Who is trying to narrate those traits for political or relational purposes?**
4. **What does the person themselves choose to do with them?**

V24's most important causal discipline is to keep all four visible at once.

# 2. No.225 — Curious and Toga: recognition becomes narrative possession

## 2.1 Curious does not merely interrogate Toga; she claims editorial rights over her life

The volume opens exactly where V23 stopped. Curious's interest in Toga is simultaneously tactical, journalistic, ideological, and personal. She has accumulated school and family information, knows the public story of Toga's disappearance, and repeatedly asks what kind of life produced a girl capable of stabbing someone she liked and drinking blood.

The epistemic danger is that much of this material arrives through **Curious's explanatory voice**.

By `V24:p019`, she states:

> `私は知っています あなたの苦しみ!!`
>
> `何故あなたは狂気に至ったか!!!`
>
> `あなたの心で世論を揺るがす! 全ては解放軍の為!!`

This is not neutral recognition. Curious announces that she already knows what Toga's suffering means and explicitly intends to make Toga's heart useful to public opinion and MLA politics.

The scene therefore creates a distinction that will govern the rest of the volume:

> **To be accurately observed is not the same thing as being granted authorship over one's own meaning.**

Curious may possess real facts. She may even identify genuine mechanisms of repression. But her purpose is appropriation: a biography becomes political evidence before the subject has consented to the interpretation.

## 2.2 `普通の暮らし` is the first battlefield

Curious asks why Toga abandoned `普通の暮らし`—ordinary life. Toga's reply at `V24:p020-021` destabilizes the premise:

> `"普通の暮らし"ってなんですか?`

and then:

> `私も普通に生きるのです`

The line cannot be treated as an innocent appeal to tolerance. Toga's conduct includes assault and killing. But the wording reveals that “ordinary” is not functioning as a neutral statistical description. It is a normative demand about which desires may be embodied, how one may smile, what forms of attraction are intelligible, and what Quirk-conditioned impulses can appear in public without being pathologized.

V24 will repeatedly show characters fighting over words that pretend to describe reality while actually assigning status:

- `普通`;
- `異常者`;
- `解放`;
- `個性`;
- `異能`;
- `人間`;
- `小市民`;
- `ヒーロー`.

The politics of naming is therefore not decoration. It is part of how the volume distributes personhood.

# 3. No.226 — Toga's childhood: desire, social correction, and the mask

## 3.1 The source confirms coercive normalization, but not monocausal innocence

Chapter 226 supplies direct retrospective material that V23 did not authorize.

A young Toga is shown treating a dead bird as `カアイイ` while her parents react with fear and eventually describe her as `悪魔の子` (`V24:p024`). The chapter then places her development alongside `個性カウンセリング`, which is presented as an attempt to coordinate self-understanding with social expectations but also as an imperfect program capable of pressing individuality into acceptable form (`V24:p026`).

Curious states that Toga's blood-drinking Transform Quirk naturally gave her strong attraction toward blood. The narrative then visualizes the conjunction of two things:

> `血と憧れ`

At `V24:p027`, the text frames blood and ordinary human admiration as having become fused in a way society could not accept. The next causal step is explicit:

> `だからあなたはフタをした`
>
> `自身を抑圧し仮面を作った`

The parental speech is equally concrete:

> `やめなさい! 何をしてるの!`
>
> `その笑い方をやめなさい!`
>
> `不気味な顔だ`
>
> `まるで異常者だ!`

This is strong evidence that Toga experienced coercive normalization rather than successful integration of a difficult Quirk/desire structure.

But the causal conclusion must remain narrower than “society made Toga kill.”

V24 supports:

`Quirk-conditioned attraction/desire + rejection/pathologization + suppression/masking + later violent self-assertion`.

It does **not** erase Toga's agency in stabbing, blood-taking, killing, or choosing the League. The ethical point is not that authentic desire is automatically innocent. It is that repression without understanding is not an adequate social technology for making dangerous desire livable.

## 3.2 Toga's smile is a contested diagnostic object

Curious calls Toga's bloodied smile her `素の顔` and treats it as proof that Toga embodies the darkness of superhuman society (`V24:p025`). Toga's parents had earlier treated the same affective presentation as evidence of abnormality.

Both readings look different but share a structure:

`visible face -> external diagnosis -> totalizing story about person`.

Toga resists not by demonstrating that the diagnosis is harmless, but by refusing the premise that outsiders own the meaning of the face.

This is why the visual language of masks matters. V24 does not say Toga's mask was simply fake and the “real” Toga underneath is good. It shows a person forced to perform acceptable affect until the rejected desires return in a far more dangerous form.

## 3.3 The Quirk expansion is empirically narrower than later summaries often make it

Toga uses stored Uraraka blood, transforms into Uraraka, and then—unexpectedly—uses Zero Gravity (`V24:p032-036`). Curious explicitly notes that Toga's registered Quirk understanding had been limited to outward transformation and asks whether the Quirk has “stretched” under fear of death (`V24:p035`).

Toga answers through observation rather than mechanism:

> `見てたよ お茶子ちゃんの"個性"の使い方`

and then:

> `私は恋して生きて 普通に死ぬの`

At `V24:p036`:

> `私はもっと"好き"になる`

The admissible V24 claim is therefore:

- while transformed as Uraraka, Toga can use **Uraraka's Zero Gravity**;
- she had observed how Uraraka used it;
- the change appears in an acute near-death/affective context;
- Toga herself thematically connects the moment to `好き` and her way of living.

V24 does **not yet** prove a complete universal rule for copying every transformed target's Quirk, a precise affection threshold, or the full later mechanics. Those remain later-source questions.

## 3.4 Toga defeats Curious by reproducing the hero's power without reproducing the hero's ethics

The scene is structurally sharp. Toga uses Uraraka's appearance and rescue-associated Quirk to send multiple opponents into the air and drop them. Familiar heroic affordance is detached from heroic purpose.

That is one of the cleanest demonstrations so far that:

> **Quirk affordance is not moral identity.**

The same capability can exist inside radically different decision systems. This strengthens the Shinso line of analysis but in a darker direction: apparently coercive or villain-coded Quirks can serve rescue; apparently rescue-coded Quirks can serve lethal violence.

# 4. No.227 — the movement consumes its own dead while Shigaraki's memory begins returning

## 4.1 Curious becomes a martyr almost immediately

Curious's death does not interrupt MLA political machinery. By `V24:p045-046`, the movement publicly announces that she has been killed and converts the death into mobilization.

The message is not merely grief. It insists that her sacrifice not be wasted and that she gave herself to liberation.

The transformation is:

`person -> casualty -> ideological symbol -> increased mass commitment`.

This is important because Curious had just attempted the same conversion on Toga. She wanted Toga's suffering to become movement content. Her own death is instantly subjected to the same organizational logic.

The MLA therefore treats biography as a political resource at both ends of life.

## 4.2 Shigaraki's `眠い` state becomes the entry point for memory recovery

Shigaraki is physically depleted from prolonged combat with Gigantomachia before Deika even began. V24 continues to make exhaustion analytically important rather than incidental. At `V24:p048-050`, his repeated sleepiness and bodily degradation coexist with fragmentary return of childhood memory.

The memories do not arrive as a complete explanatory monologue. They emerge in pieces: a family photograph, Hana, the buried connection to Nana, domestic speech, and affective flashes. Shigaraki describes the returning material as intensely unpleasant—`最高に気分が悪い`—even while his combat performance escalates.

The source therefore rejects a tidy therapeutic model in which remembering automatically heals. Here:

`memory recovery -> increased experiential coherence + increased distress + increased destructive capacity`.

Whether later volumes alter that relation remains OPEN.

## 4.3 Decay's field behavior expands before its origin is fully remembered

At `V24:p051`, Shigaraki's Decay propagates through clustered bodies and connected matter at a scale far beyond his earlier single-target presentation. Later, at `V24:p149`, other characters explicitly register that even things Shigaraki has not directly touched are collapsing.

The safe V24 formulation is that Decay can now **propagate through contacted structures/targets into connected material**, enormously increasing area effect.

The volume does not yet justify every later mechanical simplification. What matters narratively is that recovered memory, prolonged lethal training, physical exhaustion, and Quirk expansion are arriving together, making it difficult to isolate one single cause.

# 5. No.228–230 — Twice: identity trauma is resolved by evidence and belonging

## 5.1 Twice's first priority is Toga's survival

Twice finds Toga gravely wounded and responds with immediate panic and care. At `V24:p064`, the relational stake is explicit:

> `ただ 生きてくれ`
>
> `君は…連合の皆は俺の居場所なんだ!!`

This is not abstract villain solidarity. `居場所` identifies the League as the social place in which Twice experiences belonging.

The line becomes the emotional precondition for the chapters that follow. Skeptic tries to exploit Twice's deepest identity wound by threatening the person and group that now anchor him.

## 5.2 `心の怪我` is a precise description of Twice's failure mode

The source re-establishes Twice's known history:

- his Quirk is `二倍` / Double;
- he duplicated himself extensively;
- his copies fought over which one was original;
- he was nearly killed by them;
- afterward he could not trust his own ontological status.

The phrase `心に怪我を負ってしまった` is more useful than a generic “insanity” label. His behavioral block is tied to a specific feared proposition:

> `I may be a copy, and sufficient damage may reveal that I am not the original.`

Skeptic deliberately weaponizes that proposition by breaking Twice's body while threatening Toga.

## 5.3 V24 refuses a monocausal “society did this to him” account

The childhood/adolescent backfill is unusually careful. Twice lacked a stable family safety net and slid into isolation after a traffic/work incident. He used Double to produce company and convenience. But the narration also explicitly refuses to place all responsibility elsewhere: it notes the temptation to think other people caused his fall while acknowledging that his own bad choices accumulated (`V24:p076-079`).

The formation model is therefore plural:

`social isolation + weak safety net + personal bad decisions + Quirk affordance + self-created clone ecology + catastrophic identity conflict + later criminal-community belonging`.

That is analytically stronger than either “he was born broken” or “society made him a villain.”

## 5.4 Giran's intervention is a genuine recognition event

The flashback at `V24:p081-082` materially deepens Giran.

Giran does not tell Twice that his problems are imaginary. He normalizes the existence of people like him:

> `大丈夫 似たような人間は案外沢山いるもんだ`

and redirects the question toward trust and community:

> `誰に?`
>
> `仲間に`

He then tells Twice about a group in which Twice will be needed.

This is villain formation through **successful recognition** rather than failed recognition.

That distinction is essential. The project cannot maintain a simple equation in which recognition produces heroes and failed recognition produces villains. Giran sees a socially damaged person accurately enough to provide belonging, and that belonging helps Twice become a more stable and effective criminal actor.

Recognition is ethically underdetermined by itself. What matters also includes the community, norms, and purposes into which the recognized person is integrated.

## 5.5 `痛えのに消えねェよ俺!!`: the feared ontology is empirically falsified

The decisive Twice breakthrough is bodily.

After Skeptic's puppets break him, Twice remains. At `V24:p075-086`, panic gives way to realization:

> `俺が!? 俺だ! 俺なのか!?`

then finally:

> `痛えのに消えねェよ俺!!`

The evidentiary structure matters. Nobody talks him out of the fear. He receives disconfirming evidence under extreme stress: the damage he believed would reveal him as a duplicate does not make him disappear.

His subsequent action is then relationally directed:

> `俺は仲間を殺さない`

The breakthrough is therefore neither pure self-confidence nor pure friendship.

It is:

`embodied falsification of identity fear + attachment to endangered comrades -> restored behavioral access to self-duplication`.

## 5.6 Sad Man's Parade turns fragmentation into collective agency

In Chapter 230, Twice uses Double on himself at enormous scale. The visual field fills with versions of the person whose trauma came from not knowing which version was real.

The irony is productive rather than merely spectacular.

Earlier:

`multiplicity -> ontological terror -> self-restriction`.

Now:

`confirmed original body + accepted multiplicity -> coordinated collective force`.

At `V24:p089`, Twice frames the purpose as repayment:

> `俺を受け入れてくれたおまえたちに報いたいんだ!!`

The League's belonging structure has not “cured” every aspect of Twice's psychology. His contradictory speech and instability remain. But a specific incapacitating fear has been materially broken.

This is enough to move his model beyond a generic “comic split personality” reading.

# 6. No.230 — Geten exposes the hierarchy inside liberation

The Meta Liberation Army's politics becomes more specific through Geten.

At `V24:p099-100`, Geten states that in the future the Liberation Army seeks:

> `異能の強さが社会的地位と直結する`

and:

> `異能を高める事＝生きる事そのもの!`

This is not merely freedom from regulation.

It is a proposed status order in which ability strength becomes socially decisive.

That changes the political reading substantially. The MLA contains a real critique of suppression, but its affirmative future is not obviously egalitarian. It risks reproducing Quirk hierarchy more openly:

`liberate ability -> evaluate people through ability -> convert power differences into social differences`.

The movement can therefore oppose one coercive system while carrying another.

Geten himself embodies the doctrine. He treats training the Quirk as existence-defining and has developed extreme ice control. Later, Re-Destro notes that Geten acquired the ability to alter ice temperature after a chance trigger when Re-Destro was burned (`V24:p160`), supplying explicit in-world evidence that Quirk capability can change suddenly under particular conditions.

This matters for Toga and Shigaraki because it prevents their V24 changes from being treated as unique miracles while still leaving exact mechanisms character-specific.

# 7. No.231 — Hawks: covert proof, institutional trust, and an unresolved body

Volume 24 briefly returns to Hawks while the Deika conflict continues.

The chapter reinforces the existing model of Hawks as a covert actor living inside incompatible trust systems. He remains under scrutiny from Dabi, and Dabi's trust requirement has become severe enough to involve Best Jeanist.

The sequence shows Hawks visiting the recovering Best Jeanist, who still bears catastrophic injury from All For One. Hawks produces his feather blade in an ominous transition. Later a corpse-like package is presented as proof intended for Dabi's trust test (`V24:p109-118`).

The prospective rule is strict:

> **V24 does not establish that Hawks killed Best Jeanist.**

The source establishes that Hawks is willing to stage or participate in an evidentiary performance that requires Dabi to believe he crossed that line. Identity/authenticity and later causal explanation remain OPEN.

This is a useful continuation of Hawks's V21-V23 model: undercover legitimacy requires actions that become unreadable from outside the trust regime in which they are performed.

# 8. No.232 — `異能` and `〈個性〉`: political vocabulary has a genealogy

## 8.1 The word `個性` is not politically innocent

Chapter 232 gives a historical account of ability terminology.

A woman who bore a child with an extraordinary ability pleaded for a world in which the child could live freely. Her language treated the ability as something unique to the child—`個性`. Her appeal was mocked, and she was later killed by anti-ability hostility (`V24:p130-132`).

The later social term `個性` therefore carries an emancipatory genealogy even though contemporary regulation can still suppress ability use.

This is important because the same word has functioned throughout the series as an apparently ordinary category.

V24 makes the language historical:

- `異能` marks an older frame of abnormal or extraordinary ability;
- `個性` carries a claim that the capacity belongs to personhood;
- the MLA claims inheritance from the political struggle around that shift;
- contemporary society can use the more humane word while retaining restrictive institutions.

Terminological progress and institutional progress are not identical.

## 8.2 Re-Destro's strongest critique is paired with a dangerous essentialism

Re-Destro challenges Shigaraki by asking whether a historically weightless destructive gang can possess more significance than the accumulated struggle of the liberation movement (`V24:p133`).

But in Chapter 234 he also states:

> `"個性"は人格に直結するものだ!`

He looks at Shigaraki's destructive Quirk and asks what kind of person such a capacity implies.

This is precisely where the MLA's identity politics can harden into essentialism.

If Quirk is part of personhood, it does not follow that Quirk determines the whole person. The series has already provided counterexamples:

- Shinso's coercive-looking Brainwashing can become rescue infrastructure;
- Toga can use Uraraka's Quirk without acquiring Uraraka's ethics;
- Twice's Double changes meaning radically depending on identity state and attachment;
- Quirks can change under training and crisis.

V24 therefore supports a narrower proposition:

> **Quirk can shape embodiment, desire, social treatment, and available action without functioning as a complete moral or psychological destiny.**

# 9. No.233 — Spinner: dependency without shame

## 9.1 Trumpet attacks Spinner at the exact point of prior vulnerability

Trumpet's `煽動` turns mass politics into literal Quirk affordance: his voice strengthens the people who hear him, with effect increasing alongside the scale of the communication (`V24:p142`). Political mobilization is therefore embodied as amplification.

He then attacks Spinner's social worth. Spinner's `ヤモリ` Quirk is comparatively unimpressive—principally allowing him to adhere to walls—and Trumpet says he cannot see Spinner as someone capable of accomplishing anything (`V24:p143`).

This is the MLA hierarchy stated personally:

`weak ability -> low expected social significance`.

For Spinner, that accusation lands on an already established history of emptiness, discrimination, and Stain-following.

## 9.2 Spinner's response is unusually self-aware

At `V24:p146`, Spinner does not defend himself by inventing grand individual greatness.

He calls himself:

> `人に乗っかってなきゃ何にも出来ねェ小市民`

and then asks, in effect, why that should be uniquely shameful when the Liberation Army itself rides behind Re-Destro.

The response matters because Spinner retains the factual core of the criticism while rejecting its moral hierarchy.

He is a follower. He has needed another person's example to move. But dependence is not a disqualifying essence.

This creates a strong contrast with MLA meritocracy. Spinner's emerging value system is less individualist than the movement accusing him of weakness. His question is not “am I the strongest autonomous actor?” but “what cause/person am I willing to move with, and does that movement give my life direction?”

This strengthens the V23 model without making him ideologically complete.

# 10. No.233–234 — Shigaraki versus Re-Destro: power, memory, and the rejection of creation

## 10.1 Re-Destro tries to read Shigaraki from his Quirk

Re-Destro's Stress Quirk converts accumulated stress into power. At `V24:p150-151`, he explains that he is a person who stores anger and stress, and his body makes that accumulation visible.

He then treats Shigaraki's Decay as diagnostic of personality: five-finger touch destroys, so what does that say about the person?

This is more than combat taunting. It is a political ontology:

`Quirk -> personality -> social place`.

The battle becomes a contest between two theories of personhood as much as two abilities.

## 10.2 AFO's hands are revealed as deliberate anti-healing infrastructure

The most important V24 correction to the Shigaraki model appears at `V24:p155`.

As childhood memories surface, the disembodied family hands are named: mother, grandmother, grandfather, father. Then the earlier mentor voice explains:

> `心というものは よく出来ている`
>
> `怒りや悲しみといった負の感情は 時と共に癒されていく`
>
> `彼らを肌身離さず持ち続けなさい`
>
> `その想いが風化してしまわぬように`

This is decisive evidence of grooming.

AFO did not merely interpret an already fixed trauma. He gave Shigaraki a technology for **preventing ordinary emotional attenuation**. The hands are wearable memory cues designed to keep grief and anger close enough that they do not fade.

The manipulation is therefore not simply:

`AFO told Tenko what his past meant`.

It includes:

`AFO deliberately structured Shigaraki's daily embodiment to preserve negative affect over time`.

This substantially strengthens the claim that present Shigaraki is both an agent and the product of long-term affective engineering.

## 10.3 Memory recovery adds experience to emotion

At `V24:p157`, the text describes previously unexplained irritation sinking in the heart while lost memories return:

> `スッポリ抜けた思い出が戻っていく…`
>
> `感情に経験が伴って往く!!`

This is an unusually exact model of autobiographical integration.

Shigaraki already had affect without full narrative access. Now the experiences attached to that affect are returning.

That means V24 does not show the creation of hatred from nothing. It shows **existing affect becoming biographically legible to the person experiencing it**.

## 10.4 Quirk “awakening” is treated as interaction between latent capacity and lived stress

Re-Destro notices that Decay can function despite Shigaraki no longer satisfying the old five-finger condition (`V24:p159`). He immediately contextualizes this with an in-world precedent: under a sudden trigger, a Quirk can make a dramatic leap, as happened when Geten gained temperature-control capability after Re-Destro was injured (`V24:p160`).

He also recognizes Shigaraki's movement as the product of repeated extreme combat rather than spontaneous talent alone.

The volume therefore resists a single-factor explanation for Shigaraki's increasing power:

- the Quirk has latent developmental possibility;
- prolonged Gigantomachia combat has conditioned the body;
- Deika is an acute lethal environment;
- memory is returning;
- emotion is intensifying and becoming contextualized.

Power development is embodied history, not merely a numerical unlock.

## 10.5 Shigaraki finally answers the “what will you create?” challenge by rejecting the premise

Re-Destro's ideology assumes that political destruction should be justified by a future social order. Shigaraki does not supply one.

At `V24:p172`, with his sensations and memories sharpening, he asks:

> `俺 何をつくるって…?`

The narration says the past is returning with yesterday-like clarity. Then Shigaraki concludes:

> `俺は本当にただ`
>
> `壊すだけだ`

This is not a sophisticated political program. It is a self-definition that rejects Re-Destro's requirement that destruction prove its worth through construction.

The important caution is that this self-definition is neither wholly autonomous nor wholly imposed.

It emerges from:

- a childhood history not yet fully recovered at the start of V24;
- AFO's active preservation of resentment;
- years of villain socialization;
- present comradeship;
- extreme physical conditioning;
- Shigaraki's own repeated choices.

V24's strength is that it allows all of those causal layers to coexist.

# 11. No.235 — the Shimura household before catastrophe

## 11.1 The volume gives Tenko an ordinary heroic desire before it gives him a villain origin

The V1 tendency to read Tenko mainly backward from Shigaraki must be corrected.

At `V24:p176-177`, child Tenko still wants to be a hero. His mother asks whether he still wants that, and he answers yes. He then talks about children who are excluded from the group—`みっくん` and `ともちゃん`—and remembers inviting them into hero play.

This is not yet adult hero ethics, but it is socially diagnostic. Tenko's heroic fantasy is attached to inclusion: children who are left out can enter the game, and Tenko is delighted when he is assigned the All Might role.

The source therefore establishes a meaningful pre-villain counterfactual state:

> **Tenko's childhood identification with heroism is not merely status desire; it contains an elementary relational/inclusion impulse.**

Later transformation must therefore be modeled as transformation, not as revelation that he was always only destructive.

## 11.2 Kotaro's anti-hero rule is inherited injury converted into household law

Kotaro forbids hero talk in the house. The adults describe it as a family rule, and Tenko repeatedly experiences punishment when he violates it.

The rule is causally grounded in Nana Shimura's abandonment.

Kotaro possesses the letter in which Nana apologizes for not being able to act as a mother, says she loves him, and frames separation as necessary because of a dangerous enemy (`V24:p184-185`). Adult Kotaro's response is devastatingly concise:

> `いっそ嫌ってくれたらよかったよ`

Love does not repair the abandonment for him. It can make the wound harder because the sacrifice remains intelligible as love while still being experienced by the child as being left.

Kotaro then converts his interpretation into a maxim at `V24:p183`:

> `ヒーローというのはな…`
>
> `他人をたすける為に`
>
> `家族を傷つけるんだ`

This is one of the series' strongest examples of ethical residue across generations.

Nana's decision was made to protect family from AFO. Kotaro experiences the result as proof that heroes choose strangers over family. He then uses that wound to prohibit his son's hero aspiration.

The causal chain is tragic without being deterministic:

`Nana's protective sacrifice -> Kotaro's abandonment wound -> anti-hero household rule -> Tenko's repeated suppression`.

Each transition includes human interpretation and choice.

## 11.3 The household is not a simple “abusive father / innocent everyone else” diagram

The other adults are affectionate. They comfort Tenko, care about him, and visibly dislike Kotaro's severity.

That makes the failure more important, not less.

At `V24:p178`, the narration captures the child's experience of the whole house:

> `父の建てたこの家は`
>
> `僕を優しく否定する`

The phrase `優しく否定する` is analytically central.

Tenko is not surrounded only by cruelty. He is surrounded by people who care about him but repeatedly defer to the rule that invalidates a core aspiration. Their warmth coexists with passivity.

Only after Kotaro strikes Tenko do the adults openly withdraw compliance. At `V24:p186`, Tenko's mother says:

> `限界だよ`

and:

> `手を上げるなら もうルールには従えない`

She reminds Kotaro that he said he wanted to create a happy family. Kotaro himself answers:

> `こんなハズじゃなかったんだけどな……`

The source therefore distinguishes malicious intent from harmful system behavior. Kotaro wanted a happy family. The adults wanted peace. Their combined policy still produced a home in which Tenko experienced persistent invalidation and escalating distress.

## 11.4 Hana provides both recognition and betrayal

Hana first gives Tenko the family photograph of Nana and explicitly supports his hero dream (`V24:p179`):

> `大丈夫だよ`
>
> `私は転弧の事 応援してるから`
>
> `お父さんに内緒で 姉弟ヒーローになっちゃおう!`

For Tenko, this is intimate recognition from inside the household.

When Kotaro discovers the photograph, Hana panics and blames Tenko for wanting to see it (`V24:p182`). The narration describes children as unexpectedly cunning and simple at once.

The point is not that Hana was secretly malicious. It is that frightened children can preserve themselves through betrayal even inside a loving sibling bond.

This matters for Shigaraki's later relational model because the memory of “family” being recovered is not a single emotional color. It contains affection, alliance, fear, betrayal, passivity, violence, and failed repair.

## 11.5 Tenko's itching is somatic context, not yet a complete diagnosis

The volume repeatedly associates Tenko's itching with being at home and with emotional strain. His mother notes the scratching; Tenko says he itches at home; after Kotaro discovers the photograph, the narration links humid post-rain air, prickling skin, and itching rising from inside his body (`V24:p176`, `V24:p181`).

This is strong evidence that the symptom is stress-linked in the presentation.

V24 does not yet establish a complete medical or Quirk-biological mechanism. The safest model is:

> **itching is a recurrent embodied marker of domestic distress whose exact causal physiology remains OPEN.**

## 11.6 The origin chapter stops before the catastrophe resolves

The most important archival discipline in V24 is what the chapter does **not** show.

Tenko retreats outside and clings to Mon. The dog's body begins visibly cracking under his hand. Tenko says:

> `モンちゃん`
>
> `僕っ…やだもう…`
>
> `みんな…`
>
> `嫌いだ…`

Then Volume 24 ends at `V24:p187`.

No completed death is shown. No sequence of family deaths is shown. No later wandering child is shown. No AFO encounter is shown. No distinction between accidental and deliberate killing is yet available from this volume.

The correct V24 endpoint is therefore:

> **Tenko's Quirk appears to begin manifesting during acute family distress at the exact moment his affect condenses into generalized rejection, but the consequences and intent structure remain unresolved.**

Anything stronger belongs to V25 or later evidence.

# 12. Recognition, normalization, and villain formation: V24's causal grammar

Volume 24 materially changes the project's villain-formation model because it supplies several very different pathways inside one tankobon.

## 12.1 Toga: dangerous desire plus coercive normalization

Toga's route contains a Quirk-conditioned appetite/attraction structure that frightened her family and did not fit ordinary social expectations. The response was suppression and masking rather than successful harm-aware integration. She later rejects that mask and chooses a violent form of self-expression.

This is neither simple determinism nor simple social innocence.

## 12.2 Twice: isolation plus self-generated catastrophe plus successful criminal recognition

Twice experiences social isolation and weak support, makes damaging choices, uses his Quirk to manufacture company, suffers a catastrophe created by his own copies, and then receives a meaningful recognition intervention from Giran. The League gives him `居場所`.

The recognition works psychologically while directing him toward criminal solidarity.

## 12.3 Spinner: stigma plus ideological borrowing

Spinner's heteromorph stigma and prior emptiness made Stain's public example mobilizing. V24 then shows him consciously acknowledging that he is a follower rather than pretending complete ideological originality.

His development is less “find an authentic ideology hidden inside” than “choose which external example gives movement to an otherwise inert life.”

## 12.4 Shigaraki/Tenko: family inheritance plus suppression plus grooming plus present choice

V24 supplies direct childhood material but deliberately ends before the family catastrophe completes. What is already established is enough to defeat any one-factor story:

- Nana's protective abandonment wounds Kotaro;
- Kotaro converts the wound into household anti-hero law;
- affectionate adults enforce or tolerate that law until physical violence crosses their threshold;
- Hana alternates between recognition and fear-driven betrayal;
- Tenko wants to be a hero and includes socially excluded peers in play;
- domestic distress is embodied through itching;
- AFO later deliberately keeps family remains/hands close to prevent negative emotion from fading;
- adult Shigaraki independently chooses destruction as his present self-definition.

The correct model is **causal layering**, not exonerating monocausality.

# 15. Power-philosophy updates

## 15.1 Quirk is embodied identity without being total moral destiny

Toga's blood desire, Twice's identity mechanics, Re-Destro's Stress, Geten's training doctrine and Shigaraki's Decay all show that Quirks can shape psychology, habits, social experience and available action.

But V24 repeatedly blocks the stronger equation `Quirk = moral essence`.

## 15.2 Quirks can change under lived pressure

Toga uses Zero Gravity while transformed; Shigaraki's Decay expands its activation/propagation behavior; Geten is cited as an earlier case of abrupt capability extension. Re-Destro explicitly recognizes sudden developmental leaps as possible.

The mechanism remains context-sensitive, not a universal “trauma always unlocks power” rule.

## 15.3 Liberation can become hierarchy

The MLA's strongest normative claim—ability should not be suppressed—is undercut by Geten's declaration that ability strength should directly determine social status. Freedom from one system of control can become a more explicit power aristocracy.

## 15.4 Belonging can increase destructive capacity

Twice's League attachment makes him psychologically more stable in one crucial dimension and militarily far more dangerous. Relational care is not morally self-validating.

## 15.5 AFO weaponizes the natural fading of trauma

The family hands are not only symbols. They are an imposed technique for preserving anger/grief against ordinary emotional attenuation. Power over another person can therefore operate through control of memory conditions, not only Quirk theft or physical coercion.

# 19. Japanese-language and register findings

## `普通`

Toga turns `普通` from an external demand into a first-person claim. `私も普通に生きるのです` and `私は恋して生きて 普通に死ぬの` do not mean “my behavior is socially acceptable”; they mean that Toga refuses the institution's authority to define her own experience as ontologically outside normal life.

Curious's `素の顔` and the chapter's `仮面を作った` establish a face/mask vocabulary of imposed social presentation. The analytical danger is to translate that into a simple true-self/fake-self morality. The “unmasked” self is still capable of violence.

Twice's `連合の皆は俺の居場所なんだ` names belonging spatially. It is stronger than “friends”: the League functions as a place in the social world where he can exist.

Spinner's self-label `小市民` is deliberately anti-grandiose. He accepts ordinariness and dependency while refusing the MLA inference that only great power justifies social worth.

V24 historicizes the vocabulary. `個性` is not merely a technical label but a personhood claim born from conflict over whether extraordinary capacities could belong within ordinary human life.

The phrase describing Kotaro's house is one of V24's most precise social formulations. `優しく` prevents the family from being reduced to overt cruelty; `否定する` prevents their affection from being treated as adequate protection.

## `いっそ嫌ってくれたらよかったよ`

Kotaro's response to Nana's loving letter captures the unresolved injury of protective abandonment. Love without presence is not experienced by the child as uncomplicated consolation.

## `俺は本当にただ 壊すだけだ`

Adult Shigaraki's line is not a timeless essence revealed from birth. V24 positions it after returning memories, AFO's preserved resentment, prolonged training and present ideological challenge. It is a current self-definition with a history.

# 23. V24 major model change

Volume 24 shifts the project from **inheritance as jurisdiction** to **contested personhood / self-definition under coercive normalization and ideological capture**.

The core propositions are:

1. Curious's method demonstrates that factual knowledge of a person's biography can still become a form of narrative possession when the subject's suffering is converted into political evidence.
2. Toga explicitly contests the category `普通`; she insists on defining her own violent life through `普通`, `恋`, and `好き` rather than accepting society's external normal/abnormal boundary.
3. Toga's childhood directly establishes blood attraction linked to her Quirk, parental fear/pathologization, imperfect Quirk-counseling normalization, self-suppression and a social mask.
4. This does not erase Toga's agency. V24 rejects both Quirk determinism and simple social exculpation.
5. While transformed as Uraraka, Toga directly uses Zero Gravity. The observed case is canonical; complete universal copy mechanics remain later-source OPEN.
