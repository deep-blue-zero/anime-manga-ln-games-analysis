## 2. Governing transition

V15 established:

> **responsible action under uncertainty / contested futurity**

V16 converts that problem into:

> **role-bearing courage / strategic trust under pressure**

A compact formulation is:

> **Courage is not fearlessness; it is commitment to a chosen role after vulnerability becomes known. Trust is not harmony; it is coordinated action across unresolved motives.**

The volume repeatedly removes the fantasy that competent action requires an unbroken actor, a frictionless team, or a morally unified coalition.

- Kirishima's defense breaks. His courage becomes diagnostic precisely after the body-state he trusted fails.
- Fat Gum's defense is finite. His protection works because absorbed damage can be converted into a different team role.
- Amajiki is socially anxious and self-doubting. Those states coexist with high-level judgment, adaptive combat, and a deliberate decision to hold three opponents alone so the rescue line can move.
- The Hassaikai Eight Bullets are not psychologically homogeneous. Some possess real bonds with one another even while Chisaki treats subordinates instrumentally.
- Rappa remains with Chisaki not because of organizational faith but because Chisaki is the only opponent who has defeated him.
- The League and Hassaikai retain lethal grievance, yet Shigaraki explicitly proposes `信頼を築こう` and reciprocal disclosure because coalition is strategically useful.

The V15 question—what can responsible actors do when the future is uncertain?—thus becomes a V16 question about distributed action: **what can a network rely on when every person, body, institution and alliance is breakable?**

V16's answer is not invulnerability. It is legible commitment under pressure.

## 3. The raid makes V13's `群` model operational under hostile architecture

### 3.1 Distributed heroism is now a moving system

The opening chapters immediately validate V15's institutional preparation. Police, professional heroes, sidekicks, U.A. students and support actors do not enter the Hassaikai headquarters as interchangeable fighters. Their value comes from differentiated roles.

The operation must:

- control surface resistance;
- maintain lawful evidence-backed entry;
- locate Eri;
- pursue Chisaki;
- respond to Quirk effects whose mechanics are initially unknown;
- preserve forward momentum while particular members peel away to handle local threats.

This is the practical continuation of the post-All-Might `一強` to `群` transition. The group is not merely “many heroes instead of one.” It is an **interdependent routing system** in which local expertise decides who advances and who stays.

### 3.2 Mimic turns the built environment into an adversarial participant

No.139 makes the headquarters itself unstable. Joi Irinaka / Mimic uses his Quirk to manipulate the underground space, changing walls, floors and routes so that navigation becomes combat.

The threat is important because it attacks professional coordination rather than only bodies. A corridor can no longer be assumed to remain a corridor. Route planning, formation and line-of-sight become provisional.

Aizawa's value is correspondingly epistemic as well as combative. Erasure becomes part of the team's attempt to identify and constrain the mechanism. The operation proceeds by testing hypotheses about who is controlling the environment and how much control is possible.

The volume thereby continues V15's anti-omniscience principle. Even after a carefully prepared raid begins, the heroes do not “know the level.” Professional competence is the ability to update inside an environment that resists being mapped.

## 4. Amajiki Tamaki: anxiety and operational authority coexist

### 4.1 The decisive act is role selection

Amajiki's most important V16 action is not a flashy manifestation. It is a decision about where he belongs in the network.

When three Hassaikai fighters create a local bottleneck, Amajiki tells the main line to continue and insists that he can contain the three alone (`V16:p047-053`). His `俺一人で充分だ` / `俺なら一人で三人完封できる！` should not be read as a sudden personality inversion into Bakugo-like bravado. The surrounding evidence is more specific.

He is still Amajiki:

- socially inhibited;
- visibly strained;
- highly aware of comparison with Mirio;
- vulnerable to anticipatory failure.

But he also understands the tactical problem. If the strongest/faster rescue line stops for every secondary engagement, Eri remains farther away. Amajiki claims the hold because he judges his Quirk and current condition sufficient to perform that role.

This materially changes the model. **Anxiety is not his decision policy.** It is a recurrent emotional condition within which a more capable policy can operate.

### 4.2 `サンイーター` is adaptive composition, not a fixed moveset

Amajiki's combat expands the Quirk model far beyond “he can manifest food traits.” He continually composes solutions from available biological inputs.

Across the fight he uses tentacles, shells and other consumed characteristics; crucially, he also consumes material from an opponent's crystal production and incorporates it into his own adaptive response. The pattern is not mere versatility for spectacle. His cognition is visible in the way he searches for the opponent network's dependency structure.

At `V16:p080` his reasoning condenses into `連携の起点を崩す!`: break the origin point of the coordination rather than simply overpower each body in sequence.

That supplies strong evidence for Amajiki's attention model:

- he notices how abilities combine;
- he identifies a coordination node;
- he selects a target according to system effect;
- he changes his body/toolset in response to what is locally available.

His elite status is therefore grounded in adaptive cognition as much as Quirk breadth.

## 5. Mirio and Amajiki: the “sun” relation is reciprocal

V14 and V15 made it easy to read Mirio as the bright, socially fluent senior whose confidence compensates for Amajiki's inhibition. V16 prevents that relationship from collapsing into one-way rescue.

The childhood material shows Amajiki admiring Mirio because Mirio continues moving forward even after failure. Yet Mirio explicitly tells Amajiki that Tamaki's own behavior motivates him: **despite being extremely nervous, Tamaki does not run away** (`V16:p062-065`). Mirio therefore does not merely illuminate Tamaki. Tamaki is evidence to Mirio that persistence under fear is possible.

The naming logic follows this reciprocity. Mirio's encouragement—Tamaki can be a person who “devours even the sun,” `太陽すら喰らう者`—does not create competence from nothing. It recognizes a quality already observable in Tamaki.

The relation should now be modeled as:

> mutual aspirational reinforcement with asymmetric social presentation.

Mirio is outwardly brighter and more socially fluent. Tamaki is more inhibited. But each uses the other's persistence as a reason to keep advancing.

This matters for character reconstruction: Amajiki should not be simulated as psychologically dependent on Mirio for all action, and Mirio should not be simulated as unaffected by Tamaki's example.

## 6. The Hassaikai trio: instrumentalized people can still possess genuine bonds

The three opponents confronting Amajiki complicate the villain-organization ledger.

One explicitly describes Chisaki's practice in humiliating terms: Chisaki picks up “trash like us” and reuses it. Yet the same speaker insists that even trash has pride and that being expected to perform produces a desire to answer that expectation (`V16:p061`). Later the trio names itself as trash bound by a hard/strong bond: `俺たちはゴミだが "ゴミ"なりに固い絆で結ばれているのさ` (`V16:p079`).

Amajiki does not flatten this into sentimental equivalence. His response is unusually careful:

> `おまえたちの境遇も…俺にはわからないが…その固い絆とやらは俺にもわかる!!`

He explicitly says he does **not** understand their circumstances, but he can recognize the bond (`V16:p082`). He then distinguishes `友だち` from a merely instrumental relationship: friends are not simply people who “use one another” (`V16:p083`).

Three analytical consequences follow.

First, Chisaki's instrumental organizational model does not exhaust the social life beneath him. People treated as disposable can form real lateral attachments.

Second, “villain belonging” remains plural. The League's `居場所` model, Twice's belonging, Magne's chosen habitat and the Hassaikai trio's bond are not identical, but all resist the assumption that criminal affiliation is socially empty.

Third, Amajiki's empathy is **bounded rather than appropriative**. He recognizes a relational pattern without claiming access to suffering he has not lived. That is useful character-model evidence for how he interprets opponents under pressure.

## 7. Hassaikai loyalty is heterogeneous

The background material around the Eight Bullets prevents the Hassaikai from functioning as a single psychological unit.

Older members distinguish their attachment to the previous boss from their attitude toward Chisaki. `俺たちが惚れたのは組長だ` expresses loyalty to the boss rather than unconditional endorsement of the current young head (`V16:p092-093`). There is suspicion around the boss's incapacitation and resentment toward unilateral methods.

Other members exhibit different attachment structures:

- the Amajiki trio have genuine lateral bond despite accepting a degrading “trash” frame;
- Tengai coordinates functionally with Rappa;
- Rappa's reason for remaining is personal combat access rather than loyalty;
- Irinaka's behavior is closer to organizational devotion and operational protection of Chisaki;
- Chisaki himself claims that he and the boss are sufficient for Hassaikai continuity.

This is a major correction to a simplified “Overhaul controls loyal underlings” model. The organization is held together by **different contracts with meaning**: debt, pride, bond, loyalty to the old boss, access to violence, hierarchy, coercion and strategic opportunity.

That heterogeneity makes later defections, cooperation or failures more predictable without reducing them to plot convenience.

## 8. Rappa: an embodied-power combat ethic

Rappa's worldview is unusually explicit and narrow.

At `V16:p099` he dismisses guns and blades as `不粋`. If a tool can allow “anyone” to win, he does not regard that as the kind of fight he wants. What he values is killing/fighting with `その身に宿した力`—power housed in one's own body.

This is not a general MHA thesis endorsed by the narration. It is Rappa's normative combat model.

For him:

- the body is the credential;
- repeated direct confrontation is the proof;
- defeat creates desire for rematch rather than obedience;
- an opponent earns recognition through surviving/answering force.

That explains both his battle behavior and his organizational position.

Later, Rappa tells Fat Gum that Chisaki is the only man who has defeated him (`V16:p163-165`). In their underground-ring encounter, Rappa says he “died,” only to find himself restored. After joining, he challenged Chisaki five more times and lost every time. His summary is stark: he remains there **to beat that man**.

So Rappa is not a Chisaki loyalist in the ordinary sense. Hassaikai membership gives him continued access to the one opponent who defeated the bodily power he treats as ultimate.

## 9. Shield, shield, spear, shield: roles are defined relationally

### 9.1 Tengai names the matchup as a role system

The title No.142 `盾と盾と矛と盾` is not decorative. Tengai explicitly analyzes the field as `我々は矛と盾 対してあっちは盾と盾`: on one side spear and shield; on the other, shield and shield (`V16:p101-103`).

That diagnosis exposes the immediate hero-side problem. Fat Gum and Kirishima are both built around receiving force. Against Rappa's output and Tengai's barrier, surviving is not enough. The team requires a conversion from defense to penetration.

### 9.2 Kirishima's defense actually fails

The most important fact about `烈怒頼雄斗` in V16 is that it breaks.

Kirishima explicitly experiences the failure:

- `割れた…!!`
- `痛え!! 受けきれなかった！`

His response includes shame: `強くなれた気でいた!!` (`V16:p103-104`). The story therefore refuses to validate his hero identity by discovering that his shield was secretly absolute.

Fat Gum's instruction is psychological and operational: do not let the heart break with the hardened surface. This is the first strong bridge into the subsequent backstory.

### 9.3 Fat Gum's body changes team role

Fat Gum's own Quirk makes the role conversion literal. He absorbs Rappa's attacks, but the stored/received damage consumes the defensive mass he relies on. That cost becomes a resource: accumulated impact can be released so that a body built as a shield becomes a spear capable of piercing a shield (`V16:p116-117`).

The important rule is not “Fat Gum has a secret ultimate attack.” It is:

> **receiving force changes what role his body can occupy.**

Defense and offense are not fixed personal identities.

### 9.4 Kirishima makes the conversion possible by reoccupying the broken role

Kirishima's contribution is not winning the duel. It is repeatedly re-hardening as his protection breaks: `割れたそばから硬めてけ!!` (`V16:p121`). He keeps the dangerous attention on himself long enough for Fat Gum to complete the stored-force conversion.

Fat Gum's response—`まさか逆に守られるとは…` and then gratitude that he has become a good spear—formalizes the reversal (`V16:p123`).

The junior who entered as someone needing adult protection protects the adult long enough for the adult to change roles.

This is V16's clearest image of **reciprocal professionalism**. Hierarchy remains; experience remains; but protection is not one-directional.

## 10. Kirishima's courage is built from remembered failure

### 10.1 The flashback begins with comparison and inadequacy

Kirishima's middle-school self does not possess the current persona as an essence waiting to emerge. He actively compares himself with people whose abilities and social presence look more naturally heroic.

Ashido Mina appears socially quick, conspicuous and able to use a comparatively striking Quirk. Kirishima worries that his own Hardening is plain and weak. His thought—if only he had a more powerful/flashy Quirk, perhaps he could confidently say he was aiming for U.A.—shows that self-confidence is initially entangled with Quirk legibility (`V16:p129-131`).

Crimson Riot offers an apparent counterexample: train the body and compensate with heart. But Kirishima has not yet internalized what that claim requires.

### 10.2 He freezes

During the threatening encounter involving two schoolgirls, Kirishima wants to move and cannot. His internal command `おまえが 行けよ...!!` is followed by the more desperate `どうして動いてくれないんだ...` (`V16:p132-134`).

This is crucial modeling evidence because the obstacle is not ignorance. He recognizes the danger. He knows what he believes a future hero should do. The failure occurs between value recognition and embodied action.

Ashido moves instead, improvising a response that redirects the threat. Immediately afterward, she collapses into fear—`怖かったァァ` (`V16:p135`).

That detail prevents Mina from becoming the simplistic contrast “naturally fearless girl versus cowardly boy.” She was afraid too. The difference was action threshold in that moment.

### 10.3 An unknown middle-school rescuer becomes evidence against Kirishima's excuse structure

Kirishima later hears that immediately before All Might arrived at a recent incident, another middle-school student rushed into danger to help a friend: `オールマイト登場の直前 友だちの中学生が一人飛び出ていったそうだ` (`V16:p136`).

At the V16 chronology, the significance is not that Kirishima has a relationship with this person. It is comparative evidence. Someone of similar age, without waiting for the symbol to resolve the danger first, moved.

Kirishima's response becomes harsher toward himself. If `漢気` is supposed to be about heart rather than Quirk, then his inability to move appears to disqualify the very moral identity he had been using to compensate for Quirk insecurity.

### 10.4 Crimson Riot's doctrine begins with his own failure

The interview material then revises what Kirishima thought “courage” meant.

Asked whether he is unafraid of death/danger, Crimson rejects the premise. A person who enters a deadly situation without fear would have to be a fool or otherwise abnormal (`V16:p141-142`). More importantly, he admits his own earlier failure:

- there was a life he could have saved;
- he hesitated for a moment;
- because his heart was weak in that instant, the rescue failed.

His current behavior is therefore not grounded in native fearlessness. It is grounded in memory of what failed rescue feels like.

At `V16:p143`, he explains that he knows the expression of someone who dies and the pain of not saving; **because he knows that pain, he goes in**.

### 10.5 `漢気` is explicitly de-gendered and de-romanticized

Crimson then gives one of the volume's most important definitional corrections:

> `心の在り方だ 漢とは書くが性差じゃねェ!`

`漢気` is a state/way of the heart; although written with the character for “man,” it is not a sex difference (`V16:p143`). He continues that it is not simply confidence or fearlessness. Once he decides that, as a hero, he will protect people, he lives according to that decision.

At `V16:p144`, the doctrine condenses into a life without the regret of betraying that chosen commitment.

This matters because Kirishima's masculinity-coded vocabulary can easily be flattened into machismo. The source itself blocks that reading. His adopted ideal is not “men should be tough.” It is **a deliberately chosen moral posture toward fear, protection and regret**.

## 11. Kirishima's persona is self-authored rather than discovered

After Crimson's interview, Kirishima restores U.A. as his first-choice path, acknowledges his earlier failure to the girls he could not help, trains, and deliberately changes presentation (`V16:p144-147`).

The montage makes `烈怒頼雄斗` / Red Riot a constructed identity in multiple senses:

- bodily training;
- Quirk development;
- chosen hair/presentation;
- chosen name;
- chosen speech and `漢気` vocabulary;
- a rule for what to do when fear returns.

Ashido's teasing reference to a `高校デビュー` is therefore perceptive rather than dismissive. Kirishima really is debuting a self-made version of himself.

His answer, `もう絶対後悔しねェ 俺は...守れるヒーローになる` and `情けねェ自分との決別だ!!`, frames the persona as a break with a previous behavioral state (`V16:p147`).

But V16 does not allow the old self to disappear. Present Kirishima admits:

> `今までも怖ェ思いは沢山してきた`
>
> `でもそれ以上にいつも怖かったんだ…またあの時に戻っちまうのが怖かった!!`

His greatest fear is regression into the version of himself who froze (`V16:p148`).

That creates a richer decision model than “Kirishima is brave.”

> **Fear of failing his chosen identity becomes one of the mechanisms that helps him act while afraid.**

The former failure is not cured; it becomes an active reference point.

## 12. Ashido Mina: courage, social improvisation and relational perception

V16 supplies the first concentrated Mina evidence in the V2 reread.

Three dimensions are now directly supported.

### 12.1 Fear and action can coexist

Mina moves during the middle-school threat, but she is not emotionally unaffected. Her immediate post-event collapse and `怖かったァァ` are strong negative evidence against a “fearless extrovert” model (`V16:p135`).

### 12.2 She improvises socially rather than escalating mechanically

Her response to the threatening figure is not to prove combat superiority. She rapidly invents/gives direction and redirects the situation. This is a social-spatial rescue solution under fear, not merely use of Acid.

### 12.3 She notices Kirishima's shame without turning it into public humiliation

Mina later understands that the incident mattered intensely to Kirishima. Her interactions mix teasing with tacit permission for him to change. At the U.A. transition she can joke about his transformed presentation while also recognizing the seriousness beneath it (`V16:p145-148`).

This is not enough for a broad Mina model, but it establishes a useful baseline: socially confident presentation, rapid interpersonal improvisation, fear-compatible action, and perceptive teasing that can coexist with support.

Nothing in V16 justifies converting this into a romance claim.

## 13. Fat Gum: mentorship includes allowing the junior to protect the mentor

V15 established Fat Gum as warm, blunt, containment-oriented and confidence-transferring. V16 adds command under severe bodily cost.

He does several things at once:

- recognizes Rappa's threat rapidly;
- tells Kirishima not to surrender psychologically after `烈怒頼雄斗` breaks;
- continues absorbing attack beyond the comfortable defensive use of his Quirk;
- identifies the possibility of converting accumulated damage into decisive offense;
- accepts the tactical opening Kirishima creates rather than insisting on adult monopoly over protection.

This last point is important. `まさか逆に守られるとは…` acknowledges a reversal of the expected mentor/intern direction. Fat Gum does not protect his status by denying the student's contribution. He incorporates it.

Later, when speaking about Kirishima, he emphasizes that Tamaki introduced someone who knows his own weakness. The phrase `自分の弱さを知っとる人間` (`V16:p161`) aligns Fat Gum with Crimson Riot's anti-fearlessness model: knowledge of weakness can be professional infrastructure rather than disqualification.

## 14. Chisaki's Quirk and organization both disassemble and repurpose

Rappa's history adds an important layer to Overhaul beyond V15's Eri instrumentalization.

Rappa describes having been destroyed/killed and then restored by Chisaki (`V16:p163-164`). The specific anecdote grounds the Quirk's relation to personhood at an extreme scale: bodily destruction and reconstruction can become a technique for domination without requiring permanent death.

Organizationally, Chisaki behaves analogously. Multiple Hassaikai members describe being picked up, assigned roles, used, or reorganized around his plan. Yet the volume also shows that people do not become blank components simply because he treats them that way.

This distinction is essential:

> Chisaki's model of people is instrumental; the people he instrumentalizes still generate desires, loyalties and bonds that exceed his model.

That is one of V16's clearest villain-side sources of organizational instability.

## 15. The Quirk-destroying project advances from prototype inference to an explicit market strategy

V15 ended with evidence connecting Eri's blood/cells to Quirk-destroying ammunition but preserved the boundary between prototype evidence and a fully realized commercial system.

V16 advances the claim.

In the retrospective Hassaikai planning material, Chisaki refers to `この完成品` and `血清` and explicitly links the product to a plan for yakuza restoration (`V16:p166-167`). The economic logic is now visible: sell a drug that heroes fear to appropriate buyers/enthusiasts, pair control over destruction/restoration with a serum, and use the resulting capital/leverage to rebuild organizational power.

This strengthens the anti-relational power thesis without importing later combat results.

What V16 supports:

- Chisaki believes he possesses a `完成品` and a serum;
- he has an explicit distribution/funding strategy;
- the project is tied to `ヤクザ者の復権`;
- Eri remains part of the underlying production system established in V15.

What remains OPEN:

- the exact field effect of every “completed” round;
- which named character, if any, is struck by one after the V16 boundary;
- the long-term reversibility/permanence of particular later outcomes;
- the final commercial fate of the project.

## 16. Shigaraki: strategic trust is not reconciliation

### 16.1 The League enters the Hassaikai operation without erasing grievance

The late-volume Toga/Twice secondment reveals the answer to one of V14–V15's open Shigaraki questions. He does not choose either immediate submission or immediate self-defeating revenge.

Instead, the League gives the Hassaikai selected operational access while keeping the relationship adversarial.

That is a meaningful leadership advance because the alliance is emotionally contaminated from the start: Magne is dead, Compress was injured, and neither side should be modeled as trusting the other in an intimate sense.

### 16.2 The shogi metaphor makes his model explicit

At `V16:p185-186`, the narrative moves to a shogi board. Shigaraki's explanation is organizational cognition made visual:

- seeing the whole board changes judgment;
- one attraction of shogi is the ability to use pieces captured from the opponent;
- Toga and Twice are placed into the Hassaikai arrangement;
- uncontrolled independent movement would create uncertainty;
- reciprocal disclosure is required.

Then he says:

> `信頼を築こう 今はまだ遺恨を残している`

“Let's build trust; for now, resentment still remains.”

And:

> `こっちは計画の全貌を差し出したろう 次はそっちの番だ`

The League has disclosed its plan; now reciprocity is demanded.

This is not reconciliation language. It is **trust as an intentionally constructed strategic relation under acknowledged grievance**.

### 16.3 “Captured pieces” is not simple dehumanization

The shogi frame can look like an echo of Chisaki's instrumentalization, but the social structures differ.

Chisaki repeatedly positions people beneath his authority and describes recoverable/disposable organizational material. Shigaraki, by contrast, operates inside a group whose members have already asserted chosen belonging and personal attachments. His strategic metaphor treats opponents/resources as pieces at the level of planning while his actual group governance must account for members whose attachments matter.

V16 does not prove Shigaraki has solved that tension. It does prove he can hold two ideas simultaneously:

- resentment and loyalty are real;
- useful coordination with an enemy can still be built.

That is mature enough to strengthen his existing `strong` leadership model without pretending he has become emotionally detached or institutionally stable.

## 17. Toga: recognition produces relationship-specific delight

Toga's late-volume reappearance adds a precise interpersonal marker.

After attacking Rock Lock and reentering the moving raid, she encounters Midoriya. When he identifies her—`トガヒミコ!?`—her response is immediate delight:

> `トガ!! そうだよトガです! 覚えててくれた!! ... 嬉しいなァ出久くん!!`

(`V16:p182`)

The sequence is useful because it is not generic villain taunting. The emotional emphasis is on being remembered. She corrects/repeats her name, celebrates recognition, and uses `出久くん`.

For modeling, this supports a relationship-specific rule:

> attention/recognition from a person of interest can be positively charged for Toga even in an adversarial operational context.

Her pleasure does not imply mutual intimacy from Midoriya, nor does it settle the later causal psychology of her attachments. But it gives the speech model a strong conditional marker: toward Midoriya, threat and affection-coded excitement can coexist in the same register.

Her internal `出向して良かったぁ...` near the shogi transition further shows that the externally imposed secondment can produce personally rewarding opportunity (`V16:p185`).

## 18. Mimic / Irinaka: caution can produce high-risk control

V16 also expands Joi Irinaka / Mimic beyond “the man controlling the walls.”

The source frames him as comparatively cautious/small in temperament while also showing him using a Quirk-enhancing drug to enlarge the scope of his environmental control (`V16:p174`). That creates an instructive state/trait distinction.

A cautious baseline does not imply conservative field behavior once organizational threat becomes acute. Under raid pressure, he accepts enhancement cost/exhaustion to transform the environment at scale.

His behavior therefore fits a role-specific pattern:

- personal caution;
- strong organizational defense;
- willingness to overextend when Chisaki/Hassaikai survival is threatened.

That is enough for an initial grounded model, but not yet enough for broad personality claims.

## 19. Role-bearing courage is the volume's hero-side synthesis

Across Amajiki, Kirishima and Fat Gum, V16 repeatedly separates courage from emotional invulnerability.

Amajiki:

- anticipates failure;
- remains anxious;
- still selects and carries a difficult role.

Kirishima:

- has a history of freezing;
- feels present fear;
- watches his strongest defensive identity literally fracture;
- reoccupies the protective role anyway.

Fat Gum:

- knows his defense is being consumed;
- accepts bodily transformation and risk;
- changes role from receiver/shield to attacker/spear when the team creates the opening.

The shared model is:

> **professional reliability is the ability to remain role-legible under vulnerability, not the absence of vulnerability.**

This substantially advances the V13–V15 professionalization arc. The series has moved from credentials and recipient-facing trust toward how a group behaves when plans and bodies are actively failing.

## 20. Strategic trust is the villain-side synthesis

The villain-side material offers a parallel but ethically different structure.

The Hassaikai trio trust one another laterally even while accepting a degrading organizational place. Rappa cooperates with Tengai while remaining attached to Chisaki primarily through rivalry. Older Hassaikai members distinguish the boss they love from the young head they distrust. Toga and Twice are loaned into an alliance with people responsible for Magne's death. Shigaraki explicitly attempts to build trust without pretending resentment has disappeared.

Thus V16 rejects a simple binary:

- heroes = trust;
- villains = betrayal.

Instead, trust is a **social technology** that can occur inside both protective and criminal systems. The ethical difference depends on what the relationship serves, how persons are treated, and whether reciprocity/agency are preserved—not on whether coordination exists.

This is analytically important for future League modeling. The League's capacity for care, loyalty or trust must not be confused with moral innocence; but moral wrongdoing also must not be used to erase observable social bonds.

## 23. Power philosophy update

### 23.1 Power does not determine role once and for all

Fat Gum is the clearest example: a defensive Quirk state can become offensive through accumulated cost. The meaningful unit is not “what is your Quirk?” but “what can this body do for this group now?”

### 23.2 Embodied power can become an ideology

Rappa's `その身に宿した力` makes bodily ability a criterion of legitimate combat. This differs sharply from hero professionalization, where tools, teams, institutions and delegated roles can all be legitimate extensions of action.

### 23.3 Quirk strength does not generate courage

Kirishima's past insecurity assumes that a flashier/powerful Quirk might create the confidence necessary for heroism. The flashback rejects that causal simplification. Mina acts while afraid; Kirishima learns a courage doctrine from failed rescue; present protective behavior depends on a chosen policy under fear.

### 23.4 Quirk enhancement introduces forced capacity expansion

Mimic's enlarged environmental control via a boosting drug shows another power relation: capacity can be chemically increased at bodily/operational cost. Enhancement does not erase resource limits; it relocates them.

## 24. Hero Society update

### 24.1 The raid is distributed authority in motion

V13's Commission-level rejection of another `一強`, V14's work-study pedagogy and V15's inter-agency evidence network now converge in a live operation. Students are neither decorative nor autonomous vigilantes. Their contribution is nested inside adult professional command and differentiated agency roles.

### 24.2 Professional competence includes letting the correct person stay behind

Amajiki's choice is institutionally interesting because the group does not treat “advance together” as sacred. The mission objective can require a strong member to separate, and legitimacy comes from role fit rather than rank alone.

### 24.3 Mentorship is not one-way protection

Fat Gum being protected by Kirishima extends the system's pedagogy. A supervised student can become materially necessary to an adult hero without the institution pretending student and pro are identical in experience or authority.

### 24.4 The system remains vulnerable to surprise

Mimic's architecture, Quirk-enhancement, Toga's infiltration/ambush and uncertain League involvement show that distributed systems do not eliminate uncertainty. They create more ways to update and redistribute responsibility when uncertainty arrives.

## 25. Villain formation and organization update

V16 strongly expands the villain-organization model.

1. **Hassaikai hierarchy is internally plural.** Loyalty to Chisaki, loyalty to the old boss, pride, dependency, lateral friendship, violence-seeking and debt are distinct motives.
2. **Instrumentalization does not erase lateral attachment.** The Amajiki trio are treated as disposable yet describe and demonstrate real coordination/bond.
3. **Rappa's membership is access-based.** He stays because Chisaki is the opponent he wants to defeat.
4. **Chisaki's plan is organizational-economic.** Quirk-destroying product plus serum are tied to yakuza restoration and market leverage.
5. **Mimic's devotion produces role-specific overextension.** Personal caution coexists with dangerous enhancement use in defense of the organization.
6. **League coalition behavior is strategic rather than reconciliatory.** Toga and Twice can be seconded while Magne's death remains an unresolved grievance.
7. **Shigaraki explicitly models trust as constructed reciprocity.** `信頼を築こう` is paired with acknowledgment that `遺恨` remains.

The strongest synthesis is that criminal organizations in MHA are not distinguished from hero organizations by the presence or absence of social bonds. The decisive differences increasingly concern personhood, reciprocity, legitimacy, coercion and what the network is organized to do.
