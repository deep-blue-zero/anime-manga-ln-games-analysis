# 1. Governing transition

Volume 18 moved the series toward **aftermath responsibility / plural heroic legitimacy**: victory, rank, power and succession were no longer sufficient answers by themselves. Volume 19 asks what heroic responsibility looks like when there is no immediate catastrophic enemy to defeat and when a rescued child is still not capable of ordinary happiness.

The governing transition is:

> **aftermath responsibility / plural heroic legitimacy -> ordinary joy as heroic labor / recognition as a contested social good**

Compact formulation:

> **Heroism can consist of creating conditions in which other people can enjoy being alive. Recognition can restore connection when it sees another person as a person; it can also become distorted when being seen, recorded, or remembered becomes the goal that organizes action.**

V19 repeatedly tests what people do with visibility and attention:

- Aoyama recognizes in Midoriya a body/Quirk mismatch resembling his own and converts that recognition into eccentric but genuine outreach.
- Midoriya responds by accepting the intention rather than demanding that the care arrive in a conventional form.
- Jiro initially treats music as a private hobby that does not count as heroic work; classmates reframe it as a skill capable of making people smile, while leaving participation to her own choice.
- Class 1-A designs its festival contribution not as self-display for the Hero Course but as service to students whose school life has repeatedly been disrupted by hero-related crises.
- Eri is physically safe yet cannot produce a smile on command; the ethical problem therefore becomes the restoration of ordinary pleasure, curiosity, and social participation.
- Gentle defines crime partly as media performance, explicitly uploads criminal acts, monitors audience reaction, and seeks a deed significant enough to become public/historical narrative.
- La Brava supplies filming, editing, route research and devotion; recognition inside their dyad is real even while the project it supports remains unlawful.
- Midoriya's evolving One For All similarly moves from spectacular bodily catastrophe toward precision, range, support mediation and the ability to protect a fragile ordinary event without turning the protection itself into spectacle.

The School Festival is therefore not a narrative rest after the Hassaikai raid. At the V19 boundary, its function is already structurally clear even though the performance itself has not occurred: it is an institutional attempt to create ordinary joy after a period in which U.A. has become synonymous with danger, evacuation, villain attack and exceptional security.

# 2. No.168 — Aoyama's `本性`: shared mismatch and projection-based care

## 2.1 The V18 mystery resolves locally, not globally

V18 ended by making Aoyama intentionally difficult for Midoriya to read. V19 answers the immediate behavioral question without authorizing any later hidden-biography claim.

Aoyama's unusual balcony/nighttime behavior leads into a direct explanation: he thinks Midoriya resembles him (`君は僕に似ているんだ`, `V19:p020`). The resemblance he identifies is embodied rather than ideological.

Aoyama explains that he has worn a support belt from childhood because otherwise Navel Laser sometimes leaks, and states plainly:

`僕も身体と個性が合ってないんだ☆` (`V19:p020`)

He says a doctor told him this and that he had noticed Midoriya's earlier inability to control his Quirk. The body/Quirk mismatch that V18 correctly kept OPEN is therefore now source-grounded.

This is important for Aoyama's self-conception. His flamboyant presentation coexists with a private history of bodily non-fit and technical dependence. The belt is not cosmetic characterization; it is a long-term support interface that makes ordinary control possible.

## 2.2 Aoyama notices Midoriya's acceleration and interprets through analogy

Aoyama also tells Midoriya that after the internship he looks more hurried or焦っている than before (`V19:p021`). This is a useful perception datum. Aoyama is not merely broadcasting himself. He has been observing a classmate's change in tempo/stress and tries to respond.

But the response reveals a characteristic blind spot: he assumes that the kind of surprise that would make **him** happy is a valid template for helping Midoriya.

`僕はサプライズが何よりも嬉しいと思うのさ! 僕が嬉しいと思うことをしたのさ` (`V19:p021`)

This is not empty narcissism and not fully other-centered attunement. It is **projection-based care**:

1. notice another person's distress;
2. map the other person onto a self-analogy;
3. offer the kind of treatment one would personally value;
4. expect the shared structure to make the gesture legible.

The model should preserve both sides. Aoyama is genuinely attentive and prosocial here; he is also eccentric and imperfectly calibrated.

## 2.3 Midoriya accepts intention without requiring conventional delivery

Midoriya eventually tells him the surprise succeeded (`サプライズ 大成功だよ 青山くん`, `V19:p022`). The narration then states that he and Aoyama became closer.

This is a small but valuable relationship scene because neither character must become socially typical for the interaction to work. Midoriya does not reinterpret the cheese/nighttime intrusion as an ideal communication strategy; he recognizes the care underneath it once Aoyama makes the logic legible.

The relationship transition is therefore:

`unreadable eccentric attention -> disclosed bodily analogy -> accepted unconventional care -> increased peer closeness`

## 2.4 V12's equality motive becomes ordinary social behavior

V12 had established Aoyama's desire to be `対等` with classmates and his family-linked distress at being different, while leaving the cause open. V19 now supplies the missing local mechanism without authorizing still-later disclosures:

- bodily/Quirk mismatch;
- childhood support dependence;
- awareness of being unlike others;
- identification with another student whose power appeared not to fit his body;
- desire to convert similarity into friendship.

The important correction is that Aoyama's conspicuousness should not be modeled as pure attention-seeking vanity. V12 and V19 together show that conspicuous presentation can coexist with fear of difference and a real desire for equal peer relation.

# 3. No.169 — The School Festival turns entertainment into service

## 3.1 Class 1-A begins from debt, not self-promotion

The class discussion explicitly rejects a purely Hero-Course-centered festival project. Other departments have repeatedly had their school life disrupted by crises centered on the Hero Course. The class therefore frames its contribution as something that should help other students release stress and enjoy themselves.

This matters institutionally. Class 1-A is not being asked to rescue someone from immediate physical danger. They are asked to recognize a diffuse social cost imposed by their public position and respond to it without assuming that hero students' own preferences define what the school needs.

Heroic responsibility expands from emergency intervention toward **hospitality**.

## 3.2 Todoroki articulates participatory public value

Todoroki argues that if the goal is to answer the service logic seriously, creating a place everyone can enjoy makes sense (`V19:p038-039`). Dance and music emerge not because they display combat talent but because they can generate shared participation.

This is a striking extension of the post-All-Might `群` model. Distributed heroism is no longer only multiple professionals covering different combat roles. A social system also needs people who can produce atmosphere, rhythm, technical staging, food, movement and invitation.

## 3.3 Jiro initially excludes her own music from heroic legitimacy

When classmates point to Jiro's musical ability, she reacts with embarrassment. She distinguishes it from hero activity:

`ウチのは本当只の趣味だし… 正直 表立って自慢できるモンじゃないっつーか…` (`V19:p041`)

This is a self-legitimacy problem. Jiro can possess substantial competence and still discount it because she has classified the skill as private, non-heroic and therefore not publicly meaningful.

The class response matters because it does not tell her merely that she is talented. Kaminari gives a normative reclassification:

`人を笑顔にできるかもしれない技だよな` (`V19:p042`)

and argues that this is sufficiently rooted in hero activity. Yaoyorozu then preserves Jiro's agency: the decision should be hers.

The scene therefore combines **recognition** and **authorship**:

- peers see value Jiro is discounting;
- they articulate the social effect rather than merely praising virtuosity;
- they do not claim the skill from her;
- participation becomes legitimate only through Jiro's own choice.

This is the opposite of Chisaki's V18 attachment-without-authorship pattern.

## 3.4 Ordinary skill becomes first-class character evidence

For character reconstruction, V19 is the first large Jiro expansion beyond crisis/team contexts. It establishes:

- high musical competence across instruments;
- enjoyment while playing;
- embarrassment when private skill becomes public attention;
- a tendency to minimize non-hero accomplishments;
- responsiveness to peer validation;
- willingness to become a teacher/organizer once she accepts the project;
- later assertiveness about performance roles and standards.

This is exactly the kind of low-stakes evidence the V2.1 method requires. A Jiro model built only from combat would miss the domain in which she is most visibly confident and socially generative.

# 4. No.170 — Eri proves that extraction is not recovery

## 4.1 The first request matters because it is a request

Eri's hospital condition has stabilized enough that adults consider a supervised visit plausible, and the narration notes that she has begun expressing wants after a period of hospitalization in which that was rare.

This is a major recovery-state change even before the festival occurs. V17 restored some agency inside rescue; V19 begins testing whether agency can survive after rescue as **preference**, not merely emergency cooperation.

## 4.2 Guilt persists after the coercer is gone

Eri apologizes for the suffering she believes she caused, including Mirio's Quirk loss. Aizawa has deliberately not told her about Nighteye's death because assigning that additional burden to her would be intolerable (`V19:p049`).

The ethical point is exact: removing the person who conditioned guilt does not remove the conditioning. Chisaki no longer needs to be physically present for his causal model to remain active in Eri's self-interpretation.

Midoriya recognizes that persistence in the visual return of Chisaki's language about other people dying because of her and self-injury being easier.

## 4.3 Mirio refuses the moral accounting Eri has inherited

Mirio's response rejects the idea that she owes repayment to damaged rescuers. He tells her not to keep apologizing and reframes the raid around what everyone wanted:

`皆 君の笑顔が見たくて戦ったんだよ!` (`V19:p050`)

The line is not a demand that Eri perform gratitude through cheerfulness. The next panels make that impossible reading untenable: when Eri tries physically to make a smile, she does not know how.

`笑顔って どうやればいいのか` (`V19:p050`)

The bodily awkwardness is crucial. “Smile” is not yet an available ordinary behavior. Trauma has narrowed her behavioral repertoire so far that an action other children perform without instruction becomes a conscious motor problem.

## 4.4 Midoriya revises the definition of rescue

Midoriya's internal conclusion is explicit:

`この子はまだ 全然救われてやしない` (`V19:p051`)

The reason is equally important: she still does not know `楽しいこと` or how to laugh/smile in the ordinary sense.

This is the strongest V19 development of the recognition/failed-rescue ledger. The series now distinguishes at least three rescue layers:

1. **recognize danger/suffering**;
2. **extract and stabilize the person physically**;
3. **restore access to ordinary life, relationship, pleasure and self-directed desire**.

V19 establishes layer 3 as a heroic obligation. It does **not** yet show the completed School Festival payoff.

## 4.5 Eri's curiosity is the first positive post-rescue orientation

Eri explains that she has been thinking about the people who saved her and wants to know Mirio and the others better (`V19:p054`).

This is one of the most important small shifts in her model.

Earlier agency often took the form of self-surrender under threat or movement toward an immediate rescuer. Here she expresses a future-facing, non-emergency social desire: **I want to know these people**.

The distinction matters. Curiosity is not gratitude-debt. It is a relationship-seeking preference generated by safety and interest.

The festival invitation therefore becomes ethically appropriate because it answers an expressed desire rather than using Eri as proof that the heroes succeeded.

# 5. No.171 — Gentle and La Brava: public criminality as media production

## 5.1 The manga introduces Gentle through history and mediation

The chapter opens by placing famous heroes and famous villains within historical memory. Public identity is not monopolized by moral legitimacy: criminals too can leave names that endure.

Gentle then introduces himself as someone who uploads videos of acts described as criminal behavior (`いわゆる犯罪行為と言われる動画をサイトにアップする男`, `V19:p066`).

This establishes the current behavioral model without yet establishing his origin.

He is not merely committing crime and incidentally being filmed. The recording, editing, upload, audience and persona are part of the act's design.

## 5.2 Gentle claims selectivity and a `義賊` identity

Gentle insists that he does not commit crimes at random and presents himself as a modern `義賊` (`V19:p066-067`). The example shown involves exposing a convenience-store product problem rather than simple material acquisition.

The claim should be recorded as **self-conception**, not treated as neutral moral fact. He believes his crimes have style and corrective meaning. He is still deliberately violating law, creating public danger and later planning to penetrate U.A. despite knowing the security consequences.

His moral self-narration and his actual legitimacy must therefore remain separate analytical fields.

## 5.3 Audience response matters to Gentle even when he disavows crude popularity

La Brava complains that Gentle's videos do not grow like more violent villain content. Gentle replies that violent conduct sometimes attracts people but is contrary to his style (`V19:p068`).

Yet the next page displays mediocre audience comments, and Gentle immediately turns toward a new plan. This is a precise motivational tension:

- he wants recognition;
- he wants it **for the right kind of Gentle performance**, not by abandoning his aesthetic code for generic brutality;
- lukewarm reception is therefore both a popularity problem and an identity problem.

V19 supports **recognition seeking constrained by self-authored style**. It does not yet support the later biographical explanation for why historical remembrance matters so much.

## 5.4 La Brava is not merely a sidekick camera

V19 shows La Brava doing media, planning and operational work. She films, edits, monitors reception, researches routes and helps structure the U.A. plan.

The later flashback in this volume shows her arriving as Gentle's first enthusiastic fan when his videos had little reach and occasional harsh comments (`V19:p129`). She offers to help him engrave his name in history.

Gentle remembers the relationship as genuinely important. Once they begin editing and planning together, the videos improve and reach grows (`V19:p130`). He tells La Brava that he is putting his soul into the U.A. project not only for his dream but to answer her feelings.

The dyad therefore cannot be reduced to “narcissist plus worshipper.” The available V19 evidence supports:

- La Brava gives Gentle recognition before large public recognition exists;
- her technical labor materially improves the project;
- Gentle accepts her as partner and explicitly treats her feelings as something he must answer;
- La Brava's devotion is intense and asymmetric in expression;
- their mutual recognition does not legitimate the crime it supports.

The deeper cause of La Brava's attachment remains OPEN at the V19 boundary.

# 6. No.172–173 — Building joy is distributed labor

## 6.1 Jiro changes from reluctant owner to working leader

Once Jiro agrees to the music project, her behavior changes sharply. She assigns instruments, coaches classmates, evaluates skill and becomes one of the central competence hubs in the festival preparation.

This is not a personality contradiction. The trigger is authorship plus domain competence. Jiro is uncomfortable with unsolicited public praise of a private hobby; she is substantially more direct once there is a concrete task she has chosen to own.

The conditional model is therefore:

> **Embarrassed under personal spotlight; assertive under accepted technical responsibility.**

## 6.2 Bakugo supplies an unexpected low-stakes competence

Bakugo reveals substantial drum ability and is recruited into the band. His abrasive surface register remains, but the musical context expands him beyond the combat/rivalry axis.

This matters because it gives another example of V2.1's state/trait rule: aggressiveness in speech does not imply narrowness of skill or inability to function in collaborative, noncombat systems.

He can occupy a coordinated performance role without becoming socially gentle.

## 6.3 The festival project becomes a network rather than a star vehicle

The class divides into band, dance, stage/effects and support work. No single student's talent is sufficient. The event requires:

- Jiro's music knowledge;
- instrumental performers;
- dance participation;
- technical staging;
- lighting/effects;
- rehearsal and timing;
- support equipment;
- institutional permission/security.

The structure parallels earlier distributed rescue operations while changing the object of coordination. Instead of extracting a victim from danger, the network is producing a shared affective environment.

This is why “ordinary joy as heroic labor” is not metaphorical in V19. The same institutional/coordination grammar previously applied to raids is now applied to entertainment.

## 6.4 Eri's campus visit supplies ordinary-world evidence

Eri's preliminary visit exposes her to U.A. as a school rather than as a hero institution in crisis. She sees students preparing events, costumes, food, demonstrations and interpersonal activity that have nothing to do with being pursued by Chisaki.

This does not yet complete her recovery. It does provide a crucial new context: curiosity without immediate coercion.

Her model now contains not only frightened attachment and post-trauma guilt but first exposure to **anticipatory ordinary life**.

# 7. No.174 — One For All becomes precision, range, and support-mediated design

## 7.1 All Might identifies the next problem correctly

Midoriya's post-Hassaikai power state contains a deceptive achievement. He has experienced 100%, and he can now force roughly 20% for a short period, but he cannot maintain 20% without degrading movement and damaging performance.

All Might does not mistake access for sustainable mastery.

He tells Midoriya that one thing is already clear: a ranged attack would be useful (`君も遠距離攻撃すればいい`, `V19:p089`). The problem then becomes how to derive range from a power whose current reliable mode is whole-body enhancement/contact force.

## 7.2 The answer comes from recombining previously learned stages

All Might explicitly asks Midoriya to review his developmental history (`V19:p093`):

1. produce 100% in one part;
2. control a percentage in one part;
3. control percentage through the whole body / Full Cowling;
4. Shoot Style;
5. raise the sustainable ceiling from 5% to 8%;
6. briefly draw up to about 20% at significant cost.

The decisive instruction is to combine earlier stages rather than wait for a wholly new ability.

Midoriya realizes that he can momentarily draw high output in a small body part and create wind pressure. The later `Air Force` technique is thus a recombination of existing competencies.

This is a strong self-authorship pattern:

> inheritance -> bodily adaptation -> percentage control -> domain-specific technique -> recombination -> mediated precision.

## 7.3 The power trajectory is away from obligatory bodily catastrophe

At the beginning of the series, Midoriya's characteristic relation to power was catastrophic contact: solve the problem by accepting a broken body part.

V19's development moves in the opposite direction:

- do not sustain 20% longer than the body can manage;
- use brief controlled output;
- create effect at distance;
- isolate the active body part;
- seek equipment that improves repeatability and aiming.

This does not mean self-sacrificial override has disappeared from Midoriya's model. It means his technical repertoire increasingly gives him alternatives.

The correct power-philosophy transition is therefore not “Midoriya stops risking himself.” It is:

> **the number of situations in which bodily destruction is his only available answer is shrinking.**

## 7.4 Hatsume treats support as iterative co-design

Midoriya approaches Hatsume with the effect he wants rather than a fully specified device. She recognizes the design problem and proposes equipment analogous to the requested function.

This is another form of plural heroic legitimacy. The hero's bodily technique is not contaminated by reliance on support engineering; equipment is part of making power more controllable, repeatable and situation-appropriate.

The relationship between power and support becomes:

`desired action -> embodied constraint -> technical translation -> support interface -> expanded safe option set`

## 7.5 All Might's mentorship is now less imitative

All Might also admits that he could use 100% naturally almost from the beginning and therefore cannot simply hand Midoriya the bodily intuition Midoriya lacks (`V19:p118`). His solution is to help Midoriya reconstruct sensations and generate his own method.

This strengthens the long-running correction from imitation to self-authorship. All Might is most useful when he stops being a template body and becomes a teacher who helps Midoriya reason from Midoriya's actual constraints.

# 8. The festival's institutional fragility

## 8.1 U.A. cannot treat celebration as security-normal

The festival exists under the accumulated consequences of USJ, training-camp attack, Kamino and subsequent public scrutiny. U.A.'s security discussions explicitly acknowledge pressure to cancel the event entirely (`文化祭は自粛しろと`, `V19:p110`).

The counterargument is not that risk is imaginary. It is that a school cannot orient every future activity around avoiding danger at the expense of student development.

This is a mature institutional dilemma:

- failing to respond to real risk would be negligence;
- allowing risk to abolish ordinary school life would let prior attacks continue structuring students' futures.

## 8.2 Celebration becomes a resilience problem

The school therefore strengthens security and establishes a stringent cancellation condition if an alarm indicates intrusion (`V19:p111`).

This transforms the Gentle encounter's stakes. Midoriya is not merely stopping a trespasser because rules matter abstractly. If the intrusion triggers the security regime, the ordinary event designed partly for Eri and other students can be cancelled.

The School Festival is therefore a fragile public good. Protecting it is a legitimate heroic task even though no city is about to be destroyed.

# 9. No.175–177 — Midoriya versus Gentle: incompatible projects of visibility

## 9.1 Recognition happens before combat

Midoriya encounters Gentle and La Brava outside U.A. while on a mundane errand. He recognizes Gentle through accumulated details associated with the videos/persona and immediately understands the route problem.

This is another Midoriya attention/inference scene, but unlike the Hassaikai alley, the target is not a distressed child. The threat is **institutional and temporal**: Gentle reaching U.A. can invalidate the event.

At `V19:p147`, the manga makes the clock explicit: **AM 8:32, 1 hour 28 minutes until Class A's program**.

Midoriya's urgency is therefore socially distributed. He is protecting classmates' work, other students' festival, and Eri's opportunity to encounter ordinary pleasure.

## 9.2 Gentle's Quirk externalizes his style

Gentle explains his Quirk `弾性`: he can impart elasticity to what he touches, including air (`V19:p154`).

The power is an unusually exact embodiment of his persona. It converts environment into stage mechanics: rebound surfaces, invisible air barriers, redirects and theatrical movement.

Unlike Chisaki's contact power, which expresses totalizing reorganization, Gentle's power produces surfaces that preserve form while changing response. His combat is consequently less about crushing force than about controlling trajectories and presentation.

## 9.3 Midoriya's new precision technique is immediately tested in a non-catastrophic conflict

V19 does not develop ranged precision in isolation and leave it in training. Midoriya applies the new option against Gentle within the same volume.

The timing matters analytically. His technical development allows him to answer a mobile, environmental opponent without defaulting to catastrophic 100% contact.

At `V19:p165`, his internal rule is explicit:

`出来ることは多い方がいいに決まってる!`

This is one of the clearest statements of his mature technical philosophy so far. More options are valuable because different problems demand different force profiles.

Heroic strength becomes **optionality**.

## 9.4 Both men are persistent, but persistence does not confer equal legitimacy

As the fight escalates, the visual/narrative grammar deliberately parallels refusal to yield. Midoriya and Gentle are both committed to an aim; La Brava later notes that neither will give up.

The volume must not turn that formal parallel into moral equivalence.

Midoriya's current aim is to prevent an unlawful intrusion from cancelling a communal event. Gentle's current aim is to perform an unlawful intrusion he has chosen because it will be significant to his public project. He knows U.A. is especially sensitive and interprets the heightened stakes as making the act more worthy of his persona.

The correct comparison is therefore:

- **shared persistence**;
- **different legitimacy**;
- **different beneficiaries**;
- **different relation to consent and institutional risk**.

## 9.5 Gentle rationalizes intrusion as beneficial

During planning, Gentle treats U.A.'s security posture and the festival timing as evidence of opportunity. He can also frame his intrusion as something that will strengthen the students' vigilance (`V19:p125`).

This is useful villain-model evidence because it shows self-serving moralization without requiring him to be lying consciously. Gentle can sincerely narrate a trespass as socially meaningful while choosing the interpretation that makes his desired spectacle virtuous.

His blind spot is therefore not lack of moral language. It is the ability to let self-authored aesthetic/public significance substitute for other people's right to define their own event.

## 9.6 The volume stops before La Brava changes the fight

At `V19:p185`, La Brava observes that Gentle and Midoriya both refuse to yield and decides to use her Quirk:

`使いましょう 私の個性`

The page ends before the effect is established.

Prospective lock:

- La Brava's Quirk mechanism: OPEN;
- the resulting power change: OPEN;
- Gentle's defeat/surrender/escape: OPEN;
- La Brava's final choice: OPEN;
- whether Midoriya returns in time: OPEN;
- Class 1-A's actual performance: OPEN;
- Eri's festival emotional response: OPEN.

# 10. Recognition taxonomy after V19

V19 makes “recognition” too broad to remain a single positive category. At least four distinct mechanisms are now visible.

## 10.1 Recognition as similarity detection — Aoyama -> Midoriya

Aoyama sees a body/Quirk mismatch and stress pattern that resembles his own. This produces connection, but care is filtered through self-projection.

**Risk:** mistaking similarity for identity.

**Value:** another person's unusual behavior becomes legible rather than merely strange.

## 10.2 Recognition as value attribution — Class 1-A -> Jiro

Peers see social value in a competence Jiro discounts. They tell her that making people smile can count as hero-rooted work.

**Risk:** appropriating a private skill because others value it.

**Corrective:** Yaoyorozu explicitly returns the decision to Jiro.

## 10.3 Recognition as restoration target — heroes -> Eri

The adults and students recognize that physical safety is insufficient while Eri remains organized around guilt and cannot access ordinary pleasure.

**Risk:** turning “making Eri smile” into a new demand she must satisfy for rescuers.

**Corrective:** invitation, curiosity and gradual exposure rather than forcing an emotional performance.

## 10.4 Recognition as public inscription — Gentle/La Brava

Gentle organizes crime around recorded public action and historical/public memory. La Brava's initial fandom gives him intimate recognition before broad recognition exists.

**Risk:** treating visibility or cultural significance as moral authorization.

**Current V19 limit:** the source has not yet explained why this form of recognition has become biographically necessary to Gentle or La Brava.

This taxonomy prevents a later synthesis from treating “lack of recognition” as a universal villain cause or “recognition” as automatically benevolent.

# 11. Hero society: from emergency security to the right to ordinary institutions

The School Festival material changes the hero-society ledger in a subtle but important way.

Earlier institutional questions centered on whether society can coordinate legitimate force, regulate Quirks, protect civilians and survive the loss of a single Symbol. V19 adds a different criterion:

> **Can a security-conscious institution preserve ordinary life rather than allowing permanent emergency to become the institution's identity?**

U.A. has rational reasons to fear intrusion. But a school that only becomes a fortress would fail another part of its purpose.

The festival therefore operates as a resilience test:

- acknowledge prior attacks;
- increase security;
- establish cancellation contingencies;
- still allow students to create, perform, socialize and invite a recovering child into a non-crisis environment.

This is not “security versus fun” in a trivial sense. It is the institutional version of the same problem Eri faces individually: surviving danger is necessary, but a life organized only around preventing the next injury is not yet a restored life.

## 14.1 Aoyama: grotesque comedy -> vulnerable analogy

The chapter begins with horror-comedy framing around Aoyama at the dorm window and cheese messages, then progressively replaces unreadability with bodily exposition. The form mirrors Midoriya's epistemic transition: strange image first, causal model later.

The small sequence of Eri physically pulling her mouth into a smile is deliberately awkward. The manga refuses an easy sentimental image and turns an ordinary facial expression into learned behavior. ## 14.3 Festival preparation montage

Preparation chapters use dense multi-character panels, equipment, rehearsals and cross-cut tasks. Formal density itself conveys distributed labor. Joy is not spontaneous magic; it is constructed by many bodies doing boring, technical and creative work.

## 14.4 Gentle's videos and interface culture

Gentle is introduced through screens, comments, filming and edited self-presentation. The page repeatedly embeds audience metrics and online reception inside the manga frame. His villain identity is therefore mediated twice: he performs for the camera, and the reader sees the camera-facing performance as an artifact inside the story.

## 14.5 Elasticity versus Air Force

The construction-site fight is spatial rather than merely impact-driven. Gentle creates rebound surfaces and invisible air membranes; Midoriya must read trajectories and answer at range. The visual grammar therefore validates the V19 technical transition toward optionality and precision.
