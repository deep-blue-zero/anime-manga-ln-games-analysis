# 1. Governing transition: chosen responsibility becomes the problem of whether responsibility remains relational

Volume 31 stripped away easy institutional answers and asked what people choose after professional legitimacy, family scripts, and inheritance conventions stop deciding for them. Volume 32 is the stress test. It does not ask whether Midoriya is sincere. His sincerity is unusually secure. It asks whether a rescue ethic remains good when its bearer treats himself as the one person to whom reciprocal rescue does not apply.

The volume repeatedly separates moral categories that a simpler hero/villain grammar would collapse:

- understanding a wrongdoer from excusing the wrong;
- offering rescue from assuming everyone secretly wants it;
- recognizing institutional victimization from erasing later agency;
- professional distrust from irrational anti-hero hostility;
- heroic commitment from sustainable hero practice;
- inheriting power from worshipping inherited techniques;
- choosing responsibility from owning all responsibility alone.

Muscular supplies the cleanest negative control for Midoriya's post-V31 rescue philosophy. Midoriya asks why he wants violence and whether another road existed. Muscular does not disclose a hidden crying child or misunderstood social grievance. He answers that a regret-free life is one in which he can fully enjoy what he likes, and finally reduces what is inside him to `血と闘争` - blood and battle (`V32:p037-038`). Midoriya still asks. He still tries to know. Then he stops him.

Lady Nagant supplies the opposite case. Her killings are real. So are the HPSC's secret extrajudicial orders, her exhaustion, the coercive threat attached to resignation, and the surviving restraint that Midoriya detects in the way she shoots. She can be both perpetrator and exploited instrument. Midoriya's refusal to reduce her to either category is more mature than a generic belief that every villain is innocent underneath.

Chisaki supplies a third boundary. Midoriya saves him from a bullet because imminent rescue does not require moral innocence. But when Chisaki wants access to the boss, Midoriya does not offer unconditional restoration. He asks whether Chisaki remembers what he did to Eri and says that if Chisaki can direct the desire to apologize toward Eri, Midoriya will inherit Nagant's promise and help (`V32:p175-176`). Rescue and accountability remain linked.

Meanwhile Midoriya becomes astonishingly competent. He combines six inherited tools, develops `疑似100%`, defeats Muscular without repeating the V09 arm catastrophe, outmaneuvers one of Japan's finest long-range combatants, and defeats later assassins quickly. Yet his life becomes less sustainable. He stops sleeping enough, stops eating enough, refuses All Might's support, ignores the professional coalition's request to slow down, and treats every new danger as proof that he must move faster before anyone else becomes involved.

The governing transition is therefore:

**`chosen responsibility after institutional/inherited scripts fail -> relational responsibility versus unilateral burden ownership`**

Compact formulation:

> **Volume 32 makes Midoriya technically more capable and ethically more discriminating while showing that neither improvement solves his oldest self-exclusion error. He can distinguish rescue from absolution and can use OFA without worshipping its power, but he still converts love into a reason to leave people behind. By the endpoint, the missing capability is not another Quirk. It is reciprocal heroism: allowing other agents to share risk, judgment, food, rest, and rescue.**

That is why the volume's final answer arrives before anyone gives a speech. The vestiges ask what could supplement the current Midoriya (`V32:p204`, `p215`). Bakugo and Class 1-A arrive. The actual argument belongs to Volume 33. The V32 claim is narrower and stronger: Midoriya's present configuration is incomplete, and the source identifies living peers - not another inherited power - as the missing category.

# 2. No.307 - civilian distrust is grounded before it becomes dangerous

## 2.1 Shindo encounters a public that no longer accepts delegated protection on trust

Shindo and Tatami encounter civilians refusing evacuation to Ketsubutsu. Shindo warns that the location is dangerous and supplies a practical reason: their stockpiles will not last (`V32:p009`). The civilians do not answer with abstract hatred of heroes. They answer from changed experience.

One asks whether the students can actually handle another villain attack if people gather at a shelter. Another says they repelled the criminal who attacked their own store and that the experience woke them up: `自分で育った街は自分で守る / 命は他人に預けねぇ` (`V32:p010`). On the next page the position becomes explicitly anti-delegation: `ヒーローよりも自分を信じる`, and `他人の生き方を強制できる程偉いんか ヒーローって奴は` (`V32:p011`).

Shindo does not answer by claiming moral superiority. He says they are more experienced at fighting and asks to be allowed to protect them: `あなた方よりは戦いに長けています / お守りさせて下さい`. The civilian's reply is the institutional wound in one line: `その言葉を信じて頼してきた結果がコレだろ`.

This materially strengthens the V31 hero-society reading. Public distrust is not best modeled as simple ingratitude after years of successful service. At least some of it is a rational update after a protection promise failed under correlated catastrophe.

The counterargument also remains inside the scene: rational distrust of institutional capacity does not make individual civilian risk assessment accurate. The Muscular attack is about to demonstrate that self-reliance can meet threats radically outside ordinary defensive competence.

## 2.2 The scene distinguishes autonomy from expertise

The civilians are morally entitled to make decisions about their own lives. That does not make all decisions equally safe. Shindo's position is therefore not automatically paternalistic just because he wants evacuation. His claim is narrower: professionalized combat experience has informational value.

V32 will repeat this structure throughout the volume:

`personhood/autonomy must be respected` does not entail `all judgments are equally competent`.

AFO's possession of Shigaraki violates autonomy. The HPSC's covert system corrupts professional authority. Midoriya's later refusal of support is self-authored. None of those facts makes the resulting choices good merely because they are chosen.

## 2.3 Muscular's appearance converts the abstract trust dispute into immediate threat

Muscular arrives and overwhelms Shindo. The return is useful because the series has a strong V09 baseline. Muscular is not a new philosophical problem introduced conveniently to validate Midoriya. He is an established case of violence as appetite.

Midoriya's intervention therefore permits a controlled longitudinal comparison: same opponent, same hero, radically different power-management state.

# 3. No.308 - the Muscular rematch separates inquiry from compulsory redemption

## 3.1 Midoriya now asks the question V31 made central

During the rematch Midoriya does something the V09 encounter did not have time to support. He asks Muscular why he wants to rampage (`何でそこまで暴れたいんだ`) and whether another road could have existed (`他に道はなかったのか 今筋強斗`) (`V32:p037`).

The question is evidence of a changed decision policy. After Shigaraki, Midoriya does not treat hostile conduct as self-explanatory. He seeks causal/motivational information before settling the moral route.

But the scene immediately prevents overgeneralization. Muscular says he wants to live without regret by fully enjoying what he enjoys. When Midoriya presses, Muscular rejects sympathy and says what exists inside him is `血と闘争だけだ` (`V32:p038`).

The correct rescue-model update is not:

`Midoriya believes all villains are secretly victims asking for help.`

It is:

`Midoriya now treats understanding as something worth attempting even when the target has committed severe harm; if the evidence does not disclose a usable alternative and immediate danger persists, he remains willing to stop the person by force.`

Muscular is therefore an essential control case for the Shigaraki thesis. Midoriya's willingness to look for another route is stable. The existence of another route is not presumed.

## 3.2 The rematch is a power-growth benchmark, but not a solo-victory myth

Midoriya's technical superiority is enormous compared with V09. He uses inherited Quirks as a coordinated toolset and avoids the earlier pattern in which victory required catastrophic limb destruction. Yet the actual causal chain includes Shindo. Midoriya notices that Shindo's vibration has materially weakened the muscle fibers (`V32:p040`).

That matters because an incautious reading can turn the rematch into proof that Midoriya no longer needs anyone. The source says something more interesting:

- Midoriya is much stronger;
- he is much better at reading the battlefield;
- an action by another hero creates the exploitable structural weakness;
- Midoriya recognizes and uses that contribution.

The fight therefore demonstrates **distributed causation inside apparently solitary heroism**.

## 3.3 `僕の全力だ!!` is a commitment statement, not a percentage calibration

Midoriya ends the fight with `僕の全力だ!!` (`V32:p041`). Because later pages explicitly distinguish 45%, Fa Jin and `疑似100%`, this line should not be treated as a technical statement that he discharged literal calibrated 100% OFA. It is the language of giving the encounter his full effective effort.

That distinction belongs in the vocabulary and power ledgers because MHA repeatedly uses emotional/full-effort language alongside numerical Quirk calibration.

# 4. No.309 - bodily improvement and relational unsustainability diverge

## 4.1 The old arm-loss warning is qualified, not erased

The hospital review answers an important retrospective question. Earlier medical warnings about Midoriya's arms were real, but the body and damage process are no longer identical. The doctor explains that prior damage resembled internal explosions, whereas the newer pattern allowed force to escape outward; reinforcing structures throughout the body prevented the same kind of pulverization (`V32:p051-052`).

The responsible update is:

`Midoriya has materially improved bodily compatibility/control and is not mechanically condemned to the exact old damage path.`

It is not:

`the old warning was false` or `his body is now safe from overuse`.

The doctor still tells him to be careful. V32 itself will show a new mode of self-damage: not one catastrophic arm break, but cumulative sleep, nutrition, injury and stress depletion.

The sustainability problem therefore changes form.

## 4.2 Midoriya makes the U.A. departure decision directly to Inko

Midoriya tells Inko `雄英には戻りません` (`V32:p053`). His reasoning is coherent. Shigaraki can locate him; an attack could occur at any moment; he no longer wants anyone else hurt (`V32:p054`). If AFO/Shigaraki reach full readiness, conditions will become worse, so he wants to stop them first (`V32:p055`).

Inko supplies the obvious counterevidence: he already failed to win cleanly and ended up hospitalized. Her objection is not cowardice. It is an empirical challenge to the assumption that more individual exposure automatically creates more safety.

Midoriya answers `強くなる`.

That answer is diagnostic. When confronted with relational fear produced by his own risk policy, his default solution remains increased capability rather than shared-risk redesign.

## 4.3 The hug contains both healthy reassurance and the seed of the problem

Midoriya embraces Inko and promises: `大丈夫！必ず帰ってくる！` (`V32:p056`). This is not cold abandonment. He understands that she is frightened and gives a future-oriented promise.

Yet the promise is still unilateral. Inko cannot verify or materially participate in the plan that supposedly secures her. Midoriya offers outcome reassurance instead of negotiated exposure.

This relationship-specific pattern becomes important later with All Might: affection is real; care is real; the recipient's agency over shared risk remains restricted.

## 4.4 All Might chooses accompaniment rather than command

All Might recognizes that simply forbidding Midoriya will fail: `どうせ止めても行くんだろう…`. His response is `ならば私も…！ / 君が嫌だと言おうとも！` (`V32:p057`).

This is a meaningful post-Symbol adjustment. He cannot command the successor through overwhelming power or institutional rank. He chooses proximity/support instead.

The strategy is relationally better than prohibition, but V32 will show its limit: accompaniment works only while Midoriya continues to accept being accompanied.

## 4.5 Gran Torino keeps lethal force inside the rescue framework

Gran Torino survives and gives Midoriya his cape. His central warning is deliberately uncomfortable: do not become too fixated; `殺しが救いになる事もある` (`V32:p058`).

This does not order Midoriya to kill Shigaraki. The rest of the sentence preserves discretion: remember it, whatever you decide.

The line therefore strengthens the V31 formulation:

- Midoriya is free to prefer rescue;
- rescue cannot become a dogma that makes lethal necessity conceptually impossible;
- the moral problem is case-specific judgment under extreme threat.

Later synthesis should resist turning the Shigaraki rescue project into categorical pacifism.

# 5. No.310 - the street and the vestiges both test whether reaching out is still possible

## 5.1 The heteromorphic woman shows how fear converts uncertainty into wrongful violence

Midoriya encounters civilians attacking a heteromorphic woman who is trying to flee. She says she has done nothing and is frightened; the attackers suspect deception (`V32:p064`). Midoriya does not shame them as irredeemable bigots in the moment. He says he understands why people are over-sensitive on a night like this but that the woman has no hostile intent (`V32:p065-066`).

This is a high-value social-judgment scene because Midoriya holds three things at once:

1. fear is understandable;
2. fear does not establish guilt;
3. the immediate victim still needs physical protection.

The woman later apologizes for causing trouble, saying everyone is afraid. Midoriya does not remain to receive gratitude. He says he has to go (`V32:p067`).

The scene therefore shows both the mature part of his judgment and the emerging pathology: he can mediate another person's fear with nuance, but he is already converting every completed rescue into a reason to leave immediately for the next one.

## 5.2 All Might offers food; Midoriya delegates the person and keeps moving

Midoriya asks All Might to escort the woman because he cannot stay. All Might checks his condition and offers food, including pork cutlet (`V32:p068`).

This apparently mundane act is first-class V2.1 character evidence. The relationship now includes logistical care: transport, tracking, food, condition monitoring. It also gives the later discarded bento a concrete baseline. All Might is not merely emotionally worried; he is trying to maintain the successor's body.

## 5.3 Yoichi reframes OFA's origin around a hand offered to an enemy's brother

Inside OFA, the second and third holders initially remain resistant. Yoichi's intervention resolves the impasse. The second holder was a resistance leader fighting AFO, yet when he encountered Yoichi - AFO's younger brother and therefore someone easy to classify through enemy lineage - he reached out rather than simply executing him. Yoichi tells him: `君が手を差し伸べてくれたから OFAは始まったんだ` (`V32:p076-078`).

This is one of the volume's strongest callbacks. The anti-AFO inheritance does not begin only with opposition. It begins because someone fighting AFO recognizes that relation to AFO does not erase Yoichi's separate personhood.

The second holder agrees to cooperate and tells Midoriya that from here things will accelerate (`V32:p079`). The visual/ethical relation is exact enough to matter later:

`resistance to domination + hand offered across inherited association -> OFA continuity`.

## 5.4 Inherited Quirks are explicitly de-sacralized into tools

The vestige guidance around the Muscular period and subsequent operations tells Midoriya to relax his reverence for each inherited Quirk. Because the accumulated power and handling difficulty are largest for the ninth holder, he is told to treat predecessor Quirks as things that were not originally super-finishing-moves by themselves and to build the path to victory with them as `道具`.

This is a major power-philosophy maturation. Inheritance is honored by **use**, not by ritualized imitation. The predecessor remains a person; the Quirk becomes a tool.

That distinction prevents the project from confusing relational respect with power fetishism.

# 6. No.311 - the coalition recognizes Midoriya as both weapon and failure point

## 6.1 Public hostility follows the heroes into ordinary movement

The Top 3 remain objects of direct public anger. Protest language such as `WE DON'T NEED FAKE HEROES` continues the V31 legitimacy collapse. The important V32 change is that operational planning now explicitly accounts for what public knowledge of OFA would do.

The coalition knows it is using Midoriya as bait even though he chose the role. Consent matters, but the heroes do not pretend that consent makes the arrangement morally costless.

## 6.2 Hawks's AFO analysis identifies hatred as an outsourced capability

Hawks reasons that AFO has long pursued OFA yet may not possess the hatred necessary to overwhelm its vestigial resistance. His memorable evidentiary observation is that AFO is always smiling; he hypothesizes an affective lack and therefore a need for Shigaraki's hatred (`V32:p086-090`).

This remains **Hawks's inference**, not omniscient narration. It is nevertheless high-value because later strategy is being built from it.

Current responsible formulation:

`AFO appears to outsource/appropriate Shigaraki's hatred as a capability he may not possess strongly enough himself.`

Do not convert that into a total claim that AFO feels no anger or emotion.

## 6.3 All Might recognizes the embodied cost before Midoriya does

All Might tracks Midoriya and notes that the boy is still moving despite apparently sleeping very little (`V32:p091`). This is the beginning of a repeated recognition asymmetry:

- Midoriya notices everyone else's danger;
- All Might notices Midoriya's depletion;
- Midoriya does not give the latter observation equal decision weight.

The rescue problem is now reflexive.

# 7. No.312 - Lady Nagant enters as the professional system's disavowed interior

## 7.1 Hawks's warning is specific and relational

Before the ambush, Hawks warns Midoriya that most large groups cannot seize him alive and identifies one Tartarus escapee as unusually dangerous. Lady Nagant was his predecessor/senior in the HPSC line; if she attacks, Midoriya should flee (`V32:p097-099`).

Hawks's status as `後輩` matters. Nagant is not merely a historical case he read about. She occupies the institutional role-path that precedes his own.

## 7.2 Nagant's technical identity is precision plus constructed ammunition

The volume's authorial paratext specifies the Rifle/hair-bullet system and emphasizes Nagant's extraordinary long-range precision. The exact mechanics matter because her later moral restraint is inferred partly through shot selection, not through a confession conveniently delivered before the fight ends.

AFO also supplies `エアウォーク` / Air Walk as an additional Quirk, proving that the assassin is not operating with only her original professional kit (`V32:p118-120`).

## 7.3 Chisaki's post-collapse motive narrows almost completely to the boss

Chisaki has escaped with Nagant but is not functioning as the earlier project architect. He repeatedly demands to reach `オヤジ` and later says he wants to apologize (`V32:p107`, `p150`, `p175`).

This is valuable sustained-failure evidence. His grand program, control hierarchy and restoration project have collapsed. What remains behaviorally salient is the benefactor relationship.

That does not retroactively redeem his treatment of Eri. It proves the narrower proposition already visible in V18: Chisaki can have genuine attachment while still subordinating other people catastrophically to his own project.

## 7.4 Nagant's AFO contract begins with real assent and later reveals coercive contingency

AFO does not simply mind-control Nagant into accepting the assignment. He selects her, appeals to her desire for hero society's destruction, offers Air Walk as a down payment, and frames the arrangement as a contract to finish the heroes. Nagant accepts the mission (`V32:p108-111`).

That initial agency must remain visible. Later evidence that AFO implanted a lethal contingency does not mean every step was forced.

The morally accurate relation is:

`voluntary agreement under nihilistic alignment + asymmetric hidden contingency that punishes later defection`.

That combination is characteristic AFO control: he prefers consent when consent is useful, but engineers the relationship so that revocation is not genuinely safe.

# 8. No.313 - accumulated technique outruns sustainable integration

## 8.1 The fight makes parallel processing a cognitive resource problem

Midoriya uses Danger Sense to survive, Smokescreen to change information geometry, Blackwhip/Float for movement, and attempts to integrate the third holder's still-unfamiliar power. The third holder explicitly warns that Midoriya has not reached the required level of parallel processing. Midoriya answers that there is no time and says he will learn it now because Nagant's information could lead directly to AFO/Shigaraki (`V32:p122-125`).

This is a subtle continuity with his older body policy. The object of overextension changes from bones to cognition/time.

Earlier:

`I will break the arm because the victim cannot wait.`

Now:

`I will force unmastered multi-Quirk integration because the country cannot wait.`

Technical sophistication has not erased the urgency heuristic.

## 8.2 Fa Jin is an energy-management Quirk, not free 100%

The volume's technical material defines the third holder's `発勁` as storing kinetic energy through repeated motion and releasing it later. Midoriya combines stored energy with OFA 45% and movement/centrifugal effects to produce `疑似100%` (`V32:p148`, `p153-158`).

The wording matters: **pseudo-100%** is not proof that his baseline body can now continuously output ordinary OFA 100% without consequence.

Power-model update:

`functional All-Might-class bursts can be constructed through tool combination before base OFA percentage alone reaches that level.`

## 8.3 Chisaki becomes a deliberate moral-cognitive attack surface

Nagant targets Chisaki specifically to multiply Midoriya's thoughts. She reasons that a hero educated in rescue will be slowed if a recognized enemy is placed in danger (`V32:p146-152`).

Midoriya's response is immediate. He rescues Chisaki and says discussion comes later (`V32:p156`).

This scene is highly diagnostic because it removes innocence from the rescue condition. Midoriya knows Chisaki. He knows what Chisaki did. That knowledge changes accountability, not immediate rescue eligibility.

# 9. No.314 - Nagant makes the hidden cost of clean hero legitimacy explicit

## 9.1 The HPSC used covert lethal violence to preserve the public surface

Nagant says she killed many people to maintain a false society (`V32:p135`). The source then gives concrete categories: people planning terrorism against heroes and a hero team colluding with villains for fame and money were eliminated without ordinary legal adjudication; crimes and people disappeared together under secret HPSC orders (`V32:p136`).

The key institutional claim is not merely `the HPSC did bad things`. It is more specific:

> **The organization treated public trust in heroes as infrastructure valuable enough to maintain through concealed extrajudicial killing.**

Nagant describes herself as a gear preserving that system (`V32:p137`).

This substantially revises any hero-society model in which the prewar clean surface was produced only by genuine professional conduct plus All Might deterrence. At least part of the surface was actively curated through hidden coercion.

## 9.2 The text preserves Nagant's agency while showing institutional injury

Nagant repeatedly obeyed because she believed both public face and hidden face were necessary. The burden accumulates. Children ask to touch the hand of a hero while she sees blood on it (`V32:p138-139`). She eventually asks whether killing the assigned targets actually makes society better (`V32:p140`).

When she challenges the chairman's policy and considers exit, his reply turns resignation into threat: he asks whether she understands what leaving means (`V32:p141`). She kills him.

The public version then obscures the institutional conflict, presenting a simpler story about an argument with a hero. Nagant says the HPSC protected the papier-mache surface even there (`V32:p142`).

The correct causal structure is therefore not:

`Nagant was an innocent person mechanically forced to kill.`

Nor:

`Nagant freely chose everything, so the HPSC is irrelevant.`

It is:

`a genuine heroic recruit enters a secret state-violence role, repeatedly chooses compliance inside it, becomes psychologically injured by the contradiction, is threatened when she questions exit, kills the chairman, and later radicalizes toward the belief that transparent villain domination may be preferable to concealed hypocrisy.`

## 9.3 Nagant's critique is partly true without her conclusion becoming authoritative

Her strongest line compares showing only beautiful things to brainwashing (`綺麗なものだけ見せ続けるのは洗脳と同じじゃないか?`) (`V32:p141`). The volume supports the factual premise that serious state violence was hidden from the public.

Her next normative move is not automatically endorsed: she treats an AFO-controlled future as potentially clearer/more honest than hero society's hypocrisy (`V32:p143`). Transparency of domination does not make domination good.

V2 must therefore separate:

- **FACT:** HPSC concealed extrajudicial lethal operations.
- **INFERENCE:** the concealment helped preserve public hero legitimacy.
- **NAGANT VALUE JUDGMENT:** openly bad rule may be preferable to a hypocritical clean facade.

Midoriya's answer addresses exactly that gap.

# 10. No.315 - `綺麗事` survives contact with darkness by becoming conditional and evidentiary

## 10.1 Midoriya does not answer Nagant by denying the darkness

Midoriya says he did not know, but has begun to understand that the world is not simply black and white. Fear, anger and other states are mixed together. His conclusion is not `therefore heroes were right`. It is `だからこそ そこに手を差し伸べなきゃ` (`V32:p145-146`).

That `だからこそ` matters. The hand is not offered because the world is pure. It is offered **because it is not**.

This is a stronger philosophy than early-series optimism because disillusioning evidence no longer functions as a defeater of the rescue norm.

## 10.2 Midoriya reads restraint from action rather than demanding a confession

After defeating Nagant's rifle arm, Midoriya tells her that if she had fully committed to AFO she could have disabled him with the first shot; he also notices that the shot at Chisaki was off (`V32:p160-161`).

His conclusion follows behavior:

`あなたの心はまだヒーローのままです` (`V32:p162`).

This is not clairvoyance. It is an inference from tactical choices under conditions where lethal precision was available.

That makes the scene useful for character modeling: Midoriya's recognition policy uses counterfactual behavior (`what she could have done if fully committed`) rather than merely affective intuition.

## 10.3 AFO answers moral reversibility with preinstalled punishment

Nagant begins to yield, and AFO's contingency detonates. The recorded voice states the governing principle directly: `心ってのは流動的だ / だからちゃあんと契約不履行の場合にも備えておいたよ` (`V32:p163`).

AFO understands the same fact Midoriya does - people can change - and draws the opposite governance conclusion.

Midoriya:

`because hearts are not reducible to current wrongdoing, keep open a path back when evidence supports it.`

AFO:

`because hearts are mutable, make defection physically punishable.`

This is one of the clearest power-philosophy contrasts in the V2 corpus.

## 10.4 Hawks's `先輩` restores professional relation after institutional disavowal

Hawks catches Nagant and calls her `先輩` (`V32:p165`). He later identifies himself as her `後輩` and asks how she can think the situation should be abandoned after fighting Midoriya (`V32:p169-174`).

Nagant asks, in effect, how Hawks can still have such bright eyes after being made to do terrible things by the HPSC. Hawks answers with two elements: there were people who supported him, and `俺 楽観的なんす` (`V32:p174`).

This should not be flattened into personality alone. V31 already established that institutional control does not exhaust Hawks. V32 supplies a comparative control: two people pass through related covert machinery, but one retains enough support/optimism to continue while the other collapses under the contradiction.

The institutional critique therefore remains strong without becoming deterministic.

## 10.5 Nagant's information is an act after defection, not mere recovery symbolism

Despite catastrophic injury, Nagant gives the operational lead: within two months, bring the target to the mansion in the Haibori forest (`V32:p172`). She also says several people besides her were approached (`V32:p173`).

This is the behavioral proof of changed alignment. Her moral turn is not only a softened expression. She supplies actionable intelligence against AFO after the penalty for defection has already been demonstrated.

Her survival beyond the immediate rescue remains OPEN at the V32 boundary.

# 11. No.316 - help without absolution, then AFO turns moral knowledge into psychological warfare

## 11.1 Midoriya inherits Nagant's promise conditionally

Chisaki demands to see the boss and says he wants to apologize (`V32:p175`). Midoriya asks whether he remembers what he did to Eri. Chisaki's response remains disturbing: he immediately treats Eri partly through utility (`壊理がいれば…`). Midoriya nevertheless identifies that Chisaki has someone he wants to apologize to.

His condition is precise: if Chisaki can direct that feeling toward Eri, Midoriya will inherit Nagant's promise (`V32:p176`).

This is the strongest V32 demonstration that Midoriya's rescue philosophy is not equivalent to moral amnesia.

`immediate rescue -> preserve life`;

`future assistance -> can be conditioned on accountability/recognition of the harmed person`.

## 11.2 AFO predicts Midoriya's compassion and builds traps around it

At the mansion AFO's prerecorded projection says he enjoyed the exchange with Nagant and predicted that someone like Midoriya could not abandon that kind of person (`V32:p180-181`). He claims he did not force Nagant, that it was her will, while the reader has just seen a hidden detonation contingency for contractual defection.

The gap between AFO's language and action is diagnostic. He selectively invokes autonomy to deny responsibility while designing relationships in which autonomy cannot safely reverse itself.

He generalizes the social problem into a bleak proposition: even societies that celebrate individuality ultimately manage and exclude incompatible individuals; democracy and socialism differ less than the group-life principle beneath them (`V32:p182`). He tells Midoriya that the road he chose is thorny, that every fight will wear down his heart, and that the end will not come.

Then he states the targeting transition: his interest is no longer primarily All Might. `次は 君だ!!` (`V32:p182-183`).

The mansion explodes.

## 11.3 AFO's primary attack surface is now Midoriya's burden model

The explosion is not merely an assassination attempt. AFO has identified what V32 itself identifies:

- Midoriya will pursue damaged people;
- he will keep moving when others ask him to rest;
- he will accept being bait if it might reduce danger to others;
- he experiences delay as moral risk;
- he has difficulty allowing living supporters to share the cost.

AFO therefore does not need to refute Midoriya's ethics philosophically. He can weaponize their unsustainable implementation.

This is a major villain-formation/control update: **AFO attacks relational infrastructure, not only bodies.**

# 12. No.317 - the body no longer breaks all at once; the support network is what Midoriya sheds

## 12.1 Nagant survives the immediate blast, but remains critically injured

The coalition escapes the mansion blast. Nagant is taken to Central Hospital in a condition described as so severe that the doctors are surprised she is alive (`V32:p186`). This closes the immediate `did she die in the detonation?` question while leaving later recovery/function OPEN.

## 12.2 Hero attrition is modeled as human burnout, not exposure of universal fraud

The professionals discuss continuing retirements. Desutegoro supplies the most important first-person explanation (`V32:p187-188`). He says that after running continuously with no time to recover, what comes back is lashing-out and doubt. He knows support exists, but `1の罵声が10の声援に勝るんだ` - one abusive voice outweighs ten cheers. He thought he was different; perhaps everyone did. His concluding self-appraisal is devastatingly mundane: `どうやら俺はヒーローじゃない / 人間だったみてえだ`.

The line should not be read as the manga agreeing that he was never a hero. The whole point is that he had internalized an **inhuman endurance standard** for the role.

Hero attrition therefore has at least three distinct routes in the V31-V32 evidence:

- cynical/status-dependent exit;
- capacity/fear collapse under reversed incentives;
- psychologically ordinary burnout in people who had previously acted heroically.

A system that relies on every professional being less human than the public is structurally brittle.

## 12.3 OFA secrecy and Midoriya's isolation become an information-security trap

The coalition notes that information is leaking from retired heroes and that media investigation is gradually approaching Midoriya (`V32:p189`). If OFA's reality becomes public under current conditions, multiple negative feedback loops may converge on him.

Mt. Lady asks why AFO has not simply publicized Midoriya/OFA if doing so would create pressure (`V32:p190`). The group infers that AFO may also want to avoid consequences of uncontrolled public convergence while OFA cannot yet be taken.

The important systems point is that secrecy is no longer a simple protective good. It protects Midoriya from mass targeting while also requiring a small exhausted network to carry disproportionate operational load.

## 12.4 Midoriya defeats later assassins faster while becoming less reachable

A second assassin is rapidly defeated (`V32:p191-193`). Midoriya reports that the person lacked useful information and might also explode, then moves on before All Might can stabilize the interaction.

Competence and isolation rise together.

This is exactly the kind of developmental split the V2 method is designed to preserve. A character can improve in one dimension while regressing in another.

## 12.5 The bento scene resolves what `support` means at this boundary

All Might catches up with food: `ご飯 食べてないだろ…!!` (`V32:p194`). Midoriya tells him he is fine and no longer needs All Might to follow. He points to the fact that he can now move at All-Might-equivalent levels without the old recoil (`V32:p195`).

The reasoning is not `I don't care about you.` It is the opposite. Midoriya is trying not to involve All Might.

The narration/All Might interior makes the failure explicit. He recognizes Midoriya's desire not to involve him and the successor mission, but thinks that he needs to tell the boy `頑張るな` and `休んでいい` (`V32:p196-197`). The bento falls as Midoriya leaves.

This is a major relationship-state transition.

Earlier All Might gave Midoriya OFA because Midoriya's body moved before thought for someone else.

Now All Might reaches out with food/rest language because that same person cannot stop moving.

The hand does not connect.

## 12.6 Stain is present, but his next action is not yet V32 evidence

Stain observes All Might in the aftermath of the separation. V32 does not yet supply their substantive encounter. Do not import the older combined V32-V33 reading here.

This boundary matters because V1 grouped the volumes and therefore already contains an All Might/Stain interpretation. Under V2 prospective discipline that interpretation remains **V33+ OPEN**.

# 13. No.318 - `ヤミクモ`: the problem is no longer whether Midoriya can win a fight

## 13.1 The professionals tell him to slow down; he experiences delay as enemy preparation

The coalition explicitly changes strategy because Midoriya's body cannot hold up indefinitely. Overseas hero deployment is becoming more concrete and more manpower would widen strategic options (`V32:p201`). Midoriya says he can still move and that they cannot give the enemy time to prepare.

Even the vestiges warn that his wounds are not fully healed and that this is not the moment for more overextension (`V32:p202`). He continues.

This resolves a major V31 open question. The self-exclusion problem is not hypothetical. Multiple independent supporters identify the same risk and Midoriya systematically discounts it.

## 13.2 The vestiges diagnose the contradiction from inside OFA

The vestige discussion is especially important because it removes a possible defense: perhaps only outsiders fail to understand the unique burden of OFA.

Inside the inheritance itself, the predecessors recognize what is happening. One states that Midoriya's path is correct and that the current situation does not permit simple stoppage; being the OFA holder entails this burden (`V32:p204`). Yet the discussion still asks what could **supplement** the current Midoriya.

That is the correct nuance. V32 does not say:

`Midoriya should have stayed home and done nothing.`

It says:

`the strategic necessity of action does not prove the sufficiency of solitary action.`

This is a much stronger critique than simple work-life-balance moralizing.

## 13.3 AFO's psychological attack turns every unresolved duty into a simultaneous command

Midoriya's thought sequence becomes a pile-up:

- where is AFO;
- stop Shigaraki;
- stop the League;
- everyone is in trouble (`V32:p207`).

Then:

- do not let anyone else become involved;
- complete OFA;
- make it possible for everyone to live safely and smile again (`V32:p208-210`).

The goals are individually legible. Their combination is impossible for one exhausted person.

This is why `ヤミクモ` is an apt chapter title. Midoriya is not morally aimless, but operationally he is running at everything at once.

## 13.4 Rumor turns the heroic body visually unreadable

Civilians have heard rumors of a figure who appears silently, possesses many Quirks, helps people, and may be related to AFO or a Nomu (`V32:p198`). The final description is `とてもヒーローには見えないんだって` (`V32:p199`).

The image answers V01-V03 public legibility in reverse. Early Midoriya needed power to become publicly legible as a potential hero. V32 Midoriya performs constant rescue while becoming visually illegible as a hero.

Appearance and moral function separate.

This should not be simplified into `looks don't matter`. Public readability matters enormously in hero society. The point is that visible hero iconography has become an unreliable proxy at exactly the moment institutions are failing.

## 13.5 Dictator predicts and exploits isolation/exhaustion

Dictator says the plan is working as predicted: Midoriya desired solitude and is exhausted (`独りを望み—そして疲弊`) (`V32:p211`). He controls civilians and uses numerical bodies as shields/weapons.

Midoriya understands the mechanics. He identifies the Quirk's historical defeat condition and immediately rejects large attacks because innocent bodies are in the way. Controlled civilians shout that their movement is not their will; Midoriya answers that he knows and will free them (`V32:p212-213`).

Nothing about his ethics has failed.

His **capacity to implement them alone** has failed.

That distinction is the endpoint's core.

## 13.6 Class 1-A arrives as capability, not yet as argument

Bakugo's `徹甲弾` strikes Dictator (`V32:p214`). On the final page the earlier vestige question returns: if something can supplement the current Midoriya - then Bakugo appears and calls to the others, `いたぞ てめェら!` (`V32:p215`).

The volume ends before the classmates state their case. Therefore V32 supports:

- Class 1-A has found Midoriya;
- Bakugo immediately acts to remove the controlled-civilian constraint;
- the editing identifies peers as the missing supplement category;
- the coming relational confrontation is set up.

V32 does **not** yet support:

- Bakugo's apology;
- Iida's later physical intervention;
- Uraraka's later public speech;
- the completed argument that returns Midoriya to U.A.;
- the All Might/Stain encounter.

Those remain V33+ evidence.

# 15. Hero-society model after V32

## 15.1 Trust collapse is partly evidence-sensitive

The Shindo scene requires a correction to any public-blame model that treats anti-hero sentiment as merely irrational. Some civilians have directly experienced protection failure and have adapted by preferring self-defense.

The system problem is therefore:

`failed guarantee -> rational trust withdrawal -> decentralised force -> coordination/training loss -> new harm -> further trust instability`.

## 15.2 Earlier public legitimacy was partly maintained through hidden coercion

Nagant supplies direct evidence that HPSC secret operations eliminated selected threats without ordinary legal adjudication in order to preserve the hero-society surface. Public trust was not produced only by visible hero virtue and All Might deterrence.

This does **not** establish that every agency, every era, or every pro hero knowingly participated. The source gives a specific HPSC covert program and one operative's account.

## 15.3 Exit costs matter for institutional ethics

The HPSC chairman's threat around resignation is crucial. A professional cannot be treated as fully autonomous merely because she once agreed to a role if meaningful exit later becomes unsafe.

This links hero society to the inheritance/jurisdiction theme:

`consent at entry != permanent consent to occupation/control`.

## 15.4 Hero burnout disproves the superhuman-worker assumption

Desutegoro's resignation shows that even a previously spirited, useful hero can become unable to continue when public hostility, fatigue and danger compound. Hero society's labor model historically benefited from treating emotional endurance as invisible.

The postwar question is no longer only `who deserves to be called a hero?` It is also `what material/social conditions let a human being keep doing hero work?`

## 15.5 Information concentration around OFA becomes a governance hazard

The more OFA must remain secret, the more a small set of people carries the operational/security burden. The more that group shrinks, the more Midoriya becomes a single point of failure. Public disclosure is also dangerous. V32 therefore presents secrecy as a genuine tradeoff rather than an uncomplicated virtue.

# 16. Power philosophy after V32

## 16.1 Inherited power becomes toolkit, not identity

The predecessors explicitly encourage Midoriya to treat inherited Quirks as tools. Respect for predecessor personhood is separated from imitation/reverence toward predecessor techniques.

## 16.2 Pseudo-100% is system composition

Fa Jin + 45% OFA + movement management produces `疑似100%`. Functional output can therefore emerge from architecture rather than one raw number.

This reinforces a series-wide proposition:

`effective power = capability + coordination + timing + information + relational contribution`, not only peak output.

## 16.3 Power without rest can still create incapacity

V09's warning was visible skeletal damage. V32's warning is systemic degradation: sleep, food, judgment bandwidth, wound accumulation, relation loss.

The embodiment ledger must therefore track **chronic operational depletion** as seriously as spectacular injury.

## 16.4 AFO and Midoriya share a premise about mutable hearts and diverge on governance

AFO: hearts change -> install punishment for defection.

Midoriya: hearts change -> keep recognition/recovery routes open when behavior supports them.

This is a direct philosophical opposition over whether uncertainty justifies control or hope.

# 18. Villain-formation / control updates

## 18.1 Nagant - institutional moral injury is a distinct route

Nagant is not well explained by stigma, domestic abuse, appetite, ideological purity, belonging failure, or AFO childhood grooming. Her route is **institutional moral injury under covert state service**:

## 18.4 Muscular remains appetite-centered

Repeated self-report preserves Muscular as a control against social monocausality. Not every villain route is produced by hidden institutional injustice that, once exposed, dissolves the conduct problem.

# 20. Visual grammar and motifs

The second holder's hand to Yoichi begins OFA as relational possibility. Near the end, All Might reaches toward Midoriya while the bento falls. Both are gestures toward another person under danger; one connects and creates inheritance, the other fails to connect because the successor has made distance into protection.

## 20.2 White hero iconography becomes black, ragged unreadability

Rumor says the rescuer no longer looks like a hero. Mud, blood, torn costume, Blackwhip and smoke visually approximate the threat grammar surrounding villains/Nomu. The source separates ethical action from stable costume legibility.

## 20.3 Nagant's bloodied hand versus children's handshake

The HPSC sequence superimposes children's uncomplicated hero admiration on the hand that has performed secret killings. This is visual evidence for moral injury, not merely decorative irony.

## 20.4 The bento is care scaled down to the body
