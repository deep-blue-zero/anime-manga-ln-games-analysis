# 6. No.416 — the social answer begins before the battlefield crowd arrives

## 6.1 Eri cannot simply solve the battle

The chapter checks Eri's Rewind capacity and finds it insufficient for the obvious fantasy of immediate full restoration (`V41:p081-084`). This preserves the long-standing rule that Eri is not a convenient reset device.

Her later contribution will be meaningful precisely because it is bounded.

## 6.2 Kota converts received rescue into a desire to participate

Kota openly calls himself a crybaby and explains that watching the older person he admires fight makes him think he has to do something (`V41:p085-087`).

This is a powerful longitudinal return to V08-V09. Kota once inherited hero sacrifice primarily as abandonment and resentment after his parents died. Midoriya's rescue gave him an experiential counterexample. V41 shows that the result is not blind hero worship. The boy knows his own weakness and still develops a participation impulse.

The causal chain is:

`received protection -> recognition of another's struggle -> desire to contribute within one's capacity`.

That is the network logic V41 will scale up.

## 6.3 “Everyone thinking a little about everyone” is not occupational flattening

The chapter proposes that if everyone could think just a little about everyone else, something changes (`V41:p090`).

The phrase should **not** be turned into “everyone literally becomes a professional hero.” The volume is careful about differentiated roles. Evacuation defenders remain at their posts. Some people fight. Some transport. Some give equipment. Some guide. Some cheer. Eri can contribute only within a small temporal window. Aizawa explicitly asks for currently usable positions rather than demanding identical sacrifice from all.

The stronger institutional reading is:

> Hero society becomes less fragile when concern and responsibility are distributed more broadly than the licensed profession while specialist roles and limits remain real.

## 6.4 `勧善懲悪` is explicitly insufficient for Midoriya's chosen route

An observer identifies Midoriya's refusal to perform a clean good-punishes-evil script as a thorny road (`V41:p090-093`). The source does not say simple moral opposition is always wrong. It says Midoriya's particular evidence — the crying child perception, AFO/Tenko entanglement and Shigaraki's history — has made a purely classificatory ending insufficient for him.

That distinction matters. Muscular did not require the same route; stopping him was enough. Rescue ethics remains case-sensitive.

## 6.5 The Shimura house becomes the actual interior threshold

Midoriya arrives at the remembered family house by `V41:p095`. The physical battle has turned into a threshold problem: will he enter another person's formative memory and what will he do there?

The volume's answer begins with a small social form in the next chapter: he enters with the grammar of visiting another person's home rather than conquering territory.

# 7. No.417 — the Shimura family wound is admitted without becoming a single-cause explanation

## 7.1 Nana's exclusion is relationally specific

Nana is uniquely unable to pass normally into the deepest family memory (`V41:p097-101`). This is not simply power mechanics. The separation dramatizes her special causal/relational position in the wound.

Nana calls the decision her weakness (`これは 私の弱さだ`). That is an accountability statement. She does not deny the reasons she had for leaving her child in order to fight AFO. She recognizes that a choice made under threat still wounded a family line.

The series is again distinguishing **explanation** from **exemption**.

## 7.2 `おじゃまします` is a formal recognition of entering someone else's interior space

Midoriya's approach to the house carries the etiquette of entering another person's home (`V41:p101-103`). The scene is not evidence that mental invasion has become consensual. It is a formal motif: Midoriya conceptualizes what he is doing as entering a space that is not his.

This small phrase supports the rescue-without-annexation reading. He is not acting as though another person's interior is automatically his property because he wants to help.

## 7.3 Midoriya protects Tenko before exploiting the memory

At the core abuse scene, Midoriya blocks Kotaro's strike (`V41:p104-108`). He had entered with a strategy designed to break through Shigaraki's interior. When confronted with the child being hit, his immediate rescue reflex overrides purely instrumental use of the memory.

This is a held-out validation of the V01 behavioral rule:

`visible person in danger -> rescue action can precede complete strategic calculation`.

The context has changed from a sludge villain to the interior of the series' central antagonist. The action policy remains recognizable.

## 7.4 Kotaro's grievance is inherited and partially true without legitimizing abuse

The family wound contains a harsh proposition: heroes hurt their families in order to save strangers. Nana's history gives the statement real causal content. Kotaro's pain is not fabricated.

But the source does not make truth in grievance equivalent to legitimate conduct. Kotaro's abuse of Tenko remains abuse. The broader pattern mirrors Stain and other villains: accurate diagnosis of a social wound does not grant moral jurisdiction to harm.

## 7.5 Tenko/Shigaraki has “never been able to throw it away”

The memory establishes persistence of the family wound rather than a clean separation between child Tenko and adult Shigaraki. Nana recognizes that the wound she created/left spread into Shigaraki and apologizes for not returning to take her child home (`V41:p109-112`).

This materially strengthens failed-recognition/failed-rescue analysis. Yet the next chapter immediately prevents the wound from swallowing the adult identity.

# 8. No.418 — Shigaraki rejects the pure-victim interpretation and names a chosen relational role

## 8.1 Midoriya stops trying to solve Shigaraki only through childhood

Midoriya's decisive move is not a speech saying “you were innocent, therefore the villain is fake.” He says he will collide with / bring himself against Shigaraki because Shigaraki is crying (`V41:p113-117`). The intervention becomes self-disclosure/action rather than diagnostic superiority.

That is a better rescue posture than simply telling another person what they “really are.”

## 8.2 `僕が選んだ` is the chapter's agency claim

Shigaraki answers the victim-only reading explicitly:

- `僕は…僕が 選んだんだ`
- `僕の意志で この手を繋いだんだ`

The exact phrases matter because Chapter 419 will attack them directly. At this point, the adult Shigaraki is asserting that his League bonds and villain path are not merely things done to him.

This does not make the claim metaphysically complete. V41 is about to reveal that AFO manipulated foundational conditions. But it does establish the object of the later dispute: whether manipulation of conditions nullifies all later authorship.

## 8.3 The hands are not only trauma props; they have become chosen relationship symbols

Shigaraki asks, in effect, if his choices do not matter, then what are these hands (`V41:p118-121`). Earlier, the hands began as grotesque imposed family remains and control objects. Across the series, the League creates additional relational meaning around the identity built from that history.

The adult cannot be reduced to the child without losing what happened afterward.

## 8.4 “Villains' hero” is morally compromised but relationally real

Shigaraki says he is a villain who destroys with malice and that even if hatred is broken and he becomes empty, he has to be the `ヒーロー` of the villains/outcasts who attached themselves to him (`V41:p121-123`).

This is one of the most important character-model lines in the final arc.

It establishes:

- destruction remains chosen/claimed;
- League/outcast attachment is not erased by origin rescue;
- hero vocabulary has been reappropriated inside an anti-social coalition as responsibility to one's people;
- Shigaraki's prosocial capacity is **selective**, not generalized moral rehabilitation.

Calling him a “hero to villains” is not the same as calling him morally heroic overall. It is evidence that the capacity to recognize and carry others can exist inside a destructive ethical boundary.

## 8.5 The unknown memory opens the AFO causation problem

Midoriya sees a memory of a seemingly ordinary man around the Shimura family (`V41:p124-125`). The page is prospective evidence that the causal history contains an external actor not previously admitted at this depth. It should not be pre-solved until AFO identifies his role.

At `V41:p127`, AFO reappears and says Tenko has never chosen anything.

The volume has deliberately placed two claims adjacent:

`Shigaraki: I chose.`

`AFO: You chose nothing.`

The responsible reading must adjudicate evidence rather than automatically privileging the more powerful narrator.

# 9. No.419 — `DESIGN`: extensive engineering does not make AFO the sole author of a human life

Chapter 419 is the most important causal-revision chapter in the V2 reread since Shigaraki's original origin material. It requires two corrections at once.

Any earlier model that treated Shigaraki's formative path as largely autonomous after generic social/family failure is now **too weak on AFO's direct engineering**.

Any new model that accepts AFO's `全部だ` and concludes Shigaraki never possessed meaningful agency is **too strong on total authorship**.

## 9.1 AFO's reemergence is causally linked to the collapse of Tenko's interior resistance

AFO explains that the vestige assault let him regain consciousness because Tenko's interior heart/mind was overwhelmed in the unconscious domain (`V41:p131`). This resolves the immediate final-internal-control uncertainty from V40 in a specific way: AFO's return is not evidence that Shigaraki had secretly remained obedient. It occurs after a catastrophic interior assault weakens/disrupts Tenko's state.

That distinction preserves the V40 evidence that Shigaraki had independently rejected AFO while functional.

## 9.2 AFO explicitly says felt free choice was part of the hatred-engineering design

The exchange at `V41:p132-133` is unusually explicit.

Tenko insists this was his choice. AFO answers that Tenko needed to **believe** that in order to generate the hatred force required to overcome OFA's will, and that the key was to guide while avoiding overt free-will interference.

This adds a new manipulation model:

`coercion is not always direct command; a controller can design environments so the target experiences self-authorship while desired affect/behavior emerges.`

That is more sophisticated than the earlier mentor/dependency picture.

It also creates an important epistemic limit. Because AFO *wanted* Tenko to experience choice, the existence of subjective choice cannot by itself prove absence of manipulation. But the reverse is also true: evidence of manipulated conditions does not prove every downstream relationship or decision was exhaustively authored by AFO.

## 9.3 OFA acquisition was the design objective

AFO says that through earlier OFA encounters he concluded that stealing OFA required surpassing the inherited holders in will, so he needed a vessel/soul with sufficiently concentrated hatred (`V41:p132-133`).

This changes the causal hierarchy of the Shigaraki project.

AFO did not merely notice a damaged child and opportunistically raise him as a successor. At minimum, V41 establishes deliberate long-horizon cultivation of a person whose hatred could function as an anti-OFA instrument.

The successor relation is therefore even more extractive than V40 had shown.

## 9.4 Kotaro's family environment was deliberately nudged

AFO says he avoided obvious interference and instead applied subtle prompts around Kotaro's existing family/hero grievance (`V41:p133-134`).

This is analytically crucial because it preserves causal plurality.

Kotaro's grievance about Nana did not need to be fabricated from nothing. AFO exploits and amplifies a real wound. The pattern is consistent with his broader method:

- identify an existing vulnerability;
- provide a “solution,” story or nudge;
- convert the vulnerability into dependence/desired action;
- later claim ownership over the result.

AFO's agency increases without making Nana/Kotaro/social conditions causally irrelevant.

## 9.5 Tenko's original Quirk trajectory was directly altered

The chapter says AFO removed the still-unmanifest inherited factor Tenko had received from his parents and gave him a destructive Quirk copy from Garaki's facility (`V41:p134-135`). That copy had reversibility stripped away and was driven only toward destruction.

This is a major factual revision to the origin model.

The responsible current claim is:

> **Decay as Tenko manifested it was not simply his naturally inherited Quirk emerging under stress. AFO deliberately altered the child's Quirk inheritance and supplied an engineered destructive factor.**

This sharply increases AFO's responsibility for the physical mechanics of the Shimura catastrophe.

It does **not** by itself answer every question about Tenko's affective state, later League bonds, adult ideology, or all killings committed after years of development.

## 9.6 `全部だ!!!` is both a causal boast and a character-model tell

AFO escalates from specific evidence into totality: tragedy, trials, negation, affirmation — all were given by him; `全部だ!!!` (`V41:p135-136`).

Some components are now source-confirmed. The total claim is not.

Why resist taking it literally?

1. **The speaker has a demonstrated ownership heuristic.** V40 showed him treating Yoichi, successors and stolen powers through possession grammar.
2. **The claim overreaches the specific causal evidence.** Manipulating Kotaro and supplying Decay do not logically entail authorship of every later interpersonal event.
3. **The series has repeatedly shown emergent relation beyond the mentor's design.** The League developed internal attachments and disagreements; Spinner distinguishes Shigaraki from AFO; Shigaraki rejects AFO's summons; Shigaraki's “villains' hero” role is relationally conditioned by people whose subjectivities matter.
4. **AFO's own later grief contradicts the fantasy of total control.** Yoichi remains a unique loss he cannot metabolize into replaceable possession.

The best V2 rule is:

`Record the specific manipulations as facts. Record “I authored everything” as AFO's totalizing interpretation of those facts.`

## 9.7 The body-possession objective becomes explicit again

AFO intends to erase Tomura/Tenko's remaining heart and take the body/control state (`V41:p136-140`). Midoriya is simultaneously left without the transferred factors and loses both arms in the physical struggle.

The parallel is severe:

- AFO tries to turn another person's life/body into continuation of self;
- Midoriya has just relinquished his inheritance in order to reach another person.

One side treats possession as victory. The other accepts dispossession as the cost of relation.
