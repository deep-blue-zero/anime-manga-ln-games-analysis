# 3. No.424 — the epilogue refuses to convert moral ambiguity into a victory slogan

## 3.1 Reconstruction is immediately plural

The postwar sequence moves outward from the battlefield into medical, infrastructural, and international recovery. Victory does not mean the system is suddenly whole. Bodies remain damaged, buildings remain ruined, families remain bereaved, and reconstruction requires labor distributed far beyond the final combatants.

This matters because Volume 42 is not structured as:

`villain defeated -> society fixed`.

The causal burden simply changes form.

## 3.2 Critical correction: Midoriya says he could not save Tenko's **life**

A load-bearing translation distinction occurs at `V42:p030`.

Midoriya says:

`転弧の命を救ける事は できませんでした`

He could not save Tenko's **life**.

This must not be rewritten as “could not save Tenko's heart.”

The same sequence states that Midoriya reached toward his heart and that, even after hatred was broken, Tenko remained the League's leader until the end.

At `V42:p031`, the consoling counterinterpretation is that if the crying boy was no longer there, then perhaps Midoriya did save his **heart**.

The source therefore deliberately separates:

- **life:** not saved;
- **heart/interior relation:** plausibly reached/saved;
- **adult identity:** not erased;
- **accountability:** not erased.

This is a major second-pass correction and should enter the first-pass correction ledger.

## 3.3 OFA persists as embers, not as an immediately restored inheritance

Midoriya can still perceive the `残り火` after the transfer/expending process (`V42:p032`).

The V41 endpoint was therefore correct to distinguish factor loss from immediate complete incapacity. But V42 later establishes that the embers are temporary.

At this moment, the correct state remains:

`transferable inherited factor structure lost -> residual embers persist`.

Do not use the later eight-year outcome to flatten the immediate medical/embodied state.

## 3.4 Bakugo's reaction reverses the emotional logic of the childhood relationship

At `V42:p033`, Bakugo reacts to the possibility that Midoriya is returning to `無個性` with grief.

The scene matters because the earliest Bakugo model used Quirklessness as evidence that Midoriya was beneath him. The mature Bakugo has built part of his own imagined future around continuing to compete with Midoriya.

The reversal is not that rivalry disappears. It is that rivalry no longer depends on ontological superiority.

## 3.5 Midoriya does not narrate OFA's loss as theft from his true self

Midoriya describes OFA as something he originally did not possess and as a power whose transfer depends upon an intention to give. He frames the experience as an extraordinary dream rather than evidence that his personhood has been amputated.

This does not mean OFA was emotionally trivial. Volume 41 already called it a treasure. The final state is stronger:

> **a power can become deeply constitutive of a biography without becoming the sole location of identity.**

## 3.6 All Might pluralizes “the greatest hero”

At `V42:p035`, All Might recalls the boy who ran out on the day of the sludge incident as a `最高のヒーロー` to him, then extends the description to Midoriya as someone who became that for everyone, while also affirming Bakugo and the wider network.

The singular title no longer functions as a scarce throne.

This is a direct answer to the Symbol-of-Peace concentration problem:

> **heroic excellence can be plural without becoming meaningless.**

# 4. No.425 — reconstruction includes the right to continue imperfectly

## 4.1 U.A. resumes without pretending the war never happened

The school setting returns, but this is not a reset to prewar innocence. Students carry bodily, relational, and institutional aftermath into ordinary routines.

The return of classes has analytical value precisely because the character-modeling method treats low-stakes evidence as first-class. The question is no longer only who can fight. It is who can continue living and choosing after the fight.

## 4.2 Aoyama frames accountability as movement from negative toward zero

Aoyama's departure is not staged as either:

- “all forgiven, debt erased,” or
- “betrayal fixes his identity forever.”

His reparative logic is processual. He accepts that he begins from a deficit and wants to work toward equality rather than demand immediate restoration.

This strengthens a series-wide accountability rule:

> **responsibility can remain real without converting the person into a permanently unusable moral category.**

## 4.3 Shinso's movement into the hero course converts delayed possibility into institutional inclusion

Shinso's trajectory from outsider to hero-course participant reaches a formal institutional transition. The importance is not simply reward. Earlier barriers attached social possibility to the practical shape of a Quirk. His later path demonstrates that training, institutional adaptation, and role re-evaluation can revise what the system initially treated as destiny.

## 4.4 A new endangered child prevents the epilogue from becoming self-congratulatory

The volume introduces another child in conditions of abuse, isolation, frightening Quirk difference, and social danger.

This is structurally crucial.

If the story ended only with victory speeches, the old causal field could quietly reproduce itself. The new child asks whether postwar society has learned enough to interrupt the pathway **before** a Tenko-scale catastrophe.

The answer is deferred to Chapter 429.

# 5. No.426 — the Todoroki family reaches atonement without compulsory reconciliation

## 5.1 Toya is dying, and the family is given only limited remaining time

The chapter does not produce miraculous restoration. Toya's body has reached a terminal condition. The family can speak to him, but the available future is small.

This preserves bodily consequence. Recognition arrives too late to undo everything.

## 5.2 Endeavor finally states the core failure in Toya's own terms

At `V42:p059`, Endeavor recognizes:

`俺に見てほしかったんだもんな なのに見なかった…`

Toya wanted his father to look at him, and Enji did not.

That is stronger than a generic “I was a bad father” statement because it identifies the specific recognition failure around which Toya's development organized itself.

The series does not imply that being seen would have automatically solved every later choice. It establishes that Enji's refusal to see was a central injury for which Enji is responsible.

## 5.3 Daily conversation is offered too late, and the chapter knows it is too late

Endeavor promises to come and speak every day, while explicitly recognizing the lateness.

The value of the act lies precisely in the absence of entitlement. He cannot purchase a revised childhood with present effort. He can only stop adding avoidable neglect to the remaining time.

## 5.4 Shoto asks Toya what food he likes

Shoto's question is almost aggressively ordinary.

Toya answers, effectively, “same”—the shared preference for soba.

That exchange matters more than an elaborate reconciliation speech would have. For a family whose members were repeatedly reduced to Quirks, succession functions, guilt positions, and revenge roles, a food preference is evidence of personhood outside the family project.

It later becomes part of Shoto's own final self-understanding.

## 5.5 Endeavor accepts lifetime restitution

At `V42:p065`, Endeavor states:

`犯した罪の賠償と謝罪を 一生をかけて続けていく`

He will continue compensation and apology for what he did for the rest of his life.

This is the cleanest final formulation of his atonement arc.

It does **not** say:

- he has been forgiven;
- his family must restore intimacy;
- good later conduct cancels abuse;
- suffering grants him moral credit equal to those he hurt.

The final model is:

> **atonement is an obligation of the perpetrator; reconciliation remains a choice of the harmed.**

## 5.6 Natsuo's future does not require restored fatherhood

Natsuo moves toward marriage/family life without granting Enji the relational restoration Enji might wish for.

The ending therefore refuses a common redemption shortcut in which the family must certify the offender's improvement by re-embracing him.

## 5.7 Shoto locates self-authorship inside social support

At `V42:p066`, Shoto says:

`俺にはA組皆いるから大丈夫だよ!`
`俺は自分で なりてえように なれるから!`

He can become what he wants to become **because** he has Class A around him.

This is V41's agency-without-self-sufficiency thesis translated into postwar identity.

Support does not authoredly replace him. It creates room for self-authorship.

# 6. No.427 — Spinner prevents the winners from owning the meaning of Tomura Shigaraki

## 6.1 The chapter asks a historical question, not only a criminal one

`死柄木弔とはなんだったのか`

“What was Tomura Shigaraki?”

The wording shifts from case disposition to historical meaning.

Civilian victims and observers have legitimate reasons to remember terror, destruction, and bereavement. The chapter does not suppress those perspectives. But neither does it allow public fear to be the only remaining archive.

## 6.2 Spinner's memory preserves social causality

Spinner describes the exclusion and resignation that shaped his earlier life. Tomura offered belonging, action, and a horizon that Spinner had not believed was available to him.

This does not convert Tomura into a socially beneficial leader. It records why he became meaningful to someone who had been socially abandoned.

## 6.3 `死柄木弔は俺のヒーローだった!`

At `V42:p076`, Spinner states that Tomura Shigaraki was **his hero**.

This is a powerful relational datum and a dangerous sentence to flatten.

It does not mean:

`Tomura was morally a hero in the institutional sense`.

It means:

`Spinner experienced Tomura as the person who gave him recognition, role, and belonging`.

The relationship is real even though the project it supported produced catastrophic harm.

That is exactly why villain formation cannot be explained by moral category alone.

## 6.4 Spinner introduces counterfactual self-critique

At `V42:p079`, Spinner wonders whether moving one step further might have allowed him to save his hero.

That self-questioning is important because Spinner is not left frozen as unreflective follower. He can preserve love/loyalty while questioning his own prior agency.

## 6.5 Memory and anti-repetition are intentionally placed in tension

At `V42:p080`, Spinner resolves to write:

`過去は消えない 俺が死柄木弔を紡ぐ`

The past will not disappear; he will weave/carry Tomura Shigaraki onward.

Midoriya had wanted the sadness not to continue being `紡がれ`.

The shared verb creates a productive distinction:

- destructive sadness/hatred should not be reproduced as the next person's destiny;
- the people, causes, attachments, and failures that produced the past should not be erased.

A society capable of preventing recurrence must remember more than the victor's version.

## 6.6 Midoriya becomes a courier rather than owner of Tomura's final meaning

Midoriya delivers Tomura's message to Spinner.

That is a fitting endpoint to “rescue without annexation.” Midoriya does not author the message on Tomura's behalf. He carries it.
