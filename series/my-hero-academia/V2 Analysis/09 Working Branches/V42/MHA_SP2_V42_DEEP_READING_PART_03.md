
## 7.1 Ochako's smile becomes a concealment problem

The chapter returns to Ochako's longstanding orientation toward other people's happiness. In the postwar context, the same orientation can become a mechanism for suppressing her own grief.

This is important for character reconstruction. “Likes seeing people smile” is not a simple positive trait; under stress it can produce self-erasure if she treats her own pain as socially inconvenient.

## 7.2 Toga's death remains a loss even though Toga was an enemy

The volume does not require Ochako to choose between:

- recognizing Toga's crimes; and
- grieving the person who saved her with her own blood.

That is the same anti-collapse principle applied elsewhere.

A relationship can be morally asymmetrical, politically hostile, and still emotionally real.

## 7.3 The camera did not capture their ending

Around `V42:p098`, the chapter emphasizes that the decisive interpersonal ending was not part of the public camera record.

This extends the series' critique of spectator knowledge.

Hero society is repeatedly mediated through:

- rankings;
- news;
- televised fights;
- scandal images;
- public symbols.

Ochako/Toga demonstrates that morally decisive encounters can remain private and therefore unavailable to the society trying to interpret them.

## 7.4 Midoriya notices the person who was helping everyone else

Midoriya's movement toward Ochako closes a long recognition loop. Ochako has repeatedly noticed exhausted or burdened heroes. Here, Midoriya notices her concealment.

The mature relationship is reciprocal not because they become identical but because the attentional direction is no longer one-way.

# 8. No.429 — the old woman finally makes `私が来た` civic

## 8.1 The new child is a controlled recurrence of the Tenko pathway

The chapter returns to the endangered child introduced earlier.

The structural resemblance to Tenko is deliberate:

- frightening difference;
- family abuse/confinement;
- isolation;
- escape into public space;
- visible distress;
- a society capable of looking away.

This is not a literal repetition of Tenko's biography. It is a causal test.

## 8.2 The old woman remembers the time she did not help

The old woman once encountered child Tenko and did not intervene. Fear and diffusion of responsibility allowed her to tell herself that police or heroes would surely handle it.

The ending does not portray her earlier choice as uniquely monstrous. That would defeat the social point. She represents an ordinary, understandable failure whose aggregate form is systemically dangerous.

## 8.3 `“おばあちゃんが来た”からね`

This time she approaches.

Her assurance—“Grandma is here”—is the smallest-scale transformation of All Might's arrival grammar.

The phrase matters because she has:

- no Symbol status;
- no special professional jurisdiction;
- no guarantee she can solve everything;
- no need to become the child's permanent savior.

She only has to interrupt abandonment early enough for other forms of help to become possible.

## 8.4 Earlier intervention is the deepest postwar prevention mechanism

The chapter strongly validates a long-running V2 hypothesis:

> **hero society fails most dangerously when everyone waits for a later, larger rescue system to solve a preventable early problem.**

The final answer to Tenko is not another stronger All Might.

It is a social field in which more people are willing to take the first small step.

## 8.5 But the ending is not “institutions do not matter”

Volume 42 simultaneously depicts:

- expanded Quirk counseling;
- heteromorph-discrimination work;
- reconstruction;
- continued professional hero infrastructure;
- changing public-safety priorities;
- international cooperation;
- training/education;
- new technological support;
- altered expectations around who participates in protection.

Therefore:

> **ordinary compassion cannot substitute for institutions, and institutions cannot substitute for ordinary human initiative.**

The final architecture is both/and.

## 8.6 The hand becomes a non-coercive social motif

The old woman takes the child's hand.

Across MHA, hands have carried:

- rescue;
- possession;
- inherited burden;
- family death;
- coercive grip;
- professional assistance;
- friendship;
- romantic possibility.

Here, the hand is an early offer that prevents crisis from having to become spectacular before anyone responds.

# 9. No.430 — the main serialization ending turns inheritance into vocation, education, and technology

## 9.1 The eight-year jump tests whether wartime values survive ordinary time

At `V42:p130`, the story moves eight years forward.

This matters methodologically. A wartime act can be exceptional. Eight years of adult roles provide stronger evidence about what the characters actually turned their values into.

The ending therefore gains a longitudinal validation layer rather than merely a victory montage.

## 9.2 Midoriya becomes a teacher

Adult Midoriya works at U.A.

The shallow reading is:

`lost OFA -> could not remain a hero -> settled for teaching`.

Chapter 431 later directly rejects this.

The stronger V42 reading is:

> **teaching is one of the mature forms of Midoriya's original attention to unrealized possibility.**

The child who obsessively studied heroes now helps younger people understand what they can become.

## 9.3 Adult society distributes reform across different competencies

Class A does not converge on one identical occupation or one identical model of heroism.

Members contribute through:

- frontline professional hero work;
- education;
- Quirk counseling;
- social reform;
- anti-discrimination work;
- institutional leadership/support;
- technology;
- public engagement.

This is the adult counterpart to Volume 41's differentiated battlefield network.

Plural responsibility does not require role flattening.

## 9.4 Midoriya tells a younger person that reaching out already mattered

At `V42:p137`, Midoriya reassures a younger student by pointing out that the student had already tried to extend a hand.

The adult teacher therefore transmits not only technical hero ambition but the earlier-intervention ethic.

The broader vocational line is that, after receiving/achieving a dream, one can become a person who gives another person room to dream.

This is inheritance without possession in pedagogical form.

## 9.5 OFA's embers eventually disappear

The temporary `残り火` do not remain permanently.

By the adult period, Midoriya has spent years Quirkless.

This resolves the V41/V42 immediate uncertainty:

`OFA factors expended/transferred -> embers remain temporarily -> embers eventually extinguish`.

The source does not frame the intervening Quirkless years as nullification of his hero identity.

## 9.6 All Might returns power through a social-technical network, not a mystical succession

At `V42:p140-141`, Midoriya receives an advanced support suit developed from accumulated combat data, engineering expertise, overseas collaboration, and collective financial support centered around his classmates, with Bakugo especially important.

All Might tells him:

`これも 君自身が勝ち取った力だ!!`

This too is power Midoriya earned.

The suit is thematically precise because it is the opposite of AFO's ownership architecture.

AFO's model:

`I give/take -> therefore the result is mine`.

The support-suit model:

`many people contribute -> Midoriya receives -> Midoriya remains the author of what he does with it`.

## 9.7 Technology supplements vocation rather than replacing it

The suit allows Midoriya to return to more direct professional hero activity.

Chapter 431 then establishes that he does not quit teaching.

This is important enough to treat as a final power-philosophy proposition:

> **gaining new capacity does not invalidate the self formed during incapacity.**

Midoriya does not have to choose between “real hero” and “mere teacher.”
