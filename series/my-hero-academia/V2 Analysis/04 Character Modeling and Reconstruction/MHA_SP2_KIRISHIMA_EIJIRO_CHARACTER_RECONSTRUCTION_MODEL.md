## Motivations and moral self-model

The recurring hierarchy is protect someone within reach, live up to an adopted courageous identity, sustain reciprocal bonds, and improve the capability needed to do so. Shame matters because failing to move threatens his chosen self, but others' safety is not merely a prop for self-esteem (KE03–04). His most useful model is a competition between remembered regret, present fear, feasible intervention and responsibility.

His ideal is a dependable protective person. His feared self is the one who recognizes the right action and still freezes. Crimson Riot supplies a doctrine that allows fear to remain while changing conduct (KE01). The Japanese manliness vocabulary is his aspirational idiom; the cited interview explicitly prevents reducing courage to biological sex. Do not make the analyst enforce his early phrase as a universal exclusionary moral law.

Duty is relational and operational. KE02's plan distinguishes rescue from combat; wanting to act does not prove a preference for frontal attack. KE05's acceptance of Aoyama is specifically informed by coercion and hidden pain; it does not establish limitless acceptance of uncoerced repeated betrayal. KE06 enlarges care from immediate rescue to the consequences of employing another person. Adult responsibility should not be reduced to a more powerful version of punching or shielding.

## Attention, decision policy and regulation

He notices a reachable person, an admired act, a teammate's fear and the point at which his own body can hold a line (KE02–05). Under stress, remembered failure can initially intensify self-reproach. The mature pattern is to use that memory as an instruction to re-enter action. He can accept being the enabling part of another person's offense, as with Fat Gum, and can use a second opportunity after a first delivery route fails (KE03–04).

Decision policy: identify whom intervention serves; assess a workable route; commit to the protective role; persist or re-enter after bodily disruption; accept help or recognition without treating the relation as settled debt. Fear may delay or complicate this sequence. It is incorrect to forecast automatic success, unlimited endurance or perfect tactical creativity from courage alone.

Emotional expression is often open and energetic: admiration, thanks, frustration and shame are accessible. Direct gratitude does not prevent serious self-doubt. Stable loyalty and constructed resolve differ from a temporary excited mood. A context without an endangered person or admired model may not mobilize the same intensity; ordinary private conflict remains less well sampled than peer and field behavior.

## Body, knowledge and limits

Hardening varies in strength, duration and available recovery. Unbreakable can be broken; repeated re-hardening costs pain and effort (KE03). A simulation must specify current injury, timing and what ally action the defensive interval permits. He does not know a stranger's hidden trauma simply because he recognizes fear. His own middle-school experience can be part of his contemporary memory before the reader learns it in V16; an analyst freezing only the published V10 evidence cannot silently use the later disclosure.

`PRESERVE` fear-compatible protective commitment; `REVISE` innate fearlessness and simple debt repayment; `REJECT` invulnerability; `OPEN` adult private breadth and unsampled moral conflicts. Given adult Kirishima offered rapid agency expansion with poorly supported junior staff, a concern about responsibility and workable support is better supported than automatic prestige acceptance. Medium confidence from KE06. A sudden rescue emergency would prioritize immediate protection; a distant business decision needs information rather than an invented instinctive answer.
