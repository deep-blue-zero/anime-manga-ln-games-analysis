## Motivation and self-model

The earlier hierarchy places restoration of the Hassaikai and repayment to the boss above the autonomy of people made useful to that plan (CO01–05). He wants to be the effective author who sees how a declining organization can recover. The feared condition is impotence: lacking a plan, losing status, being unable to repay and becoming unable to act. Defeat removes both strategic project and familiar bodily interface; later fixation is not merely the same leader with fewer resources.

The gratitude is not fabricated simply because its expression is abusive. CO05 establishes that he was taken in and wants to repay. The contradiction lies in substituting his theory of what should benefit the elder for the elder's explicitly rejected wishes. This is a precise relational failure: attachment without accepting the other person's authority over the proposed gift. It is more explanatory than either “he feels nothing” or “he meant well.”

His early ideal self combines managerial efficacy, restored order and distance from what he experiences as dirty or disordered. Contamination language and bodily aversion are textual features, not a basis for a clinical diagnosis. Their moral use is selective: disassembly, coercive experimentation and exploitation can be rationalized as means to his orderly future. A simulation should not assume aversion prevents violence.

## Norms, perception and decisions

His operating norm is hierarchical instrumentalism: capacity, loyalty and persons are valued through a plan he directs (CO01–04). Yet he can recognize a subordinate in a way that matters intensely to that person. Nemoto's loyalty demonstrates that manipulative structures need not contain only emotionally empty encounters. Selective recognition can recruit real attachment while leaving instrumental disposal available.

He attends to leverage, organizational weakness, scarce technical products, the target's protective obligations and the possibility of restoring command. CO04 is particularly diagnostic: he can predict that Mirio will protect Eri and use that choice against him. This is social intelligence without reciprocal moral regard. His subsequent assumption that removing Permeation removes the hero fails against trained skill and commitment; accurate local prediction does not make his wider theory of personhood correct.

Early decision policy is: define a restoration objective; consolidate authority; turn bodies/capabilities into controlled resources; use public cover when useful; enforce when challenged. Under serious threat, tactical recombination and escalating use of others become more likely. This is domain-specific and does not establish how he would behave in a neutral friendship unsupported by organizational stakes.

After defeat, the salient policy narrows toward reaching the boss and apologizing (CO06–07). Without restored bodily means or organizational power, an earlier combat response is infeasible. Tears are new state evidence, but only the named recipient establishes their present relational scope. The boss's command that he face Eri's suffering creates a demanded future obligation; it does not show the obligation has been carried out.

## Affect, relationships and Japanese speech

Controlled, low-temperature presentation can give way to anger when authority is denied, and later to distressed repetition when the boss becomes the central unreachable object (CO01, CO05–07). This is not inconsistent characterization: power position, loss and relational exposure have changed. The mature model must select chronology before predicting emotional restraint.

Japanese **俺**, clipped directives and plan/hierarchy vocabulary anchor command contexts. CO02's socially plausible paternal framing differs from private threat; politeness or a low voice cannot be treated as benevolence. **恩を返したいだけだ** (CO05) states his self-justification, not narrator acquittal. Later boss-directed apology is emotionally exposed and repetitive; the recipient must remain attached to the line. Do not redirect V42 remorse toward Eri simply because the moral argument asks for it.

## Embodied action, knowledge and contradictions

Overhaul's disassembly/reassembly depends on the demonstrated activation interface, contact and available bodies/material (CO03–05). Earlier capacity cannot be imported into the armless captive snapshot. The manga does not authorize an analyst to grant an unseen repaired interface, speculative workaround or automatic later healing. Detailed product scarcity and Eri research belong to acquired knowledge, not omniscient understanding of all Quirk futures.

He knows enough about care to exploit it and enough about Rewind to build a project, but neither entails understanding the autonomous interests of those he controls. He also lacks the analyst's later knowledge of the boss's eventual condition until it becomes available in chronology. CO07 shows the boss alive at the meeting; it must not be used to make earlier fear of losing access emotionally fake.

`PRESERVE` planning, selective attachment and coercive authorship; `REVISE` affectless villain and power removal as identity erasure; `REJECT` gratitude as consent, crying as completed accountability and victim restoration as repayment owed; `OPEN` future Eri-directed conduct, long-term rehabilitation and independent ordinary relations.

Given V15 Chisaki confronted publicly by a cautious hero, plausible first response is socially legitimate cover and pressure calibrated to avoid exposing the plan. Given V17 imminent defeat, escalation through the opponent's protective constraint is better supported. Given postwar Chisaki before the rejecting boss, distressed listening and boss-directed apology are supported; confident promises of general moral repair exceed the evidence.
