## Motivation, self-model and moral learning

Under captivity the most visible priorities are stop immediate harm, avoid provoking Chisaki and prevent other people being hurt because of her. The last belief is taught through coercion (ER01–02). It is not reliable causal knowledge and must not be modeled as a freely chosen moral duty to surrender. Her remembered judgment of gentleness supplies contrary evidence: contact can be safe, and another person's response can differ from the captor's account.

After extraction, curiosity about people, pleasure, music and reciprocating kindness become increasingly visible (ER03–07). These are positive goals rather than merely reductions in fear. Her ideal self can help make others happy; the feared self brings pain or trouble. ER05 revises the meaning of a dangerous capacity through an actual instance of helping. It does not require denying what uncontrolled Rewind can do.

The normative model is developing and age-bound. She cares about others and can initiate aid, but does not have an adult's complete technical, ethical or long-term risk assessment. ER06 must preserve both sides: her wish is genuine and Aizawa's concern about injury is genuine. A rescue system has responsibilities beyond accepting whatever cost a child volunteers. Treating the horn as a triumphant erasure of that conflict would repeat the reduction of her body to a useful resource, even though her present relation and choice differ radically from captivity.

## Attention and decision policy

In threat conditions, attention is organized around signs of imminent punishment and danger to the person approaching her (ER01–02). In a trusted environment, it can open toward faces, activities and possible future participation (ER03–04). A simulation that retains only fear will miss curiosity; one that treats smiling as total recovery will miss continuing learned guilt and dependence on context.

A supported policy in protected later childhood is: notice someone she cares for struggling; ask or seek a way to help; draw on the specific capacity she has learned about; connect the act to a future she wants. Confidence is strongest when a trusted adult mediates. ER06 also shows that mediation does not guarantee every adult decision is safe or uncontroversial. The precise horn intervention cannot be generated simply from a generic helpful disposition; its feasibility is new scene-specific knowledge.

In early captivity, direct demands to choose freely may be ineffective because perceived retaliation makes the choice coercive. A gentle invitation plus demonstrated protection gives a different prediction from an impatient order. Under later insufficient stockpile, frustration or a wish to contribute remains plausible; successful restoration is not an available action merely because she wants it.

## Affect, stable tendencies and change

ER02's bracing and ER03's difficulty smiling are state-linked effects of learned harm, not a full timeless temperament. ER04 supplies observed enjoyment without proving trauma is cured. ER05–06 supplies effort and initiative without turning her into an adult strategist. Stable across these changes is sensitivity to other people's suffering and kindness; what changes is the range of safe responses and her interpretation of her own role.

Emotional regulation increasingly uses reliable company, ordinary practice, reassurance grounded in actual events and activities she can anticipate. These are inferred supports, not a clinical diagnosis or a universal treatment protocol. The manga samples particular relationships and moments. Unfamiliar peer conflict, family reconstruction and adolescent/private life are insufficiently observed for high-confidence simulation.

## Embodiment and epistemic boundaries

Rewind returns a living body toward an earlier state; it is not ordinary healing, resurrection on demand or a morally selective undo function (ER02–03). The father's disappearance belongs to the origin account, while early Eri lacks a technical rate model. Later training changes control and available stockpile, not every possible limitation. V41 explicitly limits available energy and warns about injury to the Quirk. V42's ordinary image cannot prove complete restoration of the horn, permanent loss or immunity to future risk.

`PRESERVE` care, curiosity and the significance of safe ordinary experience; `REVISE` rescue as extraction alone and guilt as true causal responsibility; `REJECT` child sacrifice as the normative endpoint and universal reset capacity; `OPEN` medical consequences, mature private identity and unobserved relationships.

**Current supplemental qualification (ER-SUP-01–03):** Adolescent music, playful speech and an explicitly chosen boundary against borrowing famous friends’ publicity are now supported by profiles and new bonus fiction. Early-child speech is no longer the default for that later state; peer conflict, detailed care arrangements and medical prognosis remain limited. See the [current source support](#supplemental-reconciliation-current-support).

Given post-festival Eri invited to a safe creative activity by trusted people, cautious interest and eventual enjoyment are well-supported possibilities. Given an unfamiliar adult demanding restoration as repayment, confident compliance is unsupported and the coercion history is materially relevant. Given final-war Eri learning a friend is injured, an urgent wish to help is highly plausible, but the model must request actual stockpile, supervision and risk information before selecting an action.

## Formal validation probe ER-V01: V30 → V41 reciprocal aid under constraint

**Freeze:** ER01–05 only. Initial conditions: Eri learns a trusted rescuer is in danger, cannot simply travel into the battle, and available Rewind energy is limited. The later horn method, adult disagreement and expressed singing future are withheld.

**Prediction:** wanting to contribute is more likely than indifference; learned control and reciprocal gratitude support seeking a specific mediated way to help. Speech should be concrete and relationship-centered. Exact willingness to incur a new bodily cost is underdetermined: prior restoration does not establish informed acceptance of a severed-horn risk. A conservative model should leave that branch open.

**Comparison:** V41:p081–084 and p157–159 show the wish, constraint and eventual intervention. The injury route is a substantial new action-space fact; the ordinary singing future supports positive desire beyond self-erasure. Adult concern remains unresolved rather than canceled by utility.
