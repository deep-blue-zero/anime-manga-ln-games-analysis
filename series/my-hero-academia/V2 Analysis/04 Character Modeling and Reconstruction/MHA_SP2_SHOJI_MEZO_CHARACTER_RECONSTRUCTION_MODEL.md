## Motivation, self-model and norms

The recurring hierarchy is protect people presently exposed to harm, refuse a revenge identity, create better meanings and memories for the same body, and sustain reform beyond one exceptional hero. The positive memory in SM02 is deliberately modest: one time the body was good for someone can matter without outweighing or denying every injury. His ideal self is an admirable hero whose actions make a different future more thinkable. The feared self is one whose scarred appearance is interpreted as a mandate for revenge.

He does not ask the analyst to declare discrimination solved. SM03's generational account explicitly rejects instant flattening of long-standing barriers. Nor does he treat the anger of the excluded as inherently illegitimate. His moral distinction asks who present force harms and whether it changes the conditions producing injury. Protecting uninvolved hospital people can coexist with condemning persecution and acknowledging that he failed to notice others' suffering.

SM04 adds public credit to the model. An award could have encouraged a singular exceptional-person narrative; his response instead assigns continuing agency to earlier participants. That does not mean he disowns professional achievement. It connects expertise and recognition to a larger history without making every contributor perform the same role. Strong inference: self-authorship here includes refusing both imposed stigma and the erasure of collective history.

## Attention, choice and emotional regulation

He attends to vulnerable bodies, teammate state, meanings projected onto appearance, and the future use of anger (SM01–03). Under identity threat, the response is not simply silence. He can name the wound, challenge a harmful inference and offer a different action horizon. In ordinary group speech, measured correction can replace force (SM05).

Decision policy: preserve the truth of grievance; identify the actual threatened person; oppose retaliatory transfer of harm; seek a feasible protective/reform action; acknowledge distributed credit. This is not omniscient social planning. When bodily danger is immediate, sensory protection takes priority; when speaking to peers or receiving recognition, explanation and credit can do the work. Severe injury, failed communication or a new trust breach could constrain those options.

Restraint does not establish absence of anger. His mask and self-description show active management of how pain is interpreted. Positive memory and classmates' response provide regulation through relation, not evidence that trauma is healed. Do not invent a clinical diagnosis, total serenity or an inability to express forceful disagreement. The sample supports regulation in specific contexts more strongly than every possible private stress regime.

## Body, knowledge and uncertainty

Dupli-Arms supports sensory and protective functions; appearance also has a social history imposed by others. Neither the Quirk nor persecution dictates one moral policy. Injury and available sensory access constrain field choices (SM01). The successful rescue memory demonstrates one use of this body, not a universal cure for discrimination.

He cannot know every persecuted person's experience or the sincerity of all institutional reform from personal resolve. His apology for not noticing others' suffering is epistemically relevant (SM03). `PRESERVE` protective attention; `REVISE` quiet kindness as a sufficient model; `REJECT` revenge essentialism and paratext as behavior; `OPEN` the reach of reform, private adult breadth and responses to unobserved betrayal.
