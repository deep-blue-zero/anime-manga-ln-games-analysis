## Goals, self-model, and normative policy

The governing aspiration is a world organized around him: resources, power, narratives, and people become means or possessions (AF05, AF07). Yoichi is exceptional in affective importance, but exception does not supply autonomy. His demand that the brother remain is one of the clearest examples of attachment converted into ownership. AFO can understand what someone hates or wants well enough to exploit it while failing to recognize that person as an independent source of claims.

His ideal self is the singular Demon Lord who directs the story. The feared self is someone thwarted, deprived of possession, rendered irrelevant, or moved by feelings he cannot control. V40's reaction to dysregulation makes the discrepancy explicit (AF06). The birth narrative supplies unusually early arrogance as well as extreme deprivation; it does not authorize an invented diagnosis or a claim that one factor exhaustively causes his adult choices.

Normatively, giving power can be treated as a title to command. Loss of control is experienced as theft or betrayal even when another simply leaves (AF05). The model must separate this belief from the analyst's judgment. AF07's specific engineered interventions are strong causal evidence; his leap to “everything” being his creation is a totalizing ownership claim, not automatic narrator certification. Tomura's independent League relation and resistance are material counterevidence to exhaustive authorship.

## Attention, decision policy, and affect

He notices exploitable wounds, desired objects, social dependencies, replacement routes, and opponents who permit escape from his control. He can think at institutional and multigenerational scales, yet his attention devalues ordinary differentiated helpers when they do not fit the prestige hierarchy (AF01, AF03, AF07). Knowing a person's vulnerability does not mean understanding their entire relation to others.

Default policy preserves options, engineers the environment, offers a seemingly voluntary route, and converts acceptance into later control. When that route fails, he may replace a body, change a mechanism, or intensify coercion (AF02–03). Under All Might/Yoichi/Kudo-linked threat and bodily dysregulation, immediate affect can override the long game (AF04–06). “He knows it is a trap” therefore does not entail “he will decline it.” Conversely, not every encounter will produce the same fixation; a generic opponent is a weaker trigger.

Ordinary strategic presentation is conversational, controlled, explanatory and sometimes indulgently pedagogical. Anger can be concealed or weaponized; when regulation fails, denial, repetition and frantic possession claims surface. Grief is not proof of benevolence (AF07). There is insufficient evidence to supply a relaxed reciprocal friendship or healthy family mode. High confidence is restricted to the sampled coercive/strategic relationships; ordinary hospitality, romance and harmless leisure decisions are low-confidence or unsupported.

## Body and epistemic limits

Original and duplicate factors, wounded senses, acquired sensor combinations, Rewind, internal vestige presence and external survival are distinct systems. A failure in one body does not automatically settle another's control state (AF02, AF06–07). Rewind restores capacity while accelerating disappearance and destabilizing affect; it is not a costless return to an ideal younger self. Ability possession does not guarantee integration or ownership of every factor's will.

Before simulation, specify body, factor availability, rival location, remaining time, what surveillance has actually revealed, and which personal grievance is activated. He does not know every private conversation or future act. `PRESERVE` manipulation and ownership heuristic; `REVISE` affectlessness and invulnerable foresight; `REJECT` total authorship as fact and love as moral amnesty; `OPEN` ordinary reciprocal conduct for which no broad sample exists. Keep the tier strong.

## Conditional reconstruction

For **regulated planner AFO**, a useful recruit accepts help while insisting on an independent future. Likely: appear permissive while shaping conditions that preserve dependence, test the recruit's attachments, and retain a replacement option. Medium-high confidence in this domain. An immediate opportunity involving Yoichi/OFA or a severe time limit may produce overt coercion instead. A harmless dinner conversation with a genuine equal cannot be reconstructed confidently from this model; do not fill that gap with a charming-villain stereotype.

## Formal validation probe AF-V01: V38 → V39 recognized bait

**Freeze inputs:** AF01–03 and V38's evidence of affective disruption under Rewind; exclude V39 tactics and the V40–V42 origin/attachment revelations. Initial conditions: Quirkless All Might confronts him while time is scarce and reaching the successor remains strategically important.

**Frozen prediction:** personal grievance makes engagement plausible despite the obvious delay risk; a purely optimizing planner should bypass, but the actual frozen character model cannot confidently require that. Expect dismissive speech and status defense escalating if the opponent resists. Exact equipment and resulting time loss are not predictable. Ordinary allies' welfare is unlikely to outrank the central project.

**Comparison:** V39:p150–158 and the continuing fight show the bait operating, including acknowledgement of tactics without clean disengagement. This tests a conditional attention failure; it does not prove he would attack every weak challenger or forecast the entire Rewind endpoint.
