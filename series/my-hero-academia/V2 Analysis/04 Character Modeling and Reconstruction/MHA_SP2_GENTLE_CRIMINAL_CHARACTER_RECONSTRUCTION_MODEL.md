## Motivation, ideal and feared self

The early ideal is a great and memorable person with a distinctive style (GC01–02). The feared self disappears from social memory after losing the professional dream. Takeshita's failure to remember him is powerful because it makes that fear concrete; it is not evidence that the licensing system simply failed to recognize a secretly qualified hero. The earlier intervention caused genuine harm through inadequate coordination and judgment.

La Brava changes the motivational hierarchy because his dream becomes something another person has invested in (GC01, GC03). This can initially intensify persistence in a harmful project. Mutual affection is real but does not certify the U.A. intrusion as good. At defeat, however, her future becomes more important than completing that project. This is a decision-policy change with observable cost, not only a sympathetic origin disclosure.

In GC04–05, being useful and experiencing a life worth living can sustain action under the belief that no one will record it. The theatrical self persists. Development does not require becoming a person with no desire for recognition or grandeur; it changes whether recognition is a prerequisite for the act. Later accurate public acknowledgment and reintegration are compatible with that distinction.

## Normative model and attention

He wants conduct to have style and moral significance, but early self-narration can rationalize disruption as beneficial training for others (GC01). The narrative tests that claim against the school's fragile festival and other people's stakes. His own bond cannot consume their ordinary future. GC03 shows recognition of an opponent's relational burden without requiring either side to become morally identical.

Attention combines audience/history, operational staging, trajectory and La Brava's response. Under threat to the shared dream, these can narrow into persistence; under threat to her future, the objective can shift toward surrender. Under later rescue conditions, the body/environment problem becomes central (GC04–05). He still notices whether anyone can see, but the answer no longer controls whether he continues.

A supported later policy is: identify an attainable rescue contribution; apply trained Elasticity and available support; own personal limits; protect nearby people even if they frustrate the scene's desired presentation. GC05's warning to filming students is small but diagnostic. Even while they make him visible, their safety remains salient. Their refusal introduces another actor's agency, not proof he intended them to take the risk.

## Emotional regulation and temporal change

Performance, cultivated formality and humor can organize self-worth and manage shame (GC01–03). Exposure of failed history produces a different emotional temperature from a prepared video persona. Love and gratitude can strengthen resolve; they cannot erase the need for competence. Under U.A.'s load he explicitly places the limit in himself rather than in La Brava's love (GC05), preserving both physical reality and the relationship.

Joy during unseen aid is a meaningful new state, but it should not be generalized into permanent serenity. GC06 supplies social reintegration rather than a broad sample of ordinary conflicts. Long-term professional routines, friendship outside La Brava, family reconciliation and private responses to renewed failure remain underdetermined. Strong readiness supports the sampled recognition/responsibility axis, not every adult life domain.

## Embodied action and epistemic limits

Elasticity modifies touched material, including air, to shape trajectories and support loads (V19:p153–156; GC04–05). It does not supply arbitrary invulnerability or unlimited force. La Brava's Love is her own relationship-conditioned contribution, not an owned switch he can demand. GC05 explicitly preserves his capacity limit under that support. Later technical reintegration does not demonstrate a new unbounded Quirk mechanism.

Gentle does not know the students are filming until the scene reveals them. A correct simulation must use his belief state when explaining prior action. Nor can he know every consequence of an unauthorized rescue merely because his intention is good; GC02 is a counterexample to intention as competence. `PRESERVE` theatrical identity and reciprocal attachment; `REVISE` recognition as necessary motive; `REJECT` pure victimization, romance as moral certification and hero impulse as sufficient expertise; `OPEN` later private breadth and detailed institutional terms of rehabilitation.

Given rehabilitating Gentle offered a useful role without publicity, action is plausible when the task is feasible and carries a future-oriented relationship to others. Given a spectacular but harmful opportunity that again endangers La Brava or an ordinary public event, the earlier surrender supplies a reason to refuse; confidence is moderate because the manga does not repeat every temptation. Given a load exceeding his capacity, expect acknowledgment and urgent coordination rather than a claim that love guarantees success.
