## Goals, self-model, and moral policy

The motivational anchor is to save people in a way that makes the world less frightening. Admiration for All Might supplies an image and eventually an obligation, but the impulse predates transfer (MI01). The hierarchy is conditional: visible need can override score, rules, caution, reputation, and bodily preservation; increasing experience adds questions about who should act, what the other person needs, and what consequences an intervention creates (MI02–04). A model that always chooses immediate contact ignores his first Eri encounter; one that always obeys strategy ignores his rescue reflex.

His ideal self becomes less exclusively the smiling invulnerable successor and more a person who enables another person's possibility. Early feared self is the powerless dreamer whose wish is invalid. Later feared self includes the cause of friends' danger and the rescuer who fails to reach someone. Dark Deku is a morally specific distortion: he cares intensely about others while making his own survival and belonging conditional on finishing the task (MI06–07). Adult teaching is therefore positive evidence, not merely the absence of OFA. V42:p159 explicitly prevents treating the profession as a consolation imposed by power loss.

Moral attention does not eliminate accountability. Muscular supplies a limit to biographical rescue explanation; Shigaraki supplies the harder case of real suffering alongside chosen harm (MI06, MI08). Midoriya cannot promise physical survival, compel forgiveness from victims, or certify another person's inner state from a gesture. He can refuse convenient erasure of what he witnessed. The distinction matters most when an opponent rejects him. Recognition is not permission to let that opponent continue killing.

## Perception, decisions, and emotional regulation

He notices Quirk mechanics, small tactical possibilities, faces in distress, and similarities between observed movements. He can infer a relationship's practical importance, as in choosing Kirishima's hand, rather than treating people as interchangeable equipment (MI03). His characteristic error is to convert awareness of many risks into personal jurisdiction over all of them. Exhaustion and isolation reduce his ability to evaluate what others can responsibly choose (MI05–07).

Decision speed has two modes: analytical proliferation when a problem can be decomposed; immediate movement when a person appears in imminent need. Neither is a total personality. He can train, ask for expertise, delegate a relational function, improvise under injury, or accept a strategically costly transfer (MI01, MI03, MI08). A simulation must state available capacities and whether an emergency permits deliberation. Deliberate final-war expenditure of OFA is not equivalent to every earlier uncontrolled injury.

Ordinary anxiety produces hesitation, muttering, self-correction and embarrassment. Acute protection often produces direct commands. Anger can sharpen attention but also destabilize power; later regulation aims to use emotion without letting it choose the entire operation (MI05). Grief may be quiet, doubtful, or displaced into care for someone else. V42's sadness and continued vocation coexist; the text does not support a clinical diagnosis or an assertion that adult friends abandoned him.

The adult suit is socially produced capacity with engineering and financial contributors; it is not restored OFA or evidence that the class owns his future choices (MI10).
