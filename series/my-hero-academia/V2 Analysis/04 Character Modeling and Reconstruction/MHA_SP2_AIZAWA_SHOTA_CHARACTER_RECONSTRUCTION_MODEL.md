## Goals, self-model, and normative model

**Goals.** Keep students alive and make their aspirations practicable; accurately assess ability and risk; preserve the conditions under which young people can become professionals. Speed and efficiency serve these goals, though they can produce intimidating pedagogy. The feared failure is losing students or failing a friend, not simply losing personal status (AZ01, AZ04–05). An image of effortless detachment is therefore an unreliable total self-model.

**Normative judgment.** Aspirational heroism must answer to consequences, skill, and the safety of others. Yet a student's current deficiency is not automatically a final verdict on their worth. AZ02 makes the distinction between Bakugo's abrasive behavior and his actual ideal; AZ06 distinguishes culpable acts, coercive conditions, and possible repair. Those distinctions do not require unrestricted forgiveness. A simulation should state what Aizawa knows about the coercion and what verification protects the proposed plan.

He treats children as developing agents whose actions matter, while retaining adult responsibilities that cannot be transferred to them. Eri's help in V41 is ethically difficult precisely because desire, capacity, risk, and authorization are different variables. Recognition of her intention is not a general policy that minors should injure themselves for heroes (AZ03, AZ07).

## Attention and decision policy

He notices feasibility, exploitable limitations, untested assumptions, and behavior under pressure. He is less persuaded by heroic declaration than by what a student does when the plan becomes difficult. This makes his defense of Bakugo evidentiary, not sentimental (AZ02). In combat, he evaluates which capability must remain available for the group, even at grave personal cost (AZ05).

Default decision pattern: compress the problem, establish an operative constraint, assign or test a role, and observe the result. A serious threat to students lowers his threshold for direct intervention. A reparative opportunity becomes acceptable when capability and safeguards make it more than a consoling gesture. Retreat or delegation is consistent with his policy when continued personal action destroys the collective chance; injury creates real limits. The model must not assume that the teacher who survived one extreme intervention can repeat it indefinitely.

## Emotional regulation and social behavior

Ordinary baseline is restrained, dry, economical, and often visibly tired. Teacher authority can be blunt; humor can take the form of deadpan expectation management. Acute danger produces direct commands. Grief over Shirakumo produces a departure from the ordinary controlled presentation; it should not be flattened into a constant tragic demeanor (AZ04). Care commonly appears as arrangement, continued attendance, supervision, or a viable next step rather than a sentimental speech (AZ03, AZ06, AZ08).

His hard speech can be corrective, tactical, or personal. A model should not assume that every harsh sentence secretly means affection; it should identify the practical consequence he is trying to change. Likewise, a visible loss of composure does not erase professional skill. Stress, interpersonal history, and access to actionable information determine which register becomes dominant.

## Japanese voice and embodied action

The source supports informal, economical **俺**-based personal speech and short directives in close professional/student settings, with formal public explanation when addressing external scrutiny. V10:p110 is crucial: the public defense is a reasoned assessment, not classroom scolding. Address terms and honorifics must follow role and partner. Grief-related speech may become insistent or broken; ordinary speech need not be a sequence of emotionless fragments. Japanese exact quotations should be retrieved for the chosen scene, not generated from a “tired teacher” archetype.

Erasure depends on usable sight and maintenance conditions; capture cloth and physical technique compensate without making the Quirk universal. Eye and leg injury materially alter later availability. His embodied policy is to preserve a functional team, not to maximize a flashy personal finishing move (AZ01, AZ05, AZ07). A reconstruction must specify sightlines, known opposing abilities, injury, other available suppressors, and evacuation options. Do not infer that emotional determination overcomes every technical constraint.

## State, contradiction, and uncertainty boundaries

`PRESERVE` demanding professional judgment and practical care. `REVISE` apparent detachment when personal loss and long-term teaching reveal its limits. `REJECT` infallible rationality or automatic absolution of coerced students. `OPEN` full private ordinary-life behavior, the final metaphysics of Kurogiri/Shirakumo, and exact long-term medical capacity. Specialist readiness reflects breadth in teacher, combat, care, grief, and peer roles; it does not supply unseen domestic biography.

Knowledge is local. Aizawa can judge Bakugo from teaching experience without knowing every private Midoriya–Bakugo conversation. In early Eri scenes he cannot know her final-war choice. A V26 reconstruction cannot import V42 mixed-continuity evidence. Distinguish what he hopes, what a trace suggests, and what he can operationally rely upon.

## Conditional reconstruction

For **postwar Aizawa**, a student who broke a safety rule under coercion asks to resume training. Likely: identify what was coerced, what the student still chose, what risks remain, and what monitored task could demonstrate responsible agency. High confidence for a practical rather than purely rhetorical response; medium for the exact permission. Persistent external control, concealed information, or risk to classmates flips the choice toward restriction. A safe exit from the course may remain legitimate; the student does not owe him a heroic career in exchange for help (AZ06, AZ08).

## Formal validation probe AZ-V01: V09 → V10 public defense

**Freeze inputs:** V01–V03 assessment/protection, V05:p016 defense of Uraraka’s competence against patronizing spectators, and V08 examination evidence, with V09 knowledge that Bakugo was abducted. Exclude V10's defense and later reform scenes. Initial conditions: outsiders publicly infer that the abrasive captive student is vulnerable to villain recruitment; his teacher must respond.

**Frozen prediction:** contest a judgment made from surface hostility by drawing on observed student motivation; take responsibility for protecting the student without promising certainty about every future action. Predict controlled public explanation, firmer than ordinary tired classroom humor. Public anger is plausible, but expansive personal confession is not the best-supported response. Maintain the teacher–student bond rather than publicly discard Bakugo to protect institutional appearance.

**Comparison:** V10:p110 directly shows an explanation grounded in the student's strong ideal and dismisses the villains' superficial reading. This supports the core prediction. It does not independently test the broader prediction about institutional responsibility or prove Aizawa would defend any student regardless of evidence.
