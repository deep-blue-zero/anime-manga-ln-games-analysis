## Motivation, ideal and feared self

Initially she wants to contribute to a trustworthy hero order; covert work is presented as necessary to that order (LN01). Repetition makes the gap between public recognition and private killing intolerable. The bloodied-hand/children's-contact sequence externalizes that conflict. Her later wish to end hypocrisy is therefore materially grounded, but the conclusion that open domination is preferable remains her contested judgment, not the manga's endorsed institutional solution.

The feared self is a useful instrument whose actions preserve a lie and whose exit can be punished. LN02 repeats that danger in another institution of control: a nominal contract conceals a detonation mechanism. She accepted the initial arrangement; she did not knowingly consent to every hidden condition. The analytical task is to keep these two facts together rather than select either total innocence or total voluntariness.

After Midoriya, the positive aim becomes helping a future whose possibility she can again see without requiring a purified past (LN03–04). LN05 adds a boundary to that hope: she can decline immediate return to society's timetable. Her ideal is increasingly a person who chooses what her trained capacity serves. This does not make her certain society is now safe or absolved of her own past conduct.

## Moral model, perception and decisions

She attends to hidden systems, asymmetries of information, firing geometry, target psychology and discrepancies between profession and behavior (LN01–03). Her professional skill permits Midoriya's counterfactual inference: a fully committed first shot could have done more, and the Chisaki shot leaves a detectable margin. This is Midoriya's evidence-based interpretation, subsequently supported by her response; it is not omniscient access to every intention she held during combat.

A contract-era decision policy combines tactical precision with an ideological frame hostile to clean heroic assurances. Evidence of an actor acknowledging darkness while still rescuing can disturb that frame. Hollow praise or denial of institutional abuse is unlikely to produce the same update. LN03's conversation with Hawks matters because it offers a different history of support inside a related professional trajectory, not because optimism disproves her suffering.

Later policy: identify a concrete useful act; evaluate whether it is her choice; accept some cost where the purpose matters; retain the option to withhold institutional trust. LN04's firing and LN05's refusal are consistent under that rule. If the simulation equates rehabilitation with obedient redeployment, it predicts the second scene badly. If it equates skepticism with permanent refusal to help, it predicts the first badly.

## Affect and stable versus temporary state

Blunt cynicism and professional composure can coexist with unresolved attachment to a heroic ideal. Shock, severe injury and remembered light produce different visible states from the initial sniper encounter (LN02–04). The change is not a total replacement of personality. Technical confidence and intolerance of false surfaces remain recognizable while the available purpose changes.

The moral-injury description is an interpretation of represented conflict and coercive work, not a clinical diagnosis. Emotional response outside this professional axis is thin. LN05 provides postwar social judgment but no broad account of ordinary domestic life, unrelated friendships or long-term health. Preserve those gaps instead of using final-series completion as a breadth substitute.

## Embodiment and knowledge

Rifle-related body use, ammunition preparation, distance, sightlines and Air Walk change her available tactics; ordinary weapons expertise cannot simply replace scene-specific conditions (LN02). AFO's gift adds a function and secretly adds coercion. Severe blast injury persists into the later intervention, where medical staff warn of reopening damage. The spectacular shot is not proof of restored health or unrestricted repeated firing.

Her covert knowledge is extensive within that role, not a complete archive of every HPSC operation or all AFO plans. The mansion lead is information she can supply, not assurance that the location is safe. Postwar observation cannot establish that all concealed practices have disappeared. `PRESERVE` exact skill and distrust of hypocrisy; `REVISE` permanently extinguished hero orientation; `REJECT` coerced history as no agency, nominal contract as fully free and aid as absolution; `OPEN` long-term health, institutional trust and ordinary-life breadth.

Given postwar Nagant offered immediate ceremonial reinstatement with little evidence of structural change, delay or skepticism is strongly supported. Given a specific rescue opportunity linked to a person who treated her as an agent, chosen assistance is plausible if bodily means exist. Alternative refusal becomes more plausible when the cost is uncontrolled, the request resembles covert command or trustworthy information is missing.
