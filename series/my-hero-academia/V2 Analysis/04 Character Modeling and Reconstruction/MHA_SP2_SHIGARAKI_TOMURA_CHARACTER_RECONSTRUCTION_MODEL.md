## Motivational hierarchy and self-model

Childhood hero desire and adult destructive desire are not interchangeable hidden essences. The child seeks inclusion and a legitimate aspirational self; the groomed young man receives an account in which negative affect licenses destruction and ethical restraints are delegitimized (SG03). The adult also learns, leads, chooses capabilities, and forms particular obligations (SG02–04). The model must preserve both conditioning and the actions that emerge beyond the trainer's preferred jurisdiction.

The mature stated horizon is destruction of an order experienced as rejecting him and the people aligned with him. V41's obligation to be a hero for villains/outcasts is selective relation, not universal rescue (SG07). It remains meaningful even if hatred is emptied; thus affect is not the only variable keeping the commitment in place. Feared self includes being a puppet, being told his personhood is only a symptom or vessel, and failing the people for whom his leadership matters. These fears can coexist with harm to strangers.

Self-model is contested by other speakers. AFO claims total authorship; Midoriya seeks the child who cried; Re-Destro supplies liberation language; Spinner remembers a first friend and hero. None alone is a neutral complete account. Terminal Tomura uses his adult name and preserves a Spinner-directed relation (SG08). Do not replace him with a generic repentant child because that would make the rescue story easier.

## Normative judgment, attention, and decision policy

His norms are often radically exclusionary toward the wider society while allowing comrades' distinct desires. Anti-domination is strongest when his own authorship is threatened; it does not automatically become a principle protecting everyone else's autonomy (SG04, SG06–07). A model should not call a chosen destruction policy wholly imposed merely because the conditions were engineered. Conversely, choosing augmentation is not consent to total mind/body takeover.

Attention develops from irritating targets and game-like challenge toward organizational means, opponent capabilities, and the affective causes of collective action (SG01–04). He can identify Erasure as the functional brake and change tactics. AFO intrusion, memory return and bodily stress create different limits on this cognition (SG05). Analyst knowledge of the entire origin must never become contemporary self-knowledge before disclosure.

Decision policy depends first on control state, then on the current goal and relation. Under his own control he may cooperate instrumentally, learn from defeat, retain allies, or refuse a materially useful command that attempts to subordinate him. During direct override, apparent behavior belongs partly or wholly to AFO's active policy. In the inner conflict, symbolic bodily images need contextual attribution. This state-based approach is more predictive than assigning one probability to “Shigaraki will attack.”

## Emotion, relationships, and Japanese voice

Early frustration is readily expressed through irritation, scratching, threats and game vocabulary. Memory return gives affect autobiographical context; liberation changes its bodily presentation without proving that pain has healed (SG03–04). Rage can coexist with strategy, and late moments can include restraint or a slight smile without implying stable contentment. Ordinary noncombat evidence is concentrated in League relation and childhood; there is no adult rehabilitated civilian sample.

| Partner | Conditional difference |
|---|---|
| AFO | Early **先生** relation and dependence; later explicit rejection. A familiar title need not mean continuing obedience. |
| League / Spinner | Particular obligation and remembered ordinary connection. Tactical resources do not exhaust the meaning of comrades. |
| Midoriya | Early target/interlocutor; later contested recognition. Final message entrustment is local cooperation, not adoption of Midoriya's whole moral program. |
| Kotaro / family | Childhood attachment, invalidation, panic and distinct murderous intent. Later causal disclosure changes the analyst's explanation, not every original intention. |
| Nana | Family history is weaponized by AFO; terminal credit for her action is a new relation under exceptional conditions, not a fully lived grandparent bond. |

Japanese childhood **僕** and adult **俺**, rough direct address, compressed irritation, gaming terms in earlier discourse, and **先生** with changing pragmatic force belong to different snapshots. V41's villain-hero formulation expresses particular obligation; V42's adult self-naming and Spinner message retain that specificity. Exact speaker attribution is mandatory in mixed scenes: AFO's **僕** cannot simply be folded into a Tomura pronoun repertoire. Voice predictions should state whose agency is active before generating dialogue.

## Embodiment, epistemic limits, and contradiction handling

Decay changes in activation, propagation and scale; body augmentation permits actions under Erasure that active-Quirk suppression alone cannot stop (SG03–04). Later growth/fusion, factor possession, stolen Danger Sense and the availability of an internal voice are separate variables. The V41 disclosure specifies an engineered destruction-only copy, but the model cannot invent Tenko's removed original Quirk or a hypothetical restorative ability he never possessed.

`REVISE` a spontaneous-origin account with explicit engineering; `PRESERVE` family/social failures that were exploited and later relational development; `REJECT` both total AFO authorship and innocence-by-child-image; `OPEN` the exact causal weight of each formative factor and the life he might have chosen after survival. Physical death prevents validating rehabilitation. Recognition of the heart is an interpretation supported by encounter and later testimony, not access to an exhaustive terminal mental record.

## Conditional reconstruction

For **V40 Tomura under his own control**, AFO offers decisive power in exchange for abandoning a League-specific objective and accepting command. Likely: reject the ownership condition, possibly take or use available capability on his own terms, and preserve his destructive goal. High confidence for resisting active subordination; medium for the exact tactic. Actual override, severe bodily collapse, or new information can change the operative agent. A childhood Tenko or V30 overridden-body reconstruction requires a different model state, not merely a milder tone.

## Formal validation probe SG-V01: V41 → V42 separability and relation

**Freeze inputs:** SG03–07 only through V41. Initial conditions: AFO has returned internally after Tenko's destabilization; final battle continues. The archived question of whether Tomura can reappear is unresolved. The probe supplies no answer to control recovery or survival.

**Frozen prediction:** if distinct Tomura agency returns, resistance to AFO and a League-specific self-definition are better supported than generic penitence. A surviving separable person is possible, but not guaranteed. Exact action, Nana's role, and a Spinner message are unpredicted. If able to speak, adult directness and retained identity are plausible; a purely child-only voice is not the sole licensed outcome.

**Comparison:** V42:p017–020 establishes the condition: Tomura reappears distinctly, credits Nana, recognizes separation from the teacher's control, and sends the message under his adult name. This supports the conditional behavioral prediction. It does not count the fact of reappearance, Nana's intervention, or death as forecast successes.
