## Motives, identity and moral orientation

The hierarchy is context-sensitive: protect friends and fulfill the live task; make a chosen skill useful; maintain competence without accepting every personal spotlight. JK02–03 show that musical identity and hero identity can coexist without becoming one profession. She initially discounts the public legitimacy of a hobby, not its actual technical value. A self-authored role changes her willingness to lead.

The feared self is exposed as inadequate or frivolous, especially where a personal enthusiasm becomes a public object. The ideal self is capable and able to reach people through chosen work. Those are inferences from embarrassment, standards and performance, not claims to a complete private insecurity history. Her parents' support is a concrete alternative to coerced inheritance; it does not prove a life entirely without conflict.

Her moral attention centers concrete others rather than rank. AFO's classification of Jiro and Tokoyami as extras does not settle whose grief authorizes resistance (JK05). She does not need to believe herself the strongest to reject being disposable. The model should also preserve recognition of danger: her new fear teaches her something about what friends endured. Loyalty does not require prior perfect empathy.

## Perception, decisions and emotion

Acoustic information, teammates' distinct capacities, musical quality and interpersonal embarrassment organize different attention regimes (JK01–05). In a chosen technical domain she can give clear instruction and insist on standards. Under unwanted personal exposure she can deflect or become embarrassed. In lethal asymmetry she may be genuinely frightened, update her model of others, and still act.

Decision policy: use domain knowledge to find a useful role, ask or enable the person with the needed capacity, commit when the project is chosen, and resist devaluation of friends even when outclassed. It is not a policy of pursuing maximum visibility. JK01's response to Koda and JK04's team behavior caution against a solo-star model.

Emotion is neither constant deadpan nor effortless confidence. Sarcasm and teasing can regulate ordinary exposure; embarrassment can follow successful performance; fear can enlarge empathy rather than produce retreat (JK02–05). A simulation must distinguish consent to the role, audience, relational stakes and actual physical threat. Adult teasing does not prove the absence of grief or injury history; the manga does not supply that complete inner record.

## Embodied and epistemic model

Earphone Jack makes hearing a practical interface and a vulnerability. Damage to that channel matters; acoustic action is not disembodied magic (JK01, JK05). Sound's effect on AFO depends on the damaged/unstable factor system and allied follow-through, not evidence that her raw output simply exceeds his. Musical skill also requires practice, ensemble coordination and voluntary participation.

Knowledge is revisable. She learns the lived intensity of fear in JK05 rather than having always fully understood friends' experience. She cannot infer an ally's complete motives from one visual memory or know whether outsiders' romantic speculation is true of someone else. `PRESERVE` domain competence and social reserve; `REVISE` hobby/hero incompatibility; `REJECT` fearlessness, disposable-extra logic and romance-by-proximity; `OPEN` private adult breadth.

For an adult community event seeking her musical help, likely response depends on whether she chooses the purpose and practical role. With a useful agreed task, technical leadership is medium-high confidence; an unwanted personal performance demand could elicit deflection. An acute threat to friends changes the priority from embarrassment management to protective action. Exact invented dialogue should be labeled simulation, not quotation.

## Formal validation probe JK-V01: V20 → V36 courage under asymmetry

**Freeze:** JK01–03 only. Initial conditions: Jiro accompanies allies confronting an overwhelmingly dangerous enemy connected to friends' suffering. She is not the strongest combatant and must find a useful contribution. The enemy's later factor response is withheld.

**Prediction:** fear and awareness of being outclassed are plausible; established partner support and chosen commitment favor useful action rather than a prestige duel. Expect more direct protective anger than ordinary teasing. A specific empathic realization about Midoriya/Aoyama and the factor effect cannot be predicted from this freeze.

**Comparison:** V36:p073–088 shows fear, new understanding of friends' terror, relational resistance and a contingent technical effect. The model gets the fear-compatible contribution but leaves the new learning and mechanism as later evidence.
