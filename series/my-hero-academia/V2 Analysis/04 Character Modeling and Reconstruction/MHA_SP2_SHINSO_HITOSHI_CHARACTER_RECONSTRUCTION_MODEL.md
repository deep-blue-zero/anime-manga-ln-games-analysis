## Goals, self-model, and moral boundaries

His recurring priorities are legitimate hero participation, demonstrable competence, and using an ability socially coded as dangerous for useful ends (SI01–04). He wants to be judged by trained performance rather than presumed moral destiny. Early resentment toward the apparently fortunate Midoriya is real evidence, but its competitive delivery also seeks the response Brainwashing needs. Do not turn every provocation into his ordinary opinion of the target.

The feared self is excluded or recognized only as an inherently suspect instrument. The ideal self is a capable hero who earns access. Specific training and evaluation matter more than unconditional reassurance: SI02–03 show correction taken seriously and incomplete development admitted. He can want to help while still having attention captured by proving himself. That contradiction is developmental rather than grounds for calling either motive fraudulent.

His practical morality is not “coercion becomes harmless when I use it.” SI04 supplies a justified operational context, not an unrestricted license to control friends. SI05 makes the limit precise: controlling behavior does not author a person's heart. A simulation must retain consent/command conditions and the effect on the controlled person. Neither fear-based exclusion nor hero status settles the ethical question alone.

## Attention, decision policy, and emotional regulation

He notices conversational openings, voice identity, opponents' assumptions, his own training deficit, and routes around institutional mismatch. In early competition, that attention can narrow onto outperforming others (SI01, SI03). Later coordinated work puts the same precision into a distributed plan (SI04–05). His policy is to create a response condition, use available control within its limits, and integrate equipment and learned movement when the ability alone is insufficient.

Defeat can produce frustration without withdrawal from the larger objective. Success can provoke self-critique rather than self-certification. Aizawa's specific evaluation helps translate dissatisfaction into development; an unsupported “you are already perfect” response is a poor match (SI02–03). Under high stakes he is capable of firm assertion about what his power did and did not cause. Anxiety, competitive pride and service are compatible, not mutually exclusive modes.

Stable tendencies are guarded directness, technical adaptation and concern with earned legitimacy. The early applicant's grievance intensity is state-dependent. No evidence warrants a globally cynical adult, a universal distrust of classmates, or frictionless confidence after admission. Ordinary leisure and family conflict predictions should remain low confidence.

## Body, knowledge, and changing predictions

Brainwashing depends on a response; training expands the command repertoire, and equipment changes the available vocal interface (SI01–04). Physical movement and capture training remain necessary. Do not grant telepathy, memory access, omnipotent persuasion or motive rewriting. What he knows of a target must come from observation, briefing or supplied recordings, not the fact of control. If a responder is unavailable, an ally is endangered by the tactic, or the opponent understands the trigger, the preferred plan changes.

`PRESERVE` earned-access drive and tactical speech; `REVISE` the inference that early envy exhausts his motive; `REJECT` villainous essence and complete mental ownership; `OPEN` private adult breadth. In a hypothetical late-school exercise, an ally praises him for making an enemy genuinely loyal. Best-supported response: correct the causal claim, explain the controllable action, and continue monitoring the enemy's independent intent. High confidence in the distinction, medium in exact tone; early Shinso lacks the later Machia example and advanced repertoire.

## Formal validation probe SI-V01: V35 → V38 control and independent motive

**Freeze:** SI01–04 only. Initial conditions: an operation uses a familiar recorded voice to obtain a giant target's response; after external control ends, that target continues attacking its former master. The target's private reasons are not supplied.

**Prediction:** Shinso should distinguish his actionable mechanism from the target's subsequent intent, and should not treat continued action as proof of permanent authored loyalty. Expect practical clarification and attention to whether control still operates. Motive is accurate professional contribution, not possession of the target. Exact anger content and successful battle outcome remain unknown.

**Comparison:** V38:p155–156 supplies Machia's own grievance and Shinso's explicit limit on manipulating the heart, directly rechecked in Japanese. The freeze supported limited control; it did not predict this particular grievance or guarantee that control would break.
