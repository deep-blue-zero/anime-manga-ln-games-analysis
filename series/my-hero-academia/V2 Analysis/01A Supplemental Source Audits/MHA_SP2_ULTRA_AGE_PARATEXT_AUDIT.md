## Ordinary futures become specific without becoming universally predictable

Several adult profiles give recurring practices rather than birthdays alone. Midoriya visits children's facilities and combines teaching with later armored frontline activity (s0018). Shoto continues attending a children's festival, building ice castles and slides (s0034). Ochako sends money to parents who leave it untouched; their reason is not provided (s0040). Asui places her agency near family and frequently teaches at U.A. (s0046). Mina's dance appearances, Momo's research/international work, Tokoyami's examination assistance, Kaminari's disaster power supply and Jiro's music/Eri mentorship extend the available picture of working and ordinary life (s0050–0061).

These additions strengthen an already present distinction among imposed function, chosen contribution and enjoyment. They do not discover ordinary care for the first time in the corpus. Nor is pleasure devalued merely because it helps somebody: music can be a chosen practice, shared pleasure, instruction and public work. The useful analytical question is who chooses it, how another person receives it and whether their value is made conditional on usefulness. A recurring editorial assertion improves the available domain description but is not a set of independently observed reactions at every event.

Relations also become more discriminating. Iida inherits the agency while Tensei returns with support equipment as a sidekick called Big Inge (s0043). Aoyama's later licensing and Hagakure's employment of him specify continued inclusion after accountability (s0062/0064). Koda's choice to work with Shoji has meaning precisely because other people encourage independence (s0068). Class B careers, social gatherings and differently reciprocated feelings show why additional figures deserve review individually. A known job, engagement or confession alone does not supply the behavior needed to model every future conflict.

The guide's account of public recognition also has identifiable limits. A report that a villain could not bring himself to hit Chargebolt supplies one narrated-to-the-reader response, not a rule that all criminals will stand down. Dance popularity or a sold-out calendar does not measure ethical adequacy. A lower chart position caused partly by abrasive speech does not cancel Bakugo's competence or his sustained material care, while favorable team reception does not erase his earlier bullying.

## Bodily change requires new states, not retrospective cures

Support and injury must remain materially present. Tensei's return depends on equipment; Jiro's left ear is repaired but explicitly not restored to its original state. All Might's renewed walking, Endeavor's cane use, and Edgeshot's treatment/anticipated return are different trajectories, not one generalized recovery. Declining a recommended operation does not, without an attributed reason, establish self-punishment. Continued professional action does not establish absence of pain or complete restoration of previous capacity.

Costume diagrams add useful distinctions. Kirishima's covers protect someone he carries from his own hardened body (s0048). Asui's equipment extends dark vision rather than proving unrestricted unaided sight (s0046). Mineta's mask conceals overuse bleeding from an opponent; it does not prevent that bleeding (s0056). Momo's growing reference binder keeps accumulated knowledge and an external aid visible, while Jiro's equipment revisions respond to maneuverability (s0052/0060). These are affordances and intentions, not fresh performance trials.

The older runaway state of Dark Shadow cannot remain a timeless rule after the guide explicitly records training-based control in darkness (s0055). Equally, acquired control does not establish unlimited mastery under all later conditions. The manga's collective final attacks still depend on allies, light and opponent state. Reproducing them in a profile and a catalogue does not multiply their independent support.

## Heroic growth and harmful conduct remain separately testable

Mineta's section decisively rules out a clean rehabilitation inference. His tactical exam pass, concern for Midnight, sincere attachment to Midoriya, defense of Tokoyami and courage under injury/fear are real positive evidence. The later profile nevertheless describes criticism after his DMs to female fans became known (s0056). It provides no message contents, recipient ages or full consent context; the analysis must not invent them.

The following page retains sexual/popularity preoccupation and Iida's explicit condemnation, while rhetorically asking whether a supposedly hard-to-hate quality is Mineta's humanity (s0057). This editorial softening is itself an object of interpretation. It is not a victim's forgiveness, proof that all readers respond favorably, or evidence of changed boundaries. Even the exam line postpones gratification rather than renouncing the conduct. Competence, loyalty, fear, desire and harm coexist. The required chain of acknowledged wrongdoing, revised boundaries and changed conduct remains unestablished by these pages.

The same care applies to favorable summaries elsewhere. Shoto's technical learning from Endeavor expressly coexists with refusal to forgive (s0036–0037). Family cooperation during catastrophe does not settle Natsuo's nonforgiveness. Chisaki's meeting with the boss is not interchangeable with Eri-directed restitution. The later guide can describe a reunion as reconciliation while the original recipient-specific demand and unshown future compliance remain analytically decisive.

## Inheritance acquires background but not universal foreknowledge

The predecessor histories at s0026–0027 substantially expand the account of OFA succession. Names, failed resistance, dangerous transfers, flight and uncertain understanding make the chain more particular. The third holder's research and long perspective, Hikage's acceptance and isolation, Banjo's public inspiration, and En's relation to his admired predecessor distinguish ways of receiving a burden. They must not be flattened into eight people sharing one fully informed mission from the start.

En learns OFA's secret during Banjo's fatal encounter; his own later resistance has collaborators, including Torino and Nana's husband. Nana's knowledge changes after her husband's death and through En's transfer/last action. All Might's family losses and subsequent safety-driven departure give background to his account of apparent peace. These are new official expositions of earlier lives, with the stated boundaries on who knew what. Their admission does not give young successors reader knowledge of later vestiges, all transferred abilities or a guaranteed successful successor.

More detailed backstory may strengthen literary comparison while leaving dramatic compression intact. A paragraph about a resistance network does not make the main manga show every relationship, loss or deliberation. The completed reconciliation must state separately what is now known about the character and what the serialized narrative actually developed for its audience.

## Manipulation does not erase every received attachment or later choice

The book often condenses villain histories forcefully. Shigaraki's dependence on AFO, learned modes of thought and engineered opportunities require attention, but they cannot automatically extinguish the reality of every later bond. The childhood-friend account explicitly distinguishes AFO's initiating prompt from the boys' sincere attraction to Tenko (s0100). AFO's control during an operation and Shigaraki's own resistance, regained agency or final message also belong to different states.

Machia's order-dependent profile must be read with his later decision to rebel after abandonment, and the guide explicitly denies that he was a Nomu (s0190–0191). Kurogiri's final protective action is left open as Shirakumo's will, Kurogiri's, or both (s0185). The responsible model preserves that ambiguity instead of converting recognition into complete restoration of a previous person.

AFO's pages expose a different interpretive pressure. One unsigned developmental passage asserts that emotions did not form and tentatively links this to innate power (s0171). Another frames his final attachment as distorted love while preserving possession, hatred, demands and competing judgments (s0173). This is insufficient for a timeless no-emotion rule or an invented clinical explanation. Earlier developmental wording and later affective vocabulary must both survive; different reference times mean that a tension is not automatically a formally demonstrated all-life contradiction. His name, Shigaraki Zen, is reader information and not proof that every prior participant knew it.

## Institutional continuation is uneven and source-specific

Professional profiles make supporting work and later arrangements more visible. Manual receives attributed public credit from Monoma; Lady Nagant moves beyond her earlier voluntary custody, enjoys hobbies and sometimes advises Hawks; Gentle's work with La Brava's company gives a later vocational relationship. Spinner's account is published and receives major criticism alongside some recognition of its historical value. These developments refine the endpoint without certifying universal forgiveness, successful treatment or durable reform in every institution.

The world guide explicitly retains regional heteromorph prejudice. It therefore gives no support to importing UAN's broad disappearance rhetoric as a current social fact. A rescued child entering education can be a meaningful particular outcome while falling short of evidence that all similarly abandoned children now receive protection. School arrangements and prospectus testimony also require their later date and promotional form; they are not comprehensive independent institutional evaluation.

The figures and gear repeatedly show distributed ability: manufacture and electrical supply, eye-wetting and copied Erasure, transport and cooling, information work and public recognition. Such configurations deepen the existing account of shared responsibility. They do not establish that every contribution is equitably credited, that exceptional equipment is universally accessible, or that the endpoint has solved the conditions that produced the original crises.

## Explicit comparisons prevent invented contradictions

The repeated flat five-minute Copy entries need the already verified V23 conditional ten-minute tactical disclosure, and continuous-contact Erasure use is a further condition. A later printed five does not by itself demonstrate a biological regression. Earlier unknown information, such as a personal name or a holder's original Quirk, should be superseded as reader knowledge without declaring the earlier observer wrong.

Stain's affiliation remains inconsistent across the official presentation: negotiations and travel need not mean shared ideology or lasting membership, and the guide's organizational arrow/headline cannot erase the distinct purposes shown elsewhere. This differs from the arrest-credit question, where public credit and actual student intervention can both be true at their respective levels.

Re-Destro's parentage illustrates why a suspected discrepancy must be tested. UAG:s0205 explicitly calls him Destro's biological child. Root reinspection of UAN:s0256–0257 finds the broader formulation that he inherits Destro's blood. The unchanged original V23:p092 says Destro had a child unknown even to himself, without naming that child or counting subsequent generations. These checked passages do not contradict the later specificity. Preserve the precise editorial assertion and the followers' upbringing; do not manufacture a remote-descendant conflict or infer a personal father-son relationship.

The timeline wording at s0005 was similarly checked against the original V42:p129–130 graduation/embers montage and eight-years-later caption. The guide's more specific referent is compatible with the inspected sequence. No exact adult age, typo or contradictory time jump was established. These narrow verifications remain outside frozen historical readings and predictions.

## Author explanations must retain what they decline to settle

The Q&A supplies direct author information about professional teaching, visits, suit functions, creative practice and omitted backgrounds. Its answers can clarify a current model, but they are not automatically independent corroboration of the nearby unsigned profile; shared author/editorial production is a plausible common origin. Counts must distinguish records, propositions and contribution dependence rather than crediting every restatement as a separate observation.

The Shoji answer is especially instructive: an intended volume interstitial was not included. The girl withheld contact at an earlier stage and later sent thanks and a smiling photograph. This enriches reception and the character's available history while expressly identifying an omission in narrative execution. The answer on whether Ochako confessed instead asks readers to imagine from Volume 42. It does not resolve a private conversation date, formal relationship status or the mechanism of her mediated Toga encounter.

The author also distinguishes a chart draft from a finalized complete list. Partial ranks, reader polls, favorite-episode selections, Quirk votes and audience comments must retain different objects and methods. They cannot serve as a universal measure of literary depth, moral worth, in-world power or predictive readiness.

## Production history changes the planning claim, not the enacted past

The relay conversations separate broad destinations from serial discovery. The first editor, Koike Hitoshi, recalls many alternative openings; the early Quirkless rescue climax could offer achievement without a battle victory. Horikoshi specifically wanted beach cleanup to be the first heroic activity despite a concern about its visual plainness (s0308–0310). Monji Kengo's account distinguishes editorial feedback, author confidence and particular visual solutions; their disagreement over relative contribution to the Kirishima/Fat Gum sequence should remain attributed (s0311–0312).

The third editor, 頼富亮典, and Horikoshi discuss external influences, bridging large planned scenes week by week and changing an anticipated ending. Endeavor was once expected to die sometime after the Volume 21 High-End battle; his living burden is therefore a revised trajectory, not proof of a single immutable plan (s0313–0315). Horikoshi tentatively recalls moving toward fewer deaths and says wounds should remain. Neither statement guarantees that nobody would die or that every resulting wound received sufficient dramatic attention. A discarded idea in which Shigaraki killed All Might and Bakugo sought revenge remains a rejected possibility, not an unshown event in character history.

Taguchi Hikaru's discussion of casualties, the prepared public Ochako appeal and the difficult Todoroki hospital sequence gives specific occasions for deliberation (s0315–0316). Imamura Takuaki and Horikoshi discuss the prejudice sequence, Bakugo's planned revival, Toga's changed fate and the final armor return (s0317–0318). Bakugo's revival was intended when his death was staged; this does not establish that every preceding plot detail was planned from the beginning. Medical consultation mentioned during production is not a real-world survival guarantee or an empirical validation of a character model.

Toga was at one point expected to survive in custody. After discussion, the authors rejected an ambiguous forest-syringe survival hint on the stated ground that nobody would take responsibility, and chose her death. They also changed the punctuation of the ordinary-girl line. This strengthens the evidence for deliberate revision of her ending and permits close analysis of the tone; it does not make the discarded survival version canonical or resolve the ontology of every later Toga image. An editor's sadness and brightness reading is his reception, not a universal effect. Horikoshi's concern that the adult armor return might undermine the preceding story, and the editors' reassuring response, similarly identify production risk and judgment rather than settling all reader counterreadings.

The Q&A admits other execution limits directly. A Shoji-related interstitial was intended but omitted; Compress's magician gestures were insufficiently shown; Chisaki's clothing changes were deliberately withheld to avoid making him charming. Added source characterization and deliberate omission are different findings. A reader can now know more without the serialized manga having enacted more. The final revised planning conclusion should therefore be particular: some motifs, destinations and resuscitation choices were planned; other scenes, deaths and routes were reconsidered through serial collaboration.

## The new one-shot gives Eri a choice that a profile alone cannot

The five-page special one-shot at s0319–0323 is new sequential fiction, not a poll illustration, a reused manga event or a graduate testimonial. Eri arrives at U.A. with a guitar and a gift from Mandalay thanking the staff for Kota's education. She greets Aizawa and All Might, now vice-principal, and hopes to see Midoriya. The office's ordinary exchange also includes Present Mic, Snipe and Cementoss. Profile s0104 independently makes the office title readable within the same book; this is identity crosschecking, not another appointment event.

Eri plans a first street performance. She loves Deku and Mirio but does not want their fame to overwhelm that occasion, so invited heroes will watch from a building above. Aizawa understands the reason; Jiro is trying to make time. The scene supplies a discriminating act of reception: affection for rescuers coexists with a boundary on how their public visibility enters her own activity. It neither repudiates care nor requires her to perform gratitude through dependence. Her small verbal uncertainty and the teachers' response also give ordinary interaction more texture than a career endpoint statement alone.

Cementoss notices a television report of the lower-half-year chart announced the previous day. The presenter reports armored teacher Deku at No.4 and Dynamight at No.5, following the earlier profile/chart states. These are new reported ranks, not timeless identifiers. The suggestion that Bakugo was stimulated by Deku is expressly the presenter's question. It is not direct access to Bakugo's private reason. The broadcast shows Midoriya, Bakugo and Shoto heading to a scene, with familiar teasing about teaching and remedial students. It preserves teaching alongside frontline action rather than narrating teaching's abandonment.

The closing image moves from Midoriya on television to Eri playing and deciding to work hard too. Admiration circulates into a different chosen practice. No completed street concert, audience response or future success is shown. The new scene substantially strengthens the existing ordinary-future reading while also leaving a counterreading available: exceptional hero celebrity remains prominent and the drawing still links motivation to the admired hero. Its force lies in Eri deciding the terms of participation, not in a claim that the story has become independent of all heroic recognition.

Horikoshi's signed afterword at s0324 acknowledges choosing this Eri story after the Volume 42 camera-lowered statement, joking about a cast member absent from No.431. It supplies a bounded later addition rather than permission to call every subsequently imagined future an enacted sequel.

## Attribution, reception and the scope of a final fanbook

The guests are separately credited: Yoco Akiyama's montage, Betten Court's illustration, Hideyuki Furuhashi's essay and Anri Yoshi's essay (s0296–0298). Furuhashi reads self-realization and self-sacrifice through accumulated emotion and incremental action; Yoshi describes characters' apparently continuing offstage lives. These are valuable external readings, not Horikoshi statements or demonstrations of every offstage event. Their spin-off credentials do not admit their entire other works into this reconciliation.

The 2024 character poll, category preferences and selected comments measure different kinds of reception. They include main-manga figures, adaptation/spin-off characters, collectives and a named machine. A vote does not create a main-manga behavioral scene. Real contributors in credits must not be counted as fictional people because the design gives them playful hero aliases. The full all-character review must reconcile these source rosters and aliases, rather than using only subjects who received an evidence record.

The historical staff page (s0325) and separately listed publishing/editorial/design/printing personnel (s0326) make shared production explicit. They strengthen the rule that an unsigned caption cannot automatically be quoted as Horikoshi's own assertion. The signed afterword, attributed conversations, Q&A answers, editorial summaries, fictional voices and reader opinions retain separate responsibilities even when printed in the same official book.
