## Ordinary life supplies evidence at more than one level

Profiles preserve tastes that the crisis-centered story may leave peripheral: Bakugo's mountain climbing and spicy foods; Jiro's rock music; Shoji's takoyaki and squid-ink pasta; Mina's dance, natto and okra; Sero's foods that *seem* healthy; Ojiro's martial arts; Hagakure's caramel and prank programs; Koda's liking for nature. These details support characterization without requiring elaborate symbolism. They enlarge factual texture and possible context, not evidence for an unobserved restaurant decision, coping routine or private friendship.

The greater relational gain comes from the speaker-specific accounts around those facts. Mina appreciates Yaoyorozu's study gathering and hospitality; Yaoyorozu's enthusiasm for teaching accompanies her wish to be relied upon. Jiro thanks Koda for their exam success and apologizes for pressing his insect fear. Kaminari appreciates what Jiro's improvised tactic revealed about his strength but asks her never to repeat it. Tokoyami apologizes for the forest-camp burden and values Shoji's refusal to abandon him. These voices distinguish care offered, care experienced and care retrospectively acknowledged. They do not become independent repeats of the underlying manga incident.

The same form exposes limitations. Aoyama's France is explicitly self-claimed, his narcissistic presentation coexists with an observer's uncertainty, and Aizawa says he sees effort without knowing what Aoyama thinks. Aizawa's impression that Kirishima appears to have few worries must remain an impression when later insecurity is disclosed. Quiet Koda is not therefore passive; academically weak Kaminari is not without vocabulary or teachability; Sato has a specific emergency contribution despite little spotlight. A single trait summary would discard distinctions that the book itself supplies.

Adults acquire similarly particular relations. All Might wants ordinary conversation with the unresponsive Endeavor, worries about Gran Torino telling embarrassing stories, and remembers Recovery Girl's sweets. Mandalay's care for her deceased cousin's child is collective rather than solitary: the Pussycats' preferences, reciprocal praise and her promise that “we” will raise Kota give that arrangement personal substance. Fourth Kind's reportedly attempted puppetry, Lunch Rush's poor dishwashing and Manual's houseplants or conscientious patrol belong in character review even if their evidence remains too narrow for a dossier. Ordinary detail has value without an automatic promotion.

## Care, judgment and misconduct cannot be morally offset

Early praise does not share a single ethical position. Midoriya's profile celebrates unhesitating self-sacrifice, while Shoji's suitability text explicitly calls self-disregarding protection both an asset and something needing caution. That juxtaposition strengthens a historical account of changing and uneven rescue judgments; it does not prove that the endpoint critique of solitary self-erasure was fully articulated in every early passage.

Mineta receives a specific bounded disposition. His aptitude, collaborative tactics and wish for popularity remain visible alongside Asui's account of repeated unwanted touching and targeting Yaoyorozu. Her comic reference to a victims' association is the harmed peer's framing, not evidence of a formally constituted organization. The suitability page channels his motive toward admirable hero action but does not show acknowledged harm, changed consent understanding or repaired conduct. Neither competence nor friendship proves rehabilitation.

The source itself sometimes supplies gendered or sexualizing editorial framing, rather than merely quoting Mineta. Gunhead's praise of Ochako's toughness is phrased through a gender expectation; Kirishima initially evaluates Sato's liking for sweets through manliness; Yaoyorozu and Hagakure receive intrusive editorial treatment. Retaining the attribution permits criticism of the book's framing without importing it as an objective character fact. Magne's early labels and promotional wording require the same source-sensitive handling, with later explicit characterization controlling current identity.

This is a useful early affect distinction for L5/L6/S6, not an endpoint forgiveness statement. At s0140, Fuyumi's teaching aspiration is linked to guilt at being unable to help Shoto. That adds a vocational motive beyond generic mediation without making every later act reducible to guilt. The same column's qualified suggestion that the other three siblings did not receive elite training must be placed against later Toya revelations, not silently adopted as timeless family history.

The villain danger-file architecture selects different information from the heroes' personal-data format: threatening capacities, unidentified bodies, risk and investigative uncertainty dominate, while tastes are largely absent. ## THE・DEPTH adds a conditional interaction

The five-page Horikoshi-credited special at s0185–s0189 explicitly places itself on the day after final exams, identifying Volume 8 No.68. It is admitted as main-series supplemental bonus fiction with that stated chronology. Its comic exaggeration and this publication venue remain visible; it is neither a static profile nor a hypothetical reconstruction test.

Bakugo is angry about Midoriya's exam conduct after having been unable to say everything in All Might's presence. He dismisses his classmates' finger game. Ochako intercepts his attempt to confront Deku, says Bakugo impressed her in their match, and contrasts that with his intimidating behavior toward Deku. Her suggestion that this resembles keeping someone away out of fear is **her interpretation**. Bakugo visibly startles and erupts; he does not confess that account. Midoriya returns without the information needed to understand the outburst.

The interruption ends in ordinary schooling. Ochako privately recognizes that she blurted out what she thought. The narrator describes dispersed anger settling into resentment toward her, and Bakugo nevertheless answers Midnight's hero-art-history question correctly. This is not a successful reconciliation or emotional cure. It shows a particular response to an admiring but intrusive peer diagnosis, incomplete interpersonal information and intact academic attention amid resentment.

Midnight's lesson also develops an institutional/formal issue: legitimate heavily armed hero action can appear frightening to spectators, and costume history turns toward themes and identity. This is an in-world teacher's historical explanation, not an omniscient guarantee of every causal detail. It provides a concrete bridge between S4's public recognition and S7's visual mediation without converting a classroom claim into independent real-world history.

The resulting model gain is bounded but real: an additional partner and noncombat setting discriminate Bakugo's response to praise, diagnosis and perceived condescension; Ochako displays initiative, direct judgment and self-correction without becoming omniscient about the boys. L2/L7 and S1 can develop that relation. The earlier V01–V42 manga does not retroactively contain this scene, and the historical Bakugo probe remains unchanged.

## Production history qualifies claims of inevitability

The interview and archived designs give evidence about making the work. Horikoshi associates Shigaraki's hands with his liking for drawing hands; later comments describe removing some hands while balancing eeriness and a crowded design. That changes the precision of a claim about initial design intention, without eliminating later narrative functions of hands. R11's distinction between initial plan and retrospective construction remains necessary.

Discarded names, ages, abilities and character sketches belong to production history. An adult early Iida, altered Endeavor or Stain concept, or rejected name is not another canonical age, alias or life episode of the finished character. Readable Todoroki-family planning notes establish only the elements actually present; they do not show that every later revelation had been fixed from inception.

Horikoshi calls Deku and All Might a central axis and describes an anticipated sad future for All Might. That is dated creator expectation, not an authoritative prediction of death, especially against the finished manga's surviving mentor. His remark about drawing Mineta with less responsibility concerns creative freedom and ease relative to carefully maintaining a cool character; it does not acquit in-world misconduct. Creator commentary can sharpen interpretation while the manga's actual enactment and final outcomes remain separate evidence.
