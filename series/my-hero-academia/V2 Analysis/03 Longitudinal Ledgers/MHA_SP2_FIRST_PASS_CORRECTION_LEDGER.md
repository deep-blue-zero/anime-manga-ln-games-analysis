# Volume 3 second-pass corrections
































1. **The hero/villain succession architecture is already paired this early.** All Might and Shigaraki's unseen teacher each deliberately construct a successor as a public symbol; the first pass underweighted this structural mirroring.
2. **Uraraka's money motive is care, not comic materialism.** Her family economics make income a form of filial reciprocity.
3. **Midoriya is not competition-averse.** He can seek victory intensely when the competitive result carries professional, relational, or ethical meaning.
4. **The Sports Festival is labor-market infrastructure, not merely a tournament arc.** Scouting, sidekick recruitment, course transfer, Support-company exposure, Business-course learning, media spectacle, and personal market value are all explicit.
5. **Shigaraki is developmentally interpreted by the text before his origin is revealed.** The `子ども大人` / counseling / growth material establishes a formation framework without yet supplying causal biography.
6. **Aizawa's teaching model includes deliberate non-ownership of student growth.** His V03 line about students igniting one another complements rather than contradicts his severe direct interventions.
7. **Public recognition has a double edge from its first major institutionalization.** Midoriya's emergence creates opportunity and immediately makes him the field's highest-value target.
































# Volume 4 second-pass corrections
































1. **Sports Festival is not only about public visibility; it is about whether advancement and identity are self-authored.** Ojiro/Shoda, Shinso, Todoroki, Iida, Uraraka, and Hatsume each answer that question differently.
2. **Todoroki's refusal to use fire is not generic stubbornness or a simple combat handicap.** It is a trauma-conditioned strategy to reject being the tool of Endeavor's reproductive project.
3. **Endeavor's All Might rivalry is already reproductive/institutional.** He responds to inability to surpass the Symbol by engineering a successor through `個性婚`.
4. **Shinso is a structural critique of evaluation and Quirk moral coding.** Aizawa explicitly calls the robot-based entrance exam irrational for his ability, and peers repeatedly code Brainwashing as villain-suited.
5. **Shinso is also a control case against deterministic villain formation.** Stigma and grievance coexist with a sincere hero aspiration and legitimate institutional route.
6. **Midoriya is no longer simply “the disadvantaged outsider.”** He remembers exclusion but explicitly recognizes himself as presently `恵まれた` through entrusted power and relationships.
7. **Midoriya's dependence on others is constitutive rather than embarrassing.** `誰かに救けられてここにいる` makes relational support part of positive heroic identity.
8. **Ojiro and Shoda carry philosophical weight.** Their withdrawals make personal agency part of legitimate merit even when the official result would allow them to advance.
9. **Uraraka's V04 contribution is individuation, not the famous Bakugo fight.** She declines Midoriya's prepared strategy because she wants to challenge independently; the actual fight begins at the volume endpoint and must be analyzed in V05.
10. **Aizawa's rationalism is not institutional obedience.** He is willing to call U.A.'s own admission metric irrational when it poorly predicts usable capability.
































# Volume 5 second-pass corrections
































1. **Uraraka–Bakugo is primarily a recognition-quality scene, not merely “Bakugo respects women.”** The crowd patronizes; Bakugo recognizes threat; Aizawa defends that recognition; Midoriya later restores tactical authorship to Uraraka.
2. **Uraraka's defeat adds emotional-regulation evidence.** Peer-facing resilience and family-facing vulnerability coexist rather than contradict.
3. **Midoriya's Todoroki intervention is not pure altruism.** He explicitly admits both concern for Todoroki's sadness and substantial competitive frustration.
4. **`君の力` belongs to a broader ownership argument.** The target is not simply “use your full Quirk,” but the father's claim over what the Quirk means.
5. **Todoroki's fire is not reconciliation.** `お前を忘れた` makes temporary de-centering more accurate than paternal acceptance.
6. **Bakugo's status drive is evidentiary.** Official No.1 and public recognition are worthless to him without internally valid proof.
7. **The full-power legitimacy demand generalizes beyond Midoriya.** Todoroki withholding fire triggers the same causal-invalidity problem in a different relationship.
8. **All Might's succession is autobiographical but increasingly anti-replicative.** Quirkless identification explains initial selection; mentor maturity appears when he recognizes Midoriya must derive a distinct method.
9. **Recovery Girl is a structural ethical counterweight.** Healing is not permission for infinite heroic martyrdom.
10. **Midoriya's sustainability shift is relational, not merely technical.** Inko's fear becomes part of why a new method is ethically required.
11. **Stain adds a distinct villain-formation route.** Ideological purification and absolutism cannot be reduced to failed recognition or Quirk stigma.
































# Volume 6 second-pass corrections
































1. **Stain's V06 critique is not primarily a broad money/fame/rankings critique.** Those Hero Society features matter, but his direct V06 test is motive, rescue priority, self-interest, sincerity, and purification.
2. **Full Cowling is embodiment and successor differentiation, not only “sustainable lower output.”** Gran Torino explicitly identifies All Might admiration as a shackle and Midoriya reconceives Quirk as `体の一部`.
3. **Iida's vengeance is more conscious than “revenge disguised as duty.”** He hopes to personally finish Stain, receives a `私怨` warning, and proceeds anyway.
4. **Stain's diagnosis and Stain's legitimacy must be separated.** He is substantially correct about Iida's immediate moral failure and radically illegitimate in converting that diagnosis into execution.
5. **Iida's rescue from moral collapse is distributed, not Midoriya-exclusive.** Manual warns him, Midoriya finds/intervenes, Todoroki recognizes resentment and redirects identity, and Iida must himself stand.
6. **Hero names are load-bearing identity evidence.** Midoriya's Deku reclamation, Kirishima's predecessor homage, Bakugo's violent naming, and Iida's deferred Ingenium all prefigure later conduct.
7. **Hero legitimacy receives explicit legal/institutional grounding.** Manual's `私怨`/`私刑` discussion is a major Hero Society proposition.
8. **Shigaraki–Stain is a developmental encounter rather than mere villain disagreement.** Stain exposes Shigaraki's lack of conviction; the unseen teacher metabolizes the encounter into learning.
9. **Todoroki's key V06 development is trauma-conditioned recognition.** His own resentment becomes a tool for seeing Iida rather than only a reason to constrain himself.
10. **Professional heroism is heterogeneous.** Internships make civility/image, martial skill, media work, patrol, law, mentorship, and crisis response visible as distinct professional functions.
