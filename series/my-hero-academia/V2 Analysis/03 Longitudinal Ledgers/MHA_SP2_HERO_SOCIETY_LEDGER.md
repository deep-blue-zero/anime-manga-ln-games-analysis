# MHA SP2 — Hero Society Ledger

- Quirk visibility already organizes childhood status and future expectations.

Yaoyorozu's battle-trial debrief distinguishes scenario fidelity and professional judgment from nominal victory.
































**Locator:** V02:p068-070.

Thirteen explicitly describes licensing and regulation as the structure through which superhuman society appears to function.
































**Locator:** V02:p118-119.

**Locators:** V02:p105; p143.

- His activity window is shrinking.

**Locators:** V02:p076; p109-111; p128.

- Midoriya yields class leadership to Iida after demonstrated ability.

**Locators:** V02:p101-104; p130; p153-155; p174-179.
































## Current synthesis
> **Hero Society is already a system of sorting, licensing, specialization, public legitimacy, and symbolic dependency. Its central early contradiction is that All Might genuinely stabilizes the system while making it easy for the system to organize too much responsibility around one finite body.**
































# Volume 3 cumulative update
































### Sports Festival as public labor-market infrastructure
- U.A. insists on holding the festival after USJ and dramatically increases security, treating visibility as worth preserving despite risk (V03:p091).
- The festival is described as a nationally dominant event whose popularity has displaced the old Olympics (V03:p092-093).
- Pro heroes scout students there; a common professional route is recruitment into a hero's agency/sidekick structure; students have only three annual opportunities while enrolled (V03:p092-093).
































### Internal mobility and sorting
- General Studies students can use festival performance to seek Hero Course transfer, while Hero Course students can be displaced (V03:p113-115).
- The festival therefore does not only celebrate an already-selected elite; it re-adjudicates institutional classification publicly.
































### Support, business, branding, and market value
- Support Course students use the event to advertise invention/development ability to companies (V03:p157-158).
- Business Course students study hero-agency management/marketing through observation, and spectators discuss Midoriya in value/market terms after his race win (V03:p178-179).
































### Public legibility creates attack surface
- Midoriya's first-place finish makes his `存在` publicly visible and immediately assigns him the ten-million-point cavalry target (V03:p175-186).
































## Current synthesis through V03
> **Hero Society is not merely a rescue profession with rankings attached. It is simultaneously public service, dangerous labor, credentialing system, recruitment market, entertainment spectacle, personal-brand economy, and institutional sorting mechanism. Public recognition allocates opportunity while also exposing the recognized person to concentrated pressure.**
































# Volume 4 cumulative update
































### U.A.'s entrance exam has a real metric-design blind spot
- Aizawa calls the practical entrance exam `合理的じゃねぇ` in relation to Shinso: Brainwashing is powerful against people, but robot targets prevent it from demonstrating conventional practical value (V04:p134-136).
- V01's rescue-point sophistication therefore cannot be generalized into institutional omniscience.
































### Sports Festival as corrective evaluation surface
- Shinso can use public human competition to show capability the entrance exam failed to measure; despite losing, spectators explicitly recognize Hero Course-level potential (V04:p154-156).
- Hero Society's evaluation system can mis-sort and also contain second-chance routes capable of revising classification.
































### Quirk value enters family/reproductive institutions
- `個性婚` shows that social valuation of Quirks can structure marriage and reproduction, not merely school or professional ranking (V04:p095-100).
- Endeavor converts inability to surpass All Might into a next-generation family project.
































### Competition can cultivate individuation
- Iida deliberately challenges Midoriya rather than join the safest friendship-based team (V04:p017-019).
- Uraraka later cites that model when choosing not to rely automatically on Midoriya's prepared anti-Bakugo plan (V04:p176-190).
- The Sports Festival therefore produces autonomy as well as hierarchy/market sorting.
































### Agency as legitimacy of advancement
- Ojiro and Shoda voluntarily withdraw because Brainwashing left them unable to regard cavalry qualification as their own performance (V04:p113-115).
- A procedurally valid result can be internally rejected as illegitimate when personal agency is absent.
































## Current synthesis through V04
> **Hero Society is a multi-surface evaluator rather than a single meritocratic machine. Its metrics can discover hidden value, miss important abilities, and later correct themselves. The same social order also lets ranking pressure penetrate family formation, making institutional ideas about power consequential far beyond the workplace.**
































# Volume 5 — recognition quality, public judgment, and family return
































## Public visibility does not guarantee accurate recognition
- The stadium crowd sees Bakugo's repeated explosions against Uraraka and condemns them as excessive; Aizawa identifies the opposite professional meaning: vigilance produced by recognizing a dangerous opponent (V05:p007-025).
- The festival therefore successfully makes students visible while still producing interpretive error.
































**Revision:** V03 public legibility must be separated from **recognition quality**.
































## Competition surfaces real capability and distorts meaning simultaneously
- Uraraka's concealed rubble plan, Iida's close-range Recipro Burst, Todoroki's integrated potential, and Bakugo's adaptive response are genuinely surfaced by the tournament.
- Yet Bakugo's official first place is institutionally valid while subjectively `ゴミ`, and the podium physically restrains the winner who rejects the meaning assigned to him (V05:p169-175).
































## Professional interpretation versus audience sentiment
- Aizawa functions as an epistemic counterweight to crowd patronization in Uraraka–Bakugo (V05:p016).
- The series is therefore already distinguishing popularity/public reaction from professional competence assessment.
































## Hero-body governance
- Recovery Girl's refusal to repeatedly repair catastrophic self-harm shows institutional medicine setting limits on heroic bodily expenditure (V05:p101-108).
- Hero training is not legitimate if medical restoration simply externalizes the costs of unsustainable methods.
































## The festival returns students to family
- The arc closes in hospital rooms and homes: Tensei/Tenya, Shoto/Rei, Uraraka/parents, Midoriya/Inko (V05:p178-187).
- Hero students remain relationally embedded children rather than only public assets, rankings, brands, or labor-market candidates.

**Revision:** `STRENGTHEN` — Hero Society cannot be modeled as a single combat-ranking profession; its labor ecology is plural even this early.

- Manual explains heroes do not possess unlimited rights of arrest/punishment and warns that `私刑` can collapse the hero/criminal distinction (V06:p110-111).
































**Major institutional proposition:** licensing does not make the hero sovereign. Coercive legitimacy depends on law, accumulated trust, role, and public purpose.
































## Hosu demonstrates distributed emergency response
Multiple pros/students respond to simultaneous Nomu/Stain threats; no single actor contains the crisis. This materially strengthens distributed-heroism infrastructure.
































## Competence and private ethics can diverge
Endeavor's professional emergency competence coexists with already-established family instrumentalization. Public role performance cannot by itself adjudicate private relational morality.
































# Volume 7 cumulative update
































## Hero coercive authority receives a historical genealogy
- Police prioritize `統率と規格` in early superhuman disorder and do not treat Quirks as ordinary public weapons.
- Hero public force becomes tolerable through a historical record of morality/rule observance, not because powerful people possess an inherent right to act (V07:p063-067).
































## Hosu exposes rule/outcome tension
- Todoroki correctly centers the lives saved; the police chief correctly rejects “good outcome therefore rules do not matter” as a general principle.
- The actual solution is discretionary and imperfect rather than purely legalistic or vigilante.
































## Public causal truth can diverge from official hero narrative
- Students' decisive role is suppressed and licensed adults receive public credit so the unauthorized action does not become a public legal/professional crisis (V07:p066-071).
- Rankings/news/reputation must therefore be treated as socially mediated evidence, not transparent causal accounting.
































## U.A. shows adaptive institutional learning
- Final practicals are redesigned from expected robot tests to student pairs versus teachers because villain conditions have changed.
- Capture **or escape** counts as success, broadening professional competence beyond overpowering opponents.
































## Hero licensing can be instrumental
- Gran Torino indicates he obtained a license to enable lawful/free Quirk use for his purposes, complicating the idea that licensed hero is always a total identity vocation.

# Volume 13 cumulative updates


## From `一強` to `群`


The Public Safety Commission explicitly names All Might's former position as overwhelming `一強` and rejects waiting for another `“次の彼”`. Its proposed response is a more consciously coordinated `“群”のヒーロー` capable of filling the social/professional gap collectively (`V13:p046-047`). This is the clearest institutional answer so far to the single-point-of-failure problem.


## Civilian trust as professional evidence


HUC makes rescue recipients evaluators. Triage, reassurance, communication and scene management become observable professional dimensions rather than presumed moral qualities (`V13:p008-038`).


## Interoperable emergency authority


Heroes are expected to perform triage, evacuation and smooth `橋渡し` until fire/police can take over; they are not conceived as self-sufficient total emergency systems (`V13:p020-021`).


## Credentialing as conditional delegation


A provisional license allows emergency action affecting lives. The system uses subtractive performance scoring and permits remediation for failed candidates, modeling credentialing as a quality threshold plus correction rather than permanent moral sorting (`V13:p094-109`).


## Formal rank is not affective succession


Endeavor inherits the No.1 position while public reassurance does not automatically transfer. Post-Symbol society experiences a psychological as well as combat-capability vacuum (`V13:p119-121`, `p143-145`).


## Competing network formation


The same weakened center stimulates League dispersal/recruitment and broader organizational seeking. Commission coordination and villain coalition-building are competing responses to the post-Symbol network vacuum (`V13:p124-132`, `p143-145`).


# Volume 14 cumulative updates

## Society has plural successors

Nezu tells students across Hero, Support, General and Management/Business courses that all are `社会の後継者` (`V14:p063`). The post-Symbol succession problem is therefore explicitly civic: society must reproduce not only champions but the institutions, technologies, management capacity and ordinary actors around them.

## Internship as bridge from credential to professional judgment

The new internship/work-study layer follows provisional licensing but is not automatic. Faculty debate first-year exposure after repeated League contact, while Mirio presents internship experience as the mechanism by which difficult capacities become professional strength (`V14:p104-122`, `p144-148`). Credentialing grants bounded authority; supervised field experience develops judgment.

## Succession legitimacy can be institutionally contested

Nighteye accepts the formal fact that All Might transferred OFA by his own will while disputing whether Midoriya was the best choice (`V14:p178`, `p196`). Hero society therefore contains legitimate disagreement about succession criteria rather than a single uncontested authority.

## Organized villain governance expands

Shie Hassaikai's emergence makes the villain field organizationally plural. League coalition logic now competes with yakuza hierarchy/planning for post-AFO underworld authority (`V14:p124-140`). This is adjacent to hero-society analysis because public-order institutions confront not isolated offenders but rival organizational forms.


# Volume 15 cumulative updates

The Eri alley encounter shows a core institutional limit: visible distress creates moral concern but does not by itself provide enough lawful/operational knowledge to dismantle a criminal organization without risking disappearance of the target. Midoriya/Mirio report rather than pursue after Eri returns (`V15:p019-025`).

**Institutional implication:** professional restraint can preserve rescue capacity even while leaving moral residue.

Police, Nighteye's office, Ryukyu, Fat Gum, Aizawa and other heroes combine street evidence, drug seizures, projectile analysis, organizational mapping and carefully bounded Foresight into a raid threshold (`V15:p125-183`).

**Institutional implication:** V13's abstract post-`一強` turn toward a coordinated `群` becomes operational bureaucracy. Distributed trust includes disagreement, jurisdiction, evidence sharing, confidentiality and responsibility assignment.

## Students inside professional systems

Licensed/work-study students are neither treated as unrestricted pros nor categorically excluded. Rock Lock voices risk skepticism; Nighteye assumes responsibility; Aizawa supplies supervisory governance; agencies still use student capacity inside the operation (`V15:p125-158`).

**Institutional implication:** provisional authority is conditional integration into adult systems, not autonomous hero status.

## Confidentiality as professional burden

The investigation's nondisclosure rules prevent Midoriya from processing the Eri case freely with classmates (`V15:p169`).

**Institutional implication:** professionalization imposes legitimate information boundaries that can intensify private distress; healthy support networks must sometimes function without access to case details.

# Volume 16 cumulative updates

## Distributed authority under hostile routing

The Hassaikai raid makes the post-Symbol `群` model operational: police, pros, sidekicks, staff and licensed students move as differentiated roles while Mimic actively disrupts the environment (`V16:p007-053`, `p171-183`).

## Mission legitimacy can require separation

Amajiki's decision to hold three opponents alone is accepted because it preserves the rescue line's forward movement. Professional contribution is therefore role-fit under mission objectives, not merely remaining physically grouped (`V16:p047-083`).

## Mentorship permits reciprocal protection

Fat Gum accepts Kirishima's protection and credits the student's role rather than preserving adult status through denial. Licensed student participation is shown as genuinely consequential without erasing adult command/experience (`V16:p116-123`).

## Distributed systems remain surprise-vulnerable

Mimic's spatial control, enhancement use and the League's concealed/ambiguous participation show that multi-agency systems do not create certainty. Their advantage is adaptive redistribution of responsibility and information.


## V17 - rescue networks, capability loss and forecast governance

- The raid demonstrates professional resilience as role handoff: Mirio preserves Eri through isolation and Quirk loss; Aizawa/Nighteye/Midoriya reconnect; exterior teams continue routing support. No single capability completes the operation by itself.
- Nighteye's Foresight becomes a governance problem as well as a power: long-horizon prediction carries psychological/decision cost, so he self-restricts to short tactical windows until escalation forces a longer look (`V17:p128-150`).
- Midoriya's response establishes a professional norm distinct from certainty: credible prediction informs risk but does not, by itself, erase an active rescue obligation.
- Completed Quirk-destroying ammunition demonstrates that villain infrastructure can attack the professional basis of hero identity directly, not only injure bodies.

## Volume 18 - aftermath institutions and legitimacy beyond rank

- The Hassaikai operation remains a multi-institution event after Chisaki falls: police, heroes, medical staff, local damage control, Quirk containment and school supervision carry consequences forward (`V18:p025-091`).
- Nighteye's death demonstrates that successful rescue does not erase professional cost already incurred.
- Remedial licensing explicitly evaluates capacity to reach difficult children rather than merely subdue threats. Social/emotional leadership is therefore a licensing competency (`V18:p113-166`).
- Quirk spectacle can be public reassurance/connection rather than force; the remedial solution makes social legibility an operational skill.
- Endeavor confronts the gap between being ranked No.1 and carrying the Symbol of Peace's social function. `No.1` is a starting line, not a transferred legitimacy certificate (`V18:p167-174`).
