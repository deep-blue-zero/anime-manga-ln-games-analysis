## Major V07 analytical propositions

1. Volume 7 is organized around **crisis residue becoming future structure**. Recognition matters only when what has been recognized changes later conduct, relationship, institution, or inheritance.
2. Iida's recovery is not a reset. Tensei's ordinary-care ideal, Iida's apology, retained hand injury, conviction/method distinction, and prevention doctrine turn moral failure into an ongoing practice of accountability. The retained injury is both a plausible accountability mnemonic and a possible self-punitive mechanism; later evidence must distinguish them.
3. Midoriya explicitly acknowledges that he noticed Iida's distress without seeing its depth. `友だちなのに` refines the recognition thesis into stages: notice, infer severity, decide whether to press a boundary, and intervene early enough to alter trajectory.
4. Stain's physical defeat becomes symbolic amplification. Gran Torino compares the pressure generated by his ideology/obsession to the same **kind of symbolic mechanism** as All Might's Symbol-of-Peace conviction; this is a comparison of social mechanism, not a moral equivalence.
5. Villain formation becomes infrastructural: diffuse grievance or malice can acquire a charismatic frame, media circulation, and an organizational `受け皿`, then aggregate into movement-scale force.
6. Hosu's police aftermath establishes hero coercive authority as historically conditional on `統率と規格`, licensing, supervision, accumulated trust, and rule restraint. A causally lifesaving act can still be professionally unauthorized.
7. The suppression of the students' direct causal role proves that official public hero narratives are not transparent records of who actually caused a rescue; law, age, supervision, and institutional discretion mediate public credit.
8. OFA/AFO relational opposition receives explicit origin-level support: All For One takes, owns, assigns, and builds dependency; One For All becomes power whose identity depends on succession beyond the current bearer.
9. All Might can transmit dangerous historical truth while still failing to transmit painful personal truth. His inability to tell Midoriya that he may not remain beside him is a care-shaped epistemic failure, not malicious concealment.
10. U.A. responds to the changing villain environment by redesigning the final practical exam, supplying positive evidence that institutions in MHA can learn and alter pedagogy after crisis.
11. Bakugo's `Origin` grounds his heroic ideal in All Might as the hero who always wins from bad odds. His willingness to prefer defeat over borrowing Midoriya's strength proves that Midoriya-specific hierarchy injury can temporarily override even that core value.
12. Midoriya's response to Bakugo is increasingly sophisticated: he rejects shouting as failed communication and tries to move Bakugo through **Bakugo's own anti-surrender value**, not by demanding that Bakugo become Midoriya-like.
13. The volume closes on a formal inversion: Bakugo says he would rather lose than borrow Midoriya's power, then orders Midoriya to use Bakugo's stored explosive power in a shared tactic. The relationship's closed circuit has been breached, not healed.
