# CM-U01: キッシング・グーラミー

Kyoichi Otomo describes himself as someone others impose upon. This chapter makes that description both an explanation of his vulnerability and a means of evading responsibility. Imagase exploits it, Chikako rejects the marriage organized around it, and Kyoichi repeatedly acts against its literal version. The chapter ends with renewed contact rather than a demonstrated resolution of those conflicts. This is a reading of the revised Japanese witness through this component alone.

## The accommodation account and its counterexamples

Kyoichi’s account of the earlier affairs allocates beginnings and endings elsewhere: women insisted, he could not refuse, events were brief or already finished. His acknowledged involvement is evidence; his explanation of each woman’s intention is not an independent account from her. Imagase’s criticism identifies a repeated rhetorical pattern, but his interest in gaining access prevents it from becoming a neutral diagnostic verdict. Kyoichi’s request that Imagase wear a suit, his two initiated confrontations, his shopping invitation, his explicit hotel refusals and his café departure all show choice. “No agency” would be false even within this chapter. The stronger provisional formulation is that Kyoichi often prefers responsive action and explains unwanted consequences through other people’s initiative (O003, O011–O012, O019, O021, O026, O031).

Responsive accommodation has real value as well as costs. Quitting smoking for Chikako is a concrete reported concession; shopping creates a genuinely pleasurable exchange in which she chooses objects and he initiates the outing. Those details resist reducing him to indifference or her to a material obstacle. Yet his own language of ease and avoidance of rejection joins Chikako’s complaint about waiting for instructions. His offer to do anything does not answer her objection: it makes her specify the content again. Her testimony converts an apparently compliant husband into a partner whose responsibility for initiating a shared life is disputed (O014–O015, O019–O020, O028–O030). This supports a relational pattern, not a clinical condition or proof that he never cared.

Chikako’s investigation initially appears through Kyoichi’s fear of being found out. Her later account gives it a divorce/compensation purpose. That is a disclosure-driven revision of the analyst’s available causal story, not a contradiction requiring the wife to have changed her mind on the page. The reader must preserve the earlier fear without endorsing its premise. She remains responsible for her own reported new partner and financially interested investigation; her justified criticism of waiting does not make every action of hers beyond criticism. Her inner life and partner’s perspective remain unavailable. Kyoichi’s fidelity argument toward Imagase likewise exposes inconsistency without falsifying the principle that partners deserve consideration (O024, O028–O030).

## Leverage, desire and granular permission

The first transaction has a visible mechanism: an investigator controls damaging marital information, Otomo expects disclosure to threaten his household, and suppression is exchanged for access. Imagase calls it help, kneels and professes devotion; the report still defines the cost of refusal. The formal contrast between senior-address and practical control matters more than either man's physical height. The first quiet kiss sequence cannot remove the bargaining context established immediately before it (O002–O006; CM-INF-001).

Japanese wording keeps the dispute precise. キスだけ (“only a kiss,” O002/O004) names a limit, while 人が好い (“good-natured” or susceptible to imposition, O012) is Kyoichi's self-description. Imagase's 貴方を好きにできる at sp0022 means “can do as I please with you”; it is not the liking/attraction construction in 男が好き at sp0014. At sp0019, 指図できる立場じゃない denies his standing to give directions. These short, source-checked glosses distinguish attraction, self-explanation and power rather than merging them through the word 好き. No official English comparison is available.

The chapter makes bodily limits granular. Kyoichi’s refusal of tongue contact is met with tie control and a denial of his standing to give directions. Later he objects when the kiss-only bargain is expanded, while Imagase exploits the wording and offers to absorb blame. These are observable conflicts over permission. The ethical judgment that such pressure violates the stated limits is supported by the refusals and the explicitly invoked leverage; it does not depend on denying Imagase affection or Kyoichi pleasure. The latter is eventually stated in inner speech. Pleasure is evidence about experience, not authorization, exclusivity or orientation (O009, O012–O018; CM-CL-002).

Imagase’s two dimensions must remain together: he uses underhanded means knowingly, and he also describes a desire extending beyond this opportunity. Neither wholly proves nor cancels the other. His claim of unique care can be sincere, possessive, strategic or some combination; his later exposure to the companion’s violence gives him vulnerability in a different relation without reversing responsibility for his conduct toward Kyoichi (O013, O024, O033–O034). The chapter does not warrant a permanent controller/victim assignment across every context.

At the café Kyoichi changes the leverage equation by accepting the risk of disclosure and walking away. The later divorce request shows why this does not guarantee the marriage’s survival. Imagase’s apology for not preventing divorce need not mean he caused it or could have prevented it. Kyoichi’s accusation that Imagase already knew everything remains unconfirmed as an exact timeline of knowledge. A broad confession or proof of a deliberately engineered investigation would exceed what is shown (O026–O034; CM-INF-004).

The reunion removes the currently displayed marital-report bargain, but does not demonstrate a comprehensive repair. Imagase proposes dating; Kyoichi refuses that classification while entertaining a trip. The final kiss has no renewed report threat and no depicted fresh protest, which is a material change in presentation. It still follows assent to a hot-spring idea, not a clearly negotiated dating or bodily agreement. The defensible endpoint is reopened contact with unresolved relationship terms (O038–O040; CM-CL-005). Later chapters may evidence change; they cannot retroactively authorize the earlier refusals.

## Manga form: who can know what

The first room is explained after its appearance; the kiss is then revisited after Kyoichi returns home. This temporal braiding makes the transaction persist in the household he tells himself it preserved. Written self-congratulation and sleeplessness occupy the same sequence, creating friction between his explanation and the page’s continuation of memory. It does not settle whether desire, shame, fear or several affects prevent sleep (O002–O010).

The four horizontal kiss panels slow visible progression without providing a measured duration or acoustic silence. The frontispiece’s tie restraint is a formal invitation to notice control; the later actual tie pull supplies narrative evidence independently. A decorative/proleptic image should never substitute for an event record. Fish and aquarium imagery return around the sighting that Kyoichi cannot leave alone, connecting the chapter title’s field with attention, enclosure and observation. No depicted fish has been identified as a biological species; the metaphor is an interpretive possibility grounded in juxtaposition, not an ichthyological assertion (O001, O006, O009, O020–O021; CM-CL-006).

Chikako’s direct speech arrives late enough to displace the men's explanatory monopoly. The divorce form materializes a decision before its reason is spoken. Blank space between the spouses at her new-partner disclosure enlarges their separation compositionally. The page does not supply her private thoughts, and therefore demands attention to what she actually says rather than a complete retrospective portrait (O027–O030).

The phone call makes access unequal in a different way: readers see the companion’s violent act while Kyoichi hears only a noise and receives the minimizing cup explanation. Imagase’s face and fragments coexist with the intimate argument carried by the telephone. That surplus knowledge complicates his claim of exclusive care, but does not prove the companion's intention, injury or a completed breakup. Kyoichi’s earlier cup impulse was attributed by Imagase; the later actual thrown cup makes a formal echo without converting the first attribution into fact (O023, O031–O035; CM-INF-005).

The ending repeats proximity after separate facial images and a six-month domestic ellipsis. Kyoichi’s earlier insistence that loss must be divorce-related is framed as persuasion of himself; the reunion makes Imagase’s continuing significance plausible.
