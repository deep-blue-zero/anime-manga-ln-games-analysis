# Sexuality and intimacy: the limits of the ordinary world

The manga makes sexuality legible through several partially connected kinds of evidence: a person's stated identity, bodily desire, chosen acts, social presentation, imagined future and attachment to a particular other. The friction among them drives the drama. Kyoichi's ordinary-man account cannot contain everything he wants; Imagase's explicit male desire does not tell him how to secure a sustainable place. Their development has social and ethical stakes without requiring the analyst to certify a final total identity for either man.

## Normality does work within an argument

Kyoichi's early appeal to a normal male world allows a married woman's invitation to seem a restoration, even while he privately thinks of apologizing to Imagase. The sequence exposes a classification that makes desire for a woman socially reassuring without making the affair ethically sound. Heterosexuality supplies familiarity in his account; it does not resolve the obligations of Nishimura's marriage or his treatment of Imagase. The serpent fantasy likewise belongs to his explanation of temptation, rather than an independent authorial verdict that male intimacy is corrupt. [sp0058](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U02_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u02-o008); [sp0059](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U02_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u02-o009); [sp0060](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U02_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u02-o010).

The first title-work choice develops that language into a fear of dependence. Kyoichi imagines that entering Imagase's world would leave him with only Imagase while Imagase could move to another man. Whether this is true is unproved. Its psychological function is clearer: the ordinary world promises alternatives, belonging and protection against being the abandoned person. His categorical refusal therefore contains both social assumptions and a particular vulnerability. Reducing it to either a fixed orientation statement or an already solved romantic confession would flatten the scene. [sp0160](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o018); [sp0163](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o021).

His eventual recognition of Imagase as a particular former lover and his final choice have weight. They do not make his attraction to Tamaki unreal. The corpus can support queer or bisexual interpretations as interpretive possibilities while retaining the limits of what he actually names. A confident final label would require more than one act, relationship or participant's accusation. The literary argument concerns how he negotiates incompatible self-accounts and commitments, rather than grading which category is most authentic. [sp0337](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U09_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u09-o077); [sp0345](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U10_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u10-o007); [sp0426](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U10_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u10-o088).

## Gendered roles offer scripts and create exclusions

Both men use gender to imagine relational work. Kyoichi contrasts domestic ease with an invented woman who demands a correct clothing answer. His miniature self then proposes treating Imagase with the consideration due a woman partner. The corrective intention still depends on a gendered analogy, and the ensuing compliment reaches an individual whose response exceeds the plan. The comedy exposes the insufficiency of the script from within its use. [sp0138](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U05_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u05-o001); [sp0139](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U05_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u05-o002); [sp0141](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U05_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u05-o004).

Imagase's imagined wifehood in U08 similarly makes ordinary recognition imaginable through a familiar social role. Feeding, cleaning, a child, daily attention and monitoring appear together; his self-critique keeps the fantasy from becoming pure domestic innocence. No actual woman, marriage or child is established. The eventual real thanks reaches him as he is, without making Kyoichi share the imagined arrangement. Desire for care and concern about gendered access are both present. [sp0256](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U08_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u08-o001); [sp0258](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U08_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u08-o003); [sp0259](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U08_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u08-o004).

The changes in sexual participation also have represented meanings without becoming universal equations. Kyoichi describes one change through becoming the man and feeling responsible. Imagase discloses vulnerability and a particular wish. Those statements are source evidence about their understandings. They cannot establish that an insertive role always confers agency, a receptive role always confers dependence, or a changed role solves emotional insecurity. U09's subsequent departure is direct counterevidence to that last claim. [sp0279](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U09_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u09-o019); [sp0287](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U09_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u09-o027); [sp0305](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U09_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u09-o045).

## Privacy differs from public invisibility

Imagase's claim that outsiders do not care about private sex cannot answer Kyoichi's bodily refusal. Equally, a desire for privacy can be meaningful while the absence of public recognition hurts a partner. These are different questions. The manga repeatedly makes trouble by letting one answer stand in for another. A private relationship can matter intensely without being named at work; its public absence can affect another person's sense of availability and a third person's belief. [sp0056](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U02_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u02-o006); [sp0219](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U07_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u07-o015); [sp0226](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U07_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u07-o022).

The girlfriend/partner difference gives this social problem linguistic form. Kyoichi's gendered denial reaches Tamaki as a wider no-partner report and reaches Imagase through her. The reader sees the formulations separately. Later Mina infers a male lover and frames Tamaki as cover; that is her angry interpretation, not an established account of why Kyoichi dated Tamaki. Tamaki is never shown receiving the completed identity disclosure. The source therefore stages social categorization through incomplete, interested speech rather than one omniscient coming-out scene. [sp0467](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U10_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u10-o129); [sp0473](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U10_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u10-o135).

The reciprocal rings preserve the same distinction at the ending. Imagase may wear a sign at work while Kyoichi cannot. Their acceptance of that difference can communicate a limited agreement without making the difference disappear. No external legal conclusion is needed to see the narrative's unequal visibility. The objects provide a sign within the relation whose public meanings remain constrained. [sp0497](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U10_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u10-o159); [sp0498](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U10_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u10-o160).

## Affection has relations beyond romance

Hummingbird makes Imagase's affection for Kamimura explicit while distinguishing it from attraction toward Sakano. The teacher's actual role is teaching; no acted relation or awareness is supplied. Imagase can criticize the boys' bodily reduction of Kamimura and recognize a related limitation in his own appearance-focused gaze. The story does not require a gay man to be morally superior or incapable of caring for a woman. It gives particular affection and self-criticism an adolescent setting. [sp0517](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_S01_DEEP_READING.md#cm-s01-o015); [sp0519](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_S01_DEEP_READING.md#cm-s01-o017).

Kamimura's independent crush and withdrawal, the public art-only defense and the delayed disclosure that friendship continued all resist a compulsory romantic triangle. The adult first-date joke before a new audience does not transform the old outing into hidden heterosexual romance. Her marriage and welcome-home testimony offer another relational language that Imagase can receive without taking her place or becoming institutionally identical to her. [sp0523](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_S01_DEEP_READING.md#cm-s01-o021); [sp0524](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_S01_DEEP_READING.md#cm-s01-o022); [sp0531](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_S01_DEEP_READING.md#cm-s01-o029).

The comic's gendered insult to Imagase remains Natsuki's banter. It can expose how romantic vanity is coded through gender without becoming the analysis's account of a new identity or a punishment for attachment. Uncertain drink-order attribution is too weak a basis for a neat gender allegory. The supplied work keeps its characters fallible and socially entangled after the serious choice. [sp0535](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_S01_DEEP_READING.md#cm-s01-o033).

The evidence supports an intimacy that changes self-understanding through a particular person while leaving categories, social expectations and public acknowledgment imperfectly aligned. The [ethics study](CORNERED_MOUSE_AGENCY_COERCION_AND_ETHICS_STUDY.md) separately treats permission and responsibility; sexual discovery is no excuse for harm. The missing Reboot story and unavailable original/English witnesses limit broader claims. Within this revised witness, the productive uncertainty is part of the characterization and social argument, rather than a puzzle requiring one final label to make the relationship intelligible.
