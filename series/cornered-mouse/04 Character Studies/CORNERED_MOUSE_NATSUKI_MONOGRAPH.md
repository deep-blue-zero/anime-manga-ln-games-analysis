# Natsuki: the demand to be chosen

Natsuki / 夏生 asks for something Kyoichi's agreeable manner has repeatedly failed to make secure: evidence that he actively wants her. Her renewed proposal, account of the old breakup, refusal to withdraw for Imagase and concrete holiday planning are choices made by a person with her own desired future. Her sharp insight coexists with contempt, gendered assumptions and interested pressure. Those tensions make her more than either the authoritative interpreter of the men or an obstacle whose disappearance completes them.

## A past relation has more than one narrator

Kyoichi explains their earlier ending as her becoming tired of him. Natsuki says she kept loving him but felt he did not love her. That correction changes the available account of the relationship's failure. His respect for her right to leave has ethical value; it can also let him avoid contesting an ending or naming what he wants. Her complaint concerns the experience of receiving kindness without the desired passion. The analysis can recognize it without making her a neutral witness to every part of him. [sp0116](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U04_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u04-o020); [sp0117](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U04_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u04-o021).

The lighter sharpens her complaint. An object from an earlier girlfriend remains with Kyoichi and is passed to Imagase for a practical reason. Natsuki sees inconsiderateness toward the current partner; Kyoichi sees a usable object; Imagase sees a chance to touch his fingers. None of those meanings wholly owns the object. Natsuki's response gives the past relationship a material disagreement, while Imagase's later explanation makes the repeated transfer newly knowing. Her former discomfort remains part of that history. [sp0115](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U04_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u04-o019); [sp0135](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U04_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u04-o039).

Her wish for a forceful kiss despite refusal belongs to her stated fantasy about their relation. It cannot become a general instruction for reading refusal as desire or supplying other people's consent. Kyoichi's objection to unilateral sex remains a boundary even when it inadequately answers her wish for passionate initiative. The manga lets their dissatisfaction and that ethical limit coexist. [sp0117](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U04_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u04-o021).

## Renewed interest creates an actual competing future

Natsuki proposes renewed dating and allows time for an answer. Kyoichi's surprise and nonrejection do not complete the agreement. The old thought of marrying after graduation is a past possible future, not a present engagement. She asks about his sex with Imagase; the question gives her knowledge of an issue without making her fully informed about every act or household term. At home Kyoichi withholds the actual proposal from Imagase, preserving different information on the two sides. [sp0144](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o002); [sp0145](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o003).

Imagase's plea that she withdraw exposes how directly her will conflicts with his. He admits his old university advice had a separating purpose and fears losing if she is serious. Natsuki refuses to surrender her own choice because he is in pain. She says she chose Kyoichi and intends forgiveness unless he ends the relation himself. The content of that intention matters: she gives herself a role in the future rather than accepting the role his devotion assigns her. [sp0151](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o009); [sp0154](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o012).

Her metaphors and judgments also injure. The ditch and piper/mouse language classifies the men from an interested position. Her demand that Kyoichi be prepared to live in Imagase's world can register a real difference between private access and commitment while retaining exclusionary assumptions about that world. She does not become ethically pure by identifying evasion. Imagase's competing claim that men need an exit likewise has an interested scope. Their exchange dramatizes incompatible ways of understanding what choosing someone requires. [sp0155](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o013); [sp0158](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o016).

## The choice exposes what she cannot receive

The café forces an answer into a shared frame. Imagase accepts the unchosen-withdraws proposal; Kyoichi addresses him directly and says he cannot choose him. Imagase's quiet acceptance and the departure of Kyoichi with Natsuki supply a real result. Natsuki has not simply manipulated a predetermined response from a passive man; he speaks and acts. At the same time his subsequent account reveals that the relief of an ordinary man/woman pairing does not settle his fear of losing Imagase. [sp0160](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o018); [sp0161](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o019).

Natsuki refuses to receive a confession that should be addressed to the absent man. This is an important boundary of her own. She can want Kyoichi and still reject serving as the audience who absorbs his concern for someone else while he avoids speaking to that person. Her dissatisfaction is not a therapist's diagnosis; she is affected by the conversation's unequal emotional destination. [sp0163](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o021); [sp0164](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o022).

The later holiday planning makes the cost concrete. She books, confirms and calls. The title-work ending does not show an answered call, cancellation or honest breakup with her. Kyoichi requires Imagase to settle another arrangement before renewed contact while leaving his own responsibility toward Natsuki unresolved on the page. That asymmetry remains in the corpus after the central reunion; the rain and bodily intimacy do not make her call disappear. [sp0176](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o034); [sp0200](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_U06_DEEP_READING.md#cm-u06-o058).

## A later life is reported without filling every missing scene

The adjacent closing comic535 reports Natsuki's marriage to a younger man. It gives her a life beyond the lost central choice and a continuing, abrasive familiarity with Imagase. Her envy of his bag and gendered judgment of his romantic vanity are interested banter. The analysis attributes the insult to her and does not turn it into an identity verdict. His earned-money retort is his reply; uncertain individual drink orders are not a basis for assigning a symbolic gender role to each person. [sp0535](../01%20Sequential%20Readings/CORNERED_MOUSE_S01_DEEP_READING.md#cm-s01-o033).

This later report qualifies a total absence-of-aftermath account, but it does not enact the earlier phone-call resolution or supply her married life in detail. The later creator note places Cassis Orange after Hummingbird; it adds approximate chronology through commentary rather than a newly witnessed reconciliation scene. The monograph therefore ends with both a reported new marriage and an unresolved historical gap.

Natsuki's recurring demand is that being treated kindly and being actively chosen should not be confused. She recognizes a failure that Chikako and Imagase also experience in different relations, but her wishes, abrasive language and concrete plans remain her own. She is capable of refusing a role, imposing a decision and judging another person unfairly. Her independent literary responsibility is substantial across U04, U06 and the closing report. The [corpus review](../07%20Audits%20and%20Handoffs/CORNERED_MOUSE_CORPUS_READINESS_AND_RECONSTRUCTION_REVIEW.md) withholds a portable behavioral model because these interested romantic scenes do not establish a repeated neutral/private/occupational baseline or the missing aftermath.
