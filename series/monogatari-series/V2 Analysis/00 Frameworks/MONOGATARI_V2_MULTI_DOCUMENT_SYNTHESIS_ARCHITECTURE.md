# 〈物語〉シリーズ V2 Multi-Document Synthesis Architecture

## Document map, primary-home rules, production phases, ledgers, and final delivery design

## 0. Why V2 should be multi-document

A single full-series response can state a convincing thesis about *Monogatari*, but the series is too large, too nonlinear, too narrator-dependent, and too linguistically self-conscious for one essay to preserve all of the evidence that produced that thesis.

The problem is not merely word count.

The series contains several analytical systems that interfere with one another when compressed into one document:

- more than two dozen novel-length installments across multiple named seasons;
- multiple narrators whose accounts cannot be treated as transparent;
- publication order, internal chronology, narrated order, and retrospective knowledge that frequently diverge;
- oddities that are literal supernatural entities while also carrying psychological, social, linguistic, and relational meaning;
- character arcs that leave Araragi's orbit and later become professional, artistic, divine, institutional, or familial;
- a specialist network with competing theories of knowledge and intervention;
- extensive wordplay, naming, kanji/ruby play, honorifics, pronouns, and stylized character voices;
- sexual and comic genre machinery that can simultaneously characterize, critique, indulge, and disturb;
- side stories whose narrative value lies partly in ordinary relational continuity rather than major plot;
- later books that transform the scale from adolescent private crisis to adult work, family, institutional response, markets, and social monster production.

The V2 corpus should therefore use a layered architecture.

The governing architectural principle is:

> **Separate documents by governing question, not by whichever character or theme happens to appear in the same volume.**

A second principle is:

> **Every major subject has one primary analytical home. Other documents may invoke it, but they should cross-reference rather than reproduce the same deep dive.**

A third principle is:

> **Reader-facing interpretation and evidentiary infrastructure are different layers. The final reader should not have to read every ledger, but every major claim should be recoverable through them.**

A fourth principle is specific to *Monogatari*:

> **No specialist document may stabilize a claim until the relevant narrator and chronology entries have been audited.**

# I. Proposed corpus layers

The complete V2 project should have five layers.

## Layer A — Governing framework

1. `MONOGATARI_V2_ANALYTICAL_METHOD.md`
2. `MONOGATARI_V2_MULTI_DOCUMENT_SYNTHESIS_ARCHITECTURE.md`
3. `SOURCE_INVENTORY.md`
4. `PROJECT_DECISIONS_AND_SCOPE.md`

These govern how the project works.

## Layer B — Sequential deep-reading archive

One canonical file for each supplied numbered novel or principal collection:

> `MONOGATARI_V2_VXX_DEEP_READING.md`

These are the archival interpretive backbone.

They preserve:

- publication-local reading;
- retrospective correction;
- narrator audit;
- oddity mechanics;
- relationship changes;
- Japanese-language evidence;
- locators;
- open questions.

They are not intended to be the final human-readable synthesis.

## Layer C — Longitudinal ledgers

Recommended:

1. `L01_PUBLICATION_AND_STORY_CHRONOLOGY_LEDGER.md`
2. `L02_NARRATOR_AND_FOCALIZATION_LEDGER.md`
3. `L03_ODDITY_CASE_RULE_AND_RESIDUE_LEDGER.md`
4. `L04_CHARACTER_ROLE_AND_SELF_STORY_LEDGER.md`
5. `L05_RELATIONSHIP_AND_ADDRESS_LEDGER.md`
6. `L06_SPECIALIST_ETHICS_AND_KNOWLEDGE_LEDGER.md`
7. `L07_BODY_APPETITE_AND_MATERIAL_RESIDUE_LEDGER.md`
8. `L08_JAPANESE_NAMES_WORDPLAY_TERMS_AND_PASSAGE_INDEX.md`
9. `L09_FIRST_PASS_V2_CLAIM_REVISION_LEDGER.md`

These are retrieval and audit infrastructure.

## Layer D — Reader-facing specialist synthesis

Proposed numbered corpus `00–20`, described below.

## Layer E — Continuous synthesis and archival delivery

1. `MONOGATARI_V2_FULL_SERIES_SYNTHESIS.md`
2. final `00_README_AND_CORPUS_MAP.md`
3. `CORPUS_MANIFEST.md`
4. `DELIVERY_AUDIT.md`
5. artifact checksums
6. source checksums/inventory where the environment permits
7. machine-readable corpus index if useful

# III. Continuous synthesis

## `MONOGATARI_V2_FULL_SERIES_SYNTHESIS.md`
### The reader-facing continuous argument

**Target:** approximately 35,000–55,000 words.

This should be drafted only after Documents 01–20 and the ledgers are substantially stable.

It should not reproduce their section structure.

It should instead read as a continuous literary argument moving through the series' transformations.

A provisional architecture could be:

1. story as survival technology;
2. the wound beneath the rescuer;
3. oddities as literal narrative bodies;
4. decentering Araragi and correcting the male narrator;
5. roles that save and roles that imprison;
6. fake, real, copy, recognition, and constructed personhood;
7. self-sacrifice, care, and the ethics of intervention;
8. bodies, appetite, sex, blood, and material residue;
9. post-resolution life: labor, art, godhood, work, and institutions;
10. home, marriage, family, and shared abnormality;
11. language as the medium through which monsters and persons become legible;
12. what "Monogatari" ultimately means after the supplied final volume.

The final thesis should emerge from the evidence.

Do not simply promote the first-pass formulation to dogma.

## `25_MAJOR_CHARACTER_RECONSTRUCTION_PROFILES.md`
### Strongly recommended derived-use interface

**Function:** Materialized reconstruction layer for high-fidelity character simulation, hypothetical scenarios, dialogue generation, and cross-series interaction work.

This document does **not** replace the character monographs, Document 12, Document 18, or Document 24. It compiles their current conclusions into one operational interface so a future reconstruction task does not have to re-join the entire specialist corpus from scratch.

**Authority ceiling:** derived use only.

The governing rule is:

> **D25 is a materialized view, not a new primary home.**

If compilation exposes a genuinely new interpretation, contradiction, or voice rule, update the appropriate canonical home first and only then propagate the result into D25.

**Prerequisites before first full compilation:**

- relevant major-character documents are substantially mature;
- `12_RELATIONSHIPS_LOVE_FRIENDSHIP_CARE_RIVALRY_AND_NONPOSSESSION.md` is substantially mature;
- `18_JAPANESE_LANGUAGE_NAMES_WORDPLAY_COMEDY_GENRE_AND_METAFICTION.md` is substantially mature;
- `24_CHARACTER_RELATIONSHIP_AND_REVISION_STATE_INDEX.md` is substantially mature;
- Hyakumonogatari and Mijikanamonogatari evidence ceilings remain in force;
- exact Japanese wording still routes to Document 23 / L08 / primary sources when precision matters.

**Target:** roughly 15,000–25,000 words for the first major-character implementation.

Initial coverage should prioritize characters for whom the corpus supports both longitudinal behavioral reconstruction and relationship-conditioned voice, likely including:

- Araragi;
- Hitagi;
- Shinobu;
- Hanekawa;
- Nadeko;
- Hachikuji;
- Kanbaru;
- Oikura;
- Kaiki;
- Ougi;
- Ononoki;
- Tsukihi.

Expand only when evidence density justifies it.

### Rough per-character schema

| Field | Reconstruction purpose |
|---|---|
| **Character / canonical state band** | Identify the exact era/state being instantiated; do not merge adolescent, transitional, and adult states. |
| **Stable personality core** | Traits and dispositions that remain recognizable across states. |
| **Mutable traits / developmental deltas** | What actually changes, strengthens, weakens, or becomes differently governed over time. |
| **Attentional model** | What the character notices first, ignores, overweights, eroticizes, fears, or treats as diagnostic. |
| **Decision hierarchy** | Which values normally win when loyalty, self-protection, duty, pride, curiosity, guilt, love, or institutional role conflict. |
| **Default behavioral heuristics** | Likely action under uncertainty, embarrassment, boredom, affection, rivalry, anger, guilt, threat, and ordinary social pressure. |
| **Emotional defenses and tells** | How feelings are displaced or revealed through jokes, silence, formality, aggression, self-deprecation, overexplanation, avoidance, etc. |
| **Speech baseline** | Pronouns, address, register, sentence texture, directness, politeness, lexical habits, and recurrent forms. |
| **Dialogue procedure** | Who initiates, reacts, interrupts, corrects, deflects, escalates, repairs, yields, or turns seriousness into comedy. |
| **Relationship-conditioned modes** | How behavior and register change with each major interlocutor; this is mandatory rather than optional. |
| **Stress / crisis mode** | What becomes exaggerated or suppressed under severe pressure and what decisions become more or less likely. |
| **Knowledge boundary** | What this state of the character knows, does not know, misremembers, or only infers. |
| **Self-story / blind spots** | Important inaccurate, incomplete, defensive, or historically local beliefs about self and others. |
| **Negative constraints / caricature traps** | Things the character is unlikely to say or do without extraordinary justification; common fandom or simulation distortions to avoid. |
| **Scenario-transfer boundaries** | Where extrapolation is strong, conditional, thin, or out-of-distribution: domestic, professional, romantic, crisis, unfamiliar-group, cross-series, etc. |
| **Diagnostic exemplars** | Short source-routed Japanese/behavioral exemplars sufficient to recover the pattern without reproducing long prose. |
| **Confidence / provenance** | HIGH / MODERATE / LOW / OPEN plus routing back to the monograph, D12, D18, D24, ledgers, and primary source. |

### Relationship-conditioned submatrix

For every important pair, preserve at minimum:

| Interlocutor | State band | Address/register | Typical initiative pattern | Comedy/conflict procedure | Affection/care expression | Boundary behavior | Stress deviation | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The purpose is to prevent generic voice reconstruction. Araragi-with-Hitagi, Araragi-with-Hachikuji, Araragi-with-Nadeko, and Araragi-with-Shinobu are not interchangeable instances of a single "Araragi voice"; the same principle applies throughout the cast.

### Operational use

Routine hypothetical-scenario work should be **derived-first, source-verified**:

1. load the relevant D25 character capsules;
2. resolve state and relationship condition through D24;
3. escalate to the character monograph / D12 / D18 when the scene is relationship-sensitive, ethically difficult, linguistically exact, or out-of-distribution;
4. reopen Japanese primary material only when exact wording, contradictory evidence, thin relationship-register cells, or confidence limits require it.

D25 should optimize retrieval and consistency, not hide uncertainty.

# VI. Primary-home rules

These rules are central to avoiding a 200,000-word corpus that says the same thing twelve times.

## 1. Character versus theme

Example:

- Araragi's self-sacrifice as part of **his developmental psychology** → Document 04.
- Self-sacrifice as a **series-wide ethical problem** → Document 14.
- Blood/regeneration as **body mechanics** → Document 16.
- Vampirism as **oddity metaphysics** → Document 03.

Each document may cross-reference the others, but only one owns the full treatment of each dimension.

## 2. Character versus relationship

Example:

- Hitagi's capacity to love → Document 05.
- Araragi/Hitagi as a changing dyad → Document 12.
- marriage, surname, and household institution → Document 13.

## 3. Character versus language

Example:

- Hitagi's voice as character defense → Document 05.
- feminine sentence endings, polite aggression, address terms, and translation problems as a language system → Document 18.

## 4. Oddity versus metaphor

Example:

- tiger mechanics and oddity classification → Document 03.
- what jealousy and self-rejection mean for Hanekawa → Document 07.

## 5. Side story versus primary character home

A side story may supply decisive evidence for Hitagi, Nadeko, or Hachikuji.

The interpretation should ultimately live in the relevant character/relationship document, while Document 19 explains the side story's release position, local function, and role in the larger surplus-life corpus.

# VII. Recommended length and scale

These are planning ranges, not quotas.

## Sequential archive

For 25+ novel-length works:

- ordinary volume: ~5,000–8,000 words;
- unusually dense or multi-arc volume: ~8,000–12,000 words;
- short collections may vary.

Likely sequential archive: **160,000–240,000 words**, depending on exact corpus.

## Specialist reader-facing layer

Documents 01–20 combined:

**~150,000–220,000 words** is plausible.

This is a ceiling, not a goal.

If primary-home discipline works, the mature specialist layer may be closer to 120,000–170,000 words.

## Continuous synthesis

**~35,000–55,000 words.**

It should be considerably shorter than the specialist corpus.

## Ledgers/indexes

Potentially **30,000–60,000+ words**, but these are reference infrastructure rather than ordinary reading.

## Total package

The complete archival project may therefore become large—potentially **350,000–500,000 words** across all layers.

That is acceptable if the reader-facing layer remains navigable.

The point of the architecture is precisely to avoid forcing a reader to consume the entire archive for every use case.

# IX. Why this architecture should improve on the first pass

The first synthesis was able to state powerful full-series propositions, but its one-document form necessarily compressed:

- differences among narrators;
- the exact evolution of oddity mechanics;
- the timing of later corrections;
- character voice and Japanese register;
- the distinction between Araragi's version of someone and that person's own narration;
- minor but persistent relationship development;
- specialist philosophical differences;
- the side-story ecosystem;
- institutional scale changes;
- counterevidence to elegant full-series theses.

V2 should preserve the strengths of that first reading while making the evidence structure explicit.

The old synthesis should therefore be treated neither as something to discard nor as something to defend.

It is the hypothesis set V2 is designed to test.

A useful revision rubric is:

| V1 claim | V2 outcome |
|---|---|
| Confirmed | Evidence supports substantially the same claim |
| Strengthened | More primary evidence or better formulation |
| Narrowed | True only for part of the corpus or under conditions |
| Complicated | Requires a second truth or counterexample |
| Weakened | Evidence is thinner than first assumed |
| Overturned | Later/closer reading supports a materially different model |
| Unresolved | Text sustains more than one live interpretation |

The V2 corpus becomes valuable precisely where it can say:

> "The first pass was directionally right, but too smooth."

# X. Final architectural standard

The completed corpus should permit three very different users to work from the same project:

### The reader

Can read 00, selected specialist documents, and the continuous synthesis without touching the technical ledgers.

### The analyst

Can trace a theme or character across volumes through the specialist documents and state indexes.

### The auditor

Can move from a mature claim through a specialist document, evidence ledger, volume analysis, locator, and original Japanese source.

That traceability chain is the final standard:

> **final synthesis → primary-home specialist document → V2 volume analysis → evidence ledger → Japanese source locator.**

For *Monogatari*, one extra link is mandatory:

> **... → narrator/focalization state.**

Because the work's central object is not merely the story.

It is the person telling it.
