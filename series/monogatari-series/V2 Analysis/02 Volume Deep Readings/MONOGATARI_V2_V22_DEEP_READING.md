# <MONOGATARI> V2 — Volume 22 Deep Reading
## 『撫物語』
### 第零話「なでこドロー」

> **Publication-local core:** 『撫物語』 turns Sengoku Nadeko's problem of inconsistent selfhood into literal form. The fifteen-year-old narrator draws four caricatured models of prior Nadekos—quiet Oto-Nadeko, ingratiating Kobi-Nadeko, furious Gyaku-Nadeko, and divine Kami-Nadeko—so that several versions of “herself” can divide the labor required for her manga dream. They immediately reject the job, because the dream belongs to present Nadeko rather than to the past-character models from which they were drawn. The resulting crisis demonstrates that earlier selves are neither disposable strangers nor sovereign truths. They are histories current Nadeko must bear, but every act of recollection is also a present act of representation. She can remember badly, stereotype herself, sentimentalize victimhood, overvalue anger, romanticize power, or redraw a role more honestly. Growth therefore means neither discovering one authentic Nadeko nor killing the previous ones. It means **accepting continuity while retaining the right and duty to revise how that continuity is lived and represented**.
>
> **Retrospective V2 core:** V22 is the strongest confirmation yet of the anti-essentialist line running from V11 through V19–V21. V19 said an aggressive or happier remainder does not monopolize truth; V21 said hardship does not reveal one `本当の自分`. V22 goes further by making the narrator herself the author of competing self-models. The crisis exposes a second ethical problem alongside selfhood: **authorship creates responsibility**. Kobi-Nadeko is hollow because current Nadeko drew “social ease” as a stereotype; Kami-Nadeko is dangerously simplified power; Oto-Nadeko is wrongly presumed helpless because present Nadeko still wants to protect the image of herself as victim. Nadeko's repair is therefore artistic and practical at once: redraw more honestly, memorialize what was used up, take present risk rather than hiding behind proxies, preserve life around effort, and build an adulthood in which manga, paid work, friendship, and future love can coexist. The volume's mature formula is not “leave the past behind.” It is: **carry the past without letting it dictate the only future available.**

# 2. Local thesis: character continuity without character imprisonment

V22 opens with one of the clearest self-theories in the series.

Nadeko imagines reading her fifteen years as fifteen volumes of a long-running manga and concludes that its protagonist would appear `キャラがブレブレ`: goals change, first person changes, design changes, speech changes, hobbies change, and even the loved boy appears differently depending on circumstance.

She is tempted to say that the girl who did earlier things feels like somebody else.

Then she rejects the escape.

`現在の自分を大切にするように、かつての自分も大切にしなきゃいけない`

This does not mean every past state is equally admirable. It means disowning the past would be another way of protecting the present self from responsibility.

Ononoki supplies the complementary principle. Nobody remains exactly the same across mood, body state, weather, context, audience, or recent experience; even the receiver's condition changes what an utterance becomes. None of that excuses conduct. Rather:

`それらすべてをひっくるめて、己自身として背負わなくちゃいけない`

V22's philosophy therefore begins between two rejected extremes:

1. **discontinuity escape:** “that was basically another person, so it is not mine”;
2. **identity imprisonment:** “because I once was/said/wanted X, X must forever define the real me.”

The middle position is historical continuity with corrigibility.

That is the conceptual foundation on which every later shikigami confrontation rests.

# 3. Retrospective V2 thesis: V19's anti-`true self` rule becomes an ethics of authorship

V19 established that `裏側` is not automatically `真実`. V21 established that an isolated or suffering Karen is not privileged as the one `本当のあたし`. V22 asks a more difficult question:

> What if the person who creates the simplified “true self” is the person herself?

Nadeko draws her past.

But she does not draw documentary reconstructions. Ougi later reminds her that all four shikigami were **drawn today by today's Nadeko**. Their supposed pastness is characterization.

That distinction is central.

- Kobi-Nadeko is not a time-traveling factual record of “summer Nadeko.”
- Gyaku-Nadeko is not anger isolated in pure form from history.
- Kami-Nadeko is not the divine Nadeko restored in complete psychological fidelity.
- Oto-Nadeko is not an untouched innocent recovered from before contamination.

They are **current representations built from remembered roles**.

This gives V22 a powerful authorial analogue to psychological memory. A remembered self can be both historically grounded and presently edited. The ethical task is not to stop representing, because identity necessarily requires representation. It is to become more accountable for the simplifications representation introduces.

Kobi makes this explicit. Nadeko drew her as an empty stereotype of effortless sociability. When Kobi later saves her and asks to be drawn cuter next time, Nadeko promises:

`次は、空っぽじゃなくしてあげるから。私の憧れを、誠実に投影してあげるから。`

That is not merely a joke about manga craft. It is a self-revision ethic.

> **The remedy for a reductive self-story is not no story. It is a more honest next draft.**

# 4. Narrative architecture

`なでこドロー` has six movements.

## Movement I — effort becomes a life-design problem (§§001–005)

Nadeko establishes the plurality/accountability thesis, then confronts an ordinary crisis: her parents tell her to stop “foolish” drawing and work after middle-school graduation. She has approximately ten months.

The narrative shifts the question from “does she want manga badly enough?” to “what kind of life makes sustained effort possible?”

## Movement II — four self-portraits become independent agents (§§006–010)

Ononoki proposes multiplying Nadeko so five Nadekos can parallelize practice. Nadeko draws four past-character designs. All four materialize; all four flee.

The shortcut fails because labor cannot be transferred simply by calling a copy “me.” The dream is specific to present Nadeko.

## Movement III — recovery begins through social worlds (§§011–018)

Nadeko and Ougi pursue the shikigami. Kobi enters Nadeko's current school and becomes socially central. Nadeko experiences intense envy of ordinary belonging, then captures Kobi. A call with Kanbaru confirms that changed voice/presentation need not mean disappearance. Nadeko then meets Oikura, who supplies a crucial lesson about situated speech and adulthood under uncertainty.

## Movement IV — prior aggression and specialist failure return (§§019–025)

At the Araragi home, Gyaku-Nadeko attacks Nadeko with carving tools. Nadeko is forced into embodied reflection on her own earlier violence. Separately, Oto/Kami lure Ononoki to Kita-Shirahebi Shrine and dismember her. Nadeko repairs the specialist and learns that professional competence includes surviving, tracking, escalating, and delegating—not preventing every failure.

## Movement V — bookstore war and creator responsibility (§§026–033)

The bookstore becomes a paper ecology of Nadeko copies. Kobi is reactivated and sacrificed saving present Nadeko. Kami mass-produces Oto-derived bodies and treats them instrumentally. Nadeko defeats Kami by redrawing Gyaku as current Nadeko, using a second proxy, disguising herself among the copies, and taking the decisive personal risk.

Victory remains ethically bitter because her proxies are destroyed.

## Movement VI — the quiet self was never merely passive (§§034–031/coda sequence)

Nadeko reconstructs Oto as the likely hidden organizer of much of the day's diversion—not as a master strategist in the strong forensic sense, but as the one self-model whose passive desire let others create space for her. She finds Oto waiting at Naoetsu High for Koyomi.

Nadeko does not tell her that the love was fake. She tells her that waiting must end and promises future love, future movement, and future art.

The after-story then shifts from crisis to infrastructure: Shinobu teaches operational limits to Nadeko's new ability; old hurtful words are revisited without demanding permanent speaker-stasis; Gaen offers paid specialist work; Nadeko's future becomes plural rather than purified.

# 5. Narrator and focalization audit

## 5.1 Nadeko is more self-aware, not transparent

V22 gives Nadeko extraordinary meta-awareness. She names:

- her own prior victim stance;
- the protective function of passivity;
- her attraction to effort as moral self-image;
- the difference between school absence as choice and inability;
- the appeal of ordinary social belonging;
- the possibility that she stereotypes past selves;
- her own tendency to interpret support as evidence of helplessness.

But none of this turns narration into omniscience.

Her most dramatic inference—Oto as the effective hidden organizer—is explicitly reconstructed late from clues. The text preserves uncertainty. Some behavior can be explained by Oto's influence; not every move is forensically proven to have been planned by her.

V2 should therefore distinguish:

- **TF:** Oto remains active, waits at Naoetsu High, and had relationships with the other shikigami that Nadeko initially misread.
- **SI:** Oto used the others as time-buying infrastructure for her own waiting goal.
- **IT:** the reveal functions as a critique of present Nadeko's tendency to confuse passivity with innocence/lack of agency.

## 5.2 Self-critique can itself become performance

Nadeko is so ready to call herself `駄目`, `馬鹿`, socially unfit, and culpable that V16's warning about self-condemnation remains relevant. V22 repeatedly prevents her from converting self-attack into truth.

Ononoki, Oikura, Shinobu, and later Gaen's reported evaluation all provide different corrective pressures:

- cost must be recognized without treating the dream itself as foolish;
- one parental utterance is not the whole parent;
- one old cruel statement is not the whole speaker forever;
- one failed operation can reveal recovery competence.

The narrative therefore preserves self-critique while challenging self-sentencing.

## 5.3 “Character” vocabulary is deliberately double

Nadeko constantly uses manga vocabulary—`キャラ`, `モブ`, `主人公`, `作者`, `NPC`, `描き直す`, `作品`—to understand people and shikigami.

This is not only metafictional play. It is her emerging cognitive vocation. Drawing becomes a language for:

- decomposition;
- exaggeration;
- role assignment;
- simulation;
- memory;
- planning;
- and repair.

But V22 also makes that language ethically dangerous. To call Kobi a stereotype is to risk drawing her as empty; to make copies into NPCs is to make them easier to expend.

The narrator is learning not merely to tell stories, but to take responsibility for what storytelling does to persons.

# 6. Chronology and temporal placement

The exact calendar day is not stated. The source gives several anchors:

- Nadeko is **fifteen**.
- Her school refusal began in the previous November; excluding the god interval, she has been continuously absent since February.
- Returning to school is described as approximately **half a year** after her last ordinary attendance.
- Oikura has returned to the area **this April** for university.
- approximately **ten months** remain until Nadeko's middle-school graduation.
- Karen is now in high school.
- Ougi is socially legible to Nadeko as a male Naoetsu High second-year student.

These support a **spring / early-summer post-Final-Season placement**, approximately ten months before graduation, without licensing an invented date.

The main chase, school incident, Araragi-house encounter, shrine confrontation, bookstore climax, and Oto confrontation occur within one extended day. The Shinobu/Gaen material is near-term aftermath.

The story also repeatedly activates earlier time layers without treating them as frozen truth:

- Oto is “still in last June” psychologically;
- Kobi models a prior social-performance period;
- Gyaku models the post-haircut outburst period;
- Kami models godhood;
- current Nadeko interprets all four from the present.

V22 thus has a simple physical chronology and a complex representational chronology.

# 7. Causal reconstruction

The event chain is unusually useful because it begins from an apparently rational productivity hack.

1. Nadeko's parents impose a post-graduation work expectation.
2. Nadeko believes she needs a visible manga result before that deadline.
3. Ononoki identifies the unsustainable arithmetic of brute-force effort.
4. She proposes multiplying Nadeko via shikigami rather than multiplying daily hours.
5. Nadeko draws four prior self-models.
6. Ononoki helps materialize them.
7. The shikigami refuse to labor for a dream that is not theirs and escape.
8. Their characterizations become oddity abilities and action tendencies.
9. Nadeko and allies pursue them.
10. Nadeko discovers that her own assumptions about each self-model influence her interpretation of their behavior.
11. Kami's escalation turns the bookstore into a mass-produced copy ecology.
12. Nadeko wins by using representation more deliberately—but still incurs proxy costs.
13. The remaining Oto confrontation reveals that the most passive self-model had her own goal.
14. The cleanup and Gaen offer convert failed experimentation into a possible vocational route.

The central causal lesson is not “magic is dangerous.” It is:

> **A shortcut can preserve the same ethical problem it was designed to solve.**

The original problem was that Nadeko wanted to spend more of “herself” on effort than one life could safely provide. The shikigami plan answers by producing more selves to spend. The arithmetic works; the theory of agency does not.

# 8. Oddity dossiers

## 8.1 Drawing-to-shikigami materialization

V20 established that Nadeko's drawing could participate in an artificial oddity event. V22 confirms a broader ability: under Ononoki-mediated conditions, Nadeko's drawings can become three-dimensional agents, and later she can materialize ordinary depicted objects herself.

The rule remains bounded.

- representation quality matters;
- categorization/naming appears to shape behavior;
- shikigami autonomy is real enough to resist creator intention;
- simultaneous control is limited for present Nadeko;
- shikigami can produce lower-fidelity shikigami;
- food copied by drawing fails as functional food;
- reproduction of machines/organics requires testing;
- creator replacement is a recognized risk if copy/original distinctions collapse.

V22 therefore rejects an unlimited “Nadeko can draw anything into perfect reality” interpretation.

## 8.2 Kobi-Nadeko: social accommodation as coercive field

Kobi's defining property is not generic friendliness. She performs empty agreement so thoroughly that classmates form around her as a `人の壁`.

Nadeko's reaction is double:

- she envies the apparent ordinary belonging;
- she recognizes that the effect has become oddity-like influence over others.

This is important because the volume does not denounce sociability itself. Nadeko genuinely wants connection. The critique targets frictionless universal accommodation as both hollow self-erasure and a potential form of social coercion.

## 8.3 Gyaku-Nadeko: disinhibited body and labor refusal

Gyaku's exaggerated violence literalizes the energy of the classroom outburst, but the narrative carefully exposes caricature. Nadeko even notices that the carving tools actually belong more directly to Oto-era history. The self-models bleed into one another.

Her oddity-like property is physical limiter release. Yet her motive is comic and concrete: she refuses forced labor.

This matters. The “angry Nadeko” is not secretly a pure liberatory self. She is anger plus current author's labor caricature plus borrowed tools plus present situation.

## 8.4 Kami-Nadeko: power without adequate deliberation

Kami's strength is enormous, but Nadeko describes her cognition as simplified/childlike. She uses lures, copies, and disposable bodies, then chooses `両方殺そっ` when confused.

V22 thereby strengthens V11: godhood was not authentic liberation. Power can magnify a narrowed model of agency while reducing the need to negotiate with other minds.

## 8.5 Oto-Nadeko: shyness, people-repelling, and protected passivity

Oto's principal ability is described as `人見知りゆえの人払い`: shyness externalized into a field that clears spaces around her.

Nadeko initially reads Oto as victim of the stronger three. Later evidence reverses the simplification. Oto lent Gyaku tools; may have proposed exchanges; and appears to have benefited from the others' diversion while she pursued her own goal.

The correct conclusion is **not** “Oto was secretly the evil mastermind.” The stronger claim is:

> Passivity is a mode of agency too, and being genuinely vulnerable does not make a person causally inert.

That is a direct refinement of V02/V11's victim + agent architecture.

## 8.6 Ononoki's damaged body as professional residue

Kami and Oto's shrine trap dismembers Ononoki. The professional is not invulnerable. Her skill lies partly in what survives failure:

- detachable body;
- a hidden tracking hand;
- ability to be repaired;
- escalation options;
- willingness to let Nadeko lead her own recovery operation.

Specialist competence becomes recoverability, not zero-error perfection.

# 9. Character-pressure analysis: Sengoku Nadeko

## 9.1 From being drawn by others to drawing herself

Nadeko's early series identity is dominated by external reading:

- Araragi's remembered shy childhood girl;
- classmates' cute girl;
- Tsukihi's diagnosis;
- Kuchinawa's ventriloquized desire;
- Kaiki's counter-story;
- readers' archetypal expectations.

V22 shifts her position. She is now literally the illustrator.

That does not instantly emancipate her. She discovers that **self-authorship can reproduce the same reductions once imposed from outside**.

She draws Kobi too empty.
She draws Gyaku too cleanly violent.
She reads Oto too innocently.
She reads Kami too straightforwardly as the controlling threat.

Becoming author therefore creates responsibility rather than sovereignty.

## 9.2 Manga is a chosen future, not hidden essence

V13's manga material proved that Nadeko had a private practice and imaginative life exceeding Araragi's model. V22 strongly advances it into a vocation.

But the volume repeatedly refuses to call manga her one true self.

Nadeko is also:

- a daughter;
- a missing student;
- a friend of Tsukihi and Ononoki;
- an acquaintance capable of reconnecting with Kanbaru and Oikura;
- a former god;
- a potential specialist;
- a girl who may love again.

Her final future is explicitly **two pairs of sandals**, not purification into Artist Nadeko.

## 9.3 Effort as a new avoidance technology

One of V22's harshest insights is that improvement itself can become an escape.

Nadeko recognizes that the image of herself staying home, denying leisure, and grinding at drawing felt `ストイック` and impressive. The cost—schooling, social capacity, family negotiation, physical sustainability—became invisible because effort was morally flattering.

`努力に逃げずに、人から隠れずに`

Her promise to Oto eventually names this directly: she will not **escape into effort**.

That is a major maturation of the series' rescue/autonomy framework. Self-improvement can be self-erasure when it lets a person avoid living.

## 9.4 “I exist”

When Kanbaru hears Nadeko again, Nadeko knows her voice, pronoun, hair, and behavior have changed. Her answer is not to prove she is still the same cute girl.

`活動しています。存在しています`

The formulation is deliberately modest. Continuity is not sameness of presentation. Existence is demonstrated by ongoing activity.

## 9.5 Becoming an older sister to her own past

The Oto confrontation ends not with killing, sealing, or humiliating the youngest-looking self. Nadeko hugs her, promises future love, and pats her head in the way Oikura had patted hers.

She notices that she has grown taller.

`千石撫子、十五歳。`

The movement is subtle: she becomes capable of extending care backward without surrendering present direction to the past.

# 10. Relationship-state analysis

## 10.1 Nadeko and Ononoki: constructed personhood becomes friendship

V20 already showed Ononoki narrating affect that exceeded the simplest “emotionless doll” description. V22 advances that development from inside Nadeko's life.

Ononoki now visits Nadeko's room roughly four times a week, ostensibly to complain about Tsukihi and to model for drawing practice. The arrangement is reciprocal:

- Ononoki receives respite from surveillance work;
- Nadeko receives a technically extraordinary art model;
- both receive company without requiring the relationship to mimic conventional school friendship.

Ononoki later says explicitly that one benefit of dealing with Tsukihi has been becoming Nadeko's friend. When Nadeko calls her a good person, Ononoki replies in effect that if Nadeko sees goodness in her, it says something about Nadeko as well.

Nothing here requires discovering a “human heart” secretly buried inside the doll. Ononoki remains artificial, role-built, professionally deployed, physically detachable, and self-consciously strange. Friendship is not evidence against construction. It is something constructed beings can participate in.

The most important relationship development comes when Ononoki's behavior crosses the line from assignment into private concern. Her later employment maneuver for Nadeko is framed as business, but Nadeko correctly notices how implausibly convenient that explanation is.

The source leaves some of Ononoki's planning inferential, but one claim is strong:

> **Professional role and personal friendship coexist rather than cancel each other.**

That substantially strengthens V14/V20's model of her personhood.

## 10.2 Nadeko and Ougi: the critic function survives Araragi decentering

V22 is important evidence that Ougi's post-V18/V19 role is not merely an Araragi-specific narrative device.

Nadeko encounters Ougi as the socially stabilized male high-school student she believes him to be. Ougi performs ignorance, leaks knowledge, supplies transport, causes trouble, creates opportunities, and repeatedly refuses the clean position of friend or enemy.

Nadeko concludes:

`敵か味方かわかりません。おそらくどちらでもない`

That is a mature Ougi formulation.

Ougi's function is diagnostic friction. He does not simply give Nadeko the answer. He forces her to articulate what she already knows, supplies logistical disturbance, and allows her own inference to become actionable. The line `僕は何も知らないよ──きみが知っているんだよ` reprises the self-knowledge mechanism without returning to V18's punitive self-execution model.

Ougi therefore continues as critic/safety valve/partner **outside Araragi focalization**.

## 10.3 Nadeko and Oikura: parallel injury without merger

The park conversation with Oikura is one of the volume's most important non-supernatural scenes.

Both girls have histories of:

- school absence;
- family dysfunction;
- taking words literally and catastrophically;
- intense attachment to Araragi without healthy relational access;
- difficulty imagining ordinary social futures.

Yet the novel does not collapse them into mirrors.

Oikura is now a university student and is herself skipping classes because fitting in remains difficult. She does not arrive as recovered mentor. Her advice gains authority partly because she openly includes herself in the warning.

The key principle is:

`その厳しい意見が、親のすべてだと思わないこと`

A harsh utterance can be real and harmful without exhausting the speaker's total position across all times and moods.

This directly links the opening Ononoki rule to family ethics. It also anticipates the later Shinobu scene.

Oikura then offers a minimal adulthood formulation: one can remain anxious about the future and still, if one keeps living, become an adult.

That is deliberately less triumphant than “everything works out.” It gives Nadeko continuity without guarantee.

The physical head-pat matters because Nadeko's hair/body have historically been sites of projection, defense, eroticization, and violation. Here touch is experienced as pleasant care from a woman whose own development is incomplete. V22 thereby gives bodily contact a new relational register without pretending prior harm disappeared.

## 10.4 Nadeko and Kanbaru: recognition after visible change

The brief telephone reconnection with Kanbaru is structurally small but thematically precise. Nadeko knows she no longer sounds or appears like the girl Kanbaru remembers. Rather than apologizing for that discontinuity, she states continued existence.

The relationship is not suddenly intimate. What matters is that a former peripheral connection remains retrievable even after role change.

## 10.5 Nadeko and Tsukihi: non-gentle continuity

Tsukihi remains the friend who does not romanticize Nadeko. She helps, visits, brings clothes, covers for damage, and also says cruel or intrusive things. V22 does not revise V11's violation of Nadeko's hair into benign wisdom. It does, however, show that their relationship survives disagreement, injury, and change.

This is useful because it prevents relational continuity from requiring retrospective moral purification.

## 10.6 Nadeko and Shinobu: old enemies without obligatory reconciliation

The epilogue gives one of the strongest examples of post-conflict relation in the series.

Nadeko and Shinobu have attempted to kill each other thousands of times in the prior crisis. They now eat doughnuts, test magic, discuss manga, and revisit a hurtful earlier statement.

Nadeko does not call this `和解`.

That restraint matters.

A relationship can become conversational without the past being erased, forgiven in full, or reclassified as harmless.

The same possibility is extended toward Hitagi. Nadeko does **not** expect forgiveness. She becomes curious about who Hitagi actually is beyond the preserved “romantic rival” role.

That is exactly the kind of non-possession V2 has been tracking:

> future relation can remain possible without entitlement to absolution.

# 11. Rescue, help, and intervention ethics

V22 contains multiple intervention models and makes none of them pure.

## 11.1 Parents: legitimate boundary, uncertain method

Nadeko's parents have materially supported her after disappearance, hospitalization, and withdrawal. Their demand that she work after compulsory school is not automatically abusive or irrational. Ononoki treats middle-school graduation as a defensible boundary because Nadeko's drawing life is being subsidized by others.

But the text also refuses to make the parents' wording complete truth. Oikura warns that a single harsh sentence can be mood-bound, indirect, regretted, or a clumsy attempt to provoke another decision.

The ethical lesson is not “ignore parents” or “obey parents.” It is:

> **interpret the person and the relationship, not merely the literal sentence.**

The same rule applies to specialists, friends, enemies, and narrators.

## 11.2 Ononoki's shortcut: good care can preserve a bad premise

Ononoki notices Nadeko's time problem and proposes parallel labor. The idea is technically creative. It also accepts Nadeko's initial premise that the answer is somehow to compress ten thousand hours into ten months.

This is a classic intervention risk:

> solving the stated problem without first asking whether the stated problem should govern the life.

The crisis later forces both characters to revise toward sustainable plurality.

## 11.3 Kobi's rescue: a created person exceeds her assigned function

Kobi catches the collapsing shelf and saves Nadeko. Nadeko had earlier reduced her to social emptiness. The rescue demonstrates that the character can act beyond the creator's initial ethical valuation.

Yet Kobi is then crushed.

The scene refuses easy sentiment. Nadeko receives real rescue from a self she authored badly, and that rescue creates debt rather than a celebratory proof that Kobi was “good all along.”

## 11.4 Using proxies: risk-sharing versus expendability

Nadeko's final plan reactivates Gyaku and creates an additional current-Nadeko proxy. Both are destroyed as decoys.

Nadeko correctly sees resemblance to Kami's use of disposable copies.

Ononoki offers a distinction: present Nadeko herself takes the greatest risk. She is not directing expendable bodies from a safe zone.

That distinction is ethically relevant—but it is not total absolution.

V2 should preserve both truths:

1. **STRENGTHEN:** willingness to bear the decisive risk distinguishes shared-risk intervention from pure instrumentalization.
2. **OPEN / VJ:** a creator still owes ethical attention to created agents used as disposable tactical resources, even if she also risks herself.

Nadeko's decision to memorialize them in art is therefore not payment that cancels the cost. It is one available form of responsibility after irreversible use.

## 11.5 Ononoki's cleanup boundary

After the oddity threat is resolved, Ononoki tells Nadeko that ordinary physical cleanup is now professional work and Nadeko is no longer wanted on scene.

This is mature division of labor.

Nadeko owns the causal responsibility but does not become more ethical by attempting tasks beyond her capacity. Professional responsibility can mean taking over where a minor cannot adequately repair consequences herself.

This is an important correction to “take responsibility” rhetoric:

> **responsibility does not require doing every repair personally.**

## 11.6 Shinobu's skill audit as prevention

Nadeko reads Shinobu's post-crisis experiments as a disguised lesson in operational limits. This is a strong inference rather than stated motive. Still, the sequence clearly functions as a safety audit:

- what can be copied;
- whether copied food is edible;
- whether machines work without internal knowledge;
- whether living things reproduce life;
- where fidelity fails.

That is V14 prevention at a new scale: teach the dangerous ability's boundaries before the next emergency.

## 11.7 Gaen's employment offer: recovery competence as professional signal

Ononoki reports Gaen's principle:

`失敗しない奴より、失敗したときに、それを取り戻せる奴が好き`

This should remain a character-reported account of Gaen's evaluation, not universal truth. But it captures V22's mature professional thesis:

> **zero failure is not the only measure of competence; correction under novel conditions is itself a capability.**

That is especially apt in a series where specialist knowledge is repeatedly incomplete.

# 12. Specialist knowledge and metaphysics

## 12.1 Shikigami as proxy, not identical self

Ononoki describes shikigami as stronger in代理人 character than vampire thralls. This is a useful model, not a complete metaphysical treatise.

Nadeko's four creations demonstrate why proxy identity is unstable:

- they are modeled on her;
- they are made from her drawing ability/history;
- they are still capable of goals that conflict with present Nadeko;
- their destruction does not physically kill present Nadeko;
- they can be redrawn with altered design.

The volume therefore gives no basis for either extreme:

- “they are literally the same continuous consciousness as Nadeko”;
- “they are meaningless disposable drawings.”

They are created agents in a morally relevant intermediate state.

## 12.2 Naming and design have causal force

Kobi's characterization becomes more strongly ingratiating after the label is fixed. Clothing, hair, tools, and role expectations similarly become behavioral affordances.

V22 thereby extends Monogatari's longstanding name/wordplay concern into explicit authorial mechanics:

> **description does not merely reflect an entity; it can help produce the entity that later seems to justify the description.**

This is especially important for character analysis. Archetypal naming can become self-fulfilling epistemology.

## 12.3 Nested shikigami and degradation

Gyaku can create a second shikigami under Nadeko's broader representational capacity, but the resulting current-Nadeko copy behaves more like a simple NPC. The reason is unresolved.

Possible models include:

- generation depth reduces fidelity;
- the shikigami lacks creator-level intentional complexity;
- current Nadeko's own self-model is underdescribed;
- control bandwidth is the limiting factor.

V22 does not settle the mechanism. Record the effect; leave cause OPEN.

## 12.4 Hachikuji's shrine and specialist impartiality

Ononoki resists casually drawing the current deity into cleanup of a mess she and Nadeko created. She worries that gratuitous divine favoritism could undermine trust in the shrine/deity.

This is a subtle institutional extension of V18's Hachikuji choice. Once the deity role becomes socially operative, personal friendship cannot automatically authorize preferential use of institutional power.

## 12.5 Materialization is not perfect creation

The bad doughnut is a critical limit case. Appearance can be reproduced while function fails.

That fits a wider Monogatari rule:

> representation and reality can become causally entangled without becoming identical.

A drawn object can be materially present and still fail the internal properties that make the original useful.

# 13. Japanese voice and address

V22 is one of the strongest voice documents for Nadeko because it lets her compare and perform multiple prior registers directly.

## 13.1 Current Nadeko

Current Nadeko's narration is:

- self-mocking;
- highly metatextual;
- manga-literate;
- more assertive than V02;
- capable of analytic qualification;
- still prone to self-devaluation;
- increasingly willing to state desire directly.

The key change is not simply vocabulary. It is ownership. She can say `私は今、漫画家を目指しています` without hiding behind third-person cuteness or an invented snake voice.

## 13.2 Oto-Nadeko

Oto retains quiet, high, disappearing speech; her final question is direct precisely because the rest of her register avoids assertion:

`じゃあ、もう、好きじゃないの？`

The sequence makes visible that silence and passivity can contain stubborn desire rather than absence of desire.

## 13.3 Kobi-Nadeko

Kobi's speech is hyper-accommodating and semantically empty. She agrees with everyone. The linguistic performance is social lubricant taken to the point where person-specific content nearly vanishes.

This is not merely “fake speech.” It is a functional register whose success becomes its danger.

## 13.4 Gyaku-Nadeko

Gyaku uses `俺様`, sentence-final aggression, `ああん!?`, and deliberately delinquent phrasing. V11 had already shown that aggressive voice is not privileged as essential truth. V22 makes the point comic by turning it into an overdrawn character style.

Yet the anger she carries can still be real.

The distinction is:

> genuine affect can be expressed through a constructed voice without making the voice the person's essence.

## 13.5 Kami-Nadeko

Kami's speech is simplified, casual, and often detached from the violence she orders. The chilling `両方殺そっ` is dangerous not because it reveals hidden sophistication but because overwhelming capacity no longer requires reflective language.

## 13.6 Address changes and recovered relational distance

Nadeko's references are also part of her development:

- Oikura becomes `育お姉ちゃん`, an affectionate but not childlike relational bridge;
- Araragi remains largely unnamed in sections where the old fixation is being dismantled;
- Hitagi shifts from abstract rival to a person Nadeko might someday want to know;
- Ononoki's `撫公` simultaneously teases and marks a durable personal relation.

# 14. Names, wordplay, drawing, and translation-sensitive terms

## 14.1 `撫物語` / `なでこドロー`

The volume title compresses Nadeko's name (`撫`) into the series' naming grammar. `ドロー` works across several fields:

- draw an image;
- draw/produce something from paper;
- draw as in a card/game action;
- pull out or summon;
- potentially a draw/stalemate rather than clean victory.

The story literalizes drawing as character production and tactical action.

## 14.2 Oto / Kobi / Gyaku / Kami

The self-labels are not neutral archive names. They are creative descriptions that influence how current Nadeko sees and therefore draws the past.

Translation should preserve their compact caricatural quality rather than making them formal psychological categories.

- `おと撫子`: quiet/meek coding;
- `媚び撫子`: ingratiating / currying-favor coding;
- `逆撫子`: reverse/against-the-grain plus `逆撫で` punning aggression;
- `神撫子`: god Nadeko.

The later joke-variants `ルマ撫子` and `クール撫子` are situational labels, not new ontological identities.

## 14.3 `自意識ゆえの被害者意識`

Nadeko's proposed Oto skill—roughly victim-consciousness arising from self-consciousness—is deliberately self-accusatory. It should not be translated/used as proof that her earlier external victimization was unreal.

The novel itself explicitly rejects `いじめられるほうも悪い` logic.

## 14.4 `活動しています。存在しています`

This modest pair matters. “I am active. I exist.” It describes identity as ongoing practice rather than preserved appearance.

## 14.5 `二足の草鞋`

The final idiom literally imagines wearing two pairs of straw sandals: holding two occupations/roles at once. It is the opposite of a purification arc. Nadeko's adult future is allowed to be multiple.

# 15. Body, appetite, sexuality, comedy, and material life

## 15.1 Hair is no longer the whole body-story

Nadeko's hair has carried extraordinary symbolic weight: concealment, cuteness, eroticized gaze, Tsukihi's intervention, aggression after the haircut, later short-hair self-presentation.

V22 refuses to make any haircut the final truth.

The most important new bodily moment is Oikura touching/patting Nadeko's head and Nadeko experiencing it positively. This does not erase the autonomy violation of prior hair-cutting. It proves only that similar bodily locations can acquire different meanings across consent and relationship.

## 15.2 Clothing as authored role

The shikigami clothes are selected to make them legible as different character periods. Those designs then affect behavior and tactical possibilities.

V22 repeatedly jokes about embarrassing clothing, but beneath the comedy is a serious rule: body presentation is part of role construction, not a transparent window into essence.

## 15.3 `生活、大事です`

Before the final confrontation Nadeko insists on eating and resting.

This simple phrase is one of the volume's most important corrections.

The earlier arithmetic treated bodily needs as wasted hours. V22 reverses the hierarchy:

> **life is not the interruption of effort; life is the substrate that makes effort possible.**

Food, sleep, toilets, bathing, clothing, fatigue, and injury belong inside the productivity model rather than outside it.

## 15.4 Sexualization and self-awareness

The novel repeatedly jokes about Oto-derived near-nudity, bloomers, school swimsuit, and embarrassing public exposure. Nadeko's current narration is acutely aware that earlier presentation was sexualized or implausible.

V2 should retain the dual observation familiar from prior volumes:

- the text is self-aware about the absurd gaze;
- self-awareness does not mean the work ceases to participate in the sexualized comedy.

No “mature Nadeko” purity should be manufactured by pretending this layer vanished.

# 16. Family, home, school, and institution

## 16.1 The Nadeko household: support, dependency, and failed negotiation

Nadeko's parents have sustained her materially while she draws and remains out of school. Nadeko recognizes that she has treated the conditions of her effort as given rather than negotiated.

The volume's criticism runs in both directions:

- Nadeko has failed to build a viable environment for effort;
- her parents' prior overindulgence may have delayed autonomy;
- their new hard line may be understandable without being the entirety of their position;
- literal command-obedience is not the same as healthy family communication.

The family problem is therefore not solved by deciding who is “right.” It is a coordination failure in which care, guilt, fear, and dependency are poorly translated.

## 16.2 School: both real loss and non-total institution

Kobi's school episode proves that Nadeko still desires the social world she abandoned. Her failure to become part of the class hurts because ordinary belonging is not fake or beneath the artistic dream.

At the same time, Oikura gives the necessary limit:

`学校に行けなくなったくらいでは、人生は終わらない`

School matters. School is not all life.

That is more mature than either romanticizing withdrawal or treating attendance as moral worth.

## 16.3 Specialist work as alternative institutional bridge

Gaen's reported offer creates an unexpected route between isolation and ordinary employment. Nadeko does not need to become socially generic before she can contribute. Her unusual history can become domain competence under adult supervision.

This must not be read as “trauma was worth it because it produced a job.” The causal history remains costly. The point is that damaged history can be repurposed without being justified retroactively.

# 17. Major themes

## 17.1 Plurality without fragmentation

A person can be contextually many without becoming many unrelated moral subjects.

## 17.2 Accountability without stasis

One must own earlier words/actions without requiring lifelong repetition of earlier beliefs.

## 17.3 Effort has a budget

Time, body, relationships, money, education, and other people's labor are all part of the cost of effort.

## 17.4 Representation creates consequences

How a person is drawn, named, or categorized can shape the later behavior used as evidence that the category was correct.

## 17.5 Failure is an event, not an identity

V22 repeatedly values what happens **after** a plan fails.

## 17.6 Ordinary life is not the enemy of ambition

School, meals, sleep, friendship, work, romance, and manga are competing goods to be integrated, not contaminants to be sacrificed to one pure calling.

## 17.7 Victimhood and agency remain relation-specific

Nadeko can be a real victim of an external curse and still later recognize evasive agency. Acknowledging agency must not become retrospective victim blaming.

## 17.8 Love can end as waiting without becoming false

Oto's love is not dismissed as childish unreality. Current Nadeko stops organizing life around it.

## 17.9 Forgiveness is not the price of future contact

Nadeko can imagine knowing Hitagi better without expecting forgiveness.

## 17.10 Art can carry residue

Drawing cannot undo destroyed shikigami or past harm. It can preserve memory, make debt visible, and produce a more honest next representation.

# 18. Counterreadings, disconfirming evidence, and limits

A strong V22 reading needs to resist several attractive overstatements.

## Counterreading A — “The four shikigami are literally Nadeko's past selves returned”

**Rejected.** Ougi's correction and Nadeko's own narration make clear that they were drawn **today** by present Nadeko. Their past resemblance is characterization, not time travel or perfect memory reconstruction.

What survives from the stronger claim is that current self-understanding is materially structured by remembered past roles.

## Counterreading B — “Oto-Nadeko was the evil mastermind all along”

**Downgraded to SI/IT.** Nadeko reconstructs Oto as the hidden beneficiary/organizer of diversion. Evidence supports more agency than Nadeko initially granted her: tool lending, clothing coordination, survival through copies, and the final waiting goal. But the full degree of orchestration remains retrospective inference.

The important revision does not require villainizing Oto. It requires abandoning the equation of quietness with causal innocence.

## Counterreading C — “Nadeko admits the bullying/curse was her fault”

**Rejected.** In the car with Ougi she explicitly refuses `いじめられるほうも悪い` logic and continues to judge what others did as terrible. Her correction is about her own avoidance, failure to communicate, and tendency to organize the world through permanent victim status.

V02's external curse victimhood remains true.

## Counterreading D — “Kobi proves ordinary friendship is fake”

**Rejected.** Nadeko's envy of Kobi is sincere and painful. V22 treats ordinary belonging as a genuine good. The problem is Kobi's contentless universal accommodation and oddity-level influence, not friendship or school sociality themselves.

## Counterreading E — “The manga dream is the authentic Nadeko and school/social life are distractions”

**Rejected.** The volume's entire effort critique is directed against that purification. Manga is chosen and serious, but life is a multi-good structure.

## Counterreading F — “The parents are simply correct because Nadeko is spoiled”

**Rejected as total account.** Their material boundary is intelligible, and Nadeko recognizes dependency. Oikura also warns against reading one harsh utterance as the whole parent. The family relationship remains underdescribed and ethically mixed.

## Counterreading G — “Nadeko has now fully matured beyond romantic fixation”

**Rejected.** Her promise to Oto does not erase love. She promises to remember the happiness of loving, to love someone again, and to turn the failed romance into story. The change is from **waiting** to **carrying**.

## Counterreading H — “Nadeko and Hitagi are reconciled”

**Rejected.** Nadeko explicitly does not expect forgiveness. She only becomes willing to imagine knowing Hitagi as a person rather than preserving her as a rival role.

## Counterreading I — “Ononoki deliberately engineered every failure so Nadeko would get hired”

**Rejected as TF.** Nadeko strongly infers that Ononoki's later choices gave her room to earn a record of recovery. The initial four-shikigami escape and Ononoki's dismemberment are not established as planned.

## Counterreading J — “Gaen's praise means failure is good”

**Rejected.** The reported criterion values recovery **after** failure. It does not romanticize preventable damage. The bookstore still required costly professional cleanup.

## Counterreading K — “Risking herself makes Nadeko's use of proxy lives automatically ethical”

**Rejected.** Shared risk distinguishes her from Kami's safe-zone instrumentalization, but Nadeko's moral unease is intentionally preserved. Art as memorial is response to residue, not erasure of it.

## Counterreading L — “Shinobu forgetting her cruel line makes the harm unreal”

**Rejected.** Nadeko says directly that the remark still struck and shaped her. The revision concerns permanent identity claims about the speaker, not whether the recipient was hurt.

## Counterreading M — “A changed speaker has no responsibility for past words”

**Rejected.** The opening and Shinobu scene must be read together. Nadeko refuses both disowning the past and treating past speech as an eternal command that the speaker never change.

## Counterreading N — “Drawing skill is unrestricted reality warping”

**Rejected.** The epilogue immediately audits functional failures, including inedible copied food. Capability remains conditional and partly unknown.

# 20. How V22 revises V20–V21 and the Final Season freeze

## 20.1 V19 anti-essentialism becomes operational

V19 said a hidden/reversed self lacks authenticity monopoly. V22 makes that rule practical. Nadeko can literally redesign a self-model and watch changed design create new behavior.

The question shifts from “which self is true?” to:

> **Which representation are you using, what does it leave out, and what responsibility follows when the representation acts in the world?**

## 20.2 V20 decentering becomes self-authorship

V20 showed characters existing beyond Araragi's gaze. V22 makes that development especially decisive for Nadeko. Koyomi is almost entirely absent as active person. His importance survives as residue inside Oto.

Nadeko's new adulthood infrastructure is built through:

- Ononoki;
- Tsukihi;
- Ougi;
- Kanbaru;
- Oikura;
- Shinobu;
- Gaen;
- her parents;
- her own artistic practice.

That is a major decentering of the romance that once organized her world.

## 20.3 V21 “origin without sovereignty” applies to self-history

V21 showed that Kiss-shot's taught voice/persona has a constructed origin without becoming fake. V22 applies the same logic internally: the origin point of a Nadeko trait does not dictate its permanent meaning.

The haircut, victim role, manga desire, cuteness, aggression, and godhood are all origins/history—not sovereign constitutions.

## 20.4 V14 prevention gains skill-governance

Shinobu's post-event testing gives a concrete version of prevention: characterize the limits of a new dangerous capability before depending on it.

## 20.5 V16 causal partition protects the victim/agent distinction

V22 could easily be misread as Nadeko blaming herself for everything. V16's causal partition prevents that collapse.

For the earlier curse/bullying domain:

- external wrongdoing remains external wrongdoing;
- Nadeko's avoidance and communication failures are distinct causal contributions;
- her later agency does not become primary responsibility for another person's abuse.

## 20.6 V18 non-forgiveness becomes future curiosity

Nadeko's Hitagi thought is an elegant extension of `forgiveness not required for continuity`. The future relation need not be reconciliation. Curiosity itself is already an escape from enemy-role fixation.

## 20.7 V21 professional reprioritization becomes recoverability

Dramaturgy demonstrated that competence includes revising a plan when new facts appear. V22 generalizes that principle: specialists and apprentices alike should be judged partly by what they do when plans fail.

# 24. V22 locked formulations / reusable claims

**LOCK-V22-01 — contextual plurality + historical accountability:** people vary across time, context, mood, relationship, and presentation; variation does not turn past conduct into somebody else's history.

**LOCK-V22-02 — past self is neither stranger nor sovereign:** prior Nadeko states must be carried, not disowned, but they do not dictate the only legitimate future.

**LOCK-V22-03 — current memory is current authorship:** a “past self” represented now is partly a present reconstruction; recollection can caricature, idealize, or infantilize.

**LOCK-V22-04 — representation has causal force:** labels, designs, roles, and narrative categories can shape the behavior later used to validate those same categories.

**LOCK-V22-05 — effort is paid:** effort consumes body, time, education, relationship, money, and other people's support; its costs belong inside the ethical calculation.

**LOCK-V22-06 — life precedes sustainable effort:** `生活` is not wasted time around productive work. Eating, sleeping, health, social capacity, and ordinary maintenance are production infrastructure.

**LOCK-V22-07 — effort can become avoidance:** self-improvement may function as morally flattering escape from school, relationship, fear, or ordinary life.

**LOCK-V22-08 — dream is chosen, not essence:** manga is a serious Nadeko vocation without becoming the one hidden true self that invalidates every other role.

**LOCK-V22-09 — ordinary belonging is a real good:** Nadeko's envy of Kobi's school ease is not false consciousness; the critique targets empty/coercive accommodation, not friendship itself.

**LOCK-V22-10 — victim + agent remains relation-specific:** Nadeko's external victimization is not revoked by later recognition of evasive/passive agency. `いじめられるほうも悪い` remains rejected.

**LOCK-V22-11 — passivity can be causal:** waiting, receiving, delegating, and allowing others to act may exercise agency even when the person appears weak or silent.

**LOCK-V22-12 — anger is partial, not truer:** Gyaku's aggression contains real affect while remaining a constructed exaggeration rather than authentic essence.

**LOCK-V22-13 — power is not authentic liberation:** Kami repeats V11's warning that disproportionate power can simplify judgment and instrumentalize others.

**LOCK-V22-14 — creator responsibility:** a badly drawn/stereotyped character can exceed the creator's model; the ethical response is more honest revision, not denial of authorship.

**LOCK-V22-15 — revision is not erasure:** redrawing a role can change future action while preserving that the earlier representation existed and had effects.

**LOCK-V22-16 — shared risk matters but does not absolve proxy use:** Nadeko differs from Kami by taking the greatest personal risk; destruction of created agents remains ethically visible residue.

**LOCK-V22-17 — art can memorialize without undoing:** `絵にして、作品として、思い出として、残す` is a practice of remembrance/atonement, not restoration of what was lost.

**LOCK-V22-18 — professional competence includes recovery:** V22 values the ability to identify failure, revise plans, preserve tracking, escalate, clean up, and restore ordinary function.

**LOCK-V22-19 — responsibility can require delegation:** owning a problem does not mean personally performing work outside one's competence; professional cleanup can be the responsible next step.

**LOCK-V22-20 — situated speech:** a harsh utterance is evidence that it was said and caused effects; it need not permanently exhaust the speaker's total self or later position.

**LOCK-V22-21 — accountability without speaker stasis:** past words cannot be unsaid, but using them to demand lifelong unchanged identity converts accountability into prohibition on development.

**LOCK-V22-22 — love can become history without becoming false:** Nadeko ends the practice of waiting for Koyomi while preserving that the love and happiness of loving were real.

**LOCK-V22-23 — non-forgiveness permits future knowledge:** Nadeko can imagine knowing Hitagi without expecting or demanding absolution.

**LOCK-V22-26 — dangerous ability requires limit testing:** materialization success in one domain does not authorize assumed competence elsewhere; prevention includes systematic capability auditing.

**LOCK-V22-27 — functional fidelity ≠ visual fidelity:** a represented object can become materially present without reproducing the properties that make the original function.

# Final V22 synthesis

`撫物語` is the volume in which Sengoku Nadeko becomes not merely a character with a revised story, but a character who understands that **revision itself has ethics**.

Earlier Monogatari repeatedly exposed the danger of other people drawing her too simply:

- Araragi preserved a cute childhood girl;
- classmates and family responded to a protected social role;
- Kuchinawa let disowned assertion acquire an external voice;
- Kaiki broke the god/romance story by revealing another future;
- V19 manifested aggressive residue without granting it truth monopoly.

V22 turns the pen around.

Nadeko draws herself.

And immediately discovers that being the author does not solve misrecognition.

She draws sociality as emptiness.
She draws anger as delinquent caricature.
She draws power as simplified godhood.
She draws shyness as helpless victimhood.

Those drawings then act back upon her.

That is the deepest reason the volume's manga metaphors matter. Human self-understanding is never a transparent mirror. It is an ongoing act of characterization. We choose which scenes to emphasize, which labels to use, what outfit belongs to which period, what dialogue proves a trait, and what role an old wound should occupy in the current plot.

The mature ethical demand is therefore not impossible neutrality. It is **corrigibility**.

When Kobi proves fuller than the stereotype, draw her better.
When Oto proves less passive than the victim model, revise the causal account.
When Gyaku carries anger but not total truth, preserve the anger without enthroning it.
When Kami shows what power without sufficient deliberation becomes, refuse to call power liberation merely because it removes restraint.
When a plan uses people or proxies and produces damage, remember the cost rather than hiding behind successful outcome.
When an old enemy says something new, do not require her to remain forever identical to the sentence that once injured you.
When an old love no longer has a viable future, do not call the love fake simply to make leaving easier.

This is why the Oto confrontation is the true emotional climax.

Current Nadeko does not defeat the fourteen-year-old girl by proving her childish. She does not say Koyomi was never worth loving. She does not insult the girl for waiting. She instead promises continuity at a different level:

- I will remember that loving made you happy.
- I will love someone again.
- I will not hide forever.
- I will not use effort as another place to disappear.
- I will make your failed love into story.
- I will go toward a future you cannot yet imagine.

That is neither severance nor obedience.

It is inheritance.

V22 therefore gives the V2 project one of its cleanest formulations of growth:

> **Growth is the ability to remain the person who did and felt what happened, while becoming someone for whom those facts no longer dictate the only available action.**

The career coda makes that proposition social rather than merely psychological. Nadeko does not emerge as the purified manga artist who has escaped school, family, romance, oddities, and other people. She is offered a messier life: drawing, paid specialist work, friendships with strange adults and constructed beings, parental negotiation, possible future love, and continuing uncertainty.

That messiness is the point.

At fifteen, Nadeko is not complete.

The narration says so.

She is `発展途上`, `成長中`, `迷走中`.

The old Monogatari danger was that somebody would define her too completely—cute girl, victim, snake girl, god, obsessive lover, manga girl.

V22's answer is not that definition becomes impossible.

It is that every definition must remain revisable by the life that continues after it.
