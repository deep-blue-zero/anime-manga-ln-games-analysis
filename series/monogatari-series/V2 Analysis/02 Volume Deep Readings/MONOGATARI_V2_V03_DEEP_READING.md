# 〈物語〉シリーズ V2 — Volume 03 Deep Reading
## 『化物語（下）』
### 第五話「つばさキャット」

> **Publication-local core:** V03 is the first *Monogatari* volume to make asymmetrical care itself into the crisis. Hanekawa has become so good at helping, adapting, explaining, apologizing, and making herself easy for other people to live with that needing anything becomes difficult to express without shame. Araragi, meanwhile, has made her so absolute an恩人 that his gratitude becomes another way not to see her as a desiring, frightened, partial person. Black Hanekawa gives material agency to pressure Hanekawa cannot safely own, but the cat is neither a simple possession nor the singular “true Hanekawa.”
>
> **Retrospective V2 core:** V03 substantially strengthens the V1 over-adaptation thesis while correcting its temptation to romanticize “integration” in advance. The book does not yet ask Hanekawa to accept every desire as herself; it shows the costs of a self-story built around `私は変わらない`, the violence produced when need can only appear in displaced form, and the moral inadequacy of treating `一人で勝手に助かる` as a commandment against asking for or giving help. Its decisive rescue is possible only when Araragi finally says `助けて……忍`.

# 2. Publication-local thesis

V03 turns a sequence of apparently separate *Bakemonogatari* ideas into one relational problem: **what happens when care is distributed so asymmetrically that one person can give but cannot need, while another can rescue but cannot receive rescue without feeling that he has failed?**

Hanekawa's central contradiction is visible before Black Hanekawa appears. During the Golden Week flashback she explains that, because she did not want her complicated family history to determine how others saw her, she decided to become a “good child” and a “normal” one. The result is paradoxical: her attempt to be ordinary becomes extraordinary perfection. She can rank first nationally, organize a class, remain fair, converse rationally about her own painful family structure, and instinctively apologize after confiding in someone because she worries that even her venting has imposed an unpleasant emotional burden on him.

The phrase `私は変わらない` is therefore not a neutral description. It is a **self-imposed program**. Hanekawa has decided that circumstances will not be allowed to alter the person she presents to the world. That decision contains admirable agency, but V03 shows its cost. If change itself feels like surrender to the family story she refuses to be reduced to, anger, fear, dependency, resentment, erotic desire, and the wish to be prioritized become difficult to admit as ordinary human states.

Araragi's role worsens the problem. He repeatedly places Hanekawa above ordinary reciprocity: `命の恩人`, `心の恩人`, a person without whom he could not have been saved. His gratitude is sincere. Yet the gratitude becomes a pedestal. He tells Hachikuji that Hanekawa is not in a romantic category for him, assumes she would reject him, and later tells Hanekawa that repaying the debt matters more than their friendship. After the crisis, Hanekawa's unguarded response is that this is a lonely thing to hear.

The tragedy is therefore not simply “Hanekawa loved Araragi and lost.” It is that her courtship was legible only once a displaced creature explained it. Araragi had interpreted her appointment of him as vice class representative through the story she supplied—reforming an apparent delinquent—without noticing that an action atypically forceful for Hanekawa also created a reason for them to spend the final school festival preparation together. He could recognize her competence, goodness, and rescue value while failing to recognize desire.

Black Hanekawa emerges within this structure. Oshino describes the `障り猫` as catalyst and medium: the underlying problem is Hanekawa's stress. The cat has a different level of intelligence, limited memory access, its own oddity mechanics, and a brutal instrumental logic oriented toward discharging the master's stress. It can voice information Hanekawa refuses to say, but that does not make every Black Hanekawa impulse the sovereign “truth” of Hanekawa's personhood. The cat proposes sleeping with Araragi, killing stressors, and eventually killing Araragi himself. Those proposals are connected to Hanekawa's pressure while also being shaped by the oddity's own radically reductive solution logic.

V03 thus distinguishes at least four layers that must not be collapsed:

1. Hanekawa's consciously endorsed values;
2. feelings she knows but suppresses;
3. feelings/stress she cannot comfortably integrate into her self-story;
4. Black Hanekawa's oddity-mediated strategies for reducing that stress.

The local moral resolution is deliberately incomplete. Shinobu removes the oddity, but Araragi immediately acknowledges that the stressor remains. The romantic asymmetry remains. The family problem remains. Hanekawa's perfection remains. V03 resolves an acute supernatural event without resolving the person.

# 3. Retrospective V2 thesis

## 3.1 V03 is the first decisive proof that Hanekawa's perfection is adaptation, not deception

The V1 synthesis described Hanekawa as “not fake-good” but over-adapted. V03 strongly supports that formulation. Hanekawa herself traces her behavior to the wish not to be stereotyped by her background. She became good because she wanted circumstances not to define her; Araragi realizes that goodness can be something a person is **forced to become in order to survive coherently**.

The crucial retrospective correction is that “adaptive” must not be confused with “false.” Hanekawa really is intelligent, fair, courageous, generous, and organized. Her achievements are not counterfeit. The problem is that those strengths have become structurally hostile to ordinary need. Her self-regulation works so well that asking for relief feels like moral failure.

Later Hanekawa-centered texts will alter how she handles jealousy, anger, desire, and identity, but V03 already prevents an interpretation in which later integration simply uncovers a fake class-president mask. The class president is real. So is the frightened girl who does not want to go home. So is the girl in love. So is the anger. Personhood is additive before it is integrative.

## 3.2 V02's “hidden is not truer” rule applies directly to Black Hanekawa

The previous V2 volume established through Kanbaru that `裏` does not mean metaphysically or psychologically “more authentic.” V03 uses the same danger. Araragi repeatedly calls Black Hanekawa Hanekawa's `裏面`, and the cat's information about romantic stress is credible. But V03 also insists on discontinuities:

- Black Hanekawa has cat-level intelligence even while drawing on Hanekawa's knowledge;
- knowledge is shared differently from memory;
- the oddity has automatic energy-drain mechanics;
- its means-end reasoning is much simpler and more violent than Hanekawa's conscious ethics;
- it can pursue a stress-reduction strategy that would horrify conscious Hanekawa.

Therefore the strongest V2 formulation is not “Black Hanekawa is the real Hanekawa.” It is:

> **Black Hanekawa is a hybrid agency produced where Hanekawa's disowned or unprocessed pressure meets an oddity whose function is to discharge that pressure.**

What the cat reveals must be taken seriously without granting it monopoly over Hanekawa's identity.

## 3.3 Araragi's idealization is not merely inaccurate; it is causally relational

V1 claimed that idealization prevents Araragi from seeing Hanekawa's need. V03 moves this from thematic inference to strong textual support.

Araragi repeatedly describes Hanekawa as a different order of person, a `本物`, the `委員長の中の委員長`, his irreplaceable benefactor. He assumes she cannot desire him in the ordinary way. When she tells him in spring that she vented at him, he sees the confession as an abnormal crack in perfection. When she later asks him physically to come with her instead of merely emailing directions, he initially interprets the request instrumentally before realizing that she may simply be afraid.

The pedestal has an ethical cost. To idealize a helper can be to deny the helper a reciprocal claim on ordinary care.

V03 also shows that Hanekawa participates in sustaining the pedestal. Her self-presentation is extraordinarily effective. She converts distress into explanations, desire into committee work, fear into logistics, jealousy into advice, and even an accusation about vampire `魅了` into a theory that would absolve her of owning her feelings. Misrecognition is therefore relationally produced rather than assigned to one guilty observer.

## 3.4 The rescue doctrine changes decisively in V03

V01 already complicated Oshino's `助ける／助かる` distinction. V03 goes further.

Hitagi's father tells Araragi that whether Hitagi “saved herself” is not the whole question. Being there at the necessary moment can be more valuable than anything else. Araragi later uses that lesson when he realizes Hanekawa called him not simply for directions but because facing the situation alone frightened her.

Then Black Hanekawa turns Oshino's slogan against Araragi. When he refuses to call for help because “people only save themselves,” the cat answers that this is merely a borrowed sentence, not Araragi's own feeling. The fact that the recipient must participate in recovery says nothing about whether another person is allowed to want to help. Nor does it justify refusing everyone who wants to save *him*.

The climax therefore gives V2 its clearest rescue formulation so far:

> **Self-saving identifies the irreducible locus of agency. It does not erase causal help, relational presence, requests for rescue, or the helper's freedom to act.**

Araragi's decisive maturation in this volume is not successfully saving Hanekawa. It is saying `助けて……忍`.

## 3.5 The text's “weakness” rhetoric must be preserved and challenged

Araragi makes one of V03's hardest judgments: he calls Hanekawa's reliance on Black Hanekawa weakness and says that she should have endured the headache rather than externalize pressure through an entity that harms other people. There is a legitimate accountability insight inside this argument. The cat's violence cannot simply be morally erased because conscious Hanekawa does not remember it.

But Araragi goes further. He compares Hanekawa unfavorably to hypothetical people who endure similar environments without oddities and implies that she should simply have borne the stress. That is not secure as a general ethical principle. It reflects the same self-denying grammar Araragi applies to himself: suffering proves less dangerous than asking, losing oneself, or becoming a burden.

Retrospectively, V2 should record this as **narrator value judgment under pressure**, not as a mature authorial verdict. The scene is valuable precisely because the same chapter subsequently strips Araragi of his ability to survive alone. The boy who tells Hanekawa she should have endured is forced to discover that he cannot.

## 3.6 Hitagi's date is part of the Hanekawa argument, not romantic digression

The long date sequence performs crucial structural work.

Hitagi's father describes his daughter as a person who loves but does not know how to love. With the right person, he says, she entrusts her entire weight; love means asking. Hitagi then gives Araragi “everything she has” available to give: tutoring, Kanbaru, her father, and the starry sky that belonged to a rare good family memory. At the same time, she explicitly withholds sexual intimacy because of trauma and fear that forcing herself could destroy the relationship.

This is a model of **chosen dependence with boundary**:

- offer what one can offer;
- ask for what one wants;
- entrust weight voluntarily;
- preserve the right to say not yet.

Hanekawa's crisis is the negative image. She has trouble asking, trouble burdening, trouble prioritizing herself, and trouble treating desire as something that can be spoken without moral contamination. The arc therefore contrasts two traumatized girls not as romantic competitors but as different models of what recovery makes possible.

# 5. Narrator and focalization audit

## 5.1 Araragi is observant about facts and poor at motive attribution

V03 provides some of the strongest evidence yet for calling Araragi **corrigible** rather than globally unreliable.

He notices the physical facts of Hanekawa's distress quickly: the gauze on the left side of her face, the improbability of the injury, the headache, the oddity of her behavior, and later the inefficiency of summoning him in person merely to ask for directions. His observational intelligence is real.

What he repeatedly misses is motive.

- He believes Hanekawa chose him as vice representative to reform him.
- He assumes her friendliness is generic goodness.
- He assumes she is outside his romantic category and that he is outside hers.
- He interprets her request for accompaniment first as logistical efficiency.
- He accepts the `魅了` hypothesis momentarily because it offers a supernatural explanation for attention that is actually relational.

Black Hanekawa's revelation does not show that every inference Araragi makes is false. It shows a **specific blind spot around being wanted**.

## 5.2 Gratitude becomes a focalization filter

Araragi's narration of Hanekawa is saturated with debt vocabulary. She is not merely a friend; she is his `命の恩人` and `心の恩人`. This magnifies every virtue and makes ordinary mutuality seem almost disrespectful.

The distinction matters. Gratitude is ethically admirable, but when it structures perception it can become anti-reciprocal. A debtor asks how to repay. A friend asks what the other person wants. Araragi spends much of V03 in the first mode.

Hanekawa's near-sleeping protest after the climax—do not say repaying me matters more than friendship—functions as a direct correction of the narrator's relational vocabulary.

## 5.3 The Golden Week flashback is already retrospective narration

Section 006 openly resets the narrative clock to April 29. Araragi repeatedly says that he only understood the meaning of events later. This is not an untouched memory transcript. It is a later narrator organizing prior events with knowledge obtained during the current case.

Accordingly:

- details of the physical assault and Hanekawa's words are narrated as remembered event;
- claims such as “telling me may have been the final trigger” are retrospective inference;
- general statements about what Hanekawa “must” have felt require NR/SI classification, not TF.

This distinction becomes important when 『猫物語（黒）』 later returns to Golden Week with much greater narrative scope.

## 5.4 Black Hanekawa is an informant with constrained epistemology

The cat is unusually useful because it can access stress-related material Hanekawa suppresses. But V03 builds limitations into that access.

Black Hanekawa has:

- access to much of Hanekawa's knowledge;
- incomplete or different memory access;
- cat-like intelligence and highly reductive problem solving;
- direct sensitivity to the master's stress;
- inability to lie in the ordinary strategic sense, according to the text's current model.

Thus the revelation “Hanekawa loves Araragi” is strongly supported inside V03, while broader claims the cat makes about oddity ontology should remain diegetic theory.

## 5.5 “本音” is not equivalent to total personhood

Near the ending, Araragi describes Hanekawa's half-conscious words as unadorned `本音`. Those words are relationally modest: she does not confess love or demand Araragi. She says that treating debt repayment as more important than friendship is lonely and then tells him to behave properly.

This matters for V2. If Black Hanekawa were simply “the true Hanekawa,” the most direct post-cat expression of Hanekawa's own voice would be redundant. Instead, the volume preserves multiple truth channels. The cat tells one truth; the half-conscious Hanekawa tells another.

# 7. Causal plot reconstruction

## 7.1 Long-duration substrate

Hanekawa grows up in a household with no biological relation to her current parents, minimal affection or conversation, and strong concern for public appearance. She decides that she will not allow a “complicated family” to determine who she becomes. Her solution is to become exceptionally good, orderly, fair, and self-controlled.

This adaptation is not itself an oddity. It is the human condition on which the later oddity can operate.

## 7.2 Golden Week trigger and first cat incident

On April 29 Hanekawa, after being struck in the face by her father, encounters Araragi while outside the home. She talks extensively about her family, apologizes for venting, rationalizes the violence, and finally asks Araragi to keep the assault secret even from school and police. Later that day they bury a dead white cat.

V03 retrospectively connects these elements to the `障り猫` event. Oshino's current explanation is that the cat is catalyst/medium and Hanekawa's accumulated stress is the governing problem.

## 7.3 Current stress accumulation

After Golden Week, the family situation remains difficult but has reached a relative equilibrium. A new acute stressor forms much faster: Hanekawa loves Araragi, sees him begin a relationship with Hitagi, continues supporting that relationship, and refuses to prioritize her own desire over another person's happiness.

The new headaches are somatic warning signs that the previously contained cat-state is resurfacing.

## 7.4 Hanekawa's indirect help request

Hanekawa calls Araragi away from school and asks him to guide her to Oshino. The request is over-rationalized through attendance, festival logistics, and route-finding. Araragi eventually recognizes that email directions would have been enough: she wanted another person physically present because she was afraid.

This is an important causal event. Hanekawa **does ask for help**, but does so in a form compatible with her self-story as competent planner.

## 7.5 Black Hanekawa identifies the stressor

Oshino invokes Black Hanekawa to obtain information more directly. After the cat escapes, Araragi and Black Hanekawa search for missing Shinobu. The cat eventually identifies Araragi himself as the current stressor and reveals Hanekawa's romantic feelings.

The first proposed resolution is for Araragi to date Hanekawa. He refuses because his feelings are committed to Hitagi and because a relationship founded on repayment would exploit Hanekawa's debt position rather than honor it.

## 7.6 Violent reduction

The cat then uses a second solution: remove the stressor by killing Araragi through energy drain. This plan reveals the danger of treating emotional regulation as a purely technical optimization problem. The cat can reduce stress while catastrophically violating the conscious person whose stress it serves.

## 7.7 Araragi's failed martyrdom and successful request

Araragi initially accepts death as a possible repayment to Hanekawa. He stops when he realizes his death would cause further harm—especially to Hitagi and Hanekawa. Black Hanekawa then challenges his borrowed `一人で勝手に助かる` reasoning and asks whether he intends to reject everyone who wants to save him.

Unable to escape alone, he says `助けて……忍`.

Shinobu emerges from his shadow, overpowers the cat, and drains the oddity out of Hanekawa. Black Hanekawa does not resist the final extraction because this outcome also serves the master's interest.

## 7.8 Acute resolution, persistent causes

Araragi explicitly states that eliminating the cat does not eliminate the stress. The romantic asymmetry remains. Hanekawa's family remains. The underlying self-structure remains. Resolution is therefore acute and procedural, not psychological completion.

# 8. Oddity dossiers

## 8.1 障り猫 / Black Hanekawa

### Classification in V03

Oshino analogizes the phenomenon to dissociative/multiple-personality structure in ordinary language, but immediately insists that the oddity is more than a psychiatric label. The dead cat is a catalyst/medium; Hanekawa's stress supplies the human pressure; the resulting Black Hanekawa is a hybrid state.

### Observable mechanics

- cat ears and hair-color transformation accompany emergence;
- energy drain activates through touch;
- accumulated energy can make Black Hanekawa physically formidable;
- knowledge and memory access differ from conscious Hanekawa's;
- removal by Shinobu's superior energy-drain capacity extracts the oddity component;
- the cat can recur when stress accumulates because the stressor, not merely the oddity, remains.

### Agency problem

Black Hanekawa is not merely a passive symptom. It reasons, jokes, searches, bargains, deceives by omission/ambush despite the text's claim that it cannot “lie,” chooses methods, and can pursue lethal simplifications. Yet its methods are oriented toward reducing the master's stress.

V2 therefore records **distributed agency** rather than either extreme:

- “Hanekawa did nothing; the cat is wholly external” is too weak;
- “Black Hanekawa is simply Hanekawa's true self” is too strong.

### Residue

The cat can be removed while the underlying family and romantic pressures persist. This is one of the clearest early examples of supernatural resolution leaving human residue.

## 8.2 Shinobu / diminished vampire

V03 adds a different oddity-personhood problem. Araragi has begun treating Shinobu as if she were simply a child. Black Hanekawa argues that oddities depend on remaining strange, feared, revered, unwanted, desired, or otherwise categorically non-ordinary. Araragi recognizes that habitual intimacy may have flattened his perception of Shinobu's difference.

This is **diegetic metaphysical theory**, not yet a final ontology. But it matters relationally even if the metaphysics remain uncertain: Araragi has not adequately asked what it means for Shinobu to exist in her diminished state.

Her hiding place literalizes their bond. She can remain in Araragi's shadow because proximity to him supports what vampire ability she retains.

## 8.3 Oddity as “reality” when normalized

Black Hanekawa offers a compact theory:

- vampire → blood abnormality;
- cat → dissociation;
- crab → illness;
- snail → lost child;
- monkey → attacker;
- snake → pain.

The point is not that scientific explanation disproves oddities. The cat says oddities change form rather than disappear; they remain coextensive with human life.

V2 records this as an important **C03 diegetic ontology hypothesis** for later testing, not a settled authorial rule.

## 9.1 Hanekawa: “ordinary” as a perfectionistic project

One of V03's sharpest insights is that Hanekawa wants to be ordinary so intensely that she becomes extraordinary. Her perfect behavior is not a claim that she is inherently superior. It is a refusal to allow observers to say, “of course she is damaged; look at her family.”

The resulting self-story is internally impossible. Ordinary people become angry, selfish, jealous, needy, frightened, and inconsistent. Hanekawa's effort not to become a stereotype paradoxically denies her access to the full range of ordinary imperfection.

## 9.2 Araragi: the debtor who cannot accept reciprocal claims

Araragi believes Hanekawa saved his body and mind. This establishes a debt he regards as practically infinite. Yet an infinite debt is relationally dangerous because it cannot be settled. The debtor may prefer dramatic repayment to ordinary friendship precisely because ordinary friendship eliminates the heroic asymmetry.

Hanekawa's complaint that “repaying me matters more than friendship” is lonely exposes this. She does not primarily want a debtor. She wants Araragi to behave properly *within a relation*.

# 10. Relationship-state protocol

## 10.1 Araragi ↔ Hitagi

### V02 state

New romance; Hitagi has already condemned his self-sacrifice and made chosen partiality explicit.

### V03 movement

The first date deepens the relationship from declaration to **exchange of worlds**. Hitagi shows Araragi what she has: her father, a starry sky, and access to parts of her life she has protected. She tells him what she cannot yet give: sexual intimacy without fear.

This is a major relationship-state advance because intimacy is defined by truthful limits rather than maximal access.

Araragi's later refusal to die for Hanekawa is partly caused by this bond. Hitagi's existence makes his body socially non-private in a moral sense: his death would not be a cost paid only by him.

### New state

Committed romantic relation with explicit future orientation, reciprocal planning, preserved boundaries, and increasing moral constraint on Araragi's martyrdom.

## 10.2 Araragi ↔ Hanekawa

### Prior state

Friend / class-partner / benefactor relation, heavily idealized by Araragi.

### V03 movement

The relation is exposed as asymmetrical in two directions:

- Araragi thinks primarily in terms of debt and gratitude;
- Hanekawa suppresses romantic desire and attempts to support his relationship with Hitagi.

Black Hanekawa reveals that Hanekawa's love predates the current crisis. Araragi refuses romance, not because Hanekawa is unworthy or because Hitagi is morally “better,” but because his romantic desire is already directed elsewhere.

The crucial relational residue is not the rejection itself. Hanekawa's half-conscious request is that he stop subordinating friendship to repayment.

### New state

Romantic asymmetry is now textually explicit. Friendship remains possible but is burdened by debt language, unprocessed heartbreak, and Araragi's idealization.

## 10.3 Araragi ↔ Shinobu

### Prior state

A bond defined by spring-break guilt and mutual dependence but largely silent in *Bakemonogatari*.

### V03 movement

Araragi explicitly calls Shinobu his **victim** and himself the responsible party. He says that if she dies tomorrow, his own life need last no longer. This is not simple affection; it is guilt, responsibility, identification, and dependency compressed into one vow.

Black Hanekawa points out that such identification may itself be unilateral. Araragi has also begun treating Shinobu as “just a child,” potentially erasing the oddity/person she still is.

At the climax, asking Shinobu for help reactivates mutuality. She is not merely his victim to be sustained; she can act for him.

### New state

Still asymmetrical and largely nonverbal, but more visibly reciprocal. Shinobu's position in Araragi's shadow becomes both literal mechanic and relationship image.

## 10.4 Hitagi ↔ Hanekawa

V03 does not yet give a sustained direct conversation between them after the revelation, so claims about their mature friendship must remain restrained.

What it does establish is that Hitagi takes Hanekawa seriously enough not to envy her “perfection.” She treats Hanekawa as `本物` and almost `化物`, but her question is what cost such perfection imposes. Hitagi's own recovery gives her an intuitive suspicion of adaptation that looks too complete.

Retrospectively this is an important seed, not yet a mature relationship state.

## 10.5 Araragi ↔ Oshino

Oshino's departure ends the assumption that an adult specialist will remain locally available to arbitrate every case. Araragi interprets the final cat/Shinobu incident as, at least partly, a leaving test and believes Oshino decided he could handle what followed.

That interpretation is Araragi's inference, not confirmed objective fact. But structurally the effect is clear: the adolescent network must now function without its resident specialist.

# 11. Rescue, care, intervention, and non-possession audit

## 11.1 Hitagi's father supplies the strongest correction to simplistic self-saving

Araragi tells Hitagi's father that Hitagi ultimately saved herself. Her father accepts the principle without allowing it to erase causal relation. What matters is that Araragi was there when she needed someone.

This is a decisive ethical distinction:

- **agency:** Hitagi had to choose recovery;
- **presence:** another person's being there materially mattered;
- **credit:** the helper need not own the result for the help to be real.

V2 should preserve all three.

## 11.2 Hanekawa's help request is disguised but real

Hanekawa's invitation to the park is a model of how people with highly controlled self-stories may ask without admitting they are asking. She dresses the request in practical reasoning. Araragi later recognizes the simpler emotional fact: she did not want to face it alone.

That means “Hanekawa never asks for help” is too absolute even at V03. Better:

> **Hanekawa has difficulty making need explicit, so help-seeking is translated into forms compatible with competence and usefulness.**

## 11.3 Respecting refusal can collide with protection

In the Golden Week flashback, Hanekawa demands secrecy about being struck by her father. Araragi recognizes the violence as unacceptable but ultimately honors the refusal to involve school or police.

The volume does not resolve the ethical question this creates. Two values conflict:

- respecting Hanekawa's agency and explicit boundary;
- preventing continued violence toward a minor.

V2 must not retroactively pretend the answer is obvious inside the text. This is an **open intervention problem**.

## 11.4 Araragi's “Hanekawa should have endured” judgment is unstable

Araragi correctly identifies that Black Hanekawa's violence creates victims and cannot be morally neutralized as stress relief. But his stronger language—Hanekawa should have endured the headache; relying on the cat is weakness—risks converting responsibility into blame for failing to tolerate abuse, heartbreak, and accumulated stress.

The immediate narrative context undermines any simplistic stoic reading. Within pages, Araragi is physically unable to save himself. His repeated invocation of Oshino's self-saving phrase is exposed as borrowed language. He survives only after asking Shinobu for help.

A rigorous V2 reading therefore separates:

- **accountability for displaced harm** — strongly supported;
- **obligation to endure suffering alone** — not established as mature doctrine.

## 11.5 Receiving rescue becomes an ethical obligation

Black Hanekawa asks how many people want Araragi to live and whether he intends to reject every one of them. This exposes a problem in martyrdom: self-sacrifice can erase the moral agency of people who love the sacrificer.

Araragi thinks of Hitagi, Hanekawa, Kanbaru, Nadeko, and Hachikuji. Their potential grief changes his calculation. His life is no longer morally representable as property he can spend without remainder.

## 11.6 Non-possession in romantic rejection

Araragi cannot cure Hanekawa's stress by dating her without love. Doing so would turn her desire into an obligation he pays, transforming intimacy into debt settlement. His refusal is painful but non-possessive in an important sense: he does not take Hanekawa simply because she wants him and because he owes her.

The remaining problem is that he still conceptualizes her too heavily through debt. V03 corrects one form of possession while leaving another asymmetry unresolved.

# 12. Specialist ethics and metaphysical knowledge

## 12.1 Oshino's stress model

Oshino's explanation is more precise than “the cat is Hanekawa's darkness.” He distinguishes:

- catalyst/medium (`障り猫`);
- host/person (Hanekawa);
- pressure (stress);
- stressor;
- acute manifestation (Black Hanekawa);
- containment/removal;
- recurrence if the stressor remains.

This is a clinically structured model even though the ontology remains supernatural.

## 12.2 Knowledge does not equal permission

Oshino appears to have inferred the new stress source early but refuses to state delicate speculation without evidence. This is a meaningful specialist boundary. Knowing enough to suspect is not the same as having grounds to define another person's desire for them.

That restraint contrasts with Araragi's frequent compulsion to intervene immediately.

## 12.3 Specialist departure as intervention

The epilogue strongly suggests that Oshino intentionally used the end of the case as his moment to leave town. Araragi infers that Shinobu's disappearance and Black Hanekawa's escape were, at least partly, allowed to become a test or parting gift.

Evidence classification:

- **TF:** Oshino has removed his belongings and left without a note.
- **NR/SI:** Araragi reconstructs his intent and believes Oshino decided Araragi could handle the situation.
- **IT:** the departure functions structurally as removal of adult scaffolding.

V2 should not state the inferred plan as uncontested fact.

## 12.4 `お人よし` as the specialist's hidden identity

The ensemble's final judgment on Oshino is `お人よし`. The series has spent three archive volumes watching him deny savior language while repeatedly helping people through knowledge, labor, risk, and boundary-setting. The contradiction is productive rather than hypocritical.

Oshino's ethic may be summarized locally as:

> **Help without claiming ownership of another person's recovery.**

That is different from refusing to help.

# 13. Japanese voice and address audit

## 13.1 Hanekawa: controlled explanatory speech

Hanekawa's ordinary voice is strikingly organized even under stress. She can narrate a labyrinthine family history coherently, anticipate the listener's discomfort, and then apologize for causing that discomfort. Her speech does interpersonal labor while describing her inability to stop doing interpersonal labor.

Three phrases deserve longitudinal tracking:

- `私は変わらない` — self-program of continuity;
- `いい子になろうとした` — goodness described as chosen project;
- `何でもは知らないわよ──何にも、知らない` — fracture of the familiar epistemic persona.

The last is especially important. The canonical Hanekawa formula normally limits omniscience modestly: she only knows what she knows. Here, under stress, the formula collapses into “I know nothing,” exposing a sudden loss of epistemic mastery.

## 13.2 Black Hanekawa: `にゃ` register and simplified aggression

Black Hanekawa's speech is built around systematic cat-punning (`にゃ`, `にゃん`) and blunt declaratives. The register is comic, vulgar, sexualized, and often brutally instrumental. This linguistic simplification parallels the cat's problem-solving: identify stress → remove stressor.

But the comic register should not cause the analyst to dismiss the content. Some of V03's sharpest philosophical challenges arrive through deliberately foolish cat speech.

## 13.3 Araragi and debt language

Araragi's recurrent terms—`恩人`, `恩返し`, `助けられた`—are not merely descriptions of past events. They organize the current relationship. V03 demonstrates that lexical framing can become relational architecture.

Hanekawa's final objection directly challenges the hierarchy:

friendship should not be placed below repayment.

## 13.4 Hitagi and explicit request

The date is notable for Hitagi's difficulty asking directly and her father's formulation that love involves `求める`, `甘える`, and entrusting full weight. These are not soft sentimental words in context; they describe behaviors Hitagi has historically been unable to perform safely.

## 13.5 `助けて`

Araragi's request to Shinobu is one of the simplest utterances in the volume and one of the most structurally important. It is not an argument, joke, theory, or heroic declaration. It is a direct admission of inability.

Track this phrase against later Araragi rescue behavior.

# 14. Names, wordplay, ruby, and translation-sensitive structures

## 14.1 羽川翼 — `翼` as assigned role

V03 explicitly pauses over the name `翼`, noting senses related to supporting, assisting, and sheltering. Araragi asks whether the person named for helping should herself have been the one helped and wonders what role her mother assigned through the name.

This is unusually strong evidence for the V1 claim that names can function as **role scripts** rather than decorative puns.

V2 should nevertheless distinguish:

- lexical associations of `翼` — textual fact;
- claim that the mother consciously imposed a helper destiny — Araragi's speculative question;
- Hanekawa's later life as helper — longitudinal interpretation.

## 14.2 `表／裏`

V02's Kanbaru analysis already established the danger of treating `裏` as “truer.” V03 repeats `裏面` language for Black Hanekawa. The cumulative Japanese-language ledger should therefore track `表／裏` as a recurring relational metaphor whose meaning varies by case.

## 14.3 `怪異`

Black Hanekawa glosses `怪異` through the literal semantic sense of the strange/other. The speech then builds an ontology in which oddities must remain categorically non-ordinary. Translation as merely “apparition” or “aberration” can obscure how much the argument depends on the Japanese compound's ordinary semantic texture.

## 14.4 `障り／触り`

The cat's energy drain turns `障り猫` into a touch-based threat and invites `触り猫` wordplay. The pun is not merely comic; it links name, bodily contact, and mechanic.

## 14.5 `本物`

Hitagi and Araragi use `本物` to describe Hanekawa's almost frightening authenticity/competence. V2 should watch this term carefully because “real/genuine” can function as praise while also dehumanizing someone into an impossible standard.

# 15. Body, appetite, sexuality, gaze, and comedy

## 15.1 The body is where suppressed pressure becomes undeniable

V03 repeatedly converts internal or relational states into bodily facts:

- gauze covers the place Hanekawa was struck;
- headaches precede cat emergence;
- ears and hair transform;
- energy drain makes stress management literally extract vitality from others;
- Shinobu consumes the oddity as food;
- Araragi's shadow becomes Shinobu's dwelling place.

The body does not simply symbolize psychology; it is the material interface through which oddity causation operates.

## 15.2 Hanekawa's body is simultaneously diagnostic and eroticized

As in V02, the narrative can require bodily visibility while also eroticizing it. Araragi comments on cat ears, pajamas, breasts, physical contact, and the visual pleasure of Hanekawa even while she is in acute distress.

This creates an especially sharp gaze problem because Araragi's thematic error is already **not seeing Hanekawa as a whole person**. The narration's erotic attention can both disrupt the untouchable “saint” pedestal and replace it with another partializing gaze.

V2 should resist both defenses:

- “it is only fanservice and therefore analytically irrelevant”;
- “because the text is self-aware, the eroticization is automatically critique.”

Both indulgence and characterization can coexist.

## 15.3 Hitagi's sexual boundary is treated with unusual seriousness

The date sequence provides a counter-register. Hitagi explicitly discusses fear of sexual intimacy after attempted assault and worries not simply about the act but about what forcing herself could do to her feelings toward Araragi. Araragi accepts the boundary.

The contrast matters. V03 contains highly transgressive comic/erotic narration elsewhere, but it can also articulate consent, trauma, timing, and relational trust with clarity. A mature analysis must preserve that unevenness rather than force the whole volume into either “sex-positive comedy” or “problematic fanservice.”

## 15.4 Hachikuji comedy remains ethically double

Araragi's physical comedy with Hachikuji continues to cross boundaries. The routine also carries relationship information and provides a space where difficult topics—Hanekawa, romance, Shinobu—can be discussed through comic detour. Functional relationship infrastructure does not cancel the surface ethical problem.

# 16. Family, home, school, and institution

## 16.1 Hanekawa's household is a social structure before it is a supernatural cause

The household is preserved partly through `世間体`—public appearance. Hanekawa believes her parents remain together until she reaches adulthood in part because separation and relinquishing responsibility would attract condemnation. This is important because it gives the family problem a social substrate beyond private incompatibility.

V03 does **not** yet justify the later broad thesis “systems produce monsters.” It does justify a narrower claim:

> **social expectations can preserve a formally intact household whose internal relations are emotionally destructive.**

## 16.2 Home is not refuge

Hanekawa spends holidays walking because she does not want to remain in the house with her parents. When the current cat crisis frightens her, Araragi explicitly recognizes that she cannot simply retreat home.

V03 therefore establishes an address/refuge distinction without yet giving the full later `un-home` formulation. She has a house. Its existence does not make it a safe recovery environment.

## 16.3 School is both role machine and real community

The class-president identity burdens Hanekawa, but school is not depicted as a simple oppressive institution. Committee work also gives her meaningful competence, connection, and a channel through which she sought closeness with Araragi.

This duality matters for later institutional analysis. A role can constrain and sustain at the same time.

## 16.4 Police/school intervention remains unresolved

Hanekawa explicitly asks Araragi not to tell school or police about the assault. V03 does not show institutional intervention, nor does it establish whether such intervention would have helped. The omission must be recorded as an ethical/institutional open question rather than filled with contemporary assumptions.

# 17. Thematic synthesis

## 17.1 The helper who cannot need

Hanekawa's crisis is often described as repression, but V03 gives a more relationally precise pattern. She can identify what other people need with extraordinary speed while translating her own need into apology or logistics.

She is therefore not simply a person with “hidden feelings.” She is a person whose social competence makes hiddenness sustainable.

## 17.2 Love as asking versus love as erasure

Hitagi's father says love means asking and entrusting weight. Hitagi demonstrates that principle imperfectly but sincerely. Hanekawa's love moves in the opposite direction: support Araragi's romance, conceal jealousy, never steal another person's place, and turn pain inward.

V03 does not condemn altruism. It asks when altruism stops allowing the altruist to exist as an equal subject.

## 17.3 Gratitude can become another form of distance

Araragi's gratitude toward Hanekawa is morally serious and emotionally genuine. But a person kept forever as savior cannot easily become ordinary friend, lover, dependent, nuisance, or contradictory human being.

The volume's quietest correction is Hanekawa's request that friendship not be subordinated to repayment.

## 17.4 A self-story can be admirable and still become a prison

`私は変わらない` begins as resistance to stigma. Hanekawa refuses to let household circumstances determine her. That is agency.

But if the sentence becomes absolute, it forbids ordinary change. The same story that protects dignity can later prevent adaptation. This is one of the clearest V03 foundations for the full-series V1 claim that stories about the self can become prisons.

## 17.5 Externalization reduces pressure by exporting cost

Black Hanekawa is effective. Stress drops when the cat acts. That effectiveness is exactly the danger. The mechanism externalizes psychological cost onto other bodies.

This gives V03 a strong ethical model of displacement:

> **A coping mechanism can work for the person using it while remaining catastrophically harmful to everyone else.**

## 17.6 The right to help is different from ownership of recovery

Black Hanekawa's critique of Araragi gives Oshino's slogan its mature local interpretation. A helper need not claim causal authorship to exercise care. Likewise, a person who needs help does not lose agency by asking.

The moral error is not help itself. It is confusing help with ownership.

# 18. Counterreadings and adversarial tests

## Counterreading A: “Black Hanekawa is Hanekawa's true self.”

### Evidence for

- the cat is repeatedly called Hanekawa's `裏面`;
- it knows stress material conscious Hanekawa suppresses;
- some of its revelations are confirmed by context;
- Araragi describes its statements as connected to Hanekawa's real feelings.

### Evidence against

- V02 has already established that `裏` is not inherently truer than `表`;
- Black Hanekawa has distinct intelligence and memory limitations;
- it is produced through an external oddity catalyst;
- its lethal strategies contradict Hanekawa's conscious ethics;
- Hanekawa later produces her own `本音` after the cat is removed.

### V2 verdict

**Too reductive.** Black Hanekawa carries real Hanekawa material through a distinct oddity agency. “Partial truth with autonomous mechanism” is stronger than “true self.”

## Counterreading B: “Hanekawa is secretly selfish and her goodness is fake.”

### Evidence for

She admits wanting to vent at Araragi; she loves him despite his relationship; Black Hanekawa acts violently.

### Evidence against

The text repeatedly demonstrates real generosity, courage, competence, and care. Her problem is not that goodness is fraudulent; it is that goodness becomes compulsory and totalizing.

### V2 verdict

**Rejected.** Contradictory desire does not falsify virtue.

## Counterreading C: “Araragi's rejection of Hanekawa proves Hitagi is the better woman.”

The source does not make this comparative moral claim. Araragi states that he loves Hitagi, including her difficult personality. Romantic choice is not a ranking of human worth.

### V2 verdict

**Rejected as category error.** Desire is not a merit competition.

## Counterreading D: “Hanekawa should simply have asked for help.”

V03 itself complicates this. Hanekawa does ask, but indirectly. Her adaptation makes direct dependency morally difficult for her; she apologizes for venting and rationalizes violence to avoid forcing others into intervention.

### V2 verdict

**Narrowed.** Learning to ask is necessary, but inability to ask has a history and cannot be treated as a free-standing character flaw.

## Counterreading E: “Araragi is right that Hanekawa was weak and should have endured.”

His accountability argument regarding displaced violence has force. His endurance argument is less stable. It reproduces his own martyr ethic and is immediately undercut when he cannot survive without asking for help.

### V2 verdict

**Mixed and unresolved publication-locally.** Preserve the judgment as Araragi's; do not universalize it.

## Counterreading F: “Oshino's slogan means outside help is basically irrelevant.”

Hitagi's father, Hanekawa's request, Black Hanekawa's rebuke, Shinobu's intervention, and the distributed search network all contradict that strong reading.

### V2 verdict

**Rejected.** The slogan protects agency; it does not negate relation.

## Counterreading G: “The Hitagi date is unrelated romantic fanservice inside Hanekawa's arc.”

The date supplies the conceptual language—weight, asking, presence, boundaries—that the Hanekawa crisis then tests negatively.

### V2 verdict

**Strongly rejected.** The date is structural argument.

## Highest-value V03 revision 1 — over-adaptation without falsity

### V1 shorthand
> Hanekawa is perfect because she over-adapts to an unlivable home.

### V2 revision
> **V03 shows Hanekawa deliberately building “goodness” and “ordinariness” to prevent her background from determining her identity. The virtues are real; the danger is that their compulsory consistency leaves insufficient room for ordinary selfishness, need, fear, anger, and change.**

## Highest-value V03 revision 2 — Black Hanekawa is not the sovereign truth

### V1 shorthand
> Black Hanekawa is Hanekawa's emergency exhaust system rather than merely her dark side.

### V2 revision
> **The exhaust model is strongly supported, but the cat is a hybrid agent: Hanekawa supplies stress and knowledge, the oddity supplies mechanics and reductive strategy. Black Hanekawa can speak truths Hanekawa cannot say without becoming the total truth of Hanekawa.**

## Highest-value V03 revision 3 — the rescue slogan is finally broken open

### Earlier shorthand
> People save themselves; others can only lend force.

### V03 revision
> **Self-saving protects agency, not isolation. Presence can be causally indispensable, the helper may freely choose to help, and refusing everyone who wants to save you can itself become a form of relational domination.**

## Highest-value V03 revision 4 — Araragi's “weakness” judgment is not mature doctrine

### Risky first-pass reading
> Hanekawa's problem is that she refused to face her feelings and relied on the cat.

### V2 revision
> **V03 supports accountability for harm externalized through the cat, but Araragi's claim that Hanekawa should simply have endured is ethically unstable and reflects his own self-sacrificial value system. The climax subjects that value system to immediate contradiction by making him ask for rescue.**

# 20. Later-material revision map

This section uses full-series hindsight only to identify future audit routes. It does not import later conclusions into the publication-local reading.

## 20.1 『傷物語』

Must verify:

- what Hanekawa actually did during spring break;
- how much of Araragi's “she saved my mind and life” formulation is supported by the primary text;
- the origin of his benefactor idealization;
- Shinobu/Kiss-shot guilt and the precise basis for calling her his victim.

## 20.2 『猫物語（黒）』

Must revisit Golden Week in full rather than treating V03's compressed retrospective as exhaustive. Key questions:

- exact parent/household mechanics;
- cat fusion and stress mechanics;
- what Araragi knew then versus what he reconstructs now;
- whether V03's “weakness” judgment changes under fuller context.

## 20.3 『猫物語（白）』

This will be the decisive audit of the V1 integration thesis. Do not assume in advance that later self-acceptance simply confirms every V03 interpretation. Test:

- whether jealousy/anger/desire are integrated as “all me”;
- how Hanekawa narrates Araragi differently from Araragi's narration of her;
- what changes in home, body, voice, and help-seeking.

## 20.4 Later Hanekawa mobility

Travel/world-scale Hanekawa must be tested against V03's “holiday walking because home is intolerable” substrate. Mobility may be liberation, avoidance, vocation, or several of these at once. Do not pre-label it.

## 20.5 Later Araragi–Shinobu relation

V03's concern about treating Shinobu as an ordinary child should become a longitudinal test of whether intimacy can preserve categorical difference without dehumanizing either party.

# 24. Compact reusable formulations

These formulations are approved for later synthesis **provided their evidence routing remains attached**.

### Hanekawa

> **Hanekawa's goodness is not a fake mask hiding an evil self. It is a real virtue that became compulsory enough to make ordinary need difficult to inhabit.**

> **She tries so hard to be an ordinary girl untouched by her biography that the performance of ordinariness becomes extraordinary perfection.**

> **Black Hanekawa is not the one true Hanekawa. It is a hybrid agency in which real disowned pressure acquires an oddity's brutally reductive means of expression.**

### Araragi and Hanekawa

> **Araragi's idealization of Hanekawa is a form of misrecognition precisely because it begins in gratitude rather than contempt. A benefactor placed on a pedestal becomes difficult to perceive as someone who can want, fear, envy, ask, or need.**

> **The almost-romance fails not because Hanekawa is less worthy than Hitagi, but because gratitude cannot be converted into romantic obligation without falsifying both people.**

### Rescue ethics

> **`一人で勝手に助かる` describes where agency finally resides; it does not mean that presence, intervention, accompaniment, or rescue requests are unreal.**

> **V03's decisive rescue act is Araragi saying `助けて`.**

> **A person's right to spend his own body is morally complicated once other people have genuine claims of love, grief, and dependence on his continued existence.**

### Hitagi contrast

> **Hitagi's date and Hanekawa's crisis are paired models of trauma: one girl is learning to ask, entrust weight, and state boundaries; the other has made herself so useful that need can emerge only indirectly.**

### Self-story

> **`私は変わらない` begins as resistance to stigma and becomes dangerous only when continuity hardens into a prohibition on ordinary change.**

### Oddities

> **The `障り猫` case strongly supports oddity-as-narrative-body language only if “body” does not erase literal supernatural agency, external catalyst, and the distinction between a person's pressure and the mechanism that acts on it.**

### Methodological warning

> **Do not convert Araragi's statement that Hanekawa should have endured into the series' mature moral position. V03 itself makes his self-sufficiency collapse minutes later.**
