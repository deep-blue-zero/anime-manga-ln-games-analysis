# MONOGATARI V2 — `短物語` Supplementary Deep Reading

## 0. Purpose and authority boundary

This artifact performs the controlled V2 deep reading of the **39 prose stories collected in `短物語`**. It is deliberately neither a “Volume 31” nor a bag of trivia. The stories were written across many different publication contexts and then compiled much later; some sit cleanly between numbered events, some revisit earlier events after the reader already knows much more, some are meta-comic, and some were written in 2024 as new bridges or reflective pieces.

The governing placement audit therefore imposes a dual coordinate system on every story:

1. **first-release horizon** — what numbered-series material existed for the reader when the short was first published;
2. **internal-story horizon** — where the depicted or remembered event belongs inside the fiction, to the degree that the text permits placement.

Those coordinates are independent.

> **A later-published prequel can revise what the reader now understands about an earlier event without becoming knowledge the earlier characters already possessed.**

That rule is indispensable for the 2017 `傷物語` insertions, the explicitly pre-`忍物語` `まよいウェルカム`, and the four 2024 collection-new bridge stories.

The source class is **SUP-A: authored supplementary prose**. Accordingly, these stories have high authority for locally explicit voice, relationship state, mundane biography, and event detail when chronology is secure. Major plot and oddity/metaphysical claims remain conditional and must be reconciled with the numbered novels rather than silently superseding them.

### Evidence labels

- **TF** — textual/source-form fact
- **NR** — narrator report
- **CD** — character/diegetic claim
- **SI** — strong inference
- **IT** — interpretation
- **UA** — unresolved ambiguity
- **RC** — retrospective correction/revision
- **VJ** — value judgment

### Governing proposition

The strongest source-wide formulation is:

> **`短物語` is an archive of local states: it preserves how a person, relationship, rule, or self-story looks at a particular point without granting that point permanent sovereignty over what the person later becomes.**

That makes the collection unusually compatible with the V2 corpus's larger concern with historical continuity without identity possession.

### 1.2 Collection architecture

The book contains **39 stories**, here indexed `SHORT-001–SHORT-039`.

The preface explicitly says that most of the previously published pieces were short-shorts written around anime-related products and venues, often emphasizing small interactions or events rather than mainline plot. It also says the stories are kept **basically in writing order**, not internally chronological or character-sorted order, and are reproduced mostly in their original form.

That statement prevents a common archival error:

> **The table of contents is a writing/release-history sequence, not a story-chronology sequence.**

The preface also directly identifies:

- `まよいウェルカム` as a prequel to `忍物語`;

# 3. Story-by-story deep readings

## 3.1 SHORT-001 — `ひたぎブッフェ`

**Release horizon:** 2010 guidebook; numbered reader through V08.  
**Internal horizon:** high-school dating period after the crab/monkey relationship state is established; exact adjacency remains OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

The story makes Hitagi's recovery unusually material. Her five-kilogram weight gain is not merely a punchline about appearance. She explains that the crab condition constrained what she could wear **and what she could eat**. Being able to eat until full is therefore part of regaining permission to live inside an ordinary body.

Her formulation that being able to like something lets her feel slightly saved and slightly able to forgive herself is especially important. It is not a metaphysical claim that appetite reveals a “true Hitagi.” It is a report that ordinary pleasure can function as a small reparative practice after a condition structured by bodily estrangement and self-hatred.

This strengthens a recurring V2 distinction:

> **Recovery is not only symptom removal. It includes restored access to ordinary embodied goods that the symptom had made difficult or morally suspect.**

Araragi's comic observations about her throwing cake rather than a fork also imply relational softening, but remain narrator framing rather than proof that aggression or cruelty has vanished. The short captures an intermediate Hitagi: recognizably sharp, already relationally safer, not morally completed.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN embodied-recovery model; PRESERVE incomplete relational change.

## 3.2 SHORT-002 — `まよいルーム`

**Release horizon:** 2010 guidebook; through V08.  
**Internal horizon:** ghost-era Hachikuji during Araragi's exam-study period; pre-deity.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

The comic premise is an “abduction” of Hachikuji into Araragi's room, but the analytically important material is Hachikuji's own account of her post-`迷い牛` condition. The old binding that prevented her from reaching destinations has ended; nevertheless, she remains fundamentally a road-dwelling ghost. She analogizes this to invitation rules associated with vampires and says that **the road is her dwelling**.

This is useful local CD about her state. Because it is supplementary, it may clarify but should not override stronger numbered oddity rules if tension emerges.

The relational core is quieter. Hachikuji removes the backpack and hair ties that visually define her wandering public identity, puts on pajamas, and sleeps in Araragi's bed. Araragi realizes that she has effectively been walking for more than a decade and allows her to rest.

The scene thus gives literal form to an idea later central to the series:

> **A person defined by movement can still deserve rest without the rest having to become a new permanent destination.**

It is a miniature act of hospitality rather than a cure. Hachikuji remains what she is; Araragi provides a temporary place in which her defining burden need not be continuously enacted.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN Araragi–Hachikuji care beneath gag hostility; local oddity rule remains CONDITIONAL.

## 3.3 SHORT-003 — `するがコート`

**Release horizon:** 2010 guidebook; through V08.  
**Internal horizon:** high school after Kanbaru/Hitagi relation is re-established; exact date OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Araragi challenges Kanbaru to basketball and secretly primes his own body by letting Shinobu drink blood. Even with supernatural enhancement, he loses 120–0.

His real motive is not victory. Hitagi has been worried that Kanbaru seemed low after leaving basketball, and Araragi creates a situation in which Kanbaru can play at full strength against an opponent she need not protect. Kanbaru, meanwhile, interprets the “duel” through her own comic dating grammar.

The short is useful because care is not transparent here. Araragi disguises concern as rivalry and even cheats at the terms of the contest. The result is visibly restorative for Kanbaru, but the method remains characteristically indirect and ethically messy.

The strongest formulation is:

> **A lost practice can remain a real source of joy without becoming proof of a hidden vocational essence.**

Basketball matters to Kanbaru because it is part of her lived history, not because abandoning it would make later Kanbaru inauthentic.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN ordinary-care-through-activity; PRESERVE nonessentialist view of athletic identity.

## 3.4 SHORT-004 — `なでこプール`

**Release horizon:** 2010 guidebook; through V08.  
**Internal horizon:** exam-candidate Araragi and middle-school Nadeko; likely before the later rupture, exact date OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Nadeko asks Araragi specifically to teach her swimming. She minimizes the legitimacy of consuming Kanbaru's or Karen's time while treating Araragi's availability differently, and she openly ties the lesson to wanting his attention. Her deliberately adventurous swimsuit is another attempt to manage how he sees her.

The short therefore supplies early evidence that Nadeko's Koyomi-centered self-presentation is not purely passive. She can choose situations, appearance, and excuses in ways designed to produce recognition.

That does **not** convert every shy behavior into manipulation. Nor does Araragi's eroticized/adultifying gaze become an objective account of Nadeko's intention. The better model is:

> **Early Nadeko combines genuine social inhibition with strategic attempts to control the conditions under which Koyomi sees her.**

This strongly complements Hyakumonogatari's evidence that quietness is not the same thing as obedience.

The final swimming/vampire gag is not sufficient authority for a universal vampire-water rule.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN Koyomi-centered self-presentation; REJECT passive/obedient-only early Nadeko.

## 3.5 SHORT-005 — `つばさソング`

**Release horizon:** 2010 guidebook; through V08.  
**Internal horizon:** high school after the recent cat crisis; exact adjacency OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Hanekawa takes Araragi to karaoke as a stress-release exercise. She is absurdly competent on her first attempt, but the important turn is self-analytical: she recognizes that trying deliberately to be “normal” is itself not normal, and that pursuing normality as a goal lacks a meaningful dream.

Her proposed solution is not “discover the true Hanekawa.” It is to stop suppressing all visible individuality so aggressively that pressure has nowhere to go. She asks Araragi to cut her braids.

This is a strong early statement of the series' later anti-essentialism:

> **Self-expression can be a change in strategy rather than the unveiling of a hidden, ontologically truer person.**

Araragi cutting the braids is relationally complicated. He is both a trusted person and one source of the emotional stress around lost love. His participation therefore should not be simplified into either liberating rescuer or harmful possessor.

He keeps the cut braids. That is evidence of Araragi's behavior and attachment, not ownership over Hanekawa's transition.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN suppression→integration model; REJECT true-self simplification.

## 3.6 SHORT-006 — `ひたぎネック`

**Release horizon:** 2012-09-28; through V14.  
**Internal horizon:** immediately before Hitagi is introduced to the Fire Sisters.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Hitagi wants the family introduction. For her, being introduced to Karen and Tsukihi is not administrative trivia; it is social recognition that she is really Araragi's girlfriend and may belong in a part of his life from which romance could otherwise remain isolated.

The desire is colored by her broken family history. She imagines whether having had a younger sister might have changed her household or allowed Araragi to meet her mother. That counterfactual is emotionally revealing but not causal fact.

Araragi explicitly recognizes that being lovers does not give him total access to Hitagi's feelings. This is a remarkably early precursor to the V30 family thesis:

> **Intimacy creates legitimate claims to inclusion and recognition without creating total epistemic access.**

His own older preference for private, self-contained relationships is also already loosening from the earlier `人間強度` ideology.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN belonging-without-total-access; family incorporation becomes desired recognition, not identity possession.

## 3.7 SHORT-007 — `かれんアームレッグ`

**Release horizon:** 2012-09-28; through V14.  
**Internal horizon:** late summer vacation, relatively early in Araragi/Hitagi dating.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Karen reacts dramatically to learning that her brother has a girlfriend. The most reliable relationship evidence is not the deliberately extreme incest-adjacent joke language; it is the degree to which secrecy mattered and the way Karen experiences a girlfriend as a threat to sibling exclusivity.

Her physical fearlessness also coexists with emotional childishness. Araragi anticipates being hit and instead receives a much less easily managed emotional collapse.

The source must be protected against comic over-reading:

> **Possessive sibling hyperbole is not evidence of literal romantic desire, nor is it a normative account of sibling relations.**

It does, however, show that Araragi's movement into romance has consequences for a pre-existing family system. A new bond changes the expectations surrounding old bonds even when nobody has formally consented to that rearrangement.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN family-system displacement; reject literalization of comic incest rhetoric.

## 3.8 SHORT-008 — `つきひエターナル`

**Release horizon:** 2012-09-28; through V14.  
**Internal horizon:** immediately after the V06 Kagenui/Ononoki confrontation, before summer vacation ends.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Tsukihi attacks the secrecy around Hitagi. Araragi internally wants to answer that he has just protected Tsukihi from an existential supernatural threat, but he cannot disclose the event.

The story therefore captures a crucial asymmetry:

> **Material protection does not automatically repair the interpretive harms created by withheld information.**

From Tsukihi's position, the visible issue is that her brother entered a serious relationship and concealed it. From Araragi's position, the invisible issue is that he has just protected her in a way she cannot know. Both local perspectives are real, and neither automatically cancels the other.

This becomes a strong early precursor to V30's disclosure problem. Araragi can care deeply, act beneficially, and still leave people with materially distorted models because of what he withholds.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN secrecy/disclosure distinction; protection ≠ full relational transparency.

## 3.9 SHORT-009 — `しのぶハウス`

**Release horizon:** 2012-09-28; through V14.  
**Internal horizon:** night after the `ガハラサミット` cluster.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Shinobu feels excluded and attacks the exclusivist implications of Araragi's dramatic vow by forcing him to rank herself, Hitagi, Karen, and Tsukihi. Araragi refuses the ranking game and says that in the impossible-choice situation he would reach for Hanekawa—to ask for help.

This is unusually early evidence for distributed-help grammar:

> **Refusing to rank relationships can lead not to indecision but to seeking another agent capable of expanding the option set.**

Shinobu's jealousy is still real. The scene demonstrates that a new romance can threaten a pre-existing supernatural bond even when the relationships are non-equivalent and should not be put on one linear scale.

Araragi's reflections on `人は一人で勝手に助かるだけ` must remain subordinate to later V2 corrections: the slogan never erases the material fact of assistance.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN non-ranked relational plurality and distributed aid; PRESERVE jealousy as relationship-state evidence, not sovereignty claim.

## 3.10 SHORT-010 — `つばさボード`

**Release horizon:** 2013-07-06; through V15.  
**Internal horizon:** exact date OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Hanekawa.

Hanekawa and Hitagi discuss information, knowledge, ignorance, and irreversible learning. Hanekawa says knowledge that is not used can be functionally indistinguishable from ignorance; Hitagi emphasizes that excessive information can immobilize and that modern life can force knowledge upon people who might rationally wish not to carry it.

The story is excellent evidence for Hanekawa's epistemic self-presentation. Her famous `何でもは知らないわよ。知ってることだけ` here sits inside a larger argument that **having information is not identical to having wisdom, agency, or capacity to act**.

This anticipates later specialist epistemology without becoming a universal doctrine. Information has costs. Un-knowing is usually unavailable once knowledge has entered a person's life. Growth may therefore arrive as burden rather than voluntary enrichment.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN knowledge/access ≠ wisdom/action; high-value Hanekawa epistemic voice.

## 3.11 SHORT-011 — `まよいキャッスル`

**Release horizon:** 2013-08-17; through V15.  
**Internal horizon:** ghost-era Hachikuji; exact point OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Hachikuji.

A conversation about having one year to live becomes a critique of compulsory optimization. Araragi suggests that under a terminal horizon he might do nothing at all—not because nothing matters, but because the demand to extract maximal achievement, pleasure, or meaning from limited time can itself become another burden.

This is not suicide advocacy and should not be converted into a stable Araragi doctrine. Its value is as a local counter-pressure against the series' many rescue, effort, and self-improvement imperatives:

> **Non-achievement and rest can be legitimate uses of finite time.**

Hachikuji ends by wishing him a long life and returning to their name-bite routine. The philosophical thought experiment thus terminates in ordinary relational warmth rather than nihilism.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN legitimacy of non-optimization; PRESERVE local/conditional status.

## 3.12 SHORT-012 — `ひたぎコイン`

**Release horizon:** 2013-09-13; through V15.  
**Internal horizon:** high school; discussion explicitly draws on Hitagi's two years concealing her condition.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Araragi asks how Hitagi survived years of concealment because he anticipates having to hide his own vampiric traits. Hitagi distinguishes forms of deception and demonstrates with a coin trick that a successful lie may not require successfully convincing the target of the literal proposition.

Her key practical claim is that deception can be outcome-oriented: sometimes the point is not to be believed, and sometimes one must choose between appearing good and obtaining what one regards as the better result.

This is **Hitagi's survival-derived technique**, not objective ethical doctrine. It is especially useful beside later Kaiki material because it distinguishes:

- factual falsity;
- performance;
- audience management;
- motive;
- desired outcome;
- moral reputation.

Her closing wish for a relationship with less `表裏` also prevents the short from becoming a celebration of manipulation. A technique learned under coercive conditions can remain useful while the person simultaneously wants relationships in which the technique is less necessary.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN deception/performance distinctions; survivor technique ≠ moral universal.

## 3.13 SHORT-013 — `なでこミラー`

**Release horizon:** 2013-09-21; through V15.  
**Internal horizon:** middle-school Nadeko before later god/self-authoring developments; exact point OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Nadeko.

Nadeko and Tsukihi discuss *Jekyll and Hyde*, opposites, destructive relationships, and an imagined `逆撫子`. Nadeko imagines her inverse as bright, socially capable, eye-contacting, and serious. She wants to meet that other Nadeko even if the encounter results in criticism because it would reveal another possible version of herself.

This is an extraordinarily valuable **publication-early precursor** to later multiplied-Nadeko material. It is not proof that later selves were already secretly planned within the character, and the source must not be used to turn retrospective resonance into destiny.

Two early traits matter more immediately:

1. Nadeko already understands “serious” as something that would characterize an opposite self, undermining outsider equations of quietness with dutifulness.
2. Her `撫子が怒られたら済む話だよ` formulation contains a self-negating willingness to solve a relational problem by absorbing blame or punishment herself.

The short thus strengthens both **plural-self curiosity** and a much more dangerous **self-sacrificial blame economy**.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN early possible-self imagination and self-blame precursor; REJECT later destiny backprojection.

## 3.14 SHORT-014 — `しのぶサイエンス`

**Release horizon:** 2013-10-26; through V16.  
**Internal horizon:** speculative Shinobu/Ononoki conversation; exact date OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Shinobu.

Shinobu and Ononoki use Asimov, robots, rulers, creators, and tools to debate control. The conceptual center is Ononoki's observation that creators and users are usually distinct and that tools are made precisely to do what human beings cannot; by design, such tools may exceed the hands that made them.

The discussion also rejects fantasy solutions in which social order is redeemed merely by finding a superior ruler. Superior beings can themselves be governed, instrumentalized, or turned into tools.

This is among the most important publication-horizon precursors in the compilation:

> **Creation does not imply control, and superiority does not imply legitimate sovereignty.**

That proposition becomes central much later in Monster Season, but here it already exists as speculative character philosophy by the V16 reader horizon.

The authority ceiling matters. This is CD/IT spoken through a science-fiction discussion, not a literal law of Monogatari creation ontology. It can strengthen the history of an idea without settling later Ononoki/Nadeko metaphysics.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN creator/creation non-sovereignty as early thematic proposition; oddity ontology remains OPEN.

## 3.15 SHORT-015 — `ひたぎフィギュア`

**Release horizon:** 2013-11-22; through V16.  
**Internal horizon:** shortly after Ononoki begins living in the Araragi household; exam-study period.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Hitagi sees Ononoki in her doll-like cover mode and demands an explanation as Araragi's girlfriend. Araragi recognizes a promise not to keep oddity secrets from Hitagi while also confronting the practical fact that some information is difficult to explain locally without revealing a much larger hidden structure.

This is an important refinement of disclosure ethics:

> **A duty not to deceive is not the same thing as an obligation to make every fact immediately intelligible in isolation.**

That distinction does not provide a blanket excuse for secrecy. It identifies a real communication problem: background dependencies, third-party confidentiality, and dangerous context can make disclosure non-atomic.

Hitagi's girlfriend status gives her genuine relational standing, but the short does not make that standing unlimited. Ononoki's immobile doll presentation also must not be interpreted as consent to being treated as property.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN disclosure as contextual process; girlfriend standing ≠ unlimited jurisdiction.

## 3.16 SHORT-016 — `ひたぎサラマンダー`

**Release horizon:** 2013-11-23; through V16.  
**Internal horizon:** broad/OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Kaiki.

Kaiki and Hitagi discuss *Fahrenheit 451*, rereading, performance, and change. Kaiki notices Hitagi's ability to make herself the clown so that another person becomes the audience. His account is interested narrator interpretation, not neutral diagnosis.

The most important moment is Hitagi's own distinction. Asked whether she has “changed,” she frames the relevant transformation not as `変心` but `恋心`.

That does not mean romantic love is the singular explanation of all change. It shows Hitagi resisting a model in which change simply means exchanging one essence for another.

The rereading discussion also gives a useful archival metaphor: the same book can yield different readings at different times without the earlier reading becoming unreal. That is **IT**, not a diegetic method rule, but it closely parallels the V2 revision discipline.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN change-with-continuity; Kaiki's performativity reading remains interested NR.

## 3.17 SHORT-017 — `ひたぎスローイング`

**Release horizon:** 2014-04-02; through V18.  
**Internal horizon:** Kanbaru's retrospective account of first meeting Hitagi in middle school.  
**Narrator:** Kanbaru.

This is one of the collection's most consequential prehistory stories. Pre-crab Hitagi is socially central, graceful, athletically gifted, surrounded by followers, and capable of using status deliberately. She recruits Kanbaru toward track, is rejected harshly, then returns alone after recognizing that Kanbaru's public rejection of a popular girl could expose Kanbaru to retaliation from Hitagi's own social orbit.

Hitagi engineers a basketball free-throw contest and wins narrowly, 10–9. Kanbaru infers that the close score was deliberate: Hitagi publicly settles the challenge, demonstrates approval of Kanbaru, and protects Kanbaru's face while discouraging followers from ostracizing her.

The exact intentionality of the close score remains **Kanbaru NR/SI**, not TF. But the social outcome and Kanbaru's interpretation are powerful evidence of how she remembers Hitagi's care.

The relationship begins through a complex mixture of:

- asymmetrical prestige;
- indirect protection;
- social manipulation;
- athletic admiration;
- and Kanbaru's voluntary over-acceptance of indebtedness when she declares herself Hitagi's “dog.”

This is neither simple coercion nor pure egalitarian friendship.

> **Protective influence can be genuine while still operating through asymmetrical social power.**

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN pre-crab Hitagi social competence and indirect protective governance; Kanbaru's motive attribution remains SI.

## 3.18 SHORT-018 — `するがパレス`

**Release horizon:** 2014-04-23; through V18.  
**Internal horizon:** current Kanbaru/Araragi relationship established; exact date broad/OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Kanbaru.

A discussion of A. A. Milne, expectation, and commissioned art becomes a compact creator/audience ethics conversation. Kanbaru values fulfilling expectations and identifies herself as a “soldier type” who can enjoy obeying someone she admires. That is her self-story, not proof of an immutable submissive essence.

Hitagi's counterpoint is that some creators cannot create on command. The conversation converges on a permissive model:

> **The creator may make what they like; the reader may read it how they like.**

This is not a denial that commissions, audiences, or relationships influence creation. It rejects the stronger claim that influence creates ownership of meaning.

In publication history, this strengthens the fact that creator/audience non-sovereignty exists well before V26–V29 make creation an explicit supernatural and ethical problem.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN creator/audience independence; Kanbaru's obedience language remains self-story rather than essence.

## 3.19 SHORT-019 — `よつぎフューチャー`

**Release horizon:** 2014-05-28; through V18.  
**Internal horizon:** deliberately meta/chronologically unstable.  
**Narrator:** Ononoki.

Ononoki and Araragi discuss *Momo*, time, mortality, stories, waste, and empathy. The short openly jokes about narration rules and supplementary-story conventions; exact chronology is therefore weak authority by design.

Several ideas are nevertheless analytically valuable as character philosophy. Ononoki argues that works “for children” are often adults' images of children. Araragi proposes empathy rather than lesson extraction as a purpose of reading. The conversation eventually reverses an efficiency reading of the story: the apparently “wasted” time may be exactly what makes life valuable.

The strongest formulation is:

> **Not every meaningful expenditure of time needs to justify itself as productive.**

Ononoki's claims that her dead state changes the meaning of time remain rhetorical CD, not a hard metaphysics of corpse temporality.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN anti-efficiency / anti-lesson extraction; chronology and ontology remain guarded.

## 3.20 SHORT-020 — `おうぎトラベル`

**Release horizon:** 2014-05-28; through V18.  
**Internal horizon:** late high school before Hanekawa's post-graduation world travel.  
**Narrator:** Ougi.

Ougi asks Hanekawa about world travel. Hanekawa does not frame travel as abandoning books or intellectual life; she frames the world itself as a producer and collector of stories. She still intends to carry books, because books read while traveling become differently situated experiences.

This is a strong correction to any reading in which Hanekawa's later movement outward must be an anti-intellectual repudiation of her former self.

> **Travel extends her knowledge practice into embodied world-contact; it does not require erasing the reader she already was.**

Ougi's dislike of Hanekawa's optimistic synthesis is explicit and useful. Ougi has access and intelligence but remains an interested interlocutor rather than a neutral commentator on Hanekawa.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN continuity between reading/intellect and global travel; Ougi neutrality rejected.

## 3.21 SHORT-021 — `するがニート`

**Release horizon:** 2014-08-16; through V18.  
**Internal horizon:** dreamlike Kanbaru/Tooe encounter; literal chronology/ontology intentionally weak.  
**Narrator:** Kanbaru.

Kanbaru converses with her dead mother about Tiptree, parent-child inheritance, reading, growth, and time. Because the scene is dreamlike and Tooe is deceased, it should be treated as **Kanbaru's internalized maternal voice/self-dialogue**, not unqualified evidence that Tooe literally returned.

One of the sharpest observations is that “a child watches the parent's back” also means the parent has turned their back on the child. The aphorism destabilizes idealized inheritance without denying influence.

Kanbaru also articulates a developmental shift in scale: what once constituted the entire world can become merely one part of a larger world. Family love can remain real while ceasing to be sovereign.

Tooe's remembered/imaged `薬になれなきゃ毒になれ。でなきゃあんたはただの水だ` must remain character philosophy, not V2 doctrine. It is precisely the kind of memorable line whose rhetorical force exceeds its moral reliability.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN inherited-voice-without-sovereignty; literal Tooe encounter remains UA.

## 3.22 SHORT-022 — `ろうかゴッド`

**Release horizon:** 2014-09-19; through V19.  
**Internal horizon:** Rouka after middle-school graduation and before/around the demon-collection trajectory; relevant to V10 prehistory.  
**Narrator:** Kaiki.

Kaiki meets Rouka while rumors about his scams are being neutralized by her advice to stop worrying, leave things alone, and forget. He recognizes that her “life advice” operates with the structure of a placebo or scam: the reassurance can work even without solving the underlying situation.

Rouka says she was treated as mature/distant and that she collects other people's unhappiness. Kaiki fabricates a story about exploiting a girl; Rouka replies that the girl will recover because Kaiki is not as important in her life as he imagines.

That response becomes the story's strongest anti-sovereignty proposition:

> **A person may be causally important in another life without being as central to that life as their own ego imagines.**

Kaiki in turn interprets Rouka as deceiving herself by absorbing others' unhappiness so that she need not face her own. That is Kaiki NR/SI, not objective diagnosis.

His foreshadowed intention to give her a “devil” is stronger than mere gag material but should not be expanded beyond what the short actually establishes.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN Rouka prehistory and Kaiki/Rouka interpretive relation; Kanbaru's prior knowledge remains unchanged.

## 3.23 SHORT-023 — `しのぶフィギュア`

**Release horizon:** 2014-11-21; through V19.  
**Internal horizon:** self-parodically unstable; the story itself jokes about when it occurs.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Araragi opens by calling Shinobu indispensable, `一心同体`, and like part of his body—then immediately reverses the bodily metaphor to say that if anyone is a part, he is part of her body. That asymmetry matters. The language is intense relational rhetoric, not proof of fused numerical personhood.

The plot escalates into two apparently real Shinobus created through unexplained “vampire power,” with the story explicitly leaning into merchandising/meta logic. The duplication is not acceptable as a general vampire capability rule. The text itself treats the chronology and mechanism as the joke.

This story is therefore a useful authority-ceiling test:

> **A supplementary short can strongly evidence how Araragi speaks about a bond while being almost useless as hard supernatural mechanics.**

The later V28/V30 rule remains controlling: shared bond and joint standing do not erase separate personhood.

**Transition:** PRESERVE Araragi–Shinobu extraordinary relational centrality; REJECT duplication as general vampire law.

## 3.24 SHORT-024 — `かれんブラッシング`

**Release horizon:** 2015-07-09; through V19.  
**Internal horizon:** broad school-era Araragi/Karen family comedy.  
**Narrator:** Karen.

Karen's fear of dental treatment contrasts sharply with her physical combat confidence. That alone is a useful character correction: bodily bravery in one domain does not produce generalized fearlessness.

The rest is intentionally excessive sibling/fetish comedy. Karen entrusts herself to Araragi's pseudo-dentistry with astonishing credulity, while Shinobu appears as the disguised assistant. The scene repeatedly sexualizes the examination and ends in a mouth-to-mouth “treatment” gag.

The responsible analytical use is narrow:

- Karen's older-brother trust can make her unusually compliant with Araragi in comic situations;
- her physical confidence coexists with ordinary fear and embarrassment;
- Shinobu is normalized as a household accomplice in Araragi's absurd routines.

What it **cannot** establish is equally important:

> **Comic compliance is not a general consent norm, sexualized sibling framing is not evidence of literal incest desire, and Araragi's claimed supernatural saliva treatment is not a reliable physiology rule.**

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN domain-specific vulnerability and sibling trust; DOWNGRADE fetish-gag literalization.

## 3.25 SHORT-025 — `つきひブラッシング`

**Release horizon:** 2015-07-09; through V19.  
**Internal horizon:** post-graduation; Nadeko described as substantially recovering.  
**Narrator:** Tsukihi.

Tsukihi wants her extremely long hair cut, partly in relation to Nadeko's current appearance. The ordinary act of copying or borrowing another person's style is important precisely because it is **influence without identity transfer**. Tsukihi can take a cue from Nadeko without becoming Nadeko or making Nadeko author of her appearance.

The story also contains a useful memory asymmetry: Tsukihi has forgotten cutting Nadeko's bangs in the past even though Araragi remembers the event. Shared history need not remain equally salient to every participant.

The comic accident in which Araragi burns Tsukihi's hair and then attempts a supernatural repair invokes her phoenix regeneration. Shinobu observes that Tsukihi would restore herself anyway. This is supporting domestic familiarity with her condition, not a license to expand V06 mechanics beyond the stronger numbered evidence.

Tsukihi's phrase that Nadeko is `だいぶん快方` is also carefully limited: **improving is not completed recovery**.

## 3.26 SHORT-026 — `こよみヒストリー`

**Release horizon:** 2015-11-27; through V20.  
**Internal horizon:** meta-retrospective composite, not one continuous scene.  
**Narrator:** Araragi/meta-memory montage.

The short revisits prior character voices in compressed sequence and makes its form the point: Araragi's “history” is populated by people whose remembered words and injuries continue to structure him.

Nadeko is remembered through what Araragi did and did not do; Hanekawa is associated with moving toward the future; the silent/elliptical final presence is linked to origin, injury, and recurrence. The text is intentionally too composite to treat these as one diegetic conversation.

Its strongest contribution is methodological:

> **Remembering an earlier state does not restore it as current authority. History remains causally active precisely because the present is not identical to it.**

That makes `こよみヒストリー` useful synthesis evidence but poor chronology evidence.

**Transition:** PRESERVE memory/provenance without event inflation; no new hard chronology.

## 3.27 SHORT-027 — `よつぎストレス`

**Release horizon:** 2015-12-23; through V20.  
**Internal horizon:** playful/uncertain interaction among Black Hanekawa, Shinobu, and Ononoki.  
**Narrator:** Black Hanekawa.

Black Hanekawa and Shinobu are struggling against an ape oddity when Ononoki destroys half of it with `例外のほうが多い規則`. Ononoki insists she did not “save” them; she did her job. The text distinguishes causal rescue from helper intention in a way later V29 will make ethically central.

Black Hanekawa envies—or immediately retracts envying—Ononoki's apparent absence of stress. Ononoki describes emotion as a tool and claims that human beings also use stress as entertainment, including their own misfortune. These are deliberately provocative CD, not objective psychology.

The final structure is particularly useful. Three oddities orient toward their respective superiors differently:

- Shinobu → bond;
- Ononoki → work;
- Black Hanekawa → the unresolved question of what her relation to Hanekawa is *for*.

This refuses a single creator/familiar template.

> **Similar dependency structures can carry different justificatory grammars: affection, employment, projection, duty, or unresolved self-relation are not interchangeable.**

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN aid-without-intent and plurality of dependent relations; do not promote stress theory to doctrine.

## 3.28 SHORT-028 — `人として`

**Release horizon:** 2017-01-14; reader already through V23.  
**Internal horizon:** Kizumonogatari spring-break insertion, before the final Araragi/Kiss-shot resolution.  
**Narrator:** Kiss-shot.

This late-written prequel gives Kiss-shot's perspective on Guillotine Cutter's final intervention. He says Araragi will not be able to kill Kiss-shot and proposes using his own death as the instrument that might force Araragi into doing so. He explicitly describes his own life as a tool or blade available for the mission.

Kiss-shot's own difficulty is now stated directly: she can allow almost anyone to kill her, but the specific servant who saved her is precisely the person least capable of becoming the required killer.

This materially **revises the reader's understanding of V04**, but it cannot be turned into V04 Araragi knowledge.

The ethical distinction is also sharp. Guillotine Cutter criticizes suicide that entangles Araragi while simultaneously offering his own death as an instrumental strategy. That tension is not resolved by calling him either hypocrite or saint; it exposes how professional absolutism can make a person's own life into mission property.

The listed “full-course” vampire powers remain local dialogue/scene evidence and should not override later capability limits without corroboration.

**Transition:** RC V04 reader model; PRESERVE Araragi's publication-local uncertainty; specialist self-instrumentalization remains ethically contested.

## 3.29 SHORT-029 — `どうかして`

**Release horizon:** 2017-01-14; through V23.  
**Internal horizon:** Kizumonogatari resolution, Hanekawa/Meme observing Araragi and Kiss-shot.  
**Narrator:** Hanekawa.

Hanekawa calls the horrific compromise “good” and immediately interrogates what that reaction says about her. She imagines that, had she encountered the dying vampire earlier, she might simply have accepted the natural order and walked away. This is valuable because Hanekawa does not narrate herself as innately incapable of abandonment.

## 3.30 SHORT-030 — `そして`

**Release horizon:** 2017-01-14; through V23.  
**Internal horizon:** exactly 360 days after the spring-break fight with Dramaturgy; Araragi has graduated high school.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

The chronology is unusually strong. Dramaturgy returns and says he has worked repeatedly with Hanekawa abroad, then renews his attempt to recruit Araragi into specialist work.

Araragi refuses. Crucially, the refusal is not merely fear. Dramaturgy tells him that after the spring-break catastrophe and a year of consequences he must no longer believe in his future. Araragi answers that he **still does**.

This matters enormously for anti-teleology:

> **Post-Kizu supernatural competence did not make a specialist career inevitable, and post-graduation uncertainty did not already contain the later police trajectory as destiny.**

The short also offers a strong relational ethic through Hanekawa's reported position that helping someone's enemy does not automatically mean becoming that person's enemy. That is a non-zero-sum model of aid, though the precise wording comes through Dramaturgy's report.

Black Hanekawa apparently remains relevant abroad, but Araragi himself is surprised; the supplement should not be used to force unshown continuity beyond the local report.

## 3.31 SHORT-031 — `どうして`

**Release horizon:** March 2017 distribution; through V23.  
**Internal horizon:** Meme retrospectively narrates the spring-break moment when child-form Kiss-shot drags unconscious Araragi.  
**Narrator:** Meme.

Meme claims his theft of Kiss-shot's heart was not only balance among hunters and vampire but an attempt to reset the balance inside Kiss-shot herself between accumulated death wish and survival instinct. He wanted centuries of temporal sediment temporarily stripped of jurisdiction so that she might confront the present.

This is an important **later self-explanation by Meme**. It should not be retroactively promoted to a fact the original V04 reader could have known or to proof that every consequence was part of a benevolent master plan.

Meme then sees Kiss-shot clinging humiliatingly to life for Araragi and says the high-school boy, not Meme, has pierced her heart in the figurative sense. He offers the cram-school ruin not “to save” her but to allow continuation and remove an immediate escape route.

That intervention mixes compassion and paternalistic jurisdiction. Meme thinks he is creating conditions for choice, but he is also manipulating the field of available choices.

## 3.33 SHORT-033 — `まよいウェルカム`

**Release horizon:** 2017-03-26; reader through V23.  
**Internal horizon:** explicitly immediately before V24 `忍物語`.  
**Narrator:** Suicide-Master.

Suicide-Master arrives in Japan and accidentally dies while avoiding Hachikuji's shrine. The resulting conversation is one of the collection's most important time-and-identity pieces.

Hachikuji warns that six hundred years without contact can make a former companion effectively different. She uses her parents' failed marriage and changing historical terminology to ask how something once correctly chosen can later become wrong without the original event itself being rewritten.

Her strongest demand is symmetrical:

> **If you reject “the past was better,” also reject “the past was embarrassing.”**

Suicide-Master agrees that even if present Kiss-shot disappoints her, the earlier relationship will not thereby become invalid.

This is almost a direct statement of the V2 archival ethic:

> **A past state can remain genuine without remaining current authority.**

The punchline then becomes the doorway to V24: Hachikuji says the vampire Suicide-Master seeks is no longer there.

No V24 outcome may be backprojected into this encounter despite the explicit prequel placement.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN history-without-sovereignty and anti-retroactive-invalidity; V24 remains future to the scene.

## 3.34 SHORT-034 — `よつぎスノードーム`

## 3.36 SHORT-036 — `そだちペナルティ`

**Release horizon:** new 2024 story; through V30.  
**Internal horizon:** Manase University after a semester exam; exact university year OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

Sodachi turns a disappointing exam result into a self-imposed punishment: if she missed her target, she must go on what amounts to a date with Araragi in an escape room. She even throws Araragi's abandoned `人間強度` doctrine back at him to explain having no friends.

The obvious comic interpretation—“she secretly wanted the date”—would be analytically lazy. What the text supports more strongly is Sodachi's continuing tendency to structure life through punitive constraints and overcontrol.

Yet she also explicitly identifies a form of effort that *was* worthwhile. Facing Araragi in high school, instead of continuing to flee him, prevented her from spending life in what she calls a `負の数の気持ち`.

This makes the short a particularly good correction to outcome-only models of effort:

> **Effort can matter because it changes one's relationship to a problem even when it does not guarantee the desired external result.**

Araragi and Sodachi successfully cooperate approximately six years after their earlier childhood collaboration. The ending's elaborate “not hate” formulation preserves ambivalence rather than collapsing their history into sentimental reconciliation.

## 3.37 SHORT-037 — `しのぶトゥナイト`

**Release horizon:** new 2024 story; through V30.  
**Internal horizon:** working-adult Araragi, university graduate and police officer, immediately before a long United States assignment; bridge between V23-like adult state and V30.  
**Narrator:** Araragi.

This is the most important Family-ethics precursor among the new collection stories. Araragi explicitly looks back at the Araragi–Shinobu bond and calls the arrangement potentially unfair or exploitative because Shinobu must accompany his life decisions. Shinobu also says she has twisted Araragi's life, making the causal burden reciprocal. Reciprocal causation still does not mean fused personhood.

## 3.38 SHORT-038 — `なでこパスト`

**Release horizon:** 2024 Off & Monster Season / YOASOBI source prose; through V30.  
**Internal horizon:** Off/Monster reflective period; exact numbered adjacency remains OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Ononoki.

Nadeko describes feeling as though she is continually fighting a dead past self whom others can still see more clearly than they see the current Nadeko. The problem is not simply remembering her past; it is living under other people's preference for an earlier, more legible version.

She also admits something crucial: around Araragi she herself often performed that older self more strongly than necessary and benefited from the childish image. > **Past identity is evidence and residue, not an opponent whose defeat would authorize the present.**

## 3.39 SHORT-039 — `しのぶフューチャー`

**Release horizon:** 2024 Off & Monster Season / YOASOBI source prose; through V30.  
**Internal horizon:** Off/Monster reflective period; exact Monster/Family position remains OPEN.  
**Narrator:** Ononoki.

Shinobu fears that six centuries of life have exhausted the possibility of genuinely new experience. Ononoki suggests that the problem may not be an empty future but a self that has learned too effectively to avoid friction. Long life can produce sophistication, categorization, and pattern recognition until novelty is preprocessed into something already known.

The conversation does not solve the problem by demanding spectacular reinvention. Instead, it discovers that calm life itself may be novel for someone whose earlier existence was defined by catastrophe and predation.

The most concrete relational surprise is Shinobu calling Ononoki a **friend**. Ononoki notes the improbability: Shinobu once nearly killed her, yet the future has produced mundane counseling intimacy.

That provides the story's strongest anti-determinist evidence:

> **Continuity of history does not bound the set of future relationships to those predictable from the past.**

The repeated `悪くてもいいんだよ` from the paired Nadeko/Shinobu counseling stories is not a doctrine that bad outcomes are desirable. It rejects the idea that change is legitimate only when every revision can be certified as improvement.

The final idea that the present conversation might itself be a carefully rewritten past from the perspective of the future is meta-fictional reflection, not evidence of literal timeline rewriting.

**Transition:** STRENGTHEN future openness and Shinobu–Ononoki relational revision; exact chronology remains OPEN.

# 4. Cross-story synthesis

## 4.1 The collection's deepest structural function: preserving intermediate states

The numbered novels tend to organize interpretation around crises. `短物語` repeatedly shows what exists between crises:

- Hitagi eating cake;
- Hachikuji sleeping;
- Kanbaru playing basketball;
- Hanekawa singing;
- family members learning about relationships;
- Ougi watching a movie;
- Sodachi playing an escape game;
- Shinobu sharing doughnuts before relocation.

These ordinary scenes matter because they prevent crisis-state personality from becoming total personality.

> **A character is not only the person they become under maximum narrative pressure.**

The collection therefore increases reconstructability of the cast without flattening the mainline.

## 4.2 Recovery becomes ordinary and embodied

Several stories define repair through access to ordinary life rather than dramatic cure:

- Hitagi can enjoy food and weight;
- Hachikuji can sleep in a room;
- Kanbaru can play her sport again;
- Hanekawa can sing, cut her hair, and experiment with expression;
- Nadeko can be “substantially recovering” without being finished;
- Sodachi can spend time with the person she once could not face;
- Shinobu can find novelty in a calm shared life.

This strengthens a corpus-wide model:

> **Recovery is the regained ability to have ordinary options; it is not proof that the history producing the wound has vanished.**

## 4.3 Disclosure is not binary

`つきひエターナル`, `ひたぎフィギュア`, `しのぶハウス`, and `しのぶトゥナイト` form a long publication-history line toward V30.

They distinguish:

- caring action from known action;
- protecting someone from explaining that protection;
- secrecy from practical difficulty of explanation;
- pre-existing bonds from new romantic/family standing;
- present acceptance from retroactive authorization.

V30 does not invent these problems ex nihilo. It systematizes a problem the series has repeatedly staged in smaller forms.

The correct historical claim is not “the series always had the V30 doctrine fully formed.” It is:

> **Earlier supplementary scenes repeatedly supplied the components that V30 later organizes into a more explicit jurisdictional ethics.**

## 4.4 Creation, audience, tools, and sovereignty have a long conceptual prehistory

`しのぶサイエンス`, `するがパレス`, and `よつぎフューチャー` are especially important.

By the V16–V18 reader horizon, supplementary prose already contains explicit resistance to:

- superior-ruler fantasy;
- creator control over powerful tools;
- reader ownership of creator intention;
- mandatory productivity;
- lesson-only reading.

Later supernatural creation problems do not need to be treated as sudden thematic inventions. But the early philosophical conversations remain **CD/IT**, not evidence that later mechanics were already established.

## 4.5 Family influence without family sovereignty

`ひたぎネック`, `かれんアームレッグ`, `つきひエターナル`, `するがニート`, `つきひブラッシング`, and `しのぶトゥナイト` collectively show family as a field of real causal pressure:

- inclusion matters;
- secrecy hurts;
- new bonds displace old expectations;
- parental inheritance persists;
- siblings imitate and influence one another;
- shared supernatural lives constrain future options.

Yet none of this proves that family determines identity.

Kanbaru's internalized Tooe is influential but not sovereign. Tsukihi can imitate Nadeko without becoming her. Shinobu can travel because of Araragi's career while still having her own view of the travel.

This is the Family Season thesis in historical miniature:

> **Shared life creates standing and constraint without creating identity possession.**

## 4.6 Retrospective Kizumonogatari insertions: revision without counterfeit foresight

SHORT-028–032 form a deliberately late-published return to spring break.

They add:

- Kiss-shot's view of Guillotine Cutter;
- Hanekawa's self-questioning about whether she would have saved Kiss-shot;
- Meme's professional/client framing;
- Dramaturgy's one-year-later recruitment;
- Meme's later explanation of the stolen-heart intervention;
- the hunters' perception of Kiss-shot's psychological vacancy.

These stories legitimately change what the **later reader** can infer about V04.

They do not justify writing later knowledge backward into:

- V04 Araragi;
- V04 Hanekawa beyond what she says locally;
- the hunters as future-seeing strategists;
- Meme as an omniscient architect.

The governing rule remains:

> **Retrospective elaboration may deepen causality without erasing earlier uncertainty.**

## 4.7 The adult bridge de-teleologizes the future

SHORT-030, 035, 036, and 037 are unusually useful because they occupy transitions that the numbered spine leaves comparatively sparse.

They show:

- Araragi explicitly declining specialist recruitment after graduation;
- Ougi becoming capable of ordinary post-graduation leisure;
- Sodachi and Araragi maintaining an ambivalent university relationship;
- adult Araragi becoming a police officer while auditing how that career constrains Shinobu.

The result is anti-destiny rather than destiny.

Araragi's eventual adult profession becomes **one realized future among futures he once considered, rejected, or could not yet imagine**.

## 4.8 Past and future as paired anti-essentialist problems

The collection closes with a deliberate pair:

- `なでこパスト` — the past self threatens to become sovereign through fixed social memory;
- `しのぶフューチャー` — the future threatens to disappear when every new event is classified as repetition.

Nadeko needs to let the past remain part of her without fighting it as an enemy. Shinobu needs to recover the possibility that continuity can still contain novelty.

Together:

> **Identity requires neither defeat of the past nor conquest of the future. It requires enough continuity to remain answerable to history and enough openness that history cannot exhaust what comes next.**

That is the strongest reason these apparently minor stories belong in the mature V2 corpus.

# 5. Narrator and voice findings

## 5.1 Araragi narration remains relationship-indexed

Hyakumonogatari demonstrated this through dialogue. `短物語` demonstrates it through prose focalization. Araragi's internal register shifts depending on whether he is narrating:

- Hitagi's recovery;
- Hachikuji's wandering;
- Kanbaru's sport;
- Nadeko's self-presentation;
- his sisters' invasions of privacy;
- Shinobu's jealousy;
- Ononoki's surveillance;
- Ougi's opaque interiority;
- Sodachi's punitive self-structure;
- adult Shinobu cohabitation.

His narration is therefore not a neutral wrapper around “the real character.” It is a dynamic social instrument.

## 5.2 Non-Araragi narrators materially alter the epistemic field

The collection becomes especially valuable whenever the narrator changes.

- **Hanekawa** narrates knowledge as burden and questions her own capacity for rescue.
- **Nadeko** reveals possible-self curiosity and later explicitly admits performing earlier versions of herself.
- **Kanbaru** reconstructs Hitagi's social competence and Tooe's internalized voice.
- **Kaiki** reads performativity and deception everywhere, including in Rouka.
- **Ougi** narrates Hanekawa through an openly resistant stance.
- **Black Hanekawa** sees affect as substance and Ononoki as suspiciously professional.
- **Kiss-shot** narrates professional hunters through aristocratic contempt and death-wish logic.
- **Meme** narrates his own interventions as balance and “kindness,” which is exactly why his account must remain interested.
- **Suicide-Master** interprets historical change through the perspective of almost pathological longevity.
- **Ononoki** turns both Nadeko and Shinobu into counseling objects while continually reminding the reader that her own emotion model is nonhuman/unstable.

Narrator diversity therefore supports the V2 rule:

> **Access is viewpoint, not omniscience.**

# 10. Final `短物語` synthesis

`短物語` earns its place in the mature V2 corpus not because every short reveals a hidden fact, but because the collection is a dense **archive of intermediate states**.

It catches characters between the large named crises by which readers usually remember them. Hitagi is learning to eat. Hachikuji is allowed to sleep. Kanbaru gets to play. Hanekawa experiments with expression. Nadeko imagines an opposite self before later creating multiple selves. Hitagi wants family recognition while Araragi learns that intimacy does not mean total access. Black Hanekawa encounters a professional oddity whose causal rescue is not motivated by rescue. Kiss-shot, Hanekawa, Meme, and the hunters re-enter spring break from later publication horizons without receiving magical access to the future. Araragi rejects the specialist path after graduation before eventually entering a very different adult profession. Ougi becomes someone with whom he can simply watch a movie. Sodachi turns continued contact into an ambivalent achievement rather than a clean reconciliation. Adult Araragi notices that a bond he calls `一心同体` can still be structurally unfair. Nadeko learns that a past self can be a mirror instead of an enemy. Shinobu discovers that the future can surprise her by turning an enemy into a friend.

Across those states, the collection repeatedly rejects one tempting error:

> **because an earlier state was real, it must remain sovereign.**

The opposite error is rejected too:

> **because a later state supersedes it, the earlier state must have been fake.**

The better model is historical continuity under revision.

That is why the dual-horizon method is not bureaucratic metadata. It is analytically homologous to the collection's own treatment of people. A story has both a release history and an internal position. A person has both a history and a current state. Neither coordinate can be collapsed into the other without losing something important.

The strongest project-level conclusion is therefore:

> **`短物語` treats identity as an archive rather than an essence: earlier selves, relationships, beliefs, and wounds remain retrievable and causally real, but retrieval does not grant them permanent authority over the present or future.**

That formulation strongly complements the Off Season principle of binding without sovereignty, Monster Season's rejection of causal jurisdiction, and Family Season's model of negotiated shared standing—without pretending those later syntheses were fully present in the earliest shorts.
