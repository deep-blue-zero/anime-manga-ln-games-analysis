# <MONOGATARI> V2 — Volume 26 Deep Reading
## 『余物語』
### 第四話「よつぎバディ」 / 第五話「よつぎシャドウ」

> **Publication-local core:** 『余物語』 is a volume about **surplus consequence**: children, creations, relationships, and desires do not remain inside the role or intention that produced them. 「よつぎバディ」 begins by refusing to reduce Araragi and Ononoki to one relational noun—`僕と彼女との関係性は、『僕達』でいい`—and then expands that anti-reductive principle into a case of catastrophic parenthood. 家住羽衣's parents loved their daughter intensely but did not respect her as a person: `愛してはくれた。でも、敬意は払ってくれなかった`. Their attempt to preserve an eternal baby produced imprisonment, developmental arrest, possible oddityization, violence, and a woman who later recreates family as simulation and repeats the structure she escaped. Rescue can keep her alive, but Araragi refuses to call that "making it in time." The case then reflects back onto Ononoki herself. She creates a teddy-bear familiar without authorization, and the act immediately produces obligations that neither professional contract nor recoverability can erase. 「よつぎシャドウ」 takes the same question into Nadeko's new specialist apprenticeship. Nadeko's private desire for affordable housing is not purified away; instead she learns to distinguish ordinary desire from the harmful machinery used to realize it. A would-be tenant wants lower rent and outsources lethal snake curses to make a room stigmatized. **The volume's mature ethical rule is not "do not create" and not "do not desire." It is: origin creates responsibility without sovereignty; love requires respect; desire requires a means audit; and whatever one creates—child, work, familiar, relationship, or intervention—may become more than the role assigned to it.**
>
> **Retrospective V2 core:** V26 consolidates several lines that have been developing since Final and Off Season. V21's `origin is causal without sovereignty`, V22's `representation can have causal force`, V23's `binding without imprisonment`, V24's `precedent is not protocol`, and V25's `growth requires time and authorship` converge here into a theory of **responsibility after emergence**. Iezumi demonstrates the horror of treating a person as the product one intended to make. Ononoki demonstrates that even a created being can become a creator, and that creation cannot be ethically reduced to technique. Nadeko demonstrates that art can become epistemic practice without ceasing to be art, while her housing motive shows that helping need not be selfless to be legitimate. The hanging-room case adds material political economy: supernatural harm can be commissioned for ordinary financial advantage. Monster Season therefore moves specialist ethics away from heroic purity and toward governance: ask what was created, by whom, under what conditions, who bears risk, whose desire is being served, whether the mechanism is actually supernatural, and what obligations survive after the creator's plan has failed or been exceeded.

## 1.3 Narrator architecture

Unlike V25, which temporarily yielded a major block of narration to Kujaku inside an Araragi story, V26 keeps each of its two story units formally stable:

- **Araragi narrates all of 「よつぎバディ」.**
- **Nadeko narrates all of 「よつぎシャドウ」.**

That stability is not neutral. The paired narrators let V26 compare two people at different stages of learning not to make rescue into self-definition.

Araragi is still the university freshman who can be drawn into another person's emergency with alarming speed. Yet his narration now repeatedly audits jurisdiction. He questions whether he is entitled to interpret abuse, whether a saved life counts as a solved life, whether a morally forceful anti-suicide statement can itself be cruel, whether a parent-child reunion would constitute repair, and whether creating a supernatural helper is a legitimate means simply because it works.

Nadeko, meanwhile, is attempting to build an adult future while still a middle-school student. Her narration is unusually sensitive to self-interest. She repeatedly worries that wanting housing makes her impure. The story does not reward that shame. Ononoki and Gaen instead force her to learn a more difficult professional distinction: **private motive is not the same thing as corrupt method**.

The narratorial pairing therefore tests two complementary failures:

- Araragi's temptation to over-own responsibility;
- Nadeko's temptation to believe responsibility requires purification of self-interest.

## 1.4 Title and afterword caution

The title `余物語` invites interpretation through `余`—remainder, excess, surplus, what is left over—but the prose does not provide one single authoritative title gloss. Any reading of the title as "surplus consequence" or "what exceeds the maker" is therefore **IT**, not TF.

The afterword is unusually relevant because it explicitly distinguishes creator and creation. Nishio writes, in summary, that creator and work are not identical; a work is part of its creator, yet that part can exceed the whole, and deviation from an initial plan is not automatically artistic failure. He closes the reflection with `親はなくても子は育つ`.

That paratext strongly reinforces the volume's fiction-level interest in created beings and creator responsibility. It does **not** establish literal diegetic metaphysics. The afterword may strengthen the reading that V26 is interested in creations exceeding plans; it cannot by itself prove how Ononoki, Nadeko's drawings, or Iezumi's fabric phenomena work.

# 2. Macro thesis — respect is what prevents love from becoming ownership

The most devastating sentence in V26 is not a supernatural rule. It is Iezumi's description of her parents:

> `愛してはくれた。でも、敬意は払ってくれなかった。`

The volume does not deny that they loved her. It makes that fact part of the horror.

Their daughter is cherished as:

- something cute;
- something to dress;
- something to keep small;
- something to feed only enough to preserve a chosen state;
- something whose body can be medically managed;
- something whose intellectual and physical development can be split according to parental preference;
- something hidden from the world so the fantasy does not have to confront outside norms.

In ordinary sentimental discourse, "love" can function as moral exoneration. V26 refuses that move. Love without respect can become a form of objectification more intimate than hatred because it supplies endless reasons to continue the violation.

The distinction is crucial:

- **love** answers how strongly I value you;
- **respect** answers whether I recognize you as a subject whose growth, refusal, limits, interests, and future are not mine to determine.

Iezumi's parents fail the second test. Their affection becomes an argument against her personhood.

This extends the series' longstanding anti-possession logic. Araragi's rescue pathology, Senjougahara's relationship autonomy, Hanekawa's loss of permanent epistemic availability, Hachikuji's present ownership of godhood, Nadeko's self-authorship, and Tsuzura's `能力があるからって...それにならなきゃいけないとは限らない` all reject the same hidden premise: that caring, knowing, creating, rescuing, or causing someone gives jurisdiction over what she must become.

V26 makes the point brutally domestic:

> **A person can be genuinely loved and still be denied personhood by the form that love takes.**

# 3. Macro thesis — origin creates responsibility, not ownership

The second major line joins Iezumi to Ononoki and Nadeko.

V21 already established that origin does not create permanent sovereignty: Suicide-Master can be a formative maker, teacher, sire, and namer without owning Kiss-shot's later identity. V22 showed Nadeko drawing prior versions of herself that act beyond simple historical reproduction. V25 showed her drawn snakes accepted as a valid ritual solution, while leaving their exact ontology open.

V26 generalizes that architecture.

A creator may be responsible for what is made without retaining complete control over what it becomes.

This appears at several scales:

- Iezumi's parents try to make an eternal baby. The person survives the design and becomes a woman whose life cannot be reduced to the object they intended.
- Iezumi tries to simulate husband and daughter. The simulation reproduces the trauma she knows rather than generating the family she hoped to possess.
- The daughter doll escapes containment as animated blanket/fabric residue rather than remaining a passive prop.
- Ononoki creates a teddy familiar as an investigative device. The act is useful and professionally transgressive at once, and she immediately bears responsibility for control, disposal, and consequences.
- Nadeko draws environments and bodies as a way of knowing. Her representation is increasingly capable of affecting the domain it represents.
- The hanging-room client purchases a curse as a means to an economic end. Outsourcing execution does not outsource moral authorship.
- The afterword explicitly says that a created work may exceed the creator's plan.

The governing V2 principle after V26 is therefore:

> **Origin is causally important, but origin is not sovereignty. Responsibility may persist after control ends.**

That principle is equally useful for parenthood, art, supernatural manufacture, institutional design, and relationships.

# 4. Narrative architecture

## Movement I — a relationship that cannot be captured by one noun (`よつぎバディ` §001)

V26 opens by making classification itself the problem. Araragi tries friend, enemy, watcher, watched, aggressor, victim, third party, understander, partner, housemate, interested party, witness, lawyer, executioner. Each label is partly true and therefore misleading when made total.

He finally concludes:

> `僕と彼女との関係性は、『僕達』でいい。`

The line is not anti-language. Names are useful for recognition and coordination. The point is that a long relationship can exceed the administrative convenience of a single category.

The closing phrase—until a long acquaintance becomes a long goodbye—also anticipates the story's concrete consequence. Ononoki's household assignment will end. The relationship will not.

## Movement II — the alleged abuser asks to be helped (§§002–005)

Iezumi Hagoromo summons a nineteen-year-old student and says she is abusing—or fears she will abuse—her three-year-old daughter.

Araragi's immediate discomfort matters. Much of his adolescence placed him on the child/victim side of stories involving parental failure. Oikura's history, Hachikuji's family rupture, Hanekawa's household, Nadeko's neglectful relational environment, and others have made abusive adults narratively available to him largely as causes of someone else's wound.

Iezumi forces a different encounter:

> What does it mean to face a person who may be doing harm without converting that person either into a monster or into a victim whose pain excuses the harm?

Araragi also refuses the deterministic abuse-cycle simplification. Being abused may be causally relevant to later parenting, but "abused child becomes abusive parent" is neither destiny nor exoneration.

## Movement III — the apartment stages a family that does not exist (§§006–010)

The apartment produces a sequence of nested boundaries:

- building;
- locked apartment;
- locked child room;
- heavy cage.

Inside the cage is not a child but a handmade cloth doll shaped as one, stabbed in the back with a fruit knife.

The room itself is developmentally frozen: infant furnishing remains around a purported three-year-old. Araragi calls it `愛情の残骸`—the remains of affection.

The phrase is exact. The problem is not absence of past care. It is care preserved after it has ceased to recognize change.

## Movement IV — the investigators begin overfitting (§§011–020)

Araragi and Ononoki initially map the strange daughter onto known categories: changeling, Tsukihi-like replacement, murdered child, fabricated child, family conspiracy.

V24's warning returns: precedent is not protocol.

Familiar categories help generate hypotheses, but the novel repeatedly punishes the desire to make the new case resemble an old case quickly enough to feel understood.

Ougi's later contribution similarly expands possibility without becoming omniscient answer. The presence of two apparently worked dolls can imply multiple makers or actors, but inference is not fact.

## Movement V — fabric turns simulation into active residue (§§021–028)

When Araragi returns, the cage is broken and the child doll gone. Cloth and discarded garments become active threats. The "daughter" seems to have escaped not by becoming human but by becoming mobile material.

Water settles the attackers.

Ononoki reads the phenomena professionally but does not sentimentalize them. Being a created doll-like being does not make every doll her kin or every artificial life form psychologically equivalent to her.

That refusal matters. Shared ontology does not equal shared personhood—one of V21's key locks carried into a new domain.

## Movement VI — Ononoki becomes creator (§§026–033)

A discarded teddy-bear keychain becomes the route toward Iezumi's location. Ononoki proposes making it into a familiar by embedding her own eye.

Araragi recoils. His history of unwanted vampirization makes creation-by-transformation ethically charged for him. A technically possible act is not automatically neutral because it produces useful information.

The improvised paid contract between Araragi and Ononoki is important but insufficient. Payment establishes that Ononoki is not simply being ordered as household property. It does **not** settle the status of the being they create.

Ononoki acknowledges responsibility for containment and destruction if the familiar becomes dangerous.

V26 therefore asks a question V22-V25 had been approaching from art:

> **What does a creator owe a creation that exists because it was useful to make?**

## Movement VII — a life is saved, but Araragi refuses the rescue fantasy (§034)

The familiar leads them to the university rooftop. Iezumi has been lying there through severe deprivation, apparently attempting to die slowly.

Araragi finds her alive.

He says:

> `僕は間に合ったとは言えない。`

This is one of Monster Season's cleanest corrections to adolescent rescue grammar.

The physical rescue matters enormously. It does not become restoration.

## Movement VIII — the confession explains without purifying (§§029–035)

Iezumi's multilingual letter reveals an extreme childhood of loving objectification, bodily confinement, developmental arrest, and violence. It also reveals later acts of falsification, identity manufacture, fraudulent marriage registration, and a simulated family.

Her victimhood helps explain these acts.

It does not make every later act harmless or legitimate.

The story refuses both simplifications:

- "she suffered, therefore she is innocent of everything";
- "she did harmful/illegal things, therefore the childhood no longer matters."

## Movement IX — Hanekawa converts a solved rescue into an unsolved person (§035)

About another week later, Hanekawa's debrief opens the case rather than closing it.

She questions literal details of the confession, offers alternate reconstructions of the father/mother violence, interprets fabric behavior through Iezumi's deprivation history, and advances the possibility that Iezumi survived because obsessive parental treatment partially oddityized her.

That last hypothesis is powerful and provisional.

The story does not grant the investigators omniscience simply because the case is over.

## Movement X — Ononoki loses the assignment, not the relationship (§035)

Gaen reacts severely to Ononoki's unauthorized creation. Her right eye is confiscated temporarily; she is ordered away from the Araragi household and forbidden proximity to Araragi/Shinobu.

The institutional assignment ends.

The opening thesis survives the test: no one noun exhausts what Ononoki and Araragi are to each other. "Household monitor" can end while `僕達` persists.

## Movement XI — Nadeko faces the material problem of leaving home (`よつぎシャドウ` §§001–003)

The second story shifts from extreme childhood imprisonment to a more ordinary but still coercive housing problem. Nadeko's parents tell their fifteen-year-old daughter that if she does not attend high school, she must work and leave home after middle-school graduation.

The story does not need to equate this with Iezumi's childhood to make the pairing meaningful. Both concern adults converting developmental responsibility into a condition the child must satisfy.

Nadeko's problem is concrete: work, rent, guarantor, residence.

Ononoki offers connection rather than total rescue.

## Movement XII — Gaen turns a housing need into a trial apprenticeship (§§003–004)

Gaen offers a deal: solve a suspicious apartment case, and she will use her real-estate network to help Nadeko obtain affordable housing.

Nadeko recognizes the arrangement as a test.

The key ethical distinction is that the test does not require Nadeko to pretend she lacks self-interest. Her housing need is openly part of the exchange.

## Movement XIII — drawing becomes investigative method (§§005–007)

Nadeko begins by sketching the room.

This matters because her art has now accumulated at least four functions across the V2 corpus:

1. expression;
2. self-representation;
3. potentially generative supernatural practice;
4. epistemic instrument.

She uses the act of drawing to notice spatial relations and test hypotheses.

Ononoki's concern that a sufficiently powerful representation of hanging might itself become dangerous shows why artistic capability now belongs in the specialist-risk ledger.

## Movement XIV — supernatural causation turns out to have an economic client (§§007–008)

Three successive residents have attempted hanging. Their neck marks resemble snake scales. The recurrence looks like a haunted room.

But the distribution is wrong for a room that simply wants residents dead. Only one person in each household is targeted, and discovery by family/partners repeatedly prevents death.

Nadeko and Ononoki invert the question:

> Who benefits if the room finally becomes stigmatized as an accident property?

The answer is not mystical destiny. It is rent.

A prospective tenant who cannot afford the unit has reason to want its price depressed and has outsourced a real lethal curse to a professional.

## Movement XV — desire is separated from its machinery (§008)

Gaen's formulation is the ethical endpoint:

> `欲は誰でも持つ、そのこと自体は責められない。けれど、その欲を叶える蛇は、責めなければならない`

Wanting affordable housing is ordinary.

Trying to obtain it by subjecting strangers to lethal curse mechanisms is not.

This is exactly the distinction Nadeko herself needs because she wants housing too.

## Movement XVI — V27 is opened but not imported (§008)

Gaen says the case is not finished and asks Nadeko to meet the old friend connected to her original curse history.

That is the current boundary.

V26 does **not** establish the future meeting's content, motives, truth status, or outcome. V27 remains quarantined.

# 6. Araragi and Ononoki: `僕達` as anti-reductive relationship ethics

The opening inventory of labels is easy to read as comic throat-clearing, but it is one of the volume's most important formal statements.

Araragi can truthfully say that Ononoki has been, at different moments:

- an enemy;
- an executioner;
- a monitor;
- a housemate;
- a partner;
- a witness;
- a victim of his decisions;
- someone who has injured him;
- someone who has saved him;
- someone employed by Gaen/Kagenui;
- someone embedded in his family household;
- someone whose own friendships now exceed him.

To choose one of those relations as the "real" one would repeat precisely the error V26 condemns elsewhere: treat a subject as the function she performs for the observer.

`僕達` is therefore not vagueness. It is a refusal of false exhaustiveness.

That matters because Ononoki's character has been repeatedly threatened by function-language. She is:

- a corpse doll;
- a shikigami;
- a specialist tool;
- an assigned monitor;
- a constructed being.

Every term names something real. None settles the person.

V21 already established that being constructed does not make a later biography less real. V26 now applies the same rule relationally:

> **A relationship can be genuine without possessing one final ontological label.**

This also protects the ending of the story from being misread. When Gaen terminates Ononoki's Araragi-household assignment, the institutional relation changes. The personal history does not vanish with the job description.

The line thus anticipates V23's adult model of durable relationship: continued connection need not mean unbroken role continuity.

### Claim transition

**STRENGTHEN — V23 `binding without sovereignty`.** V26 shows that even naming can become a miniature sovereignty claim when one category is used to exhaust a relationship's meaning.

# 7. Facing an abusive parent as a person without converting harm into excuse

## 7.1 Iezumi breaks Araragi's familiar moral geometry

When Iezumi asks for help because she fears abusing her daughter, Araragi initially experiences almost a category error. He has encountered parental abuse before, but usually through the person harmed by it.

That difference matters.

It is easier to retain moral clarity when the abusive adult exists mainly as absent cause. Iezumi is present, articulate, distressed, professionally accomplished, polite, and actively asking to be stopped before or while harm occurs.

The volume does not use those traits to soften the category of abuse. Instead, it tests whether Araragi can hold two facts at once:

1. **the child must be protected**;
2. **the adult doing or fearing harm is still a person rather than an explanatory monster.**

This is not moral equivalence. It is a more difficult distribution of personhood.

V26 is particularly careful about the common abuse-cycle proposition. Iezumi's childhood later supplies overwhelming causal material for why motherhood, child growth, cages, dolls, bodily control, and love itself become psychologically distorted for her. But Araragi resists saying that abused children necessarily become abusive parents.

That distinction protects both survivors and victims:

- survivors are not assigned a destiny of reenactment;
- later harmful conduct is not excused as inevitable.

### V2 lock

> **Trauma may explain the architecture through which harm becomes thinkable. Explanation does not convert the later act into fate or innocence.**

## 7.2 Respectable competence does not establish domestic legitimacy

Araragi notes that Iezumi looks like a `ちゃんとした人`.

She is:

- an associate professor;
- multilingual;
- formally polite;
- professionally embedded;
- apparently functional.

The novel immediately undercuts the inference that social competence predicts parental safety.

This extends V23's institutional skepticism. Public role and professional legitimacy are real goods, but they are domain-bounded. A person can be competent in one domain and dangerous in another.

The same lesson applies to Iezumi's parents. The father is a pediatrician. The mother works in children's clothing. Both possess socially legible child-centered expertise. Their professions do not protect their daughter from being turned into an object.

Indeed, their expertise can be incorporated into the mechanism of control.

### V2 lock

> **Expertise about children is not equivalent to respect for a child. Professional competence does not create relational jurisdiction.**

# 8. `愛してはくれた。でも、敬意は払ってくれなかった` — love without respect

Iezumi's confession is one of the series' clearest statements about the moral insufficiency of love.

Her parents' project is not described primarily as hatred. Their affection is intense.

They want:

- the cute baby;
- the dependent baby;
- the dressable baby;
- the controllable baby;
- the permanently available baby.

What they do not want is the subject who grows beyond the image.

That makes the cage more than a physical prison. It is an attempt to freeze relational ontology.

> You are the baby we love. Therefore you must remain what our love requires.

V26's answer is devastating:

> **Love that requires the beloved not to develop can become a technology of imprisonment.**

The distinction between `愛` and `敬意` is therefore not decorative. Respect requires the parent to encounter the child as someone whose future cannot be completely authored in advance.

The parents' horror is precisely that they are creators who cannot tolerate creation exceeding plan.

This connects directly to the afterword's creator/work reflection. A work may diverge from the plot. A child must.

The analogy must not be flattened—children are not artworks—but the volume intentionally places these problems beside one another. Parent, creator, specialist, and curse-client all confront the same temptation: the thing I caused should remain answerable to my intention.

V26 refuses that premise.

# 9. Iezumi as survivor, later agent, and unreliable autobiographer

## 9.1 The letter is evidence, not omniscience

Iezumi's multilingual confession is the densest source of her childhood history, but the method requires careful classification.

The letter supports **NR/CD about what Iezumi believes, remembers, or chooses to confess**. It does not automatically convert every causal reconstruction into TF.

The largest unresolved point concerns the father's death and mother's disappearance.

Iezumi's letter presents one sequence:

- she persuades her father to stab her;
- he eventually does;
- her mother discovers the scene;
- the father dies;
- the mother flees.

Hanekawa and Araragi later consider a darker reconstruction: Iezumi may have killed the father herself after gaining access to the knife, while her mother may have fled from the daughter or taken blame to shield her.

That reconstruction is plausible **SI/IT/UA**. It is not established TF.

This is important because V26 is about autobiographical simulation. Iezumi may be narrating herself through a version of events that preserves a tolerable role distribution.

The project must therefore avoid saying either:

- "Iezumi definitely murdered her father";
- or "the letter's version is definitely complete."

## 9.2 Victimhood and later agency coexist

Iezumi survives extraordinary abuse.

She later:

- falsifies identity/history;
- enters a fraudulent legal marriage arrangement for residence/status;
- fabricates parts of an adult social biography;
- constructs a simulated household;
- recreates a child figure;
- cages that figure after becoming unable to love it;
- attempts suicide rather than directly surrendering herself.

Those acts occur within constrained conditions, but they remain acts.

V26 refuses the idea that recognizing agency dishonors victimhood. The opposite is closer to the series' mature ethics: denying later agency would again turn Iezumi into an object of explanatory pity.

The correct formulation is relationally specific:

> **Iezumi can be radically victimized by her parents and still become a morally responsible agent in later relationships.**

This continues V22's rule that victim and agent are not mutually exclusive global identities.

---

# 10. Simulation and traumatic recursion

Iezumi's apartment is a simulated family.

The husband figure is emotionally thin because the legal husband himself was largely instrumental in Iezumi's manufactured social existence. The daughter figure matters more. Iezumi attempts to create a family relation in which she can finally occupy the adult/parent side.

But she only knows parenthood through the structure that formed her.

The simulation therefore begins repeating:

- infant-room preservation;
- objectification;
- non-growth;
- cage;
- discarded affection;
- violent residue.

The novel's point is not that victims inevitably repeat abuse. It is that **simulation constructed only from known reality can reproduce the very reality one wants to escape**.

Hanekawa's formulation—`現実から学ぶ限り、現実しか生まれない`—captures the problem.

She then argues for `理想`: some image of better-than-given relation is required if the person is to produce more than repetition.

That proposition should remain dialogic, not universal dogma. Ideals can also become coercive. The same series has repeatedly shown disastrous idealization.

But in this case the point is narrower:

> **A person cannot build a different relational future if the only legitimate model is the harmful relation already experienced.**

This gives `理想` a practical rather than utopian function: create representational space for something not yet known.

That is especially important beside Nadeko's art. Drawing is literally the practice of making a form that does not have to be a photographic copy of existing reality.

---

# 11. `愛情の残骸` — material residue as evidence of changing relation

The phrase `愛情の残骸` recurs around the baby room and later the teddy keychain.

Its force lies in temporality.

Residue proves that affection existed.

Residue also proves that the relation changed.

The baby room is not simply "loveless." It contains obsolete furniture and decoration from a period of investment. The horror is that the environment remains fixed after the subject supposedly grows.

The teddy keychain similarly appears to be a discarded access object tied to the child room. It becomes useful precisely because it is a leftover from an earlier relational state.

V26 therefore adds a material corollary to the series' memory ethics:

> **Objects can preserve the trace of a relation without proving the relation remains what it was.**

This is the same reason old love, old role, old wound, or old self cannot be treated as present command merely because evidence of the past survives.

---

# 12. The fabric phenomena: survival mechanism, familiar system, and epistemic uncertainty

The animated fabrics are among V26's most important places to resist premature certainty.

What is strongly supported:

- cloth and garments attack Araragi/Ononoki;
- items associated with the apartment and with recently discarded clothing can become active;
- water causes the fabric entities to settle;
- the behavior appears connected to the same underlying source rather than requiring a wholly independent oddity for every garment;
- Iezumi's history prominently involves extreme restriction of food/water and clothing control.

What remains inferential:

- whether Iezumi herself is the direct conscious "controller";
- whether the fabrics are familiars in a strict technical sense;
- whether water is literally a weakness versus metaphorical/functional satiation;
- how much of the behavior encodes Iezumi's trauma history;
- whether the escaped daughter-doll possesses durable independent subjectivity.

Hanekawa's "water as feeding" interpretation is a strong SI because it explains why material associated with an underfed/dehydrated survivor becomes quiet after receiving water.

It should not be promoted to mechanistic TF without later confirmation.

### V2 methodological value

V26 repeatedly presents explanations that are **useful enough to guide intervention without becoming metaphysical certainty**.

That is mature specialist reasoning.

---

# 13. Ononoki creates a familiar: professional consent does not solve creation ethics

## 13.1 Araragi's reaction is historically grounded

When Ononoki proposes creating a teddy familiar, Araragi reacts more strongly than a purely technical observer would.

He has lived the condition of being transformed into a supernatural category without anything resembling ordinary informed consent.

That history gives him a legitimate sensitivity:

> **being made into something useful to someone else can itself be a violation.**

This does not mean every created supernatural entity is equivalent to his vampirization. It means the experience appropriately raises the burden of justification.

## 13.2 The paid contract matters—and does not absolve

Araragi improvises payment to Ononoki for the work.

This is ethically better than treating her as a household instrument who must obey because Gaen once placed her there.

The payment marks:

- separate agency;
- professional choice;
- exchange;
- responsibility.

But it cannot answer the status of the teddy familiar.

A contract between creator and client cannot confer consent on the being brought into existence.

This is one of V26's sharpest extensions of V21/V25:

> **Consent among the agents who perform an intervention does not automatically settle the ethics of the subject produced by it.**

## 13.3 Creator responsibility begins immediately

Ononoki says that if the familiar becomes dangerous, she will be responsible for stopping or destroying it.

The line is severe but important. Creation produces a continuing duty.

The duty is not proof of ownership.

Indeed, V26's afterword points the other direction: creation can exceed the maker.

Thus:

> **The more a creation becomes autonomous, the less "I made it" can function as a sovereignty claim—and the more the maker may need to answer for risks created by the act of making.**

This is a useful foundation for later Nadeko-art analysis.

---

# 14. Ononoki's body and recoverability: cost remains cost

Ononoki removes her own right eye to animate the teddy.

The series' supernatural bodies repeatedly create a dangerous ethical illusion: if damage can be repaired, perhaps bodily expenditure does not matter.

V23 already rejected this with Nozomi.

V26 strengthens it.

Ononoki's constructed corpse body is highly recoverable. That does not make self-dismemberment morally or professionally free.

The act has:

- bodily meaning;
- disciplinary consequence;
- operational risk;
- symbolic cost;
- institutional repercussions.

Her later eyepatch is material residue of the choice and of Gaen's response.

### Claim transition

**STRENGTHEN — V23 recoverable body still counts as worker body.**

---

# 15. The rooftop rescue: survival is not restoration

Araragi finds Iezumi before her body irreversibly fails.

A conventional rescue story would make that temporal success the climax.

Araragi refuses:

> `僕は間に合ったとは言えない。`

The distinction is central.

He can say:

- she is alive;
- the attempt has been interrupted;
- medical care is possible.

He cannot honestly say:

- the childhood has been repaired;
- the criminal/legal situation is solved;
- the simulated family has been made healthy;
- suicidal desire has been resolved;
- the person's self-concept is restored;
- the supernatural mechanism is understood.

This is the Monster Season correction to the old `間に合う` fantasy.

> **Preventing the final irreversible outcome does not mean arriving before the damage.**

V23's `personal resolution ≠ material disposal` becomes here:

> **biological survival ≠ existential restoration.**

---

# 16. Suicide, intervention, and Araragi's self-critique

At the rooftop, Araragi tells Iezumi that the mistake was trying to die.

That statement is emotionally understandable and causally consequential. It may even be what he can say in the moment.

V26 does not leave it unexamined.

Later he asks whether telling a person who has endured extreme suffering that suicide is sinful/wrong might itself be cruel.

This self-critique prevents the project from converting the rooftop line into universal doctrine.

The novel does **not** endorse suicide. It also does not treat moral condemnation as sufficient care.

The more precise V26 position is:

> **Intervening to preserve life can be justified without claiming complete moral jurisdiction over how the suffering person should evaluate her own pain.**

Araragi's growth lies partly in being able to act decisively and then question the moral adequacy of his own language.

That is not weakness. It is `R` in the broad ethical sense of reflective self-audit.

### Anti-backprojection lock

Do not use V26 to claim that Araragi has arrived at one final theory of suicide intervention. He remains conflicted.

---

# 17. Iezumi's possible oddityization: causal benefit does not redeem abuse

Hanekawa doubts that a biologically ordinary human infant could survive in the described condition for roughly twenty years.

She proposes that Iezumi may have been partially oddityized by the obsessive parental treatment itself.

The hypothesis explains several otherwise difficult facts:

- survival under developmentally impossible confinement;
- arrested bodily state;
- fabric animation;
- inability to die straightforwardly even when attempting self-destruction.

The textual status remains **SI**.

The ethical status is clearer.

Even if the abuse incidentally created the mechanism that allowed survival, the consequence does not redeem the abuse.

This is a particularly strong application of V24's intent/outcome distinction:

> **A harmful practice can produce a later benefit without becoming retrospectively justified.**

Indeed, the same cause can:

- preserve life;
- deform life;
- create capacity;
- create suffering.

That ambiguity is not moral balance. It is causal complexity.

---

# 18. Ignorance as limited protection

Araragi calls Iezumi's lack of awareness of her possible oddity status `唯一の救い`.

The phrase is dangerous if universalized.

The series ordinarily values truth, self-knowledge, and accurate causal understanding. V26 presents a case in which disclosure may itself destroy a fragile self-concept without producing useful agency.

The strongest narrow reading is:

> **Information has welfare consequences. Full disclosure is not automatically therapeutic merely because the information is true.**

That does not license arbitrary paternalistic secrecy. The burden is precisely why Gaen is needed: a domain specialist may be able to contain the hazardous mechanism while minimizing unnecessary identity damage.

The question should remain OPEN at principle level:

- Who has standing to decide that ignorance is protective?
- How long can that judgment remain legitimate?
- What if the hidden mechanism later affects others?

V26 presents a local intervention problem, not a universal secrecy doctrine.

---

# 19. Family reconciliation is not an automatic good

Hanekawa floats an image in which the escaped daughter-like blanket might return to Iezumi's bedside.

Araragi resists the sentimentality.

His formulation is one of V26's clearest anti-essentialist family claims:

> `『子供を愛さない親なんていない』が愛のない言葉なら、『子供にとっては親がすべて』も、愛のない言葉`

The first cliché erases abusive or absent parental feeling.

The second erases the child's right to develop a world beyond the parent.

The series' mature family ethic therefore refuses blood/role absolutism.

A child may:

- love a parent;
- fear a parent;
- need a parent;
- leave a parent;
- build attachments elsewhere;
- refuse reunion.

None of those relations is invalid merely because parenthood exists.

### Claim transition

**STRENGTHEN — V23 binding without imprisonment.** Kinship is another binding relation that does not grant permanent jurisdiction.

---

# 20. Hanekawa as debrief partner: expertise without restored epistemic sovereignty

V23 had already dismantled the idea that Araragi possesses permanent interpretive access to adult Hanekawa.

V26, which is internally earlier, must not be rewritten by that future fact. But publication-locally, V23 is already known to the reader. The comparison is therefore useful as retrospective V2 evidence.

In V26, Hanekawa remains extraordinarily useful.

She:

- returns from international mine-removal work;
- evaluates the letter and scene;
- separates what is known from what may have been self-deception;
- interprets material clues;
- offers the oddityization hypothesis;
- helps Araragi think through the limits of his rescue.

Yet she explicitly resists the nostalgic fantasy that "old Hanekawa" would simply have solved everything perfectly.

This is consistent with the V23 anti-jurisdiction lock.

Expertise is real.

The expert is not omniscient.

---

# 21. Gaen's response to Ononoki: governance after unauthorized creation

Gaen becomes unusually severe after learning that Ononoki created a familiar without authorization.

What is fact:

- the creation violated specialist rules/expectations;
- Gaen responds strongly;
- Ononoki loses temporary access to her right eye;
- the Araragi-household assignment ends;
- proximity restrictions are imposed.

Araragi suspects that Gaen's visible anger may partly function to protect Ononoki from harsher institutional handling, especially from Kagenui or the logic by which a dangerous constructed tool might otherwise be disposed of.

That is **SI**, not confirmed motive.

The larger institutional point is firmer:

> **Useful success does not retroactively authorize an unreviewed high-risk intervention.**

The teddy worked.

That fact does not make the governance problem disappear.

This strongly parallels V24's correct-answer/defective-proof distinction.

---

# 22. Ending the household assignment does not erase Ononoki's social world

The story's consequence matters beyond punishment.

Ononoki is removed from Araragi's household.

That shift reveals how much independent social life she has accumulated.

Most importantly, `よつぎシャドウ` opens with her spending increased time with Nadeko.

Nadeko is not merely "Araragi's old acquaintance" through whom Ononoki remains attached to him. The friendship is its own relation.

This is a major character lock:

> **Ononoki's relationships are not extensions of her assignment.**

The same applies to her personhood generally. A tool can acquire a social world that the original task did not predict.

That is the afterword's creator/work proposition rendered as character development.

---

# 23. Nadeko's housing problem: ordinary material need belongs inside character analysis

`よつぎシャドウ` begins with housing rather than metaphysics.

That choice is analytically important.

Nadeko intends not to attend high school. Her parents respond with a new condition: if she chooses that route, she must work and leave home after middle-school graduation.

The story does not present this as a clean emancipation narrative.

At fifteen, independent housing requires:

- money;
- employment;
- guarantor arrangements;
- legal-adult cooperation;
- safety;
- access to ordinary infrastructure.

Nadeko's dream of becoming a manga artist therefore cannot be separated from rent.

This is one of Monster Season's increasingly strong materialist corrections to earlier supernatural drama. Desire is embedded in:

- housing;
- labor;
- institutions;
- contracts;
- transportation;
- property value.

V23 brought oddity work into public institutions. V26 brings Nadeko's self-authorship into the rental market.

The result is valuable because it prevents "follow your dream" from becoming a purely psychological slogan.

> **Authorship requires material conditions in which the authored life can continue.**

---

# 24. Nadeko and parental conditions: understanding does not require total accommodation

Nadeko can partially understand what her parents are doing. They may hope the difficulty of immediate work/housing will push her back toward high school.

That interpretive generosity is consistent with her post-V22 growth.

But understanding can become another form of over-accommodation.

Ononoki's counterpressure is useful: Nadeko needs some of Tsukihi's capacity for selfish assertion.

This does not mean becoming inconsiderate. It means refusing the belief that maturity consists of absorbing every adult condition without making a counterclaim.

V25 already gave Nadeko `わがままが必要だった`.

V26 makes that principle material:

> **Wanting somewhere to live is not moral impurity.**

---

# 25. Ononoki as mentor: help should reduce impossible barriers, not erase effort

Ononoki offers to connect Nadeko with Gaen's work and housing network.

She also says she will not simply make everything easy for Nadeko.

That proposition needs careful handling because the corpus has already rejected hardship-as-virtue.

The strongest reading is not:

> difficulty is morally good.

It is:

> **assistance should not require the recipient to surrender authorship of the life being built.**

Ononoki can:

- open a door;
- introduce a professional network;
- protect Nadeko in dangerous work;
- explain specialist norms.

Nadeko still has to:

- choose whether to participate;
- reason through the case;
- make decisions;
- live with the result.

This is distributed support rather than abandonment and rather than total rescue.

---

# 26. Gaen's quid pro quo: private motive and public work can coexist

Gaen offers Nadeko an explicit exchange:

> solve the apartment problem, receive help finding affordable post-graduation housing.

The arrangement is not altruistically pure.

That is the point.

Nadeko's private benefit is visible from the beginning. Gaen's professional benefit is visible too. The landlord wants a dangerous property problem solved. Nadeko needs housing. Gaen needs capable junior labor.

The story treats negotiated interest as potentially legitimate rather than morally embarrassing.

This is a major correction to Araragi-style selflessness.

Ononoki later states it bluntly: Nadeko is no longer a god. She does not need to act from pure selfless service.

Araragi's `無私の献身` is admirable in one register and unsustainable as universal professional norm.

### Claim transition

**STRENGTHEN — V23/V25 effort and service need not become moral self-erasure.**

---

# 27. The hanging room: case facts before interpretation

Gaen gives Nadeko a bounded problem.

Three successive, unrelated households occupy the same high-end apartment.

In each household:

- one resident attempts hanging from the chandelier;
- a family member or cohabitant discovers the person;
- death is prevented;
- the household moves out soon afterward.

The victims' necks display scale-like bruising, suggesting `蛇切縄`.

The sequence escalates in tempo:

- the first household remains for years before the event;
- the next for a much shorter period;
- the third for only weeks.

The third victim comes closest to death.

That progression suggests a mechanism becoming more efficient through repetition.

But V26 does not let "three hangings in one room" become instant proof of a haunted room.

The distribution of targets matters more than the sensational surface.

---

# 28. Specialist epistemology: `なんでもかんでも怪異現象として取り扱うのは...無責任`

One of the volume's strongest professional rules comes from Ononoki:

> `なんでもかんでも怪異現象として取り扱うのは危険である以上に、無責任でもある`

This is an important limit on the entire Monogatari method.

The series is about oddities. That does not mean every strange event should be interpreted supernaturally.

A specialist who assumes oddity causation too quickly can:

- miss ordinary violence;
- miss financial motive;
- miss crime;
- misallocate intervention;
- increase fear;
- create new rumor;
- treat people as metaphysical symptoms.

V23's Wind Rumor Section already encoded the same public responsibility: the institution exists to remove fear, not produce it.

V26 supplies the epistemic corollary:

> **Professional specialization includes knowing when one's specialty may not apply.**

That is a mature theory of expertise.

---

# 29. Drawing as epistemic instrument

Nadeko sketches the apartment because drawing is the strongest practiced method she has for attending to space.

This should not be romanticized as magical intuition.

The process is concrete:

- translate room into lines;
- observe proportions;
- locate chandelier and relation to floor/walls;
- externalize case structure;
- compare subjects;
- test what changes when a figure is added.

Drawing becomes a form of reasoning.

This is an important expansion of Nadeko's artist identity. Her art is no longer only:

- dream;
- self-expression;
- route to professional future;
- symbolic reconciliation with past selves.

It is also **method**.

The series thereby avoids a false divide between artistic and analytical intelligence.

Nadeko's craft trains perception.

---

# 30. Representation as risk: ability requires limit testing

Ononoki warns that a sufficiently convincing drawing of hanging might itself become dangerous, especially given what Nadeko's drawings have already demonstrated.

That warning is analytically important even if no catastrophe follows in the scene.

V22 established that drawings can act beyond simple representation.

V25 suggested increasingly generative capacity.

V26 adds professional risk management:

> **The more representation acquires causal capacity, the more artistic skill becomes an operational hazard that requires limits, testing, and protection.**

This does not transform Nadeko into a weapon by definition.

It means capability now carries safety questions.

V23's ability-does-not-create-duty remains binding. Capability may create a need for safe handling without creating a duty to build a career around it.

---

# 31. Ononoki as test subject: recoverability still does not erase risk

Ononoki volunteers as the hanging figure because she is already dead/constructed and unusually recoverable.

Again the series uses supernatural durability to tempt instrumental reasoning.

The correct V2 reading stays consistent with Nozomi and Ononoki's own eye sacrifice:

> **Reduced biological vulnerability changes risk calculation; it does not make a person a costless test object.**

Consent matters here. Ononoki volunteers.

But consent is one variable, not moral nullification of cost.

---

# 32. Nadeko's own Snake-Cutting Rope history: precedent without over-identification

The neck marks naturally invoke Nadeko's past.

She was once bound by `蛇切縄`, and that experience gives her real phenomenological insight into how terrifying constriction can be.

But V26 carefully prevents personal experience from becoming total epistemic authority.

Her own case involved:

- Kaiki's fraudulent charm system;
- shrine accumulation;
- a fake mechanism becoming anomalously real through local conditions;
- personal curse motive.

The apartment case has different distribution and incentive structure.

Nadeko's history is useful evidence.

It is not a template.

This is V24's `precedent is not protocol` translated into trauma-informed expertise.

---

# 33. Victim perspective is not enough: the discoverers matter

Nadeko can imagine the pain of the hanged victims because of her own constriction experience.

She then realizes she has underweighted the people who discovered them.

This is a small but important expansion of causal ethics.

Harm propagates through networks.

A failed suicide/curse attempt affects:

- target;
- finder;
- family;
- partner;
- landlord;
- future tenants;
- emergency systems.

V26 repeatedly rejects single-subject analysis. Iezumi's parents' "love" cannot be evaluated only through parental feeling; the child matters. A curse-client's desire cannot be evaluated only through the client; unknown tenants matter.

> **A responsible case model tracks recipients and bystanders, not merely actor intention.**

---

# 34. The economic inversion: who wants an accident property?

The apartment owner wants to avoid death because a fatality would stigmatize the unit and depress its value.

That obvious incentive initially frames the owner as victim of the curse.

Nadeko and Ononoki invert it:

> Who wants the rent to fall?

This is the story's strongest material insight.

A supernatural mechanism is embedded in an ordinary market incentive.

The would-be renter cannot afford the current unit.

If the room becomes known as an accident/haunted property, the price may drop enough to make it accessible.

This turns curse analysis into political economy.

The motive is not ancient hatred or cosmic fate.

It is housing affordability transformed into predatory strategy.

That does not make the housing desire trivial. Quite the opposite. The motive works because it mirrors Nadeko's own urgent need.

The ethical distinction is therefore sharper:

> **A legitimate material need can motivate an illegitimate mechanism.**

---

# 35. Outsourced harm: delegation does not outsource responsibility

The would-be tenant does not need to personally understand or execute `蛇切縄`.

A professional curse provider supplies the lethal means.

This creates at least two responsibility nodes:

1. **client** — wants a harmful outcome and commissions it;
2. **provider** — operationalizes desire into dangerous mechanism.

The market structure matters because it can distance the client from the body of the victim.

V26 refuses that moral laundering.

> **Outsourcing execution does not convert intentional harm into neutral service procurement.**

The provider's responsibility also cannot erase the client's.

This is a useful institutional parallel to Araragi/Ononoki's teddy contract. In one case, contracting professional labor creates a useful but ethically fraught familiar. In the other, contracting specialist labor creates deliberate third-party danger.

Contracts organize action.

They do not purify it.

---

# 36. Kaiki versus the `洗人` provider: mechanism matters without moral whitewashing

Gaen rejects Kaiki as the likely provider because his Snake-Cutting Rope operation was fraudulent; he would use a fake snake to frighten/manipulate, not release a real lethal curse into an ordinary household.

This distinction is analytically useful.

It should **not** be converted into moral exoneration of Kaiki.

Kaiki's fraud can still:

- exploit fear;
- manipulate vulnerable people;
- produce real downstream harm;
- become real under anomalous circumstances.

V26's narrower point is that different harmful professions have different methods and risk profiles.

The real curse provider is described as the inverse of a specialist who loosens troubles: someone who satisfies desire.

That makes the provider dangerous not because desire itself is evil, but because the service removes friction between want and harmful execution.

---

# 37. `欲は誰でも持つ` — desire is not the offense

Gaen's formulation deserves to become a longitudinal lock:

> `欲は誰でも持つ、そのこと自体は責められない。けれど、その欲を叶える蛇は、責めなければならない`

This is not a blanket celebration of appetite.

It is a distinction among:

- desire;
- entitlement;
- means;
- victimization;
- professional infrastructure.

Nadeko wants affordable housing.

The culprit wants affordable housing.

The morally decisive difference is not the existence of want.

It is what each person is willing to make others bear in order to satisfy it.

This is one of the cleanest anti-purity formulations in Monster Season:

> **Ethics should audit the path by which desire becomes action, not demand desirelessness as proof of goodness.**

---

# 38. Nadeko's `犯人は私`: moral identification without false causal guilt

Nadeko repeatedly says:

> `犯人は私でした。`

Taken literally, the statement is false. She did not commission the curse and did not cause the three hangings.

Her narration immediately clarifies the intended meaning. She recognizes her own capacity for self-centered desire inside the culprit's motive.

This is self-audit, not confession to the crime.

The distinction matters because Nadeko has a history of over-identifying guilt.

A bad reading would turn the line into:

> Nadeko is still responsible for everything bad because she is selfish.

The stronger reading is the opposite:

> **Maturity includes recognizing morally dangerous structures inside oneself without appropriating causal responsibility for another person's acts.**

This is exactly the balance she struggled to reach in V25.

---

# 39. Private motive is compatible with helping

Nadeko worries that she is solving the case because she wants housing.

Ononoki tells her that this is acceptable.

She is not a god.

The statement carries enormous retrospective weight.

Nadeko's prior divine role and Araragi's selfless-helper identity both tempt a model in which legitimate help must be motive-pure.

V26 rejects it.

Professionals can have:

- salaries;
- careers;
- housing needs;
- curiosity;
- pride;
- relationships;
- private goals.

The relevant question is whether those motives distort judgment or cause others to be used improperly—not whether the motives exist.

### V2 lock

> **Self-interest is not the opposite of ethical service. Undisclosed or unchecked self-interest can become a conflict; ordinary private motive need not be eliminated.**

---

# 40. Client desire is not final professional authority

Ononoki also articulates a mature specialist principle: professionals cannot simply treat a client's stated desire as final command.

This matters in both stories.

Iezumi asks for help in a way that ultimately conceals a different route to confession and suicide.

The apartment curse-client asks for a service that would realize an ordinary housing desire by harming strangers.

A professional must therefore distinguish:

- what the client says she wants;
- what she may actually be seeking;
- what intervention is safe;
- what third parties would bear;
- what the professional is willing to do.

This is a direct answer to simplistic customer-service ethics.

> **Service is not obedience. Expertise includes refusal.**

---

# 41. Specialist formation: Nadeko is not required to become Araragi

Gaen is evaluating Nadeko as a possible professional.

Ononoki explicitly contrasts her with Araragi.

Araragi's `ふた心ない無私の献身` is admirable but not a scalable professional standard. Ononoki says Gaen has effectively graded that posture as failing/incomplete, and imagines it becoming more adult by the time Araragi graduates university.

Publication-locally, this is a character/specialist judgment, not omniscient future fact.

Retrospectively, V23 shows a twenty-three-year-old Araragi who:

- delegates;
- works inside public institutions;
- respects labor boundaries more than before;
- negotiates rather than unilateralizes;
- leaves some residue to others.

That later development **STRENGTHENS** the trajectory without turning Ononoki's line into destiny or prophecy.

Nadeko's specialist path must remain her own.

She should not need to reproduce Araragi's sacrificial adolescence in order to qualify as serious.

---

# 42. Housing, work, and autonomy: help must be materially usable

Gaen's promised housing assistance is a useful case of concrete support.

A purely moral speech about Nadeko choosing her life would be insufficient if the life were materially impossible.

The series increasingly understands autonomy as infrastructural.

To choose not to attend high school meaningfully, Nadeko needs:

- legal support;
- income;
- safe housing;
- landlord access;
- guarantor structures;
- professional pathways.

This is the same move V23 made for Araragi/Senjougahara's relationship. Love does not solve geography; route design matters.

Here:

> **choice without infrastructure can become symbolic freedom only.**

Gaen's network can expand Nadeko's option set without determining the final choice.

---

# 43. Creator/work afterword: `創作物は創作者の一部`

The afterword gives V26 its clearest paratextual statement:

- creator and creation are not perfectly identical;
- a creation is part of its creator;
- sometimes that part exceeds the whole;
- deviation from plan does not automatically mean failed work;
- creation may become better or worse than intended;
- `親はなくても子は育つ`.

This resonates with the fiction at multiple levels.

### Iezumi

Her parents attempt to preserve a planned child-state. The person exceeds it.

### Iezumi's simulated daughter

The created family object ceases to remain a passive prop and enters the case as active residue.

### Ononoki

A created shikigami/corpse doll has a social world, preferences, ethics, professional judgment, and creative agency unavailable in a pure-tool model.

### Teddy familiar

An investigative creation immediately generates responsibilities not reducible to its intended function.

### Nadeko's art

Representation increasingly produces knowledge and possible causal effects beyond simple authorial intention.

### The novel itself

The afterword says the original plot was substantially different. The finished work exceeded the plan.

The strongest disciplined interpretation is:

> **V26 treats creation as relation rather than possession.**

The creator matters.

The creator is not the whole explanation.

# 44. `余` as remainder/surplus — interpretive title reading

A useful but explicitly interpretive reading of `余物語` is that V26 studies what remains after an act of creation, love, rescue, or desire exceeds its initial frame.

Remainders include:

- `愛情の残骸` in the baby room;
- Iezumi's adult life after the parental project fails;
- simulated family after family itself was never learned;
- fabric residue that becomes active;
- Ononoki's relationship after assignment ends;
- Nadeko's housing need after godhood ends;
- guilt after direct ritual obligation has been completed;
- curse effects after desire is outsourced;
- creator responsibility after control is lost.

This title reading should remain **IT**.

It is analytically productive because the afterword itself is concerned with creations that outgrow plans, but the text does not declare one official `余` definition.

# 45. Character deepening — Araragi Koyomi

V26's Araragi remains intervention-prone but is measurably different from his earlier adolescent self.

## Strengthened traits

- **Rapid responsiveness:** he still cannot ignore a direct plea involving a child.
- **Self-deprecating narration:** humor continues to regulate anxiety and seriousness.
- **Relational attentiveness:** he notices Ononoki as more than assignment/tool.
- **Moral sensitivity to coercive creation:** his own vampirization history shapes reaction to familiar manufacture.

## Revised traits

- **Jurisdiction awareness:** he more readily asks whether a problem is his to solve.
- **Rescue humility:** `僕は間に合ったとは言えない` refuses victory inflation.
- **Self-critique after intervention:** he revisits the cruelty potentially embedded in his own anti-suicide language.
- **Family anti-essentialism:** he rejects compulsory reconciliation narratives.

## Retrospective V23 bridge

V26 does not yet contain adult police Araragi. But it supplies real developmental prehistory for that later competence:

> consult, investigate, rescue, then admit what was not solved.

The arc is therefore **STRENGTHENED**, not made inevitable.

# 46. Character deepening — Ononoki Yotsugi

V26 is one of the most important Ononoki volumes because it shows her simultaneously as:

- created being;
- professional;
- friend;
- mentor;
- creator;
- rule-breaker;
- person vulnerable to institutional discipline.

That plurality destroys any remaining usefulness of a "tool versus person" binary.

Her most important developments:

1. **She rejects ontology-as-kinship.** Doll-like entities are not automatically "like her" in psychologically meaningful ways.
2. **She exercises professional choice.** The improvised contract matters because she is not merely ordered by household function.
3. **She creates.** This reverses the direction of her own origin story.
4. **She accepts creator responsibility.** She does not treat the familiar as morally neutral equipment.
5. **She bears consequences.** Gaen's discipline proves institutional membership constrains even a highly capable immortal body.
6. **She has independent relationships.** Nadeko is her friend, not merely another node in Araragi's social network.
7. **She mentors rather than dominates.** In `シャドウ`, she protects Nadeko while letting Nadeko perform the reasoning.

### Locked formulation

> **Constructed function and personhood are not rival explanations of Ononoki. V26 adds a third term: a created person can herself become creator and professional author of action.**

# 47. Character deepening — Iezumi Hagoromo

Iezumi should not be flattened into either:

- "abusive mother";
- or "tragic abuse victim."

Both are relevant and incomplete.

Her character architecture includes:

- extreme childhood objectification;
- developmental deprivation;
- intense intelligence/language acquisition;
- uncertain violent family history;
- manufactured legal/social adulthood;
- desire for ordinary family life;
- inability to distinguish simulation from repetition;
- possible oddity-mediated survival;
- shame;
- suicidality;
- indirect desire to confess.

The most important interpretive point is that her later family simulation is not proof that she "really is" her parents.

It is evidence that trauma supplies powerful default models when no alternative has become emotionally available.

Her case is therefore a warning against both destiny and purity:

> **A survivor may reproduce structures she condemns. That repetition neither makes the original abuse deserved nor makes the later harm irrelevant.**

# 48. Character deepening — Hanekawa Tsubasa

V26's Hanekawa is still a high-capacity interpretive partner, but the volume itself refuses the mythology of perfect Hanekawa.

Her role is strongest when she:

- introduces alternative hypotheses;
- separates causal possibility from moral conclusion;
- challenges Araragi's self-congratulatory rescue frame;
- translates material detail into psychological/supernatural possibility.

Her line about current/old Hanekawa potentially making different choices should be treated as present self-description, not objective ranking of selves.

V23's later anti-epistemic-jurisdiction formulation remains compatible: Hanekawa can be brilliant without becoming an oracle.

# 49. Character deepening — Nadeko Sengoku

V26 advances Nadeko from self-authorship toward **professional authorship under material constraint**.

Her significant developments are:

- she is planning work and housing rather than remaining suspended in middle-school aftermath;
- she does not demand that all private motive disappear before acting ethically;
- she uses art as disciplined observation;
- she compares a perpetrator's motive to her own desires without collapsing into false causal guilt;
- she accepts that expertise may require skepticism toward supernatural explanations;
- she distinguishes a legitimate desire from harmful means;
- she is willing to continue a case because she can help, while the deeper career question remains open.

The best compact formulation is:

> **Nadeko is learning not to become desireless, but to become answerable for how desire is translated into form.**

That applies equally to manga, housing, romance, godhood, and specialist work.

# 50. Character deepening — Gaen Izuko

Gaen's V26 role is notably institutional.

In `バディ`, she responds to unauthorized creation by enforcing limits.

In `シャドウ`, she recruits a young candidate through an explicit exchange and bounded case assignment.

This produces a useful contrast:

- **discipline** when a professional exceeds unauthorized risk;
- **opportunity** when a novice has relevant ability and support.

Neither action should be idealized.

Gaen remains capable of manipulation, opacity, and asymmetric information.

But V26 strengthens the view that mature specialist practice requires governance rather than charismatic individual problem-solving alone.

# 52. Specialist-ethics synthesis

V26 provides one of the strongest cumulative statements of what a "specialist" should be after V24-V26.

A specialist should be able to:

1. **separate domain expertise from total person-knowledge**;
2. **distinguish client request from justified intervention**;
3. **rule out ordinary causes before supernaturalizing**;
4. **track third-party risk**;
5. **audit financial/material incentives**;
6. **recognize when precedent does not transfer**;
7. **treat created mechanisms as responsibility-bearing interventions**;
8. **admit uncertainty after successful resolution**;
9. **delegate and govern high-risk capability**;
10. **allow private motive without demanding saintly self-erasure**.

This is a substantial move away from the early-series image of the lone eccentric expert who knows the answer.

Monster Season's professional ideal is plural, bounded, auditable, and institutionally constrained.

# 53. Japanese-language observations

## 53.1 `僕達`

Araragi's choice of `僕達` rather than a role noun is deliberately ordinary. The point is not to coin a new technical relationship category. It is to accept deixis/plurality where classification would overclaim.

## 53.2 `愛` versus `敬意`

Iezumi's paired verbs are analytically exact. `愛する` can coexist with possession. `敬意を払う` introduces recognition of standing and separateness.

## 53.3 `愛情の残骸`

`残骸` usually evokes wreckage/remains. Attached to affection, it makes old care materially present while emphasizing that its living relational context is gone.

## 53.4 `無私の献身`

Ononoki's description of Araragi's selflessness is not pure praise. `無私` marks absence of self-interest, and the surrounding dialogue treats that posture as professionally unscalable.

## 53.5 `欲`

Gaen's `欲は誰でも持つ` does not mean every appetite is harmless. The grammar separates possession of desire from the mechanism used to satisfy it.

## 53.6 `犯人は私`

Nadeko's phrase deliberately overstates identity with the culprit before she qualifies it. The rhetorical excess dramatizes moral recognition, not factual confession.

## 53.7 `創作物は創作者の一部`

The afterword's `一部` resists identity while preserving relation. The creation is neither wholly independent of origin nor reducible to it.

# 54. Visual/paratext audit

The prose is the governing evidence for V26. Illustrations and cover material function as paratext.

The volume's visual emphasis on Ononoki is consistent with the title and two-story structure: she is the shared "buddy/shadow" figure linking Araragi's and Nadeko's halves.

Her eyepatch after the familiar-creation incident is especially important as visual residue because it makes professional consequence visible across the transition from `バディ` to `シャドウ`.

No illustration should be used to override prose ambiguity about:

- Iezumi's childhood violence;
- the mother's/father's exact death sequence;
- the ontology of the daughter doll;
- the ontology of Nadeko's drawings;
- the identity/operation of the V27-facing curse provider beyond what V26 states.

# 60. Final synthesis

『余物語』 is one of Monster Season's strongest volumes for converting the series' older interpersonal ethics into a theory of **creation and professional responsibility**.

The volume begins with an apparently small point: Araragi cannot name his relationship with Ononoki using one word.

That becomes the governing method.

Do not reduce:

- Ononoki to tool;
- Iezumi to abuser;
- Iezumi to victim;
- a parent to love;
- a child to what the parent intended;
- a created being to creator purpose;
- Nadeko to former god;
- art to expression;
- a specialist to obedience;
- a client to desire;
- desire to guilt;
- a supernatural case to supernatural explanation.

The tragedy of Iezumi is that her parents did reduce. They loved a baby-object strongly enough to prevent the person from becoming legible. Her own later simulation then shows how trauma can reproduce the forms available to it without making reproduction inevitable destiny.

Araragi's most mature act is not finding her on the rooftop. It is refusing to pretend that finding her means he arrived before the damage.

Ononoki's most important act is not simply creating the teddy. It is entering the moral position of creator herself and discovering that useful manufacture creates obligations and institutional risk.

Nadeko's most important development is not solving a curse case. It is learning that she does not need to become desireless to be ethical. She can want a cheap apartment. What matters is whether her means treat other people as expendable.

The two stories therefore converge on one rule:

> **Care without respect becomes ownership. Creation without responsibility becomes instrumentalization. Desire without a means audit becomes predation.**

The positive alternative is not detachment.

It is relation without sovereignty.

Parents may love while respecting growth. Creators may make while accepting emergence. Professionals may help while retaining limits. Nadeko may desire while refusing harmful means. Ononoki may be made and still make. Araragi may rescue while admitting what he cannot restore.

That is the most important significance of `余`: after every attempt to define, make, save, own, or solve someone, **something remains**.

The ethical question is what one does with that remainder.
