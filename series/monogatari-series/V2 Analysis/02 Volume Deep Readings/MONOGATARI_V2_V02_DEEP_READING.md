# Monogatari Series V2 - Volume 02 Deep Reading
## Bakemonogatari (Naka)
### Suruga Monkey / Nadeko Snake

> **Publication-local core:** V02 is the book in which the simple language of hidden truth becomes dangerous. Kanbaru's case shows that an ugly unconscious wish can be genuinely hers without exhausting who she is; Nadeko's case shows that an oddity can be imposed by another person's malice rather than generated by the victim's contradiction. The volume therefore rejects both moral innocence and psychological reduction.
>
> **Retrospective V2 core:** V02 is an early laboratory for the series' mature ethics of plural selfhood, corrigible narration, triage, and responsibility. It repeatedly asks the reader not to identify a person with a single role: not the school star, not the jealous killer, not the shy victim, not the rescuer, and not the person who appears most morally pure from Araragi's current field of vision.

# 2. Publication-local thesis

V02 makes the rescue structure of V01 more morally difficult by introducing two very different forms of supernatural entanglement.

In `Suruga Monkey`, the problem is not that Kanbaru lacks a hidden truth waiting to be exposed. It is that she contains **contradictory truths at once**. She wants to be beside Hitagi. She also hates Araragi for occupying that place. She wants to become faster through her own effort. She also wanted the classmates who humiliated her to suffer. The Rainy Devil does not reveal that the ugly wish was the only real wish. The climax explicitly rejects that reduction: the murderous impulse is the **back side** of a wish whose benevolent/front side remains real. The contract works precisely because both sides must continue to count.

This distinction is central. V02's psychology is not a crude depth model in which the unconscious is the authentic self and conscious virtue is a lie. Kanbaru's kindness, loyalty, discipline, love, jealousy, resentment, and murderous fantasy are all hers. Moral adulthood begins not by discovering which one is the "true" Kanbaru, but by refusing to outsource responsibility for any of them.

`Nadeko Snake` then breaks a second possible simplification. Nadeko's curse is not principally an oddity generated by her repression. Oshino classifies it as something sent through another person's malice. Nadeko really has been targeted. The victim's psychology still matters to how Araragi sees her and how she seeks help, but the supernatural harm has an external perpetrative vector.

Together, the arcs reject two attractive but false universal rules:

1. **Not every oddity is the afflicted person's disowned psyche.**
2. **Not every hidden impulse is more authentic than the person's stated values.**

The volume's local ethical problem is then sharpened by Araragi himself. In the Kanbaru climax, Hitagi condemns the assumption that Araragi's death is an acceptable price for resolution. In the Nadeko climax, Kanbaru has to restrain Araragi because his wish to save even the curse-senders threatens to displace attention from the person actually dying in front of him.

V02 therefore moves the series from the question "Who participates in recovery?" toward a harder one:

> **What does responsibility require when every person involved has more than one morally relevant claim, and when helping one person may expose another to harm?**

# 3. Retrospective V2 thesis

Full-series hindsight makes V02 unusually important because later Monogatari repeatedly returns to the exact mistakes this volume teaches the reader not to make.

## 3.1 Kanbaru: owning contradiction without becoming the contradiction

Later Kanbaru material will turn inherited devil-parts, guilt, prevention, and Gaen Tooe's legacy into a long adult vocation. V02 already supplies the ethical grammar that makes that development possible. The Rainy Devil is dangerous because it converts an internal contradiction into an externalized mechanism, but the resolution does not ask Kanbaru to say, "My love was fake; murder was the truth." It asks her to survive the fact that her love contained violent possessiveness without reducing the love to that violence.

That distinction becomes the basis for a mature Kanbaru who can later treat dangerous inheritance as something to manage for other people's sake rather than as proof that she is secretly monstrous.

## 3.2 Nadeko: first-person misrecognition before Nadeko ever narrates

Later Nadeko volumes famously revise the "shy, cute victim" image. V02 is already more self-critical than the V1 synthesis sometimes remembered. The opening explicitly tells us that Araragi barely remembers the child he is about to describe. He proposes traits such as introversion, few words, and downward gaze, then immediately admits that he may be mixing her up with someone else. He even reflects that six years is enough for a person to become unrecognizable to her former self.

Thus later Nadeko narration does not retroactively reveal that V02 was a simple lie. It reveals how a **poorly grounded role can become narratively powerful because the narrator and reader find it convenient**.

At the same time, retrospective reading must not erase Nadeko's actual victimization in this volume. She is cursed by peers, physically constricted, frightened, and in genuine danger. Later agency complicates victimhood; it does not cancel it.

## 3.3 Araragi: universal compassion reaches its first triage crisis

V01 established that Araragi's helping impulse is distinctive. V02 names its danger more directly. Hitagi identifies his self-sacrifice as structurally independent of his supernatural healing: she says he would do the same foolish thing even in an ordinary mortal body. Then, in `Nadeko Snake`, Kanbaru identifies another problem: a helper can become so attached to universal rescue that he fails to prioritize the concrete person whose life is presently under his care.

This does not mean Monogatari endorses letting curse-senders die. The text leaves the curse's return morally ugly and unresolved. What it does establish is a **triage problem**: moral universality does not exempt a person from choosing under conditions of limited power.

## 3.4 Place becomes part of oddity causation

The ruined shrine is not merely scenery. Oshino describes it as a former spiritual center that has become a kind of pocket or accumulation point where unformed "bad things" gather, activated in part by the presence of the vampire queen. The site's condition affects Kanbaru, amplifies curse efficacy, and later makes the invisible snake materially traceable through soil, grass, and sound.

This is an early precursor to the later series' social and institutional monster ecology, but V02 does **not** yet justify the stronger V1 shorthand that "systems produce monsters." At this stage, the source supports a narrower proposition: **places can accumulate, magnify, route, and redistribute supernatural conditions.**

# 5. Narrator audit - Araragi Koyomi

## 5.1 Rumor literacy improves, person literacy remains uneven

V02 begins by warning the reader not to confuse school rumor with truth. Kanbaru is so famous that contradictory claims circulate simultaneously: fierce and gentle, loyal and cold, modest and arrogant. Araragi explicitly concludes that rumor is only rumor and cannot be taken whole. This is a significant narratorial competence.

Yet the very next movement shows his recurring weakness: he assumes there can be no meaningful relation between himself and Kanbaru because **he** has never perceived one. She has already been tracking him, knows about his relationship with Hitagi, and has a history that intersects his present far more strongly than he realizes.

Araragi is therefore good at detecting that public stories are unreliable but worse at noticing how much of another person's story exists outside his own awareness.

## 5.2 Nadeko opening as explicit memory audit

The Nadeko opening is one of the strongest early pieces of narrator self-criticism in Bakemonogatari. Araragi says he remembers her name, but little else. He proposes a shy, quiet, downcast child and then repeatedly retracts confidence. The traits may belong to another one of his sister's friends, or even to someone from his own childhood. He then generalizes: six years can change a person enough that past and present selves might not recognize each other.

This means the text itself marks "shy Nadeko" as a **low-confidence narrator reconstruction before it becomes an archetype**.

V2 should consequently resist two opposite errors:

- treating Araragi's early Nadeko portrait as transparent truth;
- treating later revisions as proof that everything about the early portrait was false.

The local evidence supports a third position: the current Nadeko really is withdrawn and frightened, but Araragi's sense that this is simply "the same girl as before" rests on admitted memory failure.

## 5.3 Araragi's self-evaluation is often too harsh

After Hitagi resolves the Kanbaru relational crisis, Araragi narrates himself as a useless third wheel who did nothing. That is emotionally revealing but factually excessive. He was essential in connecting Kanbaru to Oshino, eliciting disclosure, surviving the devil long enough for the contractual contradiction to become visible, and creating the social route through which Hitagi re-entered Kanbaru's life.

His self-condemnation therefore should not be accepted as objective moral accounting. V02 strengthens the V2 narrator profile: Araragi is often **more reliable about his failures than about the scale of his contribution**. Self-deprecation can distort in the same way self-aggrandizement can.

## 5.4 The rescuer's field of vision becomes a theme

During the snake climax Araragi says, in effect, that he has learned nothing and can see nothing. The phrasing matters because the volume repeatedly links ethical error to perceptual scope:

- he did not see Kanbaru's relation to him until it became violent;
- he misclassified Kanbaru's whole wish through a simple front/back story;
- he initially tells Oshino about one snake because he believes there is one curse;
- he wants to save unseen senders while the visible victim has just been freed;
- he cannot know who sent the second curse or what happens to the curse after return.

The book does not solve this by demanding omniscience. It teaches a more modest norm: **act without pretending your field of vision is the whole moral field.**

# 6. Chronology audit

## 6.1 Suruga Monkey

The arc begins near the end of May, shortly before the June uniform change. Araragi says roughly ten days have passed since he and Hitagi began dating; later he ties the relationship's beginning to Mother's Day, May 14, and describes about two weeks as having elapsed. The slight imprecision is ordinary retrospective narration rather than a reason to force false exactness.

Key local sequence:

- approximately late May, Friday: Araragi encounters Hachikuji on the way home; Kanbaru approaches; he has already noticed approximately three days of stalking;
- Friday night: Araragi is attacked by the raincoat figure;
- Saturday: he visits Kanbaru's home, hears the family/Monkey's Paw account, and brings her to Oshino;
- Saturday night: planned confrontation with the Rainy Devil; Hitagi arrives; contract is neutralized;
- Sunday: aftermath and Kanbaru's continuing left-arm condition.

The arc explicitly says Kanbaru's transformed arm took roughly four days to become fully active because her conscious restraint and concern for Araragi suppressed the Rainy Devil's access to the murderous wish.

## 6.2 Nadeko Snake

The dates are unusually explicit:

- June 11, Sunday: Araragi and Kanbaru meet at the school gate and perform Oshino's shrine errand; they encounter the ruined shrine and evidence of severed snakes; Nadeko is seen on the mountain route;
- June 12, Monday: Araragi's suspicions sharpen through school/bookstore encounters; Nadeko's condition is disclosed; Oshino identifies the curse and prepares the ritual;
- night of June 12: cleansing at the shrine; second snake appears; Araragi is injured; Kanbaru restrains him and allows the snake to depart;
- June 13, Tuesday: epilogue; physical recovery underway; second-sender theory explicitly framed as speculation.

## 6.3 Retrospective chronology note

The V2 reading spine still preserves archive order rather than internal story order. V02 itself refers backward to spring break, Golden Week, May 8, and May 14; those earlier-in-story events will receive their primary-source audit when the relevant Drive artifacts are reached. No later source should silently replace the uncertainty preserved here.

# 7. Causal plot reconstruction

## 7.1 Suruga Monkey

1. Kanbaru has a mummified left hand inherited from her mother, preserved as one of the only surviving material links to parents who died when she was in elementary school.
2. As a child in a hostile new social environment, she wishes to become faster. The object acts through violence against classmates while her own subsequent training also makes her faster.
3. Kanbaru comes to believe the object is a Monkey's Paw: an object that grants wishes in distorted ways and teaches the folly of resisting fate.
4. Years later, after separation from Hitagi and then learning that Hitagi and Araragi are dating, Kanbaru wishes to be beside Hitagi.
5. The object expands into a beast arm and the rainy-coat figure attacks Araragi.
6. Kanbaru follows Araragi for days. Her conscious concern for him inhibits full activation until jealousy becomes certain.
7. Oshino identifies the object not as Monkey's Paw but as Rainy Devil. The difference is morally significant: Kanbaru's preferred label let her treat violent fulfillment as something contrary to her intention rather than as a manifestation of one side of her wish.
8. Oshino offers several possible routes: Araragi dies; the arm is removed; or the contract is made impossible to fulfill.
9. Araragi refuses amputation because basketball is too important to Kanbaru and chooses bodily confrontation.
10. Rainy Devil nearly kills him. Hitagi arrives and announces that Araragi's death would lead her to kill Kanbaru.
11. The devil cannot strike Hitagi because doing so would make the surface wish -- remaining near Hitagi -- impossible. It also cannot kill Araragi while Hitagi's threat would make that same wish impossible. The contract is double-bound.
12. Hitagi physically holds Kanbaru's beast left arm and human right arm. Kanbaru confesses love; Hitagi refuses romantic reciprocity but invites continued proximity.
13. Kanbaru survives and remains socially connected, but her arm remains transformed and she leaves basketball. Resolution has residue and cost.

## 7.2 Nadeko Snake

1. A classmate performs an occult curse against Nadeko after a romantic conflict; the ritual unexpectedly catches a real supernatural mechanism.
2. The ruined shrine functions as a supernatural accumulation/amplification site, strengthening conditions around the curse.
3. Nadeko researches folk countermeasures and cuts snakes, but her self-directed ritual is incomplete/incorrectly situated and does not free her.
4. Araragi and Kanbaru encounter evidence of the ritual during Oshino's separate shrine-balancing errand.
5. Araragi later recognizes Nadeko, sees suspicious occult research, and elicits disclosure with Kanbaru's social help.
6. Nadeko's body carries constricting scale-like marks. She explicitly asks Araragi for help.
7. Oshino identifies `Jagirinawa` / the snake-binding curse as a deliberately sent oddity, explains its lethality, and prepares a cleansing ritual.
8. Araragi has described one curse. The ritual appears to succeed unusually quickly because one snake is removed -- but a second snake remains.
9. The second snake attacks. Araragi attempts to fight it and is badly injured; vampire-derived healing does not neutralize the damage normally.
10. Kanbaru restrains Araragi rather than attacking the snake. She reasons from snake behavior that if they stop provoking it, it will leave.
11. The snake departs toward its sender, implying curse-return. Kanbaru tells Araragi not to mistake whom he is supposed to save.
12. Nadeko is fully freed from the constriction and thanks Araragi. He experiences the thanks as morally painful because he had attempted to save the curse-sender(s) as well.
13. The epilogue speculates that the rejected boy might have sent a second curse but explicitly marks this as unsupported inference. The fate of either sender is unknown.

# 8. Oddity dossiers

## 8.1 Rainy Devil

### Literal mechanics

- mummified left-hand object that assimilates into the user's body;
- low-level devil identified by Oshino as Rainy Devil;
- exchanges up to three wishes for the user's soul/life/body;
- strongly associated with violence and negative affect;
- reads or realizes the "back side" of wishes rather than simply granting literal surface phrasing;
- remains constrained by contractual logic: the front wish cannot be wholly ignored;
- partial embodiment matters: because only an arm is present, Kanbaru's conscious restraint can still inhibit activation;
- transformed left arm persists after the immediate contract is neutralized.

### Human conflict

The central conflict is not "Kanbaru is secretly murderous." It is that love and resentment coexist. Her conscious wish to be near Hitagi and unconscious wish to remove Araragi are both causally relevant. The devil weaponizes contradiction by routing the ugly side through violence.

### Naming problem

`Monkey's Paw` is not an innocent mistake. It gives Kanbaru a narratively comfortable causal model: wishes are perversely granted *against* intention. Oshino's renaming matters because it returns responsibility to the person without reducing her to the violent impulse.

### Residue

The beast arm remains. Kanbaru leaves basketball. The outcome is therefore not exorcistic reset but a new life organized around an unresolved body.

## 8.2 Snake-binding curse

### Literal mechanics

- deliberately sent curse/oddity rather than spontaneous self-generated apparition;
- manifests as invisible constriction leaving scale-like traces on the body;
- potentially lethal once it reaches the head/face;
- can be cut/removed through ritual under correct conditions;
- can return toward sender when detached from victim;
- multiple curses can coexist, and incomplete diagnosis can therefore produce false confidence;
- site conditions can magnify and make the oddity materially legible.

### Human conflict

The victimization is real. Nadeko's peers participate in ordinary adolescent romantic hostility that becomes supernaturally disproportionate because a folk curse accidentally connects with a real mechanism. The supernatural does not absolve the sender, but it also makes intent and consequence radically mismatched: a middle-school grudge can produce lethal force.

### Residue

Nadeko's physical marks disappear, but the senders' outcomes remain unknown. Araragi's moral guilt and the unresolved social environment persist. Resolution therefore removes the immediate bodily threat without closing the interpersonal case.

## 8.3 Ruined shrine as oddity ecology

The shrine introduces an ontological category that is not yet a named oddity. Oshino describes "bad things" accumulating there before they have even stabilized into individually named apparitions. The site behaves like a catchment basin. His intervention is deliberately weak: a small redirection rather than total cleansing, because a dramatic change might create another vacuum elsewhere.

This is important evidence against treating oddities solely as private psychological metaphors. V02 gives the supernatural environment spatial dynamics, gradients, accumulation, resistance, and ecological balance independent of a single afflicted person's psyche.

# 9. Character pressure matrix

## 9.1 Kanbaru Suruga

### Public role

Kanbaru enters as a school-wide star rather than an intimate person. Her fame is so intense that Araragi can learn trivial details about her without ever speaking to her. The rumors contradict one another, which is precisely the point: social legibility does not equal personal knowledge.

The public role is athletic, charismatic, confident, and nearly superhuman. Her actual interior state is less stable. She is bereaved, lives inside a family rupture, has spent years disciplining herself around a dangerous inherited object, loves Hitagi intensely, and evaluates her own worth far more harshly than the school does.

### Self-story

Kanbaru's preferred childhood story is morally attractive: her mother left an object that taught her that fate should be changed by one's own effort, not by supernatural shortcuts. She trains until she genuinely becomes fast. This self-story is not false. Oshino even concedes that her disciplined posture was not "wrong" and later had real causal effect.

But it is incomplete. Calling the object a Monkey's Paw allows Kanbaru to imagine that its violence is merely the ironic distortion of a pure conscious wish. Rainy Devil makes the uglier proposition unavoidable: violent resentment was part of what she brought to the contract.

The V2 correction is crucial: **the self-story needs expansion, not total demolition**. Her discipline was real, her love was real, and her resentment was real.

### Ethical pressure

Kanbaru is asked to own an impulse without granting it sovereignty over identity. That becomes the deepest ethical success of the arc. She does not escape responsibility by saying "the devil did it," but neither does the novel say "the killer is the real Kanbaru."

### Relational pressure

Her love for Hitagi has survived distance and rejection because it is not purely a demand for reciprocity. Hitagi's final answer is harshly honest: she does not love Kanbaru in the same way, but she still asks whether Kanbaru will remain near her. That converts the relationship from fantasy of possession into a more limited but real continuing bond.

## 9.2 Senjougahara Hitagi

V02 strengthens the V01 picture of Hitagi as someone who treats relationship as a system of truth obligations rather than mere sentiment.

She first identifies Kanbaru's stalking and predicts jealousy faster than Araragi. At the climax she arrives not to perform generic "love conquers evil" but to exploit the contract's logic while simultaneously confronting Araragi for lying to her about the oddity. Her V01 agreement with him -- no secret supernatural crisis -- is already being enforced.

Her condemnation of self-sacrifice is among the most important early character statements in the series. She does not object because Araragi's body is uniquely precious in abstract moral theory. She objects because his death would **create consequences in other people**. He cannot declare his own life an acceptable payment while ignoring what his death would make her do or become.

That is an early relational answer to self-erasure: a person's life is not solely his property when other people are structurally bound to it.

## 9.3 Araragi Koyomi

V02 exposes two linked flaws.

First, Araragi treats bodily availability as moral availability. In `Suruga Monkey`, he chooses a plan in which his own death is a possible resolution because he assumes the cost can be assigned to himself. Hitagi shows that this is false accounting: his death redistributes the cost to her and Kanbaru.

Second, he tends toward universal rescue without a stable theory of priority. In `Nadeko Snake`, once the curse turns toward its sender, Araragi tries to save the sender too. The impulse is compassionate, but Kanbaru identifies that in the concrete emergency he is losing track of the person he was actually there to protect.

V02 therefore refines the project-wide Araragi problem:

> **His flaw is not that he cares too much. It is that he often treats his willingness to bear cost as if it solved the allocation problem for everyone else.**

## 9.4 Sengoku Nadeko

### Local person, not retrospective archetype

Publication-locally, Nadeko is a frightened middle-school student subjected to a lethal curse. She researches countermeasures, kills snakes in desperation, hides bodily marks, and eventually asks Araragi for help. These are concrete facts and must not be erased by later knowledge.

### Narrated role

At the same time, the text is unusually explicit that Araragi's childhood memory of her is weak. He constructs "shy, quiet, looking down" through uncertain recall and then encounters a present Nadeko whose behavior partially fits the role. This creates a feedback loop: a low-confidence old story begins to organize how he interprets current evidence.

The V2 position after V02 is therefore:

> Nadeko's victimhood is real; the completeness of the "shy innocent girl" identity is not established.

### Agency already present

Even before later books, the current volume contains evidence that she is not inert. She independently researches occult material, captures and kills snakes despite fear, lies to her parents about staying out, agrees to a dangerous ritual, and directly requests help. Oshino himself notes that the behavior sits uneasily with Araragi's "quiet and passive" description.

The later series does not need to invent agency ex nihilo. It will reveal how badly Araragi weighted the agency already present.

## 9.5 Oshino Meme

Oshino's role becomes more complex than "specialist who refuses to save."

He:

- corrects names and thereby corrects responsibility;
- prices help and settles debts;
- refuses overpowered intervention when it would destabilize a supernatural ecology;
- routes weaker actors to a task because their limited force is more appropriate than his own;
- accepts that a partial, non-dramatic alteration is often safer than purification;
- gives Araragi ritual knowledge while deliberately declining to become the center of the action;
- is willing to let Kanbaru experience discomfort as part of debt settlement, which keeps his ethics from becoming purely benevolent.

His practice is therefore a mixture of agency, balance, exchange, proportionality, and strategic withholding.

# 10. Relationship-state audit

## 10.1 Araragi - Hitagi

V02 immediately tests the V01 relationship pact. Araragi conceals Kanbaru's attack and explains injuries as an accident. Hitagi explicitly recalls their agreement not to keep supernatural secrets from one another and treats the lie as a relational violation.

This is important because the romance does not merely reward Araragi for rescue. It establishes **accountability against rescue behavior**. Hitagi's role is not to cheer his sacrifice; it is to force him to recognize that unilateral danger-taking is also a kind of concealment.

State change after V02:

- affection remains secure;
- mutual knowledge expands;
- Hitagi becomes an active brake on Araragi's unilateral self-sacrifice;
- the relationship's truth rule gains enforcement rather than remaining a romantic promise.

## 10.2 Hitagi - Kanbaru

The relationship is repaired but not reset to middle school.

Kanbaru confesses love openly. Hitagi refuses false symmetry: she does not say she loves Kanbaru equally, and she does not sentimentalize the answer. Yet she asks Kanbaru to stay close and apologizes for making her wait.

This is one of the series' earliest examples of **non-possession as relational survival**. Kanbaru does not receive the romantic object she wanted, but she is not expelled from Hitagi's life. The relation becomes durable by accepting asymmetry.

## 10.3 Araragi - Kanbaru

The relationship moves from stranger/stalker/rival to junior/senior trust with unusually rapid intensity.

Several things matter:

- Kanbaru tells him an extremely private family and supernatural history;
- Araragi refuses the easy arm-amputation solution partly because he understands basketball's value to her;
- Kanbaru sees the extremity of his helping impulse and later chooses to restrain it;
- in the Nadeko arc she becomes an indispensable social and physical partner rather than a former antagonist;
- she tells him that her respect would exist even apart from Hitagi.

By the end of V02, Kanbaru is already developing a distinctive relation to Araragi: admiration without romantic exclusivity, loyalty without obedience, and the willingness to physically stop him when his moral impulse becomes dangerous.

## 10.4 Araragi - Nadeko

The relation is structurally unequal in memory.

Nadeko remembers `Koyomi-onii-chan`; Araragi barely remembers what he called her. The asymmetry matters. She carries a relatively vivid childhood connection while he carries a vague role. The volume never proves what emotional significance Nadeko assigns to him beyond affection/trust and embarrassment, so retrospective romantic certainty must be quarantined.

During the ritual, the repeated request that he "watch" her serves a literal function -- monitoring the body/curse -- while also intensifying the relation between being seen, bodily exposure, and Nadeko's desire for his attention. Later books will make that axis much more important, but V02 should record it without prematurely resolving it.

## 10.5 Kanbaru - Nadeko

Kanbaru is essential because she can do something Araragi cannot: regulate the social atmosphere around a frightened younger girl.

She recognizes that Nadeko is psychologically unstable, deliberately raises the room's energy rather than mirroring despair, and succeeds in making her laugh. Araragi explicitly credits Kanbaru with opening Nadeko emotionally when he would have frozen.

This relationship is brief but analytically important. It shows that care need not take Araragi's form. Kanbaru's method is **social modulation** rather than heroic rescue.

# 11. Rescue / intervention audit

## 11.1 Kanbaru asks to be helped; Oshino asks "with what?"

The Rainy Devil consultation contains one of the clearest refinements of the `tasukeru / tasukaru` problem. When Kanbaru asks whether Oshino will help her, he repeats the familiar refusal to "save" and says he only lends force. But he then asks a sharper question: **what outcome does she actually want?**

Possible goals are not equivalent:

- fulfill the wish;
- cancel the wish;
- restore the arm;
- preserve basketball;
- avoid killing Araragi;
- remain near Hitagi.

This matters because "help" is too vague to be ethical until the desired state is specified.

## 11.2 Araragi's body as a falsely private resource

Araragi's decision to fight the devil is understandable. He wants to preserve Kanbaru's arm and basketball, and his vampire-derived healing makes physical confrontation seem less absurd than it would be for an ordinary person.

But Hitagi's intervention reveals the conceptual error: Araragi treats his life as if it were a private resource whose expenditure harms only him. Her response makes the relational externality explicit. If he dies, the consequences do not end at his body.

This is the strongest source support so far for the later V1 formulation that Araragi's self-sacrifice is not morally pure merely because he volunteers for it.

## 11.3 The Kanbaru resolution is not Araragi's victory

Araragi's plan fails physically. The decisive mechanism combines Oshino's understanding of the contract, Hitagi's arrival/threat, and Kanbaru's own double-sided wish. Hitagi then performs the relational act Araragi cannot: she holds both the beast arm and the human arm and answers Kanbaru directly.

Araragi's narration calls himself useless. V2 should reject the binary. He was neither savior nor irrelevant. He was a **causal contributor whose preferred mode of resolution was not the one that solved the central relationship problem**.

## 11.4 Nadeko and the ethics of triage

The snake climax is the volume's hardest rescue problem.

Araragi sees that allowing the curse to leave may return it to whoever sent it. His refusal to accept that outcome is not contemptible; it reflects his refusal to write off even people who harmed Nadeko. But his power is limited, he is poisoned/injured, and his attempt to fight the curse risks worsening the immediate situation.

Kanbaru therefore restrains him and says not to mistake the person he should save.

The source supports a narrow triage proposition:

> **When power is limited and harms cannot all be neutralized, the helper still has to identify the concrete duty generated by the present intervention.**

It does not support a broader proposition that curse retaliation is just, or that perpetrators never deserve rescue. The fate of the senders is intentionally left outside knowledge.

## 11.5 Gratitude as moral discomfort

Nadeko thanks Araragi after the curse disappears. Instead of accepting hero status, he experiences the thanks as unbearable because his own intention had extended to the people who cursed her.

This is ethically revealing in two directions.

- Positive: he refuses to simplify himself into the righteous rescuer of an innocent victim.
- Negative: his guilt risks recentering the crisis around his own moral purity rather than Nadeko's actual recovery.

V2 should track this tension longitudinally. Araragi is often admirable because he resists clean moral self-congratulation, but his demand to be universally blameless can itself become a form of self-centeredness.

# 12. Specialist ethics and metaphysics

## 12.1 Naming is responsibility allocation

Oshino's correction from Monkey's Paw to Rainy Devil is not taxonomy for taxonomy's sake. The Monkey's Paw story foregrounds unintended consequences: the wish is granted perversely. That narrative lets Kanbaru protect a morally clean conscious self against an alien mechanism.

Rainy Devil changes the question. The oddity does not invent the violent backside from nothing; it finds a negative wish and realizes it. The name therefore reallocates responsibility without collapsing agency into blame.

This is a major example of a series-wide rule V2 should preserve:

> **A better supernatural name can be a better moral description.**

## 12.2 Oshino's ecology of proportion

At the ruined shrine, Oshino does not seek total purification. He says a dramatic change is undesirable because displacing a supernatural concentration can create unknown effects elsewhere. The best intervention is a small distortion of the natural flow, not domination.

This extends V01's balance ethic into spatial ecology. Intervention has externalities, and expert power is itself a factor in the system being managed.

## 12.3 Expertise creates limits as well as capability

Oshino sends Araragi and Kanbaru to the shrine partly because **he is too expert/strongly legible to the supernatural field**. His presence could provoke a stronger reaction and create a vacuum. This is analytically important: expertise does not simply increase what a specialist may safely do. It can alter the observed system and therefore constrain intervention.

## 12.4 Debt, money, and exchange

The volume repeatedly treats supernatural help through exchange language:

- Hitagi's earlier fee;
- Kanbaru's debt settlement;
- Araragi's enormous prior debt to Oshino;
- the devil's soul-for-wishes contract;
- front/back wish fulfillment as contractual obligation.

This produces a striking formal parallel. Human specialist practice and demonic transaction both operate through exchange, but their ethics differ. Oshino uses exchange to set boundaries and prevent dependency; Rainy Devil uses exchange to exploit contradiction.

The shared vocabulary should be tracked rather than treated as accidental.

# 13. Japanese voice and address audit

## 13.1 Kanbaru's high-energy deference

Kanbaru's voice combines several registers that do not collapse into one archetype:

- formal respect toward `Araragi-senpai`;
- athletic-club hierarchy and discipline;
- startling sexual frankness;
- confident declarative rhythm;
- self-deprecating judgments about her own value;
- almost courtly praise of Araragi and Hitagi.

The friction is characterizing. A translation that renders her only as "perverted athletic girl" loses the way **deference and outrageousness coexist in the same sentence-level persona**.

Her address terms also encode relationship quickly. `Senjougahara-senpai` remains an object of reverence even after romantic rejection; `Araragi-senpai` moves from rival target to admired senior without the honorific disappearing.

## 13.2 Nadeko's third-person self-reference

Nadeko repeatedly uses her own name, `Nadeko`, in place of a first-person pronoun. In V02 this functions as part of a younger/cute, vulnerable speech position, especially when she says she hates what has happened to "this body" and asks `Koyomi-onii-chan` for help.

V2 must record this linguistically without assuming the full later interpretation yet. Later volumes will make self-naming, cuteness, role performance, and authorship central. At V02, we can say only that the speech form **makes the narrated self sound younger and more role-bound than ordinary neutral first-person speech would**.

## 13.3 `Koyomi-onii-chan`

The childhood address term preserves an intimacy that Araragi's memory has largely lost. It places him in an older-brother role before he has decided what Nadeko means to him now. Oshino immediately jokes about that role assignment, demonstrating again that address terms in Monogatari do not merely describe relationships; they actively frame them.

## 13.4 Hitagi's flat directness

Hitagi's answer to Kanbaru is linguistically important because the emotional content is delivered without a compensatory sentimental register. She states that she does not like Kanbaru "that much," then asks whether Kanbaru will remain nearby and apologizes for making her wait.

The blunt refusal of romantic reciprocity and invitation to continued relation occupy the same speech act. This is quintessential Hitagi: care without false verbal warmth.

# 14. Names, wordplay, ruby, and translation-sensitive structures

## 14.1 `Kanbaru` and divinity/devil contrast

Oshino notices the irony of a girl whose surname contains the character associated with "god" carrying a devil arm. He explicitly treats this as suggestive rather than decisive. V2 should follow that caution: the name creates thematic resonance, not proof of metaphysical destiny.

## 14.2 `Gaen Tooe`

Oshino asks about Kanbaru's mother's name and pauses over it, then says the background is not relevant "in this case." Publication-locally, this is a dangling specialist reaction with no resolved meaning. Retrospectively it is obvious foreshadowing of the Gaen network, but V02's local layer must preserve it as unexplained.

## 14.3 Front / back language

The Rainy Devil analysis repeatedly uses the spatial pair `omote / ura` -- front and back, face and reverse. Translation as simply "conscious/unconscious" is too narrow. The text is not making a clinical model. It is describing a wish as a two-sided object whose sides do not cancel one another.

The climax explicitly reasons: if a front has a back, a back also presupposes a front. That reversal is the key to the arc's ethics.

## 14.4 Curse-return language

The Nadeko climax plays on verbs of going back/returning and the proverb about digging two holes when cursing another. The snake does not simply disappear; Araragi recognizes it as **returning the curse**. The epilogue then calls his unsupported second-sender speculation a `snake tail` -- the Japanese idiom for something superfluous/unnecessary. The arc ends by punning on its own temptation to over-explain.

That is narratologically significant: the text labels the final explanatory theory as surplus rather than giving the reader closure.

# 15. Body, appetite, sexuality, gaze, and comedy

## 15.1 Kanbaru's body: talent, inheritance, weapon, loss

Kanbaru's body is overdetermined before the oddity appears. It is the body of a school athletic star, the body through which she disciplined grief into speed, the body desired/admired through school rumor, and then the body colonized by an inherited devil arm.

The Rainy Devil does not merely make her violent. It converts the athletic competence that defined her social value into combat technique. During the climax, Araragi realizes that the devil's improved footwork depends on **Kanbaru herself cooperating through embodied skill**. The distinction between person and oddity is therefore porous: the arm supplies monstrous force, while Kanbaru's trained body supplies technique.

The residual beast arm then removes her from basketball. This is a major material cost. The arc's resolution is not psychological insight followed by bodily restoration.

## 15.2 Nadeko's body: victim evidence and erotic spectacle

The scale marks must be seen to diagnose the curse. Bodily exposure is therefore causally relevant. But the novel repeatedly arranges that necessity through fetish-coded devices: near-nudity, bloomers, later a school swimsuit, commentary on her middle-school body, and extended banter about adult male desire.

A rigorous V2 reading should refuse both apologetic extremes.

- It is true that the gaze is thematized. Nadeko asks Araragi to look, fears being looked at erotically, and the curse itself is visible through the body.
- It is also true that the prose materially indulges sexualized attention toward a middle-school girl. Self-awareness does not neutralize participation.

The best formulation after V02 remains:

> **Bakemonogatari makes erotic looking part of the epistemic problem while also deriving entertainment from that looking.**

The contradiction should be preserved, not resolved in the text's favor.

## 15.3 `Watch me` as double-function language

During the ritual Nadeko asks Araragi to watch her carefully. The literal reason is obvious: someone needs to monitor the marks and ritual process. Yet because the arc has already made being-seen erotically and socially charged, the wording produces a second register of meaning.

Later Nadeko material will greatly expand the problem of being looked at, being made cute, and turning from object of gaze into maker of images. V02 is not enough to prove that full arc, but it is enough to enter the phrase as a longitudinal marker.

## 15.4 Comedy as social regulation

Kanbaru gives V02 some of its most excessive sexual comedy, but `Nadeko Snake` also shows a practical function. Kanbaru deliberately raises the emotional energy around Nadeko because matching her despair would deepen collapse. Some jokes are therefore consciously used as affect regulation.

This strengthens but also narrows the V1 "comedy as camouflage" thesis. Comedy in Monogatari can hide feeling, but it can also **manage interpersonal pressure, restore conversational agency, and prevent a room from freezing around trauma**.

# 16. Family, home, school, and institution

## 16.1 Kanbaru's family rupture

Kanbaru's oddity history is inseparable from family materiality.

Her parents eloped against family approval and died in a highway pileup shortly after her mother gave her the wooden box. Her paternal grandparents then disposed of the parents' belongings and tried to raise Kanbaru as if she were unrelated to them. The box is therefore not just an occult artifact. It becomes one of the only external containers of parental memory left to her.

This makes disposal ethically complicated. Asking Kanbaru simply to throw away or sever the object would not be equivalent to throwing away a dangerous toy. The object is bound to grief, inheritance, and the right to maintain continuity with dead parents.

## 16.2 School as rumor machine and status allocator

Kanbaru's social identity is generated institutionally through school circulation. Her basketball performance makes her a "star," but rumor then creates incompatible personality versions around that star. V02 therefore extends V01's role analysis from class-level position to school-wide reputation ecology.

The school also structures Kanbaru's loss: the transformed arm is not only a private body problem but the end of participation in the activity that made her legible and valuable within the institution.

## 16.3 Nadeko's peer world and absent adult mediation

The curse originates in middle-school romantic conflict and occult fashion. The extraordinary harm grows from an ordinary social ecology in which children experiment with rituals they do not understand. Adults are largely absent from the peer conflict itself.

This does not yet justify saying "school produces the monster." The causal chain includes peer malice, popular occult practice, a real supernatural mechanism, and the shrine's amplifying field. What V02 supports is a **multi-layer social causality** rather than a purely private wound.

## 16.4 Ruined shrine and neglected infrastructure

The shrine is physically abandoned yet supernaturally consequential. Its social abandonment does not make it empty; it makes it available as a catchment point for unnamed supernatural residue. This is an important early example of a Monogatari institution persisting after ordinary human use has vanished.

# 17. Thematic synthesis

## 17.1 A person is not the worst thing inside her

Kanbaru's arc is one of the series' earliest arguments against moral essentialism. The murderous wish matters because it is hers. But precisely because it is hers, it must be understood alongside other wishes that are also hers.

The text's formal logic is unusually exact: the back side cannot exist without a front. If interpretation identifies the hidden violent side as the only authentic side, it repeats the Rainy Devil's own distortion.

## 17.2 A victim is not only a victim

Nadeko's arc creates a complementary problem. The fact that she is genuinely victimized does not license Araragi or the reader to know her completely through victimhood. Her independent actions, uneven memory relation with Araragi, and low-confidence characterization all exceed the "cute girl in danger" position.

This is not victim-blaming. It is the opposite: recognizing personhood requires allowing victims agency, opacity, and contradiction without making their suffering less real.

## 17.3 Moral intention does not settle moral allocation

Araragi wants everyone to survive. That intention is better than cruelty, but the snake climax demonstrates that goodness of intention does not solve triage. Limited agents must choose which intervention they can actually perform.

V02 therefore introduces a recurring Monogatari distinction between **universal moral wish** and **situated responsibility**.

## 17.4 The helper can be the person who must be restrained

Kanbaru's development across the two arcs is structurally elegant. She begins as the person whose hidden wish nearly kills Araragi. By the second arc she becomes the person who physically stops Araragi from turning his own rescue impulse into another dangerous escalation.

The former "case" becomes an ethical counterweight to the protagonist almost immediately. This is one of the strongest early signs that Monogatari heroines are not meant to remain solved objects in Araragi's orbit.

## 17.5 Interpretation itself can become an oddity-like trap

Kanbaru's Monkey's Paw theory and Araragi's Nadeko memory perform parallel acts. Each converts partial evidence into a story that is emotionally convenient.

- Kanbaru: violence happened "against" the clean wish.
- Araragi: Nadeko is the shy girl he half-remembers.

Neither story is wholly false. The danger lies in mistaking a useful frame for exhaustive personhood.

# 18. Counterreadings and stress tests

## 18.1 Counterreading: the Rainy Devil proves the unconscious is the true self

**Temptation:** The devil reveals what Kanbaru "really" wants: Araragi dead.

**Problem:** The climax explicitly says the murderous wish is only the back side. The front wish remains causally binding and is what prevents the devil from harming Hitagi. The contract cannot be understood if the surface wish is dismissed as false.

**V2 judgment:** reject the essentialist reading. The arc is about contradictory ownership, not depth-authenticity.

## 18.2 Counterreading: Kanbaru is absolved because a devil controlled her

**Temptation:** Supernatural possession means the violence is not hers.

**Problem:** Oshino's reclassification directly attacks this comfort. The devil draws on negative wishes present in Kanbaru and eventually uses her trained body/technique. Her conscious discipline restrains the oddity, proving that human agency remains causally active.

**V2 judgment:** reject full absolution while preserving diminished/complex agency.

## 18.3 Counterreading: Nadeko's later development means her V02 victimhood is fake

**Temptation:** Later books expose the shy role, so the snake arc must be an early deception.

**Problem:** V02 provides explicit external curse mechanics and physical danger. Oshino identifies human malice as the sending vector. Nadeko's independent actions do not make her less victimized.

**V2 judgment:** later material should **complicate identity, not retroactively revoke victim status**.

## 18.4 Counterreading: Kanbaru is endorsing vengeance when she lets the curse return

**Temptation:** `Do not mistake whom to save` can be read as a moral sentence against the curse-senders.

**Problem:** Kanbaru's immediate reasoning is practical triage and snake behavior. The text never confirms what happens to the sender(s), and Araragi's discomfort remains ethically meaningful.

**V2 judgment:** the scene is a forced-priority problem, not a clean retributive ethic.

## 18.5 Counterreading: Oshino's balance talk is benevolent neutrality

**Temptation:** He simply seeks minimal harm.

**Problem:** He deliberately allows Kanbaru discomfort as part of settling debt and can be severe about exchange. His own presence alters systems, and his information control is strategic.

**V2 judgment:** Oshino is proportional and anti-heroic, but not morally neutral or conventionally gentle.

## 18.6 Counterreading: sexualized Nadeko scenes are justified because they expose the male gaze

**Temptation:** Self-awareness turns fanservice into critique.

**Problem:** The prose still creates and prolongs the spectacle. Critical framing and participation coexist.

**V2 judgment:** preserve the contradiction; do not use thematic sophistication as exculpation.

# 19. V1 claim audit

V02 materially revises several claims from the first-pass Monogatari analysis.

## 19.1 Oddities as contracts with unbearable personal truth

**V1 tendency:** Each oddity can be understood as a contract between a person and an unbearable truth.

**V02 status: NARROWED / COMPLICATED.**

Rainy Devil supports a contract model strongly, but Nadeko's curse is externally sent through peer malice. A global formulation that makes every oddity primarily the afflicted person's psychic bargain cannot survive V02.

Better:

> Oddities can embody, exploit, amplify, transmit, or impose human conflict. Their human meaning does not imply one uniform causal psychology.

## 19.2 Kanbaru's denied violent wish is still hers

**V1 status after V02: STRENGTHENED + COMPLICATED.**

The violent backside is genuinely Kanbaru's. But V1 sometimes phrased this in a way that could imply hidden violence is the "real" wish. V02 explicitly denies that implication. The front wish is equally necessary to the contract.

Better:

> Kanbaru must own the murderous wish without allowing it to erase the equally real love, discipline, and desire for proximity that coexist with it.

## 19.3 Nadeko as innocent victim whose cute surface is unstable

**V1 status after V02: STRENGTHENED + NARROWED.**

The victimhood is strongly supported. The instability of Araragi's "shy girl" construction is also more explicit than V1 remembered because the narrator openly audits his poor memory. But claims about avoidance, performed cuteness, or hidden agency beyond what V02 shows must remain retrospective until later Nadeko sources.

## 19.4 Araragi's universal rescue impulse is ethically unstable

**V1 status after V02: STRONGLY STRENGTHENED.**

Hitagi directly attacks self-sacrifice, and Kanbaru directly tells him not to mistake whom he is supposed to save. This is no longer merely a later-series interpretation projected backward; V02 gives explicit primary-source language.

## 19.5 Comedy is defensive camouflage

**V1 status after V02: COMPLICATED.**

V02 contains defensive joking, but Kanbaru also intentionally uses comic energy to pull Nadeko out of psychological collapse. Comedy is not only avoidance; it can be social care technology.

## 19.6 "Kanbaru admits the devil was hers; Nadeko lets the snake speak for her"

**V1 status after V02: NARROWED / PARTLY MISPLACED.**

The Kanbaru half is useful if "hers" means responsible relation to contradictory wishes rather than total identity. The Nadeko half is too compressed for `Nadeko Snake`: the snake here is primarily an externally sent curse. That shorthand belongs more plausibly to later Nadeko material and should not be imported into V02.

# 24. Compact reusable formulations

These are concise formulations suitable for later synthesis **only if future volumes do not overturn them**.

### V02-R01 - Kanbaru
> The Rainy Devil does not reveal Kanbaru's one true wish. It weaponizes one side of a double wish. V02's ethical demand is ownership without essentialism: the murderous resentment is hers, but so are the love and discipline that resist it.

### V02-R02 - front/back
> In Monogatari, hidden does not automatically mean more authentic. A back side presupposes a front side, and interpretation becomes violent when it lets either side erase the other.

### V02-R03 - Araragi self-sacrifice
> Araragi's body is not a private moral currency. Volunteering himself does not erase the costs his death would impose on people bound to him.

### V02-R04 - rescue triage
> Universal compassion does not eliminate priority under limited power. In the Nadeko crisis, Araragi's desire to save even the senders becomes dangerous when it prevents him from accepting the concrete duty in front of him.

### V02-R05 - Nadeko
> V02 establishes Nadeko as a real victim but not a fully known person. The "shy girl" exists partly inside Araragi's explicitly uncertain memory before later Nadeko ever gets to narrate herself.

### V02-R06 - victimhood and agency
> Agency does not cancel victimhood. Nadeko can research, act, conceal, kill snakes, choose ritual, and ask for help while still being genuinely subjected to another person's curse.

### V02-R07 - oddity causation
> V02 breaks any one-to-one psychology of oddities: Rainy Devil exploits a user's contradiction; the snake curse is sent by another person; the ruined shrine amplifies supernatural conditions spatially.

### V02-R08 - Oshino
> Oshino's balance ethic includes proportionality and observer effect: sometimes the expert is too powerful to intervene safely, and a deliberately weak correction is more responsible than purification.

### V02-R09 - Kanbaru as counter-rescuer
> Kanbaru moves from case to ethical counterweight within one volume: the girl whose hidden wish attacks Araragi becomes the person willing to restrain him when his rescue impulse loses its sense of priority.

### V02-R10 - comedy
> Comedy in Monogatari is not only camouflage. V02 shows it operating as social regulation: Kanbaru deliberately uses absurdity and energy to pull a frightened Nadeko back into interaction.

### V02-R11 - gaze
> Nadeko Snake turns looking into both diagnostic necessity and erotic spectacle. The text can critique the gaze while participating in it; self-awareness does not cancel the material sexualization.

### V02-R12 - resolution residue
> Neither V02 arc ends in reset. Kanbaru keeps the beast arm and loses basketball; Nadeko loses the bodily curse but not the unresolved social history or uncertainty about the curse's return.

# V02 closing judgment

V02 is the point where Bakemonogatari's early ethical model becomes decisively anti-essentialist.

The easy reading of `Suruga Monkey` would say that the monster reveals what Kanbaru really wanted. The source itself refuses that. The ugly wish is genuine, but it is only the reverse side of another genuine wish. Kanbaru's problem is not falsity; it is plurality without ownership. The Rainy Devil becomes dangerous by making one side act as though it were the whole person.

The easy reading of `Nadeko Snake` would reverse the mistake and define Nadeko wholly through innocence. The source again resists. She is genuinely victimized, yet Araragi's concept of who she is begins in poor memory and role language. Her agency is visible even in crisis, while her body is simultaneously transformed into evidence and spectacle by the narration.

And the easy reading of Araragi would call his indiscriminate willingness to help simple virtue. V02 attacks that simplicity twice. Hitagi says he cannot spend his life without counting the people who would bear the consequence. Kanbaru says he cannot turn the wish to save everyone into an excuse for refusing priority when a specific person depends on him.

The volume's strongest reusable proposition is therefore:

> **A morally serious person is not the person with only good motives. It is the person who can own incompatible motives, recognize limited knowledge, accept relational costs, and still choose a situated responsibility without pretending that choice resolves the whole moral field.**
