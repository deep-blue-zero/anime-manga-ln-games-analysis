# 〈物語〉シリーズ V2 — Volume 10 Deep Reading
## 『花物語』
### 第変話「するがデビル」

> **Publication-local core:** 『花物語』 is less a story about Kanbaru “defeating another oddity” than a story about what remains after a supernatural crisis has already become part of a person's biography. Kanbaru begins her third year having spent almost a year treating guilt as permanent self-surveillance: she binds her left arm to a pillar at night, checks local crime reports every morning, and interprets the burden as a form of responsibility. Numachi Rouka has built the opposite-looking but structurally comparable system: she collects other people's unhappiness because their suffering makes her own ruined life easier to inhabit. Neither system resolves the past. Both are repetitive technologies for living beside damage. Their eventual basketball game does not discover which girl is morally “right.” It creates the first completed contest between two former rivals and lets Numachi experience something she never properly had while alive: receiving a pass, playing relationally, and losing legibly.
>
> **Retrospective V2 core:** V10 is a major maturation point for the project's responsibility thesis. V04–V09 progressively separated responsibility from sacrificial self-punishment; V10 makes the distinction explicit in Kanbaru's own language: `自分を責めることは反省じゃない。内罰も自罰も違う。` The volume does not absolve her of the Rainy Devil violence, nor declare her guilt false. It argues that indefinite self-policing is not therefore a morally superior form of atonement. At the same time, Numachi's “unhappiness collection” complicates rescue ethics: a listener can genuinely help while acting from selfish motives, and a moment of genuine compassion can occur inside an ethically compromised person without purifying her whole character. The governing question shifts from **“who is really good?”** to **“what kind of relation and future does this act make possible?”**

# 2. Publication-local thesis

The publication-local temptation is to read 『花物語』 as a simple graduation story:

- Kanbaru is stuck in the past.
- Numachi embodies unhealthy attachment to misfortune.
- Kanbaru defeats Numachi.
- Kanbaru cuts her hair.
- Kanbaru moves forward.

Every part of that sequence contains some truth, but as a total reading it is too clean.

The volume is more interested in **what counts as moving forward when the past cannot be undone**.

Kanbaru's opening confession immediately blocks a “dark side exorcised” model. She says she has hated others and hated herself. More importantly, she directly revisits the logic of the Rainy Devil:

`自分の暗黒面を、まるで別物のように切り離した──だけど結局、あれは自分自身を悪魔に作り変えただけのことだった。`

The mistake was not possessing anger, jealousy, hatred, or murderous impulse. The mistake was treating these as though they could be externalized into a separate being whose acts would not belong to her.

That is exactly the anti-essentialist correction V02 required. The “dark” part is not the truer self. But neither is it unreal.

V10 then asks what comes **after ownership**. Kanbaru has accepted that the murderous wish was hers. Yet she has turned that acceptance into a punitive routine: binding the arm while sleeping and checking the news every morning in case she has committed some forgotten crime. She describes this as avoiding the sin of ignorance. The practice gives her a feeling of responsibility because she can never fully stop suspecting herself.

The volume's mature proposition is:

> **Owning wrongdoing is necessary. Making permanent suspicion of oneself into an identity is not the same thing as repair.**

Numachi supplies a parallel failure mode. She does not police herself. She listens to other people's unhappiness. Her method helps clients because talking redistributes emotional pressure and time often changes the practical problem. But she also openly admits she listens because other people's misery makes her own ruined life feel less uniquely bad.

So both girls construct repetitive post-damage systems:

- Kanbaru: **I will never stop checking whether I became the monster again.**
- Numachi: **I will never stop collecting proof that other people are miserable too.**

Neither system is reducible to hypocrisy. Both produce real effects. Both are partly self-serving. Both prevent the past from simply becoming past.

『花物語』 is therefore not “moving on” versus “refusing to move on.” It is an inquiry into **which forms of continuity remain livable**.

# 3. Retrospective V2 thesis

The V2 reread places V10 at an unusually productive crossroads of earlier findings.

V02 established that the Rainy Devil does not reveal Kanbaru's single authentic wish; `表` and `裏` are both hers.

V04–V09 progressively separated moral responsibility from self-destruction. Araragi and Kiss-shot repeatedly try to turn their own suffering or death into payment; later volumes expose why this does not settle the obligations created by survival.

V05–V08 destabilized “fake/real” as a sufficient axis for persons. Borrowed justice can become owned action; nonhuman origin can coexist with real personhood; split-off Hanekawa affects can be real without becoming privileged authentic selves.

V10 brings these together.

Kanbaru's problem is no longer whether the murderous wish was “really hers.” She already knows it was. Her problem is what ethical life should follow that recognition.

The key retrospective correction is:

> **Responsibility after self-recognition must eventually become future-directed practice rather than indefinite retrospective punishment.**

This does not mean forgetting. The volume repeatedly insists that Kanbaru will forget and remember, gain and lose, love and dislike different things over time. It means that memory cannot be the sole organization of her life.

Numachi adds a second V2 thesis:

> **Impure motive does not erase useful care, and useful care does not purify impure motive.**

That is the V05 fake/real logic translated into ethics of listening. Numachi does not need to be secretly altruistic for her conversations to help people. Nor does the fact that people feel better transform her into a saint. One moment of sincere compassion for another Rouka can coexist with years of voyeuristic self-medication.

The volume repeatedly refuses moral totalization at the level of persons.

Kanbaru realizes that people she hates have friends. People she loves may love people she dislikes. Kaiki can be an enemy to Hitagi and Karen while being a strangely kind adult to her. Numachi can enjoy collecting misery and still experience genuine pity. Kanbaru can act from selfish `見てられない` and still produce an ending Numachi seems to value.

The V2 formulation is therefore:

> **A person's moral identity is not the arithmetic sum of isolated good and bad acts, but neither can those acts be dissolved into a single “true character.” Monogatari increasingly treats ethical identity as relational, situational, continuous, and revisable.**

# 4. Architecture and arc map

The volume's 32-section movement can be understood in seven phases.

## Phase A — self-audit after graduation

Kanbaru enters third year after Araragi, Hitagi, and Hanekawa graduate. She dreams of her mother, binds the Rainy Devil arm at night, checks crime reports, and recognizes how much her identity depended on older senpai remaining physically nearby.

## Phase B — rumor and old rival

Ougi introduces the rumor of `悪魔様`, an entity who resolves student worries. Kanbaru initially fears this may be evidence of her own unconscious supernatural activity. Investigation leads to Numachi Rouka, the middle-school basketball rival whose `泥沼ディフェンス` once defined the opposite pole of Kanbaru's athletic identity.

## Phase C — unhappiness as collection

Numachi explains her counseling practice. She receives worries, promises to solve them, and usually does nothing, trusting time to transform the client's emotional relation to the problem. She admits the practice exists partly because hearing worse stories reduces her own misery.

## Phase D — bodily restoration and Kaiki

Kanbaru's demonic arm abruptly returns to human form because Numachi has taken the active devil component. Kanbaru encounters Kaiki, receives partial information about Tooe, the Rainy Devil, and a collector gathering devil parts, and confronts the fact that a person she morally hates can be personally considerate toward her.

## Phase E — Numachi's biography and collector identity

Numachi reveals talent, injury, social isolation, the origin of her unhappiness collection, and the case in which genuine compassion apparently transferred a devil leg into her body. She has since pursued other devil parts across Japan, turning a single moment of care into an ongoing collecting practice.

## Phase F — death revelation and decision

Karen, Tsukihi, and Higasa independently establish that Numachi died by suicide three years earlier. Kanbaru runs herself to collapse, meets graduate Araragi, talks through her uncertainty, and decides that her own discomfort is itself a legitimate reason to act—even if Numachi's system has beneficiaries and even if intervention cannot be framed as objective justice.

## Phase G — first real game, disappearance, and postscript

Kaiki delivers the Rainy Devil head. Kanbaru lures Numachi into a one-possession 1-on-1. The decisive tactic is a pass to her opponent, followed by a steal and dunk. Numachi realizes basketball is a team sport and says she has finally been able to lose properly. She disappears, leaving devil parts behind. Kanbaru refuses to call the outcome rescue, chooses not to inherit Numachi's whole mission, stops treating self-punishment as reflection, cuts her hair, and begins looking forward.

# 5. Narrator and focalization audit

## 5.1 Kanbaru as first sustained narrator

V10 is the first archive-spine volume to let Kanbaru control a full narrative. Her opening sentence already marks the difference from Araragi:

`神原駿河が馬鹿だという話をこれからしようと思う。`

She narrates herself as object, jokes about her own stupidity, directly solicits the listener's story, and repeatedly anticipates that her interpretation may be partial.

This is not a transparent access point to “the real Kanbaru.” It is a different self-construction.

Kanbaru is unusually willing to make herself look ridiculous, vulgar, unstable, obsessive, or cowardly. That candor gives her narration ethical force. Yet she also constructs strong explanatory stories about others—Numachi's injury, motives, suicide, ghost mechanics, and final desire—that the text does not always independently verify.

## 5.2 The image of Kanbaru changes with the observer

Kanbaru explicitly notes that Araragi may see her as a reckless hot-blooded person while someone else could see her as cool or unemotional. Numachi interprets her social energy as clowning designed to fit into the team.

Kanbaru's own reaction is not “Numachi finally sees the true me.” She admits the reading is not wholly wrong.

The volume therefore reinforces the cumulative narrator principle:

> **Other people's readings can be correcting without being total.**

## 5.3 Narration after the older protagonist leaves school

Araragi's absence changes what can become narratively central. He enters late, mainly as a conversational support and practical helper. He does not diagnose Numachi, fight Numachi, or provide the supernatural solution.

Kanbaru explicitly tells him there is nothing he can do for the decisive part.

This is a structural decentering event. A former “case character” becomes the subject of her own ethical problem and uses Araragi as one relationship among several rather than as narrative sovereign.

## 5.4 Self-knowledge is improving but not complete

Kanbaru is more psychologically explicit than V02 Araragi's Kanbaru. She recognizes jealousy, loneliness, guilt, envy, desire for punishment, attraction to both women and men, and her wish to remain connected to senpai.

Yet one of the volume's final insights is something she has to admit only after Numachi disappears:

`私はあいつが──羨ましかったんだ。`

She envied Numachi.

Her narration is therefore best classified as:

> **high self-observation + strong retrospective rhetoric + genuine capacity for correction + persistent motive discovery during narration.**

## 5.5 Afterword as paratextual support

Nisio's afterword compares self-image to hearing a recording of one's own voice and suggests that neither the self's image nor other people's image automatically owns “truth.” It even jokes that perhaps there are as many misunderstandings as people.

This strongly supports the focalization model but remains **paratext**, not diegetic law.

V10 is internally one of the clearest volumes so far despite its unusual place in publication order.

# 7. Causal plot

The causal structure is not “demon returns → fight demon.”

A more accurate chain is:

1. Kanbaru begins the school year emotionally destabilized by senpai graduation and years of guilt management.
2. Ougi's `悪魔様` rumor activates Kanbaru's fear that she may be acting unconsciously through the Rainy Devil.
3. The rumor leads her to Numachi.
4. Numachi is already a ghost/oddity who does not know she is dead and has turned unhappiness collection into her posthumous activity.
5. Numachi recognizes/targets Kanbaru partly because the active devil arm is a collectible supernatural component.
6. Contact permits Numachi to take the devil component, restoring Kanbaru's human arm.
7. Kanbaru's relief is morally destabilizing because she has treated the arm as deserved punishment.
8. Kaiki confirms a broader devil-parts collection problem and supplies partial information linked to Tooe.
9. Numachi's biography reveals how injury, talent, isolation, listening, and supernatural transfer became one posthumous practice.
10. Independent human research reveals Numachi died three years earlier.
11. Kanbaru considers leaving the situation alone because Numachi appears content and clients benefit.
12. Araragi reframes Kanbaru's own distress as a legitimate datum: `少なくともひとり、お前が困ってる`.
13. Kaiki supplies the devil head, creating a bargaining/lure object.
14. Kanbaru chooses direct contest rather than specialist intervention.
15. The game gives Numachi a completed basketball relation she lacked in life: opponent as trusted receiver/partner, followed by a legible loss.
16. Numachi disappears; no omniscient explanation confirms whether the game “caused” purification in a mechanical sense.
17. Kanbaru elects not to inherit all remaining obligations and begins withdrawing from punitive routines.

This causal chain matters because the supernatural and ethical resolutions are only partially coincident. Numachi's disappearance follows the match, but the novel does not reduce her existence to a simple boss mechanic that Kanbaru has solved.

# 8. Oddity dossiers

## 8.1 Residual Rainy Devil / Kanbaru's restored arm

The Rainy Devil remains a continuing body-history rather than an erased episode.

Before restoration:

- Kanbaru's left arm remains demonic.
- She hides it socially.
- She binds it during sleep.
- She checks crime reports for possible dissociated violence.
- The arm functions as guilt, danger, bodily alteration, and self-imposed monitoring object.

After Numachi acquires the active component:

- Kanbaru's arm becomes human again.
- restoration arrives earlier than expected;
- Kanbaru initially experiences relief as suspicious/undeserved;
- a minor scrape on the restored arm produces pain and blood that finally lets her emotionally accept the arm as **her human arm**.

V2 caution: this must not be turned into “pain makes her morally whole.” The pain is sensory evidence of bodily ownership, not divine punishment.

## 8.2 Numachi Rouka as posthumous oddity-person

Numachi is a particularly important ontological case because she apparently does not know she is dead.

Human biography:

- gifted athlete;
- former soccer prodigy;
- switches to basketball;
- suppresses offense into defensive style;
- suffers fatigue fracture;
- leaves normal school trajectory;
- develops unhappiness-collection practice;
- dies by suicide before middle-school graduation.

Posthumous state:

- appears physically continuous with her adolescent body;
- travels without ordinary material constraints;
- can create/benefit from social-space absences;
- receives students' worries;
- accumulates devil parts;
- constructs a coherent false self-history in which she remains alive and traveling on insurance money;
- does not recognize `成仏` as applicable to herself.

Kanbaru hypothesizes that Numachi has become an oddity that collects misfortune. This is a strong inference, not omniscient exposition.

## 8.3 Devil-part transfer

The Kachou Rouka case shows supernatural transfer through intense contact/compassion: Numachi hugs the afflicted girl and later discovers the devil leg has transferred to her.

Numachi treats this as almost miraculous. Kanbaru invokes Oshino's principle that oddities have reasons. Numachi counters that oddities are arbitrary and sloppy.

The text does not adjudicate the dispute.

**Ledger rule:** preserve competing causal models instead of forcing a universal law.

## 8.4 Accumulation and embodiment

Numachi's body increasingly incorporates collected devil components. She insists she intends to collect/absorb the devil rather than become it. Kanbaru fears the opposite: collecting the parts may already have turned Numachi into the devil's hands and feet.

The case repeats an important series pattern:

> **A tool adopted for coping can become an identity-organizing system before the user notices the transition.**

This applies psychologically to Kanbaru's self-surveillance as much as supernaturally to Numachi's collecting.

## 8.5 Resolution residue

Numachi disappears after the game, leaving collected devil parts arranged in roughly human form.

Open questions remain:

- Did she “pass on” because of the loss?
- Did loss break the posthumous collecting loop?
- Did basketball completion fulfill a lingering condition?
- Did the devil-part configuration collapse?
- Did she simply cease manifesting to Kanbaru?

The novel supports connection, not a fully mechanistic answer.

# 9. Character pressure analysis

## 9.1 Kanbaru: guilt after confession

V02's Kanbaru had to admit that the murderous underside of her wish belonged to her.

V10 asks what happens when that admission persists for nearly a year.

She develops a moral identity around vigilance. The strongest evidence is not abstract guilt but routine:

- bind arm at night;
- inspect morning evidence;
- compare local incidents against her possible activity;
- treat ignorance itself as culpable;
- refuse to run beside Hitagi because she feels undeserving;
- hesitate to resume sport fully despite restored body.

This is responsibility becoming **self-suspicion as lifestyle**.

Her eventual correction does not say “I was innocent.” It says:

`自分を責めることは反省じゃない。`

That is a much more mature ethical proposition.

## 9.2 Numachi: injury becomes worldview

Numachi's athletic injury is not merely tragic backstory. It reorganizes how she thinks about talent, fairness, sympathy, time, and social hierarchy.

She distrusts rhetoric that converts arbitrary talent into moral desert. She sees “effort” discourse as partly ideological: it reassures people that superior performance must have been earned rather than distributed by luck.

Yet her own response to talent was not healthy detachment. She suppressed parts of her game, isolated herself, overtrained, and broke her body.

Only after losing basketball does she fully recognize that she loved it.

This is a crucial V10 pattern:

> **Value is sometimes discovered through loss, but discovery through loss does not justify the loss.**

The same anti-retrospective-moral-alchemy rule from V09 applies.

## 9.3 Kanbaru and Numachi as mirrored post-crisis athletes

Their pairing is stronger than “rivals reunited.”

| Kanbaru | Numachi |
|---|---|
| body altered by devil | body altered by injury, death, and devil parts |
| offense/speed | defense/delay |
| guilt turned inward | misery comparison turned outward |
| checks herself for hidden harm | collects others' reported harm |
| fears becoming devil | believes she can collect devil without becoming it |
| socially integrated through clowning/energy | socially isolated through talent and defensive distance |
| wants decisive action by final act | prefers delay/avoidance/time |
| struggles to accept release from punishment | struggles to accept a completed loss |

Neither is the simple cure for the other.

## 9.4 Envy as the final admitted affect

Kanbaru eventually says she envied Numachi.

This matters because envy is precisely the kind of feeling V02 Kanbaru could have externalized into “the devil.” V10 Kanbaru can instead state it directly without making it the whole truth about either person.

That is evidence of integration.

# 10. Relationship-state protocol

## 10.1 Kanbaru ↔ Araragi

V10 changes their relationship from rescue-centered senpai/kouhai dependence into something closer to **available but non-sovereign support**.

Araragi:

- is no longer at school;
- admits he misses Kanbaru;
- listens to a problem he does not fully understand;
- tells her her own distress counts;
- offers help;
- accepts her `ない` when she says he cannot do the decisive part;
- later helps clean her room;
- takes the devil parts for disposal;
- cuts her hair because she asks him to.

He does not seize the case.

That is an important maturation of Araragi's helper role.

## 10.2 Kanbaru ↔ Hitagi

Hitagi is mostly absent physically but remains a major internal reference.

Kanbaru remembers her running form as beautiful and admits she repeatedly refused sprint races because she did not feel entitled to run beside her. This exposes how admiration became another vehicle for self-punishment.

The relationship therefore remains loving while Kanbaru starts disentangling love from moral unworthiness.

## 10.3 Kanbaru ↔ Numachi

Their relationship cannot be reduced to friendship or rivalry.

Kanbaru repeatedly says she dislikes Numachi.

Numachi repeatedly needles Kanbaru.

Yet each recognizes the other's athletic identity with a precision friends often do not possess. The decisive pass in the final game works because Kanbaru trusts an opponent's body to respond correctly.

The compact formulation is:

> **Rivalry can be a relation of recognition even where affection is weak or absent.**

## 10.4 Kanbaru ↔ Tooe

Tooe exists primarily as remembered/dreamed/internalized speech. Kanbaru respects, fears, resents, and wants someday to understand her.

The final dream is important because Kanbaru talks back.

Maturity here is not “finally accepting mother's wisdom.” It is becoming able to imagine disagreement without loss of relationship.

## 10.5 Kanbaru ↔ Kaiki

Kaiki becomes a test of moral relational plurality.

Kanbaru wants to hate him because of what he did to people she loves. Yet he is personally helpful and has a history with Tooe that makes him treat Kanbaru differently.

Kanbaru's realization should be preserved almost as a ledger rule:

`私が嫌いな人間にも友達がいる。`

`私が嫌いな人間を好きな人もいる。`

Moral evaluation survives. Totalizing identity does not.

# 11. Rescue, care, and intervention audit

V10 contains at least five different models of “help.”

## 11.1 Numachi's listening model

She listens, receives, and promises to solve problems. Often she takes no direct action.

Benefits:

- disclosure itself can reduce pressure;
- clients may prefer a listener outside ordinary social hierarchy;
- time genuinely changes many adolescent problems;
- she refers severe cases to appropriate institutions.

Problems:

- she lies about solving things;
- she uses clients' pain for self-regulation;
- avoidance can become default rather than judgment;
- her “solution” may mean only that the client stops actively worrying.

The correct V2 classification is **mixed care**, not fake care.

## 11.2 The Kachou Rouka case

Here Numachi experiences what she describes as genuine desire to help. She cannot solve pregnancy, family coercion, or boyfriend irresponsibility. She embraces the girl and repeats the same formula she normally uses cynically.

Then the devil leg transfers.

The scene's ethical importance lies in rejecting purity logic:

> A person who often acts badly can perform a genuinely compassionate act without becoming “good only.”

Likewise, the previous selfishness does not make this compassion fake.

## 11.3 The weight of being helped

Numachi suggests that the girl may have avoided returning because help creates `重み`—weight/debt. Kanbaru says “恩,” and Numachi says they may be the same.

This connects strongly to V01's weight vocabulary and the broader non-possession project.

Help can create obligation in the recipient's experience even when the helper did not explicitly demand repayment.

Therefore:

> **A helper's good intention does not control the social weight the help acquires.**

## 11.4 Kanbaru's intervention into Numachi

Kanbaru explicitly refuses to pretend she is intervening for pure rescue:

- she is disturbed;
- she cannot bear watching;
- Numachi resembles an alternate possible self;
- she wants to beat her;
- she wants proof Numachi is not her;
- she dislikes her.

This is ethically valuable precisely because the narrative does not sanitize the motive.

She eventually says:

`助けた、というつもりはない。救った、はずもない。`

That is a strong non-savior stance.

## 11.5 Araragi's late support

Araragi identifies one person who *is* troubled: Kanbaru.

`少なくともひとり、お前が困ってる。`

This matters because Kanbaru had almost erased herself from the moral accounting. She was evaluating only whether Numachi and Numachi's clients were harmed.

Araragi's intervention restores Kanbaru as a morally relevant person without telling her what outcome to choose.

That is one of his better rescue performances so far.

# 12. Specialist ethics and metaphysics

## 12.1 Kaiki as partial-information specialist

Kaiki continues to resist simple fraud/expertise binaries.

He knows:

- Tooe;
- the Rainy Devil inheritance;
- devil-part collection;
- enough about Numachi to anticipate a convergence;
- and enough to possess the devil head.

But he withholds decisive information and operates according to his own opaque incentives.

Numachi characterizes his method as providing half the information and staying in a supporting/third-party position. That is useful but remains her diegetic characterization.

## 12.2 Specialist knowledge does not equal relational ownership

Kaiki's most interesting ethical role here is indirect. He does not command Kanbaru. He gives her a dangerous object and leaves choice open:

`使いたければ使え。捨てたければ捨てろ`

That can be read as manipulative setup or respect for agency—or both.

The volume does not settle it.

## 12.3 Oshino remains present as method rather than person

Kanbaru repeatedly invokes Oshino's statements about oddities, but he is absent. This matters structurally. Specialist frameworks have become inherited tools that younger characters can use, question, or reject rather than answers delivered by an adult on site.

## 12.4 Competing oddity epistemologies

Kanbaru: oddities have reasons/mechanics.

Numachi: oddities are loose, arbitrary, `適当でいい加減`.

V10 does not supply enough evidence to choose one universally.

The best corpus rule remains:

> **Seek local causal structure without assuming all oddities obey one total ontology.**

# 13. Japanese voice and address

## 13.1 Kanbaru's narration

Kanbaru's voice is more formally self-conscious than her dialogue under Araragi focalization suggested. She uses athletic metaphors, philosophical digressions, sexual jokes, rhetorical reversals, and unusually explicit self-indictment.

She shifts readily between:

- rough colloquial comedy;
- respectful senior-address;
- literary reflection;
- sports analysis;
- deliberately melodramatic narration.

The result is not simply “tomboy voice.” It is a narrator constantly performing and revising what “Kanbaru-like” means.

## 13.2 `神原選手`

Numachi repeatedly calls her `神原選手`—Kanbaru the player/athlete.

This address matters because much of V10 concerns Kanbaru having stepped away from actual competitive basketball. Numachi continues to recognize her through a role Kanbaru herself has suspended.

The final game turns the old address back into an active identity rather than nostalgic label.

## 13.3 `悪魔様`

The honorific rumor-name dignifies what Kanbaru initially fears is monstrous. It also makes Numachi's practice sound more omnipotent than it is.

The gap between rumor-title and actual method—mostly listening and delay—is part of the novel's recurring interest in social names becoming realities larger than their bearer.

## 13.4 `見てられない`

Kanbaru's phrase that she acts because she cannot stand to watch deserves indexing.

It is not principled justice vocabulary. It is embodied, relational irritation/distress.

V10 repeatedly suggests that moral action often begins from less grand motives than moral theory later assigns to it.

## 13.5 `卒業`

Graduation functions beyond school status:

- Araragi/Hitagi/Hanekawa have literally graduated;
- Numachi “graduated” from soccer and then from ordinary life prematurely;
- Kanbaru wants to graduate from dependence on senpai;
- the final admission of envy makes Kanbaru feel she has graduated from something;
- haircut functions as `けじめ`, not magical purification.

The word should therefore be treated as a major lexical motif.

# 14. Names, wordplay, ruby, and translation-sensitive observations

## 14.1 Numachi Rouka / 沼地蝋花

`沼地` literally evokes swamp/marsh ground, directly supporting the `泥沼ディフェンス` imagery. Her play style is sticky, slowing, trapping, and refusing clean resolution—the same logic that later structures her counseling philosophy.

`蝋花` contains “wax” and “flower,” giving a brittle/preserved floral image that fits a girl whose life has stopped while her activity continues. This should be treated as interpretive resonance rather than deterministic name-etymology.

## 14.2 `花物語`

The title's flower language should not be reduced to one character. The volume is full of lives that have bloomed, withered, been preserved, or changed form. The afterword's playful possibility `ろうかゴッド` further reminds us that arc naming itself is part of series metanarrative play.

## 14.3 `悪魔` language

V10 repeatedly moves among:

- literal devil parts;
- “dark side” as self-description;
- rumor-title `悪魔様`;
- metaphorical becoming-devil through coping practice;
- one moment where “an angel can dwell in a devil.”

Translation should not flatten all these into one simple supernatural category.

## 14.4 `重み` / debt

The Numachi/Kachou discussion links the weight of gratitude and debt. Given V01's dense `重さ／重み／思い` field, this is a high-value lexical callback even if it is not proof of one metaphysical system.

# 15. Body, appetite, sexuality, comedy, and materiality

## 15.1 The restored arm

The arm is not merely a symbol. Kanbaru's entire balance, clothing practice, sleeping routine, athletic identity, and sensory life have adapted around it.

When it becomes human again, she trips because her body-map has changed.

The first scrape matters because:

- skin breaks;
- pain is ordinary rather than monstrous;
- the arm is materially vulnerable;
- Kanbaru can finally experience it as her own human limb rather than moral evidence.

## 15.2 Numachi's body as collection

Numachi's body is almost archival. Devil parts accumulate where human injury/death already interrupted her body-history. She hides them under bandages and keeps the crutch even after supernatural changes make it less materially necessary.

The injured-girl presentation also helps strangers speak to her. Thus bodily display becomes both residue and social technology.

## 15.3 Running to collapse

Kanbaru's 100-km-plus overnight run is not framed as exemplary discipline. She later recognizes that she has physically wrecked herself through poor pacing.

The episode is useful precisely because it contains both:

- genuine emotional relief from movement;
- and self-destructive excess.

The V2 reading should not romanticize it into therapy-by-suffering.

## 15.4 Sexual comedy under Kanbaru narration

Kanbaru's sexual jokes persist, including desire toward women and men and comic treatment of nudity. But focalization changes their function: under her own narration these are not merely Araragi's gaze upon a sexually aggressive girl. They become part of Kanbaru's chosen comic persona and bodily candor.

That does not eliminate questions of genre service; it changes who authors the joke locally.

## 15.5 Haircut

The final haircut is deliberately requested from Araragi as `けじめ` and because shorter hair suits sport.

It should be read as:

- practical athletic recommitment;
- relational trust;
- chosen marker of a transition;
- not evidence that long-haired Kanbaru was false.

# 16. Family, home, and institution

## 16.1 Tooe as inherited voice

Kanbaru's mother remains a powerful internal authority despite being dead.

`薬になれなきゃ毒になれ。でなきゃあんたはただの水だ。`

This is memorable, forceful, and potentially damaging advice. V10 does not simply endorse it because Tooe is charismatic.

The final dream matters because Kanbaru begins talking back.

## 16.2 Family recognition without idealization

Kanbaru wonders whether Tooe may, in her own difficult way, have treated her consistently as a daughter. This is a tentative wish, not a settled retrospective absolution.

Kanbaru also says perhaps someday she will understand her mother—but not yet.

That “not yet” is essential. Understanding is allowed to be developmental rather than compulsory.

## 16.3 Grandparents as ordinary care

Kanbaru's grandparents are comparatively quiet but important. They raised her, welcome Araragi, and provide the household stability around which her post-crisis life can proceed.

The volume's family world is therefore not reducible to Tooe's charismatic absence.

## 16.4 School after the protagonists leave

Naoetsu High continues after Araragi/Hitagi/Hanekawa graduate. Kanbaru has a class, friends, juniors, responsibilities, and future exams.

This is a significant institutional point:

> **The setting has social continuity beyond the people previously narratively privileged.**

# 17. Themes

## 17.1 Story and self-narration

Kanbaru is surrounded by stories other people tell about her:

- princely athlete;
- clown;
- hot-blooded idiot;
- cold person;
- guilty devil-user;
- beloved junior;
- possible successor to her mother.

V10 rejects the project of finding one story that abolishes the others.

## 17.2 Fake/real/personhood

Numachi's counseling can be “fake” in motive and real in effect. Her posthumous self-narrative can be factually false and psychologically real to her. Kaiki can be fraudulent and knowledgeable. Kanbaru's social persona can be performed and genuinely hers.

The recurring question is increasingly:

> **What does this construction do?**

rather than:

> **Was it pure at origin?**

## 17.3 Rescue and agency

V10 refuses both heroic rescue and absolute noninterference.

Numachi's clients often benefit from being heard.

Kanbaru intervenes despite uncertainty.

Araragi supports without seizing the case.

Numachi disappears without ever receiving a definitive explanatory lecture.

Care is therefore a field of partial actions, not a binary of “saved/not saved.”

## 17.4 Identity integration

The opening restates the problem explicitly: cutting away the dark side turned Kanbaru into a devil.

The ending demonstrates a more integrated response: she can admit envy, dislike, guilt, desire, dependence, competitiveness, and relief without exiling any one affect into a separate moral creature.

## 17.5 Adulthood

Numachi's farewell wishes ordinary human continuity for Kanbaru:

`受験したり、友達を作ったり、彼氏を作ったり、就職したり、結婚したり、子供を育てたり、親子喧嘩をしたり`

These are specifically things Numachi says she could not do.

Adulthood is therefore not primarily prestige. It is **continued participation in time**.

## 17.6 Family

Kanbaru does not need to decide immediately whether Tooe was admirable, harmful, loving, frightening, or all of these. The volume permits unfinished filial interpretation.

## 17.7 Appetite and material life

Kaiki feeding Kanbaru yakiniku, the restored arm's ordinary pain, shoes worn down by the extreme run, Araragi's car, cleaning the room, and haircut all anchor the supernatural argument in material life.

## 17.8 Judgment and apology

The volume is skeptical of global moral verdicts. Araragi's final judgment is itself anti-judgment:

`お前は正しいことをしたわけでも、間違ったことをしたわけでもない。`

This should remain his compassionate interpretation, not be promoted into authorial omniscience.

## 17.9 Art / play / creation

Basketball becomes communication. The final possession is less proof of athletic superiority than a collaboratively produced ending neither player could make alone.

# 18. Counterreadings and adversarial tests

## Counterreading A: Numachi is simply a benevolent listener

**Evidence for:** she often helps people feel better, refers serious problems elsewhere, and sometimes experiences genuine compassion.

**Counterevidence:** she openly uses others' suffering to regulate herself, makes false promises of solving problems, and turns collection into obsession.

**V2 verdict:** benevolence is real but partial. Do not purify her motive.

## Counterreading B: Numachi is simply a predatory misery voyeur

**Evidence for:** she enjoys collecting misfortune and treats it as a hobby.

**Counterevidence:** clients often receive exactly the nonjudgmental listening they seek; the Kachou case produces sincere distress; she experiences a real wish to help.

**V2 verdict:** exploitation and care can coexist in one practice.

## Counterreading C: Kanbaru saves/exorcises Numachi

**Evidence for:** Kanbaru initiates decisive action; the game is followed by disappearance.

**Counterevidence:** Kanbaru explicitly rejects rescue authorship; Numachi does not learn the full truth of her death; causal mechanism is not fully specified.

**V2 verdict:** Kanbaru creates a relational completion condition, not a demonstrably complete metaphysical cure.

## Counterreading D: Kanbaru's guilt was unnecessary from the start

**Evidence for:** Rainy Devil exploited divided wishes and supernatural mechanics.

**Counterevidence:** V02 explicitly required ownership of the murderous wish and responsibility for attempted harm.

**V2 verdict:** guilt/ownership were not mistakes; indefinite punitive surveillance is the later mistake.

## Counterreading E: Time solves everything

**Evidence for:** many adolescent worries dissipate; forgetting is part of life.

**Counterevidence:** Numachi's own time has stopped; her injury and death do not resolve; Kanbaru's self-punishment persists until she acts and reframes it.

**V2 verdict:** time is one causal resource, not a universal rescuer.

## Counterreading F: decisive action is always superior to avoidance

**Evidence for:** Kanbaru's final action creates closure.

**Counterevidence:** V10 explicitly treats avoidance as sometimes legitimate; Numachi's clients often need distance rather than confrontation; Kanbaru and Numachi agree that “do it and regret it” is shallow rhetoric.

**V2 verdict:** the volume rejects both universal actionism and universal avoidance.

# 24. Compact reusable formulations

### Kanbaru

> **V10 Kanbaru is no longer trying to discover whether the devil was “really her.” She already knows the murderous wish belonged to her. Her new problem is learning that owning a past wrong does not require organizing the rest of her life around suspicion and punishment.**

### Numachi

> **Numachi turns other people's misfortune into pain management. The practice is selfish, voyeuristic, and often genuinely helpful at the same time; V10 refuses to purify either side of that contradiction.**

### Responsibility

> **Self-punishment looks backward and asks how long the guilty self should continue suffering. Responsibility eventually has to look forward and ask what kind of person can now be lived into existence.**

### Rescue

> **Kanbaru does not claim to save Numachi. She creates one unfinished relation—a real basketball contest—and lets the meaning of that encounter remain larger than her authorship.**

### Rivalry

> **The decisive pass works because Kanbaru trusts her opponent. V10 turns rivalry into an unexpectedly exact form of recognition: the enemy can become the person whose response you know well enough to build a play around.**

### Good/bad personhood

> **A bad act does not prove every kindness false; a kind act does not convert an entire person into good. Monogatari's ethics increasingly distrusts total character verdicts.**

### Adulthood

> **For Numachi, the ordinary future—exams, friends, romance, work, marriage, children, arguments with parents—is what death removed. V10 defines adulthood less as wisdom than as the privilege of continuing through time.**

### Graduation

> **Graduation in Hanamonogatari is not erasure. It is the painful conversion of a relationship or identity from a form that cannot continue into one that might.**

### Selfhood

> **A performed self can still be yours. A disliked self can still be yours. A former self can still be yours. The problem begins when one version claims the authority to exile all the others.**

### Full-volume synthesis sentence

> **『花物語』 is the story of two damaged athletes who turned suffering into systems—one by policing herself, one by collecting others—and of the moment a pass between rivals lets both systems briefly become a game again.**
