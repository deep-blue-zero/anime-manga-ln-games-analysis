# <MONOGATARI> V2 - Volume 20 Deep Reading
## 『愚物語』
### 「そだちフィアスコ」 / 「するがボーンヘッド」 / 「つきひアンドゥ」

> **Publication-local core:** 『愚物語』 begins Off Season by removing Araragi Koyomi from the narrator's chair. Oikura Sodachi, Kanbaru Suruga, and Ononoki Yotsugi each receive a first-person story in which Araragi is absent, peripheral, or deliberately kept uninformed. The result is not a set of side anecdotes about characters whose development has already been completed. It is an adversarial test of whether the series' earlier character models survive independent focalization. They do—but only after substantial revision. Oikura does not become the cheerful domestic girl V19 briefly manifested; she remains suspicious, abrasive, lonely, and furious, yet begins constructing a home and future through choices she can call her own. Kanbaru does not inherit Tooe's mission, Rouka's collecting, or Araragi's rescue style; she chooses a different form of foolishness, aimed preventively at unknown future victims. Ononoki insists that she is an emotionless professional tool while narrating pleasure, embarrassment, fear, attachment, curiosity, rivalry, and concealment; her own account therefore becomes evidence against the simplest version of her self-description. Across all three stories, improvement does not mean ceasing to fail. It means **failing without surrendering authorship of the next attempt**.
>
> **Retrospective V2 core:** V20 is the first strong proof that Final Season's mature claims remain viable after Araragi is structurally decentered. V19's anti-essentialist rule survives: hidden, happier, harsher, more expressive, or more professional states do not monopolize personhood. V14's prevention ethic expands beyond Araragi's body into Kanbaru's decision to reduce distributed future harm before a crisis acquires a face. V16's Oikura model is strengthened: growth is small, behavioral, and abrasive rather than reconciliatory. V14/V19's Ononoki model is strengthened again: constructed presentation and role discipline do not entail absence of affect. V06's Tsukihi model, however, requires a major complication. Her continuous social personhood remains real, but Ononoki's observations suggest that phoenix regeneration may repair not only tissue but psychologically disruptive memory, allowing the subject to reset while consequences accumulate in other people and environments. That mechanism is a specialist inference rather than omniscient fact, and it must not be used to biologize every aspect of Tsukihi. Off Season therefore opens with a new scale of the series' old residue problem: **what happens when one person's resilience, one person's intervention, or one person's inherited monster distributes costs across a social field that no single protagonist can see?**

# 2. Why Off Season begins by changing narrators

The most important formal fact in V20 is not any one supernatural revelation. It is that **Araragi does not narrate the book**.

That change matters because V01–V19 repeatedly taught the reader to distrust the ease with which Araragi's descriptions become character ontology. He was unusually observant but weak at motive attribution, prone to idealization, self-condemnation, erotic/comic distortion, and over-centralization. V08, V10, V11, V13, and V19 progressively decentered his view through Hanekawa, Kanbaru, Nadeko, Kaiki, mirror residues, and other countervoices.

V20 goes further. It asks three questions that could not be answered while he remained the default focalizer:

1. **Who is Oikura when hatred of Araragi remains important but Araragi is nowhere in the room?**
2. **Who is Kanbaru after her old devil arm and Araragi-facing junior role cease to organize the entire plot?**
3. **Who is Ononoki when her flat presentation is no longer something Araragi observes from outside but something she herself performs, explains, and accidentally contradicts?**

The answer in each case is plural rather than revelatory.

V20 does not reveal the `real` Oikura, Kanbaru, or Ononoki hidden beneath prior narration. It gives three more situated narrators, each with her own blind spots.

That distinction is essential. Decentering Araragi is not the same thing as replacing one unreliable master narrator with three transparent ones.

# 3. Publication-local volume thesis

The title `愚物語` initially invites a simple reading: three people do foolish things.

That is true but inadequate.

All three narrators are intelligent. Oikura is academically formidable and hyper-analytical. Kanbaru is capable of complex inference, physical discipline, and moral reflection. Ononoki is an experienced specialist shikigami with technical knowledge and extraordinary combat ability. Their failures therefore do not come from a generic lack of intelligence.

They come from **miscalibration between a model and a world that refuses to stay inside the model**.

Oikura tries to engineer `普通` through deliberate social strategy. She treats friendship almost like a constrained optimization problem, selects an apparently isolated classmate as a low-risk target, studies a new school's hierarchy, and attempts to avoid every mistake that destroyed her prior classroom life. Yet her very intensity reproduces the old problem: she over-invests, over-interprets, presses people past their boundaries, and discovers that the apparently normal class already contains its own history of ostracism, guilt, factionalism, and hidden absence.

Kanbaru begins with a much smaller model failure. Araragi has been cleaning her room for a year; she criticizes the quality of his recent work, loses his labor, and discovers that she has no ordinary competence at managing the material environment he had been quietly maintaining. The clutter then literally reveals inherited supernatural residue. Her room is not merely messy: the mess is where a mummified Rainy Devil hand and a letter from her dead mother become visible. The domestic failure opens a distributed-risk problem far beyond the room.

Ononoki begins with the strongest claim of competence. She is a professional, a tool, a dead thing who separates work from private feeling. Yet nearly every stage of `つきひアンドゥ` is produced by her own attempted cover-up. She is caught eating ice cream; invents a magical-girl lie; stages a fake monster; underestimates the interaction between Nadeko's drawing and an artificial oddity; fails to maintain `残心`; catches fire because Tsukihi previously covered her in oil; rationalizes impending defeat; and is finally saved by Hachikuji recognizing a rule Ononoki herself created.

The shared proposition is therefore:

> **Foolishness in V20 is not simply doing the wrong thing. It is continuing to act through a model after reality has shown that the model is incomplete.**

But the volume is not anti-effort. Each story also distinguishes a destructive repetition from a productive one.

Oikura repeats intrusive intervention, but now does so while acknowledging uncertainty and accepting that the social cost may fall on herself rather than treating herself as righteous victim.

Kanbaru chooses a long preventative project, but explicitly refuses to call it her mother's inheritance or Araragi's imitation. She authors the obligation.

Ononoki ends with the comic promise that next time she will not fail, then immediately recreates the same initial condition. That ending is deliberately less mature. The reader is left to decide whether observation is genuinely changing her practice or merely furnishing a more sophisticated story about repetition.

# 4. Retrospective V2 thesis

V20 produces five major revisions to the V2 model through V19.

## 4.1 V19 anti-essentialism survives independent narration

The most important test concerns Oikura.

V19 manifested a cheerful Oikura living in the Araragi household. The V19 ledger correctly refused to call that girl the `true` Oikura. V20 now supplies direct first-person evidence. Oikura wants happiness, a home, ordinary school life, and connection—but she remains sharp, suspicious, angry, and socially difficult. Her desire for a different life was real; the cheerful V19 possibility was still only a possibility.

Direct narration therefore **strengthens** rather than overturns the V19 lock:

> A person can want a gentler relation without already containing a hidden gentle personality that merely needs unveiling.

The same rule applies to Ononoki. Her narration demonstrates emotions without making an expressive V19 Ononoki the real one. Flatness is a cultivated/constructed interface; expressive possibility is another real capacity; neither monopolizes personhood.

## 4.2 V14 prevention expands from self-management to anonymous future harm

V14 taught Araragi that emergency exception cannot remain his default care model. Prevention means acting before every problem becomes a bodily catastrophe.

Kanbaru extends that logic beyond herself and beyond known relationships.

The scattered Rainy Devil fragments may already be affecting unknown people. Kanbaru does not wait for a named victim to appear. She decides to search because somebody she has never met may foolishly use one later. Her final distinction from Araragi is explicit: if he fights for people within the reach of his eyes and hands, she will be a fool who fights for people whose faces she does not know.

That is a genuine scale change:

> **situated rescue → distributed prevention**.

It is not automatically superior. The risk is obvious: a person can convert limitless hypothetical victims into limitless personal jurisdiction. V20's safeguard is Kanbaru's insistence that the project is her choice, plus the presence of Ougi and already-active specialists. Later volumes must test whether this remains shared, bounded work or becomes a new martyr script.

## 4.3 V16's Oikura development standard is strongly confirmed

V16 refused a reconciliation cure. Oikura's progress appeared in apology, promise-keeping, altered address, and willingness to move.

V20 continues that exact scale.

She still hates Araragi. She still fantasizes violence against him. She still suspects kindness, violates privacy, interrogates classmates, and repeatedly describes herself as awful. But she also:

- accepts residence in another household because she wants a `家`;
- allows public aid and scholarship structures into her future model;
- returns to school;
- tries to make friends;
- notices when other people's kindness may be mixed rather than fake;
- refuses simplistic anti-truancy moralism;
- distinguishes personal resentment from state-level ethical obligation;
- and intervenes while admitting that her account of the class is incomplete.

This is not “healed Oikura.” It is **Oikura gaining additional action possibilities without ceasing to be Oikura**.

## 4.4 V14/V19 Ononoki personhood is strongly strengthened

V14 already established `個人的感情` and `羞恥心`. V19 established that Ononoki can change her expressive interface and that inability to express is not the same thing as absence of feeling.

V20 moves inside her voice.

She says she has no ordinary appetite, then enjoys five ice-cream flavors. She calls stress relief human imitation while reporting stress. She insists that sentiment cannot affect the Tsukihi assignment while displaying rivalry, irritation, embarrassment, curiosity, fear of destruction, pleasure, and concealment of failure. At the point of near-death she acknowledges that even an already dead corpse can be terrified of dying.

The correct conclusion is not “Ononoki secretly became human.”

It is:

> **The category `human imitation` is one of Ononoki's own explanatory defenses. The feelings and practices it classifies remain causally real regardless of whether their origin is human, constructed, copied, or supernatural.**

## 4.5 V06 Tsukihi requires a major but bounded revision

V06 correctly rejected the idea that learning Tsukihi is a phoenix proves that her personality has no `本人`. Lived history, family relation, metacognition, desire, and ordinary continuity remained evidence of personhood.

V20 does not overturn that conclusion.

It does, however, make one aspect of the phoenix mechanism much more consequential. Ononoki observes that Tsukihi forgets nearly an entire day containing an animated doll, magical-girl fiction, a giant slug, and her own pulverizing death. Ononoki's inference is that the phoenix does not merely repair a body after trauma; it may erase or reset brain/memory states whose retention would obstruct the continuing human life.

That produces a striking asymmetry:

> **Tsukihi may recover internally while consequences remain externally deposited.**

Ononoki describes the failed acts as accumulating only around the subject, becoming new `火種`, while the `炎芯` starts clean again.

This is a specialist hypothesis, not omniscient fact. It must remain `CD/SI` until later evidence confirms or narrows it. It also cannot be used to explain every ordinary instance of Tsukihi's impulsiveness. The strongest current formulation is mechanical and limited:

> **Phoenix regeneration may include selective cognitive repair under sufficiently disruptive conditions, creating a potential externality in which the subject's own traumatic learning is reduced while others retain the residue.**

# 5. Three-story architecture

V20 is not organized as escalation toward one shared climax. The three stories form a comparative triptych.

| Story | Starting problem | Error mode | Scale reached | Leaving state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `そだちフィアスコ` | how to live `普通` in a new class | over-planning, suspicious interpretation, coercive intervention | household + classroom + public safety-net/social network | class partially reassembled; Oikura remains isolated but future-facing |
| `するがボーンヘッド` | clean one's room and reconcile with Araragi | dependence, inheritance confusion, explanatory overreach | room → family legacy → nationwide anonymous risk | Kanbaru chooses self-authored preventive search |
| `つきひアンドゥ` | preserve surveillance cover | professional overconfidence, deception cascade, premature closure | bedroom → fabricated oddity → town risk → cognitive/ethical externality | surveillance continues with new hypothesis and unresolved bias |

The ordering matters.

Oikura's story is almost entirely non-supernatural. The social world alone can produce exclusion, guilt, manipulation, faction, and rescue pressure.

Kanbaru's story reintroduces an old oddity as inherited distributed material. The supernatural problem is inseparable from mundane clutter, parental legacy, and chosen future responsibility.

Ononoki's story then makes **fabrication itself** causally dangerous. A lie told to protect a professional cover becomes a story whose material pieces—Nadeko's drawing, a residual fake oddity, salt, staging, role assignment—produce a real monster.

The sequence thus moves:

> **social story → inherited supernatural material → invented story becoming supernatural material.**

That progression is a compact Off Season manifesto. The series is no longer confined to “a troubled adolescent meets an oddity.” It can study ordinary institutions, distributed legacy, professional practice, and stories that become operational systems.

# 6. Chronology map

V20's publication order and internal chronology diverge strongly.

## 6.1 `そだちフィアスコ`

The story occurs in **November**, more than a month after Oikura left Naoetsu. She is eighteen and transfers into Shishikurazaki High (`宍倉崎高校`) during her third year. She calculates that fewer than four nominal months remain before graduation and that the practically meaningful classroom period may be little more than a month.

This places the story after the principal Oikura events reconstructed in V16 but before high-school graduation.

## 6.2 `するがボーンヘッド`

Kanbaru explicitly says:

- she is eighteen;
- Araragi is now nineteen;
- this is the **first day of her last high-school summer vacation**;
- she is preparing both for entrance exams and a possible basketball return in university.

The story therefore belongs to Kanbaru's post-V10 third-year period after Araragi's graduation. No exact calendar date is supplied, and V2 should not invent one.

## 6.3 `つきひアンドゥ`

Ononoki says she began observing Tsukihi in **February** and that almost half a year has passed. She also says Karen moved into her own room when she entered high school in **April**, while Tsukihi and Nadeko are currently third-year middle-school students. The weather is explicitly that summer's severe heat.

The safest chronology is therefore:

> **post-graduation summer, approximately half a year after the February surveillance assignment; exact date unresolved.**

## 6.4 V20 chronology consequence

The book is publication-locally unified but internally scattered. “Off Season” therefore begins not as a single chronological next chapter but as **a release-order container for lives that have already escaped Araragi's linear present**.

That is formally consistent with V19's claim that an ending does not produce one clean next state.

# 7. Narrator and focalization audit

V20 adds three full narrator dossiers. Their differences are analytically more important than a generic label such as “unreliable.”

## 7.1 Oikura Sodachi — self-indicting hyperanalysis

Oikura's first-person voice opens with an enormous declaration of hatred for Araragi. She describes the hatred as identity, axis, and core, says losing it would threaten her sense of self, then concludes:

`この気持ちはきっと、恋よりも激しい。`

The line should **not** be converted into “Oikura is romantically in love with Araragi.” The comparison concerns intensity, not category. More importantly, the surrounding narration shows what the hatred does: it supplies continuity, stress relief, competitive motivation, and a reference point against which Oikura can imagine becoming happier.

Her focalization has several recurrent properties.

### A. She overstates, then audits the overstatement

Oikura calls Amiko a delinquent, a class ruler, and herself a monster, then repeatedly admits that she is choosing maliciously strong language, that her knowledge is partial, or that another interpretation remains possible.

This makes her narration rhetorically extreme but often epistemically self-correcting.

### B. She knows her motives can be ugly

When choosing an isolated classmate as a friendship target, she admits the strategic advantage: somebody already excluded may be easier for a newcomer to approach from above. This self-accusation is evidence, but not automatic total truth. Oikura can have mixed motives; instrumental calculation does not erase genuine loneliness and desire for connection.

### C. She mistrusts kindness

The “choyahoya” phase is almost unbearable because unearned hospitality activates suspicion. Later she learns that guilt really did motivate part of the class's behavior. Yet she also admits that pure kindness may have been mixed in and that she tends to reject even happiness unless she can attribute it to her own action.

This is important: Oikura's suspicion is neither simply paranoid nor simply accurate. It is **high-sensitivity detection with an overfitting risk**.

### D. She converts morality into correctness problems

Words such as `正しい`, `間違い`, `最適解`, proof, calculation, and route recur constantly. Her central confession is devastatingly simple:

`間違おうと思って間違えたことなんてない。`

She never intended to make a mistake. Her old classroom catastrophe was produced while she believed she was doing the correct thing.

This narrator therefore exposes the limit of retrospective moral certainty: knowing one has been wrong before does not produce a reliable algorithm for being right now.

### E. She can distinguish individual and systemic ethics

Oikura says Hatamoto need not feel saintly concern merely because Amiko later suffers; demanding that level of ethical impartiality from an individual is unrealistic. She distinguishes this from an ethical standard a state might be expected to hold. This is unusually sophisticated social reasoning from a narrator who simultaneously steals a phone and uses private messages coercively.

The contradiction should remain visible.

### Oikura narrator profile

> **high analytical intelligence + extreme rhetorical aggression + strong self-accusation + suspicion that often detects real structure + recurrent overfitting + growing ability to mark uncertainty + poor respect for interpersonal boundaries when she decides intervention is necessary.**

## 7.2 Kanbaru Suruga — affiliative, self-mocking, inheritance-conscious narration

Kanbaru's narration is warmer and less adversarial, but it has a different vulnerability: she readily converts relationships into inherited obligations.

The story opens with a memory problem. She cannot stabilize when Ougi became her junior/fan; the remembered origin shifts when she tries to retrieve it. She nevertheless treats the present relationship as real.

That is not evidence that memory is irrelevant. It is evidence for a V16/V19 principle:

> **uncertain origin does not automatically invalidate accumulated relation.**

Kanbaru also narrates herself through admiration. She has wanted to be like Araragi, has inherited material and questions from Tooe, and has just emerged from Rouka's collection narrative. The danger is becoming a successor defined by somebody else's unfinished project.

Her white-daydream confrontation with Tooe directly attacks that structure: `神原駿河と臥煙遠江は別人`.

Crucially, Kanbaru does not merely obey the message. She later makes the same claim in her own vocabulary. She will not become Araragi, Rouka, Tooe, or Hitagi. She must become Kanbaru.

Her narrator profile therefore differs sharply from Oikura's:

> **high affiliative orientation + self-mocking physical/material honesty + tendency to define self through admired/dead others + growing capacity to convert inheritance into chosen rather than compulsory responsibility.**

## 7.3 Ononoki Yotsugi — professional self-description under self-generated counterevidence

Ononoki is the most formally interesting narrator because the act of narration destabilizes her own ontology claims.

She says:

- she is a corpse;
- she is a tool;
- she can separate feeling from work;
- she does not have normal appetite;
- her stress and stress relief may only be `人間の振り`;
- emotional gratitude will not alter her professional treatment of Tsukihi.

Yet the narration supplies a continuous counter-record.

Ononoki:

- enjoys ice cream and dreams of future ice cream;
- becomes embarrassed at being discovered;
- dislikes Karen because she resembles Kagenui;
- seeks psychological advantage over Nadeko and is disappointed when it fails;
- calls Nadeko cute;
- fears destruction by fire;
- becomes angry at repeated errors;
- feels professional shame;
- relies on Hachikuji;
- withholds the incident from Gaen partly to conceal her own mistakes;
- wonders whether Tsukihi is influencing her judgment;
- and immediately repeats the ice-cream behavior that caused the crisis.

The right analysis is not to declare Ononoki a liar. Her terms may reflect a real ontological distinction between generated/imitated human affect and whatever an ordinary living human means by feeling. But causally and relationally, the affect is real enough to organize action.

Her narrator profile is therefore:

> **high technical confidence + professional role identification + strategic concealment + literal-minded self-objectification + strong capacity for error recognition + persistent tendency to redescribe affect as imitation even when affect has behavioral consequences.**

## 7.4 Three narrators, three forms of self-correction

The volume's narrator architecture can be summarized this way:

| Narrator | Preferred self-correction | Failure risk |
|---|---|---|
| Oikura | attack her own motive and reasoning | self-indictment becomes another total explanation |
| Kanbaru | compare herself against admired/inherited figures | selfhood becomes imitation or inheritance |
| Ononoki | classify error professionally and update a model | professional language launders attachment/bias |

This triptych itself is evidence for the V2 method. There is no universal narrator pathology.

# 8. `そだちフィアスコ` close reading

## 8.1 Hatred as continuity, not romance (§001)

Oikura begins by making hatred of Araragi almost ontological. The rhetoric is deliberately excessive: hatred is sun-sized, identity-bearing, physically nauseating, and a mechanism by which she survives other adversity. She fears its disappearance because Araragi has become an internal reference point.

Three things should be separated.

First, the hatred is real. V20 gives no warrant for treating it as a coy romantic mask.

Second, the hatred is relationally productive. Oikura imagines Araragi's happiness as a challenge she must exceed. He has become one of the people against whom she measures whether she is moving.

Third, because the hatred has become identity infrastructure, **losing hostility can itself feel like self-loss**. That is a subtler problem than reconciliation. A person may no longer need an old defense and still be frightened by what its disappearance would remove.

The opening therefore revises the simple binary “hate versus forgive.” Oikura can continue hating Araragi while using the relation to push herself toward a life she wants.

## 8.2 A home chosen for embarrassingly ordinary reasons (§002)

Oikura's new situation is one of the most concrete social changes in the series so far.

She has no parents. She is offered support by the Hakobe couple. Public assistance exists. Scholarships make university imaginable. She can attend a public school. None of this is supernatural rescue.

Her reason for accepting residence is equally concrete:

`家に住みたかった。`

She wanted to live in a home.

V16 established that a secure child can fail to recognize an abusive house as a home. V20 gives the inverse movement: Oikura, whose own home history was catastrophic, chooses to inhabit a household even though accepting kindness remains psychologically humiliating to her.

The choice is not pure gratitude. It is partly nostalgia for the rare memories in which somebody else's house functioned as shelter, partly stubborn competition with Araragi, partly a desire for ordinary life.

That mixed motive is exactly why it matters.

> **Receiving care does not need to become a morally pure feeling before it can become a real life structure.**

Her recognition of social safety nets is likewise important. She says such systems had been present but she had not seen—or had refused to see—them. The novel does not imply that a welfare system could have magically solved her childhood abuse. It does show that recovery can involve learning to perceive institutional supports that a self-isolating survival strategy had rendered unusable.

## 8.3 `目指せ普通`: ordinary life as deliberate practice (§§002–004)

Oikura chooses `目指せ普通` as her transfer strategy.

This is almost the opposite of V19's `無印` Araragi fantasy. She does not believe a pure ordinary self will emerge automatically. She treats ordinary social life as a practice requiring preparation:

- design a harmless introduction;
- avoid eccentric self-display;
- learn a new school's culture;
- make several friends;
- do not trigger conflict;
- graduate quietly.

The first self-introduction goes poorly anyway.

That failure matters because Oikura immediately confronts a truth that V19 had already formalized:

> changing town, house, school, and uniform does not automatically change the person.

Her surroundings genuinely matter, but social reset is not identity reset.

`普通` also proves relational and local rather than universal. Shishikurazaki High has different gender grouping, dress norms, smartphone policy, social hierarchy, and communication expectations from Naoetsu. Oikura cannot transfer a “normal high school” model unchanged across institutions.

This is one of V20's first systemic claims:

> **Normality is an institution-specific competence, not a neutral human baseline.**

## 8.4 Selecting Amiko: friendship as optimization problem (§§005–007)

Oikura decides she needs a female friend and chooses Kuse Amiko (`忽瀬亜美子`) because Amiko appears isolated.

She is honest about the ugliness in the decision. An isolated girl has fewer alternatives and may therefore be easier to approach. Oikura's compassion is contaminated by strategy.

But “contaminated” should not become “fake.” Oikura genuinely wants a friend. She also genuinely recognizes isolation. Her error is treating another person's vulnerability as a useful constraint in her own social plan.

Amiko refuses the approach repeatedly.

Oikura's response is revealing: she keeps trying because she has converted friendship into a task with an expected correct solution. The other person's refusal initially appears as information to be decoded rather than a preference to be accepted.

This anticipates the ethical problem of the story's climax. Oikura is learning to acknowledge other people as agents, but when she believes a social system is producing harm, she still becomes dangerously willing to override ordinary boundaries.

## 8.5 The rooftop reversal: outsider becomes recipient of local knowledge (§§008–010)

Amiko finally corners Oikura on the roof. Oikura expects intimidation. Instead Amiko gives her an extraordinary amount of information about the class.

The scene reverses Oikura's planned relation.

She had approached Amiko as the stronger newcomer extending a hand to an isolated target. Amiko instead becomes the person orienting the foreigner to a social landscape Oikura does not understand.

The dialect barrier sharpens the point. Oikura discovers that even “correct language” is a local convention. She is accustomed to treating precision as protection; now comprehension itself requires translation of tone, face, and context.

Amiko's detailed knowledge also destabilizes the “isolated delinquent” image. A person can be socially excluded while remaining highly informed about a group. Isolation is a relationship state, not epistemic absence.

## 8.6 The missing desk and the history already underway (§§009–012)

Oikura notices that the number of desks does not match her assumed class total. Someone is absent.

This small material discrepancy opens the hidden prehistory of the classroom. Again the key V2 principle is **origin without sovereignty**. Oikura does not enter a blank new school. The class already contains unfinished relations before she arrives.

On her second day Amiko is absent. The rest of the class suddenly showers Oikura with attention.

Oikura is initially tempted to enjoy it. Then suspicion returns. Over several days she infers that the hospitality is partly guilt management: the class has recently participated in the isolation of Amiko and another student, and attention to the newcomer offers a low-cost way to experience themselves as kind.

This is a sharp social analogue to earlier oddity cases. No monster is required. A group can externalize discomfort into a new relational script.

But V20 refuses total cynicism. Oikura admits that some of the kindness may be genuine. Social motive can be mixed.

## 8.7 Norika interrogation: correct suspicion does not legitimate method (§§013–014)

Oikura targets Kyakufuji Norika (`客藤乃理香`) as a likely source of information and forces a private conversation.

She knows the method is coercive. She does it anyway.

This is essential for the ethics of the arc. Oikura's later deductions are substantially correct, but correctness does not retroactively cleanse interrogation.

The story thereby restates a V16 lesson in a new register:

> **Having a better causal model is not identical to having the right to impose one's chosen disclosure method.**

Norika explains the basic structure. Amiko had been socially powerful; Suzubayashi Riri (`珠洲林リリ`) functioned as a more conventional organizer/leader; Hatamoto Shou (`旗本肖`), Amiko's childhood friend, depended heavily on Amiko's relation for her place in the class. A harsh public conflict between Amiko and Hatamoto becomes the trigger through which accumulated resentment against Amiko can be mobilized. Hatamoto stops attending. The class turns against Amiko. Amiko then also stops attending.

The causal model is distributed:

- Amiko commits a real interpersonal wrong;
- Hatamoto is hurt;
- prior class resentment supplies tinder;
- Riri's faction benefits from reorganization;
- bystanders participate or fail to resist;
- guilt then seeks a new outlet.

There is no single villain whose removal restores normality.

## 8.8 Truancy and moral jurisdiction (§§015–018)

Oikura tracks Amiko to a cram school by using school/social information and illicitly enters with an old Naoetsu student ID.

The sequence is comic, but it has ethical force. Oikura is repeatedly willing to cross procedural boundaries when she believes the official route cannot reach the person.

At the fast-food conversation, however, she refuses a conventional moral judgment: Amiko's school absence is not itself the central wrong.

Oikura is uniquely positioned to say this. She spent long periods outside school. She knows that “return to attendance” is not a complete moral target. The wrongs concern relationships, coercion, isolation, and unresolved conflict—not the symbolic offense of missing class.

This is a mature revision of institutional normality. Oikura wants to attend school, but she does not convert attendance into universal virtue.

Amiko appears to want reassurance that everything was not really her fault. Oikura refuses to provide easy absolution. Again the result is ethically mixed. Refusing false consolation preserves truth; the harshness also limits relational repair.

## 8.9 Victim/perpetrator circulation (§§017–019)

Oikura's most important social insight appears when she refuses a fairy-tale punishment structure.

The class contains people who were hurt and people who hurt others, often the same people at different moments. Amiko's prior dominance does not invalidate her current exclusion. Hatamoto's victimization does not create a duty to forgive Amiko. Riri's opposition to Amiko may contain legitimate grievance while also enabling opportunistic factional control.

Oikura says, in effect, that people know being a victim can be easier than occupying the morally ambiguous position of somebody who has also harmed others:

`被害者でいるほうがよっぽど楽だと、みんなみんな、本当は知ってるから。`

This must not be misread as victim-blaming. Oikura is not saying victimhood is pleasant or fake. She is naming the **narrative simplicity** of a role that seems to absolve one from examining one's own later agency.

That directly strengthens the V2 rule first needed for Nadeko and Kanbaru:

> **Real victimhood in one causal relation does not erase agency or responsibility in another.**

## 8.10 Game center, phone theft, and forced reconciliation (§§020–022)

Oikura accidentally sees Hatamoto socializing happily with Riri's group outside school. She interprets the scene as evidence that the class story is more managed than it appeared.

She remains appropriately uncertain: as a newcomer, she cannot know the full truth.

Then she acts anyway.

Oikura steals Riri's smartphone, guesses the password from birthday information Amiko had supplied, accesses private communication, and uses what she finds to force an immediate confrontation/reconciliation sequence.

The result is operationally successful. Amiko and Hatamoto return to school. Riri and Amiko reach an uneasy new balance. The class is no longer missing its members.

But the method is not thereby legitimized.

This is one of the clearest V20 tests of outcome bias:

> **A beneficial result is evidence of causal efficacy, not proof that privacy invasion, blackmail, or forced timing was necessary or justified.**

Oikura pays a social cost. Her own position becomes worse. She remains isolated and distrusted.

That cost does not make her a martyr, either. She explicitly rejects a self-sacrifice narrative. Some of the cost is simply the foreseeable consequence of how she chose to intervene.

## 8.11 `阿良々木派`: influence without identity surrender (§022)

When Riri asks what side or value system Oikura represents, Oikura blurts:

`阿良々木派よ。`

This is perhaps the most important relationship line in the story precisely because Araragi is absent.

Oikura has not forgiven him. She has not become his disciple. She even imagines that he might have handled the conflict more smoothly.

What she has inherited is narrower: a willingness to interfere when somebody appears trapped in a damaging relational structure, combined with the belief that a person's story should not be reduced to the role the group has assigned.

The line therefore represents **influence without assimilation**.

V20's decentering is not a declaration that Araragi no longer matters. It demonstrates a more mature form of influence: other people can carry, reject, alter, and surpass parts of what they received from him while he is not present to author the result.

## 8.12 The smartphone and the alleged father (§022)

The Hakobe couple give Oikura a smartphone because they worry about her isolation. She is genuinely pleased even though it initially contains only the household number.

That object condenses several V20 themes:

- social inclusion;
- care that Oikura did not earn through optimization;
- communication infrastructure;
- the gap between possessing a tool and possessing relationships;
- and the fact that receiving help can precede being ready to use it fully.

Then the phone delivers the story's final disruption. A drunk middle-aged man has arrived at the Hakobe home claiming to be Oikura's father.

Publication-locally, the claim is **not verified**. The correct statement is only that a man claims the role.

Oikura answers that she will return and says:

`だって私、そんなの全然、平気だから。`

The line should not be treated as proof that family trauma has been healed. It may include bravado, self-persuasion, or genuine increased capacity. What is securely established is behavioral: she turns toward the new problem rather than disappearing from it.

That is exactly the “small-change standard” V16 established.

# 9. `するがボーンヘッド` close reading

## 9.1 A relationship with an unstable origin (§001)

Kanbaru opens by admitting she cannot reliably remember when Ougi became her junior and self-proclaimed fan. Each attempt to remember seems capable of producing a different origin episode.

This is an important post-V18/V19 Ougi datum, but it should not be overinterpreted.

Publication-locally we can say:

- Ougi is present in a male-coded school relation to Kanbaru;
- Kanbaru treats `扇くん` as a real junior/fan relationship;
- the origin memory is unstable;
- accumulated familiarity nevertheless exists.

We cannot yet convert this into a total theory of Ougi rewriting everyone's memories.

The more durable thematic point is familiar from V15/V16:

> **A relation may be real even when its origin story is unstable.**

## 9.2 The room Araragi had been managing (§§001–003)

Kanbaru's initial conflict is embarrassingly mundane. She criticizes Araragi's cleaning. He stops doing it. She discovers that she is terrible at maintaining her own room.

The comedy exposes a relationship dependency that earlier narration could make charming and invisible. For roughly a year, Araragi's repeated labor had become environmental infrastructure.

Kanbaru's task is not to reject help as weakness. It is to recognize that having another person maintain her space does not automatically teach her how to do so herself.

The room is therefore both literal clutter and relational residue.

Kanbaru wants to clean it partly to prove capability and partly to reconcile with Araragi. But as objects emerge, she repeatedly stops to assign them memory and value. She finds discarding difficult because objects have become archives of life.

Ougi's interpretation—that throwing away objects can feel like declaring parts of one's own life worthless—is a useful `CD` claim, not universal psychology. It fits Kanbaru unusually well because her family history is materially concentrated in inherited spaces and objects.

## 9.3 The mummified hand returns (§003)

The appearance of a left monkey hand is not simply “the Rainy Devil is back.”

V10 had already ended Kanbaru's direct bodily possession and disposed of the collected parts known at that time. V20 therefore opens a different problem:

> **resolving one's own embodiment does not remove distributed material from the world.**

Kanbaru's first reaction is confusion precisely because she believed the previous incident had produced a much more complete disposal.

The hand then punctures a fusuma and retrieves a hidden letter.

The scene literalizes the relationship among body, house, and inheritance. A body-part legacy penetrates household architecture to reveal a message from a dead parent.

## 9.4 Tooe's body-part letter (§§004–008)

The letter is written as a grotesque sequence of bodily instructions:

- mix brain;
- gather hair;
- bind throat;
- collect eyes/ears;
- knead flesh;
- bundle bones;
- layer skin;
- connect vessels;
- and so on.

This is not merely macabre decoration. It makes Tooe's legacy look like the construction of a body from fragments—the same structural problem that the mummified Rainy Devil presents.

A separated katakana line, `ニゴリナキシカクヲヨメ`, becomes the key.

Kanbaru and Ougi test multiple readings of `シカク`: blind spot, square, qualification, assassin, and finally **four strokes**. `ニゴリナキ` excludes dakuten/handakuten-added candidates. The relevant four-stroke katakana are read as `ホ` and `ネ`: `ホネ` / **骨**.

The instruction becomes:

`骨を束ねろ` — bundle the bones.

The “bones” turn out to be the wooden framework inside the fusuma. Once disassembled and recombined, markings become a map.

This is a textbook Monogatari linguistic/material mechanism:

> orthography → body word → architecture → physical rearrangement → geographic information.

It should not be flattened to symbolism. The wordplay is operational.

## 9.5 The temptation of inheritance (§§006–009)

Kanbaru immediately suspects that Tooe wants her to continue collecting the distributed parts.

That suspicion is psychologically reasonable because V10 already made maternal legacy central to Kanbaru's relation to the Rainy Devil. But V20 resists the succession story.

Kanbaru repeatedly says she does not intend to inherit Rouka's collection or perform a daughter's cleanup duty for Tooe.

This matters because heroic narratives often turn unfinished parental work into a child's destiny. `するがボーンヘッド` explicitly says that biological/inherited relation is insufficient.

The moral question becomes:

> **If Kanbaru chooses the same action for a different reason, is it still inheritance?**

V20's answer is no, or at least not simply.

Action can be continuous while authorship changes.

## 9.6 The white-daydream Tooe (§010)

While Ougi is away speaking with Araragi, Kanbaru experiences a `白昼夢` in which Tooe appears using Rouka's body/appearance.

This is high-value psychological and thematic evidence but requires strict source classification.

It is not established as the literal ghost of Gaen Tooe. Kanbaru herself frames the experience as a daydream. The speech may reflect memory, internalized parental discourse, supernatural contact, or a deliberately unresolved combination.

The voice tells Kanbaru two things of lasting importance.

First:

`神原駿河と臥煙遠江は別人なのだ。`

Second:

`薬になれなきゃ毒になれ。でなきゃあんたはただの水だ。`

The first statement rejects compulsory inheritance.

The second is more dangerous. It prizes efficacy/intensity over harmless neutrality. Kanbaru should not automatically adopt it as series ethics. Indeed, the whole V2 project has repeatedly shown that “being effective” is not sufficient moral justification.

The speech also tells Kaiki, indirectly, to stop wandering after Tooe's back. That is best stored as part of Kanbaru's Tooe-model unless later texts independently confirm it.

## 9.7 Fragment multiplication: local mechanism, uncertain authority (§010)

The daydream provides the volume's largest oddity-scale claim.

Tooe says the mummified parts feed on wishes and unhappiness and **grow** when wishes are granted. Kanbaru remembers direct evidence from her own childhood: the hand had extended farther up the arm after a granted wish.

The reasoning becomes alarming. If distributed fragments grant wishes, they may produce more material; if those fragments break apart and spread, the system can propagate.

Two evidence layers must remain separate:

- **TF/NR:** Kanbaru remembers her own fragment growing after wish fulfillment.
- **CD/SI:** Tooe-daydream claims the distributed mummy proliferates in a cancer/pathogen-like manner and may multiply the population-scale hazard.

The physical recovery of another fragment in the coda strengthens the broader proposition that V10 did not exhaust the material system. It does not prove every numeric extrapolation in the daydream.

The safest V20 formulation is:

> **Rainy Devil material is demonstrably more distributed than V10's known collection, and wish-linked growth provides a plausible propagation mechanism. Exact population, rate, and global distribution remain unresolved.**

## 9.8 Araragi and Hanekawa offstage: learning when not to intervene (§011)

Ougi's phone call with Araragi contains a small but important update. Hanekawa is apparently in difficulty abroad. Araragi is concerned, but the current judgment is that direct involvement may obstruct rather than help.

The exact foreign situation is not explained here and should remain OPEN.

What matters for Araragi's V18/V19 trajectory is behavioral:

> **He can now recognize a situation in which caring does not authorize immediate insertion.**

That is stronger evidence for non-possession than a theoretical slogan because Araragi is offstage and receives no heroic reward for restraint.

## 9.9 From inherited burden to chosen preventive project (§§012–014)

After the cipher is solved, Kanbaru decides to pursue the mapped fragment.

Her final speech is one of the most important post-Final-Season self-authorship declarations so far.

She wanted to become like Araragi: kind, able to help. Then she rejects the imitation:

`どれだけ憧れても、私は阿良々木暦じゃない。`

She likewise refuses to become Rouka, Tooe, or Hitagi.

`私は私にならないと。`

Only after that identity separation does she define the action she wants.

If Araragi is a fool who fights for people within reach, Kanbaru will fight for people she does not know—people somewhere who may make a careless wish and hurt themselves or others.

This is not a revelation of her predestined vocation. It is a **chosen comparative ethic**.

The decision also preserves ordinary obligations. She still has entrance exams, rehabilitation, training, and a room to clean. Indeed, she chooses to reconcile with Araragi and ask for help with the room so that her effort can be redirected toward the new project.

That detail prevents a crude self-sufficiency reading.

> **Self-authorship does not require doing every task alone. It requires owning why one is delegating, receiving help, and choosing another task.**

## 9.10 `阿良々木暦を超えよう` (§014)

Kanbaru's phrase “I will surpass Araragi Koyomi” is not primarily a power ranking.

She surpasses by differentiating the scale of concern.

Araragi's ethic is intensely proximate: the girl before him, the person he can physically reach, the crisis that demands immediate action.

Kanbaru imagines a preventive ethic oriented toward unknown persons before the crisis occurs.

The strongest comparison is therefore:

| Araragi model | Kanbaru V20 model |
|---|---|
| proximate | distributed |
| response | prevention |
| identifiable person | anonymous future subject |
| bodily intervention | collection/risk reduction |
| relationally triggered | self-authored project |

This does not make Kanbaru “better.” Population-scale concern can become abstract overreach. V20 merely gives her a distinct route.

## 9.11 Coda: one fragment, seven panels, a long summer (§015)

The next day Kanbaru and Ougi retrieve a fragment from the mapped location.

It is smaller than hoped. The remaining fusuma panels remain unopened/unsolved.

The conclusion refuses completion:

`高校生活最後の夏休みは、どうやら今までで一番、長い夏になりそうだった。`

The project is now labor rather than revelation.

That is precisely what distinguishes mature responsibility from the one-climax rescue model: the problem may require repeated, boring, incomplete work.

# 10. `つきひアンドゥ` close reading

## 10.1 Surveillance report as self-characterization (§001)

Ononoki frames the story as observation report number 2761 on `阿良々木月火こと、しでの鳥`.

The grammar is deliberately depersonalizing. Tsukihi is an `対象`. Ononoki is a professional monitor. Her function, she says, is to remain capable of killing the phoenix if others become too emotionally compromised.

This is a direct specialist-ethics continuation of V06.

Kagenui's earlier category-based extermination doctrine has softened institutionally: Tsukihi is being watched rather than immediately destroyed. But the lethal option still structures the surveillance.

Ononoki's self-description—`斧乃木余接はそのための道具なのだ`—should be stored as **self-story**, not objective personhood fact.

## 10.2 Ice cream and the problem of `人間の振り` (§002)

Ononoki says she probably has no ordinary appetite. She nevertheless eats ice cream because:

- Araragi keeps supplying it;
- it tastes/feels worth doing;
- sweetness relieves stress;
- relaxation is useful during long surveillance.

She then classifies both stress and stress relief as `人間の振り`.

This creates an epistemic question V20 does not resolve:

> If an imitation has stable bodily, behavioral, relational, and motivational effects, what exactly becomes analytically useful about calling it “only” imitation?

V05/V06 already taught that fake/original does not map neatly onto real/unreal or valuable/worthless. Ononoki's own pleasure becomes another instance.

## 10.3 Tsukihi catches the doll moving (§002)

Tsukihi unexpectedly abandons her trip to school and returns home, catching Ononoki eating ice cream.

Ononoki freezes and pretends to be a doll.

Tsukihi does not accept the ordinary explanation. She tests the doll by covering it in cooking oil and preparing to light a match.

The sequence is comic and dangerous simultaneously. Ononoki correctly recognizes that fire is one of the few especially serious threats to her corpse body and that a house fire could spread to neighbors.

Her cover therefore becomes incompatible with safety. She moves.

This is another example of V20's error structure: professional role consistency becomes dangerous when conditions change.

## 10.4 The magical-girl lie (§003)

To preserve the surveillance operation, Ononoki invents an elaborate explanation: she is a justice magical girl from another dimension whose soul possesses Tsukihi's doll as a vessel.

Tsukihi enthusiastically believes her.

The lie exploits Tsukihi's attraction to justice narratives. It also requires Ononoki to make the false story operational. If she simply leaves, Tsukihi will know the doll has agency. So Ononoki decides to stage a monster battle and a departure that restores the doll to inert status.

This is the key causal transition:

> **a cover story becomes a production requirement.**

The specialist is now trapped by the fiction she authored.

## 10.5 Recruiting Nadeko: art as oddity technology (§§004–005)

Ononoki needs a controllable monster. She visits Nadeko.

Several post-V13 character facts appear incidentally but importantly:

- Nadeko remains out of school;
- she is working seriously on manga;
- Tsukihi visits her frequently;
- the old god crisis remains emotionally legible but no longer monopolizes the encounter;
- Nadeko willingly asks what she can do to help Tsukihi.

Ononoki chooses the residue of `蛞蝓豆腐`, the artificial/fake oddity Kaiki used as a treatment device around Nadeko. Ononoki says the core has long since been removed and only residue remains.

Nadeko draws a slug on manga manuscript paper. Her craft is not treated as decorative. The precision of the drawing later affects how the manifested entity appears.

During the conversation Nadeko makes a striking statement:

`世の中が甘くないことが、とても嬉しい。`

The line should not be converted into a global recovery thesis. Locally, it contrasts her prior experience of being excessively indulged/protected with the difficult reality of artistic labor. Difficulty can be welcome when it permits meaningful effort.

That is strongly compatible with V11/V13's move from passive/cute role toward authorship, but full Nadeko post-god development still belongs to later dedicated sources.

## 10.6 Staging at Rouhaku Park (§006)

Ononoki chooses Rouhaku Park rather than Kita-Shirahebi Shrine partly because she does not want Hachikuji, who is connected to Araragi, to report her activity.

That decision is a tactical error.

The fabricated ritual uses:

- the drawn paper;
- buried staging;
- salt;
- residual oddity material;
- Ononoki's magical-girl explanation;
- Tsukihi as witness/participant.

The plan assumes a weak, controllable manifestation.

Instead the slug becomes enormous.

Ononoki proposes a plausible immediate explanation: Nadeko drew with manga techniques and detailed perspective/background, so conversion into three-dimensional form may have scaled the creature unexpectedly. She also recognizes that Nadeko's drawing itself may have unusual force.

This remains mixed `CD/SI`. The exact mechanism is not fully established.

The important structural fact is firmer:

> **creative representation participates in supernatural embodiment.**

That is a major prospective route for later Nadeko specialist analysis.

## 10.7 Professional failure cascade (§006)

Ononoki initially destroys the creature with force and relaxes too soon. She explicitly identifies failure to maintain `残心` as a major mistake.

Fragments ignite. Her oil-covered body catches fire. The monster reforms/continues. Her standard techniques become less effective than expected.

This sequence is valuable because Ononoki does not suffer from ignorance in the ordinary sense. She knows many rules. The problem is model transfer:

- a familiar artificial oddity behaves at unfamiliar scale;
- a piece of art changes manifestation;
- an earlier household action creates a new fire vulnerability;
- a fabricated story adds constraints;
- local geography introduces an actor she was trying to avoid.

Professional competence therefore fails through **interaction effects** rather than simple incompetence.

## 10.8 Tsukihi saves Ononoki (§006)

When the giant slug descends to crush Ononoki, Tsukihi moves.

She pushes Ononoki clear and is pulverized herself.

Several distinctions matter.

Tsukihi does not know Ononoki is an immortal corpse. Her rescue is therefore based on ordinary perception: another person appears endangered.

Tsukihi also has no articulated survival plan. Her own immortality later prevents permanent death, but the action is not made prudent retroactively by a mechanism she does not consciously know.

Thus:

> **the rescue is genuinely other-directed and genuinely reckless at the same time.**

This is almost a mirror of early Araragi. The difference is that Tsukihi does not possess his explicit self-sacrificial ideology. She acts first.

The scene also provides anti-essentialist evidence against Araragi's old V06 temptation to treat Tsukihi's borrowed justice as absence of personhood. Whatever the source of her justice language, the relation can produce real protective action.

## 10.9 Hachikuji as local corrective actor (§006)

Ononoki reaches a point where defeat seems plausible. Hachikuji appears.

Her solution is conceptually simple. The monster began as a drawing on a sheet of paper embedded in a magical-girl staging. Hachikuji folds the paper. The manifested monster collapses accordingly.

The intervention is valuable at three levels.

First, Hachikuji sees a rule Ononoki failed to use because Ononoki became trapped inside her own production.

Second, Hachikuji's present deity role gives her a local responsibility relation to the town without turning her into a universal specialist.

Third, the incident demonstrates **redundancy** again. A professional can fail; a local actor with different knowledge can solve the immediate problem.

Hachikuji jokes through the familiar formula `失礼。一枚、嚙みました`, converting the old misnaming relation ritual into an intervention pun. The continuity is comic but also characterological: deity status does not erase Hachikuji's established voice.

## 10.10 The next-day memory gap (§007)

Tsukihi regenerates completely.

The next day she has forgotten virtually the entire incident.

Ononoki rejects the first explanation—“she forgot because dying was shocking”—and formulates the stronger one:

`死んだときのショックで忘れた` ではなく、`死んだショックを忘れた`.

The difference is causal direction.

Ononoki proposes that the phoenix mechanism resets memories whose traumatic retention would obstruct the continuing life, analogous to a self-protective cognitive repair system.

This is one of the largest metaphysical additions in V20, but it remains **Ononoki's specialist inference**. The event supports the fact of selective forgetting. The underlying mechanism is not independently demonstrated by an omniscient source.

## 10.11 Externalized residue (§007)

Ononoki then derives the disturbing social consequence.

If Tsukihi forgets catastrophic lessons while others remember their consequences, the subject may repeatedly begin again while damage accumulates around her:

`周囲にだけ、その失敗が堆積する。`

`堆積し、鬱積し──それが新たな火種となる。`

This is a much stronger risk theory than V06's “harmless immortal bird.”

But the right transition is **REVISE**, not “V06 was wrong.”

V06 correctly established that the phoenix does not behave like a conventional predatory oddity and that exterminating Tsukihi would kill a socially continuous person. V20 adds a new externality:

> a non-malicious regenerative system can still create danger if repair prevents the subject from retaining some consequences that others must carry.

Harmlessness must therefore be evaluated not only by direct attack but by **residue distribution**.

## 10.12 Ononoki withholds the report (§007)

Ononoki does not immediately recommend extermination. She chooses continued observation.

Her stated reason is scientific: the memory behavior is new and valuable enough to justify an `執行猶予` while the ecology is better understood.

She also admits a less flattering reason: reporting the incident would expose her own cascading mistakes.

Then she goes further and asks whether Tsukihi herself may be influencing the decision.

This is excellent specialist-ethics evidence because it makes bias explicit without resolving it.

> **Awareness of conflict of interest does not eliminate conflict of interest.**

The appropriate V2 status is therefore not “Ononoki becomes merciful.” It is:

- extermination recommendation deferred;
- observation continues;
- new evidence genuinely changes risk assessment;
- personal/professional embarrassment contaminates reporting;
- possible subject influence remains OPEN.

## 10.13 `アンドゥ`: reset without learning (§007)

The final joke repeats the opening. Ononoki returns to eating ice cream after Tsukihi leaves. Tsukihi turns around and comes back early again.

Ononoki ends with:

`正義の魔法少女、パート２。`

`次は死んでも、失敗しない。`

The title `Undo` therefore applies on multiple levels:

- Tsukihi's body undoes destruction;
- her memory may undo traumatic learning;
- Ononoki's cover story tries to undo exposure;
- the final scene resets the original mistake;
- professional confidence recreates itself after failure.

V20 deliberately does **not** grant Ononoki a clean learning arc.

The story's most unsettling question is whether an actor who can reset too easily can accumulate wisdom at the same rate as consequences accumulate around them.

# 11. Oddity dossiers and rule revisions

V20 contains an analytically useful asymmetry: the first story has no governing oddity, the second centers distributed inherited oddity material, and the third creates danger partly by deliberately manufacturing a supernatural scenario.

That sequence makes it especially important not to impose one supernatural theory across the whole volume.

## 11.1 `そだちフィアスコ`: no oddity required

Oikura repeatedly uses monster-like, curse-like, and disaster language, but there is no evidence that the Shishikurazaki class conflict is supernatural.

This negative evidence matters.

Many structures familiar from earlier oddity arcs occur anyway:

- role assignment;
- social rumor;
- hidden causal history;
- victims becoming agents of later harm;
- a community attempting to redistribute guilt;
- intervention that changes the immediate state but leaves residue;
- a narrator tempted to totalize a causal explanation.

Therefore:

> **Monogatari's social/psychological patterns are not evidence that every analogous conflict secretly contains an oddity.**

Oddities can materialize human/social problems, but the problems can also exist perfectly well without supernatural embodiment.

## 11.2 Rainy Devil fragments after V10

V20 reopens the material history of the Rainy Devil without undoing V10's resolution of Kanbaru's embodied condition.

### Established in V20

- a mummified left-hand fragment physically exists in Kanbaru's room;
- it is not accounted for by the set of parts Kanbaru believed had been disposed of;
- Tooe's letter/map indicates at least one additional fragment location;
- Kanbaru and Ougi retrieve another fragment at the mapped site;
- Kanbaru remembers her own fragment physically growing after a wish was granted.

### High-value but source-attributed explanation

The Tooe daydream says that fragments feed on wishes/unhappiness and proliferate through wish fulfillment, potentially producing a spreading hazard.

Because the explanation is delivered inside a self-labeled white-daydream, classify it as `CD/NR/SI`, not unqualified TF.

### V20 rule revision

V10's statement “Kanbaru is no longer carrying the devil arm” remains true.

What changes is scale:

> **personal embodiment resolution ≠ global material disposal.**

The Rainy Devil becomes a distributed-risk system rather than only a Kanbaru case.

## 11.3 Tooe's fusuma as oddity-adjacent infrastructure

The fusuma is not itself established as an oddity.

Its importance is material/linguistic:

- a mummy fragment enters/occupies the panel;
- the panel contains Tooe's hidden letter;
- the letter's body vocabulary directs attention toward “bones”;
- the wooden frame becomes a rearrangeable information carrier;
- reassembly produces a map.

This should be routed primarily to material-residue and language ledgers rather than inflated into a new supernatural entity.

## 11.4 `蛞蝓豆腐` as artificial/fake oddity residue

Ononoki describes `蛞蝓豆腐` as a weak, nonpersistent, fabricated oddity used by Kaiki in Nadeko's treatment. Its principal connection to Nadeko has already been removed.

V20 nevertheless shows residue remaining sufficient for a new manifestation when combined with art and staging.

This strengthens an older V5/V13 rule:

> **fabricated genealogy or artificial construction can have real local efficacy.**

“Fake” does not mean causally nonexistent.

## 11.5 Nadeko's drawing as manifestation amplifier

The giant slug's scale and form track Nadeko's detailed manga drawing closely enough that Ononoki explicitly treats her art as causal input.

Possible mechanisms include:

- the drawing functioning as a perceptual template;
- manga perspective/background detail altering three-dimensional scale;
- Nadeko's post-god residual relation to oddities;
- a latent mechanic of `蛞蝓豆腐` unknown to Ononoki;
- interaction among all of these.

V20 does not settle which explanation is sufficient.

**OPEN:** whether Nadeko's artistic production has a general oddity-generation capacity or only amplifies this specially prepared case.

## 11.6 Fictional role as causal constraint

Hachikuji's solution suggests that the monster remains bound to the physical paper and to the fabricated “magical girl versus monster” setup strongly enough that manipulating the source sheet can alter the manifestation.

The precise metaphysical rule is not fully explained.

What is secure is operational:

> the false story Ononoki created became part of the conditions under which the real danger could be resolved.

This is one of the clearest examples yet of **narrative fabrication becoming causal infrastructure** without requiring that the fiction's origin be true.

## 11.7 Tsukihi / `しでの鳥` — cognitive repair hypothesis

### Observable fact

After catastrophic bodily destruction and full regeneration, Tsukihi forgets a highly specific span including the doll's movement, the magical-girl story, the giant slug, and her own death.

### Ononoki's interpretation

The phoenix selectively resets memory/brain state whose persistence would interfere with continued life.

### V2 status

- selective forgetting after extreme event: **TF/NR**;
- phoenix is the cause: **strong CD/SI**;
- “all traumatic memories are automatically removed”: **OPEN / too broad**;
- “Tsukihi never learns because phoenix biology erases all consequences”: **REJECT as total-person claim**.

### New rule proposition

> **Regeneration may repair information-bearing tissue or memory state as well as gross anatomy; repair can therefore alter what future agency remembers.**

This has significant implications for responsibility and education, but later material must test threshold, selectivity, and consistency.

## 11.8 Harmlessness revised by externality

V06 largely framed the phoenix as harmless because it did not deliberately attack or prey on others.

V20 adds a new risk axis:

- direct aggression;
- catastrophic collateral behavior;
- learning/memory retention;
- residue borne by surrounding persons;
- specialist monitoring burden.

Thus “harmless” must become multidimensional.

A being can be nonpredatory and still impose risk through the way its self-repair interacts with a social environment.

# 12. Character-pressure updates

## 12.1 Oikura Sodachi — growth without personality replacement

Oikura's V20 state should become the canonical post-V16 corrective to any “reconciled/happy Oikura” shorthand.

### Preserved traits

- aggression;
- envy and inferiority;
- suspicion;
- academic confidence;
- moral rigidity;
- self-criticism;
- desire for correctness;
- difficulty receiving unearned kindness;
- fixation on Araragi.

### New capacities

- accepting a household;
- imagining higher education through social support;
- wanting friendship openly enough to pursue it;
- marking uncertainty in her own social reconstructions;
- distinguishing school attendance from moral worth;
- recognizing victim/perpetrator circulation;
- taking action without needing to believe herself morally pure;
- enduring social loss without retreating completely.

### V2 formulation

> **Oikura does not become nicer. She becomes more capable of acting while knowing that being correct, being kind, being justified, and getting a useful result are different questions.**

That is a much more convincing developmental movement than sudden warmth would have been.

## 12.2 Kanbaru Suruga — self-authorship after bodily restoration

V10 restored Kanbaru's human arm and gave her a future beyond Rouka's collection.

V20 asks what she does with that freedom.

At first, she still depends on Araragi for something as ordinary as cleaning her room. The discovery is humbling: bodily restoration and psychological insight did not automatically produce domestic competence.

Then the Rainy Devil returns not as her arm but as **worldly residue**.

Kanbaru's mature decision is to separate four possible motives:

1. daughterly inheritance from Tooe;
2. successor duty toward Rouka;
3. imitation of Araragi;
4. her own chosen concern for unknown future people.

She rejects the first three as sufficient and chooses the fourth.

This is one of the clearest demonstrations so far of the difference between **causal inheritance** and **normative authorship**.

## 12.3 Ononoki Yotsugi — the tool who narrates herself out of simple toolhood

V20 gives Ononoki the most sustained self-description yet.

Her claim to toolhood remains meaningful:

- she is constructed;
- she has a professional owner/partner relation;
- she is assigned surveillance and possible extermination work;
- her body is not human;
- her affective interface is deliberately unusual.

But “tool” cannot remain exhaustive because she demonstrates:

- preference;
- fear;
- rivalry;
- humor;
- curiosity;
- shame;
- discretion;
- self-protective concealment;
- and independent epistemic revision.

A literal instrument does not ordinarily decide whether to withhold information from its operator because the new research question is interesting.

V20 therefore strengthens the constructed-personhood model:

> **function is part of Ononoki's identity, not the limit of it.**

## 12.4 Tsukihi Araragi — impulsive continuity plus unknown self-repair

Tsukihi remains recognizably Tsukihi:

- she returns from school simply because she feels like turning around;
- reacts intensely to impossible stimuli;
- becomes excited by a justice/magical-girl story;
- inserts herself into the mission;
- protects Ononoki when danger becomes immediate.

The memory-reset hypothesis introduces new uncertainty but does not erase this continuity.

V20 also blocks a simplistic “Tsukihi is selfish chaos” reading. Her rescue of Ononoki is direct and other-oriented.

At the same time, the opening oil/fire test is reckless enough that the same story refuses to sentimentalize her.

She remains a person whose impulsiveness can be caring and dangerous.

## 12.5 Nadeko Sengoku — effort, art, and difficult sweetness

Nadeko is not a narrator in V20, so claims should remain narrower than later dedicated material will permit.

Still, several observations are strong:

- she is drawing manga seriously;
- she cares about Tsukihi;
- she can discuss her oddity history without collapsing into the old cute role;
- she welcomes a world that is not “sweet” because artistic difficulty permits meaningful effort;
- her craft can have supernatural consequences beyond Ononoki's expectation.

This strongly supports the hypothesis that art will become a principal form of Nadeko's self-authorship.

It does **not** prove that leaving school is universally therapeutic or that artistic frustration has completed her recovery.

## 12.6 Hachikuji Mayoi — deity role as local public responsibility

Hachikuji's brief intervention is disproportionately important.

She does not wait for Araragi to authorize involvement. She recognizes a dangerous event in her town, a friend/known person in danger, and a mechanism she can interrupt.

Her deity role therefore begins to look like **local supernatural civic capacity**, not merely a stable residence granted at V18.

The story still preserves boundaries: she comments that she is intruding on professional work, and she does not claim specialist omniscience.

## 12.7 Ougi — stabilized relation, unstable origin

Kanbaru's Ougi is socially real and origin-uncertain.

V19 had already established Ougi as a nonidentical critic/partner whose mismatch from Araragi is productive. V20 supplies a useful external test: Ougi can function independently with Kanbaru, solve language/material puzzles, provoke moral reflection, and participate in preventive work.

This strengthens Ougi's independent agency.

The unstable memory of how Kanbaru and Ougi met remains OPEN and should be tracked rather than normalized away.

## 12.8 Araragi Koyomi — influence after decentering

Araragi never narrates V20, yet his mature influence is visible in three different forms:

- Oikura names herself `阿良々木派` while remaining hostile to him;
- Kanbaru explicitly uses him as a model she must **not** simply imitate;
- Ononoki's daily life is materially supported by his ice-cream gifts while her professional decisions remain outside his knowledge.

This is an important structural development:

> **Araragi becomes one relation among others rather than the narrative jurisdiction through which those relations must be interpreted.**

## 12.9 Gaen Tooe — legacy as problem, not command

Tooe appears only through letter, memory, and Kanbaru's white-daydream.

Her legacy is materially powerful. It is not normatively sovereign.

The best V20 formulation is:

> **A dead parent can leave tools, dangers, riddles, and models without thereby owning the child's future response.**

Kanbaru's growth lies precisely in allowing Tooe to matter without becoming Tooe's continuation.

# 13. Relationship and address updates

## 13.1 Oikura ↔ Araragi — hatred becomes portable relation

Araragi is absent, yet Oikura talks to him internally throughout the story.

The relation now has at least four functions:

- unresolved anger;
- identity continuity;
- imagined stress regulation;
- competitive ethical reference.

The key change is that Oikura can use the relation without requiring Araragi's physical presence or reconciliation.

`阿良々木派` should therefore not be read as surrender. It is appropriation: Oikura takes one piece of his intervention ethic and makes it available to herself.

## 13.2 Oikura ↔ Hakobe couple — receiving care without sentimental trust

Oikura does not suddenly trust the couple completely. She continues to suspect that even benevolence may conceal motives.

Yet she lives with them, greets them, cares about disappointing them, accepts a smartphone, and imagines university from within the support structure they help make possible.

This is important relationally because **trust can begin behaviorally before emotional certainty arrives**.

## 13.3 Oikura ↔ Amiko — attempted friendship becomes antagonistic recognition

Oikura chooses Amiko as a friend target partly because of isolation. Amiko rejects her, then becomes her guide to class structure.

They do not end as conventional friends.

What develops is a rough recognition between two socially difficult girls who refuse each other's preferred consolations.

The relationship matters because it is not organized around sweetness. V20 allows affiliation to exist as information exchange, challenge, irritation, and reluctant mutual acknowledgment.

## 13.4 Oikura ↔ Riri / Hatamoto / Norika — intervention without belonging

Oikura enters a social system in which she has almost no prior stake and changes it dramatically.

She does not earn a stable factional home afterward.

That outcome distinguishes **impact** from **belonging**. A person can change a group without becoming accepted by it.

## 13.5 Kanbaru ↔ Araragi — dependence becomes differentiated reciprocity

The cleaning dispute initially makes Kanbaru look childish: she has allowed Araragi's labor to become expected service.

By the end she can both apologize/ask for renewed help and choose work that Araragi cannot do for her.

This is a more mature dependence than either “Araragi should keep doing everything” or “Kanbaru must prove independence by refusing all help.”

## 13.6 Kanbaru ↔ Ougi — fan/junior relation becomes investigative partnership

Ougi calls themself Kanbaru's fan and acts with exaggerated deference while also provoking, teasing, solving, and nudging.

Kanbaru uses `扇くん`; Ougi is treated as a male-coded junior in this story.

The uncertain origin does not prevent practical cooperation.

The relation also demonstrates that Ougi's post-V18 personhood is not confined to Araragi's self-critical dyad.

## 13.7 Kanbaru ↔ Tooe — daughterhood without succession contract

Kanbaru can hear a maternal voice and reject the obligation she thinks it implies.

This is non-possession applied across generations.

Tooe's death and legacy do not grant her permanent jurisdiction over Kanbaru's vocation.

## 13.8 Ononoki ↔ Tsukihi — observer and target become reciprocal risk

Ononoki's official relation is surveillance target → specialist monitor.

V20 complicates this substantially:

- Tsukihi discovers Ononoki's agency;
- Ononoki deceives Tsukihi;
- Tsukihi insists on participating;
- Tsukihi saves Ononoki bodily;
- Ononoki continues observing Tsukihi and withholds the event from superiors.

The relationship has therefore become causally reciprocal even if Ononoki refuses to call it emotionally reciprocal.

That is an important difference.

## 13.9 Ononoki ↔ Nadeko — professional request meets voluntary help

Ononoki initially approaches Nadeko instrumentally and even tries to exploit psychological leverage.

Nadeko's willingness to help makes much of that strategy unnecessary.

The relation is revealing for both:

- Ononoki learns that manipulation is not always required to obtain cooperation;
- Nadeko demonstrates voluntary usefulness without reverting to passive compliance.

## 13.10 Ononoki ↔ Hachikuji — local actor corrects specialist

Hachikuji saves Ononoki after Ononoki's professional plan fails.

No hierarchy is established in which deity automatically outranks specialist. The value comes from **different situated knowledge**.

This strengthens V17's redundancy principle: robust care systems benefit from actors whose models are not identical.

## 13.11 Nadeko ↔ Tsukihi — ordinary friendship survives oddity history

Ononoki observes that Tsukihi has been visiting Nadeko frequently after Nadeko came down from the mountain.

This is important because their relation exists outside Araragi's mediation and outside the god crisis itself.

Tsukihi's friendship becomes part of Nadeko's post-crisis social support without being framed as formal treatment.

# 14. Rescue, intervention, consent, and ethical evaluation

V20 is unusually useful for ethics because none of its major successful interventions is clean.

## 14.1 Oikura's class intervention — effective, invasive, non-exonerating

Oikura's actions contribute causally to getting absent students back into the same classroom and destabilizing an exclusionary equilibrium.

That is a real benefit.

She also:

- interrogates Norika aggressively;
- follows Amiko to a private cram-school environment;
- uses misleading credentials;
- steals Riri's phone;
- guesses the password from personal data;
- reads private communications;
- and forces a confrontation on Oikura's preferred timetable.

The mature judgment should therefore be:

> **causally effective intervention with serious autonomy/privacy violations and high outcome dependence.**

If the result had gone worse, the method would not have become a different method. V2 should resist judging it solely through success.

## 14.2 Hatamoto's refusal rights

A crucial restraint appears in Oikura's thinking: Hatamoto is not obliged to forgive Amiko merely because Amiko is now suffering.

That preserves a V17 rule.

Recognition and apology can be ethically required in some relationships without creating reciprocal relationship entitlement.

A harmed person retains the right not to renew closeness.

## 14.3 Kanbaru's anonymous prevention — morally attractive, potentially limitless

Kanbaru's decision to search for fragments is one of V20's strongest positive developments. It is proactive, not glory-seeking, and oriented toward people she may never meet.

But the very anonymity of the beneficiary creates a potential hazard.

If “some unknown person somewhere might be hurt” is enough to create an unlimited personal duty, Kanbaru could reproduce Araragi's old self-erasure at a larger scale.

The current evidence keeps the project healthier for three reasons:

- she explicitly authors the choice;
- Ougi participates;
- other specialists are already active;
- she retains ordinary life goals rather than declaring them morally expendable.

Later sources should test these safeguards.

## 14.4 Araragi's non-intervention in Hanekawa's foreign trouble

V20 provides a useful negative case.

Araragi apparently knows Hanekawa is in difficulty and still accepts that intervention may presently obstruct rather than help.

This is precisely the kind of restraint earlier Araragi struggled to practice.

The scene supports a mature formulation:

> **care includes the capacity to remain available without converting every known difficulty into one's own mission.**

## 14.5 Ononoki's magical-girl deception — cover protection creates risk

Ononoki lies to protect a surveillance operation.

The deception may be understandable professionally. It nevertheless creates the conditions for the later monster crisis.

The ethical problem is not simply “lying is bad.” It is that the lie forces Ononoki to escalate commitment to a false scenario until other people are physically endangered.

This resembles sunk-cost escalation:

> **once maintaining the cover becomes the goal, new interventions are judged by whether they preserve the cover rather than whether the original observation mission still justifies the added risk.**

## 14.6 Tsukihi's rescue — good motive, poor risk model

Tsukihi saves Ononoki without knowing the relevant ontology.

Her action is caring and causally effective in moving Ononoki clear. It also gets Tsukihi killed/pulverized.

The story therefore refuses the inference:

> noble intention → proportionate method.

This is especially important because early Araragi frequently made the same conversion.

## 14.7 Hachikuji's intervention — low-force rule recognition

Hachikuji's solution is notably non-escalatory. Rather than overpower the giant slug directly, she changes the source representation on which its manifestation depends.

This resembles Oshino's earlier preference for rule-aware minimal intervention.

Yet Hachikuji is not merely copying Oshino. Her action comes from local observation and relation.

## 14.8 Ononoki's non-report — legitimate epistemic caution plus conflict of interest

Withholding the incident from Gaen may be justified temporarily because:

- the new memory behavior changes risk classification;
- immediate extermination would destroy an ongoing person;
- more observation could materially improve the model.

It may also be unjustified because:

- Ononoki wants to hide her own failures;
- she has become embedded in the household;
- she wonders whether Tsukihi is influencing her judgment.

The correct status is **OPEN ethical conflict**, not exoneration.

# 15. Specialist ethics and knowledge

## 15.1 V20 widens the specialist ecology

Earlier seasons introduced distinct specialist philosophies:

- Oshino: negotiated balance, agency, anti-scapegoat distribution;
- Kaiki: fraud, narrative manipulation, claim-specific competence;
- Kagenui: categorical prevention/extermination toward immortal oddities;
- Gaen Izuko: system-level coordination and high-information intervention;
- Ononoki: constructed combat/tool role inside that network.

V20 begins to show what happens when specialist practice becomes **maintenance rather than episode climax**.

Ononoki is not called in after a crisis. She has been embedded in a household for months, collecting observations. Kanbaru discovers that specialists may already be searching for distributed Rainy Devil material. Hachikuji's deity role supplies local monitoring/intervention capacity. The ecology is beginning to resemble a network.

## 15.2 Surveillance is an intervention even when “nothing happens”

Ononoki initially frames observation as passive.

It is not.

Her presence changes the household:

- Araragi feeds her ice cream;
- she occupies Tsukihi's room as an object;
- Karen's room change affects surveillance convenience;
- the possibility of future extermination is built into the assignment;
- concealment requires repeated performance.

V15's “non-event causality” applies here. Months in which Tsukihi does nothing alarming are still institutionally produced data.

## 15.3 Ononoki's failure is a model-governance failure

Ononoki knows more supernatural technique than almost anyone else present. She still creates the crisis.

This is not evidence that expertise is worthless. It is evidence that expertise needs:

- model revision;
- reporting discipline;
- external review;
- awareness of interaction effects;
- and safeguards against concealment of error.

The story is therefore unexpectedly compatible with institutional safety analysis.

A competent professional can be dangerous when they are also the sole author of the incident report.

## 15.4 Hachikuji as non-specialist corrective capacity

Hachikuji's intervention is a useful counterexample to professional monopoly.

She has oddity experience and deity status, but she is not established as belonging to the specialist school. Her different relation to place and story lets her see the paper-source solution.

V20 therefore strengthens:

> **distributed competence can outperform centralized expertise when actors possess different models of the same event.**

## 15.5 Gaen Tooe as epistemic object rather than authority

Kanbaru's story contains a letter from Tooe and a daydream version of Tooe.

Neither should be converted into “Tooe explains the system.”

The letter is real material evidence. The daydream is Kanbaru's narrated encounter. Some claims are strongly supported by other facts; others remain interpretive.

This distinction is especially important because Tooe's charisma as a dead parent can tempt both Kanbaru and the reader to grant her speech more authority than the evidence warrants.

## 15.6 Specialist duty versus ordinary life

Kanbaru's decision also raises a prospective vocational question. She is not a formal specialist, yet she chooses a task that resembles specialist preventive work.

The story does not require her to abandon:

- university;
- basketball;
- friendships;
- ordinary domestic dependence.

This suggests a more sustainable model than total vocational possession by an inherited role.

Whether that balance holds is a future question.

# 16. Japanese voice, address, and linguistic characterization

V20's narrator shift makes Japanese-language analysis unusually important because voice is now direct character evidence rather than filtered through Araragi.

## 16.1 Oikura: `私`, overprecision, insult, and self-prosecution

Oikura's voice combines:

- formal/educated syntax;
- mathematical and proof vocabulary;
- aggressive hyperbole;
- obsessive parenthetical correction;
- self-directed insult;
- invented categories and mock-bureaucratic analysis;
- constant distinction between what she knows and what she suspects.

She often produces a cruel interpretation, then adds the caveat herself.

This is not indecision. It is a rhetorical habit in which every proposition is immediately cross-examined.

The voice therefore preserves the V16 Oikura while adding something Araragi's narration could not fully show: **her hostility is also her method of epistemic quality control**.

That method is useful and exhausting.

## 16.2 `目指せ普通`

Literally, “aim for ordinary/normal.”

The phrase is aspirational rather than descriptive. Oikura does not claim she has become ordinary.

Its force comes from making `普通` a chosen project rather than a natural state.

Translation should avoid making it sound like bland conformity alone. For Oikura, ordinary life means access to:

- school attendance;
- friends;
- a household;
- future education;
- social participation without catastrophe.

## 16.3 `阿良々木派`

`派` can indicate faction, school, camp, tendency, or side.

Oikura's `阿良々木派よ` is deliberately comic because she has spent the story reiterating hatred of him.

“I'm on Araragi's side” is too weak if it erases factional irony; “I belong to the Araragi school” can sound too doctrinal. The analytical point is that Oikura claims a **value-lineage/faction label without claiming emotional allegiance**.

## 16.4 Oikura and `正しい`

`正しい` appears not merely as moral praise but as a cognitive obsession.

Oikura repeatedly asks for:

- correct action;
- correct route;
- correct language;
- correct interpretation;
- correct proof.

The volume's deepest Oikura line may therefore be:

`間違おうと思って間違えたことなんてない。`

Nobody plans to be wrong while acting under a model they believe correct.

That is epistemically stronger than a generic confession of imperfection.

## 16.5 Kanbaru's relational honorifics

Kanbaru retains `阿良々木先輩`, preserving the senior/junior relation even after Araragi graduates.

Ougi is `扇くん`, and the narration treats Ougi in male-coded terms in this story.

The relation remains recognizably Kanbaru's: admiration, teasing tolerance, physical/direct language, and willingness to be provoked.

## 16.6 `ニゴリナキシカクヲヨメ`

The cipher depends on Japanese orthography and therefore requires explanatory translation rather than simple substitution.

Interpretive route:

1. `ニゴリナキ` → “without voicing marks / without murkiness”;
2. `シカク` → not merely `死角` (blind spot), `四角` (square), `資格` (qualification), or `刺客` (assassin), but **four strokes**;
3. four-stroke unvoiced katakana yield `ホ` and `ネ`;
4. `ホネ` → `骨`;
5. read the body-list instruction `骨を束ねろ` operationally;
6. bundle/rearrange the fusuma's wooden “bones.”

The wordplay cannot be preserved by one English equivalent. A translation note must show the orthographic route.

## 16.7 `薬になれなきゃ毒になれ。でなきゃあんたはただの水だ`

Tooe-daydream's line distinguishes medicine, poison, and mere water.

The rhetoric values active effect over neutrality. It is powerful character language, not safe universal ethics.

V2 should preserve its aggression rather than paraphrasing it into “be useful.” The point is precisely that Tooe's imagined voice is willing to call harmlessness inadequate.

## 16.8 `私は私にならないと`

Kanbaru's formulation is simple enough that translation should not overcomplicate it: “I have to become myself” / “I have to become me.”

Its importance comes from the list immediately surrounding it. She explicitly differentiates herself from Araragi, Rouka, Tooe, and Hitagi.

The phrase therefore means **self-authorship through differentiation**, not discovery of a hidden preexisting essence.

## 16.9 Ononoki's `僕` and deadpan leakage

Ononoki narrates as `僕`, consistent with her established stylization.

Characteristic elements include:

- `僕だよーん`;
- `いえーい、ぴーすぴーす`;
- intentionally flat/deadpan claims;
- mocking kinship labels such as `鬼のお兄ちゃん`, shortened to `鬼いちゃん`;
- professional report vocabulary mixed with insults and genre jokes.

This combination matters. The performance of flatness is linguistically busy and highly personalized.

## 16.10 `正義の魔法少女`

Ononoki invents “justice magical girl” as a cover story because it is legible to Tsukihi.

`正義` connects back to Fire Sisters discourse. The lie works because justice is already one of the household's available narrative scripts.

The phrase therefore becomes more than genre parody: a preexisting identity vocabulary is exploited to stabilize a false explanation.

## 16.11 `世の中が甘くないことが、とても嬉しい`

Nadeko's statement uses `甘い` in the ordinary metaphorical sense of leniency/ease while Ononoki's story is saturated with literal sweets and ice cream.

The juxtaposition is especially effective:

- Ononoki enjoys literal sweetness as stress relief;
- Nadeko is glad her chosen work is not socially/artistically “sweet.”

Do not flatten this into asceticism. Nadeko is not saying suffering is good. She is distinguishing effortful reality from indulgent enclosure.

## 16.12 `死んだショックを忘れた`

The syntactic inversion matters.

- `死んだときのショックで忘れた` = shock of dying caused forgetting.
- `死んだショックを忘れた` = the shock/death-event itself was what got forgotten.

The latter makes forgetting the repair target rather than an incidental symptom.

## 16.13 `アンドゥ`

“Undo” is better than “reset” for the title because the story repeatedly reverses a previous state without guaranteeing learning:

- exposure → cover story;
- destruction → regenerated body;
- traumatic event → erased memory;
- professional failure → restored confidence;
- opening ice-cream mistake → final repeated ice-cream mistake.

Undo is not forward development by itself.

# 17. Body, appetite, objects, and material residue

## 17.1 Oikura's body is no longer the primary evidence surface

Unlike earlier Oikura material, V20 focuses less on visible deprivation or household catastrophe and more on social positioning.

Still, body language remains intense:

- nausea and constriction when imagining Araragi;
- personal-space stress;
- fear of violent outburst;
- the discipline of wearing a new uniform;
- beginning to experiment with appearance/style after social failure.

Her body is now a site of future possibility rather than only historical evidence.

## 17.2 House and room as Oikura material repair

The Hakobe room matters because it is not metaphorical.

Oikura has:

- a place to sleep;
- adults who notice absence/isolation;
- a phone connection;
- a route to school;
- a plausible university future.

No amount of psychological insight substitutes for these material conditions.

## 17.3 Smartphone as social infrastructure and privacy weapon

V20 uses smartphones in two opposite ways.

For Oikura, receiving one from the Hakobes represents inclusion and concern.

Stealing Riri's smartphone represents privacy invasion and coercive access to hidden social structure.

The same object can therefore be:

- a safety/communication tool;
- a belonging symbol;
- a repository of private social evidence;
- an intervention weapon.

This is the kind of ordinary technology that later systemic analysis should track.

## 17.4 Kanbaru's room as accumulated life

The mess is not a symbolic abstraction. It impedes movement, conceals dangerous material, and demonstrates dependence on Araragi's labor.

Objects also produce hesitation because they carry memory.

The room therefore gives physical form to a common Monogatari problem:

> **how much can be discarded without treating the discarded past as worthless?**

V19 answered that one cannot carry every regret. V20 applies the same burden limit to literal possessions.

## 17.5 Fusuma bones and parental residue

The wooden frame of the fusuma becomes `骨` through Tooe's code. The house itself therefore contains an inherited body puzzle.

This is not merely visual symbolism; physical disassembly is required to reveal the map.

The material household is archive.

## 17.6 Mummified devil parts as proliferating matter

The Rainy Devil has moved from Kanbaru's body into environmental distribution.

Its danger is materially portable:

- fragments can be stored;
- lost;
- inherited;
- hidden;
- potentially grown through use;
- recollected.

This is a different supernatural scale from possession.

## 17.7 Ononoki's appetite and ice cream

Ononoki's ice-cream consumption is a major personhood detail because she herself contests what it means.

She claims no normal appetite yet:

- chooses flavors;
- consumes melted ice cream even after extinguishing a match;
- imagines future ice cream abroad;
- uses sweetness for stress relief;
- repeats consumption at the end.

The body does not need a human metabolic need for appetite-like practice to become identity-bearing.

## 17.8 Oil and fire

Tsukihi's cooking oil creates a persistent material vulnerability that later transforms a manageable combat situation into potentially fatal damage for Ononoki.

This is classic residue causality:

> an earlier comic action remains physically active after the scene's social purpose is over.

## 17.9 Tsukihi's pulverized body and complete restoration

Tsukihi is physically crushed to fragments and rapidly returns without visible injury.

The spectacle is important because it separates:

- damage magnitude;
- subjective memory;
- bodily recovery;
- social witness.

The surrounding people can retain a catastrophic event whose body evidence disappears.

This is precisely why Ononoki's externality hypothesis matters.

## 17.10 Nadeko's paper as body source

A manga manuscript sheet becomes the material anchor for a giant oddity.

Paper is simultaneously:

- art object;
- representation;
- ritual component;
- monster substrate;
- intervention handle.

This is a powerful new entry for the series' material-language system.

# 18. Family, home, school, and institutional context

## 18.1 Oikura enters a social safety net

V20 is unusually explicit that recovery is partly institutional.

Oikura's future becomes thinkable because of:

- municipal/administrative assistance;
- guardianship/foster-like household support;
- public schooling;
- scholarships;
- adults who insist education can continue.

The series has often centered extraordinary individual helpers. This story adds ordinary structures that do not require a charismatic rescuer.

That is a significant scale change.

## 18.2 Shishikurazaki High is not “normal school” in the abstract

Oikura compares institutions directly.

The new school differs in:

- smartphone rules;
- gender organization;
- hierarchy formation;
- dress discipline;
- weight given to communication ability versus grades;
- weekend schedule;
- dialect/cultural environment.

The lesson is not that one institution is simply healthier. Oikura discovers a new social conflict almost immediately.

Rather:

> **institutions distribute stress differently.**

## 18.3 Class politics without supernatural governance

The Amiko/Riri/Hatamoto episode is a small political system:

- formal teacher awareness is uncertain;
- informal leaders compete;
- ostracism redistributes status;
- guilt produces performative kindness;
- digital communication carries hidden coordination;
- absence becomes both symptom and tactic.

This is important future evidence for series-level social-system analysis.

## 18.4 Kanbaru house as inherited archive

The Kanbaru home is no longer merely a site of athletic history and family absence. It contains infrastructure intentionally altered by Tooe and Kanbaru's father.

The discovery raises a long-term question: how much of Kanbaru's current environment was designed by adults whose reasons were never communicated to her?

Again, later use does not create original consent.

## 18.5 Araragi household as surveillance site

The Araragi home is now simultaneously:

- family residence;
- Shinobu/Araragi oddity household;
- Tsukihi monitoring site;
- Ononoki's embedded work environment.

Family intimacy and specialist governance occupy the same physical space.

This creates significant ethical tension because Tsukihi is unaware of the surveillance rationale.

## 18.6 Kita-Shirahebi and Rouhaku Park as local infrastructure

Ononoki avoids the shrine because Hachikuji might report her, then chooses Rouhaku Park—another highly familiar site.

That choice fails precisely because Hachikuji's local role is not confined narrowly to the shrine building.

The town's supernatural governance is becoming spatially networked rather than resident-specialist-centric.

# 19. Major themes

## 19.1 Decentering without erasing relation

Araragi's absence is not narrative annihilation.

The three narrators demonstrate that influence can remain when jurisdiction disappears.

This is a more mature relationship model than either protagonist-centrality or total independence.

## 19.2 Failure after development

V20 rejects the idea that character development should eliminate old failure modes.

Oikura still overplans.

Kanbaru still depends on others and gets pulled by inheritance.

Ononoki still overestimates professional control.

The meaningful question becomes:

> **What changes in the response to failure?**

That is a stronger longitudinal metric than “does the character still make the same kind of mistake?”

## 19.3 Ordinary life is constructed

Oikura's `普通` requires a home, school, money, social competence, institutional support, and luck.

Kanbaru's ordinary room requires maintenance labor somebody else had been providing.

Ononoki's “ordinary” months of surveillance require constant concealment.

Ordinary life is not absence of structure. It is **structure functioning well enough to become invisible**.

## 19.4 Inheritance without obedience

All three stories concern inherited scripts:

- Oikura inherits Araragi as an internal antagonist/model;
- Kanbaru inherits Tooe's objects and Rouka's unfinished material problem;
- Ononoki inherits Kagenui/Gaen specialist role expectations;
- Tsukihi inherits phoenix ontology without conscious knowledge of it.

The ethical question is who gets to decide what to do with an inheritance.

Kanbaru gives the clearest answer: origin can explain why the problem reached her without dictating what she must become.

## 19.5 Prevention and anonymous responsibility

Kanbaru's project is V20's major expansion of rescue ethics.

The series is beginning to ask how to care for people who are not narratively present yet.

That problem is qualitatively different from Araragi's face-to-face rescue compulsion and anticipates institutional/systemic forms of intervention.

## 19.6 Resilience can create externalities

Tsukihi's possible memory reset is the most conceptually new theme.

Usually resilience is treated as uncomplicated good: a body/person returns after damage.

V20 asks what happens when recovery is **too asymmetrical**.

If one participant resets while others retain consequence, the social environment becomes the memory system the subject lacks.

This is a powerful general principle extending beyond Tsukihi:

> **A capacity that protects one actor can redistribute cost rather than eliminate it.**

## 19.7 Fiction as operating system

Oikura's class narratives, Tooe's cipher, Ononoki's magical-girl lie, and Nadeko's manga drawing all show stories organizing action.

But V20 avoids saying “everything is fiction.”

Narratives matter because they change:

- what people expect;
- what roles they accept;
- what materials they manipulate;
- and, in oddity contexts, what can physically manifest.

## 19.8 Effort without guarantee

The afterword's `才能がなくても努力できる才能` becomes persuasive only because the stories refuse to guarantee success.

Oikura does not win friendship.

Kanbaru recovers only one small fragment.

Ononoki learns something and immediately repeats her triggering behavior.

Effort matters because it keeps the next action open, not because the text promises meritocratic reward.

# 20. Counterreadings and disconfirming evidence

## Counterreading A — “Oikura's hatred is really romantic love.”

**Reject.** The opening compares hatred's intensity to love; it does not reclassify the feeling. Oikura's narration consistently describes anger, disgust, dependency, competition, and wounded attachment. Romantic attraction is not established.

A stronger formulation is that **affective categories can overlap in intensity and identity function without becoming interchangeable**.

## Counterreading B — “Direct Oikura narration proves happy V19 Oikura was fake.”

**Reject.** V19 never established happy Oikura as current fact; it established an unrealized possibility/residue. V20 confirms that Oikura genuinely wants home, happiness, and greater connection while remaining abrasive and angry.

## Counterreading C — “Oikura is healed because she can say she is fine about the father.”

**Reject.** The claimant's identity is unverified, the confrontation has not happened, and `平気` may include bravado. The source supports approach behavior, not completed healing.

## Counterreading D — “The class outcome proves Oikura's invasive methods were justified.”

**Reject.** Outcome proves causal efficacy. It does not establish necessity, proportionality, consent, or absence of better alternatives.

## Counterreading E — “The Shishikurazaki class is another oddity case without a monster.”

**Reject as ontology.** It is analytically comparable to prior cases, but V20 supplies no supernatural mechanism in `そだちフィアスコ`.

## Counterreading F — “Kanbaru accepts her mother's mission.”

**Reject.** Kanbaru repeatedly refuses inherited duty and explicitly distinguishes herself from Tooe. She later chooses a superficially similar action for her own stated reason.

## Counterreading G — “The Tooe daydream is a literal ghost and therefore objective exposition.”

**Reject as established fact.** Kanbaru labels the episode a white-daydream. Its claims can be corroborated individually, but the encounter itself does not receive omniscient status.

## Counterreading H — “Rainy Devil fragments are now proven to be infinitely multiplying worldwide at exactly triple rate.”

**Downgrade.** Kanbaru's remembered wish-linked growth is direct narrator evidence. Tooe-daydream's cancer/pathogen model is high-value explanation/inference. Exact count, geography, and reproduction rate remain open.

## Counterreading I — “Kanbaru's preventive mission is automatically more mature than Araragi's rescue ethic.”

**Reject.** Preventive scale is a meaningful innovation, but anonymous hypothetical beneficiaries can produce limitless obligation. Later evidence must test boundaries and sustainability.

## Counterreading J — “Ononoki's narration proves she is secretly human.”

**Reject.** It proves causally meaningful preference, fear, attachment, humor, shame, and self-direction. It does not settle the metaphysical category “human.”

## Counterreading K — “Ononoki's claim to be emotionless should be ignored because she obviously has emotions.”

**Reject as too simple.** Her distinction between human feeling and `人間の振り` may describe a real difference in origin or embodiment. The V2 correction is that origin does not erase behavioral/relational reality.

## Counterreading L — “Tsukihi is dangerous because she never learns.”

**Revise.** V20 supports one severe selective-memory event and Ononoki's broader hypothesis. It does not prove all learning is erased or all impulsiveness is phoenix mechanics.

## Counterreading M — “Tsukihi's immortality makes her self-sacrificial rescue safe.”

**Reject.** Tsukihi does not consciously know the relevant ontology. Survival changes outcome, not the quality of her risk information at decision time.

## Counterreading N — “Nadeko's drawing proves she is now an oddity specialist.”

**Open / premature.** Her art materially affects this manifestation and Ononoki herself raises the specialist possibility. One event is insufficient for vocational classification.

## Counterreading O — “Nadeko being happier outside school proves school was the cause and staying out is the cure.”

**Reject.** Ononoki speculates about reduced school stress, but V20 provides no controlled causal account and no final recovery verdict.

## Counterreading P — “Hachikuji has become an all-purpose professional specialist.”

**Reject.** She acts effectively as local deity/friend with relevant insight. Formal specialist status and broad expertise are not established.

## Counterreading Q — “Off Season means Araragi is no longer important.”

**Reject.** The formal achievement is subtler: he can matter without narrating or controlling. Oikura and Kanbaru explicitly define themselves partly in relation to him while making decisions he does not author.

## Counterreading R — “V20 says all people simply keep making the same mistakes forever.”

**Reject.** Repetition exists, but responses, scale, awareness, and chosen next actions change. The book rejects both perfect cure and fatalistic stasis.

## Highest-value V20 revision 1 — decentering is not disappearance

### Earlier shorthand
Araragi needs to stop being the center of other people's stories.

### V20 revision
> **The strongest evidence of decentering is not that Araragi ceases to matter. It is that his influence can be carried, contested, rejected, or exceeded by people whose actions no longer require his focalization or permission.**

## Highest-value V20 revision 2 — prevention becomes a social scale

### Earlier shorthand
Araragi must prevent emergencies before his body becomes rescue currency.

### V20 revision
> **Prevention can also mean reducing distributed risk for unknown future persons before an acute case exists. Kanbaru's chosen fragment search opens a population-oriented form of care that must itself be bounded against unlimited jurisdiction.**

## Highest-value V20 revision 3 — resilience can shift burden outward

### Earlier shorthand
Oddity resolution leaves residue.

### V20 revision
> **A regenerative system may erase or repair the affected subject's internal wound while leaving memory, danger, and consequences in the surrounding social field. Residue can therefore be produced by successful self-repair, not merely by failed cure.**

# 22. Later-material revisions of earlier V2 findings

## 22.1 V06 Tsukihi — PRESERVE + MAJOR COMPLICATION

Preserve:

- Tsukihi is not a human person replaced by an alien intruder;
- lived sibling/social continuity matters;
- phoenix ontology does not prove absence of self.

Revise:

- the body's regenerative mechanics may have cognitive consequences;
- “harmless” must include externality and learning/memory analysis;
- Araragi's V06 temptation to read all personality from ontology remains rejected.

## 22.2 V10 Kanbaru — STRENGTHEN + SCALE EXPANSION

V10's bodily restoration remains intact.

V20 shows that restoring Kanbaru's arm did not dispose of the Rainy Devil as distributed matter. Personal closure and world-level residue diverge.

Kanbaru's V10 refusal to let Rouka define her future becomes V20's positive self-authorship: she chooses a related task without becoming Rouka's successor.

## 22.3 V13 Nadeko — STRENGTHEN PROSPECTIVELY

Kaiki's intervention redirected Nadeko away from godhood and toward manga as a possible route.

V20 provides early material confirmation:

- she is practicing the craft;
- difficulty itself has value;
- art has enough material force to affect an oddity construction.

Do not yet promote this to the mature Nadeko specialist model; V22 remains the dedicated future test.

## 22.4 V14 Araragi prevention — STRENGTHEN + DECENTER

V14's prevention language was initially Araragi-centered.

V20 shows the principle traveling to another character and changing form. Kanbaru's preventive task is not “do what Araragi should have done.” It is her own response to distributed risk.

## 22.5 V14/V19 Ononoki — STRONGLY STRENGTHEN

V14 established personal feeling; V19 established editable expression and the difference between expression and affect.

V20's first-person narration now provides overwhelming evidence against the shorthand “emotionless doll,” while preserving her nonhuman/constructed identity.

## 22.6 V16 Oikura — STRONGLY STRENGTHEN

V16's small-change standard is validated.

Oikura does not reconcile into sweetness. She changes what she can do:

- live with others;
- attend school;
- imagine future education;
- seek friends;
- intervene with more explicit uncertainty;
- survive losing social approval.

## 22.7 V17 professional redundancy — STRENGTHEN

Kanbaru works with Ougi and an already-active specialist field. Ononoki is rescued by Hachikuji. No single helper owns the solution.

## 22.8 V18 self-inclusion — STRENGTHEN INDIRECTLY

Kanbaru's project is not framed as surrendering her life. She retains ordinary goals and delegates room cleaning after recognizing a different priority.

This is a healthier form of commitment than “everything else in my life is expendable because somebody may need help.”

## 22.9 V19 Oikura plurality — STRONGLY STRENGTHEN

V20 is decisive counterevidence to any authenticity reading of cheerful mirror Oikura.

She wants happiness and home without becoming temperamentally cheerful.

## 22.10 V19 Ougi independent critic — STRENGTHEN

Ougi operates as a real partner to Kanbaru, not merely an Araragi-internal function. This further supports composite independent agency after V18/V19.

## 22.11 V19 `二割増しで前進` — STRENGTHEN AS GENERAL REPAIR MODEL

All three stories preserve residue while moving forward:

- Oikura carries hatred into a new school;
- Kanbaru carries inherited material into a self-authored project;
- Ononoki carries new knowledge and old habits into another surveillance cycle.

None returns to pristine state.

# 26. Compact reusable formulations after V20

The following formulations are sufficiently stable to carry into V21 and later synthesis, while remaining revisable by later primary text.

### Off Season decentering

> **A character leaving Araragi's narration does not reveal a hidden objective self. It reveals another performed first person with different blind spots, desires, vocabularies, and authorities.**

### Oikura

> **Oikura's growth is not learning to stop hating Araragi. It is learning that hatred need not monopolize the next life she authors.**

### Ordinary life

> **In V20, “ordinary” life is not the absence of intervention. It is constructed through households, public assistance, school rules, phones, difficult friendship, routine monitoring, cleanup, and repeated maintenance.**

### Error

> **The most dangerous mistakes are usually not chosen as mistakes. `間違おうと思って間違えたことなんてない`: ethical development therefore requires corrigibility, not confidence in one's own good intentions.**

### Kanbaru

> **Inheritance supplies material, not destiny. Kanbaru can receive Tooe, Rouka, Araragi, Hitagi, the Rainy Devil, and Ougi as parts of her history while insisting `私は私にならないと`.**

### Prevention

> **Final Season taught Araragi to prevent crisis before his own body became emergency currency. V20 asks whether prevention can scale toward anonymous future people without becoming unlimited jurisdiction.**

### Ononoki

> **Constructed function and genuine affect are not opposites. Ononoki may truly be made as a tool and still acquire preferences, fears, attachments, errors, and conflicts that alter what the tool does.**

### Tsukihi

> **Regeneration is not consequence erasure. A body can return to functional baseline while residue migrates into other people, records, secrets, and environments.**

### Fiction and oddity

> **A story need not have true origins to become true enough to constrain action. V20 extends the series' fake/real problem from genealogy into operational fiction: a lie can create roles, a drawing can shape a monster, and a paper source can become a physical kill switch.**

### V20 full-volume formulation

> **『愚物語』 begins Off Season by taking Araragi out of the narrator's chair and asking whether the people previously organized around his gaze can fail, choose, and continue without being reduced either to his old descriptions or to newly privileged “true selves.” Oikura attempts ordinary life and reproduces some of the coercive certainty she is trying to escape; Kanbaru rejects inherited identity while voluntarily inheriting preventive responsibility; Ononoki's professional-tool self-story survives only by absorbing evidence of appetite, fear, attachment, technical fallibility, and conflict of interest. None is cured. All three become more legible as agents because the volume lets them make new mistakes in their own voices. Off Season therefore begins not with clean aftermath but with a harder developmental rule: a person can preserve history, accept influence, inherit residue, and still retain authorship over what they try next.**
