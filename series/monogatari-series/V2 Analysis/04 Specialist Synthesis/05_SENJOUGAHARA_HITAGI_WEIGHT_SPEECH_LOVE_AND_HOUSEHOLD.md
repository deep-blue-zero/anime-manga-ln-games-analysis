# 〈物語〉シリーズ V2 — Senjougahara Hitagi: Weight, Speech, Love, and Household

## From weaponized boundary to shared weight

## 0. Purpose and governing thesis

This document is the canonical V2 character-and-voice synthesis for **戦場ヶ原ひたぎ / Senjougahara Hitagi**, through the supplied V30 boundary and the controlled supplementary corpus.

Hitagi is easy to flatten into several familiar roles:

- traumatized girl;
- sharp-tongued girlfriend;
- "tsundere";
- jealous romantic obstacle;
- corrective partner for Araragi;
- former victim who becomes strong;
- wife at the end of a long romance.

Each description touches something real.

None is sufficient.

The strongest longitudinal formulation supported by the supplied corpus is:

> **Hitagi's development is not a movement from hostility to softness. It is a movement from controlling access as a survival requirement toward choosing where, how, and with whom weight can be shared.**

Her recovery does not erase the traits that made her initially frightening.

She remains:

- precise;
- verbally aggressive;
- status-aware;
- strategically intelligent;
- capable of withholding;
- capable of control;
- highly sensitive to relational asymmetry;
- unwilling to perform emotions merely to reassure another person.

What changes is the function of those traits.

At the beginning, speech is primarily a perimeter weapon. The demand is `沈黙と無関心`: silence and indifference. Kindness itself can be coded as hostile because attention threatens exposure.

By the end of the supplied boundary, the same person is:

- a spouse;
- a finance professional with an independent career;
- a long-distance partner;
- an adult friend of Kanbaru;
- a person who has built an independent friendship with Hanekawa;
- someone capable of using Kaiki's competence without rewriting him as good;
- someone who can negotiate with Nadeko without pretending affection;
- someone who can imagine Shinobu as family without treating family as automatic ownership.

The fundamental continuity is not "she was secretly soft all along."

It is:

> **Hitagi always cares intensely about who is allowed to define the terms of access.**

Early in the series, that produces secrecy, weapons, and deterrence.

Later, it produces:

- explicit romantic terms;
- sexual boundaries;
- truth obligations;
- chosen names;
- deliberate first-name intimacy;
- trial-based household decisions;
- direct disclosure requirements;
- a refusal to let Araragi's guilt tell her what she must feel.

The title's key term is therefore still **weight**.

In V01, weight is literal and traumatic.

Later, weight becomes relational:

- which memories to carry;
- which debts not to inherit;
- which people gain standing;
- which burdens are shareable;
- which risks cannot be assigned to someone else;
- which histories remain hers after marriage.

The mature Hitagi does not become weightless again.

She becomes better at deciding what counts as **her weight**, what can become **shared weight**, and what should never be carried merely because someone else believes she ought to.

# 1. Longitudinal state model

Hitagi's development is unusually legible if the corpus is divided by **access architecture** rather than by simple personality labels.

| State band | Access model | Dominant speech function | Relationship model | Main danger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **pre-V01 / hidden Hitagi** | minimize observation | deterrence, concealment, social barrier | relation threatens exposure | isolation becomes safety technology |
| **V01 recovered-weight Hitagi** | selective mediated trust | precise negotiation | friendship/romance as chosen future access | intensity can outrun knowledge |
| **V02–V06 protective Hitagi** | protect what is now precious | accountability + control | love creates standing | protector can become controller |
| **V08–V13 outward-relational Hitagi** | multiple independent relationships | conflict-capable care | friend/rival/client roles differentiated | strategic competence can justify secrecy |
| **V14–V18 ordinary-future Hitagi** | stabilize chosen life | practical coordination + affectionate aggression | specialness by continued choice | stability can still require explicit truth |
| **V27 retrospective Hitagi** | reinterpret past without erasing it | apology, judgment, shared-history negotiation | growth does not require self-repudiation | remorse can become another control script |
| **V30 adult Hitagi** | negotiated household/public identity | direct consultation + retained sharpness | marriage/family as shared jurisdiction | intimacy does not automatically solve disclosure |

This model blocks two common mistakes.

First, it rejects the idea that recovery means the disappearance of aggression.

Second, it rejects the opposite romanticization in which every aggressive act is automatically a harmless sign of affection.

Hitagi's sharpness can function as:

- defense;
- intimacy;
- comedy;
- retaliation;
- premise control;
- boundary clarification;
- tactical pressure.

The function must be identified from context.

# 2. Before the crab: public role, prior awareness, and social defense

## 2.1 `病弱な女の子` is a social position, not a transparent identity

Araragi's opening model of Hitagi is already a warning.

She occupies the school position of the delicate or sickly girl. The role is socially legible because her attendance, physical participation, and reserved behavior support it.

Yet the role conceals the literal abnormality: almost no physical weight.

This means Hitagi enters the series already split among:

- what the class sees;
- what Araragi thinks he knows;
- what her body is doing;
- what she actively prevents others from learning.

The assigned school role is not completely false.

It is useful cover.

That distinction matters later. Hitagi's development rarely works by exposing an earlier identity as fraudulent. More often, she moves forward by refusing to let one historically useful role retain permanent authority.

## 2.2 Hyakumonogatari revises "mutual strangers" into asymmetric prior awareness

`HYAKU-005` provides a valuable correction.

Before the V01 incident, Hitagi had noticed Araragi across earlier school years more than he noticed her.

This should not be inflated into secret romance.

The supplied evidence supports:

- prior observation;
- asymmetric awareness;
- a more socially attentive Hitagi than Araragi's early model implies.

It does **not** support:

- hidden courtship;
- destiny;
- a preexisting romantic bond.

The correction matters because it makes her opening behavior more intentional.

She is not simply an isolated girl who wakes up socially when Araragi catches her.

She has been observing a social environment while controlling her own exposure within it.

## 2.3 `本バリヤー`: reading can be deliberate anti-interaction infrastructure

Hyaku's `本バリヤー` is a small but revealing detail.

Reading is not necessarily evidence that Hitagi is oblivious to people.

It can be used as a visible **barrier** against interaction.

This fits the larger opening model:

> she does not lack social perception; she often uses social perception to reduce access.

That distinction helps explain why her verbal aggression is so calibrated.

A socially unaware person lashes out indiscriminately.

Hitagi often does the opposite.

She knows exactly what kind of discomfort, formality, threat, or absurdity will destabilize the interaction and give her control of its terms.

## 2.4 Pre-crab social prominence should not be erased by the sick-girl image

SHORT-017, through Kanbaru's retrospective perspective, adds evidence that pre-crab Hitagi could possess:

- social visibility;
- athletic competence;
- follower influence;
- active management of group retaliation.

Kanbaru's specific theory that Hitagi deliberately engineered a 10–9 free-throw result remains an interested inference rather than hard fact.

The broader correction is still important.

The near-weightless hidden schoolgirl is not the whole longitudinal Hitagi.

Illness, family catastrophe, and the crab condition contract her field of action.

They do not prove that her earlier self was naturally passive.

# 3. Weight: trauma becomes material without becoming destiny

## 3.1 The semantic field must remain plural

V01 deliberately distributes the crab case across related but non-identical terms:

- `重さ` — literal measurable weight;
- `重み` — heaviness, gravity, burden;
- `思い` — thought, feeling, attachment;
- `記憶` — memory;
- `しがらみ` — entanglement and binding ties.

Translation that reduces all of this to one English "weight" loses part of the character design.

Hitagi has not merely lost kilograms.

Her body has become a supernatural interface for a wish to lose unbearable human burden.

The literal and psychological layers coexist.

## 3.2 The past contains betrayal, not merely suffering

The case is not structured around generic sadness.

Hitagi's history includes:

- serious illness;
- a mother pulled into religious obsession;
- cult exploitation;
- attempted sexual assault;
- maternal betrayal in the crisis;
- family collapse;
- divorce;
- loss of home and economic stability.

The important psychological consequence is not simply that Hitagi is "afraid of intimacy."

Her body and family history teach a more specific lesson:

> people who claim to care can become routes through which control enters your life.

That is why "kindness" is initially so dangerous.

Attention is not neutral in her history.

Concern can become:

- interrogation;
- pity;
- religious coercion;
- family leverage;
- sexual threat;
- a demand to reveal pain for someone else's emotional satisfaction.

Her initial ethics of access are therefore intelligible even when they are extreme.

## 3.3 `沈黙と無関心`: she does not initially ask to be understood

Hitagi's demand to Araragi is not:

> please keep my secret because I am embarrassed.

It is stronger.

She asks for silence **and indifference**.

That second term matters.

Secrecy alone would still allow sympathy.

Indifference denies him the right to turn knowledge into a relationship.

The opening Hitagi wants an informational firewall.

## 3.4 `優しさも──敵対行為と看做すわよ`

Her statement that kindness can be treated as hostile is one of the most important character lines in V01.

It should not be flattened into generic "tsundere doesn't want help" coding.

The line tells us what her trauma has done to the category of benevolence.

Kindness is no longer self-authenticating.

A person saying "I want to help you" has not proven:

- that help is wanted;
- that the helper understands;
- that intervention is safe;
- that the helper will respect refusal.

That suspicion remains part of Hitagi even after she becomes capable of trust.

## 3.5 Recovery is chosen reburdening

Oshino's method is important, but Hitagi's own decision is more important.

She chooses to take back:

- feeling;
- thought/attachment;
- memory;
- weight/burden.

The series does not declare that everyone must confront every wound.

The V01 analysis explicitly preserves flight as sometimes permissible.

Hitagi's choice has force because **she** chooses it.

This is the first mature principle of her monograph:

> **healing is not the moral superiority of carrying pain. It is the restoration of authorship over whether and how the burden is carried.**

## 3.6 Recovery is embodied and ordinary

SHORT-001 grounds improvement in something wonderfully unheroic: appetite, restored weight, ordinary pleasure.

This matters because dramatic trauma analysis can make recovery too abstract.

Hitagi's body does not only become a metaphor for moral courage.

It becomes a body that can:

- eat;
- weigh something;
- enjoy something;
- participate in ordinary life.

That ordinary embodiment should be preserved beside the larger symbolism.

# 4. Speech as boundary: polished femininity carrying controlled aggression

## 4.1 Her voice is defined by contradiction of form and content

The V01 language ledger identifies a recurring cluster:

- `～わ`;
- `～のよ`;
- `～かしら`;
- `～て頂戴`;
- complete controlled sentences;
- mock-formal threats;
- `阿良々木くん`.

This matters because Hitagi's aggression is not simply blunt.

The linguistic surface can be polished, feminine, composed, even elegant.

The semantic content can be:

- humiliating;
- violent;
- sexually threatening;
- absurd;
- accusatory.

Her voice often derives force from the mismatch.

A translation that makes her merely coarse loses the character.

A translation that makes her merely refined loses the violence.

## 4.2 Feminine stylization is not passivity

Hitagi uses conventionally feminine sentence endings without surrendering conversational control.

This is analytically important because stylized femininity can be misread as ornamental genre coding.

In practice, her feminine register often helps her:

- frame the premise;
- deliver a threat without raising affect;
- force Araragi into reactive tsukkomi;
- maintain emotional distance while saying something intimate;
- make the listener decide whether the surface politeness is sincere.

The result is not a contradiction between "feminine" and "strong."

The femininity is part of the control technology.

## 4.3 Stationery and language perform the same function

The early staplers, cutters, and school supplies are memorable because they literalize what the speech already does.

Hitagi weaponizes ordinary school objects.

She also weaponizes ordinary social forms.

A polite sentence can become a blade.

A conventional address can remain intact while intimacy deepens.

A confession can arrive in English.

The character's early aesthetic is therefore built around **ordinary forms used as controlled weapons**.

## 4.4 Deadpan lowers visible affect while increasing pressure

Hyakumonogatari is especially useful because plot pressure is low.

Across repeated Araragi/Hitagi exchanges, the pair's ordinary grammar becomes clear:

> **Hitagi controls or destabilizes the premise → Araragi reacts/tsukkomis → Hitagi extends, revises, or dryly closes the frame.**

This is much more useful for reconstruction than a list of insults.

Hitagi's voice is procedural.

She often does not "win" by speaking louder.

She wins by defining what kind of conversation Araragi suddenly realizes he is having.

## 4.5 Insult can become intimacy-coded without becoming automatically harmless

Later Araragi/Hitagi exchanges use aggression as familiar play.

The project must preserve two truths.

First:

- repeated dry insult is genuinely part of their intimacy;
- Araragi expects and responds to it;
- the pattern creates rhythm, not only injury.

Second:

- comic familiarity does not retroactively make every coercive act harmless;
- the same character is capable of crossing autonomy boundaries.

This is why the monograph should never use "that's just how Hitagi shows affection" as a universal waiver.

Context determines whether aggression is:

- ritual;
- defense;
- manipulation;
- punishment;
- flirtation;
- genuine boundary violation.

# 5. Romance begins after she learns that she was not uniquely rescued

## 5.1 The romance is not a reward for saving her

One of the most important V01 relationship turns occurs when Hitagi recognizes that Araragi's kindness was not uniquely directed at her.

She effectively concludes:

> he helps people.

This could have made the initial rescue romantically disappointing.

Instead, it becomes the basis of a more interesting decision.

Hitagi wants to become special to a person whose first help was general.

That distinction protects the romance from a rescue-debt model.

## 5.2 `I love you` is controlled disclosure

Her first confession uses English.

The code switch is:

- comic;
- performative;
- emotionally controlled;
- still explicit.

It lets Hitagi make a high-stakes statement while preserving stylistic control over the form.

The later relationship does not treat the line as sufficient proof of eternal knowledge.

It begins negotiation.

## 5.3 `好きになる努力`: love is prospective work

Hitagi does not simply declare that fate has revealed a soulmate.

She speaks about making the effort to come to love/like Araragi.

That is one of the earliest statements of the series' mature relational philosophy.

Love is not validated because:

- rescue occurred;
- destiny selected them;
- pain created perfect mutual access.

Love is something they intend to **practice**.

This becomes even more important in Final Season, when specialness is explicitly tied to continued choosing.

## 5.4 Intimacy initially grows without address collapse

Hitagi continues to use `阿良々木くん`.

That matters.

The relationship becomes romantic while formal social distance remains encoded in address.

Physical and emotional intimacy grow through:

- disclosure;
- voluntary touch;
- dates;
- promises;
- arguments;
- negotiated boundaries.

The language does not instantly become first-name intimate merely because a confession happened.

This makes the later V18 name shift much more meaningful.

## 5.5 The relationship begins with an epistemic promise

The Hachikuji visibility misunderstanding produces a rule:

- do not pretend to see what you do not see;
- do not pretend not to see what you do see;
- speak disagreements rather than silently constructing motives.

This is more than paranormal case technique.

It becomes an ideal for the romance.

Notably, both characters later violate the spirit of that ideal through concealment.

The promise therefore functions as a standard against which the relationship can be judged, not evidence that they immediately become perfectly transparent.

# 6. V03: love becomes exchange without ownership

## 6.1 The starry-sky date is an offering of available self

The first planned date is important because Hitagi organizes it.

She is not a passive reward waiting for Araragi to claim romance.

The date includes:

- her father;
- her history;
- her capacity to tutor;
- Kanbaru as part of her social world;
- the starry sky as a treasured memory.

She offers what she presently has.

This should not be read as a total inventory of self.

It is a chosen act of disclosure.

## 6.2 The recovered family memory is not a restoration fantasy

The starry sky belongs to a family past that no longer exists.

Hitagi can preserve that memory as valuable without requiring:

- her mother to return;
- the marriage to be restored;
- the old household to be redeemed.

This is one of her earliest examples of a pattern that later dominates V27 and V30:

> **history can remain valuable without retaining jurisdiction over the present.**

## 6.3 Sexual boundary is direct and relationship-preserving

Hitagi tells Araragi that sexual intimacy still frightens her in the aftermath of assault and asks for time.

The importance is not merely that Araragi respects the boundary.

Hitagi herself can now do what early Hitagi could not safely do:

- disclose vulnerability;
- specify a limit;
- expect the relation to survive that limit.

The subsequent kiss does not erase the boundary.

It demonstrates that intimacy does not need to be total to be real.

## 6.4 Her father sees entrusting weight as a relational act

Hitagi's father describes her as someone who loves but does not necessarily know how to express it easily, and frames trusted intimacy through the language of entrusting weight.

The image should not be universalized into "a woman needs a man to carry her burden."

The V01 ritual already made Hitagi responsible for reclaiming her own weight.

The relational development is different:

> **having taken ownership of her weight, she can voluntarily allow another person to share some of it.**

That is the mature direction of the title metaphor.

# 7. Kanbaru: asymmetrical intimacy without fake reciprocity

## 7.1 The damaged relationship predates Araragi's romance

Kanbaru's connection to Hitagi cannot be reduced to "the girl who wanted Araragi's girlfriend."

They have a substantial middle-school history.

Hitagi's post-trauma withdrawal harms that bond.

Kanbaru retains:

- admiration;
- loyalty;
- romantic feeling;
- resentment;
- idealization.

Their later relation matters independently of Araragi.

## 7.2 Hitagi refuses the comforting lie of symmetrical feeling

At the V02 confrontation, Kanbaru states love.

Hitagi does not say:

> I love you exactly the same way.

She states non-reciprocity.

Then she asks Kanbaru to remain nearby and apologizes for the delay.

This is one of the clearest examples of Hitagi's mature potential already visible early:

> **truthful asymmetry can preserve a relationship better than fake reciprocity.**

## 7.3 Holding beast and human arms together is the right image

Hitagi physically holds Kanbaru's transformed arm and human arm.

The image is unusually strong because it refuses purification logic.

Kanbaru does not need to become:

- innocent;
- non-jealous;
- nonviolent;
- non-sexual;
- non-oddity-linked

before the relation can continue.

Hitagi can remain in relation with a person who contains incompatible material.

This is a major anti-idealization trait.

## 7.4 Pre-crab Hitagi/Kanbaru also contains power asymmetry

SHORT-017 deepens the earlier bond.

Kanbaru's self-subordinating `犬` language and Hitagi's management of follower retaliation show:

- care;
- hierarchy;
- influence;
- social risk.

The relationship should not be romanticized merely because it was intense.

The adult version becomes stronger precisely because the two can later reconnect without recreating the same adolescent status structure.

## 7.5 V30 confirms independent adult friendship

By V30, Kanbaru is not simply Araragi's route to Hitagi.

Their renewed closeness is an adult fact.

This is important for Hitagi's characterization because it proves that marriage has not collapsed her social world into the couple.

# 8. Protection can reproduce control: the Kaiki pressure test

## 8.1 Hitagi's trauma history makes Kaiki uniquely destabilizing

Kaiki is not simply an annoying ex-acquaintance.

His return touches:

- cult/fraud history;
- her mother's collapse;
- her family's economic and emotional destruction;
- her fear that the past can re-enter the present;
- Araragi's vulnerability to people she knows are dangerous.

The pressure therefore activates the oldest Hitagi response:

> control access.

## 8.2 Kidnapping Araragi is protective and coercive

Her protective commitment is genuine.

Her formulation that she will not again let go of something precious is emotionally intelligible.

But confinement and handcuffing fail the autonomy test.

This is an important character correction.

Hitagi's trauma does not merely make her a victim of others' control.

It can also make control feel justified when she is afraid of losing someone.

The series therefore refuses a simple moral progression from:

> controlled girl → harmlessly strong woman.

She can reproduce a version of the same problem.

## 8.3 `今の私`: Kaiki does not own the victim version of Hitagi

The confrontation with Kaiki produces one of her strongest identity assertions.

He has privileged access to ugly history.

That does not give him permanent authority to define her through that history.

Hitagi defends the present self.

This is not amnesia.

It is anti-ownership.

The earlier Hitagi was real.

The harmed Hitagi was real.

The present Hitagi is also real.

No perpetrator receives eternal interpretive rights merely because he knew her at a vulnerable time.

## 8.4 `悪の敵`: bounded opposition instead of heroic universalism

Hitagi resists grand universal justice language.

Her stance is more bounded.

This fits her character better than Araragi-style universal rescue.

She is often strongest when she can specify:

- this person;
- this risk;
- this relation;
- this boundary.

She does not need to become an abstract ally of justice to oppose something harmful.

## 8.5 Growth rather than "return"

Araragi explicitly resists describing Hitagi's post-Kaiki development as simply getting back an older self.

He chooses growth.

The haircut participates in that forward-facing model.

This matters because trauma stories often reward "restoration":

> become who you were before the wound.

Hitagi does not have to reverse history.

She can become someone who did not exist before it.

# 9. Hanekawa: Hitagi becomes a friend outside the romance triangle

## 9.1 V08 is essential because Araragi is not the author of the friendship

Hanekawa's V08 first-person narration radically increases the evidence available for Hitagi.

The relationship is direct.

Hitagi is not present merely as:

- Araragi's girlfriend;
- Hanekawa's romantic rival;
- a source of jealousy.

She becomes Hanekawa's friend.

## 9.2 Hitagi's care is active, physical, domestic, and conflict-capable

The sequence includes:

- Hanekawa disclosing the tiger;
- Hitagi asking whether Hanekawa has ever said `助けて`;
- searching for her;
- finding her in the ruins;
- crying;
- striking her;
- sheltering her at 民倉荘;
- insisting on rest;
- feeding her;
- arguing with her;
- later crying with her over heartbreak.

This is not gentle care in a narrow sense.

It is recognizably Hitagi care.

She does not become soft enough to qualify as a friend.

Her friendship includes anger.

## 9.3 `白過ぎる`: Hitagi attacks universal acceptance

Hitagi's critique of Hanekawa's excessive whiteness/universal acceptance is one of the strongest diegetic counterpoints to Hanekawa's self-story.

The idea is not:

> good people are secretly bad.

It is:

> refusing to dislike, prefer, reject, or burden can become another form of distance.

Hitagi is almost structurally built to recognize this because she is the opposite kind of speaker.

She distinguishes.

She says:

- I like this;
- I do not like that;
- this is unacceptable;
- stay;
- leave;
- tell me;
- do not lie.

Her values can therefore offer Hanekawa something important.

But the monograph must preserve the epistemic ceiling:

> Hitagi's critique is powerful character evidence, not omniscient authorial doctrine.

## 9.4 The friendship rejects romantic zero-sum structure

Hanekawa can like Hitagi while loving Araragi.

Hitagi can understand Hanekawa while remaining Araragi's partner.

The triangle does not require:

- female rivalry as permanent identity;
- Hanekawa's humiliation;
- Hitagi's victory;
- possession of Araragi as the sole relation between them.

This is one of the most important decenterings in Hitagi's life.

## 9.5 Leaving shelter does not mean relational rupture

Hanekawa eventually leaves the temporary domestic shelter and moves toward another living arrangement.

The relationship survives.

That detail fits a major V2 relational principle:

> **care does not require indefinite containment.**

Hitagi can offer home without requiring the person to remain inside it.

# 10. Nadeko: adversarial care without sentimental reconciliation

## 10.1 The phone call makes "the girlfriend" into a speaking person

V11 initially positions Hitagi inside Nadeko's imagination as an obstacle.

The phone call changes that.

Hitagi becomes:

- tactically intelligent;
- specific;
- verbally dangerous;
- capable of bargaining;
- unwilling to perform friendship.

This is important because Hitagi is often most ethically legible when she refuses emotional counterfeit.

## 10.2 The six-month bargain creates time, not harmony

Hitagi negotiates a survival window.

She does not pretend:

- to forgive Nadeko;
- to like her;
- to accept the murder plan;
- that conflict has disappeared.

She creates **time**.

That is a form of care or harm-reduction without sentimental language.

It also demonstrates one of her strongest adultward skills:

> reduce immediate catastrophe first; do not require emotional resolution as the price of procedural survival.

## 10.3 Address matters

Hitagi's controlled `千石さん` and later full-name address keep distance visible.

This is not coldness as failure.

It can be appropriate precision.

The address refuses to transform lethal conflict into fake intimacy.

## 10.4 Kaiki intervention is strategic commissioning

In V13, Hitagi uses Kaiki's competence.

This is one of her most mature—and morally ambiguous—acts.

She can separate:

- what Kaiki did;
- what she feels about him;
- what he can do;
- what the current case needs.

She does not have to forgive him to hire him.

She does not have to call him good.

She does not have to rewrite the past.

The relationship becomes:

> former victim/perpetrator → strategic client/operator → gratitude without forgiveness.

## 10.5 `助かりました` is bounded rescue language

Hitagi can acknowledge that Kaiki helped.

That does not create absolution.

This is exactly the kind of distinction the larger series struggles to preserve.

Causal benefit does not grant moral ownership of the beneficiary's story.

## 10.6 Secrecy from Araragi remains ethically mixed

Hitagi keeps the operation secret and treats managing/deceiving Araragi as part of her relational responsibility.

The strategy is effective.

It is also autonomy-problematic.

This prevents the Kaiki arc from turning her into a perfect strategic partner.

Her old instinct remains visible:

> if I know the danger and care enough, I may be justified in controlling the information field.

That instinct will return in reverse when V30 judges Araragi's concealment from her.

# 11. Hitagi as a constraint on Araragi's self-destruction

## 11.1 She sees earlier than Araragi that his death is not private property

V02 is a major ethical hinge.

Araragi treats his own body as a cost he can volunteer.

Hitagi objects because relationship changes the accounting.

If he dies, the consequence is not located solely in him.

This is one of the earliest direct attacks on his self-erasure model.

## 11.2 She does not worship martyrdom

The romance therefore differs from a common heroic structure.

Hitagi does not become the partner who proves love by admiring Araragi's willingness to die.

She often treats that willingness as:

- frightening;
- irresponsible;
- relationally costly.

This matters because the series does not ask her to become worthy of his sacrifice.

It asks whether sacrifice is the right form of love at all.

## 11.3 Her own control problem complicates the critique

Hitagi is not a perfect external conscience.

When frightened, she can overcontrol.

That makes the relationship more useful analytically.

They correct different versions of the same problem.

Araragi can treat his body as privately allocable.

Hitagi can treat protection as permission to manage another person's choices.

The mature romance must constrain both.

## 11.4 Accountability is one function of love

The V02 relationship state becomes explicit supernatural-truth obligation.

Love gives Hitagi standing to say:

- do not conceal this;
- your death affects me;
- your oddity life is not irrelevant to the relation.

Standing is not ownership.

That distinction becomes one of the most important throughlines into V30.

# 12. Ordinary continuity is a positive value

## 12.1 Hitagi rejects the idea that dramatic instability is deeper

Her `格好悪くていい` stance supports an unusually important series value.

Ordinary continuity can be good.

A life does not need to remain:

- dangerous;
- tragic;
- aesthetically extreme;
- supernaturally intense

in order to count as meaningful.

This is particularly powerful coming from a character introduced through spectacular trauma.

## 12.2 V14–V15 make routine relationship labor visible

Later high-school romance increasingly includes:

- study planning;
- university logistics;
- handmade food;
- exam support;
- transport;
- scheduling;
- ordinary future decisions.

These actions are not filler between "real" supernatural arcs.

They are what a durable relationship looks like when crisis is no longer the only narrative engine.

## 12.3 Absence from an arc is not disappearance from life

Hitagi is absent or peripheral in several major arcs.

The architecture explicitly treats this as evidence of decentered life rather than narrative nullification.

Her absence can mean:

- Araragi is elsewhere;
- another narrator has authority;
- the case does not concern her;
- she is continuing school/work/relationships offstage.

This becomes particularly important in Off and Monster Seasons.

A mature ensemble cannot prove character importance by forcing every major person into every case.

## 12.4 Kanbaru's V10 memory shows relational persistence without presence

Hitagi is physically absent from much of Kanbaru's narrated development.

Yet the relationship remains internal to Kanbaru's self-understanding.

This is a strong example of why absence can be evidence.

Influence can persist without narrative occupation.

# 13. Specialness by selection rather than metaphysical uniqueness

## 13.1 V17 rejects the frightening version of absolute bond

The return of Shinobu's first thrall pressures Araragi's idea of uniqueness.

Hitagi offers one of the corpus's mature relationship formulations.

An `絶対的な絆` can be frightening.

Security does not require proving that no alternative relationship was ever possible.

## 13.2 `特別な人間` is unnecessary

Hitagi distinguishes:

- being metaphysically exceptional;
- being someone's special person.

The latter is maintained through choice and effort.

This strongly develops V01's `好きになる努力`.

The romance therefore forms a consistent philosophy:

> **specialness is made through relation, not discovered as destiny.**

## 13.3 `私達はあなたを選んでいる`

This statement matters because it includes plural relation.

Araragi is not special because he is cosmically singular.

He is special because people choose him.

The same logic preserves their freedom.

If specialness is choice, then:

- relationship requires maintenance;
- alternatives can exist without invalidating the present;
- another person's prior history is not a threat by definition.

This is mature non-possession.

# 14. First names: intimacy arrives late and deliberately

## 14.1 `阿良々木くん` is not evidence of emotional distance

Hitagi retains this address through substantial intimacy.

That makes it a stable voice marker rather than a simple relationship-meter.

The pair can be:

- lovers;
- sexually negotiating;
- emotionally interdependent;
- future-planning

while `阿良々木くん` remains normal.

## 14.2 V18 makes the first-name transition explicit

White Day produces:

- `ひたぎ`;
- `暦`.

Hitagi asks for the change as her gift.

The transition is mutually enacted.

That matters because the series does not assume that marriage-style intimacy should automatically appear once romance begins.

Even address is negotiated.

## 14.3 The change is about continuity, not possession

The first-name shift occurs in a post-high-school future-facing context.

It signals:

- chosen continuity;
- greater ordinary intimacy;
- movement beyond school-role grammar.

It does not mean:

- total access;
- erased boundaries;
- permanent fusion.

The later surname problem in V30 will make that distinction even more explicit.

# 15. Retrospective apology: judging the past without declaring the past self unreal

## 15.1 V27 reveals Hitagi behind the apology monologues

A major attribution lock is necessary here.

The recurring apology material in `おうぎライト` belongs to Hitagi, not Ougi.

This matters because it gives Hitagi a retrospective voice on her own earlier aggression.

## 15.2 She can judge cutter/stapler behavior more harshly later

Growth allows Hitagi to look backward and say, in effect:

> some of that was wrong.

The project must avoid two symmetrical errors.

One:

> the later apology proves the earlier Hitagi was fake.

Two:

> because the earlier behavior was trauma-shaped, apology is unnecessary.

The mature model is:

> **the younger Hitagi was real; the adultward Hitagi can acquire reasons to judge that real self differently.**

## 15.3 `妖魔令` does not explain away remorse

The oddity compulsion matters.

It does not become a master excuse.

Hitagi thinks some apology might eventually have happened without it.

This preserves agency.

Supernatural causation can pressure an act without rewriting all underlying moral development.

## 15.4 Apology cannot own shared history

The V27 volume also interrogates apology as possible violence.

If one person performs total self-condemnation, the other can be forced into a corresponding victim role.

This fits Hitagi's broader development.

Repair must be relational.

The person apologizing does not receive unilateral authority to decide:

- what the event meant;
- when closure occurs;
- who the other person is now.

This is a sophisticated extension of her V01 access ethics.

# 16. Career and adulthood: romance does not absorb the life

## 16.1 V30 Hitagi has an independent high-responsibility career

At twenty-four, Hitagi is working as a young leader in the Japanese branch of a foreign-affiliated financial firm.

This is important for the character monograph because adult competence is not confined to emotional recovery.

She has built:

- professional identity;
- responsibility;
- career trajectory;
- institutional standing.

The relationship does not become her only adult project.

## 16.2 Marriage is materially long-distance

At the start of V30:

- Araragi is based in Washington, D.C., in an FBI-linked professional period;
- Hitagi remains in Japan;
- the newly married couple is materially long-distance.

This is a strong anti-fusion fact.

The culmination of romance is not immediate permanent cohabitation.

They continue to have:

- separate institutions;
- separate work;
- logistical constraints;
- different networks.

## 16.3 Hitagi is again staying with her father

V30 establishes her current Japan-side living situation at 民倉荘 with her father.

This should not be turned into a simple "family reconciliation completed" claim.

The important point is longitudinal.

The father relationship has survived beyond the school-era trauma context and remains part of Hitagi's adult practical life.

The absent mother remains absent.

Continuity does not require restoring the original family configuration.

## 16.4 The honeymoon is partly her project

Hitagi is an active architect of the honeymoon.

She is not simply the person being taken on a trip.

This fits the lifelong pattern:

> she wants to choose the terms under which intimacy occurs.

## 16.5 The post-honeymoon financial crisis remains underdescribed

The V30 coda produces:

- a financial crisis involving her company;
- joint crisis consequences;
- witness protection.

The source intentionally does not tell us enough to reconstruct:

- exact cause;
- Hitagi's full actions;
- career outcome;
- long-term home outcome.

The monograph therefore preserves those as OPEN.

Adult professional competence should not be filled in with invented details merely because the character seems capable.

# 17. Marriage and naming: `阿良々木ひたぎ` is her evidence, not Araragi's guilt

## 17.1 Araragi initially overinterprets the surname change

Marriage causes Araragi to worry that taking his name has erased something essential about Hitagi.

The concern can be structurally intelligible.

The mistake is epistemic.

He moves from:

> a naming institution may contain asymmetry

to:

> therefore Hitagi must experience this particular name as loss.

V30 refuses that move.

## 17.2 Hitagi explicitly likes `阿良々木ひたぎ`

This is decisive person-specific evidence.

Her statement does not establish a universal doctrine that marital surname change is good.

It establishes:

> **Araragi's guilt cannot override Hitagi's self-report about Hitagi.**

That is one of the cleanest adult relationship corrections in the entire supplied corpus.

## 17.3 `戦場ヶ原暦` shows the path was conceptually negotiable

The counterfactual possibility of Araragi becoming `戦場ヶ原暦` matters because it reveals that the naming outcome is not treated as metaphysical necessity.

Names have:

- legal consequences;
- social history;
- identity resonance.

They are still choices made inside institutions.

## 17.4 Naming matters without becoming essence

Hitagi does not cease to have a Senjougahara history because she becomes legally Araragi Hitagi.

The family-name discussion therefore rejects:

- name essentialism;
- name nihilism.

The old name matters.

The new name matters.

Neither alone contains the whole person.

## 17.5 Witness protection is the final stress test

By the ending, the relevant public names are withheld.

The family relation persists.

This proves that names are not the entire substance of family.

It does **not** prove that names never mattered.

The final lesson is functional:

> names organize recognition; relation can survive their temporary removal.

# 18. Shinobu and household: Hitagi becomes a co-author rather than a recipient of family design

## 18.1 Araragi's first mistake is procedural

He imagines bringing Shinobu into a daughter relation.

The possible content is not automatically wrong.

The method is.

He initially treats it as a benevolent plan he can present to the household.

Hachikuji and Kanbaru expose the problem before Hitagi even enters the main deliberation.

## 18.2 Hitagi has independent standing

Marriage gives Hitagi real standing in decisions that restructure:

- household risk;
- family status;
- future parenting;
- supernatural disclosure.

This is not ownership of Shinobu.

It is not ownership of Araragi.

It is standing because the proposed structure governs her life too.

## 18.3 Hitagi has independently thought about Shinobu

V30 importantly reveals that she has considered Shinobu since before the wedding.

This prevents a model in which Hitagi only reacts to Araragi's idea.

She has her own family imagination.

## 18.4 Withheld predation history matters

Hitagi cannot make an informed family decision if Araragi withholds that Shinobu historically ate humans.

When the information is finally disclosed, Hitagi gains the ability to decide from an adequate evidence state.

This sharply strengthens the V01 relational promise:

> do not manage the relationship by pretending not to see what matters.

## 18.5 Hitagi rejects inherited guilt

Her response draws on her own family history.

One relative's wrongdoing does not automatically transfer guilt to every family member.

This is a particularly powerful Hitagi proposition because she has lived inside destructive inherited burden.

She refuses both:

- innocence by concealment;
- guilt by blood/family association.

## 18.6 Her willingness to share risk is not unlimited jurisdiction

Hitagi goes very far in accepting Shinobu.

She can imagine:

- daughterhood;
- blood-sharing;
- personal risk.

But Shinobu raises hypothetical future children.

Hitagi's willingness cannot consent for them.

This preserves a key mature limit:

> **one adult can volunteer personal risk without assigning the same risk to future dependents.**

## 18.7 Trial-based integration fits Hitagi better than fiat

Hitagi proposes something more revisable than Araragi's initial fait accompli.

This is consistent with her mature relationship philosophy.

A household arrangement can be:

- attempted;
- observed;
- revised.

That is much closer to `好きになる努力` than to destiny.

## 18.8 Emotional family status is stronger than legal-adoption proof

The supplied V30 boundary strongly supports emotional eldest-daughter framing.

It does not clearly prove completed civil adoption.

D05 therefore freezes:

- **family framing: strong**;
- **formal legal adoption: OPEN**.

That distinction is especially important in a document about names and household.

# 19. Hitagi's father and the non-restoration model of family

The father relationship is one of the quietest but most important parts of Hitagi's development.

The opening family history is catastrophic.

Yet the series never requires a complete family restoration.

Her father can become:

- present;
- supportive;
- part of a date;
- a current co-resident in V30

without the mother returning or the old household being recreated.

This matters because Hitagi's entire arc resists the idea that healing means returning to a pristine original.

The same structure appears in:

- her body;
- her hair;
- her name;
- her friendships;
- her marriage.

A later state can be good without being a restoration.

# 20. Voice reconstruction: stable Hitagi and changing Hitagi

This section supplies the D05 primary-home material that future D18 and D25 should compile rather than reinvent.

## 20.1 Baseline register

High-confidence markers include:

- `私`;
- `阿良々木くん` through most of the school-era romance;
- later `暦` after the explicit V18 transition;
- `～わ`;
- `～のよ`;
- `～かしら`;
- `～て頂戴`;
- polished full clauses;
- deadpan delivery;
- controlled threats;
- precise premise-setting.

The core voice property is not any one suffix.

It is:

> **controlled linguistic surface paired with disproportionate semantic pressure.**

## 20.2 She rarely sounds emotionally sloppy by default

Hitagi can be highly emotional.

But her ordinary speech often keeps emotion under syntactic control.

Under stress, that can become:

- sharper;
- more controlling;
- more strategically explicit.

When she genuinely loses that control—crying with Hanekawa, for example—the deviation matters because it is not the baseline.

## 20.3 She often speaks in relational terms rather than abstract moral universalism

Compared with Araragi, Hitagi is more likely to frame:

- who is involved;
- what they owe;
- what they know;
- what they are allowed to decide.

This contributes to her effectiveness as his moral counterweight.

It also explains why her own failures tend to involve **overclaiming standing** rather than abstract heroism.

## 20.4 Direct dislike is part of honesty

Hitagi is capable of saying that she does not reciprocate Kanbaru's feelings equally.

She can hate Kaiki while using him.

She can bargain with Nadeko without friendship.

This means simulation should not soften her into universal empathy language.

She can care without liking.

She can protect without forgiving.

She can cooperate without moral reconciliation.

## 20.5 She uses humor to control exposure

Her jokes often do at least one of four things:

1. reassert conversational control;
2. convert intimacy into manageable form;
3. punish Araragi for an unwanted premise;
4. test whether the other person can keep pace.

The correct reconstruction is therefore not "she insults because she is embarrassed."

Sometimes embarrassment matters.

Often the joke is more strategic.

## 20.6 Araragi-specific grammar

With Araragi, the most reliable procedure is:

> **premise seizure → deadpan escalation → Araragi tsukkomi/defense → Hitagi reframing → occasional sudden sincerity.**

That last movement is important.

A serious line can land harder because the dialogue has trained the listener to expect the blade.

## 20.7 Hanekawa-specific grammar

With Hanekawa, Hitagi is less dependent on comic attack.

The relation permits:

- direct care;
- argument;
- anger;
- practical feeding/shelter;
- emotional disclosure.

She can become more openly protective because Hanekawa does not occupy the same defensive-tsukkomi role as Araragi.

## 20.8 Kanbaru-specific grammar

With Kanbaru:

- senior/junior history matters;
- direct asymmetry is allowed;
- care does not require romantic reciprocity;
- irritation and familiarity coexist.

Do not simulate Hitagi as cruelly dismissive merely because Kanbaru is sexually provocative.

The longitudinal relation is much thicker.

## 20.9 Kaiki-specific grammar

With Kaiki, speech becomes strategically hostile.

Key properties:

- low trust;
- high competence recognition;
- refusal of nostalgic ownership;
- no need to produce forgiveness.

The tone should preserve the fact that he is useful **and** hated.

## 20.10 Nadeko-specific grammar

With Nadeko, Hitagi can be coldly tactical.

She does not adopt an older-sister nurturing register merely because Nadeko is younger.

The central objective is survival and bargaining under lethal conditions.

## 20.11 Shinobu-specific adult grammar

V30 creates a more direct relation.

Hitagi is no longer only "Araragi's girlfriend who knows about the vampire."

She can address:

- predation;
- family;
- risk;
- blood;
- future children

from her own standing.

Future reconstruction should not route all Hitagi/Shinobu dialogue through Araragi's emotional interpretation.

# 21. Behavior reconstruction: how Hitagi tends to act

## 21.1 Under uncertainty

Likely tendencies:

- gather enough information to define the decision;
- challenge the premise if the offered options are artificially narrow;
- ask who has standing;
- prefer explicit terms over vague reassurance;
- use humor or aggression to regain conversational control.

## 21.2 Under threat to privacy

Early-state Hitagi:

- conceal;
- threaten;
- deter;
- demand noninvolvement.

Later-state Hitagi:

- more likely to disclose selectively;
- still highly sensitive to paternalistic intrusion;
- expects material partners to tell her what affects her.

## 21.3 Under threat to someone she loves

Persistent risk:

- overcontrol;
- conceal from the loved person;
- take unilateral protective action.

Growth makes her more capable of:

- consultation;
- strategic delegation;
- recognizing the other person's choice.

The risk never disappears completely.

## 21.4 When someone apologizes

Hitagi is unlikely to equate apology automatically with:

- forgiveness;
- reconciliation;
- restored trust.

Later corpus evidence makes her especially sensitive to the possibility that apology can itself impose a script on the other person.

## 21.5 When she dislikes someone but needs them

Kaiki provides the model.

She can:

- contract;
- negotiate;
- acknowledge successful help;
- preserve hatred;
- refuse absolution.

This is one of her most distinctive mature competencies.

## 21.6 When affection is safe

She can become:

- playful;
- sexually suggestive;
- demanding;
- practical;
- surprisingly direct;
- future-oriented.

She does not necessarily become linguistically soft.

Safe Hitagi remains Hitagi.

## 21.7 When another person refuses her

Her mature state is increasingly capable of treating refusal as information rather than betrayal.

But high-stakes protective contexts can still activate control.

This should remain a conditional rather than absolute reconstruction rule.

# 22. Negative constraints and common caricature traps

Future D25 reconstruction should explicitly block the following.

## Trap 1 — generic tsundere

Hitagi is not accurately reproduced by:

> insult → blush → deny affection.

Her aggression is usually more controlled, more verbal, more strategic, and less dependent on fluster.

## Trap 2 — trauma explains everything

Not every sharp line is a symptom.

The corpus supports genuine enduring personality:

- wit;
- competitiveness;
- preference;
- judgment;
- control.

Recovery does not erase those.

## Trap 3 — secretly soft means really kind

Hitagi can be kind.

She can also:

- dislike;
- resent;
- coerce;
- misjudge;
- strategically deceive.

"Soft underneath" is too simple.

## Trap 4 — Araragi is her entire life

False by the supplied boundary.

She has:

- father;
- Hanekawa;
- Kanbaru;
- work;
- independent judgment about Shinobu;
- professional obligations.

## Trap 5 — jealousy is her dominant relational trait

Jealous humor exists.

The mature corpus is much more interested in:

- chosen specialness;
- independent friendships;
- negotiation;
- non-possession.

## Trap 6 — marriage resolves her identity arc

V30 does the opposite.

Marriage creates new problems about:

- names;
- work;
- distance;
- disclosure;
- family standing;
- future children.

## Trap 7 — she always wants control

Control is a strong recurrent tendency.

But mature Hitagi can also:

- entrust;
- delegate;
- tolerate uncertainty;
- create trial arrangements;
- accept separate lives.

## Trap 8 — directness means full transparency

She can be verbally direct and strategically secretive.

V13 proves this.

## Trap 9 — forgiveness is required for growth

Kaiki is the clearest counterexample.

Growth can include effective cooperation and gratitude without absolution.

## Trap 10 — taking Araragi's surname means loss of Senjougahara self

V30 directly rejects imposing that interpretation on her.

# 28. Mature synthesis

Hitagi begins the series by trying to become inaccessible.

That is not the same as wanting to be alone in an abstract existential sense.

She has learned that access can be dangerous.

Family, religion, sexuality, illness, pity, and intervention have all become entangled with the possibility that another person will claim a right to define what is best for her.

So she weaponizes the perimeter.

Her body is almost weightless.

Her language is not.

She speaks with extraordinary control.

Polished femininity carries threats.

School stationery becomes weaponry.

A book can become a barrier.

Kindness can be classified as attack.

The first great change is not that Araragi melts the ice.

It is that Hitagi chooses to restore weight.

The distinction matters.

She does not recover because suffering is ennobling.

She recovers because the burden becomes hers to choose again.

That act defines the rest of her arc.

Love follows the same pattern.

Araragi's initial help was not unique to her.

Hitagi sees that.

Instead of demanding that the rescue prove destiny, she decides to pursue specialness prospectively.

`好きになる努力`.

That phrase is much closer to the mature Hitagi than any claim that she is secretly romantic beneath a hostile shell.

She is romantic in an unusually procedural way.

She wants to know.

She wants explicit answers.

She wants future effort.

She wants the right to state boundaries without the relationship collapsing.

On the starry-sky date, she offers what she has.

Not everything.

What she can offer now.

She can say that sexual intimacy is frightening.

The relation survives the limit.

This is the inverse of the opening demand for indifference.

Early Hitagi survives by making attention impossible.

Romantic Hitagi learns that attention can be negotiated.

Her other relationships prove that this development is not merely becoming a girlfriend.

Kanbaru forces her to practice asymmetrical care.

Hitagi can say:

> I do not love you in the same way.

And also:

> stay.

That is more respectful than counterfeit reciprocity.

Hanekawa forces her to practice friendship without romantic zero-sum logic.

Hitagi can:

- search;
- feed;
- shelter;
- rage;
- cry;
- criticize.

She can care for a person she does not own and whose love for Araragi she cannot erase.

Kaiki forces the most difficult separation of all:

- competence from goodness;
- gratitude from forgiveness;
- historical access from present authority.

Hitagi can use a person she hates because the case requires it.

She can acknowledge help.

She does not have to redeem him.

Nadeko reveals a colder version of the same skill.

Care need not look like affection.

Sometimes the right act is to create six months.

Sometimes the person threatening your life does not need you to pretend to like her.

Through all of this, Hitagi remains dangerous.

Her protectiveness can become control.

She can kidnap Araragi to keep him from Kaiki.

She can keep the Nadeko operation secret.

Her recovery does not automatically cure the instinct to manage access when loss feels imminent.

That is why the Araragi/Hitagi pairing is more ethically interesting than "he saves her, then she keeps him grounded."

They are capable of correcting each other's different forms of unilateralism.

Araragi says:

> my body is mine to spend.

Hitagi says:

> no, your death enters my life too.

Hitagi says:

> I know the danger, so I will control the situation.

The later series replies:

> care gives you standing, not unlimited jurisdiction.

Final Season gives the romance its mature philosophy.

The pair do not need an absolute bond that eliminates alternatives.

They do not need to be metaphysically unique people.

Specialness can be maintained through choice.

That is the adult version of `好きになる努力`.

The first-name transition is therefore perfectly placed.

`ひたぎ / 暦` arrives after years of relation.

It is requested.

It is enacted.

It is not assumed.

Then V30 turns the same logic toward marriage.

Araragi initially treats the surname change as if he knows what it must mean to Hitagi.

He is wrong.

Hitagi likes `阿良々木ひたぎ`.

That answer does not settle Japanese family law for everyone.

It settles Hitagi's own experience.

Her husband does not get to replace her evidence with his guilt.

At twenty-four, she has become someone the opening Araragi could hardly have modeled:

- married;
- professionally senior for her age;
- working in finance;
- long-distance from her husband;
- again living with her father;
- independently close to Kanbaru;
- independently thinking about Shinobu;
- still sharp enough to reject a bad ethical frame.

Marriage does not complete her.

It creates a new access problem.

Who decides what Shinobu is to the family?

What must be disclosed?

Whose risk counts?

Can two willing adults assign danger to future children?

Can love replace third-party oversight?

The mature Hitagi does not solve these questions by becoming endlessly accepting.

She solves them by insisting that the people governed by a relationship must have standing inside its design.

That is the deepest continuity of the character.

At the beginning, she controls access because other people have made access dangerous.

At the end of the supplied boundary, she participates in building structures where access can be:

- informed;
- negotiated;
- revisable;
- shared.

The best compact formulation is:

> **Hitagi's arc is not from weapon to warmth. It is from weaponized boundary to negotiated burden-sharing.**

She does not give up sharpness.

She gives it new jobs.

The same precision that once said:

> stay out

can later say:

> tell me the truth;

> do not die as though it concerns only you;

> I do not forgive you, but I can use your help;

> I like this name;

> this person may become family;

> my willingness does not decide for a future child.

That is why "weight" remains the right metaphor.

The mature Hitagi is not healed by becoming light.

She is healed, insofar as the supplied corpus allows the word at all, by becoming increasingly capable of deciding **which weight is hers, which weight is shared, and which weight she refuses to let another person assign to her.**
